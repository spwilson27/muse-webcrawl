Enteral nutrition

Enteral nutrition is the delivery of nutrients into the gastrointestinal tract, typically through a feeding tube, for people who cannot eat enough by mouth but whose gut can digest and absorb food. It is preferred over intravenous feeding when the digestive tract is functional.

Routes and formulas

Short-term access commonly uses nasogastric or nasojejunal tubes; longer-term feeding may use a gastrostomy or jejunostomy. Formulas range from standard polymeric feeds to specialized mixtures for diabetes, kidney disease, malabsorption, or critical illness.

Indications

Enteral feeding is used after stroke, in some cancers, in severe burns, in premature infants who cannot coordinate sucking and swallowing, and in disorders that make oral intake unsafe. It helps maintain gut integrity and may reduce infectious complications compared with parenteral nutrition.

Risks and care

Complications include tube displacement, aspiration, diarrhea, constipation, and metabolic abnormalities. Care teams confirm tube placement, elevate the head of the bed when appropriate, and monitor electrolytes and glucose. See also parenteral nutrition and feeding tube.
