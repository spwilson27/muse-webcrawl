Whey

Whey is the liquid remaining after milk has been curdled and strained. It is a by-product of cheese and casein manufacture and contains water, lactose, minerals and soluble whey proteins such as beta-lactoglobulin and alpha-lactalbumin.

Types and uses

Sweet whey comes from rennet-coagulated cheeses, while acid whey is produced when lactic acid bacteria or added acid curdle milk, as in strained yoghurt. Whey may be dried into powder for foods, animal feed, infant formula ingredients and protein supplements. Liquid whey is also used in baking, fermented drinks and some traditional dishes.

Nutrition and processing

Whey protein is valued for its amino-acid profile and rapid digestion. Industrial processing uses filtration to make whey protein concentrate and isolate. Because whey has high biological oxygen demand, large volumes must be managed carefully to avoid water pollution.
