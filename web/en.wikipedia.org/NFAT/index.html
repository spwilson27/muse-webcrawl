NFAT

Nuclear factor of activated T cells (NFAT) is a family of transcription factors shown to be important in immune response. One or more members of the NFAT family are expressed in most cells of the immune system, and NFAT is also involved in the development of cardiac, skeletal muscle, and nervous systems.

Family members

The family comprises five proteins in mammals: NFATc1, NFATc2, NFATc3, NFATc4 (the calcium-regulated NFATc family), and NFAT5 (TonEBP), which responds to osmotic stress. The NFATc proteins share a Rel-homology region with NF-κB family transcription factors.

Mechanism

In resting cells, NFATc proteins are heavily phosphorylated and reside in the cytoplasm. A rise in intracellular calcium activates the phosphatase calcineurin, which dephosphorylates NFAT and exposes its nuclear localisation signal; NFAT then enters the nucleus and, often cooperating with AP-1 proteins, switches on genes such as interleukin-2 that drive T-cell activation. Immunosuppressant drugs including ciclosporin and tacrolimus work by inhibiting calcineurin, thereby blocking NFAT activation.
