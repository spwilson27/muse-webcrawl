1896 United States presidential election

The 1896 United States presidential election was the 28th quadrennial presidential election, held on Tuesday, November 3, 1896. Republican former governor William McKinley of Ohio defeated Democratic former representative William Jennings Bryan of Nebraska, in a campaign dominated by the debate over the money supply — the gold standard versus "free silver."

Background

The Panic of 1893 and the ensuing depression devastated the Democratic Party of President Grover Cleveland. At the Democratic convention, Bryan's "Cross of Gold" speech won him the nomination at age 36, the youngest major-party nominee in U.S. history, on a platform of free silver coinage at a 16-to-1 ratio.

Campaign

Bryan revolutionized campaigning by touring the country by train and speaking to millions, while McKinley conducted a disciplined "front porch" campaign from Canton, Ohio, organized by Mark Hanna and financed heavily by business interests defending the gold standard. The Populist Party also endorsed Bryan with a different running mate, creating a fusion ticket.

Result and significance

McKinley carried the industrial Northeast and Midwest and won a clear victory. Historians often treat 1896 as a realigning election that began the "System of 1896," a period of Republican dominance and high voter turnout patterns that lasted until 1932.
