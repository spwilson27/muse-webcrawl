Lorica hamata

The lorica hamata is a type of mail armour used by soldiers of the Roman Republic and Empire. Made of interlocking iron rings — typically alternating rows of riveted and punched rings, each around 5–9 mm in diameter — a shirt could contain tens of thousands of rings and weigh roughly 10 kg.

The Romans adopted mail from Celtic peoples, and it remained in service for centuries, worn by legionaries and auxiliaries, often with shoulder doubling for extra protection. Flexible and repairable, it was eventually supplemented by the lorica segmentata but outlasted it, continuing into the late empire. The name derives from Latin hamus, "hook".
