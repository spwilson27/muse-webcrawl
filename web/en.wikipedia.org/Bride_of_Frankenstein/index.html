Bride of Frankenstein is a 1935 American science fiction horror film directed by James Whale for Universal Pictures. A sequel to Frankenstein (1931), it is widely regarded as one of the greatest horror films ever made and the finest of the classic Universal monster series. Boris Karloff reprises his role as the Monster, while Elsa Lanchester plays both Mary Shelley in the prologue and the Monster's mate, the Bride.


Elsa Lanchester as the Bride.

Plot

Surviving the windmill fire, the Monster wanders the countryside and befriends a blind hermit who teaches him speech. Meanwhile the sinister Dr Pretorius persuades Henry Frankenstein to resume his experiments and create a female companion for the Monster. The Bride is brought to life but recoils from the Monster in horror; despairing, he destroys the laboratory, telling Frankenstein and Elizabeth to live while declaring "We belong dead."

Production

Jack Pierce designed the Bride's iconic look: a tall, beehive-like hairstyle with lightning-streak white stripes, inspired by Nefertiti. Franz Waxman's score became a landmark of film music. The film balanced horror with camp humour and pathos, and the Monster's newfound speech divided contemporary critics even as the film succeeded at the box office.

Legacy

The film is preserved in the United States National Film Registry (1998). Its imagery — especially the Bride — has become a permanent part of popular culture, and critics note its themes of loneliness, creation, and rejection of the outsider.
