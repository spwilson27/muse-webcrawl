Western Ghats

Western Ghats (Sahyadri)
Location: Western India
Length: about 1,600 km
Highest peak: Anamudi (2,695 m)
States: Gujarat, Maharashtra, Goa, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu
UNESCO World Heritage Site: listed 2012

The Western Ghats, also known as the Sahyadri, are a mountain range running parallel to the western coast of India for some 1,600 kilometres, from the Tapti River in Gujarat southward through Maharashtra, Goa, Karnataka and Kerala to Kanyakumari in Tamil Nadu. They form the western edge of the Deccan Plateau and intercept the moisture-laden southwest monsoon winds.

Biodiversity

The range is one of the world's recognised biodiversity hotspots. Its montane rainforests and shola-grassland mosaics support thousands of endemic species, including the lion-tailed macaque, the Nilgiri tahr and a remarkable diversity of amphibians. In 2012 a chain of 39 component sites in the Ghats was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.

Geology and rivers

The Ghats are fault-block mountains formed as the western margin of the Indian plate rifted, and their eastern slopes give rise to major peninsular rivers such as the Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri, while short, swift rivers flow west to the Arabian Sea.
