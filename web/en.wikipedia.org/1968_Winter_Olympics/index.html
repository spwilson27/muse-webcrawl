The 1968 Winter Olympics, officially known as the X Olympic Winter Games, were held from 6 to 18 February 1968 in Grenoble, France. President Charles de Gaulle opened the Games, which were the first Winter Olympics broadcast in colour television and the first at which East and West Germany competed as separate teams.

Highlights

French alpine skier Jean-Claude Killy was the star of the Games, winning all three men's alpine events — downhill, giant slalom and slalom — emulating Toni Sailer's 1956 triple. His slalom victory came after Austrian Karl Schranz was disqualified in controversial fog-bound circumstances. American figure skater Peggy Fleming won the only gold medal for the United States at the Games. Italian bobsledder Eugenio Monti won two golds and the first Pierre de Coubertin medal for fair play in recognition of his sportsmanship at the 1964 Games.

Norway topped the medal table for the first time since 1952, with six golds and fourteen medals overall, ahead of the Soviet Union. Speed skater Toini Gustafsson of Sweden won two golds and a silver. The Soviet Union won the ice hockey tournament for the third consecutive time.

The Games were the first Winter Olympics at which doping tests and sex verification tests were carried out. Events were spread across venues in Grenoble and the surrounding resorts, including Chamrousse, Alpe d'Huez, Villard-de-Lans and Autrans.
