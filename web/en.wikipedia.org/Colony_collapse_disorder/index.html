Colony collapse disorder

Colony collapse disorder (CCD) is an abnormal phenomenon in which the majority of worker bees in a honey bee colony disappear, leaving behind the queen, brood and ample food stores.

History

Large-scale losses were reported in North America in the winter of 2006–2007, when some beekeepers lost 30–90% of their hives, alarming growers dependent on bees for pollination of crops such as almonds.

Causes

No single cause has been proven. Research implicates a combination of the Varroa destructor mite, viruses such as deformed wing virus, pesticides including neonicotinoids, poor nutrition and migratory beekeeping stress.
