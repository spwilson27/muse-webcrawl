The Manhattan Bridge is a suspension bridge that crosses the East River in New York City, connecting Canal Street in Lower Manhattan with Flatbush Avenue in Downtown Brooklyn. It was the last of the three suspension bridges built across the lower East River, following the Brooklyn Bridge and the Williamsburg Bridge. The bridge was designed by noted bridge engineer Leon Moisseiff and is significant as the first major suspension bridge to use deflection theory in its design, allowing a much slimmer deck structure.

History

Construction began in 1901 under the name "Bridge No. 3"; the bridge was renamed the Manhattan Bridge in 1902. It opened to pedestrian and then vehicle traffic on 31 December 1909 and fully in 1912. The ornate Manhattan approach was designed by Carrère and Hastings, architects of the New York Public Library, and includes a Beaux-Arts arch and colonnade plaza. The bridge's four subway tracks — two on each side of the lower level — carry the B, D, N, and Q trains of the New York City Subway, and their asymmetrical loading caused the deck to twist noticeably over the decades, a problem addressed in major rehabilitation programmes from the 1980s through the 2000s, in which many cables and suspenders were replaced.

For much of the 20th century the bridge's pedestrian path was closed or neglected; the walkway reopened in 2001 and a dedicated bikeway on the north side in 2004. The famous view down Washington Street in DUMBO, Brooklyn — framing the Empire State Building within one of the bridge's tower legs — has become one of New York's most photographed scenes.

Structure

The bridge is a two-level steel suspension bridge with two main cables anchored on both shores. The upper level carries four lanes of roadway; the lower level carries three lanes (configured reversibly), four subway tracks, and the pedestrian and bicycle paths on its edges. Its relatively flexible, shallow deck — a consequence of the then-novel deflection-theory design — contrasts with the deep stiffening trusses of the earlier Brooklyn Bridge.
