Kazan Kremlin

The Kazan Kremlin (Russian: Казанский Кремль, romanized: Kazansky Kreml; Tatar: Казан кирмәне) is the chief historic citadel of Russia, situated in the city of Kazan. It was built at the behest of Ivan the Terrible on the ruins of the former castle of Kazan khans. It was declared a World Heritage Site by UNESCO in 2000.

History

The Kazan Kremlin includes many old buildings, the oldest of which is the Annunciation Cathedral (1554–62), the only 16th-century Russian church to have six piers and five apses. Like many of Kazan's buildings of the period, it is constructed of local pale sandstone rather than of brick. The cathedral bell tower was erected in five tiers at the urging of Ivan the Terrible and is said to resemble the Ivan the Great Bell Tower in Moscow.

The Kremlin walls and towers were built in the 16th and 17th centuries, but there are later reconstructions too. The Spasskaya Tower, which serves as the main entrance to the citadel, was built in the 16th century. The northern wall of the Kremlin is pierced by the secret Tainitskaya Tower, so named because it used to lead to a secret water well.

The leaning Söyembikä Tower is perhaps the most famous symbol of Kazan. According to legend, the tower was built in a week by order of Ivan the Terrible for the Tatar princess Söyembikä, who threw herself from its top rather than marry the tsar. The tower's inclination, once about 194 cm, was stabilised in the 1930s and again in the 1990s.

Landmarks

  • Annunciation Cathedral (1554–1562) — the oldest surviving building of the citadel.
  • Qolşärif Mosque — rebuilt in 2005 on the site of a legendary 16th-century mosque destroyed in 1552; one of the largest mosques in Europe.
  • Söyembikä Tower — the leaning gate tower, a symbol of the city.
  • Spasskaya Tower — the main entrance tower, topped in the Soviet era by a red star.
  • Governor's Palace (1845–48) — now the residence of the President of the Republic of Tatarstan.
  • Consistory Palace and the Cannon Foundry yard.

UNESCO recognition

In 2000 the Historic and Architectural Complex of the Kazan Kremlin was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List under criteria (ii), (iii) and (iv), as the only surviving Tatar fortress in Russia and an outstanding example of a synthesis of Russian, Tatar and European architectural influences. Major restoration accompanied the millennium celebrations of Kazan in 2005.
