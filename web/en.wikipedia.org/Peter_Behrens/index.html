Peter Behrens (14 April 1868 – 27 February 1940) was a German architect, graphic and industrial designer, and typographer, regarded as a founding figure of modern industrial design. His work for the electrical company AEG is considered the first comprehensive corporate identity programme.

Career

Behrens began as a painter and graphic artist associated with Jugendstil (German Art Nouveau) and the Darmstadt Artists' Colony, where his own house (1901) was an early total work of art. In 1907 he became artistic consultant to AEG in Berlin, designing not only products — fans, kettles, lamps — but also the company's logo, graphics, showrooms and factories. His AEG Turbine Factory (1909) in Moabit, with its monumental temple-like gable and glass-and-steel construction, became an icon of industrial architecture.

Influence

Behrens's Berlin office trained three giants of modern architecture: Walter Gropius, Ludwig Mies van der Rohe and Le Corbusier all worked there around 1908–1911. He co-founded the Deutscher Werkbund, designed typefaces including Behrens-Schrift, and later works included the expressionist-brick Hoechst Administration Building (1924) and the Alexander & Gertrud tobacco factory in Linz. His integration of art, industry and typography shaped the discipline of industrial design.
