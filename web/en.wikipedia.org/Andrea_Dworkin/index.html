Andrea Rita Dworkin (26 September 1946 – 9 April 2005) was an American radical feminist writer and activist best known for her analysis of pornography, which she argued contributed to the subordination of women.

Life

Dworkin was born in Camden, New Jersey, and attended Bennington College, where she was arrested protesting the Vietnam War. She lived in the Netherlands in the early 1970s, where she experienced abuse in her first marriage — an experience she later described as formative. Returning to New York, she became involved in feminist activism and writing.

Work and activism

Her first book, Woman Hating (1974), examined misogyny in fairy tales and pornography. In Pornography: Men Possessing Women (1981) and Intercourse (1987) she developed her critique of sexuality under patriarchy. With legal scholar Catharine MacKinnon she drafted antipornography civil rights ordinances in Minneapolis and Indianapolis in the 1980s; the Indianapolis ordinance was struck down by the courts. She also wrote fiction, including Ice and Fire, and the memoir Heartbreak (2002).

Legacy

Dworkin remains one of the most debated figures of second-wave feminism, praised for her fierce moral clarity about sexual violence and criticized for her positions on pornography and sexuality.
