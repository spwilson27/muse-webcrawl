Cauvery Water Management Authority

The Cauvery Water Management Authority (CWMA) is a body constituted by the Government of India in 2018 to implement the sharing of the waters of the Cauvery (Kaveri) river among the riparian states of Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Kerala and the Union Territory of Puducherry, in accordance with the final award of the Cauvery Water Disputes Tribunal as modified by the Supreme Court of India.

Background

The Cauvery dispute, over the allocation of the river's water between upstream Karnataka and downstream Tamil Nadu, dates to agreements of 1892 and 1924 between the Madras Presidency and the Kingdom of Mysore. A tribunal constituted in 1990 delivered its final award in 2007, allocating shares of the river's assessed yield to each party. In February 2018 the Supreme Court adjusted the allocations — increasing Karnataka's share by 14.75 thousand million cubic feet, partly to meet Bengaluru's drinking-water needs — and directed the Union government to frame a scheme to implement the award.

Functions

The Authority, supported by a Cauvery Water Regulation Committee, supervises the storage, apportionment and control of Cauvery waters, monitors flows at inter-state gauging points such as Biligundlu, and issues directions on releases from reservoirs, particularly during deficit monsoon years. Its meetings — often contentious, especially over the Mekedatu project proposed by Karnataka — are a recurring focus of inter-state politics in southern India.
