Ben-Day dots

Ben-Day dots, named for illustrator and printer Benjamin Henry Day Jr., are a printing technique in which small coloured dots are spaced and combined to create shading and secondary colours in inexpensive mass printing.

History

Developed in 1879, the process became a hallmark of mid-20th-century comic books, where regularly spaced dots of cyan, magenta, yellow and black produced a wide palette at low cost.

In art

Pop artist Roy Lichtenstein enlarged and exaggerated Ben-Day dots in paintings such as Whaam! and Drowning Girl, making the mechanical dot pattern a signature of Pop Art.

Use

The dots vary in size and spacing: closely spaced large dots create darker tones, while small sparse dots create lighter tints.
