GE Aerospace is an American aircraft engine manufacturer and aerospace company headquartered in Evendale, Ohio, near Cincinnati. It is one of the world's largest suppliers of jet engines for commercial and military aircraft, and since the 2024 breakup of the General Electric conglomerate it is the legal continuation of GE itself, trading on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker GE.

History

General Electric built its first aircraft turbosuperchargers during the First World War and produced the first American jet engine, the I-A, flown in 1942. Its J47 powered early Cold War fighters, and the CF6 family became a mainstay of widebody airliners. In 1974 GE entered a joint venture with France's Snecma (now Safran) creating CFM International, whose CFM56 became the best-selling jet engine in history, followed by the LEAP engine family.

Products

Commercial engines include the GEnx for the Boeing 787, the GE90 and GE9X for the 777 family — the GE9X being the largest jet engine ever built — and, through CFM, the LEAP for the Airbus A320neo, Boeing 737 MAX, and COMAC C919. Military products include the F404, F414, and T700 turboshaft families. The company also supplies avionics, electrical power systems, and engine services, which account for a large share of revenue.

The GE breakup

In November 2021 GE announced it would split into three companies. GE HealthCare was spun off in January 2023 and GE Vernova in April 2024, leaving GE Aerospace as the remaining business and the inheritor of the GE name and listing.
