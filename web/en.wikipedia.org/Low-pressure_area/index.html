Low-pressure area

A low-pressure area, or low, is a region where atmospheric pressure at sea level is lower than in surrounding locations. Air converges and rises over a low, promoting cloud formation and precipitation, so lows are associated with unsettled weather.

Circulation

Because of the Coriolis effect, winds circulate counterclockwise around lows in the Northern Hemisphere and clockwise in the Southern Hemisphere. Extratropical cyclones form along weather fronts in the midlatitudes, while tropical cyclones — hurricanes and typhoons — are intense warm-core lows over warm oceans. Thermal lows form over heated land in summer.

Opposite

The counterpart of a low is a high-pressure area (anticyclone), associated with subsiding air and fair weather.
