Gynophobia

Gynophobia is an abnormal fear of women, classed among the specific phobias.

Gynophobia or gynephobia is an abnormal fear of women, a type of specific social phobia. The term derives from the Greek gynē (woman) and phobos (fear). It is distinct from misogyny, which denotes hatred of or prejudice against women rather than a phobic anxiety response.

Symptoms and causes

People with gynophobia may experience intense anxiety, panic, rapid heartbeat, sweating, and avoidance behaviour in the presence of women or in anticipation of encountering them. As with other specific phobias, proposed causes include traumatic experiences, learned behaviour, cultural conditioning, and underlying anxiety disorders. In earlier psychiatric literature, cases were discussed in connection with broader social anxiety and, in some historical accounts, with celibate religious traditions, though such interpretations are now treated cautiously.

Treatment

Treatment follows the general approach for specific phobias: cognitive behavioural therapy and graded exposure therapy are the mainstays, sometimes supplemented by medication for associated anxiety or depression. Prognosis is generally good when the condition is recognised and treated.

Terminology

The male counterpart concept is androphobia, a fear of men. "Gynophobia" should not be confused with "caligynephobia" or "venustraphobia," terms sometimes used for a fear of beautiful women specifically.
