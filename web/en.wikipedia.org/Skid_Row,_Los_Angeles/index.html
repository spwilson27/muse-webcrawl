Skid Row, Los Angeles

Skid Row is a neighbourhood in Downtown Los Angeles, officially the Central City East area, long associated with concentrated poverty and one of the largest homeless populations in the United States. It covers roughly fifty city blocks east of Main Street and south of Third Street.

Skid Row

Location: Downtown Los Angeles, California
Area: ~0.4 sq mi (50 blocks)
Known for: Homeless encampments, missions, SRO hotels

History

The district grew from the late 19th century as a zone of cheap hotels, saloons and services for transient railroad workers. In the 1970s the city adopted an informal policy of "containment", concentrating shelters and services there. Litigation over sweeps of encampments, notably Jones v. City of Los Angeles and later settlements, shaped enforcement for decades.

Present day

Estimates of the homeless population in Skid Row range from several thousand to over 8,000. Missions, single-room-occupancy hotels and outreach services, including the Union Rescue Mission and the Los Angeles Mission, are concentrated in the area, and it remains the focal point of regional debates over homelessness policy.
