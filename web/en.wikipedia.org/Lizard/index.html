Lizard

Lizards
Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Chordata
Class: Reptilia
Order: Squamata
Suborder: Lacertilia
Species: more than 7,000 described

Lizards are a widespread group of squamate reptiles, with over 7,000 species ranging across all continents except Antarctica, as well as most oceanic island chains. The group is paraphyletic with respect to snakes, which evolved from lizard-like ancestors. Lizards range in size from tiny chameleons and geckos a few centimetres long to the Komodo dragon, up to 3 metres in length.

Biology

Most lizards are quadrupedal with a sprawling gait, strong claws and long tails; some lineages, such as glass lizards and slow worms, are legless. Many species can shed their tails when seized by a predator (autotomy) and later regenerate them. Lizards are generally ectothermic, regulating body temperature through basking and shade-seeking. Vision — including colour vision — is important for most species, and communication often involves visual displays such as dewlap extensions, head-bobbing and bright coloration. Many lizards also use chemical signals from femoral pores.

Diet and defence

Most lizards are insectivorous, but some, like iguanas, are herbivorous, and large monitors are active predators or scavengers. Only two species, the Gila monster and the Mexican beaded lizard, were long known to be venomous, though venom glands are now recognised in many monitor lizards and others. Defensive strategies include camouflage, flight, inflation, frill displays and biting.

Relation to humans

Lizards are popular in the pet trade — bearded dragons, leopard geckos and green iguanas among them — and figure widely in mythology and folklore. In many tropical regions house geckos are welcomed as controllers of insect pests.
