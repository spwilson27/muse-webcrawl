Saturn (mythology)

Saturn (Latin: Saturnus) was a god in ancient Roman religion and mythology, associated with agriculture, wealth, time, liberation and periodic renewal. He was identified with the Greek god Cronus, and was said to have ruled during a mythical Golden Age of abundance and equality.

Saturn with his attribute, the agricultural sickle

Mythology

According to Roman tradition, Saturn was deposed by his son Jupiter and fled to Latium, where he was welcomed by Janus and taught the inhabitants agriculture. His reign was remembered as a time when no slavery or private property existed. His consort was Ops, goddess of plenty, and the state treasury was housed in his temple in the Roman Forum.

Worship

Saturn's major festival, the Saturnalia, was held in mid-December and became one of the most popular Roman celebrations. Normal conventions were inverted: slaves dined with or were served by their masters, gifts were exchanged, and gambling was permitted. The festival's customs influenced later Christmas traditions.

Legacy

Saturn gave his name to the planet Saturn, to Saturday, and to the element lead in alchemical symbolism, where his figure as a melancholic old man also fed artistic depictions of Father Time.
