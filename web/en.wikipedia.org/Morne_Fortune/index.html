Morne Fortune

[Image: Castries and its harbour seen from Morne Fortune, Saint Lucia]

Morne Fortune is a hill rising to about 850 feet (260 m) above Castries, the capital of Saint Lucia in the Caribbean. Its name derives from the French for "Hill of Good Luck," reportedly because of its commanding view of the harbour and surrounding countryside. Owing to its strategic position, the hill changed hands repeatedly in the colonial wars between France and Britain, during which Saint Lucia itself was contested fourteen times.

History

The French first fortified the hill in the late eighteenth century; the British captured and extended the works during the Napoleonic-era campaigns, most famously in 1796, when the position was stormed by forces associated with Sir John Moore. Remains of the fortifications, including the Apostles Battery, powder magazines, barracks and a military cemetery, survive on the summit. At the southern end of the ridge stands the Inniskilling Monument, commemorating the 27th (Inniskilling) Regiment of Foot.

Today

Morne Fortune is now home to the campus of the University of the West Indies Open Campus in Saint Lucia and to Government House, the official residence of the Governor General of Saint Lucia, which stands on the northern end of the ridge. The site remains a popular vantage point for views over Castries, Vigie and the north-west coast.
