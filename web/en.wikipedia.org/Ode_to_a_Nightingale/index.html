Ode to a Nightingale

"Ode to a Nightingale" is a poem by John Keats written in May 1819, either in the garden of the Spaniards Inn, Hampstead, or, according to Keats's friend Charles Armitage Brown, under a plum tree in the garden of Keats House, also in Hampstead. It is one of the "1819 odes" and was first published in Annals of the Fine Arts in July 1819.

The nightingale of Keats's ode.

My heart aches, and a drowsy numbness pains
My sense, as though of hemlock I had drunk…

Themes

In the poem, Keats contrasts the immortal song of the nightingale with human mortality, suffering, and transience. The speaker attempts to escape into the bird's world through wine and then through the "viewless wings of Poesy", but is recalled to himself by the word "forlorn", ending with the uncertain question of whether the experience was "a vision, or a waking dream".

Structure

The ode consists of eight stanzas of ten lines each, written in iambic pentameter with a shortened eighth line, rhyming ABABCDECDE. It is among the most frequently anthologized poems in English and a landmark of Romantic lyric poetry, standing beside works of Keats's contemporaries celebrating nature, such as the odes of the same annus mirabilis.
