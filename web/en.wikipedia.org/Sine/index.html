The sine function, y = sin x.

In mathematics, the sine is a trigonometric function of an angle. The sine of an acute angle in a right triangle is defined as the ratio of the length of the side opposite the angle to the length of the hypotenuse. More generally, the sine of an angle can be defined using the unit circle: for an angle θ measured from the positive x-axis, sin θ is the y-coordinate of the corresponding point on the unit circle.

Properties

Sine is an odd function, sin(−x) = −sin x, and periodic with period 2π. Its values range between −1 and 1. Together with cosine it satisfies the Pythagorean identity sin²x + cos²x = 1. The derivative of sin x is cos x, and its integral is −cos x plus a constant. Sine appears in Euler's formula, eix = cos x + i sin x, and can be expanded as a Taylor series: x − x³/3! + x⁵/5! − …

History

Sine tables were developed by Indian mathematicians — Āryabhaṭa computed a sine table in the 5th century CE — and transmitted through Arabic astronomy. The word "sine" derives from a chain of translations: the Sanskrit jyā (bowstring) became Arabic jība, read as jayb ("bay" or "fold"), and rendered into Latin as sinus.

Applications

The sine function models periodic phenomena throughout science and engineering: waves, oscillations, alternating current, and sound. Sinusoidal waves are fundamental to Fourier analysis, by which complex signals are decomposed into sums of sines and cosines.
