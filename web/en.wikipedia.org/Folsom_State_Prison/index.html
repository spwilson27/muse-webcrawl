Folsom State Prison

Folsom State Prison (FSP) is a California State Prison in Folsom, California, United States. Opened in 1880, it is the state's second-oldest prison, after San Quentin, and was one of the first maximum-security prisons in the United States. It lies adjacent to the newer California State Prison, Sacramento.

History

Construction began in 1878 using convict labour; inmates quarried the granite used for the cell blocks from the prison grounds near the American River. Folsom was the first prison in the world to have electric power, supplied by the nearby Folsom Powerhouse. The prison carried out 93 executions by hanging between 1895 and 1937, when California adopted the gas chamber. Notorious inmates included Charles Manson, who was held there in the 1950s.

In popular culture

The prison is famous as the subject of Johnny Cash's song "Folsom Prison Blues" (1955), and Cash performed at the prison in January 1968, recording the landmark live album At Folsom Prison.
