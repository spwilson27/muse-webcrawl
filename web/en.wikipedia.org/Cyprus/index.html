Cyprus

Cyprus, officially the Republic of Cyprus, is an island country in the eastern Mediterranean Sea, south of Turkey and west of Syria and Lebanon. It is the third-largest and third-most-populous island in the Mediterranean. Its capital and largest city is Nicosia.

History

Cyprus has been inhabited since the Neolithic era and was a major source of copper in the ancient world — its Latin name for copper, cuprum, derives from the island. Mycenaean Greeks, Phoenicians, Assyrians, Egyptians, Persians, Romans, Byzantines, Venetians, and Ottomans successively ruled it before it passed to British administration in 1878 and was annexed in 1914. Independence came in 1960 under a power-sharing constitution between Greek Cypriots and Turkish Cypriots.

Division

Intercommunal violence in 1963–64 and a 1974 coup aimed at union with Greece, followed by a Turkish military intervention, left the island divided. The northern third is administered by Turkish Cypriots, who in 1983 proclaimed the "Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus", recognised only by Turkey. The UN-patrolled Green Line buffer zone crosses Nicosia, the last divided capital in Europe. Reunification negotiations have continued intermittently for decades.

Geography

The island is dominated by two mountain ranges — the Troodos Mountains in the south and west, rising to Mount Olympus (1,952 m), and the Kyrenia range along the north coast — separated by the central Mesaoria plain. The climate is Mediterranean, with hot dry summers and mild wet winters.

Economy

Cyprus has a high-income economy based on services, especially tourism, shipping, financial services, and real estate. It adopted the euro in 2008 and weathered a severe banking crisis in 2012–13. Offshore natural gas discoveries in its exclusive economic zone are a focus of regional politics.
