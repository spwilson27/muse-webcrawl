History of Ireland (1801–1923)

The history of Ireland from 1801 to 1923 covers the period of the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland, created by the Acts of Union 1800, through to the establishment of the Irish Free State and Northern Ireland in 1922–23. It was an era of mass political movements, catastrophic famine, emigration, and eventually armed revolution and partition.

Union, emancipation, and famine

The Union abolished the Irish Parliament and sent Irish MPs to Westminster. Daniel O'Connell's campaign won Catholic emancipation in 1829 and nearly repealed the Union. The Great Famine (1845–1852), caused by potato blight and compounded by government policy, killed about one million people and drove another million to emigrate, beginning a century of population decline.

Home Rule to independence

From the 1870s the dominant question was Home Rule — Irish self-government within the United Kingdom — championed by Charles Stewart Parnell and the Irish Parliamentary Party, and fiercely resisted by Ulster unionists. The Third Home Rule Bill passed in 1914 but was suspended for the First World War. The Easter Rising of 1916 and the executions that followed transformed opinion; Sinn Féin won the 1918 election, proclaimed a republic, and fought the Irish War of Independence (1919–1921). The Anglo-Irish Treaty of December 1921 created the Irish Free State as a dominion, while six north-eastern counties remained in the United Kingdom as Northern Ireland; the Treaty split the nationalist movement and led to the Irish Civil War (1922–1923).
