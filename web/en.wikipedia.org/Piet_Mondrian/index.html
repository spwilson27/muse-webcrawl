Piet Mondrian

Pieter Cornelis Mondriaan (7 March 1872 – 1 February 1944), known after 1906 as Piet Mondrian, was a Dutch painter and art theorist who is regarded as one of the greatest artists of the 20th century. He was a pioneer of abstract art and a leading figure of the De Stijl movement, which he co-founded with Theo van Doesburg.

Mondrian developed a style he called neoplasticism: non-representational compositions of black horizontal and vertical lines forming rectangles filled with the primary colors red, blue, and yellow, plus white, black, and gray. Works such as Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow (1930) and his final, unfinished Victory Boogie Woogie (1942–44) are iconic images of modernism.

Life

Born in Amersfoort, Mondrian began as a painter of landscapes and trees, initially in a naturalist and then symbolist and Cubist-influenced vein after moving to Paris in 1911. During the First World War he returned to the Netherlands, where De Stijl was founded in 1917. He spent most of his later life in Paris, then fled to London in 1938 and New York in 1940, where his style loosened in response to the city's energy and jazz, as in Broadway Boogie Woogie (1942–43). He died of pneumonia in New York in 1944.

Legacy

Mondrian's grids influenced architecture, design, fashion, and popular culture, from Bauhaus aesthetics to Yves Saint Laurent's 1965 Mondrian dresses. The largest collection of his work is held by the Kunstmuseum Den Haag.
