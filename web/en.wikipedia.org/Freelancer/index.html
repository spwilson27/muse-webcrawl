Freelancer

A freelancer at work

A freelancer is a self-employed person who offers services to multiple clients, typically on a project or contract basis, rather than working as an employee of a single organization. The term originally described medieval mercenary soldiers — "free lances" — whose weapons were for hire; Walter Scott popularized it in Ivanhoe (1820).

Fields

Freelancing is common in writing and journalism, graphic design, software development, photography, translation, consulting, marketing, film and video production, and music. Online platforms such as Upwork, Fiverr, and Freelancer.com match freelancers with clients worldwide, a segment of the broader gig economy.

Advantages and disadvantages

Freelancers commonly cite autonomy, flexible hours, and the ability to choose projects and work from home. The downsides include irregular income, responsibility for one's own taxes, insurance, and retirement savings, absence of employer-provided benefits and paid leave, and the continual need to find new work. Legal classification disputes — whether a worker is an employee or an independent contractor — have prompted legislation such as California's AB5.

Scale

Surveys in the 2010s and 2020s estimated that roughly a third of the U.S. workforce had performed some freelance work, with tens of millions doing it as a primary occupation; the COVID-19 pandemic accelerated remote freelancing further.
