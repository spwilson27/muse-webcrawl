Accidental (music)

In musical notation, an accidental is a symbol that indicates the modification of the pitch of a note. The most common accidentals are the sharp (♯), which raises a note by one semitone, the flat (♭), which lowers it by one semitone, and the natural (♮), which cancels a previous alteration and restores the note to its pitch as defined by the key signature.

An accidental applies to the note it precedes and to all subsequent notes on the same line or space within the same measure (bar), unless it is cancelled by another accidental. Notes outside the measure are unaffected unless tied across the barline. Accidentals in the key signature, by contrast, apply throughout the piece wherever the note occurs.

Types of accidentals

SymbolNameEffect
♯SharpRaises the pitch by one semitone

𝄪Double sharpRaises the pitch by two semitones
𝄫Double flatLowers the pitch by two semitones

Microtonal music employs additional accidentals, such as the half-sharp and half-flat (quarter-tone accidentals) used to indicate pitches between the semitones of twelve-tone equal temperament.

History

Accidentals developed from the medieval practice of musica ficta, in which performers altered pitches according to convention without the changes being notated. The flat sign derives from a rounded letter "b" and the natural and sharp signs from a square "b", which is why a natural is sometimes called "B-square" and the German note name for B natural is "H".
