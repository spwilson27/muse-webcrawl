National Council (Switzerland)

Seats: 200
Term: 4 years
Meeting place: Federal Palace, Bern
Chamber: lower house of the Federal Assembly

The National Council (German: Nationalrat; French: Conseil national; Italian: Consiglio nazionale; Romansh: Cussegl naziunal) is the larger chamber of the Federal Assembly of Switzerland, representing the people. It has 200 seats, apportioned among the cantons in proportion to population.

Election and composition

Members (National Councillors) are elected every four years by proportional representation in multi-member cantons; the smallest cantons form single-member constituencies elected by plurality. Every canton has at least one seat. Elections coincide with those for most of the Council of States, the upper chamber representing the cantons.

Role

Together with the Council of States — the two chambers having equal power — the National Council enacts federal legislation, elects the seven members of the Federal Council (the executive) and the federal judiciary, approves the budget, and supervises the federal administration. The President of the National Council, elected annually, presides over the United Federal Assembly and is popularly regarded as the country's highest-ranking official after the Federal Council.
