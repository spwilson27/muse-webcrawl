Swedish language

Swedish is a North Germanic language spoken by about 10 million people.

Swedish (svenska) is a North Germanic language, spoken natively by about 10 million people, predominantly in Sweden and in parts of Finland, where it has equal legal standing with Finnish. It is largely mutually intelligible with Norwegian and Danish, although the degree of mutual intelligibility is asymmetric.

History

Swedish developed from Old Norse, separating from Old Danish during the early medieval period. The printing of the Gustav Vasa Bible in 1541 marks the beginning of Modern Swedish. Standard Swedish, used across the country, evolved from the dialects around Stockholm and the Mälaren valley.

Features

Swedish is written in the Latin alphabet with three additional letters — å, ä, and ö — giving a 29-letter alphabet. The language has two grammatical genders (common and neuter), a pitch accent that distinguishes some word pairs, and a large inventory of vowel sounds. Vocabulary includes many loanwords from Low German, French, and, increasingly, English.
