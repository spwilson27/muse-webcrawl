Abandonware

Abandonware is a product, typically software, ignored by its owner and manufacturer, which can no longer be found for sale, and for which no official support is available. Although such software is usually still under copyright, the owner may not be tracking copyright violations, or may have become defunct.

History

The term gained currency in the late 1990s as websites began distributing out-of-print games for obsolete platforms. Communities argued such distribution preserved works that would otherwise be lost, while industry groups such as the Entertainment Software Association maintained that the copies infringed copyright regardless of commercial availability.

Legal status and preservation

Abandonware has no legal standing: copyright persists even when a work is unmaintained. However, libraries and archives have obtained limited exemptions (for example under the U.S. DMCA) to circumvent copy protection for preservation purposes, and services such as GOG.com have commercially re-released many former abandonware titles with rights holders' permission.
