Beltrami identity

The Beltrami identity, named after Italian mathematician Eugenio Beltrami, is a special case of the Euler–Lagrange equation in the calculus of variations.

Statement

If a functional's Lagrangian does not depend explicitly on the independent variable, the Euler–Lagrange equation simplifies to the identity: L − y′ · (∂L/∂y′) = C, where C is a constant. This provides a first integral that often makes variational problems much easier to solve.

Derivation

The identity follows from applying the chain rule to the total derivative of L with respect to x and substituting the Euler–Lagrange equation. It is analogous to conservation of energy in mechanics, reflecting Noether's theorem for translation invariance.

Applications

It is used to solve the brachistochrone problem, find minimal surfaces of revolution, and derive geodesics.
