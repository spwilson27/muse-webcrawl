The Totonac are an Indigenous people of Mexico living in the states of Veracruz, Puebla and Hidalgo, in the region historically called Totonacapan along the Gulf coast and the Sierra Norte de Puebla. They speak languages of the Totonacan family.

History

The Totonac region flourished in the Classic period at El Tajín, famous for its Pyramid of the Niches and many ballcourts, and later at Cempoala, which became a major city-state. The Totonacs were subjects of the Aztec Empire, paying tribute in goods such as vanilla, of which the region around Papantla remains a famous producer.

At the Spanish conquest, the Totonacs of Cempoala became the first important allies of Hernán Cortés, joining his forces against the Aztecs in 1519.

Culture

Totonac culture is known for the Danza de los Voladores — the "flying dancers" ritual in which participants descend from a tall pole on ropes — recognised by UNESCO as Intangible Cultural Heritage. Traditional agriculture centres on maize, beans, squash, chilli and vanilla. Today the Totonac languages are endangered, with speakers numbering in the hundreds of thousands in the mid-20th century and declining since.
