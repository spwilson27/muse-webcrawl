Cahiers du cinéma is a French film magazine founded in 1951. It is widely regarded as the most influential film journal in history. Its young critics of the 1950s—François Truffaut, Jean-Luc Godard, Éric Rohmer, Jacques Rivette and Claude Chabrol—developed the politique des auteurs (auteur theory), championing directors such as Alfred Hitchcock, Howard Hawks and Jean Renoir against the French "Tradition of Quality", before becoming the core directors of the French New Wave.

History

Edited early on under the mentorship of André Bazin, the magazine's distinctive yellow covers and combative criticism reshaped film culture. After May 1968 it took a radical Maoist turn under editors including Serge Daney and Serge Toubiana; later decades saw a return to cinephile criticism. The magazine's annual top-ten lists and its canon-forming influence on directors and critics worldwide remain unmatched, and an English-language online edition has been published in the digital era.
