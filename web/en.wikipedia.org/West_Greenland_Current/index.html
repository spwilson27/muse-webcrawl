West Greenland Current

The West Greenland Current is a weak, cold ocean current that flows northward along the west coast of Greenland. It forms part of the subpolar gyre circulation of the North Atlantic and links Arctic outflow with currents around Greenland and into Baffin Bay and the Labrador Sea.

Path and composition

The current is fed by the East Greenland Current, which rounds Cape Farewell at Greenland's southern tip carrying cold, low-salinity Arctic water and sea ice, and by a contribution of warmer, saltier Atlantic water from the Irminger Current. The combined flow runs north along the southwest coast past Nuuk, weakening as branches peel off into the Davis Strait and Baffin Bay.

Role

Around northern Baffin Bay the remaining water contributes to currents flowing south along Baffin Island, eventually feeding the Labrador Current. The West Greenland Current helps moderate the climate of Greenland's inhabited southwest coast — its relative warmth keeps harbours such as Nuuk's largely ice-free — and transports icebergs calved from west Greenland glaciers southward. Changes in the current are studied for their influence on Greenland Ice Sheet melt and North Atlantic circulation.
