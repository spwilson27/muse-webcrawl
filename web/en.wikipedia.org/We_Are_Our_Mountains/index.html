We Are Our Mountains

We Are Our Mountains (Armenian: Մենք ենք, մեր սարերը, Menq enq, mer sarerë) is a large monument north of Stepanakert, the capital city of the former Republic of Artsakh (Nagorno-Karabakh). Completed in 1967, it is widely regarded as the principal symbol of the region and of its ethnic Armenian population.

Description

Location: near Stepanakert, Nagorno-Karabakh
Completed: 1967
Designer: Sargis Baghdasaryan
Material: Volcanic tuff

The sculpture, carved from red volcanic tuff, depicts the stylized heads of an elderly man and woman—figures affectionately known to Armenians as Tatik u Papik ("Grandma and Grandpa")—emerging from the hillside as if part of the mountain itself. The design represents the deep roots of the Armenian people in the highlands. Unusually for a Soviet-era monument, it bears no inscription and includes no overtly communist iconography.

History

The monument was designed by sculptor Sargis Baghdasaryan and unveiled in 1967, during the Soviet period, when Nagorno-Karabakh was an autonomous oblast within the Azerbaijan SSR. It later appeared on the coat of arms of the unrecognized Republic of Artsakh. Following the 2023 Azerbaijani offensive and the exodus of the region's Armenian population, the monument's future became a subject of concern for Armenian cultural heritage advocates.
