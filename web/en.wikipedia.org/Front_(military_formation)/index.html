Front (military formation)

A front is a large military formation, particularly in the militaries of the Soviet Union and the Russian Federation, roughly equivalent to an army group in other armies. A front typically comprised several armies — infantry, tank, artillery, and air armies — and was commanded by a senior officer, often a Marshal of the Soviet Union. Fronts were normally created for a particular campaign or strategic direction and could be disbanded or renamed as operations evolved.

Second World War

During the Eastern Front of the Second World War, the Red Army's fronts were its principal strategic formations. Famous examples include the 1st Ukrainian Front, the 1st Belorussian Front, and the Leningrad, Kalinin, and Steppe Fronts. By 1945 the two fronts that converged on Berlin — Zhukov's 1st Belorussian and Konev's 1st Ukrainian — each numbered hundreds of thousands of men with thousands of tanks and aircraft. Fronts were coordinated by representatives of the Stavka, the Soviet high command.

Other uses

Imperial Russia had used fronts in the First World War, and the term also appears in other armies with Soviet influence. In a more general sense, "front" also denotes the direction or theatre toward which a formation is oriented, and in Russian doctrine a wartime front would correspond to a peacetime military district upon mobilization.
