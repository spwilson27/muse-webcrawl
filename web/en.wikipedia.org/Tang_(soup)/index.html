Tang (soup)

Tang (탕) or guk (국) are categories of soup in Korean cuisine. Soups are an essential part of Korean meals, typically served alongside rice and shared or individual side dishes (banchan). While the terms overlap, guk generally refers to lighter, brothier soups eaten daily with rice, whereas tang often denotes heartier soups, sometimes served communally or on special occasions.

Types

Well-known Korean soups include miyeok-guk (seaweed soup, traditionally eaten on birthdays and after childbirth), tteok-guk (sliced rice-cake soup eaten at New Year), doenjang-guk (fermented soybean-paste soup), kimchi-guk, and yukgaejang (spicy beef soup). Prominent tang dishes include seolleongtang (milky ox-bone soup), samgyetang (ginseng chicken soup), galbitang (short-rib soup), gamjatang (pork spine and potato stew-soup), and maeuntang (spicy fish soup).

Role in the meal

Unlike in Western meals, soup in Korea is not a separate course but is placed with the rice at the centre of the table setting. Clear soups accompany everyday meals, while rich tang were historically associated with restaurants, market eateries, and restorative or celebratory eating.
