Social housing is housing provided at below-market rents by government agencies or non-profit organisations, typically allocated according to need and intended to ensure that low- and moderate-income households have access to secure, decent homes. It differs from purely private renting in its subsidy, allocation rules, and—often—security of tenure.

Models around the world

In the Netherlands, housing associations (woningcorporaties) own roughly a third of all homes, the highest share in Europe. Vienna is famous for its municipal housing: the city owns or manages housing for a majority of residents, including landmark interwar estates such as Karl Marx-Hof, and rents are set on a cost basis across a broad income range rather than only for the poor. In the United Kingdom, council housing expanded enormously after 1919 and again after 1945, then shrank following the Right to Buy policy introduced in 1980. In the United States, "public housing" is a narrower, means-tested programme administered by local housing authorities with federal funding, while Singapore's Housing and Development Board houses most of the population in subsidised owner-occupied flats.

History

Modern social housing grew out of nineteenth-century concerns about slum conditions and public health, philanthropic model dwellings, and municipal building programmes. After both world wars, housing shortages and reconstruction drove mass building across Europe. From the 1970s, many countries shifted from building large estates toward housing allowances, mixed-tenure development, and transfer of stock to housing associations, alongside criticism of concentrated poverty and poor maintenance on some estates.

Debates

Supporters argue social housing provides stability, affordability, and labour mobility, and can anchor mixed-income communities when broadly accessible. Critics raise concerns about allocation fairness, waiting lists, stigma, and fiscal cost. Contemporary policy debate centres on shortages of supply, energy retrofitting of older stock, and protecting social housing from conversion to market use.
