Fraser River

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Fraser River is the longest river within British Columbia, Canada, at 1,375 kilometres (854 mi). It rises at Fraser Pass near Blackrock Mountain in the Rocky Mountains and flows into the Strait of Georgia just south of the City of Vancouver. The river drains an area of about 220,000 square kilometres and is named for explorer Simon Fraser, who descended it in 1808.

Course

From its headwaters the Fraser flows northwest through the Rocky Mountain Trench, then turns south near Prince George and continues through the Fraser Canyon, where it passes through the narrow, turbulent gorge at Hell's Gate. Below Hope the river enters the broad, fertile Fraser Valley and crosses a wide floodplain past Chilliwack, Abbotsford, and Langley before splitting into distributaries around Richmond and Delta and emptying into the Strait of Georgia.

History

Indigenous peoples, including the Stó:lō and other Coast and Interior Salish groups, have lived along the river for thousands of years, relying on its enormous salmon runs. Simon Fraser's 1808 descent for the North West Company established that the river was not the Columbia, as had been hoped. The Fraser Canyon Gold Rush of 1858 brought tens of thousands of miners and led directly to the creation of the Colony of British Columbia.

Ecology and economy

The Fraser is one of the most productive salmon rivers in the world, supporting runs of all five Pacific salmon species, as well as endangered white sturgeon. Its estuary is a vital stopover for migratory birds on the Pacific Flyway. The river and its valley support agriculture, forestry, hydroelectric generation on tributaries, and the Port of Vancouver's Fraser River terminals.
