Concord, Massachusetts

Concord is a town in Middlesex County, Massachusetts, United States, about 20 miles (32 km) northwest of Boston. Settled by English colonists in 1635, it was the site, with neighbouring Lexington, of the first battle of the American Revolutionary War in April 1775. In the nineteenth century it became a centre of American literature and philosophy, home to Ralph Waldo Emerson, Henry David Thoreau, Nathaniel Hawthorne and Louisa May Alcott.

History

On 19 April 1775, colonial militiamen confronted British regulars at the North Bridge in Concord, firing what Emerson later called "the shot heard round the world" in his Concord Hymn. The day's fighting along the Battle Road marked the outbreak of armed conflict in the American Revolution.

Literary heritage

Emerson settled in Concord in the 1830s and the town became the heart of Transcendentalism. Thoreau built his cabin on Emerson's land at Walden Pond in 1845 and lived there for two years, an experience he recorded in Walden (1854). Hawthorne lived at the Old Manse and later the Wayside, where Louisa May Alcott also wrote. Sleepy Hollow Cemetery's "Authors Ridge" holds the graves of Emerson, Thoreau, Hawthorne and the Alcotts.

Geography

Concord covers about 26 square miles where the Sudbury and Assabet rivers join to form the Concord River. Walden Pond State Reservation protects the glacial kettle pond made famous by Thoreau. The town's population was about 18,500 at the 2020 census.
