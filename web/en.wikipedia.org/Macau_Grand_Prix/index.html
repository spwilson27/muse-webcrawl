The Macau Grand Prix (Portuguese: Grande Prémio de Macau) is an annual motor and motorcycle racing event held each November on the temporary Guia Circuit on the streets of Macau. Founded in 1954, it is renowned for its demanding street circuit, which combines long fast straights with tight mountain hairpins, and for launching the careers of future Formula One stars.

Cars thread the narrow Melco hairpin on the Guia Circuit.

History

The event began in 1954 as a sports car race for local enthusiasts. A Formula Libre race became the centrepiece, later replaced by Formula Pacific and then Formula Three, whose Macau race became the unofficial F3 world final; winners include Ayrton Senna (1983), Michael Schumacher (1990) and Takuma Sato (2001). Since 2024 the headline single-seater race has been held for Formula Regional cars as an FIA World Cup. The meeting also hosts the FIA GT World Cup, the Guia Race for touring cars (long part of the World Touring Car calendar) and the Macau Motorcycle Grand Prix, held since 1967.

The circuit

The 6.12 km Guia Circuit is unusual among street tracks for its mix of a long, wide main straight past the Mandarin Oriental bend and an extremely narrow mountain section, including the Melco hairpin, where overtaking is prohibited. Its walls and elevation changes make it one of the most challenging circuits in the world.
