Ethylene oxide

Ethylene oxide is an organic compound with formula C2H4O, the simplest epoxide: a cyclic ether with a three-membered ring. It is a colourless, flammable gas with a faintly sweet odour, produced on a very large industrial scale, principally as the precursor to ethylene glycol.

Production

Modern production is by direct oxidation of ethylene with oxygen over a silver catalyst at 220–280 °C, developed commercially in the 1930s–40s. The older chlorohydrin process has largely been abandoned. Annual global production exceeds 20 million tonnes.

Uses

Most ethylene oxide is converted to ethylene glycol; the rest becomes detergents (ethoxylates), solvents, plastics and other chemicals. As a gas it is a highly effective sterilant for heat-sensitive medical equipment and spices, because it penetrates packaging and kills microorganisms.

Hazards and regulation

Ethylene oxide is carcinogenic to humans (IARC Group 1), mutagenic and neurotoxic, and its explosive range in air is wide. Community exposure from sterilisation facilities has prompted tightened emissions rules in the United States and elsewhere, and workplace exposure limits are low (1 ppm as an 8-hour average under OSHA).
