Linear particle accelerator

A linear particle accelerator (often shortened to linac) is a type of particle accelerator that accelerates charged subatomic particles or ions to a high speed by subjecting them to a series of oscillating electric potentials along a linear beamline.

Particles pass through a sequence of drift tubes; radio-frequency fields switch polarity so the particles are accelerated across each gap while shielded inside the tubes. The longest linac is the 3.2 km machine at SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory.

Uses

Linacs serve as injectors for synchrotrons, drive free-electron lasers, produce X-rays for radiotherapy and industrial radiography, and are used in ion implantation.
