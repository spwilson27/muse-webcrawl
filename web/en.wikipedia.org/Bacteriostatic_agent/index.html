Bacteriostatic agent

A bacteriostatic agent or bacteriostat is a biological or chemical agent that stops bacteria from reproducing, while not necessarily killing them. Upon removal of the bacteriostat, the bacteria usually start to grow again.

Antibiotics

Bacteriostatic antibiotics limit the growth of bacteria by interfering with protein production, DNA replication or other aspects of cellular metabolism. Examples include tetracyclines, macrolides, sulfonamides, chloramphenicol and linezolid. In contrast, bactericidal agents such as most beta-lactams actively kill bacteria.

Uses

Bacteriostats are used in plastics, food preservation and clinical settings; the distinction from bactericides is concentration-dependent, since many "static" drugs kill at high concentrations or against particular species.
