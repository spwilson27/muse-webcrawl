Flemish Region

The Flemish Region (Dutch: Vlaams Gewest), usually simply called Flanders in the institutional sense, is one of the three regions of the Kingdom of Belgium — alongside the Walloon Region and the Brussels-Capital Region. It occupies the northern part of the country and covers an area of about 13,600 km² with a population of roughly 6.8 million, making it the most populous of the three regions. Its official language is Dutch.

Geography and provinces

The Flemish Region comprises five provinces: Antwerp, Limburg, East Flanders, West Flanders and Flemish Brabant. Its major cities include Antwerp (the largest city and a major port), Ghent, Bruges, Leuven, Mechelen and Hasselt. The region includes Belgium's North Sea coast and the flat polders of the west, rising to low hills in the south. Brussels is an enclave within Flemish Brabant but forms a separate region; it nonetheless hosts the Flemish institutions.

Government

Under Belgium's federal constitution, regions exercise powers over territory-related matters: economy, employment, agriculture, water policy, housing, public works, energy, transport, environment, town and country planning, and supervision of provinces and municipalities. Since 1980 the institutions of the Flemish Region have been merged with those of the Flemish Community: a single Flemish Parliament and Flemish Government, seated in Brussels, exercise both sets of powers. The head of government is the Minister-President of Flanders.

Economy

Flanders is one of Europe's wealthier regions, with a strong export economy centred on the port of Antwerp-Bruges (one of Europe's largest ports), chemicals and petrochemicals, diamonds in Antwerp, pharmaceuticals, food processing, logistics and high-technology sectors. The region's prosperity relative to Wallonia, and the resulting fiscal transfers within Belgium, are recurring themes in Belgian politics and in the Flemish movement's calls for greater autonomy.
