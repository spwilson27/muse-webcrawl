Poultry farming

Poultry farming is the raising of domesticated birds such as chickens, ducks, turkeys, and geese for the production of meat and eggs. Chickens dominate the industry worldwide, divided into two specialised lines: layers, bred for egg production, and broilers, bred for rapid growth for meat. Poultry is the world's most consumed terrestrial meat, and global egg production exceeds 80 million tonnes annually.

Production systems

Systems range from village and backyard flocks, still important across Asia and Africa, to highly industrialised operations in which tens of thousands of genetically uniform birds are raised in climate-controlled houses on formulated feed. Breeding is concentrated in a few multinational companies that supply parent stock of hybrid birds. Broilers typically reach slaughter weight in five to seven weeks; a modern laying hen produces around 300 eggs in her first laying year.

Housing and welfare

Housing systems include battery cages for layers (banned in the European Union since 2012 and being phased out in many other jurisdictions), enriched cages, barn and aviary systems, and free-range and organic production, in which birds have outdoor access. Animal welfare debates concern stocking density, beak trimming, the culling of male layer chicks, fast growth rates in broilers and associated leg disorders, and conditions during transport and slaughter.

Disease and environment

Dense flocks are vulnerable to infectious disease, most seriously highly pathogenic avian influenza (H5N1 and other strains), which has caused mass culls and sporadic human infections since the late 1990s, prompting major biosecurity regimes. Other concerns include antimicrobial resistance from antibiotic use, and the environmental footprint of feed production, manure management, and ammonia emissions — though poultry generally converts feed to protein more efficiently than beef or pork.
