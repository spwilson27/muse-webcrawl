RMS Lusitania was a British ocean liner of the Cunard Line, launched in 1906 and briefly the world's largest passenger ship. She was torpedoed by the German U-boat U-20 on 7 May 1915 off the southern coast of Ireland during the First World War, sinking in only 18 minutes with the loss of 1,198 lives.

Service

Built with government subsidy on condition she could be converted to an armed merchant cruiser, Lusitania and her sister Mauretania were the fastest liners of their day, powered by steam turbines, and Lusitania held the Blue Riband for westbound crossings. She made 201 transatlantic crossings in total.

Sinking

Sailing from New York to Liverpool with 1,959 people aboard, she was struck by a single torpedo near the Old Head of Kinsale; a second, unexplained explosion followed. Among the dead were 128 Americans, and the sinking outraged public opinion in the United States, contributing to the deterioration of German-American relations that culminated in U.S. entry into the war in 1917. Germany argued the liner was a legitimate target carrying munitions — she did carry rifle cartridges and shell casings — a controversy that continues among historians.

Wreck

The wreck lies in about 93 metres of water, badly collapsed. Its ownership and salvage rights have long been disputed, and the cause of the second explosion remains debated.
