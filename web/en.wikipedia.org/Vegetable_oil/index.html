Vegetable oil

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Vegetable oils are oils extracted from seeds, fruits, or other parts of plants. Like animal fats, they are composed mainly of triglycerides — esters of glycerol with three fatty acids. Vegetable oils have been part of human culture for millennia, used in cooking, lighting, medicine, and industry.

Olive, sunflower, and seed oils.

Major oil crops

  • Palm oil — from the fruit of the oil palm; the world's most-produced vegetable oil, mainly from Malaysia and Indonesia.
  • Soybean oil and canola (rapeseed) oil — dominant in the Americas and Europe.
  • Sunflower oil — important in Eastern Europe and Russia.
  • Olive oil — the classic Mediterranean oil, graded from extra-virgin to refined.
  • Others: coconut, peanut (groundnut), cottonseed, corn, sesame, and safflower oils.

Production

Modern production uses mechanical pressing or solvent extraction (typically hexane), followed by refining, bleaching, and deodorizing. "Cold-pressed" and "virgin" oils skip some high-temperature steps, retaining more flavor and minor nutrients.

Uses

Beyond food, vegetable oils feed into soaps, cosmetics, lubricants, paints, and biodiesel. Partial hydrogenation once converted liquid oils into shortening and margarine but created harmful trans fats, now widely restricted.
