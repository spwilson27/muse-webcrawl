Bruno Buchberger (born 22 October 1942 in Innsbruck) is an Austrian mathematician and computer scientist, professor emeritus at the Johannes Kepler University Linz. He is best known for inventing the theory of Gröbner bases and the algorithm for computing them that bears his name.

Career

Buchberger studied mathematics at the University of Innsbruck, where in his 1965 doctoral dissertation, supervised by Wolfgang Gröbner, he introduced Gröbner bases and Buchberger's algorithm for computing a canonical basis for polynomial ideals — a foundational result in computer algebra used in robotics, coding theory, cryptography and automated theorem proving. He named the bases after his advisor.

In 1985 he founded the Research Institute for Symbolic Computation (RISC) at the Johannes Kepler University in Linz, which he directed for many years, and he founded the Journal of Symbolic Computation, serving as its editor-in-chief. He also initiated the Softwarepark Hagenberg, a technology park near Linz. Buchberger developed the Theorema system for automated reasoning. Among his honours are the Paris Kanellakis Theory and Practice Award (2007) and the Herbrand Award (2018).
