Platoon (automobile)

The Platoon was an early American automobile built at the beginning of the 20th century, during the brass era of motoring when hundreds of small manufacturers produced cars in limited numbers. Like many such marques, it was offered as a light runabout-style car aimed at private motorists as car ownership spread beyond the wealthy.

Context

Early marques of this period typically used small water-cooled engines, chain or shaft drive and wooden artillery wheels, and were sold regionally through agents. Most such makes were short-lived, disappearing as mass production by larger manufacturers drove prices down in the 1910s.

Legacy

The Platoon is remembered mainly in marque registers and histories of early American automobiles, which catalogue the many small producers of the brass era.
