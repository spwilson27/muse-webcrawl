Transport in Hong Kong

Hong Kong has a highly developed transport network, reflecting its dense population and geography as a port city of islands and a mountainous peninsula. Public transport accounts for the great majority of passenger journeys, and the system is notable for its integration through the Octopus contactless smart card, introduced in 1997.

Rail

The Mass Transit Railway (MTR) is the backbone of the system, combining the former MTR and Kowloon–Canton Railway networks into an extensive metro and light rail system. The Airport Express and the Hong Kong section of the Guangzhou–Shenzhen–Hong Kong Express Rail Link connect to Hong Kong International Airport and to mainland China's high-speed rail network.

Road and buses

Franchised double-decker buses, public light buses (minibuses), taxis, and trams on northern Hong Kong Island serve road passengers. The Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macau Bridge, opened in 2018, is among the world's longest sea crossings.

Ferries and aviation

Ferry services, including the historic Star Ferry, link Hong Kong Island with Kowloon and the outlying islands. Hong Kong International Airport at Chek Lap Kok is one of the world's busiest cargo and passenger airports, and the Port of Hong Kong remains a major container port.
