Natural number

In mathematics, the natural numbers are the numbers 1, 2, 3, etc., used for counting and ordering. In some definitions they also include 0, giving the whole numbers 0, 1, 2, 3, and so on. There is no universal agreement on whether zero belongs to the set; set theorists and logicians often include it, while many number theorists begin with 1. The set of natural numbers is commonly denoted N.

History

Counting with tally marks is among the oldest mathematical practices; abstract numerals developed in ancient Egypt and Mesopotamia. The formal foundations of the natural numbers were laid in the 19th century: Giuseppe Peano's axioms (1889) characterize the natural numbers via a starting element and a successor function, with mathematical induction as a defining principle. In set theory, the von Neumann construction identifies each natural number with the set of all smaller natural numbers.

Properties

The natural numbers are closed under addition and multiplication, well-ordered (every non-empty subset has a least element), and countably infinite. They form the basis for defining the integers, rational numbers, and real numbers.
