Fruitcake

Dense cake made with candied or dried fruit, nuts and spices

Fruitcake (or fruit cake) is a cake made with candied or dried fruit, nuts and spices, sometimes soaked in spirits. Originating in ancient Rome — where a precursor combined barley mash with pomegranate seeds, raisins and pine nuts — fruitcake developed in early modern Europe as dried fruits and cheap sugar from the colonies made rich, long-keeping cakes practical, and became a centrepiece of celebrations such as weddings and Christmas.

Types and traditions

In Britain, traditional fruitcakes are dark, moist and heavily fruited, often fed with brandy over weeks of maturation, and covered with marzipan and royal icing for Christmas cake and wedding cake. Lighter versions such as Dundee cake (topped with almonds) and Genoa cake are eaten year-round. German Stollen, Italian panettone and Caribbean black cake — whose fruit may be soaked in rum for months — are related festive forms. In the United States, mass-produced fruitcakes from bakeries such as Claxton, Georgia and Corsicana, Texas became a holiday staple, and the cake acquired a comic reputation as an endlessly re-gifted present, a joke popularised by Johnny Carson.

Keeping qualities

Because of their low water activity, high sugar content and alcohol, properly made fruitcakes keep for months or years; cakes are traditionally baked a year ahead for weddings, with the top tier saved for a christening. An Antarctic expedition fruitcake from Robert Falcon Scott's 1910–1913 Terra Nova expedition was found in 2017 in "excellent condition".
