Fu (poetry)

Chinese賦
Pinyinfù
Literal meaningrhapsody; exposition

Fu (Chinese: 賦), sometimes translated as "rhapsody" or "poetic exposition", is a form of Chinese rhymed prose that was the dominant literary form of the Han dynasty (206 BC – AD 220). Fu are long, ornate compositions, typically describing a subject exhaustively in elaborate catalogues, with sections of rhymed verse interspersed with prose.

Characteristics

The fu style features parallelism, hyperbole, and exhaustive enumeration of flora, fauna, topography, and courtly activities. Composers displayed their erudition through rare vocabulary and extensive allusion. Many fu end with a moral or political admonition, often critiquing imperial extravagance.

History

The genre developed during the late Warring States period and flourished under the Han emperors. Leading practitioners included Jia Yi, Sima Xiangru, Yang Xiong, Ban Gu, and Zhang Heng. Sima Xiangru's "Shanglin fu" (Rhapsody on the Imperial Park) is among the most famous examples. Later dynasties continued to compose fu, though it gradually lost prominence as other forms rose.
