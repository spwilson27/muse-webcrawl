Forced assimilation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Forced assimilation is the involuntary cultural assimilation of religious or ethnic minority groups, during which they are forced to adopt the language, national identity, norms, mores, customs, traditions, values, mentality, perceptions, or way of life of the dominant culture. Policies of forced assimilation are often associated with nation-building projects and may involve the suppression of minority languages and religions, compulsory education in the dominant language, renaming of people and places, and in the most severe cases the removal of children from their families.

Historical examples

Indigenous peoples

Settler-colonial states frequently pursued forced assimilation of indigenous peoples. In the United States and Canada, government-funded boarding and residential schools removed Native children from their families and punished the use of indigenous languages; Australia's policies toward Aboriginal children produced the "Stolen Generations". In Scandinavia, similar boarding-school policies targeted the Sámi people.

Language policies

States have often banned minority languages in education and administration to promote a national language. Examples include the suppression of Kurdish in Turkey, Russification in the Russian Empire and Soviet Union, the Magyarisation of minorities in the Kingdom of Hungary, and historical restrictions on Welsh, Breton, and Catalan in Britain, France, and Spain respectively.

Religious minorities

Forced conversion and assimilation have been imposed on religious minorities at various times, including the forced conversions and expulsions in early modern Spain and Portugal directed at Jews and Muslims, and later campaigns against religious practice under officially atheist regimes.

International law

Forced assimilation is addressed in modern human-rights instruments. The United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples (2007) states that indigenous peoples have the right not to be subjected to forced assimilation or destruction of their culture, and the 1948 Genocide Convention criminalises the forcible transfer of children of one group to another when committed with intent to destroy the group.
