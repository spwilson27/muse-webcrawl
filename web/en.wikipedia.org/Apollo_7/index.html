Apollo 7

CrewWally Schirra, Donn Eisele, Walter Cunningham
Launch11 October 1968
Landing22 October 1968
Duration10 days, 20 hours

Apollo 7 was the first crewed mission of NASA's Apollo program, flown in October 1968. Commanded by Wally Schirra with Donn Eisele and Walter Cunningham, it tested the Apollo command and service module in Earth orbit for eleven days, restoring confidence in the program after the Apollo 1 fire.

Mission

Launched on a Saturn IB on 11 October 1968, the crew tested the CSM's systems, performed rendezvous manoeuvres with the spent S-IVB stage, and made the first live television broadcasts from an American crewed spacecraft. The mission achieved all its objectives, clearing the way for Apollo 8's flight to the Moon two months later.

The "mutiny"

The crew, suffering from head colds and friction over scheduling, clashed with mission control — most famously refusing to wear helmets during re-entry for fear of ruptured eardrums. Though the flight was a technical success, none of the three astronauts flew in space again; they nevertheless received NASA's Distinguished Service Medal (belatedly presented to the families and Cunningham in 2008).
