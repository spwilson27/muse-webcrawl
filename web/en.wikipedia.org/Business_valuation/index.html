Business valuation

Business valuation
Process in finance and accounting
Purposes: Sale, taxation, litigation, reporting
Approaches: Income, market, asset-based

Business valuation is a process and a set of procedures used to estimate the economic value of an owner's interest in a business. Valuation is used by financial market participants to determine the price they are willing to pay or receive to effect a sale, and is also required for tax, divorce, estate and financial reporting purposes.

Approaches

Three standard approaches are the income approach — notably discounted cash flow (DCF) analysis, which values expected future cash flows — the market approach, which uses multiples (such as EV/EBITDA) from comparable companies and transactions, and the asset-based approach, which values the net assets of the business.

Standards and adjustments

Professional bodies (such as the IVSC internationally and AICPA, ASA and NACVA in the United States) set valuation standards and define standards of value, including fair market value and fair value. Valuations may apply discounts for lack of control and lack of marketability, or premiums for control.

Context

Because value depends on purpose, date and assumptions about the future, different methods can produce materially different results, and valuation conclusions are a matter of professional judgement as well as technique.
