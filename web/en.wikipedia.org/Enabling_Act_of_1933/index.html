Enabling Act of 1933

Full titleLaw to Remedy the Distress of People and Reich
Passed23 March 1933
Reichstag vote444 in favour, 94 against
EffectAllowed the cabinet to enact laws without the Reichstag, including laws deviating from the constitution

The Enabling Act of 1933 (German: Ermächtigungsgesetz), officially the Law to Remedy the Distress of People and Reich, was a law passed by the German Reichstag on 23 March 1933 that gave Adolf Hitler's cabinet the power to enact laws — including laws that violated the Weimar Constitution — without the involvement of the Reichstag or the Reich President. It is widely regarded as the legal foundation of the Nazi dictatorship and the decisive step in the destruction of the Weimar Republic.

The Reichstag met in the Kroll Opera House after the Reichstag fire

Background

Hitler became Chancellor on 30 January 1933. After the Reichstag fire of 27 February, the Reichstag Fire Decree suspended civil liberties and enabled mass arrests of Communists. Elections on 5 March, held amid intimidation, gave the Nazis and their nationalist allies a bare majority. To govern without parliament, Hitler sought an enabling act requiring a two-thirds majority as a constitutional amendment.

Passage

The Reichstag met on 23 March in the Kroll Opera House, ringed by SA and SS men. The 81 Communist deputies had been arrested or driven into exile and were excluded; the Centre Party voted in favour after receiving assurances from Hitler. Only the Social Democrats, led by Otto Wels, voted against, Wels declaring that freedom and life could be taken but not honour. The act passed 444 to 94.

Provisions and consequences

The act empowered the cabinet to legislate, conclude treaties, and spend money without parliament, and its laws could "deviate from the constitution" so long as they did not abolish the Reichstag, Reichsrat, or presidency as institutions. Initially valid for four years, it was renewed and remained in force throughout the Nazi era. Within months the Nazis used it to destroy the federal states (Gleichschaltung), ban all other political parties (July 1933), and rule Germany by decree.
