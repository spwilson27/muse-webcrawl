Fits and clearances

In mechanical engineering, fits and clearances describe the dimensional relationship between mating parts, typically a shaft and a hole, and the degree of tightness or looseness of their assembly.

Types of fit

Three broad classes are recognised. A clearance fit always leaves a gap between the parts, allowing free movement, as in a journal bearing. An interference fit (or press fit) makes the shaft always larger than the hole, so the parts must be forced together and are held by friction. A transition fit may result in either a small clearance or a small interference depending on the actual sizes within tolerance.

Standard systems

The ISO system of limits and fits (ISO 286) designates tolerances with letter-and-number codes — capital letters for holes, lower-case for shafts, and numbers for tolerance grades — such as H7/g6 for a common sliding fit. Designers choose either a hole-basis system, in which the hole size is held constant, or a shaft-basis system.
