Traveling Wilburys

The Traveling Wilburys were a British-American supergroup consisting of Bob Dylan, George Harrison, Jeff Lynne, Roy Orbison and Tom Petty. They formed in 1988 after recording a Harrison B-side, "Handle with Care," that record executives judged too good to use as a throwaway track, and they went on to record two albums characterised by a rootsy, collaborative style and a fictional backstory in which the members were half-brothers, sons of the wandering Charles Truscott Wilbury.

Albums

Their debut, Traveling Wilburys Vol. 1 (1988), was a critical and commercial success, reaching multi-platinum status in several countries and winning the Grammy Award for Best Rock Performance by a Duo or Group. Roy Orbison died in December 1988, shortly after its release. The remaining four members recorded a follow-up, deliberately misnumbered Traveling Wilburys Vol. 3 (1990), which was dedicated to Orbison's memory ("Lefty Wilbury").

Each member adopted a pseudonym: Harrison was "Nelson Wilbury", Dylan "Lucky Wilbury", Lynne "Otis Wilbury", Orbison "Lefty Wilbury" and Petty "Charlie T. Wilbury Jr."; on Vol. 3 the names changed to Spike, Boo, Clayton and Muddy Wilbury respectively, with drummer Jim Keltner as "Buster Sidebury".

Legacy

The group never toured. Their albums were out of print from 1995 until the 2007 box set The Traveling Wilburys Collection, which topped the UK album chart. Harrison's death in 2001 and Petty's in 2017 ended any possibility of further work, but the albums are regarded as a late-career highlight for all five members.
