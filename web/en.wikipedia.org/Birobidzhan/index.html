Birobidzhan

Birobidzhan (Russian: Биробиджа́н; Yiddish: ביראָבידזשאַן) is a town and the administrative centre of the Jewish Autonomous Oblast in the Russian Far East, located on the Trans-Siberian Railway near the border with China. Its population was about 68,000 at the 2021 census. The town is named after the rivers Bira and Bidzhan.

Birobidzhan railway station, with signage in Russian and Yiddish.

History

The settlement grew around the Tikhonkaya railway station, founded in 1915. In 1928 the Soviet government designated the district for Jewish settlement, and in 1934 the Jewish Autonomous Oblast was established with Birobidzhan as its capital; the town received city status in 1937. Jewish settlers arrived from Ukraine, Belarus, and abroad during the 1930s, and Yiddish was promoted in schools, a theatre, and the newspaper Birobidzhaner Shtern. Purges from 1936–38 and renewed repression in the late 1940s devastated the region's Jewish institutions.

Present day

Jews today form a small minority of the population, but Yiddish street signage, the Birobidzhan Synagogue (opened 2004), and the Yiddish-language newspaper survive. The economy is based on light industry, food processing, and transport. The town lies near the Bira River, about 170 kilometres west of Khabarovsk.

Climate

Birobidzhan has a monsoon-influenced humid continental climate, with warm, wet summers and very cold, dry winters typical of the Amur River basin.
