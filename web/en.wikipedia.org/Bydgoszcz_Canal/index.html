Bydgoszcz Canal

The Bydgoszcz Canal (Polish: Kanał Bydgoski, German: Bromberger Kanal) is a canal in Poland connecting the Vistula River, via the Brda River, with the Oder River, via the Noteć River. It runs through the city of Bydgoszcz and is about 24.7 km (15.3 mi) long. Built between 1772 and 1775 under Frederick the Great of Prussia, it is one of the oldest canals in Poland still in use, and it created the first direct water link between the basins of Poland's two great rivers.

History

The idea of linking the Brda and Noteć was raised as early as the 1760s. After the First Partition of Poland in 1772 brought the region under Prussian control, Frederick II ordered the canal's construction; work was carried out in 1773–1775 using thousands of labourers, many of whom died of disease in the marshy terrain. The canal opened to navigation in 1775.

During the 19th century the canal carried large volumes of timber, grain and industrial goods between the Vistula and Oder systems and drove the growth of Bydgoszcz (then Bromberg) as a trading city. It was modernized in 1905–1917 with new locks and a straightened course through the city, parts of the old channel surviving as the Old Bydgoszcz Canal (Stary Kanał Bydgoski), now a landscaped park corridor.

The canal today

The canal remains navigable and forms part of the E70 international inland waterway. Its locks, weirs and towpaths are protected monuments. The Old Canal section in Bydgoszcz, lined with historic locks and plane trees planted in the 19th century, is a popular recreational area, and the canal features in the city's Museum of the Bydgoszcz Canal.
