The Olympiastadion is a sports stadium at Olympiapark Berlin in Berlin, Germany. It was built for the 1936 Summer Olympics on the site of the earlier Deutsches Stadion, designed by Werner March in a monumental Neoclassical style faced with limestone. During the Games it held up to 110,000 spectators and witnessed the four gold medals of American sprinter Jesse Owens.

History

The original Deutsches Stadion (1913) was demolished in 1934 when the Nazi regime, having initially considered merely expanding it, decided on a grand new complex as a propaganda showcase, including the Maifeld parade ground and the Langemarck Hall. After the Second World War — which the stadium survived with minor damage — the British occupation forces used the surrounding grounds, while the stadium hosted football and other sports.

World Cups and renovation

The Olympiastadion was a venue for the 1974 FIFA World Cup. Between 2000 and 2004 it underwent a major renovation designed by Gerkan, Marg and Partners, lowering the pitch, adding a sweeping roof over all seats while preserving the historic fabric and the opening of the Marathon Gate, and reconfiguring it to a capacity of 74,475. It hosted six matches of the 2006 FIFA World Cup, including the final, in which Italy defeated France — the match remembered for Zinedine Zidane's headbutt on Marco Materazzi. The stadium hosted the 2009 World Athletics Championships, where Usain Bolt set world records in the 100 m and 200 m, and the 2015 UEFA Champions League Final.

Today

The stadium is the home ground of Hertha BSC and the annual venue of the DFB-Pokal (German Cup) final, held there every year since 1985. Its distinctive blue running track was installed in 2004. It is also a major concert venue.
