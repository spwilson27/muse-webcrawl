Seven Sisters (oil companies)

The Seven Sisters was a term coined in the 1950s by Italian businessman Enrico Mattei to describe the seven multinational oil companies that dominated the global petroleum industry from the mid-1940s to the early 1970s.

The companies

The original Seven Sisters were:

  • Anglo-Persian Oil Company (later British Petroleum, then BP)
  • Gulf Oil
  • Standard Oil of California (later Chevron)
  • Texaco
  • Royal Dutch Shell
  • Standard Oil of New Jersey (later Exxon)
  • Standard Oil of New York (later Mobil)

Decline

The group's dominance eroded with the rise of OPEC, the nationalization of oil reserves in producing countries during the 1970s, and the growth of state-owned companies. Through mergers, the seven were consolidated into four supermajors: BP, Chevron, ExxonMobil, and Shell. The Financial Times later used "new Seven Sisters" for the most influential state-owned oil companies outside the OECD.
