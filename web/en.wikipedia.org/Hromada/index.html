Hromada

A hromada (Ukrainian: громада, literally "community" or "assembly") is a basic unit of administrative division in Ukraine, similar to a municipality. Following the decentralisation reform launched in 2014, hromadas were formed by the voluntary amalgamation of villages, towns, and cities, and became the lowest level of Ukraine's administrative-territorial structure.

Hromadas are the basic self-governing communities of Ukraine.

Amalgamation reform

Under the reform, "amalgamated territorial communities" (об'єднана територіальна громада) were created between 2015 and 2020 by merging smaller rural and urban councils, which gained direct budgetary relations with the state budget and responsibility for local services such as schools, primary healthcare, roads, and utilities. In June 2020 the Cabinet of Ministers of Ukraine defined the administrative centres and territories of hromadas across the country, completing the map of about 1,470 hromadas (excluding temporarily occupied territories), which then elected councils and heads in the October 2020 local elections.

Types and governance

Hromadas are classified as urban, town (settlement), or rural according to their administrative centre. Each elects a council and a mayor or head, and its territory is subdivided informally into starosta districts representing the constituent settlements. The reform is widely regarded as one of post-2014 Ukraine's most successful structural changes, strengthening local government finances and resilience.
