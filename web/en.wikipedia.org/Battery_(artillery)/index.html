Battery (artillery)

Unit typeArtillery unit
Typical size6–8 guns; 100–200 personnel
Part ofArtillery battalion or regiment
Equivalent (infantry)Company

A battery in military terminology is a unit or system of artillery — guns, howitzers, mortars, or rocket launchers — grouped together under a single command for purposes of fire control and administration. Historically the term also referred to a fortified emplacement housing a group of coastal or fortress guns. In modern land armies, a battery is a company-sized artillery unit, typically consisting of between six and eight guns and roughly 100 to 200 soldiers, commanded by a major or captain and forming part of an artillery battalion or regiment.

Organization

A typical field artillery battery comprises a firing battery of gun sections, a headquarters element, and fire direction centre personnel who compute firing data. Batteries may be organized for tube artillery (cannon batteries), rockets and missiles, air defence, or headquarters and target acquisition functions. Naval usage applies "battery" to the armament of a warship or a group of guns on one deck or in one turret group, and to shore-based coastal artillery.

History

The grouping of guns into permanent batteries became standard in the 18th and 19th centuries as artillery professionalized; Napoleonic armies made extensive use of horse artillery batteries that could manoeuvre with cavalry. In sieges and coastal defence, fixed batteries of heavy guns guarded harbours and fortresses — a usage preserved in place names such as Battery Park in New York. Armies in the American Civil War typically fielded batteries of six guns (four in many Confederate units), and the two World Wars saw batteries of howitzers and heavy guns deployed on an industrial scale.

Not to be confused with the electric battery.
