Parmigiano Reggiano

Parmigiano Reggiano (Italian: [parmiˈdʒaːno redˈdʒaːno]), also known in English as Parmesan, is a hard, granular Italian cheese made from cow's milk in a delimited area of Emilia-Romagna and Lombardy — the provinces of Parma, Reggio Emilia, Modena, Bologna west of the Reno river, and Mantua south of the Po. It is produced under a Protected Designation of Origin (PDO) and has been called the "King of Cheeses." Within the European Union, the name "Parmesan" is legally protected as a translation of Parmigiano Reggiano; outside the EU, generic "parmesan" cheeses are widely made.

Production

The cheese is made in copper-lined vats from the partly skimmed evening milk mixed with the whole morning milk of cows fed only on grass and hay grown in the zone; no additives or silage are permitted. Natural whey starter and calf rennet curdle the milk; the curd is cut into rice-sized grains, cooked, settled, and gathered into moulds that form wheels of about 38–40 kg. Wheels are brined for about three weeks and then aged on wooden shelves in storehouses. At 12 months, consortium inspectors hammer-test every wheel; those that pass are fire-branded with the oval Parmigiano Reggiano mark, while inferior wheels have their rinds scored to remove the markings (and may be sold as lesser cheese).

Characteristics and use

Long ageing concentrates flavour and produces crunchy crystals of the amino acid tyrosine. The cheese is rich in umami, calcium, and protein, and becomes more piquant and complex at 24 and 36 months; wheels aged 40 months and beyond are prized delicacies. It is grated over pasta, risotto and soups, shaved into salads, and eaten in chunks with balsamic vinegar or honey. The hard rind flavours stocks and sauces.

History

The cheese's method was developed by Benedictine and Cistercian monks in the Middle Ages — it was already famous when Boccaccio described a mountain of grated Parmesan in the Decameron (c. 1348–1353). The producers' consortium, the Consorzio del Formaggio Parmigiano Reggiano, was founded in 1934. Roughly 3.8–4 million wheels are produced each year by several hundred small dairies (caseifici) supplied by thousands of farms. In May 2012, earthquakes in Emilia damaged ageing warehouses and hundreds of thousands of wheels, prompting a public campaign to buy the fallen cheese.

Protection and imitations

In 2008 the European Court of Justice confirmed that "Parmesan" is an evocation of the PDO reserved to authentic product in the EU. Elsewhere — notably the United States — "parmesan" is a generic term for hard grating cheeses, many made by industrial methods quite unlike the original. Grana Padano, another Italian PDO grana cheese aged for a shorter minimum period, is its closest traditional relative.
