Juan Atkins

Born: September 12, 1962, Detroit, Michigan, U.S.
Genres: Techno, electro
Occupations: DJ, record producer
Labels: Metroplex
Associated acts: Cybotron; Model 500; Infiniti; the Belleville Three

Juan Atkins (born September 12, 1962) is an American musician widely credited as the originator of techno music, specifically Detroit techno. Along with Derrick May and Kevin Saunderson — collectively known as the Belleville Three — he pioneered the electronic dance music sound that emerged from Detroit in the 1980s.

Early career and Cybotron

Atkins grew up in Detroit and Belleville, Michigan, absorbing the futuristic funk of Parliament-Funkadelic, the electronic music of Kraftwerk, and the eclectic radio programming of Detroit DJ Charles "The Electrifying Mojo" Johnson. Studying at Washtenaw Community College, he met Rik Davis, with whom he formed Cybotron. Their singles "Clear" (1983) and "Techno City" (1984) fused electro-funk with a machine-age vision Atkins drew from Alvin Toffler's The Third Wave, whose term "techno rebels" gave the music its name.

Metroplex and Model 500

In 1985 Atkins founded the Metroplex label and, recording as Model 500, released "No UFO's," generally regarded as the first Detroit techno record. Tracks such as "Night Drive (Thru-Babylon)," "The Chase," and "Off to Battle" established the template: Roland TR-808 and TR-909 rhythms, synthesizer sequences, and Afrofuturist imagery. Metroplex also launched the careers of May and Saunderson, whose labels and releases carried the sound to Chicago and then to Europe, where it exploded in the late-1980s rave era.

Later work

Atkins continued recording as Model 500 and Infiniti, collaborating with European producers such as Moritz von Oswald in the Borderland project, and DJing worldwide. He has been honoured in his home city, including an exhibition on Detroit techno at the Detroit Historical Museum, and is universally cited — with May and Saunderson — as a founding father of techno.
