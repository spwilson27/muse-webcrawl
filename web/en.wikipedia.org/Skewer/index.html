Skewer

Skewer Materials: Metal; wood; bamboo
Uses: Grilling kebabs; fondue; garnishes; craft

A skewer is a thin metal or wood stick used to hold pieces of food together. The word may derive from the Middle English skeuier or the Middle High German spiz. Skewers are used while grilling or roasting meats and fish, and in other culinary applications, and are fundamental to dishes such as kebabs and satay found across the Middle East, the Mediterranean and Asia.

Types and materials

Metal skewers are typically stainless steel with a pointed tip and a looped or decorative handle for turning; flat-bladed metal skewers help prevent food from spinning. Wooden and bamboo skewers are inexpensive and disposable; they are usually soaked in water before grilling so that they do not burn. Skewers vary in length from short cocktail sticks used for canapés and olives to long swords used for Brazilian churrasco.

Culinary uses

Skewered dishes include Middle Eastern kebab, Greek souvlaki, Japanese yakitori, Southeast Asian satay, French brochettes and Peruvian anticuchos. Threading ingredients on skewers exposes a large surface area to heat and marinade and allows small pieces to cook evenly without falling through the grill. Skewers are also used to test the doneness of cakes, to truss poultry, and to hold together stuffed or layered foods while cooking.

Other uses

Beyond cooking, skewers are used in crafts, for cleaning narrow passages, and in science education. In chess, a "skewer" is also the name of a tactic in which a valuable piece is attacked and, when it moves, exposes a less valuable piece behind it.
