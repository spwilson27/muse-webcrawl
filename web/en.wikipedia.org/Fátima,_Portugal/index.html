Fátima, Portugal

Fátima is a city and civil parish in the municipality of Ourém, in the Santarém District of central Portugal. It is world-famous as a major Catholic pilgrimage site, centred on the Sanctuary of Fátima, built on the site where three shepherd children reported apparitions of the Virgin Mary in 1917.

The apparitions

Between May and October 1917, Lúcia dos Santos and her cousins Francisco and Jacinta Marto reported a series of apparitions of a lady identified as Our Lady of the Rosary at the Cova da Iria. The events culminated on 13 October 1917 in the so-called "Miracle of the Sun", witnessed by a large crowd. The apparitions were declared worthy of belief by the Bishop of Leiria in 1930, and the "Three Secrets of Fátima" associated with the visions became widely known. Francisco and Jacinta were canonised by Pope Francis in 2017.

The Sanctuary

The Sanctuary of Fátima includes the Basilica of Our Lady of the Rosary (built 1928–1953 in neo-Baroque style, with a 65-metre tower), the Chapel of the Apparitions, and the vast Basilica of the Holy Trinity, consecrated in 2007 and one of the largest churches in the world, seating over 8,600 people. Millions of pilgrims visit annually, particularly on 13 May and 13 October, and many approach the chapel on their knees along a long marble path.

Name and history

The town's name derives, according to legend, from Fátima, a Moorish princess of the twelfth century who converted to Christianity. Before 1917 it was an obscure rural parish; the pilgrimage economy transformed it into a city (elevated in 1997) of hotels, religious shops and services for visitors.
