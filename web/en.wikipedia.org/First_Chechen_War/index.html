The First Chechen War, also known as the First Chechen Campaign, was fought from December 1994 to August 1996 between the Russian Federation and the Chechen Republic of Ichkeria, which had declared independence from Moscow in 1991 under President Dzhokhar Dudayev. The war ended in a humiliating defeat for Russia and de facto independence for Chechnya.

Background

Chechnya, a predominantly Muslim republic in the North Caucasus, had a long history of resistance to Russian rule, including the mass deportation of the Chechen people to Central Asia in 1944. As the Soviet Union collapsed, Dudayev seized power in Grozny and declared independence. President Boris Yeltsin refused to recognize the declaration; after failed negotiations and a botched covert attempt to overthrow Dudayev, Russian forces invaded on 11 December 1994.

Course of the war

Russian armored columns advancing on Grozny were ambushed in the city during the disastrous New Year's Eve assault of 1994–95, in which entire units were destroyed in the streets. After weeks of heavy artillery and air bombardment, Russian forces captured the ruined capital in March 1995 and pushed into the mountains, but Chechen fighters under commanders such as Aslan Maskhadov and Shamil Basayev waged effective guerrilla warfare. Dudayev was killed in a Russian airstrike in April 1996. In August 1996 Chechen forces infiltrated and retook Grozny, trapping thousands of Russian troops and forcing Moscow to negotiate.

Khasavyurt Accord and aftermath

On 31 August 1996, Russian Security Council secretary Alexander Lebed and Maskhadov signed the Khasavyurt Accord, ending the war and deferring a decision on Chechnya's status until 2001. Russian forces withdrew, and Maskhadov was elected president of Ichkeria in 1997. The war left tens of thousands dead — estimates of civilian deaths range from 30,000 to 100,000 — and Grozny in ruins. Chechnya's de facto independence collapsed into lawlessness, and the conflict resumed with the Second Chechen War in 1999.
