Tunisian Revolution

Date17 December 2010 – 14 January 2011
LocationTunisia
OutcomePresident Zine El Abidine Ben Ali flees; democratic transition begins

The Tunisian Revolution, also called the Jasmine Revolution, was a campaign of civil resistance in Tunisia in late 2010 and early 2011 that forced President Zine El Abidine Ben Ali to flee the country after 23 years in power. It was the first uprising of the Arab Spring.

Catalyst

On 17 December 2010, street vendor Mohamed Bouazizi set himself on fire in Sidi Bouzid to protest police harassment and unemployment, igniting protests that spread nationwide despite a violent crackdown.

Fall of Ben Ali

On 14 January 2011, after weeks of demonstrations and a general strike, Ben Ali fled to Saudi Arabia. A national unity government was formed, and in October 2011 Tunisia held free elections for a Constituent Assembly, which adopted a new constitution in 2014.

Legacy

Tunisia's transition was widely regarded as the Arab Spring's relative success story, though the country later faced economic stagnation and political turmoil, including President Kais Saied's consolidation of power after 2021.
