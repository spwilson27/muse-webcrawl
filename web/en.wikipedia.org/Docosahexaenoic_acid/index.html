Docosahexaenoic acid

Chemical formulaC22H32O2
Molar mass328.49 g/mol
TypeOmega-3 fatty acid

Docosahexaenoic acid (DHA) is an omega-3 fatty acid with the chemical structure 22:6(n-3): a 22-carbon chain with six cis double bonds. It is a primary structural component of the human brain, cerebral cortex, skin, and retina, and is among the most abundant fatty acids in neural cell membranes.

Sources and synthesis

DHA is obtained from the diet, principally from fatty fish and fish oils, and from algal oils, the original source of omega-3s in the marine food chain. Humans can convert the plant-derived omega-3 alpha-linolenic acid (ALA) to DHA, but the conversion is inefficient, so dietary intake is the major determinant of DHA status. DHA is present in human breast milk and is added to many infant formulas.

Biological role

In the retina, DHA constitutes a large fraction of the fatty acids in photoreceptor outer-segment membranes, where its flexibility supports the function of rhodopsin. In the brain it is concentrated at synapses. DHA is also the precursor of specialised pro-resolving mediators such as resolvins and neuroprotectin D1, which participate in the resolution of inflammation.

Health research

DHA has been studied extensively in relation to infant development, cognitive ageing, and cardiovascular health. While observational studies link fish consumption to favourable outcomes, clinical trials of DHA supplements have produced mixed results, and health authorities generally recommend obtaining omega-3s from food sources.
