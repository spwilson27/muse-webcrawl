Anhedonia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Anhedonia is the inability or reduced ability to feel pleasure from activities that are usually enjoyable, such as eating, music, social interaction or hobbies. The term derives from the Greek an- (without) and hēdonē (pleasure), and was introduced by the psychologist Théodule-Armand Ribot in 1896.

Types

Researchers distinguish social anhedonia (reduced pleasure from interpersonal contact) from physical anhedonia (reduced pleasure from sensory experiences such as food or touch). A further distinction is drawn between anticipatory anhedonia (reduced ability to look forward to rewards), consummatory anhedonia (reduced pleasure during the activity itself) and motivational anhedonia.

Causes and associations

Anhedonia is a core symptom of major depressive disorder and one of the negative symptoms of schizophrenia. It also occurs in substance use disorders, Parkinson's disease, post-traumatic stress disorder and after some infections. It is linked to dysfunction in the brain's reward system, particularly dopaminergic pathways involving the nucleus accumbens and prefrontal cortex.

Treatment

Treatment generally targets the underlying condition, using psychotherapy, behavioural activation and medication. Anhedonia can be slow to respond to standard antidepressants, and it is assessed clinically with scales such as the Snaith–Hamilton Pleasure Scale.
