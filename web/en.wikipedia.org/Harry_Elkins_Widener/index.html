Harry Elkins Widener

Harry Elkins Widener (January 3, 1885 – April 15, 1912) was an American businessman, book collector, and graduate of Harvard University who died in the sinking of the RMS Titanic. In his memory, his mother Eleanor Elkins Widener donated the Widener Library to Harvard, now the centrepiece of the Harvard library system.

Harry Elkins Widener
Born: January 3, 1885, Philadelphia
Died: April 15, 1912, North Atlantic
Alma mater: Harvard University
Known for: Book collecting; Widener Library namesake

Life

Widener was the son of George Dunton Widener and Eleanor Elkins Widener, heirs to streetcar fortunes in Philadelphia. He graduated from Harvard in 1907 and became an avid and discerning collector of rare books, assembling notable holdings of Shakespeare, Dickens, and Robert Louis Stevenson, among others.

Death and memorial

Returning from a book-buying trip to England with his parents aboard the Titanic, Widener and his father perished when the ship sank; his mother survived in a lifeboat. Eleanor Widener gave Harvard $2 million to build a library in her son's memory, on condition that his collection form its core. The Harry Elkins Widener Memorial Library opened in 1915.
