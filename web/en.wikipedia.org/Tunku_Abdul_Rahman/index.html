Tunku Abdul Rahman Putra Al-Haj ibni Almarhum Sultan Abdul Hamid Halim Shah (8 August 1903 – 6 December 1990) was a Malaysian statesman who served as the first prime minister of Malaysia, from the independence of the Federation of Malaya in 1957 and the formation of Malaysia in 1963 until his resignation in 1970. He is widely regarded as Malaysia's "founding father" — the architect of Malayan independence and the formation of Malaysia.

Early life and education

Born in Alor Setar, Kedah, Tunku was the seventh son of Sultan Abdul Hamid Halim Shah of Kedah. He studied at Cambridge University (St Catharine's College), where he was admitted to the Bar at the Inner Temple in London. He later served in the Kedah civil service, including as district officer.

Road to independence

Tunku became president of the United Malays National Organisation (UMNO) in 1951. He forged the Alliance (Perikatan) coalition with the Malayan Chinese Association (MCA) and the Malayan Indian Congress (MIC), which won the 1955 federal elections decisively. As Chief Minister, he led the Merdeka Mission to London in 1956 and negotiated independence from Britain. On 31 August 1957, at Stadium Merdeka in Kuala Lumpur, he famously proclaimed independence, chanting "Merdeka!" seven times before a jubilant crowd.

Formation of Malaysia

In 1961 Tunku proposed merging Malaya with Singapore, Sabah, Sarawak, and Brunei. Malaysia was proclaimed on 16 September 1963; Singapore separated in 1965. His tenure saw the Indonesia–Malaysia confrontation (Konfrontasi) and the formation of ASEAN in 1967, of which he was a driving force. He also served as the first Secretary-General of the Organisation of the Islamic Conference (now OIC).

Later years

Following the 13 May 1969 racial riots, Tunku's influence waned, and he resigned in 1970, succeeded by Tun Abdul Razak. He remained a revered elder statesman and wrote newspaper columns critiquing later governments. He died in Kuala Lumpur on 6 December 1990.
