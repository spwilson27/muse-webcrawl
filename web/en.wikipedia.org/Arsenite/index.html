Arsenite

In chemistry, an arsenite is a chemical compound containing an arsenic oxoanion in which arsenic has oxidation state +3. The parent acid is arsenous acid, H3AsO3, a weak acid existing in solution as As(OH)3; its salts contain anions such as AsO33− and protonated forms H2AsO3− and HAsO32−. In environmental and biological usage "arsenite" commonly denotes dissolved trivalent arsenic, As(III), generally.

Parent acidArsenous acid, H₃AsO₃
Oxidation stateAs(III)
ExamplesSodium arsenite, potassium arsenite (Fowler's solution)

Chemistry

Arsenite ions have a pyramidal geometry with a lone pair on arsenic, in contrast to the tetrahedral arsenates. Arsenites are reducing agents and are oxidized to arsenates (As(V)) by oxidants such as chlorine and ozone — a step used in water treatment to make arsenic easier to remove. Sodium arsenite was widely used as a herbicide and in sheep dips; copper arsenite is the pigment Scheele's green.

Toxicology

As(III) is generally more toxic than As(V) in acute exposure: arsenites bind strongly to thiol (sulfhydryl) groups of proteins, inhibiting enzymes including pyruvate dehydrogenase and glutathione reductase, a mechanism central to arsenic poisoning. Certain bacteria can oxidize arsenite for energy or reduce arsenate to arsenite in detoxification and respiration.
