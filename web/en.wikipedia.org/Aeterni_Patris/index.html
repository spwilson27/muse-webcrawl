Aeterni Patris

TypeEncyclical
PopeLeo XIII
Date4 August 1879
SubjectRestoration of Christian philosophy

Aeterni Patris ("Of the Eternal Father") was an encyclical issued by Pope Leo XIII on 4 August 1879. It is subtitled "On the Restoration of Christian Philosophy in Catholic Schools in the Spirit of the Angelic Doctor, St. Thomas Aquinas." The encyclical sought to revive scholastic philosophy, especially Thomism, as the proper framework for Catholic theology and philosophy, and it is widely credited with launching the modern Neo-Thomist movement.

Content

Leo praised philosophy rightly used as a handmaid to faith, surveyed the Church Fathers and scholastics, and singled out Thomas Aquinas as the pre-eminent model. He urged Catholic seminaries and universities to teach Aquinas's doctrine while remaining open to genuine advances in the sciences.

Legacy

The encyclical spurred institutions such as the Higher Institute of Philosophy at Louvain under Désiré Mercier and influenced twentieth-century Catholic intellectuals including Étienne Gilson and Jacques Maritain. Its prescriptions shaped Catholic seminary education until the Second Vatican Council era.
