Financial independence

Financial independence is the status of having enough income or wealth to pay one's living expenses for the rest of one's life without having to be employed or dependent on others. Income earned without working a conventional job is commonly called passive income. Those who achieve financial independence before a typical retirement age are often associated with the FIRE movement (Financial Independence, Retire Early).

Approach

The standard approach is to accumulate income-generating assets — such as investment portfolios, rental property, or business interests — until the income they produce covers living expenses. A common rule of thumb holds that a portfolio of roughly 25 times annual expenses, withdrawn at about 4 per cent per year (the "4% rule", derived from the Trinity study of historical returns), has a high probability of lasting through retirement. Reaching the target depends chiefly on the savings rate: the proportion of income saved and invested.

FIRE movement

The FIRE movement popularised aggressive saving — often 50 per cent or more of income — combined with frugality and low-cost index investing, drawing on books such as Your Money or Your Life (1992) and blogs of the 2010s. Variants include "Lean FIRE" (independence on modest spending), "Fat FIRE" (independence at a high standard of living), and "Barista FIRE" (part-time work supplementing investments). Critics note the approach's dependence on continued market returns, healthcare costs, and the assumption of high salaries that make extreme savings rates possible.
