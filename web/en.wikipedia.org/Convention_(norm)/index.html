Convention (norm)

A convention is a set of agreed, stipulated, or generally accepted standards, social norms, or other criteria, often taking the form of a custom. Certain types of rules or customs may become law and regulatory legislation may be introduced to formalize or enforce the convention (for example, laws defining which side of the road vehicles must drive on). In a social context, a convention may retain the character of an "unwritten law" of custom.

General conventions

Conventions range from greetings and table manners to the conventions of measurement and documentation. Many are arbitrary in themselves — such as driving on the left or right — but valuable because everyone follows them consistently. Philosophers such as David Lewis analyzed conventions as regularities in behavior sustained by common knowledge and mutual expectation, solutions to recurring coordination problems.

Conventions in government and law

In constitutional systems, constitutional conventions are unwritten rules of political practice considered binding on those who follow them, such as the convention that a head of state acts on ministerial advice. In international law, conventions are also formal multilateral treaties. In other fields the term denotes agreed technical standards, such as sign conventions in physics and mathematics, and conventions in the arts and literature, where a convention is a device or setting accepted by audiences as necessary to suspend disbelief.
