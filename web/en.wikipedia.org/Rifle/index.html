Rifle

A rifle is a long-barrelled firearm designed for accurate shooting, distinguished by a barrel cut with a helical pattern of grooves (rifling) that impart spin to the projectile, stabilising it in flight and improving accuracy and range. Rifles are used in warfare, law enforcement, hunting and target shooting.

TypeLong gun

History

Rifled barrels appeared in the German-speaking lands in the late 15th and early 16th centuries, but tight-fitting bullets were slow to load, so rifles long remained specialists' weapons for hunters and sharpshooters while smoothbore muskets armed line infantry. The invention of the expanding Minié ball in the mid-19th century made muzzle-loading rifles practical for mass armies, and breech-loading designs with metallic cartridges — such as the Dreyse needle gun, the Chassepot and the Mauser — completed the transition. By the First World War the bolt-action magazine rifle was the standard infantry weapon; the Second World War brought widespread semi-automatic rifles and the first assault rifles.

Types

  • By action: muzzle-loading, bolt-action, lever-action, pump-action, semi-automatic, fully automatic, single-shot.
  • By role: assault rifle, battle rifle, carbine, sniper rifle, designated marksman rifle, hunting rifle, target rifle.

Operation

Modern rifles fire cartridges consisting of a case, primer, propellant and bullet. Rifling typically consists of grooves cut or hammer-forged into the bore with a constant or increasing twist rate matched to the projectile, giving gyroscopic stability over long ranges.
