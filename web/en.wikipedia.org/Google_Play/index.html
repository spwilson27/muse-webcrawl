Google Play

Google Play, also known as the Google Play Store or Play Store, is a digital distribution service operated and developed by Google. It serves as the official app store for certified devices running on the Android operating system and its derivatives, as well as ChromeOS, allowing users to browse and download applications developed with the Android software development kit (SDK) and published through Google. Google Play also offers a digital media store with books, films, and formerly music and magazines.

History

Google Play was launched on 6 March 2012, uniting the Android Market (launched 2008) with Google Music and the Google eBookstore under one brand. The store grew rapidly with Android's market share; it passed one million published apps in 2013 and billions of downloads annually. Google Play Books and Play Movies & TV followed, while Google Play Music was shut down in 2020 in favour of YouTube Music, and Play Newsstand evolved into Google News.

Policies and business model

Developers register for a one-time fee and distribute free or paid apps; Google historically took a 30 percent commission on sales, later introducing a 15 percent tier for the first $1 million of annual earnings and for subscriptions. Google Play Protect scans apps for malware. The store's dominance and billing rules have drawn antitrust scrutiny, including the Epic Games v. Google case in the United States, in which a 2023 jury found Google had maintained an illegal monopoly over Android app distribution and billing.
