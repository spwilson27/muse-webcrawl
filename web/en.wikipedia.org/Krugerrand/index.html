The Krugerrand (/ˈkruːɡərænd/; Afrikaans: [ˈkry.ərˌrant]) is a South African coin, first minted on 3 July 1967 to help market South African gold and produced by the Rand Refinery and the South African Mint. The name is a compound of Kruger, the former South African president Paul Kruger (depicted on the obverse), and rand, the South African unit of currency. On the reverse side of the Krugerrand is a pronking springbok, South Africa's national animal, designed by Coert Steynberg.

Design and specifications

The Krugerrand is minted in 22-karat gold alloyed with copper, giving it a distinctive orange tint and greater durability than pure gold coins. The full-size coin contains exactly one troy ounce (31.103 g) of fine gold and weighs 33.93 g overall, with a diameter of 32.77 mm. Fractional sizes — one-half, one-quarter and one-tenth ounce — were introduced in 1980. The coin carries no face value; it is legal tender in South Africa and its value is tied directly to the gold price, traditionally quoted in rands.

History

By the 1970s the Krugerrand had become the world's best-known bullion coin, accounting for the majority of the global gold coin market and popularising the concept of one-ounce bullion coins with private investors. During the 1970s and 1980s, some Western countries forbade import of the Krugerrand because of South Africa's policy of apartheid; the United States banned imports in 1985. The bans were lifted after apartheid ended in 1994, and the Krugerrand remains in production, joined since 2017 by a silver version. Its success inspired other national bullion coins, including the Canadian Maple Leaf, American Gold Eagle and Australian Nugget.
