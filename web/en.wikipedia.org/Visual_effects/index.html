In filmmaking and video production, visual effects (sometimes abbreviated VFX) is the process through which imagery is created or manipulated outside the context of a live-action shot. The integration of live-action footage and other live-action footage or computer-generated imagery (CGI) elements to create realistic imagery is called VFX.

VFX involves the integration of live-action footage (which may include in-camera special effects) and generated imagery (digital or optical, animals or creatures) which look realistic, but would be dangerous, expensive, impractical, time-consuming or impossible to capture on film. Visual effects using CGI have more recently become accessible to independent filmmakers with the introduction of affordable animation and compositing software.

History

Early visual effects were achieved in camera and in the optical printer: Georges Méliès pioneered stop tricks, substitutions and multiple exposures from the 1890s; matte paintings, glass shots, miniatures and rear projection became standard studio tools. Milestones of the photochemical era include King Kong (1933), 2001: A Space Odyssey (1968) and Star Wars (1977), for which Industrial Light & Magic (ILM) revived and computerised motion-control photography.

The digital era began with the first computer-generated sequences in films such as Tron (1982) and the morphing of Terminator 2: Judgment Day (1991). Jurassic Park (1993) demonstrated photorealistic CGI creatures; Toy Story (1995) was the first fully computer-animated feature; and The Matrix (1999), the Lord of the Rings trilogy and Avatar (2009) pushed performance capture and virtual production forward.

Techniques

Major VFX techniques include digital compositing (layering images, keyed over blue or green screens), matte painting and digital set extension, 3D modelling, animation and simulation of particles, fluids, cloth and destruction; match moving and camera tracking; rotoscoping and clean-up; and motion capture of performers' movements and facial expressions. VFX is distinguished from special effects (SFX), which are created on set during filming — pyrotechnics, mechanical rigs, animatronics and prosthetics — although modern productions blend the two extensively. The Academy Award for Best Visual Effects has been presented since 1977.
