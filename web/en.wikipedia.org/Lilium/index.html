Lilium

Lilium is a genus of herbaceous flowering plants growing from bulbs, all with large, prominent flowers. They are commonly called lilies — true lilies, to distinguish them from other plants with "lily" in the name. The genus comprises around 110 species native to temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere, extending into the subtropics.

Description

Lilies grow from scaly underground bulbs, producing leafy stems bearing whorled or scattered leaves and flowers with six tepals, often fragrant and in colours ranging from white and yellow to orange, pink, and red, frequently spotted. Many species are widely cultivated as ornamentals, and hybrid groups — Asiatic, Oriental, trumpet, and Orienpet — dominate the garden and cut-flower trade.

Culture and uses

Lilies have deep symbolic associations with purity and renewal, feature prominently in art and heraldry (the fleur-de-lis), and some species have edible bulbs used in East Asian cooking and traditional medicine. Note that lilies are highly toxic to cats, in which ingestion can cause acute kidney failure.
