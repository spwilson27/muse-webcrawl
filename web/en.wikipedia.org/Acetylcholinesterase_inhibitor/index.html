Acetylcholinesterase inhibitor

Acetylcholinesterase inhibitor
Target: Acetylcholinesterase enzyme
Effect: Increases acetylcholine levels
Examples: Donepezil, physostigmine, organophosphates

An acetylcholinesterase inhibitor (AChEI) is a chemical or drug that inhibits the enzyme acetylcholinesterase from breaking down the neurotransmitter acetylcholine into acetate and choline. This increases the level and duration of action of acetylcholine in synapses.

Medical uses

Reversible inhibitors such as donepezil, rivastigmine, and galantamine are used to treat symptoms of Alzheimer's disease and other dementias. Physostigmine and neostigmine are used in anesthesia and for myasthenia gravis.

Toxic agents

Irreversible inhibitors include organophosphate insecticides such as malathion and nerve agents such as sarin and VX. Poisoning produces a cholinergic crisis treated with atropine and oximes such as pralidoxime.
