Cybercrime

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cybercrime (also called computer crime) is a crime involving a computer or computer network. The computer may have been used in committing the crime, or it may be the target. Cybercrime ranges from fraud, trafficking in stolen data, and identity theft to cyberespionage, ransomware attacks, and acts of cyberwarfare by state-sponsored groups.

Classification

  • Crimes against persons — cyberstalking, harassment, distribution of illicit material.
  • Crimes against property — unauthorised access, vandalism, malware, ransomware, theft of data.
  • Crimes against government — cyberterrorism and attacks on critical infrastructure.
  • Financial crimes — phishing, carding, business email compromise, and online fraud.

Scale and response

Cybercrime is typically transnational, complicating investigation and prosecution. International cooperation is framed by instruments such as the Council of Europe's Budapest Convention (2001), the first international treaty on crimes committed via the Internet. Law enforcement agencies including the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) record losses in the billions of dollars annually. Defensive practice — patching, multifactor authentication, backups, and security awareness — forms the field of computer security.
