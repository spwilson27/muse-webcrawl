Hyperthermia

Hyperthermia
Field: Emergency medicine
Definition: Core body temperature above normal due to failed thermoregulation
Normal core temperature: ~37 °C (98.6 °F)

Hyperthermia, also known simply as overheating, is a condition in which an individual's body temperature is elevated beyond normal due to failed thermoregulation. It occurs when the body produces or absorbs more heat than it dissipates, in contrast to fever, in which the hypothalamic set point is raised.

Causes

The most common causes include heat stroke, adverse reactions to drugs, and reactions to general anaesthesia (malignant hyperthermia). External factors such as hot environments, exertion, dehydration, and inadequate clothing or ventilation contribute. Certain drugs—including stimulants and some psychiatric medications—can impair sweating or increase heat production.

Classification

Heat-related illnesses range from mild heat cramps and heat syncope through heat exhaustion to heat stroke, a life-threatening emergency marked by core temperature above 40 °C (104 °F) and central nervous system dysfunction such as confusion, seizures, or coma.

Treatment

Treatment centres on rapid cooling: moving the patient to a cool environment, removing clothing, applying cold water or ice packs, and in hospital, cooled intravenous fluids or immersion. In heat stroke, rapid whole-body cooling within the first half hour greatly improves survival. Malignant hyperthermia is treated with dantrolene. See also Hypothermia and Thermoregulation.
