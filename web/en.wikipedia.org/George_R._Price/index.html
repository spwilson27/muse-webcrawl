George Robert Price (October 6, 1922 – January 6, 1975) was an American population geneticist. Originally a physical chemist who worked on the Manhattan Project, he later moved to London and made fundamental contributions to evolutionary theory, most notably the Price equation.

Scientific work

Price developed a mathematical identity, now called the Price equation, describing how the frequency of a gene changes over time. Although first derived as a statistical covariance relationship, it provided a powerful new framework for understanding natural selection. Working informally with W. D. Hamilton and John Maynard Smith, Price helped formalize theories of kin selection and the evolution of altruism, and co-developed an application of game theory to animal behaviour.

Later life

In 1973 Price, a lifelong atheist, underwent a religious conversion and devoted himself to helping the poor and homeless, giving away his possessions. Suffering from depression and ill health, he died by suicide in London in 1975.
