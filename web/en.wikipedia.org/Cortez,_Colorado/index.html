Cortez is a home rule municipality in southwestern Colorado, United States, and the county seat of Montezuma County. Situated in the Montezuma Valley near the Four Corners, where Colorado meets Utah, Arizona, and New Mexico, it serves as a commercial centre for the surrounding farming and ranching country and as a gateway to major archaeological sites.

History

The area has been inhabited for millennia; the Ancestral Pueblo peoples built the cliff dwellings and great houses of the surrounding region. The town was founded in 1886 during the development of irrigation works that diverted water from the Dolores River into the Montezuma Valley, and was named for the Spanish conquistador Hernán Cortés. Ranching, dryland farming — especially pinto beans — and later tourism shaped its economy.

Geography and sights

Cortez lies at an elevation of roughly 6,200 feet in high desert country, with the Sleeping Ute Mountain prominent to the southwest. Nearby attractions include Mesa Verde National Park, famous for its Ancestral Puebloan cliff dwellings; the Canyons of the Ancients National Monument; the Ute Mountain Tribal Park; and the Cortez Cultural Center, which interprets the region's Native American and pioneer heritage. The town is also a stop on the San Juan Skyway scenic byway.

Demographics

The population was about 8,800 at the 2020 census. The community includes a significant Native American population, and the headquarters of the Ute Mountain Ute Tribe are located to the south at Towaoc.
