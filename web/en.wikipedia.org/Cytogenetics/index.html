Cytogenetics

Cytogenetics is a branch of genetics that is concerned with how chromosomes relate to cell behaviour, particularly their behaviour during mitosis and meiosis. Techniques used include karyotyping, analysis of G-banded chromosomes, other cytogenetic banding techniques, and molecular cytogenetics such as fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) and comparative genomic hybridization (CGH).

History

Chromosomes were first observed in plant cells by Karl Wilhelm von Nägeli in 1842, and their behaviour in animal cells was described by Walther Flemming in 1882. The chromosome theory of inheritance, proposed by Theodor Boveri and Walter Sutton in 1902–1903, linked chromosomes to heredity. The correct human chromosome number of 46 was established only in 1956 by Joe Hin Tjio and Albert Levan, using improved tissue-culture and colchicine techniques. In 1959, Jérôme Lejeune showed that Down syndrome results from an extra copy of chromosome 21 — the first chromosomal disorder identified in humans. The development of banding techniques around 1970, notably Q-banding by Torbjörn Caspersson and G-banding, allowed each chromosome to be identified individually and launched modern clinical cytogenetics. The Philadelphia chromosome, described by Peter Nowell and David Hungerford in 1960, was later shown to be a reciprocal translocation t(9;22) and became the first consistent chromosomal abnormality linked to a cancer, chronic myeloid leukaemia.

Techniques

Karyotyping

A karyotype is produced by arresting dividing cells in metaphase with colchicine, swelling them in a hypotonic solution, fixing, and staining. The chromosomes are photographed, cut out and arranged in pairs by size, centromere position and banding pattern. Karyotyping detects aneuploidies (such as trisomy 21, 18 and 13), large deletions and duplications, and balanced rearrangements such as translocations and inversions.

Banding

G-banding (Giemsa banding after mild trypsin treatment) produces a pattern of light and dark bands unique to each chromosome. R-banding gives the reverse pattern, while Q-banding uses quinacrine fluorescence. High-resolution banding on prometaphase chromosomes can reveal several hundred to over a thousand bands per haploid set.

Molecular cytogenetics

FISH uses fluorescently labelled DNA probes that bind to specific sequences, allowing detection of microdeletions (for example the 22q11.2 deletion in DiGeorge syndrome), gene rearrangements in cancers, and chromosome enumeration in interphase nuclei. Chromosomal microarray (array CGH and SNP arrays) detects submicroscopic copy-number variants across the genome and has largely replaced karyotyping as a first-tier test for developmental disorders, though it cannot detect balanced rearrangements.

Applications

  • Prenatal diagnosis — analysis of amniotic fluid or chorionic villus samples.
  • Postnatal diagnosis — investigation of congenital abnormalities, intellectual disability and disorders of sex development.
  • Haematology and oncology — classification and prognosis of leukaemias and lymphomas; monitoring of minimal residual disease.
  • Reproductive medicine — investigation of recurrent miscarriage and infertility from parental balanced translocations.
