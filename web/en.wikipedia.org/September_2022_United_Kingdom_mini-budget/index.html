September 2022 United Kingdom mini-budget

The September 2022 United Kingdom mini-budget, officially known as The Growth Plan, was a fiscal statement presented by Chancellor of the Exchequer Kwasi Kwarteng on 23 September 2022 under the new prime minister, Liz Truss. Although not formally a budget, it proposed the largest package of tax cuts in decades, to be funded by increased government borrowing. The announcement triggered a sharp fall in the pound sterling and a surge in government bond yields, leading to intervention by the Bank of England and, within weeks, the reversal of most of the measures and the resignation of Truss.

Measures

The statement proposed cutting the basic rate of income tax from 20p to 19p a year early, abolishing the 45p additional rate of income tax in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, reversing the recent increase in National Insurance contributions, cancelling a planned rise in corporation tax, cutting stamp duty, and removing the cap on bankers' bonuses. It also set out an energy price guarantee to shield households from soaring gas and electricity prices. The total package was estimated to cost around £45 billion in tax cuts by 2026–27, and unusually, the government declined to publish accompanying forecasts from the Office for Budget Responsibility.

Market reaction

Financial markets reacted negatively: sterling fell to a record low against the US dollar, approaching $1.03, while yields on UK government bonds (gilts) rose dramatically. The spike in long-dated gilt yields threatened pension funds using liability-driven investment strategies, prompting the Bank of England on 28 September to announce emergency purchases of up to £65 billion of long-dated gilts to restore market functioning. The International Monetary Fund publicly urged the UK to reconsider the measures.

Reversal and aftermath

On 3 October the government abandoned the plan to abolish the 45p rate, and on 14 October Kwarteng was dismissed and replaced by Jeremy Hunt, who on 17 October reversed almost all of the remaining tax measures. Truss resigned on 20 October, after 45 days in office — the shortest premiership in British history — and was succeeded by Rishi Sunak.
