Fishbourne Roman Palace

LocationFishbourne, West Sussex, England
Builtc. AD 75
OccupiedUntil fire, c. AD 270
Discovered1960; excavated by Barry Cunliffe

Fishbourne Roman Palace is a Roman residence near Chichester in West Sussex, England, and the largest Roman domestic building yet discovered in Britain. It dates to around AD 75, within a generation of the Roman conquest.

History

The site was first occupied by a Roman military supply base established shortly after the invasion of AD 43. The palatial residence that replaced it comprised four wings around a formal courtyard garden, with some 100 rooms, many decorated with mosaics, painted plaster, and imported marble. It is often associated with Cogidubnus (Tiberius Claudius Togidubnus), the pro-Roman king of the local Regni tribe, though this attribution is debated. The palace was destroyed by fire around AD 270 and never rebuilt.

Excavation and museum

The remains were discovered in 1960 during the digging of a water main and excavated through the 1960s under Barry Cunliffe. Part of the north wing is preserved under a modern museum building, where visitors can see celebrated mosaics including the Cupid on a dolphin pavement.
