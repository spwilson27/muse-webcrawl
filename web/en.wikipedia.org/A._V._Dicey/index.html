Albert Venn Dicey (4 February 1835 – 7 April 1922) was a British Whig jurist and constitutional law theorist. He is best known as the author of Introduction to the Study of the Law of the Constitution (1885), the foundational popular exposition of the unwritten British constitution, in which he articulated the doctrines of parliamentary sovereignty and the rule of law.

Career

Educated at Balliol College, Oxford, Dicey was called to the bar in 1863, became a fellow of All Souls, and in 1882 succeeded as Vinerian Professor of English Law at Oxford, a chair he held until 1909. He was an ardent Liberal Unionist and a leading intellectual opponent of Irish Home Rule.

Doctrine

Dicey's classic formulation of the rule of law comprised three principles: the absolute supremacy of regular law over arbitrary power, equality before the law of all classes, and the idea that constitutional principles in Britain are the result of the ordinary law developed by the courts. His account of unlimited parliamentary sovereignty remains influential in Commonwealth constitutional law, though later scholars have criticized elements of both doctrines.
