ASML Holding N.V. (commonly shortened to ASML) is a Dutch multinational corporation founded in 1984 that develops and manufactures photolithography machines used to produce computer chips. Headquartered in Veldhoven, Netherlands, ASML is the world's largest supplier of lithography systems and the sole supplier of extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography machines, which are essential for manufacturing the most advanced semiconductor nodes. It has at times been Europe's most valuable technology company.

History

ASML began as a joint venture between Philips and ASMI, working from a shed beside a Philips building in Eindhoven. Its PAS 2000 stepper appeared in 1984, and after years of losses the company broke through in the 1990s with the PAS 5500 platform. ASML became fully independent and went public in 1995. The TWINSCAN dual-stage scanner (2001) and immersion lithography (mid-2000s) consolidated its lead over Nikon and Canon; the acquisition of Cymer (2013) secured EUV light-source technology, and a stake in Carl Zeiss SMT underwrote the precision optics EUV requires. After more than a decade of development, EUV systems entered high-volume manufacturing around 2018–2019 at customers including TSMC, Samsung, and Intel.

Products

ASML sells deep-ultraviolet (DUV) immersion and dry scanners under the TWINSCAN name, and EUV systems (the NXE series, and the high-NA EXE series) that use 13.5 nm light generated from a tin plasma and cost from roughly €150 million to over €350 million each. It also provides computational lithography software (through Brion), e-beam and optical metrology (including the acquired HMI), and refurbished older systems.

Geopolitics and export controls

Because of its chokepoint position in the chip supply chain, ASML has been central to technology competition between the United States and China. Dutch export licenses for EUV shipments to China were never granted, and from 2023 the Netherlands and the United States progressively extended controls to advanced immersion DUV systems, making China policy, service restrictions, and regional capacity expansion recurring features of the company's reporting.
