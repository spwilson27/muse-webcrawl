Old Master

In art history, an Old Master is a painter of recognised skill who worked in Europe before about 1800, or a work by such an artist. The term is also applied to the drawings and prints of the period ("Old Master prints" and "Old Master drawings"). It has no precise boundary, but generally covers art from the early Renaissance (c. 1300) through the 18th century.

Usage

The term arose from the guild system, in which a "master" was a fully qualified craftsman, and gained its present art-historical sense in the 18th and 19th centuries as collecting and connoisseurship developed. Auction houses such as Christie's and Sotheby's maintain dedicated Old Master departments. Works by the most famous Old Masters command the highest prices in the art market; Leonardo da Vinci's Salvator Mundi sold for $450.3 million in 2017, the highest price ever paid for a painting.

Attribution

Because workshop production was common, attributions are graded carefully: "by", "attributed to", "workshop of", "circle of", and "follower of" carry distinct meanings in catalogues and sales. Scientific methods — X-radiography, infrared reflectography, dendrochronology, and pigment analysis — assist connoisseurs in authentication.

Notable Old Masters

Representative figures include Giotto, Jan van Eyck, Leonardo da Vinci, Michelangelo, Raphael, Titian, Albrecht Dürer, Pieter Bruegel the Elder, Caravaggio, Peter Paul Rubens, Rembrandt van Rijn, Johannes Vermeer, Nicolas Poussin, Diego Velázquez, and Francisco Goya.
