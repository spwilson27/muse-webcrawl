Petén Basin

LocationPetén Department, northern Guatemala
RegionMaya Lowlands, Mesoamerica
Area~35,854 km² (the Petén Department)
Major sitesTikal, Uaxactún, El Mirador, Calakmul (nearby, Mexico)
PeriodMiddle Preclassic to Postclassic Maya

The Petén Basin is a geographical subregion of Mesoamerica, primarily in the present-day department of Petén in northern Guatemala, extending into adjacent parts of Mexico and Belize. Covered by dense tropical rainforest, it formed the heartland of the ancient Maya civilization, where the Classic-period Maya city-states reached their greatest power.

Geography

The basin is a low-lying karst limestone plain, mostly between 100 and 300 metres above sea level, drained by rivers such as the San Pedro Mártir and dotted with lakes, of which Lake Petén Itzá is the largest. Seasonal swamps called bajos cover large areas. The region's rainforest is one of the largest remaining tracts in Central America and is protected in part by the Maya Biosphere Reserve.

History

The Petén was settled by Maya peoples by the Middle Preclassic period (c. 1000–600 BC). In the Late Preclassic, the enormous city of El Mirador — with some of the largest pyramids in the Maya world — dominated the Mirador Basin in the north. During the Classic period (c. AD 250–900), Tikal became one of the most powerful Maya capitals, locked in a long rivalry with Calakmul to the north. Other major cities included Uaxactún, Yaxhá, Dos Pilas, and Piedras Negras.

The Classic Maya collapse of the 9th century emptied most of the basin's cities, but the Itza Maya kingdom survived around Lake Petén Itzá with its capital at Nojpetén (modern Flores). It remained independent until 1697, when it became the last Maya kingdom conquered by the Spanish.

Archaeology

The Petén Basin is one of the most intensively studied archaeological regions in the Americas. In 2018, lidar surveys revealed more than 60,000 previously undocumented structures beneath the forest canopy, transforming understanding of the scale of Maya settlement, agriculture, and warfare in the region.
