Maison Carrée

LocationNîmes, France
Builtc. 19–16 BCE (dedicated 2–5 CE)
TypeRoman temple
UNESCOWorld Heritage Site (2023)

The Maison Carrée (French for "square house") is an ancient Roman temple in Nîmes, southern France. It is one of the best-preserved Roman temple façades in the world, raised on a high podium in the Corinthian order and originally dedicated to Gaius and Lucius Caesar, the grandsons and adopted heirs of Augustus.

History

Built in the late 1st century BCE, the temple survived because it was continuously in use — as a church, a town hall, a stable during the Revolution, and eventually a museum. Its form strongly influenced neoclassical architecture, including Thomas Jefferson's design for the Virginia State Capitol. It was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2023.

Architecture

The temple is a hexastyle pseudoperipteral design, about 26 metres long and 15 wide, with engaged columns along the cella walls and a deep porch. A modern Norman Foster-designed art museum, the Carré d'Art, faces it across the square.
