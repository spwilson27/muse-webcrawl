Ham (chimpanzee)

Ham (July 1957 – January 19, 1983), also known as Ham the Chimp and Ham the Astrochimp, was the first great ape launched into space. On 31 January 1961, Ham flew a suborbital mission aboard the Mercury-Redstone 2 capsule, becoming the first hominid in space and demonstrating that tasks could be performed there, three months before Alan Shepard became the first American in space.

Early life and training

Ham was born in French Cameroon and brought to the United States in 1959, where he was trained at Holloman Air Force Base in New Mexico — his name is commonly said to be an acronym for the Holloman Aerospace Medical Center. He was one of a group of chimpanzees taught to perform simple tasks in response to lights and sounds, receiving rewards (banana pellets) and mild electric shocks for errors. For the flight he was known simply as "No. 65", so that a fatal outcome would not be publicly attached to a named animal; he was given the name Ham only after the successful mission.

The flight

Mercury-Redstone 2 launched from Cape Canaveral, Florida, and flew a 16-minute suborbital trajectory, reaching an altitude of about 253 km (157 miles) and splashing down in the Atlantic Ocean. Ham performed his lever-pulling tasks in weightlessness only slightly slower than on Earth, proving that a crew member could work during spaceflight. The capsule lost pressure and Ham's spacesuit protected him; he was recovered alive, suffering only a bruised nose.

Later life

After the flight Ham lived at the National Zoo in Washington, D.C., until 1981, when he was moved to the North Carolina Zoo. He died in 1983, aged 25. His remains were buried at the International Space Hall of Fame in Alamogordo, New Mexico.
