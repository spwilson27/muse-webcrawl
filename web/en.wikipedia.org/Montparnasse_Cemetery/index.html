Montparnasse Cemetery (French: Cimetière du Montparnasse) is a cemetery in the Montparnasse quarter of Paris, in the city's 14th arrondissement. Created in 1824 from farmland on the southern outskirts of Paris, it is the second largest cemetery in the city after Père Lachaise and is famous as the burial place of many of France's intellectual and artistic elite, as well as publishers and politicians.

History

The cemetery was created at the beginning of the 19th century along with several others outside the old city, after churchyard burials within Paris were banned for health reasons. The site had been farmland attached to a charity hospital, and three windmills once stood on the property; one, the Moulin de la Charité, still survives. The cemetery was originally known as Le Cimetière du Sud (Southern Cemetery). It is divided into sections by avenues, and the construction of boulevard Raspail split it into the large and small cemeteries (grand and petit cimetière).

Notable burials

Among those buried at Montparnasse are:

  • Simone de Beauvoir and Jean-Paul Sartre, who share a grave
  • Charles Baudelaire, poet
  • Samuel Beckett, writer
  • Serge Gainsbourg, singer-songwriter
  • Julio Cortázar, writer
  • Marguerite Duras, writer
  • Émile Durkheim, sociologist
  • Man Ray, artist
  • Guy de Maupassant, writer
  • Constantin Brâncuși, sculptor
  • Susan Sontag, writer

The site today

The cemetery is a quiet, tree-lined green space popular with walkers; its 19 hectares contain around 1,200 trees. From the cemetery, the Tour Montparnasse rises nearby, one of Paris's rare skyscrapers. The cemetery remains in use for burials.
