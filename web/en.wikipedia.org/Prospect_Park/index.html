Prospect Park is a 526-acre (213 ha) public park in the New York City borough of Brooklyn. Designed by Frederick Law Olmsted and Calvert Vaux, who considered it their masterpiece, it opened in 1867, shortly after their work on Central Park in Manhattan. The park is operated by the Prospect Park Alliance in partnership with the New York City Parks Department.

Design and features

The park is organised as a sequence of landscapes: the 90-acre Long Meadow, one of the longest unbroken meadows in any US urban park; the wooded Ravine, Brooklyn's only forest, through which a restored watercourse runs; and a 60-acre lake. Landmarks include the neoclassical Boathouse (1905), the Bandshell, the LeFrak Center at Lakeside with roller and ice skating, and the Prospect Park Zoo (opened 1935). Grand Army Plaza, with its Soldiers' and Sailors' Arch (1892), forms the park's northern entrance; the park is flanked by the Brooklyn Botanic Garden and the Brooklyn Museum to the east.

The land's history includes a pass used during the Battle of Long Island (1776) in the American Revolutionary War, commemorated at Battle Pass. After decades of underfunding and decline in the mid-20th century, the Prospect Park Alliance (founded 1987) led a long restoration of the woodlands, watercourse and historic structures, and the park now receives around ten million visitors a year for concerts, sports, farmers' markets and recreation.
