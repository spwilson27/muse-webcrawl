Battle of Strasbourg

Date357 CE
LocationNear Argentorate (Strasbourg), Gaul
ResultRoman victory
BelligerentsRoman Empire vs. Alamanni
CommandersJulian (the Apostate) vs. Chnodomarius

The Battle of Strasbourg, also known as the Battle of Argentorate, was fought in 357 CE between the Roman army under the Caesar Julian and the Alamannic confederation led by King Chnodomarius. Though outnumbered — Julian fielded perhaps 13,000 men against a much larger Alamannic host — the Romans won a decisive victory that restored Roman control of the Rhine frontier in Gaul. The battle is described in detail by the historian Ammianus Marcellinus, who served in the campaign.

Julian's infantry broke the Alamannic centre after hard fighting.

Background

During the 350s, Alamannic warbands had overrun much of Roman Gaul east of the Rhine and raided widely. Julian, appointed Caesar in 355, spent two campaigning seasons rebuilding Roman authority. In 357 the Alamanni under Chnodomarius, who had earlier defeated a Roman force under the magister peditum Barbatio, concentrated near Strasbourg to confront Julian.

The battle

Julian deployed his infantry in a dense formation with cavalry on the wings. The Roman right-wing cavalry was initially driven back by the Alamannic horse, but the infantry line held against repeated Alamannic assaults. Chnodomarius and his nobles dismounted to fight in the front rank. After prolonged close combat, the Alamannic centre broke; the fugitives were driven into the Rhine, where many drowned. Chnodomarius was captured and sent to Rome. Ammianus reports Roman losses of about 243 soldiers and four tribunes, against some 6,000 Alamanni dead on the field.

Aftermath

The victory allowed Julian to campaign across the Rhine and to rebuild frontier forts, stabilising Gaul. It greatly enhanced his reputation with the army, which in 360 acclaimed him Augustus in Paris, setting him on the path to sole rule of the empire.
