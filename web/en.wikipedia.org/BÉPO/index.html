BÉPO

BÉPO is an ergonomic keyboard layout optimized for typing French, inspired by the Dvorak layout for English. Its name comes from the first letters of the top-left row of keys, analogous to QWERTY and AZERTY. The layout places the most frequent letters of French on the home row and balances the load between the hands.

Design

BÉPO was developed by a community under the French Dvorak association, with an initial release in 2008. The design targets an average finger travel far lower than AZERTY, direct access to all French diacritics (é, è, ê, à, ù, ç), typographic characters such as the non-breaking space, guillemets, and typographic apostrophe, and support for characters of many other European languages and for programming symbols.

Standardization

In 2019, the French national standardization body AFNOR published standard NF Z71-300, which established two layouts: an improved AZERTY and BÉPO, making BÉPO the first alternative French layout to receive an official standard. The layout is supported natively in major operating systems including Linux distributions and, more recently, Windows and macOS.
