MKUltra

Project MKUltra was a human experimentation programme conducted by the United States Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) from 1953 to 1973, designed to develop procedures and identify drugs that could be used during interrogations to weaken individuals and force confessions through brainwashing and psychological torture.

Scope

Authorised by CIA Director Allen Dulles in April 1953 and run by Sidney Gottlieb of the agency's Technical Services Staff, MKUltra encompassed well over a hundred subprojects at universities, hospitals, prisons, and pharmaceutical companies. Experiments involved the administration of psychoactive substances — especially LSD — often to unwitting subjects, as well as hypnosis, sensory deprivation, electroshock, and other methods. One notorious subproject, Operation Midnight Climax, dosed unsuspecting men in CIA safehouses in San Francisco and New York.

Exposure

Most MKUltra records were ordered destroyed in 1973 by Director Richard Helms, but a cache of some 20,000 financial documents survived and was released under the Freedom of Information Act in 1977. Congressional investigations — the Church Committee and a joint Senate hearing in 1977 — brought the programme to public attention. The death of Army scientist Frank Olson in 1953, after he was covertly dosed with LSD, became its most famous case; his family received an apology and settlement from President Gerald Ford's administration.

Legacy

MKUltra became a touchstone in debates over government secrecy, research ethics, and informed consent, and it has had an outsized presence in popular culture and conspiracy literature far beyond the documented record.
