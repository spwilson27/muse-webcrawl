Hilma af Klint

Born26 October 1862, Solna, Sweden
Died21 October 1944 (aged 81), Danderyd, Sweden
NationalitySwedish
Known forPainting; abstract art, symbolism

Hilma af Klint (26 October 1862 – 21 October 1944) was a Swedish artist and mystic whose paintings are now recognized as among the first abstract works in Western art history. She belonged to the group "The Five" (De Fem), women artists influenced by Theosophy who conducted séances and believed their paintings were guided by spiritual forces.

Life and work

Af Klint studied at the Royal Swedish Academy of Fine Arts in Stockholm, graduating with honours in 1887, and supported herself with naturalistic portraits and landscapes. In 1906, following years of spiritual practice, she began the monumental cycle The Paintings for the Temple (1906–1915), comprising 193 works including the enormous series The Ten Largest (1907), which depict the stages of life in swirling, symbolic abstractions. Her abstract paintings predate the first purely abstract compositions by Wassily Kandinsky by several years.

Recognition

Convinced the world was not ready for her work, af Klint stipulated that her abstract paintings not be shown until 20 years after her death. They were first exhibited in 1986 in Los Angeles, and major retrospectives followed, notably at the Moderna Museet (2013) and the Solomon R. Guggenheim Museum in New York (2018–2019), the latter becoming the museum's most-visited exhibition ever. Her work has forced a revision of the standard history of abstract art.
