Camille Jordan


Placeholder image: Portrait of Camille Jordan

Marie Ennemond Camille Jordan (French: [ʒɔʁdɑ̃]; 5 January 1838 – 22 January 1922) was a French mathematician, known both for his foundational work in group theory and for his influential Cours d'analyse.

Work

Jordan was a master of the theory of permutation groups and finite groups; his book Traité des substitutions et des équations algébriques (1870) gave a comprehensive account of Galois theory and introduced the concept of composition series, with the Jordan–Hölder theorem stating their essential uniqueness. The Jordan curve theorem asserts that a simple closed curve divides the plane into an interior and an exterior region.

In analysis, his name attaches to Jordan measure (content), the Jordan normal form (canonical form) of a matrix, Jordan's lemma in complex analysis, and the Jordan decomposition of measures and of bounded-variation functions. He studied at the École Polytechnique, worked as an engineer, and taught at the École Polytechnique and the Collège de France.
