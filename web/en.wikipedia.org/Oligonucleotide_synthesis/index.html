Oligonucleotide synthesis

Oligonucleotide synthesis is the chemical synthesis of relatively short fragments of nucleic acids with defined chemical structure (sequence). The technique is extremely useful in modern laboratory practice because it provides rapid, inexpensive access to custom-made oligonucleotides of the desired sequence. Most synthesis is performed by solid-phase methods using phosphoramidite chemistry, automated on instruments that can produce many sequences in parallel.

Phosphoramidite method

The chain is assembled on a solid support (controlled pore glass or polystyrene), starting from the 3′ end. Each cycle adds one nucleotide through four steps:

  1. Detritylation — removal of the 5′-dimethoxytrityl (DMT) protecting group with acid.
  2. Coupling — the next phosphoramidite monomer reacts with the free 5′-hydroxyl, activated by a weak acid such as tetrazole.
  3. Capping — unreacted chains are acetylated to prevent them from participating further.
  4. Oxidation — the phosphite triester linkage is oxidised (typically with iodine) to a stable phosphate triester.

After the final cycle, the oligonucleotide is cleaved from the support and deprotected, usually with concentrated ammonium hydroxide. Coupling efficiencies above 99% allow routine synthesis of oligonucleotides up to roughly 200 bases, though purity decreases with length; products are often purified by HPLC or gel electrophoresis.

Applications

Synthetic oligonucleotides are used as primers for PCR and DNA sequencing, as probes for detection and imaging, in gene synthesis and site-directed mutagenesis, and as therapeutic agents, including antisense oligonucleotides and aptamers. Modified backbones (such as phosphorothioates) and bases can be introduced to improve stability or function.

History

Early solution-phase syntheses in the 1950s–60s, including the work of H. Gobind Khorana's group on the phosphodiester approach, were laborious. The phosphoramidite method developed by Marvin Caruthers and colleagues in the early 1980s, combined with automation, transformed the field and remains the standard.
