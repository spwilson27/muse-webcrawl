Stationarity (statistics)

In the mathematical theory of probability and statistics, stationarity is a property of a stochastic process stating that its statistical properties do not change over time. Formally, a process is strictly stationary if the joint distribution of any collection of its values is the same regardless of when they are observed; weak (wide-sense) stationarity requires only that the mean and the autocovariance function are time-invariant.

Importance in time series analysis

Most statistical forecasting methods assume that time series can be rendered approximately stationary through mathematical transformations such as differencing (taking differences between consecutive observations) or detrending. A non-stationary series has a mean, variance, or autocorrelation structure that varies with time, which can produce misleading regression results (spurious regression) if not handled.

Tests

Common tests for stationarity include the augmented Dickey–Fuller test and the Phillips–Perron test (whose null hypothesis is the presence of a unit root), and the KPSS test, whose null hypothesis is stationarity. Processes with a unit root, such as a random walk, are classic examples of non-stationarity; cointegration techniques are used to analyze relationships between them.
