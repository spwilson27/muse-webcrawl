Plant hormone

Plant hormones, or phytohormones, are signal molecules produced within plants that regulate growth, development and responses to the environment, often acting at very low concentrations and at sites distant from where they are made.

Classes

The major classes are auxins, which promote cell elongation and root initiation; gibberellins, which stimulate stem growth and germination; cytokinins, which promote cell division; abscisic acid, which mediates stress responses and dormancy; and ethylene, a gaseous hormone involved in fruit ripening and senescence. Brassinosteroids, jasmonates, salicylic acid and strigolactones are also recognised.

Uses

Synthetic plant hormones and growth regulators are widely used in agriculture and horticulture for rooting cuttings, fruit thinning and setting, weed control and delaying or accelerating ripening.
