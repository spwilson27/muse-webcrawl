Plant hunting

Plant hunting, also called plant collecting, is the practice of travelling — often to remote regions — to find, collect and bring back new plants for gardens, herbaria, nurseries and scientific study. Plant hunters transformed horticulture in the 18th and 19th centuries by introducing thousands of ornamental and economic species to Europe and North America.

Notable plant hunters

Famous collectors include Joseph Banks, who accompanied Captain Cook; David Douglas, after whom the Douglas fir is named; and Ernest Henry Wilson and George Forrest, who collected extensively in western China. The invention of the Wardian case in the 19th century greatly improved the survival of plants on long sea voyages.

Modern collecting

Today collecting is regulated by the Convention on Biological Diversity and the Nagoya Protocol, which require permits and benefit-sharing with source countries.
