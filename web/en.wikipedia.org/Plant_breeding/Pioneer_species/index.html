Pioneer species

A pioneer species is a hardy species that is among the first to colonise barren or disturbed environments in ecological succession. Pioneer species typically tolerate harsh conditions, grow quickly, and produce many small, easily dispersed seeds or spores.

Characteristics

Examples include lichens and mosses on bare rock, fireweed and grasses after fire, and alder and lupins on volcanic deposits, which enrich the soil with nitrogen. By stabilising soil, adding organic matter and moderating the microclimate, pioneers make the site suitable for later-successional species that eventually replace them.

In succession

In primary succession pioneers colonise surfaces where no soil exists; in secondary succession they are the first to recolonise after disturbance. Their life histories favour dispersal and rapid reproduction over competitive ability.
