Gödel's incompleteness theorems

Gödel's incompleteness theorems are two theorems of mathematical logic, published by Kurt Gödel in 1931, which show inherent limitations of formal axiomatic systems capable of modelling basic arithmetic.

First theorem

Any consistent formal system within which a certain amount of elementary arithmetic can be carried out is incomplete: there are true statements about the natural numbers that can neither be proved nor disproved within the system. Gödel's proof constructs a self-referential statement amounting to "this statement is not provable".

Second theorem

The second theorem states that such a system cannot prove its own consistency, assuming it is consistent. This undermined Hilbert's programme of giving finitary consistency proofs for all of mathematics.

Significance

The theorems are widely regarded as among the most important results in twentieth-century logic, with implications discussed in mathematics, computer science and philosophy of mind.
