New York University (NYU) is a private research university in New York City, United States. Chartered in 1831 by the New York State Legislature, NYU was founded by a group of New Yorkers led by Albert Gallatin, a former U.S. Secretary of the Treasury. It has become one of the largest private universities in the United States by enrollment, with a total student body of more than 50,000.

NYU's main campus is centered around Washington Square Park in Greenwich Village, Manhattan, with additional facilities throughout New York City. The university comprises numerous schools and colleges, including the College of Arts & Science, the Stern School of Business, the Tisch School of the Arts, the School of Law, the Grossman School of Medicine, the Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences, and the Steinhardt School of Culture, Education, and Human Development.

Global network

NYU operates degree-granting portal campuses in Abu Dhabi and Shanghai and study-away sites in cities around the world, describing itself as a "global network university." Its admissions are highly selective, and it is classified among the top research universities in the United States.

People and achievements

NYU alumni and faculty include numerous Nobel laureates, Academy Award and Tony Award winners, heads of state, and prominent figures in business, law, medicine, the arts, and the sciences. Its film and drama programs at Tisch are among the most acclaimed in the world, and the university's economics, mathematics, philosophy, and law faculties are consistently ranked among the best in their fields. NYU's athletic teams, the Violets, compete in NCAA Division III.
