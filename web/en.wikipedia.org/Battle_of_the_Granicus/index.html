Battle of the Granicus

DateMay 334 BCE
LocationGranicus River (modern Biga Çayı), northwestern Anatolia
ResultMacedonian victory
BelligerentsMacedon and Greek allies vs. Achaemenid Empire (Persia)
CommandersAlexander the Great; Parmenion vs. local satraps; Memnon of Rhodes

The Battle of the Granicus, fought in May 334 BCE, was the first of three major battles between Alexander the Great and the Persian Empire. Having crossed the Hellespont into Asia Minor, Alexander attacked a Persian army of satraps drawn up on the steep far bank of the Granicus River and defeated it, opening western Asia Minor to his conquest.

Alexander's cavalry forcing the crossing of the Granicus.

Background

Alexander invaded Persia in the spring of 334 BCE with some 40,000 infantry and 5,000 cavalry. The Persian satraps of the region assembled their forces, including a large body of cavalry and Greek mercenary infantry, on the east bank of the Granicus. Memnon of Rhodes, the Greek commander in Persian service, had advised a scorched-earth retreat, but the satraps — distrusting him and unwilling to abandon their lands — chose battle.

The battle

Arriving late in the day, Alexander rejected Parmenion's counsel to wait until morning and attacked at once uphill across the river. A feint drew Persian cavalry toward the Macedonian left while Alexander, conspicuous in shining armour, led the Companion cavalry across on the right. In the savage cavalry melee Alexander was nearly killed — an axe blow split his helmet — and was saved by Cleitus the Black, who cut down the Persian noble Spithridates. The Macedonian phalanx then crossed and engaged the Persian centre, while the Greek mercenaries, left unsupported, were surrounded and largely slaughtered; some 2,000 were taken prisoner and sent in chains to Macedonia.

Aftermath

The victory left the Persian position in western Anatolia shattered. Alexander advanced down the coast, receiving the surrender of Sardis and the Greek cities of Ionia, and laid the groundwork for the decisive confrontation with Darius III at Issus the following year.
