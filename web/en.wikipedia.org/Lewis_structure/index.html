Lewis structure

Lewis structures, also known as Lewis dot formulae or electron dot structures, are diagrams that show the bonding between atoms of a molecule and any lone pairs of electrons that may exist. They are named after Gilbert N. Lewis, who introduced them in his 1916 article "The Atom and the Molecule".

Construction

In a Lewis structure, valence electrons are represented as dots (or crosses) around atomic symbols; a pair of dots between atoms represents a covalent bond, and double or triple bonds are drawn as two or three pairs. Structures are usually drawn to satisfy the octet rule, with formal charges minimised and placed on the most electronegative atoms where possible.

Resonance and limitations

Some molecules, such as benzene and the carbonate ion, cannot be represented by a single Lewis structure and are described by resonance hybrids of several contributing forms. Lewis structures do not directly show three-dimensional geometry, which is addressed by VSEPR theory, and they are qualitative models superseded for quantitative work by molecular orbital theory.
