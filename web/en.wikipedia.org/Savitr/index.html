Savitr

Savitr (Sanskrit: सवितृ, Savitṛ) is a solar deity in the Rigveda, one of the Adityas, hymned as the divine impeller and vivifier who sets all beings in motion and presides over the sun's rising, course and setting. His name derives from the root sū, "to impel, stimulate, bring to life". About eleven Rigvedic hymns are addressed to Savitr alone, and he is invoked in many more.

Character

Savitr is described as golden — golden-handed, golden-eyed, golden-tongued — riding a shining chariot drawn by two radiant horses. He raises his arms to bless and impel all creatures, establishes beings in their places, wakes the world at dawn and lays it to rest at evening, subjecting everything to his ordinances. Because his essential function is stimulation and impulsion, later interpretation distinguished Savitr, the sun as divine impeller (especially before sunrise, rousing life to activity), from Surya, the visible sun god himself.

The Gayatri mantra

The most famous verse addressed to Savitr is Rigveda 3.62.10, the Gayatri Mantra, composed in the gayatri metre:

tat savitur vareṇyaṃ bhargo devasya dhīmahi, dhiyo yo naḥ pracodayāt — "May we attain that excellent glory of Savitr the god; so may he stimulate our prayers (or: our thoughts)." Recited daily in the sandhya prayers of orthodox Hindus and whispered at the upanayana initiation, it became the most revered mantra in Hinduism, and the goddess Gayatri emerged as its personification.
