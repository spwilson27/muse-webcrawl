Permanent supportive housing

Permanent supportive housing (PSH) is an intervention that combines affordable housing assistance with voluntary support services to help people experiencing homelessness — particularly people with disabilities, serious mental illness, substance use disorders or other complex health needs — to live stably in the community. Unlike transitional housing, PSH has no time limit: tenants typically hold leases and pay a share of income (often 30%) toward rent.

Model

The approach is closely associated with the Housing First philosophy, which provides housing without preconditions such as sobriety or treatment participation, and then offers wraparound services such as case management, mental-health and addiction treatment, and help with benefits and employment. Housing may be provided in dedicated single-site buildings or as scattered-site apartments leased in the private market with rental subsidies.

Evidence

Numerous studies, including the Canadian At Home/Chez Soi randomised trial and research by organisations such as Pathways to Housing (which pioneered Housing First in New York in the 1990s), find that PSH markedly improves housing stability compared with treatment-first approaches, and partially offsets its costs by reducing use of emergency departments, hospitals, jails and shelters. In the United States, PSH expanded under the HEARTH Act (2009) and is a central element of federal policy to end chronic homelessness.
