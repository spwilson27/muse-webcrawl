Argyll and Bute is one of 32 unitary council areas in Scotland and a lieutenancy area. It covers much of the historic county of Argyll and the Isle of Bute, together with parts of the Inner Hebrides including Islay, Jura, Mull, Tiree, and Coll.

Geography

The area is deeply indented by sea lochs such as Loch Fyne and Loch Linnhe and includes the Kintyre peninsula and the town of Oban, the "Gateway to the Isles". Much of Loch Lomond and The Trossachs National Park lies within its eastern edge.

Economy and culture

Tourism, whisky distilling (notably on Islay), fishing, and aquaculture dominate the economy. The region is a heartland of Scottish Gaelic and hosted the early medieval Gaelic kingdom of Dál Riata, whose royal centre was at Dunadd.
