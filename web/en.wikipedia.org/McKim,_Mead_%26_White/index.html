McKim, Mead & White

Founded: 1879, New York City
Partners: Charles Follen McKim, William Rutherford Mead, Stanford White
Style: Beaux-Arts, Renaissance Revival

McKim, Mead & White was an American architectural firm based in New York City, formed in 1879 by Charles Follen McKim, William Rutherford Mead and Stanford White. It became the leading firm of the American Renaissance and Beaux-Arts movements.

Major works

The firm's buildings include the Boston Public Library, the original Pennsylvania Station in New York, Columbia University's Low Memorial Library, the Morgan Library, and the Rhode Island State House. It also designed the Washington Square Arch and numerous private houses and clubs.

Legacy

Stanford White was murdered in 1906 by Harry Thaw in a notorious scandal, but the firm continued under the same name for decades. Its classicising designs shaped American civic architecture in the early 20th century.
