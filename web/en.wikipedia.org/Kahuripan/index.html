Kahuripan (also spelled Kauripan) was an 11th-century Javanese Hindu-Buddhist kingdom in East Java, founded by Airlangga in 1019 and lasting until 1045, when it was divided between his two sons into the kingdoms of Janggala and Kediri (Panjalu).

Founding

Airlangga was the son of Udayana of Bali and Mahendradatta of the Mataram kingdom. In 1016 the Mataram capital was destroyed in the pralaya disaster, an attack attributed to the vassal king Wurawari of Lwaram. Airlangga, who survived, retreated to the jungle of Wonogiri and gathered support, gradually reconquering lost territory from his base at Watan Mas. By 1032 he had reunified much of East Java and taken the royal title with the capital at Kahuripan.

Reign and division

Airlangga's reign restored trade, agriculture and irrigation — he is credited with building the Waringin Sapta dam on the Brantas River — and patronised literature; the Old Javanese poem Arjunawiwaha was composed by Mpu Kanwa during his reign. In 1045, facing a succession dispute between his two sons, Airlangga divided the kingdom in two, with the hermit Mpu Bharada said to have drawn the boundary, and abdicated to live as an ascetic.
