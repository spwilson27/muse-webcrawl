Jewellery

Jewellery (or jewelry in American English) consists of decorative items worn for personal adornment, such as brooches, rings, necklaces, earrings, pendants, bracelets, and cufflinks. Jewellery may be attached to the body or clothing. It is commonly made from precious metals such as gold and silver, gemstones, or materials such as amber, coral, shell, and glass, and has been made for nearly every part of the body, from hairpins to toe rings.

History

Jewellery is among the oldest forms of human ornament: perforated shell beads from over 100,000 years ago are among the earliest evidence of symbolic behaviour. The gold work of ancient Egypt, the granulation of Etruscan goldsmiths, Roman cameos, Byzantine enamel, and the crown jewels and liturgical treasures of medieval Europe trace a continuous tradition in which jewellery signalled wealth, status, devotion, and identity. In South Asia, jewellery—especially gold—has played a central role in weddings and as a store of family wealth, while traditions in Africa, Oceania, and the pre-Columbian Americas developed distinctive forms in beads, feathers, and metals.

Functions and materials

Beyond decoration, jewellery serves as currency and investment, religious or magical amulet, emblem of rank or membership, and expression of personal or cultural identity. "Fine jewellery" uses precious metals and gems; "costume jewellery" uses base metals and imitation stones. In the 20th and 21st centuries, jewellers from Fabergé and Cartier to contemporary studio artists have blurred the line between craft, fashion, and art.
