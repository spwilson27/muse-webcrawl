The 1968 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XIX Olympiad, were held from 12 to 27 October 1968 in Mexico City, Mexico. They were the first Olympic Games hosted by a Latin American and Spanish-speaking country, and the first held at high altitude — Mexico City sits at about 2,240 metres (7,350 ft) above sea level, a factor that affected endurance events but aided explosive ones.

Highlights

American long jumper Bob Beamon set a world record of 8.90 metres, improving the previous mark by 55 cm — a record that stood for almost 23 years. Dick Fosbury won the high jump with his revolutionary "Fosbury flop". Sprinter Jim Hines was the first man to run the 100 metres in under 10 seconds electronically (9.95). Kenyan runners, led by Kipchoge Keino, began their nation's distance-running prominence, assisted by training at altitude.

The Games are also remembered for the protest of American sprinters Tommie Smith and John Carlos, who raised black-gloved fists on the 200 metres medal podium in a salute associated with the Black Power movement and human rights; they were expelled from the Games. Czechoslovak gymnast Věra Čáslavská won four golds and subtly protested the Soviet-led invasion of her country during medal ceremonies. Ten days before the opening ceremony, government forces killed dozens (estimates range up to several hundred) of student protesters in the Tlatelolco massacre, casting a shadow over the event.

The 1968 Games were the first at which East and West Germany competed as separate teams, and the first to feature formal drug testing and female gender verification testing. Norma Enriqueta Basilio became the first woman to light the Olympic cauldron.
