Liquid hydrogen

Boiling point: 20.27 K (−252.88 °C)
Density: 70.8 kg/m³
Chemical formula: H₂

Liquid hydrogen (H2(l)) is the liquid state of the element hydrogen. Hydrogen is found naturally in molecular H2 form; to exist as a liquid it must be cooled below its critical point of 33 K. At atmospheric pressure it boils at 20.27 K.

History

Hydrogen was liquefied by James Dewar in 1898, using regenerative cooling and his invention, the vacuum flask.

Uses

Liquid hydrogen is a common liquid rocket fuel for rocketry, used with liquid oxygen in engines such as the RS-25 of the Space Shuttle and the Vulcain of Ariane. It is also studied as an aviation and automotive fuel and is used in large volumes by industry.

Handling

Because of its very low boiling point, liquid hydrogen requires heavily insulated cryogenic storage; boil-off losses are a persistent engineering challenge. It occupies about 1/700 the volume of the gas at standard conditions.
