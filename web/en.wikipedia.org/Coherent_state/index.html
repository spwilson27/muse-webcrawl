Coherent state

In physics, a coherent state is a specific quantum state of the harmonic oscillator that most closely resembles the behaviour of a classical oscillator. Introduced by Roy J. Glauber in 1963 in his quantum theory of optical coherence, coherent states are eigenstates of the annihilation operator and describe the light produced by an ideal laser.

FieldQuantum mechanics, quantum optics
Introduced byRoy J. Glauber (1963)
Defining propertyEigenstate of annihilation operator

Definition

A coherent state |α⟩ satisfies â|α⟩ = α|α⟩, where â is the lowering (annihilation) operator and α is a complex number. Expanding in number states gives a Poisson distribution of quanta, so the photon-number uncertainty is the square root of the mean — the minimum consistent with quantum mechanics.

Properties

Coherent states are minimum-uncertainty states, with equal uncertainty in both quadratures, and they remain coherent under time evolution, following the classical trajectory of an oscillator. They form an overcomplete basis and are central to the Glauber–Sudarshan P representation of quantum optics.

Applications

Laser light, Bose–Einstein condensates, and superfluids are described using coherent states. They also appear in quantum information science, including continuous-variable quantum computing and quantum communication protocols.
