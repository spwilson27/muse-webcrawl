Valdemar I (1131 – 1182), known as Valdemar the Great, was King of Denmark from 1154 until his death. His reign, shared in its achievements with Bishop Absalon, marked a revival of Danish power after a period of civil war.

Early life and civil war

Valdemar was the son of Canute Lavard, whose murder in 1131 set off decades of conflict. After the Battle of Grathe Heath in 1157, where his rival Sweyn III was killed, Valdemar became sole king.

Reign

With Absalon, Valdemar campaigned against the pagan Wends of the southern Baltic coast, culminating in the capture of the fortress of Arkona on Rügen in 1168. He strengthened royal administration, built fortifications including the early defences of Copenhagen, and had his father canonized. He was succeeded by his son Canute VI.
