Jiang Qing

Jiang Qing (19 March 1914 – 14 May 1991), also known as Madame Mao, was a Chinese communist revolutionary, actress and political figure. She was the fourth wife of Mao Zedong, leader of the Chinese Communist Party, and became one of the most powerful figures in China during the Cultural Revolution (1966–1976) as the leading member of the so-called "Gang of Four".


Jiang Qing

Early life and career

Born Li Shumeng in Zhucheng, Shandong, she worked as a film actress in Shanghai under the stage name Lan Ping in the 1930s. She travelled to Yan'an in 1937, joined the Communist Party, and married Mao Zedong in 1938.

Cultural Revolution

During the Cultural Revolution, Jiang Qing dominated Chinese cultural life. She promoted eight "model works" (yangbanxi) — revolutionary operas and ballets that were virtually the only stage works permitted — and directed purges of artists and intellectuals. As deputy director of the Central Cultural Revolution Group, she wielded immense power and was notorious for settling personal scores.

Fall and death

Within a month of Mao's death in September 1976, Jiang Qing and her three associates were arrested by Hua Guofeng and Ye Jianying. At the nationally televised trial of the Gang of Four in 1980–81, she was defiant, receiving a death sentence with a two-year reprieve, later commuted to life imprisonment. Released on medical parole, she died by suicide in 1991.
