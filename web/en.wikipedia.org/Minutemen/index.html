Minutemen

Minutemen were members of the organized New England colonial militia companies trained in weaponry, tactics, and military strategies during the American Revolutionary War. They were known for being ready at a minute's notice, as their name suggests, and were the first armed militia to arrive or await a battle.

Origins

The practice of designating a portion of the militia for rapid response dated to the earliest decades of the Massachusetts Bay Colony and had been used in King Philip's War. In the autumn of 1774, as relations with Britain deteriorated after the Powder Alarm, the Massachusetts Provincial Congress recommended that militia companies elect officers and set aside a quarter of their men as minutemen, ready to march on the shortest notice.

Lexington and Concord

On the night of April 18–19, 1775, minutemen and militia assembled to intercept a British expedition sent to seize military stores at Concord. At Lexington Green, minutemen under Captain John Parker faced the British advance guard; shots were exchanged in the first fighting of the war. Later that day, militia and minutemen from dozens of towns engaged the British column at Concord's North Bridge and harried it all the way back to Boston.

Legacy

The minuteman became a central figure of American national mythology, commemorated by Daniel Chester French's statue The Minute Man (1875) at Concord and by Ralph Waldo Emerson's "Concord Hymn" celebrating "the shot heard round the world". The name was later adopted by the Minuteman intercontinental ballistic missile and by various citizen movements.
