Chaac

RoleMaya rain god
DomainRain, thunder, lightning, water
SymbolsLightning axe, long curling snout

Chaac (also spelled Chac or Chaahk) is the Maya god of rain, thunder and lightning. He was one of the most important and enduring deities of the Maya pantheon, worshipped especially in the dry northern lowlands of Yucatán, where rain depended on cenotes and seasonal storms.

Iconography

Chaac is depicted with a long, curling, trunk-like nose, reptilian features and S-shaped ears, often wielding a lightning axe with which he strikes the clouds to produce thunder and rain. Stone masks of Chaac with protruding snouts decorate the façades of Puuc-style buildings such as those at Uxmal and Kabah, including the famous Codz Poop ("Palace of the Masks").

Worship

As bringer of rain for the milpa (maize fields), Chaac received offerings at cenotes and in rain-petition ceremonies that in modified form continue in Yucatec Maya communities today. In Maya cosmology he could be a single god or four Chaacs associated with the cardinal directions and their colours.
