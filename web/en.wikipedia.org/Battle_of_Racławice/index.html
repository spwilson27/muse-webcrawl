Battle of Racławice

The Battle of Racławice was fought on 4 April 1794 near the village of Racławice in Lesser Poland between Polish forces under Tadeusz Kościuszko and a Russian detachment commanded by General Alexander Tormasov. It was the first major battle of the Kościuszko Uprising and a celebrated Polish victory, famous for the charge of peasant scythemen.

Background

Following the Second Partition of Poland, Kościuszko proclaimed an insurrection at Kraków on 24 March 1794 and marched to join other Polish forces. Russian columns moved to intercept him before he could link up and threaten Warsaw. Kościuszko's army of about 4,000–6,000 men — regulars, militia and some 2,000 peasant volunteers armed with war scythes — met Tormasov's roughly 3,000–5,000 troops on the hills near Racławice.

The battle

After an exchange of artillery fire, Kościuszko personally led a bayonet charge supported by the scythemen, who stormed and captured the Russian cannon on the hill. The peasant Wojciech Bartosz distinguished himself by extinguishing a cannon's fuse with his cap; Kościuszko promoted him and gave him the surname Głowacki. The Russians retreated with losses of around 800–1,000 men; Polish losses were a few hundred.

Significance

Though strategically modest — Kościuszko could not exploit the victory — Racławice became a national legend symbolising the participation of the peasantry in Poland's struggle for independence. The battle is commemorated by the monumental Racławice Panorama in Wrocław, a giant cycloramic painting completed for the battle's centenary in 1894.
