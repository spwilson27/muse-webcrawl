Folding@home

Folding@home
Launch: 1 October 2000
Developer: Vijay Pande / Pande Lab, Stanford University
Type: Volunteer (distributed) computing
Purpose: Simulate protein folding and dynamics for disease research

Folding@home (FAH or F@h) is a distributed computing project for scientific research, simulating protein folding, computational drug design, and other types of molecular dynamics. It uses the idle resources of volunteers' personal computers.

Background

The project was launched in 2000 by Professor Vijay Pande of Stanford University. Its simulations have produced results relevant to Alzheimer's disease, Huntington's, cancer, and infectious diseases.

Volunteer computing model

Participants install client software that downloads work units, processes them using CPU or GPU resources, and returns results. Points and teams gamify participation.

COVID-19

In early 2020 the project mobilized against COVID-19, simulating the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein; participation surged past 1.5 million devices and aggregate performance surpassed an exaFLOPS, making it briefly the world's most powerful computing system.
