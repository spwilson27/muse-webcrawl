P (complexity)

In computational complexity theory, P (also known as PTIME or DTIME(nO(1))) is the complexity class containing those decision problems that can be solved by a deterministic Turing machine in polynomial time — that is, in time O(nk) for some constant k, where n is the size of the input.

Significance

P is generally regarded as the class of problems that are "efficiently solvable" or "tractable", an idea formalised by the Cobham–Edmonds thesis. Problems in P include sorting, searching, finding greatest common divisors, primality testing, and linear programming. P is contained in NP, the class of problems whose solutions can be verified in polynomial time; whether P equals NP is one of the most famous open questions in mathematics and computer science and one of the Millennium Prize Problems.

Properties

P is closed under many operations and contains the class L (logarithmic space). Polynomial-time reductions between problems in P are used to compare their difficulty. Problems complete for P under log-space reductions, such as the circuit value problem, are believed unlikely to be efficiently parallelisable.
