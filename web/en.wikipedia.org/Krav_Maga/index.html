Krav Maga (קרב מגע, "contact combat") is a military and civilian self-defence system developed in Israel. It was created by Imi Lichtenfeld, a Hungarian-born Slovak Jewish athlete who in the 1930s taught fighting skills to Jewish communities in Bratislava defending themselves against antisemitic violence, and who after emigrating to Mandate Palestine became chief instructor of physical training for the Haganah and later the Israel Defense Forces.

Principles

Krav Maga emphasises practical, instinctive techniques and realistic training scenarios. Its principles include neutralising the threat as quickly as possible, simultaneous defence and attack, targeting vulnerable points such as the eyes, throat and groin, using nearby objects as weapons, and awareness of surroundings and escape. It draws on boxing, wrestling, judo, aikido and street-fighting experience, and includes defences against armed attacks and multiple assailants.

Development and spread

After Lichtenfeld's retirement from the IDF, he adapted the system for civilians; it spread internationally from the 1980s through organisations founded by his students. Krav Maga is taught to military and law-enforcement units in many countries and to civilians in gyms worldwide, with gradings typically from practitioner levels upward. Its aggressive, no-holds-barred approach — including techniques barred in combat sports — distinguishes it from competitive martial arts.
