Railway station

A railway station, also called a train station or railroad station, is a facility along a railway where trains stop to load or unload passengers and/or freight. Stations typically consist of at least one platform beside the tracks and a station building providing services such as ticket sales, waiting rooms and information. The earliest stations date from the 1830s; the oldest purpose-built station building still in use is often considered to be at Liverpool Road, Manchester (1830).

Facilities

Stations range from simple halts with a single platform to vast terminals. Common elements include platforms (side, island or bay), footbridges or subways between platforms, canopies, departure boards, ticket offices or machines, and interchanges with buses, trams, metro lines and taxis. Major stations may include retail areas, left-luggage facilities and lounges.

Types

A terminus is an end-of-line station where trains reverse; a through station lies on a running line. Junction stations serve intersecting lines. Some stations are divided between high-level and low-level parts or between different operators. Goods stations historically handled freight separately from passenger facilities.

Architecture

Nineteenth-century stations were often grand civic monuments with trainsheds of iron and glass — examples include London St Pancras, New York's Grand Central Terminal and Mumbai's Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus. Many historic stations are now protected heritage structures, while modern stations emphasise interchange efficiency and accessibility, with step-free access increasingly required.

Operations

Station stops are governed by timetables; dwell times allow boarding and alighting. The busiest stations in the world by passenger numbers include Shinjuku in Tokyo and major Indian and European hubs. Stations are usually named after the place they serve, though some retain names of former companies or nearby landmarks.
