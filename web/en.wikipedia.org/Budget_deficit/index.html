Budget deficit

A budget deficit occurs when an individual, business, or government spends more money than it receives in income or revenue during a particular period. The opposite of a budget deficit is a budget surplus; when revenues and spending are equal, the budget is balanced. For governments, persistent deficits increase public debt, since the shortfall must usually be financed by borrowing.

Causes

Deficits can arise from deliberate policy, such as tax cuts or increased spending intended to stimulate a weak economy, or from economic downturns that automatically reduce tax revenue and raise spending on unemployment benefits and other safety-net programs. Structural deficits persist even when the economy is at full employment, whereas cyclical deficits reflect the temporary effects of the business cycle.

Government deficits

Governments typically finance deficits by issuing bonds. Deficit spending is a central tool of Keynesian fiscal policy during recessions, but economists debate the long-run effects of large deficits and accumulated debt, including interest costs, inflation risk, and possible crowding out of private investment.

Measurement

Deficits are commonly expressed as a percentage of gross domestic product (GDP), which allows comparison across countries and over time. Primary deficits exclude interest payments on existing debt.
