Neil Halstead

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Neil Halstead (born 7 October 1970) is an English musician, singer and songwriter, best known as a founding member, lead vocalist and guitarist of the shoegaze band Slowdive and, later, of the indie folk group Mojave 3. He has also released several solo albums in a folk-influenced style.

Slowdive

Halstead formed Slowdive in Reading in 1989 with Rachel Goswell, Christian Savill, Nick Chaplin and Simon Scott. The band released three albums—Just for a Day (1991), Souvlaki (1993) and Pygmalion (1995)—before disbanding in 1995. Slowdive reformed in 2014 and released the albums Slowdive (2017) and Everything Is Alive (2023) to critical acclaim.

Mojave 3 and solo work

After Slowdive's dissolution, Halstead, Goswell and Chaplin formed Mojave 3, releasing five albums between 1995 and 2006, including Excuses for Travellers (2000). Halstead's solo albums are Sleeping on Roads (2001), Oh! Mighty Engine (2008), recorded in Cornwall, and Palindrome Hunches (2012), the latter produced with the Wallis Bird and featuring members of Seamus Fogarty's band circles; it was recorded at a primary school on the Isle of Wight. He also formed the side project Black Hearted Brother (2013).

Style

Halstead's songwriting moved from the layered, effects-heavy textures of Slowdive toward acoustic, folk- and country-tinged arrangements, influenced by artists such as Bert Jansch, Nick Drake and Leonard Cohen.
