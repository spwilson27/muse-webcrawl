Great Books

The Great Books are the books considered to constitute the essential canon of Western literature and thought — works of enduring importance in philosophy, history, science, mathematics, poetry, drama and fiction. The phrase is associated especially with the Great Books movement in 20th-century education and with the 54-volume set Great Books of the Western World, published by Encyclopædia Britannica in 1952.

The Great Books movement

Educators Robert Maynard Hutchins and Mortimer Adler promoted the idea that liberal education should be built around reading and discussing the classic works themselves, rather than textbooks. Adler and Hutchins launched a Great Books curriculum at the University of Chicago, and John Erskine had earlier pioneered a similar course at Columbia University. St. John's College adopted an all-required Great Books curriculum in 1937 and still follows it.

The Britannica set

The 1952 set, edited by Adler with Hutchins as editor-in-chief, collected works by 74 authors — from Homer, Plato and Aristotle through Aquinas, Shakespeare, Newton and Darwin to Freud and William James — along with the two-volume Syntopicon, an index of 102 "Great Ideas". A second edition in 1990 expanded the set to 60 volumes and added more 20th-century authors.

Criticism

Canons of Great Books have been criticized for over-representing European men and for freezing a particular cultural tradition in place; later lists and curricula have sought to broaden the range of authors and civilizations included.
