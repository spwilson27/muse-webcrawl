Teetotum

A teetotum is a form of spinning top most commonly used for gambling games. It has a polygonal body — typically four, six, eight or twelve sides — with each face marked with a letter or symbol indicating the result of the spin. The player spins the top, and when it falls, the uppermost letter determines whether the player wins, loses, takes from, or puts into a common pool of counters or stakes.

History

Teetotums have been known since antiquity; the Roman game of totum, from which the name may derive (via the Latin letter T marked on one face, for totum, "all"), is an early example. The English teetotum became popular in the 18th and 19th centuries. A common four-sided version bears the letters T (take all), H (take half), P (put down a stake) and N (take nothing). German versions include the letters of the Dreidel used at Hanukkah, a related four-sided top.

Variations

The game of put and take uses a teetotum, and the top also appears in board games such as Snakes and Ladders variants and in the game of Teetotum in Georgian and Victorian parlour entertainment. Multi-sided teetotums with numbers served as dice substitutes in an era when dice were associated with disreputable gambling.
