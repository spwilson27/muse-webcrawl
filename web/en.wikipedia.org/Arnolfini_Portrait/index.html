Arnolfini Portrait

The Arnolfini Portrait (or The Arnolfini Wedding) is a 1434 oil painting on oak panel by the Early Netherlandish painter Jan van Eyck. It depicts the Italian merchant Giovanni di Nicolao Arnolfini and his wife in their home at Bruges, and is held by the National Gallery in London.

Description

The full-length double portrait shows the couple in a richly furnished interior. Details long studied by art historians include the convex mirror on the rear wall, which reflects two figures entering the room, the small dog at the couple's feet, a single burning candle in the chandelier, discarded clogs, and oranges on the windowsill. Above the mirror van Eyck signed and dated the work in Latin: "Johannes de Eyck fuit hic 1434" — "Jan van Eyck was here 1434".

Interpretation

Erwin Panofsky famously interpreted the painting as a marriage document, reading its objects as disguised religious symbolism. Later scholars have questioned whether the scene records a wedding at all, suggesting instead a betrothal, a memorial portrait, or a legal record of a marriage already contracted.
