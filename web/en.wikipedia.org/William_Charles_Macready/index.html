William Charles Macready

[Image: William Charles Macready]

William Charles Macready (3 March 1793 – 27 April 1873) was an English stage actor, regarded as one of the leading tragedians of the 19th century. He managed the theatres at Covent Garden and Drury Lane in London and was noted for his scholarly, naturalistic approach to Shakespearean roles and his efforts to restore Shakespeare's original texts in place of the adapted versions then current.

Career

Macready made his London debut in 1816 at Covent Garden. Over the following decades he performed the great tragic roles — Hamlet, Macbeth, King Lear, Othello, and Virginius — in Britain and on tours of the United States and France. As a manager (1837–1839 at Covent Garden; 1841–1843 at Drury Lane) he introduced rehearsals of ensemble casts, historically researched costumes, and abolished some entrenched abuses of the stage.

Astor Place Riot

His rivalry with the American actor Edwin Forrest culminated in the Astor Place Riot in New York on 10 May 1849, when a mob protesting Macready's performance at the Astor Opera House was fired on by militia; more than twenty people were killed. Macready retired from the stage in 1851. His extensive diaries, published posthumously, are an important source for 19th-century theatre history.
