Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania, officially the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania, is a U.S. state located in the Mid-Atlantic, Northeastern, and Appalachian regions of the United States. It borders Delaware to its southeast, Maryland to its south, West Virginia to its southwest, Ohio to its west, Lake Erie and New York to its north, the Delaware River and New Jersey to its east, and the Canadian province of Ontario to its northwest.

History

The region was inhabited by Native American peoples including the Lenape, Susquehannock, and Iroquois Confederacy long before European colonisation. In 1681, King Charles II granted a large land charter to William Penn, for whom the colony ("Penn's woods") was named, in settlement of a debt owed to his father. Penn established a frame of government noted for religious tolerance and democratic principles. Philadelphia served as the nation's capital during much of the American Revolutionary War and again from 1790 to 1800; the Declaration of Independence and the United States Constitution were both drafted there. Pennsylvania was the second state to ratify the Constitution.

Geography

The state spans the Appalachian Mountains, which run diagonally from southwest to northeast, with the Allegheny Plateau in the northwest and fertile valleys in the southeast. Major rivers include the Delaware, the Susquehanna, and the Ohio, formed at Pittsburgh by the confluence of the Allegheny and Monongahela.

Economy

Pennsylvania's economy is diversified across manufacturing, agriculture, energy, finance, healthcare, and higher education. It is a leading producer of steel, natural gas from the Marcellus Shale, and mushrooms. Major cities include Philadelphia, Pittsburgh, Allentown, Erie, and the capital, Harrisburg.
