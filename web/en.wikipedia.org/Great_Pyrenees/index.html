Great Pyrenees

A Great Pyrenees, a large livestock guardian breed from the Pyrenees mountains.

The Great Pyrenees, known in France as the Pyrenean Mountain Dog (French: Chien de montagne des Pyrénées), is a large breed of livestock guardian dog originating in the Pyrenees mountains between France and Spain. Calm, patient, and strongly protective, the breed has guarded flocks of sheep against wolves and bears for centuries and is still widely used for that purpose today.

History

Mountain guardian dogs of this type have existed in the Pyrenees for millennia. By the 17th century the breed had become fashionable at the French court: the Dauphin is said to have adopted one, and in 1675 the breed was proclaimed the "Royal Dog of France"; the Marquis de Lafayette brought the first pair to the United States in 1824. Numbers declined in the 20th century but the breed survived through continued use by shepherds and organized breeding in France, Britain, and North America.

Characteristics

The Great Pyrenees is a very large, muscular dog with a weather-resistant double coat, usually white, sometimes with patches of grey, tan, or reddish-brown. Unusually, the breed has double dewclaws on the hind legs. Bred to work independently on remote pastures, it tends to be self-confident, somewhat stubborn, nocturnally alert, and inclined to bark at perceived threats, while remaining gentle and tolerant with children and livestock.
