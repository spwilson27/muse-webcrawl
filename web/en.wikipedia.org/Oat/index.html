Oat

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Oat (Avena sativa) in the field

The oat (Avena sativa), sometimes called the common oat, is a species of cereal grain grown for its seed. Oats are used widely as livestock feed and for human consumption, especially as oatmeal and rolled oats in porridge, and in oat milk.

Cultivation and history

Oats probably originated as a secondary crop — a weed of wheat and barley fields in the Fertile Crescent — and were domesticated in Bronze Age Europe, flourishing in cool, moist climates where other cereals fare poorly, such as Scotland and Scandinavia. World production is led by Russia and Canada. Unlike wheat, oats are usually sown in spring and tolerate rain well.

Nutrition and uses

Oats are rich in soluble fibre, notably beta-glucan, which has been shown to help lower LDL cholesterol; they are also a good source of protein, manganese, phosphorus, and B vitamins. The groats are processed into steel-cut oats, rolled oats, and oat flour. Straw is used as animal bedding, and oats are a major component of horse feed. Though oats themselves contain no gluten, they are frequently contaminated with wheat during processing; certified gluten-free oats are available for people with coeliac disease.

Agronomy

Oat plants grow to about 1.5 m with a fibrous root system and panicles of spikelets. Important diseases include crown rust and stem rust.
