Pre-exposure prophylaxis for HIV prevention

Pre-exposure prophylaxis, commonly known as PrEP, is the use of antiretroviral medication by people who do not have HIV to prevent acquiring it. PrEP is used by people considered at high risk of HIV exposure, such as sexually active adults with partners living with HIV, people who inject drugs, and others with ongoing risk factors.

Medications

The most widely used PrEP regimen is a daily oral combination of tenofovir disoproxil fumarate and emtricitabine (marketed as Truvada and in generic forms), approved by the US Food and Drug Administration for prevention in 2012. A second oral option, tenofovir alafenamide/emtricitabine (Descovy), followed. Long-acting injectable cabotegravir, given every two months, was approved in 2021, and the twice-yearly injectable lenacapavir was approved in 2025.

Effectiveness

When taken as prescribed, PrEP is highly effective: studies show it reduces the risk of acquiring HIV through sex by about 99%. Effectiveness drops sharply with poor adherence. The PROUD and IPERGAY studies in Europe demonstrated around 86% risk reduction in real-world use among men who have sex with men.

Guidelines

The World Health Organization recommends PrEP as an additional prevention choice for people at substantial risk of HIV infection. Users are advised to undergo regular HIV testing, kidney-function monitoring, and screening for other sexually transmitted infections.
