Monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance

SpecialtyHaematology
PrevalenceAbout 3% of people over 50
Progression riskAbout 1% per year to multiple myeloma or related disorders

Monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance (MGUS) is a premalignant, asymptomatic condition in which an abnormal monoclonal protein (M protein) is found in the blood, produced by a small clone of plasma cells in the bone marrow, without evidence of end-organ damage.

Diagnosis

MGUS is usually discovered incidentally on serum protein electrophoresis. Diagnostic criteria include an M protein concentration below 30 g/L, fewer than 10 percent clonal plasma cells in the bone marrow, and the absence of myeloma-defining events such as hypercalcaemia, renal failure, anaemia, or bone lesions.

Prognosis and management

Most people with MGUS never progress, but the condition carries an average risk of about 1 percent per year of developing multiple myeloma, Waldenström macroglobulinaemia, amyloidosis, or another lymphoproliferative disorder. Management is periodic monitoring rather than treatment.
