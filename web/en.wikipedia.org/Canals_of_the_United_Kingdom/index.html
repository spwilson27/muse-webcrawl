Canals of the United Kingdom

The canals of the United Kingdom are a major part of the network of inland waterways in the United Kingdom. They have a colourful history, from use for irrigation and transport, through becoming the focus of the Industrial Revolution, to today's role in recreation and tourism.

History

Although the Romans built some navigable waterways, the modern canal age began with the Bridgewater Canal (1761), which halved the price of coal in Manchester. During "Canal Mania" of the 1790s thousands of miles were built, including the Grand Union, Kennet and Avon, and Leeds and Liverpool canals. Railway competition from the 1840s caused a long decline, and many canals were abandoned.

Today

Most of the surviving network in England and Wales is managed by the Canal & River Trust. Narrowboats, locks, aqueducts such as Pontcysyllte, and canal-side towpaths make the system popular for leisure boating, walking and heritage tourism.
