Ketu (mythology)

Ketu, the descending lunar node, depicted with a serpent's tail.

Ketu (Sanskrit: केतु) is the descending (south) lunar node in Hindu astrology and astronomy, counted among the nine Navagraha. Ketu is a "shadow planet" (chāyā graha) with no physical body; astronomically it is the point where the Moon's orbit crosses the ecliptic going southward. Its counterpart, Rahu, is the ascending node.

Mythology

According to the Puranic myth of the churning of the ocean of milk, the asura Svarbhanu disguised himself among the gods to drink the nectar of immortality, amrita. The Sun and Moon alerted Vishnu, who cut the asura in two with his discus — but the nectar had already passed his throat, so both halves became immortal: the head became Rahu and the tailed body became Ketu. In iconography Ketu is shown riding a vulture, holding a mace, sometimes with a serpent's body.

Astrological significance

In Jyotiṣa, Ketu is generally regarded as a malefic influence associated with detachment, loss, spirituality and sudden events, and is linked with the legendary cause of eclipses together with Rahu: the nodes are said to "swallow" the Sun and Moon. Ketu is exalted in some traditions in Scorpio or Sagittarius; astrologers hold its periods (daśā) capable of producing both material reversals and spiritual insight. Ketu's gemstone is cat's eye (chrysoberyl), its colour smoky, and it is associated with the south-west direction in some schemes.
