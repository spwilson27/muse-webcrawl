Terrine (food)

Terrine
CourseStarter / appetizer
Place of originFrance
Serving temperatureCold or at room temperature
Main ingredientsForcemeat, meat, fish or vegetables; aspic

A terrine (French: [tɛ.ʁin]) is a French forcemeat loaf similar to a pâté, made with more coarsely chopped ingredients. Terrines are usually served cold or at room temperature, sliced from a loaf cooked or set in an earthenware mould — also called a terrine — and are a staple of charcuterie.

Preparation

Meat, game, seafood or vegetables are chopped or ground, seasoned, often mixed with fat, eggs or cream as a binder, and layered or packed into a lined mould. Meat terrines are traditionally cooked in a water bath, then pressed under a weight while cooling so the loaf firms and slices evenly; a layer of aspic may coat the top. Vegetable and fish terrines are often set with gelatin instead of being baked.

Varieties

Classic examples include terrine de campagne (a rustic pork and liver loaf), terrines of duck, rabbit or game birds, salmon terrines, and layered vegetable terrines. Sweet dessert terrines of fruit in jelly are also made. In the Loire region, rillettes-like preparations and pâtés en terrine are regional specialities.

Service

Terrine is served in thick slices, typically with cornichons, mustard, chutney and toasted bread, as a first course or part of a charcuterie board.
