Constantin Brâncoveanu

Constantin Brâncoveanu (1654 – 15 August 1714) was Prince of Wallachia from 1688 until his death. A member of the Craiovești boyar family and nephew of Prince Șerban Cantacuzino, he pursued a careful balancing policy between the Ottoman Empire, the Habsburgs, and Russia while fostering a cultural golden age in Wallachia.

Reign and culture

Brâncoveanu founded monasteries and churches — most famously Horezu Monastery — and gave his name to the Brâncovenesc style, a synthesis of Ottoman, Venetian, and local motifs in Romanian architecture. His court at Bucharest and Mogoșoaia Palace supported printing, education, and scholars such as Antim Ivireanul.

Execution and sanctity

Suspected of treasonous negotiations with Austria and Russia, Brâncoveanu was deposed in 1714, taken to Constantinople, and executed on 15 August 1714 together with his four sons and his adviser Ianache Văcărescu, after refusing to convert to Islam. The Romanian Orthodox Church canonized them as martyrs in 1992, commemorated on 16 August.
