The Great Pyramid of Cholula, also known by its Nahuatl name Tlachihualtepetl ("made-by-hand mountain"), is a huge temple-pyramid complex in Cholula, Puebla, Mexico. It is the largest pyramid by volume known to exist in the world today, and the largest monument ever constructed anywhere, with a base covering over 45 acres.

History

The pyramid was built in several superimposed stages beginning around the 3rd century BCE and continuing over many centuries, dedicated to the god Quetzalcoatl. By the time of the Spanish conquest, the overgrown pyramid resembled a natural hill. The Spanish built the church of Nuestra Señora de los Remedios (Iglesia de Nuestra Señora de los Remedios) on its summit in 1594, which still stands today.

Archaeology

Excavations beginning in the 1930s revealed a network of tunnels—about 8 kilometres in total—dug through the pyramid, exposing earlier construction stages, murals, and altars. The site remains an important pilgrimage and tourist destination, with a site museum and a reconstructed section of the pyramid's south side.
