Muhammad XII of Granada

Muhammad XII (c. 1460 – 1533), known in Spanish as Boabdil (from Abū ʿAbd Allāh) and also called el Chico ("the Little") or el Zogoibi ("the Unfortunate"), was the twenty-second and last Nasrid ruler of the Emirate of Granada in al-Andalus, on the Iberian Peninsula. His surrender of Granada on 2 January 1492 ended the Christian Reconquista and nearly eight centuries of Muslim rule in Iberia.

Reign

Boabdil seized the throne in 1482 with the support of his mother Aixa, rebelling against his father Abu'l-Hasan Ali. Captured by Castilian forces at the Battle of Lucena in 1483, he was released as a Castilian vassal and used against his father and his uncle, Muhammad XIII (al-Zagal), whose civil war fatally divided the emirate. Ferdinand and Isabella exploited the division during the decade-long Granada War, capturing Málaga, Baza and Almería before besieging Granada itself in 1491.

Surrender and exile

Under the Treaty of Granada (1491), Boabdil handed over the city and the Alhambra on 2 January 1492, receiving in return lordship over the Alpujarras. Legend recounts that, looking back at Granada from a mountain pass — the Puerto del Suspiro del Moro, "Pass of the Moor's Sigh" — he wept, and his mother reproached him for crying "like a woman for what he could not defend as a man". He soon sold his rights and crossed to Fez in Morocco, where he died around 1533.
