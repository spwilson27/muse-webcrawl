Cuba–United States relations are the bilateral relations between the Republic of Cuba and the United States of America. Relations began in the early 19th century; the US intervened in the Spanish–American War of 1898, after which Cuba became independent but remained under strong American influence, formalized by the Platt Amendment.

The Revolution and embargo

After Fidel Castro's revolution overthrew the Batista government in 1959, relations deteriorated rapidly. The United States imposed a comprehensive trade embargo beginning in 1960, backed the failed Bay of Pigs invasion in 1961, and confronted the Soviet Union in the Cuban Missile Crisis of October 1962.

Thaw and reversal

In December 2014 Presidents Barack Obama and Raúl Castro announced a restoration of diplomatic relations; embassies reopened in Havana and Washington in 2015. Subsequent US administrations tightened sanctions again, and relations remain strained.
