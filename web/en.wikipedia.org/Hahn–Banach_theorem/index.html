Hahn–Banach theorem

The Hahn–Banach theorem is a central result in functional analysis, named after Hans Hahn and Stefan Banach, who proved it independently in the late 1920s. In its analytic form it states that a linear functional defined on a subspace of a vector space, and dominated there by a sublinear function, can be extended to the whole space while preserving the domination.

Forms and consequences

A standard corollary for normed spaces is that a bounded linear functional on a subspace extends to the whole space with the same norm. From this follow fundamental facts: the dual space of a nonzero normed space separates points, every nonzero vector has a functional attaining its norm, and weak topologies behave well. The geometric form of the theorem yields separation theorems: disjoint nonempty convex sets, one of them open, can be separated by a hyperplane — a result with applications in optimisation, game theory, and mathematical economics.

Foundations

The general proof uses Zorn's lemma and hence the axiom of choice; the theorem is weaker than the full axiom of choice but is not provable in ZF set theory alone. Over separable spaces, weaker choice principles suffice.
