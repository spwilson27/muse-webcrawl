Sun Yat-sen

Sun Yat-sen (12 November 1866 – 12 March 1925) was a Chinese revolutionary, statesman, and political philosopher who founded the Republic of China and is honored as its "Father of the Nation." His political philosophy, the Three Principles of the People — nationalism, democracy, and the people's livelihood — remains foundational to both the Republic of China on Taiwan and, in adapted form, the People's Republic of China.

Early life

Sun was born in Cuiheng, Guangdong, and educated in Hawaii, where he attended Iolani School and Oahu College. He later studied medicine in Hong Kong, graduating from the Hong Kong College of Medicine for Chinese in 1892, but soon turned to revolutionary politics against the Qing dynasty.

Revolution

Sun founded the Revive China Society in 1894 and later the Tongmenghui in Tokyo in 1905. After a series of failed uprisings, the Wuchang Uprising of 10 October 1911 triggered the Xinhai Revolution, which overthrew the Qing. Sun was inaugurated as provisional president of the Republic of China on 1 January 1912, but soon yielded the presidency to Yuan Shikai. He went into exile in Japan after Yuan's authoritarian turn, later returned, and in 1919 reorganized his party as the Kuomintang (KMT).

In 1923–24 Sun allied with the Chinese Communist Party and accepted Soviet support to rebuild the KMT, establishing the Whampoa Military Academy under Chiang Kai-shek. He died of liver cancer in Beijing in 1925 while negotiating national reunification. Both the Republic of China and the People's Republic of China claim his legacy.
