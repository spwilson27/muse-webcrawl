Gluteus minimus

The gluteus minimus is the smallest of the three gluteal muscles of the buttock. It lies deep to the gluteus medius, on the outer surface of the ilium, and works with that muscle to abduct the hip and stabilise the pelvis during locomotion.

Gluteus minimus
OriginOuter surface of the ilium, between the anterior and inferior gluteal lines
InsertionGreater trochanter of the femur
NerveSuperior gluteal nerve (L4–S1)
ActionsAbduction and medial rotation of the hip

Structure

The gluteus minimus is a fan-shaped muscle arising from the external surface of the ilium between the anterior and inferior gluteal lines, and from the margin of the greater sciatic notch. Its fibres converge into a tendon inserting on the anterior surface of the greater trochanter. The superior gluteal nerve and vessels emerge above the piriformis and run forward between the gluteus minimus and gluteus medius, supplying both muscles.

Function

Like the gluteus medius, the gluteus minimus abducts the thigh and, more importantly during gait, prevents the pelvis from dropping on the unsupported side when one foot is lifted from the ground. Its anterior fibres also medially rotate the hip. Tendinopathy of the gluteus minimus and medius tendons at their insertion on the greater trochanter is a common cause of lateral hip pain, a condition sometimes grouped under the label greater trochanteric pain syndrome.
