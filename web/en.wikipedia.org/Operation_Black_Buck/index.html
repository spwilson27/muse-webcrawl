Operation Black Buck

Operation Black Buck
Date: 1 May – 12 June 1982
Location: Port Stanley, Falkland Islands
Unit: RAF No. 44 Squadron (Vulcans)
Result: Runway damaged; strategic signal sent

Operation Black Buck was a series of seven very-long-range bombing missions flown by Royal Air Force Avro Vulcan bombers during the 1982 Falklands War against Argentine positions at Port Stanley airfield. Staged from Ascension Island, each round trip covered about 12,200 km (7,600 miles) and took some 16 hours, making them the longest bombing raids in history at the time. They required an elaborate chain of Victor tanker aircraft refuelling one another as well as the bomber.

The raids

The first raid, on 1 May 1982, saw Vulcan XM607, flown by Flight Lieutenant Martin Withers, drop 21 1,000-lb bombs across the runway, one of which cratered it and prevented its use by Argentine fast jets. Subsequent Black Buck missions attacked the runway again, struck radar sites with US-supplied Shrike anti-radiation missiles, and — in one dramatic episode — a Vulcan (XM597) diverted to Rio de Janeiro after a refuelling probe broke, causing a minor diplomatic incident with Brazil.

Assessment

The raids consumed enormous fuel and effort for modest physical damage, and their cost-effectiveness was debated. Nevertheless they demonstrated that mainland Argentina was within reach of British air power, compelled Argentina to keep interceptor fighters back to defend Buenos Aires, and provided a major boost to British morale. The campaign is the subject of Rowland White's book Vulcan 607.
