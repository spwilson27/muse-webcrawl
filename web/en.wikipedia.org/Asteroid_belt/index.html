The asteroid belt is a torus-shaped region of the Solar System between the orbits of Mars and Jupiter, occupied by numerous irregularly shaped bodies called asteroids or minor planets. About half of the belt's mass is contained in its four largest objects: the dwarf planet Ceres and the asteroids Vesta, Pallas and Hygiea.

Formation

The belt consists of primordial material that never accreted into a planet because Jupiter's gravitational perturbations kept collision velocities too high. Contrary to popular depictions, the belt is mostly empty space: the total mass is only about 3% of the Moon's, and spacecraft pass through it safely. Gaps in the belt — the Kirkwood gaps — occur at orbital resonances with Jupiter.

Exploration

Pioneer 10 was the first spacecraft to traverse the belt in 1972. NASA's Dawn mission orbited Vesta (2011–2012) and Ceres (2015–2018), the first spacecraft to orbit two extraterrestrial bodies. Other missions, including Galileo, Hayabusa and OSIRIS-REx, have visited individual asteroids.
