R-360 Neptune (Ukrainian: Р-360 «Нептун», R-360 "Neptun") is a Ukrainian subsonic anti-ship cruise missile developed by the Luch Design Bureau in Kyiv. Based on the Soviet Kh-35 design but with substantially improved range and electronics, the Neptune system (RK-360MC) is deployed as a mobile coastal-defence complex of truck-mounted launchers, command vehicles, and radar. The missile flies at low altitude over the sea, guided by inertial navigation with an active radar seeker for terminal homing, and carries a 150 kg warhead over a range of up to about 300 km.

Development and service

Neptune was publicly unveiled in 2015, flight-tested from 2016, and formally accepted into service with the Ukrainian Navy in 2020, entering operational service in 2021. Development accelerated after Russia's 2014 annexation of Crimea left Ukraine without much of its naval capability.

Operational history

The system gained worldwide attention during the Russo-Ukrainian War: on 13 April 2022, Ukrainian forces struck the Russian Black Sea Fleet flagship, the cruiser Moskva, with two Neptune missiles; the ship sank the following day while under tow — the largest warship sunk in combat since the Falklands War. Neptune batteries subsequently conducted further strikes against Russian naval and air-defence targets around the Black Sea, and a modified land-attack variant was publicly acknowledged by Ukraine in 2023.
