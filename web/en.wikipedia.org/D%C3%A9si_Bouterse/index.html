Desiré Delano "Dési" Bouterse (13 November 1945 – 23 December 2024) was a Surinamese military officer and politician who dominated his country's politics for four decades. He led the 1980 coup d'état (the "Sergeants' Coup") that made him the de facto head of state of Suriname from 1980 to 1988, and he later served as the democratically elected President of Suriname from 2010 to 2020 as leader of the National Democratic Party (NDP), which he founded in 1987.

Military rule

Bouterse was a sports instructor and non-commissioned officer in the Surinamese army who had trained in the Netherlands. After leading the February 1980 coup against the government of Henck Arron, he ruled through the National Military Council. His regime is associated above all with the December murders of 1982, in which fifteen prominent critics of the military government — journalists, lawyers, academics, and trade unionists — were killed at Fort Zeelandia in Paramaribo. During the Surinamese Interior War (1986–1992), government forces fought the Jungle Commando of Ronnie Brunswijk; the war included the 1986 Moiwana massacre of Maroon civilians.

Presidency and convictions

After his party won the 2010 elections, Bouterse was elected president by the National Assembly and re-elected in 2015. His government was criticised for an amnesty law passed in 2012 that halted his trial for the December murders; the trial later resumed, and in November 2019 a Surinamese court-martial sentenced him in absentia to 20 years in prison for his role in the killings. The conviction was upheld on appeal in 2023, but Bouterse went into hiding rather than report to prison. In 1999 he had also been convicted in absentia in the Netherlands on drug-trafficking charges. He lost the 2020 election to Chan Santokhi, and died in hiding in December 2024.
