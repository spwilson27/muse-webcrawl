Chester (song)

Chester
GenrePatriotic hymn / fife-and-drum tune
Written1770 (hymn version); verses added 1778
ComposerWilliam Billings

"Chester" is a patriotic hymn composed by William Billings and one of the most popular tunes sung in the colonies during the American Revolutionary War. Billings first published the tune as a hymn in The New-England Psalm-Singer (1770) and added new verses in The Singing Master's Assistant (1778), beginning "Let tyrants shake their iron rod, and Slav'ry clank her galling chains".

During the Revolution

The song's defiant verses made it a favourite marching tune of the Continental Army; tradition holds that it served the young United States much as a national anthem before any was officially adopted. It remained part of the shape-note and Sacred Harp singing traditions in the American South.

Modern arrangements

In the 1950s the American composer William Schuman arranged "Chester" as the third part of his New England Triptych for orchestra, then expanded the arrangement into the standalone overture Chester (1957), which became a staple of the concert band repertoire. The tune also appears in Schuman-derived settings used widely by school and military bands.
