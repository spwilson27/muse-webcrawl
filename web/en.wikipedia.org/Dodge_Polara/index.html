Dodge Polara

The Dodge Polara is an automobile introduced in the United States for the 1960 model year as Dodge's top-of-the-line full-size car. Its name derives from the star Polaris. After a series of changes in market position, the Polara nameplate ended in 1973, when it was replaced by the Dodge Monaco.

History

The first Polaras of 1960–1961 were flamboyant full-size cars in the tailfin era. For 1962 the Polara was downsized to an intermediate platform, and in 1965 it returned to the full-size Chrysler C-body, where it remained for the rest of its production life, serving both as a family car and in police service.

Police use

Later Polaras were popular as police cars: the 1969 Polara with the 440-cubic-inch V8 was among the fastest police sedans of its era and was famously used by the California Highway Patrol.
