Grande Chartreuse (French: [ɡʁɑ̃d ʃaʁtʁøz]) is the head monastery of the Carthusian religious order, located in the Chartreuse Mountains north of Grenoble in the commune of Saint-Pierre-de-Chartreuse, Isère, France. It was founded in 1084 by Saint Bruno of Cologne and six companions, who sought a life of solitude and contemplation in a remote alpine valley known as the "desert" of Chartreuse.

History

The monastery has been destroyed and rebuilt repeatedly — by avalanche in 1132, and by fire eight times. The monks were expelled during the French Revolution and again under the anti-clerical laws of 1903, when they spent nearly four decades in exile at Farneta in Italy before returning in 1940. The present buildings, largely of the 17th century, shelter a small community living in near-total silence in individual cells arranged around a great cloister.

Chartreuse liqueur

The monastery is the source of Chartreuse, the green and yellow herbal liqueurs distilled by the monks from a secret recipe of 130 botanicals said to derive from a manuscript given to the order in 1605. Because the monks do not receive visitors inside the enclosure, a museum at nearby La Correrie presents Carthusian life. The monastery became widely known through Philip Gröning's 2005 documentary film Into Great Silence.
