Battle of Angaur

Battle of Angaur
Date: 17–30 September 1944 (main fighting)
Location: Angaur Island, Palau Islands
Combatants: United States vs Empire of Japan
Result: American victory

The Battle of Angaur was fought in the Pacific campaign of the Second World War on the small coral island of Angaur in the Palau group, from 17 to 30 September 1944, in parallel with the much larger and bloodier Battle of Peleliu nearby. The US Army's 81st Infantry Division landed against a Japanese garrison of about 1,400 troops under Major Goto Ushio.

Soldiers of the 81st Infantry Division come ashore on Angaur.

Battle

After naval and air bombardment, the 322nd and 323rd Regimental Combat Teams landed on 17 September against light opposition and quickly overran most of the island, which the Americans wanted for an airfield and its phosphate deposits. The Japanese survivors withdrew to a strongpoint of caves and ridges in the island's northwest — the "Bowl" and Romauldo Hill — where they resisted for nearly two weeks. American forces reduced the position with artillery, flamethrowers, and finally bulldozers that sealed cave entrances. The island was declared secure on 30 September, though mopping up continued into October. US casualties were about 264 killed and 1,335 wounded; nearly the entire Japanese garrison was killed. An airfield was promptly built and used for operations against the Philippines.
