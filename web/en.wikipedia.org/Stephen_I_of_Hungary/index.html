Stephen I (Hungarian: I. István; Latin: Stephanus I; c. 975 – 15 August 1038), also known as Saint Stephen, was the last Grand Prince of the Hungarians and the first King of Hungary, reigning from about 1000 until his death. He is credited with converting the Hungarians to Christianity, establishing the Hungarian state and church organization, and defeating the pagan and nomadic opposition to his rule. He is the patron saint of Hungary and was canonized in 1083.

Early life and accession

Stephen was born as Vajk, the son of Grand Prince Géza and Sarolt, and was baptized as a child, taking the name Stephen after the first Christian martyr. He married Gisela of Bavaria, sister of Emperor Henry II. On Géza's death in 997, Stephen defeated his pagan relative Koppány, who claimed the throne by the older custom of seniority, and had his body quartered and displayed at the gates of four fortresses.

King of Hungary

Stephen was crowned king with a crown sent by Pope Sylvester II, according to tradition with the approval of Emperor Otto III. He organized Hungary into counties administered by royal officials (ispáns), founded dioceses including the archbishopric of Esztergom, established monasteries, and issued two books of laws drawing heavily on Western European models. He suppressed the tribal chieftains who resisted Christianization, including his uncle Gyula in Transylvania and Ajtony in the south.

Death and canonization

Stephen's only surviving son, Emeric, died in a hunting accident in 1031, precipitating a succession crisis. Stephen died on 15 August 1038 and was buried at Székesfehérvár. He was canonized by Pope Gregory VII in 1083, together with Emeric and Bishop Gerard. His feast day, 20 August, is Hungary's national day, and his crown — the Holy Crown of Hungary — became the central symbol of Hungarian statehood.
