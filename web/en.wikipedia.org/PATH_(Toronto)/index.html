PATH (Toronto)

PATH is a network of underground pedestrian tunnels, elevated walkways and at-grade sections that connects office towers, retail and transit in Downtown Toronto, Ontario, Canada. According to Guinness World Records, PATH is the largest underground shopping complex in the world, with more than 30 kilometres (19 miles) of tunnels and about 371,600 square metres (4 million square feet) of retail space.

The network links roughly 75 buildings — including the Toronto Eaton Centre, First Canadian Place, Scotia Plaza, the Toronto-Dominion Centre and Roy Thomson Hall — as well as six Toronto subway stations and the Union Station railway terminal. More than 200,000 commuters use the system daily, and it connects to about 50 office towers housing some 5,000 businesses.

History

The first underground connections in Toronto date to 1900, when the T. Eaton Co. tunnelled beneath James Street to connect its main store with the Annex building. The network expanded greatly from the 1960s and 1970s as new office towers incorporated basement retail connected to neighbouring buildings and subway stations. In the late 1980s the City of Toronto introduced the unified PATH name, signage and coloured wayfinding system — the letters of the logo representing different directions of travel — to help pedestrians navigate the privately owned segments of the network.

Wayfinding

Because the tunnels are built and owned by individual building owners, layouts and signage were historically inconsistent. The city's PATH signage uses colour-coded arms and maps at decision points; a revised wayfinding strategy with more consistent design has been introduced progressively since the 2010s. The system is coordinated by the Toronto Financial District Business Improvement Area in partnership with the city and building owners.
