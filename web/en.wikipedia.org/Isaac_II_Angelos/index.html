Isaac II Angelos

Isaac II Angelos (Greek: Ἰσαάκιος Κομνηνός Ἄγγελος; c. 1156 – January 1204) was Byzantine emperor from 1185 to 1195, and again from 1203 to 1204. His deposition and blinding by his brother Alexios III, and his brief restoration by the Fourth Crusade, set in motion the events that led to the sack of Constantinople.

Rise to power

Isaac, a great-nephew of Alexios I Komnenos through his mother, narrowly escaped execution under the tyrannical Andronikos I. In September 1185, after killing the emperor's agent Stephen Hagiochristophorites, he took refuge in Hagia Sophia and was proclaimed emperor by the Constantinopolitan mob, which then lynched Andronikos.

First reign

His reign saw the disastrous Norman invasion of 1185 — Thessalonica was sacked before the invaders were crushed at Demetritzes — and an oppressive tax to fund his wedding provoked the Vlach-Bulgarian uprising of Asen and Peter, which restored the Bulgarian Empire. The Third Crusade strained relations further when Frederick Barbarossa marched through Byzantine territory in 1189–90. On Cyprus, Isaac Komnenos was overthrown by Richard the Lionheart in 1191.

Deposition and restoration

In April 1195 Isaac was deposed, blinded, and imprisoned by his elder brother Alexios III. His son Alexios IV escaped to the West and persuaded the Fourth Crusade to restore his father; the crusaders installed Isaac and Alexios IV in July 1203. Unable to pay the crusaders and facing popular rebellion, the two were overthrown by Alexios V Doukas in January 1204; Isaac died shortly afterwards, reportedly of shock.
