Green space

Urban green spaces provide recreation, cooling and habitat in cities.

Green space (or greenspace) is an area of vegetated land, typically within or near an urban environment, that is partly or entirely covered with grass, trees, shrubs or other vegetation. Green spaces include parks, community gardens, allotments, cemeteries, playing fields, green corridors along rivers and railways, and protected belts such as the green belts surrounding some cities.

Benefits

Research in urban planning and public health associates access to green space with improved mental and physical health, opportunities for exercise and social contact, and mitigation of the urban heat island effect through shade and evapotranspiration. Vegetated surfaces also improve air quality, support biodiversity, reduce surface-water flooding by absorbing rainfall, and sequester carbon. Property values near well-maintained parks tend to be higher, which can contribute both to municipal revenues and to concerns about "green gentrification".

Planning

Standards for provision vary: the World Health Organization has suggested that residents should live within about 300 metres of a green space of at least 0.5 hectares. London's Metropolitan Green Belt, established in the 20th century, is among the best-known green belt policies, intended to check urban sprawl. Modern approaches connect individual spaces into green infrastructure networks — linked corridors that allow wildlife movement and water management at landscape scale.
