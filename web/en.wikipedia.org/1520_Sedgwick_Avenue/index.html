Known forBirthplace of hip hop

1520 Sedgwick Avenue is a 102-unit high-rise apartment building in the Morris Heights neighborhood of the Bronx in New York City. It is widely recognized as the birthplace of hip hop: on August 11, 1973, DJ Kool Herc hosted a back-to-school party in the building's recreation room, where he pioneered the technique of extending the percussive "break" sections of funk records using two turntables, with Coke La Rock and Clark Kent performing as early MCs.

Recognition

In 2007, the New York State Office of Parks, Recreation and Historic Preservation recognized the building as the birthplace of hip hop, and it was added to the New York State Registry of Historic Places. Efforts by residents and supporters, including DJ Kool Herc, helped protect the building's affordable housing status when it was threatened by a sale to private investors.

Legacy

The rec-room parties at 1520 Sedgwick Avenue are regarded as the founding event of hip hop culture, which grew from the Bronx to become a global musical and cultural movement.
