Virtual reality headset

A virtual reality headset (or VR headset) is a head-mounted display that lets the user experience virtual reality. It provides one or two small high-resolution displays in front of the eyes, lenses to focus the image, and sensors that track the wearer's head motion so the displayed view updates accordingly.

[Image: A white standalone virtual reality headset photographed against a plain background.]

Types

Headsets fall into three categories: tethered (connected to a PC or console, e.g. Valve Index and PlayStation VR2), standalone (self-contained with onboard processors, e.g. Meta Quest 3), and smartphone-based viewers, an early low-cost class largely abandoned. Key specifications include resolution per eye, refresh rate (typically 90–120 Hz), field of view, and tracking method — external base stations versus inside-out camera tracking.

History

The consumer era began with the Oculus Rift (2016) and HTC Vive, followed by the standalone Oculus Quest (2019), which popularized untethered VR; Apple's Vision Pro (2024) extended the category into high-end "spatial computing" with mixed reality.
