Beechcraft King Air

The Beechcraft King Air is a line of American utility aircraft produced by Beechcraft. Introduced in 1964, the King Air family of twin-turboprop, pressurized aircraft became the best-selling business turboprops in the world, with more than 7,600 delivered. The line began with the Model 90 series, followed by the Model 100, and the larger Super King Air 200 and 300/350 series, the latter still in production as the King Air 260 and 360.

Development

Beechcraft combined the airframe of its piston-engine Queen Air with Pratt & Whitney Canada PT6A turboprops to create the King Air 90, which entered service in 1964 and was adopted by the U.S. Army. The stretched Model 100 followed in 1969. The Super King Air 200 (1974) added a T-tail and higher performance, becoming the definitive variant for corporate, air-ambulance, cargo, and military surveillance roles; the 300/350 series added further stretch and power.

Operators

King Airs serve with airlines, corporations, flight schools, and air forces worldwide. Military versions include the U.S. C-12 Huron and numerous intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance conversions. The type is prized for short-field performance, reliability, and the ability to operate from small airports.
