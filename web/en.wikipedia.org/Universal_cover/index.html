Universal cover

In topology, a universal cover of a topological space X is a covering space of X that is simply connected. It is "universal" in the sense that it covers every other connected covering space of X: for any other covering of X there is a unique covering map from the universal cover onto it, making the universal cover the largest and simplest covering space.

Existence

A connected, locally path-connected space has a universal cover if and only if it is semilocally simply connected. The universal cover can be constructed concretely as the space of homotopy classes of paths starting at a chosen base point, topologised appropriately.

Examples

The universal cover of the circle is the real line, with the covering map given by the exponential function winding the line around the circle. The universal cover of the torus is the plane, and that of the real projective plane is the 2-sphere. The group of deck transformations of the universal cover is isomorphic to the fundamental group of X.
