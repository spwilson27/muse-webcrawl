Impeachment of Bill Clinton

The impeachment of Bill Clinton occurred on December 19, 1998, when the United States House of Representatives approved articles of impeachment against Bill Clinton, the 42nd president of the United States, for perjury and obstruction of justice. The charges stemmed from a sexual harassment lawsuit filed against Clinton by Paula Jones, and from Clinton's sworn testimony denying a sexual relationship with White House intern Monica Lewinsky.

Background

Independent Counsel Kenneth Starr investigated the Clintons' Whitewater real estate dealings and, after expansions of his mandate, the Lewinsky affair. Clinton had denied under oath in the Jones case, and publicly in January 1998, that he had had "sexual relations" with Lewinsky. After evidence emerged contradicting that denial, Starr submitted a report to Congress in September 1998 arguing that Clinton had committed impeachable offenses.

House proceedings

On December 19, 1998, the House voted, largely along party lines, to approve two articles of impeachment: Article I, charging perjury before a federal grand jury, passed 228–206; Article II, charging obstruction of justice, passed 221–212. Two other proposed articles (a second perjury count and abuse of power) failed.

Senate trial and acquittal

The Senate trial began in January 1999, presided over by Chief Justice William Rehnquist. On February 12, 1999, the Senate acquitted Clinton: the perjury article failed 45–55 and the obstruction article failed 50–50 — both well short of the two-thirds majority required for conviction and removal. Clinton completed his second term in office.

Clinton was the second U.S. president to be impeached, after Andrew Johnson in 1868. He was later held in contempt of court for misleading testimony in the Jones case and agreed to a five-year suspension of his Arkansas law license.
