The Republicans (French: Les Républicains, pronounced [le ʁepyblikɛ̃]; LR) is a liberal-conservative political party in France, largely descended from the Gaullist tradition. It is the principal party of the French centre-right for much of the Fifth Republic's history through its predecessor organisations, most recently the Union for a Popular Movement (UMP), which was renamed The Republicans in 2015 under the leadership of Nicolas Sarkozy.

History

The party traces its lineage through the Union for the New Republic, the Union of Democrats for the Republic, and the Rally for the Republic (RPR), founded by Jacques Chirac in 1976. The RPR merged into the UMP in 2002. After Sarkozy's renaming of the party in 2015, François Fillon won its primary for the 2017 presidential election but was eliminated in the first round amid a financial scandal — the first time the mainstream right failed to reach the runoff under the Fifth Republic. Subsequent leaders included Laurent Wauquiez, Christian Jacob, and Éric Ciotti, whose 2024 decision to ally with the National Rally split the party.

Ideology

LR espouses liberal conservatism: support for free markets and lower taxes, a strong state role in order and national sovereignty, conservative positions on immigration, and attachment to Gaullist independence in foreign policy. It is a member of the European People's Party.

Electoral results

The party and its predecessors supplied presidents Jacques Chirac and Nicolas Sarkozy. It long dominated the Senate and local government, though its national vote share has declined since 2017 amid competition from Emmanuel Macron's centrists and Marine Le Pen's National Rally.
