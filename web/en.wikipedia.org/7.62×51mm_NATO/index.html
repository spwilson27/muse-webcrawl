7.62×51mm NATO

The 7.62×51mm NATO is a rimless bottlenecked rifle cartridge developed in the 1950s as the standard service cartridge for NATO forces. It is a full-power cartridge used in battle rifles, designated marksman rifles, sniper rifles, and medium machine guns.

History

Developed from the American .300 Savage and experimental T65 series, the cartridge was adopted by the United States in 1954 and standardised by NATO shortly afterward. Its commercial equivalent is the .308 Winchester. It chambered rifles such as the M14, FN FAL, and Heckler & Koch G3, and machine guns such as the M60 and FN MAG.

Characteristics

The cartridge uses a 51 mm case and fires bullets typically weighing 9.3–11.3 g (143–175 grains) at muzzle velocities around 850 m/s. It is effective at ranges beyond 800 metres and remains widely used for long-range and vehicle-mounted weapons even after smaller intermediate cartridges became standard for assault rifles.
