ADSL

Full nameAsymmetric digital subscriber line
StandardITU-T G.992.1 and successors
Typical downstreamUp to 8–24 Mbit/s

Asymmetric digital subscriber line (ADSL) is a type of digital subscriber line (DSL) technology, a data communications technology that enables faster data transmission over copper telephone lines than a conventional voiceband modem can provide.

How it works

ADSL uses frequencies above the voice band, separated from ordinary telephone service by filters or splitters. It is "asymmetric" because most bandwidth is allocated to downstream traffic toward the user. Successive standards — ADSL2 and ADSL2+ — raised maximum downstream rates to about 12 and 24 Mbit/s respectively.

Deployment

ADSL was the dominant form of residential broadband in the early 2000s. Speed falls with distance from the exchange or DSLAM, and the technology has increasingly been displaced by VDSL, cable, and fibre-to-the-premises services.
