César Pelli (12 October 1926 – 19 July 2019) was an Argentine-American architect who designed some of the world's tallest buildings and major urban landmarks. He founded the firm now known as Pelli Clarke Pelli Architects and served as Dean of the Yale School of Architecture from 1977 to 1984.

Early life and career

Born in Tucumán, Argentina, Pelli studied architecture at the Universidad Nacional de Tucumán before emigrating to the United States in 1952 to study at the University of Illinois. He worked for a decade in the office of Eero Saarinen, contributing to projects such as the TWA Flight Center.

Major works

His best-known buildings include the Petronas Towers in Kuala Lumpur (the world's tallest buildings from 1998 to 2004), One Canada Square at Canary Wharf in London, the World Financial Center in New York, the International Finance Centre in Hong Kong and the Salesforce Tower in San Francisco.

Awards

Pelli received the AIA Gold Medal in 1995 and numerous other honours for his contribution to skyscraper design.
