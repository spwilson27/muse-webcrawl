Kröller-Müller Museum

Location: Houtkampweg 6, Otterlo, Netherlands
Established: 13 July 1938
Founder: Helene Kröller-Müller

The Kröller-Müller Museum is a national art museum and sculpture garden located in the Hoge Veluwe National Park in Otterlo, the Netherlands. Founded by art collector Helene Kröller-Müller, it opened in 1938 and holds one of the largest collections of paintings by Vincent van Gogh in the world.

Collection

The museum's Van Gogh collection is second only to that of the Van Gogh Museum in Amsterdam, and includes works such as The Potato Eaters and a version of Sunflowers. Other artists represented include Piet Mondrian, Georges Seurat, Odilon Redon, Pablo Picasso and Juan Gris.

Sculpture garden

The museum's sculpture garden, one of the largest in Europe, covers some 25 hectares and displays works by Auguste Rodin, Henry Moore, Jean Dubuffet and many others, set in woodland and lawns around the museum building.

History

Helene Müller married Anton Kröller in 1888 and, advised by the art educator H. P. Bremmer, assembled a large collection of modern art. The museum building was designed by the Belgian architect Henry van de Velde.
