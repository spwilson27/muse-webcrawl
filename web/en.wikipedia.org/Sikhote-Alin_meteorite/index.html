Sikhote-Alin meteorite

The Sikhote-Alin meteorite is an iron meteorite that fell in the Sikhote-Alin mountains of Primorye in the far east of the Soviet Union on 12 February 1947. It was one of the largest meteorite falls ever observed: the incoming body, estimated at some 900 tonnes in the atmosphere and roughly 70–100 tonnes surviving in collected fragments, broke apart at low altitude and showered fragments over an elliptical strewn field of about 1.3 km².

The fall

At about 10:38 local time, witnesses saw a fireball brighter than the Sun moving across the sky, accompanied by deafening detonations and a smoke trail up to 32 km long. The meteoroid entered at an estimated 14.5 km/s; at an altitude of about 5.6 km it fragmented violently. Trees were snapped and bark stripped over a wide area, and at least 122 impact craters were found, the largest 26 m across and 6 m deep.

Composition and specimens

Sikhote-Alin is classified as an IIAB coarse octahedrite, composed of about 93% iron and 6% nickel with traces of cobalt, phosphorus, sulfur, and germanium. Expeditions by the Academy of Sciences of the USSR, beginning under Vasily Fesenkov and including Evgeny Krinov, recovered over 23 tonnes of material; individual fragments are famous for their thumbprinted regmaglypts and shrapnel-like shapes. Specimens are held in museums worldwide.
