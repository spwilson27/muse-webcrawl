Europe is a continent located entirely in the Northern Hemisphere and mostly in the Eastern Hemisphere. It comprises the westernmost peninsulas of the continental landmass of Eurasia and is bordered by the Arctic Ocean to the north, the Atlantic Ocean to the west, the Mediterranean Sea to the south, and Asia to the east. Europe is conventionally separated from Asia by the watershed of the Ural Mountains, the Ural River, the Caspian Sea, the Greater Caucasus, the Black Sea and the waterways linking the Black Sea to the Aegean Sea.

Covering about 10.18 million km², or 2% of Earth's surface, Europe is the second-smallest continent by area. Its population of roughly 745 million is the third-largest of any continent. Politically, Europe is divided into about fifty sovereign states, the largest by area wholly within Europe being Ukraine, and the largest by population being Russia, which spans both Europe and Asia.

Geography and climate

Europe's coastline is highly indented, with major peninsulas including Scandinavia, Iberia, Italy and the Balkans, and large islands such as Great Britain, Ireland and Iceland. The continent's relief ranges from the high Alps, Pyrenees and Caucasus (Mount Elbrus, at 5,642 m in Russia, is the highest peak) to the low-lying Great European Plain stretching from France to the Urals. Major rivers include the Volga (the longest in Europe), the Danube, the Rhine and the Dnieper. Much of western and northern Europe has a temperate maritime climate moderated by the North Atlantic Drift, the Mediterranean south has hot dry summers, and the interior east has a continental climate with cold winters.

History

Europe was the home of classical Greek and Roman civilisation, foundational to Western culture in government, philosophy, law, science and art. After the fall of the Western Roman Empire, medieval Europe saw the rise of feudal kingdoms, the Catholic Church's dominance, and later the Byzantine Empire in the east and Islamic states in Iberia and the Balkans. The Renaissance, beginning in Italy in the fourteenth century, revived classical learning and art; the ensuing Age of Discovery saw European powers build global colonial empires. The Reformation divided Western Christianity, the Enlightenment transformed political thought, and the Industrial Revolution, beginning in Britain in the late eighteenth century, reshaped the world economy.

The twentieth century was dominated by the two World Wars, which began in Europe and devastated the continent, and by the Cold War division between the capitalist West and communist East. Since 1989–91 that division has ended; the European Union, founded by treaty in 1993 from earlier communities dating to 1951, now includes 27 member states and much of the continent's population and economy, with a single market and, for 20 members, the euro currency.

Economy and demographics

Europe is one of the world's wealthiest regions, with highly developed economies in services, finance, manufacturing and technology. Its population is ageing and roughly stable; population growth in many countries depends on immigration. The most widely spoken native languages are Russian, German, French, English, Italian, Spanish, Polish and Ukrainian; most European languages belong to the Indo-European family. Historically predominantly Christian, Europe is now religiously diverse and among the most secular regions of the world.
