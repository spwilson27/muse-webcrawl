Stock split

A stock split is a corporate action in which a company increases the number of its outstanding shares by issuing more shares to existing shareholders in proportion to their holdings, while proportionally reducing the price per share. The company's total market capitalization is unchanged immediately after the split.

Mechanics

In a 2-for-1 split, each share becomes two shares worth half as much each; a shareholder with 100 shares at 0 ends with 200 shares at 0. Splits are expressed as ratios (2:1, 3:1, 10:1) and effected via a stock dividend. A reverse split does the opposite, consolidating shares — often to raise a low share price above exchange listing minimums.

Rationale

Companies split stock to lower the nominal price per share, potentially improving liquidity and accessibility for small investors and employees, and sometimes as a signal of confidence. Notable examples include Apple's 4-for-1 split in 2020 and Nvidia's 10-for-1 split in 2024.
