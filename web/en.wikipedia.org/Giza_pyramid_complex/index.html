Giza pyramid complex

The Giza pyramid complex, also called the Giza necropolis, is an archaeological site on the Giza Plateau in Egypt, on the outskirts of modern Cairo. Built during the Fourth Dynasty of the Old Kingdom (c. 2600–2500 BC), the complex includes the three great pyramids — of Khufu, Khafre, and Menkaure — the Great Sphinx, several cemeteries, a workers' village, and an industrial complex. It is part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Memphis and its Necropolis".

The pyramids

The Great Pyramid of Khufu (Cheops), originally 146.6 metres tall, is the largest and oldest of the three and the only one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World still in existence. The pyramid of Khafre appears taller because it stands on higher bedrock and retains part of its smooth limestone casing at the apex. The pyramid of Menkaure is the smallest, at about 65 metres. Each pyramid was originally encased in polished white Tura limestone and formed the centre of a mortuary complex with temples, causeways, and subsidiary pyramids.

The Great Sphinx

The Great Sphinx is a limestone statue of a reclining lion with a human head, generally believed to represent Pharaoh Khafre. At 73 metres long and 20 metres high, it is the largest monolith statue in the world. Its nose was already missing in antiquity.

Construction

The pyramids were built by skilled Egyptian labourers — recent research indicates paid, rotating workforces housed in a purpose-built town, rather than slaves. Stones were quarried locally, with finer limestone from Tura and granite from Aswan transported by boat on the Nile. How the massive blocks were raised remains debated, with ramps of various kinds the leading hypotheses.
