McGill University

McGill University is a public research university in Montreal, Quebec, Canada. Founded in 1821 by royal charter from a bequest by fur trader James McGill, it is one of Canada's oldest and most prestigious universities, with instruction in English.

Campus and academics

The main downtown campus sits at the foot of Mount Royal; a second campus in Sainte-Anne-de-Bellevue hosts agriculture and environment. McGill has 11 faculties and about 40,000 students, with an unusually international student body. Its medical school is among Canada's top-ranked; Ernest Rutherford did Nobel-winning work at McGill, and affiliates include 12 Nobel laureates and 149 Rhodes Scholars.

History

Classes began in 1829. The university admitted women from 1884 through the Royal Victoria College. McGill played a major role in the origins of ice hockey, football, and basketball — James Naismith was a McGill graduate.

Identity

Teams are the Redbirds and Martlets; the motto is Grandescunt Aucta Labore ("By work, all things increase and grow").
