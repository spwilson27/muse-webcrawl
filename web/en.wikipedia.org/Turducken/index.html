Turducken

CourseMain dish
Place of originUnited States (Louisiana)
Main ingredientsTurkey, duck, chicken, stuffing

A turducken is a dish consisting of a deboned chicken stuffed into a deboned duck, which is in turn stuffed into a deboned turkey, with layers of stuffing between the birds. The name is a portmanteau of turkey, duck and chicken. It is associated with Cajun cuisine in Louisiana and with festive meals such as Thanksgiving in the United States.

Origins

Multi-bird roasts have a long history: Roman and medieval cooks produced elaborate nested roasts, and the early 19th-century French gastronomer Grimod de La Reynière described his rôti sans pareil of seventeen birds. The modern turducken is often credited to Louisiana chef Paul Prudhomme, though the Hebert's Specialty Meats shop in Maurice, Louisiana also claims to have introduced it in 1985.

Preparation

Each bird is fully deboned except for the turkey's wing and leg bones. The cavities and layers are filled with stuffing — commonly cornbread or sausage — seasoned in Cajun style, and the roast is cooked slowly for many hours until all layers reach a safe temperature.

Popular culture

American football broadcaster John Madden popularised the dish during NFL Thanksgiving broadcasts, presenting a turducken to the winning team and helping make it a national novelty.
