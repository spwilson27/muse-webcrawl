id Software LLC is an American video game developer based in Richardson, Texas. It was founded on February 1, 1991, by John Carmack, John Romero, Tom Hall, and Adrian Carmack (no relation to John). The company is considered a pioneer of the first-person shooter genre and of 3D graphics technology.

History

The founders met while working at Softdisk in Shreveport, Louisiana, where John Carmack developed a technique for smooth side-scrolling on the PC, used in the Commander Keen series published by Apogee. After forming id (short for "Ideas from the Deep"), the studio produced Wolfenstein 3D (1992), which popularised the first-person shooter, and the landmark Doom (1993), distributed as shareware and credited with helping to bring networked multiplayer deathmatch and user-created modifications into the mainstream.

Quake (1996) introduced fully 3D polygonal graphics and online play. Creative disputes led to John Romero's departure in 1996, and the studio continued with Quake II and Quake III Arena. ZeniMax Media acquired id Software in 2009; John Carmack, the last co-founder still at the company, resigned in 2013. Under ZeniMax and later Microsoft (which acquired ZeniMax in 2021), id released the reboots Doom (2016) and Doom Eternal (2020), and Doom: The Dark Ages (2025).

Technology

Id's game engines, from the Doom engine through the successive id Tech series, were widely licensed and influential. The company historically released its older engines under the GNU General Public License, a practice that sustained large open-source communities.
