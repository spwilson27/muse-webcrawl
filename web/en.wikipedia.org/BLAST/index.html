BLAST

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

BLAST (Basic Local Alignment Search Tool) is an algorithm and program for comparing biological sequence information, such as the amino acid sequences of proteins or the nucleotide sequences of DNA and RNA. Introduced in 1990 by Stephen Altschul and colleagues at the U.S. National Institutes of Health, BLAST finds regions of local similarity between a query sequence and sequences in a database, allowing researchers to infer functional and evolutionary relationships. A BLAST search is one of the most common operations in bioinformatics.

How it works

BLAST uses a heuristic: it breaks the query into short "words," finds database sequences containing matching or near-matching words scored by a substitution matrix (such as BLOSUM62), and extends those seed matches in both directions, keeping alignments whose scores exceed a threshold. Results are ranked using expect values (E-values) derived from Karlin–Altschul statistics, estimating how many alignments of that score would arise by chance in a database of that size.

Variants

The BLAST family includes blastp (protein–protein), blastn (nucleotide–nucleotide), blastx (translated nucleotide against protein), tblastn and tblastx. Later versions such as BLAST+ and faster tools (e.g., DIAMOND for very large protein searches) extend its capabilities. Public web services at the NCBI and other institutes make BLAST available to any researcher with a browser.

Significance

The original BLAST paper is among the most cited in all of science, and the tool has been fundamental to genomics, from annotating new genomes to identifying genes responsible for disease.
