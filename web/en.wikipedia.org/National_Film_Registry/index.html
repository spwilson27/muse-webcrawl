The National Film Registry (NFR) is the United States National Film Preservation Board's selected collection of films, chosen for preservation in the Library of Congress. Established by the National Film Preservation Act of 1988, the registry annually selects 25 films deemed "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant".

Selection

Films become eligible ten years after their original release. The Librarian of Congress makes the final selections after reviewing nominations from the public and recommendations of the National Film Preservation Board and Library staff. The registry encompasses Hollywood classics, documentaries, silent films, avant-garde works, home movies, and newsreels — from The Great Train Robbery (1903) to recent features.

Purpose

Selection signals a film's importance and supports its preservation, though the Library does not hold archival copies of every title; the registry encourages studios, archives, and rights holders to preserve the works. The program grew out of concern in the 1980s over colourisation of black-and-white classics and the deterioration of nitrate film stock.

Notable inclusions

Early selections included Citizen Kane, Casablanca, Star Wars, and Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs. Later additions have broadened to include works such as Do the Right Thing, the Zapruder film, and music and industrial films.
