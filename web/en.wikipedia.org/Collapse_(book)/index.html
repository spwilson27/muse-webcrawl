Collapse (book)

Collapse: How Societies Choose to Fail or Succeed is a 2005 book by the American academic Jared Diamond, author of the Pulitzer Prize-winning Guns, Germs, and Steel (1997). The book examines the collapse of past societies — including those of Easter Island, the Maya, the Anasazi, and the Norse settlements of Greenland — and asks what lessons they hold for the modern world, while also discussing societies that solved severe environmental problems.

Framework

Diamond proposes a five-point framework of factors that may contribute to societal collapse: environmental damage, climate change, hostile neighbours, loss of essential trading partners, and — the factor he considers most important — the society's own response to its environmental problems. Case studies of failure are contrasted with successes such as Tikopia, Iceland, Tokugawa-era Japan's reforestation, and the Dominican Republic's environmental policies under Balaguer.

Reception

Collapse was a commercial success and was shortlisted for the Royal Society's science book prize. It was widely reviewed and praised for its scope and readability, but some archaeologists and anthropologists criticized particular case studies — especially the Easter Island narrative — arguing that Diamond overstated ecological self-destruction relative to disease, slaving raids, and colonial contact, and that he drew too direct a line between past collapses and present policy choices.
