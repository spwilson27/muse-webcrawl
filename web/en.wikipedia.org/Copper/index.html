Copper is a chemical element with the symbol Cu and atomic number 29. It is a soft, malleable, and ductile metal with very high thermal and electrical conductivity. A freshly exposed surface of pure copper has a pinkish-orange colour. Its electrical conductivity is second only to silver among pure metals, making it indispensable in electrical wiring and electronics.

History

Copper was one of the first metals used by humans; copper beads found in Iraq date to about 9000 BC, and it was smelted from ores by around 5000 BC. Alloying copper with tin produced bronze, giving the Bronze Age its name, while alloying with zinc produces brass. The Romans mined copper extensively on Cyprus, hence the Latin name cuprum. Copper compounds have long been used as pigments, and copper surfaces have recognised antimicrobial properties.

Production

The largest copper mines are in Chile (the world's leading producer), Peru, China, and the United States. Most copper comes from sulfide ores such as chalcopyrite, extracted by mining, froth flotation, smelting, and electrorefining to produce high-purity cathode copper. Copper is highly recyclable without loss of quality, and a large fraction of all copper ever mined remains in use.

Applications

Copper is used principally as a conductor of heat and electricity — in power generation and transmission, building wiring, telecommunications, and electronics. Other uses include plumbing, roofing and cladding (which develop a green patina of basic copper carbonate), coinage, industrial machinery, and alloys such as bronze, brass, and cupronickel. Demand has grown with renewable energy systems and electric vehicles, which use several times more copper than conventional cars.

Biological role

Copper is an essential trace element in plants and animals, occurring in enzymes such as cytochrome c oxidase and superoxide dismutase. In humans both deficiency and excess are harmful; the genetic disorder Wilson's disease causes copper accumulation in tissues. Many molluscs and arthropods use the copper-containing protein haemocyanin to transport oxygen.
