Mods and rockers

Mods and rockers were two conflicting British youth subcultures of the early to mid-1960s. The mods were known for tailored suits, Italian motor scooters such as Vespas and Lambrettas, and modern jazz, R&B, and soul music. The rockers, descended from the ton-up boys, wore leather jackets, rode heavy motorcycles, and listened to 1950s rock and roll.

Clashes

Rivalry between the groups erupted in widely publicized clashes at seaside resorts during bank holiday weekends in 1964, most famously at Clacton, Brighton, Bournemouth, and Margate. Although actual violence was limited, intensive press coverage provoked a "moral panic", a term analyzed by sociologist Stanley Cohen in his 1972 book Folk Devils and Moral Panics.

Legacy

The mod style influenced fashion and music for decades, feeding into the mod revival of the late 1970s and Britpop; the 1979 film Quadrophenia, based on The Who's album, dramatized the conflict. Rockers evolved into the biker subculture.
