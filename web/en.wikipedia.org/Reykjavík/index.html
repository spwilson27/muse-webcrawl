Reykjavík

Reykjavík is the capital and largest city of Iceland, and the world's northernmost capital of a sovereign state. With a population of roughly 140,000 in the city and about 250,000 in the Capital Region, it is home to well over half of Iceland's population, and is the centre of the country's government, economy, and culture.

History

According to the Landnámabók, Reykjavík was founded in about 874 by the Norse settler Ingólfr Arnarson, who threw his high-seat pillars overboard and settled where they washed ashore; the name means "smoky bay", after the steam of nearby hot springs. For centuries a small farmstead, it grew after 1786, when it became a trading town, and expanded rapidly in the 20th century.

Geography and culture

The city sits on the Seltjarnarnes peninsula in Faxaflói bay, beneath Mount Esja. Most of its heating and electricity comes from geothermal and hydroelectric power. Landmarks include Hallgrímskirkja church, the Harpa concert hall, and the Sun Voyager sculpture. Reykjavík is also known for the 1986 Reagan–Gorbachev summit and a lively music scene.
