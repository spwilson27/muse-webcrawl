Middle East

The Middle East is a geopolitical region encompassing the Arabian Peninsula, the Levant, Turkey, Egypt, Iran and Iraq, with definitions varying by source. Centered on Western Asia and often including Egypt in North Africa, it is home to several of the world's earliest civilizations and the birthplace of Judaism, Christianity and Islam.

Terminology and definitions

The term "Middle East" originated in British usage in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, describing the area between the "Near East" and India. Common definitions include Bahrain, Cyprus, Egypt, Iran, Iraq, Israel, Jordan, Kuwait, Lebanon, Oman, Palestine, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, Syria, Turkey, the United Arab Emirates and Yemen. Broader concepts such as the "Greater Middle East" extend to North Africa and Central Asia.

History

The region includes Mesopotamia and ancient Egypt, cradles of writing, cities and law; the Persian empires; the spread of Islam and the Arab caliphates from the seventh century; the Crusades; and centuries of Ottoman rule. After the First World War, Britain and France partitioned much of the Ottoman Arab world under the mandate system, drawing borders that shaped modern states. The discovery of vast oil reserves in the twentieth century made the region central to global energy politics, and the establishment of Israel in 1948 and the ensuing Arab–Israeli conflict have been defining features of its modern politics.

Geography and economy

Much of the region is arid, with major rivers — the Nile, Tigris and Euphrates — sustaining agriculture since antiquity. The Persian Gulf states hold a large share of the world's proven petroleum and natural gas reserves, while cities such as Dubai have diversified into finance, trade and tourism. The region's population exceeds 400 million, predominantly Muslim, with significant Christian and other minorities.
