Four-day workweek

The four-day workweek is an arrangement of the workweek in which an employee works four days rather than five, either with reduced total hours at the same salary or with the standard number of hours compressed into longer days. It is one of the most prominent flexible-work proposals of the 21st century and has been tested by governments and companies on several continents.

Models

The best-known model is "100-80-100": employees receive 100 percent of their salary for 80 percent of the time, committing to deliver 100 percent of their previous output, typically by cutting meetings, streamlining processes, and protecting focused work time. Alternative models include the compressed 4/10 schedule (four ten-hour days), half-Friday or staggered-day arrangements, and seasonal four-day weeks. Non-profit 4 Day Week Global, founded by Andrew Barnes and Charlotte Lockhart after Barnes's 2018 experiment at New Zealand's Perpetual Guardian, has coordinated multi-company pilot programmes with academic research partners.

Findings from pilots

In the 2022 United Kingdom pilot — the largest to date — 61 companies trialled the schedule for six months; revenue rose slightly on average, burnout and sick days fell sharply, and 54 of the companies continued the policy after the trial ended, with 31 making it permanent. Earlier Icelandic public-sector trials (2015–2019) covered about 1% of the country's workforce and were judged an "overwhelming success," preceding nationwide agreements shortening hours. Trials in the United States, Ireland, and Australia reported large improvements in self-rated well-being and no loss of revenue. Studies also report environmental benefits from reduced commuting, with one UK estimate suggesting a four-day week could cut the country's carbon footprint significantly by 2025-era targets.

Adoption and obstacles

Some employers, including software and professional-services firms, have adopted the schedule permanently as a recruitment tool, while others reverted after trials. Sectors requiring continuous staffing face scheduling costs, and implementation research stresses that the productivity gains come from reorganising work, not from exhorting staff to work faster. Legislative proposals for a four-day standard week have been introduced in several jurisdictions, including the United States and Spain, without yet becoming general law.
