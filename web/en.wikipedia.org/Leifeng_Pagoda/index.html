Leifeng Pagoda

Leifeng Pagoda (Chinese: 雷峰塔; pinyin: Léifēng Tǎ) is a five-storey pagoda located on Sunset Hill south of West Lake in Hangzhou, Zhejiang, China. Originally constructed in 975 CE, the pagoda collapsed in 1924 and was rebuilt in its modern form in 2002.

History

The original pagoda was built in 975 CE, during the Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms period, by Qian Hongchu, king of Wuyue, reportedly to celebrate the pregnancy of his favourite concubine, Huang Fei. The brick-and-wood structure was damaged during the Ming dynasty, when Japanese pirates (wokou) set fire to its wooden eaves, leaving the bare brick core. During the Qing dynasty it remained a celebrated sight — "Leifeng Pagoda in Evening Glow" was counted among the Ten Scenes of West Lake — but local superstition that its bricks warded off illness led residents to steal bricks from its base. The weakened tower finally collapsed on 25 September 1924, an event famously commented on by the writer Lu Xun.

Legend

The pagoda is inseparable from the Legend of the White Snake, one of China's great folk tales. In the story, the white snake spirit Bai Suzhen falls in love with the scholar Xu Xian; the monk Fahai, disapproving of the union, imprisons Bai Suzhen beneath Leifeng Pagoda. Her eventual release — in later versions, through the devotion of her son — made the pagoda a symbol of love's triumph over repression.

Reconstruction

In 2002 a new pagoda, built of steel and copper over the protected ruins of the original foundation, opened to the public. Excavation of the old foundations in 2001 uncovered a gilded miniature pagoda — the Ashoka-style reliquary associated with Qian Hongchu — along with coins, mirrors and Buddhist sutras. Escalators and lifts inside the modern tower carry visitors to viewing platforms overlooking West Lake.
