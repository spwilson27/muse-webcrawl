Dingwall

Dingwall (Scots Gaelic: Inbhir Pheofharain) is a town and former royal burgh in the Highland council area of Scotland. It lies near the head of the Cromarty Firth, about 14 miles (23 km) northwest of Inverness, and has a population of roughly 5,500.

History

The name Dingwall derives from the Old Norse Þingvöllr, "assembly field", recalling a Norse parliament or thing that met here when the area was under Scandinavian influence. The town became a royal burgh in 1226 under Alexander II. Dingwall Castle, once a stronghold of the Earls of Ross, stood near the shore; little remains today. The town grew as a market and administrative centre for Ross-shire and was historically the county town of Ross and Cromarty.

Transport and sport

Dingwall railway station stands on the Kyle of Lochalsh Line and the Far North Line, with the junction between the two just west of the town. The town is home to Ross County F.C., based at Victoria Park (the Global Energy Stadium), who play in the Scottish league system and won the Scottish League Cup in 2016.
