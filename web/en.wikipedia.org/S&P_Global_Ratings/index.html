S&P Global Ratings, formerly Standard & Poor's, is an American credit rating agency and a division of S&P Global. It issues credit ratings for the debt of public and private companies and other public borrowers such as governments and governmental entities. It is one of the "Big Three" credit rating agencies, alongside Moody's and Fitch.

Ratings

The agency assigns letter-grade ratings expressing its opinion of a borrower's creditworthiness. Long-term ratings range from AAA (highest quality) through investment-grade categories (BBB and above) to speculative grades, down to D for default. Sovereign ratings, such as the United States' AA+ rating assigned after the 2011 downgrade from AAA, are closely watched by markets.

History

The business traces its origins to the Poor's Publishing company, founded by Henry Varnum Poor, whose financial manuals began in the 1860s. Standard Statistics and Poor's merged in 1941 to form Standard & Poor's, which was acquired by McGraw Hill in 1966. The ratings business was rebranded as S&P Global Ratings in 2016.

Criticism

S&P and its peers were heavily criticised for assigning high ratings to mortgage-related securities before the 2008 financial crisis. In 2015 S&P agreed to pay about $1.5 billion to settle United States government claims over its pre-crisis ratings.
