Metropolitan archbishop

A metropolitan archbishop, or simply metropolitan, is an archbishop who presides over an ecclesiastical province in the Catholic Church and in some other Christian traditions. The metropolitan's own diocese is the metropolitan archdiocese, and the other dioceses in the province are its suffragan sees.

Role

In the Latin Church, the metropolitan has limited authority over the suffragan dioceses of the province: he convokes provincial councils, ensures the faith is observed, may conduct visitations where a suffragan bishop has neglected them, and receives the pallium from the pope as a sign of communion. Appeals from suffragan tribunals may be heard by the metropolitan's court. In Eastern Orthodoxy and Oriental Orthodoxy, metropolitan bishops likewise head provinces or important autocephalous and autonomous churches.

History

The office arose in late antiquity, when the bishop of a provincial capital (metropolis) exercised oversight of neighbouring bishops, a structure confirmed by the Council of Nicaea in 325.
