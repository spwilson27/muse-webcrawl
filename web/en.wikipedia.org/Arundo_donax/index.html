Arundo donax

Arundo donax is a tall perennial cane. It is one of several so-called reed species. Its common names include giant cane, elephant grass, carrizo, arundo, Spanish cane, Colorado river reed, and wild cane. It is native to the Mediterranean Basin and the Middle East to India, and is now widely naturalised and invasive in many mild temperate, subtropical and tropical regions.

Description

Arundo donax generally grows to 6 metres (20 ft) in height, or in ideal conditions can exceed 10 metres, forming dense stands from thick rhizomes. The hollow stems are 2 to 3 centimetres in diameter, with alternate grey-green sword-like leaves 30 to 60 centimetres long. It flowers in late summer with a silky cream plume 40 to 60 centimetres long.

Ecology and invasiveness

The species is among the fastest-growing terrestrial plants, able to grow several centimetres per day. In riparian zones of California and elsewhere it displaces native vegetation, consumes large quantities of water, and increases fire risk, and it is consequently listed among the world's worst invasive alien species.

Uses

Giant reed has been cultivated for thousands of years for thatching, fencing, stakes, and the manufacture of flutes and other woodwind instruments; reeds for clarinets, saxophones, oboes and bassoons are still made from its cane. It has also been investigated as a bioenergy crop.
