Convenience food

Convenience food, also called tertiary processed food, is food that is commercially prepared—often through industrial processing—to optimise ease of consumption. Such food is usually ready to eat without further preparation, though it may require minimal heating.

History

Convenience foods developed alongside canning in the 19th century and expanded rapidly after the Second World War with frozen dinners, instant noodles (invented by Momofuku Ando in 1958), processed cheese, and prepared sauces. The rise of supermarkets, refrigeration, and microwave ovens made them a staple of modern diets.

Criticism

Critics associate heavy consumption of convenience foods with high levels of salt, sugar, fat, and additives, and with health problems such as obesity and cardiovascular disease. The category overlaps with what nutrition researchers classify as ultra-processed food. Manufacturers have responded with reformulated products marketed on health and sustainability grounds.
