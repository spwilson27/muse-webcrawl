The Southern African Development Community (SADC) is an intergovernmental organisation of sixteen member states in Southern Africa, headquartered in Gaborone, Botswana. Its goals include promoting economic development, peace and security, and regional integration among its members.

History

SADC grew out of the Southern African Development Coordination Conference (SADCC), founded in Lusaka in 1980 by the "Frontline States" and their partners to reduce economic dependence on apartheid-era South Africa. In 1992 the organisation was reconstituted as SADC by treaty signed at Windhoek, Namibia, shifting its focus from coordination to deeper integration; South Africa joined in 1994 after the end of apartheid.

Members and integration

Members include Angola, Botswana, Comoros, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Eswatini, Lesotho, Madagascar, Malawi, Mauritius, Mozambique, Namibia, Seychelles, South Africa, Tanzania, Zambia and Zimbabwe. SADC launched a free trade area in 2008 and maintains organs on politics, defence and security; its military standby force has deployed in operations including in Mozambique and the DRC. The SADC Parliamentary Forum and the SADC Tribunal (suspended in 2010) form part of its institutional framework.
