The Republican National Committee (RNC) is the primary committee of the United States Republican Party. It is responsible for developing and promoting the Republican brand and political platform, coordinating fundraising and voter mobilisation, and organising the Republican National Convention, at which the party's presidential nominee is formally selected.

Structure

The RNC consists of 168 members: the party chairperson of each state and territorial party organisation and two committee members (one man and one woman) from each state and territory. The committee elects a national chair, who serves as the party's chief executive and public spokesperson. When a Republican occupies the White House, the president effectively selects the chair; in opposition, the chair plays a larger independent role. The RNC works alongside the National Republican Senatorial Committee and the National Republican Congressional Committee, which focus on Senate and House races.

Functions

Between conventions, the RNC raises funds, maintains voter databases and data infrastructure, recruits and trains candidates and campaign staff, conducts opposition research and communications, and coordinates get-out-the-vote operations with state parties. Every four years it sets the rules for the presidential primary process, allocates delegates, and stages the national convention.

History

The Republican Party was founded in 1854, and its first national committee was established at the party's first national convention in 1856, which nominated John C. Frémont. Over the following century and a half, the RNC has managed the party through eras of dominance and minority status alike, evolving from a convention-organising body into a permanent, professional campaign organisation. Notable chairs include Mark Hanna, who modernised party fundraising in 1896, and Haley Barbour and Reince Priebus in the modern era; following the 2012 election, the RNC's "Growth and Opportunity Project" (the "autopsy report") recommended outreach and reform measures that shaped subsequent debate within the party.
