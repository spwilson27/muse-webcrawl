Tórshavn

Tórshavn
Country: Faroe Islands (Denmark)
Island: Streymoy
Population: about 14,000 (town); ~22,000 (municipality)
Meaning of name: "Thor's harbour"

Tórshavn is the capital and largest town of the Faroe Islands, a self-governing territory within the Kingdom of Denmark. It is situated on the south-east coast of Streymoy, the archipelago's largest island, and takes its name from Thor, the Norse god of thunder: "Tórshavn" means "Thor's harbour".

The town grew around the Viking-age assembly site on the Tinganes peninsula, where Norse settlers established one of the world's oldest parliaments around the ninth century. The old town, Reyn, preserves turf-roofed wooden houses painted in the traditional red of the prime minister's office buildings that still occupy Tinganes.

History

Tórshavn developed from a trading post under Norway's Bergen merchants and the Danish crown's trade monopoly, which lasted until 1856. It was granted town rights in 1866 and became the Faroese capital as the islands gained home rule in 1948. During the Second World War it was the headquarters of the British occupation of the Faroes.

Culture and transport

The town hosts the Nordic House cultural centre, the University of the Faroe Islands, and ferry connections to Denmark and Iceland via the Smyril Line terminal. Its football stadium, Tórsvøllur, hosts the national team.
