The Huanggutun incident (Japanese: Kōgoton jiken) was the assassination of the Fengtian clique warlord Zhang Zuolin on 4 June 1928, when a bomb planted by officers of the Japanese Kwantung Army exploded under his personal train near Huanggutun railway station on the outskirts of Shenyang, Manchuria.

Background

Zhang Zuolin, ruler of Manchuria, had been backed by Japan but increasingly acted independently, and his forces had suffered defeats at the hands of the National Revolutionary Army during the Northern Expedition. Radical officers of the Kwantung Army, notably Colonel Daisaku Kōmoto, hoped that killing Zhang would create chaos allowing Japan to seize Manchuria outright.

The assassination

In the early hours of 4 June 1928, explosives placed on a bridge where the South Manchuria Railway crossed the Peking–Mukden line detonated as Zhang's train passed underneath. Zhang was mortally wounded and died several hours later. His death was concealed for over two weeks while his son, Zhang Xueliang, was installed as his successor.

Aftermath

The plot failed in its immediate aim: the Japanese government of Prime Minister Tanaka Giichi did not exploit the incident, and the Kwantung Army did not move. Zhang Xueliang, contrary to Japanese expectations, declared his allegiance to the Nationalist government in Nanjing in December 1928, nominally reunifying China. The failure of the plot and the army's frustration with Tokyo's restraint contributed to the more elaborate Mukden Incident of 1931.
