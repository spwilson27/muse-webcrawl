Uludağ (pronounced [uˈɫudaː]), the ancient Mysian Olympus, is a mountain in Bursa Province, Turkey, with an altitude of 2,543 metres (8,343 ft). It is the highest mountain of the Marmara region and one of Turkey's most popular winter sports and skiing centres.

Geography

Uludağ rises immediately south of the city of Bursa. Its northern slopes are gentle and plateau-like, while the southern face is steep. Meltwater from the mountain feeds streams and the famous thermal springs of Bursa. Much of the mountain is protected as Uludağ National Park, established in 1961, which preserves forests of fir, beech and juniper as well as alpine meadows.

History

In antiquity the mountain was known as Olympus of Mysia or Bithynia — one of several mountains in the Greek world named Olympus. In the Byzantine era it became a centre of monastic life, earning it the Turkish name Keşiş Dağı, "Mountain of the Monks", before it was renamed Uludağ ("Great Mountain") in 1925 on the suggestion of the geographer Osman Şevki Bey.

Tourism

Uludağ is Turkey's oldest and busiest ski resort, reached from Bursa by road and by one of the world's longest gondola lifts, the Bursa Uludağ Gondola. In summer the national park is popular for camping, trekking and picnics, and the mountain gives its name to a well-known Turkish soft drink, Uludağ Gazoz, and to the spring water bottled from its slopes.
