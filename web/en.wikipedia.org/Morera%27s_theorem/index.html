Morera's theorem

In complex analysis, a branch of mathematics, Morera's theorem, named after Giacinto Morera, gives an important criterion for proving that a function is holomorphic. It states, roughly, that if a continuous complex-valued function defined on an open set in the complex plane satisfies that its integral around every closed curve vanishes, then the function must be holomorphic.

Statement

Let f be a continuous function on an open set D in the complex plane. If the contour integral of f over every closed rectifiable curve in D is zero, then f is holomorphic on D. Equivalently, it suffices to check triangles: if the integral over the boundary of every triangle contained in D vanishes, then f is holomorphic.

Proof idea

Morera's theorem is essentially a converse of Cauchy's integral theorem. One constructs an antiderivative by integrating f along paths from a fixed base point; the vanishing of integrals over triangles shows the antiderivative's complex derivative equals f, and since derivatives of holomorphic functions are holomorphic, f itself is holomorphic.

Applications

The theorem is used to prove that uniform limits of holomorphic functions are holomorphic, and to establish holomorphicity of functions defined by integrals and of the Riemann zeta function's analytic continuation.
