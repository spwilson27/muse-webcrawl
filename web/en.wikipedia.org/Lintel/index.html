Lintel

A lintel is a horizontal structural member that spans an opening — such as a doorway, window, or fireplace — and supports the weight of the structure above it. Lintels are the horizontal element of the post-and-lintel system, one of the oldest methods of construction, used in monuments such as Stonehenge and in Egyptian and Greek temples.

Materials and form

Lintels may be made of stone, wood, steel, or reinforced concrete. Because materials like stone are weak in tension, stone lintels can span only short distances; longer spans traditionally use arches instead. In modern construction, steel angles or prestressed concrete lintels are set into walls above openings, while decorative non-structural lintels may serve purely as an architectural feature.

Decorative lintels

In many traditions the lintel is elaborately decorated: carved stone lintels are famous in Maya architecture (such as those of Yaxchilán), in Khmer temples, and in medieval church doorways, where the carved panel above the door is often called a tympanum framed by the lintel below.
