Sir Thomas More (7 February 1478 – 6 July 1535) was an English lawyer, judge, social philosopher, author, statesman and Renaissance humanist. He served Henry VIII as Lord Chancellor from 1529 to 1532 and is best known for Utopia, which coined the term for an imagined ideal commonwealth.

Career

More rose through the law and Parliament, became a trusted counsellor of Henry VIII, and succeeded Cardinal Wolsey as Chancellor in 1529. A friend of Erasmus, he wrote humanist works, religious polemics against the Reformation, and the History of King Richard III.

Downfall and execution

More opposed the King's annulment of his marriage to Catherine of Aragon and his break with Rome. He resigned the chancellorship in 1532, and in 1534 refused to swear the Oath of Supremacy acknowledging Henry as head of the Church of England. Tried for treason on contested testimony, he was beheaded on Tower Hill on 6 July 1535, reportedly declaring himself "the King's good servant, and God's first".

Legacy

Canonised by the Catholic Church in 1935 and named patron saint of statesmen and politicians by Pope John Paul II in 2000, More is commemorated in the Church of England as a Reformation martyr. His life inspired Robert Bolt's play and film A Man for All Seasons.
