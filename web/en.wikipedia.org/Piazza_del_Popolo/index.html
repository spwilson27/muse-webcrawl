Piazza del Popolo

The Piazza del Popolo is a large urban square in Rome, Italy, at the northern gate of the Aurelian Walls, where the Via Flaminia enters the city. Its name derives from the church of Santa Maria del Popolo, which stands beside the Porta del Popolo.

Layout and history

The square took its present monumental neoclassical form under the architect Giuseppe Valadier between 1811 and 1822. At its centre stands an ancient Egyptian obelisk of Ramesses II, brought to Rome by Augustus and erected here by Pope Sixtus V in 1589.

Churches and features

On the south side stand the twin churches of Santa Maria dei Miracoli and Santa Maria in Montesanto, framing the view down the Via del Corso. Three streets — the Corso, Via del Babuino and Via di Ripetta — radiate from the square in the famous "trident" arrangement. Terraces link the piazza to the gardens of the Pincian Hill above.
