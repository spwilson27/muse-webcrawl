Buteyko method

The Buteyko method is a breathing retraining technique developed in the 1950s by the Soviet physician Konstantin Buteyko. It is principally promoted as a complementary treatment for asthma and other breathing disorders.

Principles

Buteyko theorised that chronic hidden hyperventilation lowers carbon dioxide levels in the blood, contributing to bronchospasm and other symptoms. The method teaches patients to breathe nasally, reduce breathing volume, and practise breath-holding exercises measured by a "control pause". Lifestyle advice on sleep posture, exercise, and diet accompanies the breathing techniques.

Evidence

Clinical trials have found that Buteyko training can reduce asthma symptoms and reliance on reliever medication, though evidence for objective improvement in lung function is lacking. Medical bodies generally regard it as a possible adjunct to, but not a replacement for, conventional asthma therapy. Similar breathing retraining approaches are used for dysfunctional breathing and hyperventilation syndrome.
