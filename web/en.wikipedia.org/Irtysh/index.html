Irtysh

The Irtysh (Russian: Иртыш; Kazakh: Ертіс, Ertis; Chinese: 额尔齐斯河, É'ěrqísī Hé) is the chief tributary of the Ob and the second-longest river in Asia. Flowing from its source in the Mongolian Altai in Xinjiang, China, it runs 4,248 kilometres (2,640 mi) through Kazakhstan and into Russia, where it joins the Ob near Khanty-Mansiysk.

Course and tributaries

In Kazakhstan the river passes through Lake Zaysan and the cities of Öskemen (Ust-Kamenogorsk), Semey, and Pavlodar; in Russia it flows through Omsk and Tobolsk. Major tributaries include the Ishim, the Tobol, and the Om. With the Ob, the Irtysh forms one of the largest drainage basins in the world, covering about 1.6 million km2.

Use

The river is dammed at several points for hydroelectric power and supplies water to canals, including China's Irtysh–Karamay–Ürümqi Canal. Navigation is possible over much of its length in the ice-free season, and it has long been a major transport artery across Siberia. Water withdrawals in China and Kazakhstan have raised environmental and diplomatic concerns downstream.
