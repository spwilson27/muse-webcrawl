Pascha

Pascha is the principal feast of the Eastern Orthodox Church and Eastern Catholic Churches, celebrating the resurrection of Jesus Christ. The word derives from the Aramaic and Hebrew Pesach, Passover, reflecting the feast's roots in the Jewish festival.

Date

The date of Pascha is computed by a lunisolar method: it falls on the first Sunday after the first full moon on or after the spring equinox, using the Julian calendar in the traditional computus, so it often falls later than Western Easter.

Celebration

Pascha is preceded by Great Lent and Holy Week. The Paschal Vigil begins late on Holy Saturday night with the Midnight Office, a candlelit procession, and the proclamation "Christ is risen!" (in Greek, Christos anesti). Festal foods such as pascha cheese and kulich are blessed and eaten to break the fast.

Greetings

The Paschal greeting—"Christ is risen!" answered by "Truly he is risen!"—is exchanged in many languages throughout the Paschal season, which extends to the Ascension.
