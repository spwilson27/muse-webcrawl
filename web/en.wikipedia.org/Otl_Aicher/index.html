Died1 September 1991, Günzburg, Germany

Otl Aicher (13 May 1922 – 1 September 1991) was a German graphic designer and typographer, one of the leading figures of post-war German design. He is best known for the pictogram system he developed for the 1972 Munich Olympic Games and for the corporate identity of Lufthansa.

Life and work

Born in Ulm, Aicher was a friend of Hans and Sophie Scholl of the White Rose resistance group and was himself imprisoned briefly for refusing to join the Hitler Youth organization (he had declined membership); he was drafted into the Wehrmacht and deserted near the war's end. In 1953 he co-founded the Ulm School of Design (Hochschule für Gestaltung Ulm) with Inge Scholl and Max Bill, a school that carried Bauhaus thinking into the postwar era. He married Inge Scholl in 1952.

Aicher's design studio produced the influential visual identity for Lufthansa (1962 onward) and, as design commissioner for the 1972 Munich Olympics, the famous set of geometric sports pictograms — based on stick figures rendered in a strict grid — which became the template for public information symbols worldwide. His typefaces include Rotis (1988), named after his home village of Rotis, and Traffic. His writings include The World as Design and analogous and digital. He died in 1991 after being struck by a car while mowing.
