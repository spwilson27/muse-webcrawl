Information privacy

Information privacy, or data privacy, is the relationship between the collection and dissemination of data, technology, the public expectation of privacy, and the legal and political issues surrounding them. It concerns the rights of individuals to control how their personal information is collected, used, stored and shared, and the obligations of organizations that process such data.

Information privacy governs how personal data may be collected, used and shared.

Principles

Modern data protection frameworks build on principles such as lawfulness and transparency, purpose limitation, data minimization, accuracy, storage limitation, security, and accountability, first articulated in the OECD Privacy Guidelines (1980) and the earlier Fair Information Practice Principles developed in the United States in the 1970s.

Legal frameworks

The European Union's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR, applicable from 2018) is the most influential comprehensive law, granting individuals rights of access, rectification, erasure and portability, and imposing heavy fines for violations. Other laws include the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA/CPRA), Brazil's LGPD, and sectoral regimes such as HIPAA for health data in the US. Data breaches, surveillance by states and companies, and the data practices of large technology platforms are continuing public concerns.

Techniques

Privacy can be supported technically by encryption, anonymization and pseudonymization, differential privacy, privacy-enhancing technologies, and "privacy by design" approaches, though perfectly anonymizing rich datasets has proven difficult in practice.
