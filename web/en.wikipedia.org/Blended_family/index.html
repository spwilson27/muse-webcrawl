Blended family

A blended family formed when two partners with children marry.

A blended family, also called a stepfamily, is a family in which at least one parent has children who are not genetically related to the other parent or spouse. Either or both parents may remarry or repartner after the death of a spouse, divorce, or separation, bringing together children from previous relationships; the couple may also have children together.

Terminology and structure

Family sociologists distinguish simple stepfamilies, in which only one partner brings children, from complex or blended families in which both do. Children may live with the household full-time or divide time between two households. Relationships include stepparents, step-siblings, and half-siblings. The term "blended family" is often preferred for its positive connotation, though some family therapists caution that it can suggest an ease of integration that new families may not experience.

Prevalence and adjustment

Blended families are common in many Western countries as a result of high rates of divorce and remarriage; a substantial share of children will spend part of their childhood in a stepfamily household. Research indicates that adjustment commonly takes several years and proceeds best with gradual stepparent involvement, consistent routines, and respect for children's bonds with both biological parents. Legal rights of stepparents — in custody, medical consent, and inheritance — vary widely by jurisdiction and are often more limited than those of biological or adoptive parents.

In popular culture, blended families have been depicted in series such as The Brady Bunch and Modern Family.
