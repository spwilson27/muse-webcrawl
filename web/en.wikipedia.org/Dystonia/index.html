Dystonia

Dystonia is a neurological movement disorder characterized by sustained or intermittent muscle contractions that cause abnormal, often repetitive, movements and postures. These movements are typically patterning, twisting and may be tremulous.

Classification

Dystonia may be focal (affecting one body part, such as in cervical dystonia or writer's cramp), segmental, multifocal or generalized. It can be inherited, acquired (from brain injury, stroke or drugs), or idiopathic. Childhood-onset generalized dystonia is often associated with the DYT1 (TOR1A) gene mutation.

Pathophysiology

The disorder is thought to involve dysfunction of the basal ganglia and related circuits, with impaired inhibition and abnormal sensorimotor plasticity.

Treatment

There is no cure, but symptoms can be managed with botulinum toxin injections for focal forms, oral medications such as anticholinergics, and deep brain stimulation of the globus pallidus for severe generalized dystonia.
