Academy Award for Best Cinematography

Presented byAcademy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences
First awarded1929 (for 1927/28 films)
Category ofAcademy Awards (Oscars)

The Academy Award for Best Cinematography is an Academy Award awarded each year to a cinematographer (director of photography) for work in one particular motion picture, recognising excellence in the camera and lighting craft that creates a film's visual style. It has been presented since the first Academy Awards ceremony in 1929.

History

At the first ceremony the award could honour a cinematographer's body of work for the year: Charles Rosher and Karl Struss won for Sunrise: A Song of Two Humans. From 1939 (the awards for 1940) until 1966, the Academy gave separate awards for black-and-white and colour cinematography, reflecting the industry's transition between the two formats; the categories were merged from 1967. The first winner in the colour era was Georges Périnal for The Thief of Bagdad. Emmanuel Lubezki won three consecutive awards (for Gravity, Birdman and The Revenant, 2013–2015), a record streak. Rachel Morrison became the first woman nominated in the category, for Mudbound (2017).

Selection

Nominees are chosen by members of the Academy's Cinematographers Branch, and the winner is voted on by the full Academy membership. Unlike some craft categories, the award recognises a single film rather than a year's overall output.
