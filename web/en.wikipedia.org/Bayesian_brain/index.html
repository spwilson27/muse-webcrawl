Bayesian brain

The Bayesian brain hypothesis proposes that the nervous system represents information in the form of probability distributions and updates those representations approximately according to Bayes' rule, combining prior beliefs with incoming sensory evidence.

Key ideas

Perception, on this view, is inference: the brain maintains a generative model of the causes of its sensations and infers the most probable causes. Related frameworks include predictive coding, in which higher levels of the cortical hierarchy predict lower-level activity and only prediction errors are passed upward, and the free-energy principle proposed by Karl Friston.

Evidence and criticism

Human behaviour in many perceptual and motor tasks approaches statistically optimal cue combination, and neural responses can often be fit by probabilistic models. Critics note that such models risk being unfalsifiable and that the brain's actual algorithms may only loosely approximate Bayesian inference.
