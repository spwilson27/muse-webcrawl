Skandha

The five aggregates (pañca-skandha) that constitute a sentient being

In Buddhism, the skandhas (Sanskrit: स्कन्ध, "heaps" or "aggregates") are the five aggregates of clinging that constitute a sentient being and give rise to the sense of a personal self. The doctrine holds that what is conventionally called a "person" is in fact a changing combination of these aggregates, none of which is permanent or constitutes a soul — a teaching closely connected with the concept of anattā (non-self).

The five aggregates

  • Form (rūpa) — the physical body and material phenomena.
  • Feeling (vedanā) — sensations experienced as pleasant, unpleasant or neutral.
  • Perception (saṃjñā) — recognition and identification of objects.
  • Mental formations (saṃskāra) — volitions, habits and composite mental states.
  • Consciousness (vijñāna) — awareness of an object through the senses or the mind.

Role in Buddhist thought

According to the early discourses, clinging to the aggregates as "I" or "mine" is a root cause of suffering (dukkha). Insight meditation analyzes experience into the aggregates to reveal their impermanence (anicca). The five-skandha scheme appears across Theravāda, Mahāyāna and Vajrayāna traditions, and the Heart Sutra famously proclaims that "form is emptiness, emptiness is form," in relation to all five.
