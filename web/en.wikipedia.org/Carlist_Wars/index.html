Carlist Wars

Dates1833–1876 (three wars)
LocationSpain
ResultLiberal/Isabelline victory; Carlism survives as a movement

The Carlist Wars (Spanish: Guerras Carlistas) were a series of civil wars that took place in Spain during the 19th century. The protagonists were factions over the succession to the throne and the nature of the Spanish monarchy: the Carlists, supporters of Infante Carlos of Spain and his descendants, defended a traditionalist, absolutist monarchy and regional fueros, while the Liberals supported Queen Isabella II and, later, constitutional government.

The three wars

The First Carlist War (1833–1840) followed Ferdinand VII's repeal of Salic law so his daughter Isabella could succeed; it ended with the Convention of Vergara. The Second Carlist War (1846–1849) was smaller and largely confined to Catalonia. The Third Carlist War (1872–1876) accompanied the upheavals of the Sexenio Democrático and ended after the restoration of Alfonso XII.

Carlism

Carlism persisted as a political movement with its own pretenders, motto ("Dios, Patria, Fueros, Rey"), and strongholds in Navarre and the Basque Country. Carlist requetés later fought on the Nationalist side in the Spanish Civil War of 1936–39.
