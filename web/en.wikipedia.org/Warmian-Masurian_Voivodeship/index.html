Warmian-Masurian Voivodeship

Warmian-Masurian Voivodeship (Polish: województwo warmińsko-mazurskie) is one of the 16 voivodeships (provinces) into which Poland is divided. It is located in northeastern Poland, bordering Russia's Kaliningrad Oblast to the north, the Podlaskie Voivodeship to the east, the Mazovian Voivodeship to the south, and the Pomeranian Voivodeship and the Vistula Lagoon to the west. Its capital and largest city is Olsztyn.

Geography

The province covers an area of about 24,192 km² (9,341 sq mi) and has a population of roughly 1.4 million. It takes its name from the historical regions of Warmia and Masuria. The landscape is famous for the Masurian Lake District, with over 2,000 lakes — the largest being Śniardwy and Mamry — connected by rivers and canals, making the region Poland's premier area for sailing and water tourism. Much of the land is forested or agricultural, and population density is among the lowest in Poland.

History

The territory formed the southern part of East Prussia before 1945 and was transferred to Poland after World War II, when its German population was expelled and the region was resettled with Poles, including many displaced from Poland's former eastern territories, and Ukrainians resettled under Operation Vistula. The modern voivodeship was created on 1 January 1999 from the former Olsztyn, Elbląg, and Suwałki voivodeships.
