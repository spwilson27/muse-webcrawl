A Clockwork Orange is a 1971 dystopian crime film adapted, produced, and directed by Stanley Kubrick, based on Anthony Burgess's 1962 novel of the same name. Set in a near-future Britain, it follows Alex DeLarge (Malcolm McDowell), a charismatic juvenile delinquent whose pleasures are classical music — especially Beethoven — rape, and "ultra-violence", and who, after his capture, undergoes an experimental aversion therapy, the Ludovico Technique, promoted by the government. The film employs disturbing, violent imagery to explore psychiatry, youth gangs, and the conflict between individual free will and state control.

Plot

Alex narrates his exploits with his "droogs" Pete, Georgie, and Dim, from the Korova Milk Bar to a night of assaults, a rival gang fight, and the invasion of a writer's home. After murdering a woman with a phallic sculpture, Alex is betrayed by his droogs and imprisoned. Two years into a 14-year sentence he volunteers for the Ludovico Technique, which conditions him to become violently ill at violence and at Beethoven's Ninth Symphony. Released and unable to defend himself, he encounters his former victims and droogs — now policemen — and attempts suicide. Recovering in hospital, he finds the conditioning gone ("I was cured, all right"), as the government, embarrassed, offers him a job.

Production and music

Kubrick filmed largely on locations around London with innovative techniques including handheld cameras, fast and slow motion, and wide-angle lenses. The score, arranged by Wendy Carlos, combines classical excerpts — Rossini overtures, Elgar, and Beethoven's Ode to Joy — with pioneering Moog synthesizer realizations; the film's use of "Singin' in the Rain" during a brutal attack became one of cinema's most notorious juxtapositions. Burgess's invented teen argot, Nadsat, is retained in Alex's narration.

Reception and controversy

The film was a commercial success and was nominated for four Academy Awards including Best Picture. In Britain it was linked in press coverage to alleged copycat crimes; Kubrick, receiving threats, asked Warner Bros. to withdraw it from UK distribution in 1973, and it remained unavailable there until his death in 1999. It was rated X on its original U.S. release. The film is now routinely ranked among the greatest ever made — it appears in the AFI's lists and in the top tier of the BFI/Sight and Sound polls — and was selected for the United States National Film Registry in 2020.

Themes

Central themes include the morality of behavior modification without consent, whether goodness chosen freely differs from goodness imposed, institutional hypocrisy, and the state's interest in crime as political theater. Burgess's novel's redemptive final chapter was omitted at Kubrick's decision (it had also been cut from the American edition he read).
