John I Tzimiskes

Bornc. 925, near present-day Çemişgezek, Turkey
Died10 January 976, Constantinople

John I Tzimiskes (Greek: Ἰωάννης ὁ Τζιμισκής; c. 925 – 10 January 976) was senior Byzantine emperor from 969 to 976. An accomplished general from an Armenian military family, he came to the throne after the assassination of Nikephoros II Phokas — a murder in which he was deeply implicated — and went on to extend Byzantine power in the Balkans and the Near East.

Rise to power

Tzimiskes distinguished himself in campaigns against the Arabs under his uncle Nikephoros Phokas, but was dismissed after falling under suspicion. In December 969 he conspired with the empress Theophano and others to murder Nikephoros in the imperial palace. As the price of his coronation, Patriarch Polyeuktos required him to punish the assassins, exile Theophano, and restore church property.

Campaigns

In 971 Tzimiskes defeated the invasion of Sviatoslav of Kiev, which had overrun Bulgaria, winning the Battle of Dorostolon and annexing eastern Bulgaria to the empire. In 974–975 he campaigned in Syria and Mesopotamia, capturing Damascus, Beirut and other cities and reaching the approaches to Jerusalem, though the great cities of the interior were not permanently held. These campaigns restored Byzantine prestige in the East and secured tribute from Aleppo and other states.

Death and assessment

Tzimiskes died suddenly in January 976 shortly after returning from his eastern campaign, possibly of typhoid or poison. He was succeeded by Basil II, whose long reign he had made possible. Historians rank him among the most capable soldier-emperors of the Macedonian era.
