Petroleum industry in Norway

First discoveryEkofisk, 1969
Major fieldsEkofisk, Statfjord, Troll, Johan Sverdrup
State companyEquinor (formerly Statoil)
Sovereign fundGovernment Pension Fund Global

The petroleum industry in Norway began with the discovery of the Ekofisk field in the North Sea in December 1969 and production from 1971. Oil and natural gas from the Norwegian continental shelf transformed Norway from a moderately prosperous maritime nation into one of the world's wealthiest countries per capita, and Norway became Western Europe's largest producer of oil and gas.

A production platform in the Norwegian North Sea

Norwegian policy combined state participation — through Statoil (now Equinor) and later Petoro — with high taxation of resource rents and, from 1990, the channeling of revenues into the Government Pension Fund Global, the sovereign wealth fund designed to convert petroleum wealth into diversified financial assets for future generations. Landmark fields include Statfjord, once the North Sea's largest, the Troll gas field, and the giant Johan Sverdrup oil field, brought on stream in 2019.

Significance

Petroleum has accounted for a large share of Norwegian exports, government revenue and GDP, and the industry underpins major supply and technology clusters in Stavanger, Bergen and elsewhere. At the same time, continued exploration — including in the Barents Sea — is contested because of climate policy, and Norway's role as a key gas supplier to Europe grew sharply after 2022.
