Buses in London

Buses in London form one of the largest urban bus networks in the world. Services are operated under contract to Transport for London (TfL), with vehicles in the capital's famous red livery — the double-decker bus being an icon of the city.

Network and operation

Around 700 routes are served by some 8,000–9,000 buses operated by private companies including Arriva, Stagecoach, Go-Ahead and RATP under TfL contracts that set routes, fares and service levels. Passengers pay with Oyster cards, contactless payment or travelcards; cash is no longer accepted on board.

History

Horse-drawn omnibuses began in 1829 and motor buses followed in the early 1900s. The AEC Routemaster, introduced in 1956 with its open rear platform, became the classic London bus; its successor, the New Routemaster, entered service from 2012. TfL has been converting the fleet to hybrid, electric and hydrogen power.
