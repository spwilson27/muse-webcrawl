Fair value


Fair value measurement illustration

Fair value is used, in fields such as economics, finance and accounting, as a rational and unbiased estimate of the potential market price of a good, service, or asset. The derivation takes into account such objective factors as the costs associated with production or replacement, market conditions, and matters of supply and demand. Subjective factors may also be considered, such as the risk characteristics, cost of and return on capital, and individually perceived utility.

In accounting, fair value is used as an indication of the value of an asset, where the market value is unknown or unavailable. Under United States generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP), fair value is defined, in accordance with Financial Accounting Standards Board Statement No. 157, as "the price that would be received to sell an asset or paid to transfer a liability in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date."

Fair value measurements

The International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS 13) define a hierarchy of inputs used in fair value measurements. Level 1 inputs are quoted prices in active markets for identical assets or liabilities. Level 2 inputs are observable inputs other than quoted prices, such as quoted prices for similar assets. Level 3 inputs are unobservable inputs, developed using the best information available in the circumstances, which might include the entity's own data.

Market value and fair value

Fair value is broadly similar to market value, though the two terms are not strictly identical. Market value refers to the price at which an asset would trade in a competitive auction setting, whereas fair value is an estimate of the price in an orderly transaction between willing, knowledgeable market participants. In illiquid markets, where few comparable transactions exist, fair value must be estimated using valuation techniques such as discounted cash flow analysis or comparable company analysis.
