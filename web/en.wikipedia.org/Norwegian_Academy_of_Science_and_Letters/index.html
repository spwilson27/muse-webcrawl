Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters (Norwegian: Det Norske Videnskaps-Akademi, DNVA) is a learned society based in Oslo, Norway. Its purpose is to support the advancement of science and scholarship in Norway, to represent Norwegian research internationally, and to promote interdisciplinary understanding.

History

The Academy was founded in 1857 under the name Videnskabsselskabet i Christiania (the Society of Sciences in Christiania, as Oslo was then known). It traces its origins to the scholarly circle around the Royal Frederick University, founded in 1811. The Academy was modelled on the great European academies of science and initially served as the principal forum for Norwegian research at a time when the country had few dedicated research institutions.

Organisation

The Academy is divided into two sections: the Division of Mathematics and Natural Sciences, and the Division of Humanities and Social Sciences. Its membership comprises Norwegian scholars elected for life, as well as foreign members. The Academy is presided over by a president elected from among its members, and it meets regularly in its villa in Drammensveien in Oslo.

Activities and prizes

For more than a century the Academy appointed the committee that awards the Nobel Peace Prize, a function later transferred to the Norwegian Nobel Institute. The Academy administers several prestigious awards and prizes in mathematics and science, and it has a close historical association with the Abel Prize in mathematics, whose recipient is selected by a committee appointed by the Academy. It also awards the Kavli Prize, together with the Kavli Foundation and the Norwegian Ministry of Education and Research, in astrophysics, nanoscience, and neuroscience.

The Academy supports research centres, notably the Centre for Advanced Study (CAS) in Oslo, publishes scholarly works, and hosts lectures and symposia open to the public.
