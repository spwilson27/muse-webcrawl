Shennong

Shennong (Chinese: 神農, "Divine Farmer"), also known as the Emperor of the Five Grains, is a mythological Chinese deity and cultural hero credited in Chinese tradition with teaching humans agriculture and herbal medicine. He is counted among the legendary Three Sovereigns and is said to have lived around 2800 BCE.

Legend

According to tradition, Shennong invented the plough and other farming tools, taught people to cultivate the five grains, and established markets. He is said to have personally tasted hundreds of herbs to determine their medicinal properties — sometimes poisoning himself many times a day and relying on tea, which he also discovered, as an antidote.

Shennong Ben Cao Jing

The foundational Chinese pharmacopoeia, the Shennong Ben Cao Jing ("Shennong's Classic of Materia Medica"), was attributed to him, though it was compiled much later, around the Han dynasty. It catalogues 365 drugs derived from plants, animals, and minerals, classifying them into grades, and remained a cornerstone of traditional Chinese medicine for two millennia.

Worship

Shennong is venerated as a patron of farmers and pharmacists, with temples dedicated to him across China and Taiwan. The Shennongjia mountainous district in Hubei province bears his name.
