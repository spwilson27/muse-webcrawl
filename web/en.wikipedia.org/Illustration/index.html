Illustration

An illustration is a decoration, interpretation, or visual explanation of a text, concept, or process, designed for integration in print and digital published media such as posters, flyers, magazines, books, teaching materials, animations, video games, and films. Illustrations are typically created by an illustrator and serve to clarify, embellish, or bring a subject to life.

History

Illustrated manuscripts existed in medieval scriptoria long before printing, and woodcut illustrations appeared in early printed books in the fifteenth century. The nineteenth century saw a "golden age" of illustration, with advances in printing enabling artists such as Gustave Doré, Arthur Rackham, and N. C. Wyeth to reach mass audiences through illustrated books and magazines. In the twentieth century, illustration became central to advertising, editorial design, and children's publishing.

Techniques and contexts

Traditional techniques include pen and ink, watercolour, gouache, woodcut, and etching; contemporary illustrators frequently work digitally with raster and vector software. Fields of practice include editorial, book, scientific and technical, medical, fashion, and concept illustration for film and games. Scientific illustration, in particular, emphasises accuracy in depicting natural history and anatomical subjects.
