Peano axioms


Giuseppe Peano (1858–1932) published the axioms in 1889.

The Peano axioms, also known as the Dedekind–Peano axioms, are a set of axioms for the natural numbers presented by the Italian mathematician Giuseppe Peano in 1889. They characterise arithmetic in terms of a starting element (zero) and a successor function.

The axioms

In modern form: (1) 0 is a natural number. (2) Every natural number n has a successor S(n), also a natural number. (3) 0 is not the successor of any natural number. (4) Distinct natural numbers have distinct successors; the successor function is injective. (5) Induction: if a property holds for 0, and holding for n implies holding for S(n), then it holds for all natural numbers.

Significance

The axioms underpin formal arithmetic. Peano arithmetic (PA) is the first-order theory using these axioms with an induction schema; Gödel's incompleteness theorems showed that any consistent such system strong enough to express basic arithmetic cannot prove its own consistency. The second-order version of the axioms is categorical: any two models are isomorphic.
