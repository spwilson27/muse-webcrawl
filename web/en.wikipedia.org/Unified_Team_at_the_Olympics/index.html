Unified Team at the Olympics

The Unified Team (French: Équipe unifiée, Russian: Объединённая команда) was the name used for the sports team of the former Soviet Union (except the Baltic states) at the 1992 Summer Olympics in Barcelona and the 1992 Winter Olympics in Albertville, as well as at many world championships and other competitions in the months following the dissolution of the Soviet Union.

Background

The Soviet Union was dissolved in December 1991, only months before the 1992 Olympic Games. Because the former Soviet republics had not yet established separate National Olympic Committees recognized in time — and because athletes had already qualified as a single team — the International Olympic Committee agreed that athletes from twelve of the fifteen republics would compete together one final time as the Unified Team. The three Baltic states — Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania — which had been IOC members before the Second World War, sent their own independent teams.

Competitions

At the Albertville Winter Games, the Unified Team finished second in the medal table with 9 gold, 6 silver, and 8 bronze medals. At Barcelona, the team topped the medal standings with 45 gold medals, finishing ahead of the United States. Individual standouts included gymnast Vitaly Scherbo, who won six gold medals in Barcelona. The team competed under the Olympic flag, and the Olympic Hymn was played at medal ceremonies in place of a national anthem.

Aftermath

After 1992, each former republic formed its own National Olympic Committee and competed independently. The Unified Team concept was also briefly used at some 1992 world championships in sports such as ice hockey before full separation took effect. The twelve republics represented were Armenia, Azerbaijan, Belarus, Georgia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Moldova, Russia, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, Ukraine, and Uzbekistan.
