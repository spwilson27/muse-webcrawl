Villa
Origin: Roman country estate
Types: villa rustica, villa urbana, villa maritima
Later use: suburban and holiday residences worldwide

A villa is a type of house originating in ancient Rome: originally the country estate of an upper-class Roman, comprising a residential complex set within a working farm. The concept survived and evolved through the Italian Renaissance and into modern usage, where "villa" denotes anything from a luxury holiday home to a suburban detached house, depending on the country.

Roman villas

The Roman villa combined the owner's residence (pars urbana) with farm buildings and slave quarters (pars rustica). Famous examples include Hadrian's Villa at Tivoli and the Villa of the Papyri at Herculaneum. Pliny the Younger's letters describe his seaside and Tuscan villas in loving detail.

Renaissance and modern

Andrea Palladio's villas in the Veneto — such as the Villa Rotonda — revived the type in the 16th century and influenced country-house architecture across Europe and America. In modern usage the word varies: a Mediterranean holiday house, an Indonesian resort unit, or, in Britain and Australasia, a modest attached dwelling.
