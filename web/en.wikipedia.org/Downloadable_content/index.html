Downloadable content

A digital storefront listing downloadable content for a game

Downloadable content (DLC) is additional content created for an already released video game, distributed through the Internet by the game's publisher. It can be of several types, ranging from aesthetic outfits to a similar amount of content as an expansion pack. DLC became prevalent in the 21st century with the rise of online-capable consoles and digital distribution platforms such as Steam, Xbox Live, and PlayStation Network.

History

Precursors included expansion packs sold at retail and content distributed via early online services such as Sega's GameLine and the Satellaview. The term gained wide use in the mid-2000s; a notorious early controversy was the 2006 “horse armor” sold for The Elder Scrolls IV: Oblivion, which became a byword for poor-value microtransactions.

Forms

DLC includes new levels, maps, story missions, characters, weapons, vehicles, and cosmetic items. It may be free or paid, sold individually, in bundles, or through season passes. “On-disc DLC”—content already present on the game media but locked until purchase—has attracted particular criticism.

Reception

While DLC can extend a game's life and fund continued development, it is criticized when perceived as withholding content from the base game, fragmenting multiplayer communities, or pressuring players through aggressive monetization.
