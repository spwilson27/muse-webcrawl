Oshkosh is a city in Winnebago County, Wisconsin, United States, situated where the Fox River enters Lake Winnebago. It is the county seat and, with about 66,000 residents, the largest city on the lake. The city is named after Oshkosh, a chief of the Menominee Nation, and grew to prominence in the 19th century as a lumber centre — "Sawdust City".

History

Fur traders and settlers arrived in the 1830s; the city was incorporated in 1853. Sawmills along the Fox River processed pine from northern Wisconsin, and by the 1870s Oshkosh was one of the largest lumber-producing cities in America. Devastating fires, including the Great Fire of 1875, repeatedly destroyed much of the city, which was rebuilt in brick. The lumber era left a legacy of manufacturers, including the Oshkosh B'gosh clothing company (founded 1895), famous for children's overalls, and today's Oshkosh Corporation, a maker of specialty trucks and military vehicles.

AirVenture

Each summer Oshkosh hosts EAA AirVenture, the world's largest airshow, organised by the Experimental Aircraft Association. The week-long fly-in draws more than 600,000 visitors and some 10,000 aircraft to Wittman Regional Airport, temporarily making its control tower the busiest in the world.

Education

The University of Wisconsin–Oshkosh, founded in 1871, is one of the largest universities in the state.
