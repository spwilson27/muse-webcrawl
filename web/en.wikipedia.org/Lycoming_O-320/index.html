The Lycoming O-320 is a large family of naturally aspirated, air-cooled, horizontally opposed four-cylinder, direct-drive aircraft engines produced by Lycoming Engines. Introduced in 1953, the O-320 family includes 92 variants and more than 68,000 engines have been delivered, making it one of the most widely used light aircraft engines in the world. Variants produce between 150 and 160 horsepower (112–119 kW).

The "320" designation refers to the engine's approximate displacement of 320 cubic inches (5.24 litres). The carburetted O-320, the fuel-injected IO-320, the aerobatic AEIO-320 and the helicopter vertical-mount HIO-320 subfamilies share the same basic architecture.

Design and development

The O-320 is a four-cylinder, horizontally opposed ("flat-four"), air-cooled engine. Cylinders have a bore of 5.125 inches (130.2 mm) and a stroke of 3.875 inches (98.4 mm), giving a displacement of 319.7 cu in (5,239 cc). The engine uses a conventional wet-sump lubrication system, dual magnetos in most installations, and a single carburettor or fuel-injection system depending on variant.

Compression ratios vary by model: the 150 hp versions typically use 7.00:1 compression and can run on 80/87 avgas, while the 160 hp versions use 8.50:1 compression and require 100/130 or 100LL fuel. The engine's simple design, modest weight (about 244 lb / 111 kg dry for many variants) and long overhaul life (commonly 2,000 hours TBO) have made it a standard in flight training fleets.

Variants

O-320 (carburetted)

The basic series, including the widely used O-320-D series fitted to the Piper PA-28 Cherokee family and the O-320-E series fitted to the Piper PA-28-140 and many Cessna conversions. The O-320-A and -B models powered early Piper PA-23 Apache twins and the PA-28-150.

IO-320 (fuel injected)

Fuel-injected models producing 150–160 hp, used notably in the Piper PA-30 Twin Comanche (IO-320-B series, with counter-rotating LIO-320 counterparts) and the Mooney M20 series in some installations.

AEIO-320 (aerobatic)

Aerobatic variants with modified oil systems permitting sustained inverted flight, used in competition and training aerobatic aircraft.

HIO-320 (helicopter)

Vertical-mount versions developed for light helicopters.

Applications

  • Cessna 172 Skyhawk (via conversions and later production variants of related types)
  • Piper PA-28 Cherokee family (Warrior, Cherokee 140/150/160)
  • Piper PA-23 Apache
  • Grumman American AA-5 Traveler/Cheetah series (related O-320 variants)
  • Mooney M20 (early models)
  • Numerous homebuilt aircraft, including Van's RV series
