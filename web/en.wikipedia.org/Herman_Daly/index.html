Herman Edward Daly (1938–2022) was an American ecological economist. He was a leading figure in the development of ecological economics and the theory of the steady-state economy, arguing that the economy is a subsystem of a finite, non-growing ecosphere and that perpetual material growth is neither possible nor desirable. He worked at Louisiana State University, as a senior economist at the World Bank (1988–1994), and later at the University of Maryland School of Public Policy.

Ideas

Influenced by his teacher Nicholas Georgescu-Roegen and the latter's work on entropy and economics, Daly argued that mainstream economics ignores the physical scale of the economy relative to the environment. He distinguished growth (quantitative increase in throughput of matter and energy) from development (qualitative improvement), and proposed that society should pursue "development without growth" once an optimal scale is reached. His policy framework rests on three goals — sustainable scale, just distribution, and efficient allocation — addressed in that order, reversing the priorities of conventional economics.

Steady-state economics

In works such as Steady-State Economics (1977) and For the Common Good (1989, with theologian John B. Cobb Jr.), Daly proposed institutional arrangements including resource depletion quotas auctioned by the state, limits on income inequality, and stabilisation of population. He also co-developed, with Robert Costanza and others, the Index of Sustainable Economic Welfare (later the Genuine Progress Indicator) as an alternative to GDP.

Influence and recognition

Daly co-founded the International Society for Ecological Economics and its journal Ecological Economics (1989). He received the Right Livelihood Award (1996), the Sophie Prize (1999) and the Nicholas Georgescu-Roegen Award. His ideas underpin much of the modern degrowth and post-growth literature, although steady-state economics remains outside the mainstream.
