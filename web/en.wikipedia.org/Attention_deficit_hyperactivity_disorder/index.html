Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder

Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) is a neurodevelopmental disorder characterised by symptoms of inattention, hyperactivity, impulsivity and emotional dysregulation that are excessive and pervasive, impairing in multiple contexts, and developmentally inappropriate. Symptoms arise before age 12 and must persist for at least six months for a diagnosis.

ADHD involves persistent patterns of inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity.

Signs and symptoms

ADHD is divided into three presentations: predominantly inattentive, predominantly hyperactive-impulsive, and combined. Inattentive symptoms include difficulty sustaining attention, forgetfulness, disorganisation and frequent loss of items; hyperactive-impulsive symptoms include fidgeting, excessive talking, restlessness, interrupting and difficulty waiting. Many adults experience the condition with less overt hyperactivity but continuing inattention and impulsivity.

Causes

ADHD is highly heritable, with twin studies estimating heritability at around 70–80%. Environmental risk factors include prematurity, low birth weight, prenatal alcohol or tobacco exposure, and some environmental toxins, though no single cause accounts for most cases. Neuroimaging studies report small average differences in brain structure and function, especially in circuits involving dopamine and executive function.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is clinical, based on diagnostic criteria such as those of the DSM-5, evidence of symptoms across settings (for example, home and school), and exclusion of other explanations. Rating scales completed by parents, teachers and patients are commonly used.

Management

Treatment typically combines behavioural interventions, educational support and medication. Stimulant medications such as methylphenidate and amphetamine formulations are the most effective pharmacological treatments; non-stimulants including atomoxetine and extended-release guanfacine are alternatives. Around 5% of children and 2.5% of adults worldwide are estimated to meet diagnostic criteria.
