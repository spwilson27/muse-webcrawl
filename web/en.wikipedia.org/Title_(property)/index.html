Title (property)

In property law, title is a bundle of rights in a piece of property, especially land, in which a party may own either a legal interest or an equitable interest. Title is the legal evidence of ownership: it answers the question of who is entitled to possess, use, enjoy, encumber and transfer the property. A person may hold title without physical possession, and possession without title, as with tenants and squatters.

Legal and equitable title

Common-law systems distinguish legal title, the ownership recognised by courts of law and usually recorded in a public registry, from equitable title, the beneficial interest recognised by courts of equity. The classic case is the trust: a trustee holds legal title to property while the beneficiary holds the equitable title and the right to benefit from it. When the two are united in one person, they merge into full ownership. In land transactions, an executory contract commonly passes equitable title to the buyer at signing, with legal title following at closing when the deed is delivered.

Transfer and registration

Title to land is usually transferred by a deed — a written instrument that may contain warranties about the quality of the title (a general warranty deed) or none (a quitclaim deed). Jurisdictions secure titles through recording acts, under which deeds are filed in a public land registry so purchasers can search the "chain of title", or through registration of title systems such as the Torrens system, in which the register itself is conclusive. Because defects in a chain of title can destroy value, buyers often purchase title insurance and obtain title searches before closing. Title can also pass by inheritance, court order, or adverse possession after long, open and hostile occupation.

Defects and quiet title

A "cloud on title" is any unresolved claim or encumbrance — an old lien, an ambiguous boundary, a forgery in the chain — that casts doubt on ownership. An owner may bring a quiet title action asking a court to settle all competing claims. Related concepts include marketable title (title free from reasonable doubt), the distinction between title and colour of title, and Aboriginal title, the inherent right of Indigenous peoples to ancestral lands.
