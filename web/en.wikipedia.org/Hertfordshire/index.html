Hertfordshire is a ceremonial county in the East of England, bordered by Bedfordshire, Cambridgeshire, Essex, Buckinghamshire, and Greater London. Its county town is Hertford, while its largest settlements include Watford, Hemel Hempstead, Stevenage, and the cathedral city of St Albans. Much of the county lies within the London commuter belt.

History

The area was part of the territory of the Catuvellauni before the Roman conquest; the Romans built the city of Verulamium near modern St Albans, one of the largest towns of Roman Britain. The county takes its name from Hertford, a burgh established under Edward the Elder. In the twentieth century Hertfordshire pioneered planned urban development: Letchworth (1903) was the world's first garden city, followed by Welwyn Garden City, and after 1946 the new towns of Stevenage, Hemel Hempstead, and Hatfield.

Geography and economy

The chalk hills of the Chilterns cross the north-west of the county; the south is heavily suburbanized. The county is a center for the film industry — Elstree Studios and Warner Bros. Studios Leavesden, where the Harry Potter films were made, are located there — as well as for pharmaceuticals, aerospace at Stevenage, and retail headquarters.

Transport

Radial motorways and railways to London — including the M1, M25, West Coast Main Line, and Midland Main Line — make Hertfordshire one of England's principal commuter counties.
