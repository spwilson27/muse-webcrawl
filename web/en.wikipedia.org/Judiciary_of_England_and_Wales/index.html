Judiciary of England and Wales

The judiciary of England and Wales comprises the judges and other officers of the courts and tribunals of England and Wales. Headed by the Lord Chief Justice, it is constitutionally independent of the executive and legislature, a principle reinforced by the Constitutional Reform Act 2005.

Structure

At the apex sits the Supreme Court of the United Kingdom, created in 2009 to replace the Appellate Committee of the House of Lords. Below it are the Court of Appeal, the High Court of Justice (with King's Bench, Chancery, and Family divisions), the Crown Court, the County Court, and magistrates' courts, alongside a unified tribunals system.

Judges

Judicial offices range from Supreme Court justices and Lords Justices of Appeal to High Court judges, circuit judges, district judges, and lay magistrates, who are unpaid volunteers deciding most criminal cases. Since 2006 appointments have been made on merit by the independent Judicial Appointments Commission. Judges enjoy security of tenure; senior judges can be removed only on an address by both Houses of Parliament.

Reform

The Constitutional Reform Act 2005 created the Supreme Court, reformed the office of Lord Chancellor, and placed a statutory duty on ministers to uphold judicial independence. The Lord Chief Justice became head of the judiciary and its representative to Parliament and government.
