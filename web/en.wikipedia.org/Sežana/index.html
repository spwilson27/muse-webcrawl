Sežana (pronounced [sɛˈʒaːna]; Italian: Sesana) is a town in the Littoral region of Slovenia, near the border with Italy. It is the seat of the Municipality of Sežana. Sežana is located on the Karst Plateau, about 17 kilometres (11 miles) from Trieste, Italy.

History

Sežana developed as a settlement along the road connecting Trieste with the interior of the Habsburg lands. It remained a small village until the mid-19th century, when the construction of the Austrian Southern Railway, linking Vienna with Trieste, brought a railway station to the area in 1857. The station spurred rapid growth, and Sežana became an important transport and administrative centre for the surrounding Karst villages.

After the First World War, the town passed to the Kingdom of Italy under the Treaty of Rapallo. Following the Second World War, it became part of Yugoslavia and later of independent Slovenia in 1991.

Geography and climate

The town sits at an elevation of about 360 metres on the limestone Karst Plateau. The climate is transitional between Mediterranean and continental, strongly influenced by the cold northeasterly bora wind, which can blow with great force in winter.

Economy and transport

Sežana is a border crossing and logistics centre on the A3 motorway and the railway line between Ljubljana and Trieste. The surrounding countryside is known for the production of Teran wine from the local terra rossa soils.

Notable people

  • Srečko Kosovel (1904–1926), Slovenian poet, was born in Sežana.
  • Danilo Türk, former president of Slovenia, was born in nearby Maribor but has family ties to the region.
