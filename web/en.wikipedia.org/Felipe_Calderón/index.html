Felipe de Jesús Calderón Hinojosa (born 18 August 1962) is a Mexican politician who served as the 63rd president of Mexico from 2006 to 2012, as a member of the National Action Party (PAN). A lawyer by training, he previously served as a federal deputy, national chairman of the PAN, and Secretary of Energy under President Vicente Fox.

Presidency

Calderón won the closely contested 2006 presidential election by a margin of less than one percentage point over Andrés Manuel López Obrador, who disputed the result and staged mass protests. Shortly after taking office, Calderón launched the "Mexican drug war", deploying the military against drug cartels; the ensuing violence claimed tens of thousands of lives during his term and remains highly controversial. His administration also enacted a major health-care expansion (Seguro Popular) and hosted the 2010 UN Climate Change Conference in Cancún.

Later life

After leaving office, Calderón took up a fellowship at Harvard University and founded the Global Commission on the Economy and Climate. He left the PAN in 2018 and attempted to register a new political party, México Libre, with his wife Margarita Zavala; the registration was denied by the electoral authority in 2020.
