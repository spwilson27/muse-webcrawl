Prince Albert of Saxe-Coburg and Gotha (Franz August Karl Albert Emanuel; 26 August 1819 – 14 December 1861) was the husband of Queen Victoria from their marriage on 10 February 1840 until his death in 1861. Initially he was given little power, but in time he became Victoria's trusted adviser and held considerable influence over her decisions. In 1857, Victoria gave him the formal title of Prince Consort.

Albert was born at Schloss Rosenau, near Coburg, Germany, into the Saxon ducal house of Saxe-Coburg and Gotha. He was educated privately at home and later at the University of Bonn. His marriage to Victoria, his first cousin, was arranged by his uncle Leopold, King of the Belgians.

Public role

Albert devoted much of his energy to public causes, including educational reform, the abolition of slavery, workers' welfare, and the application of science and art to manufacturing industry. His most celebrated achievement was the Great Exhibition of 1851, held in the Crystal Palace in Hyde Park, London, which showcased British and international industry and was an enormous success. The profits funded the museums of South Kensington — the Victoria and Albert Museum, the Science Museum, and the Natural History Museum.

Death and legacy

Albert died at Windsor Castle on 14 December 1861, aged 42, officially of typhoid fever, though modern speculation includes Crohn's disease or stomach cancer. Victoria entered deep mourning, wearing black for the remaining forty years of her life, and British court life was profoundly changed. Memorials to Albert include the Albert Memorial in Kensington Gardens and the Royal Albert Hall in London.
