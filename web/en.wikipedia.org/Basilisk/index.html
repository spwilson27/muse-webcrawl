In European bestiaries and legends, a basilisk is a legendary reptile reputed to be a "king of serpents" that can cause death with a single glance. Its name derives from the Greek basilískos, "little king," referring to the crown-like crest on its head.

Description

Pliny the Elder, in his Natural History (1st century AD), described the basilisk of Cyrene as a small snake, not more than twelve fingers in length, whose venom and crawling touch scorched grass and broke stones, and which bore a white spot like a diadem on its head. Medieval accounts transformed the creature into a monster hatched from a rooster's egg incubated by a serpent or toad, often depicted with a rooster's head and wings and a serpent's tail — merging with the related cockatrice tradition.

Powers and weaknesses

The basilisk's gaze and breath were said to be lethal, its venom so potent that it could kill a man through his weapon. According to legend it could be destroyed by the crowing of a rooster, by seeing its own reflection, or by the bite of a weasel, which was said to be immune to its powers.

In culture

The basilisk appears throughout medieval and early modern literature and heraldry, in Chaucer and Shakespeare, and in modern fantasy, notably as the monster in Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets. The real genus of lizards Basiliscus — the "Jesus lizards" that run on water — takes its name from the myth.
