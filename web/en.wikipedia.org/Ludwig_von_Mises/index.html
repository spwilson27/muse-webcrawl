Ludwig von Mises

Born: 29 September 1881, Lemberg, Austria-Hungary (now Lviv, Ukraine)
Died: 10 October 1973, New York City
School: Austrian School
Main work: Human Action (1949)

Ludwig Heinrich Edler von Mises was an Austrian-American economist and philosopher, a central figure of the Austrian School of economics, known for praxeology – the study of human action – and for his critique of socialism and interventionism.

Life

Mises studied at the University of Vienna under Eugen von Böhm-Bawerk. His Theory of Money and Credit (1912) integrated money into marginal utility theory. His 1920 essay on economic calculation argued socialist central planning could not rationally allocate resources without market prices. He taught in Vienna and Geneva, emigrating to the United States in 1940, where he taught at New York University.

Works

Socialism (1922), Liberalism (1927), Human Action (1949), The Anti-Capitalistic Mentality (1956) and Theory and History (1957). He mentored F. A. Hayek and Murray Rothbard, and the Mises Institute was founded in his name in 1982.
