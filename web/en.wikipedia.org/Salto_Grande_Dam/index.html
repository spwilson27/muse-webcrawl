Salto Grande Dam

The Salto Grande hydroelectric complex on the Uruguay River.

The Salto Grande Dam is a large hydroelectric dam on the Uruguay River, between Concordia in Argentina and Salto in Uruguay. It is a joint binational project of the two countries, administered by the Mixed Technical Commission of Salto Grande (CTM).

Construction and operation

Construction began in 1974 and the first turbines entered service in 1979; the plant was completed in the early 1980s. The powerhouse contains 14 Kaplan turbines with a total installed capacity of about 1,890 MW, shared equally between Argentina and Uruguay, making it a cornerstone of both national grids. A road and railway bridge over the dam connects the two countries.

The reservoir

The dam created a reservoir of roughly 780 km² on the upper Uruguay River. The project required the relocation of communities in both countries, including the Argentine town of Federación, which was rebuilt as Nueva Federación. The original falls of Salto Grande, a series of rapids that gave the site its name, were submerged by the reservoir.

Importance

Salto Grande was a landmark of South American integration and for many years supplied a large share of Uruguay's electricity. Together with Yacyretá and Itaipú, it forms part of the great hydroelectric system of the Río de la Plata basin.
