The Pulitzer Prize for Drama is one of the seven American Pulitzer Prizes annually awarded for Letters, Drama and Music. It was first given in 1918 and recognises a distinguished play by an American author, preferably original in its source and dealing with American life.

History

Joseph Pulitzer's will provided for an award for the American play "best representing the educational value and power of the stage". The first recipient was Jesse Lynch Williams for Why Marry? (1918). No award was given in some years, and the scope was broadened to include musicals; Stephen Sondheim's Sunday in the Park with George and other musicals have won.

Notable winners

Eugene O'Neill won the prize four times (Beyond the Horizon, Anna Christie, Strange Interlude, and posthumously Long Day's Journey into Night). Other celebrated winners include Tennessee Williams (A Streetcar Named Desire, Cat on a Hot Tin Roof), Arthur Miller (Death of a Salesman), August Wilson, who won twice, Edward Albee, and in the twenty-first century Lynn Nottage, the first woman to win twice, for Ruined and Sweat. Lin-Manuel Miranda's Hamilton won in 2016.

Selection

The winner is chosen by the Pulitzer board from nominations made by a drama jury, which traditionally submits three finalists. Eligible productions must be staged in the United States during the eligibility period.
