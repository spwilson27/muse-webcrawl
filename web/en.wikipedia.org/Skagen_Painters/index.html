Skagen Painters

The Skagen Painters (Skagensmalerne) were a group of Scandinavian artists who gathered each summer from the late 1870s in the fishing village of Skagen at the northern tip of Denmark. Inspired by the Barbizon school and French naturalism, they painted the local fishermen, landscapes and the distinctive luminous quality of the region's light.

Skagen Painters

Years active: c. 1870s–1900s
Location: Skagen, Denmark
Key figures: Michael Ancher, Anna Ancher, P. S. Krøyer, Viggo Johansen

History

Michael Ancher arrived in 1874 and married Anna Kirstine Brøndum, a native of Skagen and one of the group's finest painters. The Brøndum family's hotel became the colony's social hub. The Norwegian-born P. S. Krøyer joined in 1882, bringing virtuoso technique; his Hip, Hip, Hurrah! and Summer Evening on Skagen's Southern Beach are among the group's best-known works. Other members included Viggo Johansen, Karl Madsen, Oscar Björck and the Swedish and Norwegian painters drawn by Skagen's reputation.

Legacy

Their works are centred at Skagens Museum, founded in 1908, which holds more than 1,800 works by members of the colony.
