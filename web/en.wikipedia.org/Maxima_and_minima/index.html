Maxima and minima

In mathematical analysis, the maxima and minima (the respective plurals of maximum and minimum) of a function, known collectively as extrema (the plural of extremum), are the largest and smallest value of the function, either within a given range (the local or relative extrema), or on the entire domain (the global or absolute extrema). Pierre de Fermat was one of the first mathematicians to propose a general technique, adequality, for calculating maxima and minima.

Definitions

A function f has a global maximum at x* if f(x*) ≥ f(x) for all x in the domain; similarly for a global minimum. A local maximum occurs at x* if f(x*) ≥ f(x) for all x in some neighbourhood of x*. The value of the function at a maximum point is called the maximum value of the function, and point where the maximum value is attained is called the maximum point.

Finding extrema

For differentiable functions, local extrema in the interior of the domain occur at critical points where the derivative is zero (Fermat's theorem) or undefined. The first derivative test and the second derivative test classify such points: if the second derivative at a critical point is positive, it is a local minimum; if negative, a local maximum. By the extreme value theorem, a continuous function on a closed bounded interval attains its global maximum and minimum.

Multivariable functions

For functions of several variables, critical points satisfy ∇f = 0, and the Hessian matrix of second derivatives determines whether a point is a local maximum, local minimum, or a saddle point. Finding global extrema is the core problem of mathematical optimization, with applications throughout science, engineering, and economics.
