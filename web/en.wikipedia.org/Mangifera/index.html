Mangifera is a genus of flowering plants in the cashew family, Anacardiaceae. It comprises roughly 70 recognised species of evergreen trees native to tropical Asia, especially the Malay Archipelago, the Indian subcontinent, and mainland Southeast Asia. The genus is best known for Mangifera indica, the common mango, one of the world's most important tropical fruit crops.

Description

Mangifera species are evergreen trees, often large, reaching 15–30 metres or more in height with dense, rounded crowns. The leaves are simple, alternate, and leathery, often reddish or purple when young. The flowers are small, whitish or pinkish, and borne in large terminal panicles; trees may be monoecious with male and bisexual flowers on the same inflorescence. The fruit is a drupe, varying greatly in size, shape, skin colour, and flesh quality between species. Like other members of the Anacardiaceae, many species exude a resinous sap that can irritate the skin.

Distribution and ecology

The centre of diversity of Mangifera is in western Malesia — Peninsular Malaysia, Sumatra, Java, and Borneo — where the majority of species occur in lowland rainforest. Many wild species are now threatened by deforestation. Species are pollinated by insects and the fruits are dispersed by mammals, including bats and primates.

Species and uses

Besides M. indica, several species are cultivated locally for their fruit, including:

  • Mangifera caesia (binjai) — a whitish-fleshed fruit eaten fresh and used in sambals.
  • Mangifera foetida (bachang or horse mango) — strongly scented fruit used in curries and pickles.
  • Mangifera odorata (kuini) — a fragrant hybrid-origin species popular in Malaysia and Indonesia.
  • Mangifera casturi (Kalimantan mango) — a fruit from South Kalimantan, now extinct in the wild.
  • Mangifera pajang (bambangan) — a large-fruited Bornean species.

Some species yield useful timber, and wild relatives of the mango are conserved as genetic resources for breeding resistance to pests and diseases.
