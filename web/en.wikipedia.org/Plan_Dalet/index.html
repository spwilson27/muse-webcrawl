Plan Dalet

Date10 March 1948 (adopted)
AuthorHaganah general staff
Context1948 Palestine war

Plan Dalet (Hebrew: Tokhnit Dalet, "Plan D") was a plan adopted by the Haganah, the main Jewish paramilitary organization in Mandatory Palestine, on 10 March 1948, as the British Mandate drew to a close and the civil war phase of the 1948 Palestine war escalated. Successor to Plans A (1937), B (1946), and C (1946–47), it set out to secure the territory allotted to the Jewish state under the UN Partition Plan, Jewish settlements outside it, and the corridors linking them, in anticipation of invasion by neighbouring Arab states.

Contents

The plan instructed brigades to occupy police forts, take control of government services, protect supply routes, and, where Arab villages served as bases for hostile forces, to destroy or expel resistance; it specified that villages that resisted could be destroyed and their inhabitants expelled beyond the state's borders, while inhabitants of non-resisting villages would remain under military administration. Brigades were assigned areas of operation across the country, and a series of named operations (including Nachshon, Yiftach, and Danny) were carried out under its framework in April–July 1948.

Historiography

Plan Dalet is among the most debated documents of the 1948 war. Historians such as Benny Morris read it as a military plan for defence and territorial control rather than a blueprint for systematic expulsion, whereas Ilan Pappé and Walid Khalidi argue it amounted to a master plan for the ethnic cleansing of Palestine. The debate is central to competing accounts of the Palestinian exodus (the Nakba) of 1948.
