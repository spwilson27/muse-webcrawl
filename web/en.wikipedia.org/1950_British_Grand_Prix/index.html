The 1950 British Grand Prix, formally known as the Royal Automobile Club Grand Prix d'Europe Incorporating The British Grand Prix, was a Formula One motor race held on 13 May 1950 at the Silverstone Circuit in Northamptonshire, England. It was the first race of the inaugural FIA World Championship of Drivers.

Race report

The race was dominated by the Alfa Romeo 158 "Alfetta" cars, which occupied the front row of the grid. Giuseppe Farina led from pole position and won the race, setting the fastest lap. His teammates Luigi Fagioli and Juan Manuel Fangio finished second and third on the road, though Fangio later retired with an oil leak, promoting Reg Parnell to third.

Significance

As the opening round of the first-ever Formula One World Championship, the event is regarded as the birth of the modern championship era. King George VI attended the race, the first time a reigning British monarch had attended a motor race in Britain.
