Trigeneration

Trigeneration, or combined cooling, heat and power (CCHP), is the simultaneous generation of electricity and useful heating and cooling from a single fuel or heat source. It extends cogeneration (combined heat and power) by using some of the recovered heat to drive an absorption chiller that provides air conditioning or refrigeration, so that surplus heat in warm months — when heating demand is low — is put to productive use.

How it works

A prime mover such as a gas engine, gas turbine or fuel cell generates electricity and produces waste heat. Heat recovery equipment captures this heat for space heating, domestic hot water or process heat; a portion can be diverted to an absorption refrigerator, which uses heat rather than mechanical compression to produce chilled water (typically using lithium bromide–water or ammonia–water cycles). Overall fuel utilisation can reach 80–90%, compared with roughly 50% for separate generation of electricity, heat and cooling.

Applications

Trigeneration is used in buildings and districts with simultaneous year-round demands for power, heat and cooling: hospitals, hotels, airports, universities, data centres, shopping centres and district energy networks in warm climates. By locating generation at the point of use it also avoids transmission losses, though economics depend on fuel and electricity prices, load profiles and carbon policy.
