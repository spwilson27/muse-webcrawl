Frizzled

Frizzled is a family of atypical G protein-coupled receptors that serve as receptors in Wnt signalling pathways and other signalling processes. The name derives from the frizzled phenotype of Drosophila mutants, in which the hairs and bristles of the cuticle lose their normal coordinated orientation. Frizzled proteins span the cell membrane seven times and possess an extracellular cysteine-rich domain that binds Wnt ligands.

Function

In the canonical Wnt pathway, Frizzled works with the co-receptors LRP5/6 to stabilise β-catenin, which enters the nucleus and regulates gene transcription. In non-canonical pathways, Frizzled receptors control planar cell polarity — the coordinated orientation of cells within a tissue plane — and calcium signalling. Humans have ten Frizzled genes (FZD1–FZD10). Because Wnt signalling governs embryonic development and stem-cell maintenance, Frizzled receptors are implicated in cancer, bone disorders such as osteoporosis, and degenerative diseases, making them targets for drug development.

Related proteins

Secreted frizzled-related proteins (sFRPs) contain the cysteine-rich domain but no transmembrane segment, and they act as modulators or inhibitors of Wnt signalling. Smoothened, the receptor central to Hedgehog signalling, is a distant relative within the same class F of GPCRs.
