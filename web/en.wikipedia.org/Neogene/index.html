Neogene

The Neogene is a geologic period and system that spans from 23.03 to 2.58 million years ago. It is the second period of the Cenozoic Era, following the Paleogene and succeeded by the Quaternary. The Neogene is divided into the Miocene (23.03–5.33 Ma) and Pliocene (5.33–2.58 Ma) epochs.

Climate and life

The Neogene saw continued global cooling and drying, the expansion of grasslands, and the retreat of paratropical forests. Continents approached their modern configuration; North and South America were joined by the Isthmus of Panama late in the period, enabling the Great American Interchange of species and altering ocean circulation. Mammals and birds evolved toward modern forms — grazing horses, elephants, and large predators flourished — and kelp forests and coral reefs took on modern character. In Africa, the hominin lineage emerged and diversified during the Pliocene.

Events

The Miocene included the dramatic Messinian salinity crisis, when the Mediterranean Sea largely dried up around 5.96–5.33 million years ago.
