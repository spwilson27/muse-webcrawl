Rails-to-Trails Conservancy


Placeholder image: Rails-to-Trails Conservancy

Rails-to-Trails Conservancy (RTC) is an American nonprofit organization dedicated to creating a nationwide network of trails from former railway lines. Founded in 1986 and headquartered in Washington, D.C., it works to convert abandoned rail corridors into multi-use public trails.

History and work

RTC emerged as thousands of miles of American track were abandoned after deregulation. It advocates for railbanking — a provision of the National Trails System Improvements Act of 1983 that preserves corridors for interim trail use — and publishes trail guides via TrailLink. Its legal defence of railbanking survived constitutional challenge in the 1990 US Supreme Court case Preserving the Rails-to-Trails Conservancy v. ... litigation era, notably Preseault v. ICC (1990).

Impact

The United States now has more than 25,000 miles of rail-trails. RTC's flagship initiative, the Great American Rail-Trail, aims to connect a route across 12 states from Washington, D.C. to Washington state.
