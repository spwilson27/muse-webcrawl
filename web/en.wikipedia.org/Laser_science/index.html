Laser science

A laser experiment in a research laboratory

Laser science or laser physics is a branch of optics that describes the theory and practice of lasers. It is concerned primarily with quantum electronics, laser construction, optical cavity design, the physics of producing a population inversion in laser media, and the temporal evolution of the light field in the laser.

Overview

Laser science is also concerned with the physics of laser propagation, particularly the physics of Gaussian beams, laser applications, and associated fields such as nonlinear optics and quantum optics. The field studies how laser light interacts with matter and how lasers can be designed for particular wavelengths, powers, and pulse durations.

History

The foundations of laser science were laid by Albert Einstein's 1917 paper on stimulated emission of radiation. Charles Townes, Nikolay Basov, and Alexander Prokhorov developed the maser in the 1950s, and Theodore Maiman demonstrated the first working laser, a ruby laser, in 1960.

Research areas

Major research areas include ultrafast lasers and attosecond science, high-power laser systems, laser spectroscopy, laser cooling of atoms, and the development of new gain media such as fibre lasers and semiconductor lasers. Laser science underpins applications in medicine, telecommunications, manufacturing, and fundamental physics.
