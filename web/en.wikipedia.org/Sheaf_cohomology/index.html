Sheaf cohomology

In mathematics, sheaf cohomology is the application of homological algebra to analyse the global sections of a sheaf on a topological space. Broadly speaking, sheaf cohomology describes the obstructions to solving a geometric problem globally when it can be solved locally: the higher cohomology groups Hi(X, F) measure the failure of local data for a sheaf F to patch together into global data.

Definitions

Sheaf cohomology is defined as the right derived functors of the global sections functor. Concretely it may be computed by Čech cohomology from an open cover, by resolutions with injective or flasque sheaves, or via Godement and de Rham-type resolutions. For a constant sheaf, sheaf cohomology recovers ordinary singular cohomology of the space.

Applications

Sheaf cohomology is foundational in algebraic geometry and complex analysis: it underlies Serre duality, the Riemann–Roch theorem, the theory of Picard groups via H1(X, O*), and the exponential sequence relating holomorphic and topological line bundles. Jean Leray introduced the notions of sheaf and spectral sequence in the 1940s; Alexander Grothendieck's work recast algebraic geometry on this foundation.
