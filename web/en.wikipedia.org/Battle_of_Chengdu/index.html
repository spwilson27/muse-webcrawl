Battle of Chengdu

Battle of Chengdu
Date: 213–214 AD
Location: Chengdu, Yi Province (modern Sichuan, China)
Result: Liu Bei victory
Part of the wars at the end of the Han dynasty

The Battle of Chengdu, also known as the Siege of Chengdu, was fought in 213–214 during the end of the Han dynasty. The warlord Liu Bei seized control of Yi Province from his kinsman Liu Zhang, culminating in the surrender of the provincial capital, Chengdu.

Background

Liu Zhang, governor of Yi Province, had invited Liu Bei into Sichuan in 211 to help against the warlord Zhang Lu in Hanzhong. The alliance broke down when Liu Zhang learned that Liu Bei planned to take the province for himself. War followed: Liu Bei turned south from Jiameng Pass, defeated Liu Zhang's generals at Fu County and Mianzhu, and advanced on Chengdu with reinforcements led by Zhang Fei, Zhao Yun, and Zhuge Liang.

The siege

Liu Bei invested Chengdu in 214. Although the city was well provisioned and could have held out for a long time, the defection of the defender Ma Chao — a famous general recently arrived in Liu Bei's service — demoralised the inhabitants when he appeared before the walls with troops. Liu Zhang, unwilling to let his people suffer, opened the gates and surrendered after a siege of several dozen days. Liu Bei treated him generously, allowing him to keep his property and seal, and took over the governorship of Yi Province.

Significance

Possession of the fertile Sichuan basin gave Liu Bei the territorial foundation from which he later proclaimed the kingdom of Shu Han in 221, one of the Three Kingdoms. The campaign is a celebrated episode of the historical novel Romance of the Three Kingdoms.
