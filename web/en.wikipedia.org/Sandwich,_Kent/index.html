Sandwich, Kent

Sandwich is a historic town and civil parish in the Dover District of Kent, England, on the River Stour near the English Channel coast. Once one of the principal ports of medieval England as the northernmost of the Cinque Ports, it is now about three kilometres from the sea, the river having silted up.

History

Sandwich was a major port from the Saxon period onward and became a limb of the Cinque Ports confederation. It was attacked repeatedly by the French during the Hundred Years' War, and its town walls were strengthened in the medieval period. Flemish and Walloon refugees settled here in the 16th century, bringing new industries. The silting of the Stour ended its days as a seaport, but preserved its medieval street plan and many timber-framed buildings almost intact, making it one of the best-preserved medieval towns in England.

The sandwich

The town's name is immortalised by the food: John Montagu, 4th Earl of Sandwich (1718–1792), is famously said to have ordered meat served between slices of bread so that he could eat without leaving the gaming table, and the dish took the title's name. The story, popularised in the 1760s–70s, is partly legend, but the association with Sandwich is universally accepted.

Modern town

Today Sandwich is a quiet market town of about 5,000 people, with three famous links golf courses nearby — Royal St George's, Prince's and Royal Cinque Ports — which regularly host The Open Championship. The Pfizer research site at Sandwich, where Viagra was developed, was long a major employer.
