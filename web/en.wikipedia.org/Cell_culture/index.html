Cell culture

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cell culture is the process by which cells are grown under controlled conditions outside their natural environment, typically in flasks or plates containing a nutrient medium, at controlled temperature, humidity and gas composition. The term usually refers to culturing cells derived from multicellular eukaryotes, especially animal cells. Cell culture is one of the major tools of cell and molecular biology, providing model systems for physiology, drug screening, vaccine and antibody production, and tissue engineering.

History

Ross Harrison demonstrated nerve-fibre growth in frog tissue in 1907, and Alexis Carrel developed early tissue-culture technique and sterile practice. A landmark came in 1951 with HeLa, the first immortal human cell line, derived from Henrietta Lacks's cervical cancer without her knowledge — a case that later reshaped research-ethics and consent rules. Antibiotics, defined media, and trypsinisation in the 1950s made culture routine; the polio vaccine was an early industrial-scale triumph of cultured cells.

Techniques

Primary cultures are taken directly from tissue and have limited lifespans; cell lines are subcultured (passaged) repeatedly and may become immortalised. Adherent cells grow as monolayers on treated plastic; suspension cells float freely. Cultures require sterile ("aseptic") technique, incubators at 37 °C with 5% CO2, and media such as DMEM or RPMI supplemented with serum. Contamination — especially by mycoplasma — and misidentified lines are persistent problems; authentication by DNA profiling is now standard. Advanced systems include 3D cultures, spheroids, organoids, and microfluidic "organ-on-a-chip" devices.

Applications

Cultured cells are used to test drug efficacy and toxicity, produce monoclonal antibodies and viral vaccines, study cancer and infection, generate stem-cell therapies, and manufacture engineered tissues. In industry, large bioreactors of CHO cells produce therapeutic proteins worth billions of dollars annually.
