Choked flow

Choked flow is a phenomenon in fluid dynamics in which the mass flow rate through a restriction, such as a nozzle, orifice, or valve, becomes independent of the downstream pressure. It occurs when the fluid velocity at the narrowest point (the throat or vena contracta) reaches the local speed of sound, i.e. Mach 1.

Mechanism

For a compressible gas flowing from a high-pressure reservoir through a converging nozzle, lowering the downstream pressure increases the flow until the throat reaches sonic velocity. Beyond that point, further reductions in downstream pressure cannot propagate upstream, because pressure disturbances travel only at the speed of sound; the mass flow therefore "chokes" at a maximum value determined by upstream pressure, temperature, and throat area.

Applications

Choked flow governs the design of rocket nozzles, steam and gas safety relief valves, flow-control orifices, and natural gas pipelines. Engineers use it to size pressure-relief systems, since the maximum discharge rate through a relief valve is fixed by choked-flow conditions at the valve throat.
