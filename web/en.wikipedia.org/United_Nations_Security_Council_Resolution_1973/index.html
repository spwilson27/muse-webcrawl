United Nations Security Council Resolution 1973, adopted on 17 March 2011, formed the legal basis for military intervention in the Libyan Civil War. The resolution authorized member states to take "all necessary measures" to protect civilians, imposed a no-fly zone over Libya, and tightened sanctions against the regime of Muammar Gaddafi, while excluding a foreign occupation force on Libyan territory.

Background

In February 2011, protests in Libya inspired by the wider Arab Spring were met with violent repression by the Gaddafi government, escalating into civil war. On 26 February 2011 the Security Council adopted Resolution 1970, imposing an arms embargo, travel bans, and asset freezes, and referring the situation to the International Criminal Court. As government forces advanced on the rebel-held city of Benghazi and Gaddafi threatened to show "no mercy", France, Lebanon, and the United Kingdom drafted a further resolution, with support from the United States and a call for a no-fly zone from the Arab League.

Key provisions

  • Demanded an immediate ceasefire and an end to attacks on civilians
  • Authorized member states, acting nationally or through regional organizations, to take all necessary measures to protect civilians and civilian populated areas
  • Established a ban on all flights in Libyan airspace — a no-fly zone — except humanitarian flights
  • Authorized member states to enforce the arms embargo and inspect cargo
  • Strengthened asset freezes and extended the travel ban to additional individuals
  • Explicitly excluded "a foreign occupation force of any form on any part of Libyan territory"

Vote

The resolution passed with ten votes in favour and five abstentions — Brazil, China, Germany, India, and Russia — and no votes against. Both Russia and China, which hold veto power, chose to abstain; the Arab League's support for intervention was widely seen as decisive in preventing a veto.

Implementation

Military operations began on 19 March 2011, when French aircraft struck Libyan armoured columns approaching Benghazi. The United States and United Kingdom launched cruise missile strikes under Operation Odyssey Dawn, and on 31 March NATO assumed command of all operations as Operation Unified Protector. The campaign ended on 31 October 2011, following the death of Gaddafi and the declaration of liberation by the National Transitional Council.
