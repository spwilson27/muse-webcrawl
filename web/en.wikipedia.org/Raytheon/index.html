Raytheon is an American aerospace and defence business, now one of the three principal business units of RTX Corporation (formerly Raytheon Technologies). Founded in 1922 as the American Appliance Company in Cambridge, Massachusetts, the company became a major defence contractor, producing guided missiles, radars, sensors and air and missile defence systems.

History

The company took the name Raytheon in 1925, from words meaning "light of the gods", for its rectifier tubes. During the Second World War it mass-produced magnetrons for radar—its engineer Percy Spencer discovered the heating effect of microwaves while working on them, leading to the Amana Radarange microwave oven in 1947. In the Cold War Raytheon developed the AIM-9 Sidewinder, AIM-120 AMRAAM and MIM-104 Patriot missile systems. Raytheon merged with the defence businesses of Hughes Aircraft in 1997.

RTX era

In April 2020 Raytheon Company merged with United Technologies to form Raytheon Technologies, combining Raytheon with Collins Aerospace and Pratt & Whitney. In 2023 the merged company reorganised into three businesses—Collins Aerospace, Pratt & Whitney and Raytheon—and renamed itself RTX. The Raytheon business, headquartered in Arlington, Virginia, remains among the world's largest producers of guided missiles and air defence systems, including Patriot, NASAMS, Tomahawk and Standard Missile interceptors.
