Psychrophile

Psychrophiles or cryophiles are extremophilic organisms capable of growth and reproduction in low temperatures, ranging from about −20 °C to +10 °C. They are found in permanently cold environments such as polar regions, the deep sea, permafrost and alpine glaciers. True psychrophiles have an optimal growth temperature of about 15 °C or lower, whereas psychrotolerant organisms can grow at low temperatures but grow faster at moderate ones.

Adaptations

Psychrophiles maintain membrane fluidity by increasing the proportion of unsaturated fatty acids, produce cold-active enzymes with flexible structures that trade stability for activity at low temperature, and synthesise antifreeze proteins and cryoprotectants that limit ice crystal damage. Their enzymes are of industrial interest for cold-wash detergents and food processing.

Examples

Examples include the bacterium Psychrobacter, the snow alga Chlamydomonas nivalis, which colours snow red, and methanogens recovered from Siberian permafrost. Psychrophiles are also studied by astrobiologists as analogues for possible life in cold environments such as Mars and Europa.
