Chloroform
Formula: CHCl3
Name: Trichloromethane
Appearance: Colourless, sweet-smelling liquid
Boiling point: 61.2 °C

Chloroform (trichloromethane) is an organic compound with formula CHCl3. A colourless, dense, sweet-smelling liquid, it was historically used as an anaesthetic and is now mainly an industrial solvent and chemical intermediate.

History

Prepared independently around 1831, chloroform was adopted as a surgical anaesthetic by James Young Simpson in 1847 and famously used in childbirth, but its narrow safety margin and links to sudden death and liver damage led to its replacement by safer agents.

Production and uses

Chloroform is produced by chlorination of methane. Its largest use is in making hydrochlorofluorocarbon refrigerants and fluoropolymers such as PTFE; it is also a laboratory solvent. It is a suspected carcinogen and is regulated as a hazardous substance; traces form when drinking water is chlorinated.
