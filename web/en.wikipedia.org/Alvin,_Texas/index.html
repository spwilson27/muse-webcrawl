Alvin, Texas

Alvin is a city in northeastern Brazoria County, Texas, United States, within the Houston metropolitan area. Its population was about 27,000 at the 2020 census. The city is best known nationally as the hometown of Baseball Hall of Fame pitcher Nolan Ryan.

History

Alvin grew up around a Santa Fe Railway shipping point established in the early 1880s; the settlement was first called Morgan, after Santa Fe official Alvin Morgan, and the name Alvin was adopted when the town was incorporated in 1893. The surrounding prairie supported cattle ranching and farming, including fig orchards and truck crops shipped by rail.

Alvin holds a notorious weather record: during Tropical Storm Claudette in July 1979, 43 inches (about 1,090 mm) of rain fell in 24 hours — the United States record for 24-hour rainfall. The city has also been affected by Gulf hurricanes, including Ike in 2008.

Geography and community

Alvin lies on the flat Gulf Coastal Plain along State Highway 35 and the BNSF Railway line between Houston and Galveston. Alvin Community College, founded in 1948, serves the city. Nolan Ryan, who grew up in Alvin, is commemorated by the Nolan Ryan Center and a statue on the college grounds, and the Nolan Ryan Foundation is based in the area.
