Pantanal

The Pantanal is a natural region encompassing the world's largest tropical wetland area, and one of the world's largest wetlands of any kind. It is located mostly within the Brazilian states of Mato Grosso and Mato Grosso do Sul, extending into parts of Bolivia and Paraguay, and covers an estimated 140,000 to 195,000 square kilometres depending on the seasonal flood.

Hydrology

The Pantanal is a vast, gently sloping depression fed by the upper Paraguay River and its tributaries, including the Cuiabá, Taquari and Miranda rivers. During the rainy season (roughly November to March), up to 80% of the floodplains are submerged; the water then drains extremely slowly, sustaining marshes, lagoons and seasonally flooded grasslands and forests through the dry season.

Wildlife

The ecosystem is one of the most biologically rich in the Americas, famed for its dense and easily observed wildlife: jaguars, capybaras (the world's largest rodent), giant otters, marsh deer, caimans by the millions, giant anteaters, hyacinth macaws, jabiru storks and hundreds of fish species. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and a Ramsar wetland of international importance.

Threats

Most of the Pantanal is privately owned cattle ranch land. Threats include deforestation in the surrounding highlands, sedimentation of rivers, proposed waterway projects, and increasingly severe droughts and wildfires, notably the devastating fires of 2020.
