Thinner

A paint thinner is a solvent used to thin oil-based paints and varnishes, to adjust their viscosity for application, and to clean painting equipment after use. Common thinners include white spirit (mineral spirits), turpentine, acetone, naphtha and toluene, as well as proprietary blends. Lacquer thinner is a mixture of solvents, typically including acetone and esters, formulated to dissolve lacquers.

Uses

Thinning reduces the thickness of paint so that it flows and levels properly when brushed, rolled or sprayed. Thinners are also used to degrease surfaces and to remove dried paint from brushes and tools. The thinner must be compatible with the coating: water-based paints are thinned with water, not solvent.

Health and safety

Most paint thinners are flammable and release volatile organic compounds (VOCs). Inhalation of vapours can cause dizziness, headache and, with prolonged or deliberate abuse, serious organ damage; inhalant abuse of thinners is a recognised public-health problem. Regulations in many jurisdictions limit the VOC content of coatings and require labelling of solvent hazards. Water-based and low-VOC alternatives have become increasingly common.
