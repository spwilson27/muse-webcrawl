Output gap

The output gap is the difference between actual output of an economy and its potential output — the maximum sustainable level of production given available labour, capital and technology. It is usually expressed as a percentage of potential GDP.

Interpretation

A positive output gap occurs when actual output exceeds potential, often associated with inflationary pressure; a negative gap indicates slack, unemployment above its natural rate, and disinflationary pressure. Central banks and finance ministries use estimates of the gap in setting monetary and fiscal policy.

Measurement

Potential output is unobservable and must be estimated, using production-function approaches or statistical filters, so real-time output gap estimates are uncertain and frequently revised.
