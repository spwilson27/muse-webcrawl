Mount Aragats

Elevation4,090 m (13,420 ft)
ListingCountry high point
LocationAragatsotn Province, Armenia
TypeStratovolcano

Mount Aragats is an isolated four-peaked stratovolcano in northwestern Armenia and the country's highest point, rising to 4,090 metres (13,420 ft) at its northern summit. It stands about 40 kilometres northwest of the capital, Yerevan, and dominates the Aragatsotn Province landscape, visible from much of the Ararat plain.

Geography

The volcano's four summits surround a crater some 400 metres deep and about 3 kilometres in circuit; the northern summit is the highest, followed by the western, eastern and southern peaks. A small glacial lake, Kari Lake, lies on the mountain's southern flank at about 3,190 metres. Aragats formed in the Pliocene to Pleistocene epochs; no historical eruptions are recorded.

On the mountain

Near Kari Lake stands a cosmic-ray research station of the Yerevan Physics Institute, founded in 1943. On the southern slopes, at about 2,140 metres, the Byurakan Observatory, founded by Viktor Ambartsumian in 1946, is a major Armenian astronomical institution. The seventh-century fortress of Amberd and the church of Vahramashen sit on the mountain's southeastern slopes. In 2009 a monumental Armenian alphabet installation of carved stone letters was created on the eastern slope to mark the 1,600th anniversary of the Armenian alphabet.

Legends

According to tradition, Saint Gregory the Illuminator prayed on the summit, where a lamp miraculously burned; one of the mountain's features is still called the Lamp of the Illuminator. Armenian folk etymology connects the name with Ara the Beautiful, the legendary king.
