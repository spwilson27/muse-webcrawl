Nephthys

Nephthys (Greek: Νέφθυς) is a goddess of ancient Egyptian religion, a member of the Ennead of Heliopolis. Her Egyptian name, Nebet-Hut, means "Lady of the Temple Enclosure". She was the daughter of the earth god Geb and the sky goddess Nut, sister of Isis, Osiris, and Set, and the wife of Set. In art she is shown as a woman wearing a headdress formed from the hieroglyphs of her name, or as a kite (a bird of prey) mourning at the bier of the dead.

Mythology

Despite her marriage to the chaotic Set, Nephthys is portrayed in the Osiris myth as a loyal ally of Isis. After Set murdered and dismembered Osiris, Nephthys helped Isis search for the body, lament over it, and reassemble it; the two sisters, often depicted as kites at either end of the coffin, became the archetypal divine mourners, and Nephthys was invoked in funerary texts as a protector of the dead and of the canopic jars. By Set she was sometimes said to be the mother of the jackal-headed funerary god Anubis, though other traditions name different parents.

Role and worship

Nephthys was associated with death, the night, mourning, and childbirth, and with the protection of the deceased — a benevolent funerary role quite unlike her husband's destructive character. Unlike Isis and Osiris she had few independent temples, but she was widely worshipped as part of the funerary cult, appearing on coffins, in the Book of the Dead, and as one of the four goddesses guarding the shrine of canopic jars and the coffin of Tutankhamun.
