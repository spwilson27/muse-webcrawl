Plantar fasciitis is a disorder of the plantar fascia, the thick band of connective tissue that runs along the sole of the foot from the heel bone (calcaneus) to the toes and supports the arch. It is one of the most common causes of heel pain, affecting roughly one in ten people at some point in life, and is especially frequent in runners and in adults between 40 and 60 years of age.

Signs and symptoms

The characteristic symptom is a sharp, stabbing pain on the underside of the heel, typically worst with the first steps after getting out of bed in the morning or after long periods of sitting, and easing with activity before returning after prolonged standing or walking. Pain is usually felt near the medial calcaneal tubercle, where the fascia attaches to the heel bone.

Causes

Plantar fasciitis is generally considered a degenerative irritation (fasciosis) from repetitive microtrauma and tensile overload rather than a primarily inflammatory condition. Risk factors include prolonged weight-bearing, sudden increases in running mileage, obesity, pes planus (flat feet) or pes cavus, tight Achilles tendon or calf muscles, and occupations requiring long standing on hard surfaces.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is usually clinical, based on history and localized tenderness. Imaging is reserved for atypical or persistent cases: ultrasound may show thickening of the fascia, and radiographs sometimes reveal a heel spur, which also occurs in many pain-free people and is not itself the cause of pain. Differential diagnosis includes calcaneal stress fracture, tarsal tunnel syndrome, fat pad atrophy, and inflammatory arthropathies.

Treatment

Most cases resolve within months with conservative care: relative rest, ice, plantar fascia-specific stretching and calf stretching, supportive footwear and orthotic insoles, night splints, and nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs. For persistent cases, options include corticosteroid injections (used cautiously because of rupture and fat-pad risks), extracorporeal shockwave therapy, and, rarely, surgical partial plantar fascia release.

Epidemiology

Plantar fasciitis accounts for a large share of foot complaints seen in primary care and sports medicine; an estimated two million Americans are treated for it annually. It occurs in both athletic and sedentary populations.
