Migrant worker

DefinitionA person who migrates for employment, within or across countries
Global estimate~169 million international migrant workers (ILO)
Key instrumentUN Migrant Workers Convention (1990)

A migrant worker is a person who migrates from one country to another (or within their own country) with a view to being employed otherwise than on their own account. The term is defined in the United Nations Convention on the Protection of the Rights of All Migrant Workers and Members of Their Families (1990). Migrant workers frequently send remittances home, which constitute a major source of income for many developing economies.

Types

Migration for work may be temporary or permanent, documented or undocumented, and skilled or low-skilled. Seasonal agricultural laborers, construction workers, domestic workers, and professionals moving under intra-company transfers all fall under the broad category. Some countries admit workers through guest worker programs tied to specific employers, which can increase workers' vulnerability.

Issues and rights

Migrant workers may face exploitation, unsafe working conditions, wage theft, discrimination, and barriers to healthcare and legal recourse. International frameworks such as the ILO's migration conventions and the Global Compact for Migration aim to protect their rights, though ratification of the UN convention is concentrated among countries of origin rather than destination states.

Economic significance

According to the International Labour Organization, international migrant workers number roughly 169 million, about 5 percent of the global workforce. Remittance flows to low- and middle-income countries exceed foreign direct investment in many years, underscoring the economic weight of labor migration.
