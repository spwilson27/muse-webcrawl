Henry Jenkins III (born June 4, 1958) is an American media scholar and Provost Professor of Communication, Journalism, and Cinematic Arts at the University of Southern California (USC). He is widely regarded as one of the founding figures of fan studies and is best known for his work on participatory culture, transmedia storytelling, and media convergence.

Career

Jenkins earned his bachelor's degree in political science and journalism from Georgia State University, a master's degree in communication studies from the University of Iowa, and a PhD from the University of Wisconsin–Madison. Before joining USC in 2009, he spent two decades at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, where he directed the Comparative Media Studies graduate program and co-founded the Education Arcade and the Convergence Culture Consortium.

Major works

His book Textual Poachers: Television Fans and Participatory Culture (1992) helped legitimate the academic study of fandom, portraying fans as active creators rather than passive consumers. Convergence Culture: Where Old and New Media Collide (2006) popularized the concepts of media convergence and transmedia storytelling. Later works include Participatory Culture in a Networked Era (2015) and Popular Culture and the Civic Imagination (2020).

Concepts

Jenkins is credited with popularizing "transmedia storytelling," the practice of dispersing a narrative across multiple media platforms, and with developing the idea of "spreadable media." He has also been a prominent advocate for media literacy education and for the positive civic potential of fan communities.
