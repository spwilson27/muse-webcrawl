Standard cubic foot

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A gas meter measuring standard cubic feet

A standard cubic foot (scf) is a unit of measure used in the natural gas industry (and other industrial gas applications) to quantify the amount of gas in a volume of one cubic foot at standardised temperature and pressure conditions. Because gas is compressible, a cubic foot of volume contains different amounts of substance depending on temperature and pressure; the "standard" convention allows consistent accounting and trade.

Definitions

There is no single universal standard: common conventions include 60 °F (about 15.6 °C) at 14.696 psi (1 atm) in US customary practice, while other industries use 0 °C or 15 °C and 100 kPa. The conditions used must therefore be stated. Related units include the standard cubic metre, thousand standard cubic feet (Mscf), and million standard cubic feet (MMscf). The corresponding quantity in SI is sometimes expressed via the normal cubic metre.

Relation to moles

By the ideal gas law, one standard cubic foot of an ideal gas at commonly used US conditions contains roughly 1.2 moles, enabling conversion between volumetric sales units and mass or energy content.
