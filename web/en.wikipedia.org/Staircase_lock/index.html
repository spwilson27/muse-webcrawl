Staircase lock

Staircase lock
Type: Canal lock arrangement
Feature: Chambers share gates directly
Example: Foxton Locks, England

A staircase lock (or lock staircase) is an arrangement of canal locks in which the top gate of one chamber is also the bottom gate of the next, so boats pass directly from one chamber to another without an intervening pound.

Operation

Staircases save space and water-works construction on steep gradients but are slow and require careful sequencing: ascending and descending boats cannot easily pass within the staircase. Famous examples include the Foxton Locks on the Grand Union Canal and the locks at Bingley Five Rise on the Leeds and Liverpool Canal in England.

Comparison

In a flight of locks, by contrast, chambers are separated by short pounds of water, allowing more flexible operation.
