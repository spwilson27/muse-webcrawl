The Glastonbury Festival (formally the Glastonbury Festival of Contemporary Performing Arts and known colloquially as Glasto) is a five-day festival of contemporary performing arts held near Pilton, Somerset, England, in most summers. In addition to contemporary music, the festival hosts dance, comedy, theatre, circus, cabaret, and other arts. Leading pop and rock artists have headlined, alongside thousands of others appearing on smaller stages and performance areas. Films and albums have been recorded at the festival, and it receives extensive television and newspaper coverage.

Glastonbury is attended by around 200,000 people, making it the largest greenfield music and performing arts festival in the world. It is organised by dairy farmer Michael Eavis on his Worthy Farm. Most festival staff are unpaid volunteers, which helps the festival raise millions of pounds for charity organisations including Oxfam, Greenpeace, and WaterAid.

History

The first festival — then called the Pilton Pop, Folk & Blues Festival — was held on 19 September 1970, the day after Jimi Hendrix died; about 1,500 people paid £1 (with free milk from the farm) to see acts including T. Rex, who replaced headliners The Kinks. In 1971 the festival took on its hippie-era character and introduced the first Pyramid Stage, built from scaffolding and metal sheeting as a replica of the Great Pyramid of Giza. The festival grew through the 1980s and 1990s, with the CND as a beneficiary, and in the 1990s became a major televised event. A perimeter "super-fence" erected in 2002 ended the era of mass gatecrashing. Periodic fallow years (e.g. 2012, 2018) allow the farm and village to recover; the 2020 and 2021 festivals were cancelled due to the COVID-19 pandemic, replaced by a livestreamed event from Worthy Farm in 2021.

The site

The festival occupies roughly 1,500 acres of farmland. Major performance areas include the Pyramid Stage (headliners), the Other Stage, West Holts (formerly the Jazz World), the John Peel Stage (renamed Woodsies in 2023), and The Park. Night-time areas such as Shangri-La, Block9, and the South-East Corner host electronic music, art installations, and immersive theatrics. The Green Fields area emphasises crafts, healing, and environmental campaigning, reflecting the festival's countercultural roots.

Culture and charities

Glastonbury is known for its mud in wet years, for legendary headline sets (in recent decades including David Bowie in 2000, Beyoncé in 2011, and Paul McCartney in 2022), for surprise performances, and for its "Glastonbury spirit" of communal celebration. Ticket sales typically sell out within minutes. The festival donates a large share of profits to partner charities and local causes.
