Fee-for-service

Fee-for-service (FFS) is a payment model in which services are paid for separately and unbundled. In health care, it gives an incentive for physicians to provide more treatments because payment is dependent on the quantity of care, rather than its quality or outcome.

Fee-for-service is the traditional model of paying for medical care and remains common in many systems. Critics argue it encourages overutilisation, fragmentation and rising costs, and health policy has increasingly moved toward alternatives such as capitation, bundled payments and value-based purchasing, in which payment is linked to outcomes and cost-effectiveness. In other professional contexts, such as law and consulting, fee-for-service simply denotes charging for each item of work performed.
