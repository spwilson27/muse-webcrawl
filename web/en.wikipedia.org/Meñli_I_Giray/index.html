Meñli I Giray

Meñli I Giray (1445–1515) was Khan of the Crimean Khanate three times — in 1466, 1469–1475, and 1478–1515 — and a central figure in the consolidation of the Crimean Tatar state. He was a son of Hacı I Giray, founder of the Giray dynasty.

Reign

After struggles with his brothers, Meñli was restored to power with Ottoman support in 1478, and the Khanate became a vassal of the Ottoman Empire, a status that endured until 1774. In 1502 his forces destroyed the remnants of the Golden Horde's Great Horde, capturing its capital Sarai — an event often taken as the final end of the Golden Horde.

Relations with Muscovy and Poland-Lithuania

Meñli maintained a long alliance with Ivan III of Moscow against the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, campaigning in Ukrainian lands, including the sack of Kyiv in 1482. Relations soured late in his reign, and Crimean raids on Muscovite territory began. He married Nur Sultan, an influential khanum who conducted diplomacy with Moscow and Kazan.

Legacy

Meñli built the Zincirli Madrasa and the khan's palace complex at Salaçıq near modern Bakhchysarai. His sons included Mehmed I Giray, who succeeded him, and Sahib I Giray. He is buried in the türbe at Salaçıq.
