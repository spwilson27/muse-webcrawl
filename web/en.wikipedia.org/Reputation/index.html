Reputation

A reputation is the social evaluation of a person, group, or organisation with respect to some criterion, generally resulting from the distribution of opinions within a community. Reputation is a ubiquitous, spontaneous, and highly efficient mechanism of social control.

Social science perspectives

Sociologists and economists treat reputation as a signal of expected future behaviour built from past actions. In game theory, reputation effects help sustain cooperation, as in repeated games where players forgo short-term gains to preserve a trustworthy image. Pierre Bourdieu treated reputation as a form of symbolic capital.

Online reputation

With the rise of the internet, reputation systems — ratings, reviews, and scores — became central to e-commerce, social media, and the sharing economy. Organisations also practise reputation management, monitoring and influencing public perception.
