The Eskaleut languages (also called Eskimo–Aleut) are a language family native to the Arctic regions of Alaska, northern Canada, Greenland, and the Chukotka Peninsula of far-eastern Siberia. The family divides into two branches: the Eskimo branch (Inuit and Yupik languages) and the Aleut (Unangam Tunuu) branch, spoken in the Aleutian Islands.

Classification

The Inuit languages form a dialect continuum stretching from Alaska across Arctic Canada to Greenland, including Inupiaq, Inuvialuktun, Inuktitut, and Greenlandic (Kalaallisut). The Yupik languages of western and southwestern Alaska and Siberian Yupik are more internally diverse. Aleut, though clearly related, diverged much earlier and is sometimes treated separately.

Features

Eskaleut languages are famously polysynthetic: long words are built from a root plus chains of suffixes, so that a single word can express the content of an entire sentence in languages such as English. They are also ergative in alignment and use both prefixes (in Aleut and some Yupik varieties) and suffixes in word formation.

Status

Many languages in the family are endangered, though Greenlandic is official in Greenland and Inuktitut has official status in Nunavut and the Northwest Territories of Canada.
