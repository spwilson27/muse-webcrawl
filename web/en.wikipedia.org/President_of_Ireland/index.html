The President of Ireland (Irish: Uachtarán na hÉireann) is the head of state of Ireland and the supreme commander of the Defence Forces. The president is directly elected by citizens for a term of seven years and can be re-elected only once. The presidency is a largely ceremonial office, but the president does exercise certain limited powers, often at their own discretion, such as referring bills to the Supreme Court for a ruling on constitutionality. The official residence is Áras an Uachtaráin in Phoenix Park, Dublin.

History

The office was established by the Constitution of Ireland adopted in 1937, replacing the Governor-General of the Irish Free State. Douglas Hyde, a founder of the Gaelic League, became the first president in 1938. Subsequent presidents have included Éamon de Valera, Cearbhall Ó Dálaigh, Patrick Hillery, Mary Robinson (1990–1997, the first woman president), Mary McAleese, and Michael D. Higgins, elected in 2011 and re-elected in 2018. Mary Robinson resigned shortly before the end of her term to become United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights.

Functions and powers

The president formally appoints the Taoiseach (prime minister) on the nomination of Dáil Éireann, appoints other ministers on the Taoiseach's nomination, summons and dissolves the Dáil (though a Taoiseach who has lost the Dáil's confidence may be refused a dissolution), signs bills into law, and represents Ireland abroad on state visits. As an ordinary citizen's safeguard, the president may, after consulting the Council of State, refer a bill to the Supreme Court under Article 26, and may convene a meeting of either House of the Oireachtas. If the presidency falls vacant, a Presidential Commission — the Chief Justice and the chairs of both Houses — exercises the functions.

Election

Candidates must be Irish citizens aged at least 35, nominated by at least 20 members of the Oireachtas or by at least four county or city councils, or (if a former or retiring president) self-nominated. Elections use instant-runoff voting (the single transferable vote in a single seat). If only one candidate is nominated, no poll is held.
