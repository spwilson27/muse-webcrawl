Music sequencer

A music sequencer (or simply sequencer) is a device or application software that can record, edit, or play back music in the form of note and performance information, typically using MIDI data rather than recorded audio. Sequencers underpin modern music production, from hardware drum machines to full digital audio workstations.

History

Early sequencers were analog devices, such as the step sequencers built into modular synthesizers in the 1960s, which stepped through a fixed series of control voltages. The introduction of MIDI in 1983 allowed digital sequencers to control multiple synthesizers precisely. Dedicated hardware sequencers from companies such as Roland and Akai — notably the MPC series — became central to electronic dance music and hip hop.

Types

  • Step sequencers divide time into a grid of steps, each triggering a note or sound.
  • Piano-roll sequencers display notes as bars on a grid of pitch against time, the dominant editing view in DAWs.
  • Trackers present music as columns of hexadecimal note and effect data scrolling vertically.
  • Software sequencers integrated into digital audio workstations such as Ableton Live, FL Studio, Logic Pro, and Cubase combine MIDI sequencing with audio recording.

Function

A sequencer stores events — note on/off, velocity, controller movements — against a timeline measured in beats. Events can be quantized to correct timing, transposed, copied into loops, and routed to software instruments or external hardware.
