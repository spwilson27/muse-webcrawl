Narrow-body aircraft

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A narrow-body aircraft, also known as a single-aisle aircraft, is an airliner arranged along a single aisle permitting up to 6-abreast seating in a cabin below 4 metres (13 ft) of width. In contrast, a wide-body aircraft is a larger airliner designed with multiple aisles and a fuselage diameter of more than 5 metres (16 ft), allowing 7- to 11-abreast seating.

Characteristics

Narrow-body airliners typically seat between about 100 and 240 passengers and are used for short- and medium-haul flights, though newer types such as the Airbus A321XLR are designed for longer routes. The most common seating layout in economy class is 3+3, as on the Boeing 737 and Airbus A320 families, the two best-selling jet airliner families in history.

History

Early jet airliners such as the de Havilland Comet, Boeing 707, and Douglas DC-8 were narrow-bodies. The type came to dominate commercial aviation after deregulation and the growth of hub-and-spoke and later point-to-point networks. Modern narrow-bodies include the Boeing 737 MAX and Airbus A320neo families, powered by fuel-efficient engines such as the CFM LEAP.

Market

Single-aisle aircraft account for the majority of the world's commercial fleet and of new deliveries by number. Airlines value their lower trip costs and flexibility on routes with moderate demand.
