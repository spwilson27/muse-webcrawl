Wind power in Scotland is the country's most developed renewable electricity technology. Scotland's exposed coasts and hills give it some of the best wind resources in Europe, and wind farms — onshore and, increasingly, offshore — generate the equivalent of a large and growing share of Scottish electricity consumption.

Development

Commercial wind development began in the 1990s and accelerated under UK support schemes and devolved Scottish planning policy favouring renewables. Whitelee Wind Farm, near Glasgow, with 215 turbines and 539 MW, was for years the largest onshore wind farm in Europe. Offshore development followed in the North Sea and Moray Firth: Beatrice (588 MW) and Seagreen (1,075 MW) are among the largest projects, and the Hywind Scotland floating array, commissioned in 2017, was the world's first floating wind farm. On windy days wind output frequently exceeds total Scottish electricity demand, with surplus exported to the rest of Great Britain.

Policy and debate

The Scottish Government has set targets for net zero by 2045 and for expanding onshore capacity, and publishes statistics showing renewables generating the equivalent of nearly all gross electricity consumption in some recent years. Development has also attracted debate over landscape and visual impact, effects on peatland and birds, grid-connection delays, and "constraint payments" made when the transmission network cannot carry wind output south.
