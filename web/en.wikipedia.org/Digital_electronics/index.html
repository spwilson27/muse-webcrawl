Digital electronics

Digital electronics is a field of electronics involving the study of digital signals and the engineering of devices that use or produce them. This is in contrast to analog electronics, which work primarily with continuous signals. Despite the name, digital electronics designs include important analog design considerations, and the boundary between the two is not always sharp.

An integrated circuit, the basic building block of modern digital systems.

Representation of signals

Digital circuits represent information using discrete values, most often two voltage levels interpreted as binary 0 and 1. Logic gates combine these signals according to Boolean algebra to perform operations such as AND, OR, and NOT. Because signals are discrete, digital circuits are relatively resistant to noise and can be cascaded to build complex systems without degrading the signal.

Components and systems

Basic digital building blocks include logic gates, multiplexers, flip-flops, registers, and counters. Combinational circuits produce outputs that depend only on current inputs, while sequential circuits have memory and depend on input history. Large digital systems such as microprocessors are built from millions or billions of transistors, typically implemented in CMOS technology. A central component of many digital circuits is the diode-based or transistor-based switch.

Design

Engineers describe digital systems using hardware description languages such as Verilog and VHDL, then synthesize and verify designs before fabrication. Design concerns include clock distribution, timing closure, power consumption, and minimization of logic.
