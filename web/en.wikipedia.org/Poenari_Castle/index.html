Poenari Castle

Type: Fortress

Location: Argeș County, Romania

Built: 13th century; rebuilt 15th century

Associated with: Vlad the Impaler

The ruined keep of Poenari Castle, perched on a cliff in the Făgăraș foothills.

Poenari Castle, also known as Poenari Citadel (Romanian: Cetatea Poenari), is a ruined castle in Romania near the village of Arefu in Argeș County, on a canyon formed by the Argeș River valley. It stands on a steep cliff on the right side of the Transfăgărășan road in the foothills of the Făgăraș Mountains.

History

The castle was first erected in the early 13th century by the rulers of Wallachia. In the 14th century it was one of the main citadels of the Basarab rulers. In the following decades the fortress changed hands several times and was eventually abandoned.

In the 15th century, Vlad III Dracula (Vlad the Impaler), recognizing the strategic potential of a fortress perched on a steep precipice, repaired and consolidated the structure, making it one of his main strongholds. According to legend, Vlad used prisoner labor — including captured boyars and their families — to rebuild the castle, working them until, as the story goes, their clothes fell off.

Description

The ruin is accessible by climbing 1,480 concrete stairs to the top. The surviving walls and towers, five in number, are built of stone bound with mortar. The citadel is frequently contrasted with Bran Castle, which is marketed to tourists as "Dracula's Castle" despite having only a tenuous connection to Vlad; Poenari is regarded by historians as the genuine castle of Vlad III.
