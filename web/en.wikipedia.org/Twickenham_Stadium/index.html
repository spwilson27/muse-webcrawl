Twickenham Stadium in Twickenham, southwest London, is a rugby union stadium owned by the Rugby Football Union (RFU), the governing body for the sport in England. With a capacity of 82,000, it is the largest dedicated rugby union stadium in the world and the home of the England national rugby union team. It is informally known as "HQ" or "Twickers", and the ground famously resounds to the song "Swing Low, Sweet Chariot" at England matches.

History

The RFU bought the site — a market garden nicknamed the "Cabbage Patch" — in 1907, and the first international was played there in 1910, England defeating Wales. Successive redevelopments, culminating in the completion of the South Stand in 2008, transformed it into a modern four-tier bowl. Twickenham has hosted the Rugby World Cup final in 1991 and 2015, and also stages the Premiership final, the Varsity Match, and major concerts. It houses the World Rugby Museum.
