Johann Friedrich Karl Asperger (18 February 1906 – 21 October 1980) was an Austrian physician and paediatrician. He described a pattern of behaviour in children that later came to be called Asperger syndrome, a diagnosis incorporated into autism spectrum disorder in modern classification systems.

Work on autism

Working at the University Children's Clinic in Vienna, Asperger published in 1944 a study of four boys he described as having "autistic psychopathy", characterised by impaired social interaction and nonverbal communication, intense narrow interests, and often fluent but pedantic speech, alongside average or above-average intelligence. His work, published in German during the Second World War, remained little known in the English-speaking world until Lorna Wing's 1981 paper introduced the term "Asperger's syndrome". The diagnosis entered DSM-IV in 1994 and was folded into autism spectrum disorder in DSM-5 (2013).

Conduct under Nazism

Historical research published in 2018 by Herwig Czech documented Asperger's cooperation with the Nazi regime's child "euthanasia" programme, including referring children to the Am Spiegelgrund clinic in Vienna, where children were killed. These findings have prompted debate over the use of his name in diagnosis.

Legacy

Asperger spent his career in Vienna, holding the chair of paediatrics at the University of Vienna from 1962 until his retirement. Whatever the judgment of his wartime record, his clinical descriptions remain a landmark in the history of autism research.
