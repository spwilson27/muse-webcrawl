Biathlon

Biathlon
Governing body: International Biathlon Union (IBU)
Olympic sport: since 1960
Equipment: cross-country skis, .22 calibre rifle

The biathlon is a winter sport that combines cross-country skiing and rifle shooting. It is treated as a race, with contestants skiing through a cross-country trail whose distance is divided into shooting rounds. The shooting rounds are not timed per se, but depending on the competition, missed shots result in extra distance or time being added to the contestant's total.

History

The sport has its roots in the skiing traditions of Scandinavia, where military patrols combined skiing and marksmanship. A military patrol competition appeared at the 1924 Winter Olympics, and the modern biathlon was developed in the 1950s. It became an Olympic sport at the 1960 Winter Olympics in Squaw Valley, with women's events added in 1992.

Competition formats

Major formats include the individual (20 km for men, 15 km for women, with four shooting bouts and one-minute penalties per miss), the sprint, the pursuit, the mass start, and the relay. Athletes shoot at five targets from 50 metres, alternating between prone and standing positions. In most formats a missed target requires skiing a 150-metre penalty loop.

Equipment

Biathletes use lightweight cross-country skis and a small-bore rifle chambered for .22 LR ammunition, carried on the back during the skiing sections. Targets are 45 mm in diameter when shooting prone and 115 mm standing.
