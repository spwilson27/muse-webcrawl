Gerrard Winstanley (1609–1676) was an English Protestant religious reformer, political philosopher, and activist during the period of the Commonwealth of England. He was the founder and leader of the Diggers, who called themselves the True Levellers, a group that in 1649 began digging and planting common land at St George's Hill in Surrey in protest against the enclosure of the commons and the poverty of the landless poor.

Life

Winstanley was born in Wigan, Lancashire, and worked in the cloth trade in London before being bankrupted during the Civil War and moving to Surrey, where he worked as a cowherd. In a series of pamphlets from 1648 onward he developed a radical theology and social philosophy grounded in the idea that the earth was a "common treasury" given to all. On 1 April 1649 the Diggers began cultivating St George's Hill; the colony was harassed by local landowners and soldiers and was eventually driven off, with further colonies elsewhere also suppressed by 1650.

Ideas

Winstanley's writings, especially The New Law of Righteousness (1649) and The Law of Freedom in a Platform (1652), dedicated to Oliver Cromwell, advocated common ownership of land, the abolition of buying and selling, and a commonwealth organised without money, landlords, or a professional clergy. Historians have often described him as an early advocate of communism and agrarian socialism, though his arguments were framed in religious language. His works were largely forgotten until the late nineteenth century and have since influenced socialist and green movements.
