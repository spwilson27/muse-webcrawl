Varnish

Varnish is a clear transparent hard protective coating or film. It is not a stain, and usually has a yellowish shade from the manufacturing process and materials used, but it may also be pigmented as desired. It is sold commercially in various shades. Varnish is primarily used as a wood finish where, intended to be visible, it allows the natural grain of the wood to show through.

Composition

Traditional varnishes consist of a drying oil (such as linseed or tung oil), a resin (such as amber, copal, rosin, or shellac), and a volatile solvent or thinner. On application, the solvent evaporates and the oil and resin cure by oxidation and polymerisation to form a hard, glossy or satin film that resists water, heat and abrasion. Modern finishes include polyurethane and acrylic "varnishes".

Uses

Varnish is applied to furniture, flooring, musical instruments — the varnish of Cremonese violins is famous — boats (as spar varnish), and paintings, where a final varnish layer protects oil and acrylic works and saturates their colours. Related finishes include lacquer, shellac and French polish.
