Wood Buffalo National Park

Wood Buffalo National Park is the largest national park in Canada, covering 44,741 square kilometres (17,275 sq mi) of boreal forest, wetlands and plains in northeastern Alberta and the southern Northwest Territories. It was established in 1922 to protect the last free-roaming herds of wood bison, and was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983.

Wildlife and landscape

The park protects the largest free-roaming, self-regulating wood bison herd in the world, numbering several thousand animals. It contains the Peace–Athabasca Delta, one of the world's largest inland freshwater deltas and a Ramsar wetland, which provides nesting habitat for the endangered whooping crane — the park holds the species' only natural nesting site. Other wildlife includes moose, wolves, lynx, black bears and vast numbers of migratory waterfowl. Features include salt plains, sinkholes and karst formations, and some of the Earth's last remaining examples of pristine boreal plains.

Dark sky preserve and pressures

In 2013 the park was designated the world's largest dark-sky preserve. The park faces pressures from upstream hydroelectric development, oil sands activity affecting delta water levels, and climate change; UNESCO has repeatedly reviewed its conservation status.
