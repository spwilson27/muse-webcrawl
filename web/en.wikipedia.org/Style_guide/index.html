A style guide, or style manual, is a set of standards for the writing, editing, formatting, and design of documents within a particular field or organization. Style guides codify choices on matters where language allows variation — spelling, punctuation, capitalization, number style, abbreviations, and citation format — so that many writers and editors produce consistent text.

Types

General guides serve whole professions: journalists commonly follow the Associated Press Stylebook, book publishers in the United States rely on The Chicago Manual of Style, academics use the MLA Handbook (humanities) or the APA Publication Manual (social sciences), and newspapers such as The New York Times maintain their own manuals. Governments publish guides for official writing, and large organizations maintain house styles as part of their brand and design systems.

Contents

A typical style guide addresses mechanics (commas, hyphens, capitalization), word usage and preferred spellings, formatting of numbers and dates, citation and referencing systems, and, in design-oriented guides, typography, color, and layout. Modern digital style guides, such as those for software documentation and web content, also cover tone of voice, accessibility, and inclusive language.

Wikipedia's manual

Wikipedia itself maintains a Manual of Style governing article formatting, spelling conventions, and layout across the encyclopedia — an example of a large collaborative project using a style guide to unify the work of thousands of editors.
