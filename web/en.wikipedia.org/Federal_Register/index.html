The Federal Register is the official daily record of US federal agency rulemaking.

The Federal Register (FR or Fed. Reg.) is the official journal of the federal government of the United States that contains government agency rules, proposed rules, and public notices. It is published every weekday, excluding federal holidays, by the Office of the Federal Register of the National Archives and Records Administration.

Contents and process

Each issue is organized into four sections: presidential documents (executive orders and proclamations), rules and regulations (final rules with legal effect), proposed rules (notices of proposed rulemaking inviting public comment), and notices (meetings, grant opportunities, and other announcements). Under the Administrative Procedure Act, publication in the Federal Register provides official notice of agency action; most final rules take effect at least 30 days after publication. Once published, rules are codified in the Code of Federal Regulations, which is updated annually.

History and access

The Register was created by the Federal Register Act of 1935, after confusion over New Deal regulations — including a case in which the government enforced a rule it had rescinded — highlighted the need for a single authoritative source. The first issue appeared on 14 March 1936. Modern access is provided through govinfo and the federalregister.gov website, which offers searchable, structured versions of documents beginning in 1994 and unofficial previews of documents before publication. See also Code of Federal Regulations and Administrative Procedure Act.
