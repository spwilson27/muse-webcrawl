Politics of South Korea

SystemUnitary presidential constitutional republic
Head of state and governmentPresident
LegislatureNational Assembly (unicameral)
Current republicSixth Republic (since 1987)

The politics of South Korea take place in the framework of a presidential representative democratic republic, whereby the President is head of both state and government, and of a multi-party system. The government exercises executive power, legislative power is vested in the unicameral National Assembly, and the judiciary is independent.

History

The Republic of Korea, founded in 1948, passed through authoritarian periods under Syngman Rhee, Park Chung-hee, and Chun Doo-hwan, punctuated by military coups. Mass protests in June 1987 forced democratic reforms; the current Sixth Republic's constitution provides for a directly elected president limited to a single five-year term.

Political parties

South Korean politics is dominated by two broad blocs — a conservative bloc (currently the People Power Party) and a liberal bloc (currently the Democratic Party of Korea) — with high party turnover, frequent renaming and mergers, and strong regional voting patterns centred on the Jeolla and Gyeongsang provinces.

Features

Distinctive features include compulsory military service shaped by the unresolved conflict with North Korea, the political influence of large family-owned conglomerates (chaebol), and a vigorous tradition of street protest. Presidential impeachments — Roh Moo-hyun in 2004 (overturned) and Park Geun-hye in 2016–17 (upheld, leading to her removal) — have tested the constitutional order.
