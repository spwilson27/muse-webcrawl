Mary Stevenson Cassatt (22 May 1844 – 14 June 1926) was an American painter and printmaker who spent most of her adult life in France, where she exhibited with the Impressionists. She is celebrated for her intimate depictions of the social and private lives of women, especially the bond between mothers and children.

Career

Born into a wealthy Pennsylvania family, Cassatt studied at the Pennsylvania Academy of the Fine Arts before moving to Paris in 1866. Invited by Edgar Degas, she showed in four of the Impressionist exhibitions between 1879 and 1886; Degas became a close friend and mentor. With Berthe Morisot and Marie Bracquemond she was one of the artists later called les trois grandes dames of Impressionism.

Work

Her mature works include The Child's Bath (1893), the first painting by an American woman purchased by a French museum-in-waiting collection (now the Art Institute of Chicago), and The Boating Party. Influenced by Japanese woodblock prints, she produced a celebrated series of colour prints in 1890–91. As an adviser to collectors such as Louisine Havemeyer, Cassatt also helped bring major European art into American museums.
