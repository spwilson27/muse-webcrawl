Ossetians

The Ossetians are an Iranian ethnic group indigenous to Ossetia, a region spanning both slopes of the Greater Caucasus Mountains. They speak Ossetic, an Eastern Iranian language of the Indo-European family, and are divided between North Ossetia–Alania in Russia and South Ossetia, whose status is disputed between Georgia and Russia.

Origins

The Ossetians descend from the Alans, a Sarmatian nomadic people who migrated into the Caucasus during the early medieval period and were pushed into the mountain valleys by the Mongol invasions of the 13th century. Their language is the last surviving descendant of the Scytho-Sarmatian dialects once spoken across the Eurasian steppe.

Language and culture

Ossetic has two main dialects, Iron and Digor, and is written in Cyrillic. Ossetian culture preserves the Nart sagas, a cycle of heroic epics shared with other peoples of the Caucasus. Traditional religion blends Orthodox Christianity (adopted by most Ossetians), Islam (practised by a minority, especially Digors), and the indigenous Uatsdin faith with pre-Christian Iranian and Caucasian elements. The ritual three pies (æртæ чъирийы) remain a hallmark of Ossetian ceremonial life.

Demographics

Most Ossetians live in North Ossetia, whose capital is Vladikavkaz; a large diaspora exists in Moscow, Turkey and Syria. The 2008 Russo-Georgian War, fought partly over South Ossetia, led Russia to recognise South Ossetia as independent, a status accepted by only a handful of states.
