Street food
DefinitionReady-to-eat food sold by vendors in public places
Global consumersEstimated 2.5 billion people daily
Notable hubsBangkok, Mexico City, Istanbul, Marrakech

Street food is ready-to-eat food or drink sold by a hawker, or vendor, in a street or at other public places, such as markets, fairs, parks, and road junctions. It is often sold from a portable food booth, food cart, or food truck and is meant for immediate consumption. Some street foods are regional, but many have spread beyond their regions of origin.

According to a 2007 study by the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations, 2.5 billion people eat street food every day. Street food is integral to the culinary cultures of many countries, particularly in Asia, Latin America, and Africa, where it provides affordable meals to urban workers and plays a major social role.

History

The tradition of selling prepared food in public spaces dates back to antiquity. In ancient Greece, small fried fish were sold in the streets; in ancient Rome, cooked food was sold to plebeians who lacked private kitchens. In the Aztec capital Tenochtitlan, vendors sold more than fifty types of tamales. Street food flourished in industrial cities of the 19th century, feeding factory workers in London, Paris, and New York.

Regional varieties

  • Asia: noodle soups, satay, dumplings, banh mi, takoyaki, and night-market snacks in Taiwan and Thailand.
  • Latin America: tacos, elotes, arepas, salteñas, and anticuchos.
  • Middle East: falafel, shawarma, simit, and koshari.
  • Africa: suya, bunny chow, and grilled plantain.
  • Europe and North America: hot dogs, crêpes, churros, pretzels, and food-truck cuisine.

Health and regulation

Governments regulate street food vending through licensing and hygiene inspections, though enforcement varies widely. Concerns include foodborne illness, use of public space, and vendor livelihoods. In recent decades, some street food stalls have gained international recognition, including hawker stalls in Singapore awarded Michelin stars.
