Gene expression

FrameworkCentral dogma of molecular biology

Gene expression is the process by which information from a gene is used in the synthesis of a functional gene product that enables the production of an end point, such as a protein or a functional RNA molecule. These products are often proteins, but in non-protein-coding genes such as transfer RNA (tRNA) and small nuclear RNA (snRNA), the product is a functional RNA. The process is used by all known life — eukaryotes, prokaryotes, and viruses — to generate the macromolecular machinery for life.

Mechanism

In genetics, gene expression is the most fundamental level at which the genotype gives rise to the phenotype. It typically begins with transcription, in which RNA polymerase produces a messenger RNA (mRNA) copy of the gene's DNA sequence. In eukaryotes the primary transcript is processed — 5′ capping, splicing of introns and polyadenylation — then exported to the cytoplasm, where ribosomes perform translation, reading the mRNA's codons (see the genetic code) to assemble a polypeptide, which folds and may undergo post-translational modification.

Regulation

Expression is tightly regulated at every step, allowing cells with identical genomes to differentiate into neurons, muscle or liver cells. Regulatory mechanisms include transcription factors and enhancers, chromatin accessibility governed by DNA methylation and histone modification (epigenetics), alternative splicing, mRNA stability, microRNAs and RNA interference, and feedback inhibition. The classic model is the lac operon of E. coli, described by Jacob and Monod. Dysregulated expression underlies many diseases, including cancers driven by overexpressed oncogenes.

Measurement

Expression levels are measured transcriptome-wide by RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) and DNA microarrays, and for individual genes by quantitative PCR, Northern blotting and reporter-gene assays. At the protein level, Western blotting and mass spectrometry are used. Differential expression analysis of such data is a staple of functional genomics.
