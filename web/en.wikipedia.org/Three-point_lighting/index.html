Three-point lighting

Three-point lighting is a standard method used in visual media such as theatre, video, film, still photography, computer graphics, and portraiture. It uses three lights — the key light, the fill light, and the back light — to illuminate a subject in a dimensional, controlled way, shaping the subject's form while managing shadows and contrast. The technique developed in classical Hollywood cinema and remains a foundational lighting scheme for interviews, portrait photography, and studio work.

The three lights

Key light

The key light is the dominant light source. It is usually placed to one side of the camera, at roughly a 30–60 degree angle to the subject, and shines directly on the subject, defining its principal form and casting the main shadows. Its position relative to the subject's face determines effects such as Rembrandt lighting.

Fill light

The fill light is placed on the opposite side of the camera from the key, typically at the subject's face level, and shines at lower intensity (achieved with a weaker lamp, greater distance, diffusion, or bouncing). It "fills" the shadows created by the key light, controlling the lighting ratio and the amount of visible detail in shadow areas without eliminating modelling.

Back light

The back light (also called a rim light, hair light, or edge light) is placed behind the subject, often high, and shines toward the camera. It creates a rim of light around the subject's head and shoulders, separating the subject from the background and giving a sense of depth — particularly important for darker subjects against dark backgrounds.

Variations

A fourth light, the background light, illuminates the backdrop itself. Reducing the fill to almost nothing produces low-key, high-contrast lighting typical of film noir, while strong, balanced fill produces high-key lighting used in sitcoms and commercials. Outdoor shooting often uses the sun as key light with reflectors or scrims as fill. Modern LED panels and virtual production setups replicate the same principles.
