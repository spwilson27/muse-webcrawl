Rail trail

A rail trail is a shared-use path built on the corridor of a disused railway. Because railways require gentle gradients and wide curves, their abandoned rights-of-way convert readily into trails for walking, cycling and sometimes horse riding. Rail trails range from short local paths to multi-hundred-kilometre routes and are promoted for recreation, tourism and active transport, as well as for preserving corridors for possible future rail use ("railbanking" in the United States).

History

Conversions began in the mid-twentieth century as rail networks contracted. In the United States, the Illinois Prairie Path (1963) and Wisconsin's Elroy-Sparta State Trail (1967), which runs through former rail tunnels, were early examples. The Rails-to-Trails Conservancy, founded in 1986, helped popularise conversions; tens of thousands of miles of rail trail now exist in the US. In Britain, routes such as the Bristol and Bath Railway Path followed the Beeching closures of the 1960s. Australia, New Zealand, Germany and other countries have extensive networks.

Characteristics

Surfaces may be asphalt, crushed stone or natural earth. Trails often retain railway features — bridges, viaducts, tunnels, cuttings and former stations — giving them heritage value. Because corridors are continuous and largely free of road crossings, rail trails are attractive for long-distance touring; many form part of national cycle networks.

Controversies

Conversion can be opposed by adjoining landowners over privacy, trespass and questions about whether the corridor should revert to them; in the US, such disputes have produced major court cases over railbanking and compensation. In some places, reactivation of rail service on a corridor has been proposed, leading to "rails with trails" designs in which the path runs beside a restored line.

Notable trails

Examples include the Great Allegheny Passage and Katy Trail (United States), the Monsal Trail and Tissington Trail (England), the Otago Central Rail Trail (New Zealand), and the Murray to Mountains Rail Trail (Australia).
