Braun GmbH is a German consumer products company founded in 1921 by engineer Max Braun in Frankfurt am Main. The company is best known for its electric shavers, small household appliances and its influential industrial design tradition, associated above all with designer Dieter Rams, whose "less, but better" philosophy shaped Braun products from the 1950s onward and later influenced generations of product designers worldwide.

History

Braun began by manufacturing components for radio sets and, by the 1930s, complete radios. In 1950 it launched the S 50, its first electric foil shaver. Gillette acquired a controlling stake in 1967, and Braun passed with Gillette to Procter & Gamble in 2005. In 2012 De'Longhi acquired perpetual rights to manufacture Braun-branded household appliances (such as irons, blenders and coffee makers), while P&G retained the personal care business.

Design

Under Rams, head of design from 1961 to 1995, Braun products embodied functionalist principles: clarity, simplicity and honesty of materials. Iconic products include the SK 4 record player (with Hans Gugelot, 1956, nicknamed "Snow White's coffin"), the T 1000 world receiver, the ET 66 calculator and the Lectron system. Rams's ten principles of good design have become a canonical statement of modernist industrial design.
