Shimon bar Yochai

Shimon bar Yochai was a second-century rabbi and tanna in Roman Judea, traditionally attributed as the author of the Zohar.

Shimon bar Yochai (Aramaic: רשב״י, Rashbi) was a 1st–2nd-century CE rabbi (tanna) in Roman Judea, a leading disciple of Rabbi Akiva. He is frequently quoted in the Mishnah and is traditionally regarded as the author of the Zohar, the foundational work of Kabbalah, though modern scholars generally date that text to thirteenth-century Spain.

Life and legend

According to Talmudic traditions, bar Yochai fiercely criticised Roman rule and, condemned by the authorities, fled with his son Eleazar and hid in a cave in the Galilee for twelve (or thirteen) years, sustained by a carob tree and a spring, studying Torah until Elijah announced the emperor's death. He is associated with the town of Meron in the Upper Galilee, and his tomb there is a major pilgrimage site.

Lag BaOmer

The anniversary of his death, on Lag BaOmer (the 33rd day of the Omer count), is celebrated in Israel and by Jewish communities worldwide with bonfires, weddings and festivities, centred on the pilgrimage to his tomb at Meron, which draws hundreds of thousands of visitors. In kabbalistic tradition the day marks the revelation of the hidden light of the Torah — the "holy luminary" Rashbi being credited with revealing its esoteric secrets.
