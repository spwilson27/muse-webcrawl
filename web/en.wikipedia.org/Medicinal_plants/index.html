Medicinal plants

Medicinal plants are plants used in herbal medicine for their therapeutic properties. They have been used since prehistoric times in traditions including Ayurveda, traditional Chinese medicine, and ancient Egyptian and Greek medicine, and they remain a primary source of healthcare for much of the world's population.

From plants to drugs

Many modern medicines derive from plant compounds: aspirin from willow bark salicylates, morphine from the opium poppy, quinine and artemisinin against malaria, digoxin from foxglove, and the cancer drug paclitaxel from the Pacific yew. Ethnobotanists study traditional uses to guide drug discovery.

Regulation and safety

Herbal products vary in potency and can interact with prescription drugs, so their sale is regulated differently across countries, between food supplements and licensed medicines.
