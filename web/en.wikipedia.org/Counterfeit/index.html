Counterfeit

TypeFraud, forgery
TargetsCurrency, goods, documents, pharmaceuticals, art
RelatedCounterfeit money, intellectual property infringement

To counterfeit is to make an imitation of something — money, goods, documents, or works of art — with the intention that it be taken for the genuine article, usually for profit or advantage. Counterfeiting is among the oldest forms of fraud: counterfeit coins circulated in antiquity, and the trade in fake branded goods and currency is today estimated by the OECD to run to hundreds of billions of dollars annually.

Counterfeit money

Counterfeit currency undermines confidence in money and has historically been punished severely; it was treated as treason in England, and counterfeiting United States currency was a principal reason for founding the US Secret Service in 1865. Modern banknotes incorporate security features such as watermarks, security threads, colour-shifting inks, intaglio printing, and polymer substrates to resist imitation. High-quality "supernotes" have been attributed to state-level actors.

Counterfeit goods

Counterfeit consumer goods — luxury products, clothing, electronics, spare parts, and medicines — infringe trademarks and can endanger health and safety, most seriously in the case of falsified pharmaceuticals, which the World Health Organization estimates make up a significant share of medicines in some low- and middle-income countries. Enforcement involves customs seizures, trademark litigation, and international cooperation.

Documents and art

Forged passports, certificates, and artwork form further categories; famous art forgers such as Han van Meegeren and Elmyr de Hory sold imitations that fooled experts for years.
