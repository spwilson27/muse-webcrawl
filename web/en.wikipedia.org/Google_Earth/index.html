Google Earth is a web- and computer-based program that renders a 3D representation of Earth based primarily on satellite imagery. The program maps Earth by superimposing satellite images, aerial photography, and GIS data onto a 3D globe, allowing users to see cities and landscapes from various angles. Users can enter addresses and coordinates, or browse with a mouse or keyboard.

[ Image placeholder: A view of Earth in Google Earth ]

Google Earth originated as EarthViewer 3D, developed by Keyhole, Inc., a company Google acquired in 2004; it was re-released under its present name in 2005. It displays satellite images of varying resolution, generally 15 m or finer in populated areas, overlaid with terrain, 3D buildings in many cities, and layers of geographic data.

Features

Features include Street View integration, a flight simulator, historical imagery going back decades, and views of the Moon, Mars, and the sky (Google Sky). Google Earth Outreach supports nonprofits, and the platform has been used to publicise deforestation, discover archaeological sites, and locate a lost home — the widely reported story of Saroo Brierley, who found his childhood home in India using the program. Earth Engine, a related platform, provides planetary-scale geospatial analysis for scientists.

Versions

Google Earth is offered as a free web and mobile version and as Google Earth Pro (formerly a paid product, free since 2015), which adds measurement, high-resolution printing, and GIS data import tools. A major web redesign in 2017 added guided "Voyager" tours and knowledge cards.
