Relief pitcher

In baseball and softball, a relief pitcher or reliever is a pitcher who enters the game after the starting pitcher is removed because of fatigue, ineffectiveness, injury, ejection, or for strategic reasons such as facing a particular batter.

Roles

Modern bullpens are organised into specialised roles. The closer pitches the final innings of close games and earns saves; setup men work the innings just before the closer; middle relievers cover the middle innings; long relievers enter early when a starter exits prematurely; and specialists may be used against specific hitters.

History

Relief pitching grew from an occasional practice into a central feature of the game during the 20th century, with milestones including the emergence of dedicated closers in the 1970s and 1980s and the "opener" strategy, in which a reliever starts the game, popularised in the late 2010s.
