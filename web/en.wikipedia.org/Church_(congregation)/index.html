Church (congregation)

A church is a religious organization or congregation that meets in a particular location. Many are formally organized, with constitutions and by-laws, maintain offices, are served by clergy or lay leaders, and, in nations where this is permissible, often seek non-profit corporate status. Local churches may be independent or members of a larger religious body, convention, or denomination.

The English word "church" derives from the Old English cirice, ultimately from the Greek kyriakon, "of the Lord." In the New Testament, the Greek word ekklesia—meaning an assembly or congregation—came to denote the community of Christians, both locally and universally. In Christian theology the term is used in several senses: the local congregation, a denomination, a church building, and the whole body of the faithful, sometimes called the Church universal.

Local church

A local church typically gathers for worship, prayer, teaching, the celebration of sacraments or ordinances such as baptism and the Eucharist, fellowship, and service to its community. Its leadership structures vary widely: episcopal polities vest authority in bishops; presbyterian polities in elders; and congregational polities in the local membership itself. Churches may be parish churches serving a geographic community, as in the Church of England, or gathered congregations formed by voluntary membership, as in Baptist and free-church traditions.

Church as organization and denomination

Beyond the local level, "church" may denote a denomination—such as the Roman Catholic Church, the Eastern Orthodox Church, or the Lutheran Church—an organized body sharing doctrine, governance, and history. Some traditions reserve the term "church" for bodies meeting particular theological criteria, referring to other groups as ecclesial communities or sects. The study of the church's nature is the branch of theology called ecclesiology, which addresses questions of authority, unity, sacraments, and mission.

Role in society

Congregations have historically provided education, poor relief, healthcare, and social cohesion, and they remain significant providers of voluntary and charitable activity. In some states a church may be established by law; in others, constitutional provisions separate church and state. Modern usage also extends the word loosely to the congregations of other religions, though terms such as mosque, synagogue, and temple are preferred for those faiths' own assemblies.
