Second Summer of Love

The Second Summer of Love is a name given to the summers of 1988 and 1989 in the United Kingdom, when acid house music and the drug MDMA (ecstasy) fuelled an explosion of rave culture. The name consciously echoes the original Summer of Love of 1967 in San Francisco.

Outdoor raves and the smiley-face symbol defined the summers of 1988–89.

Origins

British DJs including Paul Oakenfold, Danny Rampling, and Nicky Holloway encountered house music and ecstasy on holiday in Ibiza in 1987, and brought the sound back to London clubs such as Shoom and Heaven. Acid house—built on the squelching basslines of the Roland TB-303—spread rapidly, while promoters began staging large unlicensed parties in warehouses and fields, some drawing tens of thousands of people. The yellow smiley face became the movement's emblem.

Impact and backlash

The phenomenon transformed British youth culture and nightlife, launching superstar DJ careers, club brands, and the modern dance-music industry, and shifted mainstream pop toward electronic sounds through acts emerging from the scene. Tabloid moral panic and several crowd incidents led to police crackdowns, culminating in the "acid house parties" legislation in the Entertainments (Increased Penalties) Act 1990 and, later, the Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994, which controversially targeted gatherings playing music "characterised by the emission of a succession of repetitive beats."
