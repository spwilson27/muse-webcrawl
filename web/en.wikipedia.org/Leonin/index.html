Léonin

Léonin (fl. c. 1150s–1201), sometimes Leoninus, was the earliest known significant composer of polyphonic organum, associated with Notre Dame Cathedral in Paris and its school of composition.

Work

The music theorist known as Anonymous IV credited Léonin with the Magnus liber organi, the great book of organum — two-voice settings of chant for the liturgical year, in which a slow-moving chant voice supports a florid upper line. His successor Pérotin later revised the collection, sometimes expanding passages into three or four voices.

Legacy

Almost nothing is known of Léonin life beyond his music, but the Magnus liber marks the first large body of polyphony attributable to a named composer in Western music.
