Lyme Regis is a town and civil parish in West Dorset, England, about 25 miles (40 km) west of Dorchester and east of Exeter. Situated on Lyme Bay on the English Channel coast, the town is noted for the fossils found in the cliffs and beaches of the surrounding coast, which forms part of the Jurassic Coast, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The harbour wall, known as the Cobb, appears in Jane Austen's novel Persuasion and in the film of John Fowles's novel The French Lieutenant's Woman.

History

The settlement is first recorded in 774, when Cynewulf, King of Wessex, granted land at Lyme to the abbots of Sherborne. The "Regis" suffix was added after the town was granted a royal charter by Edward I in 1284. In 1644, during the English Civil War, the town withstood an eight-week siege by Royalist forces. In 1685 the Duke of Monmouth landed at Lyme Regis at the start of his ill-fated rebellion against James II; his defeat at Sedgemoor was followed by the Bloody Assizes, and twelve men were hanged on the beach where Monmouth had landed.

In the Georgian and Victorian periods Lyme developed as a seaside resort. Jane Austen visited in 1803 and 1804 and set part of Persuasion in the town. John Fowles, author of The French Lieutenant's Woman (1969), lived in Lyme Regis and served as curator of the town museum.

Geology and fossils

The cliffs around Lyme Regis expose rocks of the Blue Lias and Charmouth Mudstone formations, laid down as marine sediments in the late Triassic and early Jurassic periods, roughly 210–195 million years ago. The unstable cliffs are subject to frequent landslips, which continually expose new fossils: ammonites, belemnites, ichthyosaurs, plesiosaurs and other marine reptiles.

The town is the birthplace of Mary Anning (1799–1847), the pioneering fossil collector whose finds — including the first scientifically described ichthyosaur skeleton and the first complete plesiosaurus — transformed the young science of palaeontology. The Lyme Regis Museum stands on the site of her home and fossil shop, and the town hosts an annual fossil festival.

The Cobb

The Cobb is a large artificial harbour wall, first built in the medieval period and rebuilt many times after storm damage; the present structure dates mainly from the early 19th century. It provides Lyme's only sheltered harbour and features prominently in the literature of the town, including the scene in Persuasion in which Louisa Musgrove falls from the steps known as Granny's Teeth.

Transport

Lyme Regis has no railway station; the town was served from 1903 to 1965 by a station on the Axminster to Lyme Regis railway branch line. The nearest station is now Axminster, on the West of England line. The town lies on the A3052 coast road.
