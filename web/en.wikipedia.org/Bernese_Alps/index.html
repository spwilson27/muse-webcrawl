Bernese Alps

The Bernese Alps (German: Berner Alpen, French: Alpes bernoises) are a mountain range of the Alps located in western Switzerland. Although the name suggests that they are located in the Bernese Oberland region of the canton of Bern, many of the highest peaks lie on the borders with the cantons of Valais, Fribourg, and Vaud. The range is among the most glaciated in the Alps and contains the largest glacier in the Alps, the Aletsch Glacier.

Geography

The Bernese Alps form the watershed between the Rhône, flowing south-west to the Mediterranean, and the Aare, flowing north-east to the Rhine. The main chain runs from the Sanetsch Pass in the west to the Grimsel Pass in the east. The highest summit is the Finsteraarhorn (4,274 m), and the range includes the famous north faces of the Eiger (3,967 m), Mönch (4,107 m), and Jungfrau (4,158 m), which overlook the Lauterbrunnen and Grindelwald valleys. The Jungfrau-Aletsch region is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Other notable peaks include the Schreckhorn, Gross Fiescherhorn, Aletschhorn, and the Wildstrubel.

Tourism and transport

The Bernese Oberland is one of Switzerland's classic tourist regions, with resorts such as Interlaken, Grindelwald, Wengen, and Mürren. The Jungfrau Railway, opened in 1912, tunnels through the Eiger and Mönch to the Jungfraujoch station at 3,454 m, the highest railway station in Europe. The range is famous in mountaineering history for early ascents during the golden age of alpinism and for the deadly north face of the Eiger, first climbed in 1938.
