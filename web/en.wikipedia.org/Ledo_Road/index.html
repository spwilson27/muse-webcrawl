Ledo Road

Ledo Road (Stilwell Road)
Route: Ledo, India – Kunming, China
Length: approx. 1,079 miles (1,736 km)
Built: 1942–1945
Commander: Gen. Joseph Stilwell

The Ledo Road, renamed the Stilwell Road in 1945, was a military supply road built during the Second World War connecting Ledo in Assam, India, through northern Burma to Kunming in China, where it joined the Burma Road.

Construction

After Japanese forces cut the Burma Road in 1942, the Allies needed an overland route to supply China. Construction began in December 1942 under American engineers, with much of the labour performed by African-American engineering battalions, Indian, Burmese and Chinese workers. The road crossed jungle, mountains and the Hukawng Valley at enormous cost in lives, chiefly from disease; it was dubbed "a mile a minute" in human cost by its builders.

Use

The first convoy reached Kunming in early 1945. By then airlift over "the Hump" was delivering more tonnage, and the road's strategic importance was brief, though it also carried a fuel pipeline to China.
