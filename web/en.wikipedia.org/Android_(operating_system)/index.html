Android (operating system)

Developer: Google, Open Handset Alliance

Written in: Java, Kotlin, C, C++

OS family: Unix-like (Linux kernel)

Initial release: 23 September 2008

Source model: Open source (AOSP) with proprietary components

Android is a mobile operating system based on a modified version of the Linux kernel, designed primarily for touchscreen devices such as smartphones and tablets. It was developed by Android Inc., founded in 2003 by Andy Rubin and others, which Google acquired in 2005. The first commercial Android device, the HTC Dream, launched in 2008.

History

Android was unveiled in 2007 alongside the Open Handset Alliance, a consortium of hardware, software and telecom companies. Versions were named after desserts (Cupcake, Donut, Éclair, through Pie) before Google switched to plain numbers with Android 10 in 2019. Android became the world's best-selling smartphone platform in 2011 and now powers over three billion active devices, with a global market share above 70 percent.

Architecture

The system stacks a Linux kernel, hardware abstraction layer, native libraries and the Android Runtime (ART), which executes applications written in Java or Kotlin. Apps are distributed mainly through the Google Play store, though alternative stores and sideloading are permitted. The open-source core, the Android Open Source Project (AOSP), is released under the Apache License, while Google Mobile Services are proprietary.

Criticism

Android has been criticised for software fragmentation, slow security updates on many devices, and Google's control over manufacturers, which led to a €4.34 billion European Commission antitrust fine in 2018. Variants of Android also run on televisions, cars (Android Auto), wearables (Wear OS) and embedded devices.
