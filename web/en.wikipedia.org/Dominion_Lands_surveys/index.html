Dominion Lands surveys
Country: Canada
Begun: 1871
Coverage: Western Canada (Prairies and northern territories)
Unit: Township and range system

The Dominion Lands Survey (DLS) is the method used to divide most of Western Canada into one-square-mile (640-acre) sections for agricultural and other settlement. Begun in 1871, it is among the largest survey systems ever undertaken, covering roughly 800,000 square kilometres across Manitoba, Saskatchewan, Alberta, and parts of British Columbia and the North.

System

Land is organized by meridians, townships, and ranges. Each township is about six miles square and contains 36 sections; each section is divided into quarter sections of 160 acres, the standard homestead unit under the Dominion Lands Act of 1872. Legal descriptions such as "NW-25-42-3-W5" identify a quarter section by section, township, range, and meridian.

History

The survey was modeled loosely on the United States Public Land Survey System and was rushed ahead of settlement following the transfer of Rupert's Land to Canada in 1870. The grid pattern it imposed remains visible in the road allowances and field boundaries of the Prairies today.
