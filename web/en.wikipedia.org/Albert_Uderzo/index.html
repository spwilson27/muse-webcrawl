Albert Uderzo

Albert Uderzo (French: [albɛʁ ydɛʁzo]; 25 April 1927 – 24 March 2020) was a French comic book artist and scriptwriter. He is best known as the co-creator, with René Goscinny, of the Asterix series, one of the most popular comics in the world.

Early career

Born in Fismes, Marne, to Italian immigrant parents, Uderzo taught himself to draw and worked in animation and advertising before meeting writer René Goscinny in 1951. Together they created the series Oumpah-pah and, in 1959, launched Asterix in the first issue of the magazine Pilote.

Asterix

Uderzo drew the adventures of the indomitable Gaulish village resisting Roman occupation, while Goscinny wrote the scripts. After Goscinny's sudden death in 1977, Uderzo continued the series alone as writer and artist, founding his own publishing company. Though some critics felt the later albums lacked Goscinny's wit, they remained bestsellers. Asterix has sold hundreds of millions of copies and been translated into over a hundred languages.

Later life

Uderzo retired from drawing in 2011, handing Asterix to a new creative team. He died of a heart attack at his home in Neuilly-sur-Seine in March 2020, aged 92.
