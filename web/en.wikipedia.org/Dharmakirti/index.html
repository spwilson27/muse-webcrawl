Dharmakirti

Dharmakīrti
Bornc. 6th–7th century CE, South India
SchoolBuddhist logic (pramāṇa)
Main interestsEpistemology, logic, philosophy of language

Dharmakīrti (c. 6th–7th century CE) was an influential Indian Buddhist philosopher and logician who worked at the monastic university of Nalanda. Building on the work of Dignāga, he systematised the Buddhist school of epistemology and logic known as pramāṇa, and his writings became foundational for later Indian and Tibetan Buddhist philosophy.

Dharmakirti, a founder of the Buddhist school of logic.

Works

Seven treatises are attributed to Dharmakirti, headed by the Pramāṇavārttika ("Commentary on [Dignāga's] Compendium on Valid Cognition"), his magnum opus. Others include the Pramāṇaviniścaya, the Nyāyabindu ("A Drop of Logic"), the Hetubindu, the Vādanyāya on debate, the Sambandhaparīkṣā on relations, and the Santānāntarasiddhi, a defence of other minds against solipsism.

Philosophy

Dharmakirti held that there are exactly two means of valid cognition (pramāṇa): perception (pratyakṣa), which grasps the unique, momentary particular (svalakṣaṇa), and inference (anumāna), which operates on conceptual universals (sāmānyalakṣaṇa). His theory of exclusion (apoha) explains how words can refer without real universals. He defended momentariness, argued for the Buddha's authority on the basis of his compassion and reliability, and developed sophisticated arguments deployed for centuries by Indian and Tibetan debaters.

Legacy

Dharmakirti's system dominated Buddhist scholasticism in India and Tibet; commentators such as Dharmottara, Prajñākaragupta and the Tibetan translators preserved and elaborated his ideas, and his criteria for the triple-conditioned logical mark (trairūpya) remained central to debate traditions.
