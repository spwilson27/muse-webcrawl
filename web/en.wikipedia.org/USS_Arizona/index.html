USS Arizona was a standard-type battleship built for the United States Navy in the mid-1910s. Named for the 48th state, she was the second and final ship of the Pennsylvania class. After commissioning in 1916, she served in the First World War era fleet, was modernised in 1929–1931, and spent the 1930s with the Pacific Fleet.

Arizona is remembered above all for her catastrophic loss during the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor on 7 December 1941, when a bomb detonated her forward magazines, killing 1,177 officers and crew — nearly half of all American personnel killed in the attack. Her sunken hull remains in place as the centrepiece of the USS Arizona Memorial.

Service history

Built by the Brooklyn Navy Yard, Arizona was commissioned too late to see combat in the First World War and spent most of the war training on the US East Coast, in part because of concerns about fuel oil supplies in Britain. In 1919 she escorted President Woodrow Wilson to the Paris Peace Conference. In the interwar years she participated in Fleet Problems (large-scale naval exercises) and, after her 1929–31 modernisation — which added tripod masts, anti-torpedo bulges and improved anti-aircraft armament — she was transferred to the Pacific. She was at Pearl Harbor as part of Battleship Row when the Japanese attacked.

Attack on Pearl Harbor

Moored inboard of the repair ship Vestal on the morning of 7 December 1941, Arizona was struck by several bombs from high-altitude bombers. At about 08:06, a bomb penetrated near the No. 2 turret and detonated in the black powder magazine, igniting the main magazines. The resulting explosion destroyed the forward part of the ship, collapsing her foremast and conning tower; she sank within minutes, settling on the harbour bottom with her superstructure above water. Fires burned for two days. Among the dead were Rear Admiral Isaac C. Kidd and Captain Franklin Van Valkenburgh, who were awarded the Medal of Honor posthumously.

Unlike most of the other battleships sunk or damaged that day, Arizona could not be salvaged and raised. She was formally struck from the Naval Vessel Register in 1942, and her surviving superstructure was removed. Her main battery turrets were partly salvaged; one gun turret's guns were later installed as coastal artillery on Oahu.

Memorial

The USS Arizona Memorial, dedicated in 1962, is a white concrete structure spanning the mid-portion of the sunken hull without touching it. Designed by architect Alfred Preis, it houses a marble wall engraved with the names of the 1,177 men who died aboard. The wreck continues to leak small amounts of fuel oil — sometimes called the "tears of the Arizona" — and the remains of the ship are a protected war grave administered by the National Park Service as part of the Pearl Harbor National Memorial. Survivors of the crew may choose to have their ashes interred within the wreck by Navy divers.
