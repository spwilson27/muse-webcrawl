Yatra

Yatra (Sanskrit: यात्रा, lit. 'journey', 'procession') are journeys to sacred places, in particular pilgrimages in Hinduism and other Indian religions. A yatra is typically undertaken in large groups, sometimes numbering into the hundreds of thousands. Yatras are an important part of the practice of many dharmic traditions, and they often culminate in religious festivals such as the Kumbh Mela or the Char Dham Yatra.

Types of yatra

Among the best-known yatras is the pilgrimage to the four sacred sites of the Char Dham in Uttarakhand — Yamunotri, Gangotri, Kedarnath and Badrinath. In Madhya Pradesh, the Narmada Parikrama involves circumambulating the Narmada River. Rath yatra, most famously held at Puri in Odisha, involves the procession of deities on enormous temple chariots.

Significance

Yatras are believed to confer spiritual merit (punya) on participants. The journey itself, often arduous, is considered a form of ascetic practice, and arrival at the sacred destination is associated with darshan, the auspicious sight of the deity. Historically, yatras also served as occasions for trade, social gathering and the exchange of news across regions.
