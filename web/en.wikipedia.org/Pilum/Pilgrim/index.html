Pilgrim

Pilgrim
DefinitionA traveller on a journey to a holy place
EtymologyLatin peregrinus, "foreigner, wanderer"
Major traditionsChristianity, Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism, Judaism, Sikhism

A pilgrim (from the Latin peregrinus) is a traveller who journeys to a holy place for religious reasons. Pilgrimage is practised in many religions: Christians travel to Jerusalem, Rome and Santiago de Compostela; Muslims perform the Hajj to Mecca; Hindus gather at the Kumbh Mela and visit the Char Dham; Buddhists visit Lumbini and Bodh Gaya.

History

Pilgrimage is among the most ancient forms of travel, attested in the ancient world at shrines such as Delphi and in Egypt. In medieval Europe pilgrims were a familiar sight on the roads, protected by custom and hospitality networks, and identifiable by badges such as the scallop shell of St James or the palm of a Jerusalem pilgrim.

Modern pilgrimage

Today pilgrims number in the hundreds of millions annually, travelling both from devotion and from cultural interest. The English word also gives its name to the Pilgrim Fathers, the settlers of Plymouth Colony in 1620.
