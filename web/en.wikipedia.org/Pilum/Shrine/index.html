Shrine
DefinitionA holy or sacred place dedicated to a specific deity, ancestor, hero or saint
EtymologyOld English scrīn, "chest, cabinet for relics"
ExamplesShinto shrines, saints' shrines, martyria

A shrine (Latin: scrinium, "case or chest for books or papers"; Old French: escrin, "box or case") is a sacred or holy space dedicated to a specific deity, ancestor, hero, martyr, saint, daemon, or similar figure of awe and respect, at which they are venerated or worshipped. Shrines often contain idols, relics, or other such objects associated with the figure being venerated.

Types

Shrines are found in many religions. In Christianity, shrines to the Virgin Mary and the saints — often housing relics — became major pilgrimage destinations, such as those at Lourdes and Canterbury. In Shinto, the jinja is a shrine housing the kami. Buddhist, Hindu, Sikh and Taoist traditions all maintain shrines, ranging from monumental temple complexes to small domestic shrines in the home.

Shrines and pilgrimage

Major shrines anchor pilgrimage routes and religious festivals, and portable shrines are carried in procession in traditions from Japan to Catholic Europe.
