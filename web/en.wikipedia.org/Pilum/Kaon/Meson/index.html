Meson

A meson is a subatomic particle composed of an equal number of quarks and antiquarks, usually one of each, bound together by the strong interaction. Mesons are bosons — they have integer spin — and all are unstable, decaying within fractions of a second into lighter particles.

Meson
Composition: One quark + one antiquark
Spin: Integer (0 or 1)
Examples: Pion, kaon, J/ψ
Predicted: Hideki Yukawa, 1935

History

Mesons were proposed by Hideki Yukawa in 1935 as carriers of the strong force binding protons and neutrons in the nucleus. The first true meson, the pion, was identified in cosmic rays in 1947 by Cecil Powell's group. The muon, discovered earlier, was once called a "mu meson" but is actually a lepton. Yukawa received the 1949 Nobel Prize in Physics for his prediction.

Types

The lightest mesons are the pions (π), followed by kaons (K) containing strange quarks, and heavier families such as the eta, rho and omega mesons, the charmed D mesons, the bottom B mesons, and the J/ψ — whose 1974 discovery confirmed the charm quark.

Properties

Mesons mediate the residual strong force between nucleons and are classified by their total angular momentum, parity, and flavour quantum numbers such as strangeness, charm and bottomness.
