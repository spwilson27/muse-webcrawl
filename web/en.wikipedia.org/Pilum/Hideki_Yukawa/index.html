Hideki Yukawa (Japanese: 湯川 秀樹; 23 January 1907 – 8 September 1981) was a Japanese theoretical physicist. He was the first Japanese Nobel laureate, receiving the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1949 "for his prediction of the existence of mesons on the basis of theoretical work on nuclear forces".

Meson theory

In 1934–35 Yukawa proposed that the strong force binding protons and neutrons in the atomic nucleus is carried by a new particle of intermediate mass — the meson — exchanged between nucleons. The associated Yukawa potential falls off exponentially with distance, explaining the short range of the nuclear force. A particle matching his prediction, the pion, was discovered in cosmic rays in 1947 by Cecil Powell's group.

Later life

Yukawa worked at Kyoto University and was the first director of its Research Institute for Fundamental Physics, later renamed the Yukawa Institute for Theoretical Physics. After the Second World War he became a prominent advocate of nuclear disarmament and signed the Russell–Einstein Manifesto.
