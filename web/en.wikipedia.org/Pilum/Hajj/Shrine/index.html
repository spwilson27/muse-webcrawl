Shrine

A shrine is a sacred or holy place dedicated to a specific deity, ancestor, hero, martyr, saint, or similar figure of reverence, at which they are venerated or worshipped. Shrines often contain idols, relics, or other such objects associated with the figure being venerated, and are found in many of the world's religions.

Shrine
Type: Sacred place
Found in: Christianity, Islam, Shinto, Buddhism, Hinduism and others
Purpose: Veneration, pilgrimage, offerings

Types of shrines

Shrines range from great pilgrimage centres to small domestic altars. Christian shrines are often associated with the Virgin Mary, saints, or relics; notable examples include Lourdes, Fátima and Santiago de Compostela. In Islam, shrines (mazar, dargah) are frequently built over the tombs of Sufi saints and attract pilgrims, although some traditions discourage tomb veneration. In Shinto, the jinja is the shrine housing a kami. Hindu temples and Buddhist stupas also function as shrines visited by devotees performing pilgrimage.

Pilgrimage and offerings

Pilgrims travel to shrines to pray, seek healing or blessings, and leave offerings such as candles, flowers, ex-votos or money. The Kaaba in Mecca — destination of the Hajj pilgrimage in Islam — is Islam's holiest shrine, toward which Muslims pray.
