The Hajj is an annual Islamic pilgrimage to Mecca, Saudi Arabia, the holiest city for Muslims. It is one of the Five Pillars of Islam and a religious duty (fard) that must be carried out at least once in a lifetime by every Muslim who is physically and financially capable of undertaking the journey.

Rites

Pilgrims enter a state of consecration called ihram, wearing simple white garments that symbolise equality. The rites, performed over five to six days during Dhu al-Hijjah, include circling the Kaaba (tawaf), walking between the hills of Safa and Marwah (sa'i), standing in prayer on the plain of Arafat (wuquf), spending the night at Muzdalifah, and stoning the pillars at Mina (ramy al-jamarat). The festival of Eid al-Adha concludes the Hajj.

History

Muslims trace the origins of the Hajj to the prophet Abraham (Ibrahim), and the pilgrimage in its Islamic form was established by Muhammad, who performed his Farewell Pilgrimage in 632 CE.
