In nuclear physics and particle physics, the strong interaction, also called the strong force or strong nuclear force, is one of the four known fundamental interactions, alongside electromagnetism, the weak interaction and gravitation. It is the force that binds quarks together into protons, neutrons and other hadrons, and (as a residual effect) binds protons and neutrons into atomic nuclei despite their electric repulsion.

Quantum chromodynamics

The strong interaction is described by quantum chromodynamics (QCD), in which quarks carry a "colour charge" and exchange gluons. Two distinctive properties follow: colour confinement, which prevents isolated quarks from being observed, and asymptotic freedom, by which the force weakens at very short distances. Unlike other forces, the strong force does not diminish with separation — the energy of the gluon field grows until it produces new quark–antiquark pairs.

Residual strong force

The nuclear force holding nuclei together is a residual of the colour force, analogous to the van der Waals force between molecules. Hideki Yukawa's 1935 theory modelled it as the exchange of mesons, a picture still useful in nuclear physics.
