Hideki Yukawa

Hideki Yukawa (Japanese: 湯川 秀樹, 23 January 1907 – 8 September 1981) was a Japanese theoretical physicist and the first Japanese Nobel laureate. He is best known for predicting the existence of the meson as the carrier of the strong nuclear force.

Hideki Yukawa
Born: 23 January 1907, Tokyo, Japan
Died: 8 September 1981, Kyoto, Japan
Known for: Meson theory, Yukawa potential
Awards: Nobel Prize in Physics (1949)

Meson theory

In 1935 Yukawa published his theory of mesons, proposing that the force holding the atomic nucleus together is mediated by a new particle with a mass roughly 200 times that of the electron. This particle, later called the pi meson or pion, was discovered in cosmic radiation in 1947 by Cecil Powell's group, confirming Yukawa's prediction and earning him the 1949 Nobel Prize in Physics.

Career

Yukawa studied at Kyoto Imperial University, lectured at Osaka Imperial University, and later founded the Research Institute for Fundamental Physics at Kyoto. He spent several years in the United States at Princeton and Columbia after the Second World War before returning to Japan. He was also active in movements for nuclear disarmament, signing the Russell–Einstein Manifesto in 1955.

Legacy

The Yukawa potential, the Yukawa interaction, and the concept of meson exchange remain foundational in nuclear and particle physics; meson theory helped frame the interactions of later-discovered particles such as kaons within the strong interaction.
