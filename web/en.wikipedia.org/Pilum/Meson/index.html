Meson

Meson
CompositionOne quark and one antiquark
StatisticsBosonic (integer spin)
InteractionStrong, weak, electromagnetic, gravity
ExamplesPion, kaon, rho, J/ψ

In particle physics, a meson is a hadronic subatomic particle composed of an equal number of quarks and antiquarks, usually one of each, bound together by the strong interaction. Because mesons are composed of quark subparticles, they have a meaningful physical size, a diameter of roughly one femtometre.

History

The name "meson" (from Greek mesos, "middle") reflects the intermediate masses of the first such particles between the electron and the proton. Hideki Yukawa predicted mesons in 1935 as carriers of the nuclear force; the muon was at first mistaken for his particle before the pion was identified in 1947.

Properties

All mesons are unstable, the longest-lived lasting only a few hundredths of a microsecond. They are classified by flavour quantum numbers (including isospin, strangeness and charm) and by spin and parity into families such as the pseudoscalar and vector mesons.
