Strong interaction

The strong interaction (or strong force, strong nuclear force) is one of the four fundamental interactions of nature. It binds quarks together into protons, neutrons and other hadrons, and its residual effects hold atomic nuclei together against the electromagnetic repulsion of their protons.

Strong interaction
Carrier: Gluon
Acts on: Quarks, gluons (colour charge)
Theory: Quantum chromodynamics (QCD)
Range: ~1 femtometre (residual)

Quantum chromodynamics

The strong interaction is described by quantum chromodynamics (QCD), in which quarks carry a "colour charge" and exchange gluons. Colour confinement ensures quarks are never seen in isolation, while asymptotic freedom means the force weakens at very short distances — behaviour opposite to everyday forces. Hideki Yukawa's earlier meson-exchange picture correctly describes the residual nuclear force at longer range, with pions and other mesons exchanged between nucleons.

Nuclear force

The residual strong force between protons and neutrons is strong enough to bind nuclei up to iron, making fusion in stars possible, but falls off so rapidly with distance that very heavy nuclei become unstable.

Strength

On a scale where electromagnetism has strength 1, the strong interaction is roughly 137 times stronger, though it acts only over subatomic distances.
