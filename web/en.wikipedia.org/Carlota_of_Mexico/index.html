Born: 7 June 1840, Laeken, Belgium
Died: 19 January 1927, Meise, Belgium

Carlota of Mexico (born Princess Charlotte of Belgium; 7 June 1840 – 19 January 1927) was a Belgian princess who became Empress of Mexico as the wife of Emperor Maximilian I.

Early life and marriage

The only daughter of King Leopold I of Belgium and Louise of Orléans, Charlotte was a granddaughter of Louis-Philippe of France and a first cousin of Queen Victoria. In 1857 she married Archduke Ferdinand Maximilian of Austria. The couple lived at Miramare Castle near Trieste while Maximilian served as governor-general of Lombardy-Venetia.

Empress of Mexico

In 1864 the couple accepted the Mexican crown offered under French sponsorship during Napoleon III's intervention in Mexico. Carlota was an energetic consort: she presided over the council when Maximilian travelled, promoted infrastructure and charity, and adopted the Spanish name Carlota. As French troops withdrew and the empire collapsed, she sailed to Europe in 1866 to plead for aid from Napoleon III and Pope Pius IX — unsuccessfully.

Later life

During that mission her mental health broke down, and she never returned to Mexico. Maximilian was executed by the restored republican government in 1867. Carlota lived in seclusion in Belgium for sixty years, dying at Bouchout Castle in 1927.
