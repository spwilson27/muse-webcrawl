Monsoon

A monsoon is a seasonal change in the direction of the prevailing, or strongest, winds of a region. Monsoons cause wet and dry seasons throughout much of the tropics. The term originally referred to the seasonal reversing wind in the Indian Ocean and southern Asia, accompanied by corresponding changes in precipitation, but is now applied to similar systems worldwide.

Most summer monsoons have a dominant westerly component and a strong tendency to ascend and produce copious amounts of rain, caused by the warm waters of the oceans evaporating and the air rising. Winter monsoons, in contrast, predominantly have an easterly component and a strong tendency to diverge, subside, and cause drought.

Causes

Monsoons are caused by the greater amplitude of the seasonal cycle of land temperature compared with that of nearby oceans. This differential warming happens because heat in the ocean is mixed vertically through a "mixed layer" that may be fifty metres deep, while the land surface conducts heat slowly, with the seasonal signal penetrating only a metre or so. Additionally, the specific heat capacity of liquid water is significantly higher than that of most materials that make up land. Together, these factors mean the temperature of the surface of the ocean does not change as much as the land's temperature does over the course of a season.

Major monsoon systems

The most prominent monsoons in the world are the South Asian monsoon, which affects India and its neighbouring countries; the East Asian monsoon, affecting China, Korea, and Japan; the West African monsoon; the Australian monsoon; and the North American monsoon, which brings rainfall to parts of Mexico and the southwestern United States.

The South Asian monsoon

The Indian summer monsoon typically lasts from June to September. Vast areas of western and central India receive more than 90% of their total annual precipitation during this period, and south and southeast India receive more than 75% of their total annual rainfall. The monsoon is vital to agriculture in the region, and its onset, breaks, and withdrawal are closely watched.
