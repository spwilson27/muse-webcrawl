Tabla

Classification: Membranophone (hand drums)
Origin: Indian subcontinent
Components: Dayan (right) and bayan (left)
Used in: Hindustani music, qawwali, fusion

The tabla is a pair of hand drums from the Indian subcontinent, the principal percussion instrument in Hindustani classical music. It consists of two drums: the smaller, usually wooden dayan (or dahina), played with the dominant hand, and the larger, usually metal bayan, played with the other hand.

Construction

Each drum has a head (pudi) of layered goatskin with a central black spot (syahi or gatta) made of iron filings, rice paste and other materials, which gives the tabla its rich, bell-like overtones. Straps and wooden dowels adjust the tension and tuning; the dayan is tuned to the tonic of the performance.

Technique and repertoire

Tabla strokes have names (bols) that players recite as a form of notation and solfège. The instrument accompanies vocal and instrumental music, provides the rhythmic cycle (tala), and features in virtuosic solo performance. Noted exponents include Alla Rakha and his son Zakir Hussain. Tabla has also entered fusion, jazz and popular music worldwide.
