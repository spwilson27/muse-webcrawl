Radiation protection

The radiation warning trefoil.

Radiation protection, also known as radiological protection, is the science and practice of protecting people and the environment from the harmful effects of exposure to ionizing radiation. It rests on the principles of justification of practices, optimisation of protection, and application of dose limits, as recommended by the International Commission on Radiological Protection (ICRP) and regulated nationally in most countries.

Principles and practice

The guiding optimisation principle is known as ALARA — keeping doses "as low as reasonably achievable". Practical protection for external radiation relies on time (minimising exposure duration), distance (dose rate falls with the inverse square of distance), and shielding (materials such as lead, concrete or water appropriate to the radiation type). Internal exposure is controlled by containment, ventilation and protective clothing against contamination.

Dose limits

ICRP recommendations commonly adopted include an occupational effective dose limit of 20 mSv per year averaged over five years (with no more than 50 mSv in a single year) and a public limit of 1 mSv per year above background. Dosimeters are used to monitor occupational exposure. Radiation effects are divided into deterministic effects (such as acute radiation syndrome at high doses) and stochastic effects (increased cancer risk), whose management motivates the linear no-threshold model used in regulation.
