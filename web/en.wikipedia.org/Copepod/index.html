Copepod

Copepods are a group of small crustaceans found in nearly every freshwater and saltwater habitat. Some species are planktonic, others are benthic, and some continental species live in damp terrestrial habitats. They are usually 1 to 2 mm long, with a teardrop-shaped body and large antennae.

Ecology

Planktonic copepods are among the most abundant multicellular animals on Earth and form a crucial link in aquatic food webs, grazing on phytoplankton and in turn serving as food for fish larvae, krill-eating whales, and seabirds. Many species undertake daily vertical migrations, rising to feed at night and sinking by day.

Life cycle and parasites

Copepods develop through a series of nauplius larval stages. Some species are parasitic on fish and marine mammals, and certain freshwater copepods serve as intermediate hosts for parasites of humans, including the Guinea worm.
