Hip hop
OriginThe Bronx, New York City, 1970s
Key elementsDJing, MCing (rap), breakdance, graffiti
PioneersDJ Kool Herc, Afrika Bambaataa, Grandmaster Flash

Hip hop is a cultural movement and music genre that emerged in the Bronx, New York City, during the 1970s among African American, Latino and Caribbean American youth. It is generally described as comprising four elements: DJing (turntablism), MCing or rapping, breakdancing (b-boying), and graffiti art, with knowledge sometimes counted as a fifth element.

Origins

Jamaican-born DJ Kool Herc is widely credited with founding hip hop at parties in 1973 at 1520 Sedgwick Avenue, where he extended funk and soul breaks using two turntables while an MC rhymed over the beat. Afrika Bambaataa organised the movement through his Universal Zulu Nation, and Grandmaster Flash refined techniques such as cutting and scratching.

Golden age and commercialisation

Rap records broke through with the Sugarhill Gang's "Rapper's Delight" (1979). The mid-1980s to early 1990s "golden age" brought Run-DMC, LL Cool J, the Beastie Boys, Public Enemy and A Tribe Called Quest. In the 1990s, East Coast–West Coast rivalry, gangsta rap (N.W.A, Tupac Shakur, the Notorious B.I.G.) and alternative scenes made hip hop the dominant force in American popular music, a position it consolidated in the 2000s and 2010s through artists such as Jay-Z, Eminem, Kanye West, Kendrick Lamar and Drake, and through global regional scenes.

Influence

Hip hop has reshaped fashion, language, film and advertising worldwide, and remains a major vehicle for social commentary on race, poverty and urban life.
