Ruminant

Ruminants are herbivorous mammals of the suborder Ruminantia that acquire nutrients from plant-based food by fermenting it in a specialized multi-chambered stomach prior to digestion, principally through microbial actions. The process typically requires the fermented ingesta (known as cud) to be regurgitated and chewed again — "chewing the cud".

Digestion

In the rumen and reticulum, bacteria, protozoa, and fungi break down cellulose and ferment carbohydrates into volatile fatty acids, which the animal absorbs as its main energy source. The animal regurgitates cud for finer mastication, after which material passes to the omasum and the "true stomach" (abomasum) and intestines. Rumen microbes also synthesise B vitamins and microbial protein, but fermentation also produces methane, a greenhouse gas of environmental concern.

Importance

There are about 200 species of ruminants. Domesticated ruminants — cattle, sheep, and goats — are of immense economic importance, providing milk, meat, hides, and wool, and converting grassland unsuitable for crops into human food.
