Caledonia land dispute

The Caledonia land dispute (also called the Grand River land dispute, or in Haudenosaunee terms the Reclamation at Kanonhstaton) is an ongoing dispute between the Six Nations of the Grand River and the governments of Canada and Ontario over land near Caledonia, Ontario. It came to wide public attention in February 2006, when protesters from Six Nations occupied a housing development under construction called Douglas Creek Estates, asserting that the land lay within the Haldimand Tract granted to the Six Nations in 1784.

Background

The Haldimand Proclamation of 1784 granted the Six Nations land six miles deep on each side of the Grand River, from its mouth to its source, in compensation for lands lost in what became the United States. Over the following two centuries much of the tract was sold or taken, and Six Nations claims that many transactions were invalid. The underlying dispute forms part of the broader Grand River land claims, subject of litigation since the 1990s.

The 2006 occupation

On 28 February 2006, protesters occupied the subdivision site, renaming it Kanonhstaton ("the protected place"). An Ontario Provincial Police raid on 20 April 2006 failed to remove them and was followed by road blockades, confrontations, and lengthy court-ordered injunctions. The Ontario government purchased the developer's land in June 2006 for $12.3 million and later compensated the developer further; the site remained occupied and undeveloped for years. Related occupations occurred in 2020 at the McKenzie Meadows development (renamed 1492 Land Back Lane), which expanded the dispute south of Caledonia.

Legacy

The dispute is cited as a major case in Canadian Indigenous land rights and the role of injunctions and policing in such conflicts. The Douglas Creek Estates site remained undeveloped long after the events, and the larger land claim remains unresolved.
