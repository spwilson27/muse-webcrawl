Food web

FieldEcology
ComponentsProducers, consumers, decomposers
Key conceptsTrophic levels, food chains, keystone species

A food web is the natural interconnection of food chains and a graphical representation of what-eats-what in an ecological community. Ecologists can broadly organize all life forms into one of two categories called trophic levels: either the autotrophs or the heterotrophs. To maintain their bodies, grow, develop, and reproduce, autotrophs produce biomass from inorganic compounds — these are the primary producers, chiefly photosynthetic plants and algae. Heterotrophs consume biomass and include herbivores, carnivores, omnivores and decomposers.

Food chains and trophic levels

A food chain is a linear sequence of organisms through which nutrients and energy pass. Real feeding relationships are far more complex: most species eat, and are eaten by, more than one other species, so ecologists map them as webs of intersecting chains. Energy transfer between trophic levels is inefficient — typically only about ten per cent of energy is passed on — which limits the length of food chains and produces the familiar ecological pyramid.

History

The concept grew out of Charles Elton's Animal Ecology (1927), which introduced food cycles and the pyramid of numbers. Charles Sutherland Elton's contemporary, and later G. Evelyn Hutchinson and his students, formalised the study of trophic structure. Robert Paine's experiments removing the starfish Pisaster ochraceus from rocky shores in the 1960s demonstrated the outsized influence of keystone species on web structure.

Types of food web

  • Connectance webs — show feeding links only.
  • Energy flow webs — weight links by the quantity of energy transferred.
  • Functional (interaction) webs — reflect the strength of each species' effect on others.

Importance

Food web analysis underpins fisheries management, conservation biology and the prediction of how the loss of forage fish, the spread of invasive species, or the collapse of a forest ecosystem will cascade through a community. Apex predators often exert top-down control, while detritus-based webs recycle nutrients through decomposers.
