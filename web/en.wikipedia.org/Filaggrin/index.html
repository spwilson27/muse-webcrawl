Filaggrin

Filaggrin (filament aggregating protein) is a filament-associated protein that binds to keratin fibers in epithelial cells. Ten to twelve nearly identical filaggrin units are encoded within the profilaggrin gene, which is expressed in the granular layer of the epidermis. Filaggrin and its breakdown products are essential to the formation and hydration of the skin barrier.

Function

Profilaggrin is a large, highly phosphorylated precursor stored in keratohyalin granules. During terminal differentiation of keratinocytes it is dephosphorylated and cleaved into filaggrin monomers, which aggregate keratin filaments into dense bundles, flattening the cells of the stratum corneum. Filaggrin is later degraded into amino acids that form much of the "natural moisturizing factor" that keeps the outer skin hydrated and acidic.

Clinical significance

Loss-of-function mutations in the FLG gene cause ichthyosis vulgaris, a common disorder of dry, scaly skin, and are the strongest known genetic risk factor for atopic dermatitis (eczema). FLG mutations are also associated with increased risk of asthma and food allergies in the context of eczema — the "atopic march" — supporting the idea that a defective skin barrier permits allergic sensitisation. Variations in filaggrin repeat number are especially common in populations of European and East Asian ancestry.
