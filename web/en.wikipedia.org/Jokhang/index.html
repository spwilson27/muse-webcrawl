Jokhang

The Jokhang (Tibetan: ཇོ་ཁང་), also known as the Qoikang Monastery, Jokhang Temple, Jokhang Monastery and Zuglagkang, is a Buddhist temple in Barkhor Square in Lhasa, the capital city of Tibet. Tibetans generally consider this temple as the most sacred and important temple in Tibet. The temple is currently maintained by the Gelug school, but it accepts worshipers from all sects of Buddhism.

History

The temple was founded in the 7th century by King Songtsen Gampo to house the Jowo Mikyo Dorje statue of the Buddha brought by his Nepalese queen Bhrikuti; it later came to enshrine the Jowo Śākyamuni statue brought by his Chinese queen Wencheng, now the most venerated object in Tibetan Buddhism. The temple's founding is tied to the legend of the supine demoness, whose body the geomantic plan of Tibet's temples was meant to pin down.

Architecture and pilgrimage

The Jokhang blends Tibetan, Nepalese, and Chinese architectural styles across four floors of chapels around a central hall containing the Jowo statue. Pilgrims circumambulate the temple along the Barkhor circuit, many prostrating for the entire route. The Jokhang is a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the Historic Ensemble of the Potala Palace.
