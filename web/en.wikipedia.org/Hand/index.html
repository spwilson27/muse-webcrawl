Hand

Hand
Latin: manus
System: Musculoskeletal
Artery: Ulnar and radial arteries
Nerve: Ulnar, median, radial nerves
Bones: Carpals, metacarpals, phalanges

A hand is a prehensile, multi-fingered appendage located at the end of the forearm or forelimb of primates such as humans, chimpanzees, monkeys and lemurs. The human hand normally has five digits: four fingers plus one thumb, with a total of 27 bones (including the sesamoids variation).

Structure

The hand divides into the palm and dorsum, containing carpals in the wrist, metacarpals in the palm, and phalanges in the fingers. Muscles are intrinsic (within the hand, including thenar and hypothenar groups) and extrinsic (in the forearm). See Humerus for the upper arm.

Function

The opposable thumb enables precision and power grips, tool use and gesture. Rich sensory innervation, especially in fingertips, supports fine touch.

Clinical

Common conditions include carpal tunnel syndrome, fractures, arthritis and Dupuytren's contracture. Surgical repair relates to Hip replacement as orthopaedics more broadly.
