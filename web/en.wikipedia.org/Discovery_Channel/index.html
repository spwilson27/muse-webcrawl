The Discovery Channel (known as The Discovery Channel from 1985 to 1995, and often referred to simply as Discovery) is an American cable television network and flagship channel of Warner Bros. Discovery. Founded by John Hendricks, it launched on June 17, 1985, and originally provided documentary television programming focused primarily on science, technology, and history.

Programming

Since the 2000s the channel has shifted toward reality television and pseudo-scientific entertainment alongside its documentaries. Long-running and popular series include MythBusters, Deadliest Catch, Dirty Jobs, Gold Rush, How It's Made, and the annual Shark Week programming event, first aired in 1988 and considered the longest-running cable television event in history.

International reach

Discovery Channel is available in many countries and languages through regional versions and sister networks, and its brand extends to related channels such as Animal Planet, Science Channel, and Investigation Discovery.
