A sonobuoy (a portmanteau of sonar and buoy) is a small expendable sonar system dropped or ejected from aircraft or ships conducting anti-submarine warfare or underwater acoustic research. On hitting the water, the buoy floats on the surface while a hydrophone descends on a cable to a selected depth; acoustic signals are relayed by radio transmitter to the launching platform, allowing operators to detect, classify, and localize submarines over wide areas by laying patterns of buoys.

Types

Passive sonobuoys only listen: omnidirectional types (such as LOFAR buoys) detect radiated noise, while directional DIFAR buoys also provide a bearing to the contact. Active sonobuoys emit sonar pulses and listen for echoes. Special-purpose types include bathythermograph buoys, which measure the temperature profile needed to predict sonar performance, and commandable buoys that can be activated or scuttled remotely. Sonobuoys are designed to sink after a set operating life.

History and use

Sonobuoys were developed during World War II for locating U-boats and became a primary wide-area search sensor of maritime patrol aircraft such as the P-3 Orion and its successor the P-8 Poseidon during and after the Cold War. They are also used in marine science, for example to record whale calls and ambient ocean noise, and in oil and gas exploration support.
