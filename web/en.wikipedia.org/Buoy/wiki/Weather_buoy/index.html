TypeMoored or drifting instrumented buoy

A weather buoy is a buoy equipped with sensors that collect weather and ocean data within the world's oceans and lakes. Data collected include air temperature, barometric pressure, humidity, wind speed and direction, sea surface temperature, and wave energy spectra from which significant wave height and period are derived. Weather buoys may be moored to the seabed at fixed stations or released to drift with ocean currents.

Networks and uses

In the United States, the NOAA National Data Buoy Center (NDBC) operates a network of moored buoys around the coasts, the Great Lakes, and offshore waters, reporting hourly observations used in weather forecasting, storm warnings, and marine safety. Internationally, tropical moored arrays such as TAO/TRITON in the Pacific, PIRATA in the Atlantic, and RAMA in the Indian Ocean monitor phenomena including El Niño–Southern Oscillation. Thousands of drifting buoys coordinated through the Data Buoy Cooperation Panel measure surface temperature, pressure, and currents.

Design

Moored weather buoys range from 3-metre discus buoys to 12-metre hulls carrying meteorological masts, solar panels, and batteries. Data are transmitted to shore by satellite and distributed in near real time through the Global Telecommunication System. Vandalism, shipping collisions, and harsh sea states are persistent operational challenges, and stations in hurricane-prone regions provide critical measurements as storms pass.
