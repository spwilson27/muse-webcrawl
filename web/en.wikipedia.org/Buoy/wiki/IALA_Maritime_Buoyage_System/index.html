The IALA Maritime Buoyage System is the international standard system of maritime navigational marks — buoys and beacons — used to indicate channels, hazards, and other features to mariners. It was developed by the International Association of Marine Aids to Navigation and Lighthouse Authorities (IALA) and implemented from 1980, harmonizing dozens of previously conflicting national systems.

Regions

The world is divided into two buoyage regions. Region A covers Europe, Africa, most of Asia, Australia, and New Zealand; Region B covers the Americas, Japan, Korea, and the Philippines. The regions differ in the colours of lateral marks: when returning from sea (entering harbour), port-hand marks are red in Region A but green in Region B — remembered in Region B by the phrase "red, right, returning".

Types of marks

Lateral marks define the edges of a channel, using can (cylindrical) and conical shapes, colours, and topmarks. Cardinal marks indicate where safe water lies relative to a hazard by compass quadrant, painted black and yellow with two cones as topmarks and characteristic white light rhythms. Other categories are isolated danger marks (black with red bands, two spheres), safe water marks (red-and-white stripes, single sphere), special marks (yellow, for purposes such as recreation zones and cable areas), and emergency wreck marking buoys (blue and yellow), introduced in 2006 after the wreck of the Tricolor.
