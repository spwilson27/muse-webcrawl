Manzanillo, Colima

Country: Mexico
State: Colima
Founded: 1873 (as a port)
Elevation: ~10 m
Known for: Busiest container port in Mexico; sport fishing

Manzanillo is a city and port in the Mexican state of Colima, on the Pacific coast. It is the seat of the municipality of Manzanillo and is Mexico's busiest port, handling much of the country's Pacific container trade, alongside a long-established beach tourism industry.

History

The bay was used from the colonial era; ships were built there for Pacific exploration, and the harbour was formally opened to commerce in the 19th century. Rail links to Guadalajara and the interior turned Manzanillo into a major gateway. The old town sits on a peninsula between Manzanillo Bay and the Cuyutlán lagoon, while resort development spread along Santiago Bay to the north.

Economy

The port handles containers, bulk cargo and vehicles and ranks among the largest on Latin America's Pacific coast. Tourism centres on the bays' beaches — Playa La Audiencia, Las Brisas, Santiago and Olas Altas among them — and the town promotes itself as a sport-fishing destination, especially for sailfish, celebrated with an annual tournament and a giant sailfish sculpture on the waterfront.

Geography and climate

Manzanillo has a tropical savanna climate with a wet summer season and high humidity. The area is exposed to Pacific hurricanes; Hurricane Patricia, among the strongest on record, passed near the coast in 2015.
