Blended whisky

A blended whisky (or blended whiskey) is the product of blending different types of whiskies and sometimes also neutral grain spirits, colorings, and flavorings. It is generally the product of mixing one or more higher-quality straight or single malt whiskies with less expensive spirits and other ingredients. This allows for a lower priced end product, although expensive "premium" varieties also exist.

Scotch whisky

Blended Scotch whisky constitutes the great majority of Scotch sold worldwide. It is a blend of one or more single malt Scotch whiskies with one or more single grain Scotch whiskies. The practice of blending was pioneered in the 19th century by firms such as Andrew Usher and later popularized by brands including Johnnie Walker, Dewar's, Chivas Regal, and Ballantine's. Under Scotch whisky regulations, the age statement on a blend refers to the youngest whisky in it, and all components must be matured in oak casks in Scotland for at least three years.

Other varieties

Blended whiskies are also produced in Ireland, Canada, Japan, and the United States. American blended whiskey may contain a substantial proportion of neutral spirits, whereas Canadian whisky is often a blend of flavouring whisky with a lighter base whisky. Japanese blends, produced by houses such as Suntory and Nikka, follow the Scotch model closely.

Blending

The master blender's task is to combine dozens of component whiskies so the finished product is consistent from batch to batch, balancing the character of malts with the smoothness of grain whiskies. Blends are typically married together in casks or vats before bottling.
