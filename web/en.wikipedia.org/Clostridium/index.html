Clostridium

Clostridium is a genus of Gram-positive, rod-shaped bacteria that are obligate anaerobes capable of producing endospores. The name derives from the Greek klōstēr (κλωστήρ), "spindle", referring to the shape many species take when sporulating. Species of Clostridium are widely distributed in soils, sediments, and the intestinal tracts of animals and humans. The genus historically included several of the most important bacterial pathogens of humans, as well as species used industrially for solvent production.

Characteristics

Clostridia are anaerobic: most cannot grow in the presence of oxygen, although some tolerate brief exposure. They form highly resistant endospores that survive heat, desiccation, and disinfectants, germinating when conditions become favourable. Most species are motile by peritrichous flagella and ferment a wide range of organic substrates, producing organic acids, alcohols, carbon dioxide, and hydrogen.

Pathogenic species

Several species produce potent toxins. Clostridium botulinum produces botulinum toxin, the cause of botulism and also the active ingredient in therapeutic and cosmetic preparations. Clostridium tetani produces tetanospasmin, which causes the spastic paralysis of tetanus. Clostridium perfringens is a cause of gas gangrene and a common agent of foodborne illness. The organism long known as Clostridium difficile, a major cause of antibiotic-associated colitis, has been reclassified as Clostridioides difficile.

Taxonomy

Genomic studies showed that the classical genus was polyphyletic, grouping organisms on the basis of morphology and spore formation rather than evolutionary relationship. In the 2010s many species were moved to new genera such as Clostridioides, Hathewaya, Paeniclostridium, and Paraclostridium, leaving a more restricted Clostridium centred on the cluster containing C. butyricum and its relatives.

Industrial uses

The acetone–butanol–ethanol fermentation performed by Clostridium acetobutylicum was developed during the First World War — the Weizmann process — to supply acetone for cordite manufacture, and interest in clostridial fermentations has revived with the search for renewable biofuels and platform chemicals.
