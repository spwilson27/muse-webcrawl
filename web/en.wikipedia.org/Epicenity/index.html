Epicenity

Epicenity refers to the quality of a word, name, or grammatical form that is common to both sexes, or to a lack of gender distinction. A word described as epicene has a single form that does not vary according to the sex of its referent. The term derives from the Greek epikoinos, "common".

Epicene forms do not change to mark masculine or feminine gender

In grammar

In languages with grammatical gender, epicene nouns denote beings of either sex while keeping a fixed gender. In English, which has little grammatical gender, the concept applies mainly to words such as "spouse", "parent", "cousin", and "friend", and to singular they constructions. Terms like "actor" used for performers of any sex are also treated as epicene, in contrast to gendered pairs such as "actor/actress".

In names

Given names used for both males and females — such as Alex, Jordan, or Taylor in English — are called epicene or unisex names. Their popularity varies across cultures and periods.

In biology

In zoology, "epicene" can describe organisms or structures in which the sexes are not readily distinguished.
