Giant slalom (GS) is an alpine skiing and alpine snowboarding discipline. It involves skiing between sets of poles ("gates") spaced at a greater distance from each other than in slalom but less than in Super-G. Giant slalom and slalom make up the technical events in alpine ski racing; this category separates them from the speed events of Super-G and downhill.

Course and technique

A giant slalom course has a vertical drop of 250 to 450 metres for men and 250 to 400 metres for women, with turning gates consisting of paired red and blue poles. Racers typically reach speeds of up to 40 km/h and use carving technique on skis with a sidecut radius regulated by the FIS — currently a minimum of 30 metres for World Cup equipment. Competitions are normally decided over two runs on different courses, with the times added together.

History

The first giant slalom is widely credited to have been set in 1935 on the Mottarone in Italy by Count Guido Schmidt. The discipline was introduced to the World Championships in 1950 at Aspen and became an Olympic event at the 1952 Winter Olympics in Oslo. Originally raced over a single run, the two-run format became standard in the 1960s. Notable practitioners include Ingemar Stenmark, who dominated the discipline in the 1970s and 1980s, and Marcel Hirscher and Mikaela Shiffrin in the modern era.
