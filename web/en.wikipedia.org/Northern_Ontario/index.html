Northern Ontario

Northern Ontario
Country: Canada
Province: Ontario
Area: about 806,000 km² (about 87% of Ontario)
Population: about 790,000 (2021, approx.)
Largest city: Greater Sudbury

Northern Ontario is a primary geographic and quasi-administrative region of the Canadian province of Ontario, the other being Southern Ontario. Occupying roughly 87 percent of the province's land area but containing only about 6 percent of its population, it extends north from the French River and Lake Nipissing to Hudson Bay, and west to the Manitoba border.

Geography

Most of the region lies on the Canadian Shield, a landscape of thin soils, exposed Precambrian rock, thousands of lakes, and boreal forest. The Hudson Bay Lowlands in the far north are among the world's largest wetlands. Major cities include Greater Sudbury, Thunder Bay, Sault Ste. Marie, North Bay, and Timmins.

Economy and culture

Mining (notably nickel and copper around Sudbury, and gold at Timmins and Red Lake), forestry, and hydroelectric power dominate the economy. The region has a distinct identity from the south, with a significant Franco-Ontarian population, large Indigenous communities, and periodic political movements proposing that Northern Ontario become a separate province. Resource dependence, long distances, and population decline in smaller communities are persistent challenges.

Administration

For provincial purposes the region is divided into districts rather than counties, and it is represented federally and provincially by its own set of ridings. Sudbury serves as the principal service and government centre for the northeast, and Thunder Bay for the northwest.
