Computer scientist

A computer scientist is a scholar who specialises in the academic study of computer science, including the theory of computation, algorithms, information, and the design of computing systems. Computer scientists work in universities, industrial research laboratories, and technology companies.

Work

Computer scientists develop new approaches to computation, analyse the properties and limits of algorithms, design programming languages and software systems, and study areas such as artificial intelligence, computer networks, databases, security, and human–computer interaction. Unlike software engineers, whose focus is primarily on building products, computer scientists are typically concerned with underlying principles and research questions, though the roles overlap in practice.

Education

Most research computer scientists hold a doctoral degree in computer science or a related field, following undergraduate study in computer science, mathematics, or engineering. Professional bodies such as the Association for Computing Machinery (ACM) and the IEEE Computer Society support the discipline.

Recognition

The Turing Award, presented annually by the ACM, is widely regarded as the highest honour in computer science. Notable computer scientists include Alan Turing, Ada Lovelace, Donald Knuth, Edsger W. Dijkstra, Grace Hopper, Tim Berners-Lee, and Barbara Liskov.
