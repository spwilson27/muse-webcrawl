Biscuit

A biscuit is a flour-based baked food product. The word has different meanings in different varieties of English. In most of the English-speaking world, including the United Kingdom, a biscuit is a small, typically sweet or savoury, crisp baked item also called a cookie in North America. In the United States and parts of Canada, a "biscuit" is instead a soft, leavened bread, similar to a scone, commonly served with meals such as fried chicken or covered in gravy.

Etymology and history

The word derives from the Latin bis coctus — "twice cooked" — via Old French, because early biscuits and ship's biscuits (hardtack) were baked twice to dry them out so they would keep on long voyages. Industrial production in 19th-century Britain, by firms such as Huntley & Palmers of Reading, turned biscuits into a mass-market food.

British biscuits

Popular British types include digestives, rich tea, custard creams, bourbons, shortbread, ginger nuts, Hobnobs, and Jaffa Cakes (whose classification as cake or biscuit was famously litigated for tax purposes). Biscuits are commonly eaten with tea, and "dunking" them is a well-known custom.

North American biscuits

The American biscuit is a quick bread made with baking powder or baking soda, often made with buttermilk, served warm as a side dish, in breakfast sandwiches, or in dishes such as biscuits and gravy and strawberry shortcake.
