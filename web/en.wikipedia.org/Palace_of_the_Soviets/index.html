Palace of the Soviets

Location: Moscow, Soviet Union

Architect: Boris Iofan (winning design)

Status: Never completed

The Palace of the Soviets was a project to construct a monumental administrative centre and congress hall in Moscow, intended as the ultimate symbol of the Soviet state under Joseph Stalin. Had it been built at its planned height of about 415 metres, topped by a 100-metre statue of Vladimir Lenin, it would have been the tallest building in the world.

Competition and design

The Cathedral of Christ the Saviour was demolished in 1931 to clear the site. An international competition (1931–33) drew entries from Le Corbusier, Walter Gropius and others, but the winning scheme by Boris Iofan, developed with Vladimir Shchuko and Vladimir Gelfreikh, was a vast stepped neoclassical tower in the emerging Stalinist style.

Construction and abandonment

Foundations were laid in the late 1930s, but the German invasion in 1941 halted work; steel was diverted to the war effort. After the war the project was never resumed, and under Nikita Khrushchev the foundation pit became the Moskva open-air swimming pool. The cathedral was rebuilt on the site in the 1990s.

Legacy

The palace epitomises Stalinist monumental architecture and the related doctrine of Socialist realism. Its stepped silhouette influenced the post-war "Seven Sisters" skyscrapers of Moscow.
