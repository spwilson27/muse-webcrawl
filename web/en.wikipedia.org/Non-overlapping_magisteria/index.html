Non-overlapping magisteria

Non-overlapping magisteria (NOMA) is a principle proposed by palaeontologist Stephen Jay Gould holding that science and religion occupy separate domains of teaching authority ("magisteria"): science covers the empirical realm of facts and theories, while religion addresses questions of ultimate meaning and moral value, so the two need not conflict.

Reception

Gould set out the idea in a 1997 essay in Natural History and expanded it in his book Rocks of Ages (1999). It has been endorsed by bodies such as the US National Academy of Sciences as a way of reconciling evolution with religious belief, but criticised by others — including Richard Dawkins — who argue that religions do make factual claims, and by some theologians who reject such a strict separation.
