The Surrender of Breda (Spanish: La rendición de Breda), also known as Las Lanzas ("The Lances"), is a painting by the Spanish Golden Age painter Diego Velázquez. It was completed during the years 1634–35 and commemorates the surrender of the Dutch city of Breda on 5 June 1625 to the Spanish Army of Flanders under Ambrogio Spinola during the Eighty Years' War.

Subject

The painting shows the moment when Justin of Nassau, the Dutch commander, hands the keys of the city to the Genoese-born Spanish general Ambrogio Spinola. Rather than a scene of humiliation, Velázquez depicts a courteous exchange between gentlemen: Spinola greets his defeated opponent with a hand on his shoulder, an unusual note of magnanimity in victory painting. Behind Spinola stands the forest of upright lances of the Spanish tercios that gives the picture its popular name, Las Lanzas.

History

The work was commissioned for the Hall of Realms (Salón de Reinos) in the Buen Retiro Palace in Madrid, where it formed part of a cycle celebrating Spanish military victories of the reign of Philip IV. It is now in the collection of the Museo del Prado in Madrid, where it is regarded as one of Velázquez's masterpieces and one of the finest history paintings of the Baroque period.

Composition

Velázquez, who never visited Breda, drew on accounts of the event and possibly on a print of the siege. The composition balances the ordered Spanish ranks on the right against the looser Dutch group on the left, with a panoramic landscape of the besieged city receding behind them.
