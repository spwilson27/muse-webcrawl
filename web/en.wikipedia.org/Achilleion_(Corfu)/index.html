Achilleion (Corfu)

Location: Gastouri, Corfu, Greece
Built: 1889–1891
Commissioned by: Empress Elisabeth of Austria
Theme: Achilles and Greek mythology

The Achilleion is a palace on the Greek island of Corfu, built for Empress Elisabeth of Austria (known as Sisi) between 1889 and 1891. Designed by the Italian architect Raffaele Caritto in a Pompeian neoclassical style, the palace is devoted entirely to the mythological hero Achilles, whom Elisabeth admired as an embodiment of beauty, strength, and the Greek soul.

History

Elisabeth visited Corfu from 1861 and fell in love with the island. After the suicide of her only son, Crown Prince Rudolf, in 1889, she had a villa on the site demolished and built the Achilleion as a retreat from the Viennese court. She spent extended periods there until her assassination in 1898.

After her death the palace passed to her heirs and in 1907 was purchased by Kaiser Wilhelm II of Germany, who used it as a summer residence and replaced some of its decoration, adding the monumental bronze statue of Achilles Victorious. During the First World War the building served as a military hospital; during the Second it was used by Axis forces. Afterwards it became state property, served for a time as a casino, and is now a museum.

Artwork

The palace and gardens are filled with classical sculpture. The most famous works are Ernst Gustav Herter's Dying Achilles (1884) in the garden — the emotional centre of the palace, showing the hero pulling the arrow from his heel — and the fresco in the main hall depicting Achilles dragging the body of Hector around the walls of Troy. The gardens also hold statues of the nine Muses, Apollo, and other figures of Greek myth.
