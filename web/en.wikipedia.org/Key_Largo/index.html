Key Largo

Key Largo is the first and largest island of the Florida Keys.

Key Largo
Location: Florida Keys, Monroe County, Florida
Length: about 33 miles (53 km)
Population: roughly 12,000 (community of Key Largo)
Known for: John Pennekamp Coral Reef State Park

Key Largo is an island in the upper Florida Keys archipelago and the largest of the Keys, lying about an hour's drive south of Miami. Its name derives from the Spanish Cayo Largo, "long key". The island is roughly 33 miles (53 km) long and is connected to the mainland by the Overseas Highway (U.S. Route 1) and by Card Sound Road.

Geography and attractions

Key Largo is largely composed of fossilised coral limestone. It is home to two state parks: the Dagny Johnson Key Largo Hammock Botanical State Park, protecting one of the largest tracts of West Indian tropical hardwood hammock in the United States, and the John Pennekamp Coral Reef State Park, established in 1963 as the first undersea park in the United States. Offshore lies the Key Largo National Marine Sanctuary area of the Florida Keys National Marine Sanctuary, with coral reefs, the wreck of the USS Spiegel Grove, and the underwater statue Christ of the Abyss. The island markets itself as the "Diving Capital of the World".

History and culture

The island was inhabited by Calusa and Tequesta peoples before Spanish contact. In the 19th and early 20th centuries it supported pineapple plantations, sponge and fishing communities, and a station on Henry Flagler's Florida East Coast Railway. The 1948 film noir Key Largo, starring Humphrey Bogart and Lauren Bacall, made the name famous, although it was filmed largely in a studio; the Caribbean Club bar on the island, which claims a connection to the production, remains a local landmark, as does the steamboat African Queen from the Bogart film of that name, now kept at Key Largo.
