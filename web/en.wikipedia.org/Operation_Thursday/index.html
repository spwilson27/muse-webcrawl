Operation Thursday

Operation Thursday
Date5 March – August 1944
LocationBurma (Myanmar)
UnitChindits (Special Force), Brig. Orde Wingate
Strength~9,000–12,000 men
ResultChindit strongholds established; mixed strategic effect

Operation Thursday was the second, larger expedition of the Chindits — long-range penetration groups of the British and Indian armies — behind Japanese lines in Burma during the Second World War. Launched on 5 March 1944 under Brigadier Orde Wingate, it inserted some 9,000 men by glider and transport aircraft to establish fortified "strongholds" astride Japanese supply lines.

Gliders landing Chindits at the "Broadway" stronghold in Burma.

The operation

Unlike the first Chindit expedition of 1943 (Operation Longcloth), which had marched in on foot, Thursday relied on air power: gliders towed by Dakotas of the USAAF's No. 1 Air Commando Group landed troops at clearings codenamed Broadway and Chowringhee, after which engineers built airstrips so C-47s could fly in the bulk of the force with artillery and supplies. Strongholds named White City, Blackpool and Aberdeen were established near Indaw to block the railway supplying Japanese forces facing the Chinese-American advance in the north.

Wingate's death and aftermath

Wingate was killed in an air crash on 24 March 1944 and succeeded by Brigadier Walter Lentaigne. The Chindits fought hard defensive battles, notably at White City and in the failed attack on Indaw; the Blackpool stronghold had to be abandoned in May. Weakened by casualties, disease and exhaustion, the survivors were evacuated or attached to General Stilwell's forces by August 1944.

Assessment

Historians debate Thursday's value: it disrupted Japanese communications and drew off reserves during the Imphal and Kohima crisis, and proved air-supplied deep-penetration warfare feasible, but at very high cost to an elite force. It remains a landmark in special operations and air mobility.
