Kim Gordon

Kim Althea Gordon (born April 28, 1953) is an American musician, singer, songwriter, and visual artist. She rose to prominence as the bassist, guitarist, and vocalist of the alternative rock band Sonic Youth, which she co-founded with Thurston Moore in 1981 and which became one of the most influential bands of the American underground before its dissolution in 2011–12.

Early life

Gordon was born in Rochester, New York, and grew up in Los Angeles, where her father was a sociology professor at UCLA. She studied at the Otis Art Institute, immersed herself in the downtown New York art world after moving east in 1980, wrote art criticism, and co-founded Sonic Youth with Thurston Moore and Lee Ranaldo in 1981.

Sonic Youth

With Sonic Youth Gordon helped pioneer the use of alternate guitar tunings and feedback, contributing vocals and bass on signature songs including "Kool Thing", "Bull in the Heather" and "Shadow of a Doubt". The band's albums Daydream Nation (1988) and Goo (1990) were critical landmarks. Gordon also directed music videos, produced Hole's debut album Pretty on the Inside (1991), and co-founded the fashion label X-Girl in 1994. Her marriage to Moore ended in 2011 amid the band's split, chronicled in her memoir.

Later work

Gordon formed the guitar duo Body/Head with Bill Nace, releasing Coming Apart (2013) and No Waves (2016), and the project Glitterbust with Alex Knost. Her debut solo album No Home Record (2019), produced by Justin Raisen, drew acclaim for its abrasive, beat-driven sound; a second solo album, The Collective, followed in 2024. Her memoir Girl in a Band (2015) was a New York Times bestseller. She continues to exhibit visual art internationally.
