Northern Mali conflict
Date16 January 2012 – present
LocationNorthern Mali (Azawad); wider Sahel
BelligerentsMali and allies; Tuareg separatists (MNLA, later CMA); jihadist groups (Ansar Dine, AQIM, MUJAO, later JNIM and IS-GS)
OutcomeOngoing; French intervention 2013; UN mission MINUSMA 2013–2023

The Northern Mali conflict is an armed conflict in the north of Mali that began in January 2012 with a rebellion by Tuareg separatists of the National Movement for the Liberation of Azawad (MNLA), who sought independence for the northern region they call Azawad. The rebellion was soon hijacked by Islamist armed groups, and instability was compounded by a military coup in Bamako in March 2012.

Background

Tuareg rebellions against the Malian state had erupted repeatedly since independence in 1960, driven by marginalisation of the arid north. The fall of Muammar Gaddafi in Libya in 2011 sent heavily armed Tuareg fighters back into Mali, tipping the balance against government forces in early 2012.

Course of the conflict

By April 2012 the MNLA and its Islamist allies had seized the northern cities of Kidal, Gao, and Timbuktu, and the MNLA declared Azawad independent. The Islamist groups—Ansar Dine, al-Qaeda in the Islamic Maghreb, and MUJAO—then turned on the MNLA and imposed strict sharia law, destroying shrines in Timbuktu. When jihadist forces advanced south toward Bamako in January 2013, France intervened militarily in Operation Serval, retaking the northern cities within weeks. The United Nations mission MINUSMA deployed later in 2013, and a peace agreement was signed in Algiers in 2015.

Despite the accord, violence spread into central Mali and neighbouring Burkina Faso and Niger, with groups affiliated to al-Qaeda (JNIM) and the Islamic State contesting territory. France ended Operation Barkhane in 2022 after relations with Mali's military juntas (which seized power in 2020 and 2021) collapsed; MINUSMA withdrew in 2023 at the junta's demand, and fighting between the army, separatists, and jihadists continued.

Humanitarian impact

The conflict has displaced hundreds of thousands of people, destroyed cultural heritage in Timbuktu, and produced widespread food insecurity across the Sahel.
