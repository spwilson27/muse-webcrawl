eBird

eBird is an online database of bird observations providing scientists, researchers and amateur naturalists with real-time data about bird distribution and abundance. It was launched in 2002 by the Cornell Lab of Ornithology at Cornell University and the National Audubon Society. Originally restricted to sightings from the Western Hemisphere, eBird expanded to cover the whole world in 2010.

How it works

Participants submit checklists of birds seen or heard at a location, along with effort information such as duration and distance travelled. Observations are screened by automated filters and reviewed by volunteer regional experts. The data are freely available for download and have been used in hundreds of scientific publications, conservation assessments and status-and-trends models.

Scale

eBird is among the largest citizen science projects in the world, with well over a billion bird observations submitted by hundreds of thousands of participants. Its companion mobile app allows field submission, and the related Merlin Bird ID app, also from the Cornell Lab, uses eBird data to suggest likely species. May's Global Big Day, run through eBird, has recorded more than 7,000 species in a single day.
