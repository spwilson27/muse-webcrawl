Nadi

Nadi (Sanskrit: नाडी, nāḍī, "tube, channel, river") is a term for the channels through which, in traditional Indian medicine and yogic theory, the energies of the subtle body — such as prana (life-force) — are said to flow. The concept parallels the meridians of traditional Chinese medicine and the sen lines of Thai massage.

Overview

Yogic texts give varying numbers of nadis, from 72,000 to several hundred thousand, but three are considered principal: the ida, associated with the left side of the body, lunar and cooling qualities; the pingala, associated with the right side, solar and heating qualities; and the sushumna, the central channel running along the spinal column, through which the dormant energy kundalini is said to ascend when awakened.

Function

In hatha yoga, practices such as pranayama (breath regulation), including alternate-nostril breathing (nadi shodhana), are intended to clear and balance the nadis so that prana can flow freely through the sushumna. The channels are said to intersect at the chakras, the energy centres of the subtle body.
