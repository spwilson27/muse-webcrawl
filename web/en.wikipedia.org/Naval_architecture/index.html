Naval architecture

Naval architecture is an engineering discipline concerned with the design, construction, maintenance and operation of marine vessels and structures. It combines mechanical, electrical, electronic and materials engineering with hydrostatics, hydrodynamics and structural analysis.

Scope

A naval architect determines a ship's hull geometry, stability, buoyancy, resistance and propulsion, structural strength, and seakeeping and manoeuvring behaviour, and ensures compliance with classification society rules and international conventions such as SOLAS and MARPOL. The field covers cargo ships, warships, passenger vessels, submarines, yachts and offshore platforms.

History

Shipbuilding was an empirical craft until the 17th–18th centuries, when figures such as Fredrik Henrik af Chapman applied mathematics to hull design. The profession was formalised in the 19th century with institutions such as the Royal Institution of Naval Architects (founded 1860) and William Froude's work on model testing and scaling laws.
