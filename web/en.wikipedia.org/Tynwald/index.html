The Tynwald (Manx: Tinvaal), sometimes referred to formally as the High Court of Tynwald or as Tynwald Court, is the legislature of the Isle of Man. It has two branches: the directly elected House of Keys and the indirectly chosen Legislative Council. The two branches sit separately for most purposes but come together as Tynwald Court for certain functions, including financial business.

Tynwald claims to be the oldest continuously operating parliament in the world, tracing its origins to Norse assemblies of the Viking settlers of the island; its name derives from the Old Norse Þingvǫllr, "assembly field", cognate with Iceland's Þingvellir.

Tynwald Day

Each year on Tynwald Day (5 July, or the following Monday), the two branches meet in the open air on Tynwald Hill at St John's. Laws passed during the year are promulgated — read out in English and Manx Gaelic — and petitions for redress may be presented. A law that is not promulgated within 18 months lapses.
