Dove


A white dove, a traditional symbol of peace.

Dove is a common name for members of the bird family Columbidae, which also includes pigeons. In ordinary usage, "dove" tends to denote the smaller, slimmer species and "pigeon" the larger ones, but there is no strict scientific distinction. The family comprises over 300 species found worldwide.

Characteristics

Doves are stout-bodied birds with short necks and small bills, feeding mainly on seeds, fruits, and plants. Many are strong fliers with a distinctive head-bobbing gait. Common species include the mourning dove of North America, the Eurasian collared dove, and the rock dove — the wild ancestor of the domestic pigeon.

Symbolism

Doves carry rich cultural meaning. In the Bible, a dove returns to Noah's ark with an olive branch, and the Holy Spirit descends in the form of a dove. White doves — often actually white homing pigeons — are released at weddings and ceremonies and serve as an emblem of peace, used prominently since Picasso's 1949 lithograph for the World Peace Congress.
