Ascites

Ascites is the abnormal build-up of fluid in the abdomen. Technically, it is more than 25 ml of fluid in the peritoneal cavity, although volumes greater than one litre may occur. Symptoms may include increased abdominal size, increased weight, abdominal discomfort, and shortness of breath. Complications can include spontaneous bacterial peritonitis.

Causes

In the developed world, the most common cause is portal hypertension due to liver cirrhosis, accounting for the majority of cases; other causes include cancer, heart failure, tuberculosis, and pancreatic disease. Ascites is classified using the serum-ascites albumin gradient (SAAG) into high-gradient (transudative) and low-gradient (exudative) forms.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is typically based on an examination together with ultrasound or a CT scan. Testing the fluid, obtained by paracentesis, can help determine the underlying cause. Physical signs include flank dullness and shifting dullness, and a fluid thrill in large-volume ascites.

Treatment

Treatment often involves a low-salt diet, diuretics such as spironolactone and furosemide, and avoidance of alcohol. If the condition does not respond to these, large-volume paracentesis or a transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS) may be required. Refractory ascites carries a poor prognosis, and liver transplantation may be considered.
