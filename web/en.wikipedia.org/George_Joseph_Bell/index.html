George Joseph Bell (1770–1843) was a Scottish jurist and legal writer, professor of Scots law at the University of Edinburgh, and one of the principal institutional writers on the law of Scotland. He was the younger brother of the surgeon John Bell and of Charles Bell, the anatomist and neurologist.

Life and career

Bell was admitted as an advocate in 1791 and quickly established a reputation in commercial law. From 1822 he held the chair of Scots law at Edinburgh. He served as a commissioner on the reform of bankruptcy law, and his work contributed to the modernisation of Scottish commercial and insolvency practice in the early nineteenth century.

Works

His best-known book, Principles of the Law of Scotland (1829), set out the whole of Scots private law in a systematic numbered-paragraph form modelled on the institutional writers, and remained a standard student text through many editions. He also wrote Commentaries on the Law of Scotland and on the Principles of Mercantile Jurisprudence (1804, repeatedly expanded), a treatise that drew on the reported decisions of both Scottish and English courts and was influential in shaping Scots commercial law. His Inquiries into the Contract of Sale of Goods and Merchandise and his examinations of the bankruptcy statutes further consolidated his authority.

Legacy

Bell's Principles continues to be cited in Scottish courts as an institutional source of law, giving his formulations an authority that persists well beyond his lifetime.
