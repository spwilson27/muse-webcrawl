The Primeira Liga is the top-level professional football league in Portugal. Founded in 1934 as an experimental competition and officially recognised from the 1938–39 season, it is contested by 18 clubs that play each other home and away over 34 rounds. It is organised by the Liga Portuguesa de Futebol Profissional.

The Big Three

Portuguese football is dominated by the "Big Three" (Os Três Grandes): Benfica, FC Porto, and Sporting CP. Between them they have won nearly every league title; only Belenenses (1945–46) and Boavista (2000–01) have broken the monopoly. Benfica holds the record with 38 championships. The fiercest rivalries are the Lisbon derby between Benfica and Sporting and O Clássico between Benfica and Porto.

European competition

The league consistently ranks among Europe's top leagues in the UEFA coefficient rankings. Its champions and runners-up qualify for the UEFA Champions League, with further places in the Europa League and Conference League. Portuguese clubs have won European trophies including Porto's European Cup triumphs of 1987 and 2004 and Benfica's of 1961 and 1962.

Format

The bottom two clubs are relegated to the Liga Portugal 2, while the 16th-placed club enters a promotion/relegation play-off. The league has also been a notable exporter of playing and coaching talent to Europe's wealthiest leagues, with clubs such as Porto and Benfica renowned for player development and profitable transfers.
