United States Coast Guard

The United States Coast Guard (USCG) is the maritime security, search and rescue, and law enforcement service branch of the United States Armed Forces and one of the eight uniformed services of the United States. Uniquely among the military services, it is both a branch of the armed forces and a federal law enforcement agency, operating in peacetime under the Department of Homeland Security (and formerly the Department of Transportation and the Treasury) but transferable to the Department of the Navy in wartime or by presidential order. It traces its founding to the Revenue Cutter Service, created by Alexander Hamilton on 4 August 1790 — making it the oldest continuous seagoing service in the United States.

History

The modern Coast Guard was formed on 28 January 1915 by merging the Revenue Cutter Service and the Life-Saving Service; the Lighthouse Service was added in 1939 and the Bureau of Marine Inspection and Navigation in 1942. It has served in every American war since 1790, including World War II (as part of the Navy, losing the cutter Taney's sister ships in action and landing troops at Normandy), and its only Medal of Honor recipient, Douglas Munro, died at Guadalcanal in 1942. In 2003 it was transferred from the Department of Transportation to the newly created Department of Homeland Security — the largest reorganisation since its founding.

Missions

The Coast Guard has eleven statutory missions, grouped under homeland security and non-homeland-security roles: ports, waterways and coastal security; drug interdiction; aids to navigation; search and rescue; living marine resources (fisheries enforcement); marine safety; defence readiness; migrant interdiction; marine environmental protection; ice operations (its polar icebreakers are the nation's only heavy icebreaking capability); and other law enforcement. Its search-and-rescue service responds to tens of thousands of cases a year, and its drug interdiction operations seize record quantities of cocaine in the eastern Pacific and Caribbean transit zones.
