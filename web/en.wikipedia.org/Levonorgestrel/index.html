Levonorgestrel

Levonorgestrel is a synthetic progestin (progestogen) medication used in birth control. It is the active ingredient in emergency contraceptive pills such as Plan B, in hormonal intrauterine devices (IUDs) such as Mirena, and in some combined and progestin-only contraceptive pills and implants.

Uses

As emergency contraception, a 1.5 mg dose taken as soon as possible after unprotected sex — ideally within 72 hours — works mainly by preventing or delaying ovulation. Hormonal IUDs release small amounts of levonorgestrel locally for three to eight years depending on the device, providing highly effective long-acting contraception.

Safety

Levonorgestrel is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines. Common side effects include nausea, changes in menstrual bleeding, breast tenderness and headache; serious complications are rare.
