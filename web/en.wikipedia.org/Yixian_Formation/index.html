The Yixian Formation (Chinese: 義縣組) is a geological formation in Jinzhou, Liaoning province, China, known for its exceptionally preserved fossils of the Early Cretaceous Jehol Biota. Formed roughly 125 to 121 million years ago, it consists largely of lacustrine sediments interbedded with volcanic basalts and tuffs, whose ash layers allow precise radiometric dating.

Palaeontology

The formation's fine lake sediments, combined with rapid burial by volcanic ash, preserved soft tissues including feathers, fur, and stomach contents. It yielded the first widely accepted feathered non-avian dinosaurs, including Sinosauropteryx and Caudipteryx, which transformed understanding of the dinosaur–bird transition. Other finds include the early bird Confuciusornis, early flowering plants, diverse mammals such as Repenomamus, pterosaurs, fish, and insects.

Setting

During deposition the region was a landscape of lakes and active volcanoes among temperate forests. Periodic volcanic events caused mass mortality among lake organisms, contributing to the concentration of well-preserved specimens that makes the Yixian one of the world's great Konservat-Lagerstätten.
