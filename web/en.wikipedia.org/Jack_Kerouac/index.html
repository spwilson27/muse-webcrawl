Jack Kerouac

Born: 12 March 1922, Lowell, Massachusetts, U.S.
Died: 21 October 1969, St. Petersburg, Florida, U.S.
Occupation: Novelist, poet
Movement: Beat Generation
Notable works: On the Road, The Dharma Bums, Big Sur

Jack Kerouac (born Jean-Louis Lebris de Kérouac; 12 March 1922 – 21 October 1969) was an American novelist and poet who, alongside William S. Burroughs and Allen Ginsberg, was a pioneer of the Beat Generation. His most famous work, On the Road (1957), became a defining text of post-war American counterculture, celebrated for its depiction of restless travel, jazz, friendship and spiritual searching, rendered in his signature "spontaneous prose".

Early life

Kerouac was born in Lowell, Massachusetts, to French-Canadian parents, and spoke French before English. A gifted athlete, he won a football scholarship to Columbia University, but injuries and disagreements with his coach ended his sporting career. After serving in the US Naval Reserve and merchant marine, he settled in New York, where he met Ginsberg, Burroughs and Neal Cassady — the circle that became the Beats. His first novel, The Town and the City (1950), was a conventional, Wolfe-influenced family saga.

On the Road and fame

Between 1947 and 1950 Kerouac made the cross-country journeys — with Cassady, the model for Dean Moriarty — that fill On the Road. In April 1951 he typed the novel in three weeks on a single scroll of paper, in the breathless, improvisational style he compared to bebop jazz. Rejected for years, it was published in edited form in 1957 to immediate fame; the critic Gilbert Millstein's review in The New York Times hailed it as the testament of a generation. Kerouac, shy and increasingly alcoholic, was ill at ease as the media's "king of the Beats".

Later work

A burst of publication followed: The Dharma Bums (1958), drawing on his intense engagement with Buddhism; The Subterraneans; Doctor Sax; Maggie Cassidy; Desolation Angels; and Big Sur (1962), a harrowing account of alcoholism and breakdown. He regarded his books as chapters of one vast autobiographical sequence, "The Duluoz Legend". His poetry includes Mexico City Blues (1959).

Death and legacy

Kerouac spent his final years in Florida and Lowell, living with his third wife Stella and his mother. He died in 1969, aged 47, of internal bleeding caused by cirrhosis after years of heavy drinking. His reputation has grown steadily: his scroll manuscript tours as a literary relic, his Lowell childhood home and grave are pilgrimage sites, and On the Road remains a touchstone for writers, musicians and travellers.
