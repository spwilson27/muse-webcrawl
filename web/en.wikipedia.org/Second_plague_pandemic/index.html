Second plague pandemic

The second plague pandemic was a series of epidemics of bubonic plague caused by the bacterium Yersinia pestis, beginning with the Black Death of 1346–1353 and recurring in waves across Europe, Asia and North Africa for roughly four centuries, until the early 19th century.

Black Death

The pandemic reached Europe in 1347 from Central Asia via trade routes and Mongol armies, killing an estimated 30–60% of Europe's population in its first and most devastating wave, and causing enormous social, economic and religious upheaval.

Later waves

Plague returned repeatedly, roughly every decade or generation in many cities: notable outbreaks include the Great Plague of London (1665–66), the Great Plague of Marseille (1720), plague in the Ottoman Empire and Persia, and the last major European outbreaks in the 18th and early 19th centuries. The second pandemic is distinguished from the first (the Plague of Justinian, 6th–8th centuries) and the third pandemic, which began in China in the 19th century and spread worldwide by steamship.
