Chief Whip

The Chief Whip is a political leader whose task in a legislature is to administer the whipping system that tries to ensure members of a political party attend and vote as the party leadership desires. The office is a central one in Westminster-style parliamentary systems, including the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, India and Ireland.

United Kingdom

In the UK Parliament, each party has a Chief Whip in both the House of Commons and the House of Lords. The Government Chief Whip in the Commons — formally Parliamentary Secretary to the Treasury — manages the government's legislative programme, negotiates "through the usual channels" with the opposition, and maintains discipline among government MPs, reporting MPs' concerns back to the Prime Minister. Whips convey the importance of votes through the weekly circular also called "the whip", in which items are underlined once, twice or three times; defying a three-line whip can lead to the whip being withdrawn from an MP.

The role is surrounded by conventions of secrecy: the Government Chief Whip traditionally sits in Cabinet but speaks little in the chamber, and the whips' offices gather intelligence about the views and vulnerabilities of backbenchers.
