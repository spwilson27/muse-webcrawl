Arthur Cayley

Born: 16 August 1821, Richmond, Surrey

Died: 26 January 1895, Cambridge

Fields: Mathematics

Arthur Cayley (1821–1895) was a prolific British mathematician who worked largely in pure mathematics. He helped found the modern British school of pure mathematics and published nearly a thousand papers.

Work

Cayley developed the theory of matrices, including the Cayley–Hamilton theorem stating that every square matrix satisfies its own characteristic equation. With J. J. Sylvester he created invariant theory, and he contributed to group theory (Cayley's theorem), algebraic geometry, and the theory of n-dimensional geometry. He also characterised the octonions, sometimes called Cayley numbers.

Career

Trained as a lawyer, Cayley practised conveyancing for 14 years while publishing mathematics, before becoming the first Sadleirian Professor of Pure Mathematics at Cambridge in 1863. He was president of the Royal Astronomical Society and a fellow of the Royal Society.
