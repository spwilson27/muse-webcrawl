The International Federation of the Phonographic Industry (IFPI) is the organisation that represents the interests of the recording industry worldwide. It is a non-profit members' organisation registered in Switzerland and founded in Italy in 1933 by the phonographic industry. It operates a secretariat based in London, with regional offices in Brussels, Hong Kong, Miami, Abu Dhabi, Singapore and Nairobi.

Activities

IFPI's stated mission is to promote the value of recorded music, campaign for the rights of record producers, and expand the commercial uses of recorded music. Its activities include:

  • Advocacy and public policy — lobbying governments on copyright law and enforcement.
  • Licensing — coordinating collecting of performance and broadcast royalties in some markets.
  • Anti-piracy — coordinating enforcement actions against copyright infringement of recorded music, including actions against file-sharing services such as The Pirate Bay.
  • Market research and data — publishing the annual Engaging with Music report and global recorded-music revenue statistics, long known through the Global Music Report.
  • Charts and awards — overseeing the IFPI Global Single Chart and the annual Global Recording Artist of the Year award.

History

The IFPI was founded by the recording industry in Rome in 1933, in the era of the 78 rpm gramophone record, initially to promote the rights of record producers under copyright law. It grew alongside the international record business through the LP, cassette, CD, download, and streaming eras. In the 2000s the organisation was a prominent actor in litigation against peer-to-peer file-sharing networks and the operators of infringing sites.
