Hannah Höch (German: [ˈhana ˈhøːç]; 1 November 1889 – 31 May 1978) was a German Dada artist. She is best known for her work of the Weimar period, when she was one of the originators of photomontage — a type of collage in which the images used are actual photographs or photographic reproductions.

Career

Höch studied applied art in Berlin, and from 1916 to 1926 worked in the handicrafts department of the Ullstein publishing house, designing patterns for women's magazines. She became the only prominent woman associated with Berlin Dada, participating in the First International Dada Fair in 1920, though the male Dadaists — including her partner Raoul Hausmann — treated her with condescension and excluded her from some activities.

Work

Her best-known piece is Schnitt mit dem Küchenmesser Dada durch die letzte Weimarer Bierbauchkulturepoche Deutschlands ("Cut with the Kitchen Knife Dada Through the Last Weimar Beer-Belly Cultural Epoch in Germany"), a massive photomontage satirizing Weimar politics and society. Her work used imagery from mass media to critique gender roles, the "New Woman," and contemporary politics. Under the Nazi regime her art was condemned as "degenerate"; she spent the war years in quiet seclusion in Berlin-Heiligensee. After 1945 she continued to produce collages and was rediscovered and honored in her later years.
