National symbols of Canada

The national symbols of Canada are the symbols used in Canada and abroad to represent the country and its people. Over centuries they have grown from Indigenous and colonial traditions to the modern emblems adopted since Confederation in 1867.

The maple leaf and flag

The maple leaf is Canada's best-known symbol, used on military insignia from the nineteenth century and at the centre of the national flag adopted in 1965, replacing the Canadian Red Ensign. The colours red and white were proclaimed the national colours by King George V in 1921. The Royal Coat of Arms of Canada, also dating from 1921, combines the emblems of England, Scotland, Ireland and France with maple leaves.

Other official symbols

Canada's national anthem is "O Canada", officially adopted in 1980. The beaver — emblem of the fur trade — was proclaimed the national animal symbol in 1975, and the Canadian horse and the maple tree (1996) are also official symbols. The national winter sport is ice hockey and the summer sport is lacrosse, so declared in 1994. "God Save the King" is the royal anthem, and the monarch is head of state. Provincial and territorial symbols — flags, flowers, birds and tartans — supplement the national set.
