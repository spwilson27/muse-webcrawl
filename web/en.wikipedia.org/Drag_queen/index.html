Drag queen

A drag queen is a person, usually male, who uses drag clothing and makeup to imitate and often exaggerate female gender signifiers and gender roles for entertainment purposes. Drag queens are part of a broader drag culture that also includes drag kings (typically women performing masculinity). Historically, drag queens have usually been gay men, and drag culture has been closely associated with LGBTQ communities, though performers of any gender or orientation may take part.

History

Female impersonation has a long history in theatre, from the all-male casts of ancient Greek drama and Shakespearean England — where women were barred from the stage — to pantomime dames and vaudeville "female impersonators" such as Julian Eltinge. The term "drag" is often traced to Polari, the slang of British gay and theatre communities, where "drag" referred to clothing worn by the opposite sex. In the United States, drag balls flourished in Harlem in the 1920s and 1930s, and drag became a fixture of gay bar culture after the Second World War.

Drag balls and houses

Ball culture, originating in Black and Latino LGBTQ communities in New York City, organised competitions ("balls") in which participants "walked" in categories such as executive realness and evening gown. Competitors belonged to "houses" — chosen families led by a "mother" or "father" — a culture documented in the 1990 film Paris Is Burning and the television series Pose.

Mainstream popularity

The 1994 film The Adventures of Priscilla, Queen of the Desert and, above all, the reality competition series RuPaul's Drag Race (premiered 2009) brought drag queens into mainstream popular culture, spawning international franchises and turning performers such as RuPaul, Bianca Del Rio and Trixie Mattel into celebrities.
