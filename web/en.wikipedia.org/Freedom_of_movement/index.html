Freedom of movement

Freedom of movement
ConceptCivil liberty / human right
Key instrumentsUniversal Declaration of Human Rights, Art. 13; ICCPR, Art. 12
RelatedRight to travel, free movement of workers (EU), Schengen Area

Freedom of movement, mobility rights, or the right to travel is a human rights concept encompassing the freedom of an individual to travel and reside within the territory of a state, and to leave that state and return to it. The right includes not only visiting places, but changing the place where the individual resides or works.

In international law

Article 13 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948) provides that everyone has the right to freedom of movement and residence within the borders of each state, and the right to leave any country, including his own, and to return to his country. Article 12 of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights makes similar provisions legally binding for its parties, subject to permissible restrictions for national security, public order and public health.

Regional arrangements

The European Union guarantees the free movement of persons — one of its four freedoms — allowing EU citizens to live and work in any member state, while the Schengen Area abolishes internal border checks among participating countries. Comparable arrangements exist in the Nordic Passport Union, the Trans-Tasman Travel Arrangement between Australia and New Zealand, and other regional blocs.

Restrictions

Freedom of movement may be limited by visa requirements, exit controls, internal passport systems, quarantine measures, and sanctions. Historically, systems such as serfdom, the Soviet propiska and China's hukou restricted internal movement.
