Citizen's arrest

A citizen's arrest is an arrest made by a private citizen — that is, a person who is not acting as a sworn law-enforcement official. In common law jurisdictions, the practice dates back to medieval England and the English common law, in which sheriffs encouraged ordinary citizens to help apprehend law breakers.

Legal basis

Despite the practice's name, in many jurisdictions the arresting person is usually designated as a person with arrest powers, who need not be a citizen of the jurisdiction or country in which they are acting. In most common law countries, a citizen may arrest a person who is committing, or whom they reasonably suspect is committing, a serious (indictable) offence, or to prevent a breach of the peace.

United States

Each state, with the exception of North Carolina, permits citizen arrests if the commission of a felony is witnessed by the arresting citizen, or when a citizen is asked to help apprehend a suspect by police. The exact scope varies by state. A person who makes a citizen's arrest is exposed to civil and criminal liability if the arrest proves unjustified, and generally must deliver the arrested person to the police promptly.

Risks

Private citizens do not enjoy the qualified immunity granted to police officers, and can be held liable for false imprisonment, assault, or worse if the detention is unlawful. Some jurisdictions have narrowed or repealed citizen's arrest statutes following controversial cases.
