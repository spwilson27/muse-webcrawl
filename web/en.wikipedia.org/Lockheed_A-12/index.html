The Lockheed A-12 is a retired high-altitude, Mach 3+ strategic reconnaissance aircraft built by Lockheed's Skunk Works for the United States Central Intelligence Agency (CIA). It was developed under the codename Oxcart as a successor to the U-2 spy plane, and was the precursor to the better-known SR-71 Blackbird.

Development

Designed by Clarence "Kelly" Johnson's team, the single-seat A-12 made extensive use of titanium and was designed to cruise above 85,000 feet at speeds over Mach 3.2, relying on speed and altitude to evade interception. It first flew from Groom Lake (Area 51) in Nevada in April 1962.

Operational service

The A-12 flew operational missions over Southeast Asia and North Korea in 1967–1968 under Operation Black Shield from Kadena Air Base, Okinawa. It was retired in 1968 in favour of the SR-71, and the surviving airframes were placed in storage; most are now on museum display.
