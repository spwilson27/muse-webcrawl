The Jolof Empire (also Djolof or Wolof Empire) was a West African state of the Wolof people that ruled parts of present-day Senegal from about 1350 to 1549. It was a confederation of several kingdoms acknowledging the overlordship of the Buurba (king) of Jolof.

Origins

Tradition attributes the empire's founding to Ndiadiane Ndiaye, a semi-legendary figure who united the Wolof-speaking states between the Senegal and Gambia rivers. The empire grew wealthy controlling trade routes and, from the 1440s, commerce — including the slave trade — with the Portuguese on the coast.

Structure and society

Jolof society was strongly hierarchical, divided between royalty, nobles, free commoners, artisans organized in castes, and slaves. The vassal kingdoms of Waalo, Cayor, Baol, Sine and Saloum paid tribute to the Buurba and followed him in war.

Decline

In 1549 the vassal ruler of Cayor, Amari Ngone Sobel, defeated and killed the last Buurba at the Battle of Danki, breaking the empire apart into independent kingdoms. A rump Jolof kingdom survived inland until it was absorbed into French colonial Senegal in the late nineteenth century.
