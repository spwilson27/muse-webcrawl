Situational awareness

Maintaining awareness of a dynamic environment is critical in aviation and air traffic control.

Situational awareness or situation awareness (SA) is the understanding of an environment, its elements, and how it changes with respect to time or other factors. It is usually defined as the perception of the elements in the environment within a volume of time and space, the comprehension of their meaning, and the projection of their future status. SA is important for effective decision-making in complex, dynamic environments such as aviation, air traffic control, military operations, emergency medicine, power-plant operation and driving.

Endsley's model

The most widely used framework, developed by Mica Endsley from work with fighter pilots, distinguishes three levels: (1) perception of elements in the environment; (2) comprehension of the current situation; and (3) projection of future status. SA is shaped by task and environmental factors (workload, complexity, automation, interface design) and by individual factors such as experience, training and attention. Loss of SA is a major contributing factor in human-error accidents investigated in aviation and process industries.

Measurement and team SA

Techniques for measuring SA include freeze-probe methods such as SAGAT (Situation Awareness Global Assessment Technique), in which a simulation is paused and operators are queried about the state of the world, and real-time probe and rating methods such as SPAM and SART. Research also examines shared and team SA — how crews build a common picture through communication and shared displays — and, in cyber security, awareness of threats in network operations centres.
