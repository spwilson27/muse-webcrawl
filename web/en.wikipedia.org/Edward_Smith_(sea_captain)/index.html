Edward Smith (sea captain)

Edward John Smith (27 January 1850 – 15 April 1912) was a British sea captain and naval officer, best known as the captain of RMS Titanic, who perished when the ship sank on its maiden voyage in 1912. The senior captain of the White Star Line, Smith was nicknamed the "Millionaires' Captain" for the wealthy passengers who preferred to sail under his command.

Captain Edward J. Smith of the White Star Line.

Career

Smith went to sea as a teenager and joined the White Star Line in 1880. Over the following decades he commanded a succession of the line's largest vessels, including Majestic, Baltic, Adriatic, and Olympic. His reputation for calm competence made him the natural choice to take the new Titanic on her maiden voyage in April 1912, after which he was expected to retire.

The Titanic disaster

Titanic left Southampton on 10 April 1912 for New York. On the night of 14 April, despite wireless warnings of ice, the ship struck an iceberg at 23:40 and sank in the early hours of 15 April with the loss of more than 1,500 lives. Smith's conduct during the evacuation — overseeing the loading of lifeboats and relaying distress calls — was widely praised, and accounts hold that he went down with his ship. His body was never recovered.

Memorials

A statue of Smith stands in Lichfield, Staffordshire, not far from his birthplace in Hanley, where a plaque also marks his home. He has been portrayed in numerous films about the disaster, most famously by Bernard Hill in James Cameron's Titanic (1997).
