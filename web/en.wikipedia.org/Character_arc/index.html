Character arc

A character arc is the transformation or inner journey of a character over the course of a story. As the narrative progresses, the character's beliefs, personality, values or worldview change — often gradually and in response to conflict — so that by the end they are meaningfully different from who they were at the beginning.

Types

Arcs are commonly classified as positive (the character grows, overcomes a flaw or false belief, as in a typical hero's journey), negative (the character declines, is corrupted, or falls, as in tragedy), or flat (the character remains essentially unchanged while changing the world around them — a pattern common in long-running series and some action heroes). A disillusionment arc combines elements of both, as a character abandons a false belief and accepts a harsher truth without necessarily becoming a better person.

Relation to plot structure

Character arcs are frequently mapped onto plot structures such as the three-act structure or the hero's journey, with the midpoint and climax serving as turning points in the transformation. In serialised television, arcs may extend across whole seasons or series.
