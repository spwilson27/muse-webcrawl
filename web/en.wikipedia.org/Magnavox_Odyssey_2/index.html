The Magnavox Odyssey 2 (stylized as Odyssey²) is a second-generation home video game console released in 1978 by Magnavox, a subsidiary of Philips. In Europe it was marketed as the Philips Videopac G7000, and in Brazil as the Philips Odyssey. It competed with the Atari 2600, the Intellivision, and the Fairchild Channel F.

Hardware

Unlike most of its rivals, the Odyssey 2 featured a built-in full alphanumeric membrane keyboard, which made it suitable for educational games and simple computer programming. The system was based on the Intel 8048 microcontroller and used interchangeable ROM cartridges. Standard controllers were hard-wired joysticks. An optional module, The Voice, added synthesized speech and sound effects to compatible games.

Games

The console's library included titles such as Pick Axe Pete!, K.C. Munchkin! — a maze game similar to Pac-Man that became the subject of a landmark copyright lawsuit, Atari, Inc. v. North American Philips — and the "Master Strategy Series" board-game hybrids Quest for the Rings, Conquest of the World, and The Great Wall Street Fortune Hunt. The Chess Module add-on gave the console enough computing power to play chess.

Legacy

The Odyssey 2 sold around two million units before being discontinued in 1984 in the wake of the video game crash of 1983. It is remembered for its keyboard, its voice module, and the legal precedent set by its Pac-Man-like hit game.
