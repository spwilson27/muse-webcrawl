Exponential smoothing

Exponential smoothing is a family of forecasting methods for univariate time series data in which forecasts are weighted averages of past observations, with the weights decaying exponentially as observations get older. The methods were developed in the 1950s by Robert Goodell Brown and Charles C. Holt and are widely used in inventory control, sales forecasting and signal processing.

Simple exponential smoothing

In the simplest form, suitable for data with no trend or seasonality, the smoothed value is updated by st = αxt + (1 − α)st−1, where α between 0 and 1 is the smoothing factor. Values of α near 1 follow recent observations closely; values near 0 produce a stable, slowly changing forecast. The forecast for the next period equals the current smoothed value.

Holt's method and Holt–Winters

Holt extended the method to data with a trend by adding a trend equation, and Peter Winters added a seasonal component, producing the Holt–Winters method, which handles level, trend and seasonality with three smoothing parameters. Seasonal terms may be additive or multiplicative. The methods are collectively known by the acronym ETS (error, trend, seasonality) after the state-space classification developed by Rob Hyndman and colleagues, which places exponential smoothing within a statistical framework with prediction intervals.

Applications

Because the methods are simple, fast and require storing only a few values, they were historically attractive for computerised inventory systems covering thousands of stock items. They remain standard baselines in forecasting practice and competitions.
