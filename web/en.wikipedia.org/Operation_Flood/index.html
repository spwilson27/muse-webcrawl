Operation Flood

Operation Flood was a rural development programme launched in India in 1970 by the National Dairy Development Board (NDDB), chaired by Dr Verghese Kurien. Often called the "White Revolution", it transformed India from a milk-deficient country into the world's largest milk producer, using the cooperative dairy structure pioneered at Anand, Gujarat (the "Anand pattern", embodied by the Amul brand).

Village milk cooperatives were the foundation of the White Revolution.

Structure

The programme built a national milk grid linking village-level producers' cooperatives to urban markets. Farmers — including landless labourers and women — delivered milk twice daily to village cooperatives, which handled chilling and testing; district unions processed the milk and state federations marketed it. The system eliminated middlemen, returned the majority of the consumer price to producers, and used proceeds from donated European skimmed milk powder and butter oil (via the World Food Programme and the European Economic Community) to finance development.

Phases

Phase I (1970–1980) connected 18 of India's premier milksheds to consumers in the four major metropolitan cities. Phase II (1981–1985) expanded to 136 milksheds and over 34,000 village cooperatives. Phase III (1985–1996) consolidated the system, adding veterinary services, artificial insemination, feed supply and member education, and expanded membership to some 9–10 million farmers.

Impact

India's milk production rose from about 20 million tonnes in the late 1960s to over 100 million tonnes by the mid-2000s, overtaking the United States in 1998, while per-capita milk availability roughly doubled. Critics noted pressures on indigenous cattle breeds and regional imbalances, but Operation Flood is widely regarded as one of the most successful rural development programmes in the world.
