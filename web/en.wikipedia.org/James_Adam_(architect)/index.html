James Adam (21 July 1732 – 20 October 1794) was a Scottish architect and furniture designer, and the younger brother and business partner of the celebrated architect Robert Adam. Together, the Adam brothers developed the distinctive neoclassical style known as the "Adam style," which dominated British architecture and interior design in the later eighteenth century.

James Adam (1732–1794), Scottish architect

Early life

James Adam was born in Edinburgh, the third son of William Adam (1689–1748), the foremost Scottish architect of his generation. His elder brothers John and Robert also became architects. The family firm, founded by their father, undertook major building projects across Scotland.

Career

In 1758 James joined his brother Robert in London, where they established the firm that would become famous as the Adelphi partnership. James travelled to Italy in the early 1760s to study classical antiquities, following the route Robert had taken a few years earlier. On his return he became a full partner in the business, contributing to the design of houses, interiors, furniture, and decorative schemes.

Among the projects associated with James Adam are the interiors of Kedleston Hall in Derbyshire (in collaboration with Robert), Syon House, and work on the Adelphi development in London. After Robert's death in 1792, James carried on the practice briefly before his own death two years later.

The Adam style

The Adam style was characterised by light, elegant neoclassical ornament drawn from Roman and Greek sources, integrated schemes in which architecture, furniture, carpets and even door furniture were designed as a unified whole. The brothers published their designs in The Works in Architecture of Robert and James Adam (1773–1779, with a further volume in 1822).

Death

James Adam died in London on 20 October 1794 and was buried in Westminster Abbey alongside his brother Robert.
