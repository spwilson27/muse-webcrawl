The Social Contract

The Social Contract, Or Principles of Political Right
Author: Jean-Jacques Rousseau
Country: Netherlands (published)
Language: French
Publication date: 1762

The Social Contract, originally published as Du contrat social; ou, Principes du droit politique (1762), is a book by Jean-Jacques Rousseau that argues that legitimate political authority derives from a social contract agreed upon by all citizens for their mutual preservation.

It opens with the famous line: "Man is born free, and everywhere he is in chains."

Argument

Rousseau proposes that individuals surrender their natural rights to the community, governed by the general will — the will of the people as a sovereign body. Sovereignty is inalienable and indivisible; government is merely its agent. The book also discusses the lawgiver, forms of government, and a civil religion.

Influence

Banned in France and Geneva on publication, the work profoundly influenced the French Revolution, republicanism, and modern political and social theory, as well as debates about democracy and totalitarianism.
