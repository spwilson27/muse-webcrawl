Increase Mather
Born: 21 June 1639, Dorchester, Massachusetts
Died: 23 August 1723, Boston
Occupation: Puritan minister, author, president of Harvard College

Increase Mather (1639–1723) was a New England Puritan clergyman, author and educator, one of the most influential figures in the early history of the Massachusetts Bay Colony. He was the father of Cotton Mather.

Career

A graduate of Harvard College and Trinity College Dublin, Mather became minister of Boston's North Church and served as president of Harvard from 1685 to 1701. As the colony's agent in London he helped negotiate the charter of 1691.

Salem witch trials

During the Salem witch trials of 1692–93, Mather's Cases of Conscience (1692) argued that spectral evidence was unreliable and that it were better ten suspected witches escape than one innocent person be condemned; the work is credited with helping to bring the trials to an end.

Writings

Mather published around 130 works, including histories, sermons and an early account of King Philip's War.
