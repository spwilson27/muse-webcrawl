Cell Broadcast

TypeMobile network broadcast messaging
Standards3GPP (GSM, UMTS, LTE, 5G)
UsesEmergency alerts (EU-Alert, Wireless Emergency Alerts, Earthquake Early Warning)
Message sizeUp to 93 characters per page (GSM); extended in later systems

Cell Broadcast (CB) is a method of sending messages to multiple mobile telephone users in a defined area at the same time. It is defined by the ETSI GSM committee and 3GPP and is part of the 2G, 3G, 4G LTE, and 5G standards. Unlike SMS, which is a point-to-point service, Cell Broadcast is a one-to-many, geographically targeted service: a message is transmitted once by each base station and received by every handset in the area that is configured to listen to the relevant message channel, so it is not affected by network congestion.

Technical features

In GSM, a Cell Broadcast message consists of up to 15 pages of 93 characters. Messages are distinguished by a message identifier that indicates the subject and the alert level, allowing phones to filter categories. Because messages are broadcast rather than addressed to individual subscribers, no phone numbers are needed and recipients remain anonymous. In LTE and 5G the same concept is carried by the Public Warning System (PWS), which includes the Commercial Mobile Alert System (CMAS) used in the United States.

Public warning use

Cell Broadcast is the leading technology for mobile public warning systems worldwide because messages reach all phones in a danger area within seconds, in the handset's own language where supported, with a distinctive alarm sound and vibration. It powers Japan's earthquake and tsunami warnings (as part of J-Alert delivery), the United States' Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA), the Netherlands' NL-Alert, and EU-Alert implementations that EU member states were required to deploy under the European Electronic Communications Code. Other uses include weather warnings, missing-child alerts, and location-based information services.
