Paavo Nurmi

Born: 13 June 1897, Turku, Finland
Died: 2 October 1973, Helsinki, Finland
Nickname: The Flying Finn
Sport: Long-distance running

Paavo Johannes Nurmi (13 June 1897 – 2 October 1973) was a Finnish middle-distance and long-distance runner. He was nicknamed the "Flying Finn" and dominated distance running in the early 20th century. Nurmi set 22 official world records at distances between 1500 metres and 20 kilometres, and won nine gold and three silver medals in the Summer Olympics. At his peak, he was undefeated in 121 straight races at distances from 800 metres upwards.

Career

Born to a working-class family in Turku, Nurmi began systematic training while serving in the Finnish Army. At the 1920 Antwerp Olympics he won gold in the 10,000 m, the individual cross country, and the team cross country. At Paris 1924 he achieved an unprecedented feat, winning the 1500 m and 5000 m finals within about an hour of each other, plus the 3000 m team race and both cross country events — five gold medals at one Games. At Amsterdam 1928 he won the 10,000 m and took silver in the 5000 m and 3000 m steeplechase. Famous for racing with a stopwatch to pace himself evenly, he revolutionised the training and racing of distance runners.

Later life

Nurmi planned to end his career with the marathon at the 1932 Los Angeles Olympics, but the International Amateur Athletic Federation suspended him over allegations that he had received excessive expenses on a tour of Germany — a disputed professionalism ruling that ended his Olympic career. He retired from racing in 1934 and later coached Finnish runners, ran a successful menswear business in Helsinki, and became wealthy through construction and the stock market. In 1952 he lit the Olympic flame at the Helsinki Games. He died in 1973 and received a state funeral.
