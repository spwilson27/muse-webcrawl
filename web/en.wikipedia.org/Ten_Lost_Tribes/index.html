Ten Lost Tribes

The Ten Lost Tribes were the ten of the Twelve Tribes of Israel that were said to have been deported from the Kingdom of Israel after its conquest by the Neo-Assyrian Empire around 722 BCE. The tribes of Reuben, Simeon, Dan, Naphtali, Gad, Asher, Issachar, Zebulun, Manasseh, and Ephraim formed the northern kingdom; their population was exiled by the Assyrian kings, and in later Jewish, Christian, and other traditions their descendants were believed to survive somewhere beyond the known world, to return at the end of days.

Historical background

Assyrian records and the Hebrew Bible describe deportations of Israelites under Tiglath-Pileser III, Shalmaneser V, and Sargon II, who in his annals claims to have carried off 27,290 inhabitants of Samaria. Assyrian policy deliberately resettled conquered peoples across the empire to break local identities, and deported Israelites were placed in regions such as Halah, Habor, and Media. Historians generally hold that only part of the population was exiled — including elites and craftsmen — while many remained and merged with the population that became known as the Samaritans, and others fled south to Judah. The exiles themselves were gradually absorbed into surrounding peoples.

Later claims and legends

The fate of the lost tribes became a fertile subject of speculation. Apocryphal works such as 2 Esdras placed them beyond the river Sambatyon. Over the centuries, many groups have been identified — or have identified themselves — as descendants of the tribes, including the Beta Israel of Ethiopia, the Bene Israel of India, the Bnei Menashe of north-east India, the Pashtuns of Afghanistan, and various Native American peoples in early modern and Mormon thought. In the 19th century, British Israelism claimed the peoples of Britain and north-western Europe as their heirs. No claim has been substantiated by mainstream historians or genetic evidence for a population-scale survival, though Jewish tradition continues to anticipate their ingathering.
