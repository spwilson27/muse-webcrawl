Heinrich event

A Heinrich event is a natural phenomenon in which large groups of icebergs break off — or calve — from glaciers and traverse the North Atlantic, dropping rock debris as they melt. Occurring during periods of glacial maximum in the last ice age, these events are recorded in marine sediment cores as distinctive layers of ice-rafted debris, mostly limestone and dolomite from Hudson Bay, Canada. They are named after German marine geologist Hartmut Heinrich, who described them in 1988.

Evidence and timing

Heinrich layers consist of coarse lithic grains far from any coast, dropped by melting icebergs; the debris is characteristically rich in detrital carbonate matching bedrock around Hudson Strait, pointing to surging of the Laurentide Ice Sheet through that strait. Six major events (H1–H6) occurred roughly 16, 24, 31, 38, 45 and 60 thousand years ago, each lasting a few hundred to about a thousand years. They tend to occur near the end of the cold phases of Dansgaard–Oeschger cycles, during so-called "Heinrich stadials".

Mechanism and climate effects

The leading explanation is an internal ice-sheet instability — the "binge–purge" model of Douglas MacAyeal — in which the Hudson Strait ice stream thickens until basal warming and lubrication trigger a massive surge and armada of icebergs, after which the thinned ice re-stabilises. The resulting freshwater pulse into the North Atlantic weakened Atlantic meridional overturning circulation, cooling the Northern Hemisphere while the Southern Ocean warmed (the "bipolar seesaw"), shifting tropical rain belts and weakening the Asian monsoon — effects recorded in Greenland ice cores and cave deposits worldwide.

Related phenomena

Similar, smaller ice-rafting events from other ice sheets (the "precursor" or C events from the European ice sheet) precede some Heinrich layers. The Young Dryas cold interval around 12,900 years ago is sometimes compared with Heinrich events, though its trigger remains debated. Understanding Heinrich events informs modern concerns about ice-sheet instability and freshwater forcing of ocean circulation.
