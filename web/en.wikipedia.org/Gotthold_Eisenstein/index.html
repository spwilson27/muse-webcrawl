Gotthold Eisenstein


Ferdinand Gotthold Max Eisenstein (1823–1852).

Ferdinand Gotthold Max Eisenstein (16 April 1823 – 11 October 1852) was a German mathematician who made fundamental contributions to number theory and analysis despite dying of tuberculosis at the age of 29. Carl Friedrich Gauss reportedly said that there had been three epoch-making mathematicians — Archimedes, Newton, and Eisenstein.

Life

Born in Berlin to a family in difficult financial circumstances, Eisenstein displayed striking mathematical talent as a schoolboy. Alexander von Humboldt became his patron, obtaining stipends that allowed him to study. At the University of Berlin he attended lectures by Dirichlet. During the revolutions of 1848 he was briefly imprisoned for republican sympathies. Kronecker was among his students and close associates; Riemann heard him lecture on elliptic functions in 1847.

Work

Eisenstein proved the law of cubic reciprocity and the quartic (biquadratic) reciprocity law using elliptic and lemniscate functions, results that foreshadowed class field theory. His name survives in the Eisenstein integers (the ring Z[ω] of complex numbers a + bω with ω a cube root of unity), the Eisenstein criterion for irreducibility of polynomials, Eisenstein series in the theory of modular forms, and the Eisenstein prime. His collected works fill two substantial volumes published decades after his death.
