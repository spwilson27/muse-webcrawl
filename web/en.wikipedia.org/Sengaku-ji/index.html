Sengaku-ji

LocationTakanawa, Minato, Tokyo, Japan
DenominationSōtō Zen Buddhism
Founded1612

Sengaku-ji (泉岳寺) is a Sōtō Zen Buddhist temple in Takanawa, Minato ward, Tokyo, Japan. It is famous as the burial place of Asano Naganori and the forty-seven rōnin of the Akō incident, one of the most celebrated episodes of loyalty and revenge in Japanese history.

History

The temple was founded in 1612 by Tokugawa Ieyasu near Edo Castle; after a fire it was rebuilt at its present site in 1641. Following the vendetta of 1703, in which the forty-seven masterless samurai avenged their lord Asano Naganori by killing Kira Yoshinaka, the rōnin were ordered to commit seppuku and were buried beside their master at Sengaku-ji.

Legacy

The graves remain a popular site of pilgrimage. Each year on 14 December a festival commemorates the rōnin, and a small museum displays artefacts associated with the incident, including armour, weapons and the receipt for Kira's head.
