Hongi

HongiCulture: Māori (New Zealand)
Action: pressing noses (and sometimes foreheads) together
Meaning: sharing the breath of life (ha)

The hongi is a traditional Māori greeting in New Zealand in which two people press their noses together, sometimes including the forehead (rae). Through the hongi, participants exchange the ha, the breath of life, symbolising the meeting of the breath of life from the gods and the unity of the two people.

Significance

The greeting traces to the creation tradition in which the god Tāne fashioned the first woman, Hineahuone, from earth and breathed life into her nostrils. Performing a hongi moves a visitor's status from waewae tapu (sacred stranger) toward that of tangata whenua (people of the land) within the ceremony of welcome, the pōwhiri.

Modern use

The hongi is performed at formal welcomes, tangihanga (funerals) and official occasions, including by members of the New Zealand armed forces and during welcomes for foreign dignitaries. In 2020 its use was temporarily discouraged during the COVID-19 pandemic because of its close-contact nature.
