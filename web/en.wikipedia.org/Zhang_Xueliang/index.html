Zhang Xueliang (Chinese: 張學良; 3 June 1901 – 15 October 2001), nicknamed the "Young Marshal" (少帥), was a Chinese warlord and general who ruled Manchuria and parts of northern China in the early 20th century. He was the eldest son of the Manchurian warlord Zhang Zuolin.

Succession and the Northeast Flag Replacement

After his father was assassinated by the Japanese Kwantung Army in the Huanggutun incident of June 1928, Zhang succeeded him as leader of the Fengtian clique. Later that year he declared the Northeast Flag Replacement, pledging allegiance to Chiang Kai-shek's Nationalist government in Nanjing and nominally reunifying China.

Xi'an Incident

Ordered to exterminate the Communists in the north-west rather than resist Japan, Zhang grew sympathetic to a united front. On 12 December 1936, together with General Yang Hucheng, he detained Chiang Kai-shek at Xi'an in the Xi'an Incident, forcing Chiang to agree to form the Second United Front against Japan before releasing him.

House arrest and later life

Zhang accompanied Chiang back to Nanjing as a gesture of contrition and was placed under house arrest — first on the mainland, then in Taiwan — for over fifty years. He was released in 1990 after Chiang Ching-kuo's death, emigrated to Honolulu, Hawaii, and died there in 2001 at the age of 100.
