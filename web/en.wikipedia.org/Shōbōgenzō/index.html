Shōbōgenzō

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Shōbōgenzō (Japanese: 正法眼蔵, "Treasury of the True Dharma Eye") is the master work of the Japanese Zen master Eihei Dōgen (1200–1253), founder of the Sōtō school of Zen Buddhism in Japan. Written between 1231 and 1253, the year of Dōgen's death, it is a collection of fascicles (essays) expounding Buddhist practice and philosophy in strikingly original language.

Contents

The text exists in several recensions, most commonly the 75-fascicle and 12-fascicle versions. Well-known fascicles include Bendōwa ("On the Endeavor of the Way"), Genjōkōan ("Actualizing the Fundamental Point"), Uji ("Being-Time"), Sansui-kyō ("The Mountains and Rivers Sutra"), Busshō ("Buddha Nature") and Shōji ("Life and Death"). Dōgen's prose moves fluidly between Chinese sources, kōans, and his own intricate wordplay, reinterpreting doctrine through the unity of practice and enlightenment (shushō ittō) and the identity of sitting meditation (zazen) with realization itself.

Editions and influence

Long preserved within the Sōtō school, the Shōbōgenzō became widely studied only in the modern era and is now regarded as one of the great works of Japanese religious literature and Buddhist philosophy worldwide. Numerous translations into English and other languages appeared in the twentieth and twenty-first centuries. A separate work, the Shōbōgenzō Zuimonki, records Dōgen's informal talks as compiled by his disciple Ejō.
