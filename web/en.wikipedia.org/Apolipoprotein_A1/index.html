Apolipoprotein A1

Apolipoprotein A1
GeneAPOA1 (chromosome 11)
Main lipoproteinHDL, chylomicrons
FunctionLCAT activation, reverse cholesterol transport

Apolipoprotein A1 (ApoA-I) is the major protein component of high-density lipoprotein (HDL) in plasma, encoded by the APOA1 gene. It is also present on chylomicrons. ApoA-I promotes cholesterol efflux from tissues to the liver for excretion.

Function

Secreted by liver and intestine, lipid-poor ApoA-I accepts cellular free cholesterol and phospholipid via the ABCA1 transporter, forming nascent discoidal HDL. ApoA-I is the principal activator of lecithin-cholesterol acyltransferase (LCAT), which esterifies cholesterol and matures HDL. It interacts with CETP-mediated lipid exchange indirectly through HDL remodelling.

Clinical significance

Low ApoA-I and HDL associate with premature coronary artery disease. Rare mutations cause familial HDL deficiency and amyloidosis; the ApoA-I Milano variant is associated with unusual cardioprotection despite low HDL. The ApoB/ApoA-I ratio is a strong risk marker.
