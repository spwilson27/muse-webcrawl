Brodick

LocationIsle of Arran, North Ayrshire, Scotland
PopulationApprox. 600
Known forMain ferry port of Arran; Brodick Castle

Brodick (Scottish Gaelic: Tràigh a' Chaisteil) is the main village on the Isle of Arran, in the Firth of Clyde, Scotland. It is halfway along the east coast of the island, in Brodick Bay below Goat Fell, the island's highest mountain. The name is derived from Norse roots meaning "broad bay". Brodick is the island's principal ferry port, with regular sailings to Ardrossan on the mainland.

History

Brodick was long a small harbour settlement serving the surrounding farms and the seat of the Dukes of Hamilton at nearby Brodick Castle. The arrival of steamers in the 19th century turned it into a popular holiday resort for visitors from Glasgow, earning Arran the nickname "Scotland in Miniature". A new ferry terminal opened in 2018.

Brodick Castle

Brodick Castle, just north of the village, was a stronghold of the Hamilton family and is now cared for by the National Trust for Scotland. Its gardens are noted for collections of rhododendrons, and the castle grounds form part of Arran's country park. The castle's history stretches back to a Viking-era fortification on the site.

Transport and amenities

Caledonian MacBrayne ferries connect Brodick with Ardrossan, and buses circle the island from the terminal. The village has hotels, shops, a golf course, and a brewery, and serves as the usual starting point for ascents of Goat Fell (874 m).
