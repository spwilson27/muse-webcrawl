Cava is a sparkling wine of Denominación de Origen (DO) status from Spain. It is produced mainly in the Penedès region of Catalonia, around the town of Sant Sadurní d'Anoia, using the traditional method of secondary fermentation in the bottle, the same technique used for Champagne.

Production

The principal grape varieties are the white Catalan grapes Macabeo, Xarel·lo, and Parellada; Chardonnay and Pinot Noir are also permitted, the latter used for rosé Cava. By law, Cava must age on its lees for a minimum of nine months; Reserva wines age at least 18 months and Gran Reserva at least 30 months.

History

Sparkling wine production in Catalonia began in the 19th century; Josep Raventós of Codorníu produced the first bottles in 1872 after visiting Champagne. The name Cava (Catalan for "cellar" or "cave") was adopted in the 1970s after Spain agreed to stop using the term champaña.

Styles

Cava is classified by sweetness from Brut Nature (no added sugar) through Extra Brut, Brut, Extra Seco, Seco, Semiseco, to Dulce. Most Cava sold is white brut.
