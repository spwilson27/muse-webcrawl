Story of Your Life

Author Ted Chiang

A heptapod logogram, the aliens' written language

"Story of Your Life" is a science fiction novella by American writer Ted Chiang, first published in the anthology Starlight 2 in 1998. It won the Nebula Award for Best Novella and the Theodore Sturgeon Award. The story was adapted into the film Arrival (2016), directed by Denis Villeneuve.

Plot

Linguist Louise Banks is recruited by the U.S. military to communicate with seven-limbed aliens — heptapods — who have arrived on Earth. As she deciphers their non-linear written language, which expresses meaning in semagrams rather than sequential speech, her perception of time begins to change: she experiences her life, including her future daughter's birth and death, outside linear sequence.

Themes

The novella explores the Sapir–Whorf hypothesis — that language shapes thought — along with free will, determinism, and the variational principles of physics such as Fermat's principle of least time, which mirror the heptapods' teleological worldview.

Reception

The story is regarded as one of the finest science fiction works of its era and anchors Chiang's collection Stories of Your Life and Others (2002).
