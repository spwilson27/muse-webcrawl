High-pressure area

A high-pressure area, also called a high or anticyclone, is a region where the atmospheric pressure at the surface of the Earth is greater than its surrounding environment. Winds within high-pressure areas flow outward from the higher pressure area near their center into the lower pressure areas further from their center.

High-pressure systems are associated with descending air, which suppresses cloud formation and generally brings calm, settled weather. They are the opposite of low-pressure areas, in which air rises and weather tends to be stormier.

Formation and structure

High-pressure areas form when air masses cool, become denser, and sink toward the surface, or when air converges aloft and descends. As air descends it warms adiabatically, which inhibits the formation of clouds. The descending air spreads outward at the surface in a process known as divergence.

Because of the Coriolis effect, surface winds around a high-pressure area circulate clockwise in the Northern Hemisphere and counterclockwise in the Southern Hemisphere. This rotation is the reverse of that around a cyclone, hence the name anticyclone.

Types

  • Subtropical highs — semi-permanent features of the general circulation, such as the Azores High and the Pacific High, located near 30 degrees latitude in the descending branches of the Hadley cells.
  • Polar highs — formed over the poles by intense cooling of air near the surface.
  • Blocking highs — slow-moving or stationary anticyclones that can divert the jet stream and lock weather patterns in place for days or weeks.
  • Cold-core and warm-core highs — distinguished by whether the air in the column is colder or warmer than the surroundings.

Effects on weather

High pressure typically brings clear skies, light winds, and dry conditions. In summer, a persistent high can produce heat waves; in winter, clear nights under a high allow rapid radiative cooling, leading to frost and fog. Air pollution can accumulate beneath the temperature inversions that often cap anticyclones, degrading air quality in cities.
