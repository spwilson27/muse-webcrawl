The Jazz Singer

Directed byAlan Crosland
StarringAl Jolson, May McAvoy, Warner Oland
Released6 October 1927
Running time89 minutes
StudioWarner Bros.

The Jazz Singer is a 1927 American part-talkie musical drama film directed by Alan Crosland and produced by Warner Bros. using the Vitaphone sound-on-disc system. Starring Al Jolson, it tells the story of Jakie Rabinowitz, the son of a Jewish cantor, who defies his father to become a jazz singer.

Significance

Although not the first film with synchronised sound, The Jazz Singer was the first feature-length motion picture with synchronised dialogue sequences, including Jolson's famous line "You ain't heard nothin' yet!" Its enormous commercial success effectively ended the silent film era and established Warner Bros. as a major studio.

Reception and legacy

The film received an honorary Academy Award for pioneering talking pictures. It was selected for preservation in the National Film Registry in 1996. Modern assessments also discuss its use of blackface performance, a convention of the era now widely criticised.
