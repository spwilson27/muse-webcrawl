Freeport, Maine

Freeport is a town in Cumberland County, Maine, United States. The population was 8,737 at the 2020 census. Freeport is best known as the home of the outdoor retailer L.L.Bean, whose flagship store anchors a large outlet-shopping district that draws millions of visitors annually.

History

The area was originally part of North Yarmouth and was settled in the seventeenth century around the Harraseeket River, where shipbuilding, mast production, and fishing developed. Freeport was incorporated as a separate town on 14 February 1789. In the nineteenth century it was a shipbuilding centre and is traditionally associated with the "mast landing" trade. Leon Leonwood Bean founded L.L.Bean in 1912 to sell his Maine Hunting Shoe, and the company grew from a one-room shop into a mail-order and retail empire headquartered in the town.

Geography and attractions

Freeport lies on Casco Bay about 17 miles northeast of Portland. Attractions include Wolfe's Neck Woods State Park, the Desert of Maine (a natural sand deposit and tourist site), and the village's Main Street shopping district, where the L.L.Bean flagship — famous for its giant boot sculpture and for being open 24 hours a day for most of its history — stands among dozens of outlet stores.
