General Grant (tree)

The General Grant tree is a giant sequoia (Sequoiadendron giganteum) located in the Grant Grove section of Kings Canyon National Park in California. It is the second-largest tree in the world by trunk volume, surpassed only by the General Sherman Tree in neighbouring Sequoia National Park.

The tree stands about 81.5 m (267 ft) tall with a circumference at the ground of roughly 32.8 m (107.6 ft) and a trunk diameter of 8.8 m (28.9 ft) at 4.5 feet above the ground. Its total volume is estimated at around 1,320 cubic metres (46,600 cubic feet).

History

The tree was named in 1867 by Joseph Hardin Thomas after Ulysses S. Grant, the Union Army general of the American Civil War who later became the 18th President of the United States. In 1926, President Calvin Coolidge proclaimed it the Nation's Christmas Tree, and each December a ceremony is held at its base. On 28 March 1956, President Dwight D. Eisenhower declared the tree a National Shrine, a memorial to the men and women who have died in military service to the United States — the only living individual or object so designated.

Grant Grove

Grant Grove was originally established as General Grant National Park in 1890 to protect the sequoia grove, and was later incorporated into Kings Canyon National Park when that park was created in 1940. Other notable trees in the grove include the Centennial Tree and the Robert E. Lee Tree.
