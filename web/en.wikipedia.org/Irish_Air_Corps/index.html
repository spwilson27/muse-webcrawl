Irish Air Corps

Founded1922 (as the Military Air Service)
HeadquartersCasement Aerodrome, Baldonnel, County Dublin
RoleAir defence support, maritime patrol, transport, training
ParentIrish Defence Forces

The Irish Air Corps (Irish: Aerchór na hÉireann) is the air component of the Defence Forces of Ireland. Based at Casement Aerodrome, Baldonnel, it is the smallest of Ireland's three services, with a fleet of fixed-wing aircraft and helicopters and roughly 700 personnel.

History

The corps traces its origins to the Military Air Service founded in 1922 during the Irish Civil War era, making it one of the world's older independent air arms. Constrained by Irish neutrality, it operated modest fleets through the Emergency (the Second World War) and the Cold War, focusing on army cooperation, fishery protection, and training.

Roles and equipment

Current roles include maritime patrol with CASA CN-235 and (from the mid-2020s) C295 aircraft, pilot training on Pilatus PC-9M turboprops — which also provide a limited ground-attack capability — helicopter operations with AW139s in transport and emergency medical roles, ministerial air transport, and support to the Garda Síochána. The Air Corps has no jet combat aircraft; policing of Irish airspace is conducted in cooperation with the Royal Air Force under a longstanding arrangement.
