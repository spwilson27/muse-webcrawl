Webcast

A live presentation delivered as a webcast

A webcast is a media presentation distributed over the Internet using streaming media technology to distribute a single content source to many simultaneous listeners/viewers. A webcast may either be distributed live or on demand. Essentially, webcasting is "broadcasting" over the Internet.

Uses

The generally accepted use of the term webcast is the transmission of linear audio or video content over the internet. It is used for corporate earnings calls, investor relations presentations, conferences and seminars, e-learning lectures, product launches, concerts, and religious services. Clients for webcasts include media players and web browsers with streaming plug-ins; modern webcasts typically use HTML5-based adaptive streaming.

History

Early webcasts in the mid-1990s delivered low-bandwidth audio, such as the first live concert webcast by the band Severe Tire Damage in 1993. As broadband spread in the 2000s, webcasting became a mainstream channel for events and distance learning. The rise of platforms such as YouTube Live, Twitch, and Zoom extended live webcasting to very large audiences, and webcast recordings distributed afterward on demand overlap with podcasts and video-on-demand services.

Webcast vs. streaming

Unlike general streaming of personalised, on-demand content, a webcast traditionally refers to a scheduled, one-to-many presentation — analogous to a television broadcast — often with audience interaction through chat, polls, and Q&A tools.
