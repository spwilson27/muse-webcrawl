Space law

Earth orbit, the primary domain of space law

Space law is the body of law governing space-related activities, encompassing both international and domestic agreements, rules, and principles. The field addresses a wide variety of matters, such as space exploration, liability for damage, weapons use, rescue efforts, environmental preservation, information sharing, new technologies, and ethics.

International treaties

The foundation of international space law is the Outer Space Treaty of 1967, which declares space the province of all mankind, prohibits national appropriation of celestial bodies, bans weapons of mass destruction in orbit, and makes states responsible for their national space activities. It is supplemented by the Rescue Agreement (1968), the Liability Convention (1972), the Registration Convention (1976) and the Moon Agreement (1984), the last of which has few parties. The United Nations Committee on the Peaceful Uses of Outer Space (COPUOS) is the principal forum for developing the law.

National law and commercial activity

States license and supervise private space activity through national legislation, such as the US Commercial Space Launch Act and the UK Outer Space Act 1986. Growing commercial activity — including satellite mega-constellations, space tourism and proposed asteroid mining — has raised new questions about the applicability of the treaties, addressed in part by instruments such as the Artemis Accords of 2020.

Issues

Contemporary issues in space law include the mitigation of space debris, the militarisation of space, spectrum and orbital-slot allocation through the International Telecommunication Union, and the legal status of resources extracted from celestial bodies.
