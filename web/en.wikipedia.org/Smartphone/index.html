A modern touchscreen smartphone

A smartphone is a mobile phone with advanced computing capabilities and connectivity, distinguished from earlier mobile phones by its extensive use of software, internet access, and multimedia functionality alongside core telephony. Modern smartphones typically feature a touchscreen interface, a mobile operating system, high-resolution cameras, and the ability to run third-party applications ("apps") distributed through digital storefronts.

Smartphones are the most widely used computing devices in the world. By the early 2020s, billions of people owned one, and smartphones had become the primary means of internet access in many countries.

History

Early devices

The IBM Simon Personal Communicator, released in 1994, is widely regarded as the first smartphone: it combined a mobile phone with a touchscreen, email, fax, and personal digital assistant (PDA) functions. Through the late 1990s and early 2000s, devices such as Nokia's Communicator series, the BlackBerry line with its physical keyboards and push email, Palm's Treo, and Microsoft's Windows Mobile handsets brought smartphone features to business users and enthusiasts.

The touchscreen era

The launch of Apple's iPhone in 2007 transformed the industry. The iPhone paired a large capacitive multi-touch display with a desktop-class web browser and, from 2008, the App Store. Google's Android operating system, launched in 2008, spread the model across many manufacturers, led by Samsung. By the early 2010s smartphones had overtaken feature phones in global sales, and rival platforms such as Windows Phone and BlackBerry 10 faded from the market.

Hardware

A modern smartphone is built around a system on a chip (SoC) integrating a central processing unit, graphics processor, memory, and modems for cellular networks (such as 5G), Wi-Fi, and Bluetooth. Other common components include accelerometers, gyroscopes, GPS receivers, fingerprint or face-recognition sensors, and multiple camera modules. Displays are typically OLED or LCD panels of 6 inches or more, and power comes from rechargeable lithium-ion batteries.

Software

Two operating systems dominate: Google's Android and Apple's iOS. Software is distributed mainly through the Google Play Store and the Apple App Store. Applications cover communication, navigation, photography, banking, entertainment, and productivity, making the smartphone a general-purpose platform.

Impact

Smartphones have reshaped communication, commerce, media consumption, and social life, enabling the rise of social media, ride-hailing, mobile payments, and streaming services. They have also raised debates about privacy, attention, addiction, and the effects of constant connectivity on mental health.
