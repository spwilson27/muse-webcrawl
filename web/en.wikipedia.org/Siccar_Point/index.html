Siccar Point

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Siccar Point is a rocky promontory in Berwickshire on the east coast of Scotland, famous in the history of geology as the site of Hutton's Unconformity. Here, gently sloping red sandstone of Devonian age rests directly on nearly vertical greywacke of Silurian age — a junction that provided James Hutton with dramatic evidence of the immense timescales of geological processes.

Hutton's visit

The Scottish naturalist James Hutton visited Siccar Point by boat in 1788, accompanied by John Playfair and James Hall. Hutton had already found unconformities at Arran and Jedburgh, but Siccar Point showed the phenomenon with exceptional clarity: the older strata had been deposited as sediments, tilted vertical, uplifted and eroded, before the younger sandstones were laid down on top. Playfair later wrote that "the mind seemed to grow giddy by looking so far into the abyss of time."

Geology

The lower rocks are Silurian greywackes (about 435 million years old), folded and tilted almost vertical during the Caledonian orogeny. The upper rocks are Upper Devonian Old Red Sandstone (about 370 million years old), deposited by rivers on the eroded surface. The gap in the record — the unconformity — represents tens of millions of years.

Legacy

Siccar Point remains a pilgrimage site for geologists and is often described as one of the most important geological localities in the world, emblematic of the principle of uniformitarianism and the concept of deep time.
