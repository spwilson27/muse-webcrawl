Jinghu

The jinghu (Chinese: 京胡; pinyin: jīnghú, literally "capital huqin") is a Chinese two-stringed bowed lute and the principal melodic instrument of the Peking opera orchestra. It is the smallest and highest-pitched member of the huqin family of spike fiddles.


The jinghu

Construction

The jinghu has a short neck and a small cylindrical body made of bamboo, covered on the front with snakeskin (usually python). Two steel strings — formerly silk — are tuned a fifth apart, and the bamboo bow's hair passes between them. Its sound is bright, penetrating and ornamented, designed to cut through stage singing and percussion.

Role in Peking opera

Together with the larger erhu and the yueqin (moon lute), the jinghu leads the melodic ("civil") section of the Peking opera ensemble, following and decorating the singer's line. The celebrated jinghu master Mei Lanfang's accompanist Xu Lanyuan helped codify its techniques, and the virtuoso playing associated with the Four Great Beards' schools set the modern standard. Famous modern exponents include Yan Shoupeng and Li Men.
