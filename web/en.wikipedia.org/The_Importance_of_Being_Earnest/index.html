The Importance of Being Earnest

The Importance of Being Earnest, A Trivial Comedy for Serious People is a play by Oscar Wilde, first performed on 14 February 1895 at the St James's Theatre in London. It is a farcical comedy depicting the tangled affairs of two young men about town who lead double lives to evade unwanted social obligations, both assuming the name Ernest while wooing the romantic Cecily Cardew and the sophisticated Gwendolen Fairfax. Widely considered Wilde's masterpiece, the play is celebrated for its wit and its satire of Victorian respectability.

Plot

Jack Worthing, a landowner in Hertfordshire, visits London as "Ernest", his supposed wicked brother; in London he proposes to Gwendolen, whose mother Lady Bracknell objects upon learning Jack was found as a baby in a handbag at Victoria Station. In the country, Jack's friend Algernon Moncrieff arrives impersonating Ernest and courts Jack's ward Cecily. The two "Ernests" collide, both women discover the deception and — each demanding a husband named Ernest — insist on christenings. The governess Miss Prism's confession reveals Jack is Lady Bracknell's long-lost nephew, christened Ernest after his father, so both couples may marry: "I've now realised for the first time in my life the vital Importance of Being Earnest."

Style and reception

The play's humour derives from puns, inversion of stock conventions and paradoxical epigrams ("All women become like their mothers. That is their tragedy. No man does, and that is his."). Though the run was cut short after 86 performances by Wilde's arrest and scandal, the play has never left the repertory and is regularly revived and adapted, including notable films in 1952 and 2002.
