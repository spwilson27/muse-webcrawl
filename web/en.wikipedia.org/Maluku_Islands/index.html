Maluku Islands

Country: Indonesia
Provinces: Maluku, North Maluku
Largest islands: Halmahera, Seram, Buru
Population: about 3.1 million

The Maluku Islands (also known as the Moluccas, and historically as the Spice Islands) are an archipelago in eastern Indonesia, lying between Sulawesi and New Guinea. Administratively they are divided between the provinces of Maluku and North Maluku. The islands were the original and for centuries the only source of nutmeg, mace and cloves, commodities that drew Portuguese, Spanish, Dutch and English traders and colonisers to the region from the early 16th century.

Geography

The archipelago includes large islands such as Halmahera, Seram and Buru, and many smaller island groups including the Banda Islands, the Aru Islands, the Kai Islands and the Tanimbar Islands. The region lies near the junction of several tectonic plates and is volcanically and seismically active. Ambon was the historic centre of the colonial spice trade.

History

Local sultanates such as Ternate and Tidore grew wealthy on the clove trade. The Portuguese arrived in 1512; the Dutch East India Company (VOC) later monopolised the spice trade, at times by brutal means, including the near-extermination of the Bandanese in 1621. After Indonesian independence the region saw the short-lived Republic of South Maluku rebellion (1950) and sectarian violence between 1999 and 2002.
