Mengistu Haile Mariam (born 1937) is an Ethiopian former army officer and politician who ruled Ethiopia from 1977 to 1991, first as chairman of the Marxist military junta known as the Derg and then as president of the People's Democratic Republic of Ethiopia. His rule was marked by the Red Terror, forced resettlement and villagization, famine, and civil war.

Rise to power

Mengistu emerged within the Derg, the committee of officers that deposed Emperor Haile Selassie in 1974. After internal purges, he became the junta's dominant figure in 1977 and launched the Red Terror campaign against rival leftist groups, in which tens of thousands were killed or imprisoned.

Rule and fall

With Soviet and Cuban support, Mengistu fought the Ogaden War against Somalia (1977–78) and wars against Eritrean and Tigrayan rebels, while his collectivist policies and the 1983–85 famine devastated the country. As Soviet backing waned under glasnost, rebel forces advanced; in May 1991 Mengistu fled to Zimbabwe, where he has lived under the protection of the government there. An Ethiopian court convicted him in absentia of genocide in 2006 and sentenced him to death in 2008.
