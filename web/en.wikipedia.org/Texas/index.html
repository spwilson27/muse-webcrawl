Texas

The Lone Star flag and map of Texas

Texas is the most populous state in the South Central United States and the second-largest by both area and population, after Alaska and California respectively. Nicknamed the "Lone Star State," it borders Mexico to the south and the Gulf of Mexico to the southeast; its capital is Austin and its largest city is Houston. With a population of roughly 30 million, Texas has four of the eleven largest U.S. cities.

History

Texas was claimed successively by Spain and France, then formed part of independent Mexico after 1821. American settlers in Mexican Texas rebelled in the Texas Revolution of 1835–36, establishing the independent Republic of Texas, which joined the United States by annexation in 1845—precipitating the Mexican–American War. Texas seceded during the Civil War and was readmitted in 1870. The discovery of oil at Spindletop in 1901 transformed its economy.

Geography and economy

The state's landscapes range from Gulf coastal plains and the Piney Woods of the east to the Hill Country, the Great Plains of the Panhandle, and the deserts and mountains of West Texas. Texas leads the nation in energy production—oil, natural gas, and wind power—and hosts major industries in technology (anchored by Austin and Dallas), aerospace (NASA's Johnson Space Center in Houston), agriculture, and cattle ranching.
