Eden Gardens

Eden Gardens is an international cricket stadium in Kolkata, West Bengal, India. Established in 1864, it is the oldest and second-largest cricket stadium in India and the third-largest in the world, with a current capacity of about 68,000. It is the home ground of the Bengal cricket team and the Indian Premier League franchise Kolkata Knight Riders, and is often called the "Mecca of Indian cricket".

History

The stadium takes its name from the Eden Gardens park beside which it was laid out, named after Eden sisters Emily and Fanny, siblings of Lord Auckland, Governor-General of India. The first recorded match was played in 1864. The ground hosted its first Test match in January 1934, between India and Douglas Jardine's England.

Famous matches

Eden Gardens hosted the final of the 1987 Cricket World Cup, the first World Cup final held outside England. The 2001 Test against Australia is remembered for the follow-on victory built on the 376-run partnership between V. V. S. Laxman (281) and Rahul Dravid (180). The ground also hosted the 2016 ICC World Twenty20 finals.

Atmosphere

The stadium is renowned for its passionate and knowledgeable crowds; in its peak configuration it held over 100,000 spectators. Renovations for the 2011 World Cup reduced the capacity while adding modern facilities, including a new clubhouse and improved seating.
