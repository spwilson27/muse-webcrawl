Punjabi cuisine is a culinary style originating in the Punjab, a region in the northern part of South Asia now divided between Punjab, Pakistan and Punjab, India. It is one of the most popular and recognisable regional cuisines of the subcontinent worldwide, famed for rich, buttery flavours, robust use of dairy, and cooking in the tandoor — a cylindrical clay oven. Punjabi food has deep roots in the region's agrarian culture: a farming population's hearty fare built on wheat, pulses, milk, ghee, and seasonal greens.

Ingredients and style

Wheat is the staple grain, eaten as rotis, naan, parathas, and kulchas, with rice (especially basmati) for festive dishes and biryanis. Dairy is central: milk, curd, paneer cheese, butter, cream, and lassi (a yogurt drink) appear at nearly every meal. Pulses — black lentils (urad), chickpeas, kidney beans — provide protein in a largely vegetarian home tradition, while meat dishes, above all chicken and mutton, anchor restaurant cooking. Spicing is warm rather than fiery: garam masala, cumin, coriander, turmeric, ginger, garlic, and green chillies, often started by frying onion, ginger, and tomato into a masala base enriched with cream or butter.

Signature dishes

  • Butter chicken (murgh makhani) — tandoori chicken simmered in a creamy tomato-butter sauce, invented at Delhi's Moti Mahal restaurant in the 1950s by Punjabi restaurateurs Kundan Lal Gujral and partners.
  • Dal makhani — black lentils and kidney beans slow-cooked with butter and cream.
  • Sarson da saag with makki di roti — mustard greens with cornmeal flatbread, a winter classic.
  • Tandoori chicken — yogurt-and-spice marinated chicken roasted in the tandoor.
  • Chole bhature — spiced chickpea curry with puffy fried bread.
  • Amritsari kulcha — stuffed bread crisped in the tandoor, a speciality of Amritsar.
  • Pinni and jalebi among sweets; lassi sweet or salted as the signature drink.

Dhaba and restaurant culture

The dhaba — a roadside eatery serving truck drivers and travellers — is the classic home of Punjabi cooking, offering generous thalis at low prices. After Partition in 1947, Punjabi refugees brought tandoori cooking to Delhi and then, through migration, to London, Toronto, and New York, making dishes like butter chicken and chicken tikka masala global staples. Sikh langar halls, which serve free vegetarian meals to all comers, spread Punjabi vegetarian cooking traditions of dal, sabzi, and roti.
