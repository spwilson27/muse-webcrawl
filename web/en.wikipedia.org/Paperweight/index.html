Paperweight

A paperweight is a small solid object placed on top of papers to keep them from blowing away. Beyond their practical desk function, glass paperweights became a celebrated decorative art form, prized for the intricate designs sealed inside clear glass domes.

Glass paperweights

The finest antique paperweights were produced between about 1845 and 1860 by French glass houses — Baccarat, Saint-Louis and Clichy — during the "classic period". Techniques included millefiori (bundles of coloured glass canes arranged in patterns), lampwork flowers, fruit and butterflies encased in glass, and sulphide cameo inclusions. The Crystal Palace Exhibition of 1851 helped popularise them in Britain and America, where makers such as the New England Glass Company and Millville produced their own styles.

Collecting

After a decline in the early 20th century, studio glass artists revived the form from the 1950s onward. Antique French weights and fine modern examples are actively collected and can command high prices; collectors' organisations such as the Paperweight Collectors Association support the hobby. Notable collections are held by the Corning Museum of Glass and the Art Institute of Chicago.
