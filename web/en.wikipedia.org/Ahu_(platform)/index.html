Ahu (platform)

An ahu is a stone platform found on Easter Island (Rapa Nui), built by the Rapa Nui people as the base of their ceremonial complexes and as the pedestal on which the great statues known as moai were erected.

Structure

An ahu typically consists of a massive rectangular platform of fitted stone, with a sloping ramp on the seaward or landward side and a paved plaza in front where ceremonies took place. The moai stood in a row on top of the platform, facing inland over the village. Many ahu also contained crematoria and burial chambers, serving as sacred ancestral sites.

Notable ahu

Ahu Tongariki, the largest, is about 220 metres long and supports 15 moai; it was restored in the 1990s after being devastated by a tsunami in 1960. Ahu Akivi is unusual in standing inland, with seven moai looking toward the ocean. During the island's period of internal conflict in the 18th and 19th centuries, nearly all moai were toppled from their ahu.
