Lagrange multiplier

In mathematical optimisation, the method of Lagrange multipliers is a strategy for finding the local maxima and minima of a function subject to equality constraints. Introduced by Joseph-Louis Lagrange in the late 18th century, it converts a constrained problem into an unconstrained one by adding the constraints, each multiplied by an unknown scalar — the Lagrange multiplier λ — to the objective function, forming the Lagrangian.

Method

To maximise f(x, y) subject to g(x, y) = c, one solves the system ∇f = λ∇g together with the constraint. Geometrically, at a constrained optimum the gradient of f is parallel to the gradient of g: the level curves of the objective are tangent to the constraint curve. The value of λ itself carries meaning — in economics it measures the marginal change in the optimum value per unit relaxation of the constraint, i.e., a shadow price.

Generalisations and applications

The method extends to multiple constraints and variables, and to inequality constraints via the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) conditions. It is fundamental in economics (constrained utility and cost optimisation), mechanics (constrained dynamics), information theory, and machine learning — for example in deriving support vector machines and in entropy maximisation.
