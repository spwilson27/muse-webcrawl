The Musée du Parfum, also known as the Fragonard Perfume Museum, is a private museum in Paris devoted to the history of perfume. It was established in 1983 by the Fragonard perfumery company of Grasse and is located in a Napoleon III-era town house near the Opéra Garnier in the 9th arrondissement.

Collections

The museum traces 3,000 years of perfumery, from ancient Egyptian unguent jars and Roman glass flasks to medieval pomanders, eighteenth-century toilet sets and modern commercial bottles. Exhibits include copper distillation stills, a perfume organ of the kind used by perfumers to arrange raw materials, and an extensive collection of bottles, labels and cosmetics containers. A second Fragonard museum site, in a former leather tannery near the Louvre, displays additional collections of perfume-related objects and paintings.

Grasse

The parent company's historic factory museums in Grasse, the Provençal town regarded as the world capital of perfumery, complement the Paris museum. There, visitors can see traditional enfleurage frames, extraction equipment and the production of fragrance concentrates from flowers such as jasmine, rose and tuberose.
