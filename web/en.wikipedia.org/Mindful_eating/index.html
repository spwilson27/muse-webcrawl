Mindful eating

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mindful eating is a practice of paying deliberate, non-judgmental attention to food and the experience of eating, derived from the Buddhist-derived concept of mindfulness. It directs attention to the colours, smells, textures, and flavours of food, to chewing slowly, and to internal cues of hunger and fullness, rather than eating while distracted or in response to emotional triggers.

Practice

Typical guidance includes eating slowly and without distraction, listening to physical hunger cues and eating only until full, engaging the senses fully, noticing the effects of food on feelings and the body, and cultivating gratitude for the meal. Mindfulness-based interventions applied to eating, such as Mindfulness-Based Eating Awareness Training (MB-EAT), have been studied for binge eating disorder and emotional eating.

Evidence

Research reviews suggest mindful eating can help some people reduce binge episodes and emotional eating and may support modest improvements in eating behaviour, though evidence that it directly causes substantial weight loss is mixed. It is often contrasted with "mindless eating" — consuming food under the influence of environmental cues such as package size, plate size, and the presence of others — described in the work of psychologist Brian Wansink.
