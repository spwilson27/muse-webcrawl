Raymond Queneau (21 February 1903 – 25 October 1976) was a French novelist, poet, critic and editor, known both for his playful and formally inventive writing and as a long-standing editor at the publisher Gallimard. He was a co-founder, with the mathematician François Le Lionnais, of the Oulipo (Ouvroir de littérature potentielle) in 1960, a group devoted to literature produced under formal constraints.

Life and career

Born in Le Havre, Queneau studied at the Sorbonne and was briefly associated with the surrealists in the 1920s before breaking with André Breton. He worked at Gallimard from 1938, becoming director of the Encyclopédie de la Pléiade, and was elected to the Académie Goncourt in 1951. His erudition spanned mathematics, philosophy and the history of science, all of which inform his fiction.

Works

His novel Le Chiendent (1933, translated as The Bark Tree) established his experimental style. Exercises in Style (1947) retells a trivial anecdote ninety-nine times in different rhetorical styles and became a classic of constrained writing. Zazie in the Metro (1959), a comic novel written largely in phonetic colloquial French, was his greatest popular success and was filmed by Louis Malle in 1960. His Cent mille milliards de poèmes (1961) is a book of ten sonnets with interchangeable lines, allowing 1014 possible poems.
