Dolmabahçe Palace

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Dolmabahçe Palace (Turkish: Dolmabahçe Sarayı) is a palace in Istanbul, Turkey, on the European coast of the Bosporus Strait. It served as the main administrative centre of the Ottoman Empire from 1856 to 1887 and from 1909 to 1922, in between which the sultans used Yıldız Palace. It is the largest palace in Turkey.

History

The palace was commissioned by Sultan Abdülmecid I and built between 1843 and 1856 by the Armenian court architects Garabet Balyan and his son Nigoğayos Balyan, on the site of a filled-in bay (the name means "filled garden"). Replacing the Topkapı Palace as the imperial residence, it embodied the empire's westernising Tanzimat era. Six Ottoman sultans lived in the palace. Its construction cost the equivalent of about 35 tonnes of gold.

Architecture

Dolmabahçe blends Baroque, Rococo, Neoclassical and traditional Ottoman elements in an eclectic imperial style. It contains 285 rooms, 46 halls, 6 baths and 68 toilets, covering some 45,000 square metres. The Ceremonial Hall (Muayede Salonu) holds a 4.5-tonne crystal chandelier, a gift from Queen Victoria and the largest Bohemian crystal chandelier in the world; the Crystal Staircase has Baccarat crystal balusters. The palace also houses a large collection of Hereke carpets and paintings by Ivan Aivazovsky.

Republican era

Mustafa Kemal Atatürk, founder of the Republic of Turkey, used the palace as his Istanbul residence and died there on 10 November 1938; the clock in his room is stopped at 9:05, the time of his death. Today the palace is administered as a museum by the Turkish Directorate of National Palaces.
