Occupational hygiene

Occupational hygiene (also called industrial hygiene in the United States) is the anticipation, recognition, evaluation, control, and confirmation of protection from hazards at work that may result in injury, illness, or affect the well-being of workers. These hazards fall into the categories of chemical, physical, biological, and ergonomic hazards.

Air sampling is a common exposure-assessment method used by occupational hygienists.

Role of the occupational hygienist

An occupational hygienist is a professional who applies scientific and engineering principles to anticipate workplace hazards, measure exposures to agents such as dusts, fumes, noise, and radiation, and recommend controls. The hierarchy of controls ranks elimination and substitution above engineering controls, administrative controls, and personal protective equipment.

Methods

Exposure assessment commonly involves personal air sampling, surface wipe sampling, noise dosimetry, and direct-reading instruments. Results are compared with occupational exposure limits such as threshold limit values published by professional bodies. The field complements occupational health psychology, which focuses on psychosocial aspects of worker health.

Organizations

Professional organizations include the British Occupational Hygiene Society, the American Industrial Hygiene Association, and the International Occupational Hygiene Association, founded in 1987.
