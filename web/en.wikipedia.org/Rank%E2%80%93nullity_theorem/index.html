Rank–nullity theorem

The rank–nullity theorem is a theorem in linear algebra. For a linear transformation between vector spaces, it states that the dimension of the domain equals the dimension of the kernel (the nullity) plus the dimension of the image (the rank):

dim(domain) = rank + nullity

Statement for matrices

For an m × n matrix A, the theorem says that n = rank(A) + nullity(A), where rank(A) is the dimension of the column space and nullity(A) is the dimension of the solution space of Ax = 0. The number of free variables in the system equals the nullity.

Proof sketch

One proof extends a basis of the kernel to a basis of the whole domain and shows that the images of the added basis vectors form a basis of the image, giving the dimension count directly.

Significance

The theorem is fundamental to understanding when linear systems have unique solutions, and generalises to the first isomorphism theorem for vector spaces and to index theorems for linear operators.
