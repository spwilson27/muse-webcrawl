Santiago Principles

The Santiago Principles, formally the Generally Accepted Principles and Practices (GAPP), are a voluntary set of 24 principles and practices designed to promote transparency, good governance, accountability, and prudent investment practices among sovereign wealth funds (SWFs). They were adopted by the International Working Group of Sovereign Wealth Funds at a meeting in Santiago, Chile, in October 2008.

Background

Rapid growth of sovereign wealth funds in the 2000s prompted concern in recipient countries that some funds might invest for political rather than commercial motives. Working with the International Monetary Fund, twenty-six countries with funds drafted the GAPP in 2008. In April 2009 the working group became the International Forum of Sovereign Wealth Funds (IFSWF), which now promotes implementation, peer review, and self-assessment against the principles.

Content

The principles are grouped into three areas: (1) legal framework, objectives, and coordination with macroeconomic policies; (2) institutional framework and governance structure; and (3) investment and risk management framework. They call for clear legal foundations, publicly disclosed policy purposes, operational independence, professional management, regular reporting and audits, and investment decisions based on economic and financial grounds.
