Antisocial personality disorder

ASPD is characterised by a pervasive disregard for the rights of others

Antisocial personality disorder (ASPD or APD) is a personality disorder characterised by a long-term pattern of disregard for, and violation of, the rights of others, as well as difficulty sustaining long-term relationships. Individuals with ASPD commonly show deceitfulness, impulsivity, irritability and aggressiveness, reckless disregard for safety, consistent irresponsibility, and lack of remorse.

Diagnosis

According to the DSM-5, diagnosis requires that the individual be at least 18 years old, show a pervasive pattern of disregard for others' rights since age 15, and have evidence of conduct disorder before age 15. The pattern must not occur exclusively during schizophrenia or bipolar disorder. ASPD is classified in Cluster B of personality disorders. Prevalence is estimated at roughly 0.2–3.3% of the general population, with substantially higher rates in prison populations and among men.

Causes

The causes of ASPD are considered multifactorial: genetic predisposition, abnormalities in brain structures such as the prefrontal cortex and amygdala, and environmental factors including childhood abuse, neglect, and inconsistent discipline all appear to contribute. ASPD is frequently comorbid with substance use disorders.

Treatment

No medication is approved specifically for ASPD. Psychotherapeutic approaches, including cognitive behavioural therapy and mentalization-based treatment, show modest benefit, and treatment often focuses on comorbid conditions and reducing harmful behaviour. The disorder has some conceptual overlap with psychopathy, though the two are not identical: psychopathy emphasises affective and interpersonal traits, while ASPD emphasises observable behaviour.
