Gerardus Mercator (5 March 1512 – 2 December 1594) was a Flemish geographer, cosmographer and cartographer. He is best known for the Mercator projection, the world map he published in 1569, which became the standard for nautical navigation because it represents lines of constant course (rhumb lines) as straight segments.

Life and work

Born Geert de Kremer in Rupelmonde, Mercator studied at the University of Leuven, where he mastered mathematics, astronomy and engraving and set up a workshop producing globes, instruments and maps. Suspected of heresy during the religious persecutions of 1544, he was briefly imprisoned; in 1552 he moved to Duisburg in the more tolerant Duchy of Cleves, where he spent the rest of his life.

His 1569 world map, Nova et Aucta Orbis Terrae Descriptio ad Usum Navigantium Emendate Accommodata, introduced the projection that bears his name: a conformal cylindrical projection in which meridians and parallels cross at right angles and local shapes are preserved, at the price of grossly enlarging high-latitude areas. Mercator also produced influential maps of Europe and the Low Countries and a celebrated edition of Ptolemy's maps.

The Atlas

In his final years Mercator planned a vast cosmography. Its map volumes, published from 1585 and completed posthumously by his son Rumold in 1595, were titled Atlas sive Cosmographicae Meditationes — the first use of the word "atlas" for a bound collection of maps, in honour of the mythological Titan Atlas.
