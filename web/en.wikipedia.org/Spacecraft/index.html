Spacecraft

A crewed spacecraft in Earth orbit

A spacecraft is a vehicle that is designed to fly and operate in outer space. Spacecraft are used for a variety of purposes, including communications, Earth observation, meteorology, navigation, space colonisation, planetary exploration, and transportation of humans and cargo. All spacecraft except single-stage-to-orbit vehicles cannot enter and exit space by themselves, and require a launch vehicle (carrier rocket).

History

The first spacecraft were uncrewed satellites: Sputnik 1, launched by the Soviet Union in 1957, was the first artificial object to orbit the Earth. The first crewed spacecraft was Vostok 1, which carried Yuri Gagarin in 1961. Apollo spacecraft carried astronauts to the Moon between 1968 and 1972, and space probes such as the Voyager spacecraft have explored the outer Solar System and entered interstellar space.

Types

Spacecraft are commonly divided into uncrewed spacecraft — artificial satellites, space probes, cargo spacecraft and robotic landers and rovers — and crewed spacecraft, including space capsules, spaceplanes and space stations. Subsystems typically include propulsion, power generation and storage, thermal control, guidance and navigation, communications, and, for crewed vehicles, environmental control and life support.

Reusable spacecraft

Most spacecraft have historically been expendable, but reusable systems include the US Space Shuttle (1981–2011), SpaceX's Falcon launch vehicles and Dragon capsules, and the suborbital New Shepard. Reusability is pursued to reduce the cost of access to space.
