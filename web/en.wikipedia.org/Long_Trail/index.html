The Long Trail along the spine of the Green Mountains

The Long Trail is a 272-mile (438 km) hiking trail in Vermont, United States, running the length of the state along the main ridge of the Green Mountains from the Massachusetts border to the Canada–United States border. It is the oldest long-distance hiking trail in the United States and served as an inspiration for the Appalachian Trail, with which it coincides for about 100 miles in southern Vermont.

History

The trail was conceived by James P. Taylor, a teacher who in 1909 lamented the inaccessibility of Vermont's mountains, and he founded the Green Mountain Club in 1910 to build it. Construction, mostly by volunteers, took twenty years; the final section near Jay Peak was completed in 1930. The southern portions of the trail were walked by the founders of the Appalachian Trail movement, and Benton MacKaye's vision of a continental trail grew partly from the Long Trail example.

Route and hiking

The trail crosses many of Vermont's highest peaks, including Mount Mansfield (the state's highest point), Camel's Hump, Mount Abraham, Killington Peak and Stratton Mountain — where both Taylor and MacKaye are said to have had their inspirations. Roughly 70 rustic shelters and lodges, maintained by the Green Mountain Club and the Dartmouth Outing Club, are spaced along the route. Completing the trail in one hike (an "end-to-end" trip) typically takes 20–30 days; the Green Mountain Club maintains a list of "End-to-Enders." The hiking season runs roughly from late May to mid-October, with the northern sections notorious for deep mud in spring.
