Joik

A joik (also spelled yoik) is a traditional form of song of the Sámi people performed in Sápmi (northern Norway, Sweden, Finland, and Russia's Kola Peninsula). A joik is usually sung a cappella, often with a drum accompaniment, and is traditionally very personal in nature. Rather than being a song about a person, animal, or place, a joik is said to evoke or depict its subject directly — the performer "joiks" the subject itself.

Characteristics

Joiks have slow, chant-like melodies often built on pentatonic scales, with improvised or sparse lyrics; some consist largely of syllables without lexical meaning. Each person may have their own joik, given as a gift, and joiks are also composed for places, animals such as reindeer and wolves, and events. The form is considered one of Europe's oldest living song traditions.

Suppression and revival

During Christianization and assimilation policies, joiking was condemned as sinful and suppressed in schools and churches. From the late 20th century it revived as a symbol of Sámi identity, and joik elements appear in modern Sámi music, including Norway's Eurovision entry "Spirit in the Sky" (2019), which features a joik section.
