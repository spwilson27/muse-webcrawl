The 2020 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XXXII Olympiad and branded as Tokyo 2020, were an international multi-sport event held from 23 July to 8 August 2021 in Tokyo, Japan. Originally scheduled for the summer of 2020, the Games were postponed by a year because of the COVID-19 pandemic — the first such postponement in Olympic history — though they retained the "2020" branding. Most events were held without spectators.

Background

Tokyo was awarded the Games at the 125th IOC Session in Buenos Aires in 2013, defeating Istanbul in the final vote. It was the second Summer Olympics held in Tokyo, after 1964, and the fourth Olympics overall held in Japan.

Sports and highlights

The programme featured 339 events across 33 sports, including five new sports: karate, skateboarding, sport climbing, surfing, and baseball/softball (returning). The United States topped the medal table with 39 gold medals and 113 medals overall, narrowly ahead of China. Notable moments included Caeleb Dressel's five swimming golds, the shared gold medal of Mutaz Barshim and Gianmarco Tamberi in the men's high jump, and skateboarding medals won by athletes as young as 13.

Costs and legacy

The Games were among the most expensive ever staged, and public support in Japan was mixed amid the pandemic. The Japan National Stadium, built for the Games, hosted the opening and closing ceremonies and the athletics events.
