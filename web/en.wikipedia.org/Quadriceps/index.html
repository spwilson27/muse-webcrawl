Quadriceps

The quadriceps femoris is a large muscle group forming the front of the thigh. It is the great extensor muscle of the knee, forming a major part of the thigh's bulk. As its name indicates, it has four parts: the rectus femoris, vastus lateralis, vastus medialis, and vastus intermedius.

Quadriceps femoris
LocationAnterior compartment of thigh
PartsRectus femoris; vastus lateralis, medialis, intermedius
InsertionPatella via quadriceps tendon; tibial tuberosity
NerveFemoral nerve (L2–L4)
ActionKnee extension; hip flexion (rectus femoris)

Structure

The rectus femoris arises from the hip bone and crosses both hip and knee joints; the three vasti arise from the femur and cross only the knee. All four converge into the quadriceps tendon, which encloses the patella and continues as the patellar ligament to the shin. The vastus medialis's low oblique fibres help stabilise the kneecap.

Function and clinical notes

The quadriceps extends the knee in walking, running, jumping, and rising from a chair, and eccentrically controls descent. It is tested clinically via the knee-jerk reflex. Strains, contusions ("dead leg"), and tendon ruptures are common sports injuries, and quadriceps weakness is a key target in rehabilitation after knee surgery. See also femur and knee.
