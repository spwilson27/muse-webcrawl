Army Group North

Army Group North
German: Heeresgruppe Nord
Active: 1939–1945
Country: Nazi Germany
Campaigns: Invasion of Poland; Operation Barbarossa; Siege of Leningrad

Army Group North (German: Heeresgruppe Nord) was a major German military formation of the Wehrmacht during the Second World War, responsible for the northern sector of the Eastern Front.

History

Formed for the invasion of Poland in September 1939 under Fedor von Bock, Army Group North advanced through the Polish Corridor. Re-formed for Operation Barbarossa in June 1941 under Field Marshal Wilhelm Ritter von Leeb, it drove through the Baltic states toward Leningrad, which it besieged from September 1941 in cooperation with Finnish forces.

Leningrad and retreat

The army group failed to capture Leningrad and conducted the siege in which hundreds of thousands of civilians died. Soviet offensives in early 1944 lifted the siege and pushed the army group back through the Baltic states.

Courland Pocket

In October 1944 the formation was cut off in Latvia and renamed Army Group Courland (Heeresgruppe Kurland) in January 1945; it held the Courland Pocket until surrendering in May 1945.
