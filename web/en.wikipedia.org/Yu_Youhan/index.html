Yu Youhan (born 1943) is a Chinese contemporary artist, regarded as one of the founding figures of Political Pop, the movement that emerged in China in the late 1980s and early 1990s combining imagery from Cultural Revolution propaganda with the visual language of Western advertising and pop culture.

Life and career

Yu graduated from the Central Academy of Arts and Design in Beijing in 1970 and for many years taught in Shanghai. In the mid-1980s he was producing abstract paintings influenced by Western modernism, and his large abstract works were shown in early avant-garde exhibitions. Around the turn of the 1990s he began painting the satirical works for which he became internationally known.

Political Pop works

Yu's best-known paintings place the image of Mao Zedong amid bright floral patterns, Coca-Cola-style graphics, or consumer products — most famously his Mao portraits from 1990–91, including Untitled (Mao/Marilyn) (2005), which merges Mao's face with Andy Warhol's Marilyn Monroe. He has said his works explore how political icons become decorative background in a commercialising China. His paintings were included in landmark exhibitions of post-1989 Chinese art, such as "China's New Art, Post-1989" (1993) in Hong Kong, and in the 1993 Venice Biennale. Alongside Wang Guangyi and Li Shan, Yu is considered one of the originators of Political Pop.

Later work

In later years Yu returned to abstraction and to works meditating on Chinese landscape and calligraphy, including paintings inspired by visits to Yunnan. He lives and works in Shanghai, and his works are held by museums and collectors internationally.
