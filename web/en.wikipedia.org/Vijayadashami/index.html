Vijayadashami, also known as Dussehra, is a major Hindu festival celebrated at the end of Navaratri every year in the Indian subcontinent and by the Hindu diaspora. Observed on the tenth day of the Hindu month of Ashvin (September–October), it commemorates the victory of good over evil — most prominently Rama's defeat of the demon king Ravana as told in the Ramayana, and, in eastern and northeastern India, the goddess Durga's victory over the buffalo demon Mahishasura.

Celebrations

In northern India, open-air Ramlila performances re-enact Rama's story over the preceding nights, culminating in the burning of giant effigies of Ravana, Kumbhakarna, and Meghnad filled with fireworks. In Mysuru (Karnataka), the festival is a grand state celebration with a royal procession led by decorated elephants, centred on the illuminated Mysore Palace. In West Bengal, Vijayadashami marks the end of Durga Puja, when clay images of the goddess are immersed in rivers amid emotional farewells. In parts of Maharashtra and elsewhere, people exchange leaves of the apta tree as symbolic "gold," and worship weapons, vehicles, books, and tools.

In Nepal, the same festival period is celebrated as Dashain, the country's longest and most important holiday.
