Catherine Palace

Catherine Palace
Location: Tsarskoye Selo (Pushkin), Saint Petersburg, Russia
Architect: Bartolomeo Rastrelli (principal)
Style: Rococo
Known for: Amber Room

The Catherine Palace is a Rococo palace in Tsarskoye Selo (now Pushkin), about 30 km south of Saint Petersburg, Russia. It was the summer residence of the Russian tsars and is part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Historic Centre of Saint Petersburg and Related Groups of Monuments".

History

The residence originated in 1717, when Catherine I commissioned a modest stone palace. Her daughter, Empress Elizabeth, had it entirely rebuilt to designs by Bartolomeo Rastrelli, producing the vast blue-and-white façade of more than 300 metres completed in 1756. Catherine the Great later remodelled interiors in the Neoclassical taste with the architect Charles Cameron.

The Amber Room and restoration

The palace's most famous interior, the Amber Room, was decorated with amber panels backed with gold leaf; it was looted by Nazi forces during the Second World War and its original panels disappeared. A painstaking reconstruction, completed in 2003, now occupies the room. The palace itself, badly damaged in the war, has been largely restored and is open as a museum.
