Optometry

Optometry is a health care profession that involves examining the eyes and relevant structures for defects or abnormalities. Optometrists are health care specialists trained to prescribe and fit lenses to improve vision, and in many countries are trained to diagnose and treat various eye diseases. In the United States and Canada, optometrists are those who hold a Doctor of Optometry (OD) degree; elsewhere, the profession is regulated under varying titles and scopes of practice.

Scope of practice

Optometrists perform comprehensive eye examinations, measure refractive error (myopia, hyperopia, astigmatism, presbyopia), and prescribe spectacles and contact lenses. Depending on jurisdiction, they may also prescribe therapeutic pharmaceutical agents to treat conditions such as glaucoma, conjunctivitis, and anterior uveitis, perform minor surgical procedures, and provide low-vision rehabilitation. Patients requiring surgery or specialist care are referred to ophthalmologists.

History

The profession grew out of the optical trade of spectacle makers. The first schools of optometry opened in the United States in the 1850s and 1870s; the profession became licensed state by state beginning with Minnesota in 1901. In the 20th century, optometry education moved into universities and expanded from refraction into primary eye care, a development sometimes marked by jurisdictional disputes with ophthalmology.

Examination

A typical examination includes patient history, visual acuity measurement, refraction, binocular vision testing, colour vision and visual field screening, tonometry, and examination of the internal structures of the eye with ophthalmoscopy or slit-lamp biomicroscopy, often supplemented by retinal imaging.
