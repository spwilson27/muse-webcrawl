Melqart

Melqart (Phoenician: 𐤌𐤋𐤒𐤓𐤕, "King of the City") was the tutelary god of the Phoenician city-state of Tyre and a major deity in the Phoenician and Punic pantheons. Often identified by Greeks and Romans with Heracles (Hercules), Melqart was a god of the sea, of colonization and of the monarchy, and his cult spread throughout the Phoenician colonies of the Mediterranean, including Carthage, Malta, Sardinia and Cádiz.

Worship

The temple of Melqart at Tyre was famous in antiquity; Herodotus visited it in the fifth century BC and described its two pillars, one of gold and one of emerald. Tyrian colonists carried the cult westward, and the Phoenician sanctuary at Gadir (Cádiz), known to Romans as the temple of Hercules Gaditanus, remained famous into late antiquity. Melqart was celebrated in an annual festival of resurrection or awakening (the egersis), in which the god's death and return were ritually re-enacted — a feature that reinforced his identification with Heracles. Coins of Tyre depict the god riding a hippocamp, and he appears on Carthaginian and provincial coinage for centuries.

Identification with Heracles

The Greek interpretation equated Melqart with Heracles so thoroughly that the Strait of Gibraltar was called the Pillars of Heracles, a name some scholars connect with the paired pillars of Melqart's temples at either end of the Mediterranean. Hiram I of Tyre is traditionally credited with rebuilding the god's temple in the tenth century BC, and Tyrian tradition linked the foundation of the cult with the foundation of the city itself.
