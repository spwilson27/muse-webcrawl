Citron

KingdomPlantae
FamilyRutaceae
GenusCitrus
SpeciesC. medica

The citron (Citrus medica) is a large fragrant citrus fruit with a thick rind. It is one of the original citrus species from which all other cultivated citrus types developed through natural hybrid speciation, along with the mandarin, pomelo and papeda. Originating in northeast India and the foothills of the Himalayas, it was the first citrus fruit to reach the Mediterranean, where the Greeks and Romans knew it as the "Median apple".

Description and uses

The fruit is roughly oval, protruding at the stylar end, with a thick, bumpy, aromatic peel and very little dry, acidic pulp. It is valued chiefly for its rind, which is candied (succade) for use in baking, and for its zest and fragrance. A famous variety is the Buddha's hand, in which the fruit splits into finger-like segments. The citron, under its Hebrew name etrog, is one of the Four Species used in the Jewish festival of Sukkot, and ritually perfect specimens are carefully selected and can command high prices.
