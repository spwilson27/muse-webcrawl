Balto

Balto (1919 – March 14, 1933) was a Siberian Husky and sled dog belonging to musher Leonhard Seppala. He achieved fame as the lead dog of the final leg of the 1925 serum run to Nome, Alaska, in which diphtheria antitoxin was transported by dog sled relay across the Alaskan interior to combat an epidemic.

The serum run

In January 1925, diphtheria threatened the isolated town of Nome. With the antitoxin in Anchorage, a relay of more than 20 mushers and some 150 dogs carried the serum 674 miles in about five and a half days. Gunnar Kaasen drove the final team, with Balto in the lead, arriving in Nome on February 2, 1925. The run inspired the Iditarod Trail Sled Dog Race and became known as the "Great Race of Mercy".

Later life and legacy

Balto became a celebrity, touring the United States, but later fell into neglect at a sideshow. A campaign raised funds to buy Balto and his teammates, and they spent their remaining years at the Cleveland Metroparks Zoo. A bronze statue of Balto stands in Central Park, New York City, and his preserved body is displayed at the Cleveland Museum of Natural History. The 1995 animated film Balto retells his story.
