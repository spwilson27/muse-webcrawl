Hanafuda

TypePlaying cards
OriginJapan, late Edo period
Cards48 (12 suits of 4)
GamesKoi-Koi, Hachi-Hachi, Sakura

Hanafuda (literally "flower cards") are a style of Japanese playing cards. A deck contains 48 cards divided into twelve suits, one for each month, each suit decorated with a flower or plant of that month — such as pine for January, plum blossom for February, and cherry blossom for March — along with associated animals, objects, and ribbons.

History

Hanafuda developed in the late Edo period from earlier Japanese card games derived from Portuguese playing cards introduced in the 16th century. Because gambling with cards was repeatedly banned, designs grew increasingly abstract, eventually dispensing with numerals altogether. Nintendo, founded in 1889, began as a manufacturer of handmade hanafuda cards and remains associated with them.

Games

The best-known hanafuda game is Koi-Koi, in which players match cards by month to form scoring combinations (yaku). Other popular games include Hachi-Hachi and the Hawaiian game Sakura. Scoring depends on special cards: bright cards (hikari), animals (tane), ribbons (tanzaku), and plain cards (kasu).
