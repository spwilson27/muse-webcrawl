AppearanceBlack solid, porous or needle-like
SourceDelayed coking of heavy oil residues

UsesFuel; anodes for aluminium smelting; electrodes; calcined products

Petroleum coke, abbreviated petcoke, is a carbon-rich solid derived from oil refining, produced when heavy residual oils are thermally cracked in delayed coking units. It is the final carbon-rich material obtained from the heaviest fractions of crude oil.

Types and production

Freshly produced "green" coke takes sponge, shot or needle forms depending on feedstock and conditions. Calcining at around 1,200–1,350 °C removes volatiles and increases carbon purity. Needle coke, made from selected aromatic feedstocks, commands premium prices for graphite electrodes used in electric arc steelmaking; sponge coke of low sulphur and metal content is calcined for anodes consumed in aluminium smelting.

Uses and environmental issues

Fuel-grade petcoke, high in energy content but also in sulphur, is burned in cement kilns and power stations, sometimes in place of coal; it emits more carbon dioxide per unit energy than coal and its handling creates dust concerns, leading some jurisdictions to restrict its use or storage. Large export flows from US Gulf Coast and other refineries to India and China have drawn regulatory and public-health scrutiny. Petcoke is also used in the manufacture of titanium dioxide pigment, silicon carbide and other industrial carbons.
