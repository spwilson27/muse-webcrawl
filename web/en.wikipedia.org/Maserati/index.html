Maserati S.p.A. is an Italian luxury vehicle manufacturer founded in Bologna on 1 December 1914 by the Maserati brothers: Alfieri, Bindo, Carlo, Ettore, and Ernesto. The company's emblem is a trident, based on the weapon wielded by Neptune in the fountain in Bologna's Piazza del Nettuno. Since 2021, Maserati has been part of the Stellantis group.

History

The Maserati brothers began in the Diatto racing workshop; when Diatto suspended racing in 1926, they built the first Maserati, the Tipo 26, which won its class at the Targa Florio that year. Maserati cars won the Indianapolis 500 in 1939 and 1940 with the 8CTF driven by Wilbur Shaw. The Orsi family acquired the company in 1937 and moved it to Modena, where production is still based.

In motorsport, Juan Manuel Fangio won the 1957 Formula One World Championship in a Maserati 250F. Road cars followed from 1947 with the A6 1500; later notable models include the 3500 GT, the Ghibli (1967), the Quattroporte luxury saloon (from 1963), the Biturbo of the 1980s, and the MC12 supercar.

Ownership

Maserati passed through several owners: the Orsi family (1937–1968), Citroën (1968–1975), De Tomaso (1975–1993), and Fiat (from 1993), which paired it with Ferrari (1997–2005) and later Alfa Romeo within Fiat Chrysler Automobiles. In 2021 FCA merged into Stellantis.

Modern range

Recent models include the Ghibli and Quattroporte saloons, the Levante SUV, the Grecale SUV, the GranTurismo coupé, and the MC20 mid-engined sports car (2020), the first car with Maserati's in-house Nettuno V6 engine. The Folgore line denotes Maserati's battery-electric variants.
