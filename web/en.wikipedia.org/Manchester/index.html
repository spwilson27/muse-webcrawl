Manchester

Country: England, United Kingdom
Region: North West England
County: Greater Manchester
Population (city): about 550,000

Manchester is a city in Greater Manchester, England, with a population of roughly 550,000 within the city boundaries and part of a metropolitan area of nearly 3 million. It began as the Roman fort of Mamucium (AD 79) and grew into a market town, before becoming the world's first industrialised city during the Industrial Revolution.

History

Manchester's explosive 19th-century growth was driven by textile manufacturing — cotton spinning and weaving powered by steam — fed by the Bridgewater Canal and the Liverpool and Manchester Railway (1830), the world's first inter-city passenger railway. Nicknamed "Cottonopolis", the city was a centre of trade-union and reform movements, from the Peterloo Massacre (1819) to the Anti-Corn Law League, and of science: at its university Rutherford split the atom and the first stored-program computer (the Manchester Baby) ran in 1948.

Modern city

After the decline of textiles, Manchester reinvented itself through education, media (MediaCityUK at Salford Quays), finance and culture. It is famous for its music scene (the Haçienda, Oasis, the Smiths), for its two Premier League football clubs, Manchester United and Manchester City, and for hosting the 2002 Commonwealth Games. An IRA bomb in 1996 prompted extensive regeneration of the city centre.
