The gelada (Theropithecus gelada, sometimes called the bleeding-heart monkey) is a species of Old World monkey found only in the Ethiopian Highlands, living at elevations of 1,800–4,400 metres. It is the only living member of the genus Theropithecus, a lineage of once-widespread grazing primates, and is distinguished by the hairless patch of skin on its chest, which turns bright red in dominant males and in females varies with reproductive state.

Behaviour and ecology

Geladas are the most terrestrial of primates after humans and are predominantly graminivorous: grass blades, seeds, and underground storage organs make up most of their diet, plucked with opposable thumbs and processed while shuffling in a seated position. Their social system is multilevel: reproductive units of one male with several females join with bachelor groups into bands and herds that can number several hundred animals, among the largest aggregations of any primate. They communicate with a rich vocal repertoire, including sounds with speech-like properties.

Conservation

The species is listed as Least Concern overall, though populations face pressure from habitat conversion to agriculture and are protected notably in Simien Mountains National Park.
