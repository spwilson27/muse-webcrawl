Wikidata is a collaboratively edited multilingual knowledge graph that acts as central storage for the structured data of its Wikimedia sister projects, including Wikipedia, Wiktionary, Wikisource and others. Anyone can edit it, and its data is released under a free licence. The site, operated by the Wikimedia Foundation, launched on 29 October 2012.

Concepts

Knowledge in Wikidata is organised into items (identified by Q-numbers, such as Q42 for Douglas Adams), each representing a topic, concept or object. Items carry statements: a property (identified by a P-number) paired with a value, which may itself be another item, a quantity, a date, a string or media. Statements can be annotated with qualifiers and references. Labels, descriptions and aliases in hundreds of languages make the same item usable across all Wikimedia projects.

History

Wikidata was developed by Wikimedia Deutschland with funding from the Allen Institute for Artificial Intelligence, the Gordon and Betty Moore Foundation and Google. Its first task was centralising interlanguage links between Wikipedia articles, replacing millions of individually maintained link lists. Infobox data followed, allowing articles to draw facts such as population figures and dates from the central repository. The project grew to hold well over one hundred million items.

Uses

Beyond Wikimedia, Wikidata's data powers search-engine knowledge panels, voice assistants, research knowledge graphs and the Scholia scholarly profiles. It can be queried with SPARQL through the Wikidata Query Service, and regular database dumps are freely available.
