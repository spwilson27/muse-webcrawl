James Stanley Brakhage (14 January 1933 – 9 March 2003), better known as Stan Brakhage, was an American non-narrative filmmaker. He is regarded as one of the most important figures in 20th-century experimental film, producing nearly 350 works over a career of more than five decades.

Approach

Brakhage sought to liberate the camera from conventional representation and to render acts of seeing itself. He pioneered techniques including handheld camerawork, fast cutting, scratching and painting directly on film stock, collage of found objects onto the filmstrip, multiple exposures and distorting lenses. In Mothlight (1963) he pasted moth wings and plant matter directly onto film; in The Text of Light (1974) he filmed light refracted through a crystal ashtray.

Major works

His epic Dog Star Man (1961–64), the childbirth film Window Water Baby Moving (1959), the autobiographical "Songs" cycle, The Act of Seeing with One's Own Eyes (1971), filmed in a Pittsburgh morgue, and the late hand-painted films such as The Garden of Earthly Delights are among his best-known works.

Teaching and legacy

Brakhage taught for many years at the University of Colorado Boulder and published theoretical writings including Metaphors on Vision (1963). His vision of an "untutored eye" and his vast output profoundly influenced generations of avant-garde filmmakers.
