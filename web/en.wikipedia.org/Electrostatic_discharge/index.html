Electrostatic discharge

Electrostatic discharge (ESD) is a sudden and momentary flow of electric current between two electrically charged objects caused by contact, an electrical short or dielectric breakdown. A buildup of static electricity can be caused by tribocharging or by electrostatic induction.

Causes

Charge accumulates when dissimilar materials rub together or separate, as when walking across a carpet or removing clothing. When a charged person or object approaches a conductor, the stored energy can discharge in a spark — lightning is the largest-scale example.

Damage to electronics

ESD can permanently damage sensitive semiconductor devices, sometimes at voltages far below human perception. Failures may be catastrophic or latent, degrading reliability. The electronics industry therefore uses ESD-protected areas with grounded work surfaces, wrist straps, ionisers, and conductive packaging.

Other hazards

Sparks from ESD can ignite flammable vapours, fuel, and dust, causing fires and explosions; protective bonding and grounding are mandatory in fuel handling and munitions manufacture.
