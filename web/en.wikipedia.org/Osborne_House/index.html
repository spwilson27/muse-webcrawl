Osborne House

Osborne House

Location: East Cowes, Isle of Wight, England

Built: 1845–1851

Architect: Thomas Cubitt, with Prince Albert

Style: Italianate

Owner: English Heritage

Osborne House is a former royal residence in East Cowes, Isle of Wight, United Kingdom. The house was built between 1845 and 1851 for Queen Victoria and Prince Albert as a summer home and rural retreat. Albert designed the house himself in the style of an Italian Renaissance palazzo. The builder was Thomas Cubitt, the London architect and builder whose company built the main façade of Buckingham Palace for the royal couple in 1847.

An earlier smaller house on the site was demolished after a new, much larger house was completed. The original entrance front became the principal courtyard front of the new house. It was the family home of Queen Victoria for more than 50 years, until she died there on 22 January 1901. From 1903 to 1921 it served as an officers' convalescent home and a Royal Naval college, and today it is open to the public under the stewardship of English Heritage.

History

Queen Victoria had spent some of her childhood holidays on the Isle of Wight, staying at Norris Castle at East Cowes, and the area held happy associations for her. In October 1843 she and Albert purchased the Osborne estate of some 342 acres (138 ha) for £28,000. The new house was intended as a private family residence, away from the ceremonial constraints of the court at Buckingham Palace and Windsor Castle.

The house was designed in the Italianate style popularised by picturesque villas, with stucco facades, belvedere towers giving views over the Solent, and terraced gardens laid out in Italian fashion. Prince Albert took a close personal interest in the design, working with Cubitt, and the gardens were planted with trees and shrubs collected from around the world, including a cork oak and specimens from the royal gardens at Kew.

Swiss Cottage

In the grounds, Prince Albert built the Swiss Cottage for the royal children, an Alpine-style chalet where the princes and princesses learned domestic skills such as cooking, gardening and carpentry. Nearby stood a miniature fort, Alberta Barracks, built by the royal children with bricks, and a small museum of natural curiosities collected by the family.

Royal residence

The royal family typically stayed at Osborne for lengthy periods each year, notably in the spring for Victoria's birthday in May and in July and August, when they could enjoy sailing and swimming in the Solent. Victoria and Albert's private apartments on the first floor had views across the grounds to the sea. After Albert's death in 1861, Victoria continued to use Osborne, and it became her favoured residence in old age. She died there in January 1901, in the arms of her grandson, Kaiser Wilhelm II.

Later use

Edward VII gave the house to the state in 1902. Parts of the house became a convalescent home for officers, and from 1903 to 1921 the Royal Naval College, Osborne, educated young cadets in the stable block, among them the future kings Edward VIII and George VI. The house was first opened to the public in 1954, and Queen Victoria's private apartments were restored. Osborne is now managed by English Heritage and is a Grade I listed building.
