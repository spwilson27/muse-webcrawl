Dragør

The old town of Dragør.

Dragør is a town on the southeastern coast of the island of Amager in Denmark, just south of Copenhagen. It is the seat of Dragør Municipality in the Capital Region of Denmark and is known for its well-preserved old town of yellow-painted houses with red-tiled roofs, many built by Dutch settlers invited in the 16th century.

History

Dragør grew from a medieval fishing village serving the herring markets of the Øresund. In 1521 Christian II settled Dutch farmers on Amager, whose influence shaped the neighbouring village of Store Magleby and Dragør's maritime culture of pilots, fishermen and shipowners. The harbour served as an important pilot station for the busy Øresund shipping route.

Today

The old town is a protected conservation area popular with visitors, with a harbour, marina, museum and fort. Dragør lies close to Copenhagen Airport and the Øresund Bridge to Sweden, whose landfall is nearby.
