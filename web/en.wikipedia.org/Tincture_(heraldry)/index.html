Tincture (heraldry)

Tinctures are the colours, metals and furs used in heraldry to depict a coat of arms. The palette has been limited and largely stable since the Middle Ages, so that arms remain identifiable at a distance and in monochrome reproduction, where tinctures are shown by standardised hatching.

Categories

The principal metals are Or (gold, shown yellow) and Argent (silver, shown white). The standard colours are Gules (red), Azure (blue), Sable (black), Vert (green) and Purpure (purple); later and rarer colours are called stains, including Tenné (orange-tawny) and Sanguine (blood-red). The furs are chiefly Ermine (white with black spots) and Vair (a blue-and-white pattern of squirrel pelts), with variants such as Erminois and Pean.

Rule of tincture

The best-known heraldic convention is the rule of tincture: metal should not be placed on metal, nor colour on colour — charges should contrast with their field so as to be recognisable. Famous deliberate exceptions exist, notably the arms of the Kingdom of Jerusalem (gold crosses on silver), said to break the rule intentionally.

Hatching

A system of engraved hatching patterns for representing tinctures in black-and-white, attributed to Silvester Petra Sancta in the 17th century, assigns dots to Or, blank to Argent, vertical lines to Gules, horizontal lines to Azure, and cross-hatching to Sable, and remains in use.
