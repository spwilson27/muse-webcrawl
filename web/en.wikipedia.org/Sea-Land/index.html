Sea-Land

Sea-Land Service, Inc. was a pioneering American shipping company that launched the modern era of containerization. It was founded by trucking magnate Malcom McLean, who in April 1956 sent the converted tanker Ideal X from Newark, New Jersey to Houston loaded with 58 truck-trailer bodies — the first shipment of what became the standard intermodal shipping container.

Growth

McLean's Pan-Atlantic Steamship Company was renamed Sea-Land in 1960. The company built dedicated container ships and terminals, opened the first container port facilities at Port Elizabeth, New Jersey, and played a major role supplying the US military during the Vietnam War. Container shipping dramatically cut cargo-handling costs and revolutionised world trade.

Later history

Sea-Land passed through ownership by R.J. Reynolds and CSX Corporation. In 1999 its international liner business was acquired by Maersk, forming Maersk Sealand; the Sea-Land name survived for a time before being dropped in 2006. The brand was later revived by Maersk for its intra-Americas services in 2015.
