Greyfield land

TypePreviously developed land
Typical examplesFailed shopping malls, obsolete retail and office sites
ContaminationAbsent or minor (real or perceived issues are economic, not toxic)
Related termsBrownfield, greenfield

Greyfield land is a term in urban planning and real estate for previously developed land that is underused, obsolete or economically failing, but which — unlike a brownfield — is not significantly contaminated. The word is formed by analogy with "brownfield" (contaminated land) and "greenfield" (undeveloped land): greyfields are typically blighted by acres of grey asphalt parking lots around dead or dying buildings.

Definition and examples

The term was popularised in the early 2000s in the United States, notably in studies by the Congress for the New Urbanism and PricewaterhouseCoopers, to describe failing regional shopping malls and other stranded retail and commercial assets. Greyfields characteristically have sound infrastructure and good locations, but buildings that no longer suit market demand — enclosed malls superseded by newer centres, empty big-box stores, ageing office parks and surplus parking structures.

Redevelopment

Because they lack the clean-up costs and liability concerns of brownfields, greyfields are attractive candidates for redevelopment and densification. Common strategies include "de-malling": demolishing or opening up enclosed malls to create mixed-use, street-oriented town centres with housing, offices and public space. Advocates argue that recycling greyfields curbs urban sprawl, reuses existing infrastructure, and revives local tax bases, though projects often face zoning obstacles, fragmented ownership and anchor-tenant lease restrictions.
