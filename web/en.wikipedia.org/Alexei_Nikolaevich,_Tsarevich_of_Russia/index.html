Alexei Nikolaevich (12 August 1904 – 17 July 1918) was the last Tsarevich (heir apparent) of the Russian Empire, the fifth child and only son of Emperor Nicholas II and Empress Alexandra Feodorovna. He was born with haemophilia, inherited from his mother, and his illness — kept secret from the public — had far-reaching political consequences through the influence it gave Grigori Rasputin over the imperial family.

Early life

Alexei's birth during the Russo-Japanese War was greeted with national celebration after four grand duchesses. Within weeks, bleeding from the navel revealed his haemophilia, a condition carried through the descendants of Queen Victoria. Two sailor-nannies from the imperial navy were assigned to watch the active boy constantly, but episodes of internal bleeding repeatedly brought him near death, most famously at Spała in 1912, when his recovery coincided with Rasputin's intervention and strengthened the Empress's faith in the starets.

Revolution and death

During the First World War Alexei stayed with his father at military headquarters at Mogilev, an experience he enjoyed. After the February Revolution and his father's abdication — Nicholas abdicated for Alexei as well as himself because of the boy's health — the family was held at Tsarskoe Selo, Tobolsk and finally Yekaterinburg. On 17 July 1918 Alexei was murdered with his parents, four sisters and attendants in the Ipatiev House. His remains, missing from the main grave, were found nearby in 2007 and confirmed by DNA testing. He was canonised as a passion-bearer by the Russian Orthodox Church in 2000.

Legacy

Because Alexei's body was long unaccounted for, impostors later claimed to be the surviving Tsarevich, mirroring the claims made for his sister Anastasia. Genetic identification of the remains has since closed the question.
