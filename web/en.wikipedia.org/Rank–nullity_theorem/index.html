Rank–nullity theorem

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The rank–nullity theorem is a fundamental theorem in linear algebra which relates the dimensions of the domain, image and kernel of a linear map. It states that for a linear transformation T : V → W from a finite-dimensional vector space V, the dimension of V equals the sum of the dimension of the image (the rank) and the dimension of the kernel (the nullity):

dim V = rank(T) + nullity(T)

Statement

Let V and W be vector spaces over a field, with V finite-dimensional, and let T : V → W be a linear map. Define the rank of T as the dimension of its image (range), and the nullity as the dimension of its kernel (null space). Then

rank(T) + nullity(T) = dim V.

For a matrix A with n columns, the theorem says that the rank of A plus the dimension of its null space equals n.

Proof sketch

Take a basis of the kernel of T and extend it to a basis of V. The images under T of the added basis vectors are linearly independent and span the image of T, so they form a basis of the image. Counting basis vectors then gives the result. The argument is closely tied to the first isomorphism theorem, which identifies the image of T with the quotient space V / ker T.

Applications

The theorem tells us, for instance, that a linear map on a finite-dimensional space of the same dimension is injective if and only if it is surjective (and hence an isomorphism) — a fact failing in infinite dimensions, as shown by shift operators. It underlies the analysis of systems of linear equations: a homogeneous system with more unknowns than the rank of its coefficient matrix always has nontrivial solutions, and it governs the dimensions of solution spaces of linear differential equations and the consistency conditions in the Fredholm alternative.
