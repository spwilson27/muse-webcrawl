Guo Shoujing (Chinese: 郭守敬; 1231–1316) was a Chinese astronomer, hydraulic engineer, mathematician, and politician of the Yuan dynasty. He is regarded as one of the greatest scientists in Chinese history, noted for improving the accuracy of the Chinese calendar and for designing advanced astronomical instruments.

Early life

Guo was born in Xingtai, Hebei, and showed precocious talent in mathematics and engineering. As a teenager he reportedly built a water clock based on a diagram he had seen. He studied under officials connected with the Mongol administration and came to the attention of Kublai Khan, who valued his hydraulic engineering skills.

Calendar reform

In 1276 Kublai Khan commissioned Guo and others to reform the calendar. Guo built a series of large, precise instruments, including a simplified armillary sphere and improved gnomons, and organized observations at 27 sites across the empire. The resulting Shoushi ("season-granting") calendar, promulgated in 1281, calculated the tropical year as 365.2425 days — the same value later adopted by the Gregorian calendar — and remained in use for nearly 364 years, a record in Chinese history.

Hydraulic works

Guo directed major water projects, including the Tonghui Canal, completed in 1293, which restored a water route that brought grain barges into the Yuan capital at Dadu (Beijing). He also surveyed river systems and restored irrigation networks in northwestern China.

Legacy

The asteroid 2012 Guo Shoujing and the Guo Shoujing Telescope (LAMOST) in China are named in his honor. His instruments' precision was not surpassed in China for centuries.
