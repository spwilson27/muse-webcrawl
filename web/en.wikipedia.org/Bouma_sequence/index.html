Bouma sequence

Field: Sedimentary geology
Described by: Arnold Bouma (1962)
Deposit: Turbidite
Divisions: Ta–Te (five intervals)

The Bouma sequence describes a classic set of sedimentary structures found in turbidites, deposited by turbidity currents. It was described by Arnold H. Bouma in 1962 and remains the standard model for the internal organisation of a single turbidite bed.

Divisions

From base to top, the complete sequence comprises five intervals: Ta, a massive, normally graded sandstone; Tb, planar-laminated sand; Tc, ripple cross-laminated sand; Td, planar-laminated silt; and Te, mud representing background pelagic sedimentation.

Interpretation

The succession records the waning energy of a turbidity current as it decelerates: coarse material settles first under high flow, followed by progressively finer sediment under lower-flow-regime conditions. Real turbidite beds commonly show incomplete sequences, with upper or lower divisions missing.

Use

Bouma divisions are used in sedimentology to interpret depositional environments of deep-marine clastic systems and in petroleum geology, where turbidite sandstones form important hydrocarbon reservoirs.
