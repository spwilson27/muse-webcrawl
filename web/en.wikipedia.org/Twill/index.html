Twill

TypeTextile weave
PatternDiagonal parallel ribs
ExamplesDenim, gabardine, serge, tweed

Twill is one of the three fundamental types of textile weaves, alongside plain weave and satin weave. It is characterised by a pattern of diagonal parallel ribs created by passing the weft yarn over one or more warp yarns and then under two or more, with a step or offset between rows to form the diagonal.

Structure and notation

Twills are described by fractions such as 2/1 or 2/2: the numerator counts warp threads the weft passes over, the denominator those it passes under. A 2/2 twill, in which the weft crosses two warp threads on each side, produces the balanced diagonal familiar from serge. The direction of the diagonal may run upward to the right (Z-twill) or left (S-twill), and reversing the direction yields herringbone and other broken-twill patterns.

Properties

Twill fabrics drape well, resist soiling and wrinkling, and can be woven densely and durably, which is why they are used for workwear and upholstery. Denim is a warp-faced 3/1 twill with indigo-dyed warp and undyed weft; gabardine, chino, drill and tweed are also twills.
