Biopiracy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Biopiracy is a term for the appropriation of biological resources and traditional knowledge — particularly from Indigenous peoples and developing countries — by corporations or researchers who seek patents or commercial benefit without fair compensation or consent.

Examples

Frequently cited cases include patents on neem-based pesticides and turmeric's wound-healing use (both challenged successfully by India), the US patent on the ayahuasca plant, basmati rice lines patented by RiceTec, and disputes over the malaria drug artemisinin and hoodia appetite suppressants, the latter leading to a benefit-sharing agreement with the San people.

Legal frameworks

The Convention on Biological Diversity (1992) affirmed national sovereignty over genetic resources, and the Nagoya Protocol (adopted 2010, in force 2014) established access and benefit-sharing rules. Critics debate the term's scope, noting the difficulty of drawing lines between biopiracy and legitimate bioprospecting.
