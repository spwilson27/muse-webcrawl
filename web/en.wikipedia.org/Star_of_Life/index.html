Star of Life

The Star of Life is a blue six-pointed star on a white background, with the Rod of Asclepius — a staff entwined by a single serpent — at its centre. It is the internationally recognised symbol of emergency medical services (EMS), appearing on ambulances, paramedic uniforms, emergency departments and first-aid equipment worldwide.

Origin

The symbol was designed in 1973 by Leo R. Schwartz, chief of the EMS branch of the United States National Highway Traffic Safety Administration, after the American Red Cross complained that the orange Omaha cross previously used on ambulances too closely resembled its own emblem. It was registered as a trademark in 1977 and subsequently adopted internationally; its use is promoted by bodies including the World Health Organization.

Meaning of the six points

The six arms of the star represent the six functions of the emergency medical care chain: detection of the emergency, reporting, response, on-scene care, care in transit, and transfer to definitive care. The Rod of Asclepius, drawn from Greek mythology, is a traditional emblem of medicine and healing.

Use

The Star of Life marks ambulances and other EMS vehicles, identifies trained personnel, and appears on signage directing patients to emergency care and on medical alert jewellery. Some services use colour variants — for example, green in parts of Europe or for pharmacy-adjacent services.
