Clement of Ohrid

Saint Clement of Ohrid (c. 840 – 27 July 916) was a medieval Bulgarian saint, scholar, writer and enlightener of the Slavs. He was one of the most prominent disciples of Saints Cyril and Methodius and is credited with a major role in creating and spreading the Glagolitic and Cyrillic scripts, particularly through the literary school he founded at Ohrid in the First Bulgarian Empire.

Life

Clement accompanied Cyril and Methodius on their mission to Great Moravia in 863, helping to translate liturgical texts into Old Church Slavonic. After Methodius's death in 885 and the expulsion of his disciples from Moravia, Clement was welcomed by Boris I of Bulgaria and in 886 sent to the region of Kutmichevitsa in the southwest, centred on Ohrid, where tradition credits him with training some 3,500 students. In 893 he became the first bishop ordained in the Slavonic language, of Drembica-Velika. He founded the monastery of Saint Pantaleimon at Plaoshnik in Ohrid, where he was buried after his death in 916.

Works and legacy

Clement wrote numerous sermons, hymns and lives of saints and is considered the founder of the Ohrid Literary School. He is one of the Seven Saints of the Bulgarian Orthodox Church and a patron of North Macedonia, where Skopje's national university library and the city of Ohrid honour his name; Sofia University in Bulgaria is also named after him. His feast day is 27 July (9 August in the civil calendar).
