Kirkwall

St Magnus Cathedral, founded in 1137, dominates the Kirkwall skyline.

Kirkwall is the largest town and capital of the Orkney Islands, off the northern coast of mainland Scotland. Situated on the Mainland, Orkney's largest island, it has a population of around 7,000 and serves as the archipelago's administrative, commercial and ferry hub, linking Orkney with Aberdeen and Shetland.

History

The town's name derives from the Old Norse Kirkjuvágr, "church bay", referring to an early church on the site. Kirkwall grew around the harbour and the great St Magnus Cathedral, begun in 1137 by Earl Rognvald Kali Kolsson in honour of his uncle, St Magnus Erlendsson, the martyred earl of Orkney. The cathedral, built of red and yellow sandstone, remains the finest medieval building in the north of Scotland. Kirkwall received royal burgh status in 1486 from James III, a few years after Orkney passed from Norway to Scotland in 1468–69.

Landmarks and culture

Near the cathedral stand the ruins of the Bishop's Palace and the Earl's Palace, the latter a notable example of Renaissance architecture in Scotland. The town hosts the Orkney Museum, the Highland Park and Scapa whisky distilleries, and the St Magnus International Festival, founded in 1977 by composer Peter Maxwell Davies and writer George Mackay Brown. Kirkwall Airport serves inter-island and mainland flights.
