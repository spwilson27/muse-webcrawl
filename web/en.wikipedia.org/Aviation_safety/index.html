Aviation safety

Aviation safety is the study and practice of managing risks in aviation. It includes preventing aviation accidents and incidents through research, training, and the design and maintenance of aircraft, as well as the regulation of air traffic and infrastructure.

History

In the early decades of flight, accidents were frequent and often fatal. Improvements in aircraft design, navigation aids, weather forecasting, crew training, and air traffic control have made commercial aviation one of the safest forms of transport. Statistically, the risk of being killed in a commercial airline accident has fallen steadily decade by decade since the 1960s.

Accident investigation

Major accidents are investigated by national bodies such as the U.S. National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) and equivalent agencies, following standards set by the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO). Investigations make use of cockpit voice recorders and flight data recorders — the so-called "black boxes" — and typically result in safety recommendations aimed at preventing recurrence.

Common causes

Human factors contribute to the majority of accidents, including pilot error and maintenance mistakes. Other causes include mechanical failure, adverse weather, controlled flight into terrain, runway excursion, and bird strikes. Modern safety programs emphasize crew resource management, fatigue management, and voluntary incident reporting systems.

Regulation

Aviation is regulated internationally by ICAO and nationally by authorities such as the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) in the United States and the European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA). They set standards for aircraft certification, pilot licensing, and airline operations.
