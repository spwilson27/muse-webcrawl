Flat track

Flat track is a form of motorcycle racing on oval dirt (or sometimes clay, crushed limestone, or decomposed granite) tracks. It is among the oldest forms of motorcycle sport in the United States, with roots in the early twentieth century, and is characterised by riders sliding their machines sideways through corners, controlling the slide with throttle and a steel shoe on the left boot. Flat track is also the name of the surface and discipline in related sports, and "flat-track" style has influenced motorcycle design broadly.

Classes and formats

Tracks are classified by length: short tracks (under 3/8 mile), half-miles, miles, and TT courses, which add a right-hand turn and a jump. The most prestigious events are the Mile races, where purpose-built twin-cylinder machines reach speeds around 140 mph (225 km/h) and riders draft inches apart. In the United States, the premier professional series is American Flat Track (formerly AMA Grand National Championship), sanctioned by AMA Pro Racing; its top class, currently Grand National Twins/SuperTwins, has historically been contested by Harley-Davidson (the XR-750, raced from 1970, became the most successful model in the sport) and, since the late 2010s, by the Indian FTR750, which ended Harley's long dominance. Amateur and vintage racing is widespread, and the sport also thrives in Australia (as dirt track) and in speedway-adjacent forms in Europe.

Technique and machinery

Traditional flat track bikes have no front brake — many classes prohibit it — and rely on engine braking, a rear brake, and the rider's steel shoe to slow and steer. Riders back the bike into corners, sliding the rear wheel while the front tracks the chosen line, a technique later adapted by road racers and celebrated in the 1971 film On Any Sunday, which popularised the sport and its star, Mert Lawwill. Legendary champions include Jay Springsteen, Chris Carr (a seven-time Grand National champion), Ricky Graham, Scott Parker (a record nine titles), Jared Mees, and Kenny Roberts, who trained on flat track before becoming a road-racing world champion.
