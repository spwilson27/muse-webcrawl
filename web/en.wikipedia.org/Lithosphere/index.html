Lithosphere

The lithosphere is the rigid, outermost shell of a terrestrial planet. On Earth it comprises the crust and the uppermost mantle, and is broken into tectonic plates that move over the more ductile asthenosphere.

Earth's lithosphere

Oceanic lithosphere is typically 5–100 km thick and denser, composed mainly of basalt and gabbro. Continental lithosphere can reach 200 km or more beneath ancient cratons and is less dense, with a granitic upper crust.

Plate tectonics

The lithosphere is divided into major and minor plates whose motions — driven by mantle convection, slab pull and ridge push — cause earthquakes, volcanism, mountain building and the drift of continents.

Interactions

The lithosphere interacts with the hydrosphere, atmosphere and biosphere through weathering, the rock cycle and nutrient cycling.
