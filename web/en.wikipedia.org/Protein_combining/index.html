Protein combining

Protein combining (or protein complementing) is a dietary theory for protein nutrition that became popular in the 1970s, holding that plant foods with complementary essential amino acid profiles should be eaten together at the same meal to form a complete protein. Classic pairings include grains with legumes, such as rice and beans.

History and reassessment

The idea was popularized by Frances Moore Lappé's 1971 book Diet for a Small Planet. Lappé herself revised the strict version in later editions, and nutrition authorities, including the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, now hold that consuming a variety of plant protein sources over the course of a day is sufficient; deliberate combining at each meal is unnecessary because the body maintains a pool of amino acids.

Basis

Individual plant proteins tend to be relatively low in one or more essential amino acids — cereals in lysine, legumes in methionine — so diverse diets easily cover requirements across meals.
