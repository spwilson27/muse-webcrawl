Wood grain

Wood grain is the longitudinal arrangement of wood fibres and the pattern produced on a cut surface by the tree's annual growth rings and cellular structure. Grain affects the strength, workability, and appearance of timber and is central to the identification of wood species.

Forms

Straight grain runs parallel to the trunk; spiral, interlocked, and wavy grains deviate from it and produce decorative figure. Quartersawn boards show straight, parallel grain, while plainsawn boards show the cathedral patterns of intersecting growth rings. Burl, bird's-eye, and curly figure arise from abnormal growth or genetic variation.

Anatomy

In softwoods the grain is formed by tracheids; in hardwoods, vessels (pores), fibres, and rays contribute. Earlywood and latewood bands within each growth ring create the visible banding.

Working properties

Planing against the grain tears the fibres, so woodworkers read grain direction before machining. Grain orientation also governs how boards warp as they dry.
