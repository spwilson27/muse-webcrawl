Utrecht is the fourth-largest city in the Netherlands and the capital of the province of Utrecht. Located in the centre of the country, it is a major railway hub and home to Utrecht University, the largest university in the Netherlands.

History

The city began as a Roman fortification, Castellum Traiectum, built on the Rhine frontier around AD 47. In the Middle Ages it became the most important city in the northern Netherlands and the seat of a powerful prince-bishop. The Treaty of Utrecht (1713) ended the War of the Spanish Succession, and the Union of Utrecht (1579) is regarded as a founding document of the Dutch Republic.

Geography and landmarks

Utrecht's medieval centre is ringed by canals, notably the Oudegracht with its unique wharf-level cellars now occupied by cafés. The Dom Tower, at 112 metres the tallest church tower in the Netherlands, dominates the skyline; it was separated from Dom Church by a tornado in 1674. The Rietveld Schröder House, designed in 1924, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and an icon of De Stijl.

Transport and culture

Utrecht Centraal is the busiest railway station in the Netherlands, and the city is known for extensive cycling infrastructure, including the world's largest bicycle parking facility. Annual events include the Festival Oude Muziek, the Netherlands Film Festival and the spring fair on the Vredenburg square.
