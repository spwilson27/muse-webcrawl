Der Judenstaat

Der Judenstaat (The State of the Jews, also commonly translated as The Jewish State) is a pamphlet written by Theodor Herzl and published in February 1896 in Leipzig and Vienna by M. Breitenstein's Verlags-Buchhandlung. It is subtitled with "Versuch einer modernen Lösung der Judenfrage" ("Proposal of a modern solution for the Jewish question"). The book is considered one of the most important texts of early Zionism.

Title page of the first edition (1896)

Argument

Herzl argued that antisemitism was ineradicable wherever Jews lived among other peoples, and that assimilation had failed. The only solution was the establishment of a sovereign Jewish state, granted by the great powers, to which Jews would emigrate in an orderly fashion. He was deliberately vague about location — Palestine, "our ever-memorable historic home", or Argentina were both canvassed — and detailed about method: a Society of Jews to negotiate politically, and a Jewish Company, modelled on chartered companies, to finance and organize the transfer of property and people.

Proposed society

Herzl sketched a modern, tolerant state: a seven-hour working day, universal suffrage, separation of religion and state (theocracy explicitly rejected), an army for defence only, and a multilingual federation in which each group kept its language. The motto on its white flag with seven gold stars would symbolize the seven working hours.

Impact

The pamphlet made Herzl the leader of political Zionism; the following year he convened the First Zionist Congress in Basel (1897), which adopted the Basel Program calling for a publicly secured home for the Jewish people in Palestine. Der Judenstaat remains the founding manifesto of the movement that led, half a century later, to the State of Israel.
