Cape Fear (1991 film)

Cape Fear is a 1991 American psychological thriller film directed by Martin Scorsese. It is a remake of the 1962 film of the same name, which was based on John D. MacDonald's 1957 novel The Executioners. The film stars Robert De Niro as convicted rapist Max Cady and Nick Nolte as Sam Bowden, the lawyer Cady blames for his imprisonment.

Plot

Released after fourteen years in prison, Max Cady stalks defence attorney Sam Bowden and his family in the fictional town of New Essex, North Carolina. Years earlier Bowden, believing Cady guilty, had buried a report that might have shortened his sentence. Cady's campaign of intimidation escalates from surveillance to violence, culminating in a brutal confrontation aboard the Bowdens' houseboat on the Cape Fear River during a storm.

Cast and production

The cast includes Jessica Lange as Leigh Bowden, Juliette Lewis as their teenage daughter Danielle, and Joe Don Baker as a private investigator. Gregory Peck, Robert Mitchum, and Martin Balsam — stars of the 1962 original — appear in supporting roles. Elmer Bernstein adapted Bernard Herrmann's score from the original film. Scorsese shot much of the film in Florida.

Reception

The film was a commercial success, grossing over $180 million worldwide, and received Academy Award nominations for De Niro (Best Actor) and Lewis (Best Supporting Actress). Critics praised De Niro's physically transformed, tattoo-covered performance and Scorsese's Hitchcockian direction, though some found the violence excessive. It is frequently ranked among the most intense thrillers of its era.
