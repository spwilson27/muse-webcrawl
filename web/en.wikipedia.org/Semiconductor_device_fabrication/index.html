Semiconductor device fabrication

Semiconductor device fabrication is the process used to manufacture semiconductor devices, typically integrated circuits (ICs) such as computer processors, microcontrollers, and memory chips that are present in everyday electrical and electronic devices. It is a multiple-step photolithographic and physico-chemical process (with steps such as thermal oxidation, thin-film deposition, ion implantation, etching) during which electronic circuits are gradually created on a wafer, typically made of pure single-crystal silicon. Several types of materials may be used (such as gallium arsenide) to create the intricate devices, although silicon is by far the most prevalent material.

Overview

The fabrication process is performed in highly specialized semiconductor fabrication plants, also known as semiconductor fabs, which contain cleanrooms where the air is extremely clean and controlled. The entire manufacturing process, from start to packaged chips ready for shipment, takes six to eight weeks and is performed in these highly specialized facilities.

Semiconductor device fabrication processes vary depending on the manufacturing firm, but the typical steps include: wafer preparation, photolithography, etching, chemical vapor deposition (CVD), ion implantation, diffusion, chemical-mechanical planarization, metallization, and testing and packaging.

Feature size

A specific semiconductor process has specific design rules on the minimum feature size of components, known as the technology node or process node. These nodes have shrunk dramatically over the decades, from tens of micrometres in the 1970s to 3 nanometres and below in advanced production in the 2020s, following the trend described by Moore's law. Smaller feature sizes allow more transistors to be packed onto a chip, increasing speed and reducing power consumption.

Photolithography

Photolithography is the most critical step in semiconductor manufacturing. A light-sensitive material called photoresist is applied to the wafer, which is then exposed to ultraviolet light through a mask (reticle) containing the circuit pattern. Modern systems use extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography with a wavelength of 13.5 nm for the finest features.

Materials

Silicon is the most common material used to build semiconductor devices, because of its abundance, low cost, and suitable electrical properties, and because it forms a high-quality insulating oxide, silicon dioxide. Compound semiconductors such as gallium arsenide and silicon carbide are used for specialized applications including high-frequency and high-power devices.
