Michiel Adriaenszoon de Ruyter (24 March 1607 – 29 April 1676) was a Dutch admiral and naval hero, widely regarded as one of the most skilled sea commanders in history. He is best remembered for his role in the Anglo-Dutch Wars of the 17th century, in which he repeatedly defeated English and French fleets and saved the Dutch Republic from invasion.

Early career

Born in Flushing (Vlissingen) to a beer porter's family, De Ruyter went to sea as a boatswain's apprentice at the age of eleven. He rose through the merchant service, gained experience against the Dunkirk privateers, and in 1641 commanded a fleet sent to aid Portugal against Spain, fighting at the Battle of Cape St. Vincent. He also campaigned against the Barbary corsairs of Salé and Algiers.

Anglo-Dutch Wars

During the First Anglo-Dutch War (1652–1654) De Ruyter served under Maarten Tromp, distinguishing himself at Plymouth and the Battle of the Kentish Knock. In the Second Anglo-Dutch War he achieved his most famous success: the Raid on the Medway in June 1667, when Dutch ships sailed up the Thames and Medway, burned much of the English fleet at Chatham and towed away the flagship Royal Charles — one of the worst defeats in Royal Navy history.

In the Third Anglo-Dutch War, heavily outnumbered by the combined English and French fleets, De Ruyter defended the Dutch coast at Solebay (1672), the Schooneveld (1673) and Texel (1673), preventing an invasion. In 1676 he was sent to the Mediterranean to assist Spain against France and was mortally wounded at the Battle of Augusta off Sicily, dying a week later. His body was given a state funeral in Amsterdam, where his monument stands in the Nieuwe Kerk.
