Paratethys

TypeAncient epicontinental sea
Existedc. 34–5 million years ago (Oligocene–Pliocene)
RegionCentral Europe to Central Asia
RemnantsBlack Sea, Caspian Sea, Aral Sea (basin), Pannonian Basin

The Paratethys Sea, or simply the Paratethys, was a large shallow inland sea that stretched from the region north of the Alps over Central Europe and into the Aral Sea of Central Asia. It formed during the late Jurassic as part of the northern margin of the ancient Tethys Ocean and became a distinct, semi-isolated basin during the Oligocene, about 34 million years ago, as the Alpine orogeny raised mountain barriers between it and the Mediterranean.

Extent and evolution

At its maximum, the Paratethys extended from the Rhône basin in western Europe to beyond the modern Aral Sea, covering an area comparable to the present Mediterranean. Because its connections to the world ocean were narrow and intermittent, its salinity fluctuated dramatically: it alternated between marine phases, brackish-water phases, and episodes of near-isolation in which endemic faunas evolved. A major drawdown and fragmentation around 12 million years ago — associated with the closure of connections and climate change — shrank the sea; its eastern part, the Sarmatian Sea, gave way to the brackish-to-freshwater Lake Pannon in the west.

Legacy

The Black Sea, the Caspian Sea, and (until its recent desiccation) the Aral Sea are surviving remnants of the Paratethys, and their distinctive brackish faunas descend from Paratethyan stock. Thick organic-rich sediments deposited in the restricted basin generated major petroleum source rocks; oil and gas fields of Romania, the Caspian region, and Central Asia owe much to Paratethys deposits such as the Maykop Suite shales.

Fauna

Isolation produced rich endemic mollusc faunas that allow fine biostratigraphic subdivision of Oligocene and Miocene stages in Central and Eastern Europe — stages such as the Egerian, Ottnangian, Sarmatian, and Pannonian are still used regionally. Whales, seals, and other marine mammals of the Paratethys included early forms studied from deposits in Georgia, Romania, and Kazakhstan.
