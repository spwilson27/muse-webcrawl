Chondrichthyes

KingdomAnimalia
PhylumChordata
ClassChondrichthyes
GroupsSharks, rays, skates, chimaeras

Chondrichthyes is a class of jawed fish whose skeletons are made primarily of cartilage rather than bone. The class includes the sharks, rays, skates, sawfish, and chimaeras — more than 1,200 living species found in oceans worldwide, with a small number in fresh water.

Characteristics

Chondrichthyans have paired fins, paired nostrils, scales (placoid denticles), and a heart with chambers in series. Most are ectothermic. Fertilisation is internal, and reproductive strategies range from egg-laying to live birth, including some species that nourish embryos through a placenta-like connection. Their cartilage skeleton is lighter than bone, and buoyancy is assisted by a large, oil-filled liver; sharks must generally keep swimming or rest on the bottom to avoid sinking.

Evolution

The group has a fossil record extending back more than 440 million years. Modern chondrichthyans divide into two subclasses: Elasmobranchii (sharks, rays, and skates) and Holocephali (chimaeras).

Conservation

Many species are threatened by overfishing for meat, fins, and cartilage, and by bycatch; slow growth and low fecundity make populations slow to recover.
