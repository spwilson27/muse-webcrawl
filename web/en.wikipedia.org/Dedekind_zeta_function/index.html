Dedekind zeta function

Named afterRichard Dedekind
Defined forAlgebraic number fields
Special caseRiemann zeta function

In mathematics, the Dedekind zeta function of an algebraic number field K, generally denoted ζK(s), is a generalisation of the Riemann zeta function. It is defined as a sum over the nonzero ideals of the ring of integers of K, weighted by their norms: ζK(s) = Σ N(I)−s, convergent for Re(s) > 1.

Properties

The function admits analytic continuation to the whole complex plane (except a simple pole at s = 1) and satisfies a functional equation. The residue at the pole is given by the analytic class number formula, linking the function's value to fundamental invariants of the field: its class number, regulator, discriminant, and numbers of real and complex embeddings.

Special values and conjectures

The values of Dedekind zeta functions at negative integers connect with algebraic K-theory (the Lichtenbaum conjectures), and factorisation properties reflect how the zeta function splits into Artin L-functions under Galois extensions. When K is the field of rational numbers, the Dedekind zeta function reduces to the Riemann zeta function.
