Franklin D. Roosevelt

32nd President of the United States
In office: 4 March 1933 – 12 April 1945
Born: 30 January 1882, Hyde Park, New York
Died: 12 April 1945, Warm Springs, Georgia
Party: Democratic
Spouse: Eleanor Roosevelt

Franklin Delano Roosevelt (30 January 1882 – 12 April 1945), often known by his initials FDR, was an American statesman and politician who served as the 32nd president of the United States from 1933 until his death in 1945. He is the only American president to have served more than two terms, winning a record four presidential elections.

Early life and career

Roosevelt was born into a wealthy family at Hyde Park, New York, and educated at Groton School and Harvard College. He followed his distant cousin Theodore Roosevelt into politics, serving in the New York State Senate and as Assistant Secretary of the Navy under Woodrow Wilson. In 1921 he contracted a paralytic illness, generally identified as polio, which left his legs permanently paralysed; he concealed the extent of his disability in public for the rest of his life. He returned to office as Governor of New York in 1929.

Presidency

Roosevelt defeated Herbert Hoover in the 1932 election amid the Great Depression. In his first 100 days he pushed through the New Deal, a sweeping programme of emergency relief, financial reform and public works, including the Civilian Conservation Corps, the Tennessee Valley Authority and, in 1935, Social Security. His "fireside chats" on radio built a direct rapport with the public.

As war spread in Europe and Asia, Roosevelt moved the United States toward supporting the Allies through Lend-Lease while formally remaining neutral. After the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor on 7 December 1941 — a date he called one that would "live in infamy" — the United States entered the Second World War. Roosevelt worked closely with Winston Churchill and, more warily, Joseph Stalin, and helped lay the foundations of the United Nations.

Death and legacy

Re-elected for a fourth term in 1944, Roosevelt died of a cerebral haemorrhage on 12 April 1945, weeks before the end of the war in Europe; Vice President Harry S. Truman succeeded him. Historians and the public consistently rank Roosevelt among the greatest American presidents, crediting him with reshaping the federal government and the presidency itself, though his record includes the internment of Japanese Americans, which he authorised by executive order in 1942.
