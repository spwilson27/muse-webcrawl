United States Military Academy

The United States Military Academy (USMA), also known metonymically as West Point or simply as Army, is a United States service academy in West Point, New York, that educates cadets for service as commissioned officers in the United States Army. The academy was founded in 1802, and it is the oldest of the five American service academies and among the oldest military academies in the world.

History

The site was first occupied by the Continental Army in 1778 as a fortified position guarding a sharp bend in the Hudson River during the American Revolutionary War. President Thomas Jefferson signed legislation establishing the academy in 1802. Colonel Sylvanus Thayer, superintendent from 1817 to 1833, is regarded as the "Father of the Military Academy" for instituting its rigorous engineering curriculum and strict disciplinary system. Graduates distinguished themselves in the Mexican–American War and the American Civil War, during which West Point alumni commanded armies on both sides.

Admissions and education

Candidates must apply directly to the academy and receive a nomination, usually from a member of Congress. Cadets receive a fully funded four-year Bachelor of Science degree and are commissioned as second lieutenants upon graduation, with an active-duty service obligation. The academic program balances the humanities and social sciences with mathematics, science, and engineering, alongside military training and physical education governed by the Cadet Honor Code: "A cadet will not lie, cheat, steal, or tolerate those who do."

Campus

The campus, a National Historic Landmark, sits on high ground overlooking the Hudson River about 50 miles north of New York City. Notable landmarks include the Cadet Chapel, Trophy Point, and Michie Stadium, home of the Army Black Knights football team.
