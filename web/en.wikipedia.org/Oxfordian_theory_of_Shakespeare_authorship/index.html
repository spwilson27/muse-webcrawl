Oxfordian theory of Shakespeare authorship

The Oxfordian theory of Shakespeare authorship contends that Edward de Vere, 17th Earl of Oxford (1550–1604), wrote the plays and poems traditionally attributed to William Shakespeare of Stratford-upon-Avon. It is the most popular alternative authorship theory.

Arguments

Proponents cite parallels between Oxford's life and the works — his travels in Italy, court experience, and biographical echoes in Hamlet — and argue a commoner could not have possessed the education and experience shown in the plays. The theory was first fully proposed by J. Thomas Looney in 1920.

Scholarly reception

Mainstream Shakespeare scholars reject the theory, pointing to contemporary documentary evidence identifying Shakespeare as the author, and noting that Oxford died in 1604, before plays such as Macbeth and The Tempest are generally dated.
