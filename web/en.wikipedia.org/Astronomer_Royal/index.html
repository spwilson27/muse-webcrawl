Astronomer Royal


The Royal Observatory, Greenwich, long the Astronomer Royal's workplace.

The Astronomer Royal is a senior post in the Royal Household of the United Kingdom, created by King Charles II in 1675. The holder was originally the director of the Royal Observatory, Greenwich, charged with improving the tables of the motions of the heavens and the places of the fixed stars to aid navigation and the determination of longitude at sea.

History

The first Astronomer Royal was John Flamsteed, appointed at the age of 28. Successors included Edmond Halley, James Bradley, Nevil Maskelyne, John Pond, George Biddell Airy, William Christie, Frank Dyson, Harold Spencer Jones, Richard Woolley and Martin Ryle. Until 1972 the post was held ex officio by the director of the Royal Greenwich Observatory.

Since the separation of the posts, the title has been an honorary distinction awarded to an eminent astronomer. Holders in recent decades have included Martin Rees (1995–2020). A separate Astronomer Royal for Scotland was created in 1834.
