The Minneapolis Police Department (MPD) is the primary law enforcement agency for the city of Minneapolis, Minnesota, the largest city in the state. Founded in 1867, the department is organised into five precincts covering the city's neighbourhoods, together with specialised units for investigations, special operations, forensics and community policing. Its chief is appointed by the mayor with city council confirmation.

History

The department grew with the city from a small force of ward constables into a modern municipal agency. Like many large American departments, it has faced recurring scrutiny over use of force, racial disparities and discipline. Controversies over high-profile incidents led to repeated reform efforts, civilian review mechanisms and changes in leadership.

George Floyd protests and reform

In May 2020, the murder of George Floyd by an MPD officer during an arrest sparked protests in Minneapolis that spread nationwide and internationally, the largest wave of demonstrations in the United States in decades. The department's Third Precinct station was abandoned and burned during the unrest. In the aftermath, the city and state undertook investigations into the department's practices; a Minnesota Department of Human Rights inquiry found a pattern of racial discrimination, and a U.S. Department of Justice investigation reached similar conclusions, leading to consent-decree negotiations and sweeping policy changes on stops, force and accountability.
