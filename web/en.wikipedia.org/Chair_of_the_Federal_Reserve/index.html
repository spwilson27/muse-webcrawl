Chair of the Federal Reserve

Office overview
AppointerPresident of the United States, with Senate advice and consent
Term lengthFour years, renewable (as chair)
First holderCharles Hamlin (as governor, 1914); Marriner Eccles (first to use the title "chairman", 1934)
DutiesChairs the Board of Governors and the Federal Open Market Committee

The chair of the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System is the head of the Federal Reserve, the central bank of the United States. The chair is the presiding officer of the Board of Governors and chairs the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC), the Fed's monetary policymaking body. The position is widely regarded as one of the most powerful in the global economy.

Appointment

The President of the United States selects one of the seven members of the Board of Governors to serve as chair, subject to Senate confirmation, for a four-year renewable term as chair; the underlying governorship runs fourteen years. By law the chair reports twice yearly to Congress on monetary policy in the Humphrey–Hawkins testimony.

Notable chairs

Long-serving and influential chairs include Marriner S. Eccles (1934–1948), for whom the Fed's Washington headquarters building is named; William McChesney Martin (1951–1970), the longest-serving chair; Paul Volcker (1979–1987), who broke the Great Inflation by sharply raising interest rates; Alan Greenspan (1987–2006); Ben Bernanke (2006–2014), who led the response to the 2007–2008 financial crisis; Janet Yellen (2014–2018), the first woman to hold the office; and Jerome Powell, appointed in 2018.
