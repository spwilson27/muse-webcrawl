Quality-adjusted life year

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


QALYs combine length and quality of life into one measure

The quality-adjusted life year (QALY) is a generic measure of disease burden, including both the quality and the quantity of life lived. It is used in economic evaluation to assess the value of medical interventions. One QALY equates to one year in perfect health. Health outcomes are attributed a value: if the health outcome is worse than perfect health but better than death, it receives a value between 0 and 1; death is valued at 0, and states worse than death can be given negative values.

The QALY is based on the number of years of life that would be added by an intervention. Each year in perfect health is assigned the value of 1.0 down to a value of 0.0 for being dead. If the extra years would not be lived in full health — for example, if the patient would lose a limb, or be blind or have to use a wheelchair — then the extra life-years are given a value between 0 and 1 to account for this.

Calculation

A QALY is calculated by multiplying the duration of time spent in a health state by the utility value associated with that state. The utility values are typically elicited from the general public or from patients using techniques such as the standard gamble, the time trade-off, or visual analogue scales, or measured with generic preference-based instruments such as the EQ-5D questionnaire.

For example, a treatment that gives a patient ten additional years at a utility of 0.7 yields 7 QALYs, while a treatment giving five years at perfect health (utility 1.0) yields 5 QALYs.

Use in cost-effectiveness analysis

QALYs are central to cost-effectiveness analysis in health care. Interventions are compared by their incremental cost-effectiveness ratio — the additional cost divided by the additional QALYs gained. Bodies such as the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) in England use cost-per-QALY thresholds, commonly cited around £20,000–£30,000 per QALY, as one input when deciding whether to recommend treatments for the National Health Service.

Criticism

The QALY framework has been criticised on ethical and methodological grounds. Critics argue that QALYs may disadvantage older people and those with disabilities, because extending the life of a person whose health state is valued below 1 yields fewer QALYs than extending the life of a person in full health. Others question whether utility weights adequately capture the value people place on health states, and whether aggregation across patients obscures distributional concerns. The related measure, the disability-adjusted life year (DALY), is used mainly to quantify disease burden rather than to evaluate interventions.
