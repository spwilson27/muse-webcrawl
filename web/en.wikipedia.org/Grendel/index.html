Grendel

Grendel is a character in the Old English epic poem Beowulf, one of the three antagonists fought by the hero Beowulf, alongside Grendel's mother and the dragon. Described as a descendant of the biblical Cain and a creature of the darkness, Grendel haunts the mead hall Heorot of the Danish king Hrothgar for twelve years, enraged by the sounds of joy and song within. Because he is protected by magic, no weapon forged by men can wound him.

In Beowulf and later works

Beowulf fights Grendel bare-handed and tears off his arm; the monster flees to his mere and dies. His severed arm is hung in Heorot as a trophy, provoking the vengeance of his mother. The poem gives Grendel little direct speech, presenting him largely through the eyes of the Danes. John Gardner's 1971 novel Grendel retells the story from the monster's nihilistic point of view, making him one of the most reinterpreted figures of early medieval literature. Grendel also appears in films, comics and games, and his name has been applied to places and even a genus of fossil animal.
