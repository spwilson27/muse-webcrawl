Artin–Wedderburn theorem

The Artin–Wedderburn theorem is a classification theorem in abstract algebra concerning semisimple rings and semisimple algebras. It states that every semisimple ring is isomorphic to a product of matrix rings over division rings, uniquely determined up to permutation of the factors.

Statement

If R is a semisimple ring, then R is isomorphic to a finite product of rings of the form Mn(D), the ring of n×n matrices over a division ring D. Conversely, every such product is semisimple. In particular, a semisimple algebra over a field is a product of matrix algebras over division algebras finite-dimensional over the field.

History

The result was first proved by Joseph Wedderburn in 1908 for finite-dimensional algebras, and Emil Artin later extended it to rings satisfying the descending chain condition on ideals (Artinian rings). The theory is fundamental to the representation theory of groups and algebras, since a group algebra over the complex numbers is semisimple and hence decomposes into matrix blocks corresponding to irreducible representations.
