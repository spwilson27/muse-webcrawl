Klaus Schwab

Klaus Martin Schwab (born 30 March 1938) is a German economist and engineer who founded the World Economic Forum (WEF) in 1971 and served as its executive chairman for more than five decades. He is best known for promoting "stakeholder capitalism" and for the WEF's annual meeting in Davos, Switzerland.

Early life and education

Schwab was born in Ravensburg in 1938. He studied mechanical engineering at ETH Zurich, earning a doctorate in engineering, and obtained a further doctorate in economics from the University of Fribourg as well as a Master of Public Administration from Harvard's Kennedy School.

World Economic Forum

In 1971 Schwab convened the first European Management Symposium in Davos, which grew into the World Economic Forum. The annual meeting brings together political leaders, business executives, academics, and civil-society figures. Schwab developed the concept of the "multi-stakeholder" platform and argued that companies should serve all stakeholders — customers, employees, communities, and shareholders — rather than shareholders alone, a theme of his books Modern Enterprise Management (1971) and Stakeholder Capitalism (2021).

The Fourth Industrial Revolution and the Great Reset

Schwab popularised the term "Fourth Industrial Revolution" in a 2015 article and 2016 book, describing the fusion of digital, biological, and physical technologies. In 2020 he co-authored COVID-19: The Great Reset, proposing that the pandemic offered an opportunity to rebuild economies more sustainably. The "Great Reset" phrase became the subject of widespread public debate and conspiracy theories.

Later career

In April 2025, Schwab stepped down as chairman of the WEF's board of trustees after 55 years of leadership, and Peter Brabeck-Letmathe served as interim chairman while the Forum investigated whistleblower allegations, which Schwab denied.
