Pope Alexander VII (born Fabio Chigi; 13 February 1599 – 22 May 1667) was head of the Catholic Church and ruler of the Papal States from 7 April 1655 to his death in 1667.

Pontificate

Chigi, a member of the great Sienese banking family, served as papal nuncio in Cologne and as a negotiator at the Peace of Westphalia, which he opposed for its concessions to Protestants. Elected pope after a long conclave, he initially resisted nepotism but eventually enriched his Chigi relatives. His reign saw disputes with Louis XIV of France, including the humiliating Treaty of Pisa (1664) after the Corsican Guard affair.

Patronage

Alexander VII was a great patron of Gian Lorenzo Bernini, commissioning the colonnade of St. Peter's Square, the Scala Regia, and the Cathedra Petri. He confirmed the conversion of Queen Christina of Sweden and received her in Rome, and condemned Jansenist propositions in 1656.
