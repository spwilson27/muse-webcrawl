Speedway World Championship

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Speedway World Championship is the premier individual championship in motorcycle speedway racing, sanctioned by the Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme (FIM). Speedway involves four riders racing four laps of an oval shale or dirt track on motorcycles with no brakes and a single fixed gear, sliding through the corners. The championship was first contested in 1936 at Wembley Stadium, London, and won by Australian Lionel Van Praag.

History

From 1936 until 1994 (with wartime interruptions), the title was decided by a one-off World Final, usually held at Wembley or other major stadiums, reached through a series of qualifying rounds. The final was famously unpredictable: a single night's racing determined the champion. Riders such as Ivan Mauger of New Zealand and Tony Rickardsson of Sweden became the most successful competitors, each winning six individual world titles; Hans Nielsen of Denmark won four.

Grand Prix era

Since 1995 the championship has been decided by the Speedway Grand Prix series, a season-long sequence of rounds held across Europe and beyond, with points accumulated over the year. The format rewards consistency and has produced champions including Nicki Pedersen, Greg Hancock, Jason Crump, Tai Woffinden, and Bartosz Zmarzlik, who dominated the series in the early 2020s.

Format of racing

Grand Prix events typically feature 16 riders competing in 20 heats, followed by semi-finals and a final. Riders score points per heat, and the championship standings aggregate results across all rounds. Related competitions include the Speedway of Nations (formerly the Speedway World Cup) for national teams and the World Under-21 Championship.
