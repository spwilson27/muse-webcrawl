Sydney

Image placeholder: Sydney Opera House and Sydney Harbour.

Sydney is the capital city of the state of New South Wales and the most populous city in Australia, with a metropolitan population of over 5.3 million. Located on Australia's east coast, the metropolis surrounds Sydney Harbour and extends westward toward the Blue Mountains.

History

Aboriginal Australians have inhabited the Sydney region for at least 30,000 years. In 1788 Captain Arthur Phillip established Sydney Cove as a British penal colony — the first European settlement in Australia — naming it after Thomas Townshend, 1st Viscount Sydney. Gold rushes of the 1850s transformed it into a major commercial port. Sydney hosted the 2000 Summer Olympic Games, widely praised as among the most successful ever held (see also Olympic swimming for an earlier Games).

Landmarks and culture

The city is defined by the Sydney Opera House (opened 1973, designed by Jørn Utzon) on Bennelong Point and the Sydney Harbour Bridge (1932). Other landmarks include The Rocks, Bondi Beach, Darling Harbour and the Royal Botanic Garden. Sydney is a major global financial, cultural and tourism centre and one of the world's most multicultural cities.

Namesake

The city gave its name to Sydney, Nova Scotia, and to the Hawker Siddeley designer Sydney Camm coincidentally sharing the name.
