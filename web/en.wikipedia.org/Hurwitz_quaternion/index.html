Hurwitz quaternion

The Hurwitz quaternions form a lattice in quaternionic space

In mathematics, a Hurwitz quaternion (or Hurwitz integer) is a quaternion whose components are either all integers or all half-integers (a mixture of integers and half-integers is not allowed). The set of all Hurwitz quaternions is denoted H and forms a ring under quaternion addition and multiplication. Hurwitz quaternions were introduced by Adolf Hurwitz in 1919.

Definition

A quaternion q = a + bi + cj + dk, with a, b, c, d real, is a Hurwitz quaternion if a, b, c, d are either all integers or all half-integers (i.e., each is an integer plus 1/2). The Hurwitz quaternions are closed under addition and multiplication, forming a subring of the quaternions that contains the Lipschitz quaternions (those with integer components) as a subring of index 2.

Properties

The norm of a Hurwitz quaternion, given by a² + b² + c² + d², is always an integer, and the ring H is a Euclidean domain with respect to this norm: for any Hurwitz quaternions α and β ≠ 0 there is a Hurwitz quaternion q with |α − qβ| < |β|. Consequently H is a (non-commutative) principal ideal domain on both sides and possesses a theory of prime factorisation, though factorisation into irreducibles need not be unique in the naive sense.

There are 24 Hurwitz quaternions of unit norm: the 8 Lipschitz units ±1, ±i, ±j, ±k and the 16 quaternions (±1 ± i ± j ± k)/2. As points in four-dimensional space the Hurwitz quaternions form the D4 lattice, one of the most symmetric lattices known; the 24 units are the vertices of a 24-cell when scaled appropriately.

Applications

Hurwitz quaternions are used in the proof of Lagrange's four-square theorem, which states that every natural number is the sum of four integer squares; the proof proceeds by establishing the result for Hurwitz prime quaternions. They also appear in the study of the (2,3,7) triangle group and Hurwitz surfaces in geometry.
