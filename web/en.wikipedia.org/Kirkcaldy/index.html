Kirkcaldy is a town and former royal burgh in Fife on the east coast of Scotland, stretching for several miles along the Firth of Forth — hence its nickname "the Lang Toun" (the long town). It is the largest settlement in Fife and is best known as the birthplace of the economist Adam Smith and as a historic centre of linoleum manufacture.

History

Kirkcaldy’s name derives from the Gaelic for "fort on the hard hill". Granted royal burgh status by Charles I in 1644, it grew as a port trading in salt, coal and textiles. In the nineteenth century it became world-famous for floorcloth and linoleum — Michael Nairn’s factories covered floors across the globe — and linoleum production, though much reduced, continues in the town today.

Notable people and culture

Adam Smith was born in Kirkcaldy in 1723 and wrote much of The Theory of Moral Sentiments there; the architect Robert Adam also had local connections, and the town was the birthplace of Prime Minister Gordon Brown. The annual Links Market, a street fair dating to 1304, is one of Europe’s longest-running funfairs, and the town’s ice hockey team, the Fife Flyers, is the oldest in Britain.
