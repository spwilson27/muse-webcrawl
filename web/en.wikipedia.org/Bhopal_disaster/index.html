Bhopal disaster
Date: 2–3 December 1984
Location: Bhopal, Madhya Pradesh, India
Cause: Methyl isocyanate gas leak
Deaths: at least 3,787 (official); estimates higher

The Bhopal disaster, also referred to as the Bhopal gas tragedy, was a chemical accident on the night of 2–3 December 1984 at the Union Carbide India Limited (UCIL) pesticide plant in Bhopal, Madhya Pradesh, India. Considered the world's worst industrial disaster, it exposed over 500,000 people to the highly toxic gas methyl isocyanate (MIC).

The leak

Water entered a storage tank containing about 42 tonnes of methyl isocyanate, triggering an exothermic reaction that raised the temperature and pressure inside the tank. The resulting gas cloud spread over the densely populated settlements surrounding the plant in the early hours of the morning. Safety systems, including the refrigeration unit, gas scrubber, and flare tower, were inadequate, switched off, or under maintenance.

Aftermath

The official immediate death toll was 2,259, with later official figures putting gas-related deaths at 3,787; other estimates suggest several thousand more died in the following weeks and years, and more than half a million people suffered injuries, many of them permanent. The health effects included severe lung damage, reproductive disorders, and immune system disorders.

Legal proceedings

In 1989 Union Carbide agreed to pay US$470 million in settlement to the Government of India. Criminal proceedings followed; in 2010, eight former UCIL officials were convicted of causing death by negligence and sentenced to two years' imprisonment, a verdict widely criticised as inadequate. The plant site remained contaminated for decades.
