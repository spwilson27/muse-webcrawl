Professional

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Modern professionals at work

A professional is a member of a profession or any person who works in a specified professional activity. The term also describes the standards of education and training that prepare members of the profession with the particular knowledge and skills necessary to perform their specific role within that profession. In addition, most professionals are subject to strict codes of conduct, enshrining rigorous ethical and moral obligations. Professional standards of practice and ethics for a particular field are typically agreed upon and maintained through widely recognised professional associations, such as the IEEE.

Definitions

Some definitions characterise a professional narrowly as a person holding a university degree and membership in a regulated occupation — the traditional learned professions of medicine, law, engineering, architecture, accounting and the clergy. Other definitions are broader and encompass any worker whose role requires specialised knowledge, autonomy and adherence to standards, as opposed to an "amateur" who pursues an activity without pay or formal training. In sport, the contrast is explicit: a professional athlete is paid to compete, while an amateur is not.

Professionalism

Professionalism describes the conduct, aims and qualities expected of a professional: competence, accountability, integrity, respect for clients and colleagues, and commitment to continuing development. Sociologists have studied how occupations pursue "professionalisation" — acquiring credentialing bodies, codes of ethics, monopolies over practice through licensing, and social status.
