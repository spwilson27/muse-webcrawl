Podcasts are episodic audio programmes distributed over the internet via RSS.

A podcast is a programme made available digitally for download or streaming as a series of episodes, usually audio, to which listeners can subscribe. The name is a portmanteau of "iPod" and "broadcast", coined by journalist Ben Hammersley in 2004. Technically, podcasts are distributed through RSS feeds containing media enclosures, a mechanism developed by Dave Winer and Adam Curry around 2000–2004, allowing automatic delivery of new episodes to subscribers.

History

Early podcasting grew with the iPod and broadband internet; Apple added podcasts to iTunes in 2005, exposing the medium to a mass audience. After a period as a niche hobbyist medium, podcasting surged in the 2010s with smartphones and hit programmes such as Serial (2014), whose first season was downloaded hundreds of millions of times and demonstrated podcasting's narrative power. Major media companies and platforms invested heavily; Spotify spent hundreds of millions acquiring studios and exclusive shows, including The Joe Rogan Experience, and video podcasts on YouTube became a dominant format in the 2020s.

Formats and genres

Common formats include interviews, solo commentary, co-hosted conversation, panel shows, narrative non-fiction and audio drama. Genres span comedy, true crime, news, education, sports and business. Production ranges from bedroom recordings to studio productions; monetisation comes from advertising (often dynamically inserted), subscriptions, crowdfunding and live events.

Audience

By the mid-2020s, surveys estimated hundreds of millions of regular listeners worldwide and several million active podcast feeds, with monthly podcast consumption becoming a majority habit among younger adults in the United States. Podcasting is noted for low production barriers and for sustaining niche topics uneconomical for broadcast radio.
