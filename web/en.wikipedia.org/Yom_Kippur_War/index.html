The Yom Kippur War, also known as the Ramadan War or October War, was fought from 6 to 25 October 1973 between Israel and a coalition led by Egypt and Syria. Egypt and Syria launched a surprise attack on Yom Kippur, the holiest day in Judaism (which that year also fell during Ramadan), to recover territory Israel had captured in the 1967 Six-Day War: the Sinai Peninsula and the Golan Heights.

Course of the war

Egyptian forces crossed the Suez Canal and overran the Bar Lev Line, while Syrian forces attacked across the Golan Heights, initially making deep gains. Israeli counterattacks in the Golan drove the Syrians back and advanced toward Damascus. In the Sinai, after an initial Egyptian success and a failed Israeli counterattack, Israeli forces under Ariel Sharon crossed the canal to its west bank and encircled the Egyptian Third Army.

Both superpowers intervened logistically — a massive US airlift to Israel and Soviet resupply of Egypt and Syria — and a late-war confrontation, including a worldwide US military alert (DEFCON 3), raised fears of escalation before a UN ceasefire took hold.

Consequences

Although Israel recovered militarily and ended the war holding additional territory, the initial Arab success shattered Israel's sense of invincibility, led to the fall of Golda Meir's government after the Agranat Commission's inquiry, and restored Egyptian confidence. The war triggered the 1973 oil crisis, when Arab producers embargoed oil exports to states supporting Israel. Diplomatically it set the stage for shuttle diplomacy, the Sinai disengagement agreements, and ultimately the Camp David Accords and the 1979 Egypt–Israel peace treaty — the first between Israel and an Arab state.
