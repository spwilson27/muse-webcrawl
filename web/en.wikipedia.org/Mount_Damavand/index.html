Mount Damavand

Mount Damavand (Persian: دماوند)

Elevation: 5,609.2 m (18,403 ft)
Prominence: 4,667 m (12th most prominent in the world)
Location: Mazandaran Province, Iran
Range: Alborz
Type: Stratovolcano (potentially active)

Mount Damavand (Persian: دماوند [dæmɒːˈvænd]) is a potentially active stratovolcano in the Alborz mountain range in northern Iran. At 5,609 metres (18,403 ft), it is the highest peak in Iran and Western Asia and the highest volcano in Asia. It lies near the southern coast of the Caspian Sea, in Amol County, Mazandaran Province, about 66 km northeast of Tehran.

Geology

Damavand is a large stratovolcano built of lava flows and pyroclastic deposits over roughly the last 1.8 million years, with its main cone formed in the last 600,000 years. Although it has no recorded historical eruptions, fumaroles near the summit crater emit sulfur vapour, and hot springs on its flanks indicate a still-hot magmatic system; the most recent activity has been dated to about 7,300 years ago.

Mythology and symbolism

Damavand holds a special place in Persian mythology and folklore. In Ferdowsi's Shahnameh, the tyrant Zahhak is chained in a cave on the mountain, to remain there until the end of the world; the hero Fereydun is associated with its slopes, and the archer Arash is said to have thrown an arrow from Damavand to mark Iran's border. The mountain is a national symbol of Iran, appearing on banknotes and in the national epic tradition, and its image is associated with resistance and Iranian identity.

Climbing

The most popular ascent route is the southern face from Polur and the mountain hut at Bargah Sevom (about 4,250 m). Climbing is usually done in summer; hazards include altitude sickness and sulfur fumes near the summit. Frozen waterfalls on its slopes are used for ice climbing in winter.
