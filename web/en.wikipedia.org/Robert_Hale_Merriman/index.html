Robert Hale Merriman (17 November 1908 – 2 April 1938) was an American doctoral student in economics at the University of California, Berkeley, who became the best-known American commander in the International Brigades during the Spanish Civil War. He commanded the Abraham Lincoln Battalion and later served as chief of staff of the XV International Brigade.

Life and service in Spain

Merriman had spent two years in Moscow in the mid-1930s studying Soviet agriculture. In January 1937 he travelled to Spain to fight for the Republic, arriving with the first American volunteers. He led the Lincoln Battalion in its first action at the Battle of Jarama in February 1937, where he was seriously wounded, and later commanded at the Battle of Brunete and in the Aragón offensive.

Death and legacy

During the disastrous retreats of spring 1938, Merriman was killed on 2 April 1938 near Gandesa, either in action or after capture by Nationalist forces; the exact circumstances remain uncertain, and his body was never recovered. He is widely believed to be one of the models—sometimes identified as the principal model—for Robert Jordan, the hero of Ernest Hemingway's novel For Whom the Bell Tolls (1940), whose author knew Merriman in Spain. His widow Marion's memoir American Commander in Spain (1986) remains a key source on his life.
