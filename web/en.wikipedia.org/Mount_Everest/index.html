Mount Everest

Mount Everest, known in Nepali as Sagarmāthā and in Tibetan as Chomolungma, is Earth's highest mountain above sea level, located in the Mahalangur Himal sub-range of the Himalayas. Its summit, on the border between Nepal and the Tibet Autonomous Region of China, rises 8,848.86 metres (29,031.7 ft) according to the 2020 survey jointly announced by Nepal and China. The mountain was named in English for Sir George Everest, a former Surveyor General of India.

Measurement and naming

The Great Trigonometrical Survey of India identified the peak (then "Peak XV") as the world's highest in 1856, computing 29,002 ft. The 2020 Nepal–China figure of 8,848.86 m superseded earlier snow-height measurements of 8,848 m (Nepal) and rock-height 8,844.43 m (China, 2005). The mountain grows a few millimetres per year as the Indian Plate pushes into Asia.

Climbing history

British expeditions of the 1920s pioneered attempts from Tibet; George Mallory and Andrew Irvine disappeared near the summit in 1924, and whether they reached it remains one of mountaineering's great mysteries. On 29 May 1953, Edmund Hillary of New Zealand and Sherpa Tenzing Norgay made the first confirmed ascent via the South Col. Reinhold Messner and Peter Habeler made the first ascent without supplemental oxygen in 1978, and Messner the first solo ascent in 1980.

Routes and dangers

The two standard routes are the Southeast Ridge from Nepal and the North Ridge from Tibet. Hazards include altitude sickness in the "death zone" above 8,000 m, the Khumbu Icefall, avalanches, extreme cold, and crowding in peak season. More than 300 climbers have died on the mountain, and over 6,000 summits had been recorded by the early 2020s.

Environment

The mountain lies within Sagarmatha National Park (Nepal), a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Decades of expeditions left tonnes of waste, prompting clean-up campaigns and rules requiring climbers to carry down their refuse.
