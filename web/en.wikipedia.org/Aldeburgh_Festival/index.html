The Aldeburgh Festival of Music and the Arts is an English arts festival devoted mainly to classical music, held annually in and around Aldeburgh, Suffolk. It was founded in 1948 by the composer Benjamin Britten, the tenor Peter Pears, and the librettist/producer Eric Crozier.

History

The first festival took place in June 1948 in modest local venues. Britten's operas were central from the start: Albert Herring featured early on, and later premieres at the festival included A Midsummer Night's Dream (1960). In 1967 the festival gained a permanent home with the opening of the Snape Maltings Concert Hall, converted from a Victorian maltings.

Today

Now run by Britten Pears Arts, the festival remains known for premieres, artist residencies, and its connection to the Britten-Pears Young Artist Programme and the Britten–Pears Foundation.
