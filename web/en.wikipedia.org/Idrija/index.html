Idrija

Country Slovenia
Population about 6,000
Known for Mercury mining, Idrija lace

Idrija is a town in western Slovenia, in the traditional region of the Slovenian Littoral. It is the seat of the Municipality of Idrija and is best known for its historic mercury mine, for centuries one of the largest in the world.

Mercury mine

Mercury was discovered at Idrija around 1490, and mining continued for roughly 500 years until the mine's closure in the 1990s. Together with Almadén in Spain, Idrija's heritage of mercury is inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List (2012). The Anthony's Shaft entrance and other mining structures are preserved as a museum.

Culture

Idrija is also famed for Idrija lace, a traditional bobbin lace, and for žlikrofi, a filled pasta dish recognised as a Slovenian culinary speciality.
