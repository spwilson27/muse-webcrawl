Bidirectional reflectance distribution function

The bidirectional reflectance distribution function (BRDF), symbol fr(ωi, ωr), is a function of four real variables that defines how light is reflected at an opaque surface. It is used in the optics of real-world light, in computer graphics algorithms, and in computer vision algorithms.

Definition

The BRDF is defined as the ratio of reflected radiance in a given outgoing direction to the incident irradiance from a given incoming direction. Formally, fr = dLr / (Li cos θi dωi), with units of inverse steradians. It was first defined in this form by Fred Nicodemus around 1965.

Properties

A physically plausible BRDF satisfies Helmholtz reciprocity — swapping the incident and reflected directions gives the same value — and conserves energy, so that a surface never reflects more light than it receives. Functions that also model transmission are called BSDFs; those limited to reflection and refraction are BTDFs and BRDFs respectively.

Models and applications

Empirical and analytic models include the Lambertian model for ideal diffuse surfaces, the Phong and Blinn–Phong models for glossy highlights, and microfacet models such as Cook–Torrance. Measured BRDFs, such as those in the MERL database of one hundred materials, are used in photorealistic rendering, remote sensing, and material appearance research.
