René Lévesque
Born 24 August 1922, Campbellton, New Brunswick
Died 1 November 1987, Montreal, Quebec
Premier of Quebec, 1976–1985
Founder of the Parti Québécois

René Lévesque (August 24, 1922 – November 1, 1987) was a Canadian politician and journalist who served as the 23rd premier of Quebec from 1976 to 1985. He was the founder of the Parti Québécois and the first Quebec political leader of a recognised party to seek, through a referendum, a mandate to negotiate the political independence of Quebec.

Early career

Born in Campbellton, New Brunswick, and raised in New Carlisle in the Gaspé, Lévesque became a well-known Radio-Canada journalist and war correspondent. Entering politics with Jean Lesage's Liberals in 1960, he was a key figure of the Quiet Revolution as Minister of Natural Resources, driving the nationalisation of Quebec's electricity companies under the slogan "Maîtres chez nous" ("masters in our own house").

Sovereignty movement

Breaking with the Liberals over their refusal to consider independence, Lévesque founded the Mouvement Souveraineté-Association in 1967 and the Parti Québécois in 1968, advocating "sovereignty-association" — political independence combined with an economic association with Canada. The PQ won the 1976 provincial election, and his government enacted the Charter of the French Language (Bill 101) and major reforms in election financing and labour law. In the 1980 Quebec referendum, his proposal to negotiate sovereignty-association was defeated, with 59.6% voting No. Re-elected in 1981, he led Quebec through the constitutional negotiations of 1981–82, which he denounced as the "Night of the Long Knives". Lévesque resigned in 1985 and died in Montreal in 1987.

Legacy

Lévesque is remembered as the father of the modern Quebec sovereignty movement and one of the most influential Canadian politicians of the 20th century. Boulevard René-Lévesque in Montreal and the René-Lévesque generating station are named for him.
