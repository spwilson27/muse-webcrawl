Alessandro Volta

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Alessandro Giuseppe Antonio Anastasio Volta (1745–1827)

Alessandro Giuseppe Antonio Anastasio Volta (18 February 1745 – 5 March 1827) was an Italian physicist and chemist who was a pioneer of electricity and power and is credited as the inventor of the electric battery and the discoverer of methane. He invented the voltaic pile in 1799, and reported the results of his experiments in 1800 in a two-part letter to the President of the Royal Society.

With this invention Volta proved that electricity could be generated chemically and debunked the prevalent theory that electricity was generated solely by living beings. Volta's invention sparked a great amount of scientific excitement and led others to conduct similar experiments, which eventually led to the development of the field of electrochemistry.

Early life

Volta was born in Como, a town in present-day northern Italy, on 18 February 1745. In 1794, Volta married Teresa Peregrini, with whom he raised three sons. His father, Filippo Volta, was of noble lineage, and his mother, Donna Maddalena, came from the family of the Inzaghis.

Work with electricity

During his early career Volta studied the chemistry of gases and discovered methane in 1776 after reading a paper by Benjamin Franklin on "flammable air". In November 1776 he found methane at Lake Maggiore, and by 1778 he managed to isolate it.

In 1779 he became a professor of experimental physics at the University of Pavia, a chair he occupied for almost 40 years. His work on the electrophorus, a device that produced static electric charge, made him famous across Europe.

The voltaic pile

Volta's most famous invention, announced in 1800, was the voltaic pile — the first electric battery. It consisted of alternating discs of zinc and copper separated by pieces of cardboard soaked in brine, and produced a steady electric current. Napoleon Bonaparte recognised Volta for his invention, making him a count and a senator of the Kingdom of Italy.

Legacy

The SI unit of electric potential, the volt, is named in his honour, as decided at the International Electrical Congress of 1881. Volta retired in 1819 and died in Como on 5 March 1827. His image appeared on the Italian 10,000 lire banknote.
