Tau Ceti

Tau Ceti, Latinized from τ Ceti, is a single star in the constellation Cetus that is spectrally similar to the Sun, although it has only about 78% of the Sun's mass and is somewhat less luminous. At a distance of just under 12 light-years from the Solar System, it is a relatively nearby star and the closest solitary G-class star. The star appears stable, with little stellar variation, and is relatively low in metals.

Planetary system

Radial velocity studies have indicated the presence of planets orbiting Tau Ceti. In 2012 five candidate planets were proposed, and later analyses refined the system to four confirmed or strongly indicated planets, designated b, e, f, and g, plus additional candidates. Tau Ceti e and f lie near or within the star's habitable zone, making them targets of interest in the search for potentially habitable exoplanets. Observations also show a substantial debris disk around the star, containing roughly ten times as much dust as the Solar System's Kuiper belt.

Search for life

Because of its proximity, Sun-like character, and stability, Tau Ceti was one of the two original targets of Project Ozma, Frank Drake's pioneering 1960 SETI radio search for extraterrestrial intelligence. It has remained a favored target for subsequent SETI observations and for proposed interstellar probes.

In fiction

Tau Ceti has been a popular setting in science fiction, appearing in works such as Ursula K. Le Guin's The Dispossessed, Isaac Asimov's Robots and Empire, and Andy Weir's Project Hail Mary.
