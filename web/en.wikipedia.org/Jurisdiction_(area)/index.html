Jurisdiction (area)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A jurisdiction is an area with a set of laws under the authority of a particular government, court, or other legal body. In this geographic sense, a jurisdiction is the territory over which legal authority extends: a country, a state or province, a county, a municipality, or a court's district may each constitute a jurisdiction for particular purposes.

Concept

The term derives from the Latin jurisdictio, "administration of justice". A jurisdiction as an area defines the spatial reach of laws and of the officials empowered to enforce them. Crossing a jurisdictional boundary can change which criminal code applies, which courts are competent, which taxes are levied, and which administrative rules govern an activity. Federal systems such as the United States, Germany, and Australia divide territory among overlapping federal, state, and local jurisdictions, each with its own competences.

Types of jurisdictional areas

Common jurisdictional areas include national territories, subnational units (states, provinces, cantons, prefectures), judicial districts, police districts, electoral districts, customs territories, and special zones such as free-trade zones or tribal lands. In international law, a state's jurisdiction is normally presumed to extend throughout its territory, including its internal waters and territorial sea, subject to rules on extraterritorial application and immunities. Maritime zones defined by the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea — territorial sea, contiguous zone, and exclusive economic zone — give coastal states graduated bundles of jurisdictional rights.

Overlaps and conflicts

Because different bodies may hold jurisdiction over the same area for different subjects, overlapping jurisdictions are routine: a city may lie within a county, a state, and a federal judicial district simultaneously. Conflicts between jurisdictions are resolved through choice-of-law rules, constitutional allocations of power, and doctrines such as preemption. Some areas, such as embassies, military bases abroad, and Indigenous reservations, have special jurisdictional arrangements that limit or share the host state's authority.
