Renal medulla

Renal medulla
Part of: Kidney
System: Urinary system
Artery: Interlobar arteries
Vein: Arcuate vein

The renal medulla is the innermost part of the kidney. It is divided into several sections, known as the renal pyramids, which are cone-shaped tissues that empty urine into the renal pelvis. The medulla contains the structures of the nephrons responsible for maintaining the salt and water balance of the blood.

Structure

The renal medulla consists of microscopic structures called the medullary collecting ducts, the loops of Henle, and the vasa recta. It is split into the outer medulla and the inner medulla. The blood supply of the medulla is derived from the vasa recta, capillary networks arising from the efferent arterioles of juxtamedullary glomeruli.

Function

The medulla regulates the concentration of urine through the countercurrent multiplication system. Its hyperosmotic interstitium, built up by the loops of Henle, allows water to be reabsorbed from the collecting ducts under the influence of antidiuretic hormone (ADH).

Clinical significance

Conditions affecting the medulla include medullary sponge kidney and renal papillary necrosis, the latter often associated with diabetes mellitus, sickle cell disease, and analgesic overuse.
