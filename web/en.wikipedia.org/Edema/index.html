Edema

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Edema (American English), also spelt oedema (British English), and also known as fluid retention, dropsy, hydropsy and swelling, is the build-up of fluid in the body's tissue. Most commonly, the legs or arms are affected. Symptoms may include skin that feels tight, the area feeling heavy, and joint stiffness that makes the affected area harder to move. Other symptoms depend on the underlying cause.

Causes

Causes may include venous insufficiency, heart failure, kidney problems, low protein levels, liver problems (including cirrhosis), deep vein thrombosis, infections, angioedema, certain medications (such as calcium channel blockers and NSAIDs), and lymphedema. It may also occur after prolonged sitting or standing, during menstruation, or during pregnancy. In "pitting edema", pressing on the swollen area leaves a temporary indentation; "non-pitting edema" occurs in conditions such as lymphedema and myxedema.

Mechanism

Six factors can contribute to the formation of edema: increased hydrostatic pressure in the capillaries; reduced plasma oncotic pressure (e.g., low albumin in kidney or liver disease or malnutrition); increased capillary permeability (as in inflammation); obstruction of fluid return via the lymphatic system; changes in the water-retaining properties of the tissues themselves; and increased sodium and water retention by the kidneys. These follow from Starling's principle governing fluid exchange across capillary walls.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the underlying cause and may include salt restriction, diuretics (such as furosemide), elevation of the affected limb, and compression stockings for chronic venous insufficiency. Pulmonary edema — fluid in the lungs, usually due to heart failure — is a medical emergency; cerebral edema, fluid accumulation in the brain, can likewise be life-threatening.

Specific types

  • Peripheral edema — swelling of the legs and arms.
  • Pulmonary edema — fluid in the lungs.
  • Cerebral edema — swelling of the brain.
  • Macular edema — swelling of the macula of the eye, often a complication of diabetes.
  • Lymphedema — swelling from lymphatic obstruction.
  • Angioedema — rapid swelling of the deeper layers of the skin, sometimes involving the airway.
