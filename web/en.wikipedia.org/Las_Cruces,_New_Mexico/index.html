Las Cruces, New Mexico

Las Cruces (Spanish: "the crosses") is the second-most populous city in the U.S. state of New Mexico and the county seat of Doña Ana County. As of the 2020 census, the city's population was about 111,000, making it the largest city in southern New Mexico. It lies in the Mesilla Valley along the Rio Grande, at the foot of the Organ Mountains, roughly 45 miles northwest of El Paso, Texas.

History

The area was inhabited by Mogollon and later Manso peoples and lay along El Camino Real de Tierra Adentro. According to tradition, the name derives from crosses marking the graves of travelers killed in an Apache attack around 1830. The town was formally laid out in 1849 after the Mexican–American War brought the region into the United States, and it grew rapidly after the arrival of the Atchison, Topeka and Santa Fe Railway in 1881, overtaking the neighboring village of Mesilla, once the county seat.

Economy and institutions

Las Cruces is home to New Mexico State University (founded 1888) and is closely tied to White Sands Missile Range and nearby Spaceport America, from which Virgin Galactic has flown crewed suborbital flights. Agriculture—especially pecans and chile peppers—remains important in the surrounding valley, and the city hosts the annual Whole Enchilada Fiesta and a large farmers' market.
