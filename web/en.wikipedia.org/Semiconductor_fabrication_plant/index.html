Semiconductor fabrication plant

A semiconductor fabrication plant, commonly called a fab, is a factory in which integrated circuits and other semiconductor devices are manufactured on silicon wafers. Fabs are among the most expensive industrial facilities in the world: a leading-edge fab can cost more than US$10–20 billion to build and equip.

Manufacturing environment

Production takes place in cleanrooms where airborne particle counts are held extremely low — often class 1 or better — because a single particle can ruin a nanoscale circuit. Workers wear full cleanroom suits ("bunny suits"). Wafers, usually 300 mm in diameter, pass through hundreds of process steps: photolithography patterning with EUV or DUV light, etching, deposition, ion implantation, chemical-mechanical polishing and repeated metrology, over a cycle time of several months.

Industry structure

Some companies (integrated device manufacturers such as Intel and Samsung) design and fabricate their own chips, while pure-play foundries such as TSMC manufacture chips designed by fabless firms. The extreme cost of new fabs and the concentration of advanced capacity in East Asia have made fabs a focus of industrial policy, including the US CHIPS and Science Act of 2022 and similar programmes in Europe, Japan and India.
