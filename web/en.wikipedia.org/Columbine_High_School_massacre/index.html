Columbine High School massacre

The Columbine High School massacre was a school shooting and attempted bombing that occurred on April 20, 1999, at Columbine High School in Columbine, Colorado, United States. Two senior students, Eric Harris and Dylan Klebold, murdered 13 people — 12 students and one teacher — and wounded 24 others before taking their own lives.

Attack

Harris and Klebold had planted propane bombs in the school cafeteria intended to cause mass casualties; when the bombs failed to detonate as planned, they began shooting students outside the school and then inside, moving through classrooms and the library, where most of the victims were killed. The attack lasted just under an hour. Police response, following the containment protocols of the time, was later criticised as too slow, prompting a national shift toward rapid-deployment tactics for active-shooter incidents.

Aftermath and legacy

Columbine became a defining event in debates over gun control, school security, bullying and media violence, and the word "Columbine" became synonymous with school shootings in the United States. The event influenced popular culture and, tragically, inspired a number of subsequent attackers. The school library where most victims died was demolished and replaced with a new library and memorial.
