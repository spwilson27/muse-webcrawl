Slide guitar

Slide guitar is a technique for playing the guitar in which the player presses a hard, smooth object — the slide — against the strings instead of fretting them with the fingers, producing a smooth glissando between notes and a characteristic vocal, wailing tone.

Technique and instruments

Slides are most often made of glass (classically the neck of a bottle, hence “bottleneck guitar”), metal, or ceramic, and are worn on the ring or little finger so other fingers remain free. Slide playing favours open tunings such as open G and open D, which allow full chords with the slide laid across the strings. The technique is used upright, in the conventional guitar position, and horizontally in lap steel and pedal steel styles.

History

The style developed in the American South in the early twentieth century, influenced by Hawaiian steel guitar and by African-American blues musicians who imitated the human voice and single-string instruments. Delta blues players such as Son House and Robert Johnson made the bottleneck a signature of the genre. Later masters include Muddy Waters, Elmore James, Duane Allman, Ry Cooder, Bonnie Raitt, and Derek Trucks. Slide guitar is central to blues, country, rock, and world styles such as Hindustani slide guitar.
