Superbike racing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Superbike racing is a category of motorcycle racing that employs highly modified production motorcycles, as opposed to the purpose-built prototypes used in Grand Prix motorcycle racing. The premier championship is the Superbike World Championship (WorldSBK), sanctioned by the FIM and first held in 1988.

Motorcycles

Superbikes are derived from standard production models available to the public. WorldSBK rules have traditionally allowed 1000 cc four-cylinder, 1200 cc twin-cylinder, and intermediate capacities for three-cylinder machines, with strict limits on modification: the frame and basic engine architecture must remain as homologated, though suspension, brakes, wheels, electronics, and many internal parts may be changed. This keeps the racing machines visually and mechanically close to showroom models.

Championships

Alongside the world championship, major national series include the British Superbike Championship, MotoAmerica Superbike in the United States, and series in Australia, Japan, and other countries. Successful WorldSBK riders include Carl Fogarty, Troy Bayliss, and Jonathan Rea, the most successful rider in the championship's history, with six consecutive titles from 2015 to 2020 for Kawasaki.

Format

A modern WorldSBK weekend features practice sessions, a Superpole qualifying session, and three races: two full-distance races and a short Superpole Race that also helps set the grid. Points are awarded to the top fifteen finishers, with reduced points in the sprint race.
