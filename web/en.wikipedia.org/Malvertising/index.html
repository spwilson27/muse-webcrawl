Malvertising

Malvertising (a portmanteau of "malicious advertising") is the use of online advertising networks to spread malware. Attackers submit advertisements — often through legitimate ad networks and exchanges — that contain malicious code or redirect viewers to malicious pages, so that ads appearing on reputable websites deliver exploits to visitors' browsers and plug-ins.

Methods

Malvertising campaigns may embed exploit kits that probe for vulnerabilities in browsers, Flash, Java or PDF readers, achieving drive-by downloads without any click. Others use deceptive creatives: fake software-update prompts, bogus virus warnings and tech-support scams. Attackers commonly pass initial vetting with clean ads and swap in malicious payloads later, target by geography and time, and fingerprint visitors to evade researchers.

Impact and defences

Major malvertising incidents have affected large news and entertainment sites, whose publishers do not directly control third-party ads served to their pages. Defences include patching, ad and script blocking, sandboxing of ad iframes, network-side scanning of creatives, and industry vetting standards. Malvertising is difficult to combat because the advertising ecosystem involves many intermediaries between advertiser and publisher.
