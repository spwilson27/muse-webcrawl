Derivative (finance)

In finance, a derivative is a contract that derives its value from the performance of an underlying entity such as an asset, index or interest rate.

Types

The main categories are futures, forwards, options and swaps. Derivatives are used for hedging risk, speculation and gaining exposure to assets without owning them.

Markets and risks

They trade on exchanges or over-the-counter. Because they involve leverage and counterparty risk, derivatives played a central role in the 2008 financial crisis, prompting reforms such as mandatory central clearing.
