Council of the European Union

Seat: Brussels, Belgium

Members: Ministers of the 27 EU member states

Presidency: Rotates among member states every six months

The Council of the European Union, often referred to in the treaties and other official documents as simply the Council, and informally known as the Council of Ministers, is the third of the seven institutions of the European Union. Together with the European Parliament, it is the EU's legislative body, and it also carries out policymaking and coordinating functions.

Composition and configurations

The Council consists of one ministerial-level representative from each member state, but it meets in ten different configurations depending on the subject — for example foreign affairs, economic and financial affairs (ECOFIN), agriculture, and environment. The Foreign Affairs Council is chaired by the High Representative of the Union for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy; the other configurations are chaired by the minister of the member state holding the rotating presidency.

Functions

The Council adopts EU legislation, usually jointly with the European Parliament; coordinates member states' policies; concludes international agreements; adopts the EU budget together with Parliament; and develops the common foreign and security policy. Most decisions are taken by qualified majority voting, weighted by member-state population, though unanimity is still required in areas such as taxation and foreign policy. Preparatory work is done by COREPER, the committee of member states' permanent representatives.
