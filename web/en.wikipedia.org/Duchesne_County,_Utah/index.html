Duchesne County is a county in the U.S. state of Utah. Its county seat is Duchesne, and its largest city is Roosevelt. The county lies in the Uinta Basin of northeastern Utah.

History

The county was created in 1915 from part of Wasatch County. It takes its name from the Duchesne River, which runs through the region; the river's name is of uncertain origin but is associated with early French trappers and traders. Much of the county's land lies within or near the Uintah and Ouray Indian Reservation, home of the Ute Indian Tribe.

Geography

Duchesne County covers about 3,256 square miles in the Uinta Basin, between the Uinta Mountains to the north and the Tavaputs Plateau to the south. The Duchesne River and the Strawberry River drain the county, and Starvation Reservoir, a popular recreation area, lies near the county seat. Kings Peak, Utah's highest point, is in the neighbouring county to the north.

Economy

The economy is based on agriculture, livestock, energy production (oil and natural gas from the Uinta Basin fields), and recreation. U.S. Route 40 crosses the county east–west, linking it with Vernal and the Wasatch Front.
