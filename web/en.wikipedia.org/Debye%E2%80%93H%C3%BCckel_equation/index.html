Debye–Hückel equation

Ionic atmosphere model underlying the Debye–Hückel theory

The Debye–Hückel equation provides a means to calculate activity coefficients of ions in dilute electrolyte solutions. It was proposed by Peter Debye and Erich Hückel in 1923, based on a model in which each ion is surrounded by an "ionic atmosphere" of oppositely charged ions.

Equation

In its limiting-law form, log10 γ± = −A |z+z−| √I, where γ± is the mean ionic activity coefficient, I is the ionic strength, z are the ionic charges, and A is a solvent- and temperature-dependent constant (about 0.509 for water at 25 °C).

Extensions

The extended Debye–Hückel equation adds an ion-size parameter and works at higher concentrations; later approaches such as the Davies and Pitzer equations extend the range further. The theory underlies much of modern electrolyte thermodynamics and electrochemistry.
