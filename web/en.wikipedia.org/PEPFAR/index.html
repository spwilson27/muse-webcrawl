The United States President's Emergency Plan for AIDS Relief (PEPFAR) is a United States governmental initiative to address the global HIV/AIDS epidemic, primarily in countries with high infection rates. Launched by President George W. Bush in 2003, it is the largest commitment by any nation to address a single disease in history.

The programme was first authorised by the United States Leadership Against HIV/AIDS, Tuberculosis, and Malaria Act of 2003 with an initial commitment of US$15 billion over five years, and it has been reauthorised several times since, including by the PEPFAR Extension Act of 2018. Cumulative U.S. investment through the programme exceeds US$100 billion.

Activities

PEPFAR works in partnership with host governments, multilateral organisations such as the Global Fund to Fight AIDS, Tuberculosis and Malaria, and non-governmental and faith-based organisations. It supports antiretroviral treatment, HIV testing and counselling, prevention of mother-to-child transmission, voluntary medical male circumcision, pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP), laboratory systems and health workforce training, and care for orphans and vulnerable children.

The U.S. government credits PEPFAR with saving more than 25 million lives and enabling millions of babies to be born HIV-free. By the early 2020s it was supporting antiretroviral treatment for over 20 million people worldwide. In its later phases, the programme's stated strategy shifted toward achieving epidemic control and the UNAIDS 95-95-95 targets, and toward sustaining host-country ownership of HIV responses. A short-term reauthorization in 2024 extended the programme amid debate over its future funding.
