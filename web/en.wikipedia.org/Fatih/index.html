The historic peninsula of Fatih, Istanbul

Fatih (Turkish pronunciation: [ˈfaːtih]) is a municipality and district of Istanbul Province, Turkey. It occupies the historic peninsula of the city — the area enclosed by the Theodosian Walls and bounded by the Golden Horn and the Sea of Marmara — which corresponds to old Constantinople. The district takes its name from Sultan Mehmed II, known as Mehmed the Conqueror (Fatih Sultan Mehmed), who captured Constantinople in 1453.

Landmarks

Fatih contains many of Istanbul's most famous monuments, including the Hagia Sophia, the Topkapı Palace, the Blue Mosque (Sultan Ahmed Mosque), the Grand Bazaar, the Süleymaniye Mosque, the Basilica Cistern, and the Hippodrome of Constantinople. The Fatih Mosque complex, built on the site of the Byzantine Church of the Holy Apostles between 1463 and 1470, houses the tomb of Mehmed II. The district also includes the neighbourhoods of Eminönü, Aksakal, Balat, and Fener, the historic seat of the Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople.

History

After the conquest of 1453, Mehmed II repopulated and rebuilt the ruined city, founding mosques, markets, and the Fatih complex of schools, hospitals, and baths. For centuries Fatih was the administrative and religious heart of the Ottoman Empire. The modern district was formed by the merger of the former districts of Fatih and Eminönü in 2009. Today it is a densely populated, comparatively conservative district and the centre of Istanbul's heritage tourism; its historic areas were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1985.
