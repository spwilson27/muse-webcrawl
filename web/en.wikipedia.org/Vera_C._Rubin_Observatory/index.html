Vera C. Rubin Observatory

The Vera C. Rubin Observatory, formerly known as the Large Synoptic Survey Telescope (LSST), is an astronomical observatory on the Cerro Pachón ridge in north-central Chile. Its main instrument, the Simonyi Survey Telescope, is a wide-field reflecting telescope with an 8.4-metre primary mirror that images the entire available sky every few nights. The observatory's principal mission is the ten-year Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST), designed to study dark matter, dark energy, the structure of the Milky Way, transient events, and potentially hazardous asteroids.

Design

The telescope uses a novel three-mirror design giving an exceptionally wide 3.5-degree field of view. Its camera, the largest digital camera ever built for astronomy at 3,200 megapixels, takes a 15-second exposure every 20 seconds, generating roughly 20 terabytes of data per night. Over the ten-year survey the observatory is expected to observe each part of the southern sky hundreds of times and catalogue tens of billions of stars and galaxies.

History

The project grew out of proposals in the 1990s and was ranked as the top ground-based priority in the 2010 United States decadal survey of astronomy. Construction began in 2015 with funding from the National Science Foundation, the Department of Energy, and private donors including Charles Simonyi and Bill Gates. In 2019 the facility was renamed in honour of astronomer Vera Rubin, whose work on galaxy rotation curves provided key evidence for dark matter. First images were released in June 2025.

Science goals

The LSST is designed to address four main areas: probing dark energy and dark matter; taking an inventory of the Solar System, including near-Earth objects; exploring the transient optical sky, such as supernovae and gamma-ray burst afterglows; and mapping the Milky Way. Its alert stream of millions of transient detections per night is expected to transform time-domain astronomy.
