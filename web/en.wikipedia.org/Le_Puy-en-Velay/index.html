Le Puy-en-Velay

Le Puy-en-Velay
Country: France
Region: Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes
Department: Haute-Loire (prefecture)
Population: approx. 19,000

Le Puy-en-Velay is a commune in south-central France, the prefecture of the Haute-Loire department in the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region. It is renowned for its cathedral, its distinctive volcanic landscape, and as a starting point of the Via Podiensis pilgrimage route to Santiago de Compostela.

Landmarks

The town is built among ancient volcanic plugs. Its cathedral, Notre-Dame du Puy, a UNESCO-recognised pilgrimage church, stands at the foot of the Rocher Corneille, which is crowned by a monumental statue of the Virgin Mary cast from cannons captured at Sevastopol. On another pinnacle stands the chapel of Saint-Michel d'Aiguilhe, reached by 268 steps carved into the rock.

Economy and culture

Le Puy is historically famous for bobbin lace-making and for the green Puy lentils (lentilles vertes du Puy), which hold protected designation of origin status. The town hosts the Roi de l'Oiseau Renaissance festival each September.
