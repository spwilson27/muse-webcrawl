Yelnya offensive

The Yelnya offensive (30 August – 8 September 1941) was a Soviet counteroffensive during the Second World War in which the Red Army's Reserve Front, commanded by Georgy Zhukov, eliminated the German salient at Yelnya, southeast of Smolensk. It was the Red Army's first successful major offensive of the German–Soviet war and earned the first units the coveted "Guards" designation.

Background

During the Battle of Smolensk in July 1941, Heinz Guderian's Panzer Group 2 seized Yelnya and its bridgehead across the Desna River, creating a salient intended as a springboard toward Moscow. Soviet forces counterattacked the salient repeatedly through August at heavy cost, while Hitler ordered the panzer groups diverted — Guderian south toward Kiev — leaving infantry of the Fourth Army to hold Yelnya.

The offensive

On 30 August, Zhukov launched a coordinated pincer attack by the 24th Army (Konstantin Rakutin) with massed artillery. German divisions, worn down and without armour support, were ordered by Hitler only reluctantly to withdraw. By 6 September Soviet troops liberated Yelnya; by 8 September the salient had been eliminated and the front pushed to a new line west of the town.

Significance

Soviet casualties were heavy — roughly 31,500 in the 24th Army's operation — but the victory was exploited for propaganda as proof the Wehrmacht could be beaten in a planned operation. Four rifle divisions (100th, 127th, 153rd, and 161st) were renamed the 1st–4th Guards Rifle Divisions on 18 September 1941, founding the Soviet Guards tradition. The success also raised Zhukov's standing; he was soon sent to Leningrad and then to the defence of Moscow.
