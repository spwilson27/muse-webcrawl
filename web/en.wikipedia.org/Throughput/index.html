Throughput

In general terms, throughput is the rate of production or the rate at which something is processed. When used in the context of communication networks, such as Ethernet or packet radio, throughput or network throughput is the rate of successful message delivery over a communication channel.

Overview

The data that these messages contain may be delivered over a physical or logical link, or it may pass through a certain network node. Throughput is usually measured in bits per second (bit/s or bps), and sometimes in data packets per second or data packets per time slot. The system throughput is the sum of the data rates delivered to all terminals in the network.

Maximum throughput

The maximum throughput of a communications channel is bounded by the channel capacity described by the Shannon–Hartley theorem, which relates achievable data rate to bandwidth and signal-to-noise ratio. In practice, protocol overhead, retransmissions, congestion, and latency reduce the achieved throughput below this theoretical ceiling.

Throughput in other fields

In manufacturing and operations management, throughput is the rate at which a system generates product or completes work, a central concept in the theory of constraints. In computing, disk throughput and instruction throughput describe analogous rates for storage devices and processors. In business, throughput accounting treats throughput as the rate at which a system generates money through sales.

Related measures

Throughput is closely related to, but distinct from, bandwidth (the maximum possible rate) and latency (the delay before transfer begins). The term goodput refers to the throughput of useful application-level data, excluding protocol overhead and retransmitted packets.
