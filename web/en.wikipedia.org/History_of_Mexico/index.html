The history of Mexico spans millennia of Indigenous civilisations — the Olmec, Maya, Zapotec, Toltec, and finally the Aztec (Mexica) Empire centred on Tenochtitlan — before the Spanish conquest led by Hernán Cortés destroyed the Aztec state in 1521. For three centuries Mexico was the core of the Viceroyalty of New Spain, the richest colony of the Spanish Empire.

Independence and the 19th century

The independence movement began with Miguel Hidalgo's Grito de Dolores in 1810 and was consummated in 1821. The young nation endured political turmoil, lost half its territory to the United States in the Mexican–American War (1846–1848), and underwent the liberal Reform era under Benito Juárez, defeating the French-backed Second Mexican Empire in 1867.

Revolution and modern Mexico

The long dictatorship of Porfirio Díaz ended with the Mexican Revolution (1910–1920), which produced the Constitution of 1917 and, eventually, seven decades of rule by the Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI). Democratic alternation came in 2000 with the election of Vicente Fox. Modern Mexico grapples with economic development, migration, and organised-crime violence while remaining a major cultural and economic power in Latin America.
