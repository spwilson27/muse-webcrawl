Adobe Acrobat is a family of application software and web services developed by Adobe Inc. to view, create, manipulate, print and manage Portable Document Format (PDF) files. The free Acrobat Reader (originally Acrobat Reader) displays and prints PDFs, while the commercial Acrobat products add creation, editing, conversion, form filling, digital signatures, and security features.

History

Acrobat was launched in 1993 alongside the PDF format, which Adobe co-founder John Warnock had proposed in the "Camelot" project to preserve document appearance across platforms. Early adoption was slow because the reader was initially not free, but after Adobe made Reader free of charge, PDF became the dominant standard for fixed-format documents; the format became an open ISO standard in 2008.

Products

The line moved to a subscription model as Adobe Acrobat DC ("Document Cloud") in 2015, with Acrobat Standard and Acrobat Pro tiers, cloud storage, and integration with Adobe's other services. Features include optical character recognition of scans, redaction, preflight for print, and accessibility checking.
