Texas City is a city in Galveston County in the U.S. state of Texas, located on the southwest shoreline of Galveston Bay. It is a major deep-water port and petrochemical centre on the Texas Gulf Coast, part of the Houston metropolitan area.

History

Investors from Minnesota organized the Texas City Improvement Company in the 1890s, dredging a channel and building a port in the hope of rivalling Galveston and Houston. The town was laid out in 1893 and incorporated in 1911. Refining began in the 1910s, and during the Second World War the city hosted a major tin smelter and expanded refining capacity.

1947 disaster

On 16 April 1947, the French ship Grandcamp, loaded with ammonium nitrate fertilizer, caught fire and exploded at the docks, triggering a second explosion on the High Flyer the following day. The Texas City disaster killed at least 581 people, injured thousands, and remains one of the deadliest industrial accidents in United States history.

Economy

The Port of Texas City is one of the largest privately owned ports in the United States. Refineries and chemical plants operated by companies such as Marathon Petroleum and Valero dominate the economy; the BP Texas City refinery explosion of 2005, which killed 15 workers, led to major safety reforms and record fines.

Geography

The city is protected from Galveston Bay by the Texas City Dike, a levee extending about five miles into the bay, built after the catastrophic 1900 Galveston hurricane.
