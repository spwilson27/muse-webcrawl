Manila massacre

The Manila massacre (Filipino: Pagpatay sa Maynila) refers to the mass murder of Filipino civilians carried out by Imperial Japanese forces in the city of Manila during the Battle of Manila in February 1945. Estimates of the death toll range from 100,000 to 240,000 civilians.

Background

As American and Filipino forces advanced on Manila in early 1945, Japanese naval troops under Rear Admiral Sanji Iwabuchi refused orders to evacuate and instead fortified the city. During the month-long battle, Japanese forces committed systematic atrocities against the civilian population, including mass executions, bayoneting of hospital patients and refugees, and the burning of buildings with people inside them.

Aftermath

The massacre, together with the battle itself, left much of Manila in ruins; the city was among the most devastated Allied capitals of the Second World War. General Tomoyuki Yamashita was tried by a United States military commission and executed in 1946, in a case that set a precedent for command responsibility for war crimes.
