Mod (video games)

A mod (short for "modification") is an alteration or addition to a video game created by players or fans rather than the game's original developers. Mods range from small tweaks and new items to new levels, characters, graphical overhauls, and total conversions that transform a game into an essentially new one. Games with official modding tools or documented file formats tend to attract the largest modding communities.

History

Modding grew with the PC gaming culture of the 1990s. id Software's Wolfenstein 3D and especially Doom (1993), whose WAD file structure separated game data from the engine, encouraged user-created levels. Several famous games began as mods: Counter-Strike started as a mod for Half-Life (1998), Team Fortress as a mod for Quake, and the MOBA genre grew out of Defense of the Ancients, a custom map for Warcraft III. Distribution hubs such as Mod DB and Nexus Mods, and platform features such as Steam Workshop, made mods easy to find and install.

Types

Common categories include user-interface mods, gameplay and balance tweaks, graphical and texture packs, new content (quests, maps, weapons), bug-fix patches, and total conversions. Mods are generally free and distributed under the terms of the base game's licence, though experiments with paid mods — such as Steam's short-lived 2015 programme — have been controversial.

Legal and industry aspects

End-user licence agreements typically restrict commercial exploitation of mods, and publishers vary in how actively they support modding. Successful mods have launched development careers and, in cases such as Counter-Strike and Dota 2, become major commercial franchises in their own right.
