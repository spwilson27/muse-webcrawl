The National Fascist Party (Italian: Partito Nazionale Fascista, PNF) was a political party in Italy, created by Benito Mussolini as the political expression of Italian fascism and founded in Rome in November 1921. It ruled Italy from 1922, when Mussolini became prime minister after the March on Rome, until 1943, when Mussolini was deposed and the party was dissolved by royal decree.

Origins

The party grew out of the Fasci Italiani di Combattimento, founded by Mussolini in Milan in March 1919, and its squadristi paramilitary violence against socialists and trade unions during the turmoil after the First World War. As a party it combined nationalism, anti-communism, corporatism, and the cult of the Duce.

Rule

Following the March on Rome in October 1922, King Victor Emmanuel III appointed Mussolini prime minister. The Acerbo Law of 1923 guaranteed two-thirds of parliamentary seats to the largest party; after the crisis provoked by the murder of the socialist deputy Giacomo Matteotti in 1924, Mussolini proclaimed a dictatorship in January 1925. Italy became a one-party state: rival parties were banned, the press was censored, and the PNF was fused with the state, governing through institutions such as the Grand Council of Fascism.

The regime pursued empire — invading Ethiopia in 1935 — enacted racial laws against Jews in 1938, and allied with Nazi Germany, entering the Second World War in 1940. In July 1943, after the Allied invasion of Sicily, the Grand Council voted against Mussolini; he was arrested and the PNF was dissolved. German forces then installed him as head of the Italian Social Republic in the north, whose party was the Republican Fascist Party.
