Hickory

Hickory is a common name for trees composing the genus Carya, which includes around 20 species in the walnut family (Juglandaceae). Five or six species are native to China, Indochina, and India (Assam), as many as twelve are native to the United States, four are found in Mexico, and two to four are native to Canada. A number of hickory species are used for food (pecans and hickory nuts), lumber, and other products.

Description

Hickories are deciduous trees with pinnately compound leaves and large nuts enclosed in a husk that splits into four valves at maturity. They are wind-pollinated, monoecious trees bearing male catkins and small female flowers. Familiar North American species include shagbark hickory (Carya ovata), shellbark hickory (C. laciniosa), mockernut (C. tomentosa), pignut (C. glabra), bitternut (C. cordiformis), water hickory (C. aquatica), and the pecan (C. illinoinensis), the most commercially important member of the genus. Hickories are ecologically significant mast producers: their nuts feed squirrels, bears, deer, ducks, and many other animals.

Uses

Hickory wood is exceptionally hard, stiff, dense, and shock-resistant, and has long been used for tool handles, axe and hammer hafts, drumsticks, baseball bats (historically), wheel spokes, and furniture; Andrew Jackson's nickname "Old Hickory" derived from its toughness. Hickory is a premier firewood and charcoal wood, and burning hickory — or hickory sawdust and chips — is the classic fuel for smoking meats in American barbecue. The nuts of shagbark and shellbark hickories are sweet and edible, and Indigenous peoples of North America rendered hickory nut oil and milk; the pecan is a major commercial nut crop. Shagbark hickory bark is also made into a flavouring syrup.

Etymology

The name "hickory" derives from a shortening of earlier forms such as pocohicora / pawcohiccora, from an Algonquian word (compare Virginia Algonquian pawcohiccora) for a milky drink made from pounded hickory nuts.

Threats

Hickories can be affected by the hickory bark beetle, fungal diseases, and the broader pressures of forest change; the Asian long-horned beetle and other pests are monitored threats. Several Asian species of Carya are rare and of conservation concern.
