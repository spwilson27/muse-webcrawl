Kazunomiya Chikako, also known as Princess Kazu (和宮親子内親王, 1846–1877), was a daughter of Emperor Ninkō of Japan and the wife of Tokugawa Iemochi, the 14th shōgun of the Tokugawa shogunate.

Marriage

Her marriage in 1862 was a political union arranged under the policy of kōbu gattai, the reconciliation between the imperial court and the shogunate. She famously set conditions for the match, including retaining her imperial lifestyle in Edo Castle. After Iemochi's death in 1866, she took the name Seikan'in-no-miya and became a Buddhist nun.

Later life

During the Boshin War she interceded, together with Tenshō-in (Atsuhime), to spare Edo Castle from attack. She died in 1877, possibly from an infection, and is remembered as a tragic figure of the Bakumatsu period.
