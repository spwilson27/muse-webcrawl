USDA soil taxonomy

USDA soil taxonomy is a soil classification system developed by the United States Department of Agriculture and the National Cooperative Soil Survey. It provides an elaborate classification of soil types according to several parameters, most commonly their properties, in a hierarchical structure.

Hierarchy

The system has six levels: order, suborder, great group, subgroup, family, and series. There are twelve soil orders, including Alfisols, Andisols, Aridisols, Entisols, Gelisols, Histosols, Inceptisols, Mollisols, Oxisols, Spodosols, Ultisols, and Vertisols.

Basis of classification

Unlike some other systems, USDA taxonomy relies heavily on measurable soil properties such as moisture and temperature regimes, diagnostic horizons, and chemical characteristics rather than presumed genesis.

Use

It is the standard for soil surveys in the United States and has been adopted or adapted by many other countries.
