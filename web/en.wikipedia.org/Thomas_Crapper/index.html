Thomas Crapper (28 September 1836 – 27 January 1910) was an English plumber and businessman. He founded Thomas Crapper & Co. in London, a sanitary equipment company. Although he did not invent the flush toilet, he did much to increase its popularity and developed a number of related inventions, including the ballcock valve and the U-bend plumbing trap.

Life and career

Crapper was born in Thorne, South Yorkshire, one of several children of a steamboat captain. At the age of eleven he moved to London and was apprenticed to his brother George, a master plumber in Chelsea. In 1861 he established his own plumbing business, which grew into a leading manufacturer and retailer of sanitary ware. His showrooms on the King's Road were among the first anywhere to display bathroom fittings openly, at a time when many Victorians considered such items unmentionable.

Crapper held nine patents, including the floating ballcock and improved methods of flushing. His firm received royal warrants and fitted plumbing for members of the royal family, including at Sandringham House. He retired in 1904, selling the company, which continued to trade under his name.

Legacy and the word "crap"

Popular myth credits Crapper with inventing the flush toilet and with giving rise to the word "crap". In fact the word derives from older roots — possibly the Dutch krappe or Middle English crappe, meaning chaff or refuse — and pre-dates him. The association of his surname with toilet humour nevertheless became widespread, particularly among American servicemen stationed in England during the First World War, who encountered his name stamped on cisterns. Manhole covers bearing the Crapper name remain a curiosity in Westminster Abbey, and the company name has been revived for a modern range of sanitary products.
