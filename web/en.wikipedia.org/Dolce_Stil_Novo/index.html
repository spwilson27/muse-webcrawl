Dolce Stil Novo

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Dolce Stil Novo (Italian for "sweet new style"; in modern Italian dolce stil nuovo) is the name given to the most important poetic movement of 13th-century Italy. Influenced by troubadour poetry and by the Sicilian School, the style's hallmarks were its themes of divine love, the lady as a spiritual guide, and the refinement of the vernacular language.

History

The movement flourished in Florence and Bologna between roughly 1280 and 1310. Its founder is usually considered to be the Bolognese poet Guido Guinizelli, whose canzone Al cor gentil rempaira sempre amore laid out the movement's philosophy: love resides in the noble heart, and the beloved woman is an angelic figure whose beauty ennobles the lover. The Florentine Guido Cavalcanti became its most influential theorist and practitioner, and the young Dante Alighieri adopted and transformed the style in his Vita Nuova (c. 1294), a prosimetrum combining lyric poems with prose commentary. Other poets of the school included Lapo Gianni, Gianni Alfani, Dino Frescobaldi and Cino da Pistoia.

Characteristics

Stilnovist poetry celebrated the donna angelicata, the "angelic woman", whose greeting alone could bring salvation to the lover. Love was treated as a metaphysical and psychological force, analysed with scholastic precision; Cavalcanti in particular explored the physiology and psychology of love in his celebrated canzone Donna me prega. The poets favoured harmonious, musical verse forms — the sonnet, the canzone and the ballata — and a polished, elevated vernacular that moved Italian poetry decisively away from feudal and Provençal models.

Legacy

The term itself comes from Dante's Purgatorio (canto XXIV), where the poet Bonagiunta da Lucca uses the phrase to describe the new manner of Dante and his contemporaries. The Stil Novo profoundly influenced Petrarch and, through him, the whole subsequent tradition of European love lyric.
