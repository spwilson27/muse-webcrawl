Tarzan

Tarzan is a fictional character created by American writer Edgar Rice Burroughs, first appearing in the novel Tarzan of the Apes (magazine publication 1912; book 1914). An archetypal feral child, Tarzan is the son of British aristocrats marooned on the African coast, raised by the great apes of the Mangani tribe after his parents' deaths; his ape name means "white skin". He grows to become a peerless hunter and linguist who eventually reclaims his identity as John Clayton, Viscount Greystoke.

The novels

Burroughs wrote two dozen Tarzan books, beginning with Tarzan of the Apes and The Return of Tarzan, introducing Jane Porter, Tarzan's love and later wife, lost civilizations such as the ruined city of Opar, and a full ape language of which Burroughs compiled a lexicon. The series' mix of adventure, romance, and lost-world fantasy made it a pillar of pulp fiction.

Adaptations

Tarzan has appeared in dozens of films, beginning with silent star Elmo Lincoln (1918). Johnny Weissmuller's MGM films of the 1930s–40s, with Maureen O'Sullivan as Jane, fixed the popular image — including the famous (and partly manufactured) "Me Tarzan, you Jane" line and the Tarzan yell. A long-running newspaper comic strip (from 1929), radio and television series, and Disney's animated Tarzan (1999) renewed the character for new generations.

Cultural impact

Tarzan became a global byword for the noble savage and the wild man of the woods, spawning imitators, parodies, and scholarly debate about the books' imperial and racial assumptions. The character remains Burroughs's most famous creation; the town of Tarzana, California, is named after his ranch.
