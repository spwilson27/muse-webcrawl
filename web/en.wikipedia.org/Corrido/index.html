Corrido

A corrido is a popular narrative ballad tradition of Mexico and the southwestern United States, usually recounting heroic deeds, battles, outlaws, tragedies and current events, sung to waltz or polka rhythms.

History

Descended from Spanish romances, corridos flourished during the Mexican Revolution (1910–1920), celebrating figures such as Pancho Villa and Emiliano Zapata, and were spread by broadsides and early recordings.

Modern forms

Later corridos addressed migration, labour struggles and drug trafficking; the controversial narcocorrido subgenre narrates the lives of traffickers. Artists such as Los Tigres del Norte and Chalino Sánchez brought the form to mass audiences, and corridos tumbados emerged in the 2020s.
