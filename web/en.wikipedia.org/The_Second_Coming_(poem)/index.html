The Second Coming (poem)

The Second Coming

Author: W. B. Yeats

Written: 1919

First published: 1920

Form: Lyric poem, two stanzas

"The Second Coming" is a poem by the Irish poet William Butler Yeats, written in 1919 in the aftermath of the First World War and the Russian Revolution, and first published in 1920. Drawing on Yeats's private system of historical cycles or "gyres", set out in A Vision, the poem describes a world spinning out of control toward an ominous revelation.

Text and themes

The poem opens with the famous lines "Turning and turning in the widening gyre / The falcon cannot hear the falconer; / Things fall apart; the centre cannot hold". The second stanza conjures a vision of a "rough beast" slouching towards Bethlehem to be born, a monstrous anti-messiah heralding a new, terrifying age rather than the Christian second coming of its title.

Influence

Phrases from the poem — "things fall apart", "the centre cannot hold", "the best lack all conviction", "slouches towards Bethlehem" — have entered common usage. Chinua Achebe took the title of his novel Things Fall Apart from it, and the poem is among the most quoted in English.
