Canntaireachd

Canntaireachd (Scottish Gaelic pronunciation: [ˈkʰaun̪ˠt̪ɛɾʲəxk]) is a system of vocables used to notate and orally transmit the music of the Great Highland bagpipe, particularly the classical repertoire known as pìobaireachd or ceòl mòr ("great music"). The word derives from Gaelic roots meaning "singing" or "chanting."

System

In canntaireachd, syllables represent the notes of the pipe scale, with consonants indicating the melody notes and vowels distinguishing pitches, while additional syllables encode grace notes and embellishments such as throws, grips, and crunluaths. A teacher sings the tune to the pupil, who learns to associate each vocable with a finger movement, allowing long and complex compositions to be memorized without written music.

History

The system was the primary vehicle for preserving pibroch for centuries within hereditary piping families such as the MacCrimmons of Skye, pipers to the MacLeods of Dunvegan. In the early nineteenth century Joseph MacDonald and later the Nether Lorn manuscript recorded canntaireachd in written form, with distinctive spelling conventions. As staff notation spread, oral canntaireachd declined but never disappeared.

Modern use

Canntaireachd is still taught by pibroch specialists and used in competition circles to convey phrasing and expression that staff notation captures imperfectly. Recordings of master pipers singing canntaireachd are valued sources for the interpretation of historical tunes.
