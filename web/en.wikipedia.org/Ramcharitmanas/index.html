Ramcharitmanas

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ramcharitmanas (Devanagari: रामचरितमानस, Rāmacaritamānasa), literally "the Lake of the Deeds of Rama", is an epic poem in the Awadhi language composed by the 16th-century Indian poet-saint Tulsidas (c. 1532–1623). It retells the Sanskrit Ramayana of Valmiki, the story of the god-king Rama, in the vernacular language accessible to ordinary people. The work is considered one of the greatest achievements of Hindi literature and is central to the devotional (bhakti) tradition of Hinduism, particularly the worship of Rama in northern India.

Composition

According to tradition, Tulsidas began composing the work in Ayodhya in 1574 CE, on Ram Navami, the birthday of Rama, and completed it around 1576–1577, largely in Varanasi. The poem consists of seven books (kāṇḍas), following the structure of the Sanskrit Ramayana: Bālakāṇḍa (childhood), Ayodhyākāṇḍa, Araṇyakāṇḍa (forest), Kiṣkindhākāṇḍa, Sundarakāṇḍa, Laṅkākāṇḍa (the war in Lanka) and Uttarakāṇḍa. It is written chiefly in the chaupai and doha metres, interspersed with other verse forms.

Content and theology

While following Valmiki's narrative, Tulsidas reshaped the story in significant ways. Rama is presented throughout not merely as an ideal king and hero but as the supreme God (Para Brahman), incarnate out of compassion for humanity. The poem emphasises bhakti — loving devotion to Rama — as the surest path to liberation, open to people of every caste and gender, and it softens or explains episodes of the Sanskrit epic that trouble later readers. The framing device presents the tale as narrated by the god Shiva to his wife Parvati, and retold by the crow-sage Kakbhushundi to Garuda. Famous passages include the character of the boatman Kevat, the devotion of Shabari, and hymns such as the Shri Ramchandra Kripalu Bhajman.

Influence

Because it was composed in Awadhi rather than Sanskrit, the Ramcharitmanas brought the Rama story to a mass audience and became the most widely read and recited scripture of northern India. Public recitation (path) of the text, the annual Ramlila dramatic performances based on it, and the Sundarakanda recitations in temples and homes keep it at the centre of religious life. Mahatma Gandhi praised Tulsidas and the work highly, and the poem has been translated into English many times, including the prose translation by F. S. Growse in the 19th century.
