The First Bulgarian Empire was a medieval Bulgarian state founded in 681 AD in the north-eastern Balkans. It grew out of the settlement of the Bulgars, a Turkic people led by Asparuh, south of the Danube after their victory over the Byzantine army at Ongal in 680. At its height under Tsar Simeon I in the early tenth century, the empire stretched from the Black Sea to the Adriatic and from the Carpathians to northern Greece, and stood as the chief land rival of the Byzantine Empire in southeastern Europe.

Expansion and conversion

Under Khan Krum (803–814) the empire destroyed an Avar khaganate remnant, seized Sofia, and in 811 defeated and killed Emperor Nikephoros I at the Battle of Pliska. Boris I accepted Orthodox Christianity in 864, a decision that bound Bulgaria to the Byzantine cultural world while securing an autocephalous church. His successors welcomed the disciples of Saints Cyril and Methodius, whose schools at Preslav and Ohrid developed the Cyrillic script and a rich Slavonic literature.

Simeon the Great

Simeon I (893–927), educated in Constantinople, waged a series of wars against Byzantium, winning the crushing victory of Achelous in 917 and assuming the title of Emperor (Tsar) of the Bulgarians and the Romans. His reign is regarded as Bulgaria's medieval golden age, marked by the building of the new capital Preslav and a flourishing of letters.

Decline and fall

After Simeon's death the empire weakened under internal strife, Magyar and Pecheneg raids, and Rus' invasion. Emperor Basil II — remembered as the "Bulgar-slayer" — conquered the empire piecemeal, and after the Battle of Kleidion (1014) Bulgaria was fully incorporated into the Byzantine Empire by 1018. Bulgarian statehood was restored with the Second Bulgarian Empire in 1185.
