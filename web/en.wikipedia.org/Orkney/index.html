Orkney is an archipelago in the Northern Isles of Scotland, situated off the north coast of the Scottish mainland across the Pentland Firth from Caithness. The islands form one of Scotland's 32 council areas. About 20 of the roughly 70 islands are inhabited; the largest island, known as Mainland, contains the towns of Kirkwall (the capital) and Stromness.

History

Orkney has been inhabited since the Mesolithic. Its Neolithic monuments—including Skara Brae, the Ring of Brodgar, the Stones of Stenness, and the Maeshowe chambered cairn—form the Heart of Neolithic Orkney UNESCO World Heritage Site. Pictish inhabitants were displaced or absorbed by Norse settlers from the late 8th century, and Orkney became an earldom under Norway. The islands were pledged to Scotland in 1468 in lieu of a dowry for Margaret of Denmark's marriage to James III. Scapa Flow served as a major Royal Navy anchorage in both world wars; the German fleet was scuttled there in 1919.

Economy and culture

Farming—especially beef cattle—fishing, whisky distilling (Highland Park and Scapa), tourism, and renewable energy are key industries. Orkney is a world leader in marine renewable energy testing through the European Marine Energy Centre (EMEC). The Orkney dialect preserves traces of the extinct Norn language, and Orcadian culture retains strong Norse influences.
