American Pie is a 1999 American teen sex comedy film directed by Paul Weitz (in his directorial debut) and written by Adam Herz. It is the first film in the American Pie theatrical series. The film follows five best friends in their senior year at a Michigan high school who make a pact to lose their virginity before graduating.

The title is borrowed from the Don McLean song "American Pie", and refers to a scene in which the protagonist Jim (Jason Biggs) is caught in a compromising act with a freshly baked apple pie, after being told that it resembles the sensation in question. The film also popularised the slang term "MILF", delivered by John Cho's character.

Plot

Jim Levenstein, Kevin Myers, Paul Finch, and Chris "Oz" Ostreicher vow to lose their virginity by prom night, spurred by the antics of their obnoxious classmate Steve Stifler (Sean William Scott). Their schemes unfold through encounters with exchange student Nadia, band camper Michelle (Alyson Hannigan) — whose "This one time, at band camp" refrain became a catchphrase — and Jim's awkward but well-meaning father (Eugene Levy). By prom night the friends' plans resolve in ways that complicate the pact's original spirit.

Reception and legacy

Made for about $11 million, the film grossed over $235 million worldwide and revived the teen sex comedy genre. It spawned three theatrical sequels — American Pie 2 (2001), American Wedding (2003), and American Reunion (2012) — as well as a series of direct-to-video spin-offs under the American Pie Presents banner.
