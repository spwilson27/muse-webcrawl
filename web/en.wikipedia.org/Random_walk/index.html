Random walk

A random walk is a mathematical object that describes a path consisting of a succession of random steps on some mathematical space, such as the integers. The term was introduced by Karl Pearson in 1905.

Simple random walk

The simplest example is the walk on the integers in which at each step the walker moves +1 or −1 with equal probability. The expected position after n steps is zero, while the typical displacement grows like the square root of n. A famous result is that the one- and two-dimensional random walks are recurrent (returning to the origin with probability 1), while in three or more dimensions they are transient — a fact summarised by George Pólya.

Applications

Random walks model Brownian motion and diffusion, stock price movements in finance, foraging paths of animals, polymer configurations, and search algorithms such as random walks on graphs used in computer science.
