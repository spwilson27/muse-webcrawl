Selfridges

Selfridges, also known as Selfridges & Co., is a chain of high-end department stores in the United Kingdom that is operated by Selfridges Retail Limited, part of the Selfridges Group. It was founded by Harry Gordon Selfridge in 1908.

The flagship store on London's Oxford Street is the second largest shop in the UK (after Harrods) and opened on 15 March 1909. Other Selfridges stores are in Manchester's Exchange Square and the Bullring in Birmingham, which opened in 2003 with a distinctive futuristic facade covered in aluminium discs.

History

Harry Gordon Selfridge, an American who had worked at Marshall Field's in Chicago, arrived in London determined to build a department store that would make shopping an entertaining social activity rather than a chore. He invested £400,000 in a then-unfashionable western stretch of Oxford Street. The store revolutionized retail with its dramatic window displays, in-store restaurants, and the philosophy that customers should feel free to browse without obligation to buy.

After Selfridge's retirement and death, the chain passed through several owners, including the Lewis's group and Sears Holdings. In 2003 it was acquired by Canadian businessman Galen Weston and his family for £598 million. In December 2021, the Weston family sold the Selfridges Group to a joint venture between Thai conglomerate Central Group and Austrian company Signa Holding.

Architecture

The Oxford Street store is a Grade II* listed building designed principally by Daniel Burnham's firm with a classical beaux-arts facade, ionic columns, and the famous Queen of Time clock above the main entrance. It was constructed in phases beginning in 1908 and completed in its present form in the 1920s.

In popular culture

The store and its founder were the subject of the ITV/PBS period drama series Mr Selfridge (2013–2016), starring Jeremy Piven.
