Summerhill School

Summerhill School is an independent (i.e. fee-paying) British day and boarding school in Leiston, Suffolk. It was founded in 1921 by Alexander Sutherland Neill with the conviction that the school should be made to fit the child, rather than the other way around. It is run as a democratic community and is considered the oldest and most famous of the democratic schools; attendance at lessons is optional, and pupils and staff have equal votes in the school meeting that makes the rules.

Philosophy

Neill believed that children's happiness and emotional health mattered more than academic achievement imposed by compulsion, and that freedom (which he distinguished from licence) allowed children to develop at their own pace. His 1960 book Summerhill: A Radical Approach to Child Rearing became an international bestseller and influenced progressive education worldwide.

Later history

After Neill's death in 1973 the school was led by his wife Ena and then by his daughter Zoë Readhead. In the late 1990s Summerhill fought and won a notable legal battle against the UK government's inspection regime, securing recognition of its distinctive philosophy. It inspired democratic and free schools internationally, including the Sudbury schools in the United States.
