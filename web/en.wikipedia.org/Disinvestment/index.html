Disinvestment

Disinvestment refers to the action of an organization or government selling or liquidating an asset or subsidiary. It is the opposite of investment. The term is used both for straightforward commercial disposals and for politically motivated campaigns in which investors withdraw funds from a company, industry, or country to pressure it to change its behaviour.

Forms and motives

Governments pursue disinvestment (privatization) when selling state-owned enterprises to raise revenue or improve efficiency. Companies divest divisions to focus on core businesses, raise capital, or satisfy regulators. Activist campaigns have targeted apartheid-era South Africa in the 1980s, tobacco, Sudan during the Darfur conflict, and — most prominently in recent years — fossil fuel companies, with universities, pension funds, and foundations pledging to divest holdings on climate grounds.

Effects and debate

Economists debate how much direct financial impact divestment has, since sold shares are bought by others; supporters argue its power lies in stigmatization and political signalling, while critics contend engagement as shareholders may be more effective. In economics, disinvestment can also mean a fall in capital stock when depreciation exceeds gross investment.
