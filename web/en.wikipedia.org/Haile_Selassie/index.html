Haile Selassie I (born Ras Tafari Makonnen; 23 July 1892 – 27 August 1975) was Emperor of Ethiopia from 1930 to 1974. A defining figure of modern Ethiopian and African history, he modernised Ethiopia, led its resistance to Fascist Italy's invasion, and became a symbol of African independence. Members of the Rastafari movement regard him as God incarnate.

Rise to power

As Ras Tafari, regent and heir apparent from 1916, he drove Ethiopia's entry into the League of Nations (1923) and abolished the legal status of slavery. He was crowned emperor in 1930 upon the death of Empress Zewditu, taking the regnal name Haile Selassie ("Power of the Trinity").

War, exile and restoration

When Italy invaded in 1935, Haile Selassie led his armies in person before going into exile in Bath, England, in 1936. His address to the League of Nations that year — warning that "it is us today; it will be you tomorrow" — became a famous indictment of collective security's failure. British and Commonwealth forces, with Ethiopian patriots, liberated the country in 1941 and restored him to the throne.

Later reign and fall

After the war he pursued modernisation, founded the Organisation of African Unity (1963, headquartered in Addis Ababa), and federation with Eritrea. But land inequality, the concealed Wollo famine of 1973, and economic crisis undermined his regime; the Marxist Derg deposed him in September 1974. He died the following year in disputed circumstances, officially of complications after surgery.
