Shapur I

Shapur I (Middle Persian: 𐭱𐭲𐭯𐭥𐭩; c. 215 – 270 CE), also known as Shapur the Great, was the second Sasanian King of Kings of Iran. He reigned from 240 to 270 CE, succeeding his father Ardashir I, founder of the Sasanian Empire.

Reign

Shapur consolidated the empire his father had founded and campaigned extensively against the Roman Empire in the west. He fought the emperors Gordian III, Philip the Arab, and Valerian. Gordian died during his invasion of Mesopotamia in 244, Philip sued for peace and paid a large ransom, and in 260 at the Battle of Edessa Shapur captured Valerian — the only Roman emperor ever taken prisoner in battle — along with tens of thousands of Roman soldiers.

The captured Romans were settled in Persis and Khuzestan, where their engineering skill contributed to works such as the great dam-bridge at Shushtar, known as the Band-e Kaisar (Caesar's Dam).

Domestic achievements

Shapur founded or refounded several cities, including Bishapur and Gundeshapur, the latter becoming a famed centre of learning and medicine. A notable administrator, he promoted religious tolerance in practice while supporting the Zoroastrian priest Kartir, and he received the prophet Mani at court. Trilingual inscriptions on the Ka'ba-ye Zartosht at Naqsh-e Rostam record his victories in Greek, Parthian, and Middle Persian.

Legacy

His rock reliefs at Naqsh-e Rostam and Bishapur, showing Roman emperors kneeling, dead, and captive before him, remain among the most striking monuments of Sasanian art. He was succeeded by his son Hormizd I.
