Historically black colleges and universities

NumberOver 100 in the United States
DefinitionInstitutions founded before 1964 with the mission of educating African Americans

Historically black colleges and universities (HBCUs) are institutions of higher education in the United States that were established before the Civil Rights Act of 1964 with the intention of primarily serving African Americans. Most were founded in the aftermath of the Civil War, often by religious organisations and the Freedmen's Bureau, when black students were largely excluded from existing colleges.

History

The earliest HBCUs include Cheyney University of Pennsylvania (1837) and Lincoln University (1854). The Second Morrill Act of 1890 required states practising segregation to establish land-grant institutions for black students, expanding the sector in the South. HBCUs educated a large share of African American professionals, including Martin Luther King Jr., Thurgood Marshall, and Kamala Harris.

Present day

There are more than 100 HBCUs, enrolling a diverse student body while retaining their historic mission. They award a disproportionate share of bachelor's degrees earned by African Americans, particularly in STEM fields, and have experienced renewed enrolment and philanthropic support in recent years.
