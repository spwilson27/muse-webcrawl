Mondelēz International, Inc. is an American multinational confectionery, food, and beverage company based in Chicago, Illinois. The stylized corporate name carries a macron (¯) over its second "e" — a diacritic evoking length and quality in the invented word — although the name is also frequently written without it as "Mondelez".

Name

The name Mondelēz was coined ahead of the 2012 demerger of Kraft Foods. It combines monde, derived from the Latin mundus and cognate words for "world" in Romance languages, with delēz, a made-up element suggesting "delicious". Kraft employees worldwide were invited to suggest names for the new snack company, and the winning entry was proposed by employees in Vienna and the United States. The macron in the logo distinguishes it from a Russian near-homophone that was noted, with some amusement, when the name was announced.

Company

The company holds the global snack portfolio formerly owned by Kraft Foods, including Oreo cookies, Cadbury and Milka chocolate, Toblerone, Ritz crackers, belVita breakfast biscuits, Halls cough drops, and Trident gum. It sells products in around 160 countries and employs roughly 90,000 people.
