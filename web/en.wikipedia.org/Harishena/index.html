Harishena

Period4th century CE
RoleCourt poet and minister of Samudragupta
Known forPrayaga Prashasti (Allahabad Pillar inscription)

Harishena was a fourth-century Sanskrit poet and courtier of the Gupta emperor Samudragupta (reigned c. 335–375 CE). He is best known as the composer of the Prayaga Prashasti, the celebrated panegyric inscription recording Samudragupta's conquests.

The Allahabad inscription

The prashasti is engraved on the Allahabad pillar — an Ashokan pillar also bearing edicts of Ashoka and a later inscription of the Mughal emperor Jahangir. Written in refined classical Sanskrit, in a mixture of verse and prose (champu), it describes Samudragupta's military campaigns across northern and southern India, his personal accomplishments in music and poetry, and his administration.

Offices

Inscriptions credit Harishena with several high offices, including mahadandanayaka (chief judicial officer) and sandhivigrahika (minister of war and peace). He was the son of Dhruvabhuti, who had held similar office under Chandragupta I.

Historical significance

Because contemporary narrative histories of the Gupta period are lacking, Harishena's inscription is one of the principal primary sources for reconstructing Samudragupta's reign and the political geography of fourth-century India.
