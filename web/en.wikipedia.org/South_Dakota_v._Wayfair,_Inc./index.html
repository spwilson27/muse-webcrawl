South Dakota v. Wayfair, Inc.

South Dakota v. Wayfair, Inc.
Court: Supreme Court of the United States
Decided: 21 June 2018
Citation: 585 U.S. 162
Holding: states may require out-of-state sellers to collect sales tax even without a physical presence in the state

South Dakota v. Wayfair, Inc., 585 U.S. 162 (2018), is a landmark decision of the United States Supreme Court holding that states may charge sales tax on purchases made from out-of-state sellers even if the seller has no physical presence in the taxing state. The decision overruled National Bellas Hess, Inc. v. Department of Revenue of Illinois (1967) and Quill Corp. v. North Dakota (1992), which had barred such taxes under the dormant Commerce Clause.

Background

In 2016 South Dakota, seeking to challenge Quill in an era of rapidly growing e-commerce, enacted a law requiring out-of-state sellers with more than $100,000 of sales or 200 separate transactions in the state to collect and remit sales tax. The state sued the online retailers Wayfair, Overstock.com and Newegg; the South Dakota courts, bound by Quill, ruled for the retailers, and the state appealed.

Opinion

Writing for a 5–4 majority, Justice Anthony Kennedy held that the physical-presence rule was "unsound and incorrect", artificial, and a growing burden on state revenues as internet commerce expanded. The Court found that Wayfair's "economic and virtual contacts" with South Dakota satisfied the substantial-nexus requirement of Complete Auto Transit, Inc. v. Brady, and noted features of the South Dakota law — its de minimis threshold, non-retroactivity, and the state's membership in the Streamlined Sales and Use Tax Agreement — that prevented undue burdens on interstate commerce. Chief Justice Roberts dissented, arguing that Congress, not the Court, should change the rule.

Impact

Following the decision, nearly all states with a sales tax adopted "economic nexus" statutes and marketplace-facilitator laws requiring platforms such as Amazon and eBay to collect tax on third-party sales, fundamentally changing the taxation of online retail in the United States.
