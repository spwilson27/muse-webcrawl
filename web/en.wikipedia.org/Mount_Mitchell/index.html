Mount Mitchell

Mount Mitchell, known in Cherokee as Attakulla, is the highest peak of the Appalachian Mountains and the highest peak in mainland North America east of the Mississippi River. It is located in the Black Mountain range of the Blue Ridge Mountains, part of the Appalachian Mountain chain, in Yancey County, North Carolina. The mountain is protected by North Carolina's Mount Mitchell State Park, the state's first state park, established in 1915, and lies within the Pisgah National Forest.

Prominence6,089 ft (1,856 m)

History

The peak is named for Elisha Mitchell, a professor at the University of North Carolina who measured its elevation in the 1830s and 1840s and argued that it exceeded Grandfather Mountain and all other peaks in the East. A dispute with his former student, Congressman Thomas Clingman, led Mitchell to return in 1857 to verify his measurements; he fell to his death at a waterfall now known as Mitchell Falls, and his body was later reinterred at the summit, where his grave remains. A weather station and an observation tower now stand near the top, reached by a road from the Blue Ridge Parkway.

Geography and climate

The summit has a subalpine climate — cool summers and severe winters with frequent wind, fog and icing — resembling that of southern Canada rather than the southeastern United States. Snow has been recorded in every month of the year. The high elevations support a spruce–fir forest of red spruce and Fraser fir, an ecosystem relict of the last ice age that has suffered badly from logging, fire, the invasive balsam woolly adelgid, and acid precipitation.

Ecology

The mountain's forests host rare species adapted to the cold, moist environment, including the northern flying squirrel and the spruce–fir moss spider. Efforts continue to restore Fraser fir stands devastated during the twentieth century.
