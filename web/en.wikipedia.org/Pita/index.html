Pita

Pita (also pitta) is a family of yeast-leavened round flatbreads baked from wheat flour, common in the Mediterranean, Levant, and neighbouring areas. It includes the widely known version with an interior pocket, known in English as "pocket pita", which forms when steam puffs up the dough during high-temperature baking, leaving a hollow centre when cooled. Pita is used to scoop sauces or dips such as hummus, or to wrap kebabs, gyros, and falafel in sandwiches.

History

Flatbreads are among the oldest breads, with evidence of bread-making by the Natufian culture some 14,500 years ago in Jordan. The word "pita" may derive from Greek pēktos, "solid/congealed", or via the Aramaic and Arabic breads of the Levant (khubz arabi).

Preparation

Most pitas are baked at high temperatures (450–475 °F / 232–246 °C), causing the flattened rounds of dough to puff up dramatically from steam. Varieties include Greek pita (thick, soft, pocketless), Levantine pocket pita, Turkish pide, and Cypriot pita.
