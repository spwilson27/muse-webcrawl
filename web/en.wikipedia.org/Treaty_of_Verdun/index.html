Treaty of Verdun

Treaty of Verdun
Signedc. 10 August 843
LocationVerdun, Francia
SignatoriesLothair I; Louis the German; Charles the Bald
ContextCarolingian civil war following the death of Louis the Pious

The Treaty of Verdun (French: Traité de Verdun), agreed in August 843, divided the Carolingian Empire among the three surviving sons of Louis the Pious, grandsons of Charlemagne. It ended the civil war that had followed Louis's death in 840 and is often treated as a founding moment in the separate histories of France and Germany.

Terms

  • Lothair I, the eldest, retained the imperial title and received Middle Francia — a long central strip from the North Sea through Lotharingia and Burgundy to Italy — known as Lotharingia in its northern part.
  • Louis the German received East Francia, roughly the lands east of the Rhine, forerunner of the Kingdom of Germany.
  • Charles the Bald received West Francia, forerunner of the Kingdom of France.

Consequences

The middle kingdom proved unstable and was subdivided again by the Treaty of Prüm (855) and the Treaty of Meerssen (870). The contested borderlands between the western and eastern kingdoms — especially Lotharingia, later Lorraine — remained a source of Franco-German conflict for more than a thousand years.
