Jordan algebra

In abstract algebra, a Jordan algebra is a nonassociative algebra over a field whose multiplication satisfies the commutative law and the Jordan identity. Jordan algebras were introduced in 1933 by the physicist Pascual Jordan in an attempt to formalize the algebra of observables in quantum mechanics.

Definition

A Jordan algebra is a vector space equipped with a bilinear product x ∘ y satisfying:

  • x ∘ y = y ∘ x (commutativity),
  • (x ∘ y) ∘ (x ∘ x) = x ∘ (y ∘ (x ∘ x)) (the Jordan identity).

Any associative algebra can be made into a Jordan algebra with the symmetrized product x ∘ y = (xy + yx)/2; such algebras, and their subalgebras, are called special Jordan algebras. The exceptional (non-special) examples include the algebra of 3×3 Hermitian matrices over the octonions, the Albert algebra, which is 27-dimensional and plays a role in exceptional geometry and mathematical physics.

Structure theory

The classification of finite-dimensional simple Jordan algebras over algebraically closed fields was completed through work by Jordan, John von Neumann, Eugene Wigner, and later Nathan Jacobson and others. Jordan algebras remain important in quantum mechanics, projective geometry, Lie theory (via the Tits–Koecher–Kantor construction), and the theory of symmetric cones in optimization.
