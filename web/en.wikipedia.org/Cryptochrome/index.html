Cryptochrome

Cryptochromes (from the Greek κρυπτός χρώμα, "hidden colour") are a class of flavoproteins found in plants and animals that are sensitive to blue light. They are involved in the circadian rhythms of plants and animals, and in the sensing of magnetic fields in a number of species.

Function

In plants, cryptochromes mediate responses to blue light such as inhibition of stem elongation, control of flowering time, and entrainment of the circadian clock. In animals, cryptochrome proteins (CRY1 and CRY2 in mammals) act as core components of the molecular circadian oscillator, repressing the CLOCK-BMAL1 transcription factor complex in a feedback loop of roughly 24 hours.

Magnetoreception

Cryptochromes are the leading candidate molecules for the light-dependent magnetic compass of migratory birds. According to the radical pair mechanism, photoexcitation of the flavin adenine dinucleotide (FAD) cofactor generates radical pairs whose reaction yields are influenced by the Earth's magnetic field, potentially giving the bird a visual sense of field direction.

Evolution

Cryptochromes are evolutionarily related to photolyases, enzymes that repair UV-damaged DNA, but most cryptochromes have lost or reduced DNA-repair activity and instead serve signalling roles.
