Peter Waage (29 June 1833 – 13 January 1900) was a Norwegian chemist and professor at the University of Christiania (now the University of Oslo). Together with his brother-in-law Cato Maximilian Guldberg, he formulated the law of mass action, one of the foundational principles of chemical kinetics and equilibrium.

Early life and education

Waage was born in Flekkefjord in southern Norway, the son of a ship's captain. He studied medicine and then chemistry at the University of Christiania, where he came under the influence of the chemist Adolph Strecker. After graduating, he travelled to France and Germany to further his studies.

Career

In 1862 Waage became a lecturer at the University of Christiania, and in 1866 he was appointed professor of chemistry, a post he held for the rest of his life. He was also director of the university's chemical laboratory. His research interests ranged widely, from analytical chemistry to the chemistry of Norwegian mineral waters and industrial processes such as fish meal production.

Law of mass action

Between 1864 and 1879, Waage and Guldberg published a series of papers — first in Norwegian, then in French and German — establishing that the rate of a chemical reaction and the position of chemical equilibrium depend on the concentrations (the "active masses") of the reacting substances raised to powers given by their stoichiometric coefficients. Their 1864 paper is now recognised as a landmark in physical chemistry, although it was initially overlooked, partly because it appeared in Norwegian.

Legacy

The law of mass action underlies modern treatments of chemical equilibrium, chemical kinetics, and the equilibrium constant. Waage is commemorated in Norway as one of the country's most significant scientists of the nineteenth century.
