Body modification

Body modification (or body alteration) is the deliberate altering of the human anatomy or human physical appearance, often for aesthetic, social, spiritual or cultural reasons. It encompasses a wide spectrum of practices, from widely accepted forms such as ear piercing, tattooing and circumcision to more extreme modifications including scarification, implants and tongue splitting.

Types

Common categories include: adornment, such as tattooing, body piercing and ear stretching; surgical augmentation, such as breast implants and other cosmetic surgery; reshaping, such as tightlacing, foot binding (historically) and neck elongation with rings; and cutting or removal practices, including scarification, branding, subdermal implants, tongue splitting and nullification. Some modifications are ornaments that can be removed, while others are permanent.

History and culture

Body modification is ancient and worldwide: Ötzi the Iceman (c. 3300 BC) bore tattoos; tattooing and scarification marked status, rites of passage and group identity in many indigenous cultures; and practices such as Chinese foot binding and Victorian tightlacing reshaped bodies to cultural ideals of beauty. In the late 20th century the modern primitives movement and punk and alternative subcultures revived and popularised piercing and tattooing in the West, moving them into the mainstream.

Motivations and controversy

People modify their bodies for self-expression, beauty, group affiliation, religious or spiritual reasons, sexual enhancement, or to mark life events. Debates surround consent, age limits, health risks — including infection and complications from unregulated practitioners — workplace acceptability, and the ethics of extreme procedures performed outside medical settings.
