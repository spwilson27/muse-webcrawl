Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem

The Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem is an essential principle for digital signal processing linking the frequency range of a signal and the sample rate required to avoid a type of distortion called aliasing. The theorem states that the sample rate must be at least twice the bandwidth of the signal to avoid aliasing.

In practice, due to the possible accumulation of errors and timing jitter, a somewhat larger sample rate is often applied. The sampling theorem was implied by the work of Harry Nyquist in 1928 and rigorously proven by Claude Shannon in 1949; related results were independently developed by Vladimir Kotelnikov in the Soviet Union.

Statement

If a function x(t) contains no frequencies higher than B hertz, it is completely determined by giving its ordinates at a series of points spaced 1/(2B) seconds apart. The minimum sampling rate, 2B, is called the Nyquist rate for a signal of bandwidth B; half of it, B, is the Nyquist frequency.

Reconstruction

The original bandlimited signal can be reconstructed by interpolating its samples with sinc functions (the Whittaker–Shannon interpolation formula), in which each sample contributes a sinc pulse scaled by the sample value, and the sum reproduces the continuous waveform exactly in the ideal case.

Aliasing

If a signal is sampled below the Nyquist rate, frequency components above half the sampling rate "fold back" into the measured band and appear as spurious lower frequencies — the aliasing phenomenon. To prevent this, signals are passed through an anti-aliasing low-pass filter before sampling. A familiar example is the 44.1 kHz sampling rate of compact discs, chosen to exceed twice the ~20 kHz limit of human hearing.

History

Harry Nyquist's 1928 paper "Certain topics in telegraph transmission theory" established the sampling criterion in the context of telegraphy. Claude Shannon's 1949 paper "Communication in the presence of noise" gave the rigorous form central to information theory. E. T. Whittaker and others had anticipated aspects of the interpolation result earlier.
