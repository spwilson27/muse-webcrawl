Housing Act of 1937

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Housing Act of 1937 (Pub. L. 75-412), also known as the Wagner–Steagall Housing Act, is a United States federal law that created the United States Housing Authority within the Department of the Interior and provided federal loans and subsidies for local public housing agencies to build low-income housing. It remains the legal foundation of the American public housing programme.

Enacted: 1 September 1937
Sponsors: Senator Robert F. Wagner; Rep. Henry B. Steagall
Created: United States Housing Authority
Signed by: President Franklin D. Roosevelt

Provisions

The Act authorised $500 million in loans to local housing authorities for slum clearance and the construction of dwellings for low-income families, tied to the demolition of an equivalent number of unsafe dwellings. Rents were to be set at levels affordable to the poorest tenants, with federal annual contributions covering the gap between rents and operating costs. Unlike the earlier Public Works Administration housing programme, construction and management were decentralised to local authorities.

Legacy

The Act launched the construction of hundreds of thousands of public housing units. It was substantially reshaped by later legislation, notably the Housing Act of 1949 and the Housing and Community Development Act of 1974, which created the Section 8 rental assistance programme within the 1937 Act’s framework. The Faircloth Amendment (1999) capped the number of public housing units at their 1999 levels.
