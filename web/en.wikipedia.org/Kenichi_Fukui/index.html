Kenichi Fukui (福井 謙一, 4 October 1918 – 9 January 1998) was a Japanese chemist, known as the first Asian person to receive the Nobel Prize in Chemistry, which he shared in 1981 with Roald Hoffmann for their theories of chemical reactivity.

Work

In 1952 Fukui proposed that chemical reactions are governed by the interaction of the frontier orbitals of the reacting molecules — the highest occupied (HOMO) and lowest unoccupied (LUMO) molecular orbitals. Long overlooked in the West, frontier molecular orbital theory became a cornerstone of theoretical chemistry; Hoffmann independently developed the related Woodward–Hoffmann rules for pericyclic reactions.

Life

Fukui spent his career at Kyoto University, where he was professor of physical chemistry, and served as president of Kyoto Institute of Technology.
