Strict liability (criminal)

Strict liability
Field: Criminal law
Mens rea: Not required

In criminal law, strict liability is liability imposed without proof of fault — the prosecution need not show intent, knowledge, recklessness or negligence (mens rea) as to one or more elements of the offence; it is enough to prove the prohibited act (actus reus).

Scope

Strict liability offences are usually regulatory and minor: selling alcohol to minors, many traffic and food-safety offences, pollution and statutory rape in some jurisdictions. Courts generally presume Parliament or a legislature intends mens rea unless a statute clearly displaces it.

Criticism

Critics argue strict liability can punish the blameless; defenders reply it encourages high standards of care in activities affecting public welfare. Related concepts include absolute liability and vicarious liability.
