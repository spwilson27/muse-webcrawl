Butterfat

Butterfat or milkfat is the fatty portion of milk. Milk and cream are often sold according to their butterfat content, which in whole cow's milk is typically about 3–4 per cent; butter itself is around 80 per cent butterfat.

Composition

Butterfat consists of triglycerides of fatty acids including relatively high proportions of short-chain and saturated fatty acids such as butyric and palmitic acid, together with smaller amounts of unsaturated acids. The fat occurs in milk as microscopic globules surrounded by membranes of phospholipids and proteins.

Uses and measurement

Butterfat is churned into butter, concentrated as ghee or anhydrous milk fat, and determines the richness of cream, cheese and ice cream. Its content in milk is measured by methods such as the Gerber and Babcock tests, and dairy farmers are commonly paid partly on butterfat yield.
