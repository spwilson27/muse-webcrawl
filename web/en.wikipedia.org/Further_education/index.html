Further education

Further education (often abbreviated FE) in the United Kingdom and Ireland is additional education to that received at secondary school that is distinct from the higher education (HE) offered in universities and other academic institutions. It may be at any level in compulsory secondary education, from entry to higher level qualifications such as awards, certificates, diplomas, and other vocational, competency-based qualifications (including those previously known as NVQs) through awarding organisations.

Further education colleges in England offer courses to students over 16, including A-levels, T Levels, apprenticeships, vocational programmes such as BTECs, and basic skills and adult education courses. Since 2011 participation in education or training has been compulsory in England until the age of 18, though not necessarily in a school setting, and FE colleges enrol a large share of 16- to 18-year-olds.

Provision and funding

The sector includes general further education colleges, sixth form colleges, land-based and specialist colleges, and independent training providers. Funding in England comes principally through the Education and Skills Funding Agency for younger students and through devolved adult education budgets, while Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland run their own arrangements. In the Republic of Ireland, further education and training is coordinated by SOLAS and delivered through Education and Training Boards.

The term is used differently in some other countries: in the United States and Canada the corresponding concept is generally "continuing education" or post-secondary vocational education, often delivered by community colleges.
