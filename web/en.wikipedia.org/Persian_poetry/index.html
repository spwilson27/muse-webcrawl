Persian poetry

Persian poetry (Persian: شعر فارسی) refers to the body of poetry written in the Persian language, one of the richest and most influential poetic traditions in world literature. It flourished from the 9th century onward in Persia (modern Iran) and spread across Central Asia, Anatolia, and the Indian subcontinent.

History

After the Arab conquest of Persia, Persian re-emerged as a literary language in the 9th and 10th centuries under the Samanid dynasty. Rudaki is often regarded as the father of Persian poetry. Ferdowsi's epic Shahnameh (Book of Kings), completed around 1010 CE, preserved Persian language and myth and remains its most celebrated work.

Major poets

Classical masters include Omar Khayyam, famed for his quatrains (rubaiyat); Rumi, whose Masnavi is a cornerstone of Sufi literature; Saadi, author of the Gulistan and Bustan; Hafez, whose lyric ghazals are beloved throughout Iran; Attar; and Jami. Persian poetic forms — the ghazal, masnavi, qasida, and ruba'i — influenced Ottoman Turkish, Urdu, and other literatures.
