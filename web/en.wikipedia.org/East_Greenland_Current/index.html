East Greenland Current

The East Greenland Current is a cold, low-salinity ocean current that flows southward along the east coast of Greenland from the Arctic Ocean through the Fram Strait and the Denmark Strait toward Cape Farewell at Greenland's southern tip. It forms a major part of the circulation of the Greenland Sea and the subpolar North Atlantic.

Properties

The current carries large quantities of sea ice and fresh water south from the Arctic, including much of the ice exported through Fram Strait. Its waters come from Arctic surface water, Pacific inflow through the Bering Strait, river runoff, and melting ice, making it fresher and colder than the neighbouring Atlantic waters.

Importance

Because it supplies fresh water to the regions where deep water forms in the North Atlantic, the East Greenland Current is an important component of the Atlantic meridional overturning circulation. Changes in its ice and freshwater export, driven by Arctic warming, are monitored for their potential effects on ocean circulation and climate.
