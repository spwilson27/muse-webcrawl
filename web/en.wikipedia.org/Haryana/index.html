Haryana

Haryana is a state in northern India, carved out of the former state of Punjab on linguistic grounds on 1 November 1966. It surrounds the national capital territory of Delhi on three sides and shares its capital, Chandigarh, with Punjab. Haryana is one of India's wealthiest and most economically developed states, with major agricultural and industrial sectors.

Geography

The state lies largely in the fertile Indo-Gangetic plain, watered by the Yamuna river on its eastern border and the Ghaggar in the north, with the Aravalli hills in the south. The climate is continental, with hot summers, a monsoon season, and cool winters.

Economy

Haryana is a leading producer of wheat, rice, and milk, and its southwestern districts near Delhi — Gurugram in particular — have become major centres for information technology, automobiles, and manufacturing. Maruti Suzuki established its first plant in Gurugram, and the city remains a hub for the auto industry.

History and culture

The region includes Kurukshetra, revered as the battlefield of the Mahabharata, and was the site of the three battles of Panipat (1526, 1556, 1761) that shaped Indian history. Haryanvi, a group of Hindi dialects, is widely spoken.
