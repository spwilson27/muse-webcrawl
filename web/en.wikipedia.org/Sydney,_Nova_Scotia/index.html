Sydney, Nova Scotia

Image placeholder: the Big Fiddle on Sydney's waterfront.

Sydney is a former city and urban community on the east coast of Cape Breton Island in Nova Scotia, Canada, situated on Sydney Harbour. Since 1995 it has formed part of the Cape Breton Regional Municipality. Sydney was founded in 1785 by British Loyalists and served as the capital of the Colony of Cape Breton Island until 1820, when the colony was merged into Nova Scotia.

History and industry

The city grew into one of Canada's main steelmaking centres after the Dominion Iron and Steel Company opened its plant in 1901, drawing on nearby coalfields worked since the 18th century. The steel plant and coal mines dominated the local economy for most of the 20th century; their closure around 2001 forced a painful economic transition. The legacy of the industry includes the Sydney Tar Ponds, a major contaminated site that underwent a long remediation programme completed in the 2010s.

Today

Sydney is the largest urban centre on Cape Breton Island and a port of call for cruise ships visiting the Cabot Trail region. Landmarks include the world's largest fiddle and bow on the waterfront, honouring Cape Breton's Celtic musical heritage. The community shares its name with the much larger Sydney, Australia.
