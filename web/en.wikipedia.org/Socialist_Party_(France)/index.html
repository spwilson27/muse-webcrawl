The Socialist Party (French: Parti socialiste, PS) is a social-democratic political party in France. Founded in its modern form in 1969 and refounded at the 1971 Épinay Congress under François Mitterrand, it was for decades one of France's two dominant parties, alternating in power with the centre-right.

History

The PS descends from the French Section of the Workers' International (SFIO, founded 1905). Mitterrand united most of the non-communist left, won the presidency in 1981 — the left's first victory under the Fifth Republic — and served until 1995, enacting abolition of the death penalty, decentralisation, retirement at 60 and the fifth week of paid holidays, before a turn to austerity in 1983. Lionel Jospin served as prime minister (1997–2002) in "cohabitation" with President Jacques Chirac. François Hollande won the presidency in 2012.

After 2017, squeezed between Emmanuel Macron's centrism and Jean-Luc Mélenchon's left, the party collapsed to under 2% in the 2022 presidential vote (Anne Hidalgo) but rebuilt locally and through alliances: it joined the NUPES coalition (2022) and the New Popular Front (2024), remaining a major force in local government and the Senate.

Organisation

The party is led by a First Secretary (Olivier Faure since 2018) and is famous for its organised internal currents ("motions") voted on at congresses. Its symbol is the red rose in a fist, adopted in the 1970s. It is a member of the Party of European Socialists and the Progressive Alliance.
