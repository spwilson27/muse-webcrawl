Centenarian

A centenarian is a person who has reached the age of 100 years. Because life expectancy has risen globally, the number of centenarians has increased substantially; the United Nations estimated there were more than half a million centenarians worldwide in the early 21st century, with numbers projected to keep growing.

Demographics

Centenarians are far more common among women than men; in most countries women account for roughly four-fifths or more of people aged 100 and over. Japan has the highest proportion of centenarians of any large country, with Okinawa frequently cited in studies of longevity. The United States has the largest absolute number of centenarians.

Research

Studies such as the New England Centenarian Study examine genetics, lifestyle, and disease resistance in the very old. Many centenarians remain free of major age-related diseases until very late in life, a pattern described as the "compression of morbidity." People who reach 110 years are termed supercentenarians; the longest verified human lifespan is that of Jeanne Calment of France, who died in 1997 aged 122 years and 164 days.

Cultural recognition

In several countries centenarians receive official recognition: congratulatory messages from heads of state, traditional gifts in Japan, and in some places a place in local celebration and media coverage of their birthdays.
