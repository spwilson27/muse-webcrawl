Washington Heights is a neighbourhood in the northern part of the New York City borough of Manhattan. It is bordered by the Harlem River to the east and the Hudson River to the west, extending roughly from 155th Street in the south to Dyckman Street in the north. Named for Fort Washington, a fortified position held by the Continental Army during the American Revolutionary War, the area occupies some of the highest natural ground on Manhattan island.

History

The Battle of Fort Washington in November 1776 ended in a British victory and the capture of the fort's garrison. The Morris–Jumel Mansion (1765), Manhattan's oldest surviving house, served as Washington's headquarters. In the 19th century the area was rural estates; the naturalist John James Audubon lived there, and Audubon Terrace later became a cultural complex. The George Washington Bridge, opened in 1931, linked the neighbourhood to New Jersey. In the 1930s many German Jewish refugees settled in the area around 181st Street, earning it the nickname "Frankfurt on the Hudson".

Demographics and culture

From the 1960s onward Washington Heights became home to one of the largest Dominican communities outside the Dominican Republic, and it is often called the centre of Dominican culture in the United States. Spanish is widely spoken, and the area's commercial streets, especially St. Nicholas Avenue and Broadway, are lined with Dominican restaurants and businesses. The neighbourhood is also home to Yeshiva University, the Columbia University Irving Medical Center and NewYork-Presbyterian Hospital. Fort Tryon Park contains The Cloisters, the medieval art branch of the Metropolitan Museum of Art. The musical In the Heights (2005), by Lin-Manuel Miranda and Quiara Alegría Hudes, is set in the neighbourhood.
