Prithvi Narayan Shah

Prithvi Narayan Shah
Born: 7 January 1723, Gorkha, Nepal
Died: 11 January 1775, Devighat, Nuwakot
Reign: King of Gorkha (1743–1768); first King of Nepal (1768–1775)

Prithvi Narayan Shah (1723–1775) was the last ruler of the Kingdom of Gorkha and the first monarch of the unified Kingdom of Nepal. He is credited with launching the unification campaign that brought the many small principalities of the Himalayan region together into a single kingdom, and he is honoured in Nepal as the founder of the modern nation.

Early life

Prithvi Narayan was born in the Gorkha palace, the eldest son of King Nara Bhupal Shah. He ascended the throne of Gorkha in 1743 at the age of about twenty, inheriting a small hill state situated strategically between the Kathmandu Valley and the trade routes to Tibet.

Unification campaign

Beginning with the capture of Nuwakot in 1744, Prithvi Narayan spent more than two decades conquering and annexing neighbouring states. After several attempts and sieges he conquered the three Malla kingdoms of the Kathmandu Valley — Kathmandu (1768), Lalitpur and Bhaktapur (1769) — and moved his capital to Kathmandu, laying the foundation of the Kingdom of Nepal. His forces also expanded westward and into the eastern hills before his death.

Policies and legacy

His counsels, recorded as the Divyopadesh ("Divine Counsel"), set out advice on governance, foreign policy — famously describing Nepal as "a yam between two boulders", China and British India — and the preservation of the kingdom's independence. The Shah dynasty he founded ruled Nepal, with interruptions, until the monarchy was abolished in 2008. His birthday is celebrated in Nepal as National Unity Day (Prithvi Jayanti).
