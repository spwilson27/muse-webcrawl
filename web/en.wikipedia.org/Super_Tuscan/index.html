Super Tuscan

Super Tuscans (or Super Tuscans / Supertoscani) is a term used since the early 1980s to describe a category of high-quality Tuscan wines that do not conform to the traditional rules of the region's DOC and DOCG appellations, often because they use non-traditional grape varieties or blends.

History

The movement is generally traced to Sassicaia, a Bordeaux-style wine made by the Marchese Mario Incisa della Rocchetta at Tenuta San Guido in Bolgheri, first released commercially in 1968. In the 1970s producers such as Piero Antinori created wines like Tignanello (first released in 1971), which blended Sangiovese with Cabernet Sauvignon and omitted the white grapes then required by Chianti Classico rules. Because they fell outside the appellation regulations, these wines had to be labelled with the lowly vino da tavola designation despite commanding higher prices than most DOCG wines.

Regulatory response

The success of the Super Tuscans prompted reforms: the Indicazione Geografica Tipica (IGT) category was created in 1992, allowing the wines a more prestigious classification as Toscana IGT, and Bolgheri received its own DOC. Chianti Classico rules were also changed to permit 100% Sangiovese wines and the use of international varieties.

Notable wines

Well-known Super Tuscans include Sassicaia, Tignanello, Ornellaia, Masseto, and Solaia. They are typically among Italy's most expensive and internationally acclaimed wines.
