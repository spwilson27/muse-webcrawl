WRN (gene)

WRN (Werner syndrome RecQ helicase)
Species: Human
Chromosome: 8p12
Protein: DNA helicase with 3'–5' exonuclease activity
Associated disease: Werner syndrome

WRN is a human gene that encodes the Werner syndrome ATP-dependent helicase, a member of the RecQ family of DNA helicases. The protein is unusual among helicases in also possessing a 3′ to 5′ exonuclease activity. It functions in DNA replication, repair, recombination, telomere maintenance, and the cellular response to DNA damage.

Function

The WRN protein helps unwind unusual DNA structures such as G-quadruplexes and Holliday junctions, processes stalled replication forks, and participates in base excision repair and non-homologous end joining. It interacts with proteins including p53, Ku, PCNA, and components of the telomere shelterin complex (notably TRF2), contributing to the stability of chromosome ends during replication.

Werner syndrome

Loss-of-function mutations in WRN cause Werner syndrome, an autosomal recessive adult-onset progeroid disorder characterised by premature ageing: short stature, early greying and loss of hair, bilateral cataracts, type 2 diabetes, atherosclerosis, osteoporosis, and a sharply increased risk of cancer — particularly sarcomas — with death commonly in the fifth or sixth decade. Cells from Werner syndrome patients show genomic instability, accelerated telomere shortening, and a characteristic pattern of chromosomal rearrangements called variegated translocation mosaicism.

Research significance

Because WRN deficiency mimics many features of normal ageing at an accelerated pace, the gene is intensively studied in ageing biology. More recently, cancer cells with microsatellite instability have been shown to depend on WRN, making the helicase a promising drug target, and WRN inhibitors have entered development.
