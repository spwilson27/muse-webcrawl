James Bay

James Bay (French: Baie James) is a large body of water on the southern end of Hudson Bay in northeastern Canada, between the provinces of Quebec (east) and Ontario (west). It is about 443 km long and 217 km wide, and is named after Captain Thomas James, who explored the area in 1631.

Geography and ecology

The bay is shallow and brackish, fed by major rivers including the La Grande, Eastmain, Rupert, Harricana, Moose and Albany. Its coastal marshes and tidal flats are internationally important stopover habitat for migratory waterfowl and shorebirds, especially snow geese and Canada geese. Akimiski Island is the largest of many islands in the bay. The region is the traditional homeland of the Cree peoples, whose communities line both shores.

History

Henry Hudson wintered in the bay in 1610–11 before his crew mutinied. The Hudson's Bay Company, chartered in 1670, established fur-trading posts such as Moose Factory and Fort Rupert around its shores.

Hydroelectric development

The James Bay Project, launched by Quebec in 1971, dammed the La Grande and neighbouring rivers to build one of the world's largest hydroelectric complexes. The 1975 James Bay and Northern Quebec Agreement with the Cree and Inuit was Canada's first modern land-claims settlement. The project reshaped the region's rivers and remains central to debates on Indigenous rights and environmental change.
