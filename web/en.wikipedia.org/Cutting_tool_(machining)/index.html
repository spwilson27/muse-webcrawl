Cutting tool (machining)

Indexable carbide inserts and solid end mills, common cutting tools for milling machines.

In the context of machining, a cutting tool or cutter is any tool that is used to remove material from a workpiece by means of shear deformation. Cutting may be accomplished by single-point or multipoint tools. Single-point tools are used in turning, shaping, planing and similar operations, and remove material by means of one cutting edge. Milling and drilling tools are often multipoint; grinding tools are composed of many tiny abrasive grains.

Geometry

Every cutting tool has one or more sharp cutting edges and two surfaces: the rake face, over which the chip flows, and the flank, which clears the newly cut surface. Key angles include the rake angle, clearance angle, and the wedge angle between the faces. Geometry is chosen to balance cutting forces, chip evacuation, surface finish and tool life; a built-up edge may form on the rake face when cutting ductile materials at low speeds.

Materials

Cutting tool materials must be harder than the workpiece at the cutting temperature. Common classes include:

  • Carbon and high-speed steels — tough and cheap, but soften above about 600 °C.
  • Cemented carbides — tungsten carbide grains in a cobalt binder; the workhorse of modern machining, often coated with TiN, TiCN or Al2O3 layers.
  • Ceramics — alumina and silicon nitride tools for high-speed finishing of hard materials.
  • Cubic boron nitride (CBN) — for hardened steels; second in hardness only to diamond.
  • Polycrystalline diamond (PCD) — for abrasive non-ferrous materials and composites.

Tool wear and life

Tools degrade by flank wear, crater wear, chipping and plastic deformation. The relationship between cutting speed and tool life is commonly described by Taylor's tool life equation, VTn = C, formulated by F. W. Taylor in the early twentieth century. Coolants and lubricants extend life by reducing friction and removing heat from the cutting zone.

Types

Major categories include lathe tools and boring bars (single-point), drills, reamers, taps, end mills, face mills, gear cutters, broaches, saws and abrasive wheels. Modern CNC machines load cutters automatically from tool magazines using standardised holders such as CAT, BT and HSK tapers.
