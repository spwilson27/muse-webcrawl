Santos Futebol Clube, commonly known as Santos, is a Brazilian sports club based in Vila Belmiro, a district of the port city of Santos in São Paulo state. Founded on 14 April 1912, its football team is one of Brazil's most storied clubs, renowned worldwide as the home of Pelé and, later, of Neymar.

The Pelé era

Santos rose to global fame in the late 1950s and 1960s with a dazzling side built around Pelé, Coutinho, Pepe and Gilmar. The club won six Brazilian titles in that decade, consecutive Copa Libertadores and Intercontinental Cups in 1962 and 1963 — beating Benfica and Milan — and toured the world so relentlessly that its exhibitions were credited with briefly halting the Biafran war in Nigeria in 1969. The team surpassed 10,000 goals in its history faster than any other club, earning the nickname Peixe ("Fish") from the city's maritime heritage.

Later years

Santos won further national titles in 2002 and 2004 and, with the teenage Neymar and Ganso, a third Libertadores in 2011 before losing the FIFA Club World Cup final to Barcelona. The club's academy, the Meninos da Vila, is famed for producing Brazilian internationals. A severe financial and sporting crisis led to the club's first-ever relegation from Série A in December 2023, followed by immediate promotion back as Série B champions in 2024 and the return of Neymar in January 2025.

Stadium and rivals

Santos has played at the Estádio Urbano Caldeira, known as Vila Belmiro, since 1916. Its principal rivalries are the Clássico da Saudade against Palmeiras, the San-São against São Paulo FC, and matches with Corinthians.
