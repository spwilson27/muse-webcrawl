H.323

H.323, the ITU-T umbrella standard for packet-based multimedia communication.

H.323 is a recommendation from the ITU-T that defines protocols for real-time audio, video, and data communication over packet-based networks, including IP networks. First approved in 1996, it was one of the earliest standards for voice over IP (VoIP) and videoconferencing.

Architecture

An H.323 system consists of terminals (endpoints), gateways (connecting to other networks such as the public telephone network), multipoint control units (MCUs) for conferences, and gatekeepers, which provide address translation, admission control, and bandwidth management for a "zone." Signalling uses the H.225.0 RAS and call-control protocols and H.245 for media capability negotiation, with audio and video transported over RTP.

History and use

H.323 grew out of ISDN videoconferencing standards (H.320) and was widely deployed in enterprise videoconferencing systems and early VoIP carrier networks in the late 1990s and 2000s. Although the simpler, text-based Session Initiation Protocol (SIP) has largely displaced it in new deployments, H.323 equipment remains in use in many organisations, and the standard was revised repeatedly, with version 8 published in 2022.
