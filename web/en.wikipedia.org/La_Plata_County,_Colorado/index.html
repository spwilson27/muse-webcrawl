La Plata County is a county located in the southwestern corner of the U.S. state of Colorado. The county seat and most populous city is Durango. The county is named for the La Plata River and the La Plata Mountains; la plata is Spanish for "the silver".

Geography

The county covers roughly 1,700 square miles of mountainous terrain in the San Juan Mountains and high desert. Elevations range from about 6,000 feet in the south to over 13,000 feet at peaks in the north. The Animas River flows south through Durango.

History

The area was home to Ancestral Puebloan peoples and later the Ute. La Plata County was created by the Colorado territorial legislature in 1874. Mining, the Denver and Rio Grande Railroad (which reached Durango in 1881), ranching and later tourism shaped its economy. The Southern Ute Indian Reservation occupies the southern part of the county.

Communities

  • Durango (county seat)
  • Bayfield
  • Ignacio
  • Unincorporated communities including Hermosa and Hesperus

Recreation

The county is known for the Durango and Silverton Narrow Gauge Railroad, Purgatory Resort skiing, and access to the San Juan National Forest.
