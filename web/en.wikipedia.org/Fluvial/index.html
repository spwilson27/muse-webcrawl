Fluvial

In geography and geology, fluvial refers to the processes associated with rivers and streams and the deposits and landforms created by them. When a stream creates landforms — by eroding sediment in one place and depositing it in another — those features are described as fluvial. The term derives from the Latin fluvius, meaning river.

Fluvial processes

The main fluvial processes are erosion, transportation and deposition. Running water erodes its bed and banks by hydraulic action, abrasion and solution; carries the load in solution, suspension and by traction along the bed; and deposits sediment when its velocity and competence fall.

Fluvial landforms

Characteristic landforms include V-shaped valleys, waterfalls, meanders, oxbow lakes, levees, floodplains and deltas. The pattern a river network makes across a landscape is called its drainage pattern, and the evolution of fluvial landscapes over geological time is studied within geomorphology.

Sediment and deposits

Sediment deposited by running water is called alluvium. Sorting of grains by size is a hallmark of fluvial deposits, which form important aquifers and placer deposits, and whose layering is studied by sedimentologists to reconstruct past environments.

Study

Fluvial geomorphology examines how river channels adjust their form to water and sediment supply, a balance commonly summarised by Lane's relation. Understanding fluvial processes is essential for flood management, river restoration and civil engineering.
