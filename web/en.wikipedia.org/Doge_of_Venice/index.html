The Doge of Venice (Venetian: Doxe de Venexia) was the chief magistrate and leader of the Republic of Venice between 726 and 1797. The word derives from the Latin dux, "leader". The doge was elected for life by the city-state's aristocracy, though in practice his powers were increasingly constrained by councils.

Over more than a thousand years, 120 men held the office. The doge combined the roles of head of state, military commander and religious figurehead, presiding over ceremonies such as the Festa della Sensa (the Marriage of the Sea).

Election

The doge was chosen through an elaborate multi-stage electoral system designed to prevent any single family from dominating. From 1268, a complex procedure involving successive rounds of sortition (lottery) and voting among members of the Great Council selected an electoral college of 41, who then elected the doge — a system admired by later political theorists as a guard against corruption.

Powers and constraints

Early doges wielded near-monarchical power, but constitutional reforms progressively limited the office. By the late Middle Ages the doge governed alongside the Minor Council, the Senate and the Council of Ten; he could not open dispatches alone, leave the Doge's Palace without permission, or own property abroad. The promissione ducale, an oath sworn at election, enumerated these restrictions.

Regalia

The doge's distinctive insignia included the corno ducale, a stiff horn-shaped cap worn over a linen coif, and robes of crimson velvet trimmed with ermine. He carried no sword, symbolizing that his authority was civil rather than military.

Notable doges

  • Enrico Dandolo (1192–1205) — led the Fourth Crusade's diversion to Constantinople
  • Francesco Foscari (1423–1457) — presided over Venice's mainland expansion
  • Andrea Gritti (1523–1538) — Renaissance patron
  • Ludovico Manin (1789–1797) — the last doge, who abdicated before Napoleon

End of the office

The office was abolished in 1797 when Napoleon dissolved the Republic of Venice. The title survives in the similarly named office of the Doge of Genoa.
