Transilien is the brand name given to the commuter rail network operated by SNCF in the Île-de-France region of France, serving Paris and its suburbs under contract to Île-de-France Mobilités. The name is a play on Francilien, the demonym for residents of the region. The network derives from the suburban services that have radiated from Paris's mainline stations since the 19th century.

Network

Transilien services are organised by line and by the Paris terminus they serve:

  • Gare du Nord: lines H and K
  • Gare Saint-Lazare: lines J and L
  • Gare Montparnasse: line N
  • Gare de l'Est: line P
  • Gare de Lyon: line R
  • La Défense–La Verrière: line U

SNCF also operates RER lines C, D, and E in full, and shares operation of lines A and B with the RATP. Transilien trains generally do not cross central Paris (unlike the RER), terminating at the historic mainline stations, with the notable exception of services through the RER tunnels.

Rolling stock and operations

The fleet includes the double-deck Z 2N family, the Francilien (Z 50000) single-level trains introduced from 2009, and newer Regio 2N and RER NG stock on shared lines. Services run from about 5 a.m. to 1 a.m., with frequencies from a few trains per day on rural branches to a train every few minutes on busy inner-suburban sections.

History

Paris suburban rail dates to the 1837 line to Le Pecq, the first railway in the Paris region. The Transilien brand was created in 1999, accompanied by minimum service standards for cleanliness, information, and rolling-stock refurbishment. Since 2021, Île-de-France Mobilités has been progressively opening Transilien lines to competitive tender under EU rules.
