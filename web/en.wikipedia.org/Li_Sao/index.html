Li Sao

The Li Sao (Chinese: 離騷; pinyin: Lí Sāo; often translated "Encountering Sorrow" or "On Encountering Trouble") is a Chinese poem from the anthology Chu Ci (Songs of Chu), traditionally attributed to Qu Yuan of the state of Chu during the Warring States period.

Content

Written in the first person, the poem recounts the speaker's noble birth, his cultivation of virtue (symbolised by fragrant plants), his slander and dismissal at court, and his fantastical journeys through the heavens in search of a worthy lord and a fair lady. Rich in shamanistic and mythological imagery, it protests the corruption of the age and the poet's frustrated loyalty.

Significance

The Li Sao is the longest and most famous poem of the Chu Ci and one of the foundational works of Chinese literature, profoundly influencing later sao-style poetry. Qu Yuan's legendary death by drowning in the Miluo River is commemorated in the Dragon Boat Festival.
