Triangle

A triangle is a polygon with three corners and three sides, one of the basic shapes in geometry. The corners, also called vertices, are zero-dimensional points while the sides connecting them, also called edges or segments, are one-dimensional line segments. A triangle has three internal angles, each one bounded by a pair of adjacent edges; the sum of angles of a triangle always equals a straight angle (180 degrees). The triangle is a plane figure and its interior is a planar region, sometimes called a triangular region.

Types of triangle

Triangles can be classified by their sides: an equilateral triangle has three sides of equal length; an isosceles triangle has two sides of equal length; and a scalene triangle has all its sides of different lengths. They can also be classified by their angles: a right triangle has one angle of 90°, an obtuse triangle has one angle greater than 90°, and an acute triangle has all angles less than 90°.

Area

The area of a triangle is half the product of a base and the corresponding height: A = ½·b·h. Heron's formula gives the area from the three side lengths alone: A = √[s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c)], where s is the semiperimeter.

Centres

Several special points are defined for every triangle, including the centroid (intersection of the medians), the circumcenter (centre of the circumscribed circle), the incenter (centre of the inscribed circle), and the orthocenter (intersection of the altitudes).
