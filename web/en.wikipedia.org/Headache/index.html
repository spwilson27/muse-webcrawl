Headache

A headache is pain in the head, face or upper neck. It is among the most common complaints in medicine: most adults experience headaches each year, and headache disorders are a leading cause of disability worldwide. Headaches are classified as primary — where the headache itself is the disorder, as in migraine, tension-type headache and cluster headache — or secondary, where pain results from another condition such as infection, trauma, vascular disease or medication overuse.

Primary headaches

Migraine causes recurrent moderate-to-severe, often one-sided, pulsating attacks lasting 4–72 hours, frequently with nausea and sensitivity to light and sound; about a third of sufferers experience aura. Tension-type headache, the most common type, produces a bilateral pressing or tightening pain of mild-to-moderate intensity. Cluster headache is rare but excruciating, with attacks of severe unilateral pain around the eye accompanied by tearing and nasal congestion, occurring in clusters over weeks. Other primary types include chronic daily headache and new daily persistent headache.

Secondary headaches and warning signs

Secondary headaches may signal conditions ranging from sinusitis and caffeine withdrawal to meningitis, subarachnoid haemorrhage, giant cell arteritis or brain tumours. Clinicians screen for "red flags" — a thunderclap (sudden maximal) onset, fever with neck stiffness, new headache after age 50, progressive worsening, neurological deficits, or headache after head injury — which warrant urgent imaging or other investigation. Most headaches, however, need no imaging when the history and examination fit a primary disorder.

Mechanisms and treatment

The brain itself lacks pain receptors; headache pain arises from structures including blood vessels, meninges, muscles and nerves. Migraine involves activation of the trigeminovascular system and the neuropeptide CGRP. Acute treatment ranges from simple analgesics (paracetamol, ibuprofen) and triptans for migraine to oxygen and verapamil for cluster headache. Preventive options include beta blockers, amitriptyline, topiramate, onabotulinumtoxinA for chronic migraine, and CGRP monoclonal antibodies introduced from 2018. Overuse of acute medication can itself cause medication-overuse headache, so management emphasises correct diagnosis and limiting acute drug days.
