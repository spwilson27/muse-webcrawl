Ellora Caves

The Ellora Caves are a multi-religious rock-cut cave complex with inscriptions dating from the 6th century CE onwards, located in the Aurangabad district of Maharashtra, India. There are over 100 caves at the site, all excavated out of the vertical face of the Charanandri hills, 34 of which are open to public. These consist of 17 Hindu caves (caves 13–29), 12 Buddhist caves (caves 1–12) and 5 Jain caves (caves 30–34), built in proximity, demonstrating religious harmony prevalent during this period of Indian history.

Kailasa temple

The most remarkable of the cave temples is the Kailasa temple (Cave 16), named for Mount Kailash, the home of the god Shiva. It is notable for its vertical excavation — carvers started at the top of the original rock and dug downward — and is considered one of the most astonishing rock-cut temples in the world due to its size, architecture and sculptural treatment. It was built mainly during the reign of the Rashtrakuta king Krishna I (c. 756–773).

Recognition

The Ellora Caves were designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983.
