Pelopidas

Bornc. 420 BC, Thebes
Died364 BC, Cynoscephalae
OccupationTheban general and statesman
Known forVictory at Leuctra; Sacred Band of Thebes

Pelopidas (c. 420–364 BC) was an important Theban statesman and general in Greece, best known, alongside his close friend Epaminondas, for the Theban hegemony that followed the defeat of Sparta. He was the leader of the Sacred Band of Thebes and a central figure in the liberation of Thebes from Spartan occupation.

Life

Pelopidas came from a wealthy and distinguished Theban family but was known for giving away much of his fortune. Exiled when Sparta installed an oligarchy and garrison in Thebes in 382 BC, he returned in disguise in 379 BC and led the daring assassination of the pro-Spartan polemarchs, restoring democracy.

As boeotarch he repeatedly campaigned against Sparta. At the Battle of Tegyra (375 BC) his Sacred Band defeated a larger Spartan force—the first time the unit's reputation was made. At the Battle of Leuctra (371 BC), Pelopidas led the Sacred Band in the charge that broke the Spartan right wing and killed King Cleombrotus, shattering Spartan military prestige.

Pelopidas subsequently campaigned in Thessaly and Macedonia, where he was once captured by the tyrant Alexander of Pherae and rescued by Epaminondas. In 364 BC, while again fighting Alexander at the Battle of Cynoscephalae, Pelopidas won the battle but was killed rushing at the tyrant. Plutarch wrote his biography in the Parallel Lives.
