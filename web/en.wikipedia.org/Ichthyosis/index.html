Ichthyosis

Ichthyosis
SpecialtyDermatology
SymptomsDry, thickened, scaly skin
CausesUsually genetic

Ichthyosis is a family of genetic skin disorders characterised by dry, thickened, scaly skin. The name comes from the Greek ichthys, fish, referring to the fish-scale appearance of the skin. More than 50 types are recognised, most of them inherited.

Types

The most common form is ichthyosis vulgaris, usually caused by mutations in the filaggrin gene (FLG), affecting about 1 in 250–1,000 people. Other forms include X-linked ichthyosis, lamellar ichthyosis, epidermolytic ichthyosis, and the severe congenital harlequin-type ichthyosis.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms range from mild fine scaling, often on the legs, to severe generalised thickening with fissuring, ectropion, impaired sweating, and risk of infection and overheating. Many forms are present at birth, sometimes with a collodion membrane.

Treatment

There is no cure; management centres on regular emollients and moisturisers, keratolytics such as urea and salicylic acid, bathing, and in severe cases oral retinoids such as acitretin. Genetic counselling may be offered to affected families.

Other animals

Ichthyosis also occurs in domestic animals, notably in several dog breeds where genetic tests are available.
