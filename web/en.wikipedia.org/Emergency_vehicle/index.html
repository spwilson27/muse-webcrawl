Emergency vehicle

An emergency vehicle is a vehicle used by emergency services — police, fire and emergency medical services — to respond rapidly to incidents. Emergency vehicles are typically fitted with special visual and audible warning equipment, such as light bars and sirens, and in most jurisdictions are granted exemptions from certain traffic rules, for example proceeding through red lights or exceeding speed limits, when responding to an emergency, subject to duties of care.

Types

Common emergency vehicles include police cars and motorcycles, fire engines and other fire apparatus, ambulances, and specialist units such as rescue vehicles, hazardous materials units and coastguard and mountain rescue vehicles. Some services use unmarked vehicles fitted with discreet warning equipment.

Equipment and markings

To be conspicuous, vehicles use flashing lights in colours regulated by jurisdiction — blue in much of Europe, red and blue in North America — together with high-visibility liveries such as Battenburg markings, retroreflective striping, and sirens with several selectable tones. Two-way radios and mobile data terminals connect crews to dispatch systems.

Right of way

Road traffic laws generally require other road users to yield to emergency vehicles displaying lights and sirens, and systems such as emergency vehicle preemption can change traffic signals to give them a green light.
