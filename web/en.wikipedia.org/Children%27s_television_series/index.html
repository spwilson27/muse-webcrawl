Children's television series

A children's television series is a television program designed for and marketed to children, normally scheduled for broadcast during the morning and afternoon hours or, in the era of dedicated channels and streaming, at any time. Such programming spans animation, live action, educational shows, and puppetry.

History

Children's television began with early broadcasts such as the BBC's For the Children (1937) and American programs like Howdy Doody (1947). The debut of Sesame Street in 1969 transformed educational programming by applying research on child development and curriculum goals. Saturday-morning cartoon blocks dominated in the United States from the 1960s to the 1990s, before dedicated networks such as Nickelodeon, Cartoon Network, and the Disney Channel, and later streaming platforms, reshaped the field.

Regulation and education

Many countries regulate advertising to children and require educational content; in the United States the Children's Television Act (1990) obliges broadcasters to air educational programming for children.
