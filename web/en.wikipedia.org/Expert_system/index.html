Expert system

In artificial intelligence, an expert system is a computer system that emulates the decision-making ability of a human expert. Expert systems are designed to solve complex problems by reasoning through bodies of knowledge, represented mainly as if–then rules, rather than through conventional procedural code. They were among the first truly successful forms of AI software, developed from the 1960s and commercially prominent in the 1970s and 1980s.

Architecture

An expert system typically consists of a knowledge base, containing domain facts and rules elicited from human experts by knowledge engineers, and an inference engine that applies the rules to known data to reach conclusions, using strategies such as forward chaining (data-driven) and backward chaining (goal-driven). A user interface and an explanation facility — which can justify conclusions by tracing the rules fired — complete the system. Shells such as EMYCIN separated the inference machinery from the knowledge base so new domains could be built more quickly.

History and examples

Early landmark systems included DENDRAL (begun 1965), which inferred molecular structures from mass spectrometer data, and MYCIN (1970s), which diagnosed blood infections and recommended antibiotics at a level comparable to specialists. XCON (R1), deployed by Digital Equipment Corporation from 1980, configured VAX computer systems and saved the company millions of dollars annually, demonstrating commercial value. Expert systems were a focus of Japan's Fifth Generation Computer project and of substantial corporate investment.

Decline and legacy

Expert systems suffered from brittleness outside narrow domains, the difficulty and cost of acquiring and maintaining knowledge (the "knowledge acquisition bottleneck"), and inability to learn. By the early 1990s the field contracted during the AI winter. Nevertheless, their ideas persist in business rules engines, diagnostic and configuration software, clinical decision support, and modern approaches that combine rules with machine learning.
