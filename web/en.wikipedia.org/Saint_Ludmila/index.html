Saint Ludmila

Saint Ludmila (Czech: Svatá Ludmila; c. 860 – 15 September 921) was a Bohemian duchess and Christian martyr, the wife of Bořivoj I, the first Christian duke of Bohemia, and the grandmother and regent of Saint Wenceslaus. She is revered as the patron saint of Bohemia and is regarded as a foundational figure of Czech Christianity and statehood.

Life

Ludmila was born around 860, traditionally at Mělník, daughter of the Sorbian prince Slavibor. She married Bořivoj I, duke of Bohemia, around 874. The couple were baptised, according to tradition by Saint Methodius himself, and founded some of the earliest churches in Bohemia, including the church of St Clement at Levý Hradec and the church of Our Lady in Prague.

Saint Ludmila, depicted with the veil with which she was strangled.

Regency and death

After the deaths of her husband and sons, Ludmila became regent and guardian of her young grandson Wenceslaus in 921, sharing power — uneasily — with his mother Drahomíra, who favoured the pagan party. On the night of 15 September 921, assassins sent by Drahomíra's faction strangled Ludmila at her castle at Tetín, traditionally with her own veil, which became her principal attribute in art. She was buried at Tetín, and when Wenceslaus had her remains translated to St George's Basilica at Prague Castle in 925, reported miracles at her tomb led quickly to her veneration as a saint.

Legacy

Ludmila is the first Czech saint and a symbol of Czech national and Christian identity. Her feast day is 16 September. The oratorio Svatá Ludmila by Antonín Dvořák celebrates her story, and churches across the Czech lands are dedicated to her, including the prominent Church of St Ludmila in Prague's Vinohrady district.
