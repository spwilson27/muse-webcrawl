Barbary Wars

The Barbary Wars were a series of two wars fought by the United States and Sweden against the Barbary states of North Africa — Tripoli, Algiers, Tunis, and Morocco — in the early 19th century. The First Barbary War (1801–1805) and the Second Barbary War (1815) ended the practice of American merchant ships paying tribute to avoid seizure by Barbary corsairs.

Background

For centuries the Barbary states had exacted tribute from European powers in return for safe passage of shipping. After independence, the United States lost British protection and paid escalating tributes; by 1800 the payments constituted a substantial share of the federal budget. President Thomas Jefferson refused Tripoli's demand for increased tribute, prompting Yusuf Karamanli to declare war in 1801.

Conflicts

The First Barbary War saw the burning of the captured frigate USS Philadelphia by Stephen Decatur and the march of William Eaton's force to Derna in 1805, commemorated in the Marine Corps hymn's "shores of Tripoli". The Second Barbary War, fought under James Madison in 1815 under Decatur and William Bainbridge, forced Algiers to end piracy against American ships, followed by similar treaties with Tunis and Tripoli.
