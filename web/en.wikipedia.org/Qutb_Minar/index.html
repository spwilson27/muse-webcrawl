The Qutb Minar (also spelled Qutub Minar or Qutab Minar) is a minaret and victory tower in the Mehrauli area of South Delhi, India. At 72.5 metres (238 feet) high, it is the tallest brick minaret in the world and the tallest structure in Delhi. It forms part of the Qutb complex, a UNESCO World Heritage Site that also includes the Quwwat-ul-Islam Mosque, the Iron Pillar of Delhi, and the Alai Darwaza.

History

Construction of the tower is believed to have begun around 1199 under Qutb-ud-din Aibak, founder of the Mamluk dynasty of the Delhi Sultanate, who completed the first storey. His successor and son-in-law Iltutmish added three further storeys around 1220. The fifth and final storey was added after lightning damaged the tower in 1369, when Firoz Shah Tughlaq repaired it and rebuilt the top levels in marble and sandstone.

The tower commemorated the victory of Muhammad of Ghor over the Rajput ruler Prithviraj Chauhan in 1192 and marked the beginning of Muslim rule in northern India. Its name is generally derived from Qutb-ud-din Aibak, though some associate it with the Sufi saint Qutbuddin Bakhtiar Kaki.

Architecture

The Qutb Minar is built primarily of red sandstone, with the upper storeys faced in marble. It has five distinct storeys, each marked by a projecting balcony supported on muqarnas corbels. The tower tapers from a diameter of about 14.3 metres at the base to about 2.7 metres at the top. Its surfaces are carved with intricate inscriptions in Arabic calligraphy, including verses from the Qur'an, and ornate geometric and floral decoration. The first three storeys have differing fluting patterns: the first alternates angular and rounded flutes, the second is round, and the third is star-shaped.

A spiral staircase of 379 steps rises inside the tower. Public access to the interior was closed after a stampede accident in 1981.

The Qutb complex

Surrounding monuments include the Quwwat-ul-Islam Mosque (begun 1193), the earliest mosque built in Delhi after the Ghurid conquest; the Iron Pillar, a 7-metre Gupta-era column famous for its corrosion resistance; the Alai Darwaza gateway (1311); the unfinished Alai Minar; and the tombs of Iltutmish and Imam Zamin.
