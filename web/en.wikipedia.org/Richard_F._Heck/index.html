Richard F. Heck


Richard F. Heck (1931–2015)

Richard Fred Heck (August 15, 1931 – October 9, 2015) was an American chemist credited with the discovery and development of the Heck reaction, a palladium-catalysed coupling that forms a carbon–carbon bond between an aryl or vinyl halide and an alkene. He shared the 2010 Nobel Prize in Chemistry with Ei-ichi Negishi and Akira Suzuki "for palladium-catalyzed cross couplings in organic synthesis".

Education and career

Heck was born in Springfield, Massachusetts, and studied at the University of California, Los Angeles, earning his PhD in 1954 under Saul Winstein. After postdoctoral work in Zürich and at UCLA, he worked at the Hercules Powder Company in Delaware, where in the late 1960s he carried out the foundational experiments on palladium-catalysed reactions of organomercury compounds and then of aryl halides with alkenes. In 1971 he joined the University of Delaware, where he remained until his retirement in 1989, systematically developing the reaction that bears his name. He later lived in the Philippines with his wife, Socorro, and died in Manila in 2015.

The Heck reaction

The Heck reaction couples an unsaturated halide or triflate with an alkene in the presence of a palladium catalyst and a base to give a substituted alkene. Published in practical form by Heck in the 1970s (with related work by Tsutomu Mizoroki in Japan), it became one of the most widely used methods for constructing carbon frameworks in pharmaceutical and fine-chemical synthesis, and a cornerstone of the cross-coupling chemistry recognised by the 2010 Nobel Prize.
