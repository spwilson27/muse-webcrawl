Staggers Rail Act

The Staggers Rail Act of 1980 is a United States federal law that deregulated the American railroad industry to a significant extent, and it marked the first major change to the regulatory framework established by the Interstate Commerce Act of 1887. The act was named for its sponsor, Congressman Harley O. Staggers of West Virginia, and was signed into law by President Jimmy Carter on October 14, 1980.

Provisions

The act allowed railroads to set rates for most traffic without prior approval of the Interstate Commerce Commission, permitted confidential contracts between railroads and shippers, eased restrictions on abandoning unprofitable lines, and facilitated mergers. The ICC retained oversight where a railroad was found to have market dominance over particular traffic.

Effects

Deregulation is widely credited with revitalizing an industry that had been in steep decline, with several major railroads bankrupt in the 1970s. After 1980, railroads shed excess trackage, improved productivity, and regained freight market share, while real freight rates fell substantially even as industry profitability recovered. The rail network shrank in mileage but carried far more ton-miles per mile of track. Critics note that consolidation left some captive shippers facing high rates, a tension later addressed in part by the Surface Transportation Board, successor to the ICC after 1995.
