The Battle of Tarawa was fought in the Pacific Theater of World War II from November 20 to 23, 1943, on Betio Island in the Tarawa Atoll of the Gilbert Islands. United States Marine Corps forces landed on the tiny island and overwhelmed its Japanese defenders after some of the fiercest fighting of the Pacific war. It was the first American offensive in the critical central Pacific sector.

Background

To establish airfields within range of the Marshall Islands, the U.S. needed the Gilberts. Betio, at the southern end of Tarawa Atoll, held a Japanese airstrip defended by elite naval landing troops with extensive concrete bunkers, pillboxes and coastal guns.

The battle

A heavy naval and air bombardment preceded the landing, but unexpectedly low tides left many landing craft stuck on the reef, forcing Marines to wade ashore under machine-gun fire. Despite heavy losses, the Marines established a foothold, and over three days systematically destroyed the strongpoints with tanks, flamethrowers and demolition charges. The island was declared secure on November 23.

Aftermath

The high cost of taking a small island — about 1,000 Marines killed in 76 hours — shocked the American public but produced hard lessons in amphibious warfare: longer preliminary bombardments, better tide reconnaissance, and more tracked landing vehicles, all applied in later operations. Photographs and film from Tarawa brought the reality of the Pacific war home.
