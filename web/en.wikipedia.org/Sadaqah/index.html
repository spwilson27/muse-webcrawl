Sadaqah

A sadaqah box: voluntary charity is a central virtue in Islam.

Sadaqah (Arabic: صدقة, "charity", "benevolence", plural ṣadaqāt) in the modern context has come to signify voluntary charity in Islam. The term stems from the Arabic root word sidq (صدق), meaning sincerity, and implies giving out of compassion, love, friendship, or generosity.

Distinction from zakat

Sadaqah differs from zakat, the obligatory almsgiving that is one of the Five Pillars of Islam and is calculated as a fixed proportion of a Muslim's wealth. Sadaqah is voluntary and may be given at any time and in any amount. Islamic tradition holds that sadaqah need not be monetary at all: according to a well-known hadith, "every act of kindness is a form of sadaqah," including smiling at another person, removing an obstacle from a road, or planting a tree from which people and animals benefit.

Types and significance

A perpetual form, sadaqah jariyah ("flowing charity"), includes works whose benefit continues after the giver's death, such as endowing a well or school, funding education, or establishing a waqf (charitable endowment). Believers commonly give sadaqah seeking God's blessing, expiation of sins, or protection from calamity, and in gratitude or on behalf of deceased relatives. Friday giving and charity during Ramadan are especially emphasized.
