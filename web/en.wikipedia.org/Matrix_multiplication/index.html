Matrix multiplication

Binary operation producing a matrix from two matrices

In mathematics, particularly in linear algebra, matrix multiplication is a binary operation that produces a matrix from two matrices. For two matrices A (of size m × n) and B (of size n × p), the product C = AB is an m × p matrix whose entry cij is the dot product of the i-th row of A with the j-th column of B: cij = Σk aikbkj. The product is defined only when the number of columns of A equals the number of rows of B.

Properties

Matrix multiplication represents the composition of linear maps, and correspondingly it is associative and distributes over addition, but it is not commutative: in general AB ≠ BA, and one of the two products may be undefined while the other is not. The identity matrix acts as a neutral element. Scalar multiplication and the ordinary product of numbers are special cases.

Algorithms

The naive algorithm takes O(n³) operations for square matrices. Strassen's algorithm (1969) first showed this could be beaten, reducing the exponent to about 2.807; subsequent methods, from Coppersmith–Winograd to recent work, have pushed the exponent below 2.372. In practice, highly optimised implementations exploit cache blocking and parallelism, and matrix multiplication is the computational core of computer graphics, scientific computing and the training of neural networks.
