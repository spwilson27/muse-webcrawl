The U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) is a federal agency of the United States government that enforces civil rights laws against workplace discrimination. It was created by Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and began operations on 2 July 1965.

Jurisdiction

The EEOC investigates and enforces laws prohibiting employment discrimination based on race, colour, religion, sex (including pregnancy, sexual orientation, and gender identity), national origin, age (40 or older), disability, and genetic information. Its remit covers Title VII of the Civil Rights Act, the Equal Pay Act of 1963, the Age Discrimination in Employment Act of 1967, the Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990, and the Genetic Information Nondiscrimination Act of 2008. Most employers with at least 15 employees are covered (20 for age discrimination).

Process

Individuals generally must file a charge of discrimination with the EEOC before suing a private employer in court. The agency may mediate, investigate, sue on behalf of claimants, or issue a right-to-sue notice. The Commission also oversees equal opportunity compliance in the federal workforce through hearings and appellate review.
