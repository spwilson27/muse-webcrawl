Office of Management and Budget

The Office of Management and Budget (OMB) is the largest office within the Executive Office of the President of the United States. OMB's primary functions are to assist the President in overseeing the preparation of the federal budget and to supervise its administration in Executive Branch agencies. It also evaluates the effectiveness of agency programs, policies, and procedures, assesses competing funding demands among agencies, and sets funding priorities.

History

The Bureau of the Budget was created by the Budget and Accounting Act of 1921 and originally placed in the Department of the Treasury before moving to the Executive Office of the President in 1939. In 1970, President Richard Nixon reorganized it as the Office of Management and Budget, adding management and regulatory oversight responsibilities to its budget role.

Functions

OMB prepares the President's annual budget proposal to Congress, issues circulars (such as Circular A-76 and A-130) governing agency management, reviews proposed regulations through its Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs (OIRA), and scores legislation for compliance with budget rules such as pay-as-you-go under the Statutory Pay-As-You-Go Act. During government shutdowns, OMB directs agencies on implementing funding lapses.

Director

The Director of OMB is appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate, and is a member of the Cabinet. The office employs roughly 500 staff, most of them career civil servants organized into Resource Management Offices that mirror agency jurisdictions.
