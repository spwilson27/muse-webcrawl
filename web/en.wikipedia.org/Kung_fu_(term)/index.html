Kung fu (term)

Kung fu (Chinese: 功夫; pinyin: gōngfu) is a Chinese term referring to any discipline or skill achieved through hard work and practice, not necessarily martial arts. However, in popular use — especially outside China since the late 20th century — it has become synonymous with Chinese martial arts, known in Chinese as wushu.

Etymology and meaning

The phrase combines the characters 功 (gōng, "work, merit, achievement") and 夫 (fū, "man"). In its original sense, a person was said to have good kung fu in calligraphy, cooking, or any art perfected over time. The association with fighting arts grew through martial-arts literature, Hong Kong cinema and the popularity of figures such as Bruce Lee in the 1970s.

Usage

In Chinese contexts the more precise term for the martial arts is wushu (武術, "martial art"), while gongfu retains its broader meaning of cultivated skill. English-language usage, shaped by films and television series such as Kung Fu (1972–75), uses the term almost exclusively for the martial arts.
