Sea ducks

Group: Tribe Mergini

Family: Anatidae

Habitat: Coastal and marine waters

The sea ducks (tribe Mergini) are a group of ducks adapted to life in marine environments. The tribe includes the eiders, scoters, goldeneyes, mergansers, the harlequin duck, the long-tailed duck and the extinct Labrador duck.

Characteristics

Sea ducks are diving ducks that feed mainly on molluscs, crustaceans and other aquatic invertebrates, which many species swallow whole and crush in a powerful gizzard. Most breed in northern latitudes — in the tundra and boreal forest of North America and Eurasia — and winter at sea along temperate coasts.

Species

Well-known members include the common eider, prized for its insulating down; the colourful harlequin duck of fast-flowing northern streams and rocky coasts; the surf, white-winged and black scoters; and the fish-eating mergansers, which have slender, serrated bills unlike those of other ducks.

Conservation

Several sea duck populations have declined, owing to oil spills, bycatch in fishing nets, habitat changes and hunting, and they are the focus of monitoring programmes in North America and Europe.
