Allied-occupied Germany was the country of Germany after its defeat in World War II, divided from 1945 into four occupation zones administered by the United States, the United Kingdom, France and the Soviet Union. Berlin, inside the Soviet zone, was likewise divided into four sectors. The arrangement had been agreed by the Allies in the London Protocol and confirmed at Yalta and Potsdam.

The zones

The American zone covered Bavaria, Hesse and northern Württemberg-Baden plus Bremen; the British zone the northwest, including Hamburg and the Ruhr; the French zone the southwest along the Rhine; and the Soviet zone the east, including Brandenburg, Saxony and Mecklenburg. East of the Oder–Neisse line, German territories were placed under Polish and Soviet administration, and millions of ethnic Germans fled or were expelled westward.

From occupation to division

The four powers governed jointly through the Allied Control Council and pursued demilitarisation, denazification, decentralisation and reparations. As the Cold War began, the Western zones were merged economically (the Trizone) and given a new currency in 1948, prompting the Soviet blockade of Berlin and the Allied airlift. In 1949 two states emerged: the Federal Republic of Germany (West) and the German Democratic Republic (East). West Germany regained broad sovereignty in 1955; Germany reunified in 1990.
