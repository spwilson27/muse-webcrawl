Wilhelm II (Friedrich Wilhelm Viktor Albert; 27 January 1859 – 4 June 1941) was the last German Emperor and King of Prussia, reigning from 1888 until his abdication in 1918, which marked the end of the German Empire and the 300-year rule of the House of Hohenzollern in Brandenburg and Prussia.

Early life

Wilhelm was the eldest son of Crown Prince Frederick (later Frederick III) and Victoria, Princess Royal, daughter of Queen Victoria. A breech birth left him with a withered left arm, a disability that affected his upbringing and character. He came to the throne in the "Year of the Three Emperors" (1888), succeeding his grandfather Wilhelm I and his father, who died after only 99 days as emperor.

Reign

Wilhelm soon clashed with the veteran chancellor Otto von Bismarck and forced his resignation in 1890. Under his "personal rule", Germany pursued an assertive foreign policy (Weltpolitik), built a battle fleet to rival Britain's Royal Navy, and pressed its colonial ambitions — policies that strained relations with the other great powers. His erratic interventions in diplomacy, such as the Daily Telegraph affair of 1908, repeatedly damaged Germany's position.

First World War and abdication

In the July Crisis of 1914 Wilhelm gave Austria-Hungary the famous "blank cheque" of support and approved Germany's war plans. During the First World War he served as nominal supreme war lord, though real power passed to the generals Hindenburg and Ludendorff. As Germany faced defeat in November 1918, revolution broke out; Wilhelm's abdication was announced on 9 November, and he fled to exile in the Netherlands.

Exile and death

Wilhelm lived at Doorn in the Netherlands for the rest of his life. The Dutch government refused Allied demands for his extradition as a war criminal. He wrote memoirs, followed German politics with bitterness, and died in 1941 during the German occupation of the Netherlands. His remains rest in a mausoleum at Doorn.
