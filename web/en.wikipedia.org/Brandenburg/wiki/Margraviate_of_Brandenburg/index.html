The Margraviate of Brandenburg (German: Markgrafschaft Brandenburg) was a major principality of the Holy Roman Empire from 1157 to 1806. As one of the seven original electorates, its margrave served as an imperial elector. It played a pivotal role in German history and formed, with the Duchy of Prussia, the core of the later Kingdom of Prussia and ultimately the German Empire.

History

In 1157 Albert the Bear, an Ascanian count, recovered the fortress of Brandenburg from Slavic hands and styled himself Margrave of Brandenburg, beginning German eastward settlement (the Ostsiedlung) across the Elbe. After the Ascanian line died out (1320), the margraviate passed to the Wittelsbachs and then the Luxembourgs; Emperor Charles IV confirmed its electoral rank in the Golden Bull of 1356 and made Tangermünde a residence. In 1415 Frederick of Hohenzollern, burgrave of Nuremberg, received Brandenburg; his descendants ruled it for five centuries.

Brandenburg-Prussia

In 1618 the Hohenzollern electors inherited the Duchy of Prussia, creating the personal union known as Brandenburg-Prussia. Devastated in the Thirty Years' War, the territory was rebuilt under Frederick William, the "Great Elector" (r. 1640–1688), whose standing army and the Edict of Potsdam (1685) welcoming Huguenot refugees strengthened the state. In 1701 Elector Frederick III crowned himself King in Prussia at Königsberg, and the margraviate was absorbed into the growing Prussian monarchy, though it formally persisted until the Empire's dissolution in 1806. Its capital was Potsdam's neighbouring city, Berlin.
