The House of Hohenzollern (German: Haus Hohenzollern) is a German royal dynasty whose members were princes, electors, kings, and emperors of Prussia, Germany, and Romania. Originating in Swabia around the castle of Hohenzollern near Hechingen, the family rose from counts in the 11th century to become one of Europe's most powerful dynasties.

Branches

The family split into two main branches. The Swabian (Catholic) branch held the principalities of Hohenzollern-Hechingen and Hohenzollern-Sigmaringen until 1849 and supplied, in a cadet line, the kings of Romania (1866–1947), beginning with Carol I. The Franconian (later Brandenburg-Prussian) branch acquired Nuremberg's burgraviate, then in 1415 the Margraviate of Brandenburg and electorate, and in 1618 the Duchy of Prussia.

Prussia and the German Empire

Frederick William, the "Great Elector" (r. 1640–1688), built Brandenburg-Prussia into a military power; his son Frederick crowned himself King in Prussia in 1701. Under Frederick the Great the kingdom became a European great power. In 1871, after the Franco-Prussian War, Wilhelm I was proclaimed German Emperor, and the Hohenzollerns ruled the German Empire until Wilhelm II abdicated in the November Revolution of 1918 at the end of the First World War.

After 1918

The monarchy was abolished in Germany, and the Romanian branch lost its throne in 1947. The family retains extensive private property, and long-running disputes with the German state over assets seized in the Soviet occupation zone have periodically drawn public attention. The current head of the Prussian line is Georg Friedrich, Prince of Prussia (born 1976), great-great-grandson of Wilhelm II.
