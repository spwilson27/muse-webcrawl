The German Empire (Deutsches Reich), also known as Imperial Germany or the Kaiserreich, was the period of the German Reich from the unification of Germany in 1871 until the November Revolution in 1918, when the German Reich changed its form of government from a monarchy to a republic.

Unification and the Bismarck era

The Empire was proclaimed at Versailles on 18 January 1871 after Prussia's victories over Denmark, Austria and France. King Wilhelm I of Prussia became Emperor; Otto von Bismarck, architect of unification, served as Chancellor until 1890, building alliance systems to isolate France, pioneering social insurance (health, accident and old-age pensions) while persecuting socialists and, in the Kulturkampf, Catholics. Germany industrialised rapidly and acquired colonies in Africa and the Pacific from 1884.

The Wilhelmine era and World War I

Wilhelm II dismissed Bismarck in 1890 and pursued Weltpolitik and a battle fleet rivalling Britain's, fuelling the naval arms race. In 1914 Germany backed Austria-Hungary after Sarajevo and invaded Belgium and France; the First World War ended in defeat, blockade-driven hunger at home, revolution, and Wilhelm's abdication and flight on 9 November 1918. The Weimar Republic followed.
