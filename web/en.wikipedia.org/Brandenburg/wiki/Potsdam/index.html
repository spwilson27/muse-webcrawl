Potsdam is the capital and largest city of the German state of Brandenburg. It lies on the River Havel, immediately southwest of Berlin, and is famed for its palaces and parks, designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1990.

History

First mentioned in 993 as the Slavic settlement Poztupimi, Potsdam grew after the Great Elector made it a residence in the 17th century. Under Frederick the Great it became the summer seat of the Prussian kings, who built Sanssouci Palace (1745–47), the New Palace, and extensive gardens, and issued the Edict of Potsdam encouraging immigration. In 1945 the city hosted the Potsdam Conference at Cecilienhof Palace, where the Allied leaders settled the postwar order for Germany. During the Cold War, the Glienicke Bridge linking Potsdam to West Berlin became famous for spy exchanges.

Sights and institutions

Beyond Sanssouci Park, landmarks include the New Garden with the Marble Palace, Babelsberg Park, the Dutch Quarter, and the Einstein Tower. Potsdam is a major science city: the University of Potsdam, the Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research, GFZ and AIP institutes, and the historic Babelsberg film studios (founded 1912), among the world's oldest large-scale studios.
