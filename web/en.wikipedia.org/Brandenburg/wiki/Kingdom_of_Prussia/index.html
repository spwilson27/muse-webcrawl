The Kingdom of Prussia was a German state that existed from 1701 to 1918. Ruled by the Hohenzollern dynasty, it originated from the union of the Margraviate of Brandenburg and the Duchy of Prussia (Brandenburg-Prussia) and grew, largely through the military successes of Frederick the Great and the diplomacy of the Congress of Vienna, into the dominant power of northern Germany. In 1871 Prussia became the leading and largest state of the unified German Empire, comprising roughly two-thirds of its territory and population.

Rise

In 1701 Elector Frederick III of Brandenburg crowned himself "King in Prussia" as Frederick I at Königsberg, the ducal capital outside the Holy Roman Empire. His grandson Frederick II ("the Great", r. 1740–1786) seized Silesia from Austria in the War of the Austrian Succession, defended it through the Seven Years' War, and participated in the Partitions of Poland, joining Brandenburg and Prussia territorially and doubling the state's size. Prussia became renowned for its disciplined army, efficient bureaucracy, and religious toleration.

Napoleonic era and reform

Catastrophic defeat by Napoleon at Jena-Auerstedt in 1806 forced a programme of reform — the Stein-Hardenberg reforms, abolition of serfdom, and Scharnhorst's army reforms — that modernised the state. Prussian troops under Blücher contributed decisively to Napoleon's defeats at Leipzig (1813) and Waterloo (1815). At the Congress of Vienna Prussia gained the Rhineland and Westphalia, giving it the industrial heartlands of the future Germany and the role of guardian of the Rhine frontier against France.

Unification of Germany

Under Minister-President Otto von Bismarck from 1862, Prussia won the wars against Denmark (1864), Austria (1866), and France (1870–71) that cleared the way for German unification. King Wilhelm I was proclaimed German Emperor at Versailles on 18 January 1871. Thereafter Prussia remained the Empire's dominant state — its king was emperor, its minister-president usually imperial chancellor — though its conservative three-class franchise became a focus of democratic criticism.

End

Following Germany's defeat in the First World War, Wilhelm II abdicated on 9 November 1918 and the kingdom became the Free State of Prussia within the Weimar Republic. Prussia as a legal entity was formally abolished by the Allied Control Council in 1947.
