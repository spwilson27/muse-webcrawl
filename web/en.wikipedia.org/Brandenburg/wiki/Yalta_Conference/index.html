The Yalta Conference (codenamed Argonaut), held 4–11 February 1945, was the World War II meeting of the heads of government of the United Kingdom, the United States and the Soviet Union to discuss the postwar reorganization of Germany and Europe. The conference was held near Yalta in Crimea, Soviet Union, within the Livadia, Yusupov and Vorontsov palaces.

Participants

The "Big Three" were Winston Churchill (United Kingdom), Franklin D. Roosevelt (United States), and Joseph Stalin (Soviet Union). It was the second of three major wartime conferences, preceded by Tehran (1943) and followed by Potsdam (July 1945).

Agreements

The leaders agreed that Germany would be divided into four occupation zones, with France receiving a zone carved from the British and American zones. They issued a Declaration of Liberated Europe pledging free elections in the liberated states. Poland's eastern border was moved westward to the Curzon Line, and its government was to be reorganized with the inclusion of democratic leaders; its western border was deferred to a later peace conference. Stalin agreed that the Soviet Union would enter the war against Japan within three months of Germany's surrender, in return for territorial concessions including South Sakhalin and the Kuril Islands. The conference also finalized plans for the United Nations, with agreement on Security Council voting and membership for the Ukrainian and Byelorussian Soviet republics.

Controversy

Yalta became intensely controversial. Critics in the West later accused Roosevelt and Churchill of conceding Eastern Europe to Soviet domination, since the Red Army already occupied most of the region and Stalin subsequently installed communist governments in violation of the pledge of free elections. Defenders replied that the agreements largely ratified military realities and secured Soviet entry into the Pacific war and the United Nations.
