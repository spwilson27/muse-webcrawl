River surfing

River surfing is the sport of surfing on standing waves in rivers, on tidal bores, or on artificial river waves constructed in whitewater parks. Unlike ocean surfing, where the wave moves and the surfer rides with it, in river surfing the wave is stationary and the water flows past the surfer.

Forms

In standing-wave surfing, a submerged obstacle creates a hydraulic jump that a surfer can ride indefinitely. Famous natural spots include the Eisbach wave in Munich, Germany, surfed since the 1970s, and waves on the Snake River in Wyoming. Tidal bore surfing, practised on rivers such as the Severn in England and the Qiantang in China, involves riding a moving wave generated by the incoming tide, sometimes for many kilometres.

Equipment

River surfboards are generally shorter and more buoyant than ocean shortboards, and helmets and personal flotation devices are commonly recommended because of the risk of submerged rocks and recirculating currents. Artificial waves in dedicated parks have made the sport accessible far from suitable natural rivers.
