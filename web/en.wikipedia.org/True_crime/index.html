True crime

True crime
GenreNon-fiction
MediaBooks, magazines, film, television, podcasts
SubjectReal crimes, especially murder

True crime is a non-fiction literary, podcast and film genre in which the author examines a real crime and details the actions of the real people associated with and affected by it. The genre most often focuses on murder; studies of readership have found that a large share of true crime content concerns serial killers and homicide investigations.

History

Popular accounts of crime date back centuries, from execution broadsides and pamphlets about notorious murderers to the detective magazines of the early twentieth century, such as True Detective (founded 1924). Truman Capote's In Cold Blood (1966), a "non-fiction novel" about the Clutter family murders, is widely credited with establishing the modern form, and Vincent Bugliosi's Helter Skelter (1974) became one of the best-selling true crime books of all time.

Modern popularity

In the 2010s the genre expanded rapidly into streaming television and podcasting. Programmes such as Making a Murderer and the podcast Serial drew mass audiences and, in some cases, led to renewed legal scrutiny of the cases they covered. True crime now constitutes a major segment of the publishing, television and podcast industries, with an audience that skews heavily female.

Criticism

Critics and victims' families have raised ethical concerns about the genre, including the risk of retraumatising victims, glamourising perpetrators, and profiting from the suffering of others. Defenders argue that well-made true crime can expose miscarriages of justice and teach audiences about personal safety.
