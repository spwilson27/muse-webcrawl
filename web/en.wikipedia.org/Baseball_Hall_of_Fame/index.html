The National Baseball Hall of Fame and Museum is a history museum and hall of fame in Cooperstown, New York, operated by private interests. It serves as the central point of the history of baseball in the United States, honoring the game's greatest players, managers, executives, and umpires.

History

The Hall of Fame was established in 1936 by Stephen Carlton Clark, who sought to bring tourists to Cooperstown, a town associated — through the now-debunked Doubleday myth — with the invention of baseball. The first five inductees, chosen by the Baseball Writers' Association of America (BBWAA), were Ty Cobb, Babe Ruth, Honus Wagner, Christy Mathewson, and Walter Johnson. The museum was dedicated on 12 June 1939.

Election process

Players are eligible five years after retirement and are elected by BBWAA members if named on at least 75% of ballots; they remain on the ballot for up to ten years. Candidates who fall short may be considered later by era committees (the successors to the Veterans Committee). Managers, umpires, and executives are considered through the committee process. In 2020, the Hall integrated the Negro leagues' history further, and Major League Baseball recognized the Negro leagues as major leagues.

Museum

The museum houses artifacts from baseball history, including equipment from milestone games, the plaques of inductees in the Plaque Gallery, and exhibits covering the game's origins, women's baseball, and baseball in popular culture. Its library and archives are a major research resource.
