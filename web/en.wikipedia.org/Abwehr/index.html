Abwehr

Formed: 1920

Dissolved: 1944 (absorbed into the RSHA)

Chief (1935–1944): Admiral Wilhelm Canaris

Role: German military intelligence

The Abwehr (German for "resistance" or "defence") was the German military intelligence service for the Reichswehr and the Wehrmacht from 1920 to 1944. Nominally a counter-intelligence organisation, it conducted espionage, sabotage and counter-espionage abroad under the High Command of the Armed Forces (OKW).

Under Canaris

From 1935 the Abwehr was led by Admiral Wilhelm Canaris, who expanded it greatly while privately opposing Hitler. The service ran agents and clandestine networks worldwide, supported the Brandenburg special forces, and competed bitterly with the SS intelligence services of Heinrich Himmler and Reinhard Heydrich. Remarkably, several senior Abwehr officers — including Hans Oster and Hans von Dohnanyi — formed a nucleus of the military resistance to Hitler, using their positions to protect agents, pass information and aid plots against the regime.

Decline and dissolution

Abwehr operations suffered major failures, notably in Britain, where its agent network was captured and turned by MI5 in the Double-Cross System. After a series of scandals and defections, Canaris was dismissed in February 1944 and the Abwehr was absorbed into the Reich Security Main Office (RSHA) under the SS. Canaris was arrested after the 20 July plot to assassinate Hitler and was executed at Flossenbürg concentration camp in April 1945.
