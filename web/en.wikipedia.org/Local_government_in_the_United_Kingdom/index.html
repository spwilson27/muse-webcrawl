Local government in the United Kingdom

Local government in the United Kingdom is organised separately in its four constituent countries — England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland — with responsibility for local government devolved to the Scottish Parliament, the Senedd and the Northern Ireland Assembly, while Westminster retains it for England.

England

England has a patchwork of county and district councils, unitary authorities, metropolitan boroughs and London boroughs, supplemented by combined authorities and civil parishes.

Scotland

Scotland has 32 single-tier council areas, created in 1996, responsible for all local services in their areas, from mainland authorities such as Glasgow and Edinburgh to the three island councils.

Wales

Wales has 22 single-tier principal areas — counties and county boroughs — alongside community councils at the most local level.

Northern Ireland

Northern Ireland has 11 districts, reorganised in 2014. Because of the region's history, its councils have historically held fewer powers, with education, health and housing run by regional boards and departments.

Functions and elections

Across the UK, councils deliver education, social services, planning, housing, environmental health, refuse collection and local transport, funded through local taxation (council tax, and rates in Northern Ireland), fees and grants. Elections are generally held every four or five years.
