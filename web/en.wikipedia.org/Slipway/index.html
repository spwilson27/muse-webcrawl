Slipway

A slipway, also known as a boat ramp or launch, is a ramp on the shore by which ships or boats can be moved to and from the water. They are used for building and repairing ships and boats, and for launching and retrieving small boats on trailers. Slipways commonly have rails, greased ways or rollers to reduce friction.

Shipbuilding slipways

In shipyards, vessels are constructed on slipways inclined towards the water so that, once complete, they can be launched stern-first into the water by gravity. Large modern ships are usually built in dry docks instead, but slipways remain common for repair yards, where ships are hauled out sideways or end-on.

Lifeboat slipways

Lifeboat stations such as those of the Royal National Lifeboat Institution (RNLI) often house boats on slipways so they can be launched rapidly down the ramp into the sea in an emergency, then recovered by winch afterwards.
