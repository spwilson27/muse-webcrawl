Formula Three, also called Formula 3, abbreviated as F3, is a third-tier class of open-wheel formula racing. The various championships held in Europe, Australia, South America and Asia form an important step for many prospective Formula One drivers. Formula Three has traditionally been regarded as the first major step for F1 hopefuls — nearly every Formula One driver, from Ayrton Senna to Lewis Hamilton, raced in F3 at some point.

History

Formula 3 evolved from post-war 500 cc motorcycle-engined racing cars built by Cooper and others in Britain. National championships flourished across Europe in the 1960s and 1970s, and the European championship produced future stars including Senna (British F3 champion 1983) and Mika Häkkinen. After a period of competing regional series, the FIA unified the top level in 2019 as the FIA Formula 3 Championship, run on the Formula One support bill.

The cars

Modern FIA F3 cars are spec chassis (Dallara) powered by 3.4-litre naturally aspirated V6 engines producing about 380 horsepower, with six-speed gearboxes, DRS and halo safety devices. Regional series such as the Formula Regional European Championship occupy a level adjacent to F3 in the modern FIA ladder.

Notable champions

Graduates of F3 championships include Senna, Alain Prost, Nelson Piquet, Michael Schumacher, Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri. The Macau Grand Prix, run to F3 rules for decades, was a traditional showcase for young talent.
