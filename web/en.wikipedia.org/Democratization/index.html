Democratization

Democratization, or democratisation in British English, is the transition to a more democratic political regime, including substantive political changes moving in a democratic direction. It may be a transition from an authoritarian regime to a full democracy, a transition from an authoritarian political system to a semi-democracy, or a transition from a semi-authoritarian political system to a democratic political system.

Waves of democratization

Samuel P. Huntington described democratization as occurring in waves: a first long wave in the 19th and early 20th centuries, a second wave after the Second World War, and a "third wave" beginning with Portugal's Carnation Revolution in 1974 and encompassing transitions in Southern Europe, Latin America, East Asia and, after 1989, Eastern Europe. Each wave has been followed by a partial "reverse wave" of democratic breakdowns.

Causes and conditions

Scholarship links democratization to economic development and modernization, education, a strong civil society, elite divisions, regime crises, international pressure and neighbourhood effects. Transitions may be driven from below by mass protest, negotiated ("pacted") between regime and opposition elites, or imposed after military defeat. The consolidation of democracy — making it resilient against backsliding — is distinguished from the initial transition.
