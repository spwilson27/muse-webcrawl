Lockerbie bombing

Pan Am Flight 103
Date: 21 December 1988
Site: Lockerbie, Scotland
Deaths: 270 (259 on board, 11 on the ground)

The Lockerbie bombing was the terrorist destruction of Pan Am Flight 103 over the town of Lockerbie, Dumfries and Galloway, Scotland, on 21 December 1988. The Boeing 747, en route from London Heathrow to New York JFK, was destroyed by a bomb in the cargo hold, killing all 259 people on board and 11 people on the ground — the deadliest terrorist attack in the history of the United Kingdom.

Investigation and trial

The joint Scottish–US investigation concluded the bomb, a Semtex device in a cassette player inside a suitcase, had been routed from Malta via Frankfurt. Libyan intelligence officer Abdelbaset al-Megrahi was convicted of murder by a Scottish court sitting in the Netherlands in 2001; his co-accused was acquitted. Libya accepted responsibility in 2003 and paid compensation, though the verdict has remained controversial. Megrahi was released on compassionate grounds in 2009 and died in 2012. Another Libyan suspect, Abu Agila Masud, was taken into US custody in 2022.

Legacy

Memorials stand at Lockerbie, at Arlington National Cemetery, and at Syracuse University, which lost 35 students on the flight. The disaster led to major reforms in aviation security and baggage screening.
