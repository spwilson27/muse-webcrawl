Mount Tomanivi

Elevation1,324 m (4,344 ft)
LocationViti Levu, Fiji
TypeExtinct volcano

Mount Tomanivi, formerly known as Mount Victoria, is an extinct volcano in the north-central highlands of Viti Levu and, at 1,324 metres (4,344 ft), the highest mountain in Fiji. It rises in the Nadrau Plateau region, the watershed from which several of Viti Levu's great rivers — the Rewa, Navua, Sigatoka and Ba — flow outward to the coasts.

Ecology

The mountain's cloud-forest slopes are among the most important bird habitats in Fiji: the surrounding forest is designated an Important Bird Area and holds populations of rare species including the long-legged thicketbird and the shy ground dove, together with much of Fiji's endemic montane flora. The slopes are reached from the village of Navai, whose people act as guides for the popular summit climb.

Climbing

The ascent from Navai is Fiji's best-known mountain walk, taking several hours through village land and montane forest to an open summit ridge. The mountain takes its former colonial name, like the nearby town of Tavua's hinterland peaks, from Queen Victoria.
