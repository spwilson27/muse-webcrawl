Whernside is a mountain in the Yorkshire Dales in northern England. At 736 metres (2,415 ft) it is the highest of the Yorkshire Three Peaks — the others being Ingleborough and Pen-y-ghent — and the highest point in the modern county of North Yorkshire (though it historically lay in the West Riding, and the summit ridge forms the border with Cumbria).

Description

Unlike its craggier neighbours, Whernside is a long, smooth whaleback ridge rising above Dentdale and the head of the Ribble valley. The Settle–Carlisle railway's Ribblehead Viaduct lies at its foot. Walkers typically ascend from Ribblehead or from Dent. As one of the Three Peaks, it is climbed as part of the Yorkshire Three Peaks Challenge, a 24-mile route over all three summits usually completed within twelve hours.
