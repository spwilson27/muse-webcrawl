11 Downing Street

11 Downing Street in London is the official residence of the Chancellor of the Exchequer, the United Kingdom's finance minister. It adjoins the better-known 10 Downing Street, the official residence of the prime minister, and forms part of the same complex of buildings in the City of Westminster.

History

The houses of Downing Street were built in the 1680s by Sir George Downing on the site of Hampden House. No. 11 was occupied by the Chancellor of the Exchequer from the early nineteenth century; the first Chancellor to live there is generally identified as Henry Addington. The interiors of Nos. 10, 11 and 12 were extensively rebuilt behind their facades in the 1960s after surveys found the structures in poor condition.

Use

Although smaller in public profile than No. 10, No. 11 is in fact the larger private flat. Since 1997 several prime ministers and chancellors have informally swapped residences: Tony Blair lived in No. 11 while his Chancellor, Gordon Brown, used the flat at No. 10, and a similar arrangement occurred under later governments. The Chancellor's Budget is traditionally photographed being held aloft in its red box outside the door of No. 11 on Budget day.
