Blackstreet

Blackstreet, pioneers of new jack swing.

Blackstreet (often stylized as BLACKstreet) is an American R&B group founded in 1991 by record producers Teddy Riley and Chauncey Hannibal in New York City. One of the leading acts of the new jack swing era, the group is best known for the 1996 single "No Diggity," featuring Dr. Dre and Queen Pen, which reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100 and won the Grammy Award for Best R&B Performance by a Duo or Group with Vocals.

History

The group's self-titled debut album appeared in 1994 and included the hits "Booti Call" and "Before I Let You Go." After personnel changes, the lineup of Riley, Hannibal, Levi Little, and Dave Hollister recorded the second album, Another Level (1996), which became their commercial peak, selling millions of copies worldwide and producing "No Diggity" and "(Money Can't) Buy Me Love." Hollister departed and was replaced by Mark Middleton for Finally (1999), which featured Janet Jackson on "Girlfriend/Boyfriend." A fourth album, Level II, followed in 2003.

Blackstreet's lineup changed many times over the years, leading to disputes over the group's name and rival touring versions. Teddy Riley, who had earlier founded the group Guy and co-created new jack swing, remained the constant creative center of the group.
