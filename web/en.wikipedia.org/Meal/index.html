A meal is an eating occasion that takes place at a certain time and includes the consumption of food prepared for that occasion. Meals are traditionally shared socially, and their number, timing, size and composition vary widely between cultures and historical periods.

Daily meals

In much of the modern world the day is structured around three meals. Breakfast is eaten in the morning to break the overnight fast; lunch is the midday meal, historically the main meal of the day in many societies; and dinner (or supper) is eaten in the evening. Additional named occasions include brunch, elevenses, afternoon tea and various ceremonial feasts.

Structure

Formal meals in Western dining may be divided into courses — appetizer, soup, main course and dessert — while in many other cuisines dishes are served together for diners to share. Religious and cultural rules shape meals through practices such as fasting, the communal iftar that breaks the Ramadan fast at sunset, and festive meals at holidays.

History

The pattern of fixed communal meals developed with settled agriculture and changed with industrial working hours, which compressed the midday meal and made the evening meal the largest in industrial societies. Prepared and convenience foods, eating out and snacking have since blurred traditional meal structures.
