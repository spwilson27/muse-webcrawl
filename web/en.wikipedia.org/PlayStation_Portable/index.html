The PlayStation Portable (PSP) is a handheld game console developed and marketed by Sony Computer Entertainment. It was first released in Japan on December 12, 2004, in North America on March 24, 2005, and in PAL regions on September 1, 2005. It was the first handheld installment in the PlayStation line of consoles and competed primarily with Nintendo's Nintendo DS as part of the seventh generation of video game consoles.

The PSP was the most powerful portable console when launched. It featured a large 4.3-inch widescreen display, a built-in Wi-Fi radio for internet access and online play via the PlayStation Network, and multimedia capabilities including video and music playback. Games were distributed on the proprietary Universal Media Disc (UMD) optical format, which was also briefly used for films.

History

Sony announced the PSP at E3 2003 and unveiled the device at a press conference before E3 2004. Although Nintendo's DS outsold it substantially, the PSP sold more than 80 million units worldwide, making it a commercial success and establishing Sony in the handheld market. Several revised models were released: the slimmer PSP-2000 ("Slim and Lite") in 2007, the PSP-3000 with an improved screen in 2008, the slide-out PSP Go in 2009, which relied entirely on digital downloads, and the budget PSP-E1000 ("Street") in 2011.

Notable games for the system include Grand Theft Auto: Liberty City Stories, God of War: Chains of Olympus, Monster Hunter Portable 3rd, Crisis Core: Final Fantasy VII and Metal Gear Solid: Peace Walker. The Monster Hunter series in particular drove enormous sales of the console in Japan.

Hardware

The original PSP is powered by a MIPS R4000-based CPU capable of running at up to 333 MHz, with 32 MB of main memory (later models have 64 MB). Its 480 × 272 pixel display was unusually sharp for a handheld of its era. In addition to gaming, the system could play videos and music, display photos, and browse the web. The PSP also supported connectivity with the PlayStation 3 and, through Remote Play, could stream content from it.

Legacy

Production of the PSP ended in 2014, shortly after the release of its successor, the PlayStation Vita. The system remains popular with homebrew and emulation communities, in part because early firmware versions were readily exploited. Its multimedia-first design anticipated the convergence of gaming and media functions that later handhelds and smartphones would adopt.
