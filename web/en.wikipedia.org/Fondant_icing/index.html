Fondant icing

Fondant icing, commonly just fondant (from French fondant, "melting"), is an icing used to decorate cakes and pastries. It is made from sugar, water, gelatin, vegetable shortening and glycerol, sometimes with glucose or corn syrup. Two main forms exist: poured fondant, a thick liquid used as a coating or filling (as on petit fours and the tops of iced buns), and rolled fondant, a dough-like paste rolled out and draped over cakes to give a smooth, flawless finish.

Preparation and use

Rolled fondant is kneaded until pliable, coloured or flavoured as desired, rolled to a few millimetres thick and smoothed over a cake usually coated first with buttercream or jam to help it adhere. It can be modelled into figures and flowers, embossed and painted, making it the standard covering for wedding and celebration cakes. Because it is almost entirely sugar, it keeps cakes fresh by sealing them, though some bakers and consumers dislike its sweet, chewy texture and remove it before eating.

History

Fondant developed from earlier sugar-paste and almond-paste traditions of European confectionery. The name refers to its melt-in-the-mouth quality — the same word is used for soft-centred chocolates such as fondant creams, whose centres are made from poured fondant.
