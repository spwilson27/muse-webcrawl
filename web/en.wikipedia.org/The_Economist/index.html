The Economist is a British weekly newspaper published in printed magazine format and digitally. Founded in 1843 by James Wilson to campaign against the Corn Laws, it covers international news, politics, business, finance, science, and culture.

Editorial stance and style

The paper describes its outlook as classical and economic liberalism, supporting free markets, free trade, and social liberalism. Articles are published anonymously, under a tradition of collective editorial responsibility, and written in a distinctive dry, witty style governed by a famous style guide. Its red masthead and covers are well known; the paper refers to itself as a newspaper despite its magazine format.

Audience and influence

The Economist is read by senior executives and policymakers worldwide and is known for features such as the Big Mac index, a light-hearted measure of purchasing-power parity, and its annual Democracy Index.
