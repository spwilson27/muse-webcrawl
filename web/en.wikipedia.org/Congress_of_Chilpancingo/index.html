Congress of Chilpancingo

The Congress of Chilpancingo, also known as the Congress of Anáhuac, was the first congress of independent Mexico, convened by the insurgent leader José María Morelos at Chilpancingo, in present-day Guerrero, on 13 September 1813 during the Mexican War of Independence.

Proceedings

Morelos opened the congress with an address and presented his document Sentimientos de la Nación ("Feelings of the Nation"), which called for American independence from Spain, the abolition of slavery and the caste system, equality before the law, and limits on the power of the Church and the military. The congress elected Morelos generalissimo and head of the insurgent government.

Declaration of Independence

On 6 November 1813 the congress issued the Solemn Act of the Declaration of Independence of Northern America, formally severing Mexico from Spanish rule and repudiating any allegiance to Ferdinand VII. The congress later moved with the insurgent government and produced the Constitution of Apatzingán in October 1814.

Legacy

Although the insurgent government was soon overwhelmed by royalist forces and Morelos was captured and executed in 1815, the Congress of Chilpancingo is remembered as the first national constituent body of Mexico and the source of foundational principles of Mexican republicanism.
