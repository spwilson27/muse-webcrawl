Triptan

Triptans are a family of tryptamine-based drugs used as abortive medication in the treatment of migraines and cluster headaches. This drug class was first marketed commercially in the 1990s, beginning with sumatriptan (1991), and although their effectiveness is well established, they do not prevent future attacks or cure migraine.

Mechanism of action

Triptans are selective agonists of the serotonin 5-HT1B and 5-HT1D receptors. Activation of these receptors causes constriction of cranial blood vessels and inhibits the release of pro-inflammatory neuropeptides such as CGRP from trigeminal nerve endings, interrupting the migraine process.

Members

The class includes sumatriptan, rizatriptan, zolmitriptan, naratriptan, eletriptan, almotriptan and frovatriptan. They differ in half-life, bioavailability and available formulations, which include tablets, nasal sprays and subcutaneous injections — the latter being especially useful for cluster headache and for patients with vomiting.

Side effects and contraindications

Common side effects include tingling, warmth, flushing, dizziness and a sense of heaviness or pressure in the chest ("triptan sensations"). Because of their vasoconstrictive action, triptans are contraindicated in patients with coronary artery disease, uncontrolled hypertension and certain other vascular conditions. Overuse can lead to medication-overuse headache, and combining triptans with serotonergic antidepressants carries a small theoretical risk of serotonin syndrome.
