First Opium War

Date1839–1842
LocationChina
ResultBritish victory; Treaty of Nanking
BelligerentsUnited Kingdom vs. Qing dynasty

The First Opium War (1839–1842), also known as the Opium War or the Anglo-Chinese War, was a series of military engagements fought between Britain and the Qing dynasty of China. The conflict arose over the British trade in opium and China's sovereignty and efforts to suppress the drug trade.

Background

British merchants, facing a large trade deficit from purchases of Chinese tea, increasingly smuggled Indian opium into China despite Chinese prohibitions. In 1839 the imperial commissioner Lin Zexu confiscated and destroyed some 20,000 chests of opium at Canton (Guangzhou), prompting Britain to send an expeditionary force.

Course of the war

British naval superiority proved decisive. Forces seized positions along the coast, occupied Zhoushan, blockaded ports, and in 1842 advanced up the Yangtze River, capturing Zhenjiang and threatening Nanjing, forcing the Qing government to negotiate.

Treaty of Nanking

The Treaty of Nanking (1842) ceded Hong Kong Island to Britain, opened five treaty ports (including Canton and Shanghai) to foreign trade, abolished the Cohong monopoly, and imposed an indemnity of 21 million silver dollars. It was the first of the "unequal treaties" between China and Western powers and is often taken to begin China's "century of humiliation".
