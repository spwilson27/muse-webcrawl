Water scarcity

Water scarcity is the lack of sufficient available water resources to meet the demands of water usage within a region. It already affects every continent and around 2.4 billion people live in water-stressed countries. Water scarcity involves water stress, water shortage or deficits, and water crisis.

Definitions and measurement

Physical scarcity occurs when natural water resources are insufficient for demand; economic scarcity occurs when human, institutional, and financial constraints limit access despite adequate resources. Indicators include the Falkenmark index of renewable water per capita and the ratio of withdrawals to renewable supply used in the UN's water-stress statistics.

Impacts

Scarcity undermines food security, sanitation, and public health; forces time-consuming water collection, disproportionately by women and children; degrades ecosystems dependent on environmental flows; and can intensify disputes between regions and states sharing rivers and aquifers.

Mitigation

Because scarcity is partly a problem of quality as well as quantity, purifying and reusing water is a key mitigation: wastewater reuse, brackish-water treatment, and desalination all expand usable supply. Conservation, drought planning, crop choice, and aquifer recharge complement treatment-based approaches.
