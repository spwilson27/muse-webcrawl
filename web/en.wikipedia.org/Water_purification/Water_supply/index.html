Water supply

Water supply is the provision of water by public utilities, commercial organisations, community endeavours or by individuals, usually via a system of pumps and pipes. A water supply system or water supply network is a system of engineered hydrologic and hydraulic components that provide water supply.

From source to tap

The supply chain begins at a source — surface water from rivers and reservoirs or groundwater from wells and springs. Raw water passes through a treatment works where it is purified, then enters storage and a pressurized distribution network. Utilities maintain disinfectant residual and pressure throughout the network to keep the water safe until it reaches the tap.

History

Organized water supply dates to ancient aqueducts and qanats. Roman aqueducts delivered water to public fountains and baths; in the 19th century, cities such as London and New York built reservoir-and-aqueduct systems and, after the link between water and cholera was understood, added filtration and chlorination — transforming urban mortality rates.

Service quality

Well-run supplies are continuous, adequate in quantity, affordable, and safe. Where piped supply is absent or unreliable, households rely on vendors, boreholes, and storage tanks, often at far higher cost per litre.
