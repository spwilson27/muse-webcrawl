Eagle, Globe, and Anchor
Emblem of: United States Marine Corps
Adopted: 1868 (current design 1955)
Elements: Bald eagle, globe, fouled anchor

The Eagle, Globe, and Anchor (EGA) is the emblem of the United States Marine Corps. It consists of a bald eagle with outstretched wings perched atop a globe showing the Western Hemisphere, in front of a fouled anchor. The emblem symbolizes the Corps' mission: the eagle represents the United States, the globe the worldwide areas in which Marines serve, and the anchor the naval traditions of the Marine Corps.

The Eagle, Globe, and Anchor is the official emblem of the U.S. Marine Corps.

History

Early Marine insignia borrowed from British Royal Marines designs, including a globe showing the Eastern Hemisphere. In 1868 a board convened by Commandant Jacob Zeilin recommended a new emblem; the design adopted featured a globe of the Western Hemisphere. The present design was standardized in 1955 under Commandant Lemuel C. Shepherd Jr., and the emblem was formally adopted as the Marine Corps seal and emblem, with the eagle, globe, and anchor placed on a scarlet background encircled by a blue band bearing the words "Department of the Navy — United States Marine Corps".

Use

The emblem is worn on the uniforms of Marines, most famously on the cover (cap) and on dress blue buttons and collar devices. Marine recruits receive the emblem at the completion of the Crucible training event, marking their transformation from recruit to Marine.
