Millennium Prize Problems

The Millennium Prize Problems are seven well-known complex mathematical problems selected by the Clay Mathematics Institute in 2000. The Clay Institute has pledged a US$1 million prize for the first correct solution to each problem. To date, only one — the Poincaré conjecture — has been solved.

[Image: The Clay Mathematics Institute announcement of the Millennium Prize Problems, Paris, 2000]

The problems

  1. P versus NP problem — whether every problem whose solution can be verified quickly can also be solved quickly.
  2. Hodge conjecture — concerning algebraic cycles on non-singular complex algebraic varieties.
  3. Poincaré conjecture (solved) — every simply connected, closed 3-manifold is homeomorphic to the 3-sphere.
  4. Riemann hypothesis — all non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function have real part 1/2.
  5. Yang–Mills existence and mass gap — prove that quantum Yang–Mills theory exists and has a mass gap.
  6. Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness — whether smooth solutions always exist for the equations of fluid flow.
  7. Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture — relating the rank of an elliptic curve to its L-function.

The solved problem

The Poincaré conjecture was proved by Russian mathematician Grigori Perelman, who posted a proof using Richard Hamilton's Ricci flow programme in 2002–2003. After verification, Perelman was offered the Fields Medal (2006) and the Millennium Prize (2010); he declined both.
