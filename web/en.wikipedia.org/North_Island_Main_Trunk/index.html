North Island Main Trunk

The North Island Main Trunk (NIMT) is the principal railway line in the North Island of New Zealand, connecting the capital city Wellington with the country's largest city, Auckland, via Paraparaumu, Palmerston North, Taihape, Taumarunui, Te Kuiti, Hamilton, and Pukekohe. At about 682 kilometres (424 mi) in length, it is the backbone of the KiwiRail network in the North Island.

History

Construction began in the 1870s from both ends under the public works policies of Julius Vogel. The central section across the volcanic plateau was the last and most difficult to build, involving major viaducts such as the Makatote Viaduct and the ingenious Raurimu Spiral, which allows trains to climb 139 metres in a short distance. The last spike was driven in 1908, and the prime minister, Sir Joseph Ward, drove the first through train from Wellington to Auckland that November.

Electrification

The central section between Palmerston North and Hamilton was electrified at 25 kV AC in the 1980s as part of the Think Big energy projects, allowing long-distance freight to be hauled electrically over the steepest grades. The Wellington and Auckland suburban networks at either end are separately electrified.

Services

The line carries intensive freight traffic and the Northern Explorer passenger train between Auckland and Wellington, a journey of roughly eleven hours. Commuter services operate at the Wellington and Auckland ends.
