Beaumaris Castle (Welsh: Castell Biwmares) is a medieval castle in the town of Beaumaris on the Isle of Anglesey in Wales. Built as part of Edward I's campaign to conquer north Wales, it was designed by the Savoyard master mason James of St George and is regarded as one of the finest and most technically perfect examples of concentric castle design in Europe — though it was never completed.

Construction

Work began in 1295 after Edward's second Welsh campaign; the castle's name derives from the Norman French beau marais, "fair marsh". Its defences form a nearly symmetrical "walls within walls" plan: a moat ringed by an outer wall with 12 towers, inside which stands a higher inner wall with 6 large towers, two gatehouses, and a dock that allowed supply ships to reach the castle at high tide. At peak construction around 2,600 workmen were employed. Funds and royal attention later shifted to Scotland, work slowed after about 1298, and the castle was left unfinished around 1330: the inner towers were never raised to their intended height and some battlements were never built.

Later history

Despite never being finished, the castle was defensible and changed hands during the Glyndŵr Rising in the early 15th century and again during the English Civil War. Today it is in the care of Cadw and forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Castles and Town Walls of King Edward in Gwynedd". See also Conwy Castle.
