Marine conservation

Marine conservation, also known as ocean conservation, is the protection and preservation of ecosystems in oceans and seas through planned management in order to prevent the over-exploitation of marine resources.

Threats

Marine ecosystems face overfishing, habitat destruction, pollution — including plastics and nutrient runoff — ocean acidification and warming driven by climate change, invasive species and coastal development. These pressures have driven declines in fish stocks, coral bleaching events and the loss of kelp forests and seagrass meadows.

Strategies

Conservation tools include marine protected areas (MPAs) and no-take reserves, catch quotas and gear restrictions, habitat restoration, international agreements such as the Convention on Biological Diversity, and the 2023 High Seas Treaty. The Kunming–Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework set a target of protecting 30% of the ocean by 2030 ("30 by 30").

Effectiveness

Well-enforced reserves have been shown to increase fish biomass and spill over benefits to surrounding fisheries, while "paper parks" lacking enforcement deliver few gains. Marine conservation increasingly involves local and Indigenous communities in governance.
