Multivariable calculus


Field: Mathematics
Topics: Partial derivatives, multiple integrals, vector fields
Key theorems: Green's, Stokes', divergence theorem

Multivariable calculus (also known as multivariate calculus) is the extension of calculus in one variable to functions of several variables: the differentiation and integration of functions involving multiple variables, rather than just one.

Differential calculus

Partial derivatives measure the rate of change of a function with respect to one variable while holding the others constant. They assemble into the gradient vector, which points in the direction of steepest ascent, and into Jacobian and Hessian matrices for vector-valued and second-order analysis. Directional derivatives, the chain rule, tangent planes, and optimization with Lagrange multipliers are central tools.

Integral calculus

Double and triple integrals extend integration over regions and volumes, with coordinate systems (polar, cylindrical, spherical) chosen to fit the geometry. Line integrals accumulate a function along a curve, and surface integrals over surfaces; vector calculus then relates these through the gradient, divergence, and curl operators.

Fundamental theorems

The capstone results — Green's theorem, the Kelvin–Stokes theorem, and the divergence (Gauss) theorem — connect integrals over a region to integrals over its boundary, generalizing the fundamental theorem of calculus. They are the mathematical backbone of classical electromagnetism and fluid dynamics.

Applications

Multivariable calculus is used throughout physics and engineering, in economics (marginal analysis and optimization), in probability, and in machine learning, where gradient-based optimization of many-variable loss functions is fundamental.
