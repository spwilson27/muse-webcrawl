Querétaro, officially the Free and Sovereign State of Querétaro, is one of the 32 federal entities of Mexico, located in north-central Mexico in the Bajío region. Its capital and largest city is Santiago de Querétaro.

History

The region was inhabited by Otomi and Chichimeca peoples before the Spanish conquest; the city of Querétaro was founded in 1531. The state played a central role in Mexican independence: the conspiracy that launched the movement in 1810 was hatched in the city of Querétaro. In 1867 Emperor Maximilian I was captured and executed on the Cerro de las Campanas, ending the Second Mexican Empire. Mexico's current constitution was promulgated in the city in 1917.

Geography and economy

Querétaro ranges from the highlands of the Sierra Gorda in the north to the fertile Bajío plains in the south. Its historic centre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, noted for its baroque colonial architecture and its great aqueduct of 74 arches. In recent decades the state has become a major centre for aerospace, automotive and information-technology industries.
