Swimming at the Summer Olympics

An Olympic swimming final in a 50-metre pool.

Swimming has been a sport at every modern Summer Olympic Games, beginning with Athens 1896. It is one of the most popular and medal-rich sports on the Olympic programme, governed internationally by World Aquatics (formerly FINA). Women's swimming was introduced at Stockholm 1912. Open-water marathon swimming (10 km) was added at Beijing 2008, and a mixed 4 × 100 m medley relay debuted at Tokyo 2020.

Events

The pool programme comprises events in four strokes — freestyle, backstroke, breaststroke, and butterfly — over distances from 50 m to 1500 m, plus individual medley events (200 m and 400 m) and relays (4 × 100 m and 4 × 200 m freestyle; 4 × 100 m medley). In recent Games the programme has included 35 pool events plus two marathon events.

History

The earliest Olympic races were held in open water (the Bay of Zea in 1896 and the River Seine in 1900). The 1908 London Games introduced a purpose-built 100-metre pool. Butterfly emerged as a separate stroke in 1956. The 2008–2009 era of polyurethane supersuits produced a flood of world records before textile-only rules were imposed in 2010.

Notable swimmers

Michael Phelps of the United States is the most decorated Olympian of all time with 28 medals, including 23 golds, won between 2004 and 2016. Other greats include Katie Ledecky, Ian Thorpe, Mark Spitz, Kristin Otto, Caeleb Dressel, and Léon Marchand.

Medal table

The United States leads the all-time swimming medal table by a wide margin, followed by Australia, which has a strong swimming tradition, then Germany/East Germany, Hungary, and Japan.
