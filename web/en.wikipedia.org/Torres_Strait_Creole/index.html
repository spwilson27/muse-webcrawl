Torres Strait Creole, known locally as Yumplatok, is an English-based creole language spoken in the Torres Strait Islands and parts of Cape York in far north Queensland, Australia. It is the main lingua franca of Torres Strait Islander communities and is also spoken by many Aboriginal people in the region.

Origins

The language developed in the 19th century from the Pacific pidgin varieties brought by bêche-de-mer and pearl-shell traders, missionaries and labourers. It drew vocabulary mainly from English, with influences from the Indigenous languages of the Torres Strait — Kala Lagaw Ya and Meriam Mir — as well as from other Pacific and Asian languages.

Features

Torres Strait Creole has a distinctive pronoun system distinguishing inclusive and exclusive "we" (reflected in its name, from "you-me-fellow-talk"), and a rich vocabulary for the marine environment of the strait. It exists on a continuum with varieties closer to or further from standard English, sometimes described as ranging from a basilect to an acrolect.

Use today

The creole is the first language of many Islander children and is used in broadcasting, including Indigenous community radio, alongside the traditional languages and English.
