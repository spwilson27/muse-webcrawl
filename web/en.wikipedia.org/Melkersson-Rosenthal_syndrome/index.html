Melkersson–Rosenthal syndrome

Melkersson–Rosenthal syndrome is a rare neurological disorder characterized by a classic triad of symptoms: recurring facial paralysis, persistent swelling of the face and lips (granulomatous cheilitis), and a fissured tongue (lingua plicata). The complete triad is present in only a minority of patients, and symptoms typically begin in childhood or young adulthood.

Signs and symptoms

Attacks of painless but disfiguring lip and facial swelling may last hours to weeks and can recur irregularly; over time swelling may become permanent and the tissue firm. Facial palsy in the syndrome resembles Bell's palsy and may affect either side. The fissured tongue, with grooves on its surface, is the least common feature. Biopsy of affected tissue may show non-caseating granulomas similar to those of Crohn's disease or sarcoidosis, with which the syndrome has been associated.

Cause and treatment

The cause is unknown; hereditary factors, immune dysfunction and infections have all been proposed, and some familial cases follow an autosomal dominant pattern with variable expression. There is no cure; treatment aims at reducing swelling and symptoms, using corticosteroids (including injections into the lips), other anti-inflammatory or immunosuppressive drugs, and occasionally surgery for severe persistent enlargement.
