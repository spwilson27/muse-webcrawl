Phenotypic trait

DefinitionA distinct variant of a phenotypic characteristic
CategoriesMorphological, physiological, behavioural
Determined byGenotype, environment, and their interaction

A phenotypic trait, simply trait, or character state is a distinct variant of a phenotypic characteristic of an organism. It may be inherited, acquired through environmental influence, or arise from an interaction between the two. Traits are the units on which natural and artificial selection act, from eye colour and flower shape to disease resistance and behaviour.

Shell colour in grove snails: a classic polymorphic phenotypic trait

A trait may be discrete or continuous. Discrete traits, such as the flower colour of Mendel's peas, fall into distinct classes and are often controlled by one or a few genes. Continuous or quantitative traits — height, yield, blood pressure — vary over a range and are typically polygenic, influenced by many loci together with the environment; they are studied by quantitative genetics using tools such as genome-wide association studies to map quantitative trait loci (QTL).

Nature and nurture

The phenotypic value of a trait is conventionally decomposed into genetic and environmental components, P = G + E, plus a genotype-by-environment interaction. Heritability measures the proportion of variation in a trait attributable to genetic differences in a given population and environment. A single trait influenced by several genes, and a single gene influencing several traits (pleiotropy), are both common in real organisms.
