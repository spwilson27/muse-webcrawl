Rocky Mountain Fur Company

Rocky Mountain Fur Company
Founded: 1822, St. Louis, Missouri
Founders: William Henry Ashley, Andrew Henry
Defunct: 1834
Industry: Fur trade

The Rocky Mountain Fur Company was formed in St. Louis, Missouri, in 1822 by William Henry Ashley and Andrew Henry. Among the company's employees were the famous mountain men Jedediah Smith, William Sublette, David Jackson, Thomas Fitzpatrick, Jim Bridger, and James Beckwourth; at its peak, the partnership included Smith, Jackson, and Sublette.

History

Ashley and Henry advertised in 1822 for "enterprising young men" to ascend the Missouri River to its source and trap beaver in the Rocky Mountains. Departing from earlier practice of trading with Native Americans at fixed posts, the company sent trappers to collect pelts themselves. In 1825 Ashley instituted the first rendezvous, an annual gathering in the mountains where trappers exchanged furs for supplies—a system that defined the mountain man era.

Ashley sold his interest in 1826 to Smith, Jackson, and Sublette, who in 1830 sold out to a partnership of Thomas Fitzpatrick, Milton Sublette, Jim Bridger, Henry Fraeb, and John Baptiste Gervais, who kept the Rocky Mountain Fur Company name. Facing intense competition from John Jacob Astor's American Fur Company and declining beaver prices as silk replaced beaver felt in hat fashion, the company dissolved after the 1834 rendezvous.

Legacy

The company's trappers explored much of the American West, blazing trails later used by emigrants, including South Pass across the Continental Divide, the routes to California and Oregon, and the shores of the Great Salt Lake.
