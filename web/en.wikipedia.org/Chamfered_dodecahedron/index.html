Chamfered dodecahedron

The chamfered dodecahedron (also called the truncated rhombic triacontahedron) is a convex polyhedron with 80 vertices, 120 edges, 12 regular pentagonal faces and 30 hexagonal faces. It can be constructed by chamfering — truncating the edges of — a regular dodecahedron, or equivalently by truncating a rhombic triacontahedron.

Construction

Chamfering a dodecahedron replaces each of its 30 edges with a new hexagonal face, while its 12 pentagonal faces remain. The resulting solid has the same icosahedral symmetry as the dodecahedron itself. The hexagonal faces of a chamfered dodecahedron are generally not regular; they become closer to regular when the chamfer depth is chosen appropriately, though a fully regular form is impossible for all faces simultaneously.

Related polyhedra

The chamfered dodecahedron is notable in chemistry because its structure approximates that of certain fullerenes and virus capsids. Its dual polyhedron is formed by joining the face centres. It is related to the Goldberg polyhedra family of geodesic-like polyhedra with icosahedral symmetry.
