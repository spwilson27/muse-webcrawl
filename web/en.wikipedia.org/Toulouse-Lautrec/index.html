Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec

Born24 November 1864, Albi, France
Died9 September 1901 (aged 36), Saint-André-du-Bois, France
Known forPainting, printmaking, posters
MovementPost-Impressionism, Art Nouveau

Comte Henri Marie Raymond de Toulouse-Lautrec-Monfa (24 November 1864 – 9 September 1901), known as Toulouse-Lautrec, was a French painter, printmaker, draughtsman, caricaturist, and illustrator whose immersion in the colourful and theatrical life of Paris in the late 19th century allowed him to produce a collection of enticing, elegant, and provocative images of the modern, sometimes decadent, affairs of those times.

Early life

Toulouse-Lautrec was born in Albi, Tarn, in the Midi-Pyrénées region of France, the firstborn child of Comte Alphonse and Comtesse Adèle de Toulouse-Lautrec. After the death of his younger brother, his parents separated. As a child he suffered from health problems; at ages 13 and 14 the bones in his thighs fractured in separate accidents, and due to a congenital condition the legs ceased to grow normally, leaving him with the torso of an adult but the legs of a child, standing about 1.52 m (5 ft 0 in) tall.

Career

In Paris he studied under portraitist Léon Bonnat and later Fernand Cormon. He became a chronicler of Montmartre nightlife, frequenting the Moulin Rouge cabaret, which opened in 1889 and commissioned his famous posters. His lithographic posters — for performers such as Jane Avril, Aristide Bruant, and La Goulue — elevated advertising to art and are icons of the Art Nouveau style. His paintings, including At the Moulin Rouge and The Hangover, depict cabarets, theatres, brothels, and their inhabitants with empathy and psychological depth.

Later life and death

A lifelong alcohol dependence worsened in his final years. He suffered a breakdown, was briefly confined in a sanatorium, and died at his mother's estate in Saint-André-du-Bois on 9 September 1901, aged 36.

Legacy

The Musée Toulouse-Lautrec in Albi, opened in 1922 in the Palais de la Berbie, holds the largest public collection of his works. His posters and paintings remain defining images of Belle Époque Paris.
