Refugee travel document

A refugee travel document is an identity and travel document issued to a refugee by the country in which they lawfully reside, enabling them to travel internationally when they cannot obtain a passport from their country of origin. It is provided for under Article 28 of the 1951 Convention Relating to the Status of Refugees.

Form and use

The document resembles a passport booklet and typically states that the holder is a refugee under the 1951 Convention. Holders generally still need visas for destination countries, and the document usually does not permit travel to the refugee's country of origin. The earlier Nansen passport, created after the First World War, was a historical precursor.

Related documents

Stateless persons may receive an analogous document under the 1954 Convention Relating to the Status of Stateless Persons. Several countries also issue alien's passports or certificates of identity to other residents unable to obtain national passports.
