Orbicularis oculi muscle

OriginFrontal bone; medial palpebral ligament; lacrimal bone
InsertionLateral palpebral raphe
NerveFacial nerve (temporal and zygomatic branches)
ActionCloses the eyelids

The orbicularis oculi is a muscle in the face that closes the eyelids. It arises from the nasal part of the frontal bone, from the frontal process of the maxilla in front of the lacrimal groove, and from the anterior surface and borders of the medial palpebral ligament. Its fibres form a complete ellipse around the orbit without interruption at the lateral side.

Structure

The muscle is divided into three parts: the orbital part, which surrounds the orbit and produces forceful closure of the eye, as in winking against resistance; the palpebral part, within the eyelids themselves, responsible for gentle involuntary closure during blinking and sleep; and the lacrimal part (Horner's muscle), which helps draw tears through the lacrimal apparatus. The palpebral part is further divided into preseptal and pretarsal portions.

Function

Orbicularis oculi is the sphincter muscle of the eyelids. Its contraction closes the eye, protects the cornea, and drives the blink reflex. It is innervated by the facial nerve (cranial nerve VII), so facial nerve palsy can impair eye closure, leading to exposure keratopathy. Contraction of the orbital part also contributes to expressions such as squinting and the genuine ("Duchenne") smile, in which the muscles around the eyes contract along with the mouth muscles.
