Semi-closed primary

A semi-closed primary is a type of primary election in the United States in which voters who are not affiliated with a political party (independents or unaffiliated voters) may choose to vote in one party's primary, while registered members of a party may vote only in their own party's primary. It sits between closed primaries (party members only) and open primaries (any voter may vote in any primary).

By state

States using semi-closed rules include New Jersey, Connecticut, Massachusetts and others, though the details differ: in Massachusetts, unaffiliated voters may pull a ballot for either party without changing their registration, while in some other states voting in a primary temporarily registers the unaffiliated voter with that party. Party rules and litigation over states' power to set their primary systems have sometimes altered how the rules operate in practice.

Effects

Supporters argue semi-closed primaries broaden participation beyond registered partisans while protecting parties from organised raiding by rival supporters. Critics note that they can still exclude many registered independents who dislike formal affiliation and that the varying state rules confuse voters.
