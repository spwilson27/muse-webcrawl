Relic of the tooth of the Buddha

The relic of the tooth of the Buddha (Sinhala: දළදා වහන්සේ) is the foremost sacred relic of Buddhism in Sri Lanka, believed to be a tooth of Gautama Buddha. It is enshrined in the Temple of the Tooth (Sri Dalada Maligawa) in Kandy, where it is venerated daily and carried in procession during the annual Esala Perahera festival.

History

According to Sri Lankan chronicles, the tooth was retrieved from the Buddha's funeral pyre at Kushinagar and kept in Kalinga, India, before being smuggled to Sri Lanka in the 4th century CE, hidden in the hair of Princess Hemamali. Possession of the relic became bound up with legitimate kingship: whoever held the tooth was held to hold the right to rule the island, so it was guarded in the royal capital wherever that moved — Anuradhapura, Polonnaruwa, and finally Kandy.

The Portuguese claimed to have destroyed a tooth relic at Goa in 1560, an event Buddhists dispute. In 1998 the Temple of the Tooth was bombed by the LTTE; the relic survived. The relic itself is rarely displayed; it rests within a series of nested golden caskets on a lotus flower base, shown to devotees only by proxy.
