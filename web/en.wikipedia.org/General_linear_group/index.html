General linear group

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, the general linear group of degree n is the set of n×n invertible matrices, together with the operation of ordinary matrix multiplication. This forms a group, because the product of two invertible matrices is again invertible, and the inverse of an invertible matrix is invertible. The group is so named because the columns (or rows) of an invertible matrix are linearly independent, hence the vectors/points they define are in "general linear position".

For a vector space V over a field F, the general linear group of V, written GL(V) or Aut(V), is the group of all automorphisms of V, i.e. the set of all bijective linear transformations V → V, together with functional composition as group operation. When V has finite dimension n, GL(V) and GL(n, F) are isomorphic.

Properties

The special linear group SL(n, F), the subgroup of matrices with determinant 1, is a normal subgroup of GL(n, F). Over the real numbers, GL(n, ℝ) is a Lie group of dimension n2 with two connected components, distinguished by the sign of the determinant; its Lie algebra consists of all n×n matrices with the commutator bracket. Over a finite field with q elements, GL(n, q) is a finite group whose order is

(qn − 1)(qn − q)…(qn − qn−1).

Related groups

Related constructions include the projective linear group PGL (the quotient by scalar matrices), the special linear group, orthogonal and symplectic groups (subgroups preserving a bilinear form), and the group of units of a ring, of which general linear groups are a special case. The representation theory of GL(n) is a central topic in algebra, connecting combinatorics (Schur polynomials, Young tableaux) with geometry.
