The West Highland Way (Scottish Gaelic: Slighe na Gàidhealtachd an Iar) is a linear long-distance footpath in Scotland, running 154 kilometres (96 miles) from Milngavie, on the outskirts of Glasgow, to Fort William in the Scottish Highlands. Officially opened on 6 October 1980 by Lord Mansfield, it was Scotland's first designated long-distance route, and is now one of the country's Great Trails.

Route

The trail follows old drovers' roads, military roads built after the Jacobite risings, and disused railway lines. From Milngavie it passes through Mugdock Country Park and along the eastern shore of Loch Lomond — the most rugged section of the route — before climbing through Glen Falloch to Crianlarich and Tyndrum. It then crosses the expanse of Rannoch Moor to the Kingshouse Hotel in Glencoe, ascends the Devil's Staircase, the highest point on the trail, descends to Kinlochleven, and crosses the Lairigmor pass before the final stretch through Glen Nevis to Fort William, beneath Ben Nevis.

Usage

An estimated 120,000 people use the path each year, of whom some 36,000 walk its full length, typically over five to eight days. Most walkers travel south to north. Baggage-transfer services and a range of hotels, bunkhouses, and campsites serve the route; wild camping is permitted subject to the Scottish Outdoor Access Code.
