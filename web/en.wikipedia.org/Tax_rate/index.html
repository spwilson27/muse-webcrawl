Tax rate

Tax rates may be quoted as marginal, average (effective), or statutory rates.

In a tax system, the tax rate is the ratio (usually expressed as a percentage) at which a business or person is taxed. There are several methods used to present a tax rate: statutory, average, marginal, and effective. These rates can also be presented using different definitions applied to a tax base: inclusive and exclusive.

Statutory rate

A statutory tax rate is the legally imposed rate. An income tax code may have multiple statutory rates for different income levels, and a sales tax may have a flat statutory rate.

Marginal rate

The marginal tax rate is the rate applied to the last dollar (or other unit) of the tax base. In a progressive income tax, additional income is taxed at the rate of the bracket into which it falls. The marginal rate is the relevant rate for decisions about earning additional income, and it is generally higher than the taxpayer's average rate under a progressive system.

Average and effective rates

The average tax rate is the total tax paid divided by the total tax base. The effective tax rate measures the tax actually paid as a proportion of some measure of income, taking account of deductions, credits, and exemptions; it usually differs from the statutory rate and is widely used to compare the real tax burden across taxpayers or jurisdictions.

Inclusive and exclusive rates

A tax rate may be expressed as tax-inclusive (tax as a percentage of a price including the tax) or tax-exclusive (tax as a percentage of the price excluding tax). Income taxes are generally quoted inclusively and sales taxes exclusively, a distinction that matters when comparing rates across countries.
