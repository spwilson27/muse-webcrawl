Medici Fountain

The Medici Fountain (French: fontaine Médicis) is a monumental fountain in the Jardin du Luxembourg in the 6th arrondissement of Paris. Built in about 1630 by Marie de' Medici, the widow of King Henry IV of France and regent of King Louis XIII, it was designed in the Italian Renaissance style of the grottoes and fountains of the queen's native Florence. It was moved and extensively altered in the 19th century, when the sculptor Auguste Ottin added its famous central group of statues depicting Polyphemus surprising Acis and Galatea.

The Medici Fountain, Jardin du Luxembourg, Paris.

History

Marie de' Medici commissioned the Luxembourg Palace and its gardens as a reminder of her childhood home, the Palazzo Pitti in Florence. The fountain, probably designed by Tommaso Francini, the Florentine fountain-maker brought to France by the queen, took the form of a grotto façade decorated with rusticated stonework and statues, in front of which water flowed into a basin.

In the 1860s, during Baron Haussmann's renovation of Paris, the fountain stood in the path of the new rue Auguste-Comte. The architect Alphonse de Gisors moved the fountain some thirty metres to its present location, restored the grotto, and added the long, tree-shaded reflecting basin in front of it. Auguste Ottin sculpted the dramatic central group (1866): the giant Polyphemus, in bronze, leaning over a rock to spy on the lovers Acis and Galatea, carved in marble below.

Description

The fountain consists of an Italianate grotto façade with three niches framed by rustication and topped by the Medici arms. The long rectangular basin, bordered by plane trees and stone vases, reflects the grotto and is populated by goldfish. The Medici Fountain is considered one of the most romantic spots in Paris and a celebrated example of 17th-century garden design as reshaped by the Second Empire.
