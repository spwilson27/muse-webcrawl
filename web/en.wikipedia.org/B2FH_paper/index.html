B²FH paper

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The B²FH paper, published in 1957 in Reviews of Modern Physics under the title "Synthesis of the Elements in Stars," is the foundational scientific paper of stellar nucleosynthesis theory. Its authors were E. Margaret Burbidge, Geoffrey Burbidge, William A. Fowler, and Fred Hoyle — the "B²FH" being drawn from their initials.

AuthorsE. M. Burbidge, G. Burbidge, W. A. Fowler, F. Hoyle
Published1957, Reviews of Modern Physics
SubjectStellar nucleosynthesis

Content

The paper described how nearly all chemical elements beyond hydrogen and helium are forged in stars: through hydrogen and helium burning, the slow neutron-capture s-process, the rapid r-process in explosive events, the p-process for proton-rich nuclei, and other mechanisms, matching the observed cosmic abundances. It famously predicted that technetium — an element with no stable isotopes — should be observed in red giant stars if it were being made there, as had recently been reported.

Impact and recognition

B²FH established nuclear astrophysics as a field, explaining the origin of the elements and earning Fowler a share of the 1983 Nobel Prize in Physics (awarded for theoretical studies of nuclear reactions important in the formation of the chemical elements). The exclusion of the Burbidges and Hoyle from that prize has often been debated; Hoyle's earlier prediction of a resonant state of carbon-12, confirmed experimentally, is one of the paper's famous antecedents.
