Haldimand Proclamation

The Haldimand Proclamation was issued on 25 October 1784 by Frederick Haldimand, Governor of the Province of Quebec, granting a tract of land to the Mohawk and other Six Nations (Haudenosaunee) peoples who had allied with Britain and lost their lands in the newly independent United States after the American Revolutionary War.

Terms

The proclamation authorised the recipients to take possession of lands along the Grand River in present-day Ontario, extending six miles (about 10 km) on each side of the river from its mouth at Lake Erie to its source — approximately 950,000 acres (385,000 hectares). The grant followed a Crown purchase of the land from the Mississaugas in May 1784.

The Mohawk, led by Joseph Brant (Thayendanegea), settled in the tract and founded communities at present-day Brantford and the Six Nations of the Grand River reserve, today the most populous First Nations reserve in Canada.

Disputes

The extent and legal character of the grant have been disputed for more than two centuries. Portions of the tract were sold, leased or surrendered over time, sometimes under circumstances the Six Nations contest. The present Six Nations reserve comprises about 46,500 acres — roughly 5% of the original grant. Land claims based on the Haldimand Proclamation remain active, most prominently the Caledonia land dispute (Kanonhstaton / Douglas Creek Estates) beginning in 2006.
