Los Angeles (US: /lɔːs ˈændʒələs/; Spanish: Los Ángeles, lit. 'The Angels'), often referred to by its initials L.A., is the most populous city in the U.S. state of California. With a population of roughly 3.9 million residents within the city limits as of 2020, Los Angeles is the second-most populous city in the United States, behind only New York City; it is also the commercial, financial and cultural center of Southern California.

Los Angeles is known for its Mediterranean climate, ethnic and cultural diversity, the Hollywood film industry, and its sprawling metropolitan area. The city lies in a basin in Southern California adjacent to the Pacific Ocean, extending through the Santa Monica Mountains and into the San Fernando Valley. It covers about 469 square miles (1,210 km²), and is the county seat of Los Angeles County, the most populous county in the United States.

History

The area comprising modern-day Los Angeles was first settled by the indigenous Tongva and Chumash tribes. Spanish explorer Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo claimed the area for the Spanish Empire in 1542. The city was founded on September 4, 1781, under Spanish governor Felipe de Neve, on the village of Yaanga. It became part of the First Mexican Republic in 1821 following the Mexican War of Independence. After the Mexican–American War, Los Angeles and the rest of California were purchased as part of the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo in 1848, becoming part of the United States.

Oil was discovered in the city and surrounding area in the 1890s, and by 1923 California was producing a quarter of the world's petroleum output. The completion of the Los Angeles Aqueduct in 1913, delivering water from Eastern California, spurred the city's rapid growth in the early 20th century.

Geography

Los Angeles is divided into numerous districts and neighborhoods, including Downtown, Hollywood, the Westside, the San Fernando Valley and South Los Angeles. The city is irregularly shaped and includes a narrow strip extending south to San Pedro and the Port of Los Angeles, one of the busiest container ports in the world. The Los Angeles River runs through the city for about 51 miles.

Economy

The economy of Los Angeles is driven by international trade, entertainment (television, motion pictures, video games and recorded music), aerospace, technology, petroleum, fashion, apparel and tourism. The city is home to major studios in neighboring Burbank and Culver City, and hosts the headquarters of numerous Fortune 500 companies. The twin ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach together form one of the largest port complexes in the world.

Culture

Los Angeles is frequently billed as the "Creative Capital of the World" because, according to some sources, one in every six residents works in a creative industry. It is home to institutions such as the Los Angeles County Museum of Art (LACMA), the Getty Center, the Walt Disney Concert Hall and the Hollywood Bowl. The city hosted the Summer Olympic Games in 1932 and 1984, and is scheduled to host them again in 2028.

Transportation

Los Angeles is known for its extensive freeway network and car culture, though it also operates a growing Metro Rail and bus system. Los Angeles International Airport (LAX) is one of the busiest airports in the world.
