Halberd

A halberd (also spelled halbert or halbard) is a two-handed polearm that came to prominent use from the 13th through the 16th centuries. The halberd consists of an axe blade topped with a spike mounted on a long shaft. It always has a hook or thorn on the back side of the axe blade for grappling mounted combatants — pulling horsemen from their mounts and dragging them to the ground.

History

The halberd rose to fame as the weapon of Swiss infantry in the Burgundian Wars of the 1470s, complementing the pike in their pike squares. Its combination of spear point, axe blade, and hook made it versatile against both infantry and cavalry, and it was widely adopted across Europe, becoming a standard weapon of German Landsknechte and Spanish tercios. As firearms spread, the halberd declined as a battlefield weapon but persisted into the 17th century as a weapon of sergeants and guards, used to dress ranks and signal orders. It survives today chiefly as a ceremonial weapon, most famously carried by the Papal Swiss Guard.

Design

A typical halberd shaft was about 1.5 to 1.8 metres (5 to 6 feet) long. The head combined three functions: a spike for thrusting, a broad axe-like blade for cutting blows, and a rear beak or hook. Metal langets ran down the shaft to protect the wood from being cut through.
