Baden-Powell Trail

The Baden-Powell Trail is a hiking trail approximately 48 kilometres long that traverses the North Shore Mountains above Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada, from Horseshoe Bay in West Vancouver to Deep Cove in North Vancouver. It is named after Robert Baden-Powell, the founder of the Scout movement.

Route

The trail was built in 1971 by Scouts and Girl Guides to mark the centennial of British Columbia's entry into Confederation. It crosses the municipalities of West Vancouver and North Vancouver, passing through forested mountain terrain, crossing creeks and passing landmarks such as Cypress Mountain, Grouse Mountain, Lynn Canyon and Mount Seymour's lower slopes. The route climbs and descends repeatedly, with total elevation gain of well over a thousand metres.

Use

The trail is popular with hikers, trail runners and mountain bikers on permitted sections. It forms the course of an annual trail-running event and connects with numerous side trails on the North Shore. Black bears are common in the area, and hikers are advised to be prepared for steep, rooty and often wet conditions.
