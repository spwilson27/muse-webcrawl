Fixed asset

A fixed asset, also known as a long-lived asset or property, plant and equipment (PP&E), is an item of value owned by a business that is used over a long period — generally more than one accounting year — in the production of goods or services, rather than being held for sale in the ordinary course of business.

Characteristics

Fixed assets include tangible items such as land, buildings, machinery, vehicles, and computers. On the balance sheet they are initially recorded at cost and, except for land, are depreciated over their useful lives to allocate their cost to the periods they benefit. In accounting standards such as IFRS (IAS 16) and US GAAP, fixed assets are classified as non-current assets.

Fixed versus current assets

The term "fixed" does not mean the asset is physically immovable; it distinguishes long-term productive assets from current assets such as cash, inventory, and receivables, which are expected to be converted into cash within a year.
