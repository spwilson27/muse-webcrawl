Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy

Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy is a 1974 spy novel by British author John le Carré. It follows retired intelligence officer George Smiley, who is secretly tasked with unmasking a Soviet mole — codenamed "Gerald" — at the highest level of the British Secret Intelligence Service, known as the Circus. The novel is the first part of the "Karla trilogy" and is widely regarded as one of the greatest spy novels ever written.

Plot

After a disastrous operation in Czechoslovakia ("Testify") ends agent Jim Prideaux's career and disgraces the Circus leadership, Smiley is brought out of forced retirement. Working from files and interviews — including the dismissed researcher Connie Sachs and agent Peter Guillam — he narrows the suspects to four senior officers, codenamed Tinker, Tailor, Soldier and Poorman after the nursery rhyme. The mole proves to be the charismatic Bill Haydon, who has been trading Circus secrets to the Soviet spymaster Karla while feeding London carefully curated "gold" via the Witchcraft source.

Adaptations

The BBC adapted the novel in 1979 as a seven-part serial starring Alec Guinness as Smiley, a performance considered definitive. A feature film directed by Tomas Alfredson, starring Gary Oldman, was released in 2011 to critical acclaim.
