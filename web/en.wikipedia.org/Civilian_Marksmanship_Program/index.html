The Civilian Marksmanship Program (CMP) is a national organization dedicated to training and educating U.S. citizens in responsible uses of firearms and airguns through gun safety training, marksmanship training, and competitions. The CMP is a federally chartered 501(c)(3) corporation that places its highest priority on serving youth through gun safety and marksmanship activities that encourage personal growth and build life skills.

History

The programme's origins lie in the National Board for the Promotion of Rifle Practice (NBPRP), created by Congress in 1903 at the urging of President Theodore Roosevelt, who wanted American civilians trained in marksmanship so they could serve effectively in wartime. The Director of Civilian Marksmanship ran the programme within the U.S. Army for most of the twentieth century. In 1996, Congress privatised the programme, transferring it to the newly created Corporation for the Promotion of Rifle Practice and Firearm Safety, which operates as the CMP.

Activities

The CMP administers the National Matches at Camp Perry, Ohio, together with the National Rifle Association, and runs regional CMP Games matches, clinics, and junior programmes including the JROTC air rifle championships. To fund its mission, the CMP sells surplus U.S. military rifles — most famously the M1 Garand, along with M1903 Springfields, M1 carbines, and surplus M1911 pistols — to qualified U.S. citizens who meet statutory eligibility requirements, including membership in an affiliated club and proof of marksmanship-related activity. Its main facilities are at Camp Perry and the CMP Talladega Marksmanship Park in Alabama, opened in 2015.
