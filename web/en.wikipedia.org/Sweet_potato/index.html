Sweet potato

The sweet potato (Ipomoea batatas) is a dicotyledonous plant of the morning glory family, Convolvulaceae, grown for its large, starchy, sweet-tasting tuberous roots. It is an important food crop worldwide, valued especially for orange-fleshed varieties rich in beta-carotene.

History

Sweet potato was domesticated in the Americas at least 5,000 years ago. Remarkably, it was also present in Polynesia before European contact — linguistic and genetic evidence suggests pre-Columbian transfer across the Pacific. After Columbus it spread to Europe, Africa and Asia, becoming a staple famine-relief crop in China and Japan.

Cultivation and varieties

The plant is a herbaceous perennial vine propagated from slips. Cultivars range from white and yellow to orange and deep purple flesh; orange varieties combat vitamin A deficiency, while purple types are rich in anthocyanins. Despite the name, it is only distantly related to the common potato and is not a yam, though the names are confused in North American commerce.

Nutrition

Cooked sweet potato provides complex carbohydrates, fibre, potassium, vitamin C and, in orange forms, exceptional amounts of vitamin A. Its glycaemic index is moderate and varies with cooking method.
