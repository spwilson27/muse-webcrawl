Nepheline

Category: Tectosilicate, feldspathoid
Formula: (Na,K)AlSiO₄
Crystal system: Hexagonal
Mohs hardness: 5.5–6
Colour: White, grey, brown, colourless

Nepheline, also called nephelite (from the Greek nephelē, “cloud”), is a rock-forming mineral in the feldspathoid group – a silica-undersaturated aluminosilicate with the formula (Na,K)AlSiO₄. It occurs in silica-poor igneous rocks such as nepheline syenites and phonolites.

Properties

Nepheline has a low silica content and high alumina relative to other silicates. Crystals are rare and usually hexagonal; more commonly the mineral appears as glassy or greasy-looking grains. Its greasy lustre on fracture surfaces led to the older name elæolite (“oil stone”). When a fragment is immersed in acid, it becomes cloudy, the origin of the name nepheline.

Occurrence and uses

Important localities include the Kola Peninsula in Russia, Ontario in Canada, and Larvik in Norway. Nepheline syenite is mined as a source of alumina, alkalis, and silica for the glass and ceramics industries, since nepheline lowers melting temperatures and improves chemical durability of the product.
