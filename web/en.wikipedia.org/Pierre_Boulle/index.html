Pierre Boulle

Pierre Boulle (20 February 1912 – 30 January 1994) was a French novelist best known for two works adapted into celebrated films: The Bridge over the River Kwai (1952) and Planet of the Apes (1963).

Life

Born in Avignon, Boulle trained as an electrical engineer and worked on rubber plantations in Malaya. During World War II he joined the Free French forces and served as a secret agent in China, Burma, and French Indochina; he was captured by Vichy forces on the Mekong River and sentenced to hard labor. His wartime experiences informed his fiction. After the war he settled in Paris and became a full-time writer.

Works

Le Pont de la rivière Kwaï drew on his captivity; the 1957 film adaptation won the Academy Award for Best Picture, with Boulle credited for the screenplay. La Planète des singes (1963), a satirical science-fiction novel, launched the long-running Planet of the Apes film franchise beginning in 1968. Boulle wrote numerous other novels and story collections.
