The Port of Vancouver is the largest port in Canada and the fourth largest in North America by tonnes of cargo. Administered by the Vancouver Fraser Port Authority, it stretches along roughly 350 km of shoreline from Point Roberts and the Fraser River to Burrard Inlet, encompassing the city of Vancouver and neighbouring municipalities.

Operations

The port's 29 terminals handle containers, bulk commodities such as grain, coal, potash and sulphur, liquid bulk, automobiles, breakbulk forest products and cruise passengers at Canada Place. It trades with more than 170 economies and is a principal gateway for Canada's Asia-Pacific commerce, supporting hundreds of thousands of jobs across the country.

History

Commercial shipping grew with the timber trade after the 1860s and expanded rapidly after the Canadian Pacific Railway reached the coast in 1887, making Vancouver Canada's Pacific terminus. The present port authority was formed in 2008 by amalgamating the Vancouver, Fraser River and North Fraser port authorities.
