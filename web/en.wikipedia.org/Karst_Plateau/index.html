Karst Plateau

The Karst Plateau (Slovene: Kras; Italian: Carso; German: Karst) is a limestone plateau region in southwestern Slovenia extending into northeastern Italy, between the Vipava Valley, the Gulf of Trieste and the Brkini Hills. It is the type region for karst topography — landscapes formed by the dissolution of soluble rock — and the scientific discipline of karst studies takes its name from this plateau.

Geography

The plateau covers roughly 700 square kilometres and rises from about 300 m near the coast to over 600 m inland. Its surface is dotted with dolines (sinkholes), dry valleys, poljes and disappearing streams such as the Reka River, which sinks at the Škocjan Caves and resurfaces near the Timavo springs by the Adriatic. The strong, cold bora wind shapes vegetation and traditional stone architecture across the plateau.

Karst science

Because its features were described early by scholars of the Habsburg Empire, the German form of the region's name, Karst, became the international scientific term for dissolution landscapes in limestone, dolomite and gypsum. The Škocjan Caves, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, contain one of the largest known underground river canyons in the world.

Culture and economy

Villages of the plateau, such as Štanjel and Lipica, are known for dry-stone construction, the ruby-red Teran wine made from Refosco grapes grown in the iron-rich red soil (terra rossa), air-dried Karst prosciutto, and the Lipizzaner horse stud at Lipica, founded in 1580. Karst shepherd and Lipizzan traditions are inscribed in regional heritage lists.
