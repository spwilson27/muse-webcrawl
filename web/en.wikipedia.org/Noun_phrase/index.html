Noun phrase

A noun phrase (NP) is a phrase that has a noun or an indefinite pronoun as its head and that functions in a sentence as a subject, object, or predicative expression. Noun phrases are very common cross-linguistically and every language that has nouns and pronouns is considered to have them.

Structure

A noun phrase consists of a head noun together with any dependents: determiners (such as the, a, this), adjectives, prepositional phrases, and relative clauses. For example, in "the big house on the hill", the head is house, modified by the determiner the, the adjective big, and the prepositional phrase on the hill.

Theoretical approaches

In traditional grammar the noun is the head; in generative grammar since the 1980s, many linguists have adopted the determiner phrase (DP) hypothesis, under which the determiner rather than the noun is the head of the phrase.

Function

Noun phrases typically serve as arguments of verbs, as subjects and objects, and as complements of prepositions.
