Operation Radd-ul-Fasaad

Operation Radd-ul-Fasaad
Date22 February 2017 – present
LocationPakistan
ObjectiveEliminate residual terrorism and consolidate gains of previous operations
Conducted byPakistan Army, Pakistan Air Force, Pakistan Navy, civil armed forces and law-enforcement agencies

Operation Radd-ul-Fasaad (Urdu: آپریشن رد الفساد, "rejection of strife/discord") is a nationwide counterterrorism operation launched by the Pakistan Armed Forces on 22 February 2017. Announced by the army's media wing, Inter-Services Public Relations (ISPR), in the wake of a wave of suicide bombings that struck the country that month — including the bombing of the Lal Shahbaz Qalandar shrine in Sehwan — the operation aimed to indiscriminately eliminate residual and latent threats of terrorism across all four provinces, consolidate the gains of earlier operations, and further secure Pakistan's borders.

Objectives and scope

According to the ISPR statement, the operation involved broad-based security and counterterrorism operations by the Rangers in Punjab, continuation of ongoing operations elsewhere, and a countrywide crackdown on illegal weapons and explosives. Air Force, Navy, civil armed forces and civilian law-enforcement agencies all participated. A deweaponization campaign and explosive control formed part of the stated programme, alongside border security management.

Course and results

The operation ran in parallel with the follow-up to Operation Zarb-e-Azb in North Waziristan and the Karachi targeted action. Military officials periodically reported intelligence-based operations across the country, the arrest or killing of militants, and the recovery of arms caches. Official summaries released on anniversaries of the launch claimed tens of thousands of intelligence-based operations and a marked reduction in terrorist incidents, though attacks by groups such as the Pakistani Taliban and Islamic State affiliates continued, particularly after 2021.

Criticism

Commentators debated the operation's scope and accountability, noting the expanded military role in internal security and questioning aspects of the national counterterrorism narrative, while the government presented it as a central pillar of the National Action Plan.
