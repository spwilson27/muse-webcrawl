FIM Motocross World Championship

Category: Motocross
Country: International
Inaugural season: 1957
Classes: MXGP, MX2

The FIM Motocross World Championship is the premier series of motocross racing, organized by the Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme (FIM). The series runs as a worldwide tour of Grand Prix events on outdoor natural-terrain and man-made circuits.

History

The championship traces its origins to the 1947 Motocross des Nations team event, with an individual European championship beginning in 1952 and elevated to world championship status in 1957. Early competition featured 500cc two-stroke machines; classes were later reorganized and four-stroke machinery became dominant in the 2000s.

Classes

The two main classes are MXGP, for riders up to 23 years and older on 450cc machines, and MX2, restricted to riders aged 23 and under on 250cc machines. A women's world championship (WMX) is also held. Each Grand Prix consists of two points-paying motos per class.

Champions

The most successful rider in championship history is Stefan Everts of Belgium, who won ten world titles. Other multiple champions include Joël Robert, Roger De Coster, Antonio Cairoli, Jeffrey Herlings and Tim Gajser.
