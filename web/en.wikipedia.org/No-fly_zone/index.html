No-fly zone

A no-fly zone, also called a no-flight zone (NFZ) or air exclusion zone, is a territory or area established by a military power over which certain aircraft are not permitted to fly. Such zones are usually established over an adversary's territory during a conflict, with enforcing forces ready to attack prohibited aircraft and suppress air defences.

Historical examples

Following the 1991 Gulf War, the United States, United Kingdom, and France maintained no-fly zones over northern and southern Iraq to protect Kurdish and Shia populations. In 1993 NATO enforced a no-fly zone over Bosnia and Herzegovina under Operation Deny Flight. In 2011, UN Security Council Resolution 1973 authorized a no-fly zone over Libya during its civil war, enforced by NATO under Operation Unified Protector.

Legal and strategic considerations

No-fly zones are a form of military intervention short of full-scale war, but enforcing them typically requires strikes on air-defence systems and continuous combat air patrols. Their legal basis usually rests on a Security Council mandate or a claim of humanitarian necessity.
