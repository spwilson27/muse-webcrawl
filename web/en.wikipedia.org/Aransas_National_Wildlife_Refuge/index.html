The Aransas National Wildlife Refuge is a protected area on the Gulf Coast of Texas, best known as the principal wintering ground of the endangered whooping crane. Established in 1937, the refuge covers roughly 115,000 acres of barrier island, tidal marsh, oak woodland, and coastal prairie across several units, including part of Matagorda Island.

Wildlife

The wild flock of whooping cranes, which migrates some 2,400 miles from breeding grounds in Wood Buffalo National Park in Canada, winters at Aransas feeding on blue crabs and other marsh foods in shallow tidal flats. From a low of about 15 birds in 1941, the flock has grown to several hundred through protection and conservation. The refuge also shelters alligators, javelinas, bobcats, hundreds of bird species, and sea turtles on Gulf beaches.

Management

Managed by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, the refuge uses prescribed burning, water management, and habitat protection to maintain coastal ecosystems. Threats include sea-level rise, reduced freshwater inflows to the bays, and industrial accidents such as oil spills.
