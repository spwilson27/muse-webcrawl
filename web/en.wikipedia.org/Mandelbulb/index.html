Mandelbulb

The Mandelbulb is a three-dimensional fractal, constructed in 2009 by Daniel White and Paul Nylander using spherical coordinates. A canonical 3D Mandelbrot set does not exist, since there is no 3D analogue of the 2D space of complex numbers; the Mandelbrot set cannot be extended straightforwardly. The Mandelbrot set cannot be extended to 3D because the underlying algebra (complex numbers) has no natural three-dimensional counterpart; the Mandelbulb instead approximates the idea using a "power" operation defined in spherical coordinates.

A ray-traced image of the 3D Mandelbulb for the iteration z → z⁸ + c.

Formula

For a point represented in spherical coordinates (r, θ, φ), the nth-power operation is defined as:

(r, θ, φ)ⁿ = (rⁿ, nθ, nφ)

One then iterates v → vⁿ + c, where c is the point being tested, and points whose iterates escape to infinity are excluded from the set. The most commonly rendered version uses n = 8, whose bulbous, intricately lobed surface gives the fractal its name.

History

Before 2009, three-dimensional renderings of quadratic fractals had been explored by several researchers, including Rudy Rucker and, in the 1980s–2000s, various attempts at quaternion Julia sets. White and Nylander's spherical-coordinate formula, developed and publicised through the Fractal Forums community, produced the first widely shared, richly detailed "true 3D Mandelbrot-like" images. The Mandelbulb has since become a popular subject for ray-traced fractal artwork and 3D printing.
