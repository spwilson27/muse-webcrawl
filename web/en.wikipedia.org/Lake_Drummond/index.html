Lake Drummond is a freshwater lake at the centre of the Great Dismal Swamp, straddling the border of southeastern Virginia and northeastern North Carolina. It is one of only two natural freshwater lakes in Virginia, the other being Mountain Lake. The lake covers about 3,142 acres and is remarkably shallow, averaging around six feet deep.

Formation and ecology

The lake's origin is debated; theories include a peat burn, a meteorite impact, or tectonic activity. Its dark, acidic water is stained by tannins from surrounding cypress and juniper trees. Bald cypress growing in the lake support abundant bird life, and the lake lies within the Great Dismal Swamp National Wildlife Refuge.

History

The lake is named for William Drummond, a colonial governor of North Carolina who tradition says discovered it while hunting in the 1660s. George Washington surveyed the swamp and promoted the Dismal Swamp Canal, completed in the early 19th century, whose feeder ditch still connects to the lake. The lake has inspired folklore, including Thomas Moore's poem "The Lake of the Dismal Swamp."
