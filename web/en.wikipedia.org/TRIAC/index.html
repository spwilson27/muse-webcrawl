TRIAC

TRIAC — a three-terminal electronic component that conducts current in either direction when triggered. Subtype of thyristor; name derived from "triode for alternating current".

A TRIAC (triode for alternating current; also bidirectional triode thyristor or bilateral triode thyristor) is a three-terminal electronic component that conducts current in either direction when triggered. The term TRIAC is a genericised trademark.

Thyristors, including TRIACs, are distinguished from other switches by being able to be turned on not only by a current into the gate but also by a rate of change (dv/dt) of the voltage at the terminals. A TRIAC can be triggered by a gate signal in either polarity, unlike a conventional thyristor (SCR) which only conducts in one direction.

Operation

Once triggered, the device continues to conduct until the current through it drops below a certain threshold value, the holding current, such as at the end of a half-cycle of alternating current (AC) mains power. This makes the TRIAC a convenient switch for AC circuits, allowing the control of very large power flows with milliampere-scale control currents. In addition, applying a trigger pulse at a controlled phase angle in an AC cycle allows control of the percentage of current that flows through the TRIAC to the load (phase control), commonly used, for example, in controlling the speed of low-power induction motors, in dimming lamps, and in controlling AC heating resistors.

Applications

Low-power TRIACs are used in many applications such as light dimmers, speed controls for electric fans and other electric motors, and in the modern computerized control circuits of many household small and major appliances.
