Henry VII (28 January 1457 – 21 April 1509) was King of England from 22 August 1485 until his death in 1509. He was the first monarch of the House of Tudor. Henry became king after defeating Richard III at the Battle of Bosworth Field, the culmination of the Wars of the Roses, and he was the last king of England to win his throne on the field of battle.

Early life and accession

Henry was born at Pembroke Castle in Wales, the posthumous son of Edmund Tudor, Earl of Richmond, and Lady Margaret Beaufort, a descendant of John of Gaunt. His claim to the throne was tenuous, descending through the legitimised Beaufort line. He spent much of his youth in exile in Brittany while the Yorkists held power. In August 1485 he landed at Milford Haven with French support, marched through Wales gathering support, and defeated Richard III at Bosworth, where Richard was killed. Henry was crowned on 30 October 1485 and in January 1486 married Elizabeth of York, uniting the Lancastrian and Yorkist lines; the Tudor rose combined the emblems of both houses.

Reign

Henry faced Yorkist pretenders, notably Lambert Simnel (defeated at Stoke Field in 1487) and Perkin Warbeck, who troubled him through the 1490s. To secure his dynasty he strengthened royal finances, avoiding costly wars and accumulating a large treasury; enforced the law against retaining armed followers; used bonds and recognisances to control the nobility; and extended royal justice through the Court of Star Chamber. His financial exactions, executed by agents such as Empson and Dudley, made him unpopular by the end of his reign.

In foreign policy he pursued trade treaties (such as the Intercursus Magnus with Burgundy), the Treaty of Étaples with France (1492), and marriage alliances: his son Arthur married Catherine of Aragon, and his daughter Margaret married James IV of Scotland.

Death and succession

Arthur's early death in 1502 and Queen Elizabeth's death in 1503 darkened Henry's last years. He died at Richmond Palace on 21 April 1509 and was succeeded peacefully by his second son, Henry VIII — the first uncontested English succession in decades. He is buried in the Henry VII Lady Chapel at Westminster Abbey, which he built.
