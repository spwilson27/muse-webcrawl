Fleet Marine Force

The Fleet Marine Force (FMF) is a component of the United States Navy and Marine Corps consisting of Marine Corps units assigned to naval fleets for amphibious and expeditionary operations. Established in 1933, it formalised the Marine Corps' role as the Navy's land combat arm operating from the sea.

Organisation

The Fleet Marine Force is organised into Marine Expeditionary Forces aligned with the Atlantic and Pacific fleets. Its units include ground combat, aviation, and logistics elements combined into Marine Air-Ground Task Forces (MAGTFs) of varying size, from Marine Expeditionary Units aboard amphibious ships to full expeditionary forces.

Personnel and qualification

Navy personnel — particularly hospital corpsmen, chaplains, and religious program specialists — serve embedded with Marine units and may earn the Fleet Marine Force warfare qualification, authorising them to wear the FMF insignia. The FMF played a central role in the island-hopping campaigns of the Second World War and remains the basis of US amphibious doctrine.
