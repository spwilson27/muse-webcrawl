Atum

Major cult centre: Heliopolis
Consort: Iusaaset (in some traditions)
Offspring: Shu and Tefnut

Atum is an ancient Egyptian creator deity, central to the Heliopolitan cosmogony. A self-created god who emerged from the primordial waters of Nun, Atum was said to have produced the first divine pair, Shu (air) and Tefnut (moisture), beginning the genealogy of the Ennead.

Mythology

In the Pyramid Texts Atum appears as both creator and a solar god associated with the setting sun, complementing Ra as the morning sun. He was often depicted as a man wearing the double crown of Upper and Lower Egypt, or as a serpent.

Worship

His principal cult centre was Heliopolis. In later periods Atum was closely identified with Ra as Ra-Atum, embodying the sun at evening and its renewal.
