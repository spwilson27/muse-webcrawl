Child actor

A child actor or child actress is a child or adolescent who performs on stage, in film, or on television. Child performers have been prominent since the early days of cinema, with stars such as Shirley Temple, Jackie Coogan, and Judy Garland achieving worldwide fame.

Regulation

Because of the vulnerability of minors, many jurisdictions regulate child performance through limits on working hours, requirements for on-set teachers and chaperones, and trust arrangements protecting earnings — notably California's Coogan Act, named after Jackie Coogan, whose parents spent his fortune, which requires a portion of a child performer's earnings to be set aside in a blocked trust account.

Issues

The transition to adult roles and the psychological effects of early fame are recurring themes in discussion of child actors; some achieve lasting adult careers, while others leave the industry or struggle with its pressures.
