Waterfowl

Waterfowl are birds belonging to the family Anatidae, within the order Anseriformes. The group includes ducks, geese, and swans — birds strongly adapted to life on and around water, with webbed feet, waterproof plumage, and (in most species) broad, flattened bills.

Characteristics

Waterfowl have dense, water-repellent feathers maintained by preen oil, and their webbed feet make them strong swimmers. Many species are migratory, travelling long distances between breeding grounds in northern latitudes and wintering areas further south. Their bills vary with diet: dabbling ducks strain small invertebrates and plant matter from shallow water, while geese graze on grasses and mergansers have serrated bills for catching fish.

Taxonomy and species

The family Anatidae contains roughly 170 species in about 50 genera. Familiar members include the mallard (Anas platyrhynchos), the Canada goose (Branta canadensis), and the mute swan (Cygnus olor). The magpie goose of Australia and northern New Guinea is sometimes placed in its own family, Anseranatidae.

Relationship with humans

Several species have been domesticated, notably the domestic duck (derived from the mallard and the Muscovy duck) and domestic geese (from the greylag and swan goose). Waterfowl are hunted for sport and food, farmed for eggs, meat, and down, and are central to wetland conservation efforts worldwide.
