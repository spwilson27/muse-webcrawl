King Crimson are an English progressive rock band formed in London in 1968. The group is widely regarded as a foundational influence on the progressive rock movement. Throughout their history they underwent numerous personnel changes — with guitarist Robert Fripp the only constant member — and drew on a wide range of influences including jazz, classical, folk, heavy metal, gamelan, industrial, and electronic music.

History

The band emerged from the trio Giles, Giles and Fripp. Their debut album, In the Court of the Crimson King (1969), is considered a landmark of progressive rock, combining Mellotron-driven symphonic textures with the jazz and classical leanings of the title track and "21st Century Schizoid Man". After a period of unstable line-ups and albums including In the Wake of Poseidon (1970) and Lizard (1970), Fripp rebuilt the band around Bill Bruford and John Wetton for Larks' Tongues in Aspic (1973), Starless and Bible Black (1974), and Red (1974) before disbanding the group in 1974.

King Crimson reformed in 1981 with Fripp, Bruford, Adrian Belew, and Tony Levin, producing the gamelan- and new-wave-influenced trilogy Discipline (1981), Beat (1982), and Three of a Perfect Pair (1984). A "double trio" line-up in the 1990s recorded THRAK (1995). The final incarnation, formed in 2013, was an eight-piece band (briefly seven) featuring three drummers and including Jakko Jakszyk, Mel Collins, Tony Levin, and Fripp; it toured extensively until 2021.

Musical style and legacy

King Crimson's music is noted for complex time signatures, interlocking guitar patterns (notably Fripp and Belew's work in the 1980s), Mellotron and later digital textures, and a balance of composition and improvisation. The band has influenced generations of progressive, metal, and alternative artists. In 2019 their catalogue was released to streaming services, greatly expanding their audience.
