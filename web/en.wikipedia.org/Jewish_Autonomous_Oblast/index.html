The Jewish Autonomous Oblast (JAO; Russian: Евре́йская автоно́мная о́бласть; Yiddish: ייִדישע אויטאָנאָמע געגנט) is a federal subject of Russia in the Russian Far East, bordering Khabarovsk Krai and Amur Oblast and sharing a border with China along the Amur River. It is the world's only Jewish autonomous region outside Israel, and Russia's only autonomous oblast.

History

The Soviet government designated the Birobidzhan district for Jewish settlement in 1928 as part of a project to give Soviet Jews a territorial home, an alternative to Zionism, and to settle and fortify the Soviet Far East. The oblast was formally established in 1934, and Yiddish was proclaimed an official language; Jewish schools, a theatre, and a Yiddish newspaper were founded. However, the project was undermined by the remoteness and harsh climate, the Great Purge, wartime and post-war antisemitic campaigns, and Stalin's suppression of Jewish culture; Jewish population share peaked around the late 1940s at perhaps a quarter to a third of residents and then steadily declined.

Present day

Since the mass emigration of Russian Jews to Israel in the 1990s, ethnic Jews form only a small minority (around 1–2 percent) of the oblast's roughly 150,000 inhabitants. Yiddish retains symbolic status: it appears on the regional coat of arms, is taught in some schools, and is still published in the newspaper Birobidzhaner Shtern.
