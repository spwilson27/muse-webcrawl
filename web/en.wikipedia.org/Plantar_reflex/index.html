Plantar reflex

The plantar reflex is a reflex elicited when the sole of the foot is stimulated with a blunt instrument. In healthy adults, the response is flexion: the toes curl down and inward and the foot everts slightly (a "down-going" or flexor response). An abnormal response in adults—extension of the big toe and fanning of the other toes, known as the Babinski sign—indicates damage to the corticospinal tract (an upper motor neuron lesion).

Examination

The examiner strokes the lateral sole of the foot from the heel toward the toes with a blunt object such as the end of a tendon hammer handle, using firm but not painful pressure. The first movement of the big toe is observed: downward (flexor) is normal in adults; upward (extensor) is abnormal.

Interpretation

The extensor plantar response is normal in infants up to about two years of age, because their corticospinal tracts are not yet fully myelinated. In older children and adults, a Babinski sign suggests a lesion anywhere along the corticospinal pathway, from the cerebral cortex to the spinal cord, and is a classic finding in conditions such as stroke, multiple sclerosis, and spinal cord compression. The sign takes its name from the French neurologist Joseph Babinski, who described it in 1896.

Related methods

Alternative techniques for eliciting the response, used when the standard method is equivocal or the patient is ticklish, include the Chaddock sign (stroking the lateral foot below the ankle) and the Oppenheim sign (stroking down the shin).
