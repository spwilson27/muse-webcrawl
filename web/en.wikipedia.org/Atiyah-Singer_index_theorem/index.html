Atiyah–Singer index theorem

Atiyah–Singer index theorem
Field: Differential geometry, topology
Proved by: Michael Atiyah and Isadore Singer, 1963
Recognition: Abel Prize 2004 (in part for this work)

The Atiyah–Singer index theorem is a celebrated result in differential geometry, proved by Michael Atiyah and Isadore Singer in 1963, which relates the analytical index of an elliptic differential operator on a compact manifold to a topological quantity computed from characteristic classes. In its statement, analysis (the difference between the dimensions of the kernel and cokernel of the operator) equals topology (an integral of Chern and Todd classes over the manifold).

Statement

For an elliptic differential operator D between vector bundles on a compact manifold X, the analytic index index(D) = dim ker D − dim coker D equals the topological index, defined via the Chern character of the symbol of D and the Todd class of the tangent bundle of X. Special cases recover classical theorems: setting D to the de Rham operator gives the Euler characteristic (Gauss–Bonnet), the signature operator gives the Hirzebruch signature theorem, and the Dolbeault operator gives the Riemann–Roch theorem.

History and proof

Atiyah and Singer announced the theorem in 1963 and published the proof in a series of papers in the Annals of Mathematics in 1968, using K-theory and cobordism. Later proofs used heat kernel methods: the index can be computed from the short-time asymptotics of the heat kernel, an approach developed in the 1970s and 1980s by Patodi, Gilkey, Bismut, and others, and influential in theoretical physics through the work of Edward Witten.

Applications

The theorem has deep applications in geometry, topology, and mathematical physics, including anomaly cancellation in string theory and gauge theory, and it led to generalizations such as the Atiyah–Patodi–Singer index theorem for manifolds with boundary.
