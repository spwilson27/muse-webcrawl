Port

A port is a maritime facility comprising one or more wharves or loading areas where ships load and discharge cargo and passengers. Ports are located on coasts, estuaries, rivers, lakes or canals, and are usually chosen for sheltered water and access to land transport such as roads and railways.

Types

Ports are classified by purpose: cargo ports (including specialised container, bulk and oil terminals), passenger and ferry ports, fishing ports, marinas and naval bases. "Inland ports" sit on navigable rivers or lakes — Duisburg in Germany is among the world's largest — while a dry port is an inland terminal directly connected to a seaport by road or rail.

Operations

Modern ports use specialised equipment: ship-to-shore gantry cranes for containers, straddle carriers, tugboats and pilotage services. Intermodal containerisation, introduced commercially in 1956, revolutionised port design and economics, concentrating trade at large deep-water hubs. The world's busiest container ports by throughput include Shanghai, Singapore and Ningbo-Zhoushan.

Governance

Ports are typically managed by a port authority, often under a "landlord" model in which the authority owns the land and private operators run terminals. Ports are major economic gateways but also sources of air pollution, dredging impacts and coastal development pressure.
