1948 Palestine war

Date30 November 1947 – 20 July 1949
LocationBritish Mandate of Palestine, Sinai Peninsula, southern Lebanon
ResultIsraeli victory; 1949 Armistice Agreements; creation of the Palestinian refugee problem
PhasesCivil war (Nov 1947 – May 1948); regular war (May 1948 – Mar 1949)

The 1948 Palestine war was fought in the territory of what had been, until 15 May 1948, the British Mandate for Palestine. It is generally divided into two phases: the 1947–1948 civil war in Mandatory Palestine, fought between Jewish and Palestinian Arab militias after the UN Partition Plan of November 1947, and the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, fought between Israel and the regular armies of neighbouring Arab states from 15 May 1948.

Civil war phase

Fighting broke out the day after the United Nations General Assembly adopted Resolution 181 recommending partition. Between December 1947 and March 1948 Arab irregulars, including the Arab Liberation Army, attempted to blockade Jewish Jerusalem and attack Jewish transport; from April 1948 the Haganah moved to the offensive under Plan Dalet, capturing Tiberias, Haifa, Jaffa, Safed and other mixed towns. Massacres and reprisals, such as the killings at Deir Yassin in April 1948, accelerated the flight of the Palestinian Arab population; by mid-May some 250,000–300,000 Palestinians had already become refugees.

Regular war phase

On 14 May 1948 the State of Israel was proclaimed, and the next day Egyptian, Transjordanian, Syrian, Lebanese and Iraqi forces entered Palestine. The ensuing war — interrupted by two UN truces — saw Israel expand beyond the partition borders, capturing the Galilee, the Negev and the corridor to Jerusalem. By the armistices of 1949 Israel controlled about 78 per cent of the Mandate; Jordan held the West Bank and Egypt the Gaza Strip.

Consequences

By the war's end more than 700,000 Palestinian Arabs — over half the Arab population of Palestine — had fled or been expelled and were barred from returning, an event commemorated as the Nakba. Israel absorbed hundreds of thousands of Jewish immigrants and refugees, including many from Arab countries. No Palestinian state was created, and the armistice lines (the Green Line) remained Israel's de facto borders until the Six-Day War of 1967.
