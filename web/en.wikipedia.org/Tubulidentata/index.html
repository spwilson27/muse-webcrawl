Tubulidentata is an order of mammals whose only living member is the aardvark (Orycteropus afer) of Africa. The name means "tube teeth", referring to the unique structure of the teeth: each tooth is made of many parallel hexagonal tubes of dentine surrounding a central pulp cavity, and the teeth lack enamel.

Characteristics

The aardvark is a medium-sized, nocturnal mammal with a pig-like snout, long ears and powerful digging limbs. Its unusual teeth grow continuously and are worn down by its diet of ants and termites. Unlike true anteaters and pangolins — to which it was once thought related — the aardvark represents an ancient, isolated lineage.

Evolution and classification

Genetic studies place Tubulidentata within the superorder Afrotheria, a group of African mammals that also includes elephants, hyraxes, elephant shrews and tenrecs. Fossil tubulidentates, such as species of Myorycteropus and Leptorycteropus, are known from the Miocene of Africa, and the group later spread to southern Europe and Asia before contracting to its present relict distribution.

Conservation

The aardvark is listed as Least Concern by the IUCN, though its numbers are difficult to assess because of its secretive, nocturnal habits. It plays an important ecological role: its abandoned burrows shelter many other animals, and its digging influences soil and termite populations.
