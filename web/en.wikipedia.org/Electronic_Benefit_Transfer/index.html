Electronic Benefit Transfer

Electronic Benefit Transfer (EBT) is an electronic system used in the United States that allows state welfare departments to issue benefits via a magnetically encoded payment card. Recipients use the card at authorised retailers to pay for food and other approved goods.

Programmes

EBT is used for the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP), which replaced paper food stamps, and for cash benefits such as Temporary Assistance for Needy Families (TANF). The Special Supplemental Food Program for Women, Infants, and Children (WIC) also uses EBT cards in most states.

How it works

Benefits are deposited monthly into an account linked to the card. At the checkout, the cardholder swipes the card and enters a personal identification number; the purchase amount is deducted from the balance. Separate balances are kept for food and cash benefits.

History

EBT pilots began in the 1980s, and by 2004 the system had been implemented nationwide for SNAP. The cards reduced stigma, fraud from paper coupon trafficking, and administrative costs.
