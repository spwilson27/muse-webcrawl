Restoration literature

Restoration literature is the body of English literature written during the Restoration period, conventionally dated from 1660 — when Charles II was restored to the throne and the theatres reopened — to about 1688/1700.

Characteristics

The period is marked by a reaction against Puritan austerity: wit, satire, sexual frankness and courtly elegance dominate much of its writing. Genres include Restoration comedy (the "comedy of manners" of Etherege, Wycherley and Congreve, including The Way of the World, 1700), heroic drama, satirical verse (Dryden's Absalom and Achitophel), the poetry and drama of John Dryden and the Earl of Rochester, and John Bunyan's allegory The Pilgrim's Progress (1678).

Notable authors

Major figures include John Dryden, William Congreve, William Wycherley, George Etherege, John Wilmot, Earl of Rochester, John Bunyan, Samuel Butler, and Aphra Behn, one of the first English women to earn a living by writing.
