Brighton Rock (film)

Poster for the 1948 film adaptation

Brighton Rock is the name of two British crime films adapted from Graham Greene's 1938 novel Brighton Rock: the acclaimed 1948 film directed by John Boulting, and a 2010 version directed by Rowan Joffé.

1948 film

Produced by the Boulting brothers, with a screenplay by Greene himself and Terence Rattigan, the 1948 film stars Richard Attenborough as Pinkie Brown, the teenage razor-gang leader of a Brighton racecourse racket; Carol Reed-era stalwarts Carol Marsh as Rose, Hermione Baddeley as Ida Arnold, and William Hartnell as Dallow complete the cast. Shot partly on location in Brighton, it is regarded as a classic of British film noir. Its ending softens the novel's bleak conclusion: the gramophone record Pinkie made for Rose is scratched, so she hears only the fragment "I love you" rather than his message of hatred.

2010 film

Rowan Joffé's adaptation relocates the action to 1964, amid Mods and Rockers clashes, and stars Sam Riley as Pinkie, Andrea Riseborough as Rose, and Helen Mirren as Ida. It premiered at the Toronto International Film Festival in September 2010 and received mixed reviews, with praise for Riseborough's performance.

Themes

Both films retain Greene's preoccupation with Catholic guilt, damnation, and the contrast between Pinkie's and Rose's absolute moral world and Ida's cheerful "right and wrong" humanism.
