Mobile virtual network operator

A mobile virtual network operator (MVNO) is a wireless communications services provider that does not own the wireless network infrastructure over which it provides services to its customers. An MVNO enters into a business agreement with a mobile network operator (MNO) to obtain bulk access to network services at wholesale rates, then sets retail prices independently for its own customers.

History

The MVNO model emerged in Europe in the late 1990s as regulators sought to increase competition. Virgin Mobile, launched in the United Kingdom in 1999 as a joint venture with One2One (later T-Mobile), is widely regarded as the first commercially successful MVNO. The model spread to North America, Asia, and other regions, and MVNOs have targeted niches such as budget customers, ethnic communities calling internationally, business users, and machine-to-machine (M2M/IoT) services.

Types

MVNOs differ in how much infrastructure they operate themselves:

  • Branded resellers / light MVNOs rely on the host operator for nearly all systems, contributing mainly brand, marketing, and distribution.
  • Full MVNOs operate their own core network elements (such as HLR/HSS, billing, and packet core), renting only radio access from the host.

Intermediaries called mobile virtual network enablers (MVNEs) and aggregators (MVNAs) provide platforms and wholesale access that let smaller brands launch as MVNOs.

Regulation and market

Regulators in some countries mandate wholesale access or otherwise encourage MVNO entry to promote competition; in others, MVNOs exist purely by commercial negotiation. There are well over a thousand MVNOs worldwide, serving hundreds of millions of subscriptions, though their market share varies dramatically by country.
