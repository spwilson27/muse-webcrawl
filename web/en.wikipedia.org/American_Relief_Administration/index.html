American Relief Administration

Founded1919
Dissolved1924
DirectorHerbert Hoover
PurposeFood relief in Europe after World War I

The American Relief Administration (ARA) was an American relief mission to Europe and later Soviet Russia after the First World War. Directed by Herbert Hoover, who had led food relief efforts during the war, the ARA was established by Congress in 1919 to provide food supplies and other aid to war-devastated countries.

Operations

The ARA operated in more than twenty countries, feeding millions of people — at its peak, over ten million daily — with a particular focus on children through its European Children's Fund. Following an appeal by Maxim Gorky and negotiations with the Soviet government under the 1921 Riga agreement, the ARA mounted a major relief operation during the Russian famine of 1921–22, feeding some eleven million people in the Volga region and helping to curb epidemics of typhus.

Legacy

The ARA wound down its Russian operations in 1923 and dissolved by 1924. The mission cemented Hoover's reputation as a humanitarian administrator and served as a model for later American foreign-aid programs.
