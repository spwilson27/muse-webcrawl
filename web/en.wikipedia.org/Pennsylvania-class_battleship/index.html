The Pennsylvania class was a class of two super-dreadnought battleships built for the United States Navy just before the First World War: USS Pennsylvania (BB-38) and USS Arizona (BB-39). They were enlarged versions of the preceding Nevada class, carrying twelve 14-inch guns in four triple turrets.

Design

The class introduced the "all or nothing" armour scheme in refined form, with armour concentrated over the magazines, machinery, and turrets. A single funnel and tripod masts distinguished their profile as built; both ships were extensively modernised in the early 1930s with new tripod masts carrying gun-control tops, additional anti-aircraft guns, and torpedo bulges.

Service

Both ships spent the interwar years with the Battle Fleet in the Pacific. On 7 December 1941 Arizona was destroyed in the attack on Pearl Harbor when a bomb detonated her forward magazines, killing 1,177 officers and men; her wreck remains as a memorial. Pennsylvania, in drydock during the attack, survived and served throughout the Pacific War, providing shore bombardment at numerous landings and fighting at Surigao Strait. She was expended as a target in the Operation Crossroads atomic tests in 1946.
