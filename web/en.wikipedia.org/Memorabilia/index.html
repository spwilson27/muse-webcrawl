Memorabilia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Memorabilia (from the Latin memorabilis, "memorable") are objects kept or collected because of their historical interest, especially those associated with memorable people or events. The term covers a wide range of collectible items, including autographs, sporting equipment used in famous matches, film props, concert posters, and political ephemera.

Collecting

Collecting memorabilia is a large hobby and business. Value depends on rarity, condition, provenance, and the fame of the associated person or event; items are traded through specialist dealers, auctions, and online marketplaces. Authentication is a central concern because forgery, particularly of autographs, is widespread, and third-party authentication services are commonly used.

Categories

  • Sports memorabilia — signed balls, jerseys, trading cards, and equipment.
  • Music memorabilia — instruments, stage clothing, and posters from notable performers.
  • Film and entertainment memorabilia — props, costumes, and production materials.
  • Historical and political memorabilia — campaign items, documents, and artifacts of public events.

Museums

Institutions such as sports halls of fame and entertainment museums preserve memorabilia for public display, treating items as material evidence of cultural history.
