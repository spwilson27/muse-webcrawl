Chess

Chess is a two-player strategy board game played on a checkered board with 64 squares arranged in an 8×8 grid. Each player controls sixteen pieces — one king, one queen, two rooks, two bishops, two knights and eight pawns — and aims to checkmate the opponent's king, placing it under attack with no legal escape. Chess is one of the world's most popular games, played by millions of people worldwide.

Rules

White moves first. Each piece type moves in a distinct pattern: the king one square in any direction, the queen any distance along ranks, files and diagonals, the rook along ranks and files, the bishop along diagonals, the knight in an L-shape, and pawns forward with special moves including the initial two-square advance, captures en passant, and promotion on the last rank. Special rules include castling, and the game ends in checkmate, resignation, or a draw by stalemate, agreement, the fifty-move rule or threefold repetition.

History

Chess evolved from the Indian game chaturanga in the 6th century, spreading through Persia as shatranj and into Europe, where the modern moves of the queen and bishop emerged in the late 15th century. The first World Championship was held in 1886. The international federation FIDE, founded in 1924, governs competition today.

Competition and computers

Elite chess is organised around the World Chess Championship and the Candidates Tournament. Players are rated by the Elo system. Computers have surpassed human play since IBM's Deep Blue defeated Garry Kasparov in 1997, and modern chess engines such as Stockfish and AlphaZero far exceed the strength of the best humans.
