Horse show

A hunter class underway at a horse show

A horse show is a judged exhibition of horses and ponies. Competing events range from small local shows to large international competitions, and classes may evaluate the rider's equitation, the horse's conformation and manners, or performance in disciplines such as show jumping, dressage, hunter classes, western pleasure and driving.

Organization

Shows are usually organized by breed associations, discipline federations (such as the United States Equestrian Federation) or the international governing body, the FEI. Entries are divided into classes by discipline, level, rider age or experience, and horse type. Judges place competitors against a standard of ideal performance or score predetermined movements, as in dressage tests.

Common classes

English-style shows include hunter and jumper classes over fences, equitation, and flat classes such as hunter under saddle. Western shows feature pleasure, trail, reining and horsemanship. In-hand ("halter") classes judge conformation alone. Gymkhana and timed events such as barrel racing emphasize speed.

History

Formal horse shows grew out of agricultural fairs in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, where breeding stock was exhibited and judged. The Dublin Horse Show dates to 1864 and the National Horse Show in the United States to 1883.
