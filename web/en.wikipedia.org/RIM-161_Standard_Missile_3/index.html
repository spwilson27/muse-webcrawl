RIM-161 Standard Missile 3

An SM-3 launch from a U.S. Navy cruiser

The RIM-161 Standard Missile 3 (SM-3) is a ship-based surface-to-air missile system developed by Raytheon for the United States Navy as part of the Aegis Ballistic Missile Defense System. Unlike conventional air-defence missiles, the SM-3 intercepts ballistic missiles outside the atmosphere during their midcourse phase, destroying them by direct collision with a kinetic warhead.

Design

The SM-3 is launched from the Mark 41 Vertical Launching System. Its first three stages are adapted from the SM-2 Block IV, while the fourth — the Lightweight Exo-Atmospheric Projectile (LEAP) kinetic warhead — separates in space and homes on the target using an infrared seeker, manoeuvring with a divert and attitude control system. Later Block IIA missiles, developed jointly with Japan, have larger rocket motors and a more capable warhead.

Operational history

The system has conducted numerous successful intercept flight tests against ballistic missile targets. In February 2008, an SM-3 from USS Lake Erie was used to destroy the malfunctioning USA-193 spy satellite at an altitude of about 247 km, an operation announced as a safety measure to prevent its hydrazine tank surviving re-entry. SM-3s are deployed on U.S. and Japanese Aegis destroyers and at Aegis Ashore sites in Romania and Poland.
