Fruit butter

Fruit butter
TypeFruit spread / preserve
Main ingredientsFruit pulp, sugar, spices
ExamplesApple butter, pumpkin butter, plum butter

A fruit butter is a sweet spread made by cooking fruit pulp with sugar (and often spices) for a long time at low heat until it becomes thick, smooth, and dark, then puréeing it to a buttery consistency. Despite the name, fruit butters contain no dairy butter; the term refers to their spreadable texture.

Preparation

Fruit — traditionally apples, but also pears, plums, peaches, or pumpkins — is cooked down slowly, historically in large copper kettles over an open fire and stirred for hours, in a process once associated with communal autumn work parties. The long cooking concentrates sugars and flavours and gives apple butter its characteristic deep brown colour. Cinnamon, cloves, and other spices are typical flavourings.

Varieties and history

Apple butter originated in medieval monastic orchards in what is now Belgium and Germany and was carried to North America by German settlers, becoming a staple of Pennsylvania Dutch and Appalachian foodways. European plum butters include German Pflaumenmus and Polish powidła. Fruit butters are served on bread, used as a condiment with meats, or as an ingredient in baking.
