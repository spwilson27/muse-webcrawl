Isère

Country: France

Region: Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes

Prefecture: Grenoble

Department number: 38

Area: 7,431 km2

Population: ~1.27 million (2019)

Isère (French pronunciation: [izɛʁ]; Arpitan: Isera; Occitan: Isèra) is a landlocked department in the southeastern region of Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes, France. Named after the river Isère, it had a population of about 1,271,000 in 2019. Its prefecture is Grenoble, and its other important towns include Bourgoin-Jallieu, Vienne and Voiron.

Geography

Isère includes part of the French Alps, with major mountain ranges including the northern Vercors, Chartreuse, Taillefer and the Écrins massif, which contains the Barre des Écrins, at 4,102 m the department's highest point. The river Isère rises near the Col de l'Iseran and flows through Grenoble before joining the Rhône. The department is home to famous ski resorts including Alpe d'Huez and Les Deux Alpes.

History

The department was created during the French Revolution on 4 March 1790 from part of the province of Dauphiné, the territory of the Dauphins of Viennois whose emblem, the dolphin, appears in the departmental arms. Grenoble hosted the 1968 Winter Olympics, which spurred modernisation of the city and its mountain infrastructure. Isère's economy combines agriculture (notably walnuts, a protected AOC product), hydroelectric power, and a major high-technology sector around Grenoble's research centres.
