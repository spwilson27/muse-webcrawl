The Yellowhead Highway is a major interprovincial highway in western Canada, forming the northern route of the Trans-Canada Highway system. It runs from the Pacific coast at Prince Rupert, British Columbia, through Edmonton and Saskatoon, to Winnipeg, Manitoba.

Name and history

The highway takes its name from the Yellowhead Pass through the Rocky Mountains near Jasper, itself named for Pierre Bostonais, a blond-haired ("tête jaune") Iroquois-Métis trapper and guide. The corridor follows historic fur-trade and early railway routes; the highway association was formed in 1947 and the route was progressively paved and officially opened in the 1970s.

Route

Designated Highway 16 in British Columbia, Alberta, Saskatchewan, and Manitoba, the Yellowhead passes Jasper National Park, where it meets the Icefields Parkway, and serves northern resource and agricultural communities across the prairies.
