Curonian Spit National Park (Lithuania)

Curonian Spit National Park (Lithuanian: Kuršių nerijos nacionalinis parkas) is one of five national parks in Lithuania. It was established in 1991 to protect the unique ecosystems of the Lithuanian half of the Curonian Spit, a 98-kilometre narrow sand peninsula separating the Curonian Lagoon from the Baltic Sea, together with the surrounding lagoon and sea waters. The southern half of the spit, in Russia's Kaliningrad Oblast, is protected by a separate Russian national park.

Landscape

The park is famous for its shifting sand dunes — among the highest in Europe — including the Parnidis Dune near Nida, which rises about 52 metres above sea level. Centuries of deforestation allowed drifting sand to bury entire fishing villages, and in the 19th century large-scale planting of mountain pines, led by figures such as forester George David Kuwert, stabilized the dunes. Today roughly 70 percent of the park's land area is forested, mainly with Scots and mountain pine.

Wildlife and settlements

The spit lies on a major bird migration route, and its forests and wetlands support moose, wild boar, red deer, and beavers. The park's settlements — Nida, Juodkrantė, Preila, Pervalka, and Smiltynė — are administered within the municipality of Neringa and retain traditional fishermen's houses and weathervane traditions. Juodkrantė is known for the Hill of Witches, an outdoor sculpture trail, and for a large grey heron and cormorant colony.

Protection status

The Curonian Spit was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2000 as a transboundary cultural landscape shared by Lithuania and Russia. The park is also part of the Natura 2000 network and is listed as a protected area of international importance.
