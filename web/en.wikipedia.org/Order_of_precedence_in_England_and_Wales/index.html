Order of precedence in England and Wales

The order of precedence in England and Wales is the hierarchy of officials and dignitaries used at ceremonial and state occasions to determine seating, processions, and the order in which persons are announced. It is distinct from the orders of precedence of Scotland and Northern Ireland, reflecting their separate legal and heraldic traditions.


A state procession at Westminster

Outline

The sovereign heads the order. Below the monarch come:

  • The sovereign's spouse or widow/widower, and other members of the royal family by their set order.
  • The Archbishop of Canterbury, the most senior churchman.
  • The Lord High Chancellor (Lord Chancellor).
  • The Archbishop of York.
  • The Prime Minister.
  • The Lord President of the Council, the Speaker of the House of Commons, the Lord Speaker, and the President of the Supreme Court.
  • Ambassadors and high commissioners, then peers by rank (dukes, marquesses, earls, viscounts, barons), bishops, and senior officeholders.

Basis

Precedence derives from a mixture of statute (beginning with the House of Lords Precedence Act 1539), royal warrants, and custom administered by the Earl Marshal and the College of Arms. Separate tables exist for gentlemen and ladies, and local precedence — for example at county occasions, where the Lord-Lieutenant represents the sovereign — follows its own rules.
