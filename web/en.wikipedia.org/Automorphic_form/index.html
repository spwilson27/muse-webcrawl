Automorphic form

In harmonic analysis and number theory, an automorphic form is a well-behaved function from a topological group to the complex numbers (or a vector space) that is invariant under the action of a discrete subgroup, subject to growth and smoothness conditions. Automorphic forms generalize modular forms, which correspond to the group SL(2, R).

Definition and examples

Classically, a modular form is a holomorphic function on the upper half-plane transforming in a prescribed way under a congruence subgroup of the modular group. The general theory replaces the upper half-plane with a symmetric space for a Lie group G and the modular group with a discrete subgroup, often an arithmetic subgroup. Poincaré pioneered the study of such functions in the 1880s.

Automorphic representations

Modern treatments use representation theory: an automorphic representation is an irreducible representation of the adele group occurring in the space of square-integrable automorphic functions. This framework underlies the Langlands program, which predicts deep connections between automorphic forms, Galois representations, and L-functions, and was central to the proof of Fermat's Last Theorem via the modularity of elliptic curves.

Applications

Automorphic forms are used throughout number theory, including the theory of L-functions, the trace formula, and arithmetic geometry.
