Integrated care

Integrated care, also known as integrated health, coordinated care, comprehensive care, seamless care, or transmural care, is a worldwide trend in healthcare reforms and new organizational arrangements focusing on more coordinated and integrated forms of care provision. It brings together the different components of care — primary, secondary, community, mental health, and social care — around the needs of the patient rather than the structures of providers.

Rationale

Fragmented services can duplicate assessments, delay treatment, and fail people with multiple long-term conditions. Integrated care seeks shared records, multidisciplinary teams, care planning, and aligned funding and governance so that patients experience a continuous service. The World Health Organization has promoted integrated, people-centred health services as a global strategy.

Examples

In England, the Health and Care Act 2022 established Integrated Care Systems across the country. Other frequently cited models include Kaiser Permanente in the United States, the Basque Country's integrated health organizations in Spain, and Canterbury in New Zealand.
