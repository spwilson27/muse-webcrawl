Donn Alan Pennebaker (15 July 1925 – 1 August 2019) was an American documentary filmmaker and one of the pioneers of direct cinema (cinéma vérité), known for an observational, fly-on-the-wall style made possible by lightweight synchronised-sound cameras that he helped develop. With his wife and collaborator Chris Hegedus he founded Pennebaker Hegedus Films.

Career

Pennebaker, who had studied engineering at Yale and worked in electronics, joined Drew Associates, the Time-Life documentary unit led by Robert Drew. There he worked on Primary (1960), following John F. Kennedy and Hubert Humphrey in the Wisconsin primary, a landmark of observational documentary. His own company produced Dont Look Back (1967), an intimate portrait of Bob Dylan's 1965 England tour, and Monterey Pop (1968), the first major rock festival film, shot at the 1967 Monterey International Pop Festival. He directed the David Bowie concert film Ziggy Stardust and the Spiders from Mars (1973), Original Cast Album: Company (1970), and, with Hegedus, The War Room (1993), a behind-the-scenes account of Bill Clinton's 1992 presidential campaign that was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Documentary Feature.

Honours

In 2012 Pennebaker received an Academy Honorary Award (Governors Award) for lifetime achievement, one of the few documentary filmmakers so honoured. His films are credited with shaping the modern music documentary and the political campaign documentary alike.
