Georges Braque (13 May 1882 – 31 August 1963) was a major French painter, collagist, draughtsman and sculptor. Together with Pablo Picasso, he founded Cubism, one of the most influential art movements of the twentieth century.

Early work

Braque trained as a house painter and decorator in Le Havre before studying art in Paris. He first painted in the Fauvist style, exhibiting with Matisse and Derain, but after seeing Picasso's Les Demoiselles d'Avignon in 1907 he began the experiments that led to Cubism. His 1908 landscapes at L'Estaque prompted a critic to describe forms reduced to "cubes", giving the movement its name.

Cubism and collage

From 1909 to 1914 Braque and Picasso worked in close partnership, developing Analytic Cubism's fragmented, monochromatic images. In 1912 Braque invented papier collé, pasting paper into his drawings, a foundational step for collage. His service in the First World War ended the collaboration; he was seriously wounded in 1915.

Later life

After the war Braque developed a more lyrical, independent style, painting still lifes, interiors and the celebrated Ateliers series. He died in Paris in 1963 and received a state funeral.
