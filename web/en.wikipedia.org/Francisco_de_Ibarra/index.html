Francisco de Ibarra (c. 1539–1575) was a Spanish explorer and conquistador who founded the city of Durango and became the first governor of the province of Nueva Vizcaya in northern New Spain, in present-day northwestern Mexico.

Explorations

Born in the Basque town of Eibar, Ibarra came to Mexico as a youth under the patronage of his uncle Diego de Ibarra, a wealthy Zacatecas miner. In the 1550s and 1560s he led expeditions north from Zacatecas into the Sierra Madre Occidental in search of silver and rumoured rich cities, founding mining settlements including San Juan del Río and Indé.

Governor of Nueva Vizcaya

In 1562 Ibarra was appointed governor and captain general of the newly organised province of Nueva Vizcaya. In 1563 he founded the Villa de Durango, which became the provincial capital. His later expeditions pushed into present-day Chihuahua and Sinaloa and toward the Gulf of California, though the mineral wealth he sought proved elusive and resistance by Indigenous peoples such as the Tepehuán made the frontier precarious. He died in 1575, still in his thirties.
