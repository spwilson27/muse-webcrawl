2015 Nepal earthquake

The 2015 Nepal earthquake, also known as the Gorkha earthquake, struck Nepal on 25 April 2015 at 11:56 local time with a magnitude of 7.8. Its epicentre was east of the Gorkha District at Barpak, about 77 km northwest of Kathmandu. It was the worst natural disaster to strike Nepal since the 1934 Bihar–Nepal earthquake.

Geology

The earthquake occurred on the Main Himalayan Thrust, where the Indian Plate is subducting beneath the Eurasian Plate. The rupture propagated eastward toward Kathmandu, amplifying shaking in the Kathmandu Valley.

Damage and casualties

The earthquake killed nearly 9,000 people and injured more than 22,000. Hundreds of thousands of homes were destroyed, and historic buildings in the Kathmandu Valley, including UNESCO World Heritage Sites in Durbar Square, Bhaktapur and Patan, were severely damaged. The quake also triggered an avalanche on Mount Everest that killed 21 people.

Aftershocks

A major aftershock of magnitude 7.3 struck on 12 May 2015 near Dolakha, killing more than 150 people and causing further destruction.

International response

India launched Operation Maitri, its largest relief effort, and countries including China, the United States, the United Kingdom and Pakistan sent rescue teams and aid. Reconstruction proceeded under the National Reconstruction Authority established by Nepal.
