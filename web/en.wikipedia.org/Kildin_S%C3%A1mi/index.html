Kildin Sámi (Kildin Sámi: кӣллт са̄мь кӣлл, kīllt sām' kīll) is a Sámi language spoken on the Kola Peninsula in northwestern Russia, in and around the Lovozero district of Murmansk Oblast. It is the largest of the Eastern Sámi languages but is severely endangered, with only a few hundred speakers, most of them elderly.

Writing and revitalisation

Kildin Sámi has been written in both Latin and Cyrillic scripts; a Cyrillic orthography developed in the Soviet era is the basis of the modern standard, refined in dictionaries and school materials produced from the 1980s onward. Teaching in schools, a local radio presence, and community language work aim to maintain the language, though intergenerational transmission has largely been interrupted by decades of Soviet-era Russification and the forced relocation of Sámi communities.
