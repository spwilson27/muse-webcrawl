Ligue 2 is the second division of French professional football, part of the Ligue de Football Professionnel (LFP) together with the top flight, Ligue 1. Founded in 1933 as Division 2, it sits directly above the third-tier Championnat National. The league adopted its current name in 2002, and since the 2024–25 season has comprised 18 clubs, reduced from 20.

Format

Each club plays the others twice, home and away, over a season running from August to May. The top two clubs are promoted automatically to Ligue 1, while a play-off system involving the clubs finishing third to fifth — and a promotion/relegation play-off against a Ligue 1 side — decides a possible third promotion. The bottom clubs are relegated to the Championnat National, subject to financial review by the DNCG, French football's financial watchdog, which can impose administrative relegation.

History

The second division was created a year after professional football was legalised in France, initially experimenting with north and south groups. Historically, many famous clubs have spent time in the division — including AS Monaco (whose participation reflects Monaco's place in the French league system), Olympique de Marseille, and Paris Saint-Germain in their early years. Le Havre is the most successful club in the competition's history with six titles.

Notable features

Ligue 2 is regarded as a strong development league, known for producing players who move to Europe's top divisions; its Friday-evening fixture slot and multicultural squads are distinctive features. Title sponsorship has attached commercial names to the competition at various times, most recently a fast-food delivery sponsor from 2024.
