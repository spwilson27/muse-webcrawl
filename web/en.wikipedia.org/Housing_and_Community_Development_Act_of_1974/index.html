Housing and Community Development Act of 1974

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Housing and Community Development Act of 1974 (Pub. L. 93-383) is a United States federal law that reshaped federal housing and urban policy. It created the Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) programme and the Section 8 housing assistance programme, and amended the Housing Act of 1937.

Enacted: 22 August 1974
Signed by: President Gerald Ford
Created: Community Development Block Grants; Section 8

Section 8

Section 8 of the amended 1937 Act authorised rental assistance for low-income households in privately owned housing: tenants pay roughly 30 per cent of their income in rent and the federal government pays the remainder directly to the landlord. The programme marked a shift away from building large public housing projects toward subsidising households in the existing housing stock; it later evolved into the Housing Choice Voucher programme.

Community Development Block Grants

Title I consolidated several categorical urban programmes (including urban renewal and Model Cities) into a single block grant distributed to cities and urban counties by formula, giving local governments broad discretion to fund housing rehabilitation, public facilities and economic development benefiting low- and moderate-income residents.

Other provisions

The Act also amended federal mortgage insurance programmes, strengthened fair housing enforcement, and required localities receiving funds to prepare housing assistance plans assessing the needs of lower-income residents.
