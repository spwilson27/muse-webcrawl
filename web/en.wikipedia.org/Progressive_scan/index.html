Progressive scan

Progressive scanning (alternatively referred to as noninterlaced scanning) is a way of displaying, storing, or transmitting moving images in which all the lines of each frame are drawn in sequence. This is in contrast to interlaced video used in traditional analog television systems, where only the odd lines, then the even lines of each frame are drawn alternately.

Usage

Progressive scan is used in computer monitors, digital television standards such as 480p, 720p and 1080p, DVD and Blu-ray video, and most modern displays. It avoids the flicker and combing artefacts of interlaced video and scales more cleanly to other resolutions, at the cost of requiring roughly twice the bandwidth of interlaced video at the same frame rate.

History

The system was used starting in the early 1930s in Baird's experimental transmissions and was standard in computer displays from the 1970s onward. With the transition to digital broadcasting and flat-panel displays, progressive scan became the dominant format.
