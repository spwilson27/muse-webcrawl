Norton Simon Museum

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Norton Simon Museum is an art museum located in Pasadena, California, United States. It was previously known as the Pasadena Art Institute and the Pasadena Art Museum, and it holds the extensive collections assembled by industrialist Norton Simon (1907–1993), widely regarded as one of the most remarkable private art collections ever formed by a single individual.

History

The museum occupies a distinctive modernist building designed by architects Ladd & Kelsey, completed in 1969, notable for its curved exterior covered in custom-glazed ceramic tiles. In 1974, financially troubled, the Pasadena Art Museum agreed to assume Norton Simon's collection and debts in exchange for his assuming control of the institution; the museum was renamed for him in 1975.

Norton Simon, who had made his fortune in food processing and consumer products, began collecting art seriously in the 1950s and, advised by dealers such as Duveen Brothers, amassed Old Master paintings, Impressionist and Post-Impressionist works, and one of the finest collections of South and Southeast Asian sculpture outside Asia. In the 1990s the building was renovated under architect Frank Gehry, and the gardens were redesigned by Nancy Goslee Power.

Collections

The museum's European holdings span the 14th to the 20th century and include works by Raphael, Rembrandt, Goya, Manet, Degas (including a large group of his bronzes), Van Gogh, Cézanne, and Picasso. Its collection of works by Degas is among the most comprehensive in existence.

The Asian art collection encompasses Indian, Himalayan, Chinese, and Southeast Asian sculpture and painting over some two thousand years. The museum's sculpture garden, inspired by Monet's garden at Giverny, displays 20th-century sculpture around a pond.
