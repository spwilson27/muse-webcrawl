Numic languages

FamilyUto-Aztecan
RegionGreat Basin and Colorado Plateau, western United States
BranchesWestern, Central, Southern
LanguagesShoshoni, Comanche, Paiute, Ute, Mono, Kawaiisu, Chemehuevi

The Numic languages are a branch of the Uto-Aztecan language family spoken by Indigenous peoples of the Great Basin, the Colorado Plateau, and adjacent areas of the western United States. The name comes from the common Numic word for "person" (cognate forms such as nɨmɨ). Numic languages are notable for having spread rapidly and recently — within roughly the last thousand years — across a huge territory from a comparatively small homeland.

Branches

Western Numic comprises Mono (Monache) and Northern Paiute, spoken in eastern California, Nevada, and southeastern Oregon. Central Numic comprises Shoshoni, Comanche (which split from Shoshoni around the 1700s as its speakers moved onto the southern Plains), Panamint (Timbisha), and the extinct Gosiute variety. Southern Numic comprises Ute, Southern Paiute (including Chemehuevi), and Kawaiisu, spoken in Utah, Colorado, Arizona, and southern California and Nevada.

The Numic expansion

Linguistic and archaeological evidence suggests the Numic homeland lay near the southwestern corner of the Great Basin, around southeastern California. From there, Numic-speaking groups spread northward and eastward across the Basin — a movement sometimes called the "Numic spread" — in a relatively short period, probably aided by a highly mobile hunting-and-gathering adaptation centred on seeds and small game. All Numic languages are now endangered, though revitalisation programmes exist in several tribal communities.
