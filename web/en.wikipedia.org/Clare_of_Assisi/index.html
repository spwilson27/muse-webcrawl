Clare of Assisi (born Chiara Offreduccio, sometimes spelled Clair, Claire, or Clara; 16 July 1194 – 11 August 1253) was an Italian saint and one of the first followers of Francis of Assisi. She founded the Order of Saint Clare, commonly referred to today as the Poor Clares, and after her death the order was renamed in her honour. She is venerated as a saint in the Roman Catholic Church and is the patron saint of television, among other causes.

Early life

Clare was born in Assisi, in Umbria, the eldest daughter of Favorino Sciffi, Count of Sasso-Rosso, and his wife Ortolana. Ortolana belonged to a noble family and was a devout woman who undertook pilgrimages to Rome, Santiago de Compostela and the Holy Land. As a child Clare was noted for her piety and devotion to prayer. Although there is no record of her formal education, she likely learned Latin as well as needlework and the management of a household.

Conversion and flight

As a teenager Clare heard Francis of Assisi preaching in the church of San Giorgio and in the cathedral of San Rufino, and she became deeply drawn to his way of life. On Palm Sunday, 20 March 1212, she left her father's house secretly, accompanied by her aunt Bianca and another companion, and went to the chapel of the Porziuncola, where Francis received her. There she exchanged her rich clothing for a plain tunic, and Francis cut her hair, marking her consecration to Christ. She was initially placed with Benedictine nuns at San Paolo and then at Sant'Angelo di Panzo, resisting her family's attempts — at times violent — to bring her home. Her younger sister Caterina (later called Agnes) joined her sixteen days later.

The Poor Clares

Clare and her companions eventually settled at the church of San Damiano, which Francis had restored. There she became abbess and led the community for more than forty years. The sisters lived a life of radical poverty, manual work and contemplative prayer, depending on alms and refusing to own property or fixed revenues. Clare resisted repeated pressure from popes and churchmen to accept a mitigated rule that would permit the order to hold possessions. In 1247 Pope Innocent IV issued a rule allowing property, but Clare composed her own Rule of Life, modelled on the Franciscan rule, insisting on the "privilege of poverty." Innocent IV approved her rule on 9 August 1253, two days before her death — making her the first woman in Church history to write a rule of religious life that received papal approval.

Later life and death

Clare's health was fragile; she was frequently ill during the last twenty-seven years of her life, yet she continued to govern her monastery and to influence the Franciscan movement through letters, including four surviving letters to Agnes of Prague. She is credited with defending Assisi in 1240 when Saracen mercenaries in the service of Emperor Frederick II attacked San Damiano: according to tradition she carried the Blessed Sacrament in a monstrance to the convent wall, and the attackers fled — the origin of her common iconography. Clare died on 11 August 1253 and was canonized by Pope Alexander IV only two years later, in 1255.

Legacy

The Basilica of Saint Clare in Assisi, built shortly after her canonization, houses her remains and the crucifix that, according to tradition, spoke to Francis. In 1958 Pope Pius XII named her patron saint of television, because as an ill woman confined to her bed she was said to have seen and heard Christmas Mass projected on the wall of her room. Her feast day is 11 August.
