Linguistic rights

Linguistic rights (or linguistic human rights) are the human and civil rights concerning the choice of language or languages for communication in private and public life. They include the right to use one's mother tongue, to receive education in it, to access public services and courts in a language one understands, and to maintain and develop one's linguistic identity.

International instruments

Article 27 of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights guarantees persons belonging to linguistic minorities the right to use their own language. Other instruments include the UN Declaration on the Rights of Persons Belonging to National or Ethnic, Religious and Linguistic Minorities (1992), the European Charter for Regional or Minority Languages (1992), and UNESCO's Universal Declaration of Linguistic Rights (Barcelona, 1996), a civil-society document.

Debates

Scholars distinguish "tolerance-oriented" rights (freedom from interference in private language use) from "promotion-oriented" rights (positive state support, such as mother-tongue schooling). Linguistic rights intersect with debates over language endangerment, nation-building, official-language policy, and the rights of indigenous peoples; critics note tensions between promoting minority languages and ensuring a shared public language.
