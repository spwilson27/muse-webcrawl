Andrew Viterbi

Andrew James Viterbi (born Andrea Giacomo Viterbo, March 9, 1935) is an Italian-American electrical engineer and businessman. He is best known for inventing the Viterbi algorithm, used for decoding convolutionally encoded data in communications systems, and for co-founding Linkabit Corporation and Qualcomm Inc., where the CDMA digital cellular technology he helped develop became a world standard. He is also a major philanthropist in engineering education.

Early life and education

Viterbi was born in Bergamo, Italy, to Jewish parents who fled Italian fascist racial laws, emigrating to the United States in 1939. Raised in Boston, he studied electrical engineering at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, receiving bachelor's and master's degrees in 1957, and earned his PhD in electrical engineering from the University of California, Los Angeles, in 1962.

Career

After graduate study he worked at the Jet Propulsion Laboratory on digital communications for space missions. At UCLA, where he became a professor, he published his landmark 1967 paper describing an error-correction decoding method — the Viterbi algorithm — now ubiquitous in mobile phones, deep-space communications, speech recognition (via hidden Markov models) and DNA sequence analysis. In 1968 he co-founded Linkabit with Irwin Jacobs and Leonard Kleinrock; in 1985 Viterbi and Jacobs co-founded Qualcomm, which commercialised Code Division Multiple Access (CDMA) technology for mobile telephony.

Philanthropy

In 2004 Viterbi and his wife Erna donated $52 million to the University of Southern California, whose engineering school was renamed the USC Viterbi School of Engineering. He has also given substantially to MIT, UCLA, Technion and other institutions, and serves on numerous boards. His memoir, Reflections of an Educator, Researcher and Entrepreneur, appeared in 2023.

Awards

Viterbi received the National Medal of Science in 2007, the IEEE Medal of Honor in 2010, and was elected to the National Academy of Engineering and the National Academy of Sciences. He is a member of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences.
