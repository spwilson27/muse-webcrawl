Sixth form

In the education systems of England, Northern Ireland, and Wales, and in Commonwealth countries such as Jamaica and Trinidad and Tobago, the sixth form represents the final two years of secondary education (Years 12 and 13), during which students typically study for A-level examinations.

The term derives from the traditional numbering of forms in English public schools, where the sixth form was the senior-most form. Students aged 16 to 18, called sixth formers, usually take three or four subjects in depth.

Sixth forms may be part of a secondary school or separate sixth form colleges. Attendance became part of compulsory education or training in England until age 18, though students may also pursue apprenticeships or vocational qualifications.
