Keel

The keel is the bottom-most longitudinal structural element of a water vessel (ship or boat). On some sailboats there may also be a hydrodynamic keel, a fin to which a weight is often attached. The keel is typically the first part of a ship's hull to be built, and the phrase "laying the keel" marks the formal beginning of construction.

Structural keel

In wooden and steel shipbuilding the structural keel runs along the centreline of the hull from bow to stern, to which the frames and planking are attached. It contributes to longitudinal strength and protects the hull if the vessel runs aground.

Hydrodynamic keels

On sailing yachts, a fixed fin keel (often ballasted with lead or iron) provides lateral resistance to prevent leeway and acts as a counterweight to the wind's heeling force. Variants include bilge keels, wing keels, bulb keels, canting keels and retractable daggerboards and centreboards.
