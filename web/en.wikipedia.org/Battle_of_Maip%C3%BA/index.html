The Battle of Maipú was fought near Santiago, Chile on 5 April 1818, between South American rebels and Spanish royalists during the Chilean War of Independence. The Patriot forces, under José de San Martín's command and including Bernardo O'Higgins, defeated the royalist army of Mariano Osorio, securing Chilean independence.

Background

After the Patriot disaster at Cancha Rayada on 19 March 1818, San Martín reorganised the Army of the Andes near Santiago. The royalists advanced on the capital but were intercepted on the plains of Maipú, southwest of the city.

The battle

San Martín's artillery opened the battle, and the Patriot infantry and cavalry gradually broke the royalist line. A royalist reserve battalion held out in the village of Lo Espejo until overwhelmed. O'Higgins, arriving late on the field after being wounded at Cancha Rayada, embraced San Martín in the famous "Abrazo de Maipú".

Aftermath

The royalist army in Chile was destroyed, with some 2,000 killed and over 2,000 captured, including Osorio's army ceasing to exist as a fighting force. Maipú guaranteed the independence of Chile and freed San Martín to prepare the naval expedition that would liberate Peru.
