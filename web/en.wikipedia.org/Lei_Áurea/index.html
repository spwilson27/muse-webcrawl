Lei Áurea

The Lei Áurea (Golden Law), officially Imperial Law No. 3,353, was the law that abolished slavery in Brazil. Princess Isabel, acting as regent, signed it on 13 May 1888, freeing the remaining enslaved people — roughly 700,000 — without compensation to owners.

Background

Brazil was the last Western country to abolish slavery. Earlier steps included the Law of Free Birth (1871), freeing children born to enslaved mothers, and the Sexagenarian Law (1885). By the 1880s abolitionism was a mass movement, provinces such as Ceará freed their enslaved populations early, and mass escapes undermined the institution.

Consequences

The Golden Law was only two articles long and made no provision for integrating freed people, who received no land or support. Discontented landowners withdrew support from the monarchy, contributing to its fall in 1889. 13 May is commemorated in Brazil, though Black movements often prefer 20 November, honouring Zumbi of Palmares.
