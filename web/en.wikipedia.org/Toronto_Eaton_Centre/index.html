Toronto Eaton Centre

Toronto Eaton Centre
Location: Downtown Toronto, Ontario, Canada
Opened: 1977
Stores: ~230
Anchor: Hudson's Bay, Nordstrom (former)

CF Toronto Eaton Centre, commonly the Eaton Centre, is a major shopping mall and office complex in downtown Toronto, Ontario, Canada. Named for the Eaton's department store chain that anchored it, the centre occupies a full city block bounded by Yonge, Queen and Dundas streets and is one of the most visited tourist attractions in Toronto, drawing upwards of 50 million visitors a year.

Architecture

Designed by Eberhard Zeidler and opened in phases from 1977, the mall is famous for its soaring glass-vaulted galleria, modelled on the Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II in Milan. Suspended from the ceiling is Flight Stop, a celebrated sculpture by Michael Snow depicting 60 fibreglass Canada geese in flight. The centre connects directly to the Queen and Dundas subway stations and to the PATH underground walkway network.

History

The project reshaped downtown Toronto retailing at the expense of many older storefronts along Yonge Street. Eaton's itself went bankrupt in 1999; its flagship space was subsequently occupied by Sears and later Nordstrom and Hudson's Bay. The mall is now operated by Cadillac Fairview and branded "CF Toronto Eaton Centre".
