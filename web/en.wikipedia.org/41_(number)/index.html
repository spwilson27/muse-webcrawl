41 (forty-one) is the natural number following 40 and preceding 42. It is the 13th prime number and the sum of the first six primes (2 + 3 + 5 + 7 + 11 + 13). It is also a twin prime with 43, a super-prime, and a Newman–Shanks–Williams prime.

In mathematics

41 appears in Euler's famous prime-generating polynomial n² + n + 41, which yields prime numbers for every integer n from 0 to 39 — a property connected to 41 being a Heegner number. 41 is the smallest integer whose reciprocal has a maximal period length (5 digits being irrelevant here; its reciprocal repeats every 5 digits, 1/41 = 0.02439…). It is also the sum of two squares, 4² + 5².

In science and culture

41 is the atomic number of niobium, a transition metal used in superconducting alloys. In popular culture, the number appears in the name of the Canadian rock band Sum 41, and George H. W. Bush is often called "Bush 41" to distinguish the 41st U.S. president from his son, the 43rd. In Japanese culture, 41 can be read as a pun associated with death in some contexts because of its pronunciation in certain combinations.
