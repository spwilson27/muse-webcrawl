The Sengoku period (戦国時代, Sengoku jidai, "Warring States period") is a period in Japanese history of near-constant civil war, social upheaval and political intrigue, roughly from the Ōnin War of 1467 to the unification of Japan around 1600. The authority of the Ashikaga shogunate collapsed, and provincial warlords known as daimyō fought for control of the country.

Unification

The process of reunification was driven successively by Oda Nobunaga, Toyotomi Hideyoshi and Tokugawa Ieyasu. Nobunaga's campaigns from the 1560s broke the power of rival daimyō and Buddhist warrior leagues; Hideyoshi completed the conquest of Honshu, Shikoku and Kyushu by 1590; and Ieyasu's victory at the Battle of Sekigahara in 1600 led to the establishment of the Tokugawa shogunate in 1603.

Society and warfare

The period saw the introduction of firearms from Portugal in 1543, massed infantry tactics, castle building on a large scale, and the phenomenon of gekokujō — the low overthrowing the high — as men of humble origin rose to power.
