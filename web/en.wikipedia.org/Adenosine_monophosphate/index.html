Adenosine monophosphate

Chemical formula: C10H14N5O7P
Molar mass: 347.22 g/mol
Also known as: 5′-adenylic acid, AMP

Adenosine monophosphate (AMP), also known as 5′-adenylic acid, is a nucleotide consisting of the nucleobase adenine, the sugar ribose, and a single phosphate group. It is an ester of phosphoric acid and the nucleoside adenosine.

Role in metabolism

AMP is a component of RNA and a key player in cellular energy metabolism. It is formed by the hydrolysis of adenosine diphosphate (ADP) and can be interconverted with ATP and ADP by adenylate kinase (2 ADP ⇌ ATP + AMP). A rising AMP-to-ATP ratio signals low cellular energy and activates the enzyme AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK), a master regulator of metabolism. AMP also results from the breakdown of cyclic AMP by phosphodiesterases and from ATP during RNA synthesis.

Flavor

Salts of AMP-related nucleotides such as disodium inosinate and guanylate are used as flavor enhancers; AMP itself can suppress bitterness perception.
