Orthogonalization

The Gram–Schmidt process converts a set of vectors into mutually orthogonal vectors spanning the same subspace.

In linear algebra, orthogonalization is the process of finding a set of orthogonal vectors that span a particular subspace. Closely related is the process of orthonormalization, which produces an orthonormal set — orthogonal vectors of unit length — spanning the same subspace.

Methods

The best-known method is the Gram–Schmidt process, which successively subtracts from each vector its projections onto the previously constructed orthogonal vectors. Although conceptually simple, classical Gram–Schmidt is numerically unstable; the modified Gram–Schmidt algorithm and orthogonal transformations based on Householder reflections or Givens rotations are preferred in computation. These methods are closely connected with the QR decomposition of a matrix.

Applications

Orthogonalization underlies many algorithms in numerical linear algebra and signal processing, including least-squares solvers, the Arnoldi and Lanczos iterations for eigenvalue problems, and the construction of orthogonal polynomial families such as the Legendre and Chebyshev polynomials.
