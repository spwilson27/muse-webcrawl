Fang Binxing (Chinese: 方滨兴; born 17 July 1960) is a Chinese computer scientist and academician of the Chinese Academy of Engineering. He is widely credited as the principal architect of China's Internet censorship and surveillance system, the "Great Firewall of China" (formally the Golden Shield Project), in whose development he played a key technical and policy role.

Career

Fang studied at the Harbin Institute of Technology, earning his doctorate in computer science. He worked at the National Computer Network Emergency Response Technical Team/Coordination Center of China (CNCERT) and was a leading figure in designing the filtering infrastructure deployed at China's international gateways. From 2007 to 2013 he served as president of the Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications.

Public image

Fang is a controversial public figure in China, frequently criticised online for his association with internet censorship. In a widely reported 2011 incident, a student threw a shoe and eggs at him during a lecture at Wuhan University. Fang has publicly defended China's system of internet management, arguing that it is necessary for national security, while also acknowledging using VPNs himself to test blocked websites.
