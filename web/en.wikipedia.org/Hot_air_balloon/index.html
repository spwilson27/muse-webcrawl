Hot air balloon

A hot air balloon is a lighter-than-air aircraft consisting of a large fabric envelope filled with heated air and a gondola or basket carrying passengers and a heat source, usually an open-flame propane burner. Because the hot air inside is less dense than the surrounding air, the balloon rises. Direction is controlled only indirectly, by changing altitude to find winds blowing different ways.

History

The brothers Joseph-Michel and Jacques-Étienne Montgolfier demonstrated the first untethered manned flight at Versailles and Paris in 1783; Jean-François Pilâtre de Rozier and the Marquis d'Arlandes made the first free flight on 21 November 1783. Modern ballooning dates from Ed Yost's development of the propane burner and ripstop nylon envelope in the 1950s and 1960s.

Operation and sport

The pilot heats the air to climb and vents air (or lets it cool) to descend; a parachute valve at the crown allows rapid descent. Ballooning is a popular recreational activity and sport, with festivals such as the Albuquerque International Balloon Fiesta drawing hundreds of balloons, and competitive events testing precision navigation. In 1999 Bertrand Piccard and Brian Jones completed the first non-stop balloon circumnavigation of the Earth.
