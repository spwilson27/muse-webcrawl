Women's Social and Political Union

The Women's Social and Political Union (WSPU) was a women-only political movement and the leading organisation campaigning for women's suffrage in the United Kingdom. Founded in Manchester in 1903 by Emmeline Pankhurst and her daughters, including Christabel and Sylvia, it became famous for its militant tactics under the motto "Deeds, not words."

Militant campaign

From 1905, after Christabel Pankhurst and Annie Kenney were arrested for heckling a Liberal meeting, WSPU members — dubbed "suffragettes" by the press — disrupted meetings, chained themselves to railings, and from 1912 escalated to window-smashing and arson. Many were imprisoned and went on hunger strike, enduring forcible feeding; Emily Davison died after stepping in front of the King's horse at the 1913 Derby.

First World War and outcome

The WSPU suspended militancy in 1914 and supported the war effort. In 1918 the Representation of the People Act enfranchised women over 30 meeting a property qualification, and full equal voting rights followed in 1928.
