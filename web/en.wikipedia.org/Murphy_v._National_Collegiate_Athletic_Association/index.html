Murphy v. National Collegiate Athletic Association

Murphy v. NCAA
CourtSupreme Court of the United States
DecidedMay 14, 2018
Citation584 U.S. 453
OutcomePASPA held unconstitutional under the anti-commandeering doctrine

Murphy v. National Collegiate Athletic Association, 584 U.S. 453 (2018), was a United States Supreme Court case concerning the Tenth Amendment and the Professional and Amateur Sports Protection Act of 1992 (PASPA), a federal law that prohibited most states from authorising sports betting. The Court held that PASPA's prohibition on state authorisation of sports gambling violated the anti-commandeering doctrine, because it impermissibly dictated what state legislatures may and may not do.

Background

New Jersey voters approved a constitutional amendment to allow sports betting in 2011, and the state legislature enacted a sports wagering law. The major sports leagues and the NCAA sued, and the case — originally captioned Christie v. NCAA after Governor Chris Christie — reached the Supreme Court after Phil Murphy became governor. Justice Samuel Alito delivered the opinion of the Court.

Impact

The decision struck down PASPA in its entirety and opened the way for states to legalise sports betting; in the years following, a majority of U.S. states enacted sports wagering laws, creating a large regulated industry.
