Lever action

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A lever action is a type of action for repeating firearms in which the shooter cycles the breech mechanism by moving a lever, typically a loop-shaped lever located underneath the receiver around the trigger guard area. Moving the lever down and back up ejects the spent cartridge case, cocks the hammer, and chambers a fresh round from the magazine. Lever actions are most commonly associated with rifles such as the Winchester Model 1873, but the mechanism has also been used in shotguns, pistols, and a small number of military arms.

History

The lever action emerged in the United States in the mid-19th century. Early designs included the Jennings rifle and the Volcanic repeating arms of the 1850s, which used weak "Rocket Ball" ammunition. The breakthrough came with the Henry rifle of 1860, designed by Benjamin Tyler Henry, which fired a .44 calibre rimfire metallic cartridge and held 16 rounds in a tubular magazine under the barrel. The Henry and its successor, the Winchester Model 1866, saw use in the American Civil War era and on the frontier.

Winchester Repeating Arms refined the design through the Models 1873 and 1886, while John Marlin's firm produced rival designs with side ejection. John Browning's Winchester Model 1894 became one of the best-selling sporting rifles of all time and popularized the .30-30 Winchester cartridge for deer hunting. In 1895 Winchester produced a lever action with a box magazine, the Model 1895, strong enough for military cartridges; it was issued in quantity to the Imperial Russian Army during the First World War — the lever action's largest military use.

Design

Most classic lever actions feed from a tubular magazine beneath the barrel, which historically limited them to flat-nosed bullets (to avoid a pointed bullet detonating the primer of the round ahead of it under recoil). Later designs such as the Savage Model 99 used rotary or box magazines and could fire pointed, high-velocity cartridges. Modern lever actions by Marlin, Henry Repeating Arms, and others remain popular for hunting and sport shooting.

Advantages and disadvantages

The lever action is fast to cycle while keeping the rifle shouldered, is ambidextrous, and has a slim profile well suited to carrying on horseback or in a scabbard. Compared with bolt actions it is generally less strong, harder to fire from a prone position, and less convenient to unload. Although largely superseded for military use by bolt-action and self-loading designs, the lever action endures as an iconic design of the American West.

Notable examples

  • Henry rifle (1860)
  • Winchester Model 1873 — "the gun that won the West"
  • Winchester Model 1894
  • Marlin Model 336
  • Savage Model 99
  • Winchester Model 1887 lever-action shotgun
