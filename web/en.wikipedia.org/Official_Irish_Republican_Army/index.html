Official Irish Republican Army

The Official Irish Republican Army or Official IRA (OIRA) was an Irish republican paramilitary group whose goal was to create an independent, socialist republic encompassing all of Ireland. It formed in December 1969 after a split in the republican movement, when the leadership of the Irish Republican Army moved towards Marxism and away from traditional militarism. The other faction became known as the Provisional IRA.

The split

The split of 1969–70 arose from disputes over abstentionism (whether republicans should take seats in parliaments in Dublin, Belfast, and London), the direction of the civil rights campaign in Northern Ireland, and the IRA's failure to defend nationalist areas during the violence of August 1969. The Officials, led by figures such as Cathal Goulding, embraced a Marxist analysis and sought to unite Protestant and Catholic workers.

Campaign

The Official IRA carried out a campaign of bombings and shootings in the early 1970s, including the Aldershot bombing of February 1972, which killed seven people and was widely condemned. It also became embroiled in feuds with the Provisional IRA and, later, the Irish National Liberation Army. In May 1972 the Officials declared a ceasefire, which they largely maintained thereafter, though the organisation retained weapons and was accused of involvement in criminal activity and occasional killings in later decades.

Politics

The Officials' political wing, Official Sinn Féin, renamed itself Sinn Féin The Workers' Party and then, in 1982, the Workers' Party of Ireland. The Official IRA was reported to have decommissioned its weapons in 2010 as part of the Northern Ireland peace process decommissioning arrangements.
