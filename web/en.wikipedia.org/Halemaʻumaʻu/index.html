Halemaʻumaʻu

Halemaʻumaʻu (Hawaiian for "house of the ferns") is a pit crater within the much larger summit caldera of Kīlauea volcano on the island of Hawaiʻi, in Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park. In Hawaiian tradition it is the home of Pele, the volcano goddess. For most of the period from 1823 to 1924, and again from 2008 to 2018, Halemaʻumaʻu contained an active lava lake, one of the most reliable and accessible displays of volcanic activity in the world.

Recent activity

An explosive eruption in 1924 greatly enlarged the crater and ended the long-lived lava lake. A new eruptive vent opened in Halemaʻumaʻu in March 2008, building a lava lake that persisted for a decade. In May 2018, during the lower East Rift Zone eruption, the summit lava lake drained and the crater floor collapsed in a series of explosions, deepening Halemaʻumaʻu by more than 500 metres. A water lake then formed at the bottom — the first recorded at Kīlauea's summit — before an eruption beginning on 20 December 2020 vaporised it and re-established a lava lake. Further eruptions in 2021, 2023 and afterwards have alternated between lava lakes and fountaining episodes within the crater.

Dimensions

Following the 2018 collapse, Halemaʻumaʻu is roughly 1.5 km across and more than 500 m deep, occupying the southwestern part of the Kīlauea caldera floor.
