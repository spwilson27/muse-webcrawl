Internationalization and localization

In computing, internationalization and localization (American) or internationalisation and localisation (Commonwealth) are means of adapting computer software to different languages, regional peculiarities and technical requirements of a target locale. The terms are frequently abbreviated to the numeronyms i18n (where 18 stands for the number of letters between the first i and the last n in the word internationalization) and L10n respectively.

Definitions

Internationalization is the process of designing a software application so that it can be adapted to various languages and regions without engineering changes. It is a one-time design step: externalizing translatable text into resource files, supporting Unicode character encodings, and making formatting of dates, numbers, currencies and units locale-dependent.

Localization is the process of adapting internationalized software for a specific region or language by translating text and adding locale-specific components, such as local imagery, legal requirements, and cultural conventions. A localized product is commonly described as a combination of language and region, for example en-GB for British English or pt-BR for Brazilian Portuguese.

Concerns

Successful localization must handle matters including:

  • Character encoding, collation (sorting) and text direction, including right-to-left scripts such as Arabic and Hebrew;
  • Date and time formats, calendars, time zones and week numbering;
  • Number formats, decimal separators, currencies and units of measurement;
  • Keyboard layouts, input methods and text expansion — translated text often needs substantially more (or less) space than the source text;
  • Cultural sensitivities, such as colours, symbols and imagery that may carry different connotations in different markets.

Globalization

Together with the business processes of marketing and supporting a product in many countries, internationalization and localization are sometimes grouped under the term globalization (abbreviated g11n).
