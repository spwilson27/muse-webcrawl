The National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) is a United States government institute, part of the National Library of Medicine within the National Institutes of Health, that develops and maintains public databases of biological and biomedical information. Founded in 1988 and based in Bethesda, Maryland, it is one of the world's most-used scientific resources.

Databases and tools

NCBI operates GenBank, the annotated archive of publicly available DNA sequences; PubMed, the search engine for biomedical literature; PubMed Central, the free full-text archive; and the Entrez cross-database search system. It also maintains databases of genes, genomes, proteins, chemical compounds (PubChem) and clinical variants (ClinVar), and provides the BLAST family of sequence-alignment tools, used to compare biological sequences. Founding director David Lipman led the centre from 1989 to 2017.

Role

NCBI's resources are freely available worldwide and receive millions of users daily; data deposited there underpin research across genomics, medicine and biotechnology, and deposition in GenBank is a standard condition of publication for new sequence data.
