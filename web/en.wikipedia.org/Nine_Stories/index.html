Nine Stories

Nine Stories is a collection of short stories by American writer J. D. Salinger, published in April 1953 by Little, Brown and Company. It comprises nine stories, most of which had previously appeared in The New Yorker between 1948 and 1953. The collection is considered a landmark of post-war American short fiction.

Contents

  1. "A Perfect Day for Bananafish" — Seymour Glass, on holiday in Florida with his wife Muriel, tells a young girl a parable about bananafish; the story ends with his suicide.
  2. "Uncle Wiggily in Connecticut"
  3. "Just Before the War with the Eskimos"
  4. "The Laughing Man"
  5. "Down at the Dinghy" — featuring Boo Boo Glass and her son Lionel.
  6. "For Esmé—with Love and Squalor" — a soldier's wartime encounter with a young English girl and its aftermath.
  7. "Pretty Mouth and Green My Eyes"
  8. "De Daumier-Smith's Blue Period"
  9. "Teddy" — the precocious, spiritually gifted Teddy McArdle aboard an ocean liner.

Themes

Several stories involve members of Salinger's fictional Glass family. Recurring themes include the loss of innocence, the gulf between children and the adult world, post-war trauma, and Eastern religious thought, particularly Zen Buddhism. In Britain and some other countries the collection was published as For Esmé—with Love and Squalor, and Other Stories.
