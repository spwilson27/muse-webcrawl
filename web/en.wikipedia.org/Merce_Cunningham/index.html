Mercier Philip "Merce" Cunningham (April 16, 1919 – July 26, 2009) was an American dancer and choreographer who was at the forefront of American modern dance for more than 50 years. He was also known for his longtime collaboration with composer John Cage, his partner in life and work. Together with Cage and visual artists such as Robert Rauschenberg and Jasper Johns, Cunningham developed a radical approach in which dance, music, and design were created independently and combined only at performance.

Career

Cunningham studied at the Cornish School in Seattle and danced with the Martha Graham Dance Company from 1939 to 1945, creating roles in works such as Appalachian Spring. He presented his first solo concert with Cage in 1944 and formed the Merce Cunningham Dance Company at Black Mountain College in the summer of 1953. Despite early poverty and critical hostility, the company toured the world and eventually gained broad acclaim.

Choreographic method

Cunningham rejected the narrative and psychological conventions of modern dance. His key innovations included:

  • Chance procedures — using coin tosses and the I Ching to determine sequences, spatial patterns, and combinations, freeing movement from personal taste.
  • Independence of the arts — choreography, score, and décor coexist in the same time and space without illustrating one another.
  • Movement for its own sake — any movement can be dance; the centre of the stage holds no privilege, and there are no fixed points of focus.
  • Technology — late in life he choreographed with the computer program LifeForms (DanceForms), producing works such as BIPED (1999).

Major works include Suite by Chance (1953), Winterbranch (1964), RainForest (1968), Sounddance (1975), and Ocean (1994). In 2009 he announced the Legacy Plan for the company, which undertook a final world tour and dissolved at the end of 2011, while the Merce Cunningham Trust preserves his repertory.
