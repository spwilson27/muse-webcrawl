Australian Overland Telegraph Line

LocationPort Augusta to Darwin, Australia
Length3,200 km (2,000 mi)
Completed1872
Key figureCharles Todd

The Australian Overland Telegraph Line was a telegraph line that ran 3,200 kilometres (2,000 miles) between Port Augusta in South Australia and Darwin in what is now the Northern Territory, connecting Australia's settled south with the international telegraph network via Java.

Construction

The project was championed by Charles Todd, South Australia's Superintendent of Telegraphs. Construction began in 1870 and was divided into northern, central, and southern sections. Workers erected more than 36,000 poles through harsh desert terrain. The line was completed on 22 August 1872, when the sections were joined near Frew's Ponds.

Significance

Before the line, news from Europe took weeks to reach Australia by ship; afterwards, messages could arrive in hours. Repeater stations along the route — including Alice Springs, named after Todd's wife — became nuclei of outback settlement, and the line's route later influenced roads and the Central Australia Railway.
