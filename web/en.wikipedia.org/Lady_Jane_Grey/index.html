Lady Jane Grey

Lady Jane Grey
Born: c. 1537
Died: 12 February 1554, Tower of London
Reigned (disputed): 10–19 July 1553

Lady Jane Grey (c. 1537 – 12 February 1554), the "Nine Days' Queen", was an English noblewoman and de facto Queen of England and Ireland from 10 July until 19 July 1553. A great-granddaughter of Henry VII through his daughter Mary Tudor, she was named heir by Edward VI in his "Devise for the Succession", bypassing his half-sisters Mary and Elizabeth in an attempt to keep England Protestant.

Jane was proclaimed queen on Edward's death but support rallied to Mary, and Jane's father-in-law John Dudley, Duke of Northumberland, who had engineered her marriage to his son Guildford and her accession, was arrested. Jane was deposed after nine days, tried for treason, and — after her father's involvement in Wyatt's rebellion — executed at the Tower of London aged 16 or 17, along with Guildford Dudley. Renowned for her learning, she became a Protestant martyr in popular memory.
