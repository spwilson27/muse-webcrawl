Extremism

Extremism is "the quality or state of being extreme" or the advocacy of extreme measures or views. The term is primarily used in a political or religious sense to refer to an ideology that is considered by the speaker or by some implied shared consensus to be far outside the mainstream attitudes of society. It can also be used in an economic context.

Definitions

There is no single agreed definition of extremism. Political scientists have characterized it by features such as intolerance of dissent, rejection of democratic norms and pluralism, dogmatism, and a Manichaean view of the world. Peter T. Coleman and Andrea Bartoli describe extremism as a complex phenomenon that, while often associated with violence, is not necessarily violent; "violent extremism" is distinguished as a subset involving the endorsement or use of violence for ideological goals.

Types

Extremism is discussed in connection with far-right and far-left politics, religious fundamentalism, single-issue movements, and separatist movements. Governments maintain various programmes for countering violent extremism (CVE) and for deradicalization.

Criticism of the term

Scholars note that the label "extremist" is relative and often pejorative: positions once considered extreme, such as the abolition of slavery or women's suffrage, later became mainstream. Critics argue the term can be used to delegitimize dissent and that it describes distance from a perceived centre rather than any fixed content.
