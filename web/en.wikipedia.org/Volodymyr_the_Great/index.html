Volodymyr I Sviatoslavych or Vladimir I Sviatoslavich (Old East Slavic: Володимѣръ Свѧтославичь; c. 958 – 15 July 1015), given the epithet "the Great", was Prince of Novgorod from 970 and Grand Prince of Kiev from 978 until his death in 1015. He is best known for the Christianisation of Kievan Rus' in 988, which shaped the religious and cultural development of Ukraine, Russia and Belarus, and he is venerated as a saint in both the Eastern Orthodox and Catholic churches.

Early reign

The youngest son of Sviatoslav I, Volodymyr was appointed to Novgorod by his father in 970. After Sviatoslav's death, civil war broke out between his brothers Yaropolk and Oleg; Volodymyr fled to Scandinavia, returned with a Varangian army, killed Yaropolk and by 980 ruled all of Kievan Rus'. As a pagan he promoted the cult of Perun and other Slavic gods, erecting a pantheon of idols on the hill at Kiev.

Christianisation

According to the Primary Chronicle, Volodymyr sent envoys to study the religions of his neighbours before choosing Byzantine Christianity — a narrative known as the "testing of the faiths". In 988 he was baptised, reportedly at Chersonesus in Crimea, married the Byzantine princess Anna Porphyrogenita, and ordered the mass baptism of the people of Kiev in the Dnieper River. He built the Church of the Tithes in Kiev and sponsored schools and churches across his realm.

Later reign and legacy

Volodymyr consolidated his state, campaigning against the Pechenegs and building fortified lines south of Kiev, and placed his many sons in charge of subject towns — a practice that fuelled succession conflict after his death. His son Yaroslav the Wise emerged victorious. Volodymyr's image appears on modern Ukrainian hryvnia banknotes, and the great monument to him (1853) above the Dnieper is a symbol of Kyiv.
