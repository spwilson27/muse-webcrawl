Analytic continuation

In complex analysis, analytic continuation is a technique to extend the domain of a given analytic function. A function defined and holomorphic on a small domain can frequently be extended to a larger domain by re-expanding its power series around new points along a path, each expansion overlapping the previous one. The identity theorem guarantees that when such an extension exists along a path, its values are uniquely determined.

Definition and method

Formally, if f is analytic on a domain U, a function g analytic on a larger domain V containing U and agreeing with f on U is an analytic continuation of f to V. A standard method uses chains of overlapping discs: the Taylor series of the function is expanded at a point inside the disc of convergence, then re-expanded at a nearby point, progressively extending the known domain.

Examples

The Riemann zeta function is initially defined by a series converging only for Re(s) > 1, but analytic continuation extends it to a meromorphic function on the whole complex plane (with a single pole at s = 1), which assigns values such as ζ(−1) = −1/12. Similarly, the gamma function is continued beyond its integral definition to all complex numbers except the non-positive integers.

Obstructions

Continuations may be multi-valued when paths wind around singularities, a phenomenon captured by the monodromy theorem, and some domains have natural boundaries across which no continuation is possible.
