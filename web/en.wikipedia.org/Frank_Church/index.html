Frank Forrester Church III (July 25, 1924 – April 7, 1984) was an American lawyer and politician. A member of the Democratic Party, he served as a United States Senator from Idaho from 1957 to 1981. He is best known for chairing the Church Committee, the Senate select committee that investigated abuses by U.S. intelligence agencies in 1975–1976.

Early life

Church was born and raised in Boise, Idaho. A prize-winning orator in high school, he served in the U.S. Army during the Second World War as a military intelligence officer in the China-Burma-India theater. He graduated from Stanford University and Stanford Law School, and practiced law in Boise before winning election to the Senate in 1956 at age 32.

Senate career

Church became a leading liberal voice on foreign policy, an early critic of the Vietnam War, and a champion of wilderness preservation; he played a central role in passage of the Wilderness Act of 1964. In 1975 he chaired the Senate Select Committee to Study Governmental Operations with Respect to Intelligence Activities — the Church Committee — which exposed illegal surveillance, assassination plotting, and covert abuses by the CIA, FBI, and NSA, and led to lasting reforms including the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act and permanent Senate intelligence oversight. He sought the Democratic presidential nomination in 1976. In 1980 he narrowly lost re-election, part of a conservative sweep that year.

Legacy

The Frank Church–River of No Return Wilderness in central Idaho, designated shortly before his death from pancreatic cancer in 1984, is named in his honor and is one of the largest wilderness areas in the contiguous United States.
