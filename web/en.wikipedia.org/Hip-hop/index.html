Hip-hop

Stylistic originsFunk, disco, dub, R&B, reggae, jazz
Cultural origins1970s, the Bronx, New York City
Key elementsMCing, DJing, breakdancing, graffiti

Hip-hop (also spelled hip hop) is a cultural movement and music genre that emerged in the Bronx, New York City, in the 1970s. It is traditionally described as comprising four elements: MCing (rapping), DJing, breakdancing (b-boying), and graffiti art. DJ Kool Herc is widely credited as a founding figure; his back-to-school party on 11 August 1973 at 1520 Sedgwick Avenue is often cited as the genre's birth.

History

From block parties in the Bronx, hip-hop spread through the 1980s with acts such as Run-DMC and the Beastie Boys, entered a "golden age" of innovation in the late 1980s and early 1990s, and became one of the world's most commercially successful genres. Regional styles — East Coast, West Coast, Southern, and many international variants — developed alongside subgenres from boom bap to trap.

Elements and culture

Afrika Bambaataa, founder of the Universal Zulu Nation, promoted hip-hop as a culture of peace, unity, love, and having fun, adding knowledge as a fifth element. Beyond music, hip-hop encompasses fashion, language, and visual art and has had a profound global influence on popular culture.
