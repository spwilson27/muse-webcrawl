The Venera programme (Russian: Венера, "Venus") was a series of space probes developed by the Soviet Union between 1961 and 1984 to explore Venus. The probes were designed in two types — flyby/atmospheric probes and landers — and were the first human-made objects to enter another planet's atmosphere, land on another planet, and return images from another planet's surface.

Missions

Early flights suffered failures common to the era; Venera 4 (1967) returned the first direct atmospheric measurements, and Venera 7 (1970) made the first soft landing, transmitting weak data from the surface. Venera 9 and 10 (1975) returned the first black-and-white photographs of the Venusian landscape. Later, heavily armoured spherical landers survived the ~460 °C, 90-atmosphere surface for up to about two hours; Venera 13 and 14 (1982) returned colour images and recorded the sound of Venusian wind. Vega balloons (1985) continued the exploration begun by the Venera orbiters.
