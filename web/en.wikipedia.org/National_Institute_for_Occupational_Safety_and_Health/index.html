The National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health (NIOSH) is a United States federal agency responsible for conducting research and making recommendations for the prevention of work-related injury and illness. It is part of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention within the Department of Health and Human Services.

History and role

NIOSH was created by the Occupational Safety and Health Act of 1970, alongside the regulatory Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA). Unlike OSHA, NIOSH has no enforcement power; it supplies the scientific basis for workplace standards, publishes recommended exposure limits, and investigates hazards through Health Hazard Evaluations requested by workers or employers.

Programmes

The institute certifies respiratory protective equipment — its N95 approval rating became a household term during the COVID-19 pandemic — and conducts landmark research on topics from coal miners' pneumoconiosis and firefighter safety to workplace violence and the health of World Trade Center responders.
