California Assembly Bill 5 (2019)

AB 5
Full title: Assembly Bill No. 5, Chapter 296, Statutes of 2019
Author: Assemblymember Lorena Gonzalez
Signed: 18 September 2019 by Governor Gavin Newsom
Effective: 1 January 2020

California Assembly Bill 5 (AB 5) is a California statute that codified and expanded the "ABC test" for determining whether workers are employees or independent contractors under state wage orders and labour law. Signed by Governor Gavin Newsom in September 2019 and effective from 1 January 2020, it was authored by Assemblymember Lorena Gonzalez of San Diego.

The ABC test

AB 5 codified the test adopted by the California Supreme Court in Dynamex Operations W. v. Superior Court (2018). Under it, a worker is presumed to be an employee unless the hiring entity proves all three conditions: (A) the worker is free from the control and direction of the hiring entity in the performance of the work; (B) the work is performed outside the usual course of the hiring entity's business; and (C) the worker is customarily engaged in an independently established trade, occupation or business of the same nature as the work performed.

Exemptions and effects

The bill exempted dozens of occupations — including doctors, lawyers, insurance agents, commercial fishermen, and certain freelance writers and photographers — which remained governed by the older multi-factor Borello test. AB 5 most visibly affected the "gig economy": app-based ride-hailing and delivery companies such as Uber, Lyft and DoorDash faced reclassification of their drivers as employees. Freelance journalists and other independent professionals also reported lost work due to submission caps originally included for some categories, later relaxed by follow-up legislation (AB 2257, 2020).

Proposition 22

In November 2020 California voters approved Proposition 22, a ballot initiative funded heavily by app-based companies, which exempted those companies from AB 5 while granting their drivers certain benefits. The measure survived subsequent constitutional challenges in the California courts.
