Structure of atenolol

Atenolol is a beta blocker medication primarily used to treat high blood pressure (hypertension) and heart-associated chest pain (angina). It belongs to the class of beta blockers that are selective for the β1 adrenergic receptor, which is found mainly in heart tissue. Atenolol is generally not recommended as a first-line treatment for hypertension in otherwise uncomplicated cases, but it remains widely used, particularly in combination with other agents.

Medical uses

Atenolol is used in the management of hypertension, angina pectoris, and acute myocardial infarction, as well as for certain heart rhythm disorders and the prevention of migraine in some patients. By blocking β1 receptors, it reduces heart rate, cardiac contractility, and blood pressure, thereby lowering the oxygen demand of the heart muscle.

Pharmacology

Atenolol is hydrophilic, which limits its penetration into the central nervous system and may reduce the incidence of some central side effects compared with lipophilic beta blockers such as propranolol. It is not substantially metabolized by the liver and is excreted largely unchanged by the kidneys, so dose adjustment is needed in patients with renal impairment. Its elimination half-life is roughly six to seven hours, though its clinical effect lasts about 24 hours, allowing once-daily dosing. Atenolol was developed at Imperial Chemical Industries (ICI) and introduced in 1976 as a successor to earlier beta blockers.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include fatigue, dizziness, cold extremities, and a slow heart rate (bradycardia). Atenolol should not be stopped abruptly in patients with coronary artery disease, as sudden withdrawal can worsen angina or precipitate a heart attack. It may mask some symptoms of low blood sugar in people with diabetes, and it is generally avoided in patients with asthma because even β1-selective agents can affect the airways at higher doses.

History

Atenolol was patented in 1969 and approved for medical use in the United States in 1981. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines and is available as a generic medication. It is sold under brand names including Tenormin.
