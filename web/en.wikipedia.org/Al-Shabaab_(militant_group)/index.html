Al-Shabaab (militant group)

Full name: Harakat al-Shabaab al-Mujahideen

Active: 2006 – present

Ideology: Salafi jihadism

Allegiance: Al-Qaeda (since 2012)

Area: Somalia and East Africa

Harakat al-Shabaab al-Mujahideen, commonly known as al-Shabaab ("The Youth"), is a Sunni Islamist militant group based in Somalia and active in East Africa. It emerged as the militant youth wing of the Islamic Courts Union, which controlled Mogadishu in 2006, and developed into an insurgency after Ethiopian forces intervened in Somalia.

History

After Ethiopia's intervention in December 2006, al-Shabaab waged a guerrilla war and by 2008–2009 controlled much of southern and central Somalia, including parts of Mogadishu, imposing a strict interpretation of sharia law in areas under its control. In 2011, under pressure from African Union Mission in Somalia (AMISOM) forces and during a severe famine, the group withdrew from Mogadishu but retained rural strongholds. In 2012 it formally pledged allegiance to al-Qaeda.

Attacks

Al-Shabaab has carried out numerous high-casualty attacks, including the 2013 Westgate shopping mall attack in Nairobi, Kenya (67 killed), the 2015 Garissa University attack (148 killed), and the October 2017 Mogadishu bombings, which killed more than 500 people and rank among the deadliest terrorist attacks worldwide. It continues to fight the Somali federal government and the African Union Transition Mission (ATMIS), financing itself through taxation and extortion in areas it controls or influences.
