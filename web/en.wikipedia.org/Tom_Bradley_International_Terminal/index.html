The Tom Bradley International Terminal (TBIT) is the main international terminal at Los Angeles International Airport (LAX) in Los Angeles, California. The terminal is named for Tom Bradley, the longtime mayor of Los Angeles who served from 1973 to 1993, and who championed the development of a modern international gateway for the city ahead of the 1984 Summer Olympics.

The terminal handles the majority of LAX's long-haul international traffic and is served by dozens of foreign-flag carriers, as well as international services operated by United States airlines. It is connected to the airport's other terminals and to the LAX people mover system.

History

The Tom Bradley International Terminal opened in June 1984, just before the start of the 1984 Summer Olympics in Los Angeles, giving LAX a dedicated, purpose-built international facility for the first time. Prior international operations had been spread among the airport's domestic terminals.

By the early 2000s the terminal was heavily congested, and planning began for a comprehensive modernization. The LAX modernization program included a $737 million renovation of the existing terminal and the construction of a major new addition, the West Gates, a satellite concourse connected to the main terminal by an underground pedestrian tunnel with moving walkways.

Redevelopment

The redeveloped terminal was unveiled in stages beginning in 2013. The project, designed by Fentress Architects, added a new "Great Hall" of approximately 150,000 square feet with dining and retail space, enlarged customs and immigration facilities, new premium lounges, and a large-scale digital media program including a four-story-high "Welcome Wall" and a 72-foot-tall "Time Tower" video display. The West Gates concourse, opened to passengers in 2021, added 15 gates capable of handling wide-body aircraft including the Airbus A380.

Facilities

The terminal contains ticketing, security screening, U.S. Customs and Border Protection inspection facilities, duty-free retail, and airline lounges operated by the three global airline alliances — Star Alliance, Oneworld and SkyTeam — as well as individual carriers. Departures are organized with check-in areas on the upper level and arrivals and customs processing below.
