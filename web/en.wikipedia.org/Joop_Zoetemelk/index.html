Joop Zoetemelk

Hendrik Gerardus Joseph "Joop" Zoetemelk (born 3 December 1946) is a Dutch former professional racing cyclist. He started and finished the Tour de France 16 times, a record at the time, and won the race in 1980, having finished second six times. He also won the Vuelta a España in 1979 and the UCI Road World Championship in 1985, becoming its oldest winner at age 38.

Career

Zoetemelk won gold in the team time trial at the 1968 Mexico City Olympics before turning professional. During the 1970s he repeatedly finished runner-up at the Tour behind Eddy Merckx and Bernard Hinault, earning a reputation as the eternal second. His persistence was rewarded in 1980 when, riding for TI–Raleigh, he won the Tour de France. His 1985 world road race title in Italy capped a remarkably long career; he retired in 1987.

Legacy

Zoetemelk is regarded as the greatest Dutch stage racer of his era. A well-known Dutch saying runs "Joop Zoetemelk is the best Dutchman" in a bus, reflecting his popularity. He later settled in France, where he ran a hotel.
