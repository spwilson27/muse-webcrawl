Foreign relations of China

China maintains one of the world's largest diplomatic networks.

The foreign relations of China are conducted by the People's Republic of China (PRC) through its Ministry of Foreign Affairs, under the direction of the Chinese Communist Party's Central Foreign Affairs Commission. China has full diplomatic relations with the great majority of the world's states — over 180 countries — and maintains one of the largest diplomatic networks globally. Since the economic reforms of 1978, China has become deeply integrated into the world economy as the largest trading nation, while its growing military and technological power has made its foreign policy a central subject of world politics.

Principles and history

China officially grounds its foreign policy in the Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence, first articulated in 1954: mutual respect for sovereignty and territorial integrity, mutual non-aggression, non-interference in internal affairs, equality and mutual benefit, and peaceful coexistence. The PRC took China's seat at the United Nations, including its permanent seat on the Security Council, in 1971. Under Deng Xiaoping, policy emphasized a low profile while concentrating on development; in the 2010s, under Xi Jinping, China adopted a more assertive "major-country diplomacy", launching the Belt and Road Initiative (2013) — a global infrastructure and investment programme spanning Asia, Africa, Europe, and beyond — and founding institutions such as the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank.

Key relationships

Relations with the United States are China's most consequential bilateral relationship, combining deep trade interdependence with strategic rivalry over trade, technology, Taiwan, and the South China Sea. China claims Taiwan as its territory and most states follow a "One China" policy, recognizing Beijing rather than Taipei; only a small number of states maintain formal ties with Taiwan. China has developed a "no limits" partnership with Russia, declared shortly before Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine, which Beijing has not condemned. Relations with neighbours include a close but sometimes strained partnership with Pakistan, an unresolved border dispute with India that erupted in clashes in 2020, maritime disputes with Japan, Vietnam, and the Philippines, and complex ties with both Koreas.

Multilateral engagement

China is a member of the United Nations, the World Trade Organization (joined 2001), the G20, APEC, BRICS, and the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation, and has expanded free-trade arrangements such as the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP). It is the largest contributor of peacekeeping personnel among the permanent members of the UN Security Council and the second-largest funder of the UN budget.
