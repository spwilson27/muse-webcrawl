Telencephalon

The telencephalon (plural: telencephala), also known as the cerebrum, is the largest and most developed part of the brain in humans and other mammals. It is the uppermost region of the central nervous system, developing from the prosencephalon (forebrain) during embryonic development. Together with the diencephalon, it forms the forebrain.

Structure

The telencephalon consists of two cerebral hemispheres separated by the longitudinal fissure and connected by the corpus callosum and other commissures. Each hemisphere is divided into four main lobes: frontal, parietal, temporal and occipital. The outer layer is the cerebral cortex — grey matter composed of neuronal cell bodies — which overlies white matter tracts. Deep structures include the basal ganglia, the hippocampus, the amygdala and the olfactory bulb.

Function

The telencephalon mediates the highest cognitive functions: conscious thought, perception, voluntary movement, language, learning and memory. The cerebral cortex is greatly expanded and folded (gyrified) in primates, especially humans, reaching a surface area of roughly 2,500 square centimetres.
