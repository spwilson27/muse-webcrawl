Milan–San Remo is an annual one-day road cycling race between Milan and Sanremo in northwestern Italy. First held in 1907, it is one of cycling's five Monuments and traditionally opens the spring classics season, earning the nicknames La Primavera ("The Spring") and La Classicissima. At roughly 298 kilometres it is the longest professional one-day race on the calendar.

Course

The route crosses Lombardy and Piedmont, climbs the Passo del Turchino, and follows the Ligurian coast through the short climbs of the Capi before the decisive Cipressa and the Poggio di San Remo, crested about 5 km from the finish on the Via Roma. The Poggio, introduced in 1960, is the launchpad for most winning attacks, though the race frequently ends in a reduced bunch sprint.

History and records

Eddy Merckx holds the record with seven victories (1966–1976). Other multiple winners include Costante Girardengo, Gino Bartali, Fausto Coppi, Erik Zabel, and Óscar Freire. The women's counterpart, long the Primavera Rosa, was revived as Milan–San Remo Women in 2025. Memorable editions include Gerald Ciolek's snow-shortened 2013 win and Matej Mohorič's 2022 victory using a dropper seatpost on the Poggio descent.

Character

Though its climbs are modest, the race's length makes it a test of endurance and positioning; it is often called the sprinters' classic, yet its list of winners spans pure sprinters and Grand Tour champions alike.
