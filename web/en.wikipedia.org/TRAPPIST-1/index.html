TRAPPIST-1 is a cool red dwarf star with seven known exoplanets, located about 39.5 light-years (12.1 parsecs) from the Sun in the constellation Aquarius. The star is only slightly larger than the planet Jupiter and has a mass of about 9% that of the Sun. It is one of the most intensively studied planetary systems beyond the Solar System because several of its planets lie within the star's habitable zone and are roughly Earth-sized.

Discovery and naming

The system is named after the Transiting Planets and Planetesimals Small Telescope (TRAPPIST) in Chile, whose team, led by Michaël Gillon of the University of Liège, announced the first three planets in 2016. Follow-up observations with the Spitzer Space Telescope and ground-based telescopes revealed four additional planets, and the full seven-planet system was announced in February 2017. The planets are designated TRAPPIST-1b through TRAPPIST-1h in order of orbital period.

Planetary system

All seven planets are packed into orbits smaller than that of Mercury, with periods ranging from about 1.5 days for planet b to roughly 19 days for planet h. The planets are close to a chain of mean-motion resonances, suggesting they formed farther out and migrated inward. Three planets (e, f, and g) orbit squarely within the conventional habitable zone, and planet e is considered the most Earth-like in size and received flux.

Characteristics

TRAPPIST-1 is an ultra-cool dwarf of spectral type M8V with an effective temperature near 2,560 K and a luminosity only about 0.05% of the Sun's. Estimates of its age range from about 3 to 8 billion years. Like other late M dwarfs it is magnetically active, producing flares that may affect the atmospheres of its close-in planets. Observations with the James Webb Space Telescope have begun to probe the atmospheres of the inner planets.
