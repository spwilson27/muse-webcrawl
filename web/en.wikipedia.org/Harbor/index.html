Harbor

TypeSheltered body of water
PurposeRefuge, loading and unloading of ships
StructuresBreakwaters, piers, wharves, docks

A harbor (American English) or harbour (British English) is a sheltered body of water where ships, boats, and barges can be docked safely, protected from waves, currents, and storms. Harbors may be natural — such as a deep bay or inlet sheltered by headlands — or artificial, created by constructing breakwaters, sea walls, or jetties. The term is often used interchangeably with port, though a port strictly includes the shore facilities for handling cargo and passengers.

Natural and artificial harbors

Many of the world's great ports grew around natural harbors: Sydney Harbour in Australia, the Pearl River estuary near Hong Kong, San Francisco Bay, and the ria coast of Galicia in Spain. Where nature did not provide shelter, engineers built it: artificial harbors such as Cherbourg in France protected anchorage with massive detached breakwaters, and the Mulberry harbours of the Second World War were prefabricated floating harbors towed across the English Channel for the Normandy landings.

Functions

Beyond refuge, harbors support commercial shipping, fishing fleets, naval bases, ferry terminals, and marinas for recreational craft. Harbor maintenance typically involves dredging to keep channels deep enough for vessels, pilotage services, and navigation aids such as lights and buoys. Harbor ecosystems can be biologically rich but are also vulnerable to pollution from shipping and industry. See also breakwater and dock.
