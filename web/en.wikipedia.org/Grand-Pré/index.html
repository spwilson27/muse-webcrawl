Grand-Pré

The Memorial Church at Grand-Pré National Historic Site

Grand-Pré (French for "great meadow") is a Canadian rural community in Kings County, Nova Scotia, and the site of one of the most significant episodes in Acadian history. Founded by French settlers around 1680, it became the largest Acadian settlement in the Minas Basin region, sustained by fertile marshlands reclaimed from the sea with dykes.

History

Acadian farmers built an extensive system of dykes and aboiteaux (sluices) that converted tidal salt marshes into productive farmland. By the mid-18th century, Grand-Pré was a prosperous community of several hundred families. In 1755, during the French and Indian War, British forces under Colonel John Winslow ordered the deportation of the Acadians. The men and boys of Grand-Pré were summoned to the church of Saint-Charles-des-Mines, where the removal order was read; over the following months more than a thousand residents of the area were expelled, their homes burned, in the opening of the Great Expulsion (Le Grand Dérangement).

Commemoration

Henry Wadsworth Longfellow's 1847 epic poem Evangeline made Grand-Pré famous as the home of its fictional heroine. The site is now the Grand-Pré National Historic Site of Canada, featuring a memorial church built in the 1920s and a statue of Evangeline. In 2012, the Landscape of Grand Pré was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List, recognizing both the Acadian dyke-building tradition and the site's role as a memorial to the deportation.
