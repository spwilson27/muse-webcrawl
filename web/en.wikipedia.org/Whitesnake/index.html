Whitesnake are an English rock band formed in London in 1978 by singer David Coverdale after he left Deep Purple. Initially a blues-oriented hard rock band, Whitesnake achieved their greatest commercial success in the mid-to-late 1980s with a polished hard rock and glam metal sound.

History

The early band, featuring guitarists Micky Moody and Bernie Marsden and former Deep Purple keyboardist Jon Lord, released albums such as Ready an' Willing (1980) and Come an' Get It (1981), popular in the UK and Japan. After line-up upheavals, the 1987 album Whitesnake (known as 1987 in Europe) sold over eight million copies in the US alone, propelled by the hit singles "Here I Go Again" — a number one on the Billboard Hot 100 — and "Is This Love".

Later years

The follow-up Slip of the Tongue (1989), with guitarist Steve Vai, was also a commercial success. Coverdale revived the band in the 2000s for regular touring and new albums including Good to Be Bad (2008), The Purple Album (2015) of reworked Deep Purple songs, and Flesh & Blood (2019). In 2022, health problems curtailed the band's farewell tour, and Coverdale announced his retirement from touring in 2025.
