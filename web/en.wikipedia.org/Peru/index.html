Peru

Peru, officially the Republic of Peru, is a country in western South America. It is bordered in the north by Ecuador and Colombia, in the east by Brazil, in the southeast by Bolivia, in the south by Chile, and in the south and west by the Pacific Ocean. The capital and largest city is Lima. Peru has a population of about 34 million.

History

Peruvian territory was home to several ancient cultures, including the Norte Chico civilization, one of the oldest in the world, and the Nazca, Moche, Wari and Tiwanaku cultures. In the 15th century the Inca Empire arose from the highlands of Cusco to become the largest state in pre-Columbian America. Spanish conquistadors under Francisco Pizarro conquered the empire in the 1530s, and Peru became the centre of the Viceroyalty of Peru. Peru proclaimed independence from Spain in 1821 and secured it in 1824 at the battles of Junín and Ayacucho.

Geography

Peru is divided into three broad regions: the arid Pacific coastal plain (costa), the rugged Andes mountains (sierra), which include Huascarán (6,768 m), the country's highest peak, and the vast Amazon rainforest (selva) of the east, which covers about 60% of the territory and contains the headwaters of the Amazon River.

Culture

Peruvian culture blends Indigenous, Spanish, African and Asian influences. Peruvian cuisine, featuring dishes such as ceviche and drawing on potatoes — of which Peru has thousands of native varieties — is internationally acclaimed. Major landmarks include Machu Picchu, the Nazca Lines and the historic centres of Cusco and Lima, all UNESCO World Heritage Sites. Spanish is the official language, with Quechua and Aymara co-official in areas where they predominate.
