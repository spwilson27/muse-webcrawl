Nunc dimittis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Simeon with the Christ child, by Rembrandt (detail)

The Nunc dimittis (Latin: "now you dismiss"), also known as the Song of Simeon or the Canticle of Simeon, is a canticle taken from the second chapter of the Gospel of Luke, verses 29 through 32. It records the words of the righteous Simeon, who, upon seeing the infant Jesus presented in the Temple in Jerusalem, recognised him as the promised Messiah and declared himself ready to depart in peace.

Text

In the King James Version the text reads: "Lord, now lettest thou thy servant depart in peace, according to thy word: For mine eyes have seen thy salvation, Which thou hast prepared before the face of all people; A light to lighten the Gentiles, and the glory of thy people Israel."

Liturgical use

The canticle is a fixture of evening worship. In the Roman Catholic liturgy it is sung at Compline (Night Prayer); in the Anglican tradition it is one of the two canticles of Evensong, following the second lesson; it is also used in Lutheran and Orthodox evening services and at funerals and Candlemas processions.

Musical settings

The text has been set by countless composers, including Thomas Tallis, William Byrd, Charles Villiers Stanford, Gustav Holst, and Arvo Pärt.
