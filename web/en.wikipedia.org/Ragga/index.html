Ragga

Jamaican dancehall style
Other namesRaggamuffin music
OriginJamaica, mid-1980s
Stylistic rootsDancehall, reggae, electronic music
Key artistsWayne Smith, Shabba Ranks, Chaka Demus & Pliers

Ragga, short for raggamuffin, is a subgenre of dancehall reggae that emerged in Jamaica in the mid-1980s, characterised by music built primarily with electronic instrumentation and rhythms, with deejays toasting or singing over digital "riddims". The term raggamuffin came from Jamaican street slang for a rough, streetwise youth.

History

The style is usually dated to Wayne Smith's 1985 hit "Under Mi Sleng Teng", produced by King Jammy using a Casio MT-40 keyboard preset — the first fully digital dancehall rhythm. Ragga dominated Jamaican music into the 1990s and, through artists such as Shabba Ranks and Chaka Demus & Pliers, reached international charts. In Britain, ragga's bass-heavy sound fed into jungle and drum and bass, while its influence runs through hip-hop, reggaeton and contemporary dancehall.

Characteristics

Ragga tracks typically feature synthesised basslines, drum machines, sampled riddims reused by many vocalists, and lyrics ranging from social commentary to the boastful and sexually explicit "slackness" that attracted controversy.
