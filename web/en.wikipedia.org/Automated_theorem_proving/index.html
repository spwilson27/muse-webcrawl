Automated theorem proving

Automated theorem proving (ATP) is a subfield of automated reasoning and mathematical logic dealing with proving mathematical theorems by computer programs. The system is given a set of axioms and hypotheses and a conjecture, and attempts to find a formal proof, or a counterexample, automatically.

Techniques

Important methods include resolution, superposition, tableaux calculi, and term rewriting. First-order theorem provers such as Vampire, E, and Prover9 have solved longstanding open problems in areas such as algebra; a famous example is the 1996 proof by William McCune's EQP prover of the Robbins conjecture. Satisfiability (SAT) and SMT solvers handle propositional and decidable fragments and are widely used in hardware and software verification.

Interactive proving

Related tools called proof assistants or interactive theorem provers (such as Coq, Isabelle, and Lean) construct machine-checked proofs with human guidance and have been used to verify major results, including the four color theorem and the Kepler conjecture.

Applications

ATP is used in formal verification of circuits and software, mathematics, logic puzzles, and the semantic web.
