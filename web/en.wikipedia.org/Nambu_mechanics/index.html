Nambu mechanics

FieldMathematical physics, classical mechanics
Introduced byYoichiro Nambu (1973)

Nambu mechanics is a generalisation of Hamiltonian mechanics proposed by Yoichiro Nambu in 1973. Whereas ordinary Hamiltonian mechanics describes the evolution of a system in terms of a Poisson bracket of two functions — an observable and a single Hamiltonian — Nambu mechanics employs a ternary (or higher) bracket of several functions, with multiple Hamiltonians jointly generating the motion.

Nambu bracket

For three functions f, g, h of phase-space variables, the Nambu bracket can be written as a Jacobian determinant, {f, g, h} = df ∧ dg ∧ dh expressed in coordinates. The time evolution of an observable f is then given by df/dt = {f, H1, H2}, where H1 and H2 are two Hamiltonians. The construction preserves phase-space volume automatically, generalising Liouville's theorem.

Examples and development

Nambu's original motivating example was the rigid-rotator (Euler top) equations, in which energy and the squared angular momentum serve as the two Hamiltonians. Leon Takhtajan later formulated the axiomatic basis of the theory, identifying the "fundamental identity" the bracket must satisfy. Nambu mechanics has since found applications in fluid dynamics, superintegrable systems, and as a classical precursor of structures appearing in M-theory (such as the Bagger–Lambert–Gustavsson model), where Nambu brackets arise on membrane worldvolumes.
