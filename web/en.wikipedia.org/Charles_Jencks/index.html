Charles Jencks

Jencks, architect and critic

Born21 June 1939, Baltimore, Maryland
Died13 October 2019, London
OccupationArchitectural critic, landscape designer
Known forTheorising postmodern architecture; Garden of Cosmic Speculation; Maggie's Centres (co-founder)

Charles Alexander Jencks (1939–2019) was an American cultural theorist, landscape designer, and architectural historian, best known as the leading theorist of postmodernism in architecture. His 1977 book The Language of Post-Modern Architecture famously announced that modern architecture "died in St Louis, Missouri on July 15, 1972" with the demolition of the Pruitt–Igoe housing complex.

Work

Jencks developed the idea of architecture as a multivalent language capable of "double coding" — speaking simultaneously to elites and the wider public. His later books, including The Iconic Building and The Architecture of the Jumping Universe, explored symbolism, cosmology, and complexity science.

Landscape and Maggie's Centres

At his home in Dumfriesshire, Scotland, Jencks created the Garden of Cosmic Speculation, a celebrated landform garden inspired by mathematics and cosmology, and designed landforms such as Northumberlandia. With his wife Maggie Keswick Jencks he co-founded Maggie's Centres, cancer-caring centres attached to hospitals and designed by leading architects, inspired by her experience of illness before her death in 1995.
