The Camp David Accords were a pair of political agreements signed by Egyptian President Anwar Sadat and Israeli Prime Minister Menachem Begin on 17 September 1978, following twelve days of secret negotiations at Camp David, the country retreat of the President of the United States in Maryland. The two framework agreements were negotiated and signed with the mediation of US President Jimmy Carter, and were formally witnessed at the White House.

The accords led directly to the 1979 Egypt–Israel peace treaty, the first peace agreement between Israel and an Arab state. Sadat and Begin shared the 1978 Nobel Peace Prize for their efforts.

Background

Egypt and Israel had fought four wars since 1948. Israel captured the Sinai Peninsula from Egypt in the Six-Day War of 1967 and retained it after the Yom Kippur War of 1973, despite Egypt's partial military successes at the outset of that war. UN Security Council Resolution 242 called for Israeli withdrawal from occupied territories in exchange for peace and recognition. In November 1977 Sadat made a dramatic visit to Jerusalem and addressed the Knesset, breaking the diplomatic deadlock but also provoking anger in much of the Arab world.

The negotiations

Carter invited Sadat and Begin to Camp David in September 1978 after direct talks stalled. For thirteen days the leaders negotiated in near-total seclusion; Carter shuttled American draft proposals between the two delegations, which at times refused to meet face to face. The talks repeatedly came close to collapse, and Carter threatened to end the summit and publicly assign blame before the parties agreed on the final texts.

The frameworks

The first framework, A Framework for Peace in the Middle East, dealt with the West Bank and Gaza: it proposed a five-year transitional period of Palestinian autonomy, the election of a self-governing authority, and negotiations on the final status of the territories, inviting Jordan and Palestinian representatives to participate. This part of the accords was never implemented as envisaged.

The second framework, A Framework for the Conclusion of a Peace Treaty between Egypt and Israel, provided for a peace treaty within three months, full Israeli withdrawal from the Sinai, the stationing of UN forces, free passage through the Suez Canal and the Straits of Tiran, and the establishment of normal diplomatic and economic relations.

Aftermath

The Egypt–Israel peace treaty was signed on 26 March 1979. Israel completed its withdrawal from Sinai in April 1982. Egypt was expelled from the Arab League, which moved its headquarters from Cairo to Tunis, and Sadat was widely denounced in the Arab and Islamic worlds; he was assassinated by Islamist extremists in October 1981. Nonetheless, the peace between Egypt and Israel has endured, and the accords remain a landmark of twentieth-century diplomacy.
