Claremont is a city in eastern Los Angeles County, California, United States, located about 30 miles (48 km) east of downtown Los Angeles at the foothills of the San Gabriel Mountains. It is best known as the home of the Claremont Colleges, a consortium of seven institutions of higher education, and for its tree-lined streets and historic village centre, which have earned it the nickname "City of Trees and PhDs".

History

The area was originally inhabited by the Tongva people. Claremont was founded in 1887 during the Southern California land boom, taking its name from Claremont, New Hampshire. When the boom collapsed, the town's future was secured by the founding of Pomona College in 1887, which moved to Claremont in 1889. Citrus groves dominated the local economy for decades, and the city incorporated in 1907. Additional colleges followed through the 20th century, forming the consortium modelled in part on the colleges of Oxford and Cambridge.

Geography and character

Claremont covers about 13.5 square miles on an alluvial fan sloping down from the mountains. Much of the city is residential, with neighbourhoods of Craftsman, Victorian and ranch houses shaded by a large urban forest; the city has repeatedly been recognised as a Tree City USA. The Claremont Hills Wilderness Park and Rancho Santa Ana Botanic Garden (now California Botanic Garden), devoted to native plants, lie at the northern edge of the city. The downtown "Village" around Harvard Avenue and the Claremont Colleges contains shops, restaurants, the Claremont Metrolink station and a weekly farmers' market.

Education

The Claremont Colleges comprise Pomona College, Claremont Graduate University, Scripps College, Claremont McKenna College, Harvey Mudd College, Pitzer College and Keck Graduate Institute, together enrolling roughly 8,500 students. Public schools are operated by the Claremont Unified School District. See also Pomona College.
