A B-52H Stratofortress of the United States Air Force.

The Boeing B-52 Stratofortress is an American long-range, subsonic, jet-powered strategic bomber. The B-52 was designed and built by Boeing, which has continued to provide support and upgrades. It has been operated by the United States Air Force (USAF) since the 1950s, and NASA has also flown the type as a research and launch aircraft. The bomber can carry up to 70,000 pounds (32,000 kg) of weapons and has a typical combat range of around 8,800 miles (14,200 km) without refuelling.

History and service

Beginning with the successful contract bid in June 1946, the B-52 design evolved from a straight-wing aircraft powered by six turboprop engines to the final prototype YB-52 with eight jet engines and swept wings. The B-52H, the only variant still in service, entered use in 1961. B-52s flew the massive Arc Light and Linebacker raids of the Vietnam War, launched the majority of air-launched cruise missiles in the 1991 Gulf War, and have seen action in Kosovo, Afghanistan, Iraq, and Syria.

Longevity

Superior performance at high subsonic speeds and relatively low operating costs have kept the B-52 in service despite the advent of the Mach 2 B-58 Hustler, the Mach 3 B-70 Valkyrie, the supersonic B-1 Lancer, and the stealth B-2 Spirit. A programme to fit new Rolls-Royce F130 engines and modern radar is expected to keep the type — redesignated B-52J — flying into the 2050s, roughly a century after its first flight. Its crews affectionately call it the "BUFF" (Big Ugly Fat Fellow).
