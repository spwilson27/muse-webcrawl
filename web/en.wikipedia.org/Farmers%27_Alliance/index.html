Farmers' Alliance

Founded1875 (Northern Alliance); 1877 (Southern Alliance origins)
TypeAgrarian movement, cooperative organisation
SuccessorPeople's Party (Populists)
RegionUnited States (South, Midwest, Plains)

The Farmers' Alliance was an organised agrarian economic movement among American farmers that developed and flourished in the 1880s. It included several parallel but independent political organisations — the National Farmers' Alliance (Northern Alliance), the National Farmers' Alliance and Industrial Union (Southern Alliance), and the Colored Farmers' National Alliance and Cooperative Union.

Origins and programme

The Alliances arose from farmers' grievances over falling crop prices, high railroad freight rates, expensive credit, and deflation. They organised cooperatives — stores, cotton gins, exchanges — and promoted reforms including the subtreasury plan, under which farmers could store crops in government warehouses and borrow against them, as well as railroad regulation, a graduated income tax, and the free coinage of silver.

Political legacy

Frustrated with the major parties, Alliance members helped found the People's Party (the Populists) in 1891–92, whose Omaha Platform of 1892 drew heavily on Alliance demands. Many Alliance proposals were later enacted in the Progressive Era and the New Deal.
