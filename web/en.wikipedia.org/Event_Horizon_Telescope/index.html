The Event Horizon Telescope (EHT) is a large telescope array consisting of a global network of radio telescopes. The EHT project combines data from several very-long-baseline interferometry (VLBI) stations around Earth to form a virtual telescope with an effective aperture of the diameter of the Earth, giving angular resolution sufficient to observe the surroundings of a supermassive black hole's event horizon.

Results

On 10 April 2019, the collaboration released the first direct image of a black hole: the shadow of the supermassive black hole at the centre of the galaxy Messier 87, surrounded by an asymmetric ring of emission about 42 microarcseconds across. On 12 May 2022, the EHT released the first image of Sagittarius A*, the supermassive black hole at the centre of the Milky Way.

Method

Observing at 1.3 mm wavelength, participating observatories — including ALMA, APEX, IRAM, the James Clerk Maxwell Telescope, the Large Millimeter Telescope, the Submillimeter Array, the South Pole Telescope and NOEMA — record signals with atomic-clock timestamps; the petabytes of data are shipped on hard drives to central correlators for processing.
