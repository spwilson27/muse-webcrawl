Jamón ibérico ("Iberian ham") is a variety of cured leg of pork produced in Spain and Portugal from the Iberian pig, a dark-skinned native breed. It is considered one of the world's finest hams, prized for its rich, nutty flavour and marbled, melt-in-the-mouth fat. The Portuguese equivalent is known as presunto ibérico.

Production

After slaughter, the hind legs are salted, washed, and slowly air-dried and matured in cellars (bodegas) for between one and four years, sometimes longer. The most prestigious hams come from free-ranging pigs that spend their final months in the dehesa oak parkland of western Spain, fattening on fallen acorns (bellotas). The acorn diet gives the fat a high proportion of oleic acid and a characteristic aroma, a quality reflected in the official classification system.

Classification

Spanish regulations grade the ham by breed purity and feeding: de bellota 100% ibérico (purebred, acorn-fed) carries a black label and commands the highest prices; below it are bellota hams from crossbred pigs, then de cebo de campo (partly free-range, grain-fed) and de cebo (farm-raised, grain-fed). Four protected designations of origin—Guijuelo, Dehesa de Extremadura, Jabugo, and Los Pedroches—guard the most famous producing regions.

Serving

The ham is carved by hand into thin slices with a long, flexible knife and served at room temperature, traditionally with bread and wine. A skilled cutter (cortador) is a recognised professional figure in Spanish gastronomy.
