Dentures

Dentures (also known as false teeth) are prosthetic devices constructed to replace missing teeth, supported by the surrounding soft and hard tissues of the oral cavity. Conventional dentures are removable, although many designs can be bonded or held in place by dental implants.

Types

Complete dentures replace all the teeth in an arch, while partial dentures replace some teeth and are often held by clasps attached to remaining natural teeth. Immediate dentures are inserted on the same day teeth are removed, and overdentures rest on a small number of retained roots or implants.

Materials

Modern dentures are usually made of acrylic resin, sometimes in combination with metal frameworks. Earlier dentures were made from materials including carved wood, ivory, porcelain and vulcanite rubber.

History

Wooden full dentures were invented in Japan in the 16th century. In the 18th and 19th centuries, dentures were commonly made of porcelain or carved ivory; George Washington famously wore dentures made from a combination of materials, though contrary to legend not from wood.

Care

Dentures should be cleaned daily and removed at night to rest the oral tissues. Poorly fitting dentures can cause sore spots, bone resorption and difficulty speaking or eating.
