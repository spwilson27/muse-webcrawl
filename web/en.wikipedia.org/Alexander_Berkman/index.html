Alexander Berkman

Alexander Berkman (November 21, 1870 – June 28, 1936) was a Russian-American anarchist and author. He was a leading member of the anarchist movement in the early 20th century, famous for his political activism and writing. Berkman was the one-time lover and lifelong close friend of anarchist Emma Goldman, another prominent figure in the movement.

Early life and the Homestead attentat

Born Ovsei Osipovich Berkkan in Vilnius, in the Russian Empire, Berkman emigrated to the United States in 1888. In 1892, during the Homestead Strike in Pennsylvania, he attempted to assassinate industrialist Henry Clay Frick, chairman of the Carnegie Steel Company, whom he blamed for the violent suppression of striking workers. Berkman shot and stabbed Frick, who survived; Berkman was convicted of attempted murder and served 14 years in prison, an experience recounted in his Prison Memoirs of an Anarchist (1912).

Activism and deportation

After his release, Berkman edited Emma Goldman's journal Mother Earth and his own paper, The Blast. In 1917 he and Goldman were convicted of conspiring to obstruct the draft and were deported to Russia in 1919 aboard the USAT Buford, the "Soviet Ark." Initially sympathetic to the Bolsheviks, he became disillusioned and documented his criticism in The Bolshevik Myth (1925).

Later years

Berkman spent his final years in France in poverty and ill health, editing and translating anarchist works. His book Now and After: The ABC of Communist Anarchism (1929) is regarded as a classic introduction to anarchist thought. Suffering from a painful prostate illness, he shot himself in Nice on June 28, 1936, dying shortly before the outbreak of the Spanish Civil War.
