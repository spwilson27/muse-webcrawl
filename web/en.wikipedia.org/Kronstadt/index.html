Kronstadt

Kronstadt (Russian: Кронштадт) is a Russian port city in Kronshtadtsky District of Saint Petersburg, located on Kotlin Island at the head of the Gulf of Finland, about 30 km west of Saint Petersburg.

History

Founded by Peter the Great in 1704 as a fortress and naval base defending the new capital, Kronstadt was for centuries the main base of Russia's Baltic Fleet, protected by a chain of forts. It was the site of the Kronstadt rebellion of March 1921, when sailors who had supported the Bolsheviks in 1917 rose against War Communism and were crushed by the Red Army.

During the Second World War the base resisted siege conditions. Landmarks include the Naval Cathedral of Saint Nicholas (1913), the Italian Palace and the Kotlin Island forts. A causeway and bridge complex (the Saint Petersburg Dam) now links the island to the mainland.
