Rallying is a form of motorsport in which production-based cars compete on closed public or private roads called special stages, linked by untimed road sections. Unlike circuit racing, rally crews race against the clock one at a time, and each car is crewed by a driver and a co-driver, who reads detailed pacenotes describing the road ahead. The winner is the crew with the lowest total stage time.

Format and history

Early motoring rallies such as the Monte Carlo Rally (founded 1911) were endurance and regularity trials; the sport evolved after the Second World War into the speed-based special-stage format pioneered in Scandinavia and Britain. The FIA launched the World Rally Championship (WRC) in 1973. The spectacular turbocharged four-wheel-drive Group B cars of 1982–1986 drew huge crowds but were banned after fatal accidents, giving way to Group A, the World Rally Car formula (1997), and the hybrid Rally1 cars introduced in 2022.

Events and cars

Classic WRC events include Monte Carlo, the Acropolis Rally of Greece, Rally Finland — famous for its high-speed jumps — and the Safari Rally in Kenya. Rally cars are heavily strengthened road cars with roll cages, long-travel suspension and, in top classes, around 500 horsepower in hybrid trim. Related disciplines include rally raid events such as the Dakar Rally and historic (classic) rallying, while national championships run under FIA regional rules worldwide.
