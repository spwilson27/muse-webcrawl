Centimorgan
Symbol: cM
Unit: Genetic linkage distance
Named for: Thomas Hunt Morgan

A centimorgan (cM) is a unit for measuring genetic linkage. Two loci are one centimorgan apart if recombination between them occurs in about 1% of meioses on average. It is named after geneticist Thomas Hunt Morgan and was developed through the work of Alfred Sturtevant and J.B.S. Haldane.

Relation to physical distance

In humans one centimorgan corresponds on average to roughly one million base pairs, but the ratio varies widely across the genome, between sexes, and between species because recombination is unevenly distributed in hotspots and cold regions.

Uses

Centimorgans are used to build linkage maps, locate disease genes, and in consumer genealogy to estimate relatedness from shared DNA segments. See Genetic linkage and Recombination.
