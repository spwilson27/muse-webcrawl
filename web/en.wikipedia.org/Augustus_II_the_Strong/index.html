Augustus the Strong, Elector of Saxony and King of Poland.

Augustus II the Strong (12 May 1670 – 1 February 1733), known in Saxony as Frederick Augustus I, was Elector of Saxony from 1694 and King of Poland and Grand Duke of Lithuania from 1697 until his death, with an interruption during the Great Northern War. His epithet "the Strong" derives from his reputed physical strength — he is said to have been able to break horseshoes with his bare hands.

King of Poland

On the death of John III Sobieski, Augustus secured election as king of Poland in 1697 by converting to Roman Catholicism and by lavish bribery and military pressure, outmanoeuvring the French candidate, the Prince of Conti. His ambition to recover Livonia led him into alliance with Denmark and Russia against Sweden, opening the Great Northern War (1700–1721). Charles XII of Sweden defeated him, occupied Poland, and forced his abdication by the Treaty of Altranstädt (1706) in favour of Stanisław Leszczyński. After Charles's defeat at Poltava in 1709, Augustus returned to the Polish throne, which he held, with Russian backing, until his death.

Patronage of the arts

Augustus transformed Dresden into one of the great baroque capitals of Europe. He founded the famous collections of the Green Vault, assembled an extraordinary treasury of porcelain — his passion led to the founding of the Meissen porcelain manufactory in 1710, the first European producer of hard-paste porcelain — and commissioned the Zwinger palace. Warsaw, too, was embellished under his reign.
