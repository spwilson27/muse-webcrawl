Podocin

GeneNPHS2 (chromosome 1q25–q31)
Protein familyStomatin family
Expressed inPodocytes of the kidney glomerulus
Podocin localises to the slit diaphragm between podocyte foot processes.

Podocin is a protein that in humans is encoded by the NPHS2 gene. It is a member of the stomatin family of lipid-raft-associated proteins and is expressed almost exclusively in the podocytes of the kidney glomerulus, where it forms a key component of the slit diaphragm — the junction between podocyte foot processes that forms part of the glomerular filtration barrier preventing protein from leaking into the urine.

Function

Podocin has a hairpin-like structure with both its N- and C-termini in the cytoplasm. It accumulates in cholesterol-rich lipid rafts at the insertion site of the slit diaphragm, where it acts as a scaffold, recruiting and interacting with other slit-diaphragm proteins including nephrin (encoded by NPHS1) and CD2AP, and enhancing nephrin signalling. Podocin also interacts with the channel protein TRPC6. Through these interactions it helps maintain the architecture and selective permeability of the filtration barrier.

Clinical significance

Mutations in NPHS2 are the commonest cause of autosomal recessive steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome, typically presenting in infancy or early childhood and frequently progressing to end-stage kidney disease. More than a hundred pathogenic variants have been described; the common European variant R138Q is associated with early onset, while the R229Q polymorphism can cause later-onset disease when inherited together with certain other mutations. Because the condition is steroid-resistant, affected children are managed with other immunosuppressive agents, renin–angiotensin blockade, and ultimately dialysis or kidney transplantation; recurrence after transplantation is uncommon, which distinguishes NPHS2 disease from immune-mediated forms of nephrotic syndrome.

Podocin knockout mice develop severe proteinuria and glomerulosclerosis and die of renal failure within weeks, underlining the protein's essential role in glomerular filtration.
