Yves Henri Donat Mathieu-Saint-Laurent (1 August 1936 – 1 June 2008), better known as Yves Saint Laurent, was a French fashion designer who founded his eponymous luxury fashion house in 1961. He is regarded as one of the greatest designers of the twentieth century, credited with modernising women's fashion.

Career

Saint Laurent joined the House of Dior as a teenager and became its head designer at just 21 after Christian Dior's death. With his partner Pierre Bergé he launched his own house in 1961, introducing landmark designs including the Mondrian dress (1965), Le Smoking tuxedo suit for women (1966), ready-to-wear line Rive Gauche, safari jackets, and designs inspired by art and non-Western cultures.

Legacy

He was the first living fashion designer exhibited at the Metropolitan Museum of Art (1983). He retired from haute couture in 2002; the brand continues under the name Saint Laurent. Museums dedicated to his work opened in Paris and Marrakech in 2017.
