Threaded fastener

A threaded fastener is a fastener that uses a helical screw thread to hold objects together or to transmit motion or force. Common threaded fasteners include screws, bolts, nuts, and threaded rods (studs). The thread converts rotational torque into linear clamping force, allowing joints to be assembled and disassembled repeatedly without damage.

Types

A bolt is designed to be used with a nut, while a screw typically forms its own mating thread in the material it enters or engages a pre-tapped hole. Machine screws, wood screws, self-tapping screws, set screws, and cap screws are common varieties. Threads are classified by form (such as ISO metric or Unified Thread Standard), pitch, diameter, and handedness.

Mechanics

When tightened, a threaded fastener elongates slightly and clamps the joined parts together. The reliability of the joint depends on achieving proper preload, and materials, lubrication, and tightening method all affect the relationship between applied torque and clamp load. Thread-locking methods include lock nuts, thread-locking fluid, and lock washers.
