Robert Bunsen

Robert Wilhelm Eberhard Bunsen (30 March 1811 – 16 August 1899) was a German chemist. He investigated emission spectra of heated elements and discovered caesium (1860) and rubidium (1861) with Gustav Kirchhoff.

Robert Bunsen

Born: 31 March 1811 (baptised), Göttingen
Died: 16 August 1899, Heidelberg
Known for: Bunsen burner, spectroscopy, caesium, rubidium

Career

Bunsen taught at Kassel, Marburg, Breslau and Heidelberg, where he spent most of his career. With his laboratory assistant Peter Desaga he perfected the Bunsen burner (c. 1855), a gas burner producing a hot, non-luminous flame, enabling flame tests and spectral analysis.

Other work

He studied cacodyl compounds (losing sight in one eye to an explosion), developed the Bunsen cell battery, a grease-spot photometer, and a calorimeter, and pioneered photochemistry with Henry Roscoe. He received the Copley Medal in 1877.
