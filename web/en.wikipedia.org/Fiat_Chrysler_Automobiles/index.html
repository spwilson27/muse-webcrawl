Fiat Chrysler Automobiles N.V. (FCA) was an Italian-American multinational corporation primarily known as a manufacturer of automobiles, commercial vehicles and automotive components. It was formed in 2014 by the merger of the Italian automaker Fiat S.p.A. with the American Chrysler Group, following Fiat's acquisition of Chrysler out of bankruptcy in 2009. FCA was the world's seventh-largest automaker by sales. In January 2021 it merged with the French PSA Group to form Stellantis, one of the world's largest carmakers.

History

Fiat first took a 20% stake in Chrysler in 2009 as part of the US government-brokered rescue of the American company after its Chapter 11 bankruptcy. Under chief executive Sergio Marchionne, who led both companies, Fiat progressively increased its holding, reaching full ownership in January 2014 after buying out the UAW retiree healthcare trust's share. In October 2014 the combined Fiat Chrysler Automobiles was incorporated in the Netherlands, listed in New York and Milan, and received tax residence in the United Kingdom.

Marchionne's sudden death in July 2018 brought Mike Manley to the chief executive role. FCA spun off Ferrari in 2016 and its components business Magneti Marelli in 2018. After a failed merger approach to Renault in 2019, FCA agreed in December 2019 to merge with PSA Group; the merger was completed on 16 January 2021, creating Stellantis, with the FCA brands continuing within the new group.

Operations and brands

FCA operated through regional groups in North America (NAFTA), Latin America, Asia-Pacific and Europe/Middle East/Africa (EMEA), plus the luxury brand Maserati and components operations. Its North American business, driven by Jeep and Ram trucks and SUVs, generated most of the group's profit. Major brands included Fiat, Fiat Professional, Abarth, Alfa Romeo, Lancia, Chrysler, Dodge, Jeep, Ram and Maserati. The company employed around 200,000 people and sold roughly 4.4–4.8 million vehicles annually in its final years.

Controversies

FCA faced regulatory action over diesel emissions: in 2019 it agreed to pay more than $800 million in the United States to settle claims that certain Ram and Jeep diesel vehicles used defeat-device software. It also paid penalties related to fuel-economy reporting and safety recalls.
