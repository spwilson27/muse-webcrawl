Sudbury Neutrino Observatory

The Sudbury Neutrino Observatory (SNO) was a neutrino observatory located about 2 km underground in the Creighton Mine near Sudbury, Ontario, Canada. Its detector used 1,000 tonnes of heavy water (deuterium oxide) contained in a 12-metre-diameter acrylic vessel, surrounded by ordinary water and viewed by some 9,600 photomultiplier tubes. SNO observed neutrinos produced by fusion reactions in the Sun.

The solar neutrino problem and its solution

Earlier experiments, such as the Homestake experiment of Raymond Davis Jr., had detected only about a third of the electron neutrinos predicted to arrive from the Sun — the solar neutrino problem. Because neutrinos could interact with deuterium in ways sensitive to all three flavours, SNO showed in 2001–2002 that the total flux of solar neutrinos matched solar models, while only a fraction arrived as electron neutrinos. The missing neutrinos had changed flavour en route — direct evidence of neutrino oscillation and hence of non-zero neutrino mass.

Recognition and legacy

SNO's director, Arthur B. McDonald, shared the 2015 Nobel Prize in Physics with Takaaki Kajita. SNO operated from 1999 to 2006 (including a phase with dissolved salt to enhance neutron detection). The underground laboratory has since expanded into SNOLAB, hosting experiments including the SNO+ detector, which reuses the facility with liquid scintillator.
