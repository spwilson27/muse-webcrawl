Classic cycle races

One-day classic races
SportRoad cycling
SeasonPrimarily spring (March–April) and autumn

The classic cycle races are the most prestigious one-day professional road cycling races of the international calendar, typically characterised by long histories, difficult routes, and distinctive challenges such as cobblestones, steep hills, or great distance. Most are held in western Europe, especially Belgium, France, Italy and the Netherlands, and most date from the late 19th or early 20th century.

The Monuments

Five classics stand above the rest and are known as the Monuments:

Milan–San RemoItaly1907La Primavera ("The Spring classic")

Only three riders — Eddy Merckx, Roger De Vlaeminck and Rik Van Looy — have won all five Monuments. The spring Monuments form the climax of the "spring classics" season: Milan–San Remo is the longest at nearly 300 km, Flanders and Roubaix feature cobbled sectors, and Liège–Bastogne–Liège is a hilly Ardennes race.

Other major classics

Other important one-day races include the cobbled classics of Flanders (Omloop Het Nieuwsblad, E3 Saxo Bank Classic, Gent–Wevelgem, Dwars door Vlaanderen), the Ardennes classics (Amstel Gold Race, La Flèche Wallonne), the Italian Strade Bianche with its white gravel roads, Paris–Tours, the Clásica de San Sebastián, the Bretagne Classic, and the Canadian Grand Prix Cycliste de Québec and Montréal. Since the creation of the UCI WorldTour, the calendar of top-level one-day races has broadened, and equivalent women's classics have grown in prominence.

Characteristics

Classics reward a particular type of rider — the classics specialist or puncheur — combining endurance over 200–300 km, bike-handling on cobbles or gravel, repeated explosive efforts on short climbs, and tactical sense in races where team control is difficult. Weather, especially wind and rain in the northern spring races, is often decisive.
