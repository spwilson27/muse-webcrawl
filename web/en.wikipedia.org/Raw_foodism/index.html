Raw foodism

Raw foodism, or rawism, is the dietary practice of eating food that is uncooked and usually unprocessed, typically plant-based. Adherents generally hold that foods heated above about 40–48 °C (104–118 °F) lose enzymes and nutritional value, and many avoid anything cooked entirely.

History

Modern raw foodism traces to the Swiss physician Maximilian Bircher-Benner, who promoted raw fruits and vegetables around 1900, and was developed by writers such as Herbert Shelton and, from the 1960s, Ann Wigmore. Variants include raw veganism (the most common), raw vegetarianism, and raw diets that include unpasteurised dairy, raw eggs, or even raw meat and fish.

Health claims and risks

Supporters claim benefits for weight and energy, and raw diets are indeed rich in fibre and some heat-sensitive vitamins. However, nutrition authorities note risks including vitamin B12 and vitamin D deficiency, low bone mass, foodborne illness from uncooked animal products, and reduced bioavailability of some nutrients such as lycopene and beta-carotene that cooking improves. Long-term adherence can be socially and practically demanding.
