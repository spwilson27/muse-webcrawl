Narcotics Anonymous

Narcotics Anonymous (NA) is a nonprofit fellowship of people recovering from drug addiction, founded in 1953 in Los Angeles, California. It follows a twelve-step program modelled on Alcoholics Anonymous, adapted so that the focus is on addiction as a whole rather than on any particular substance.

History

NA grew out of the Alcoholics Anonymous tradition. Early groups in the late 1940s and early 1950s used the AA steps under names such as "Addicts Anonymous". Narcotics Anonymous itself traces its founding to a July 1953 meeting in Sun Valley, California, led by Jimmy Kinnon, who is regarded as the organisation's founder. The fellowship's Basic Text, first published in 1983, remains its central literature.

Program

Membership requires only a desire to stop using drugs. Meetings are held worldwide and are free; members work the Twelve Steps with the guidance of a sponsor, attend meetings regularly, and practice the Twelve Traditions, which govern group autonomy and anonymity. NA describes itself as a spiritual, not religious, program.

Reach

By the 2020s NA reported tens of thousands of weekly meetings in more than 140 countries, with literature translated into dozens of languages.
