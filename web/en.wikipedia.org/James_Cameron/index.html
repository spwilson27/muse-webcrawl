James Francis Cameron (born August 16, 1954) is a Canadian filmmaker. A major figure in the post-New Hollywood era, he is known for pioneering advances in special effects and for directing some of the highest-grossing films of all time, including Titanic (1997) and Avatar (2009). Three of his films are among the four highest-grossing of all time, and he is credited with popularising 3D and high-frame-rate filmmaking as well as innovations in underwater filming and digital characters.

James Cameron

Early life

Cameron was born in Kapuskasing, Ontario, and raised in Chippawa (now part of Niagara Falls). His family moved to California in 1971, where he studied physics before dropping out, working as a machinist and truck driver while teaching himself film technique from library books. After seeing Star Wars in 1977 he committed himself to filmmaking, beginning with a short film and then visual-effects work for Roger Corman's New World Pictures.

Career breakthrough

His feature directorial debut was Piranha II: The Spawning (1982), but his breakthrough came with The Terminator (1984), written and directed for a modest budget, which launched the franchise and Arnold Schwarzenegger's stardom. Aliens (1986), The Abyss (1989) and Terminator 2: Judgment Day (1991) followed, each pushing visual effects forward — notably the liquid-metal T-1000 created with early CGI.

Titanic and Avatar

Titanic (1997), a romantic drama set aboard the doomed liner, became the first film to gross over $1 billion and won eleven Academy Awards, including Best Picture and Best Director. Cameron returned in 2009 with Avatar, a science-fiction epic built around performance-capture technology and stereoscopic 3D; it surpassed Titanic as the highest-grossing film of all time, a position it has largely retained since. Sequels followed, beginning with Avatar: The Way of Water (2022), itself one of the highest-grossing films ever released.

Deep-sea exploration

A passionate ocean explorer, Cameron has made dozens of submersible dives, produced documentaries about the wrecks of the Titanic and the Bismarck, and in 2012 piloted the Deepsea Challenger submersible on a solo dive to the bottom of the Mariana Trench's Challenger Deep, the deepest known point in the oceans.
