Janus kinase 3 (JAK3) is a tyrosine kinase encoded by the JAK3 gene in humans. Unlike the other Janus kinases, which are widely expressed, JAK3 is expressed predominantly in haematopoietic cells. It associates exclusively with the common gamma chain (γc) shared by the receptors for interleukins IL-2, IL-4, IL-7, IL-9, IL-15 and IL-21, and signals together with JAK1 through the JAK-STAT pathway.

Function

JAK3 is indispensable for lymphocyte development and function. On cytokine binding, JAK3 and JAK1 phosphorylate one another and the receptor, recruiting and activating STAT proteins — particularly STAT5 — which drive the proliferation and survival of T cells, B cells and natural killer cells.

Clinical significance

Inactivating mutations in JAK3 cause an autosomal recessive form of severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID) characterised by a lack of T cells and NK cells with non-functional B cells (T− B+ NK− SCID) — the same phenotype produced by mutations in the gamma chain itself, which cause X-linked SCID. Conversely, activating JAK3 mutations occur in some leukaemias and lymphomas. The JAK inhibitor tofacitinib, which blocks JAK3 and JAK1, was developed in part on the rationale that inhibiting JAK3 would suppress immunity relatively selectively.
