Jewish exodus from the Muslim world

The Jewish exodus from the Muslim world was the departure, flight, expulsion, evacuation and migration of about 900,000 Jews from Muslim-majority countries in North Africa, West Asia, and South Asia, mainly between the 1940s and the early 1970s.

Driven by the Arab–Israeli conflict, persecution, expulsion orders, and Zionist immigration, ancient communities in Iraq, Egypt, Yemen, Libya, Syria, Morocco, Algeria, and elsewhere were largely emptied. Most migrants went to Israel, with many also settling in France and the Americas. Their descendants form a large share of Israel's population.
