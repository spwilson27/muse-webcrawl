Genre fiction

Genre fiction, also widely known as popular fiction, is a term used in the book-trade for fictional works written with the intent of fitting into a specific literary genre, in order to appeal to readers and fans already familiar with that genre. Major genres include crime and mystery, fantasy, romance, science fiction, horror, thriller and Westerns. Genre fiction is often contrasted with literary fiction, though the boundary is contested and many authors move between the two.

Characteristics

Genre works tend to follow recognisable conventions of plot, setting and character — the detective's investigation, the quest structure of epic fantasy, the "happily ever after" required of category romance — and are marketed, shelved and reviewed within their genre category. The publishing industry organises imprints, bookshops and awards (for example the Hugo Award for science fiction and fantasy, the Edgar Awards for mystery) around these divisions.

History and critical status

Popular genres grew out of nineteenth-century sensational fiction, dime novels and the pulp magazines of the early twentieth century, which launched science fiction, hard-boiled crime and sword-and-sorcery as commercial categories. Although genre fiction historically received less academic attention than literary fiction, it dominates bestseller lists, and critics increasingly treat the genre/literary distinction as a marketing category rather than a judgement of quality.
