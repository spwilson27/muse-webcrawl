The Historia von D. Johann Fausten ("History of Dr. Johann Fausten") is an anonymous German chapbook published by Johann Spies in Frankfurt am Main in 1587. It is the first published version of the Faust legend — the story of the scholar Johann Georg Faust who sells his soul to the Devil — and the direct source of Christopher Marlowe's play The Tragical History of Doctor Faustus and, ultimately, of Goethe's Faust.

Contents

The book recounts how Fausten, a scholar of theology and medicine at Wittenberg, turned to necromancy and summoned the spirit Mephostophiles, with whom he signed a pact in his own blood: twenty-four years of service and fulfilment of his desires in exchange for his soul. The narrative strings together episodes of Fausten's travels, conjuring tricks at courts, debates with the devil, and growing despair, ending with the expiry of the term, his gruesome death, and a warning to readers against his example.

Influence

The Historia was an immediate bestseller, rapidly reprinted and translated — a Danish version appeared in 1588, and the English translation (the "English Faust Book", 1592) gave Marlowe his source. Framed as a Lutheran cautionary tale, it fixed the essential outlines of the Faust story that later centuries of literature, drama and opera would adapt.
