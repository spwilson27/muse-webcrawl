Cosmetology

Cosmetology (from Greek kosmētikos, "beautifying", and -logy) is the study and application of beauty treatment. Branches of speciality include hair styling, skin care, cosmetics, manicures/pedicures, non-permanent hair removal such as waxing and sugaring, and permanent hair removal processes such as electrology and intense pulsed light (IPL).

History

Cosmetic practices date back to ancient Egypt and other early civilisations, where kohl, henna, and oils were used for adornment and ritual. The modern profession developed in the late 19th and early 20th centuries with figures such as Martha Matilda Harper, who pioneered modern hair salons and franchising, and Madam C. J. Walker, who built a major cosmetics business and became one of America's first self-made female millionaires. Beauty schools and professional licensing spread in the 20th century.

Careers and training

Cosmetologists are trained and licensed in hair, skin, and nail services. Specialisations include:

  • Hairstylists — cutting, colouring, chemical texture services, and styling;
  • Estheticians — skin care, facials, and hair removal;
  • Nail technicians — manicures, pedicures, and nail enhancements;
  • Electrologists — permanent hair removal;
  • Makeup artists — cosmetic application for everyday, bridal, theatrical, and film work.

In the United States, cosmetologists must complete a state-accredited programme (commonly 1,000–1,600 hours, varying by state) and pass licensing examinations administered by state boards of cosmetology. Training includes sanitation and infection control, anatomy and physiology of hair, skin and nails, chemistry of products, and business skills.

Industry

Cosmetologists work in salons and spas, on film and television sets, in retail cosmetics, and as independent contractors renting booths or chairs. The profession is regulated in most jurisdictions to protect public health and safety, and continuing education keeps practitioners current with products and techniques.
