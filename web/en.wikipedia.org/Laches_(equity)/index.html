Laches (equity)

Laches is an equitable defence in common law jurisdictions: a party who unreasonably delays asserting a right or claim, in a way that prejudices the opposing party, may be barred from relief — summarised in the maxims "equity aids the vigilant, not those who slumber on their rights" and "delay defeats equity".

Unlike a statute of limitations, which fixes a definite period, laches is flexible and fact-specific. A court typically asks whether the delay was unreasonable and whether it caused prejudice, such as lost evidence, faded memories, or changed circumstances. The doctrine originated in the English Court of Chancery and remains important in areas such as intellectual property, where it has been limited by modern case law in patent and copyright contexts in the United States.
