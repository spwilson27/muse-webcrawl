Vermont

Vermont
Capital: Montpelier
Largest city: Burlington
Admitted to the Union: March 4, 1791 (14th)
Population: approx. 648,000
Nickname: The Green Mountain State

Vermont is a state in the New England region of the northeastern United States. It borders Massachusetts to the south, New Hampshire to the east, New York to the west, and the Canadian province of Quebec to the north. Vermont is the second-least-populous U.S. state and the sixth-smallest by area; its capital, Montpelier, is the least populous state capital in the country.

History

The territory was long inhabited by Indigenous peoples, including the Abenaki. Claimed by both New Hampshire and New York in the colonial era, the settlers of the New Hampshire Grants declared an independent Vermont Republic in 1777 — one of the first jurisdictions to abolish adult slavery in its constitution. Ethan Allen and the Green Mountain Boys captured Fort Ticonderoga in 1775. Vermont entered the Union in 1791 as the 14th state, the first admitted after the original thirteen.

Geography and economy

The Green Mountains run the length of the state, with Mount Mansfield its highest peak. The Connecticut River forms the eastern border, and Lake Champlain much of the western. Forests cover about three-quarters of the state, famed for autumn foliage. The economy rests on tourism (including skiing), dairy farming, and specialty foods; Vermont is the leading U.S. producer of maple syrup. It is also known for granite and marble quarrying, famously at Barre and Proctor.

Politics and culture

Vermont has a distinctive political culture of town meetings and, in recent decades, strongly progressive politics; it was the first state to introduce civil unions for same-sex couples (2000) and the first to enact same-sex marriage by legislation (2009). Senator Bernie Sanders of Burlington is among its best-known politicians.
