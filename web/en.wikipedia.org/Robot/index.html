Robot

A robot is a machine—especially one programmable by a computer—capable of carrying out a complex series of actions automatically. A robot can be guided by an external control device, or the control may be embedded within. Robots may be constructed to evoke human form, but most robots are task-performing machines designed with an emphasis on stark functionality, rather than expressive aesthetics.

Robots can be autonomous or semi-autonomous and range from humanoids such as Honda's Advanced Step in Innovative Mobility (ASIMO) and Boston Dynamics' Atlas, to industrial robots, medical operating robots, patient assist robots, dog therapy robots, collectively programmed swarm robots, and UAV drones such as General Atomics MQ-1 Predator, and even microscopic nano robots. By mimicking a lifelike appearance or automating movements, a robot may convey a sense of intelligence or thought of its own. Autonomous things are expected to proliferate in the future, with home robotics and the autonomous car as some of the main drivers.

Etymology

The word robot comes from the Czech word robota, meaning "forced labour" or "serf labour". It was first used in the 1920 play R.U.R. (Rossum's Universal Robots) by the Czech writer Karel Čapek and his brother Josef Čapek, who coined the term. The play tells the story of a factory that makes artificial people called roboti, who could be mistaken for humans and can think for themselves. The robots are happy to work for humans at first, but a robot rebellion leads to the extinction of the human race.

History

The concept of artificial humans predates recorded history. In Greek mythology, the god Hephaestus was said to have created mechanical servants, while the Jewish legend of the golem and the Norse clay giants are similar concepts. In ancient China, accounts of automatons date back to the 3rd century BCE. The first digitally operated and programmable robot, the Unimate, was installed in 1961 to lift hot pieces of metal from a die casting machine at an Inland Fisher Guide Plant in the Ewing Township of New Jersey. Its inventor, George Devol, and his partner Joseph Engelberger are considered the founders of modern robotics and founded Unimation, the first robot manufacturing company.

Components and design

Robots are built from several main components. Actuators are the "muscles" of a robot, typically electric motors, hydraulic cylinders, or pneumatic actuators. Sensors allow the robot to perceive its environment, including cameras, touch sensors, and range finders. A control system, often a computer, processes sensor data and directs the actuators. Robots also require a power source, such as batteries or a tethered supply, and a mechanical structure or end effector—the tool mounted at the end of a robotic arm, such as a gripper, welding torch, or drill.

Applications

Robots are used in many fields. Industrial robots dominate manufacturing, performing welding, painting, assembly, pick-and-place, packaging, and inspection. In medicine, robots assist in surgery, rehabilitation, and hospital logistics. Military robots are used for bomb disposal and reconnaissance. Space exploration relies on robotic rovers such as NASA's Perseverance and orbiters. Domestic robots include robotic vacuum cleaners and lawn mowers, while educational robots are used to teach STEM subjects. See also robotics and robot-assisted surgery.
