Parshvanatha (Sanskrit: Pārśvanātha), also known as Parshva, was the 23rd of the 24 Tirthankaras ("ford-makers") of Jainism. Unlike the earlier Tirthankaras, who are generally regarded as legendary, Parshvanatha is widely considered by scholars to have been a historical figure, conventionally dated to the 8th or 7th century BCE — some 250 years before Mahavira, the 24th Tirthankara.

Life

According to Jain tradition, Parshvanatha was born at Varanasi to King Ashvasena and Queen Vama of the Ikshvaku dynasty. At the age of thirty he renounced royal life after witnessing the suffering of a snake burning in a log, became an ascetic, and attained omniscience (kevala jnana) after 84 days of meditation. He preached for seventy years and attained moksha at Sammed Shikhar (Shikharji) in present-day Jharkhand, a site that remains the most important pilgrimage place in Jainism. Iconographically he is sheltered by the multi-hooded serpent king Dharanendra, who protected him from a storm sent by his enemy Kamath during his meditation.

Teachings

Parshvanatha taught the chaturyama dharma, the fourfold restraint: non-violence (ahimsa), truthfulness (satya), non-stealing (asteya) and non-possession (aparigraha). Mahavira later added celibacy (brahmacharya) as a fifth vow. Parshvanatha permitted his monks an upper and lower garment; the later difference over clothing became one of the distinctions between the Digambara and Śvetāmbara traditions. His community was organised into monks, nuns and lay followers, and Jain texts preserve references to his followers still existing in Mahavira's time.
