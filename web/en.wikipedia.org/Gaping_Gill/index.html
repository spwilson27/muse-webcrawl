Gaping Gill (also known as Gaping Ghyll) is a natural cave in North Yorkshire, England. It is one of the unmistakable landmarks on the southern slopes of Ingleborough — a 98-metre (322 ft) deep pothole with the stream Fell Beck flowing into it. After falling through the roof of the main chamber into a pool below, the water resurges at Ingleborough Cave.

The cave system

The main chamber of Gaping Gill is one of the largest known underground chambers in Britain, said to be large enough to contain York Minster or St Paul's Cathedral. The cave is part of an extensive system exceeding 21 km of passages connecting entrances including Bar Pot, Flood Entrance Pot, and Disappointment Pot. The first complete descent of the main shaft was made in 1895 by the French caver Édouard-Alfred Martel, who communicated with the surface by telephone.

Winch meets

Twice a year, local caving clubs rig a winch over the main shaft and lower members of the public into the chamber — a tradition dating back decades. Normally, entry is restricted to experienced cavers.
