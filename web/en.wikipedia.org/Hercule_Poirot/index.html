Hercule Poirot is a fictional Belgian detective created by British writer Agatha Christie. One of the most famous and long-lived characters in detective fiction, he appears in 33 of Christie's novels, a play, and more than 50 short stories published between 1920 and 1975.

Character

Poirot is a retired Belgian police officer who fled to England as a refugee during the First World War and settled in London as a private detective. Short, dapper, with an egg-shaped head, green eyes, patent leather shoes and a magnificent moustache he tends with obsessive care, he solves cases less by physical investigation than by psychology and reasoning — his celebrated "little grey cells". His methods privilege order, method and an understanding of human nature; his characteristic denouements gather all the suspects together before he reveals the murderer. His companions and foils include Captain Arthur Hastings, his Watson-like friend and narrator of the early stories; Chief Inspector Japp of Scotland Yard; and his efficient secretary Miss Lemon.

Notable cases

Among Poirot's most celebrated investigations are The Murder of Roger Ackroyd (1926), famous for its surprise narrator; Murder on the Orient Express (1934); Death on the Nile (1937); The A.B.C. Murders (1936); and Five Little Pigs (1942). His final case, Curtain, was written during the Second World War but published in 1975, the year Poirot "died" — receiving the unique honour of a front-page obituary in The New York Times.

Adaptations

Poirot has been portrayed on screen by many actors, including Austin Trevor, Peter Ustinov, Albert Finney, Kenneth Branagh and, most definitively for many viewers, David Suchet, who played the detective in ITV's Agatha Christie's Poirot (1989–2013), eventually filming every Poirot story Christie wrote.
