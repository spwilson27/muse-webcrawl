Jackson Pollock

Born: 28 January 1912, Cody, Wyoming, U.S.
Died: 11 August 1956, Springs, New York, U.S.
Movement: Abstract expressionism, action painting
Known for: Drip painting

Paul Jackson Pollock (28 January 1912 – 11 August 1956) was an American painter and a major figure of the abstract expressionist movement. He is famous for his "drip technique" of pouring and splashing liquid paint onto canvases laid on the floor, producing the dense, all-over webs of line that made him one of the most celebrated — and contested — artists of the twentieth century.

Early life and training

Pollock was born in Cody, Wyoming, and grew up in Arizona and California. In 1930 he followed his brother Charles to New York and studied at the Art Students League under the regionalist Thomas Hart Benton, whose rhythmic compositions influenced him even as he rejected Benton's subject matter. In the 1930s he worked for the WPA Federal Art Project, encountered Mexican muralism (especially David Alfaro Siqueiros's experimental workshop, where paint-pouring techniques were explored), and underwent Jungian psychotherapy, which encouraged his interest in myth and the unconscious.

Drip paintings

Pollock's patron Peggy Guggenheim gave him a contract and a show at her Art of This Century gallery in 1943, and bought a house for him and his wife, the painter Lee Krasner, at Springs on Long Island. There, in 1947, he developed his mature method: unrolling canvas on the barn floor and walking around it, dripping, flinging and pouring commercial enamel paint from sticks and stiffened brushes, "literally in the painting". Works such as Full Fathom Five (1947), Number 1A, 1948, Autumn Rhythm (Number 30, 1950) and Lavender Mist (1950) exemplify the style. Critic Harold Rosenberg dubbed it "action painting"; Clement Greenberg championed Pollock as the greatest American painter of his generation. A 1949 Life magazine profile — "Is he the greatest living painter in the United States?" — made him a celebrity, and Hans Namuth's 1950 photographs and film of Pollock at work became iconic.

Later years and death

Pollock's chronic alcoholism worsened in the 1950s; his output dwindled and darkened, and he produced little after 1953. On 11 August 1956, driving while intoxicated near his home, he crashed his car, killing himself and one of his two passengers. He was 44. A major retrospective at the Museum of Modern Art followed within months of his death.

Legacy

Pollock's work commands record prices — No. 5, 1948 reportedly sold privately for $140 million in 2006, then the highest price ever paid for a painting — and his drip paintings remain touchstones in debates about skill, intention and chance in art. The Pollock-Krasner House in Springs is a National Historic Landmark.
