Madhyamaka (Sanskrit for "Middle Way" or "Centrism") is a Mahāyāna Buddhist philosophical school founded by the Indian monk-philosopher Nāgārjuna (c. 150–250 CE). Madhyamaka thought had a major influence on the subsequent development of Buddhism, especially in Tibet, China, and Japan, where it remains a central subject of monastic study.

Nāgārjuna, founder of Madhyamaka and author of the Mūlamadhyamakakārikā.

Doctrine

The school's defining claim is that all phenomena are empty (śūnya) of inherent, independent existence (svabhāva): things exist only dependently, arising through causes and conditions and through conceptual designation. Nāgārjuna famously equates emptiness with dependent origination (pratītyasamutpāda) and with the "middle way" between eternalism and nihilism — emptiness is not nothingness, and Madhyamaka itself insists that emptiness, too, is empty. The teaching of two truths organises this: conventionally, things function and can be spoken of; ultimately, they lack any fixed essence.

Development

Later Indian Madhyamaka is traditionally divided into the Svātantrika branch associated with Bhāviveka, who favoured autonomous syllogisms, and the Prāsaṅgika branch associated with Candrakīrti, who restricted argument to reductio ad absurdum and whose interpretation became dominant in Tibetan Buddhism through Tsongkhapa. In East Asia, the school was transmitted as the Three Treatise school (Sanlun).
