Fort Augustus

Fort Augustus (Gaelic: Cille Chuimein) is a village in the Scottish Highlands, in the parish of Boleskine and Abertarff, at the south-west end of Loch Ness. It lies on the A82 road midway between Inverness and Fort William, where the Caledonian Canal passes through on its way between the two towns. The village has a population of roughly 650.

History

The settlement was originally called Kiliwhimin, after Saint Cummein, who built a church there in the 7th century. After the Jacobite rising of 1715, General George Wade built a fort here as part of the government's military road network in the Highlands. The village took its present name from the fort, which was itself named after Prince William Augustus, Duke of Cumberland. The original fort was captured by Jacobites in 1746 shortly before the Battle of Culloden, and a new, larger artillery fortification was constructed on the site. The fort was sold in 1867 to Lord Lovat, whose son later gave it to Benedictine monks; Fort Augustus Abbey operated on the site until 1993, and its buildings were subsequently converted into apartments known as The Highland Club.

Caledonian Canal

The village is best known for the flight of five locks that carries the Caledonian Canal, built by Thomas Telford in the early 19th century, down to Loch Ness. The locks are a popular spectator attraction in summer, when pleasure craft and tour boats pass through. Boat trips on Loch Ness, including visits to nearby Urquhart Castle, depart from the village.

Transport and amenities

A railway branch from Spean Bridge reached Fort Augustus in 1903 but closed in the 1940s. Today the village is served by buses on the Inverness–Fort William corridor and is a stopping point for walkers on the Great Glen Way. Local amenities include a village hall, primary school, shops, pubs and hotels catering to the tourist trade.
