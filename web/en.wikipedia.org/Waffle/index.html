A waffle is a dish made from leavened batter or dough that is cooked between two plates that are patterned to give a characteristic size, shape, and surface impression. There are many variations based on the type of waffle iron and recipe used. Waffles are eaten throughout the world, particularly in Belgium, which has over a dozen regional varieties.

Waffles may be made fresh or simply heated after having been commercially cooked and frozen. Common toppings include powdered sugar, butter, maple syrup, whipped cream, fruit, chocolate spread, and ice cream.

History

Precursors of the waffle date to the ancient Greeks, who cooked flat cakes called obleios between hot metal plates. In the Middle Ages, wafers and waffles (Dutch wafel) were baked over open fires using irons with long handles. Waffle irons with the characteristic grid pattern appeared by the 14th century. Belgian waffles were popularized internationally at the 1964 New York World's Fair, where the "Bel-Gem" waffle, topped with strawberries and whipped cream, became a sensation.

Varieties

  • Brussels waffles are large, light and rectangular, traditionally made with an egg-white-leavened or yeast batter.
  • Liège waffles are smaller, denser and sweeter, made from a brioche-like dough studded with pearl sugar that caramelizes on the iron.
  • American waffles are generally denser and thinner than Belgian varieties, leavened with baking powder, and often served at breakfast.
  • Stroopwafels are thin Dutch waffle cookies filled with a caramel-like syrup.
  • Scandinavian waffles are thin and heart-shaped, served with jam and sour cream or brown cheese.
  • Hong Kong waffles ("egg waffles" or eggettes) are spherical-pocketed street snacks.

Waffle irons

A waffle iron is a kitchen appliance used to cook waffles between two hinged, patterned plates. Modern electric models include temperature controls and non-stick coatings, while stovetop and cast-iron models remain in use.
