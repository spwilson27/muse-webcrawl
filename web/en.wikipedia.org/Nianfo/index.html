Nianfo

Nianfo
Chinese念佛 (niànfó)
Japanese念仏 (nembutsu)
Sanskrit rootBuddhānusmṛti ("recollection of the Buddha")
Principal phraseNāmó Ēmítuófó / Namu Amida Butsu ("Homage to Amitābha Buddha")
TraditionsPure Land Buddhism; also practised in Chan/Zen and other Mahayana schools

Nianfo (Chinese: 念佛; pinyin: niànfó), known in Japanese as nembutsu, is the central practice of Pure Land Buddhism: the mindful recitation of the name of the Buddha Amitābha, most commonly in the phrase "Namo Amituofo" (南無阿彌陀佛) in Chinese or "Namu Amida Butsu" in Japanese. The term derives from the Sanskrit buddhānusmṛti, "recollection of the Buddha".

Practitioners recite the name aloud, silently, or hold it in mind, singly or in groups, sometimes counting repetitions with prayer beads (mala). The practice may aim at meditative concentration (nianfo samadhi) and, in Pure Land teaching, at rebirth in Amitābha's Pure Land of Sukhāvatī, relying on the Buddha's vows — the "other power" — rather than solely on one's own efforts.

Development

Recollection of the Buddha was an early Buddhist meditation practice. In China, masters such as Tanluan, Daochuo, and Shandao systematised Pure Land doctrine around recitation, and the practice spread widely because it was accessible to laypeople. In Japan, Hōnen founded the Jōdo-shū school teaching exclusive nembutsu recitation, and his disciple Shinran founded Jōdo Shinshū, emphasising faith and gratitude. Chinese traditions often practise nianfo together with Chan meditation ("dual practice of Chan and Pure Land").

Forms

Classical classifications list vocal recitation, contemplation of the Buddha's image or marks, and "real-mark" recollection of the Buddha's true nature. Group recitation retreats, funeral recitation to aid the dying, and continuous recitation assemblies are common in East and Southeast Asian temples; the Vietnamese form is niệm Phật and the Korean yeombul.
