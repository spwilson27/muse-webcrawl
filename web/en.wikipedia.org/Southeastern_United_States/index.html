The Southeastern United States, also referred to as the American Southeast or simply the Southeast, is the eastern portion of the Southern United States and the southern portion of the eastern United States. It is not an official Census Bureau region, so its boundaries vary by source, but it generally comprises the states along the Gulf and South Atlantic coasts from Louisiana and Arkansas east to Florida and north to Virginia and Kentucky.

Geography

The region spans the Atlantic Coastal Plain, the Piedmont, and the southern Appalachian Mountains, including the Blue Ridge and Great Smoky Mountains. Major rivers include the Mississippi, the Tennessee, the Savannah and the Chattahoochee. The climate is mostly humid subtropical, with hot, humid summers, mild winters, and exposure to Atlantic and Gulf hurricanes.

History

The Southeast was home to Mississippian-culture peoples and later the Cherokee, Creek, Choctaw, Chickasaw and Seminole nations before European colonisation by Spain, France and England. Its plantation economy, built on enslaved labour producing tobacco, rice, indigo and then cotton, made it the core of the Confederacy during the American Civil War (1861–1865). After Reconstruction and the Jim Crow era, the region transformed in the late twentieth century with air conditioning, interstate highways, and the growth of the Sun Belt economy.

Economy and culture

Major metropolitan areas include Atlanta, Charlotte, Nashville, Orlando, Tampa and Miami. The economy mixes finance, logistics (Hartsfield–Jackson Atlanta International Airport is among the world's busiest), tourism, automotive manufacturing, research (North Carolina's Research Triangle) and agriculture. Culturally the Southeast is a heartland of country, blues, gospel and soul music, of Southern foodways, and of college football.
