Cortisol

Cortisol is a steroid hormone, a glucocorticoid, produced by the zona fasciculata of the adrenal cortex in response to stress and low blood glucose. It is often called the stress hormone. Its pharmaceutical form is hydrocortisone.

Cortisol
Formula: C21H30O5
Produced in: Adrenal cortex
Regulated by: ACTH (HPA axis)
Functions: Metabolism, stress response, anti-inflammatory

Functions

Cortisol increases blood sugar through gluconeogenesis, suppresses the immune system, aids fat, protein and carbohydrate metabolism, and decreases bone formation. Release follows a diurnal pattern, peaking in the early morning, and is driven by ACTH under control of CRH from the hypothalamus, with negative feedback.

Disorders

Chronically high cortisol causes Cushing syndrome, with central obesity, hypertension and muscle weakness. Deficiency occurs in Addison disease and causes fatigue, weight loss and low blood pressure. Cortisol is measured in blood, urine and saliva for diagnosis.
