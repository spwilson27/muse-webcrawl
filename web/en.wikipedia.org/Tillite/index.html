Tillite

Tillite is a sedimentary rock formed by the lithification of glacial till: unsorted material deposited directly by a glacier. It is characterised by clasts of many sizes and rock types, often striated, set in a fine-grained matrix, and by its very poor sorting.

Recognition

Because glaciers deposit debris without the sorting action of water, tillites contrast sharply with most sedimentary rocks. Their presence in a stratigraphic succession is strong evidence of past glaciation at that locality, even where the rocks now lie in warm climates.

Significance

Ancient tillites record major ice ages, including the Neoproterozoic glaciations associated with the "Snowball Earth" hypothesis, where tillites of similar age occur on several continents at low palaeolatitudes. Tillites deposited during the late Palaeozoic Gondwana glaciation, found across South America, Africa, India, Australia and Antarctica, were an important line of evidence for continental drift and the former supercontinent Gondwana.
