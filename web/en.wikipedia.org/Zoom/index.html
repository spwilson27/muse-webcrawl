Zoom

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Zoom may refer to several different topics. This page provides an overview of the most common meanings of the term.

Technology and software

Zoom is a videoconferencing platform developed by Zoom Video Communications, founded in 2011 by Eric Yuan and headquartered in San Jose, California. The software provides video telephony and online chat services through a cloud-based peer-to-peer platform and is used for teleconferencing, telecommuting, distance education and social relations. Its freemium model and ease of use drove enormous growth during the COVID-19 pandemic, when "Zoom" became a generic verb for video calls. The company has also expanded into phone systems, conference-room software and contact-centre services.

Optics and photography

A zoom lens is a lens whose focal length can be varied continuously, allowing the photographer to change the angle of view without changing lenses. "Zooming" also describes rapid changes in apparent scale in film, computer graphics and user interfaces (a zooming user interface, or ZUI).

Other uses

Zoom is also the name of several music releases, television programmes (including the 1972 American children's show ZOOM), record labels, airlines and retail chains. The word itself imitates the sound of something moving very fast, and by extension denotes rapid motion.
