Geogaddi is the second studio album by the Scottish electronic music duo Boards of Canada, released in February 2002 on Warp Records. The follow-up to the acclaimed Music Has the Right to Children (1998), it was among the most anticipated electronic albums of its time and was launched with listening events held in churches in several cities.

Music and themes

The album retains the duo's signature sound — warped analogue synthesizers, hip-hop-derived beats, and samples of children's voices and educational media — but is generally darker and more unsettled than its predecessor. Its 23 tracks run to exactly 66 minutes and 6 seconds on the standard edition, a duration widely interpreted as a deliberate occult reference, and the music is saturated with numerological and esoteric motifs, references to the Branch Davidians (including the track "1969", which samples a follower of David Koresh), and themes of paganism and psychological manipulation.

Reception

Geogaddi received generally favourable reviews and reached the UK Albums Chart. Retrospectively it is frequently ranked among the finest electronic albums of the 2000s, praised for its dense, paranoid atmosphere, though some listeners found it more forbidding than the nostalgic warmth of the duo's earlier work.

Track listing highlights

Tracks include "Ready Lets Go", "Music Is Math", "Beware the Friendly Stranger", "Gyroscope", "1969", "The Beach at Redpoint", and the closing near-silence of "Magic Window".
