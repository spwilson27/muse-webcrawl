Gloria Swanson (27 March 1899 – 4 April 1983) was an American actress. One of the biggest stars of the silent era, she achieved fame in films directed by Cecil B. DeMille and became a fashion icon of the 1920s, before famously staging a comeback as Norma Desmond in Sunset Boulevard (1950).

Career

Swanson began as an extra at Essanay Studios in Chicago and moved to Hollywood, where comedies for Mack Sennett preceded stardom at Paramount under DeMille in pictures such as Male and Female (1919). At her peak she earned enormous salaries and founded her own production company, producing Sadie Thompson (1928), for which she received an Academy Award nomination. Her production of Erich von Stroheim's lavish Queen Kelly (1929) collapsed unfinished, becoming one of silent cinema's legendary fiascos.

Sunset Boulevard

After years of relative retirement, Billy Wilder cast Swanson as Norma Desmond, a forgotten silent-film star living in delusion — a role that uncannily mirrored her own career. Her line "All right, Mr. DeMille, I'm ready for my close-up" became one of the most quoted in film history, and she received her third Oscar nomination.

Personal life

Swanson married six times, including to actor Wallace Beery and the French nobleman Henri, Marquis de la Falaise. Her affair with Joseph P. Kennedy, who financed her production company, was widely documented. In later life she promoted health food and macrobiotic ideas and published a bestselling autobiography, Swanson on Swanson (1980).
