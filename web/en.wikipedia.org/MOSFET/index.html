MOSFET

MOSFET
Type: Field-effect transistor
Invented: 1959 (Dawon Kahng, Mohamed Atalla)
Terminals: Gate, source, drain (body)

The metal–oxide–semiconductor field-effect transistor (MOSFET, or MOS transistor) is a type of insulated-gate field-effect transistor that is fabricated by the controlled oxidation of a semiconductor, typically silicon. It has an insulated gate, the voltage of which determines the conductivity of the device. This ability to change conductivity with the amount of applied voltage can be used for amplifying or switching electronic signals.

History

The MOSFET was invented by Mohamed Atalla and Dawon Kahng at Bell Labs in 1959. It became the fundamental building block of modern electronics and is the most frequently manufactured device in history.

Operation

Applying a voltage to the gate terminal creates an electric field that modulates the charge carriers in a channel between source and drain. MOSFETs may be enhancement-mode or depletion-mode, and n-channel or p-channel (NMOS and PMOS).

Applications

MOSFETs are used in integrated circuits (CMOS logic), power electronics, switching power supplies, and motor drives.
