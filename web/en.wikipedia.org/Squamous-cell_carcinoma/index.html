Squamous-cell carcinoma

Squamous-cell carcinoma
Type: Carcinoma of squamous epithelial cells
Common sites: Skin, lung, oesophagus, head and neck, cervix
Risk factors: UV radiation, smoking, HPV, immunosuppression

Squamous-cell carcinoma (SCC) is a type of cancer that arises from squamous cells, the flat cells that form the surface of the skin and line hollow organs and the respiratory and digestive tracts. It is one of the major forms of carcinoma.

Skin SCC

Cutaneous squamous-cell carcinoma is the second most common skin cancer after basal-cell carcinoma. It is strongly associated with cumulative ultraviolet exposure and typically appears as a firm, red nodule or a flat sore with a scaly crust. Unlike basal-cell carcinoma, skin SCC can metastasise, though most cases are cured by surgical excision.

Other sites

SCC accounts for a large share of lung cancers (linked to smoking), most oesophageal cancers worldwide, and the majority of head and neck cancers, in which human papillomavirus (HPV) is an increasingly important cause, particularly in the oropharynx.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the site and stage and may include surgery, radiation therapy, chemotherapy and immunotherapy with checkpoint inhibitors such as cemiplimab and pembrolizumab.
