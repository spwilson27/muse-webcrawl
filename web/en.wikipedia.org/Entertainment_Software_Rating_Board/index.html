Entertainment Software Rating Board

The Entertainment Software Rating Board (ESRB) is a self-regulatory organization that assigns age and content ratings to consumer video games in North America. It was established in 1994 by the Entertainment Software Association (ESA) in response to congressional hearings over violent video games such as Mortal Kombat and Night Trap.

Ratings

The ESRB rating system uses age-based categories: E (Everyone), E10+ (Everyone 10+), T (Teen), M (Mature 17+), and AO (Adults Only 18+), supplemented by content descriptors such as "Violence", "Language", "Sexual Content" and "Use of Alcohol", and by notices for in-game purchases. Ratings appear on game packaging and in digital storefronts.

Process

Publishers submit questionnaires and footage of a game's most extreme content; trained raters assign a rating, which publishers may accept or appeal. The system is voluntary, but most console manufacturers and major retailers require ESRB ratings for games sold on their platforms or in their stores.

Criticism

The ESRB has been criticized both for ratings perceived as too lenient — most prominently during the 2005 "Hot Coffee" controversy over Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas, whose rating was changed to AO after hidden sexual content was discovered — and for ratings seen as overly strict. It remains the principal video game rating system in the United States, Canada and Mexico.
