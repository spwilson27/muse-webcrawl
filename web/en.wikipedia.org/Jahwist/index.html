Jahwist

The Jahwist, also spelled Yahwist and usually designated J, is one of the sources proposed by the documentary hypothesis for the composition of the Pentateuch (the first five books of the Hebrew Bible). It is named for its characteristic use of the divine name YHWH (rendered "Jahweh" in older German scholarship), which its author uses from the very beginning of the narrative, even before the name is revealed to Moses in Exodus.

Characteristics

The J source is noted for its vivid, anthropomorphic portrayal of God, who walks in the Garden of Eden, speaks face to face with patriarchs, and bargains with Abraham. Its narratives include the Eden story, Cain and Abel, the Flood account interwoven with the Priestly version, the Tower of Babel, and much of the patriarchal history in Genesis, continuing into Exodus and Numbers.

Dating

Julius Wellhausen placed J in the 9th century BCE in the southern Kingdom of Judah. Modern scholars debate its date widely: some argue for a monarchic-era composition, others for an exilic or post-exilic (6th–5th century BCE) origin. Some recent scholarship questions whether J existed as a continuous independent document at all, proposing instead fragmented traditions later gathered by editors.

Other sources

In the classical documentary hypothesis, J is combined with the Elohist (E), Deuteronomist (D) and Priestly (P) sources, woven together by redactors into the final form of the Torah.
