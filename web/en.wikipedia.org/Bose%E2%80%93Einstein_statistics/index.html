Bose–Einstein statistics

Applies to: Bosons (indistinguishable particles)
Developed by: Satyendra Nath Bose & Albert Einstein
Year: 1924
Contrast: Fermi–Dirac statistics (fermions)

In quantum statistics, Bose–Einstein statistics describe the distribution of indistinguishable particles with integer spin — bosons — over energy states in a system in thermal equilibrium. Unlike fermions, any number of bosons may occupy the same quantum state.

History

Satyendra Nath Bose derived the statistics for photons in 1924 while working in Dhaka, devising a way of counting states that yielded Planck's law of black-body radiation without reference to classical physics. Albert Einstein recognised its importance, translated Bose's paper into German, and extended the treatment to massive particles, predicting Bose–Einstein condensation.

Applications

Bose–Einstein statistics govern the behaviour of photons in a cavity (black-body radiation), phonons in solids, and bosonic gases at low temperature. At high temperatures and low densities, both Bose–Einstein and Fermi–Dirac statistics reduce to the classical Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution.
