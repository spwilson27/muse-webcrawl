Boeing P-12

The Boeing P-12 was a pursuit (fighter) aircraft operated by the United States Army Air Corps between 1929 and 1941, and the standard Army fighter of the early 1930s. Its naval counterpart was the F4B. A single-seat biplane powered by a Pratt & Whitney R-1340 Wasp radial, it reached about 189 mph (304 km/h).

Development

The P-12 grew out of Boeing's privately funded Models 83 and 89. Production variants ran from the P-12 (Model 102) through the P-12E (Model 234), with improvements to the fuselage, cowling and landing gear. In total about 586 aircraft of the P-12/F4B family were produced, making it one of the most numerous American fighters of the interwar period.

Service

P-12s served with pursuit squadrons across the United States and in Hawaii, the Philippines and the Panama Canal Zone, and became famous for aerobatic displays. Though obsolete by the Second World War, a few remained in training and liaison roles. Foreign operators included Brazil, China, the Philippines, Spain and Thailand.
