The COVID-19 pandemic (also known as the coronavirus pandemic) was a global pandemic of coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) caused by severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2). The novel virus was first identified in an outbreak in Wuhan, China, in December 2019. The World Health Organization declared the outbreak a public health emergency of international concern on 30 January 2020 and assessed it as a pandemic on 11 March 2020. The WHO ended the emergency phase on 5 May 2023, though COVID-19 remains an ongoing health issue.

Course

During 2020 the virus spread to every continent, overwhelming hospitals in successive waves. Governments imposed lockdowns, travel restrictions, mask mandates and mass testing; the global economy suffered its sharpest contraction since the Great Depression, and schooling and daily life were disrupted worldwide. Vaccination began in December 2020 with mRNA and viral-vector vaccines developed at unprecedented speed, and over 13 billion doses were administered. Variants of concern — Alpha, Beta, Delta and the highly transmissible Omicron (late 2021) — drove further waves, after which widespread immunity from vaccination and infection reduced mortality and most restrictions were lifted during 2022–2023.

Impact

Confirmed deaths exceeded 7 million, while WHO and other estimates of excess deaths for 2020–21 alone range from about 15 to 30 million, making it one of the deadliest pandemics in history. The pandemic reshaped remote work, strained public health systems, worsened inequality, disrupted supply chains and fuelled inflation, and triggered extensive scientific collaboration as well as controversy over origins, mandates and misinformation.

Origins

The virus is generally thought to have a zoonotic origin, spilling over from bats — possibly via an intermediate host — at a Wuhan seafood market; debate over a possible laboratory origin has continued, but no conclusive evidence has settled the question publicly.
