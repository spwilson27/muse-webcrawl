Hans-Georg Gadamer

Born: 11 February 1900, Marburg, German Empire

Died: 13 March 2002, Heidelberg, Germany

Era: 20th-century philosophy

Main interests: Hermeneutics, ontology, aesthetics

Hans-Georg Gadamer (1900–2002) was a German philosopher of the continental tradition, best known for his 1960 magnum opus Truth and Method (Wahrheit und Methode), a foundational work of philosophical hermeneutics — the theory of interpretation and understanding.

Life

Gadamer studied at Marburg under Martin Heidegger, whose thought profoundly shaped his own, and completed his habilitation in 1929. He held professorships at Marburg, Leipzig, and Frankfurt before succeeding Karl Jaspers at Heidelberg in 1949, where he remained until his retirement. Famously long-lived and active, he continued lecturing and publishing well past the age of 100.

Philosophy

In Truth and Method, Gadamer argued that understanding is not a methodical procedure but a fundamental mode of human existence. His key concepts include the rehabilitation of "prejudice" (pre-judgment) as a condition of understanding, historically effected consciousness (wirkungsgeschichtliches Bewusstsein), and the "fusion of horizons" (Horizontverschmelzung) — the process by which an interpreter's horizon meets that of a text or another person. He also engaged in celebrated debates with Jürgen Habermas and Jacques Derrida.

Legacy

Gadamer's hermeneutics influenced literary theory, theology, jurisprudence, and the philosophy of history, and he is regarded alongside Heidegger as one of the most important German philosophers of the twentieth century.
