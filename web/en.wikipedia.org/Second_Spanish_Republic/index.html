Second Spanish Republic

The Second Spanish Republic (Spanish: Segunda República Española) was the democratic government of Spain from its proclamation on 14 April 1931, after the departure of King Alfonso XIII, until 1939, when it was defeated in the Spanish Civil War by the Nationalist forces of General Francisco Franco.

Reforms and polarisation

The Constitution of 1931 established a democratic, secular republic with universal suffrage (including women), regional autonomy for Catalonia (and later the Basque Country), and extensive civil liberties. The first governments under Manuel Azaña pursued military reform, land reform, separation of church and state and the curtailing of Church control of education. These measures, economic depression and social conflict — including strikes, church burnings and the Asturian miners' uprising of October 1934, crushed by the army — deeply polarised the country. Power swung between left-republican coalitions (1931–33, and the Popular Front elected in February 1936) and centre-right governments (1933–35).

Civil war and end

A military rising of 17–18 July 1936, led by generals including Franco, failed to seize the whole country and plunged Spain into civil war. The Republic, aided by the Soviet Union and the International Brigades but hampered by the Anglo-French policy of non-intervention while Germany and Italy aided the Nationalists, lost territory steadily. Barcelona fell in January 1939, Madrid in March, and Franco declared victory on 1 April 1939; a Republican government-in-exile persisted abroad until the transition to democracy after Franco's death.
