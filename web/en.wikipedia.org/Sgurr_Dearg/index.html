Sgurr Dearg (pronounced roughly "skoor jerrak"; Scottish Gaelic for "red peak") is a mountain in the Cuillin range on the Isle of Skye, Scotland. At 986 metres, it is a Munro. Its summit is crowned by the Inaccessible Pinnacle (often shortened to the "In Pinn"), a narrow blade of rock whose top, at about 985.8 m, is actually slightly lower than Sgurr Dearg's own rocky summit point by some measurements — the relative heights have long been debated, though the Inaccessible Pinnacle is universally treated as the true summit for Munro-bagging purposes.

The Inaccessible Pinnacle

The Pinnacle is a fin of gabbro rock some 50 metres long, projecting above the summit ridge. It was first climbed in 1880 by brothers Charles and Lawrence Pilkington. Its ascent — usually by the moderate but extremely exposed east ridge, followed by an abseil down the steeper west side — is the only Munro summit that requires genuine rock climbing, making it the most dreaded obstacle for those completing all 282 Munros. A lightning strike in 2007 removed a large block from the summit ridge but the pinnacle remains climbable.

Setting

Sgurr Dearg rises above Glen Brittle on Skye's west coast, within the jagged Black Cuillin, widely regarded as Britain's most dramatic and Alpine-like mountain range. Routes to the summit involve rough scrambling over gabbro and basalt, and the mountain is often combined with neighbouring Munros Sgurr na Banachdich and Sgurr a' Mhadaidh on the main Cuillin ridge.
