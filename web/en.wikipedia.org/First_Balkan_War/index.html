The First Balkan War lasted from October 1912 to May 1913 and pitted the Balkan League — the kingdoms of Bulgaria, Serbia, Greece and Montenegro — against the Ottoman Empire. The combined armies of the Balkan states overcame the numerically inferior and strategically disadvantaged Ottoman armies, achieving rapid success. As a result, the Ottomans lost almost all of their remaining territory in Europe. Ensuing differences among the allies over the division of the spoils in Macedonia led to the Second Balkan War within months.

Background

The Balkan states had won independence from Ottoman rule during the nineteenth century and sought to expel the empire from its remaining European provinces, especially Macedonia, whose mixed population each nation claimed. Encouraged by Ottoman weakness after the Italo-Turkish War (1911–12), the Balkan League was formed in 1912 under Russian auspices. Montenegro declared war on 8 October 1912, and the other allies followed.

Course of the war

Bulgarian armies won the battles of Kirk Kilisse and Lule Burgas and advanced to the Çatalca lines outside Constantinople. Serbian forces triumphed at Kumanovo and took Skopje and Monastir, while the Greek army captured Thessaloniki and advanced into Epirus, and the Greek navy dominated the Aegean, defeating the Ottoman fleet at Elli and Lemnos. The fortresses of Adrianople, Ioannina and Scutari fell after sieges in early 1913.

Outcome

By the Treaty of London (30 May 1913), the Ottoman Empire ceded all territory west of the Enos–Midye line. The great powers created an independent Albania, largely to deny Serbia an Adriatic outlet — a settlement that left Bulgaria dissatisfied with its share of Macedonia and set the stage for renewed war among the victors.
