Dark academia

The dark academia aesthetic: libraries, classics and autumnal tones

Dark academia is an internet aesthetic and social-media subculture centred on higher education, literature, classical art and the pursuit of knowledge, rendered in a dark, autumnal and nostalgic palette. Popularised on Tumblr in the mid-2010s and surging on TikTok and Instagram around 2020, it romanticises the atmosphere of old European universities: candlelit libraries, gothic architecture, Latin mottos and leather-bound books.

Characteristics

The aesthetic draws on the visual world of elite boarding schools and Oxbridge colleges — tweed blazers, plaid, turtlenecks and loafers in black, brown and beige — and on intellectual pursuits such as learning Latin and Greek, studying philosophy and poetry, and writing by hand. Donna Tartt's 1992 novel The Secret History, about a close-knit group of classics students at an elite college, is regarded as the subculture's foundational text; films such as Dead Poets Society are also central references.

History

Dark academia developed from Tumblr "academia" blogs of the 2010s, splitting into light, dark and other variants. Its popularity spiked during the COVID-19 pandemic, when students in remote schooling adopted the aesthetic as an aspirational stand-in for campus life. Book-sharing communities on TikTok ("BookTok") further amplified its core reading lists, from Greek tragedy to the Romantic poets.

Criticism

Commentators have criticised dark academia for glamorising Eurocentric, elite institutions, for its association with wealth and whiteness, and for romanticising unhealthy behaviour depicted in its source texts. Defenders respond that the community is more diverse than its imagery suggests and that its core appeal is a love of learning for its own sake.
