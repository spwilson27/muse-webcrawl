Vatican Museums

The Vatican Museums (Italian: Musei Vaticani; Latin: Musea Vaticana) are the public museums of the Vatican City. They display works from the immense collection amassed by the Catholic Church and the papacy throughout the centuries, including several of the most well-known Roman sculptures and most important masterpieces of Renaissance art in the world. The museums include some of the largest and most complex museum complexes in the world, with some 70,000 works, of which 20,000 are on display.

History

Pope Julius II founded the museums in the early 16th century to display classical sculptures, including the Laocoön and His Sons, unearthed in Rome in 1506, and the Apollo Belvedere. Successive popes expanded the collections and the Apostolic Palace buildings that house them. The museums as a public institution date to the 18th century, with the Museo Pio-Clementino founded by Clement XIV and Pius VI.

Highlights

The museums contain the Sistine Chapel, with its ceiling and The Last Judgment painted by Michelangelo, and the four Raphael Rooms, frescoed by Raphael and his workshop. Other major sections include the Gallery of Maps, the Pinacoteca (picture gallery, with works by Giotto, Leonardo, Raphael, and Caravaggio), the Egyptian and Etruscan museums, and extensive collections of classical antiquities.

Visitors

With over six million visitors annually in recent years, the Vatican Museums are among the most visited art museums in the world. The route through the museums covers some 7 km of galleries and culminates in the Sistine Chapel.
