Horse racing

Horse racing is an equestrian sport in which two or more horses, ridden by jockeys (or driven in harness racing), compete to finish a set course first. One of the most ancient of sports — chariot and ridden races featured in the ancient Olympic Games — it is now a major global industry intertwined with breeding and pari-mutuel betting.

Forms

Flat racing is run over distances from sprints of five furlongs to staying races beyond two miles, on turf or dirt. Jump racing (steeplechasing and hurdling) includes the Grand National at Aintree. Harness racing uses Standardbreds pulling sulkies at a trot or pace; endurance racing covers long distances over natural terrain.

Thoroughbreds and breeding

Most flat racing uses Thoroughbreds, a breed founded in England from three imported Arabian-line stallions — the Byerley Turk, Darley Arabian, and Godolphin Arabian — whose pedigrees are recorded in the General Stud Book. Famous races include the Kentucky Derby, the Epsom Derby, the Prix de l'Arc de Triomphe, and the Melbourne Cup.

Economics and welfare

Wagering funds most purses and industry employment. The sport faces scrutiny over equine welfare, injuries, medication rules, and the fate of retired racehorses, prompting ongoing regulatory reform.
