Functional analysis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Functional analysis is a branch of mathematical analysis whose core is the study of vector spaces endowed with some kind of limit-related structure — normed spaces, inner product spaces, and topological vector spaces — and the linear functions ("operators") defined on these spaces. It grew out of the study of function spaces and the formulation of properties of transformations of functions, such as the Fourier transform, as operators between function spaces.

History

The field crystallised in the early 20th century from work by David Hilbert on integral equations, the introduction of Hilbert space, and Stefan Banach's 1922 dissertation defining Banach spaces. John von Neumann developed the spectral theory of operators and applied the framework to give quantum mechanics its rigorous mathematical foundation in the 1930s.

Main topics

Central concepts include Banach and Hilbert spaces; bounded and unbounded linear operators; dual spaces and weak topologies; and the great theorems of the subject — the Hahn–Banach theorem, the open mapping theorem, the closed graph theorem, and the spectral theorem. Important examples of function spaces are the Lebesgue spaces Lp and Sobolev spaces, essential to the modern theory of partial differential equations.

Applications

Functional analysis underlies quantum mechanics (where observables are operators on a Hilbert space), the numerical and analytical theory of differential equations, signal processing, optimisation, and mathematical economics.
