No free lunch theorem

The no free lunch theorem (NFLT) in computational complexity and mathematical optimisation, proved by David Wolpert and William Macready in 1995–1997, states that, averaged over all possible problems, all optimisation and search algorithms perform identically.

Statement and interpretation

For search and optimisation, the theorems show that if an algorithm performs better than random search on one class of problems, it must perform worse on some other class. In machine learning, an analogous result says that no learning algorithm generalises best on all possible problems: gains on some problems are offset by losses on others. The practical lesson is that good performance requires incorporating problem-specific knowledge — assumptions and inductive biases matched to the actual distribution of problems — rather than a universally superior method.
