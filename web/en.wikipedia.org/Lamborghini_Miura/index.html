Lamborghini Miura

Manufacturer: Lamborghini
Production: 1966–1973
Designer: Marcello Gandini at Bertone
Engine: 3.9 L V12, mid-mounted transversely
Layout: rear mid-engine, rear-wheel drive

The Lamborghini Miura is a sports car produced by Italian automaker Lamborghini between 1966 and 1973. The car was the first supercar with a rear mid-engined two-seat layout, although the concept had been seen in the Ford GT40, and set the standard for high-performance sports and supercars ever since.

The Miura began as a skunkworks project by Lamborghini's young engineers Gian Paolo Dallara, Paolo Stanzani and Bob Wallace, who developed a chassis with a transversely mounted V12 behind the cabin in their spare time. Shown as a bare chassis at the 1965 Turin auto show, it was clothed by Bertone's Marcello Gandini and unveiled at Geneva in 1966 to acclaim.

Versions

The original P400 was followed by the improved P400 S (1968) and the definitive P400 SV (1971), which gained wider rear bodywork, revised suspension and up to 385 PS. A single open-top roadster and the stripped Jota experimental car were also built. Total production was about 764 cars.

Legacy

The Miura is widely credited with inventing the modern supercar formula and remains one of the most valuable and celebrated classic cars, frequently topping lists of the most beautiful automobiles ever made.
