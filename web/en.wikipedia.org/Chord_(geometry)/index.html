Chord (geometry)

A chord of a circle is a line segment whose endpoints both lie on the circle. The longest possible chord of a circle is its diameter, which passes through the centre; chords shorter than the diameter lie progressively farther from the centre the shorter they are.

Properties

Several classical theorems govern chords: a perpendicular drawn from the centre of a circle to a chord bisects the chord; equal chords are equidistant from the centre; and the intersecting chords theorem relates the products of the segments of two chords that cross inside a circle. The region between a chord and its arc is called a circular segment, while the region bounded by two radii and an arc is a sector.

Trigonometry

Chords were the basis of the earliest trigonometry. Greek astronomers such as Ptolemy tabulated chord lengths for arcs of a circle — the chord of an angle being twice the sine of half the angle — and these tables served the same purposes as modern sine tables.

Generalisation

More generally, a chord of any curve is a line segment joining two points on the curve; for a sphere or other solid, chords through the interior play a role in geometry and physics, for example in the flight paths of particles and in mean chord length estimates.
