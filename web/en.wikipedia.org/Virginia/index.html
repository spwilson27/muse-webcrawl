Virginia

Virginia, officially the Commonwealth of Virginia, is a state in the Southeastern and Mid-Atlantic regions of the United States, located between the Atlantic Coast and the Appalachian Mountains. Its capital is Richmond, and its most populous city is Virginia Beach. With a population of about 8.6 million, it is the 12th-most populous state. Virginia has a long and central place in American history: it was the first permanent English colony in North America and has produced eight U.S. presidents, earning it the nickname "Mother of Presidents". Its state motto is Sic semper tyrannis.

History

Native peoples, including the Powhatan Confederacy, inhabited the region for thousands of years before the London Company founded Jamestown in 1607, the first permanent English settlement in North America. The colony prospered on tobacco, cultivated first by indentured servants and then increasingly by enslaved Africans, first brought in 1619 — the same year the House of Burgesses, the first elected assembly in English America, convened. Virginia was central to the American Revolution; the war effectively ended with the British surrender at Yorktown in 1781. During the Civil War the state joined the Confederacy, Richmond served as the Confederate capital, and more battles were fought on Virginia soil than in any other state; the war ended with Lee's surrender at Appomattox Court House in 1865. The northwestern counties broke away in 1863 to form West Virginia.

Geography

Virginia stretches from the Atlantic Ocean and the Delmarva Peninsula in the east, across the tidal estuaries of the Chesapeake Bay, the rolling Piedmont, and the Blue Ridge and Allegheny mountains, to the Cumberland Plateau in the southwest. The highest point is Mount Rogers at 5,729 feet. Major rivers include the Potomac, Rappahannock, James, York and Shenandoah. The climate ranges from humid subtropical in the east to more continental in the mountains.

Economy and government

Northern Virginia, adjoining Washington, D.C., hosts a large concentration of federal agencies — including the Pentagon in Arlington — defense contractors and technology firms, while Hampton Roads contains the world's largest naval base at Norfolk. Agriculture, once dominated by tobacco, remains important, and the state has significant technology, education and tourism sectors. Virginia is governed by a governor, a bicameral General Assembly (the oldest continuous legislative body in the Americas), and an independent judiciary; its 95 counties and 38 independent cities reflect its distinctive system of city-county separation.
