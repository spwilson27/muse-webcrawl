The Calcutta Cup is a rugby union trophy awarded to the winner of the annual match between England and Scotland, played as part of the Six Nations Championship. It is one of the oldest international sporting trophies, contested since 1879, and celebrates the birth of international rugby in colonial India.

Origins

The Calcutta Football Club, formed by former pupils of English public schools in 1872, was an early centre of rugby in India. When the club disbanded in 1878, its members withdrew its remaining funds — silver rupees — and had them melted down and crafted into a cup, decorated with cobra handles and an elephant on the lid. The trophy was presented to the Rugby Football Union, which decided it should be competed for annually by England and Scotland.

The match

The first Calcutta Cup match was played at Raeburn Place, Edinburgh, in 1879 and ended in a draw; England won the first outright victory the following year. The fixture alternates between Twickenham in London and Murrayfield in Edinburgh. Because England and Scotland also played the very first rugby international in 1871, the fixture is the oldest of its kind in the sport.

Notable episodes

In 1988 the trophy was famously damaged when players from both teams, including England's Dean Richards and Scotland's John Jeffrey, played football with it along Princes Street in Edinburgh after a post-match dinner; Jeffrey received a six-month ban and Richards a one-match ban. Memorable matches include Scotland's 1990 Grand Slam decider victory at Murrayfield and their 2019 draw at Twickenham, in which Scotland retained the cup after recovering from 31–0 down.
