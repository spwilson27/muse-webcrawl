The Charles Stark Draper Laboratory, Inc. — better known as Draper Laboratory or simply Draper — is an American nonprofit research and development organisation headquartered in Cambridge, Massachusetts. It specialises in the design and development of advanced technology solutions, above all guidance, navigation, and control systems for military and space applications. Draper grew out of the MIT Instrumentation Laboratory founded by Charles Stark "Doc" Draper, the pioneer of practical inertial navigation.

History

Draper's laboratory at MIT developed gyroscopic gunsights in the Second World War and then, in the 1950s, demonstrated that aircraft and submarines could navigate purely by measuring accelerations and rotations — culminating in inertial guidance for the Polaris submarine-launched ballistic missile and later generations of strategic missiles, including Trident.

In 1961 the laboratory won the first major contract of the Apollo program: the design of the Apollo Guidance Computer and the guidance and navigation system that steered the command and lunar modules to the Moon, a landmark in both embedded computing and software engineering. The laboratory was divested from MIT in 1973 amid controversy over its military work, becoming an independent nonprofit while retaining Draper's name.

Work

Draper's programs span space systems (including guidance for NASA vehicles and the International Space Station), strategic and tactical missile guidance, autonomous undersea vehicles, fault-tolerant computing, microelectromechanical systems, biomedical devices, and positioning technology for GPS-denied environments. Its engineers developed early fault-tolerant flight computers used on the Space Shuttle, and its fly-by-wire and digital flight control work influenced modern aircraft.
