Dulce et Decorum est

AuthorWilfred Owen
Written1917–1918
Published1920 (posthumous)
SubjectFirst World War gas attack

"Dulce et Decorum est" is a poem written by the English soldier-poet Wilfred Owen during the First World War and published posthumously in 1920. It is one of the best-known poems of the war, famed for its vivid depiction of a gas attack and its bitter rejection of the idea that it is glorious to die for one's country.

The poem

The title quotes the Roman poet Horace: Dulce et decorum est pro patria mori — "It is sweet and fitting to die for one's country." Owen's speaker describes exhausted troops trudging from the trenches when gas shells drop; one man fails to fit his mask in time and dies in agony before the speaker's eyes, an image that haunts his dreams. The poem ends by calling the Horatian maxim "the old Lie" when addressed to children "ardent for some desperate glory".

Composition and publication

Owen drafted the poem at Craiglockhart War Hospital in Edinburgh in 1917, where he was being treated for shell shock and met Siegfried Sassoon, and revised it in 1918. He was killed in action in November 1918; the poem appeared in the 1920 edition of his poems prepared by Sassoon.

Legacy

The poem is a staple of war literature anthologies and school curricula in Britain and the Commonwealth, and its closing lines are frequently cited in debates about war remembrance and recruitment.
