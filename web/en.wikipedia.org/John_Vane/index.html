Sir John Robert Vane FRS (29 March 1927 – 19 November 2004) was a British pharmacologist who shared the 1982 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for his discoveries concerning prostaglandins and related substances, including showing how aspirin works.

Career

Vane studied chemistry at Birmingham and pharmacology at Oxford, then worked at Yale and the Royal College of Surgeons of England, where he developed the cascade superfusion bioassay that let researchers measure vanishingly small amounts of vasoactive substances.

Aspirin and prostaglandins

In 1971 Vane demonstrated that aspirin and similar anti-inflammatory drugs block the synthesis of prostaglandins, explaining both their therapeutic effects and side effects. His group also discovered prostacyclin, a potent inhibitor of platelet aggregation and vasodilator, clarifying how the endothelium controls blood vessels.

Later work

In 1986 Vane founded the William Harvey Research Institute at St Bartholomew's Hospital Medical College in London. Knighted in 1984, he remained an influential advocate of bioassay-based drug discovery until his death in 2004.
