Jalisco

Jalisco is one of the 31 states of Mexico, located on the country's Pacific coast. Its capital and largest city is Guadalajara, Mexico's second-largest metropolitan area. Jalisco is the fourth most populous Mexican state, with roughly 8.3 million inhabitants, and one of its largest economies.

Geography

Jalisco borders Nayarit, Zacatecas, Aguascalientes, Guanajuato, Michoacán and Colima, and has a long Pacific coastline including the resort city of Puerto Vallarta on the Bahía de Banderas. Lake Chapala, Mexico's largest freshwater lake, lies on its south-eastern border. The Sierra Madre Occidental crosses the state, and its volcanic highlands include the active Volcán de Colima on the Colima border.

Culture

Jalisco is widely regarded as the cultural heartland of Mexico: it is the birthplace of mariachi music, the Mexican hat dance (jarabe tapatío), charreada (Mexican rodeo), and tequila, which by law can only be produced in Jalisco and a few neighbouring areas from blue agave grown in the region. The agave landscape and historic distilleries around the town of Tequila are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Economy

Guadalajara is a major technology and electronics manufacturing hub, nicknamed Mexico's "Silicon Valley." The state is also a leading agricultural producer, exporting berries, avocados and agave products, and tourism centres on Puerto Vallarta, Lake Chapala and the Pueblos Mágicos such as Tequila, Tlaquepaque and Tapalpa.
