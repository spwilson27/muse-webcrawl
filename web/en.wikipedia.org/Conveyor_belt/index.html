Conveyor belt

A conveyor belt is a continuous loop of flexible material stretched between two or more pulleys, one of which is driven, forming the carrying medium of a belt conveyor system. Conveyor belts are among the most widely used means of transporting bulk materials and unit loads in mining, agriculture, manufacturing, warehousing, airports and parcel handling.

Construction

A typical belt consists of a carcass of one or more plies of woven fabric — historically cotton, now usually polyester, nylon or aramid, or cables of steel — embedded in rubber or polymer covers that protect the carcass and provide grip and wear resistance. The belt runs over idler rollers that support it and, on the carrying side, are often set in a trough shape to contain bulk material. A drive pulley, powered by an electric motor, moves the belt; take-up mechanisms maintain tension.

History

Primitive belt conveyors were in use by the late eighteenth century for moving grain. Oliver Evans described flour-mill conveyors in the United States, and by the 1890s steel and rubber belts were handling coal and ore. Henry Ford's moving assembly line of 1913 adapted conveyor principles to manufacturing. The longest single-flight conveyor belts, used for ore and phosphate transport, extend for tens of kilometres.

Applications

Belt conveyors move coal, iron ore, grain, aggregates and luggage; serve as checkout counters and moving walkways; and carry parts through assembly and inspection processes. Specialised variants include cleated and sidewall belts for steep inclines, pipe conveyors that wrap the belt into a tube, and sandwich-belt systems for vertical lifting.

Maintenance and safety

Belts are joined by vulcanised splices or mechanical fasteners. Common problems include mistracking, spillage, cover wear and damage from tramp metal; modern systems use rip detection, alignment switches and metal detectors. Conveyor accidents, particularly involving unexpected start-up during maintenance, are addressed through lockout–tagout procedures and guarding of nip points.
