Lathen train collision

The Transrapid test facility at Lathen

The Lathen train collision occurred on 22 September 2006 when a Transrapid magnetic levitation (maglev) train on the Emsland test track in Lathen, Lower Saxony, Germany, struck a maintenance vehicle left on the track. Twenty-three people were killed and ten injured, making it the deadliest maglev accident in history and the first to cause fatalities.

Accident

The Transrapid 08, carrying 29 passengers on a demonstration run, accelerated to about 170 km/h and collided with a wheeled maintenance vehicle that had not been cleared from the guideway. The front of the train was destroyed and debris was scattered over several hundred metres of track.

Causes

Investigators found that human error was the primary cause: staff in the control centre dispatched the train without confirming that the maintenance vehicle had left the guideway, and safety procedures on the test track were inadequate. Two employees were later convicted of negligent homicide and fined.

Aftermath

The accident severely damaged confidence in the Transrapid programme in Germany. The Emsland test facility was eventually closed, and the only commercial Transrapid line remained the Shanghai Maglev Train in China.
