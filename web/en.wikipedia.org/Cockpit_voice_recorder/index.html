Cockpit voice recorder

A cockpit voice recorder (CVR) is a flight recorder used to record the audio environment in the flight deck of an aircraft for accident and incident investigation. Together with the flight data recorder, it is one of the devices popularly called a "black box," though both are painted bright orange.

Operation

The CVR continuously records the last portion of cockpit audio — crew conversation, radio transmissions, alarms, and ambient sounds — using several microphones, overwriting older material in a loop. Modern standards require at least two hours of recording, with longer durations mandated in some jurisdictions. Recorders are built to survive impact, fire, and deep-water immersion, and are fitted with an underwater locator beacon.

History

Voice recording for investigation was developed from the 1950s, with Australian inventor David Warren's combined flight recorder unit an important early design. CVRs became mandatory on large commercial aircraft in many countries during the 1960s–1980s.
