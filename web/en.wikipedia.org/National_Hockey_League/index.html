National Hockey League

Founded: 26 November 1917, Montreal, Canada

Teams: 32 (25 in the United States, 7 in Canada)

Most titles: Montreal Canadiens (24 Stanley Cups)

Commissioner: Gary Bettman (since 1993)

The National Hockey League (NHL; French: Ligue nationale de hockey) is a professional ice hockey league in North America. Founded in 1917, it comprises 32 teams and is widely considered the premier professional ice hockey league in the world. The league is divided into the Eastern and Western Conferences, each with two divisions. At the end of each season, 16 teams contest the Stanley Cup playoffs for the Stanley Cup, the oldest professional sports trophy in North America.

History

The NHL was founded in Montreal in 1917 by the owners of the National Hockey Association, with four Canadian teams. By 1942 the league had contracted to the six teams later known as the "Original Six" — the Montreal Canadiens, Toronto Maple Leafs, Boston Bruins, New York Rangers, Chicago Black Hawks and Detroit Red Wings — who remained the league's only teams until a major expansion in 1967. Subsequent expansions, relocations and the 1979 absorption of four World Hockey Association teams brought the league to its modern form; the Vegas Golden Knights (2017), Seattle Kraken (2021) and Utah Hockey Club (2024) are among its newest members.

Game and season

Teams play an 82-game regular season. NHL games consist of three 20-minute periods; players such as Wayne Gretzky, Gordie Howe, Mario Lemieux, Sidney Crosby and Connor McDavid are among the sport's most celebrated figures. The league's labour relations have included lockouts that cancelled the entire 2004–05 season and shortened the 1994–95 and 2012–13 seasons. Internationally, the NHL supplies most players to top national teams, though its participation in the Winter Olympics has been intermittent.
