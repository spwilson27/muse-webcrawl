Vélib' (officially Vélib' Métropole) is a large-scale public bicycle sharing system serving Paris and many surrounding communes in France. Launched on 15 July 2007 with about 7,500 bicycles and 750 stations, it grew into one of the world's largest bikeshare schemes and became a model — and a generic name — for similar systems in other cities.

History

The original Vélib' was operated by the advertising company JCDecaux under a concession in which it financed the scheme in exchange for street-advertising rights, following the company's earlier Vélo'v system in Lyon (2005). The scheme quickly reached 20,000 bicycles and over 1,200 stations, though it suffered significant theft and vandalism in its early years. The name combines vélo ("bicycle") and liberté ("freedom").

In 2018 operation passed to the Smovengo consortium, which relaunched the service as Vélib' Métropole with new lighter bicycles, about a third of them electrically assisted, and an expanded area covering dozens of suburban communes. The transition was troubled by technical problems, but the system recovered and records tens of millions of journeys per year.

Usage

Users subscribe online or at terminals and release a bicycle from a docking station with a card or code; the first 30 minutes of each ride have traditionally been included in the subscription, encouraging short trips and rapid turnover. Vélib' is credited with normalizing utility cycling in Paris and influencing the design of bikeshare systems worldwide.
