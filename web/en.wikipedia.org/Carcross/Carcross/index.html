Carcross

Carcross is an unincorporated community in northern Yukon, Canada, situated on Bennett Lake and the Nares River at the northern end of the White Pass route from Skagway, Alaska. Historically known as Caribou Crossing, it was renamed Carcross in 1904. It is the home community of the Carcross/Tagish First Nation.

History

The site was a traditional crossing and meeting place for Tagish and Tlingit people, where caribou herds crossed the narrows between lakes. During the Klondike Gold Rush it became a major staging point on the route to Dawson City, and in 1900 the White Pass and Yukon Route railway reached Carcross, where the last spike of the line was driven. Mining, tourism and the railway shaped the settlement through the 20th century.

Geography and attractions

Carcross sits between Bennett and Nares lakes, connected to Whitehorse by the Klondike Highway and to Skagway by the South Klondike Highway over White Pass. Nearby is the Carcross Desert, often called the world's smallest desert — a roughly 2.6 km² area of sand dunes left by a glacial lake. The community is known for the historic steamship SS Tutshi (largely destroyed by fire in 1990), the colourful downtown carving shed of the Carcross/Tagish First Nation, and the Carcross Commons development.
