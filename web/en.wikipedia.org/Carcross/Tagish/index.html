Tagish

The Tagish are a First Nations people in northern Canada, whose traditional territory centres on Tagish Lake and the lakes district of southern Yukon and northern British Columbia. They speak Tagish, a Northern Athabaskan language closely related to Tlingit-influenced Tahltan and Kaska, and are closely connected by marriage and trade with the coastal Tlingit. Today most Tagish people are citizens of the Carcross/Tagish First Nation, based at Carcross.

Society

Traditional Tagish society was organised into two matrilineal moieties, Wolf (Dakhl'awedi) and Crow (Deisheetaan), divided into clans, with identity, property and ceremonial obligations passing through the mother's line. The Tagish practised a seasonal round of fishing for salmon and whitefish, hunting caribou, moose and sheep, and gathering, moving between camps around the lakes. Through long trade contact and intermarriage with the Tlingit, Tagish culture adopted many coastal features, including clan crests and potlatching.

Gold rush and famous individuals

Tagish people were central to the events that triggered the Klondike Gold Rush: Keish (Skookum Jim Mason), Shaaw Tláa (Kate Carmack) and Káa Goox (Dawson Charlie), together with Kate's brother-in-law George Carmack, were members of the party that discovered gold on Rabbit (Bonanza) Creek in 1896. The Tagish language became critically endangered in the 20th century; fluent speakers such as Angela Sidney (Maakoodetlaa), a renowned storyteller, worked with linguists to record Tagish narratives, and revitalisation efforts continue within the Carcross/Tagish First Nation.
