Carcross

Carcross on the shore of Bennett Lake, Yukon (placeholder)

Carcross is a village in Yukon, Canada, situated on Bennett Lake along the Klondike Highway, approximately 74 kilometres south of Whitehorse. Its population is a few hundred people. The name is a contraction of "Caribou Crossing", recalling the herds of woodland caribou that once crossed the narrows between Bennett Lake and Tagish Lake; the Tagish name for the place is Todezane. The community is the main settlement of the Carcross/Tagish First Nation.

History

The site was a traditional First Nations hunting and trading location on the route between the coast and the interior. During the Klondike Gold Rush of 1897–98, it became a major staging point: stampeders crossed the lakes here after coming over the Chilkoot or White Pass trails from Skagway, Alaska. The White Pass and Yukon Route railway reached Carcross in 1900 — where the last spike of the line was driven — and the town developed as a transportation and supply centre with sternwheelers operating on the lakes. After the Klondike Highway was completed in the 1950s, Carcross became a wayside stop for tourists travelling between Whitehorse and Skagway.

Attractions

Nearby is the Carcross Desert, a roughly 2.6 km² area of sand dunes — often called the "world's smallest desert" — left by a glacial lake. Other attractions include the White Pass & Yukon Route station, the Matthew Watson General Store (among the oldest operating stores in Yukon), historic totem poles, and mountain biking trails built under the Single Track to Success program on Montana Mountain. Emerald Lake, noted for its vivid colour, lies on the highway north of the village.
