Carcross/Tagish First Nation

The Carcross/Tagish First Nation is a First Nations self-governing government in Yukon, Canada. Its citizens are primarily of Tagish and Inland Tlingit ancestry, and its main settlement is Carcross, at the north end of Bennett Lake, with communities also around Tagish and Marsh Lake. The traditional languages are Tagish and Tlingit.

History

The Tagish people have lived in the southern Yukon lake country for thousands of years. The area became a key route during the Klondike Gold Rush of 1897–98, when stampeders passed through Carcross (then Caribou Crossing) on the way to Dawson City; the White Pass and Yukon Route railway was completed through the community in 1900. Members of the First Nation — including Keish (Skookum Jim Mason), Káa Goox (Dawson Charlie), and Shaaw Tláa (Kate Carmack) — were central figures in the gold discovery at Bonanza Creek.

Self-government

The Carcross/Tagish First Nation signed its Final Agreement and Self-Government Agreement with Canada and Yukon in 2005, among the agreements concluded under the Yukon Umbrella Final Agreement. The government operates under its own constitution, with a clan-based system of governance reflecting its matrilineal clan structure.

Culture and development

The First Nation has invested in cultural and tourism development in Carcross, including the Carcross Commons and mountain-bike trail networks on Montana Mountain built partly through its Singletrack to Success programme, which trains local youth as trail builders and guides. Language revitalisation work supports the endangered Tagish language, among the most threatened Indigenous languages in Canada.
