Carcross/White Pass and Yukon Route station

LocationCarcross, Yukon, Canada
LineWhite Pass and Yukon Route
Opened1900

The Carcross station is a railway station in Carcross, Yukon, Canada, served by the White Pass and Yukon Route, a narrow-gauge heritage railway linking Skagway, Alaska, with the Yukon interior. Carcross, formerly known as Caribou Crossing, lies at the northern end of the rail line's operating section from Skagway.

History

The White Pass and Yukon Route was built during the Klondike Gold Rush of 1898–1900 to carry prospectors and freight from the port of Skagway over White Pass to Whitehorse. The last spike was driven at Carcross on 29 July 1900, completing the line. The station building at Carcross dates to this era and is among the oldest structures in the community.

Services

Trains between Skagway and Carcross operate seasonally, mainly for cruise-ship passengers and tourists, often in combination with motorcoach transfers via the South Klondike Highway. The station houses a visitor reception area and serves as a focal point for the village.

Heritage

The railway is designated an International Historic Civil Engineering Landmark, shared with the Alaska State Museum and Canadian authorities, recognizing its construction under extreme northern conditions.
