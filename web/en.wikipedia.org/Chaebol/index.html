Chaebol

A chaebol (Korean: 재벌, lit. "rich family" or "financial clique") is a large industrial South Korean conglomerate run and controlled by an individual or family. The best-known chaebols are Samsung, Hyundai, SK, LG, Lotte and Hanwha. Chaebols are typically global multinationals consisting of many diversified affiliates, controlled by a founding family with disproportionately small direct shareholdings, maintained through complex cross-shareholding among affiliates.

History

Chaebols grew rapidly under the export-oriented industrialisation policies of Park Chung Hee's government in the 1960s–1970s, which channelled preferential credit and licences to favoured firms. By the 1990s chaebols accounted for a huge share of South Korea's GDP and exports.

Criticism and reform

Critics charge chaebols with crony capitalism, opaque governance, suppressing smaller competitors and labour, and hereditary transfers of control. The 1997 Asian financial crisis, which bankrupted Daewoo and strained others, spurred reforms requiring greater transparency and reduced cross-debt guarantees; later scandals — including the corruption case that engulfed Samsung's leadership and President Park Geun-hye — renewed debate. Defenders credit chaebols with driving Korea's industrial success and remaining competitive in semiconductors, shipbuilding, automobiles and electronics.
