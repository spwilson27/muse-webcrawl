Big Five game

The Big Five game animals are the lion, leopard, black and white rhinoceros, African elephant, and Cape buffalo. The term was coined by big-game hunters in Africa to refer to the five animals considered the most difficult and dangerous to hunt on foot. The term has since been adopted by safari tourism, for which seeing the Big Five is a major draw for visitors to game reserves in eastern and southern Africa.

The animals

  • African bush elephant (Loxodonta africana) – the largest living land animal.
  • Rhinoceros – the black rhinoceros (Diceros bicornis) and the white rhinoceros (Ceratotherium simum), both heavily threatened by poaching for their horns.
  • Cape buffalo (Syncerus caffer) – noted for its unpredictable temperament and formidable horns.
  • Lion (Panthera leo) – the largest African carnivore.
  • African leopard (Panthera pardus pardus) – elusive and nocturnal, often the hardest of the five to see.

Conservation

Several of the Big Five are of conservation concern: elephants and rhinos are targeted by poachers, and lion populations have declined across much of their range. Revenue from photographic safaris featuring the Big Five is an important source of funding for protected areas in countries such as Kenya, Tanzania, Botswana, Namibia and South Africa.
