Philippine tarsier

Philippine tarsier
Scientific name: Carlito syrichta
Family: Tarsiidae
Habitat: Rainforests of Bohol, Samar, Leyte and Mindanao
Status: Near Threatened (IUCN)

The Philippine tarsier (Carlito syrichta), known locally as the mawumag in Cebuano and Waray, is a species of tarsier endemic to the Philippines. It is one of the smallest known primates, measuring only about 8.5 to 16 cm (3.3 to 6.3 in) in height, with a long naked tail and enormous forward-facing eyes adapted for nocturnal hunting.

Description

Each of the tarsier's eyes is roughly as large as its brain, giving it exceptional night vision but fixed eye sockets; to compensate, it can rotate its head nearly 180 degrees in either direction. Its elongated hind limbs and enlarged tarsal bones allow leaps of up to five metres between trees. The Philippine tarsier's hearing extends into ultrasonic frequencies, and it communicates partly in ultrasound beyond the range of most predators.

Behaviour and diet

The species is nocturnal and primarily insectivorous, feeding on insects, small lizards, and occasionally birds. It is generally solitary or lives in small groups, marking its territory with scent. Unlike most primates it is entirely carnivorous.

Conservation

Habitat destruction and capture for the pet trade have reduced its numbers. It is legally protected in the Philippines and is a flagship species of the Philippine Tarsier Sanctuary in Corella, Bohol. Tarsiers are highly stressed by captivity and close human contact, and tourism guidelines require quiet observation at a distance.
