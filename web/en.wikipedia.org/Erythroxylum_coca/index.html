Erythroxylum coca

Kingdom: Plantae
Family: Erythroxylaceae
Genus: Erythroxylum
Species: E. coca
Binomial name: Erythroxylum coca Lam.

Erythroxylum coca is a species of flowering plant in the family Erythroxylaceae, native to western South America. It is one of the two cultivated species of coca, grown for its leaves, which have been chewed and brewed as a stimulant by Andean peoples for centuries and are the raw material from which cocaine is extracted.

Description

The plant is a shrub growing to 2–3 metres, with straight branches and small, oval, green leaves with characteristic curved lines on either side of the midrib. Its small white flowers produce red berries. It thrives in warm, humid mountain valleys of the eastern Andes in Peru, Bolivia, Colombia, and Ecuador.

Cultivation and use

Coca leaves are harvested several times a year and traditionally chewed with an alkaline substance such as lime to release their mild stimulant alkaloids, suppressing hunger, thirst, and altitude sickness. Coca cultivation is legally regulated internationally because of cocaine production, though traditional use remains legal and culturally important in Bolivia and Peru.
