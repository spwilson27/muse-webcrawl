Moderate

Moderate is a term used in politics to describe a person or position that avoids extreme views and generally occupies the centre of the political spectrum. Moderates typically favour incremental change, compromise between competing interests, and pragmatic policy-making over ideological purity.

Usage

In the United States, "moderate" is often applied to members of both major parties who dissent from their party's more ideological wings, such as moderate Republicans associated with the former Republican Main Street Partnership and moderate Democrats associated with the Blue Dog Coalition or the New Democrat Coalition. In many European multiparty systems, centrist parties explicitly brand themselves as moderate.

Criticism and analysis

Political scientists note that self-described moderates are a heterogeneous group: some hold genuinely centrist positions, others mix strong views from both left and right, and others are simply less politically engaged. Critics from both flanks sometimes portray moderation as indecisiveness, while supporters argue it reflects deliberation and openness to evidence.
