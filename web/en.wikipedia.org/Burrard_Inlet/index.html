Burrard Inlet is a relatively shallow coastal fjord in southwestern British Columbia, Canada. Formed during the last ice age, it separates the city of Vancouver and the rest of the low-lying Burrard Peninsula to the south from the coastal slopes of the North Shore Mountains, which span the districts of West Vancouver and the city and district of North Vancouver to the north.

The inlet runs roughly 25 kilometres (16 mi) from its mouth at Point Grey and Point Atkinson, between English Bay and the Strait of Georgia, eastward to Port Moody, where the Indian Arm branches off to the north. The main body of the inlet is the harbour of the Port of Vancouver, Canada's largest port.

History

The shores of Burrard Inlet were home to Indigenous peoples — principally the Tsleil-Waututh, Musqueam, and Squamish — for thousands of years before European contact. The inlet was charted by the Spanish expedition of José María Narváez in 1791 and explored the following year by Captain George Vancouver, who named it for his friend Sir Harry Burrard Neale.

During the 19th century the inlet became the centre of the region's lumber trade, with sawmills at Hastings Mill and Moodyville. The completion of the Canadian Pacific Railway to Port Moody in 1885, and shortly after to Vancouver, established the inlet as the Pacific terminus of Canada's transcontinental railway.

Crossings

Three crossings span the inlet: the Lions Gate Bridge (1938) at the First Narrows, the Ironworkers Memorial Second Narrows Crossing (1960) at the Second Narrows, and the Second Narrows Rail Bridge. A tunnel carries the SkyTrain's Expo Line beneath the inlet's western entrance via the Dunsmuir Tunnel under downtown Vancouver, and SeaBus passenger ferries cross between Waterfront Station and Lonsdale Quay.

Port and environment

The Port of Vancouver handles bulk commodities including grain, coal, potash, and containers, along with a major cruise ship terminal at Canada Place. The inlet supports salmon runs, shellfish beds, and marine bird habitat, and has been the site of environmental controversy, notably the 2007 oil spill from a ruptured pipeline in Burnaby and debates over the expansion of crude oil pipeline capacity to the Westridge Marine Terminal.
