Red beans and rice is an emblematic dish of Louisiana Creole cuisine, traditionally made with red kidney beans, vegetables of the "holy trinity" (bell pepper, onion and celery), spices such as thyme, cayenne pepper and bay leaf, and pork bones left over from Sunday's dinner, cooked slowly together and served over rice. The dish is customarily eaten on Mondays in New Orleans.

History

The Monday tradition is often traced to the household rhythms of 19th-century New Orleans: Monday was washday, and a pot of beans could simmer unattended for hours while laundry was done, flavoured with the hambone remaining from the traditional Sunday ham dinner. The dish also reflects the city's Caribbean connections — beans and rice combinations such as Haitian diri ak pwa arrived with refugees from Saint-Domingue in the early 1800s, who doubled the city's population and deepened its Creole foodways.

Preparation

Dried red beans are soaked, then simmered with the trinity, garlic, and smoked pork products such as ham hocks, pickled pork, or andouille sausage. As the beans soften, some are mashed against the side of the pot to give the dish its characteristically creamy texture. The beans are served over long-grain white rice, often with a grilled sausage on the side, cornbread, and hot sauce.

Cultural significance

Red beans and rice remains a staple of home kitchens, restaurants and festivals across Louisiana. The jazz trumpeter Louis Armstrong, a native of New Orleans, famously signed his letters "Red Beans and Ricely Yours". In 2023 a marker honouring the dish was placed in Armstrong Park, and the Monday ritual continues to be observed in homes and restaurants throughout the city.
