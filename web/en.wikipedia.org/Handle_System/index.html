Handle System

The Handle System is a proprietary technical specification for assigning, managing and resolving persistent identifiers for digital objects and other resources on the Internet. It was developed by Robert Kahn at the Corporation for National Research Initiatives (CNRI) as part of the Digital Object Architecture.

Design

A handle consists of a naming authority prefix and a local name, separated by a slash — for example 10.1000/182. The system resolves a handle to current information about the resource, such as its locations, through a distributed global resolution service backed by local handle services. Handles are designed to persist even as locations and ownership change.

Applications

The best-known application is the Digital Object Identifier (DOI) system, whose prefix range under the naming authority "10" uses Handle technology for resolution. The system is also used in digital libraries, publishing, and data repositories, and is described in IETF RFCs 3650, 3651 and 3652 as an informational specification.

Governance

Since 2014 the global Handle registry has been overseen by the DONA Foundation, a multi-stakeholder body coordinating the root resolution infrastructure.
