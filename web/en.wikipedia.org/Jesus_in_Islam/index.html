Jesus in Islam

In Islam, Jesus (Arabic: عيسى ابن مريم, romanized: ʿĪsā ibn Maryam, lit. 'Jesus, son of Mary') is believed to be the penultimate prophet and messenger of God and the Messiah (Arabic: al-Masīḥ). He is also considered to be the last prophet sent to the Children of Israel. In the Quran, Jesus is described as the Messiah (al-Masīḥ), miraculously born of a virgin, performing miracles, accompanied by his disciples, and rejected by the Jewish religious establishment.

Quranic narrative

Jesus is mentioned in the Quran 25 times by the name Isa, more often than Muhammad himself. The Quran affirms his virginal conception, his miracles — including speaking in the cradle, healing the blind and lepers, and raising the dead by God's permission — and his status as a righteous servant and prophet of God. Islam rejects the doctrine of the Trinity and the belief that Jesus is God or the son of God.

Crucifixion and ascension

The mainstream Islamic view is that Jesus was not crucified but was raised up by God to Heaven while alive; the Quran states "they did not kill him, nor did they crucify him; but [another] was made to resemble him to them" (Quran 4:157). Most Muslims believe Jesus will return to Earth before the Day of Judgment to restore justice and defeat the false messiah (al-Masīḥ ad-Dajjāl).
