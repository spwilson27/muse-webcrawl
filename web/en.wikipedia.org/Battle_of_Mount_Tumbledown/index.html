Battle of Mount Tumbledown

Mount Tumbledown, the last major Argentine strongpoint before Port Stanley, attacked on the night of 13–14 June 1982.

Date13–14 June 1982
LocationMount Tumbledown, East Falkland
ResultBritish victory
BelligerentsUnited Kingdom vs. Argentina

The Battle of Mount Tumbledown was an engagement of the Falklands War, fought on the night of 13–14 June 1982. The 2nd Battalion, Scots Guards, attacked the Argentine Marine Infantry Battalion 5 (BIM 5) dug in on Mount Tumbledown, the dominant height immediately west of Port Stanley.

Background

Following the capture of Mount Harriet and Two Sisters on 11–12 June, Tumbledown and Wireless Ridge were the last positions covering Stanley. BIM 5, a well-trained and well-equipped Argentine marine unit under Commander Carlos Robacio, had prepared strong positions among the rocky crags of the summit.

The battle

The Scots Guards attacked in three phases along the ridge from west to east. A diversionary attack by a small party on the southern slopes drew Argentine attention and fire while the main companies assaulted the western end. There followed hours of close-quarters fighting among the rocks, with Argentine marines resisting stubbornly; the eastern end of the mountain was not cleared until after dawn. A final Argentine counterattack was beaten off with artillery fire.

Outcome

The Scots Guards lost 9 men killed and 43 wounded; Argentine losses were about 30 killed. With Tumbledown and Wireless Ridge taken, the defence of Stanley collapsed, and Argentine commander Mario Menéndez surrendered all Argentine forces in the Falklands on the evening of 14 June 1982, ending the war.
