Battle of Achelous (917)

Battle of Achelous
Date: 20 August 917
Location: Achelous river, near Anchialus (modern Pomorie, Bulgaria)
Combatants: Byzantine Empire vs First Bulgarian Empire
Result: Decisive Bulgarian victory

The Battle of Achelous or Acheloos (Bulgarian: Битка при Ахелой), fought on 20 August 917 near the river Achelous and the coastal fortress of Anchialus (modern Pomorie) on the Black Sea coast of Bulgaria, was one of the largest and bloodiest battles of the Middle Ages. The army of the First Bulgarian Empire under Tsar Simeon I the Great annihilated a Byzantine expeditionary army led by the Domestic of the Schools Leo Phokas.

The battle as depicted in the Madrid Skylitzes manuscript.

Background and battle

The battle came during the Byzantine–Bulgarian war of 913–927, after Simeon had invaded Thrace and the regency government of the young Constantine VII mounted a major counteroffensive, aiming to crush Bulgaria with Pecheneg support from across the Danube — support that failed to materialise. The Byzantine army, perhaps 60,000 strong including the imperial tagmata, advanced north along the coast. Simeon concealed part of his army, including his heavy cavalry, behind hills. When the Byzantine line advanced, the Bulgarians feigned flight, then Simeon's reserve fell on the exposed Byzantine flank, causing a catastrophic rout; thousands drowned in the river or were cut down in the pursuit. Leo Phokas escaped to Mesembria.

The victory confirmed Bulgaria as the dominant power in the Balkans; Simeon pressed on to the walls of Constantinople, was recognised (in practice) as emperor, and won a further victory at Katasyrtai later in 917. Peace came only in 927, after his death.
