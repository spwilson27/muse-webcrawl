Lobelia

Scientific classification
Kingdom: Plantae
Order: Asterales
Family: Campanulaceae
Subfamily: Lobelioideae
Genus: Lobelia L.
Lobelia erinus, a popular bedding plant.

Lobelia is a genus of flowering plants comprising 415 species, with a subcosmopolitan distribution primarily in tropical to warm temperate regions of the world, a few species extending into cooler temperate regions. They are known generally as lobelias. The genus is named after the Flemish botanist Matthias de Lobel (1538–1616).

Description

Lobelia species range from small herbs to the giant lobelias of the African high mountains. The flowers are zygomorphic (bilaterally symmetrical), with five lobes, two above and three below, often in striking blue, violet, red or white. Many species contain alkaloids such as lobeline; Lobelia inflata (Indian tobacco) was traditionally used in herbal medicine and as a smoking-cessation aid.

Cultivation

Lobelia erinus (edging lobelia) is widely grown as an ornamental annual for borders, baskets and window boxes, valued for its masses of small blue, violet, pink or white flowers. Lobelia cardinalis (cardinal flower), with brilliant red spikes, is a popular perennial for moist soils in North American gardens and attracts hummingbirds.
