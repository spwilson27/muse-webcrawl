M270 Multiple Launch Rocket System

An M270 firing during a training exercise

Type: multiple launch rocket system
Origin: United States
In service: 1983–present
Calibre: 227 mm rockets; ATACMS missiles
Crew: 3

The M270 Multiple Launch Rocket System (M270 MLRS) is an American armored, self-propelled, multiple launch rocket system mounted on a tracked chassis derived from the Bradley Fighting Vehicle. It entered service with the US Army in 1983 and has been adopted by numerous NATO and allied countries.

System description

The launcher carries two pods, each holding six 227 mm rockets or one MGM-140 ATACMS tactical ballistic missile, and can be reloaded in minutes using an integrated crane. The three-person crew can fire from within the armoured cab. Unguided rockets carried hundreds of DPICM submunitions — earning the system the nickname "steel rain" in the 1991 Gulf War — while modern GMLRS rockets are GPS-guided unitary rounds with ranges beyond 80 km, and ATACMS reaches roughly 300 km.

Combat history

The M270 first saw combat in Operation Desert Storm (1991), where batteries fired thousands of rockets in preparation for the ground offensive. It has since been used in Iraq and Afghanistan. From 2022, the United States and United Kingdom supplied M270s and the related wheeled HIMARS launchers to Ukraine, where GMLRS strikes on Russian command posts and logistics attracted wide attention.

Variants

The improved M270A1 and M270A2 feature faster launchers, better fire control, and the ability to fire the extended-range ER GMLRS and the Precision Strike Missile (PrSM). European users including Britain, Germany, France, and Italy operate or are upgrading their fleets.
