Hammered dulcimer

The hammered dulcimer is a percussion-string instrument consisting of strings stretched over a trapezoidal resonant sound board, played by striking the strings with small hand-held hammers. It is a member of the zither family and an ancestor of the piano.

Construction and playing

Courses of two or more strings pass over bridges that divide them into tuned lengths, commonly giving a range of two to five octaves arranged in diatonic patterns across the board. Players hold light wooden hammers between thumb and fingers and strike, bounce and roll them across the strings. Related instruments include the Persian santur, Chinese yangqin, Hungarian cimbalom and Indian santoor.

History

The instrument spread through Europe by the medieval period and became popular in folk music in the British Isles and North America, particularly in Appalachian and old-time traditions. Its mechanism inspired the development of keyboard instruments with hammer actions.

Modern use

The hammered dulcimer features in folk, Celtic and world music, and in film and game soundtracks for its bright, shimmering tone.
