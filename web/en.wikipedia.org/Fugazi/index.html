Fugazi

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fugazi is an American post-hardcore band formed in Washington, D.C., in 1986 by guitarist and vocalist Ian MacKaye, formerly of Minor Threat. The band also includes guitarist/vocalist Guy Picciotto, bassist Joe Lally, and drummer Brendan Canty. Fugazi is noted for its DIY ethics, refusal of major-label offers, low ticket prices, opposition to moshing violence at shows, and a distinctive sound mixing punk, funk, reggae, and dub influences. The band has been on indefinite hiatus since 2003.

History

MacKaye founded Fugazi after the breakup of Embrace, recruiting Lally and Canty, with Picciotto joining as second guitarist and vocalist in 1987. Their debut EP Fugazi (1988) and the Margin Walker EP were combined as 13 Songs (1989), which included the widely known track "Waiting Room". The band's name is Vietnam-era military slang (an acronym for "Fucked Up, Got Ambushed, Zipped In [a body bag]"), taken from a veteran's memoir.

Music and albums

Subsequent albums — Repeater (1990), Steady Diet of Nothing (1991), In on the Kill Taker (1993), Red Medicine (1995), End Hits (1998), and The Argument (2001) — moved from lean post-hardcore toward more experimental arrangements, dynamic quiet-loud contrasts, and dub-influenced bass lines. All were released on MacKaye's independent label, Dischord Records.

Ethics and legacy

Fugazi famously kept concert admission around five dollars, declined merchandising beyond records, refused interviews with publications carrying alcohol and tobacco advertising, and would stop performances to address violence in the crowd. Although the band never had a chart hit, it sold millions of records independently and is regarded as one of the most influential American underground bands. The Fugazi Live Series has made hundreds of the band's concert recordings available online.
