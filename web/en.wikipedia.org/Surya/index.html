Surya

Surya driving his celestial chariot across the sky.

Surya (Sanskrit: सूर्य, "the Sun") is the Sun as well as the solar deity in Hinduism. He traditionally was one of the major five deities in the Smarta tradition, all of whom are considered equivalent deities in the Panchayatana puja and a means to realise Brahman. Other names of Surya in ancient Indian literature include Aditya, Arka, Bhanu, Savitr, Pushan, Ravi, Martanda, Mitra, Bhaskara and Vivasvan.

Surya is depicted with a Chakra, also interpreted as Dharmachakra. Surya rides a chariot driven by seven horses, which represent the seven colours of visible light, and seven days of the week. In Hinduism, Surya is considered a visible god (pratyaksha brahman) since one can see him every day.

Iconography

Surya is typically shown radiant, holding lotus flowers, accompanied by his charioteer Aruna, who is also the personification of dawn. Temples dedicated to Surya include the famous Konark Sun Temple in Odisha, a UNESCO World Heritage Site built in the form of a colossal chariot.

Worship

Surya is honoured in the Gayatri Mantra and the Aditya Hridayam, and the festival of Chhath in Bihar and Uttar Pradesh is dedicated to him. The Surya Namaskar ("salutation to the sun") sequence of yoga asanas also bears his name. In the Navagraha, Surya is the chief of the nine planetary deities.
