Prosecco

Prosecco is an Italian DOC or DOCG white wine produced in a large area spanning nine provinces in the Veneto and Friuli-Venezia Giulia regions of north-eastern Italy. It is made primarily from the Glera grape variety and is usually produced as a sparkling (spumante) or semi-sparkling (frizzante) wine, though a still (tranquillo) style also exists.

OriginVeneto and Friuli-Venezia Giulia, Italy

AppellationsProsecco DOC; Conegliano Valdobbiadene Prosecco DOCG; Asolo Prosecco DOCG
Sweetness levelsBrut, Extra Dry, Dry

Production

Unlike Champagne, which ferments in the bottle, Prosecco is normally produced with the Charmat (tank) method, in which the second fermentation takes place in large stainless-steel pressurised tanks. This preserves the grape's fresh, floral, and fruity aromas and makes production quicker and less costly, contributing to Prosecco's relatively modest price.

History

The wine takes its name from the village of Prosecco near Trieste. In 2009 the producing region was reorganised: the former IGT area became the Prosecco DOC, while the historic hills of Conegliano and Valdobbiadene received DOCG status; the Glera grape was officially renamed from "Prosecco" so that the name refers to the region and appellation rather than the variety. In 2019, the Prosecco Hills of Conegliano and Valdobbiadene were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Popularity and name protection

Prosecco sales grew enormously in the 2000s and 2010s, and it has at times outsold Champagne worldwide. Italy defends "Prosecco" as a protected designation of origin within the European Union and has contested its use for wines grown elsewhere, notably in Australia, where producers argue that Prosecco is a grape variety name; a rosé version, Prosecco Rosé DOC, was authorised in 2020.
