Battle of Bibracte

Battle of Bibracte
Date: 58 BC
Location: Near Bibracte, Gaul (modern France)
Result: Roman victory
Part of the Gallic Wars

The Battle of Bibracte was fought in 58 BC between the migrating Helvetii and six Roman legions under Gaius Julius Caesar. It was the first major battle of the Gallic Wars and took place near the Aedui oppidum of Bibracte, in modern-day France, possibly near Montmort.

Background

The Helvetii, a Celtic tribal confederation from the Swiss plateau, had resolved to migrate westward through Roman-allied territory. Caesar, governor of Gallia Narbonensis, blocked their passage at Geneva and then pursued them northward as they moved through the lands of the Sequani and Aedui. After a skirmish on the Arar (Saône), Caesar's legions shadowed the migration until supply problems forced him to turn toward Bibracte for grain; the Helvetii chose that moment to attack.

The battle

Caesar deployed four veteran legions in three lines (the triplex acies) on a hillside, with two newly raised legions guarding the baggage on the summit. The Helvetii attacked uphill and fought fiercely; the Roman flanks were then threatened by the Boii and Tulingi arriving in the Helvetian rear guard, forcing Caesar to wheel his third line about to face both ways. After fighting that lasted into the night around the Helvetian wagon laager, the tribal host broke. Caesar reported enormous numbers of migrants — modern historians treat his figures cautiously — and ordered the survivors to return home and rebuild their settlements as a buffer against Germanic peoples.
