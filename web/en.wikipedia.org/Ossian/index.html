Ossian


Claimed author: Ossian, son of Fingal
Published by: James Macpherson, 1760–1765
Genre: Epic poetry

Ossian is the narrator and purported author of a cycle of epic poems published by the Scottish poet James Macpherson, beginning with Fragments of Ancient Poetry in 1760 and continuing with Fingal (1761/62) and Temora (1763). Macpherson claimed the works were translations of ancient Gaelic poetry by Ossian, a blind bard supposedly living in the third century, son of the hero Fingal (based on the Irish hero Fionn mac Cumhaill).

The authenticity controversy

The poems were an international sensation but almost immediately controversial. Samuel Johnson demanded to see the original manuscripts and famously declared the work a forgery after Macpherson failed to produce them. Irish scholars objected that Macpherson had appropriated and recast genuine Fenian-cycle material. Modern scholarship regards the poems as largely Macpherson's own compositions, drawing on authentic Gaelic ballads and oral tradition which he reshaped, though a posthumous Gaelic edition appeared in 1807.

Influence

Despite the doubts, Ossian profoundly influenced European Romanticism. Goethe quoted the poems in The Sorrows of Young Werther, Napoleon carried them on campaign, and composers including Schubert and Mendelssohn drew on their misty, heroic melancholy. The poems helped spark interest in folk literature and national epics across Europe.
