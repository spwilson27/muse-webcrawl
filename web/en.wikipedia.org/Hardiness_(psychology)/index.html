Hardiness (psychology)

FieldPersonality psychology, health psychology
Introduced bySuzanne Kobasa (1979)
ComponentsCommitment, control, challenge

Hardiness is a personality trait, developed by psychologist Suzanne Kobasa, that describes a pattern of attitudes which buffers people against the negative health effects of stress. Kobasa identified it in studies of business executives who remained healthy despite high levels of stressful life events.

The three Cs

Hardiness consists of three interrelated dispositions: commitment (engagement with life's activities rather than alienation), control (the belief that one can influence events rather than feeling powerless), and challenge (viewing change as an opportunity for growth rather than a threat).

Research

Subsequent research, including work by Salvatore Maddi and the Hardiness Institute, has applied the concept to soldiers, athletes, students, and emergency workers. Hardiness training programmes aim to strengthen coping by reframing stressful situations and building social support.

Criticism

Critics have questioned the trait's measurement, its overlap with negative affectivity (neuroticism), and whether its three components form a single coherent construct; findings on its protective effects have been mixed.
