Tagish

Tagish may refer to the Tagish people, a First Nations people of the southern Yukon and northern British Columbia, Canada, or to their language, an Athabaskan language closely related to Tahltan and Kaska. The Tagish language is severely endangered, with only a handful of fluent first-language speakers recorded in the 21st century.

People and history

The Tagish lived around Tagish Lake and the upper Yukon River drainage, intermarrying extensively with coastal Tlingit traders, whose language and clan system many adopted. Tagish prospectors — Keish (Skookum Jim Mason), Káa Goox (Dawson Charlie), and Shaaw Tláa (Kate Carmack) — were members of the party whose 1896 gold discovery on Bonanza Creek triggered the Klondike Gold Rush.

Language

Tagish is a Northern Athabaskan language, highly similar to Tahltan and Kaska, with which it forms a dialect complex sometimes called Tahltan–Tagish–Kaska. Intensive documentation, including audio recordings of elder speakers such as Angela Sidney (a renowned storyteller), was carried out from the 1970s onward. Language revitalisation programmes are conducted through the Carcross/Tagish First Nation and the Yukon Native Language Centre.
