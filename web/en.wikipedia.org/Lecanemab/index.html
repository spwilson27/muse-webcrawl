Lecanemab

Trade nameLeqembi
TypeMonoclonal antibody
TargetAmyloid beta (soluble protofibrils)
DevelopersEisai and Biogen
RouteIntravenous infusion

Lecanemab, sold under the brand name Leqembi, is a monoclonal antibody medication used for the treatment of Alzheimer's disease. It targets amyloid beta, binding preferentially to soluble amyloid-beta protofibrils, and is intended to slow the progression of the disease in people with early Alzheimer's (mild cognitive impairment or mild dementia) with confirmed amyloid pathology.

Clinical evidence

In the phase 3 Clarity AD trial of about 1,800 participants, lecanemab reduced decline on the Clinical Dementia Rating–Sum of Boxes scale by about 27 percent relative to placebo over 18 months, alongside substantial reductions in brain amyloid measured by PET imaging. Critics note the absolute effect is modest and debate its clinical meaningfulness.

Approval

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration granted lecanemab accelerated approval in January 2023 and traditional (full) approval in July 2023. It has since been approved in several other countries, though regulators in some jurisdictions initially declined or restricted approval over the benefit–risk balance.

Safety

The principal risks are amyloid-related imaging abnormalities (ARIA) — brain swelling (ARIA-E) or microhemorrhages (ARIA-H) — which are more frequent in carriers of the APOE ε4 gene and in people taking anticoagulants. Treatment requires MRI monitoring and biweekly infusions (with a subcutaneous maintenance option approved later).
