Huntsman spider

Huntsman spiders, members of the family Sparassidae, are large, fast-moving spiders distributed worldwide in warm climates, known for hunting prey actively rather than building webs to catch it. The family includes more than 1,300 described species in over 80 genera. They were previously also known as giant crab spiders, owing to the way their legs bend forward at the joints, and as wood spiders in some regions.

Description

Sparassids are eight-eyed spiders with legs that extend sideways (laterigrade), allowing them to move quickly in any direction and to flatten themselves into narrow crevices under bark and rocks. Most are drab brown or grey, providing camouflage. Body lengths are typically 1–3 cm, but leg spans can reach 15 cm in common species, and the giant huntsman spider (Heteropoda maxima) of Laos has a leg span of up to 30 cm, the largest of any spider by span.

Behaviour

Huntsman spiders are nocturnal hunters that pursue and seize insects and other invertebrates with speed rather than silk traps, though they produce silk for draglines, shelters, and egg sacs. Many species live under loose bark or in leaf retreats; some, such as the social huntsman (Delena cancerides) of Australia, live in colonies. Females guard their egg sacs and young vigorously.

Venom

The venom of huntsman spiders is effective against insects but not considered medically dangerous to humans; a bite may cause local pain and swelling. Species of the genus Polybetes in South America were historically (and mistakenly) feared as dangerous.
