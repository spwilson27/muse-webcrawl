Limiting reagent


The limiting reagent (or limiting reactant) in a chemical reaction is the reactant that is totally consumed, limiting the amount of product formed.

The limiting reagent (or limiting reactant or limiting agent) in a chemical reaction is a reactant that is totally consumed when the chemical reaction is completed. The amount of product formed is limited by this reagent, since the reaction cannot continue without it.

Determining the limiting reagent

To find the limiting reagent, convert the amount of each reactant to moles of product using the stoichiometric ratios of the balanced equation; the reactant yielding the least product is limiting. The others are present in excess, and some of each remains unreacted.

Example

In the combustion of hydrogen, 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, if 10 mol of hydrogen react with 3 mol of oxygen, oxygen is limiting: the hydrogen requires only 5 mol of hydrogen per 3 mol of O₂, so 6 mol of water form and 4 mol of hydrogen are left over.
