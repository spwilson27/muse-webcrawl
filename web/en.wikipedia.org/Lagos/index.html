Lagos (Yoruba: Èkó) is the largest city in Nigeria and one of the largest cities in Africa, a sprawling port and financial centre on the Gulf of Guinea. It was the capital of Nigeria until 1991, when the seat of government moved to Abuja; it remains the country's economic capital.

Geography

The city spreads from the shores of Lagos Lagoon — a large lagoon protected from the Atlantic by barrier islands — across the mainland districts north and west. Key areas include Lagos Island, Ikoyi, Victoria Island, Lekki, Ikeja (the state capital) and Surulere. Its low-lying lagoon geography makes it vulnerable to flooding and sea-level rise.

History

Originally an Awori Yoruba settlement called Eko, Lagos was a major port in the transatlantic slave trade before Britain annexed it as a colony in 1861. It became the capital of colonial and then independent Nigeria, growing explosively after independence with rural-urban migration.

Economy and culture

Lagos is Nigeria's commercial hub: its ports handle most of the country's imports, and it hosts the film industry known as Nollywood, major banks, tech startups ("Yabacon Valley") and vibrant markets and music scenes, from Afrobeat's birthplace to modern Afrobeats. Traffic congestion is legendary, and a light rail system, the Lagos Rail Mass Transit, began operating in 2023.
