Horatio Nelson

Vice-Admiral Horatio Nelson, 1st Viscount Nelson (29 September 1758 – 21 October 1805) was a British flag officer in the Royal Navy famed for inspirational leadership, audacious tactics, and victories over Napoleonic France. He was killed at the height of his greatest triumph, the Battle of Trafalgar.

Career

Nelson joined the navy at twelve and rose rapidly. He lost the sight of one eye at Calvi (1794) and his right arm at Santa Cruz de Tenerife (1797). At the Battle of the Nile (1798) he destroyed the French fleet in Aboukir Bay; at Copenhagen (1801) he famously put his telescope to his blind eye to ignore a signal to disengage. His affair with Emma Hamilton, wife of the ambassador to Naples, scandalised society.

Trafalgar and death

On 21 October 1805, commanding from HMS Victory, Nelson attacked the combined French and Spanish fleets off Cape Trafalgar in two columns, breaking the enemy line. The victory ended any threat of a French invasion of Britain and established British naval supremacy for a century. Nelson was shot by a French marksman and died below decks, having confirmed the fleet's success; his reputed last words include "Thank God I have done my duty."

Legacy

Nelson's Column in London's Trafalgar Square commemorates him, and his signal "England expects that every man will do his duty" remains iconic.
