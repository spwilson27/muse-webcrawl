Watertown is a city in, and the county seat of, Jefferson County, New York, United States. It lies about 25 miles (40 km) south of the Thousand Islands and the Canadian border, along the Black River, whose falls powered the mills that gave the settlement its name and early industry.

History

Settlers arrived around 1800, attracted by the water power of the Black River. Watertown grew into a manufacturing center producing paper, machinery, and the famous Davis sewing machines. The Roswell P. Flower Memorial Library, opened in 1904, is a notable landmark. Nearby Fort Drum, home of the U.S. Army's 10th Mountain Division, is a major economic driver for the region.

Geography and climate

The city has a humid continental climate heavily influenced by lake-effect snow from Lake Ontario, making it one of the snowiest cities in the eastern United States. The Black River runs through the city with several falls and rapids used for whitewater recreation.

Notable facts

Watertown claims a place in popular culture as an inspiration cited in connection with Frank Capra's film It's a Wonderful Life, and it was the longtime home of newspaper magnate and politician Roswell Flower, a governor of New York.
