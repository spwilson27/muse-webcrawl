World Englishes

World Englishes is a term for emerging localised or indigenised varieties of English, especially varieties that have developed in territories once colonised by the British Empire or in countries where English is used as an additional language. The study of World Englishes is concerned with the identification of varieties of English used around the world and with how they have developed distinct linguistic, cultural, and pragmatic features.

Kachru's Three Circles

The most influential model of the spread of English is Braj Kachru's model of three concentric circles. The Inner Circle comprises countries where English is the primary language, such as the United Kingdom, the United States, Australia, Canada, and New Zealand. The Outer Circle includes countries where English plays an important institutional role, such as India, Nigeria, Singapore, and the Philippines. The Expanding Circle covers countries where English is taught as a foreign language, such as China, Japan, Brazil, and much of Europe.

Classification of varieties

World Englishes are commonly classified into varieties such as Indian English, Nigerian English, Singapore English, Philippine English, and South African English. These varieties differ from Inner Circle Englishes in pronunciation, vocabulary, grammar, and discourse conventions, reflecting the cultures and other languages of their speakers.

Scholarship

The journal World Englishes, founded in 1985, is a leading forum for research in the field. Scholars debate issues such as standardisation, the ownership of English, intelligibility across varieties, and the implications of World Englishes for language teaching and testing.
