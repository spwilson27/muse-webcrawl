Sherlock Holmes

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: the detective in profile with pipe and deerstalker]

Sherlock Holmes is a fictional detective created by British author Arthur Conan Doyle. Referring to himself as a "consulting detective" in his stories, Holmes is known for his proficiency with observation, deduction, forensic science and logical reasoning that borders on the fantastic, which he employs when investigating cases for a wide variety of clients, including Scotland Yard.

The character and stories have had a profound and lasting effect on mystery writing and popular culture as a whole. The stories were originally published as serials in magazines, beginning with A Study in Scarlet in 1887, and Holmes first name, "Sherlock", and surname "Holmes" became widely recognised. The canon of Holmes stories comprises four novels and 56 short stories, mostly narrated by his friend and biographer Dr. John Watson, with whom he shares rooms at 221B Baker Street, London.

Character

Holmes describes his method as the "science of deduction": from seemingly trivial details — a splash of mud, the calluses on a hand, the ash of a cigar — he reconstructs the histories and identities of strangers. He is accompanied by Watson, whose conventional intellect serves as a foil, and opposed by the criminal mastermind Professor Moriarty. He is also assisted occasionally by his elder brother Mycroft Holmes and the Baker Street Irregulars.

Although not the first fictional detective, Holmes is arguably the best known. His distinctive accessories — the deerstalker hat and curved pipe — owe as much to illustrator Sidney Paget and stage actor William Gillette as to Doyle's text. The phrase "Elementary, my dear Watson" is famous despite never appearing verbatim in the canon.

Legacy

Holmes has been portrayed in hundreds of films, television series, plays, and pastiches, holding a Guinness World Record as the most portrayed human literary character in film and television. The Sherlock Holmes Museum stands at 221B Baker Street in London.
