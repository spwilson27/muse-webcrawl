Judas Maccabeus

Judas Maccabeus (Judah Maccabee)
Died: 160 BC, Elasa
Father: Mattathias
Known for: Leading the Maccabean Revolt; rededicating the Temple

Judas Maccabeus (died 160 BC) was a Jewish priest and military leader who, together with his father Mattathias and his brothers, led the Maccabean Revolt against the Seleucid Empire and the Hellenistic influences it sought to impose on Judea. His surname, Maccabeus, is traditionally explained as deriving from the Aramaic for "hammer," reflecting his prowess in battle.

Revolt

The revolt began in 167 BC after the Seleucid king Antiochus IV Epiphanes outlawed Jewish religious practice and desecrated the Temple in Jerusalem. When Mattathias died in 166 BC, Judas assumed command of the rebel forces. Employing guerrilla tactics in the Judean hill country, he won a series of victories over larger Seleucid armies, including at Beth Horon, Emmaus (165 BC), and Beth Zur.

Rededication of the Temple

In December 164 BC Judas liberated Jerusalem and purified and rededicated the Temple — an event commemorated by the festival of Hanukkah, according to the Books of the Maccabees in which a small cruse of oil miraculously burned for eight days. Judas went on to campaign in Transjordan, Galilee, and Idumaea to protect Jewish communities there, and he sent an embassy to Rome seeking an alliance.

Death and legacy

Judas was killed at the Battle of Elasa in 160 BC, fighting the Seleucid general Bacchides. Leadership passed to his brothers Jonathan and then Simon, who ultimately secured Judean independence and founded the Hasmonean dynasty. Judas is remembered as one of the great heroes of Jewish history and, in Christian tradition, as one of the Nine Worthies.
