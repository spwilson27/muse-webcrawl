Fourteenth Army (United Kingdom)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fourteenth Army

Active: 1943–1945

Country: British Empire

Commander: Lt-Gen. William Slim

Nickname: The "Forgotten Army"

The British Fourteenth Army was a multi-national force comprising units from Commonwealth countries during the Second World War. Many of its units were from the Indian Army as well as British units, and there were also significant contributions from West and East African divisions. It was often given the nickname the "Forgotten Army" because its operations in the Burma Campaign were overlooked by the contemporary press, and remained more obscure than those in the European theatre for long after the war.

History

The Fourteenth Army was formed in India in late 1943 under Lieutenant General William Slim, previously commander of XV Corps. Its principal formations were IV Corps and XV Corps, later joined by XXXIII Corps. After halting the Japanese offensive against India at the battles of Imphal and Kohima in 1944 — among the largest defeats suffered by the Imperial Japanese Army on land — Slim's army advanced through Burma in 1944–45, retaking Mandalay and Rangoon (Operation Dracula) and driving the Japanese from the country by the war's end.

Composition and character

At over a million men at its peak, the Fourteenth Army was one of the largest Commonwealth armies of the war, drawing soldiers from Britain, India, Nepal (Gurkhas), Burma, and West and East Africa. Supply in Burma's difficult terrain depended heavily on air transport, mule companies, and local labour. Slim's memoir Defeat into Victory is widely regarded as one of the finest military memoirs in English, and Slim himself is often ranked among Britain's greatest generals.
