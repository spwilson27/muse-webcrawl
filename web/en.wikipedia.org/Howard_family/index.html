Howard family

The Howard family is an English aristocratic family founded by John Howard, created Duke of Norfolk in 1483 by King Richard III. The Howards have been Premier Peers of England for centuries, holding the dukedom of Norfolk and the hereditary office of Earl Marshal. The senior line's principal seat is Arundel Castle in West Sussex.

History

The family rose to prominence in the 15th century. John Howard, a loyal supporter of Richard III, was killed at the Battle of Bosworth in 1485, and the dukedom was forfeited. His son Thomas Howard, Earl of Surrey, restored the family's fortunes, winning the Battle of Flodden against the Scots in 1513, and was restored as Duke of Norfolk in 1514.

Under the Tudors, Howard relatives included two queens consort of Henry VIII — Anne Boleyn (whose mother was a Howard) and Catherine Howard — as well as the poet Henry Howard, Earl of Surrey. Despite periods of disgrace and execution under Henry VIII and Elizabeth I, and despite the senior line's adherence to Roman Catholicism after the Reformation, the family retained its position. Cadet branches include the Earls of Carlisle, Suffolk, and Effingham.

As Earl Marshal, the Duke of Norfolk is responsible for organising major state occasions such as coronations and state funerals.
