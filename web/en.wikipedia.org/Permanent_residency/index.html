Permanent residency

A United States Permanent Resident Card (green card).

Permanent residency is a person's legal resident status in a country or territory of which such person is not a citizen but where they have the right to reside on a permanent basis. This is usually for a permanent period; a person with such legal status is known as a permanent resident. In the United States, such a person is referred to as a Lawful Permanent Resident (LPR), colloquially a green card holder.

Overview

Permanent residency itself is distinct from the right of abode in that it may be conditional and is generally subject to requirements: permanent residents typically must actually reside in the country for a minimum period, and status can be lost through prolonged absence, serious criminal offending, or fraud in obtaining it. Permanent residents generally enjoy most of the rights of citizens — including the right to work without a permit and access to many public services — but ordinarily cannot vote in national elections, hold certain public offices, or obtain the country's passport.

Paths to permanent residency

Countries commonly grant permanent residence through channels such as:

  • Family reunification (spouses, children, and parents of citizens or residents)
  • Employment and skilled-worker immigration programs
  • Investment and business immigration
  • Refugee protection and asylum
  • Long-term residence after a period on temporary visas
  • Points-based selection systems, as in Canada and Australia

By jurisdiction

In the European Union, citizens of member states enjoy freedom of movement and may acquire permanent residence in another member state after five years of legal residence under Directive 2004/38/EC; long-term resident status for third-country nationals is governed by Directive 2003/109/EC. The United Kingdom grants settled status (indefinite leave to remain); Canada issues permanent resident cards; Australia grants permanent visas; and Japan maintains a permanent resident status (eijūsha). In many systems, permanent residence is a common prerequisite step toward naturalization as a citizen.
