Hanukkah

Hanukkah
Also: Festival of Lights
Duration: 8 nights, from 25 Kislev

Hanukkah (Hebrew: חֲנֻכָּה, "dedication"), also known as the Festival of Lights, is an eight-day Jewish festival commemorating the rededication of the Second Temple in Jerusalem in 164 BCE, following the Maccabean Revolt against the Seleucid Empire.

Story

According to tradition recorded in the Talmud, when Judah Maccabee and his followers purified the Temple they found only one small cruse of consecrated oil, enough to light the menorah for a single day — yet it burned miraculously for eight days, until new oil could be prepared. The Books of the Maccabees, which recount the revolt, frame the festival instead as a delayed celebration of Sukkot.

Observances

Each night an additional candle is lit on the nine-branched hanukkiah, using the helper candle (shamash), accompanied by blessings and songs such as Ma'oz Tzur. Customs include eating foods fried in oil, such as latkes (potato pancakes) and sufganiyot (jelly doughnuts), giving Hanukkah gelt (coins or chocolate coins) to children, and playing with the dreidel, a four-sided spinning top. Because it falls near Christmas, Hanukkah has acquired heightened cultural prominence in Western countries, though religiously it is a relatively minor festival.
