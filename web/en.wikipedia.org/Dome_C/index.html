Dome C

LocationAntarctic Plateau, 75°06′S 123°21′E
Elevation3,233 m (10,607 ft)
StationConcordia Station (France/Italy), opened 2005

Dome C, also known as Dome Charlie or Dome Circe, is a high point of the Antarctic ice sheet on the polar plateau of East Antarctica, at an elevation of about 3,233 metres. It is the site of the French-Italian Concordia Station and of the EPICA deep ice-core drilling project.

Climate

Dome C has one of the coldest, driest climates on Earth. Mean annual temperature is about −54.5 °C; winter temperatures fall below −80 °C, and the absolute humidity is extremely low, making the site comparable to a cold desert. The ice at Dome C is about 3,270 metres thick above bedrock.

Ice-core science

The European Project for Ice Coring in Antarctica (EPICA) drilled at Dome C between 1996 and 2004, recovering a core reaching ice about 800,000 years old — for a time the oldest continuous ice core record — which provided a landmark reconstruction of past climate and atmospheric composition across eight glacial cycles. The successor Beyond EPICA project aims to recover ice up to 1.5 million years old.

Astronomy

The site's high altitude, stable atmosphere, low water vapour, and long polar night make Dome C one of the best ground-based observing sites on Earth, with exceptionally good astronomical "seeing" measured above the turbulent boundary layer. Telescopes and site-testing instruments from several national programmes have operated there.
