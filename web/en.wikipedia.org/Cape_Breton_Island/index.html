Cape Breton Island is an island at the eastern end of Nova Scotia, Canada, in the Atlantic Ocean. It accounts for about 18 percent of the province's land area. The island is separated from mainland Nova Scotia by the Strait of Canso and has been joined to it since 1955 by the rock-filled Canso Causeway.

The Cabot Trail winding through the Cape Breton Highlands.

Geography

A large saltwater lake, Bras d'Or Lake, dominates the island's centre, connected to the sea by channels at both ends. The Cape Breton Highlands rise in the north, protected within Cape Breton Highlands National Park, through which the scenic Cabot Trail highway loops. The main population centre is the Cape Breton Regional Municipality in the east, centred on Sydney, historically built on coal mining and steel making.

History and culture

The island — Unama'ki — is part of the traditional territory of the Mi'kmaq people. French colonists established settlements in the seventeenth century and built the great fortress of Louisbourg, twice besieged by British forces (1745 and 1758) and now a National Historic Site and major reconstruction. In the nineteenth century large numbers of Scottish Gaelic settlers arrived, and Cape Breton retains a strong Gaelic and Celtic music culture, celebrated at the annual Celtic Colours festival. Coal mining and steel dominated the industrial east through the twentieth century before declining; tourism, fisheries, and culture now anchor much of the economy.
