Guilt (emotion)

Guilt is a moral emotion that arises when a person believes, accurately or not, that they have violated a moral standard or caused harm to others. It is characterised by feelings of remorse, regret and a desire to confess, apologise or make reparation.

Guilt and shame

Psychologists distinguish guilt from shame: guilt focuses on a specific behaviour ("I did a bad thing"), while shame focuses on the self ("I am bad"). Researchers such as June Tangney have found that guilt-proneness is generally associated with empathy and constructive responses to wrongdoing, whereas shame-proneness correlates more with withdrawal, anger and psychological symptoms.

Functions and pathology

Guilt is thought to serve a social function, motivating people to maintain relationships, reciprocate help and conform to group norms. Survivor guilt — distress at having survived when others did not — was first described in survivors of the Holocaust and combat. Excessive or misplaced guilt is a feature of depression and obsessive–compulsive disorder, where sufferers may feel responsible for events beyond their control. Cultures differ in how strongly they emphasise guilt versus shame in social control, a contrast explored by anthropologists such as Ruth Benedict.
