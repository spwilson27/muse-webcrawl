Area under the curve (pharmacokinetics)

Area under the curve (AUC)

UnitsConcentration × time (e.g. mg·h/L)
Related quantitiesBioavailability, clearance, Cmax, Tmax

In pharmacology and pharmacokinetics, the area under the curve (AUC) is the integral of the concentration of a drug in blood plasma over time after a dose. It represents the total systemic exposure to the drug and is one of the most important parameters in the study of a drug's absorption, distribution and elimination.

Definition

AUC = ∫0∞ C(t) dt

In practice, the AUC is estimated from a series of timed blood samples using the trapezoidal rule, summing the areas between consecutive sampling points from time zero to the last measurable concentration (AUC0–t), with the remaining tail to infinity estimated from the terminal elimination rate constant (AUC0–∞).

Interpretation

For an intravenous dose, AUC equals dose divided by clearance (AUC = D/CL). Comparing the AUC of an oral (or other extravascular) dose with the intravenous AUC gives the absolute bioavailability of the formulation. Two products are generally considered bioequivalent by regulators such as the FDA and EMA when the 90% confidence interval for the ratio of their AUCs (and peak concentrations, Cmax) falls within 80–125%.

Variants

  • AUC0–t — area from dosing to the last quantifiable concentration.
  • AUC0–∞ — total area including the extrapolated tail.
  • AUCτ — area over one dosing interval at steady state.
  • AUMC — area under the first moment curve, used to calculate mean residence time (MRT = AUMC/AUC).
