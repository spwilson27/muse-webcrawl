Win-win game

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A win-win game is a game in game theory designed so that all participants can profit from it in one way or another. In conflict resolution, a win-win strategy is a collaborative strategy and conflict-resolution process that aims to accommodate all disputants who must all be satisfied with the process and its outcome.

Concept

Win-win games are the opposite of zero-sum games, in which one player's gain is exactly balanced by another's loss, and of win-lose games, in which a winner's success comes at a loser's expense. Because the total of gains and losses is positive, they are also called positive-sum games. Classic examples include trade between two parties, in which both sides value what they receive more than what they give up, and many forms of negotiated agreement.

Applications

In negotiation theory, "win-win" became a popular term for interest-based bargaining, popularised by Getting to Yes (1981), which advises negotiators to focus on underlying interests rather than fixed positions, invent options for mutual gain, and use objective criteria. In business, win-win outcomes are sought in supplier relationships, joint ventures and labour negotiations, on the argument that agreements benefiting all parties are more durable than ones imposed by the stronger side. Critics note that real disputes sometimes involve genuinely incompatible interests, and that indiscriminate use of the term can obscure power imbalances.
