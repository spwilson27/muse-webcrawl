Building code

A building code (also building control or building regulations) is a set of rules that specify the standards for the design and construction of buildings and other structures. Building codes are intended to protect public health, safety and general welfare as they relate to the construction and occupancy of buildings, covering structural integrity, fire safety, egress, sanitation, ventilation, accessibility and increasingly energy efficiency.

History

Rules governing construction are ancient: the Code of Hammurabi imposed strict penalties on builders whose houses collapsed. After great urban fires and disasters, cities progressively regulated building materials and separation — London following the Great Fire of 1666 being a notable example. Modern comprehensive codes emerged in the 19th and 20th centuries.

Model codes and enforcement

In many countries, codes are developed as model codes by standards organisations and then adopted, sometimes with amendments, by national, state or municipal governments, which gives them legal force. In the United States the International Building Code (IBC), published by the International Code Council, is widely adopted; in England and Wales the Building Regulations 2010 apply, supported by Approved Documents. Compliance is enforced through planning permission, plan review, and inspections during construction, with an occupancy permit issued at completion. Codes distinguish prescriptive requirements from performance-based approaches, in which designers must demonstrate that a building meets stated performance goals.
