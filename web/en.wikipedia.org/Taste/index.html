Taste

Taste is the perception stimulated when a substance in the mouth reacts chemically with taste receptor cells located on taste buds in the oral cavity, mostly on the tongue. Taste, along with olfaction and trigeminal nerve stimulation (registering texture, pain, and temperature), determines the flavors of food and other substances. Humans have taste receptors on taste buds and other areas, including the upper surface of the tongue and the epiglottis. The gustatory cortex is responsible for the perception of taste.

The tongue is covered with thousands of small bumps called papillae, which are visible to the naked eye. Within each papilla are several taste buds. There are between 2,000 and 5,000 taste buds that are located on the back and front of the tongue. Others are located on the roof, sides and back of the mouth, and in the throat. Each taste bud contains around 50 to 100 taste receptor cells.

Basic tastes

The five basic tastes are sweetness, sourness, saltiness, bitterness, and savoriness (umami). Sweetness is produced by the presence of sugars and a few other substances. Saltiness is a taste produced by the presence of sodium ions. Sourness is acidity and is sensed by hydrogen (H+) channel proteins. Bitterness is one of the most sensitive of the tastes, and many perceive it as unpleasant, sharp, or disagagreeable, though it is sometimes desirable. Umami, the savory taste, was identified by Japanese chemist Kikunae Ikeda in 1908 and is associated with glutamates such as monosodium glutamate.

Measurement and perception

Taste is a form of chemoreception that occurs in the specialised taste receptors in the mouth. To date, there are five known types of taste receptors. The gustatory system or sense of taste is the sensory system that is partially responsible for the perception of taste. Taste is sensed in the taste buds on the tongue.

Functional roles

Taste guides organisms toward energy-rich foods and away from potentially harmful substances. Sweet taste signals carbohydrates, salt taste helps regulate electrolyte balance, sour taste can indicate unripe or spoiled food, and bitter taste often warns of toxins. The system works in concert with the sense of smell: much of what is commonly called taste is actually contributed by olfaction, which is why food seems bland when the nose is congested.
