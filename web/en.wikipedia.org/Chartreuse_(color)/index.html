Definition

Chartreuse (US: /ʃɑːrˈtruːz, -ˈtruːs/, UK: /-ˈtrɜːz/), also known as yellow-green, is a color between yellow and green. It takes its name from Chartreuse, a French liqueur made by Carthusian monks since 1737, which comes in green and yellow versions. The first recorded use of chartreuse as a color name in English was in 1892.

Varieties

In web colors, chartreuse is defined exactly halfway between green and yellow on the RGB color wheel, at hex code #7FFF00. The term is sometimes also applied to the more yellowish chartreuse yellow, close to the color of yellow Chartreuse liqueur, and historically it was used in the late 19th century for a range of yellowish greens popular in fashion.

In nature and culture

Yellow-green hues are common in nature — in new leaves, unripe fruit, and various insects and amphibians, where they serve as camouflage. Because the human eye is highly sensitive to wavelengths in this region, chartreuse and similar high-visibility yellow-greens are used for safety equipment, emergency vehicles in some countries, and the standard color of tennis balls (optic yellow).
