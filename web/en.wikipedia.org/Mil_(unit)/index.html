Mil (unit)

Unit ofLength
Symbolmil
DefinitionOne thousandth of an inch
SI equivalent25.4 micrometres
Also calledThou

A mil is a unit of length equal to one thousandth of an inch (0.001 in), equivalent to exactly 25.4 micrometres. It is widely used in engineering and manufacturing in the United States to specify thicknesses and tolerances where metric units are not customary. In British usage the same unit is traditionally called a thou, avoiding confusion with the millimetre or the Scandinavian mil (10 km).

Applications

The mil is standard for describing the thickness of plastic sheeting and bags, paper and coatings, the diameter of wire and guitar strings, and clearances and tolerances in machining. In printed circuit board design, track widths and spacings are conventionally given in mils (or "thou"), a legacy of the inch-based grid systems used in electronics CAD.

Distinctions

The mil should not be confused with the millimetre (about 39.37 mil), the angular mil used in military contexts (approximately 1/6400 of a circle, related to the milliradian), or the "mil" of the moulding and lumber trades. In materials science, corrosion rates are sometimes expressed in mils per year (mpy).

History

The unit emerged in the 19th century as machine tools made measurements to thousandths of an inch routine; Joseph Whitworth and others promoted decimal subdivisions of the inch. It survives in US customary engineering despite the general trend toward metrication elsewhere.
