Symmetry

Symmetry (from Greek συμμετρία, "agreement in dimensions, due proportion") is the property by which something remains invariant under some transformation — reflection, rotation, translation or scaling. It is a central concept in mathematics, physics, chemistry, biology, art and architecture.

In mathematics

The symmetries of an object form a group under composition. Plane patterns are classified by the 17 wallpaper groups and frieze groups; regular polyhedra have the Platonic symmetry groups. The symmetric group collects all permutations of a set and bounds the possible symmetry groups of finite objects.

In physics

Noether's theorem (1915) proves that every continuous symmetry of a physical system implies a conservation law: time-translation symmetry gives conservation of energy, spatial translation gives momentum, and rotational symmetry gives angular momentum. Gauge symmetries underlie the Standard Model, and symmetry breaking — as in the Higgs mechanism and ferromagnetism — explains how symmetric laws produce asymmetric outcomes.

In nature and art

Most animals show bilateral symmetry; flowers and starfish show radial symmetry; snowflakes show sixfold symmetry. Humans find symmetric faces attractive, and symmetry structures Islamic geometric art, Gothic cathedrals and the Sydney Opera House shells alike.
