Louis Albert Necker
Born: 10 April 1786, Geneva
Died: 20 November 1861, Portree, Isle of Skye
Known for: the Necker cube

Louis Albert Necker de Saussure (10 April 1786 – 20 November 1861) was a Swiss crystallographer and geographer, best remembered for the optical illusion known as the Necker cube, which he described in 1832.

Born in Geneva into a distinguished scientific family — he was a descendant of the botanist Horace-Bénédict de Saussure's family and related to the Necker banking family — Necker studied the shapes of crystals. In a letter to Sir David Brewster published in the London and Edinburgh Philosophical Magazine, he reported that rhomboid crystals drawn in outline appeared to flip spontaneously between two orientations, the first description of the ambiguous line drawing now called the Necker cube, a classic demonstration of multistable perception.

Necker spent his later years in Scotland, living on the Isle of Skye, where he pursued geological and antiquarian interests; he died at Portree in 1861 and is buried there. He also published work on the geology of Scotland, including a geological map of the country.
