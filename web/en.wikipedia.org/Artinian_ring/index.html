Artinian ring

In mathematics, specifically abstract algebra, an Artinian ring (sometimes Artin ring) is a ring that satisfies the descending chain condition on (two-sided) ideals; that is, every descending chain of ideals I₁ ⊇ I₂ ⊇ I₃ ⊇ … eventually stabilises. Artinian rings are named after Emil Artin, who first discovered that the descending chain condition for ideals generalises simultaneously finite rings and rings that are finite-dimensional vector spaces over fields.

Examples

Every finite ring is Artinian, as is every field and every finite-dimensional algebra over a field. The ring Z/nZ of integers modulo n is Artinian, whereas the ring of integers Z itself is not, since the chain (2) ⊋ (4) ⊋ (8) ⊋ … never stabilises. A commutative ring is Artinian if and only if it is Noetherian and every prime ideal is maximal — equivalently, it is Noetherian of Krull dimension zero.

Structure

The Artin–Wedderburn theorem classifies semisimple rings, and every Artinian ring is Noetherian by the Akizuki–Hopkins theorem. A commutative Artinian ring decomposes uniquely as a finite product of Artinian local rings, in which every element is either a unit or nilpotent. In such rings the Jacobson radical is nilpotent and there are only finitely many maximal ideals.

Modules

A module over any ring is called Artinian if it satisfies the descending chain condition on submodules. A ring is Artinian exactly when it is Artinian as a module over itself. Artinian modules, like Artinian rings, always have finite length only in combination with the Noetherian condition; a module that is both Artinian and Noetherian has a composition series.
