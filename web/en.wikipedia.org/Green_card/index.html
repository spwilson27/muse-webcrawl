A green card, officially a Permanent Resident Card, is an identity document that shows a person has permanent residency in the United States. Green card holders are formally known as lawful permanent residents (LPRs). The nickname comes from the card's historical green coloring, restored in the 2010 redesign.

Eligibility

Permanent residence can be obtained through family sponsorship, employment, refugee or asylee status, investment, or the Diversity Immigrant Visa program (the "green card lottery"), among other categories. Most applicants adjust status within the U.S. or obtain an immigrant visa at a consulate. Permanent residents may live and work anywhere in the United States and, after a statutory period (generally five years, or three if married to a U.S. citizen), may apply for naturalization.

Rights and obligations

Permanent residents must carry the card, file U.S. taxes on worldwide income, and may lose status through abandonment or certain criminal offenses. Conditional residence of two years applies to recent marriages and investor cases, after which conditions must be petitioned for removal.
