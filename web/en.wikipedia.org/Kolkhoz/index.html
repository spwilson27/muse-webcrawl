Kolkhoz

A kolkhoz (Russian: колхоз) was a form of collective farm in the Soviet Union. The name is a contraction of kollektivnoye khozyaystvo, "collective economy". Kolkhozes existed alongside state farms (sovkhozes) as the two main forms of socialist agriculture.

Organisation

Land and equipment were nominally held collectively by the farm's members, who worked the land and were paid according to labour-days (trudodni), while the state set production plans and procurement quotas. Members were permitted small private household plots, which produced a disproportionate share of meat, milk and vegetables.

History

Small voluntary collectives appeared after the 1917 Revolution; mass collectivisation under Stalin from 1929 forced peasants into kolkhozes, accompanied by dekulakisation, famine and resistance. After the Soviet collapse in 1991, most kolkhozes were reorganised into joint-stock or cooperative enterprises, though the name persisted in some successor farms in Russia, Belarus and elsewhere.
