Fano plane

The Fano plane is the finite projective plane with the smallest possible number of points and lines: seven points and seven lines, with three points on every line and three lines through every point. It is named after Italian mathematician Gino Fano.

It is the projective plane of order 2, denoted PG(2,2). Its symmetry group is PGL(3,2), of order 168, isomorphic to PSL(2,7).

Properties

Each line contains exactly three points, and each point lies on exactly three lines. Any two distinct points determine a unique line, and any two distinct lines intersect in a unique point. The complement of a line is four points forming a quadrangle.

The Fano plane cannot be drawn with all lines straight in the Euclidean plane — one line must be drawn as a circle. It provides a standard counterexample in finite geometry and is related to the Hamming (7,4) code and the Steiner system S(2,3,7).
