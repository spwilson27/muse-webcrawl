Chris Burden

Christopher Lee Burden (April 11, 1946 – May 10, 2015) was an American artist working in performance, sculpture, and installation art. He became notorious in the 1970s for extreme body art performances, and later built monumental sculptures and installations exhibited around the world.

Urban Light (2008), Burden's installation of restored street lamps at the Los Angeles County Museum of Art

Performance works

Burden's early performances tested the limits of the body and the audience's complicity. In Shoot (1971), an assistant shot him in the arm with a .22 rifle in front of a small audience. Other works included Trans-Fixed (1974), in which he was crucified on the back of a Volkswagen Beetle; Five Day Locker Piece (1971), in which he confined himself in a locker for five days; and Doorway to Heaven (1973), in which he touched exposed electrical wires to his chest. He retired from performance art in the late 1970s.

Sculpture and installation

From the 1980s onward Burden produced large-scale sculptures built from construction toys, Erector sets, and found materials. His best-known public work, Urban Light (2008), consists of 202 restored antique cast-iron street lamps arranged in a grid at the entrance of the Los Angeles County Museum of Art, and has become an icon of Los Angeles. Other works include Metropolis II, a kinetic sculpture of 1,100 toy cars moving through a model city, also at LACMA.

Death

Burden died of melanoma in Los Angeles in 2015, shortly before the opening of a solo exhibition of his work at the Los Angeles County Museum of Art.
