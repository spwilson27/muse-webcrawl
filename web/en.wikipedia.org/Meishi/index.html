Meishi

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Meishi (名刺) are Japanese business cards. In Japan, the presentation and exchange of meishi follows a formal etiquette that is an important part of business culture. A typical meishi gives the bearer's name, company, title, address and contact details, often in Japanese on one side and in another language on the reverse.

Etiquette

Cards are presented and received with both hands, with the text facing the recipient so it can be read. On receiving a card, one should read it carefully, place it on the table in front during a meeting, and never write on it, fold it, or put it in a back pocket; cards are stored in a dedicated case (meishi-ire). The exchange is often accompanied by a bow and self-introduction.

Format

The standard Japanese meishi size is 91 × 55 mm. Rank, department and corporate affiliation are usually prominent, reflecting the importance of company identity in Japanese business life.

History

The custom developed from visiting cards used in the Edo period and spread widely with the growth of modern business in the Meiji era.
