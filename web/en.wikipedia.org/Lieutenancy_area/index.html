Lieutenancy area

Lieutenancy areas are the separate areas in the United Kingdom for which a lord-lieutenant is appointed as the representative of the British monarch. In England the lieutenancy areas correspond to the ceremonial counties. Scotland has 35 lieutenancy areas, defined by statutory instrument, which only loosely correspond to its historic counties and council areas. Wales's lieutenancy areas are the eight preserved counties, while Northern Ireland has six county lieutenancy areas based on its traditional counties, plus separate lieutenancies for the cities of Belfast and Derry/Londonderry.

History

Lord-lieutenants were first appointed in the sixteenth century, chiefly as the monarch's military deputy in each county. Over time the office became largely ceremonial. In England, local government reorganisation in 1974 and 1996 redrew the map, and the Lieutenancies Act 1997 tied the English lieutenancies to the ceremonial counties. Lord-lieutenants now act as the sovereign's personal representative, presenting honours, liaising with local units of the armed forces, and supporting civic and voluntary life.
