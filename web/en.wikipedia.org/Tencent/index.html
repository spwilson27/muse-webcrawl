Tencent

Tencent Holdings Ltd. (Chinese: 腾讯控股有限公司) is a Chinese multinational technology and entertainment conglomerate headquartered in Shenzhen. Founded in 1998 by Ma Huateng (Pony Ma) and four co-founders, it is one of the world's largest companies by market capitalisation and the world's largest video game vendor. Its twin headquarters towers, Tencent Seafront Towers, stand in Shenzhen's Nanshan District.

History and businesses

Tencent began with the instant-messaging service OICQ, renamed QQ, which became China's dominant messaging platform in the 2000s, monetised through virtual goods and membership tiers. Listed in Hong Kong in 2004, the company expanded into online games (becoming the global industry leader by revenue, with titles such as Honor of Kings and stakes in Riot Games, Epic Games, and Supercell), social networking, music and video streaming, advertising, fintech, and cloud computing. South Africa's Naspers (later Prosus) bought a 46.5% stake in 2001 for about US$32 million — one of the most profitable venture investments ever made — and remains the largest shareholder.

WeChat

Launched in 2011 under Allen Zhang, WeChat (微信, Weixin in China) grew from a messaging app into China's dominant "super-app", with over 1.3 billion monthly users: it combines chat, social feeds (Moments), payments (WeChat Pay), and millions of embedded mini-programs for shopping, government services, and transport. Together with QQ it anchors Tencent's social ecosystem.

Regulation and investments

Tencent was a central target of Beijing's 2020–2022 technology crackdown: an antitrust probe forced the unwinding of its music-streaming exclusivity, a merger of its game-streaming platforms was blocked, and minors' gaming time was sharply restricted by rules its systems enforce. Its investment portfolio spans hundreds of companies worldwide, including stakes in Tesla, Spotify, Snap, Sea Limited, JD.com, Meituan, and Pinduoduo.
