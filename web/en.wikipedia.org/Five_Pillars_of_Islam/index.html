Five Pillars of Islam

Five Pillars of Islam
Arabic: أركان الإسلام الخمسة (arkān al-islām al-khamsa)
Concept: Foundational acts of worship in Islam
Source: Hadith of Gabriel (Sahih Muslim, Sahih al-Bukhari)
Obligatory for: All adult, able Muslims

The Five Pillars of Islam (Arabic: أركان الإسلام الخمسة, arkān al-islām al-khamsa) are five basic acts in Islam that are considered mandatory by believers, and are the foundation of Muslim life. They are summarized in the famous hadith of Gabriel. Some Muslims practice Sunni Islam under Maliki doctrine as though there were a sixth pillar.

Shahada — testimony

The Shahada is the Muslim profession of faith: "There is no god but God (Allah), and Muhammad is the messenger of God." Reciting it with conviction makes one a Muslim.

Salah — prayer

Salah (or salat) is the ritual prayer performed five times daily at prescribed times: dawn (fajr), noon (zuhr), mid-afternoon (asr), sunset (maghrib), and nightfall (isha).

Zakat — almsgiving

Zakat is obligatory charitable giving, typically 2.5% of a Muslim's qualifying wealth held for a lunar year, distributed to the poor and needy.

Sawm — fasting

Sawm is fasting from dawn to sunset during the month of Ramadan, abstaining from food, drink, and other physical needs. Exemptions apply for the ill, travelers, pregnant women, and others.

Hajj — pilgrimage

The Hajj is the pilgrimage to Mecca during the month of Dhu al-Hijjah, required once in a lifetime for those physically and financially able. Pilgrims perform rites including circling the Kaaba, standing at Arafat, and the stoning of the jamarat.
