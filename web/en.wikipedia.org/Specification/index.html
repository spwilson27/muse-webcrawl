Specification

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A specification often refers to a set of documented requirements to be satisfied by a material, design, product, or service. It is a common early part of engineering design and product development processes in many fields. A specification may refer to a standard document published by a standards organization, or to a set of requirements developed by a company or customer for a particular project.

Types of specifications

In engineering and manufacturing, specifications can be prescriptive, describing exactly how something must be made, or performance-based, describing the outcomes that must be achieved. A requirements specification documents what a system or product must do; a functional specification describes its functions and behavior; and a design specification describes how it will be implemented. In construction, specifications accompany drawings and describe materials, workmanship, and quality standards.

Technical standards

Many specifications are codified as standards by organizations such as ISO, ASTM International, IEEE, and national standards bodies. Compliance with a specification may be verified by testing, inspection, or certification, and statements such as "conforms to specification" are common in procurement and contracting.

In computing

In software engineering, specifications range from informal descriptions to formal specifications written in mathematical notation, which can be used to verify that a program meets its requirements. Interface specifications and file-format specifications allow independently developed components to interoperate; examples include the specifications for HTML, USB, and Internet protocols published as RFCs.

Patent specifications

In patent law, the specification is the part of a patent application that describes the invention in sufficient detail to enable a person skilled in the art to make and use it, and it concludes with the claims that define the legal scope of protection.
