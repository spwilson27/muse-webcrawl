Chernivtsi (Ukrainian: Чернівці) is a city in western Ukraine, the administrative centre of Chernivtsi Oblast and the historical capital of the Bukovina region. It lies on the upper course of the River Prut, in the foothills of the Carpathian Mountains, near the borders with Romania and Moldova.

History

First mentioned in 1408 in a charter of Alexander the Good of Moldavia, Chernivtsi became a customs and trading post. It passed to the Habsburg Monarchy in 1775, which developed it as the capital of Bukovina, then to Romania after the First World War, and to the Soviet Union in 1940 (with a Romanian interlude during the Second World War), becoming part of independent Ukraine in 1991.

Culture

The city is noted for its multicultural heritage — Ukrainian, Romanian, Jewish, German, Polish, and Armenian communities shaped its architecture and institutions. Its landmark is the Residence of Bukovinian and Dalmatian Metropolitans, now Chernivtsi University, a UNESCO World Heritage Site built in the 1860s–1880s to designs by Josef Hlávka. Chernivtsi was the birthplace of poet Paul Celan and singer Mila Kunis's hometown by upbringing.

Economy and transport

Modern Chernivtsi is a regional centre for light industry, education, and trade, served by an international airport and rail links to Kyiv, Lviv, and Romania. The Kalynivskyi market is among the largest in Ukraine.
