Valley

A valley is an elongated low area between hills or mountains, typically with a river or stream flowing through it. Valleys are formed by erosion from running water or glaciers and vary greatly in shape and size.

Types

River valleys are commonly V-shaped in their upper reaches where downward erosion dominates, becoming wider and flatter downstream. Glaciated valleys are typically U-shaped, with steep sides and flat floors, while rift valleys form where the Earth's crust is pulled apart. Dry valleys lack surface streams, and hanging valleys sit above the main valley floor, often ending in waterfalls.

Significance

Fertile valley floors with access to water have supported agriculture and settlement since ancient times, giving rise to civilizations along the Nile, Tigris–Euphrates, Indus, and Yellow River valleys. Valleys also channel transport routes through mountainous terrain.
