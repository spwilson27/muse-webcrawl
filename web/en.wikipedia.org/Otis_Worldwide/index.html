Otis Worldwide Corporation is an American company that develops, manufactures, and markets elevators, escalators, and moving walkways, and provides related maintenance services. It is the world's largest elevator company, with roughly two million units under service worldwide.

History

The company traces its origin to Elisha Otis, who in 1852 invented the safety elevator — a hoisting platform fitted with a safety brake that would engage if the lifting cable failed. He demonstrated it dramatically at the 1854 New York World's Fair, cutting the rope while standing on the platform. The Otis Elevator Company was founded in 1853 in Yonkers, New York, and its elevators made the skyscraper practical, with landmark installations in the Eiffel Tower, the Empire State Building, and later the Burj Khalifa.

Corporate history

Otis was acquired by United Technologies Corporation (UTC) in 1976. In April 2020, following the merger of UTC with Raytheon, Otis was spun off as an independent publicly traded company as Otis Worldwide Corporation, headquartered in Farmington, Connecticut. A substantial majority of its revenue comes from its service business — maintaining and modernizing installed equipment — rather than new installations.

Products and services

Otis's product lines include the Gen2 and Gen3 machine-room-less elevators, the SkyRise high-rise system, and the eView and Otis ONE connected services platforms, which use Internet-of-things sensors for predictive maintenance. Its main competitors include Schindler, TK Elevator, and KONE.
