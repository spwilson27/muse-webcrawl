Siege of Sarajevo

Date: 5 April 1992 – 29 February 1996
Location: Sarajevo, Bosnia and Herzegovina
Part of: Bosnian War

The siege of Sarajevo was the prolonged blockade of the capital of Bosnia and Herzegovina by Bosnian Serb forces during the Bosnian War, lasting from 5 April 1992 to 29 February 1996 — 1,425 days, among the longest sieges of a capital city in modern warfare.

Course

Forces of the Army of Republika Srpska surrounded Sarajevo, shelling it from surrounding hills and subjecting its roughly 400,000 remaining residents to sniper fire, notably along 'Sniper Alley'. Utilities, food and medicine were scarce; an airlift and a tunnel under the airport sustained the city. Massacres at markets, including the Markale attacks of 1994 and 1995, drew international condemnation.

End and toll

NATO air strikes in 1995 and the Dayton Agreement ended the war; the siege was formally lifted in February 1996. Some 11,000–14,000 people were killed in the city, including more than 1,500 children. Siege commanders were later convicted of war crimes at The Hague.
