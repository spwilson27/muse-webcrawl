Löllingite

Löllingite crystals

Formula: FeAs2
Category: arsenide mineral
Crystal system: orthorhombic
Hardness: 5–5.5 (Mohs)
Colour: silvery white to steel grey

Löllingite, also spelled loellingite, is an iron arsenide mineral with the chemical formula FeAs2. It was named after its type locality, the district of Lölling in Carinthia, Austria, where it was first described in 1845.

Properties

Löllingite crystallises in the orthorhombic system, typically forming prismatic crystals, star-shaped twins, and massive granular aggregates. It is opaque, metallic, and silvery white to steel grey, tarnishing darker on exposure. With a Mohs hardness of 5 to 5.5 and a high specific gravity of about 7.1–7.5, it closely resembles arsenopyrite, from which it can be distinguished by its higher density and by chemical tests.

Occurrence

The mineral occurs in medium- to high-temperature hydrothermal veins and in pegmatites, commonly associated with arsenopyrite, pyrrhotite, chalcopyrite, skutterudite, and native bismuth. Notable localities include Lölling in Austria, the Harz in Germany, and mines in Canada, Sweden, and the United States. As an arsenic-bearing mineral it has been a minor source of arsenic and is of interest chiefly to collectors and mineralogists.
