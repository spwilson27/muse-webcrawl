T-Mobile US

T-Mobile US, Inc. is an American wireless network operator headquartered in Bellevue, Washington. It is the second-largest wireless carrier in the United States, with more than 100 million customers, and is majority-owned by the German telecommunications company Deutsche Telekom. Its principal brands are T-Mobile and Metro by T-Mobile. The company provides wireless voice, messaging and data services using 5G and 4G LTE networks.

History

The company traces its origins to VoiceStream Wireless, founded in 1994, which Deutsche Telekom acquired in 2001 and rebranded as T-Mobile USA in 2002. In 2013 T-Mobile combined with MetroPCS Communications. In 2020 it completed its merger with Sprint Corporation, acquiring Sprint's customers, stores and valuable mid-band spectrum, after which the Sprint brand was retired.

"Un-carrier" strategy

Beginning in 2013 under CEO John Legere, T-Mobile adopted an "Un-carrier" marketing strategy, abolishing two-year service contracts, subsidised phone pricing and overage charges, and introducing free international data roaming and video streaming promotions. The strategy drove rapid subscriber growth and pushed rivals such as Verizon and AT&T to change their own pricing policies.

Network

T-Mobile operates the largest 5G network in the United States, built substantially on the 2.5 GHz mid-band spectrum obtained from Sprint, alongside low-band 600 MHz spectrum used for wide-area coverage.
