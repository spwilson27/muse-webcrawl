Spartacus League

The Spartacus League (German: Spartakusbund) was a Marxist revolutionary movement organised in Germany during World War I. The League was named after Spartacus, leader of the largest slave rebellion of the Roman Republic. It was founded by Karl Liebknecht, Rosa Luxemburg, Clara Zetkin and other former members of the Social Democratic Party of Germany (SPD) who opposed the party's support for the war. The League became a founding current of the Communist Party of Germany (KPD) at the end of 1918.

History

Luxemburg and Liebknecht were leading figures of the SPD's left wing. After the SPD's Reichstag delegation voted for war credits in August 1914 — Liebknecht famously cast a lone vote against in December 1914 — the anti-war opposition organised around the newspaper The Internationale and, from 1916, as the Spartacus group, named for the "Spartacus Letters" it circulated. In April 1917 the Spartacists joined the newly formed Independent Social Democratic Party (USPD) as an autonomous current. Both Luxemburg and Liebknecht spent much of the war in prison for their anti-war agitation.

Revolution and end

Released during the German Revolution of 1918–19, the leaders founded the newspaper Die Rote Fahne (The Red Flag). At a congress held 30 December 1918 – 1 January 1919, the Spartacus League merged with other groups to found the Communist Party of Germany. In January 1919, amid strikes and armed clashes in Berlin known as the Spartacist uprising, the government of Friedrich Ebert used Freikorps units to crush the revolt. Rosa Luxemburg and Karl Liebknecht were arrested and murdered by Freikorps soldiers on 15 January 1919.
