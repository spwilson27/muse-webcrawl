The guanaco (Lama guanicoe) is a camelid native to South America, closely related to the domesticated llama. Its name comes from the Quechua word wanaku. Standing between 1 and 1.2 metres (3 ft 3 in and 3 ft 11 in) at the shoulder and weighing about 90 to 140 kilograms (200 to 310 lb), the guanaco is one of the largest land mammals native to South America and the largest of the four lamoid species, the others being the llama, alpaca and vicuña.

Description

Guanacos have a cinnamon-coloured coat, with white underparts and a grey face. Their blood contains an unusually high concentration of red blood cells — about four times that of a human — which allows them to thrive at high altitudes where oxygen is scarce. Like other camelids, they have padded feet and no upper incisors, tearing vegetation against a hard dental pad instead.

Distribution and habitat

Guanacos inhabit the steppes, shrublands and mountainous regions of South America, from sea level in Patagonia to elevations of about 4,000 metres (13,000 ft) in the Andes. Their range includes Peru, Bolivia, Ecuador, Colombia, Chile and Argentina. They are well adapted to desert conditions and can go long periods without drinking free water, obtaining moisture from the vegetation they eat.

Behaviour

Guanacos live in herds composed of females and their young led by a dominant male. Bachelor males form separate herds. When threatened, guanacos emit a high-pitched bleating alarm call; predators include pumas and, for young animals, culpeo foxes. Guanacos are prey of choice for pumas across much of their range and constitute an important part of the predator's diet in Patagonia.

Conservation

An estimated 1.5 to 2 million guanacos survive, concentrated mainly in Argentina. Populations declined sharply under hunting pressure for their meat and fine wool, but have recovered in many areas under legal protection. The guanaco is considered the wild ancestor or close relative of the domestic llama, which was domesticated from guanaco stock several thousand years ago in the Andean highlands.
