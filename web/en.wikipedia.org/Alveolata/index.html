Alveolata

The Alveolata, or alveolates, are a group of protists, considered a major clade and superphylum within Eukarya. They are characterised by the presence of cortical alveoli: flattened vesicles (alveoli) packed into a continuous layer supporting the cell membrane, typically forming a flexible pellicle.

Alveolata
Domain: Eukarya
Major groups: Ciliophora, Dinoflagellata, Apicomplexa
Defining trait: Cortical alveoli

Major groups

The ciliates (Ciliophora), such as Paramecium, swim and feed with cilia and possess dimorphic nuclei. The dinoflagellates are mostly marine plankton, many photosynthetic, some responsible for harmful algal blooms ("red tides") and bioluminescence. The Apicomplexa are obligate parasites — including Plasmodium, which causes malaria, and Toxoplasma — named for an apical complex used to invade host cells; they retain a relict plastid, the apicoplast.

Evolution

Alveolates belong to the SAR supergroup with stramenopiles and rhizarians. Their common ancestor was likely a photosynthetic flagellate; plastids of dinoflagellates and apicomplexans derive from red algal secondary endosymbiosis, later lost or reduced in many lineages.
