Ismail Enver (Ottoman Turkish: اسماعیل انور پاشا‎; 22 November 1881 – 4 August 1922), better known as Enver Pasha, was an Ottoman Turkish military officer and a leader of the Young Turk Revolution. He became Minister of War in 1914 and, together with Talaat Pasha and Djemal Pasha, formed the ruling triumvirate known as the "Three Pashas" that governed the Ottoman Empire during the First World War.

Early life and rise

Enver was born in Istanbul and graduated from the Ottoman War Academy as a staff officer. Posted to Macedonia, he joined the Committee of Union and Progress (CUP) and became one of the "heroes of liberty" of the 1908 Young Turk Revolution, which restored the constitution. He gained further fame resisting the Italian invasion of Libya (1911–12) and led the 1913 raid on the Sublime Porte that returned the CUP to power; in the Second Balkan War he recaptured Edirne, earning immense prestige.

First World War

As Minister of War and de facto commander-in-chief, Enver aligned the empire with Germany and engineered its entry into the First World War in October–November 1914. His own offensive against Russia in the Caucasus ended in catastrophe at the Battle of Sarikamish (December 1914–January 1915), where his Third Army was largely destroyed in winter conditions. The regime's wartime policies culminated in the destruction of the empire's Armenian population in 1915, a campaign of deportation and massacre now recognised as the Armenian genocide, for which the Three Pashas bear central responsibility.

Exile and death

After the Ottoman defeat in 1918, Enver fled to Germany and then to Soviet Russia. Having lost influence in Anatolia to Mustafa Kemal, he travelled to Central Asia, where he joined — and then turned against the Bolsheviks to lead — the Basmachi revolt in Turkestan, pursuing a pan-Turkic vision. He was killed in action against the Red Army near Baldzhuan (in present-day Tajikistan) on 4 August 1922.
