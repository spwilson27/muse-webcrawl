Silver fox (animal)

The silver fox is a melanistic colour morph of the red fox (Vulpes vulpes), characterised by fur that ranges from black with silver-tipped guard hairs to a glossy blue-grey. Silver foxes occur naturally in the wild in small numbers and have been bred on fur farms since the 19th century.

In the fur trade

Silver fox pelts were among the most valuable furs in North America. Ranching began on Prince Edward Island, Canada, in the late 1800s, where breeders such as Charles Dalton and Robert Oulton developed profitable strains and the island became famous for fox farming.

Domestication experiment

In 1959, Soviet scientist Dmitry Belyayev began selectively breeding silver foxes in Novosibirsk for tameness alone. Within generations the foxes showed dog-like behaviour and physical changes — floppy ears, piebald coats, and wagging tails — providing a landmark model of how domestication can proceed, and the line continues as the Russian domesticated red fox.
