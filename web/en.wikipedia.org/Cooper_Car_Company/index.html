The Cooper Car Company was a British manufacturer of racing cars founded in 1947 in Surbiton, Surrey, by Charles Cooper and his son John Cooper. The company became famous in Formula One for revolutionizing the sport with the rear-engined car, winning the Constructors' Championship in 1959 and 1960 with Jack Brabham taking the Drivers' Championship both years.

History

The Coopers began by building small 500cc single-seaters (the Cooper 500) powered by motorcycle engines, driven by a young Stirling Moss among others. Because the engine sat behind the driver in these chain-driven cars, the layout was carried into Formula Two and then Formula One. The Cooper T51 of 1959 — the first rear-engined car to win the World Championship — permanently changed racing-car design; within a few years all Formula One cars were rear-engined. Cooper also won the 1958 Monaco Grand Prix (Maurice Trintignant) and the first World Championship race for a rear-engined car, Moss's victory for Rob Walker in Argentina 1958.

Mini Cooper

John Cooper's name is equally famous through the Mini Cooper, the high-performance version of the BMC Mini launched in 1961, which won the Monte Carlo Rally in 1964, 1965, and 1967. The Cooper name continues on MINI's performance models (John Cooper Works).

Decline

Charles Cooper died in 1964 and John sold the company in 1965; the works Formula One team closed after 1969, but the firm's rear-engine revolution and its drivers — Brabham, Bruce McLaren, Jochen Rindt — shaped the following decades of motor sport.
