Rockers (subculture)

The rockers, also known as leather boys or ton-up boys, were members of a British youth subculture that emerged in the late 1950s and was centred on motorcycles and rock and roll music. Rockers wore black leather jackets, rode British motorcycles such as Triumphs and BSAs, and gathered at transport cafés like the Ace Café on London's North Circular Road.

History

The subculture grew out of post-war British motorcycling culture. Young working-class men modified café racer motorcycles for speed — the name "ton-up boys" referred to doing "the ton", 100 mph (160 km/h). Rockers were famously involved in clashes with the rival mods subculture, most notably at seaside resorts such as Brighton in 1964, events dramatised in the film Quadrophenia.

Style and music

Rockers favoured the music of American rock and roll and rockabilly artists such as Elvis Presley, Gene Vincent, Eddie Cochran and Chuck Berry. Their look included pompadour hairstyles, Levi's jeans, and engineer boots. The subculture declined in the late 1960s but has seen periodic revivals, and its descendants influenced later heavy metal and biker cultures.
