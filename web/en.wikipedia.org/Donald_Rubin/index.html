Donald Rubin

Donald B. Rubin
Born: 22 December 1943, Washington, D.C., U.S.
Field: Statistics
Institutions: Harvard University; Tsinghua University
Known for: Rubin causal model; EM algorithm; multiple imputation

Donald Bruce Rubin (born 1943) is an American statistician, one of the most cited researchers in his field. He is best known for the Rubin causal model, a framework for causal inference built on potential outcomes, and for pioneering work on missing-data methods, including multiple imputation.

Contributions

Rubin's 1974 paper introduced the potential-outcomes framework for causal inference, widely used across the social and medical sciences. With Arthur Dempster and Nan Laird he formalized the EM algorithm (1977). His book Multiple Imputation for Nonresponse in Surveys (1987) established methods now standard in survey statistics. He also developed propensity score methods with Paul Rosenbaum.

Career

Rubin spent most of his career in the statistics department at Harvard University, where he served as chair, before moving to Tsinghua University in Beijing. He is a member of the U.S. National Academy of Sciences.
