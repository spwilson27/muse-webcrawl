Darjeeling Himalayan Railway

The Darjeeling Himalayan Railway, also known as the DHR or the Toy Train, is a 610 mm (2 ft) gauge railway that runs between New Jalpaiguri and Darjeeling in the Indian state of West Bengal. Built between 1879 and 1881, it is about 88 km (55 mi) long and climbs from about 100 m (328 ft) above sea level at New Jalpaiguri to about 2,200 m (7,218 ft) at Darjeeling, using six zig zags and five loops to gain altitude.

History

The line was proposed to connect the hill station of Darjeeling, a summer retreat and tea-growing centre, with the plains at Siliguri. Franklin Prestage of the Eastern Bengal Railway championed the scheme, and the Darjeeling Steam Tramway Company began construction in 1879; the railway reached Darjeeling in July 1881. It passed to Indian Railways after independence in 1948.

Engineering

To negotiate the steep gradient, engineers used loops (spirals) and reversing zig zags. The most famous feature is the Batasia Loop near Ghoom, the highest station in India. The railway's small B-class steam locomotives, built between 1889 and 1925, remain in use on the upper section, supplemented by diesel locomotives below.

Heritage status

On 2 December 1999 UNESCO declared the DHR a World Heritage Site, the second railway in the world to receive that status. It later became part of the Mountain Railways of India listing together with the Nilgiri and Kalka–Shimla railways.
