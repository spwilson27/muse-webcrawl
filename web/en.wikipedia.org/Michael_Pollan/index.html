Michael Pollan

Michael Pollan (born February 6, 1955) is an American author and journalist known for his books about food, agriculture, nature and psychedelic substances. He has been a leading voice in debates about industrial food systems and sustainable eating.

Life and career

Pollan was born on Long Island, New York, and educated at Bennington College and Columbia University. He taught at the UC Berkeley Graduate School of Journalism and has written for The New York Times Magazine, Harper's and other publications.

Works

His books include The Botany of Desire (2001), which examines the co-evolution of humans and four plants; The Omnivore's Dilemma (2006), a bestseller tracing four meals back through the food system; In Defense of Food (2008), which opens with his famous seven-word advice: "Eat food. Not too much. Mostly plants."; Cooked (2013); How to Change Your Mind (2018), on the science and history of psychedelics; and This Is Your Mind on Plants (2021).

Influence

Pollan's work helped popularize the modern food movement, farm-to-table eating and criticism of industrial agriculture. The Omnivore's Dilemma was named one of the ten best books of 2006 by The New York Times. His Netflix documentary series Cooked (2016) adapted his book of the same name.
