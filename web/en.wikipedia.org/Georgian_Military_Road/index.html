Georgian Military Road

The Georgian Military Road (Georgian: საქართველოს სამხედრო გზა) is the historic name for a major route through the Caucasus Mountains from Vladikavkaz in Russia to Tbilisi in Georgia. Roughly 208 kilometres long, it follows the valleys of the Terek and Aragvi rivers and crosses the main Caucasus ridge at the Jvari (Cross) Pass, 2,379 metres above sea level.

The road beneath Mount Kazbek, near the village of Stepantsminda.

History

The route was known in antiquity as the Darial Pass, guarded in legend and history against invaders from the steppe. The Russian Empire improved the track after the annexation of Georgia in 1801, completing a military road by 1863; the present paved highway descends from those works. The road remains the principal land link between Russia and Georgia and, beyond, Armenia.

Along the road

From Tbilisi the highway passes the ancient capital of Mtskheta, the Ananuri fortress on the Zhinvali reservoir, the ski resort of Gudauri, and the village of Stepantsminda beneath Mount Kazbek (5,047 m), crowned by the fourteenth-century Gergeti Trinity Church. Pushkin, Lermontov, and Tolstoy all travelled the road, and it features prominently in Russian literature of the Caucasus.
