Xi'an

Xi'an
Capital of Shaanxi Province, China
Historically: Chang'an
Population: approx. 13 million (metro)

Xi'an is the capital of Shaanxi Province in northwestern China and one of the oldest cities in the country. Known historically as Chang'an, it served as the capital of thirteen dynasties, including the Han and Tang, and was the eastern terminus of the Silk Road.

History

The area has been inhabited since Neolithic times (the Banpo settlement dates to about 5000 BC). Under the Tang dynasty, Chang'an was one of the largest and most cosmopolitan cities in the world. Nearby, the First Emperor Qin Shi Huang was buried with the Terracotta Army, discovered in 1974 and now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Landmarks

Xi'an's Ming-dynasty city wall is among the best preserved in China; other landmarks include the Giant Wild Goose Pagoda, the Bell and Drum Towers, and the Muslim Quarter. Today the city is a major centre for education, software and aerospace industries in western China.
