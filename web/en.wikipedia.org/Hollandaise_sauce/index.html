Hollandaise sauce

Hollandaise sauce is an emulsion of egg yolk, melted butter, and lemon juice (or a white wine or vinegar reduction), usually seasoned with salt and a pinch of cayenne pepper. It is one of the five mother sauces in French haute cuisine and is best known as an essential component of eggs Benedict.

Preparation

The sauce is made by whisking egg yolks with the acidic reduction over gentle heat until thickened, then slowly incorporating warm clarified butter to form a stable emulsion. It must be kept warm but not hot, or the emulsion breaks. Despite its name, the sauce is French; the name may refer to a Dutch-style sauce served in France or to Dutch butter prized in French kitchens.

Derivatives

Variations include béarnaise (with tarragon and shallots), mousseline (with whipped cream), and maltaise (with blood-orange juice). Hollandaise traditionally accompanies poached eggs, steamed vegetables such as asparagus, and fish.
