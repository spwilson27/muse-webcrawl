Raja yoga

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Raja yoga (Sanskrit: राजयोग, "royal yoga") is a form of yoga mentioned in the Yoga Sūtras of Patañjali and is also known simply as yoga in the context of the Hindu philosophical school also called Yoga. In the classification of yoga paths, raja yoga is the path of meditation and mental discipline, contrasted with bhakti yoga (devotion), karma yoga (action), and jñāna yoga (knowledge). Because Patañjali's system has eight limbs (aṣṭāṅga), classical raja yoga is also called aṣṭāṅga yoga in many traditional sources — though the term now also denotes a modern vigorous style of hatha yoga.

The eight limbs

Patañjali's Yoga Sūtras (c. 2nd century BCE – 4th century CE) describe eight limbs of practice:

  1. Yama — ethical restraints (non-violence, truthfulness, non-stealing, continence, non-possession).
  2. Niyama — observances (purity, contentment, discipline, self-study, surrender).
  3. Āsana — a steady, comfortable posture for meditation.
  4. Prāṇāyāma — regulation of the breath.
  5. Pratyāhāra — withdrawal of the senses from their objects.
  6. Dhāraṇā — concentration on a single point.
  7. Dhyāna — meditation, sustained attention.
  8. Samādhi — absorption, the goal of the practice, culminating in kaivalya (liberation).

The aim, per the famous second sūtra, is "the stilling of the fluctuations of the mind" (yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ).

History and modern usage

The term "rāja yoga" appears in medieval hatha yoga texts as the goal of samādhi. In the 19th century, Swami Vivekananda's book Raja Yoga (1896), based on his lectures, presented Patañjali's system to Western audiences and helped shape modern understandings of yoga as a meditative, spiritual discipline distinct from physical culture. Many modern meditation-oriented schools describe themselves as teaching raja yoga.
