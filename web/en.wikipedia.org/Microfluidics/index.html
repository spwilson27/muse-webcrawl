Microfluidics

FieldFluid mechanics, nanotechnology
Length scalesMicrometres (10−6 m)
Volumes10−9 to 10−18 litres

Microfluidics refers to the behaviour, precise control and manipulation of fluids that are geometrically constrained to a small scale (typically sub-millimetre) at which surface forces dominate volumetric forces. It is a multidisciplinary field that involves molecular analysis, molecular biology and microelectronics, and deals with very small volumes of fluids — down to femtolitres.

Behaviour of fluids at the microscale

At micrometre scales the Reynolds number is low, so flows are typically laminar rather than turbulent; streams flowing side by side mix only by diffusion. Surface tension, capillarity and electrokinetic effects become dominant, enabling techniques such as droplet microfluidics, in which discrete droplets are generated, merged, split and sorted as individual reaction vessels.

Applications

Microfluidic devices — often called "lab-on-a-chip" systems — integrate sample preparation, reaction, separation and detection on a single substrate of glass, silicon or polymers such as PDMS. Applications include point-of-care diagnostics, inkjet printheads, DNA and protein analysis, chemical synthesis, single-cell studies and organs-on-chips that mimic tissue physiology for drug testing.
