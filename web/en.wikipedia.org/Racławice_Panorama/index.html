Racławice Panorama

The Racławice Panorama is a monumental cycloramic painting depicting the 1794 Battle of Racławice.

The Racławice Panorama (Polish: Panorama Racławicka) is a monumental (15 × 114 metre) cycloramic painting located in Wrocław, Poland. The painting is one of only a few preserved relics of a 19th-century mass culture genre — the panoramic painting — and the oldest still in existence in Poland.

Conceived by the Lwów painter Jan Styka, the panorama was executed in 1893–1894 by a team of artists including Wojciech Kossak and Jan Styka himself. It depicts the Battle of Racławice of 4 April 1794, a famous episode of the Kościuszko Uprising in which Polish forces, including peasant volunteers armed with war scythes, defeated a Russian detachment. The panorama was created for the centenary of the battle and first exhibited in Lwów (now Lviv) in 1894.

History

After the Second World War, the painting was transferred from Lwów to Wrocław along with much of the city's Polish population. For decades the communist authorities delayed exhibiting it, fearing its patriotic depiction of a Polish victory over Russians. The panorama was finally restored and opened to the public in 1985 in a purpose-built rotunda, where it remains one of Wrocław's most popular attractions.

Description

Viewers observe the painting from a central platform surrounded by a faux terrain of earth, fences, vegetation and props that blends into the canvas, creating an immersive illusion of standing on the battlefield. Key figures include Tadeusz Kościuszko and the peasant hero Wojciech Bartosz Głowacki, shown capturing a Russian cannon.
