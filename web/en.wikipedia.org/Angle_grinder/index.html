Angle grinder

An angle grinder, also known as a side grinder or disc grinder, is a handheld power tool used for grinding (abrasive cutting) and polishing. Although developed initially as tools for rigid abrasive discs, the availability of an interchangeable power source has encouraged their use with a wide variety of cutters and attachments. The motor drives a geared head at a right angle on which is mounted an abrasive disc or a thinner cut-off disc, either of which can be replaced when worn.

Characteristics

Angle grinders are typically powered by an electric motor or compressed air, with cordless battery models common. Standard disc sizes include 115 mm (4½ in) and 125 mm (5 in) for general work, with larger 230 mm (9 in) machines used in construction. High rotational speeds — often around 6,600 to 11,000 rpm — make the tools effective but hazardous: kickback, disc shattering, and sparks are principal dangers, and guards, eye protection, and gloves are standard safety measures.

Uses

Fitted with different discs, the same tool can cut metal, stone, concrete, and tile; grind welds and remove rust and paint; sharpen blades; and, with wire brushes or flap discs, clean and polish surfaces. The angle grinder is a staple of metal fabrication, construction, automotive repair, and emergency rescue work.

History

The high-speed angle grinder was introduced in 1954 by the German company Ackermann + Schmitt (later FLEX). Its versatility quickly made it one of the most common power tools worldwide.
