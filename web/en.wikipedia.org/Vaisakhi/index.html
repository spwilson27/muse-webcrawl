Vaisakhi

Vaisakhi, also pronounced Baisakhi, is a major festival of the Punjab region of South Asia. It marks the spring harvest, the start of the Punjabi New Year, and, for Sikhs, one of the most significant dates in their religious calendar: the founding of the Khalsa in 1699. Vaisakhi is usually celebrated on 13 or 14 April and is observed as a public holiday in Punjab and in several other Indian states.

Harvest festival

As an ancient harvest festival, Vaisakhi celebrates the ripening of the rabi (winter) crops. Farmers thank the deity for the harvest and pray for future prosperity. The day is marked by fairs (melas), folk dances such as bhangra and gidda, and visits to temples and gurdwaras across Punjab and neighbouring regions.

Significance in Sikhism

On Vaisakhi of 1699, the tenth Sikh Guru, Guru Gobind Singh, founded the Khalsa Panth at Anandpur Sahib. According to Sikh tradition, he called for volunteers willing to give their lives; five men — the Panj Pyare, or Five Beloved Ones — came forward and were initiated with amrit (sweetened water stirred with a double-edged sword), after which the Guru himself received initiation from them. The Khalsa adopted a distinct identity, including the Five Ks. Sikhs commemorate the day with nagar kirtan processions, kirtan singing, and amrit initiation ceremonies.

Other observances

Vaisakhi coincides with new year festivals celebrated under other names across India and South Asia, including Pohela Boishakh in Bengal, Puthandu in Tamil Nadu, Vishu in Kerala, and Bihu in Assam. In 1919, the Jallianwala Bagh massacre in Amritsar took place on Vaisakhi day, an event that became a turning point in the Indian independence movement.
