Epoch (astronomy)

In astronomy, an epoch is a moment in time used as a reference point for some time-varying astronomical quantity, such as the celestial coordinates or elliptical orbital elements of an astronomical body. Because such quantities are subject to perturbations and change over time — notably precession — they are specified together with the epoch at which they are valid.

Standard epochs

The current standard epoch is J2000.0, corresponding to 1 January 2000 at 12:00 Terrestrial Time (JD 2451545.0). The earlier standard epoch B1950.0 (Besselian) was used for much of the 20th century. The "J" prefix indicates a Julian epoch, defined in multiples of the Julian year of 365.25 days from J2000.0; Besselian epochs ("B") used the tropical year and were tied to the beginning of the Besselian year.

Use

Star catalogues give positions for a stated epoch together with proper motions, allowing positions to be propagated to other dates. Orbital elements of planets, asteroids and comets are quoted for an epoch because gravitational perturbations from other bodies change them continuously. The coordinates of the equatorial coordinate system themselves shift with precession, so a right ascension and declination are incomplete without an epoch (or equinox) specification.
