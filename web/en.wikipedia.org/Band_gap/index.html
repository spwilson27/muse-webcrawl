Band gap

Band structure of a semiconductor: the band gap Eg separates the valence band from the conduction band.

In solid-state physics and solid-state chemistry, a band gap, also called a bandgap or energy gap, is an energy range in a solid where no electronic states can exist. In graphs of the electronic band structure of solids, the band gap generally refers to the energy difference (in electron volts) between the top of the valence band and the bottom of the conduction band in insulators and semiconductors. It is the energy required to promote an electron from the valence band to the conduction band.

Electrical classification

Materials are classified by their band gap: conductors have overlapping bands (no gap); semiconductors have small gaps (roughly 0.1–4 eV) that can be bridged by thermal excitation, light or doping; and insulators have large gaps (above ~4–5 eV). Silicon's band gap is 1.11 eV at room temperature; gallium arsenide's is 1.42 eV; diamond's is 5.5 eV, making it an excellent electrical insulator.

Optical properties

A material absorbs photons with energy greater than its band gap, so the gap determines the absorption edge and hence the colour and photovoltaic behaviour of semiconductors. Light-emitting diodes emit photons at approximately the band-gap energy when electrons recombine with holes, so tuning the gap — as in gallium nitride (3.4 eV) for blue LEDs — sets the emitted colour. Similar discrete energy-level transitions in gases produce the characteristic colours of the aurora. Photovoltaic design exploits gaps via the Shockley–Queisser limit and multi-junction cells, a field transformed by deep learning-assisted materials screening.

Direct and indirect gaps

In a direct band gap material (e.g. GaAs) the conduction band minimum and valence band maximum occur at the same crystal momentum, allowing efficient light emission. In an indirect band gap material (e.g. silicon) they differ, and transitions require a phonon to conserve momentum, making emission inefficient but absorption adequate for solar cells.
