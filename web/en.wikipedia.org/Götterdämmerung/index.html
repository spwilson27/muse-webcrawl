Götterdämmerung

Götterdämmerung
Music and libretto: Richard Wagner
Premiere: 17 August 1876, Bayreuth Festspielhaus
Language: German
Part of: Der Ring des Nibelungen

Götterdämmerung (German: "Twilight of the Gods"), WWV 86D, is the last in Richard Wagner's cycle of four music dramas titled Der Ring des Nibelungen (The Ring of the Nibelung). It received its premiere at the Bayreuth Festspielhaus on 17 August 1876, as part of the first complete performance of the Ring cycle.

The title is a translation into German of the Old Norse phrase Ragnarök, which in Norse mythology refers to a prophesied war among the gods that brings about the end of the world. Like the rest of the Ring, the libretto was written by Wagner himself, drawing on Norse sagas and the German Nibelungenlied.

Story

The opera opens with the three Norns weaving the rope of fate, which breaks. Siegfried leaves Brünnhilde to seek new adventures, giving her the ring as a token of love. At the hall of the Gibichungs, Hagen — Alberich's son — plots to seize the ring: Siegfried is given a potion that makes him forget Brünnhilde and fall in love with Gutrune, while her brother Gunther is to win Brünnhilde. Siegfried, disguised as Gunther by the Tarnhelm, drags Brünnhilde from her rock and takes the ring from her finger.

When the deception is revealed, Brünnhilde, in fury, joins Hagen and Gunther in plotting Siegfried's death. On a hunting trip Hagen spears Siegfried in the back; with his dying words Siegfried's memory returns and he greets Brünnhilde. At the funeral, Brünnhilde orders a pyre built, takes the ring, and rides her horse Grane into the flames. The Rhine overflows, the Rhinemaidens recover the ring, Hagen drowns, and Valhalla burns in the distance as the world of the gods ends.

Music

Götterdämmerung contains some of Wagner's most famous orchestral passages, including Siegfried's Rhine Journey, Siegfried's Funeral March, and the closing Immolation Scene, in which the great web of leitmotifs woven through the cycle is gathered together and the "redemption" theme brings the tetralogy to its conclusion.

Roles

Principal roles include Siegfried (tenor), Brünnhilde (soprano), Gunther (baritone), Gutrune (soprano), Hagen (bass), Alberich (baritone), and Waltraute (mezzo-soprano).
