United Nations Truce Supervision Organization

The United Nations Truce Supervision Organization (UNTSO) is an organisation founded on 29 May 1948 for peacekeeping in the Middle East. It was the first peacekeeping operation established by the United Nations, created to supervise the truce called for by the Security Council during the 1948 Arab–Israeli War.

Mandate and operations

UNTSO military observers — unarmed officers drawn from many member states — monitored the 1949 Armistice Agreements between Israel and its neighbours and have continued, after subsequent wars, to observe ceasefires in the region. Its personnel support the work of other UN missions, including UNDOF on the Golan Heights and UNIFIL in southern Lebanon. UNTSO headquarters is in Jerusalem, at Government House.

History

Although the armistice agreements it was created to supervise were superseded by the wars of 1956, 1967, and 1973 and later peace treaties, UNTSO has remained in place, with its observers adapting to new ceasefire and disengagement arrangements. It remains the longest-running UN peacekeeping operation.
