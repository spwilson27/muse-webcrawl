Media freedom in Russia

The media freedom in Russia concerns the constitutional guarantees of press freedom, censorship and propaganda in the Russian Federation. While Article 29 of the Russian constitution guarantees freedom of the press and prohibits censorship, international monitors such as Reporters Without Borders rank Russia near the bottom of the World Press Freedom Index, classifying the situation as "very serious."

History

The post-Soviet 1990s saw a pluralistic, if chaotic, media environment, with powerful oligarch-owned outlets frequently critical of the government. During the 2000s, under President Vladimir Putin, major national television networks — the main news source for most Russians — passed under state or state-friendly control, beginning with the takeover of NTV in 2001.

Legal framework

Since 2012, a series of laws have tightened control: the "foreign agents" law has been extended to media outlets and individual journalists; laws on "extremism" and, after the 2022 invasion of Ukraine, on spreading "false information" about the armed forces carry long prison sentences. In March 2022 most remaining independent outlets were blocked, suspended operations or were driven into exile, and foreign broadcasters were expelled or restricted.

Violence against journalists

Russia has been one of the more dangerous countries for journalists; the murders of investigative reporters such as Anna Politkovskaya (2006) drew international attention, and the Committee to Protect Journalists has documented many unsolved killings and imprisonments.

State media and propaganda

State-controlled television and outlets such as RT and Sputnik project government narratives at home and abroad, and regulators (Roskomnadzor) enforce blocking of websites and platforms deemed non-compliant.
