Bandar Seri Begawan

Bandar Seri Begawan (Jawi: بندر سري بڬاوان) is the capital and largest city of Brunei. It is the seat of government of the Sultanate of Brunei and had a population of about 100,700 in the city proper, with roughly 280,000 in the wider Brunei–Muara District. The city was renamed in 1970 in honour of Sultan Omar Ali Saifuddien III, the father of the reigning Sultan Hassanal Bolkiah; its former name was Brunei Town.

History

Settlement on the Brunei River dates back centuries; the traditional water village of Kampong Ayer, a cluster of stilt houses often called the largest water village in the world, has been inhabited for over a thousand years. The modern town grew under the British Residency established in 1906. It was heavily damaged by Allied bombing in the Second World War and rebuilt after 1945. In 1970 it was renamed Bandar Seri Begawan, and development accelerated with oil revenues, producing grand mosques, palaces and government buildings.

Landmarks

The Omar Ali Saifuddien Mosque, completed in 1958 and built on an artificial lagoon, is the city's symbol, with a golden dome and a ceremonial stone barge. The Jame' Asr Hassanil Bolkiah Mosque, built for the silver jubilee of Sultan Hassanal Bolkiah, is the largest mosque in the country. Istana Nurul Iman, the Sultan's official residence, is often described as the world's largest residential palace. Other sites include the Royal Regalia Museum and the Jerudong Park playground.

Geography and climate

The city lies on the northern bank of the Brunei River near its mouth on Brunei Bay, on the northwestern coast of Borneo. It has a tropical rainforest climate with high temperatures and rainfall throughout the year.

Transport

Brunei International Airport serves the city, and the Sultan Haji Omar Ali Saifuddien Bridge, opened in 2020, links the capital district with the exclave of Temburong.
