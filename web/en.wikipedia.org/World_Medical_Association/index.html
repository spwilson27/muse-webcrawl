The World Medical Association (WMA) is an international organization representing physicians worldwide. Founded on 18 September 1947 in Paris, it serves as an umbrella body for national medical associations and works to ensure the highest possible standard of medical care, ethics, education, and health-related human rights for all people.

History

The idea of a world organization of physicians emerged from the British Medical Association in 1945, as the Second World War ended and revelations about medical crimes committed during the war made international ethical standards an urgent concern. Delegates from 27 national medical associations met in London in 1946 to plan the association, and the first General Assembly was held in Paris in 1947.

Major declarations

The WMA is best known for its policy statements on medical ethics. The Declaration of Geneva (1948), often described as a modern version of the Hippocratic Oath, sets out the physician's duties to patients and humanity. The Declaration of Helsinki (1964) established ethical principles for medical research involving human subjects and has been revised repeatedly, most recently in 2024. The Declaration of Tokyo (1975) prohibits physician participation in torture, and the Declaration of Lisbon (1981) addresses the rights of the patient.

Structure and activities

The WMA's governing bodies include the General Assembly and the Council, which meet annually. Its secretariat is based in Ferney-Voltaire, France, near the Geneva headquarters of the World Health Organization, with which it maintains official relations. The association publishes the World Medical Journal and convenes scientific sessions on topics ranging from antimicrobial resistance to the health effects of climate change.
