Mudra

A mudra (Sanskrit: mudrā, "seal", "mark", or "gesture") is a symbolic or ritual gesture or pose in Hinduism, Jainism and Buddhism. While some mudras involve the entire body, most are performed with the hands and fingers. In Indian classical dance and theatre, hundreds of codified hand gestures (hastas) convey objects, actions, and emotions to the audience.

Mudras are used in iconography to identify deities and narrate episodes: images of the Buddha, for example, are distinguished by gestures such as the bhumisparsha (earth witness), dhyana (meditation), abhaya (fearlessness), dharmachakra (turning the wheel of dharma), and vitarka (discussion). In yoga and meditation practice, practitioners use mudras alongside pranayama and asana, and traditional texts claim they direct the flow of prana (life energy) in the body.

Common Buddhist mudras

Dhyana mudra: hands resting in the lap, palms up, right hand over the left, thumbs touching — signifying meditation and concentration. Bhumisparsha mudra: the right hand reaches down to touch the earth, recalling the Buddha's resolve not to rise until he attained enlightenment and his calling of the earth goddess to witness. Abhaya mudra: the right palm raised outward at shoulder height, signifying peace, protection, and the stilling of fear.

In yoga

Hatha yoga texts describe mudras such as jnana mudra (thumb and index finger touching), khechari mudra, and maha mudra, sometimes combined with bandhas (body locks) and breath retention. In modern yoga classes, hand mudras are commonly used at the start or end of practice to focus attention.

In dance

The Natya Shastra and the Abhinaya Darpana catalogue single-hand (asamyuta) and combined-hand (samyuta) gestures with multiple meanings each, deployed in Bharatanatyam, Kathak, Odissi, and other classical forms to narrate stories.
