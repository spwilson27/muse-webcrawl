General Electric

Founded: April 15, 1892, Schenectady, New York, U.S.

Founders: Thomas Edison, Charles Coffin, and others

Industry: Conglomerate (historical)

Fate: Split into GE HealthCare, GE Vernova, and GE Aerospace (2023–2024)

General Electric Company (GE) was an American multinational conglomerate founded in 1892 through the merger of Thomas Edison's Edison General Electric Company and the Thomson-Houston Electric Company. For more than a century it was one of the world's largest and most admired industrial companies, operating in aviation, power generation, healthcare, appliances, lighting, media, and financial services, and it was one of the original components of the Dow Jones Industrial Average.

History

From its Schenectady, New York base, GE industrialised electric lighting, power generation and distribution, and built major businesses in locomotives, jet engines (supplying the U.S. military from the Second World War onward), medical imaging, and plastics. Its research laboratory produced innovations from the tungsten filament lamp to the first American jet engine. GE owned the NBC television network and built GE Capital into one of the largest financial institutions in the United States.

Under chief executive Jack Welch (1981–2001), GE's market value rose enormously and it was repeatedly named the world's most admired company. After 2001, however, the company was hit by the financial crisis (which badly damaged GE Capital), problems in its power business, and accounting controversies; it was removed from the Dow Jones Industrial Average in 2018 after more than a century.

Breakup

In November 2021 GE announced it would split into three separate public companies focused on healthcare, energy, and aviation. GE HealthCare was spun off in January 2023, and the energy business became GE Vernova in April 2024, at which point the remaining aviation company rebranded as GE Aerospace and retained the GE name and stock symbol, ending the conglomerate's 132-year history.
