Mineral

A mineral is a naturally occurring inorganic element or compound having an orderly internal structure and a definite chemical composition, crystal form, and physical properties. Minerals are distinguished from rocks, which are aggregates of one or more minerals or mineraloids. Some 5,900 mineral species had been recognised by the International Mineralogical Association (IMA) as of the early 2020s.

Definition

The general requirements for a substance to be classed as a true mineral are that it be naturally occurring, inorganic, solid, possess an ordered internal structure (i.e., be crystalline), and have a definite chemical composition that can vary only within specified limits. Biogenic substances such as pearl and amber are generally excluded, while certain organic minerals are recognised exceptions.

Physical properties

Minerals are identified by properties including crystal habit, hardness (Mohs scale, from talc at 1 to diamond at 10), lustre, colour and streak, cleavage and fracture, specific gravity, and optical properties. Diagnostic tests may also involve taste (halite), magnetism (magnetite), fluorescence, and reaction to acid (carbonates).

Classification

The Strunz and Dana systems group minerals by chemistry and structure into classes such as native elements, sulfides, oxides, halides, carbonates, sulfates, phosphates, and silicates. Silicates — built on the silicon–oxygen tetrahedron — dominate the Earth's crust, with feldspars the most abundant group.

Formation

Minerals form by crystallisation from magma or lava, precipitation from aqueous solutions, metamorphism of existing rocks, and sedimentation. Gem-quality specimens may form in pegmatites, hydrothermal veins, and placer deposits.
