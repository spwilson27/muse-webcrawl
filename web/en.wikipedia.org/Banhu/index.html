Banhu

Banhu
Type: Bowed string instrument
Origin: Northern China
Family: Huqin

The banhu (Chinese: 板胡, bǎnhú) is a Chinese traditional bowed string instrument in the huqin family. It has two strings and a body traditionally made from a halved coconut shell covered with a thin wooden soundboard — the feature that gives the instrument its name, ban meaning "board".

Construction and sound

The banhu has a long fretless neck and is held vertically, resting against the player's left thigh. Unlike the erhu, its soundboard is wood rather than snakeskin, giving it a bright, penetrating tone well suited to carrying a melody over an ensemble. Its two strings are traditionally tuned a fifth apart.

Use

The banhu is the principal melody instrument in the "bamboo and wind" ensembles of northern China and accompanies narrative singing and regional opera, especially in Shaanxi, Shanxi, Hebei and Shandong provinces. In the 20th century, performers such as Liu Mingyuan developed solo repertoire and virtuoso techniques, including rapid bowing and imitation of operatic singing.

Related instruments

The banhu is related to the erhu, gaohu and yehu; the gaohu was developed in part from it in the 1920s for use in modern Chinese orchestras.
