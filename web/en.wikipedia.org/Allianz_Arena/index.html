Allianz Arena

Allianz Arena (known as Fussball Arena München for UEFA competitions) is a football stadium in the north of Munich, Bavaria, Germany, with a 70,000 seating capacity for international matches and 75,000 for domestic matches. Widely known for its exterior of inflated ETFE plastic panels, it is the first stadium in the world with a full color-changing exterior. The arena has been the home of FC Bayern Munich since the start of the 2005–06 season.

History

The stadium was designed by the Swiss architectural firm Herzog & de Meuron and opened on 30 May 2005. It was built for the 2006 FIFA World Cup, hosting the opening match and one semi-final. Originally co-owned by Bayern Munich and TSV 1860 Munich, Bayern bought out 1860's share in 2006, and 1860 departed the stadium entirely in 2017.

Design

The façade is made of 2,874 rhomboidal ETFE foil air panels that can be illuminated in red (for Bayern home games), blue (formerly for 1860), or white (for the German national team). The translucent shell earned the stadium the nickname Schlauchboot (“inflatable boat”).

Major events

The stadium hosted the 2012 UEFA Champions League final, matches at UEFA Euro 2020 and Euro 2024, and the 2025 UEFA Champions League final.
