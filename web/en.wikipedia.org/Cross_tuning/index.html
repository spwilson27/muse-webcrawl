Cross tuning

Cross tuning or cross-tuning (also called scordatura in classical music) is an alternative tuning used for the open strings of a string instrument. The term refers to the practice, used in folk music, of retuning the strings of an instrument such as a fiddle, so that tunes in a particular key can be played using open strings and drones that differ from the standard tuning.

Fiddle cross tunings

For the violin or fiddle, whose standard tuning is G–D–A–E, common cross tunings include:

  • AEAE (“sawmill tuning”) — used for tunes in A; the G and D strings are raised a whole step.
  • GDGD — also for A tunes, with the A and E strings lowered.
  • ADAE — for tunes in D, the G string raised to A.
  • DDAD — sometimes called “dead man's tuning” or “Dee Dad”, used for tunes such as “Bonaparte's Retreat”.
  • FCGD (“Cajun tuning”) — all strings lowered a whole step, common in Cajun music.

Uses and effects

Cross tuning makes an instrument resonate sympathetically with open strings, producing a fuller, more ringing sound and allowing drones and double stops characteristic of old-time, Appalachian and Cajun fiddling. Retuning changes string tension, so players may risk breaking strings or warping older instruments. The practice is closely related to the classical scordatura used by composers such as Heinrich Ignaz Franz Biber and Niccolò Paganini, and to the “dead” (daead) tunings of the Hardanger fiddle in Norwegian folk music.
