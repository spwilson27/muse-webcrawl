Cartoon Network (abbreviated as CN) is an American cable television channel owned by Warner Bros. Discovery through its Global Linear Businesses unit. Launched on October 1, 1992, it was the first 24-hour single-genre cable channel devoted to animation, initially broadcasting the vast Hanna-Barbera and Warner Bros. cartoon libraries acquired by Turner Broadcasting.

Cartoon Network's black-and-white checkerboard identity.

History

Ted Turner's purchase of the MGM/UA library and of Hanna-Barbera in 1991 supplied the channel's programming. Under founding president Betty Cohen, Cartoon Network launched in 1992 and quickly expanded internationally. In the mid-1990s the studio began producing original series under the "Cartoon Cartoons" banner, beginning with Dexter's Laboratory (1996), followed by Johnny Bravo, Cow and Chicken, The Powerpuff Girls, Ed, Edd n Eddy, and Courage the Cowardly Dog.

Programming

Later original hits include Samurai Jack, Foster's Home for Imaginary Friends, Ben 10, Adventure Time, Regular Show, The Amazing World of Gumball, and Steven Universe. The late-night block Adult Swim, launched in 2001, targets adult audiences and is treated as a separate channel for ratings purposes. Production is centred on Cartoon Network Studios in Burbank, California, founded as Hanna-Barbera's successor in 1994.

Influence

Cartoon Network reshaped children's television by treating animation as a channel-wide identity, incubating a generation of creators (including Genndy Tartakovsky, Craig McCracken, and Pendleton Ward) and exporting its channels to dozens of countries, where its programming is dubbed and localized.
