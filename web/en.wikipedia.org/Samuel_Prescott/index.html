Samuel Prescott (1751 – c. 1777) was an American physician and patriot who took part in the "midnight ride" of April 18–19, 1775. He is the only one of the three riders — Paul Revere, William Dawes, and Prescott — who actually reached Concord to warn of the approaching British troops.

The ride

Prescott, a young doctor from Concord, was returning home late from visiting his fiancée in Lexington when he encountered Revere and Dawes on the road. Knowing the local terrain, he joined the alarm ride. When a British patrol intercepted the party near Lincoln, Revere was captured and Dawes was turned back, but Prescott escaped by jumping a stone wall and riding cross-country to Concord, arriving about 1:30 a.m.

Later life

Prescott is believed to have served as a surgeon in the Continental Army and to have died around 1777, possibly as a prisoner of war. Longfellow's famous poem credited Revere alone, and Prescott's central role was recovered by later historians.
