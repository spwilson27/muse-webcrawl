British Shipbuilders (BS) was a public corporation that owned and managed the nationalised shipbuilding industry of the United Kingdom from 1977. It brought together most of the country's major shipyards, which had been taken into public ownership under the Aircraft and Shipbuilding Industries Act 1977.

Formation

British shipbuilding had been in long decline, losing market share to shipyards in Japan, South Korea and Europe. The Labour government of James Callaghan nationalised 27 shipbuilding companies, including Swan Hunter, Cammell Laird, Harland and Wolff (initially separate but closely linked), John Brown & Company, Vosper Thornycroft and Yarrow Shipbuilders. At formation, British Shipbuilders employed around 87,000 people and controlled roughly a quarter of world merchant shipbuilding capacity by tonnage on order, though output was far lower.

Operations

The corporation was organised into divisions for merchant shipbuilding, naval shipbuilding, ship repair and offshore construction. Its yards built vessels for the Royal Navy, including Type 42 destroyers and Invincible-class aircraft carriers, as well as tankers, bulk carriers and ferries.

Privatisation and dissolution

Under the Conservative governments of Margaret Thatcher, the corporation's warship yards were sold off during the 1980s — Vosper Thornycroft, Yarrow and others returned to private ownership — followed by merchant yards. Heavy losses and yard closures, including the closure of the North East Shipbuilders yards in Sunderland in 1988, marked the end of large-scale merchant shipbuilding in Britain. British Shipbuilders itself was dissolved in 1989, with residual liabilities transferred to the government.
