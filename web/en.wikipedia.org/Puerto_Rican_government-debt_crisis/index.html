Puerto Rican government-debt crisis

The Puerto Rican government-debt crisis was a financial crisis affecting the government of Puerto Rico, which by 2015 had accumulated roughly $72 billion in public debt that Governor Alejandro García Padilla declared unpayable. The crisis followed a decade of economic contraction after the phase-out (1996–2006) of federal tax incentives under Section 936 of the Internal Revenue Code that had driven manufacturing investment on the island.

Background

Puerto Rican bonds were attractive to investors because they are "triple tax-exempt" — exempt from federal, state, and local taxes in all U.S. jurisdictions — and, unusually, the Puerto Rican constitution prioritized general obligation debt repayment. As revenues fell, the government borrowed to cover operating deficits; its debt was repeatedly downgraded to junk status in 2014.

PROMESA and restructuring

Because U.S. bankruptcy law did not allow Puerto Rico or its municipalities to file under Chapter 9, Congress passed PROMESA in 2016, creating a federal oversight board and a court-supervised restructuring process (Title III). The Puerto Rico Electric Power Authority (PREPA) and the central government entered Title III proceedings in 2017, the largest public debt restructuring in U.S. history. A plan of adjustment for the central government's debt was confirmed in January 2022 and took effect that March, cutting general obligation and related debt substantially. Hurricane Maria (2017) and subsequent austerity measures deepened the crisis's social impact, contributing to large-scale migration to the mainland United States.
