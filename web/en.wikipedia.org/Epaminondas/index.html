Epaminondas

Bornc. 419 BC, Thebes
Died362 BC, Mantinea
AllegianceThebes (Boeotia)
RankBoeotarch

Epaminondas (Ancient Greek: Epameinōndas; c. 419–362 BC) was a Greek general and statesman of the 4th century BC who transformed the Ancient Greek city-state of Thebes, leading it out of Spartan subjugation into a preeminent position in Greek politics, called the Theban hegemony. In the process, he broke Sparta's military supremacy, liberated the Messenian helots, and laid the foundations of the Messenian state.

Battle of Leuctra

Epaminondas is most famous for the Battle of Leuctra (371 BC), where the Thebans decisively defeated Sparta. He departed from conventional phalanx tactics by massing his infantry fifty ranks deep on the left wing — the oblique order — and leading with the elite Sacred Band under his friend Pelopidas. The concentrated column smashed the Spartan right, killing the Spartan king Cleombrotus I and hundreds of full Spartan citizens, a loss from which Sparta never recovered.

Invasions of the Peloponnese

In the winter of 370/369 BC Epaminondas led the first invasion of Laconia in living memory, then freed Messenia from centuries of Spartan control and refounded the city of Messene. He campaigned repeatedly in the Peloponnese to hold together a ring of states hostile to Sparta.

Death and legacy

Epaminondas was killed at the Battle of Mantinea in 362 BC, even as his army won the field; Theban dominance did not long survive him. Ancient writers praised both his military genius and his personal incorruptibility and philosophical education. His tactical innovations influenced later commanders, including Philip II of Macedon.
