Puebla, officially the Free and Sovereign State of Puebla (Spanish: Estado Libre y Soberano de Puebla), is one of the 32 states of Mexico. It is divided into 217 municipalities and its capital city is Puebla de Zaragoza. The state is located in east-central Mexico, bordered by Veracruz, Hidalgo, Tlaxcala, the State of Mexico, Morelos, Guerrero and Oaxaca.

History

The region was home to major pre-Hispanic cultures; the great pyramid of Cholula, with the largest monumental base in the world, dates to centuries before the Spanish conquest. The city of Puebla was founded in 1531 as a Spanish settlement on the route between Mexico City and Veracruz. The state is famous for the Battle of Puebla of 5 May 1862, in which Mexican forces defeated a French army — the victory commemorated as Cinco de Mayo.

Geography

Puebla is dominated by the volcanoes of the Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt, including Popocatépetl, Iztaccíhuatl and Pico de Orizaba (Citlaltépetl), the highest mountain in Mexico. The terrain ranges from high plateaus to the tropical lowlands of the Sierra Norte.

Culture and cuisine

The state is noted for Talavera pottery, the embroidered china poblana dress, and a celebrated cuisine that includes mole poblano and chiles en nogada, both created, according to tradition, in the convents of the colonial capital.

Economy

Manufacturing — especially automobiles and textiles — anchors the economy; the metropolitan area hosts a major Volkswagen plant, one of the largest outside Germany, and an Audi plant at San José Chiapa.
