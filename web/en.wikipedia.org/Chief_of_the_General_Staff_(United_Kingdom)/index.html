Chief of the General Staff (United Kingdom)

BranchBritish Army
Reports toChief of the Defence Staff
AbbreviationCGS

The Chief of the General Staff (CGS) is the title of the professional head of the British Army. The CGS is a member of the Chiefs of Staff Committee and the Army Board, and is subordinated to the Chief of the Defence Staff.

History

The office originated with reforms following the Boer War; from 1904 to 1909 the head of the Army was the Chief of the General Staff, and from 1909 to 1964 the title was Chief of the Imperial General Staff (CIGS), reflecting the Army's imperial role. The modern title was restored in 1964. Notable holders include Field Marshals William Robertson, Alan Brooke, and William Slim.

Responsibilities

The CGS is responsible for the development and generation of land forces, advising the government on the employment of the Army, and commanding it through Headquarters Land Forces and the field army.
