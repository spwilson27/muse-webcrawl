Vinson Massif is a large mountain massif in Antarctica that is about 21 km (13 mi) long and 13 km (8.1 mi) wide and lies within the Sentinel Range of the Ellsworth Mountains. Its highest point, Mount Vinson, rises to 4,892 metres (16,050 ft), making it the highest mountain in Antarctica and one of the Seven Summits.

Discovery and naming

The massif was discovered in January 1958 by US Navy aircraft and named after Carl G. Vinson, a US congressman from Georgia who championed Antarctic exploration funding. It lies about 1,200 km (750 mi) from the South Pole, overlooking the Ronne Ice Shelf.

Climbing

The first ascent was made on 18 December 1966 by an American expedition led by Nicholas Clinch, supported by the US Navy and the National Science Foundation. Mount Vinson is not technically difficult but is extremely remote and cold, with temperatures below −40 °C; climbers fly in from Chile via Union Glacier. It is the final summit in many Seven Summits campaigns.
