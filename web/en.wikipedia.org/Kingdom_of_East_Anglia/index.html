Kingdom of East Anglia

The Kingdom of East Anglia (Old English: Ēastengla Rīċe) was one of the kingdoms of Anglo-Saxon England, corresponding roughly to the modern counties of Norfolk and Suffolk. Founded in the 6th century, it was ruled by the Wuffingas dynasty.

History

East Anglia's most powerful king, Rædwald (reigned c. 599–624/5), was recognised as a bretwalda—overlord of the southern English kingdoms—and is widely believed to be the king buried in the great ship burial at Sutton Hoo. The kingdom later produced the martyred king Edmund, killed by Danish invaders in 869 and venerated as St Edmund, patron saint of England before St George. East Anglia was conquered by the Danes, formed part of the Danelaw, and was absorbed into the kingdom of Wessex under Edward the Elder in 917–918, thereafter forming an earldom of England.
