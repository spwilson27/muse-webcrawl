Social class

A social class is a set of concepts in the social sciences and political theory centred on models of a social stratification by which people are grouped into a set of hierarchical social categories, the most common being the middle class, working class and upper class. Membership of a social class can depend on education level, wealth, occupation, network of relationships, social capital, and inherited status.

History of the concept

The term "class" entered English usage in the early 19th century, replacing earlier terms such as rank, order and estate. The Industrial Revolution, which transformed agricultural societies into industrial ones, made class distinctions based on wealth and occupation increasingly prominent compared with older hierarchies based on birth.

Major theoretical models

Marxist model

Karl Marx defined classes in terms of their relationship to the means of production. In capitalist society, the bourgeoisie owns the means of production, while the proletariat owns only its labour power, which it must sell to survive. Marx predicted that the inherent conflict between these classes — class struggle — would drive historical change.

Weberian model

Max Weber proposed a three-component theory of stratification based on class (wealth and occupation), status (prestige and honour) and party (political power). Unlike Marx, Weber saw these dimensions as partly independent of one another, producing a more complex hierarchy.

Functionalist and later models

Functionalist sociologists such as Kingsley Davis and Wilbert Moore argued that stratification serves a social function by ensuring that the most important positions are filled by the most qualified people. Later theorists, including Pierre Bourdieu, emphasised cultural capital and habitus in reproducing class advantage across generations.

Classes in modern societies

Contemporary models often divide society into upper, upper-middle, middle, working and lower classes, sometimes adding categories such as a precariat of insecure workers. Class correlates strongly with health, life expectancy, educational attainment and political behaviour, and social mobility between classes varies considerably between countries.
