Internet service provider

An Internet service provider (ISP) is an organization that provides services for accessing, using, managing, or participating in the Internet. ISPs can be organized in various forms, such as commercial, community-owned, non-profit, or otherwise privately owned.

Internet services typically provided by ISPs can include Internet access, Internet transit, domain name registration, web hosting, Usenet service, and colocation.

History

The Internet originated as a network of government and university researchers. Commercial use was restricted until the early 1990s. The first commercial ISP, The World, began serving customers in 1989. In the United States, the NSFNET restrictions on commercial traffic were lifted in 1991, and dial-up providers such as AOL, CompuServe, and Prodigy brought millions of households online during the 1990s.

Broadband access via DSL and cable modems spread in the 2000s, followed by fiber-optic and fixed wireless services in the 2010s and 2020s.

Tiers

ISPs are classified informally into tiers. Tier 1 networks are the largest backbone providers, which can reach every part of the Internet via settlement-free peering. Tier 2 networks peer with some networks but purchase transit to reach the rest. Tier 3 networks purchase transit from others and serve end users.

Regulation

ISPs are central to debates over net neutrality — whether providers may block, throttle, or charge for prioritized delivery of content. Rules have varied by country and, in the United States, by administration.
