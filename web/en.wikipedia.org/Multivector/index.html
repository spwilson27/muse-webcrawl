Multivector

In multilinear algebra, a multivector, sometimes called a p-vector, is an element of the exterior algebra of a vector space. More precisely, a p-vector is a sum of p-blades, where a p-blade is the exterior (wedge) product of p vectors.

The terminology depends on the degree: a 0-vector is a scalar, a 1-vector is an ordinary vector, a 2-vector is a bivector (representing an oriented plane segment), a 3-vector is a trivector (representing an oriented volume), and so on. In geometric algebra, a multivector more generally denotes a sum of blades of different grades, forming a general element of a Clifford algebra.

Properties

Multivectors of a fixed degree k over an n-dimensional space form a vector space of dimension C(n, k). Bivectors over three-dimensional space correspond to axial vectors and can represent rotations and angular momentum. The exterior product is antisymmetric, so the wedge of linearly dependent vectors vanishes; a nonzero p-blade spans a p-dimensional subspace with an orientation and a magnitude.

Applications

Multivectors appear in differential geometry through differential forms, in physics in the study of the electromagnetic field (represented as a bivector in spacetime algebra), and in computer graphics and robotics through geometric algebra formulations of rotations and rigid motions.
