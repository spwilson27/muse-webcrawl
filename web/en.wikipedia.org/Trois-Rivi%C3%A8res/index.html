Trois-Rivières is a city in the Mauricie administrative region of Quebec, Canada, at the confluence of the Saint-Maurice and Saint Lawrence rivers, roughly midway between Montreal and Quebec City. Founded in 1634 by Laviolette, it is the second-oldest city in French Canada after Quebec City.

History

The name, French for "Three Rivers", refers to the three channels formed by the Saint-Maurice River as it flows around two islands at its mouth. The city grew as a fur-trade post and later became a centre of the pulp and paper industry; it was long known as the pulp and paper capital of the world.

Economy and culture

Today's economy is based on manufacturing, higher education (the Université du Québec à Trois-Rivières), tourism and events such as the Grand Prix de Trois-Rivières automobile race and the FestiVoix music festival. The old town preserves several heritage buildings, including the Ursulines Monastery.
