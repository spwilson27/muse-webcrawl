Bent's Old Fort National Historic Site

Bent's Old Fort National Historic Site is a United States National Historic Site located in Otero County, Colorado, about eight miles east of La Junta. It preserves a reconstructed 1840s adobe fur trading post on the mountain branch of the Santa Fe Trail, where traders, trappers, travelers, and Plains Indian tribes came together in peaceful trade.

History

The original fort was built in 1833 by brothers William and Charles Bent, along with Ceran St. Vrain, forming Bent, St. Vrain and Company. Situated on the north bank of the Arkansas River — then the border between the United States and Mexico — the fort became the most significant settlement on the Santa Fe Trail between Independence, Missouri, and Santa Fe. It served as a hub for trade with the Southern Cheyenne and Arapaho, who exchanged buffalo robes for manufactured goods.

The fort was abandoned in 1849, after a cholera epidemic devastated the Cheyenne and trade declined. William Bent is said to have attempted to destroy the fort rather than sell it to the U.S. Army at a price he considered too low, though the extent of the damage is debated.

Reconstruction

Using archaeological excavations, period sketches, paintings, and original diaries, the National Park Service and the State of Colorado rebuilt the fort in 1976, timed for the United States Bicentennial and Colorado's centennial. Today living-history interpreters demonstrate 1840s crafts and lifeways, and the site hosts events such as a fur-trade rendezvous and a Santa Fe Trail encampment.
