Metalloproteinase

Metalloproteinase
Enzyme classProtease (peptidase)
CofactorTypically Zn2+ (also Co2+, Mn2+)
FamiliesMatrix metalloproteinases, ADAMs, astacins, serralysins

A metalloproteinase, or metalloprotease, is any protease enzyme whose catalytic mechanism involves a metal ion, most commonly zinc. The metal, usually coordinated by three amino acid ligands in the active site, activates a water molecule that attacks the peptide bond of the substrate. Metalloproteinases are one of the major groups of proteolytic enzymes, alongside serine, cysteine, threonine, and aspartic proteases.

Families

The best-known family is the matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), secreted zinc-dependent endopeptidases that degrade extracellular matrix components such as collagen. Other important groups include the ADAM (a disintegrin and metalloproteinase) family, involved in shedding cell-surface proteins, and bacterial enzymes such as thermolysin. Metalloproteinases are placed in clan MA and related clans in the MEROPS classification.

Biological roles and medicine

These enzymes participate in tissue remodelling, wound healing, angiogenesis, arthritis, and tumour invasion and metastasis. Their activity is regulated by endogenous tissue inhibitors of metalloproteinases (TIMPs) and by activation of inactive proenzymes. Synthetic inhibitors, including chelating agents such as EDTA and drugs like batimastat, have been explored therapeutically; the antibiotic doxycycline is used at sub-antimicrobial doses as an MMP inhibitor in periodontitis.
