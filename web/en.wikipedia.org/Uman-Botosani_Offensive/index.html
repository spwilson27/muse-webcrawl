Uman–Botoșani Offensive

The Uman–Botoșani Offensive (5 March – 17 April 1944) was a major Red Army operation on the Eastern Front of the Second World War, forming part of the wider Dnieper–Carpathian Offensive. Conducted principally by the 2nd Ukrainian Front under Marshal Ivan Konev, it drove Axis forces out of southwestern Ukraine and into Romania and Moldova.

Course of the offensive

Despite the spring rasputitsa (mud season), Soviet forces advanced rapidly, crossing the Southern Bug, Dniester, and Prut rivers in succession. The offensive shattered much of the German 8th Army and elements of Army Group South Ukraine, and Soviet troops crossed the Romanian border — the first time the Red Army carried the war onto the territory of an Axis satellite state. The cities of Uman, Vinnytsia, and Botoșani were captured during the advance.

Significance

The operation destroyed or badly mauled numerous German divisions and set the stage for the First Jassy–Kishinev Offensive and, later in 1944, the collapse of Romania's alignment with the Axis.
