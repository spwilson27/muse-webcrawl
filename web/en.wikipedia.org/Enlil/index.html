Enlil

Enlil, later known in Akkadian as Ellil, was one of the chief deities of the Sumerian and Mesopotamian pantheon. Associated with wind, air, earth, and storms, he was regarded as a powerful executive god whose decrees shaped the fates of kings and peoples.

Role in mythology

Enlil was the son of An and Ki and was linked with the separation of heaven and earth. In flood traditions he is portrayed as sending or permitting the deluge because human noise disturbed his rest, a role later reinterpreted in several Mesopotamian and biblical parallels.

Cult and city

His principal sanctuary was the E-kur temple at Nippur, a major religious center whose priesthood and festivals carried influence across city-states. Kings sought legitimacy by honoring Enlil and receiving kingship as his gift.

Family and legacy

Enlil's consort was Ninlil, and his children included Nanna, Ningirsu, and Ninurta in various traditions. As Babylon rose, some of Enlil's authority was absorbed by Marduk, but Enlil remained a byword for supreme divine command. Related pages include Anu, Nippur, and Mesopotamian mythology.
