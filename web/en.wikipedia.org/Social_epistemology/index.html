Social epistemology
FieldPhilosophy
FocusSocial dimensions of knowledge
Key figuresAlvin Goldman; Steve Fuller; Helen Longino

Social epistemology is a broad field of study that examines the social dimensions of knowledge, justification, and epistemic evaluation. In contrast to traditional epistemology's focus on the individual believer, social epistemology studies how testimony, peer disagreement, institutions, group deliberation, and information technologies shape what communities know.

Topics

Core topics include the epistemology of testimony (when to trust what others say), epistemic disagreement among peers, group knowledge and collective belief, expertise and its authority, and the design of truth-conducive institutions such as science, journalism, and legal systems. Alvin Goldman's "veritistic" approach evaluates social practices by their tendency to produce true belief.

Approaches

The field includes analytic work in philosophy, normative and descriptive sociology of science, and feminist and standpoint approaches (such as Helen Longino's) that argue social values and diversity are constitutive of good inquiry. Steve Fuller has advocated a more policy-oriented, sociological conception of the field.

Contemporary relevance

Social epistemology has gained prominence in analysing misinformation, echo chambers and filter bubbles, conspiracy theories, and epistemic injustice — Miranda Fricker's term for wrongs done to people specifically in their capacity as knowers.
