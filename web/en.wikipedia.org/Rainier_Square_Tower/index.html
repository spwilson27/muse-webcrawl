Rainier Square Tower is a mixed-use skyscraper in downtown Seattle, Washington, completed in 2020. At 850 feet (259 m) with 58 stories, it is the second-tallest building in Seattle after the Columbia Center. The tower is part of the Rainier Square redevelopment of a block owned by the University of Washington's Metropolitan Tract, adjacent to the 1977 Rainier Tower with its distinctive tapered pedestal designed by Minoru Yamasaki.

Rainier Square Tower, notable for its sloping "boot" profile above the podium.

Design and construction

Designed by NBBJ, the tower has a distinctive sloping profile—the upper floors step back in a form nicknamed the "Rainier boot"—that preserves views and light for the neighboring Rainier Tower. The building pioneered a new structural system, nicknamed "speed core", in which a steel-plate composite shear wall core replaced the traditional concrete core; this allowed the structure to rise roughly one floor per day and significantly shortened construction. Construction began in 2017 and the tower topped out in 2019.

Tenants and uses

The tower contains office space, 189 luxury apartments on the upper floors, and a hotel (the Hotel 1000's successor property, Hotel Theodore's block includes associated development), with retail in the podium. Amazon was originally set to lease all of the office space but subleased much of it after changing its plans in 2019; other technology and professional firms subsequently took space. The project also includes the renovated Rainier Tower plaza and retail pavilions.
