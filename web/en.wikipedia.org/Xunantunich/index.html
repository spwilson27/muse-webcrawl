Xunantunich (pronounced shoo-NAN-too-nich) is an ancient Maya archaeological site in western Belize, near the village of San José Succotz in the Cayo District. Its name, meaning "Maiden of the Rock" in Yucatec Maya, refers to a ghostly female figure said in local folklore to appear at the site. The ceremonial centre sits on a limestone ridge above the Mopan River, about 130 km west of Belize City.

History

Settlement began in the Middle Preclassic, but Xunantunich flourished in the Late Classic (600–900 CE) as a regional centre allied at times with the great city of Naranjo. After an earthquake around 900 CE damaged its buildings, the site declined and was largely abandoned by about 1000 CE.

Architecture

The core covers roughly 2.6 km² with some 25 temples and palaces around six plazas. El Castillo, at about 40 metres, is among the tallest structures in Belize; its eastern and western friezes preserve elaborate stucco masks of the sun god and other deities, now protected by replicas while originals are conserved. Two ball courts, including one of the largest in the Belize valley, lie within the core.

Excavation and tourism

Thomas Gann investigated the site in the 1890s; systematic excavation and consolidation have proceeded under Belizean and international projects since the 1990s. Reached by a hand-cranked ferry across the Mopan River, Xunantunich is one of Belize's most visited archaeological reserves.
