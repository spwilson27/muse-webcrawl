Dal makhani

Dal makhani (Hindi: दाल मक्खनी, "buttery lentils") is a popular dish originating from Punjab in the Indian subcontinent. Its primary ingredients are whole black lentils (urad dal), red kidney beans (rajma), butter, and cream, simmered slowly for many hours with tomatoes and spices to produce a rich, creamy texture.

Preparation

The lentils and beans are soaked, then cooked until tender and simmered — traditionally overnight over a low fire or on the embers of a tandoor — with a tomato-based gravy seasoned with ginger, garlic, and garam masala. Generous amounts of butter (makhan) and cream are stirred in, and the dish is often finished with a swirl of cream and served with naan or rice.

History

Although lentil dishes are ancient in Punjab, dal makhani in its modern restaurant form is associated with Delhi after Partition, and is frequently credited to the Moti Mahal restaurant chain and its founder Kundan Lal Gujral, who is also linked with popularizing butter chicken in the same period. The dish became a staple of North Indian restaurants worldwide.
