Melvil Dewey

Melvil Louis Kossuth "Melvil" Dewey (December 10, 1851 – December 26, 1931) was an influential American librarian and educator. He was the inventor of the Dewey Decimal system of library classification and a founder of the American Library Association. Although he is acknowledged for his work, Dewey has also been called out for character flaws, most notably his history of antisemitism and sexual harassment.

Career

Born in Adams Center, New York, Dewey graduated from Amherst College in 1874 and worked there as a librarian. In 1876 he published his classification scheme and helped found the American Library Association and the Library Journal. He became chief librarian of Columbia College (later Columbia University) in 1883, founded the first library school there in 1887, and in 1889 became director of the New York State Library and secretary of the University of the State of New York.

Dewey Decimal Classification

His system arranges knowledge into ten main classes, each divided decimally, allowing books to be shelved by subject with relative location rather than fixed positions. It became the most widely used library classification in the world.

Later life and controversies

Dewey promoted spelling reform, metrics, and efficiency, and founded the Lake Placid Club in New York, whose exclusion of Jewish people and others led to his forced resignation from state office in 1905. Accounts of sexual harassment led the American Library Association in 2019 to remove his name from its top award, the Melvil Dewey Medal. He died in Lake Placid, Florida, in 1931.
