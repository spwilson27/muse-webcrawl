United States men's national basketball team

The United States men's national basketball team represents the United States in international basketball competitions. Governed by USA Basketball, it is the most successful team in the sport's history, having won the great majority of Olympic gold medals and multiple FIBA World Cups.

History

American teams of amateurs dominated Olympic basketball from its introduction in 1936, interrupted famously by the controversial 1972 final loss to the Soviet Union and a boycott-affected 1980 absence. After another Soviet defeat in 1988, FIBA opened competition to professionals.

The Dream Team era

The 1992 Barcelona team — featuring Michael Jordan, Magic Johnson and Larry Bird — is widely regarded as the greatest team ever assembled in any sport, winning by an average of over 40 points and transforming basketball's global popularity. Subsequent teams of NBA stars have remained favorites, though losses in 2004 (Athens) and at the 2019 and 2023 World Cups showed growing international depth.

Recent years

The United States won Olympic gold again at Tokyo 2020 and Paris 2024, the latter with a veteran roster led by LeBron James, Stephen Curry and Kevin Durant.
