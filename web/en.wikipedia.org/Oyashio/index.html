Oyashio Current

The Oyashio (Japanese: 親潮, "parental tide"), also known as the Okhotsk Current or Kurile Current, is a cold subarctic ocean current that flows southward through the western North Pacific Ocean, from the Bering Sea and the Sea of Okhotsk along the Kamchatka Peninsula and the Kuril Islands toward the east coast of northern Japan.

Properties and effects

The Oyashio carries cold, low-salinity, nutrient-rich water. Where it converges with the warm, northward-flowing Kuroshio off Honshu, strong temperature fronts and upwelling create one of the world's richest fishing grounds, supporting sardine, saury, squid and salmon fisheries. The cold air over the current brings heavy snow to Hokkaido in winter and cool, foggy summers to the northeast Pacific coast of Japan — the phenomenon known as yamase.

Variability

The current's southern intrusion varies year to year, influencing regional climate and fish stock recruitment; its strength is monitored by Japanese oceanographic agencies. In Ainu and Japanese tradition the current is the "parent" that nourishes the sea's creatures, hence its name.
