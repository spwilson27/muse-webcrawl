Existence of God

The existence of God is a subject of debate in the philosophy of religion. A wide variety of arguments for and against the existence of God can be categorized as logical, empirical, metaphysical, or subjective. In philosophical terms, the question of the existence of God involves the disciplines of epistemology (the nature and scope of knowledge) and ontology (study of the nature of being, existence, or reality) and the theory of value (since some definitions of God include "perfection").

Michelangelo's Creation of Adam (1512), an iconic image in Western debates about God.

Positions

Theism broadly holds that God exists; monotheism affirms one God. Atheism is the absence of belief that any deity exists, or in stronger forms the belief that none exists. Agnosticism holds that the existence of God is unknown or unknowable. Other positions include deism, pantheism, panentheism, and ignosticism, which questions whether the concept "God" is defined coherently enough for the question to be meaningful.

Arguments for

Classic arguments include the ontological argument (Anselm of Canterbury, later Descartes and Gödel), which reasons from the concept of a maximally great being; the cosmological argument, from the existence of the universe to a first cause or necessary being; the teleological argument (argument from design), associated with William Paley's watchmaker analogy and modern "fine-tuning" claims; and moral arguments, as well as appeals to religious experience.

Arguments against

Counter-arguments include the problem of evil (how an omnipotent, omnibenevolent God could permit suffering), arguments from inconsistent revelations and from divine hiddenness, Occam's razor, and critiques of each positive argument from Hume and Kant onward. The burden of proof is itself contested between the camps.
