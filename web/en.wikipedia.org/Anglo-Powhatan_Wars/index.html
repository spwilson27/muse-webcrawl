Anglo-Powhatan Wars

Date1609–1646 (three wars)
LocationVirginia Colony
BelligerentsEnglish colonists vs. Powhatan Confederacy
ResultEnglish expansion; Powhatan confined to reservations

The Anglo-Powhatan Wars were three wars fought between English settlers of the Virginia Colony and the Native Americans of the Powhatan Confederacy in the early seventeenth century. The first war began in 1609 and ended with a peace settlement in 1614; the second began in 1622, and a third in 1644, ending in a treaty in 1646.

Background

The English founded Jamestown in 1607 within the territory of the paramount chief Powhatan (Wahunsenacawh). Competition for land and food, and English expansion for tobacco planting, produced chronic tension punctuated by violence, trading and diplomacy — including the marriage of Pocahontas to John Rolfe, which secured several years of peace.

The wars

Under Powhatan's successor Opechancanough, the Powhatan launched coordinated attacks in 1622 and 1644 that killed hundreds of colonists, aiming to drive the English out. The colonists responded with punitive expeditions that destroyed villages and crops. The 1646 treaty following the third war set a formal boundary between English and Native lands and established some of the earliest reservations in North America.

Consequences

The wars broke the power of the Powhatan Confederacy and confirmed English dominance in tidewater Virginia, setting patterns of dispossession that recurred across North America.
