The Appendix Probi ("Probus's Appendix") is a late Latin grammatical text consisting of 227 entries of the form "speculum non speclum" — "say speculum, not speclum" — prescribing correct spellings and pronunciations of Classical Latin against the forms actually used by contemporary speakers. Because its "errors" reflect the spoken language of its day, the appendix is one of the most valuable sources for Vulgar Latin and for the transition from Latin to the Romance languages.

Text and transmission

The text survives as an appendix to a copy of the Instituta Artium attributed to the grammarian Probus, preserved in a manuscript copied at Bobbio in the seventh or eighth century and now in Vienna. The work itself is usually dated to the third or fourth century AD, though some scholars have argued for a later, even Carolingian, origin. Each entry gives the approved classical form followed by the stigmatised variant introduced by non.

Linguistic significance

The rejected forms document systematic changes on the way to Romance: syncope of unstressed vowels (calida non calda, "warm" — compare Italian calda), loss of final consonants and of h, simplification of consonant clusters, monophthongisation of diphthongs (auris non oricla), changes of vowel quality, and analogical re-formations (e.g., masculus non masclus). Romance linguists treat the list as a snapshot of the spoken Latin from which Italian, Spanish, French, and the other Romance languages descend, and it shows that many "vulgar" features were already established centuries before the earliest Romance texts.
