Metamorphic rocks arise from the transformation of existing rock (the protolith) through a process called metamorphism, in which the original rock is subjected to temperatures greater than 150–200 °C and often elevated pressure, causing profound physical or chemical changes without melting.

Types of metamorphism

Regional metamorphism affects large volumes of crust during mountain-building events. Contact metamorphism occurs where rock is heated by an intruding magma body, producing a baked aureole. Dynamic metamorphism is associated with fault zones, and hydrothermal metamorphism involves hot, ion-rich fluids.

Classification

Metamorphic rocks are classified by texture and composition. Foliated rocks — slate, phyllite, schist, and gneiss — show a layered or banded appearance from the alignment of platy minerals. Non-foliated rocks such as marble (from limestone) and quartzite (from sandstone) lack this layering. Increasing metamorphic grade is marked by index minerals, from chlorite at low grade to sillimanite at high grade.
