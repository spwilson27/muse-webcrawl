Division (military)

A division is a large military unit or formation, usually consisting of between 10,000 and 25,000 soldiers, commanded by a major general. In most armies, a division is composed of several regiments or brigades; in turn, several divisions typically make up a corps.

Historically, the division has been the default combined arms unit capable of independent operations. Smaller cavalry, armored, airborne, and mechanized divisions were organized during the Second World War, when it was thought that units should be tailored to their missions rather than follow a standard structure.

History

The modern division system was invented by the French army in the mid-18th century, with Maurice de Saxe and François-Joseph de Brosse among its early proponents. It was widely adopted during the French Revolutionary Wars and the Napoleonic Wars, where divisions combined infantry, cavalry, and artillery under a single commander. By the First World War, divisions had become the standard large formation of most armies.

Composition

A typical infantry division includes maneuver brigades, artillery, aviation, and support elements such as logistics, engineers, signals, and medical units. Armored divisions emphasize tanks and mechanized infantry, while airborne divisions are trained for parachute and air assault operations.

Divisions are commonly classified as infantry, armored, mechanized, mountain, airborne, or air assault. Some countries also maintain artillery, aviation, and special-purpose divisions.
