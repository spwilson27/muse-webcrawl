The Peoples Temple of the Disciples of Christ, commonly shortened to the Peoples Temple, was an American new religious movement founded in 1955 by Jim Jones in Indianapolis, Indiana. The movement eventually moved to California and then to a settlement in Guyana known as Jonestown, where on November 18, 1978, a total of 918 people died in a mass murder–suicide, the largest loss of American civilian life in a single deliberate act until the September 11 attacks.

History

Jones founded the Temple as a racially integrated church preaching social justice, and it formally affiliated with the Christian Church (Disciples of Christ). The movement relocated to Redwood Valley, California in 1965, then expanded to San Francisco and Los Angeles, where it built political influence through voter mobilisation and charitable work, while former members alleged abuse, fraud, and control.

Jonestown

In 1977, under press scrutiny, Jones and hundreds of followers emigrated to the Peoples Temple Agricultural Project at Jonestown, Guyana. Conditions deteriorated, with armed guards, forced labour, and rehearsals of mass suicide ("White Nights"). In November 1978, U.S. Congressman Leo Ryan visited to investigate; as his party left, Temple gunmen killed Ryan and four others at the Port Kaituma airstrip. That evening Jones ordered his followers to drink cyanide-laced powdered drink; 909 people died at Jonestown, including more than 300 children.

Legacy

The events gave rise to the phrase "drinking the Kool-Aid" (the drink was actually mostly Flavor Aid). The Temple is a defining case study in scholarship on cults and coercive control, and survivors and victims' families continue to commemorate the dead at annual memorials in Oakland, California.
