The Instruments of the Passion, also called the Arma Christi ("weapons of Christ"), are the objects associated with the Passion of Jesus in Christian tradition — the events leading to his crucifixion. They are frequently depicted together in Christian art as heraldic-like emblems of Christ's suffering and triumph over death.

The instruments

The most commonly depicted instruments include the Cross, the Crown of Thorns, the pillar of the Flagellation and the whip, the Holy Nails, the Holy Lance that pierced Christ's side, the sponge soaked in vinegar on a reed, the hammer and pincers, the ladder, the cock that crowed after Peter's denial, the thirty pieces of silver, the Veil of Veronica, the seamless robe and the dice cast for it, and the INRI inscription placed above the cross.

In art and devotion

From the late Middle Ages, the Arma Christi appeared in Books of Hours, altarpieces, and devotional images, often held by angels or arranged around the Man of Sorrows. Indulgences were attached to prayers before such images. Many individual instruments were also venerated as relics in churches across Europe; the Crown of Thorns, for example, was acquired by Louis IX of France and housed in the Sainte-Chapelle in Paris.

Heraldry

The instruments are used in ecclesiastical heraldry and as charges in coats of arms, and individual items — such as the nails or the crown of thorns — appear in the arms of cities, orders, and families.
