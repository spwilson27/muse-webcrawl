Electroforming

Electroforming is a metal forming process in which parts are produced by electrodeposition of metal onto a conductive pattern or mandrel. The deposited metal is built up to a substantial thickness and then separated from the mandrel, yielding a free-standing part that reproduces the mandrel's surface in fine detail.

Electroforming can reproduce surface features with sub-micrometre fidelity.

Process

A mandrel is made conductive (if it is not already metal) and immersed in an electroplating bath, becoming the cathode. Metal ions from the solution or a sacrificial anode deposit onto it over hours or days. Once the desired thickness is reached, the deposit — the electroform — is stripped from the mandrel, which may be reused, melted out, or dissolved.

Applications

Electroforming is used to make moulds for compact discs and lenses, fine mesh and sieves, bellows, waveguides, and components with complex shapes and thin walls. Common metals include nickel, copper, gold, and silver. The process was developed in the 19th century from electrotyping, used to reproduce printing plates and works of art.
