Embolization

Embolization refers to the passage and lodging of an embolus within the bloodstream. In medicine, the term more commonly denotes a minimally invasive procedure performed by interventional radiologists in which embolic agents — such as coils, particles, beads, glue or alcohol — are selectively delivered through a catheter to occlude a blood vessel.

Uses

Therapeutic embolization is used to stop active bleeding, to treat aneurysms and vascular malformations, to cut off blood supply to tumours (including chemoembolization of liver tumours), and for uterine artery embolization as a treatment for fibroids. By blocking flow to a target while sparing surrounding tissue, it can serve as an alternative or adjunct to surgery.

Risks

Complications can include non-target embolization, post-embolization syndrome (pain, fever and nausea), and tissue ischemia.
