Tahrir Square (Arabic for "Liberation Square") is a major public town square in downtown Cairo, Egypt. It became internationally famous as the focal point of the 2011 Egyptian revolution that ended the presidency of Hosni Mubarak.

History

The square was originally named Ismailia Square after the 19th-century ruler Khedive Ismail, who commissioned the surrounding Paris-style downtown. It was renamed after the 1952 Egyptian revolution. Landmarks around it include the Egyptian Museum, the former Nile Ritz-Carlton, and the Mogamma government building.

2011 revolution

Beginning 25 January 2011, hundreds of thousands of protesters occupied the square for 18 days despite violent attempts to disperse them, until Mubarak resigned on 11 February. The square remained a symbolic centre of protest in the turbulent years that followed, and a large obelisk and ram-headed sphinxes were installed in a 2020 renovation.
