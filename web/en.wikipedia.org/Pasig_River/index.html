The Pasig River (Filipino: Ilog Pasig) is a river in the Philippines that connects Laguna de Bay, the country's largest lake, to Manila Bay. Stretching about 25 kilometres, it bisects Metro Manila, separating the city of Manila and the southern districts from Mandaluyong, Makati, Pasig and Taguig to the north and east. Its major tributaries include the Marikina River and the San Juan River.

History

Before Spanish colonisation, the Pasig was the principal trade artery of the Tagalog polities along its banks, including the kingdoms of Tondo and Maynila at its mouth. Under Spanish and American rule it remained a busy commercial waterway lined with warehouses, factories and grand riverside houses; the Malacañang Palace, official residence of the President of the Philippines, stands on its northern bank in Manila.

Pollution and rehabilitation

Rapid industrialisation and population growth after the Second World War overwhelmed the river. By the 1990s the Pasig was considered biologically dead, choked with industrial effluent, untreated sewage and solid waste. A long-running rehabilitation programme, led successively by the Pasig River Rehabilitation Commission and later agencies, sought to relocate informal settlers from the banks, dredge silt, police discharges and build linear parks and ferry stations. Water quality improved enough for some aquatic life to return, and the river's rehabilitation received international recognition, including the Asia Riverprize awarded in 2018. A passenger ferry service operates along the river between Manila and Pasig.

Crossings

More than a dozen bridges carry Metro Manila's traffic across the Pasig, among them the Ayala Bridge, Quezon Bridge, Guadalupe Bridge and the Binondo–Intramuros Bridge, opened in 2022, which links the historic walled city of Intramuros with the Binondo Chinatown district.
