Prithvi (Sanskrit: pṛthvī, "Earth") is a family of tactical surface-to-surface short-range ballistic missiles developed by India's Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO). It was the first missile developed under India's Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme of 1983 and the country's first indigenously developed ballistic missile.

Variants

The original Prithvi I is a single-stage, liquid-fuelled missile with a range of 150 km and a payload of up to 1,000 kg, fielded by the Indian Army from 1994. The Prithvi II, for the Indian Air Force, extends the range to about 250–350 km with a reduced payload. The naval Prithvi III is a two-stage solid-fuelled variant launched from ships, developed into the Dhanush. The missiles are road-mobile, use inertial guidance with an advanced guidance system in later versions, and can carry conventional or nuclear warheads.

Development and use

Prithvi was first test-fired on 25 February 1988 from the Sriharikota range. India conducted frequent user trials from the Integrated Test Range at Chandipur, Odisha. The Prithvi II has also been adapted for ballistic missile defence testing, and its successor in the short-range role is the solid-fuelled Prahaar and the newer Pralay missile. Two-stage interceptor missiles of India's ballistic missile defence programme, such as the Prithvi Defence Vehicle, take their name from the family.
