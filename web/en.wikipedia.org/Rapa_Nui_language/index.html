Rapa Nui language

The Rapa Nui language (Rapa Nui: Vananga Rapa Nui) is an Eastern Polynesian language of the Austronesian family, spoken on Easter Island (Rapa Nui), Chile. It is closely related to Tahitian, Hawaiian, and the other languages of the Polynesian Triangle.

History and status

Rapa Nui was the island's sole language until sustained contact with Europeans and South Americans in the 19th century. Spanish and Tahitian influence grew after the island's annexation by Chile in 1888. Today the language is endangered: most younger Rapa Nui speak Spanish as their first language, and fluent native speakers number only a few thousand, though immersion preschools (hogar re'o) and bilingual schooling promote revitalisation.

Features

Like other Polynesian languages, Rapa Nui has a small phoneme inventory, with ten consonants and five vowels, and largely open syllables. It uses reduplication productively and marks the dual number in pronouns. The historical rongorongo script, inscribed on wooden tablets, appears to have recorded an older form of the language but remains undeciphered.
