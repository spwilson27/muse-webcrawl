Juan de la Cierva

Juan de la Cierva y Codorníu (21 September 1895 – 9 December 1936) was a Spanish civil engineer and aviator who invented the autogyro, the first successful rotorcraft, whose freely spinning rotor laid the conceptual groundwork for the helicopter.

The autogyro

After a three-engined bomber he designed crashed in 1919 because of a stall, Cierva sought an aircraft incapable of stalling. His solution was a rotor that spun freely by autorotation, generating lift while a conventional propeller provided forward thrust. After early failures caused by rotor asymmetry, he introduced the flapping hinge, which equalized lift across the disc. His C.4 autogyro made the first successful flight on 9 January 1923 at Getafe, Spain.

Later work and death

Cierva founded the Cierva Autogiro Company in England in 1926, and his machines were built under licence in the United States (by Harold Pitcairn), Germany, and France. His later innovations included direct rotor control and mechanical "jump" take-off. He died in an airliner crash at Croydon Airport in 1936, aged 41. His rotor principles — especially articulated blades — were essential to Igor Sikorsky's practical helicopter.
