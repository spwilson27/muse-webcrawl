2-step garage, also known simply as 2-step, is a genre of electronic dance music and a subgenre of UK garage. It emerged in London in the late 1990s, characterised by a distinctive syncopated drum pattern in which the kick drum avoids falling on every quarter-note beat, giving the rhythm a shuffling, "skipping" feel, typically at around 130–135 beats per minute.

Characteristics

Unlike the four-to-the-floor rhythm of earlier UK garage, 2-step uses a broken beat with prominent shuffled hi-hats, deep sub-bass lines often derived from jungle and drum and bass, and soulful R&B-style vocals frequently pitched or chopped. Producers such as El-B and the duo Artful Dodger were central to the sound, and early 2-step was closely associated with pirate radio stations and London clubs.

Influence

Around 2000–2001, darker and more minimal offshoots of 2-step developed in South London, evolving into dubstep, while the breakbeat-heavy side fed into grime. Artists including Burial later revived and reimagined the 2-step rhythm within the UK's "hardcore continuum".
