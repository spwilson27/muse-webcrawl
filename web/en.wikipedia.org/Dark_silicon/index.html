Dark silicon

Dark silicon is circuitry in an integrated circuit that cannot be powered simultaneously at its full operating voltage and frequency within the chip's thermal design power (TDP) budget. As transistor density has continued to increase following Moore's law while Dennard scaling broke down, power density has risen to the point where only a fraction of a chip's transistors can be active at once.

Causes

Under classic Dennard scaling, shrinking transistors also reduced the voltage and power per transistor, keeping power density constant. Since roughly the mid-2000s, voltage has stopped scaling proportionally, so each new process generation packs in more transistors than the power budget can switch on simultaneously. Studies in the early 2010s projected that at future nodes a majority of a chip could be "dark" at any moment.

Responses

Designers manage dark silicon through aggressive power gating and clock gating, dynamic voltage and frequency scaling, and heterogeneous architectures that pair efficient and high-performance cores (such as big.LITTLE designs). Another response is specialisation: dedicating dark area to accelerators that are extremely energy-efficient for specific tasks and spend most of their time off — the "dim silicon" and co-processor approaches.
