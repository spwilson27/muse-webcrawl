Michelangelo Pistoletto (born 23 June 1933) is an Italian artist, action and object artist, and art theorist. He is acknowledged as one of the main representatives of the Italian Arte Povera movement, and his work mainly deals with the subject matter of reflection and the unification of art and everyday life.

Mirror Paintings

In 1961–62 Pistoletto created his first Mirror Paintings (Quadri specchianti), in which he fixed life-size figures, first painted in tissue paper and later silkscreened, onto polished stainless steel. The viewer's own reflection becomes part of the work, which the artist regarded as bringing art into direct contact with the present moment and the presence of the spectator.

Arte Povera and later work

From 1967 Pistoletto became a leading figure of Arte Povera, using "poor" materials such as rags, old clothing and vegetables. His Venus of the Rags (1967) places a classical statue of Venus before a mound of discarded clothes. His studio in Turin, the Lo Zoo group, staged collaborative actions blurring art and theatre. In the 1990s he founded Cittadellarte – Fondazione Pistoletto in Biella, a laboratory for art and social transformation, and developed the concept of the Third Paradise, symbolised by a reworking of the infinity sign, promoting a balanced reconciliation between nature and artifice.

Recognition

Pistoletto received the Golden Lion for Lifetime Achievement at the Venice Biennale in 2003 and the Praemium Imperiale in 2013. His works are held in major museums worldwide, including Tate Modern and the Museum of Modern Art.
