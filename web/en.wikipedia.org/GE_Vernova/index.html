GE Vernova Inc. is an American energy technology company headquartered in Cambridge, Massachusetts. It was formed from General Electric's energy businesses — GE Power, GE Renewable Energy, and GE Digital's grid software — and spun off as an independent public company in April 2024, the final step of GE's three-way breakup. The name combines "verde" and "verdant", evoking green energy, with "nova", Latin for new.

Segments

The company operates three segments. Power includes gas turbines — GE's installed base of heavy-duty turbines is the world's largest — along with steam turbines, nuclear services, and hydro. Wind supplies onshore and offshore wind turbines, including the Haliade-X, among the most powerful offshore machines built. Electrification covers grid solutions, high-voltage equipment, power conversion, and energy software.

Scale

GE Vernova states that its equipment helps generate roughly a quarter to a third of the world's electricity, with a vast installed base serviced under long-term agreements. It trades on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker GEV and was added to the S&P 500 at its spin-off.

Challenges

The wind segment faced quality and profitability problems in its early independent years, including high-profile offshore blade failures in 2024 at projects such as Vineyard Wind in the United States and Dogger Bank in the United Kingdom, even as demand for gas turbines and grid equipment surged with rising electricity demand from data centres.
