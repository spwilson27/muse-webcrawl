Charles Garnier (architect)

Jean-Louis Charles Garnier (6 November 1825 – 3 August 1898) was a French architect best known for the Palais Garnier, the Paris opera house that bears his name, and for the Monte Carlo Casino. His lavish, eclectic style became emblematic of the French Second Empire.

Early career

Garnier was born in Paris and trained at the École des Beaux-Arts, winning the Prix de Rome in 1848 and studying classical architecture in Italy and Greece. In 1860–61 he won the competition for a new Paris opera house, Napoleon III's grand project, despite being only 35 and relatively unknown; asked by the Empress Eugénie what style the building was in, he reportedly replied, "Napoleon III, Madame!"

Palais Garnier

Construction of the Palais Garnier began in 1861 and, interrupted by the Franco-Prussian War and the Commune, was completed in 1875. The building combines a highly functional plan—stage house, auditorium, and the grand staircase and foyer designed as social spaces—with opulent decoration in coloured marbles, mosaic and gilding. It later became the setting of Gaston Leroux's novel The Phantom of the Opera.

Other works

Garnier designed the casino and opera house at Monte Carlo (1878–79), the Cercle de la Librairie and several villas on the French Riviera, and wrote extensively on architecture. He died at Bordighera, Italy, in 1898.
