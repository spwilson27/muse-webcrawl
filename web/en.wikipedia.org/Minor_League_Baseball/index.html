Minor League Baseball

Minor League Baseball (MiLB) is a hierarchy of professional baseball leagues in the Americas that compete at levels below Major League Baseball (MLB). The system provides a pathway for player development: young prospects progress through progressively more competitive levels, while veteran players may use the minors to rehabilitate injuries or attempt comebacks.

Structure

Following a major reorganization in 2021, MLB assumed direct control of the affiliated minors. Each of the 30 major league clubs fields affiliated teams at four full-season levels:

  • Triple-A — the highest level, split into the International League and the Pacific Coast League, with 30 teams;
  • Double-A — 30 teams in the Eastern League, Southern League, and Texas League;
  • High-A — 30 teams;
  • Single-A (Low-A) — 30 teams.

Below these are rookie-level leagues, including the Arizona Complex League, Florida Complex League, and the Dominican Summer League. In total there are about 120 licensed affiliated minor league teams, plus independent "Partner Leagues" such as the Atlantic League, American Association, Frontier League, and Pioneer League.

History

Minor leagues have existed since the late 19th century; the National Association of Professional Base Ball Leagues was founded in 1901 to protect the interests of smaller clubs. Branch Rickey pioneered the modern "farm system" with the St. Louis Cardinals in the 1920s and 1930s, acquiring chains of minor league teams to develop talent. The classification system (once running from Class D up to Triple-A) was repeatedly revised before the 2021 restructuring, which reduced the number of affiliated teams from 160 to 120 and raised minor league player salaries.

Player development and culture

Minor league teams are fixtures of small and mid-sized American cities, known for inexpensive tickets, quirky promotions, and distinctive team names and mascots. Prospects are promoted level by level; only a minority of minor leaguers ever reach the majors. Players on a major league club's 40-man roster optioned to the minors remain under MLB contract, while most minor leaguers historically earned modest wages—an issue addressed by a first-ever minor league collective bargaining agreement ratified in 2023.
