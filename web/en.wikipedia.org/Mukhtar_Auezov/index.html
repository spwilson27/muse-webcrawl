Mukhtar Omarkhanuli Auezov (Kazakh: Мұхтар Омарханұлы Әуезов; 28 September 1897 – 27 June 1961) was a Kazakh writer, playwright and scholar, regarded as the founder of modern Kazakh literature. He is best known for the four-volume epic novel The Path of Abai (Abai Zholy), a fictionalised biography of the Kazakh poet-philosopher Abai Qunanbaiuly, which won the Stalin Prize in 1949 and the Lenin Prize in 1959.

Born in the Semipalatinsk region near Abai's winter quarters, Auezov was educated in Semipalatinsk and at Leningrad State University, and later earned a doctorate in philology. His first play, Enlik-Kebek (1917), is considered the foundation of Kazakh drama. He wrote dozens of plays, stories and scholarly works, including major studies of the Kyrgyz epic Manas and translations of classical Russian literature into Kazakh.

Legacy

Auezov's house in Almaty is a museum, and the Kazakh State Academic Drama Theatre in Almaty bears his name, as does the Institute of Literature and Art of the Kazakhstan Academy of Sciences. His works have been translated into many languages, and The Path of Abai remains a cornerstone of the Kazakh national canon.
