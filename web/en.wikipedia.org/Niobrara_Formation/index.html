Niobrara Formation

The Niobrara Formation, also called the Niobrara Chalk, is a geologic formation in North America deposited between 87 and 82 million years ago during the Late Cretaceous. It underlies much of the Great Plains of the United States and Canada, from Kansas and Nebraska to Montana and Alberta. The chalk was laid down in the Western Interior Seaway, a shallow sea that split North America in two.

Stratigraphy

The formation is divided into two members: the lower Fort Hays Limestone Member and the upper Smoky Hill Chalk Member. It is famous among palaeontologists for its beautifully preserved marine fossils, collected since the 1870s by figures such as Othniel Charles Marsh and the Sternberg family.

Fossils

The Smoky Hill Chalk has yielded giant marine reptiles including mosasaurs such as Tylosaurus, long-necked plesiosaurs such as Elasmosaurus, the giant fish Xiphactinus (including the famous "fish-within-a-fish" specimen), pterosaurs such as Pteranodon, and the flightless seabird Hesperornis. Famous Kansas exposures include Castle Rock and Monument Rocks.
