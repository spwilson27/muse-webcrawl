Eclogite

Eclogite is a dense metamorphic rock formed when basaltic or gabbroic rock is subjected to very high pressures (and moderate to high temperatures), typically in subduction zones. It consists mainly of red garnet (pyrope-almandine) and green clinopyroxene (omphacite), giving it a striking red-and-green appearance.

Formation

As oceanic crust descends in a subduction zone, basalt transforms through blueschist into eclogite at depths of roughly 45 km and beyond. Because eclogite is denser than the surrounding mantle peridotite, its formation helps pull the slab downward. Some eclogites are brought back to the surface in ultra-high-pressure terranes, occasionally bearing coesite or micro-diamonds.

Occurrences

Famous localities include the Western Gneiss Region of Norway, the Zermatt-Saas zone of the Alps, Dabie-Sulu in China, and eclogite xenoliths in kimberlite pipes, which sample the deep lithosphere.

Significance

Eclogite is a key indicator of past subduction and of the depth conditions in orogenic belts, and its role in slab pull makes it central to the mechanics of plate tectonics.
