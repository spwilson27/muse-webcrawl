The Lower East Side is a neighborhood in the southeastern part of the New York City borough of Manhattan, roughly between the Bowery and the East River from Canal Street to Houston Street. Once known for its dense working-class and immigrant population, it has historically been a center of Jewish, German, Italian, Puerto Rican and Chinese immigration to the United States.

History

During the 19th and early 20th centuries the Lower East Side was one of the most densely populated urban districts in the world, crowded with tenement buildings housing new arrivals. It was famous for its pushcarts, sweatshops, settlement houses and vibrant Yiddish theatre scene. Landmarks such as the Eldridge Street Synagogue and the Tenement Museum preserve this heritage.

Modern era

From the late 20th century the neighborhood underwent rapid gentrification, with galleries, bars, boutiques and music venues transforming its character while parts of the historic district were listed on the National Register of Historic Places.
