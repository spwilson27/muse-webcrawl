The Byerley Turk (c. 1680 – c. 1703 or later) was a stallion who became one of the three foundation sires of the Thoroughbred horse breed, alongside the Darley Arabian and the Godolphin Arabian. Every modern Thoroughbred traces its male line to one of these three stallions; the Byerley Turk's line survives chiefly through his great-great-grandson Herod (foaled 1758), a leading sire of the 18th century.

Origins and military service

According to tradition, the horse was captured from Ottoman forces at the Siege of Buda in 1686 during the Great Turkish War, and was later ridden by Captain Robert Byerley of the British Army. He is said to have served as Byerley's charger at the Battle of the Boyne in 1690 in Ireland, where legend holds that his speed saved his rider from capture. His exact breed is uncertain: like many "Turk" horses of the period he may have been an Arabian or a Turkoman horse, and debate continues among historians of the breed.

Stud career

After his military service the Byerley Turk stood at stud in Yorkshire, England, first at Middridge Grange and later at Goldsborough Hall. He was not bred to many mares of high quality by the standards of the later stud book, yet produced offspring whose descendants proved influential. His tail-male line, once the dominant one in the 18th century through Herod and his son Highflyer, has since been largely eclipsed by the Darley Arabian's line through Eclipse, though it persists in some pedigrees.
