Zeewolde is a municipality and a town in the province of Flevoland in the central Netherlands. It stands on the Southern Flevoland polder — land reclaimed from the former Zuiderzee — on the shore of the Wolderwijd lake, opposite the old Hanseatic town of Harderwijk.

History

The Southern Flevoland polder was drained in 1968, and Zeewolde was planned as an agricultural service centre for the surrounding farms. The municipality of Zeewolde was formally established on 1 January 1984, making it for many years the youngest municipality in the Netherlands. The town grew steadily with commuters and, increasingly, recreation and water sports on the bordering lakes.

Geography and nature

Zeewolde is notable for the Horsterwold, one of the largest contiguous deciduous forests in the Netherlands, planted on the young polder clay. The surrounding landscape features the landscape-art route De Verbeelding, extensive meadows, and large wind farms that make the municipality one of the country's leaders in onshore wind energy.
