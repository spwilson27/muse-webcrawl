Hypoglycin

Hypoglycin is a naturally occurring amino acid derivative found in the unripened fruit of the ackee tree (Blighia sapida) and in the seeds of box elder and other members of the soapberry family (Sapindaceae), including lychee. Hypoglycin A is concentrated in the fruit; its metabolite causes the severe poisoning known as Jamaican vomiting sickness.

Mechanism of toxicity

Hypoglycin A is metabolised in the liver to methylenecyclopropylacetic acid (MCPA), which forms non-metabolisable esters with coenzyme A and carnitine, inhibiting several acyl-CoA dehydrogenases required for the beta-oxidation of fatty acids and for gluconeogenesis. Unable to make new glucose while stores are depleted — especially in malnourished children who have fasted — victims develop profound hypoglycemia, vomiting, convulsions, coma and often death, with fatty infiltration of the liver resembling Reye's syndrome.

Outbreaks

Hypoglycin poisoning from unripe ackee has been known in Jamaica since the 19th century; ripe, properly opened ackee is the national dish ingredient and safe because hypoglycin falls as the fruit ripens. Similar outbreaks of acute encephalitis in children in lychee-growing regions of India (notably Muzaffarpur, Bihar) and Vietnam have been linked to hypoglycin and the related compound MCPG in lychees eaten on an empty stomach.
