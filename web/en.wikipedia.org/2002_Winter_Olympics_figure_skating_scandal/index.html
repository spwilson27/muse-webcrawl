2002 Winter Olympics figure skating scandal

EventPairs figure skating
VenueSalt Lake City, Utah
DateFebruary 2002
Pairs involvedBerezhnaya/Sikharulidze; Sale/Pelletier

The 2002 Winter Olympics figure skating scandal was a controversy surrounding the judging of the pairs figure skating competition at the 2002 Winter Olympics in Salt Lake City, Utah, United States. On 11 February 2002, the Russian pair Elena Berezhnaya and Anton Sikharulidze won the gold medal ahead of the Canadian pair Jamie Salé and David Pelletier, despite a visible error in the Russians' free skate and a clean performance by the Canadians.

Allegations

French judge Marie-Reine Le Gougne was accused of having been pressured to vote for the Russians as part of a vote-trading arrangement involving French ice dancing interests. She initially confessed to misconduct and then recanted. The affair produced intense international media coverage.

Resolution

On 15 February 2002, the International Olympic Committee and the International Skating Union announced that the Canadian pair would also be awarded gold medals, in an unprecedented second medal ceremony, while the Russians kept their gold. Le Gougne and French federation president Didier Gailhaguet were suspended for three years.

Aftermath

The scandal led the ISU to abandon the 6.0 judging system and adopt the ISU Judging System, a cumulative-points system first used in the 2003–04 season.
