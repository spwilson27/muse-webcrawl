Federal student aid in the United States

Federal student aid in the United States encompasses grants, loans and work-study funds provided by the United States Department of Education to students attending postsecondary institutions. Eligibility is determined chiefly through the Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA), and programmes are authorised principally under Title IV of the Higher Education Act of 1965.

Administered byU.S. Department of Education
ApplicationFAFSA
Main programmesPell Grants, Direct Loans, Federal Work-Study

Programmes

The Federal Pell Grant is the largest grant programme, awarded mainly to low-income undergraduates and not repaid. Federal Direct Loans — subsidised, unsubsidised, PLUS and consolidation loans — make up the largest share of aid volume, while the Federal Work-Study programme subsidises part-time campus employment. Institutions must be accredited and certified to participate in Title IV programmes.
