Piano

The piano is a keyboard instrument that produces sound when its keys are pressed, causing hammers to strike strings stretched over a soundboard inside a wooden case. Its name is short for pianoforte, referring to its ability to play both soft (piano) and loud (forte) — a dynamic range impossible on the harpsichord.

History

The piano was invented in Florence around 1700 by Bartolomeo Cristofori. Through the 18th and 19th centuries it evolved heavier frames (culminating in the cast-iron frame), a wider range of 88 keys (A0 to C8), and greater power, becoming the central instrument of Western music-making, teaching and composition.

Types and mechanism

Grand pianos have horizontal strings and actions; upright (vertical) pianos save space with vertical strings. Pressing a key lifts a hammer that strikes the string and rebounds, while dampers stop the sound when the key is released; three pedals — sustain, sostenuto and soft (una corda) — modify the sound.
