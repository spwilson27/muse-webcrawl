Brent oilfield

The Brent oilfield is an oil and gas field in the East Shetland Basin of the North Sea, about 186 kilometres northeast of Lerwick, Shetland Islands, Scotland. Discovered by Shell in 1971, it was one of the most significant oilfields in the United Kingdom sector, and it gave its name to Brent crude, a global oil pricing benchmark.

History

Production began in 1976 through the Brent Spar loading buoy and a system of four platforms (Alpha, Bravo, Charlie, and Delta). At its peak in 1982 the field produced over 500,000 barrels of oil equivalent per day. Its reservoirs lie in the Middle Jurassic Brent Group sandstones — the field's name is also an acronym of those formations (Broom, Rannoch, Etive, Ness, Tarbert), coincidentally matching the brent goose.

Brent Spar controversy

In 1995 a plan by Shell to dispose of the Brent Spar storage buoy by deep-sea sinking provoked a high-profile Greenpeace campaign and consumer boycotts; Shell reversed the decision, and the Spar was eventually reused in a quay project in Norway.

Decommissioning

Production wound down in the 2010s, and a major decommissioning programme removed the platform topsides, beginning with Brent Delta in 2017. Brent crude pricing continued by reference to other North Sea streams (the BFOET complex).
