Dublin Zoo (Irish: Zú Bhaile Átha Cliath) is a zoo in Phoenix Park, Dublin, Ireland. It is one of the world's oldest zoos, having opened in 1831, and is one of Ireland's most popular visitor attractions, regularly drawing more than a million visitors a year.

History

The zoo was founded by members of the medical profession interested in the study of animals while they were alive and more particularly after death. It opened on 1 September 1831 with animals from the Zoological Society of London, including monkeys, a lion, leopards and other species. The entrance fee was set low so that ordinary Dubliners could visit, and the zoo proved popular from the start.

Over the nineteenth and twentieth centuries the zoo expanded across its site in Phoenix Park. In the 1990s it adopted a master plan that replaced many older cages with large, naturalistic habitats arranged in themed zones. The zoo's lions were famous in the twentieth century: the lion cub "Slats", born at Dublin Zoo in 1919, was the model for the roaring lion logo used by the Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer film studio.

Animals and habitats

The zoo is organised into themed areas including the African Savanna, home to giraffes, zebras, ostriches, oryx and white rhinoceros; the Kaziranga Forest Trail, home to Asian elephants; Gorilla Rainforest; the Orangutan Forest; and a South American house. Other popular exhibits include the Zoorassic World reptile house, the Family Farm, and habitats for tigers, lions, snow leopards, hippos and chimpanzees.

Conservation and education

Dublin Zoo participates in international breeding programmes for endangered species coordinated through the European Association of Zoos and Aquaria (EAZA). It funds conservation projects in the wild and runs education programmes for schools and visitors, including the annual "Wild Lights" winter lantern festival.
