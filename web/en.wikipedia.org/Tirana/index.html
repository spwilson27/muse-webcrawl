Tirana

Tirana is the capital and largest city of Albania, located in the centre of the country, enclosed by Mount Dajt in the east and overlooking the Adriatic plain. It is Albania's political, economic and cultural centre and one of the largest cities on the Balkan Peninsula, with a municipal population of roughly 500,000 and a wider metropolitan population approaching 900,000.

History

Tirana was founded in 1614 by the Ottoman Albanian general Sulejman Pasha Bargjini, who built a mosque, a bakery and a hammam at what became the city's old town. It remained a modest town until 8 February 1920, when the Congress of Lushnjë proclaimed it Albania's capital. Under King Zog and then Italian occupation, grand boulevards and ministries were laid out around Skanderbeg Square. After 1944 the communist regime of Enver Hoxha industrialised the city and built the Pyramid of Tirana as a museum to Hoxha. Since the fall of communism in 1991, Tirana has grown rapidly, with colourful repainting of drab apartment blocks under mayor Edi Rama in the 2000s drawing international attention.

Landmarks and culture

Landmarks include Skanderbeg Square with the equestrian statue of national hero Skanderbeg, the Et'hem Bey Mosque, the clock tower, the National History Museum with its mosaic "The Albanians", and the Dajti Ekspres cable car. The Blloku district, once reserved for the communist elite, is now the centre of nightlife.
