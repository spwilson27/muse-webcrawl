In linguistics, derivation is the process of forming a new word on the basis of an existing word, for example happiness and unhappy from happy, or determination from determine. Derivation stands in contrast to inflection, which modifies a word's form to express grammatical categories without creating a new lexeme.

Derivational affixes

Derivation typically occurs through the addition of affixes. English derivational suffixes such as -ness, -ity, -er, -ment and -able, and prefixes such as un-, re-, and pre- can change a word's meaning and often its part of speech: for example, the suffix -ness turns adjectives into nouns. Derivational affixes may also change pronunciation and stress.

Derivation vs. inflection

Unlike inflectional endings, which never change a word's core meaning or category and are typically fully productive and regular, derivational processes may be only partially productive, may have lexical exceptions, and may alter the syntactic category of the base. Derivational affixes generally occur closer to the root than inflectional affixes.

Other processes

Besides affixation, new words are formed by compounding (e.g. blackbird), conversion or zero derivation (using a noun as a verb, e.g. to email), clipping, and blending.
