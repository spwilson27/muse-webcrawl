Six degrees of freedom

Six degrees of freedom (6DOF) describes the freedom of movement of a rigid body in three-dimensional space. A body can translate along three perpendicular axes and rotate about those same three axes, giving six independent ways it can change position and orientation.

The six movements

The three translational degrees of freedom are commonly called surge (forward/backward), sway (left/right), and heave (up/down). The three rotational degrees of freedom are roll (rotation about the longitudinal axis), pitch (rotation about the transverse axis), and yaw (rotation about the vertical axis). These terms originate in naval and aeronautical engineering.

Applications

The 6DOF model is fundamental in robotics, where a manipulator arm needs at least six degrees of freedom to position and orient its end effector arbitrarily; in flight simulation and motion platforms, which reproduce all six motions; in naval architecture and spacecraft dynamics; and in virtual and augmented reality, where headsets that track all six degrees are termed “6DOF” devices, in contrast to 3DOF devices that track rotation only. The concept also appears in video game design, notably in games such as Descent that allow completely free movement.
