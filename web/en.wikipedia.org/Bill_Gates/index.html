Bill Gates

William Henry Gates III (born 28 October 1955) is an American businessman and philanthropist. A pioneer of the personal computer revolution, he co-founded Microsoft in 1975 with his childhood friend Paul Allen and led it to become the world's largest personal computer software company. For many years Gates was the richest person in the world, and since stepping back from Microsoft he has devoted himself largely to philanthropy through the Gates Foundation.

Early life

Gates was born in Seattle, Washington, to a prominent family — his father was a lawyer and his mother served on corporate boards. At Lakeside School he gained access to a computer terminal and became an avid programmer. He enrolled at Harvard College in 1973 but left in 1975 to start Microsoft in Albuquerque, New Mexico, after he and Allen developed a BASIC interpreter for the Altair 8800 microcomputer.

Microsoft

Microsoft's breakthrough came in 1980 when IBM licensed its operating system, MS-DOS, for the IBM PC. The success of Windows from the late 1980s made Microsoft dominant in PC software and Gates a billionaire at 31. As CEO he was known for intense competitiveness and technical involvement; the company faced a landmark United States antitrust case in the late 1990s. Gates stepped down as CEO in 2000, became chairman and chief software architect, left day-to-day work in 2008, and resigned from the board in 2020.

Philanthropy

With his then-wife Melinda French Gates he founded the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation in 2000 (renamed the Gates Foundation after their divorce in 2021), which became the largest private charitable foundation in the world, focusing on global health, vaccination, malaria and polio eradication, and education. In 2010 he co-founded The Giving Pledge with Warren Buffett. In 2025 Gates announced plans to give away nearly all of his remaining wealth through the foundation over the following two decades.
