Good King Wenceslas

"Good King Wenceslas"
GenreChristmas carol
TextJohn Mason Neale (1853)
Melody"Tempus adest floridum" (13th-century spring carol)
SubjectSaint Wenceslaus I of Bohemia

"Good King Wenceslas" is a popular Christmas carol that tells the story of a Bohemian king (Saint Wenceslaus I, Duke of Bohemia) braving harsh winter weather on the Feast of Stephen (December 26) to give alms to a poor peasant. The lyrics were written by English hymnwriter John Mason Neale in 1853, set to the melody of the 13th-century spring carol "Tempus adest floridum", first published in the Finnish song collection Piae Cantiones (1582).

Story

Looking out on the Feast of Stephen, Wenceslas sees a poor man gathering winter fuel. Learning from his page where the man lives, the king orders meat, wine, and pine logs to be carried to him. As the weather worsens, the page grows faint in the snow, but the king tells him to walk in his footprints — and the page finds the saint's steps miraculously warm enough to sustain him. The carol closes with a moral addressed to rich Christians: those who bless the poor shall themselves find blessing.

The historical Wenceslaus

Wenceslaus I (c. 907 – 935) was Duke of Bohemia, famed for his piety and charity, and was murdered by followers of his brother Boleslaus. He was venerated as a saint and martyr and became the patron saint of Bohemia and of the Czech state. Although called a king in the carol, he was in life a duke; the royal title was granted posthumously by the Holy Roman Emperor.
