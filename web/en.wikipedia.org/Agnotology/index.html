Agnotology

Agnotology
FieldScience studies, epistemology
Term coined byRobert N. Proctor (with Londa Schiebinger)
FocusCulturally induced ignorance and doubt

Agnotology is the study of culturally induced ignorance or doubt, particularly the deliberate production of misleading or false scientific data and uncertainty. The term was coined by Stanford historian Robert N. Proctor, building on work with Londa Schiebinger.

Concepts

Agnotology examines ignorance not simply as an absence of knowledge but as something actively constructed and maintained — through withheld data, selective research funding, public-relations campaigns, and the strategic manufacture of controversy. The classic case is the tobacco industry's decades-long campaign to cast doubt on the link between smoking and cancer, summarized in an internal memo's phrase: "Doubt is our product."

Applications

The framework has been applied to climate change denial, pharmaceutical marketing, and the suppression or neglect of research topics, as well as to "lost" knowledge — information destroyed, forgotten, or never pursued for political or cultural reasons.
