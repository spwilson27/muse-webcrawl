Andrea Pozzo

Andrea Pozzo (30 November 1642 – 31 August 1709) was an Italian Jesuit brother, Baroque painter, architect, and theorist of perspective, celebrated for his grand frescoes using the illusionistic technique of quadratura. His masterpiece is the nave ceiling of the Church of St. Ignatius of Loyola in Rome, the Allegory of the Missionary Work of the Jesuits (1691–94).

Life and career

Pozzo was born in Trento and entered the Society of Jesus in 1665. After training in Milan and working in Lombardy and Piedmont, he was summoned to Rome in 1681. There he produced his most famous works: the ceiling of Sant'Ignazio, in which painted architecture continues the real architecture of the church and opens into a vision of Paradise; the apse and dome frescoes of the Gesù; and a celebrated fake dome painted on canvas for Sant'Ignazio, replacing an unbuilt cupola. In 1703 he moved to Vienna at the invitation of Emperor Leopold I, where he painted the ceiling of the Jesuit church and the great fresco of the Liechtenstein Garden Palace, and died in 1709.

Treatise

Pozzo's two-volume treatise Perspectiva de' pittori e architetti (1693–1700), illustrated with his own engravings, was one of the most influential manuals of perspective and stage and ceiling decoration of the Baroque era, translated into several languages and used well into the 18th century, including an edition prepared for China.

Legacy

Pozzo's single-viewpoint illusionism — the composition resolves perfectly from one marked spot on the nave floor — represents the culmination of Baroque ceiling painting and exerted wide influence across Catholic Europe and its missions.
