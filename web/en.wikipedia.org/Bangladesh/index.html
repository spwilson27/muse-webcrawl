Bangladesh, officially the People's Republic of Bangladesh, is a country in South Asia. It is the eighth-most populous country in the world and among the most densely populated, with a population of about 170 million in an area of 148,460 square kilometres (57,320 sq mi). Bangladesh shares land borders with India to the west, north, and east, and Myanmar to the southeast, and has a coastline on the Bay of Bengal to the south. Its capital and largest city is Dhaka.

Geography

The country occupies the fertile Bengal delta, formed by the confluence of the Ganges (Padma), Brahmaputra (Jamuna), and Meghna rivers — one of the largest river deltas in the world. Most of the land is low-lying alluvial plain less than twelve metres above sea level, making the country prone to monsoon flooding and cyclones. The southeast contains the Chittagong Hill Tracts, and the southwest coast hosts the Sundarbans, the world's largest mangrove forest and a habitat of the Bengal tiger.

History

The region of Bengal was historically a wealthy centre of trade, textiles, and agriculture, ruled in turn by Buddhist, Hindu, and Muslim dynasties before coming under Mughal and then British rule. At the partition of India in 1947 it became the eastern wing of Pakistan, separated from the western wing by some 1,600 kilometres of Indian territory. Disparities between the wings, the Bengali language movement of 1952, and the refusal to seat the Awami League after its victory in the 1970 elections led to the Bangladesh Liberation War of 1971, in which Bangladesh won independence as a secular republic under Sheikh Mujibur Rahman.

Economy and society

Bangladesh has achieved sustained economic growth driven by its ready-made garment industry — one of the largest in the world — remittances from overseas workers, and agriculture centred on rice and jute. It pioneered microfinance through the Grameen Bank founded by Muhammad Yunus. Bengali (Bangla) is the official language and the mother tongue of the great majority; Islam is the predominant religion, with significant Hindu and other minorities. The country's culture is marked by the Bengali New Year (Pohela Boishakh), a rich literary tradition including Rabindranath Tagore and Kazi Nazrul Islam, and the national game of cricket.
