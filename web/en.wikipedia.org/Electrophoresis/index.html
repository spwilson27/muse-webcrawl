Electrophoresis

Electrophoresis is the motion of charged dispersed particles or dissolved charged molecules relative to a fluid under the influence of a spatially uniform electric field. It is used to separate molecules by size and charge.

Principle

Charged particles migrate toward the electrode of opposite sign at a velocity depending on their charge, size, and the viscosity of the medium. Small, highly charged molecules move fastest.

Gel electrophoresis

In gel electrophoresis, samples are loaded into wells in an agarose or polyacrylamide gel. The gel matrix acts as a sieve, separating DNA, RNA, or proteins by length or molecular weight. Bands are visualised with dyes such as ethidium bromide or Coomassie blue.

Other forms

Capillary electrophoresis performs separations in narrow tubes with high resolution and automation, and was central to high-throughput DNA sequencing. Two-dimensional gel electrophoresis separates proteins first by isoelectric point and then by mass.

Applications

Electrophoresis is fundamental in molecular biology, forensics, clinical diagnostics (such as serum protein electrophoresis), and quality control of pharmaceuticals.
