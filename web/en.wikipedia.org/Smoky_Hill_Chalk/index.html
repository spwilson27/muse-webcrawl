Smoky Hill Chalk

The Smoky Hill Chalk Member of the Niobrara Chalk formation is a Cretaceous conservation Lagerstätte, or fossil deposit with exceptional preservation, located in Kansas. Named for the Smoky Hill River, it outcrops across the northwestern part of the state and is famous for its marine fossils from the Western Interior Seaway.

Fossils

The chalk was deposited on the floor of an ancient sea roughly 87 to 82 million years ago. It preserves articulated skeletons of giant marine reptiles — mosasaurs such as Tylosaurus and plesiosaurs — as well as pterosaurs (Pteranodon and Nyctosaurus), toothed birds (Hesperornis, Ichthyornis), and enormous bony fish such as the 4-metre Xiphactinus. One famous specimen is a "fish-within-a-fish": a Xiphactinus preserved with a whole Gillicus inside it, collected by George F. Sternberg.

History of study

Fossils from the Smoky Hill Chalk were collected from the 1870s onward by paleontologists including Othniel Charles Marsh, Edward Drinker Cope, and the Sternberg family. Many of its specimens formed the core of museum collections during the Bone Wars era. Exposures at Monument Rocks and Castle Rock are protected as landmarks.
