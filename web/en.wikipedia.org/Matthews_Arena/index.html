Matthews Arena

Location: Boston, Massachusetts
Opened: 1910
Owner: Northeastern University
Capacity: about 4,666 (hockey)

Matthews Arena is a multi-purpose arena in Boston, Massachusetts, and is home to the Northeastern Huskies men's and women's ice hockey teams and the men's basketball team. It is the world's oldest multi-purpose athletic building still in use, as well as the oldest arena still in use for ice hockey. It opened in 1910 as the Boston Arena.

History

The arena hosted the first games of the Boston Bruins (1924) and Boston Celtics (1946), both of which later moved to the Boston Garden. It was also home to the Boston Olympics of the Eastern Hockey League and several college hockey programs. Northeastern University purchased the arena in 1979 and renamed it in 1982 in honour of alumnus and chairman of the board George J. Matthews and his wife Hope. The building underwent major renovations in 2009–2010, including a new centre-hung scoreboard and seating.

Notable events

President Theodore Roosevelt spoke at the arena, and it hosted events ranging from boxing and the Ice Capades to concerts. It remains a fixture of Hockey East competition and one of the most atmospheric venues in American college hockey.
