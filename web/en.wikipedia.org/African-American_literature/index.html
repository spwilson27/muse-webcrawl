African-American literature

African-American literature is the body of literature produced in the United States by writers of African descent. It begins in the late 18th century with poets such as Phillis Wheatley and develops through slave narratives, the Harlem Renaissance and the modern era.

Early works

Slave narratives such as Frederick Douglass's Narrative of the Life of Frederick Douglass (1845) and Harriet Jacobs's Incidents in the Life of a Slave Girl (1861) were central to abolitionist literature and remain foundational texts.

Harlem Renaissance and beyond

The Harlem Renaissance of the 1920s brought writers such as Langston Hughes, Zora Neale Hurston and Claude McKay to prominence. Later major figures include Richard Wright, Ralph Ellison, James Baldwin, Toni Morrison — the first African-American winner of the Nobel Prize in Literature — and Maya Angelou.
