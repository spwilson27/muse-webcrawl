Tadamichi Kuribayashi

Tadamichi Kuribayashi
Born: 7 July 1891, Nagano Prefecture, Japan
Died: c. 26 March 1945, Iwo Jima
Rank: General, Imperial Japanese Army
Known for: Commanding the Japanese defence at the Battle of Iwo Jima

General Tadamichi Kuribayashi (7 July 1891 – c. 26 March 1945) was an Imperial Japanese Army general best known as the Japanese commander in the Battle of Iwo Jima. Educated at Harvard University during a posting to the United States in the 1920s, Kuribayashi had an unusually informed view of American industrial power and opposed the war with the United States, believing Japan could not win it.

Iwo Jima

Appointed to command the island's defences in 1944, Kuribayashi rejected conventional beach defence doctrine. Instead he built an elaborate network of tunnels and bunkers in the island's volcanic interior, centred on Mount Suribachi and the Motoyama plateau, and ordered his men to fight a protracted battle of attrition in which each defender would exact the maximum price. The battle lasted from 19 February to 26 March 1945; of some 21,000 Japanese defenders, almost all were killed, while American casualties exceeded the total Japanese force in number.

Legacy

Kuribayashi's letters home, published posthumously as Picture Letters from the Commander in Chief, formed the basis of Clint Eastwood's 2006 film Letters from Iwo Jima, in which he was portrayed by Ken Watanabe. His body was never identified, and the circumstances of his death remain unknown.
