Coalition Provisional Authority

The Coalition Provisional Authority (CPA) was the transitional government of Iraq established by the United States-led coalition following the 2003 invasion of Iraq. It governed Iraq from April 2003 until sovereignty was transferred to the Iraqi Interim Government on 28 June 2004.

Administration

The CPA was first headed briefly by Jay Garner and then by Ambassador L. Paul Bremer III, who served as Administrator. It exercised executive, legislative, and judicial authority in Iraq and issued binding orders and regulations.

Controversial decisions

Two early CPA orders became especially controversial: Order 1, the de-Ba'athification of Iraqi society, which removed senior Ba'ath Party members from public employment, and Order 2, which dissolved the Iraqi Army and other security institutions. Critics argue these decisions contributed to unemployment, resentment, and the subsequent insurgency.

Dissolution

The CPA formally dissolved upon the transfer of sovereignty in June 2004, ahead of Iraq's January 2005 elections.
