Ontario County, New York

Ontario County is a county in the U.S. state of New York, located in the Finger Lakes region west of Syracuse and southeast of Rochester. Its county seat is Canandaigua. As of the 2020 census, the population was about 112,000.

History

The county was established in 1789, named after Lake Ontario, which originally formed its northern boundary. The original Ontario County covered a vast territory — most of western New York — before being repeatedly subdivided as settlement advanced. The land was acquired from the Iroquois under the Treaty of Hartford (1786) and the Phelps and Gorham Purchase (1788), and Canandaigua was the site of the 1794 Pickering Treaty between the United States and the Six Nations.

Geography

Modern Ontario County includes parts of the northern shores of Canandaigua Lake and Seneca Lake, two of the Finger Lakes. Vineyards and wineries are prominent in its agricultural economy, particularly around Naples, known for grape growing and its annual Grape Festival. Major communities include the cities of Canandaigua and Geneva (shared with Seneca County), and the villages of Naples, Victor and Bloomfield. The New York State Thruway (Interstate 90) crosses the county's north.
