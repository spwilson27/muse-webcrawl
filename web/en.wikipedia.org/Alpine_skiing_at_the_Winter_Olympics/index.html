Alpine skiing at the Winter Olympics

First held: 1936, Garmisch-Partenkirchen
Events: Downhill, super-G, giant slalom, slalom, combined, team
Most successful nation: Austria
Governing body: FIS

Alpine skiing has been contested at every Winter Olympic Games since 1936, when a combined event was held in Garmisch-Partenkirchen. The programme expanded with separate downhill and slalom races in 1948, giant slalom in 1952, super-G in 1988, and a mixed team event in 2018, giving men's, women's, and mixed competitions across speed and technical disciplines.

History

The sport's Olympic debut was controversial because ski teachers were excluded as professionals, prompting a boycott by Austrian and Swiss skiers. For decades the Games doubled as the FIS World Championships in Olympic years. Toni Sailer (1956) and Jean-Claude Killy (1968) each swept all three men's events; more recently Mikaela Shiffrin and Marcel Hirscher have been dominant figures, while Kjetil André Aamodt and Janica Kostelić lead the all-time medal tables.

Events and format

Downhill and super-G are single-run speed events; slalom and giant slalom are decided over two runs; the combined pairs a downhill with a slalom run. Courses are set on the host mountain's steepest serviced pistes, and races are frequently affected by weather delays.
