Stephen William Hawking (1942–2018) was an English theoretical physicist, cosmologist and author. Born on the anniversary of Galileo's death, he developed landmark theories of black holes while living for more than five decades with motor neurone disease (ALS), diagnosed when he was 21.

Scientific work

With Roger Penrose, Hawking proved singularity theorems showing that general relativity predicts the universe began in a singularity. In 1974 he proposed that black holes emit thermal radiation — Hawking radiation — implying they can evaporate, a result that linked gravity, quantum mechanics and thermodynamics and raised the black hole information paradox. He held the Lucasian Professorship at Cambridge from 1979 to 2009.

Public life

His 1988 book A Brief History of Time sold millions of copies and made him a global cultural figure. He communicated through a cheek-operated speech synthesizer, travelled in zero gravity in 2007, and advocateded for disability awareness and caution about artificial intelligence. He died on Pi Day 2018; his ashes were interred in Westminster Abbey near Newton and Darwin.
