Wilhelm II

Wilhelm II (Friedrich Wilhelm Viktor Albert; 27 January 1859 – 4 June 1941) was the last German Emperor (Kaiser) and King of Prussia, reigning from 15 June 1888 until his abdication on 9 November 1918 during the First World War.

Early life

The eldest grandson of Queen Victoria, Wilhelm was born with a withered left arm, the result of a traumatic breech birth. He became emperor in the "Year of the Three Emperors" (1888) after the deaths of his grandfather Wilhelm I and his father Frederick III, who reigned only 99 days.

Reign

Wilhelm dismissed Chancellor Otto von Bismarck in 1890 and pursued a more personal, erratic rule, combining support for rapid industrial and naval expansion — the Tirpitz naval laws that launched an arms race with Britain — with bellicose rhetoric and a foreign policy ("Weltpolitik") that alarmed Germany's neighbours. His interventions contributed to crises over Morocco and to the diplomatic isolation exposed in the July Crisis of 1914, though historians debate the extent of his personal responsibility for the outbreak of the First World War.

War, abdication and exile

During the war Wilhelm became a largely ceremonial figurehead as real power passed to Generals Hindenburg and Ludendorff. In November 1918, amid revolution and military collapse, he abdicated and fled to the Netherlands, where he lived at Doorn until his death in 1941. The Treaty of Versailles named him for trial as a war criminal, but the Dutch refused extradition.
