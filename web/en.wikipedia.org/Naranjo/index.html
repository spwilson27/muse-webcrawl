Naranjo is a Pre-Columbian Maya archaeological site in the Petén Basin region of Guatemala. It was occupied from about the middle Preclassic period and flourished in the Late Classic as a major political centre, at times rivalrous with — and at times subordinate to — the great powers Tikal and Calakmul.

History

The city's ancient name appears to have been Wak Kab'nal. Naranjo's monuments record a long dynastic history; its fortunes turned with the arrival in 682 CE of Lady Six Sky (Wak Chanil Ajaw), a princess from Dos Pilas who refounded the dynasty and commissioned numerous stelae — an unusually prominent role for a woman in Maya inscriptions. The city was abandoned during the Terminal Classic collapse in the 9th century.

Rediscovery

Reported to the outside world in the early 20th century, the site suffered heavy looting of its carved stelae in the 1960s–70s. Conservation and mapping by Guatemalan and international teams since the 2000s have stabilised the ruins, which lie within the Yaxhá-Nakum-Naranjo National Park.
