Corporation Act 1661

The Corporation Act 1661 was an Act of the Parliament of England (13 Cha. II. St. 2 c. 1) with the long title "An Act for the well Governing and Regulating of Corporations". It required all municipal officials in England and Wales to take the oaths of allegiance and supremacy, to declare against the Solemn League and Covenant, and to have received the sacrament of the Lord's Supper according to the rites of the Church of England within the preceding year.

Purpose and effect

Passed in the aftermath of the Restoration of Charles II, the Act was aimed at excluding Presbyterians and other religious dissenters from power in the borough corporations, which returned many members of Parliament. Commissioners were empowered to remove officeholders who refused the required declarations. Together with the Act of Uniformity 1662, the Test Acts, and other measures, it formed part of the body of legislation often called the Clarendon Code, which entrenched Anglican dominance of public life.

Repeal

The sacramental test imposed by the Act was removed by the Sacramental Test Act 1828, which repealed the Corporation and Test Acts' religious requirements for office. The remaining provisions were repealed in the nineteenth century.
