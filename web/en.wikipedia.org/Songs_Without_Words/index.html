Songs Without Words


Felix Mendelssohn

The Songs Without Words (Lieder ohne Worte) are eight books of short lyric piano pieces by Felix Mendelssohn, written between 1829 and 1845. Each book contains six pieces, for a total of forty-eight. The pieces are within the grasp of pianists of moderate ability, which contributed to their popularity as domestic music in the nineteenth century and to Mendelssohn's reputation in Victorian England.

History

The first book, Op. 19, was published in 1832 in London as Melodies for the Piano; the evocative English title "Songs Without Words" proved so successful that it was retained. Mendelssohn's sister Fanny Hensel also composed similar piano songs, and the genre influenced many later composers.

Titles

Some pieces bear descriptive titles — many supplied by publishers or by Mendelssohn himself — such as "Spinning Song" (Op. 67 No. 4, also called "The Bee's Wedding"), "Venetian Gondola Song", "Spring Song" (Op. 62 No. 6), "The Hunt", and "On Wings of Song"-like lyricism throughout. Mendelssohn resisted overly literal interpretation, arguing that music expresses thoughts "not too indefinite to be put into words, but rather too definite".

Musical character

The pieces are typically in a song-like ternary form, with a singing melody supported by an arpeggiated or chordal accompaniment. They range from the introspective to the brilliant perpetuum mobile style of the "Spinning Song".
