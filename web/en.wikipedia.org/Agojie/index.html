Agojie

The Agojie, also called the Mino or Minon (meaning "our mothers" in Fon), were an all-female military regiment of the Kingdom of Dahomey, in present-day Benin, which existed from the 17th century until the late 19th century. European visitors dubbed them the "Dahomey Amazons".

History

The corps is traditionally traced to Queen Hangbè in the early 18th century, and was greatly expanded under King Ghezo, who reigned from 1818 to 1858. Recruited from palace women, volunteers, and captives, the Agojie were trained to strict discipline, swore celibacy while in service, and became the king's most loyal troops and an electoral force within the palace.

Warfare

Numbering several thousand at their peak, Agojie units fought in Dahomey's wars of expansion, including campaigns against the Yoruba states and the kingdom's conflicts with France in the Franco-Dahomean Wars of 1890 and 1892–94. Renowned for ferocity, they were armed with muskets, machetes, and clubs. The regiment was disbanded after the French conquest of Dahomey.

In popular culture

The Agojie inspired the Dora Milaje of Marvel's Black Panther stories and were depicted in the 2022 film The Woman King, starring Viola Davis.
