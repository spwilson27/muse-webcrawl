Conflict of interest

A conflict of interest (COI) is a situation in which a person or organisation is involved in multiple interests, financial or otherwise, and serving one interest could involve working against another. It arises when an individual's personal interests — family, friendships, financial factors — could compromise their judgement, decisions or actions in their professional role.

Types and examples

Conflicts may be financial, such as a researcher funded by a company whose product they are evaluating, or non-financial, such as an official deciding on a contract involving a relative's firm. In law, a lawyer cannot act for both sides in a dispute. In medicine and academia, disclosure of industry funding is routinely required by journals and institutions.

Management

The existence of a conflict of interest is not in itself wrongdoing; what matters is how it is handled. Standard remedies include disclosure to affected parties, recusal from decisions, divestment of the conflicting interest, and oversight by independent bodies. Undisclosed conflicts, by contrast, can amount to corruption or research misconduct.
