The Dinner Party

The Dinner Party is an installation artwork by feminist artist Judy Chicago. Widely regarded as the first epic feminist artwork, it functions as a symbolic history of women in Western civilization. Created between 1974 and 1979 with the help of hundreds of volunteers, it consists of a ceremonial banquet arranged on a triangular table with a total of 39 place settings, each commemorating an important woman from history or mythology.

Description

Each side of the triangular table hosts 13 settings — for Primordial goddesses through prehistory and the classical world; for women of the Roman era to the Reformation; and for women from the American Revolution to the twentieth century, ending with Georgia O'Keeffe. Each setting has a hand-painted china plate, often with a raised butterfly or vulvar form, resting on an embroidered runner worked with the techniques of the woman's era. Beneath the table, the white porcelain Heritage Floor bears the names of 999 additional women inscribed in gold. Honorees range from the goddess Ishtar and the pharaoh Hatshepsut to Sappho, Eleanor of Aquitaine, Sojourner Truth, Susan B. Anthony and Virginia Woolf.

History and reception

The work premiered at the San Francisco Museum of Modern Art in 1979 and toured internationally, drawing large crowds and heated debate about craft, representation and taste. After years of controversy over a permanent home — including a failed plan to donate it to the University of the District of Columbia — it found one in 2002 at the Brooklyn Museum, where it anchors the Elizabeth A. Sackler Center for Feminist Art, opened in 2007. It remains a landmark of feminist art and of collaborative, craft-based practice.
