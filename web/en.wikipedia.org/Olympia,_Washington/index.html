Olympia is the capital of the U.S. state of Washington and the county seat of Thurston County, located at the southern end of Puget Sound on the Deschutes River estuary. It was incorporated in 1859 and became the territorial and then state capital.

Overview

The city's most prominent landmark is the Washington State Capitol campus, whose Legislative Building (completed 1928) has one of the tallest masonry domes in the world. Olympia is home to The Evergreen State College, known for its experimental, narrative-evaluation curriculum, and Saint Martin's University in neighbouring Lacey.

Culture

Olympia had a notable role in American independent music: it was a centre of the riot grrrl movement and home to the influential independent label K Records and bands associated with the Pacific Northwest indie scene. The city is also known for its artesian water, historically used by the Olympia Brewing Company in nearby Tumwater ("It's the Water").

Geography

Olympia sits at the southern tip of Budd Inlet, with views of the Olympic Mountains to the northwest — the source of its name. The region experienced significant damage in the 2001 Nisqually earthquake.
