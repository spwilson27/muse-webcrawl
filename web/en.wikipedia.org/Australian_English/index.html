Australian English

Australian English (AusE, AusEng, AuE, AuEng, en-AU) is the set of varieties of the English language native to Australia. It began to diverge from British English shortly after the founding of the colony of New South Wales in 1788, absorbing words from Indigenous Australian languages and the dialects of settlers from across the British Isles, and forms part of a dialect continuum with New Zealand English.

Phonology and varieties

Phoneticians describe a social continuum of Australian accents — Broad, General and Cultivated — differing mainly in vowel quality; the General accent is the most common. Australian English is non-rhotic, shares vowel shifts with other Southern Hemisphere varieties, and is known for features such as the high rising terminal ("uptalk"). Unlike American English, regional variation within Australia is comparatively modest, though some lexical differences exist between states.

Spelling and vocabulary

Australian spelling mostly follows British conventions (colour, organise is also common alongside organize in some style guides) codified by the Macquarie Dictionary, the standard reference since 1981. The vocabulary includes borrowings from Aboriginal languages (kangaroo, boomerang, billabong), convict-era slang, diminutives formed with -ie and -o suffixes (barbie, arvo), and terms from bush and sporting culture.
