Winter Line

The Winter Line was a series of German and Italian defence lines in Italy during the Italian Campaign of the Second World War. The main line, the Gustav Line, ran across Italy from the mouth of the Garigliano River on the Tyrrhenian Sea to the mouth of the Sangro River on the Adriatic coast.

Defences

Centred on Monte Cassino, the Gustav Line made use of the steep slopes of the Apennine Mountains and included fortified positions with concrete bunkers, gun turrets, barbed wire, minefields, and anti-tank ditches. Two auxiliary lines, the Bernhardt Line in the west and the Hitler Line (later renamed the Senger Line) behind the Gustav Line, formed part of the system.

Battle

The Allied Fifth and Eighth Armies attacked the Winter Line from late 1943. The Battle of Monte Cassino (January–May 1944) involved four major assaults before Polish forces finally captured the abbey ruins on 18 May 1944, opening the road to Rome.
