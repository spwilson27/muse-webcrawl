Lassie Come Home

Lassie Come Home is a 1943 American Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer Technicolor film starring Roddy McDowall and canine actor Pal, in a story about the profound bond between Yorkshire boy Joe Carraclough and his rough collie, Lassie. The film was directed by Fred M. Wilcox and adapted from Eric Knight's 1940 novel Lassie Come-Home. A young Elizabeth Taylor appears in a supporting role.

Plot

In Depression-era Yorkshire, the Carraclough family, forced by unemployment, sells their beloved collie Lassie to the wealthy Duke of Rudling. Lassie escapes repeatedly but is taken to the Duke's estate in the far north of Scotland. Determined to return to Joe, she undertakes a thousand-mile journey south—enduring storms, dog catchers, and hardship—finally collapsing at the schoolyard at the exact hour Joe leaves school, restoring the family.

Legacy

The film was a major commercial and critical success and launched one of the longest-running franchises in entertainment: six MGM sequels followed, then the long-running CBS television series Lassie (1954–1973). Pal, a male rough collie, became the definitive Lassie, and the film was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry in 1993 as being "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant".
