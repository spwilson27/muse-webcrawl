Minute and second of arc

A minute of arc, arcminute (arcmin), arc minute, or minute arc, denoted by the symbol ′, is a unit of angular measurement equal to 1/60 of one degree. Since one degree is 1/360 of a turn (complete rotation), one arcminute is 1/21,600 of a turn. A second of arc, arcsecond (arcsec), or arc second, denoted ″, is 1/60 of an arcminute, 1/3,600 of a degree, and 1/1,296,000 of a turn.

The nautical mile was originally defined as the length of one arcminute of latitude on Earth's surface, and remains close to that value (1,852 metres exactly by modern definition).

Uses

Arcminutes and arcseconds are standard in astronomy, where they measure the apparent sizes and separations of celestial objects, stellar parallax, and the resolution of telescopes. The unaided human eye can resolve about one arcminute. The full Moon spans about 31 arcminutes. In cartography and navigation, positions are often given in degrees, minutes, and seconds (DMS). In firearms and optics, the "minute of angle" (MOA) is used to describe accuracy and scope adjustments; at 100 yards one MOA subtends approximately 1.047 inches.

For very small angles, the milliarcsecond (mas) and microarcsecond (μas) are used, notably in astrometry and very-long-baseline interferometry: the Gaia space mission measures stellar positions to tens of microarcseconds.

History

The sexagesimal subdivision of the degree descends from Babylonian astronomy and was systematized by Greek astronomers such as Ptolemy. The names "minute" and "second" come from the Latin partes minutae primae ("first small parts") and partes minutae secundae ("second small parts")—the same origin as the minute and second of time.
