Etymology

Etymology is the study of the origin and evolution of words — including their constituent units of sound and meaning — across time. The word itself derives from the Ancient Greek etymología, from étymon, "true sense or sense of a truth," and -logía, "study of."

Methods

Etymologists apply the comparative method, reconstructing ancestral forms by comparing cognate words in related languages, and use philological study of texts to trace changes in form and meaning. Sound change follows regular patterns, such as Grimm's law in the Germanic languages, which allows systematic reconstruction of proto-languages such as Proto-Indo-European.

Types of word formation

Words enter languages through borrowing, derivation, compounding, blending, clipping, and coinage. Over time, meanings may broaden, narrow, or shift entirely, and folk etymology can reshape a word's form based on popular misunderstanding, as when Old French crevice became English "crayfish."

History

Ancient Indian, Greek, and Roman scholars all wrote on word origins, though pre-modern etymology lacked the rigorous comparative method developed in the nineteenth century by scholars such as Franz Bopp and Jacob Grimm. Major English etymological resources include the Oxford English Dictionary.
