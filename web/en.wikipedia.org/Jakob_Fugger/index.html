Jakob Fugger of the Lily (German: Jakob Fugger von der Lilie; 6 March 1459 – 30 December 1525), also known as Jakob Fugger the Rich or sometimes Jakob II, was a major German merchant, mining entrepreneur and banker. He was a descendant of the Fugger merchant family of Augsburg and among the richest individuals of his time, with a fortune estimated at around 2 percent of Europe's GDP at his death.

Rise of the Fugger empire

The youngest son of Jakob Fugger the Elder, Jakob was sent to Venice as a young man to learn Italian accounting and banking methods. Under his leadership, the Fugger firm expanded from cloth trading into mining — especially silver, copper and lead in Tyrol, Hungary and Silesia — and into banking for the Habsburg emperors and the papacy. Fugger loans financed the imperial election of Charles V in 1519; in a famous letter Fugger later reminded the emperor that "it is well known that Your Imperial Majesty could not have gained the Roman Crown save with my help."

Banking and the Church

Fugger handled the transfer of indulgence revenues from Germany to Rome, a connection that made him a target of criticism during the early Reformation; Martin Luther attacked the Fugger-financed sale of indulgences associated with Archbishop Albert of Mainz, whose acquisition of the Mainz see Fugger had financed. Fugger also pioneered early forms of business communication, including a network of couriers that produced the Fuggerzeitungen, handwritten newsletters regarded as forerunners of newspapers.

Philanthropy

In Augsburg, Fugger founded the Fuggerei in 1516–1523, the world's oldest social housing complex still in use, where Catholic residents of modest means live for a nominal annual rent of one Rhenish guilder (about €0.88) plus daily prayers for the Fugger family. He also endowed the Fugger Chapel in St. Anna's Church, considered the first Renaissance building in Germany.

Death and legacy

Fugger died childless in 1525 and was succeeded by his nephew Anton Fugger. His wealth at death was estimated at over 2 million guilders. Modern estimates of his fortune, adjusted for inflation, frequently rank him among the wealthiest people in history.
