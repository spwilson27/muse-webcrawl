Zacatecas

Zacatecas (Spanish pronunciation: [sakaˈtekas]) is one of the 31 states which, along with Mexico City, comprise the Federal Entities of Mexico. It is divided into 58 municipalities and its capital city is Zacatecas. It is located in north-central Mexico, bordered by the states of Durango, Coahuila, Nuevo León, San Luis Potosí, Guanajuato, Jalisco, Aguascalientes, and Nayarit.

The state is best known for its rich deposits of silver and other minerals, its colonial architecture, and its importance during the Mexican Revolution. Its capital is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The economy is based mainly on mining, agriculture, and tourism.

History

The capital city was founded in 1546 after the discovery of silver in the region, and it became one of the most important mining centres of New Spain. During the Mexican War of Independence and later the Revolution, Zacatecas was strategically significant; the Battle of Zacatecas in June 1914, in which Pancho Villa's División del Norte captured the city, was one of the bloodiest and most decisive battles of the Revolution.

Geography

Zacatecas lies mostly on the Mexican Plateau, with rugged mountains in the west in the Sierra Madre Occidental. Its terrain ranges from high desert plains to pine-forested ridges. The state has a semi-arid climate with warm summers and cool winters, particularly at its high elevations; the capital sits at roughly 2,400 metres above sea level.
