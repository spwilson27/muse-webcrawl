Guitar tuning

Standard tuning assigns E–A–D–G–B–E from the lowest string to the highest.

Guitar tunings are the assignment of pitches to the open strings of guitars. The standard tuning for a six-string guitar is E2–A2–D3–G3–B3–E4: fourths between successive strings except for the major third between the G and B strings, a compromise that balances chord fingering and scale patterns.

Alternative tunings

Players use many alternatives. Drop D lowers the sixth string to D, facilitating power chords. Open tunings (open G, open D) tune the strings to a chord, favouring slide and fingerstyle playing. DADGAD is common in Celtic music. Regular tunings such as major-thirds and all-fourths tuning simplify transposition across the fretboard but make conventional chord shapes harder. Seven-string guitars and baritone guitars extend the range downward, and metal genres popularised progressively lower "drop" tunings.

Methods

Guitars are tuned by ear against a reference pitch, by harmonics, or with electronic tuners and clip-on devices that sense vibration. Intonation — the adjustment of string length at the bridge — ensures the instrument plays in tune along the whole neck.
