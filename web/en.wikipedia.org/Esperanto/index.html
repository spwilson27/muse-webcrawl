Esperanto is the world's most widely spoken constructed international auxiliary language. Created by the Polish-Jewish ophthalmologist L. L. Zamenhof in 1887, it was intended as a universal second language that would be politically neutral and easy to learn, fostering peace and international understanding. Zamenhof published the first book detailing Esperanto, Unua Libro, under the pseudonym "Doktoro Esperanto" — "the one who hopes" — from which the language took its name.

History

Zamenhof grew up in Białystok, then in the Russian Empire, a town divided among Polish, Russian, German, and Yiddish-speaking communities, and conceived of a neutral language as a bridge between them. The first World Esperanto Congress was held in Boulogne-sur-Mer, France, in 1905, which adopted the Declaration of Boulogne and fixed the Fundamento de Esperanto as the language's unchangeable core. The Universal Esperanto Association (UEA) was founded in 1908. Esperanto was proposed to the League of Nations in the 1920s and has been periodically recommended by UNESCO. The movement survived persecution under both Hitler and Stalin, who viewed its internationalism with suspicion.

Language structure

Esperanto grammar is highly regular and agglutinative, based on 16 fundamental rules. Its vocabulary derives mainly from Romance and Germanic roots. The alphabet has 28 letters and is fully phonemic — one sound per letter. Word order is relatively free thanks to the accusative case ending -n. Affixes allow a small stock of roots to generate a large vocabulary: for example, mal- reverses meaning (bona, good → malbona, bad).

Community and culture

Esperanto has an original literature, translations of world classics, music, and annual world congresses. Estimates of speakers vary widely; a small number of native speakers (denaskuloj) exist, raised in Esperanto-speaking families. The Pasporta Servo hospitality network allows speakers to stay with fellow speakers worldwide. Since the internet era, courses on platforms such as Duolingo and lernu! have brought new learners to the language.
