EU Battlegroup

EU Battlegroup
Type: Rapid reaction force
Size: ~1,500 personnel per battlegroup
Operational since: 2007
Framework: Common Security and Defence Policy (CSDP)

An EU Battlegroup is a military unit adhering to the European Union's Common Security and Defence Policy (CSDP), designed as a rapid reaction force for crisis management. Each battlegroup consists of about 1,500 troops, reinforced with support elements, drawn from one or more member states, and is able to begin deploying within days of a Council decision.

EU Battlegroups are multinational rapid-reaction units of about 1,500 soldiers.

Structure and rotation

Two battlegroups are on standby at any time, serving six-month rotations. They are provided by individual member states (framework nations) or by multinational formations such as the Nordic Battlegroup, the Balkan Battlegroup and the Visegrád Battlegroup. The concept was first proposed in 1999 and reached full operational capability in 2007.

Deployment history

Despite being available since 2007, no EU Battlegroup has ever been deployed on an operation, largely because deployment requires unanimity among member states and costs fall mainly on the contributing nations ("costs lie where they fall"). Proposals periodically surface to use them — for example during crises in Libya, Mali and the Central African Republic — but ad hoc forces have been preferred instead. The concept is being succeeded by a larger EU Rapid Deployment Capacity under the 2022 Strategic Compass.
