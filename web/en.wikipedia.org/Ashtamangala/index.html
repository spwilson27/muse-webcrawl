Ashtamangala

The Ashtamangala (Sanskrit: अष्टमङ्गल, "eight auspicious [signs]") is a sacred suite of eight auspicious symbols found in Indian religions including Hinduism, Buddhism and Jainism. The symbols, in varying forms and orders, adorn religious art, ritual objects, textiles and architecture across South and East Asia.

The eight symbols

In Tibetan Buddhism the eight symbols are: the conch shell (right-turning white conch), the endless knot, a pair of golden fish, the lotus flower, the parasol, the treasure vase, the wheel of dharma, and the victory banner. Each carries layered meanings: the conch represents the far-reaching sound of the dharma; the endless knot, interdependence and the union of wisdom and method; the golden fish, freedom from suffering and abundance; and the wheel, the Buddha's teaching itself.

Variations

Hindu lists include items such as the fly-whisk, mirror, elephant goad and lamp, while Jain lists differ between the Digambara and Shvetambara traditions, featuring symbols such as the srivatsa and the nandyavarta. The sets also appear in Chinese Buddhism, where the symbols were associated with the internal organs and later with the Eight Immortals in folk tradition.
