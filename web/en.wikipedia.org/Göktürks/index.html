Göktürks

The Göktürks (Old Turkic: Kök Türük, "Celestial Turks") were a nomadic confederation of Turkic peoples in medieval Central Asia who founded the Turkic Khaganate in 552 under Bumin Qaghan of the Ashina clan. It was the first empire to call itself "Turk", and at its height it stretched from Manchuria to the Black Sea, controlling much of the Silk Road. The Göktürks left the earliest substantial texts in a Turkic language, the eighth-century Orkhon inscriptions.

Origins and expansion

The Ashina, iron-working vassals of the Rouran, overthrew their masters in 552. Bumin's brother Istämi expanded westward, allying with Sasanian Persia against the Hephthalites and opening direct trade and diplomatic contact with Byzantium.

Division and second khaganate

Civil wars split the empire into eastern and western khaganates around 603; both eventually fell to Tang China (630 and 659). In 682 Ilterish Qaghan restored the Eastern khaganate, whose monuments — the inscriptions of Bilge Qaghan, Kül Tigin and the minister Tonyukuk — narrate Turkic history in the runiform Old Turkic script. The khaganate fell to the Uyghurs in 744.

Legacy

The Göktürks spread the name "Turk" across Central Asia; their state is celebrated in modern Turkey and the Turkic republics as the first Turkic empire, and "Göktürk" names everything from satellites to military units.
