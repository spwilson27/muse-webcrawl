Fischer Black

Fischer Sheffey Black
Born: 11 January 1938, Georgetown, Washington, D.C., U.S.
Died: 30 August 1995 (aged 57), New York City, U.S.
Alma mater: Harvard University (PhD, 1964)
Known for: Black–Scholes model, Black–Derman–Toy model

Fischer Sheffey Black (January 11, 1938 – August 30, 1995) was an American economist, best known as one of the authors of the famous Black–Scholes model of option pricing. His work, largely done outside conventional academic finance departments, helped launch the modern quantitative finance industry.

Education and career

Black graduated from Harvard College in 1959 with a degree in physics and received his PhD in applied mathematics from Harvard in 1964, with work related to artificial intelligence under Marvin Minsky. After positions at Bolt Beranek and Newman and Arthur D. Little, he joined the consulting firm of Jack Treynor, where his interest in finance began.

He later taught at the University of Chicago Booth School of Business (1971–1975) and the MIT Sloan School of Management (1975–1984), before joining Goldman Sachs in 1984, where he became a partner and co-headed the Quantitative Strategies Group.

Contributions

With Myron Scholes, Black published "The Pricing of Options and Corporate Liabilities" (1973), deriving the Black–Scholes equation for valuing European options; Robert C. Merton published a parallel derivation the same year. Black also contributed to the capital asset pricing model, the Black–Litterman model of portfolio allocation, the Black–Derman–Toy interest-rate model, and the theory of business cycles in "Exploring General Equilibrium" (1995). His 1986 presidential address to the American Finance Association, "Noise", examined the role of uninformed trading in markets.

Legacy

Black died of throat cancer in 1995. Because the Nobel Prize is not awarded posthumously, he did not share the 1997 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economics awarded to Scholes and Merton, though the committee acknowledged his contribution. The Fischer Black Prize, awarded by the American Finance Association to a leading finance scholar under 40, is named in his honour.
