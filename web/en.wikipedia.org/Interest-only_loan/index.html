Interest-only loan

An interest-only loan is a loan in which the borrower pays only the interest on the principal balance for a set term, with the principal repaid at the end of the term or refinanced. Such loans were widely used in mortgage markets in the United States, the United Kingdom, Ireland and Australia.

Mechanics

During the interest-only period, monthly payments are lower than for an amortising loan because no capital is repaid. At the end of the term the full principal falls due, so borrowers typically rely on selling the property, repaying from savings vehicles such as endowment policies, or refinancing.

Risks and regulation

Regulators tightened rules on interest-only mortgages after the 2008 financial crisis, requiring lenders to check that borrowers have a credible repayment strategy. Without one, borrowers risk negative equity or default when the principal becomes payable.
