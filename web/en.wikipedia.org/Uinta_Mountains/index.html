Uinta Mountains

The Uinta Mountains are a mountain range in northeastern Utah and, for a short distance, northwestern Colorado, in the United States. They are unusual among North American ranges in running east–west rather than north–south, extending roughly 160 km (100 miles) from the Wasatch Range toward the Green River.

The range contains Kings Peak, at 13,528 feet (4,123 m) the highest point in Utah, and more than a dozen peaks above 13,000 feet, the highest collection of alpine summits in the contiguous United States west of the Rockies. Hundreds of glacial lakes dot the high country, and the mountains give rise to major rivers including the Bear, Weber, Provo, and Green.

The core of the range consists of Neoproterozoic metasedimentary rocks of the Uinta Mountain Group, uplifted during the Laramide orogeny. Much of the range is protected in the Ashley and Wasatch-Cache National Forests and in the High Uintas Wilderness, designated in 1984. The Mirror Lake Highway (Utah State Route 150) crosses the western Uintas seasonally.
