Curve

In mathematics, a curve (also called a curved line in older texts) is an object similar to a line, but that does not have to be straight. Intuitively, a curve may be thought of as the trace left by a moving point. This is the definition that appeared more than 2,000 years ago in Euclid's Elements: "The curved line is the first species of quantity, which has only one dimension, namely length, without any width nor depth."

Definitions

In modern mathematics a curve is generally defined as the image of an interval of real numbers under a continuous map into a topological space. In differential geometry, a plane curve is often given parametrically by two functions x(t) and y(t). A curve is called simple if it does not intersect itself, and closed if its endpoints coincide.

Types of curves

Curves studied in geometry include the conic sections (circles, ellipses, parabolas, and hyperbolas), algebraic curves defined by polynomial equations, and transcendental curves such as the sine curve and the catenary. Space curves lie in three-dimensional space; the helix is a familiar example. Fractal curves such as the Koch snowflake have non-integer dimension and infinite length within a finite area.

Properties

Key properties of a smooth curve include its arc length, curvature, and torsion. The arc length is computed by integrating the speed of the parametrisation. Curvature measures how sharply a curve bends at a point, while torsion measures how it twists out of its osculating plane.
