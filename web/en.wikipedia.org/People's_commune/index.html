People's commune

The people's commune (Chinese: 人民公社) was the highest of three administrative levels in rural areas of the People's Republic of China during the period 1958–1983, when they were replaced by townships. Communes, the largest collective units, were divided in turn into production brigades and production teams. They combined economic production with local government, education, and welfare functions.

History

Communes were established during the Great Leap Forward (1958–1962), when some 750,000 agricultural cooperatives were merged into about 24,000 communes, often encompassing tens of thousands of people. Private plots were abolished, communal dining halls were set up, and labour was organized on collective lines. The system contributed to the disruption of agriculture during the Great Chinese Famine.

Dissolution

After 1978, under Deng Xiaoping's reforms, the household responsibility system restored family farming, and the communes were formally dissolved in the early 1980s.
