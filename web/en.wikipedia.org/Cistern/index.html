Cistern

A cistern (from Latin cisterna, from cista, "box") is a waterproof receptacle for holding liquids, usually water. Cisterns are often built underground to store rainwater or water delivered by aqueducts for household and agricultural use, and are distinguished from wells by their waterproof linings and by being filled from above rather than from groundwater.

History

Sealed cisterns are known from Neolithic villages of the Levant, and rock-cut and plastered cisterns were essential to settlement in the arid Near East. In the Roman world, great vaulted cisterns stored the water of aqueducts; the Basilica Cistern in Istanbul, built under Justinian I in the 6th century, is a vast underground reservoir supported on 336 marble columns. Cisterns remain common in dry regions, on islands, and in off-grid dwellings, fed by roof catchment. In modern plumbing the term is also applied to the small tank that stores water for flushing a toilet.
