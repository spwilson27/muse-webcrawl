Harborplace

LocationInner Harbor, Baltimore, Maryland, United States
OpenedJuly 2, 1980
DeveloperThe Rouse Company
ArchitectBenjamin Thompson & Associates
FormatTwo festival-marketplace pavilions (Light Street and Pratt Street)

Harborplace is a shopping and dining complex on the Inner Harbor in downtown Baltimore, Maryland. It opened on July 2, 1980, as the centrepiece of the celebrated redevelopment of Baltimore's waterfront and is one of the best-known examples of the "festival marketplace" retail format pioneered by developer James W. Rouse and his Rouse Company.

History

Harborplace consists of two glass-and-steel pavilions — the Light Street Pavilion and the Pratt Street Pavilion — filled with shops, food stalls, restaurants and pushcart vendors. Designed by Benjamin Thompson & Associates, it was modelled on Rouse's Faneuil Hall Marketplace in Boston and South Street Seaport in New York. Its opening was the capstone of mayor William Donald Schaefer's Inner Harbor renewal, which also produced the National Aquarium, the Maryland Science Center and the USS Constellation attraction, and it transformed Baltimore's image nationally and attracted millions of visitors annually.

Decline and redevelopment

From the 2010s the pavilions lost tenants and foot traffic, entered receivership in 2019 after their owner defaulted, and were sold in 2022 to Baltimore-based MCB Real Estate. MCB proposed demolishing the pavilions and building a mixed-use project with taller buildings, public space and housing — a controversial plan that, because of the site's protected park status, required a city charter amendment approved by voters in 2024.
