Flow (psychology)

In positive psychology, a flow state, also known colloquially as being in the zone, is the mental state in which a person performing an activity is fully immersed in a feeling of energised focus, full involvement and enjoyment in the process of the activity. The concept was developed by the Hungarian-American psychologist Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi, whose research from the 1970s onward examined optimal experience among artists, athletes, chess players and workers.

Characteristics

Csikszentmihalyi identified several components of flow: intense concentration on the present moment; a merging of action and awareness; loss of reflective self-consciousness; a sense of control; clear goals and immediate feedback; and a distorted sense of time, which seems to pass quickly or unnoticed. The activity becomes intrinsically rewarding — "autotelic" — so that the end goal becomes an excuse for the process itself.

Conditions

Flow is most likely when perceived challenges and perceived skills are both high and roughly in balance. If the challenge far exceeds skill the result is anxiety; if skill far exceeds the challenge, boredom. Csikszentmihalyi's research used the Experience Sampling Method, paging participants at random intervals to record their state, and found flow commonly reported at work as well as in leisure, sport, music and games. Video game designers deliberately engineer flow by dynamically adjusting difficulty to the player's ability.

Applications and criticism

Flow has been applied in education, workplace design, sports coaching, music pedagogy and therapy. Neuroscientific work has explored correlates such as transient hypofrontality — reduced activity in parts of the prefrontal cortex — though findings are not conclusive. Critics note that flow measures rely heavily on self-report and that the state is partly defined by the very features used to detect it.
