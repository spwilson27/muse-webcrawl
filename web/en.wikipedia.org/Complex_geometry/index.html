Complex geometry

In mathematics, complex geometry is the study of geometric structures and constructions arising from the complex numbers. It concerns complex manifolds, complex algebraic varieties, and holomorphic functions and maps between them, drawing on methods from differential geometry, algebraic geometry, and several complex variables.

Basic concepts

A complex manifold is a space locally modelled on complex Euclidean space ℂⁿ, with transition maps required to be holomorphic. Every complex manifold of complex dimension n is a real smooth manifold of dimension 2n with an integrable almost complex structure. Compact complex manifolds that admit a suitable metric include Kähler manifolds, whose geometry links complex, Riemannian, and symplectic structures; examples include complex projective space and complex tori.

Topics

Major themes include the classification of complex surfaces (the Enriques–Kodaira classification), Hodge theory and the Hodge decomposition of cohomology, sheaf cohomology and vanishing theorems such as the Kodaira vanishing theorem, deformation theory of complex structures (Kodaira–Spencer theory), and the study of special metrics, notably Calabi–Yau manifolds with Ricci-flat Kähler metrics guaranteed by Yau's proof of the Calabi conjecture. Complex geometry has deep connections with string theory, where Calabi–Yau manifolds serve as compactification spaces, and with mirror symmetry.
