The Virginia House of Delegates is one of the two houses of the Virginia General Assembly, the legislature of the Commonwealth of Virginia; the other is the Senate of Virginia. The House is composed of 100 members ("delegates") elected for two-year terms from single-member districts, with elections held in odd-numbered years. It is presided over by the Speaker of the House.

History

The House traces its origins to the House of Burgesses, established at Jamestown in 1619 — the first elected legislative assembly in the Americas. After independence, the 1776 Virginia Constitution created the House of Delegates as the lower house of the new General Assembly. Delegates meet in the Virginia State Capitol in Richmond, designed by Thomas Jefferson.

Composition and powers

Members must be at least 21 years old and residents of their districts. All revenue bills must originate in the House of Delegates under the state constitution. The House shares legislative power with the Senate and the Governor, and can impeach state officials, with trials held in the Senate. Notable former members include Patrick Henry, Thomas Jefferson, James Madison and James Monroe.
