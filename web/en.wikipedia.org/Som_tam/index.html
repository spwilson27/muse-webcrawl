Som tam


Som tam Thai with peanuts and dried shrimp

Som tam (Thai: ส้มตำ), also known as green papaya salad, is a spicy salad made from shredded unripe papaya. It is of Lao origin but is also widely eaten throughout Thailand, especially in the northeastern Isan region, and in neighbouring countries. The dish combines the four main tastes of Thai cuisine: sour lime, hot chilli, salty fish sauce, and sweetness from palm sugar.

Preparation

The ingredients are mixed and pounded in a mortar and pestle (krok). Green papaya is shredded into thin strips, then pounded together with garlic, chillies, long beans, cherry tomatoes, peanuts, dried shrimp, lime juice, fish sauce, and palm sugar. It is commonly served with sticky rice and grilled chicken (kai yang).

Variations

Many variations exist. Som tam Thai includes peanuts and dried shrimp and is sweeter. Som tam pu includes pickled crab. Som tam pla ra, popular in Isan and Laos, uses fermented fish sauce (pla ra / padaek) and may omit peanuts, sometimes adding other ingredients such as snails or raw seafood. Other fruits or vegetables, such as green mango, cucumber, or carrots, may replace the papaya.

History

The dish derives from the Lao salad tam maak hoong. Papaya itself was introduced to Southeast Asia from the Americas via trade routes. Som tam was listed at number 46 on the World's 50 Most Delicious Foods list compiled by CNN Go in 2011.
