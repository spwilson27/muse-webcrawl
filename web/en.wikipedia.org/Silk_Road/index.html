Silk Road

The Silk Road was a network of Eurasian trade routes active from the 2nd century BCE until the mid-15th century, connecting East Asia with the Mediterranean. Spanning over 6,400 km, it carried silk, spices, glassware, paper, gunpowder, religions, technologies and diseases between China, Central Asia, Persia, Arabia and Europe.

History

Zhang Qian's missions under Han dynasty China (138 BCE) opened regular contact with Central Asia. Routes split around the Taklamakan Desert, passing through oasis cities such as Samarkand, Bukhara, Kashgar and Dunhuang, and through the Tian Shan passes. Trade flourished under the Tang dynasty and the Mongol Empire (Pax Mongolica). Maritime routes later competed, and Ottoman-era shifts contributed to its decline.

Legacy

The term "Seidenstraße" was coined by Ferdinand von Richthofen in 1877. UNESCO lists Silk Roads sites, and China's modern Belt and Road Initiative invokes its name.
