Tyndale Bible

Tyndale Bible
Translator: William Tyndale
New Testament: 1526
Language: Early Modern English

The Tyndale Bible is the English translation of the Bible begun by William Tyndale in the 1520s, the first English New Testament printed and the first to be translated directly from Hebrew and Greek texts rather than the Latin Vulgate.

Publication

Unable to get permission to translate in England, Tyndale worked on the Continent; his New Testament was printed at Worms in 1526 and smuggled into England, where copies were publicly burned. His Pentateuch followed in 1530, but Tyndale was executed for heresy near Brussels in 1536 before completing the Old Testament.

Influence

Tyndale's phrasing profoundly shaped all later English Bibles: a large share of the King James Version's New Testament is Tyndale's wording, and coinages attributed to him include "Passover", "scapegoat" and "atonement".
