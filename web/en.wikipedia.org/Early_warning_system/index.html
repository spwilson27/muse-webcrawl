Early warning system

An early warning system (EWS) is a system of hazard monitoring, forecasting, warning dissemination and response capabilities that enables individuals, communities and organizations threatened by a hazard to prepare and act in sufficient time to reduce the possibility of harm or loss. The concept applies to natural hazards such as floods, storms, tsunamis and earthquakes, and also to military, financial, epidemiological and industrial contexts.

Elements

The United Nations framework describes four interrelated elements: (1) risk knowledge — understanding the hazards and vulnerabilities of a community; (2) monitoring and warning services — technical systems that detect hazards and forecast their impact; (3) dissemination and communication — delivering clear, timely warnings to those at risk; and (4) response capability — plans, drills and resources that allow people to act on the warning.

Examples

Tsunami warning systems in the Pacific and Indian Oceans use networks of seismometers and deep-ocean buoys to alert coastal populations. Earthquake early warning systems, such as Japan's, exploit the speed difference between seismic waves to give seconds of notice. Weather services issue storm, flood and heat warnings, while famine early warning systems such as FEWS NET monitor food security in Africa. During the Cold War, military early warning referred to radar and satellite networks such as NORAD's, designed to detect incoming missile attacks.

Effectiveness

Effective warnings must be trusted, understandable and actionable; false alarms can erode credibility. Studies of cyclones in the Bay of Bengal credit improved warning and evacuation with dramatically reducing death tolls compared with storms in the 1970s. The UN's Early Warnings for All initiative aims to ensure every person on Earth is protected by an early warning system.
