Adipose tissue

Adipose tissue (also known as body fat, or simply fat) is a loose connective tissue composed mostly of adipocytes. It is also called fatty tissue. In addition to fat cells, adipose tissue contains the stromal vascular fraction of cells including preadipocytes, fibroblasts, vascular endothelial cells, and a variety of immune cells.

Types

White adipose tissue stores energy as triglycerides in a single large droplet in each adipocyte, provides insulation and cushioning, and is distributed subcutaneously and around the internal organs (visceral fat). Brown adipose tissue, rich in mitochondria expressing uncoupling protein 1 (UCP1), generates heat by non-shivering thermogenesis and is especially important in infants and hibernating mammals; intermediate "beige" adipocytes can arise within white depots.

Endocrine function

Adipose tissue is an active endocrine organ. It secretes hormones and signaling molecules called adipokines, including leptin (which signals satiety to the brain), adiponectin (which improves insulin sensitivity), and inflammatory cytokines. Excess visceral adiposity is strongly associated with type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and metabolic syndrome.
