Cumberland River

The Cumberland River is a major waterway in the Southern United States, about 688 miles (1,107 km) long. It drains a watershed of nearly 18,000 square miles in Kentucky and Tennessee before joining the Ohio River near Smithland, Kentucky.

Cumberland River
Length: 688 mi (1,107 km)
Source: Appalachian Mountains, Kentucky
Mouth: Ohio River
Basin size: 17,728 sq mi

Course

The river begins in Harlan County, Kentucky, at the confluence of the Poor Fork and Clover Fork, and flows generally west through southern Kentucky, looping south into Tennessee — passing Nashville — before returning north through the Land Between the Lakes region to the Ohio River.

Dams and navigation

The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers maintains a series of locks and dams for navigation and flood control, creating reservoirs including Lake Cumberland, Old Hickory Lake, Lake Barkley, and Cordell Hull Lake. Wolf Creek Dam forms Lake Cumberland, one of the largest artificial lakes in the eastern United States.

History

Named for Prince William, Duke of Cumberland, the river was a vital transportation corridor for early settlers and steamboat commerce. In May 2010 severe flooding along the Cumberland caused extensive damage in Nashville.
