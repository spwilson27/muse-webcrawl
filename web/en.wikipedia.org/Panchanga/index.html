A Panchanga (Sanskrit: pañcāṅga, "five limbs") is a Hindu calendar and almanac that tabulates, for every day of the year, five attributes of time used in Hindu calendrics and Vedic astrology. Printed panchangas — published annually by famous traditional presses and temples — remain in daily use across India and the Hindu diaspora for fixing festival dates and choosing auspicious moments.

The five elements

  • Tithi — the lunar day, defined by the angular elongation of the Moon from the Sun; thirty tithis make a lunar month.
  • Vāra — the weekday, each ruled by a planet or deity (e.g. Sunday by the Sun, Monday by the Moon).
  • Nakshatra — the lunar mansion (one of 27 asterisms) in which the Moon resides that day.
  • Yoga — one of 27 combinations derived from the summed longitudes of the Sun and Moon.
  • Karaṇa — a half-tithi; eleven karanas cycle through the month.

Use

Consulting the panchanga, priests and astrologers determine muhurta — auspicious times for weddings, housewarmings, journeys and other undertakings — and periods to avoid, such as rāhu kāla. The almanac also gives festival dates, eclipses, sunrise and sunset times, and planetary positions. Two conventions for the lunar month coexist: the amanta system (month ending at new moon), used in much of South India and the west, and the purnimanta system (month ending at full moon), used in much of the north; consequently, regional panchangas sometimes differ by a month in naming, though festival days usually agree.
