Sensus communis

Sensus communis is a Latin term meaning "common sense" or "common feeling". In Aristotle's psychology and its medieval development, it denoted an inner sense that binds together the deliverances of the five external senses into a unified perception. In later philosophy — notably Giambattista Vico, the Scottish common-sense school, Kant and Gadamer — it came to mean a shared communal sense or sensibility that makes social judgement possible.

History

Aristotle's koinē aisthēsis was elaborated by Avicenna and Aquinas as the first of the interior senses. In the Critique of Judgment (1790), Kant used sensus communis aestheticus for the presupposed shared capacity underlying judgements of taste. Hans-Georg Gadamer revived the humanistic sense of the term in Truth and Method (1960) as a sense of community and practical judgement.
