Indre-et-Loire

Indre-et-Loire (French pronunciation: [ɛ̃dʁ e lwaʁ]) is a department in west-central France, in the Centre-Val de Loire region. Named after the rivers Indre and Loire, it had a population of about 612,000 in the late 2010s. Its prefecture is Tours, and its other main towns include Amboise, Chinon, Loches and Joué-lès-Tours. The department was created during the French Revolution on 4 March 1790 from most of the former province of Touraine.

Geography and landmarks

The Loire river flows east–west through the department, joined by the Cher, Indre and Vienne. Indre-et-Loire is at the heart of the Loire Valley châteaux country: within or near its borders stand Chenonceau (spanning the Cher), Azay-le-Rideau, Villandry (famous for its Renaissance gardens), Amboise and the Clos Lucé (Leonardo da Vinci's last home), Langeais, Ussé and Chinon, where Joan of Arc met the Dauphin in 1429. The western part of the Loire Valley between Sully-sur-Loire and Chalonnes is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Economy and culture

The department is known for wines including Chinon, Bourgueil, Vouvray and Montlouis-sur-Loire, and for goat cheeses such as Sainte-Maure de Touraine. The region's pure French gave Touraine the nickname "the Garden of France" (le jardin de la France), a description attributed to its fertile river plains and historic royal favour.
