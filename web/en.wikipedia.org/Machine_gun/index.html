Machine gun

A machine gun (MG) is a fully automatic, rifled auto-loading firearm designed for sustained direct fire with rifle cartridges. Other similar weapons are usually considered automatic rifles or submachine guns. Machine guns are generally categorized as light, medium, heavy, or general-purpose, depending on their calibre, weight, and intended role.

History

Early rapid-fire weapons such as the Gatling gun (1860s) required external cranking. The first true automatic machine gun was the Maxim gun of 1884, which used the recoil of each shot to load the next round and could fire hundreds of rounds per minute fed from fabric belts. Machine guns transformed warfare in the First World War, where their defensive firepower contributed to trench stalemate, and designs such as the Vickers, Lewis, and German MG 08 became iconic. In the Second World War the German MG 34 and MG 42 established the general-purpose machine gun concept.

Operation

Machine guns operate by gas, recoil, or blowback mechanisms, and fire from either an open or closed bolt. Sustained fire generates great heat, so designs employ air cooling with heavy or quick-change barrels, or in older models water jackets. Ammunition is usually fed from belts or magazines, and most machine guns are crew-served weapons operated by two or more soldiers.

Modern use

Today squad-level light machine guns such as the FN Minimi / M249, medium guns such as the FN MAG / M240, and heavy guns such as the .50-calibre M2 Browning remain standard infantry and vehicle weapons worldwide.
