Strikeout

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A batter swinging and missing for strike three.

In baseball and softball, a strikeout (or strike-out) occurs when a batter accumulates three strikes during a turn at bat, resulting in an out. It is usually denoted with the letter K in scorekeeping; a backwards K indicates the batter struck out looking (without swinging at the third strike).

Rules

A strike is called on a pitch in the strike zone the batter does not swing at, on any swing that misses, or on a foul ball — except that a foul cannot count as a third strike unless it is a foul bunt. On an uncaught third strike, the batter may attempt to reach first base if it is unoccupied (or with two outs), the so-called dropped third strike rule. A strikeout is credited to the pitcher in statistics, and the batter is charged with an at-bat but not a sacrifice.

Records

Nolan Ryan holds the Major League Baseball career record with 5,714 strikeouts; Randy Johnson ranks second. Ryan also holds the single-season record of 383, set in 1973. Four strikeouts in one inning are possible via the dropped-third-strike rule and have occurred dozens of times.

Terminology

A batter who strikes out four times in a game is said to have earned the "golden sombrero"; five strikeouts is the "platinum sombrero". Striking out the side means retiring all three batters in an inning on strikeouts.
