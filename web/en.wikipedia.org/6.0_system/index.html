6.0 system

The 6.0 system of figure skating judging was the scoring arrangement used in competitive figure skating from the early twentieth century until 2004. Under it, judges awarded each performance two marks, each out of a maximum of 6.0: one for technical merit and one for presentation (artistic impression in singles and pairs).

Scoring

After every skater performed, each judge's pair of marks produced an ordinal ranking — first place, second place, and so on — rather than an absolute score. The final placement was determined by majority rules over the judges' ordinals, which could produce famously complex and sometimes controversial calculations. A "perfect 6.0" became a cultural shorthand for a flawless performance, famously associated with marks awarded at the Winter Olympic Games.

Replacement

Following the judging scandal at the 2002 Winter Olympics in Salt Lake City, in which a French judge admitted to pressure in the pairs event vote-trading affair, the International Skating Union developed a new cumulative-points system. The ISU Judging System (also called the Code of Points) was adopted for the 2004–05 season, retiring the 6.0 system at championship level, although 6.0-style marks survived for some years in some national and non-ISU settings.
