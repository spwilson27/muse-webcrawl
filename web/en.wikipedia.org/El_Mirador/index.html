El Mirador

El Mirador is a large pre-Columbian Maya settlement, located in the north of the modern department of El Petén, Guatemala. It was occupied from about the middle Preclassic period (c. 1000 BCE) and reached its greatest extent in the late Preclassic (c. 300 BCE – 150 CE), when it was one of the largest and earliest Maya cities.

La Danta, one of the largest pyramids in the world by volume, at El Mirador.

Site description

The site is dominated by two enormous pyramid complexes: El Tigre, rising some 55 metres, and La Danta, whose platform and pyramid together reach about 72 metres, making it among the largest structures ever built by the ancient Maya. A network of raised causeways (sacbeob) linked El Mirador to neighbouring cities such as Nakbe and Tintal across the seasonal swamps of the Mirador Basin.

History

Archaeologists believe El Mirador was the capital of a powerful early Maya state, sometimes called the "Kan Kingdom" after its snake emblem glyph. It flourished centuries before the Classic-period cities such as Tikal; its monumental construction required the coordinated labour of thousands and vast quantities of limestone and stucco, whose production may have contributed to deforestation and the city's decline by about 150 CE.

Exploration

Although reported in the 1920s and photographed from the air in the 1930s, serious excavation began only in the late 1970s under projects led by Ray Matheny and later Richard D. Hansen. The site is remote—reached by a multi-day trek or helicopter—and its stucco friezes, including scenes from the Popol Vuh creation myth, are among the earliest Maya sculptures known.
