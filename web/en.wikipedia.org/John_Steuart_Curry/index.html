John Steuart Curry (November 14, 1897 – August 29, 1946) was an American painter associated with the Regionalist movement, noted for dramatic depictions of life in his native Kansas. Alongside Thomas Hart Benton and Grant Wood, he formed the best-known trio of American Regionalists of the 1930s.

Early life

Curry was raised on a farm near Dunavant, Kansas. After brief studies in Kansas City and Chicago and work as an illustrator, he studied in Paris in the 1920s, where the work of Rubens and Courbet influenced his muscular, swirling style.

Career

Curry's Baptism in Kansas (1928) and Tornado over Kansas (1929) established his reputation with their vivid rendering of Midwestern religious fervour and natural violence. In 1936 he became the first artist-in-residence at an American university, at the University of Wisconsin–Madison. His murals for the Kansas State Capitol, including the famous figure of abolitionist John Brown, proved controversial and were left unfinished when he withdrew from the project in frustration.

Legacy

Curry's work is held by major collections including the Whitney Museum of American Art and the Metropolitan Museum of Art, and he is regarded as a defining painter of the American Midwest.
