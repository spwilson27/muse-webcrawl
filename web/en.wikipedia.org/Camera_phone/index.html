A camera phone is a mobile phone which is able to capture photographs and often record video using one or more built-in digital cameras. The first commercial camera phone, the Sharp J-SH04, was released in Japan in 2000; by the mid-2000s most mobile phones included cameras, and camera phones outsold dedicated digital cameras.

History

The J-SH04 allowed users to send pictures electronically via the Sha-Mail service. Nokia, Samsung and Sony Ericsson soon popularised camera phones in Europe and North America. Early cameras offered VGA resolution (0.3 megapixels), but resolution and quality climbed steadily: autofocus, xenon flashes, optical zoom and multi-lens systems followed. The arrival of smartphones and photo-sharing applications made the camera a central selling point of mobile phones.

Technology

Modern camera phones use backside-illuminated CMOS sensors, multiple lenses for wide, telephoto and macro shots, optical image stabilisation, and computational photography techniques such as HDR merging, night modes and portrait depth effects driven by machine learning.

Social impact

Camera phones have transformed journalism, social media and everyday documentation, enabling instant sharing of images from news events — but they have also raised concerns about privacy and surveillance in public and private spaces.
