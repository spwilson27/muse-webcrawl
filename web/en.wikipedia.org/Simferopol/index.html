Simferopol is a city on the Crimean Peninsula and the capital of the Autonomous Republic of Crimea in Ukraine, administered by Russia since its annexation of Crimea in 2014, which most UN member states regard as an occupation of Ukrainian territory. It is the second-largest city on the peninsula after Sevastopol and serves as Crimea's principal political, economic and transport centre, lying on the Salhyr River in the south-central part of the peninsula.

History

The site was earlier occupied by the Scythian city of Neapolis, founded around the 2nd century BCE, whose ruins lie on the outskirts of the modern city. In the Crimean Tatar period the town of Aqmescit ("white mosque") stood here and served as a residence of a deputy of the Crimean Khan. After the Russian Empire annexed Crimea, Catherine the Great founded Simferopol — from Greek words meaning "city of common good" — on the site in 1784.

Simferopol was the headquarters of the Russian army during the Crimean War's later stages, and it changed hands repeatedly during the Russian Civil War. In 1944, following the recapture of Crimea from Nazi occupation, the Soviet authorities deported the entire Crimean Tatar population from the region. Crimea was transferred to the Ukrainian SSR in 1954.

2014 annexation and after

In February–March 2014, unmarked Russian forces seized Simferopol's airport and parliament building, and a disputed referendum preceded Russia's annexation of Crimea. Ukraine and most of the international community continue to recognise Simferopol as Ukrainian territory. The city has since been integrated into Russia's administrative system as capital of the Republic of Crimea.

Transport and culture

Simferopol is the hub of Crimean railways and roads, and its international airport was for decades the main air gateway to the peninsula; it also once operated one of the world's longest trolleybus routes, linking the city to Alushta and Yalta on the Black Sea coast. The city hosts theatres, museums and the Crimean Federal University.
