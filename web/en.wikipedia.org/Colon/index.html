Colon

Colon most commonly refers to the colon (large intestine), the last part of the digestive system in vertebrates. The name also refers to the punctuation mark :, a currency in Costa Rica and formerly El Salvador, and several places including Colón, Panama.

Anatomy

The human colon is about 1.5 metres long and consists of the ascending, transverse, descending, and sigmoid colon. Its main functions are to absorb water and electrolytes from indigestible food matter, store faeces, and host a large population of gut microbiota that ferment fibre and produce vitamins.

Health

Disorders of the colon include colorectal cancer, ulcerative colitis, Crohn's disease, diverticulitis, and irritable bowel syndrome. Screening by colonoscopy is recommended for early detection of colorectal cancer.

Punctuation

The colon : is a punctuation mark consisting of two equally sized dots aligned vertically, used to introduce lists, explanations, ratios, and dialogue.
