New York, officially the State of New York, is a state in the Northeastern United States. It borders New England to the east, Canada to the north, the Great Lakes and the Niagara River to the west, and Pennsylvania and New Jersey to the south. With about 20.2 million residents (2020), it is the fourth-most populous state; more than half live in the New York metropolitan area.

New York City, at the state's southeastern tip, is the largest city in the United States and a global centre of finance, culture, and diplomacy, home to the headquarters of the United Nations. The state capital is Albany.

History

The region was inhabited by Algonquian and Iroquoian peoples, including the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois Confederacy). Henry Hudson's 1609 voyage for the Dutch East India Company led to the colony of New Netherland; the English seized it in 1664 and renamed it for the Duke of York. New York was a battleground in the American Revolution and ratified the U.S. Constitution on 26 July 1788, the eleventh state to do so. The Erie Canal (1825) made New York City the nation's premier port and spurred the growth of upstate cities such as Buffalo, Rochester, and Syracuse.

Geography

"Downstate" — New York City, Long Island, and the lower Hudson Valley — contrasts with the largely rural and mountainous "upstate", which includes the Adirondack Mountains, the Catskills, the Finger Lakes, and the shores of Lakes Erie and Ontario. The Hudson and Mohawk rivers, the St. Lawrence River, and Niagara Falls are major features.

Economy

New York has the third-largest state economy in the U.S., anchored by finance on Wall Street, media, technology, healthcare, higher education, agriculture (notably dairy and apples), and tourism. If it were a country, its gross state product of over $2 trillion would rank among the world's top dozen economies.
