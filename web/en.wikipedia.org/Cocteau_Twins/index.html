Cocteau Twins were a Scottish rock band formed in Grangemouth in 1979 by guitarist Robin Guthrie and bassist Will Heggie, soon joined by vocalist Elizabeth Fraser. Named after a song by fellow Scots Simple Minds, the band became the defining act of the 4AD label and pioneers of dream pop and ethereal wave, famed for Fraser's otherworldly, largely wordless vocals floating over Guthrie's heavily processed guitar textures.

History

Their debut album Garlands (1982) reflected the post-punk of the time. After Heggie's departure, Guthrie and Fraser continued as a duo for Head over Heels (1983), which introduced the lush, layered sound that became their signature. Bassist and multi-instrumentalist Simon Raymonde joined in 1983, completing the classic trio line-up. Albums such as Treasure (1984), Victorialand (1986) and Blue Bell Knoll (1988) built their reputation, and Heaven or Las Vegas (1990) — with unusually intelligible lyrics inspired by the birth of Fraser and Guthrie's daughter — is widely considered their masterpiece and one of the greatest albums of its era. Later albums Four-Calendar Café (1993) and Milk & Kisses (1996) appeared on Fontana/Capitol; the band dissolved in 1997 amid personal difficulties following the end of Fraser and Guthrie's relationship.

Style and influence

Fraser's singing often treats the voice as an instrument, using glossolalia — invented syllables and fragments of words in several languages — while Guthrie's guitar work employs chorus, delay and reverb to create shimmering washes of sound. Their approach profoundly influenced shoegaze, dream pop and ambient pop artists, and Fraser's voice later reached a mass audience through her collaboration with Massive Attack on "Teardrop" (1998).
