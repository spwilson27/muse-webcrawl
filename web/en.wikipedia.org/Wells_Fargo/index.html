The stagecoach is the historic symbol of Wells Fargo.

Wells Fargo & Company is an American multinational financial services company with a significant global presence. The company operates in 35 countries and serves over 70 million customers worldwide. It is one of the "Big Four Banks" of the United States, together with JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America, and Citigroup.

History

Wells Fargo was founded in 1852 by Henry Wells and William G. Fargo, who had earlier co-founded American Express, to provide express and banking services to California during the Gold Rush. Its stagecoach lines became an icon of the American West. The modern company resulted from the 1998 merger of the original Wells Fargo of San Francisco with Norwest Corporation of Minneapolis, which adopted the Wells Fargo name and moved its headquarters to San Francisco. In 2008 it acquired Wachovia, roughly doubling in size.

Operations and controversies

The company provides retail, commercial, and corporate banking, as well as wealth management and mortgage services. In 2016 the bank was fined over revelations that employees had created millions of unauthorised deposit and credit-card accounts to meet sales targets, leading to regulatory sanctions including an asset cap imposed by the Federal Reserve in 2018.
