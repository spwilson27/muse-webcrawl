The Diary of a Young Girl

The Diary of a Young Girl, also known as The Diary of Anne Frank, is a book of the writings from the Dutch-language diary kept by Anne Frank while she was in hiding for two years with her family during the Nazi occupation of the Netherlands. It was first published in Dutch in 1947 as Het Achterhuis ("The Secret Annexe").

Background

Anne Frank (1929–1945), a German-born Jewish girl living in Amsterdam, received the red-checked diary for her thirteenth birthday in June 1942. Weeks later the family went into hiding in rooms behind her father Otto Frank's business premises at Prinsengracht 263, joined by the van Pels family and Fritz Pfeffer. The diary, largely addressed to an imagined friend "Kitty", records daily life in hiding, Anne's coming of age, and her aspirations as a writer; she revised many entries after hearing a 1944 radio appeal for wartime documents.

Arrest and publication

The hiding place was betrayed and raided on 4 August 1944. Anne died of typhus at Bergen-Belsen concentration camp in early 1945. Only Otto Frank survived. The helper Miep Gies preserved the diaries and gave them to Otto, who edited and published them. The English translation appeared in 1952, and a 1955 Broadway adaptation and 1959 film carried Anne's story worldwide.

Significance

The diary has been translated into more than 70 languages and is among the most widely read books in the world, a personal testimony to the Holocaust studied in schools everywhere. The hiding place is now the Anne Frank House museum.
