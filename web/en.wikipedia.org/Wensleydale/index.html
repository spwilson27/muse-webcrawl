Wensleydale

Wensleydale is a valley in North Yorkshire, England, the upper valley of the River Ure in the Yorkshire Dales. It is named after the village of Wensley and is one of only a few Yorkshire dales named after a village rather than its river. The dale is famous for Wensleydale cheese and for waterfalls including Aysgarth Falls and Hardraw Force.

Geography

The dale runs roughly eastward from the watershed near Garsdale Head through Hawes, Aysgarth, Leyburn and Middleham to join lower ground near Masham. Tributary valleys include Widdale, Sleddale and Bishopdale. Much of the upper dale lies within the Yorkshire Dales National Park.

Cheese

Wensleydale cheese, a crumbly white cheese, was first made by Cistercian monks at Jervaulx Abbey and later produced at Hawes, where the Wensleydale Creamery continues production. The cheese gained widespread fame through its association with the animated characters Wallace and Gromit.

History and tourism

Middleham Castle was a stronghold of Richard III, and Bolton Castle in the dale held Mary, Queen of Scots as a prisoner. The Wensleydale Railway, partly preserved as a heritage line, serves the valley. Tourism, farming and quarrying are the main economic activities.
