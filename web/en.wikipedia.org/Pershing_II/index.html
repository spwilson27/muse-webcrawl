Pershing II

The Pershing II was a solid-fuel, medium-range ballistic missile developed by Martin Marietta for the United States Army. It entered service in 1983 as a replacement for the Pershing 1a system, and was deployed primarily in West Germany during the late Cold War.

The missile was notable for its highly accurate radar-based terminal guidance system, the RADAG (Radar Area Guidance), which compared a live radar image of the target area with a stored reference image. This gave the Pershing II a circular error probable of roughly 30 metres, making it accurate enough to threaten hardened targets such as command bunkers.

Development

Development began in the 1970s as an upgrade of the Pershing 1a with a new two-stage booster, improved guidance, and the capability to carry the W85 nuclear warhead, with yields selectable between approximately 5 and 80 kilotons. The missile had a range of about 1,770 kilometres (1,100 miles).

Deployment and political controversy

The deployment of Pershing II missiles in Western Europe, decided by NATO's 1979 Double-Track Decision, was intended to counter the Soviet SS-20 Saber intermediate-range missiles. The deployments sparked massive anti-nuclear protests across Europe in the early 1980s.

Elimination

Under the 1987 Intermediate-Range Nuclear Forces (INF) Treaty, all Pershing II missiles were withdrawn and destroyed. The last missile was eliminated in May 1991, marking the end of the system.
