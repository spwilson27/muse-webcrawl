Tundra Nenets

RegionNorthwestern Siberia, Russia
Speakers~22,000 (2010)
FamilyUralic → Samoyedic → Nenets

Tundra Nenets is a Samoyedic language spoken by the Nenets people of northwestern Siberia, from the Kanin Peninsula in the west to the Yenisey River in the east. It is the larger of the two Nenets languages, the other being Forest Nenets, and is traditionally associated with nomadic reindeer herding on the tundra.

Phonology and grammar

Tundra Nenets has a rich consonant system with palatalized counterparts and a distinctive use of glottal stops. It is an agglutinative language with three numbers (singular, dual, plural) and elaborate verbal morphology including objective conjugation.

Writing system

The language is written in a Cyrillic-based alphabet standardized in the 1930s. It is used in local media and education, though it is considered endangered as younger speakers shift to Russian.
