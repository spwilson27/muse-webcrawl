Wireless LAN

A wireless local area network (WLAN) is a local area network that connects devices using wireless communication, most commonly radio waves, instead of cables. The dominant technology family is Wi-Fi, based on the IEEE 802.11 standards.

Architecture

In infrastructure mode, devices associate with a wireless access point that bridges traffic to a wired network and the internet. Devices communicate in the 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz and 6 GHz bands using channels of varying width; later standards add features such as MIMO and OFDMA to raise throughput and capacity. Ad hoc modes allow direct device-to-device links.

Security

Early WLAN security (WEP) proved weak and was replaced by WPA, WPA2 and WPA3, which authenticate users and encrypt traffic. WLANs are ubiquitous in homes, offices, campuses and public hotspots, and underpin much of the modern Internet of things.
