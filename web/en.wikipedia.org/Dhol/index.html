Dhol

The dhol is a double-headed barrel drum widely played in South Asia, especially in the Punjab, and in the Caucasus and neighbouring regions. It is slung from the neck or shoulder and played with sticks — typically a heavier bent stick (dagga) on the bass head and a thin stick (tilli) on the treble head.

Description

The shell is traditionally hollowed from a single piece of wood such as sheesham, with goatskin heads laced together with rope or, in modern instruments, tensioned with metal rings and bolts. The Punjabi dhol is larger and louder than the related dholak, which is played with the hands.

Uses

The dhol is the driving instrument of bhangra music and dance, and is heard at Punjabi weddings, festivals such as Vaisakhi and Lohri, and in modern bhangra-pop and fusion recordings. Related dhol traditions exist in Assam (where the dhol is central to Bihu celebrations), Gujarat, Rajasthan, Armenia, Azerbaijan, and Georgia.
