Piedmont (United States)

Location
Eastern United States
Extent
New Jersey to Alabama
Bordered by
Appalachian Mountains (west), Atlantic Coastal Plain (east)

The Piedmont is a plateau region in the eastern United States, lying between the Atlantic Coastal Plain and the main Appalachian Mountains from New Jersey to central Alabama. Its name, meaning "foot of the mountain," mirrors the Italian region of the same name.

Geography

The Piedmont is underlain by ancient crystalline rocks, the eroded roots of old mountain ranges, and averages 300–500 feet in elevation in its northern section, rising southward. Along its eastern edge, the Fall Line — a zone of rapids and waterfalls where hard Piedmont rock meets soft coastal sediments — powered early mills and fixed the sites of cities such as Trenton, Richmond, Raleigh, Columbia, and Augusta.

Settlement and economy

Fertile red clay soils supported tobacco and cotton farming in the colonial and antebellum eras. Today the southern Piedmont includes the urban corridor known as the Piedmont Atlantic Megaregion, with metropolitan areas such as Atlanta, Charlotte, and the North Carolina Research Triangle.
