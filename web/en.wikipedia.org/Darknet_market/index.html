Darknet market

A darknet market is a commercial website on a darknet — an overlay network that uses the public Internet but requires specific software, configurations, or authorization to access — most commonly a Tor hidden service. Darknet markets function primarily as black markets, selling or facilitating transactions involving illegal goods such as drugs, stolen data, counterfeit goods, and computer hacking services.

History

Darknet markets grew out of cypherpunk culture and early experiments such as the Black Market Reloaded. The first prominent market was Silk Road, launched in 2011 by Ross Ulbricht ("Dread Pirate Roberts"), which combined Tor with Bitcoin payments and an escrow and reputation system modelled on eBay. The FBI shut Silk Road down in October 2013 and Ulbricht was sentenced to life in prison (he was pardoned in 2025). Successor markets included Agora, Evolution, AlphaBay, and Dream Market; most were eventually closed by law enforcement, exit scams, or both. The Russian-language Hydra Market, seized by German authorities in 2022, was the largest at the time.

How they operate

Sellers list goods, buyers pay in cryptocurrency, and the market holds funds in escrow until delivery. Multisignature transactions and privacy coins such as Monero have been adopted to reduce traceability. Goods are typically shipped through ordinary postal services; digital goods such as stolen credentials are delivered electronically.

Law enforcement and debate

Major coordinated takedowns include Operation Onymous (2014) and Operation Bayonet (2017), in which Dutch police secretly operated the Hansa market to gather evidence. Supporters argue markets reduce street violence and allow quality ratings that lower harm to drug users; critics emphasize that they facilitate addiction, fraud, and other crime, and academic studies have estimated transaction volumes in the billions of dollars.
