In physics, a force is an influence that can cause an object to change its velocity — to accelerate — unless counteracted by other forces. The concept of force makes the everyday notion of pushing or pulling mathematically precise. A force has both magnitude and direction, making it a vector quantity, and is measured in the SI unit of newtons (N).

Newtonian mechanics

Newton's second law states that the net force on an object equals the rate of change of its momentum: F = dp/dt. For a body of constant mass this reduces to the familiar F = ma. Newton's third law states that forces come in equal and opposite pairs.

Fundamental forces

All forces in nature can be traced to four fundamental interactions: gravity, electromagnetism, the strong nuclear force, and the weak nuclear force. Everyday contact forces such as friction, tension, and the normal force are macroscopic manifestations of electromagnetic interactions between atoms.

Examples

Common forces studied in mechanics include weight (gravity), the normal force, friction, tension in ropes and strings, spring forces described by Hooke's law, and applied forces such as thrust and drag.
