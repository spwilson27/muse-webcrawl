Florence Nightingale

Nightingale, "The Lady with the Lamp", during the Crimean War.

Florence Nightingale (12 May 1820 – 13 August 1910) was an English social reformer, statistician and the founder of modern nursing. Nightingale came to prominence while serving as a manager and trainer of nurses during the Crimean War, in which she organised care for wounded soldiers at Constantinople. She significantly reduced death rates by improving hygiene and living standards. Nightingale gave nursing a favourable reputation and became an icon of Victorian culture, especially in the persona of "The Lady with the Lamp" making rounds of wounded soldiers at night.

Crimean War

In 1854, Nightingale led a party of 38 women volunteer nurses to the Ottoman Empire, where British troops fighting in the Crimean War were based at Scutari (now Üsküdar, Istanbul). Conditions in the military hospital were appalling — overcrowding, defective sewers, and a lack of basic supplies caused more deaths from disease than from wounds. Nightingale's insistence on sanitation, nutrition, clean water and ventilation was followed by a dramatic fall in mortality, and the Sanitary Commission's later flushing of the sewers confirmed the importance of her approach. Her nightly rounds with a lamp made her famous in the British press.

Statistics and reform

Nightingale was a pioneer in data visualisation. Her "coxcomb" or polar area diagrams showed that preventable disease, not battle wounds, was the great killer of soldiers, and she used them to lobby the government for reform. In 1858 she became the first woman elected a Fellow of the Royal Statistical Society, and she was later an honorary member of the American Statistical Association. Her monumental reports on army health and her work on hospital design influenced reform in Britain and India.

Nursing

With a public fund raised in her honour, Nightingale established the Nightingale Training School at St Thomas' Hospital in London in 1860, laying the foundation for professional nursing with trained, salaried nurses. Her book Notes on Nursing (1859) was a landmark popular guide to health and nursing care. She advocated sanitary reform in India and campaigned on workhouse infirmaries, midwifery, and district nursing.

Legacy

Nightingale's birthday is celebrated as International Nurses Day. Her emphasis on evidence, sanitation, and professional training remains foundational to modern healthcare. In 1907 she became the first woman to be awarded the Order of Merit.
