The 1Malaysia Development Berhad scandal, often called the 1MDB scandal, is one of the largest financial corruption and money-laundering cases in history. It involved the Malaysian state investment fund 1Malaysia Development Berhad, established in 2009 by Prime Minister Najib Razak, who chaired its advisory board. According to the U.S. Department of Justice, at least US$4.5 billion was misappropriated from the fund between 2009 and 2014 by high-level officials and their associates and laundered through the global financial system.

The scheme

Investigators allege the operation was masterminded by Malaysian financier Jho Low, who — though holding no official position — directed fund transactions through a web of shell companies and bank accounts in jurisdictions including Singapore, Switzerland, Luxembourg, the Seychelles and the United States. The stolen money allegedly financed luxury real estate in New York, Los Angeles and London, a private jet, artworks by Van Gogh and Monet, a superyacht (the Equanimity), jewellery, and the production of Hollywood films through Red Granite Pictures, including The Wolf of Wall Street (2013) and Dumb and Dumber To.

Goldman Sachs arranged about US$6.5 billion in bond sales for 1MDB, earning unusually high fees of roughly US$600 million; its banker Roger Ng was later convicted in the United States, and the bank paid over US$5 billion in penalties and settlements worldwide, including a US$3.9 billion settlement with Malaysia in 2020.

Exposure and fallout

The scandal broke into the open in 2015 through reporting by the Sarawak Report, The Edge and The Wall Street Journal, which revealed that nearly US$700 million had entered Najib's personal bank accounts. Najib claimed the money was a Saudi donation and was initially cleared by Malaysia's attorney general, but investigations proceeded in at least six countries. Public anger over 1MDB was a major factor in the historic defeat of Najib's coalition in Malaysia's 2018 general election — the first transfer of power since independence.

Re-elected investigations followed. In July 2020 Najib was convicted in the related SRC International case and sentenced to 12 years in prison (later reduced by a pardons board in 2024); further 1MDB trials followed. Jho Low remains a fugitive, believed to be in China, and denies wrongdoing. Malaysia and U.S. authorities have recovered several billion dollars of assets, including the seized superyacht and forfeited real estate and film rights.
