Septoplasty

Septoplasty is a corrective surgical procedure done to straighten a deviated nasal septum — the partition between the two nasal cavities. The procedure is performed entirely through the nostrils and typically takes between 30 and 90 minutes under local or general anaesthesia.

Indications

Septoplasty is indicated when a deviated septum causes nasal obstruction, recurrent sinus infections, nosebleeds, or contributes to obstructive sleep apnea. Unlike rhinoplasty, septoplasty does not by itself change the external shape of the nose, though the two procedures are often combined as septorhinoplasty.

Procedure

The surgeon makes an incision inside the nose, lifts the mucosal lining away from the septal cartilage and bone, removes or reshapes the deviated portions, and repositions the lining. Splints or packing may be placed temporarily to support healing.

Risks and recovery

Possible complications include septal perforation, bleeding, infection, changes in smell, and persistent deviation. Most patients return to normal activities within about a week, though complete healing of the septum takes several weeks.
