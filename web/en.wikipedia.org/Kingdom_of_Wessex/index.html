Kingdom of Wessex

Winchester, the royal capital of Wessex.

The Kingdom of Wessex was an Anglo-Saxon kingdom in southern England, traditionally said to have been founded in 519 by Cerdic and Cynric, and lasting until the unification of England under Æthelstan in 927. Ruled by the House of Wessex, it was the only Anglo-Saxon kingdom to survive the Viking invasions of the 9th century intact, and its kings went on to become the first kings of a united England.

Early history

Wessex grew from settlements of the Gewisse people in the upper Thames valley, expanding westward at the expense of the Britons and absorbing neighbouring peoples. Under King Egbert (r. 802–839) Wessex achieved temporary overlordship of most of England south of the Humber, and after his victory at Ellendun (825) it became the dominant Anglo-Saxon power.

Alfred the Great and the Viking wars

The kingdom's most famous ruler, Alfred the Great (r. 871–899), halted the Danish advance at the Battle of Edington (878), established a system of fortified burhs and a reorganised army and navy, issued a law code, and promoted learning and literacy in Old English. His children Edward the Elder and Æthelflæd continued the reconquest of the Danelaw, and his grandson Æthelstan completed the work, taking York in 927 and being recognised as the first king of England.

Legacy

The name Wessex survives as a regional identity in south-western England, popularised in the 19th century by the novels of Thomas Hardy, who set his fiction in a semi-fictional "Wessex" modelled on the old kingdom's territory.
