Himiko

Himiko (c. 170 – 248 CE), also known as Pimiko, was a shamaness-queen of Yamatai-koku in ancient Japan, recorded in Chinese historical texts such as the Records of the Three Kingdoms. According to these accounts, the people of Wa (Japan), after years of civil war among their kings, agreed to make the unmarried Himiko their ruler; she governed through sorcery and ritual, secluded in a fortified residence attended by a thousand women.

Diplomacy with Wei

Himiko sent embassies to the Chinese court of Wei in 239 and later years, receiving in return the title "Queen of Wa, Friendly to Wei", a gold seal, and gifts including one hundred bronze mirrors. After her death around 248, a great burial mound was raised for her; a king was installed but not obeyed, and order was restored only when a young girl, Iyo, was placed on the throne.

Historical debate

The location of Yamatai is one of the great open questions of early Japanese history, with competing theories placing it in northern Kyushu or in the Kinai region near Nara; the enormous keyhole-shaped Hashihaka kofun in Sakurai has been proposed as Himiko's tomb. Because Japanese chronicles do not name her, debate continues over whether she corresponds to figures in the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki, and she remains a popular subject in Japanese fiction, film and games.
