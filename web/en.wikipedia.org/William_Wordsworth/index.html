William Wordsworth

William Wordsworth (April 7, 1770 – April 23, 1850) was an English poet who, with Samuel Taylor Coleridge, helped launch the Romantic Age in English literature with their joint publication Lyrical Ballads (1798). He served as Poet Laureate from 1843 until his death.

DiedApril 23, 1850, Rydal Mount, Westmorland, England

Early life

Wordsworth was born in the Lake District, a landscape that shaped his imagination. He attended Cambridge University and traveled in revolutionary France, where he fathered a daughter with Annette Vallon before war forced his return. His sister Dorothy, a gifted diarist, became his lifelong companion and collaborator.

Lyrical Ballads and the great decade

Living near Coleridge in Somerset, Wordsworth published Lyrical Ballads in 1798, with a Preface (expanded 1800) that became the manifesto of English Romanticism, defining poetry as "the spontaneous overflow of powerful feelings" recollected in tranquility and written in "the real language of men." His most creative decade followed, producing "Tintern Abbey," the "Lucy" poems, the Intimations of Immortality ode, and much of his autobiographical epic The Prelude, published posthumously in 1850.

Later life

Settled at Rydal Mount in the Lakes, Wordsworth grew more conservative in politics and religion, and his later poetry was often considered less inspired, though poems such as "I Wandered Lonely as a Cloud" (1807) remained beloved. He accepted the laureateship in 1843 and died in 1850.
