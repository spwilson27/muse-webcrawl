High Seas Fleet

The High Seas Fleet (German: Hochseeflotte) was the battle fleet of the German Imperial Navy (Kaiserliche Marine) and saw action during the First World War. It was created by Admiral Alfred von Tirpitz under the Navy Laws of 1898 and 1900 as the core of Germany's challenge to British naval supremacy, triggering an Anglo-German naval arms race.

Strategy

Tirpitz's "risk theory" held that a German fleet strong enough to inflict unacceptable losses on the Royal Navy would deter Britain from war, even if it could not win an outright victory. Based primarily at Wilhelmshaven and Kiel, the fleet's dreadnought battleships and battlecruisers were intended to operate in the North Sea and wear down the British Grand Fleet through raids and ambushes of isolated squadrons.

First World War

The High Seas Fleet, commanded successively by Friedrich von Ingenohl, Hugo von Pohl, and Reinhard Scheer, fought several actions against the Royal Navy, including the raids on Scarborough, Hartlepool and Whitby (1914), the Battle of Dogger Bank (1915), and the Battle of Jutland (31 May – 1 June 1916), the war's largest naval battle. At Jutland the German fleet inflicted heavier losses but was forced to retreat, and it remained largely confined to port thereafter as Germany turned to unrestricted submarine warfare.

Mutiny and scuttling

In October 1918, orders for a final suicidal sortie triggered the Kiel mutiny, which spread into the German Revolution and the abdication of the Kaiser. Under the Armistice, the fleet was interned at the British base at Scapa Flow in Scotland. On 21 June 1919, Rear Admiral Ludwig von Reuter ordered the scuttling of the fleet; 52 of the 74 interned ships sank, and most were later salvaged for scrap.
