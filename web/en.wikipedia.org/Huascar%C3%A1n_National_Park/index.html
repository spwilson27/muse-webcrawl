Huascarán National Park

Huascarán National Park (Spanish: Parque Nacional Huascarán) is a protected area in the Cordillera Blanca mountain range of the Peruvian Andes, in the Ancash Region. Established in 1975 and inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1985, the park covers about 340,000 hectares and protects the highest tropical mountain range in the world.

Geography

The park takes its name from Mount Huascarán (6,768 m), Peru's highest peak and the fourth-highest mountain in South America. The Cordillera Blanca contains more than 600 glaciers and numerous glacial lakes, including Llanganuco and Parón, and is drained by the Santa River, which flows through the inter-Andean valley known as the Callejón de Huaylas between the Cordillera Blanca and the Cordillera Negra.

Flora and fauna

The park spans habitats from high Andean grassland (puna) above 4,000 metres to montane forest. Notable species include the Puya raimondii, the largest bromeliad in the world, queñual (Polylepis) woodland, the Andean condor, the spectacled bear, the vicuña, and the Andean mountain cat.

Tourism and hazards

The park is a major centre for mountaineering and trekking in Peru, with the Santa Cruz trek among its best-known routes. The region is also exposed to natural hazards: glacial lake outburst floods and ice avalanches from Huascarán devastated the city of Yungay in 1970 following an earthquake, killing tens of thousands.
