Monrovia is the capital and largest city of Liberia, on the Atlantic coast at Cape Mesurado. Founded in 1822 by the American Colonization Society as a settlement for freed American slaves, it was named after U.S. President James Monroe, a supporter of the colonisation project.

History

Originally called Christopolis, the settlement became the capital of independent Liberia in 1847. The city suffered extensive damage during the Liberian civil wars of 1989–2003, particularly in the 2003 sieges, and later faced the West African Ebola epidemic of 2014–2016.

Geography and economy

Monrovia lies on a peninsula between the Atlantic Ocean and the Mesurado River. Its free port is the country's principal harbour, and the economy centres on government, shipping, and the export of iron ore and rubber.
