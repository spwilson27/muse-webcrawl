Birger Magnusson (c. 1210 – 21 October 1266), usually called Birger Jarl, was a Swedish statesman, jarl and regent who played a central role in the consolidation of Sweden in the 13th century. He is traditionally credited with founding Stockholm around 1252 and with making Sweden a more unified, centrally governed kingdom.

Life and rule

Birger was a member of the powerful Folkung family (House of Bjelbo) and brother-in-law of King Erik Eriksson, whose sister Ingeborg he married. In 1248 he became jarl, the kingdom's highest office after the king. After Erik's death in 1250, Birger's young son Valdemar was elected king, and Birger ruled as regent in practice for the rest of his life, defeating a revolt by rival Folkung factions at the Battle of Herrevadsbro in 1251.

Reforms and campaigns

Birger Jarl strengthened royal authority, issued laws protecting women and the church, developed taxation and trade relations with Lübeck and the Hanseatic League, and founded or fortified towns. Tradition attributes to him the Second Swedish Crusade (c. 1249–1250), which brought Tavastia in Finland more firmly under Swedish rule. A surviving 1252 letter in which he refers to his subjects in "Stockholm" is the first mention of the city, and he is honoured as its founder.

Death and legacy

Birger died in 1266 and was buried in Varnhem Abbey. His sons Valdemar and Magnus both became kings of Sweden, founding the royal line of the House of Bjelbo. In Stockholm he is commemorated by Birger Jarls torg, a square bearing his statue, and his cenotaph at Stockholm City Hall.
