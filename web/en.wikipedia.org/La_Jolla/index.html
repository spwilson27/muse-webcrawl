La Jolla

La Jolla Cove, along the community's rugged Pacific shoreline.

La Jolla (pronounced "la HOY-ə") is a hilly, affluent seaside community within the city of San Diego, California, occupying about 11 kilometres of curving Pacific coastline. Its population is roughly 46,000. The name is often said to derive from the Spanish la joya, "the jewel", though it may come from a Kumeyaay place name. La Jolla is known for its beaches, upscale shopping and dining in "the Village", and some of the most expensive real estate in the United States.

Geography and attractions

The community wraps around Mount Soledad and includes La Jolla Cove, Children's Pool — a seawall-protected beach colonized by harbour seals — Windansea Beach, and the cliffs and sea caves of the La Jolla Underwater Park ecological reserve, popular for kayaking, snorkelling, and scuba diving. Torrey Pines State Natural Reserve and the Torrey Pines Golf Course, host of the U.S. Open, lie at its northern edge.

Institutions

La Jolla is a major centre of research and biotechnology. The University of California, San Diego (founded 1960) sits largely within it, together with the Scripps Institution of Oceanography (1903), the Salk Institute for Biological Studies (founded by Jonas Salk), Scripps Research, and the Sanford Burnham Prebys institute. The La Jolla Playhouse and the Museum of Contemporary Art San Diego anchor its cultural life.
