Orion (constellation)

Orion is a prominent constellation located on the celestial equator and visible throughout the world. It is one of the largest, most conspicuous, and most recognizable constellations in the night sky. Its name refers to Orion, a hunter in Greek mythology.

Notable features

Orion is dominated by two bright stars: the blue-white supergiant Rigel (Beta Orionis), at the hunter's foot, and the red supergiant Betelgeuse (Alpha Orionis), at his shoulder — one of the largest stars known. The three stars of Orion's Belt — Alnilam, Alnitak, and Mintaka — form one of the most famous asterisms in the sky. Hanging from the Belt is the Sword of Orion, which contains the Orion Nebula (M42), a nearby stellar nursery visible to the naked eye as a fuzzy patch. The Horsehead Nebula and the Trapezium cluster also lie within the constellation.

Mythology

In Greek myth, Orion was a giant, supernaturally strong hunter, son of Poseidon, who boasted he could kill every animal on Earth and was slain by a scorpion sent by Gaia — which is why Orion and Scorpius are never seen in the sky at the same time. The constellation was known to many ancient cultures; in Egypt its stars were associated with Osiris, and Babylonian astronomers knew it as the "Heavenly Shepherd."

Visibility

Orion is best visible in the evening sky from November to early March in the Northern Hemisphere (summer in the Southern Hemisphere). Because it straddles the celestial equator, it can be seen from nearly all inhabited latitudes.
