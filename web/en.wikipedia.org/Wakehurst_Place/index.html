Wakehurst Place

Wakehurst, historically Wakehurst Place, is a botanic garden and country estate in West Sussex, England, managed by the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew. Known as "Kew's wild botanic garden", it covers about 535 acres (217 hectares) of designed gardens, woodland, and nature reserves, and is the site of the Millennium Seed Bank, the world's largest wild plant seed conservation project.

History

The Elizabethan mansion at the heart of the estate was built around 1590 by Edward Culpeper. The gardens' modern character was created largely between 1903 and 1935 by Gerald Loder, later Lord Wakehurst, a keen plant collector who assembled extensive collections of Southern Hemisphere plants suited to the site's acidic soil and mild, damp microclimate. In 1963 the estate was bequeathed to the National Trust, which leased it to Kew from 1965.

The gardens

Wakehurst's collections are arranged by geography and habitat, including the Asian Heathland, Southern Hemisphere gardens, the Himalayan Glade, and the Loder Valley nature reserve of ancient woodland and meadow. Its specimen trees include the national collection of birches and notable conifers; the site is also famous for one of the UK's tallest Christmas trees, a giant redwood decorated each winter. The grounds lie within the High Weald Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty.

Millennium Seed Bank

Opened in 2000 in the Wellcome Trust Millennium Building, the Millennium Seed Bank stores seeds from tens of thousands of wild plant species — over two billion seeds — in sub-zero vaults, as insurance against extinction and for research and habitat restoration. Its partnerships span more than 100 countries.
