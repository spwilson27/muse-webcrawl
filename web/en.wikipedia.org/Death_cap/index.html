Death cap

Scientific nameAmanita phalloides
FamilyAmanitaceae
ToxicityDeadly poisonous (amatoxins)

The death cap (Amanita phalloides) is one of the most poisonous of all known mushrooms and is believed to be responsible for the majority of fatal mushroom poisonings worldwide. Native to Europe, it has spread widely with imported trees and now occurs on several continents.

Description

The fruiting body has a cap 5–15 cm across, typically greenish-yellow to olive and fading with age, white gills, a white stipe with a skirt-like ring, and a large volva at the base. Young specimens can resemble edible puffballs or paddy straw mushrooms, a resemblance that has caused many poisonings among foragers.

Toxicity

Death caps contain amatoxins, principally α-amanitin, which inhibit RNA polymerase II and destroy liver and kidney cells. As little as half a cap may be fatal. Symptoms are delayed 6–24 hours, beginning with severe gastrointestinal distress, followed by a deceptive remission and then liver failure. Treatment involves hospitalization, high-dose silibinin, and sometimes liver transplantation.

Famous cases

The death cap has been speculatively linked to the deaths of the Roman emperor Claudius and the Holy Roman Emperor Charles VI, among others.
