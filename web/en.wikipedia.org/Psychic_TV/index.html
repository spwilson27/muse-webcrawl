Psychic TV

Psychic TV

Origin: London, England

Formed: 1981

Genres: Industrial, acid house, experimental, psychedelic rock

Key members: Genesis P-Orridge, Alex Fergusson, Peter Christopherson

Psychic TV (also stylised as Psychick TV and PTV) is an English experimental music and video art group formed in London in 1981 by Genesis P-Orridge and Peter Christopherson after the dissolution of Throbbing Gristle, together with guitarist Alex Fergusson. The group combined industrial music, performance art, and occult themes, and later became an unlikely pioneer of acid house in Britain.

History

Psychic TV's debut album, Force the Hand of Chance (1982), was released alongside material issued through the group's associated organization, Thee Temple ov Psychick Youth (TOPY), a loose network blending art collective and magical order. In the mid-1980s the group undertook a project to release 23 live albums on the 23rd day of 23 consecutive months, earning a Guinness record for the most albums released in a year. In 1988–89 Psychic TV released a series of acid house records — some presented as compilations by fictitious acts such as Jack the Tab — helping to seed the British rave scene.

P-Orridge emigrated to the United States in the early 1990s, and the project later re-emerged as PTV3 in the 2000s, touring and recording psychedelic rock albums including Snakes (2014) and Alienist (2016). The group ended with P-Orridge's death in 2020.

Influence

Psychic TV is regarded as a bridge between first-wave industrial music, experimental video art, and British dance culture, and its network TOPY influenced later occult and chaos-magic subcultures.
