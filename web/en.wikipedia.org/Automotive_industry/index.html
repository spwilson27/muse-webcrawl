The automotive industry comprises a wide range of companies and organizations involved in the design, development, manufacturing, marketing, selling, repairing, and modification of motor vehicles. It is one of the world's largest industries by revenue and a major employer worldwide, with a long supply chain of parts suppliers, dealerships, and service businesses.

History

The modern industry began in the late 19th century with pioneers such as Karl Benz, whose 1885 Patent-Motorwagen is considered the first practical automobile. Manufacturing was revolutionized by Henry Ford, whose Model T (1908) and moving assembly line at Highland Park (1913) made cars affordable to the middle class. In the 20th century the industry was reshaped by Alfred Sloan's brand-ladder strategy at General Motors, by Japanese lean production methods pioneered by Toyota after the Second World War, and by globalization of both production and markets.

Production

World motor vehicle production has stood at roughly 85–95 million units per year in the 2020s. China is by far the largest producing country, followed by the United States, Japan, India, and Germany. Production relies heavily on industrial robots for welding, painting, and assembly, and on just-in-time delivery of components from thousands of suppliers.

Trends

The industry is undergoing a transition toward electric vehicles, driven by emissions regulation and falling battery costs; major manufacturers have announced large investments in electrification. Other major trends include autonomous driving systems, connected-car services, shared mobility, and software-defined vehicles. These shifts have brought new entrants such as Tesla and BYD into leading positions.

Safety and environment

Automotive production and use have significant environmental impacts, including greenhouse gas emissions, air pollution, and resource consumption. Governments regulate vehicle safety and emissions through standards and crash-testing programmes, which have driven advances in crumple zones, airbags, electronic stability control, and fuel efficiency.
