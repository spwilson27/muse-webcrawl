Eye color

Eye color is a polygenic phenotypic character determined by two factors: the pigmentation of the eye's iris and the frequency-dependence of the scattering of light by the turbid medium in the stroma of the iris. In humans, iris pigmentation ranges from light blue to dark brown, depending on the concentration of melanin in the iris pigment epithelium and stroma.

Genetics

Eye color was long taught as a simple Mendelian trait, but it is in fact controlled by multiple genes. The most important are OCA2 and its regulator HERC2 on chromosome 15, which account for most of the variation between brown and blue eyes. Blue eyes result from low melanin in the stroma combined with Rayleigh scattering of light, analogous to the blue of the sky; there is no blue pigment. Green and hazel eyes reflect intermediate melanin levels plus lipochrome pigment.

Distribution

Brown is the most common eye color worldwide, found in roughly 70–80% of the global population. Blue eyes are most frequent in Northern and Eastern Europe; a study found that nearly half of Estonians and Scandinavians have blue eyes. Genetic evidence suggests all blue-eyed people descend from a single common ancestor who lived 6,000–10,000 years ago near the Black Sea.

Changes and conditions

Many infants of European ancestry are born with blue or grey eyes that darken in the first years of life as melanin accumulates. Heterochromia — a difference in color between the two eyes or within one iris — may be congenital or acquired. Eye color can also change with disease or trauma.
