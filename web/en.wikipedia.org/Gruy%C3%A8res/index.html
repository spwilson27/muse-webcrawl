Gruyères

Gruyères is a town in the district of Gruyère in the canton of Fribourg in Switzerland. The medieval hilltop town, which gives its name to the well-known Gruyère cheese, is a popular tourist destination and has a population of around 2,200.

Geography and history

Gruyères stands on a hill above the Saane valley at the entrance to the upper Saane region, beneath the distinctive peak of Moléson. The counts of Gruyères ruled the surrounding county from the eleventh century until 1555, when the last count, Michael, went bankrupt and his lands were divided between Fribourg and Bern. The cobbled main street of the old town, lined with houses from the fifteenth to seventeenth centuries, is closed to cars.

Sights

The Castle of Gruyères, founded in the thirteenth century, served as the residence of the counts and now houses a museum spanning eight centuries of regional history. Since 1998 the town has also hosted the HR Giger Museum, dedicated to the Swiss surrealist artist H. R. Giger, with a Giger-designed bar opposite; Giger is buried in the town. A Tibet museum occupies a converted church. Nearby, the Maison du Gruyère cheese dairy in Pringy demonstrates the production of Gruyère cheese, and the medieval town is a common starting point for visits to the Moléson and the Cailler chocolate factory at Broc.
