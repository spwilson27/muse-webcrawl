Human resource management (HRM) is the practice of recruiting, hiring, deploying and managing an organisation's employees, and the academic discipline that studies how organisations manage people to maximise performance in service of the employer's strategic objectives. The HR department oversees areas including recruitment, onboarding, training and development, compensation and benefits, performance management, employee relations, and compliance with employment law.

History

The field grew out of early twentieth-century personnel management and welfare work, was reshaped by the human relations movement following Elton Mayo's Hawthorne studies, and broadened after the Second World War as labour law, collective bargaining and behavioural science expanded the personnel function. From the 1980s a "strategic HRM" literature (including the Michigan and Harvard models) framed employees as resources whose management should align with business strategy; Dave Ulrich's 1997 Human Resource Champions popularised the HR business-partner model.

Practice

Core activities include workforce planning and job analysis, talent acquisition, learning and development, total rewards, performance appraisal, succession planning, employee engagement, diversity and inclusion, health and safety, and HR information systems and people analytics. Professional bodies such as CIPD (UK) and SHRM (US) certify practitioners. Debates in the field concern the tension between employee welfare and managerial control, the effects of high-performance work systems, and the impact of gig work and remote working.
