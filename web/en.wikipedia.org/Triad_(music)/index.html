Triad (music)

In music, a triad is a set of three notes (or pitch classes) that can be stacked vertically in thirds. Triads are the fundamental building blocks of tertian harmony and the most common chords in Western music.

Structure

A triad consists of a root, the note a third above it (the third), and the note a fifth above the root (the fifth). Depending on the sizes of the intervals, four types of triad result:

  • Major triad: major third and perfect fifth above the root (e.g. C–E–G).
  • Minor triad: minor third and perfect fifth (C–E♭–G).
  • Diminished triad: minor third and diminished fifth (C–E♭–G♭).
  • Augmented triad: major third and augmented fifth (C–E–G♯).

Positions and inversions

When the root is the lowest note the triad is in root position. With the third in the bass it is in first inversion, and with the fifth in the bass, second inversion; inversions are indicated with figured bass symbols. Close and open position, and the doubling of notes in four-part writing, are governed by the conventions of voice leading.

Function

In tonal music, triads built on each degree of the scale take characteristic functions — tonic, dominant, subdominant and so on — and progressions of triads underpin most Western harmony from the Baroque era through contemporary popular music. Extended chords add further thirds above the fifth to make seventh and ninth chords.
