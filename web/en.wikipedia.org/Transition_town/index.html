A Transition town is a community-led initiative working to make a town, city district or village more resilient to climate change, peak oil and economic shocks. The Transition Network movement began in 2005 in Kinsale, Ireland, and 2006 in Totnes, Devon, England, where permaculture teacher Rob Hopkins developed the Transition model. Initiatives typically start with awareness-raising and an "Energy Descent Action Plan", then branch into local food, renewable energy, alternative currencies and skill-sharing projects.

Principles

Transition groups follow twelve loose principles organised around "heads, hearts and hands": raising awareness of the converging crises, reconnecting communities with land and skills, and building the parallel infrastructure — community energy schemes, local currencies such as the Totnes pound, food hubs, repair cafés and re-skilling workshops. The movement emphasises inclusivity, subsidiarity and "transitioning within" as well as practical projects.

Growth and critique

The movement grew rapidly after 2008, peaking with hundreds of registered initiatives across Britain, the United States, Australia and beyond. Critics have argued that Transition groups attract mainly already-green, middle-class participants and can struggle with diversity, burnout and measuring impact; supporters point to concrete outcomes such as community solar cooperatives, municipal food policies and lasting social networks. The Transition Network continues to coordinate an international hub of affiliated groups.
