The General Council of the Bar, commonly known as the Bar Council, is the representative body for barristers in England and Wales. Established in 1894, it promotes the Bar's interests, represents barristers on matters of policy and reform of the law, and works to uphold the administration of justice and access to justice.

Role

Following the Legal Services Act 2007, the Bar Council's regulatory functions — setting standards of conduct, education and training, and discipline — are discharged at arm's length by the Bar Standards Board, while the Council itself acts as the profession's representative and "voice". Its members are elected from practising barristers, including representatives of the circuits and specialist bar associations, and it is led by a Chair elected annually.

Activities

The Bar Council publishes guidance and ethical resources, campaigns on issues such as legal aid funding and court reform, organises the annual Bar Conference, and works internationally with bar associations abroad. Barristers pay an annual practising certificate fee, part of which funds the Council's representative work by subscription.
