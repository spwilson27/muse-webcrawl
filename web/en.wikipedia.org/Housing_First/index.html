Housing First

Housing First
Focus: Homelessness policy
Founder: Sam Tsemberis / Pathways to Housing
Origin: New York City, 1992
Adopted by: Canada, Finland, France, United States, and others

Housing First is a policy and programme model for addressing homelessness that prioritises providing permanent housing to people experiencing homelessness without preconditions — such as sobriety or participation in treatment — before addressing other issues in their lives. It contrasts with "staircase" or treatment-first models, which require homeless people to progress through shelters and transitional housing and demonstrate "housing readiness" before receiving a permanent home.

Principles

The model was developed by psychologist Sam Tsemberis and the organisation Pathways to Housing in New York City in the early 1990s. Its core principles include: immediate access to permanent housing with no readiness requirements; consumer choice in housing and services; a harm-reduction orientation; separation of housing from services, so that keeping the home does not depend on treatment compliance; and assertive community treatment or intensive case management offered on a voluntary basis.

Evidence and adoption

Randomised controlled trials, notably Canada's At Home/Chez Soi study (2008–2013), found that Housing First participants spent far more time stably housed than those receiving usual care, and the model has been widely adopted in national strategies. Finland, which made Housing First the basis of its homelessness policy from 2008, is frequently cited for sustained reductions in long-term homelessness. Evaluations differ on cost offsets and effects on health and substance use, and commentators debate the model's limits where housing supply itself is scarce.
