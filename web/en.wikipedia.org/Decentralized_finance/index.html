Decentralized finance

Short nameDeFi
TechnologyBlockchain, smart contracts
First major platformEthereum

Decentralized finance (often shortened to DeFi) is a form of finance based on blockchain technology that does not rely on central financial intermediaries such as brokerages, exchanges, or banks. Instead, it uses smart contracts — self-executing programs on public blockchains, most commonly Ethereum — to offer traditional financial services.

How it works

DeFi applications ("dApps") let users lend, borrow, trade, and earn interest on crypto-assets directly from their own wallets. Automated market makers such as Uniswap replace order-book exchanges with liquidity pools governed by algorithms, while lending protocols such as Aave and Compound set interest rates algorithmically based on supply and demand.

Stablecoins

Because most cryptocurrencies are volatile, DeFi relies heavily on stablecoins — tokens pegged to currencies such as the US dollar. Some are backed by reserves held by an issuer, while others, such as Dai, are stabilised by over-collateralised crypto deposits and smart-contract governance.

Risks and criticism

DeFi has been criticised for smart-contract bugs and exploits that have drained hundreds of millions of dollars, for the collapse of algorithmic stablecoins such as TerraUSD in 2022, and for regulatory uncertainty. Supporters argue it offers open, permissionless, and transparent financial infrastructure available to anyone with an internet connection.
