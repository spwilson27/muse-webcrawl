Interpolation space

In the field of mathematical analysis, an interpolation space is a space lying "in between" two other Banach spaces. Interpolation theory arose from the observation that many linear operators which are bounded on two endpoint spaces are automatically bounded on a whole scale of intermediate spaces, with bounds that can be estimated from the endpoint bounds.

Background

Classical results include the Riesz–Thorin theorem, which interpolates boundedness of operators between Lp spaces, and the Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem, which gives weak-type conclusions. Abstract interpolation theory, developed by Lions, Peetre, Calderón and others in the 1960s, made these ideas systematic for arbitrary pairs of Banach spaces.

Construction

Given a compatible couple of Banach spaces (X0, X1), the main constructions are the real interpolation method, based on the K-functional and producing spaces (X0, X1)θ,q, and the complex interpolation method of Calderón, using analytic functions on a strip and producing spaces [X0, X1]θ. Both methods yield intermediate spaces on which any operator bounded on both endpoints remains bounded.

Applications

Interpolation spaces provide the natural setting for Sobolev and Besov spaces, which arise as real interpolation spaces between Lp and Sobolev spaces, and they are fundamental tools in the theory of partial differential equations, harmonic analysis and approximation theory.
