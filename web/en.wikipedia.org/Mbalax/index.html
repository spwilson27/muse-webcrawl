Mbalax

Stylistic originsWolof sabar drumming, Afro-Cuban music, Western pop and rock
Cultural origins1970s, Senegal and the Gambia
Typical instrumentsSabar drums, tama (talking drum), guitar, keyboards, drum kit

Mbalax (or mbalakh) is the national popular dance music of Senegal and the Gambia. It is a fusion of Western popular music and dance forms — such as jazz, soul, Latin and rock — blended with sabar, the traditional drumming and dance music of the Wolof people.

History

Mbalax developed in the 1970s as Senegalese musicians sought to indigenize the Afro-Cuban and Western sounds that dominated Dakar nightclubs. The singer Youssou N'Dour is credited more than anyone with creating and popularising the modern form: with his band Étoile de Dakar and later Super Étoile, he foregrounded the sabar drum battery and the tama talking drum within a pop band format and sang in Wolof. Other major figures include Baaba Maal (who developed a more acoustic, Pulaar variant), Thione Seck and Omar Pène.

Characteristics

The music is built around complex, shifting rhythms played by a family of sabar drums, whose patterns traditionally accompany specific dances and can mimic the rhythms of Wolof speech. The tama is used to punctuate and to "speak" phrases. Songs often contain long percussion breaks during which dancers perform elaborate steps keyed to the drums.

Social role

Mbalax is deeply embedded in Senegalese social life, played at weddings, naming ceremonies and wrestling matches, and its lyrics frequently offer moral commentary, praise of patrons, and discussion of current events.
