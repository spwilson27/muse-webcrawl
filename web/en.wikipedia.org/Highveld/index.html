Highveld

The Highveld (Afrikaans: Hoëveld, 'high field') is the elevated eastern part of the South African inland plateau, lying mainly at altitudes between 1,500 and 2,100 metres above sea level. It covers large parts of the provinces of Gauteng, the eastern Free State, Mpumalanga, and portions of Limpopo, the Northern Cape, and KwaZulu-Natal, and extends into neighbouring countries.

Geography and climate

The Highveld is bounded in the east by the Great Escarpment, most dramatically the Drakensberg, and merges westward into the Bushveld and the Kalahari. It is characterized by wide, rolling grasslands, summer thunderstorms, and cold, dry winters with frequent frost. Major rivers, including the Vaal and the headwaters of the Orange and Limpopo, rise on the plateau.

Economy and settlement

The Highveld contains South Africa's economic heartland: the gold reefs of the Witwatersrand underlie Johannesburg and the surrounding Gauteng conurbation, South Africa's largest urban region. The eastern Highveld of Mpumalanga holds the country's principal coalfields, supplying Eskom's coal-fired power stations and the Sasol synfuels complexes at Secunda and Sasolburg. Agriculture is dominated by maize production in the "Maize Triangle", along with cattle and sheep farming.

Ecology

The region corresponds largely to the Highveld grassland ecoregion, a temperate grassland noted for its rich diversity of grasses and geophytic plants, and fauna such as the blue crane and aardvark. Much of the natural grassland has been transformed by agriculture, mining, and urbanization, and air pollution from coal burning is a significant environmental issue in the eastern Highveld.
