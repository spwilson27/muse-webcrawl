Solar mass

SymbolM☉
Value≈ 1.9885 × 1030 kg
DefinitionMass of the Sun

The solar mass (M☉) is a standard unit of mass in astronomy, equal to approximately 2 × 1030 kilograms, used to express the masses of stars, star clusters, and galaxies. It is roughly 333,000 times the mass of the Earth and about 1,047 times the mass of Jupiter.

Use

Stellar masses range from about 0.075 M☉ for the smallest red dwarfs to over 100 M☉ for the most massive stars. The mass of the Milky Way's central black hole, Sagittarius A*, is about 4 million solar masses, and whole galaxies may reach trillions of solar masses.

Value

The solar mass cannot be measured directly with high precision; it is derived from the measured product GM☉ (the heliocentric gravitational constant) divided by the gravitational constant G, whose uncertainty dominates.
