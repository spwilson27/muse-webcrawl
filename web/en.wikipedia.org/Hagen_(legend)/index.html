Hagen (legend)

Hagen (German: Hagen von Tronje; Old Norse: Högni) is a legendary warrior of the Burgundian royal court in the Germanic heroic tradition, best known from the Middle High German epic Nibelungenlied (c. 1200) and from Norse sources such as the Poetic Edda and Völsunga saga.

In the Nibelungenlied

In the Nibelungenlied, Hagen is a vassal and counsellor of King Gunther of Burgundy, depicted as grim, one-eyed, and fiercely loyal. He recognises the hero Siegfried, persuades Kriemhild to reveal the one vulnerable spot on Siegfried's body, and murders Siegfried during a hunt. Later he sinks the Nibelung treasure in the Rhine to keep it from Kriemhild, leads the Burgundians on their doomed journey to the court of Etzel (Attila), and is finally beheaded by Kriemhild with Siegfried's sword.

In Norse tradition

In the Norse versions Högni is a brother of Gunnar rather than a vassal, and he dies bravely in Atli's snake pit, laughing while his heart is cut out. Richard Wagner reshaped the character for his Ring cycle, making Hagen the son of the dwarf Alberich and the brooding architect of Siegfried's death.
