Civil law notary

A civil-law notary, or Latin notary, is a highly specialised lawyer in private practice appointed by the state in many civil law jurisdictions. Civil-law notaries draft and authenticate legal instruments — such as conveyances, wills, marriage contracts and articles of incorporation — which then carry special evidentiary and sometimes executory force as "authentic acts".

Role

Unlike the common-law notary public, whose functions are largely administrative (witnessing signatures and administering oaths), the civil-law notary is a legal professional who advises all parties impartially, verifies the legality and fairness of transactions, and keeps the originals of instruments in a protocol archive. Entry to the profession typically requires a law degree, specialist notarial training, and appointment to a limited number of state-controlled posts (numerus clausus) in countries such as France, Germany, Italy, Spain and much of Latin America.

Authentic acts

Documents executed before a civil-law notary are presumed authentic: courts accept their contents as proven without further evidence, and copies issued by the notary (expéditions) have the same force as originals. In many jurisdictions, notarised deeds can be enforced directly, like a court judgment, without litigation.

International organisation

National notariats of the Latin type cooperate through the International Union of Notaries (UINL), founded in 1948.
