Passengers of the RMS Titanic

The passengers of the RMS Titanic were the approximately 1,317 people who sailed aboard the ocean liner on her maiden voyage in April 1912, together with some 885 crew members. The ship struck an iceberg on 14 April 1912 and sank the following morning, with the loss of more than 1,500 lives.

Classes

Passengers travelled in first, second, or third class (steerage), reflecting the social stratification of the Edwardian era. First class included some of the wealthiest people of the age, among them John Jacob Astor IV, Benjamin Guggenheim, and Isidor and Ida Straus. Third class consisted largely of emigrants travelling to North America.

Survival

Survival rates differed sharply by class and sex: about 62 percent of first-class passengers survived, compared with roughly a quarter of third-class passengers. In total, about 706 people survived the sinking.

Notable passengers

Famous survivors included Margaret "Molly" Brown, the Countess of Rothes, and officer Charles Lightoller among the crew. Subsequent research, exhibitions, and the passenger lists maintained by archives have kept the individual stories central to the Titanic's cultural memory.
