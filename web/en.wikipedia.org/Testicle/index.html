Testicle

Diagram of the testicle, epididymis and spermatic cord

A testicle or testis (plural testes) is the male reproductive gonad in animals. In mammals, including humans, the paired testes are housed in the scrotum, where they produce sperm (spermatogenesis) and secrete androgens, principally testosterone.

Anatomy and function

Each testis is an oval organ suspended by the spermatic cord and covered by a fibrous capsule, the tunica albuginea. Internally it is divided into lobules containing seminiferous tubules, where sperm are produced with the support of Sertoli cells; between the tubules, Leydig cells produce testosterone. Sperm pass to the epididymis for maturation and storage, then via the vas deferens during ejaculation. Location outside the body cavity keeps the testes roughly 2–3 °C below core temperature, which is necessary for normal sperm production; the cremaster muscle adjusts their position in response to temperature.

Development and health

The testes develop in the abdomen and normally descend into the scrotum before birth; failure to descend (cryptorchidism) increases the risk of infertility and cancer. Significant conditions affecting the testicle include testicular torsion (a surgical emergency), epididymitis, varicocele, hydrocele, and testicular cancer, the most common cancer in young adult men, which is usually highly curable when detected early.
