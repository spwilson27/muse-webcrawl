Wee Cho Yaw (10 January 1929 – 3 February 2024) was a Singaporean banker and billionaire businessman. He was the chairman emeritus and honorary adviser of the United Overseas Bank (UOB), one of Southeast Asia's largest banks, and headed the bank for more than five decades.

Early life

Wee was born in Kinmen, Fujian, China, and moved with his family to Singapore as a child. His father, Wee Kheng Chiang, was a businessman who co-founded the United Chinese Bank in 1935, which later became United Overseas Bank.

Career

Wee joined the bank's board in 1958 and became managing director in 1960; he succeeded his father as chairman in 1974. Under his leadership UOB expanded across Asia through acquisitions, including Chung Khiaw Bank, Lee Wah Bank, Industrial & Commercial Bank, and banks in Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand and China. He stepped down as chairman in 2013, becoming chairman emeritus, and retired from the board in 2018.

Wealth and recognition

Wee was consistently ranked among Singapore's richest individuals, with a fortune estimated in the billions of US dollars. He received honorary doctorates and public service awards, and was known for philanthropy in education and the Chinese community.

Death

Wee died on 3 February 2024 at the age of 95. His son Wee Ee Cheong succeeded him as the bank's chief executive.
