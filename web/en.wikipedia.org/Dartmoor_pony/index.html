Dartmoor pony

Origin: Dartmoor, Devon, England
Height: not exceeding 127 cm (12.2 hands) for registered stock
Status: Rare breed (Rare Breeds Survival Trust watchlist)

The Dartmoor pony is one of the horse breeds that have lived on Dartmoor in Devon, England, for centuries, and is used for children's riding, showing and driving. Semi-feral herds still graze the moor, where ponies have been present since prehistoric times; a pony foal appears on Bronze Age artefacts found on Dartmoor.

History

Ponies were used in the medieval tin-mining industry and later worked in local mines and quarries. During the 19th and early 20th centuries many were sent to work as pit ponies in coal mines elsewhere in Britain. Crossbreeding with Shetland and other breeds, and wartime demand, reduced the pure population sharply. The Dartmoor Pony Society was founded in 1924, and a breed standard established; registered Dartmoors are strictly limited in height.

Characteristics

The breed is hardy, sure-footed and good-tempered, in colours including bay, brown, black, grey, chestnut and roan. Moorland herds today are much reduced in number, and the Dartmoor pony is listed as a rare breed; conservation grazing schemes use the ponies to manage moorland habitats.
