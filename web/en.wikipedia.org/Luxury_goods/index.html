Luxury goods

Typical personal luxury goods

Income elasticity: Greater than 1

Major groups: LVMH, Kering, Richemont

In economics, a luxury good (or up-market good) is a good for which demand increases more than proportionally as income rises, so that expenditure on the good becomes a greater proportion of overall spending. Luxury goods are in contrast to necessity goods, for which demand rises less than proportionally with income. The category includes high-end clothing and accessories, watches and jewellery, automobiles, wines and spirits, yachts, and premium services.

Characteristics

Luxury goods are typically associated with craftsmanship, heritage, scarcity, and strong brand identity. Some are described as Veblen goods, for which a higher price itself increases desirability as a signal of status (conspicuous consumption). Producers cultivate exclusivity through controlled distribution, flagship stores, and limited editions, and the industry is dominated by conglomerates — above all France's LVMH, the world's most valuable luxury group, alongside Kering and Richemont.

Market

The global market for personal luxury goods is worth several hundred billion euros annually, with China and the United States the largest consumer bases. The industry periodically faces challenges from counterfeiting, shifting tastes toward experiences and "quiet luxury", sustainability concerns, and the rise of resale platforms for pre-owned goods.
