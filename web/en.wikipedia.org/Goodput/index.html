Goodput

FieldComputer networking
MeasuresUseful application-level data delivered per unit time

In computer networking, goodput (a portmanteau of good and throughput) is the application-level throughput: the number of useful bits delivered to a destination per unit time, excluding protocol overhead, headers, and retransmitted data.

Definition and differences

Whereas throughput (or gross bit rate) counts all data transferred over a link or connection, goodput counts only the payload that is new and useful to the application. For example, in a file transfer over TCP/IP, goodput corresponds to the rate at which the file's bytes accumulate at the receiver; bytes consumed by IP and TCP headers, by retransmissions after packet loss, and by acknowledgements do not contribute. Goodput is therefore always less than or equal to throughput.

Factors affecting goodput

Goodput is reduced by protocol overhead at each layer, retransmissions caused by loss or errors, congestion-control behaviour, flow control, and encryption padding. Measuring goodput requires counting distinct application data delivered over a chosen interval, often with tools that timestamp received payload at the application layer.
