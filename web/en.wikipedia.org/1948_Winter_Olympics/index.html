The 1948 Winter Olympics, officially known as the V Olympic Winter Games, were held from 30 January to 8 February 1948 in St. Moritz, Switzerland. They were the first Winter Olympics held after a 12-year break caused by the Second World War, and St. Moritz — which had hosted in 1928 — was chosen partly because neutral Switzerland's infrastructure had survived the war intact.

Highlights

Twenty-eight nations and 669 athletes competed in 22 events. Germany and Japan were not invited because of their roles in the war. Sweden, Norway, and Switzerland each won ten medals to tie atop the medal table. Canadian figure skater Barbara Ann Scott won gold, the United States won the four-man bobsleigh, and skeleton returned to the programme on the Cresta Run for the last time until 2002.

Context

The Games marked the resumption of the Olympic movement after the cancelled 1940 and 1944 editions, and were followed later in 1948 by the Summer Olympics in London. Compare the 1932 Winter Olympics in Lake Placid.
