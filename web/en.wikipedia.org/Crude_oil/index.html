Crude oil

Crude oil
Also called: Petroleum (unrefined)
Benchmarks: Brent, WTI, Dubai
Unit: Barrel (159 litres)

Crude oil, or unrefined petroleum, is a naturally occurring liquid mixture of hydrocarbons found in underground reservoirs. Refined, it yields gasoline, diesel, jet fuel, and feedstocks for plastics and petrochemicals, making it one of the world's most important commodities.

Properties and classification

Crudes are graded by density (API gravity — "light" vs "heavy") and sulfur content ("sweet" vs "sour"). Benchmark grades include Brent (North Sea), West Texas Intermediate (WTI), and Dubai/Oman. Prices are quoted per 42-gallon barrel.

Formation and production

Crude oil formed over millions of years from buried organic matter subjected to heat and pressure. Major producers include the United States, Saudi Arabia, and Russia; the OPEC cartel coordinates output among many exporters. Extraction ranges from conventional wells to oil sands and offshore platforms, and the industry faces long-term pressure from decarbonization.
