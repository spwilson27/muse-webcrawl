Gacrux — Gamma Crucis (γ Crucis) — is the third-brightest star in the southern constellation of Crux (the Southern Cross) and the nearest red giant to the Sun. Its name is a modern contraction of "Gamma" and "Crux", coined by the astronomer Elijah Hinsdale Burritt in the nineteenth century. With an apparent magnitude of about 1.63, it marks the top of the cross's upright.

Properties

Gacrux is a red giant of spectral type M3.5 III, around 88 light-years from Earth. It has roughly 1.5 times the mass of the Sun but has expanded to some 120 times the Sun's radius, radiating over a thousand times the solar luminosity from a cool surface of about 3,600 K. It is a semi-regular variable star whose brightness fluctuates slightly, and it has likely exhausted the helium in its core, evolving toward the asymptotic giant branch.

Companion

A companion star of about sixth magnitude lies roughly two arcminutes away; it is a more distant background object rather than a true binary companion, though the pairing makes Gacrux an attractive target for binocular observation. Because Crux is a far-southern constellation, Gacrux is invisible from most northern temperate latitudes.

In culture

Gacrux appears in the national flags of Australia, New Zealand, Papua New Guinea, and Samoa as part of the Southern Cross, and in the flag of Brazil, where it represents the state of Bahia.
