Indenture

An indenture is a legal contract between two or more parties. Historically the word referred to a deed written in duplicate on a single sheet and cut apart along a wavy or "indented" line, so that the two halves could later be matched to prove authenticity.

Indentured servitude

Indentures are best known from indentured servitude, in which a person bound themselves to work for a fixed term of years — often four to seven — in exchange for passage to a colony, maintenance and eventual freedom dues. Such contracts brought many migrants to the American colonies in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries.

Apprenticeship

Apprentices were likewise bound by indentures setting out the master's duty to teach a trade and the apprentice's obligations of service and obedience.

Modern use

In modern finance, an indenture is the formal agreement governing the terms of a bond issue between the issuer and a trustee acting for bondholders.
