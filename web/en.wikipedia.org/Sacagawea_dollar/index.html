The Sacagawea dollar (also known as the "golden dollar") is a United States dollar coin introduced in 2000, replacing the Susan B. Anthony dollar. It depicts Sacagawea, the Shoshone woman who guided the Lewis and Clark Expedition, carrying her infant son Jean Baptiste Charbonneau. The coin is golden in colour because of its manganese brass cladding, but contains no gold. Its edge was smooth from 2000 to 2008 and lettered thereafter.

The obverse portrait of Sacagawea was modelled by Shoshone artist Randy'L He-Dow Teton.

Design and issue

Authorised by the United States $1 Coin Act of 1997, the coin's obverse was designed by sculptor Glenna Goodacre, who used a contemporary Shoshone college student as her model since no image of Sacagawea exists. The reverse shows a soaring bald eagle encircled by 17 stars for the states of 1804. Despite a large promotional campaign, the coin failed to circulate widely in the United States — as had its predecessor — though it became common in Ecuador and other dollarised economies where US coins are used.

Native American $1 Coin Program

Beginning in 2009, the reverse has been redesigned annually under the Native American $1 Coin Act to commemorate contributions of Native American tribes and individuals, with themes including agriculture, the Haudenosaunee Confederacy, Sequoyah's Cherokee syllabary, and Native Americans in the space programme, while Goodacre's obverse portrait remains.
