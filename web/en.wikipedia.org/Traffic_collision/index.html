A traffic collision, also known as a motor vehicle collision or car crash, occurs when a vehicle collides with another vehicle, a pedestrian, an animal, or a stationary obstruction. Traffic collisions often result in injury, disability, death, and property damage as well as financial costs to both society and the individuals involved. Road traffic injuries are a leading cause of death worldwide, killing about 1.19 million people per year according to the World Health Organization.

Terminology

Safety advocates argue against the word "accident" because it implies an unavoidable event, whereas most collisions are caused by identifiable human factors. The term "crash" is preferred by many road safety organizations and some style guides, while "collision" is used in many legal and technical contexts.

Causes

Human factors contribute to the overwhelming majority of collisions. These include speeding, driving under the influence of alcohol or drugs, distracted driving (such as mobile phone use), fatigue, and failure to obey traffic signals. Vehicle factors include brake or tire failure and poor maintenance, while environmental factors include road design, weather, and visibility. Studies of collision causation, such as the British and American studies of the 1970s and later, consistently find driver error implicated in the great majority of cases.

Types

Common collision types include head-on collisions, rear-end collisions, side-impact (T-bone) collisions, rollovers, and single-vehicle run-off-road crashes. Head-on collisions and rollovers tend to have the highest fatality rates, while rear-end collisions are among the most common overall.

Prevention

Preventive measures operate under the "safe system" approach: safer road design (divided highways, roundabouts, protected crossings), vehicle safety technology (seat belts, airbags, electronic stability control, automatic emergency braking), enforcement of speed and drink-driving laws, licensing and education, and post-crash emergency care. Global initiatives such as the Decade of Action for Road Safety aim to halve road deaths and injuries.

Legal consequences

Drivers involved in collisions are generally required to stop, exchange information, and report the event to police where there is injury or significant damage. Liability may lead to criminal charges (such as dangerous or impaired driving), civil claims for damages, and insurance consequences.
