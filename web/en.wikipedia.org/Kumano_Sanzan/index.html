Kumano Sanzan

Kumano Sanzan refers to the three grand Shinto shrines of the Kumano region in the Kii Mountains of Japan's Kii Peninsula: Kumano Hongū Taisha, Kumano Hayatama Taisha, and Kumano Nachi Taisha. Together with the pilgrimage routes connecting them, they are inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

The three shrines

Kumano Hongū Taisha, in the mountains near the Kumano River, was the original destination of pilgrims; its pavilions stand at Oyunohara after floods moved the shrine in 1889. Kumano Hayatama Taisha stands at the mouth of the Kumano River in Shingū. Kumano Nachi Taisha, near Nachi Falls, Japan's tallest waterfall, is closely associated with the Buddhist temple Seiganto-ji.

Pilgrimage

The Kumano Kodō pilgrimage routes were used by emperors, aristocrats, and commoners from the Heian period onward; retired Emperor Go-Shirakawa made the pilgrimage many times. The cult blended Shinto kami worship with Pure Land Buddhism, identifying the Kumano deities with buddhas and bodhisattvas.
