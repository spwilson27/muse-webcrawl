Personality psychology

Personality psychology is a branch of psychology that examines individual differences in characteristic patterns of thinking, feeling and behaving. It studies both how people differ and the psychological processes that make a person an integrated whole.

Approaches

Major traditions include trait theory, psychoanalytic theory, humanistic psychology (Maslow, Rogers), social-cognitive theory (Bandura, Mischel) and biological approaches. The situationist critique, emphasising behaviour's dependence on context, prompted the person–situation debate and interactionist resolutions.

Traits and measurement

The dominant modern model is the Big Five: openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness and neuroticism, derived from lexical studies and factor analysis. Personality is assessed by self-report inventories such as the NEO-PI-R, observer ratings and behavioural measures; twin studies indicate substantial heritability of traits.
