Svalbard Global Seed Vault

The Svalbard Global Seed Vault (Norwegian: Svalbard globale frøhvelv) is a secure backup facility for the world's crop diversity, located on the Norwegian archipelago of Svalbard, about 1,300 kilometres (810 mi) from the North Pole. Carved 130 metres into a sandstone mountain on Spitsbergen near Longyearbyen, it stores duplicate ("safety backup") samples of seeds held in gene banks worldwide, so that collections lost to war, disaster, equipment failure or funding cuts can be re-established.

History

Construction began in 2006, funded by the Norwegian government; the vault opened on 26 February 2008. It is managed under a tripartite agreement between the Norwegian Ministry of Agriculture and Food, the Nordic Genetic Resource Center (NordGen), and the Crop Trust. The Nordic gene bank had already stored backup seeds in an abandoned coal mine on Svalbard since 1984.

Storage and capacity

Seeds are stored in sealed three-layer foil packages inside boxes, at −18 °C (0 °F), in vault rooms protected by permafrost, which keeps temperatures well below freezing even if refrigeration fails. The vault has capacity for about 4.5 million seed samples — potentially some 2.5 billion seeds. By the mid-2020s it held well over one million samples from depositors in most countries. Depositors retain ownership of their seeds under the "black box" principle: only the depositor may withdraw its material.

Use

The first major withdrawal came in 2015, when researchers of the International Center for Agricultural Research in the Dry Areas (ICARDA) retrieved seeds to re-establish a collection moved from Aleppo, Syria, because of the civil war, at new sites in Lebanon and Morocco; many of the regenerated seeds were redeposited in 2017 and 2019. Water intrusion into the entrance tunnel from permafrost melt during an unusually warm 2016 prompted Norway to fund a major waterproofing and upgrade programme, completed in 2019; the seed chambers themselves were never breached.
