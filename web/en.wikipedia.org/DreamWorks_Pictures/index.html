DreamWorks Pictures

Founded: October 12, 1994
Founders: Steven Spielberg, Jeffrey Katzenberg, David Geffen
Headquarters: Universal City, California, U.S.
Parent: Amblin Partners

DreamWorks Pictures (also known as DreamWorks SKG) is an American film studio and distribution label of Amblin Partners. It was originally founded on October 12, 1994, as a live-action and animation film studio by Steven Spielberg, Jeffrey Katzenberg, and David Geffen (together, SKG), of which they owned 72%. The studio formerly distributed its own and third-party films, and it has produced or distributed more than ten films with box-office grosses of more than $100 million each.

History

DreamWorks was founded with ambitions spanning film, animation, television, and music. Its animation division produced The Prince of Egypt (1998), Shrek (2001) and many other hits before being spun off as the separate public company DreamWorks Animation in 2004. The live-action studio won three consecutive Academy Awards for Best Picture — American Beauty (1999), Gladiator (2000), and A Beautiful Mind (2001) — and produced successes such as Saving Private Ryan (1998, with Paramount), Minority Report (2002), and War of the Worlds (2005).

Hit by debt, the founders sold the studio to Viacom, Paramount's parent, in 2006. Spielberg and CEO Stacey Snider relaunched DreamWorks as an independent company in 2008 with financing from India's Reliance Group, distributing through Disney (2009–2015) and then Universal. In 2015 the studio became part of Amblin Partners, with DreamWorks Pictures continuing as its adult drama label — releasing films such as Bridge of Spies (2015), The Post (2017), and 1917 (2019).

Logo

The studio's logo — a boy sitting on a crescent moon, fishing — was conceived by Spielberg and painted by artist Robert Hunt; it is one of the most recognizable studio logos in modern cinema.
