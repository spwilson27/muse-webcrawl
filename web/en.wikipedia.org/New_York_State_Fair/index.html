New York State Fair

The Great New York State Fair is a 13-day showcase of agriculture, entertainment, education and technology held each August on the 375-acre New York State Fairgrounds in the town of Geddes, near Syracuse, New York. First held in Syracuse in 1841, it is among the oldest and largest state fairs in the United States, regularly drawing around a million visitors.

History

The fair was founded by the New York State Agricultural Society and rotated among cities including Albany, Buffalo, Rochester and Syracuse before settling permanently in Syracuse in 1890, on land that became the fairgrounds. Except for wartime and the 2020 pandemic cancellation, it has run continuously into the present day.

Attractions

The fair features livestock shows and agricultural competitions, a midway, national touring musical acts at its concert venues, and New York food and drink vendors. Signature sights include the annual butter sculpture — hundreds of pounds of butter carved into a themed artwork in the Dairy Products Building — sand sculptures, and the birthing centre where farm animals give birth during the fair.

Recent developments

A major state-funded revitalisation beginning in 2015 rebuilt gates, the midway and exhibition spaces and added an RV park and tram system. Attendance set records in the late 2010s, exceeding 1.3 million.
