Dirk

A dirk is a long-bladed thrusting dagger. Historically, it was a personal sidearm used by officers of the Scottish Highlands and by naval officers, among others. The dirk was used as a fighting knife and as a ceremonial weapon; the term has also been applied historically to long daggers in general (such as the "ballock dirk" forebear) and, in Scotland, it is closely associated with Highland dress, in which an ornate dirk is worn at the belt.

History

The dirk developed from the medieval ballock dagger. By the early 17th century it had become characteristic in Scotland as the companion weapon to the basket-hilted broadsword and targe: the dirk was gripped in the shield hand and used at close quarters. Early dirks were often made from the broken blades of swords and had simple wooden hilts carved with interlace patterns. Following the Jacobite rising of 1745 and the Disarming Acts, dirks — when permitted at all — became increasingly ornamental.

Naval dirks became popular sidearms for midshipmen and officers in the Royal Navy and other navies from the late 18th century through the 19th, worn with dress uniform as symbols of rank.

Scottish dirk

The traditional Scottish dirk has a single-edged blade roughly 30 cm (12 inches) long, often with fuller and sometimes a serrated or notched spine near the hilt. The hilt is typically of dark wood (bog oak or heather root) carved with basket-weave interlace and studded with brass or silver nails; pommel caps may be set with a cairngorm or other stone. The leather scabbard frequently holds two small implements — a by-knife and a by-fork — in separate pockets. Today the dirk is worn with Highland dress on formal occasions, especially by pipers and officers of Scottish regiments. The smaller sgian-dubh, tucked into the hose top, is its miniature cousin.
