Regeneration (biology)

In biology, regeneration is the process of renewal, restoration, and tissue growth that makes genomes, cells, organisms, and ecosystems resilient to natural fluctuations or events that cause damage. Every species is capable of regeneration, from bacteria to humans, though the capacity varies enormously.

Examples

Planarian flatworms can regrow an entire body from a fragment; hydra continually renew their tissues from stem cells. Many lizards shed and regrow tails, and axolotls and other salamanders regenerate limbs, spinal cord, heart and parts of the brain. Deer regrow antlers annually — the fastest-growing tissue in mammals. Humans regenerate liver tissue, skin, blood and bone, but cannot regrow limbs.

Mechanisms

Regeneration may proceed by epimorphosis, in which a blastema of dedifferentiated cells forms at the wound and redevelops, or by morphallaxis, remodelling existing tissue. Understanding these processes is a central goal of regenerative medicine.
