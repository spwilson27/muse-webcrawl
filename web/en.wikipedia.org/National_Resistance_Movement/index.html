National Resistance Movement

The National Resistance Movement (NRM) is the ruling political party in Uganda. It originated as the political wing of the National Resistance Army (NRA), the guerrilla force led by Yoweri Museveni that seized power in January 1986 after the five-year Ugandan Bush War.

History

After taking power, the NRM governed under a "no-party" or "Movement" system in which candidates stood for election on individual merit rather than party labels; the system was defended as suited to a society divided along ethnic and religious lines. In a 2005 referendum, Ugandans voted to restore multi-party politics, and the NRM registered as a political party. It has won every presidential and parliamentary election since, with Museveni re-elected in 1996, 2001, 2006, 2011, 2016 and 2021, amid opposition allegations of electoral irregularities.

Ideology

The NRM's original programme was the Ten-Point Programme, emphasising democracy, security, national unity, economic recovery and the elimination of corruption. In practice the party is a broad, dominant-party organisation associated with Museveni's leadership, economic liberalisation and, its critics say, democratic backsliding.
