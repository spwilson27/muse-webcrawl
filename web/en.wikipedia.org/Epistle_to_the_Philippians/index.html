Epistle to the Philippians

The Epistle to the Philippians is the eleventh book of the New Testament. It is a letter written by the Apostle Paul to the Christian community at Philippi in Macedonia, a church Paul had founded on his second missionary journey. Written while Paul was imprisoned, probably in the late 50s or early 60s AD, it is among the most personal and joyful of his letters.

The ruins of Philippi, the city whose church received Paul's letter

Content

Paul thanks the Philippians for their financial support, reports on his imprisonment, and urges the community to unity and humility. Chapter 2 contains the famous "Christ hymn" (Philippians 2:5–11), which describes Christ emptying himself (kenosis), taking the form of a servant, and being exalted by God — a passage central to later Christology. Paul also warns against rival teachers, commends Timothy and Epaphroditus, and exhorts the readers to "rejoice in the Lord always" (4:4), even in suffering.

Authenticity and setting

Philippians is one of the seven Pauline letters generally accepted as authentic by critical scholars. The place of writing is debated — Rome, Ephesus, and Caesarea have all been proposed — as is whether the letter was composed as a single work or combined from several shorter letters.
