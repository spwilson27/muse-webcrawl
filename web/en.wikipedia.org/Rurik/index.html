Rurik (also spelled Ruryk or Ryurik; Old Norse Hrøríkʀ; died c. 879) was a semi-legendary Varangian chieftain of the Rus' who, according to the earliest East Slavic chronicle, was invited in 862 to rule over the Slavic and Finnic tribes of the north-western lands centred on Novgorod. He is traditionally regarded as the founder of the Rurikid dynasty, which ruled Kievan Rus' and its successor principalities, and ultimately the Tsardom of Russia, until the extinction of the senior line with Feodor I in 1598.

The chronicle account

The principal source for Rurik's life is the Primary Chronicle (Tale of Bygone Years), compiled in Kiev in the early 12th century. Under the year 862 it relates that the Chuds, Slavs, Krivichs and Ves, having driven out their Varangian overlords and fallen into disorder, resolved to invite the Rus' Varangians to rule them: "Our land is great and rich, but there is no order in it. Come to rule and reign over us." Three brothers responded: Rurik settled in Novgorod (in some versions first at Ladoga), Sineus at Beloozero, and Truvor at Izborsk. Within two years the younger brothers died, and Rurik ruled alone, distributing towns among his followers. The episode is known in historiography as the "calling of the Varangians."

Historicity

Historians have long debated whether Rurik was a real person. Some have identified him with Rorik of Dorestad, a Danish Viking active in Frisia in the mid-9th century, though the identification is unproven. Archaeological evidence confirms a substantial Scandinavian presence at Staraya Ladoga and in the region by the 860s, and the historically attested rulers Igor — said in the chronicle to be Rurik's son — and his successors anchor the dynasty in documented history by the early 10th century. The story of the invitation has been central to the "Normanist controversy" over the role of Scandinavians in the founding of the Rus' state.

Legacy

The dynasty founded by Rurik ruled for over seven centuries. His descendants included Vladimir the Great, Yaroslav the Wise, Alexander Nevsky and Ivan the Terrible. Rurik's emblem, the trident-like tamga now called the tryzub, became the national emblem of Ukraine. In 1862 Russia celebrated the millennium of Rurik's supposed arrival with the Millennium of Russia monument in Novgorod.
