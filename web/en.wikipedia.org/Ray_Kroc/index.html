Ray Kroc

Raymond Albert Kroc (October 5, 1902 – January 14, 1984) was an American businessman who built McDonald's into the world's largest fast-food chain. As a travelling salesman of Multimixer milkshake machines, he visited the McDonald brothers' San Bernardino restaurant in 1954, became their franchising agent, and opened his own restaurant in Des Plaines, Illinois, in April 1955.

Building McDonald's

Kroc founded the McDonald's Corporation and, on the advice of Harry Sonneborn, its Franchise Realty Corporation, which owned the land beneath franchised restaurants — the financial engine of the business. In 1961 he bought out brothers Richard and Maurice McDonald for $2.7 million. Under his leadership the chain grew to thousands of outlets worldwide on the principles of quality, service, cleanliness, and value (QSC&V).

Later life

Kroc owned the San Diego Padres baseball team from 1974 until his death. With his third wife, Joan, he was a major philanthropist, funding work on alcoholism and other causes. His story, including the disputed credit for "founding" McDonald's, is dramatised in the 2016 film The Founder.
