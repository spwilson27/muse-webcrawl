Tonsil

The tonsils are lymphoid tissue masses that form a defensive ring (Waldeyer's ring) around the entrance to the pharynx. The term usually refers to the palatine tonsils, the two oval masses visible at the back of the throat on either side of the uvula.

Anatomy and function

Besides the palatine tonsils, the ring includes the pharyngeal tonsil (adenoids), the lingual tonsils at the base of the tongue and the tubal tonsils. As part of the mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue, tonsils sample inhaled and swallowed antigens and help initiate immune responses, a function most active in childhood.

Disorders

Tonsillitis — inflammation caused by viral or bacterial infection, classically group A streptococcus — produces sore throat, fever and swollen tonsils sometimes coated with exudate. Recurrent infections or airway obstruction from enlarged tonsils may lead to tonsillectomy, once one of the most common operations performed on children. Tonsil stones (tonsilloliths) can form in tonsillar crypts and cause bad breath.
