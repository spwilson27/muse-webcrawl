Sinigang (in relation to lechon)

Sinigang is the quintessential Filipino sour soup, and in the architecture of a Filipino feast it serves as the natural counterpoint to lechon. Where lechon is rich, fatty and celebratory, sinigang is light, brothy and sharply sour — the two are frequently served together so the tamarind broth can cut through the roasted pork's richness.

The pairing on the Filipino table

Traditional Filipino meals balance flavours across dishes: a dry or fried main, a soup, rice, and sawsawan (dipping sauces). A fiesta spread anchored by lechon will almost always include a soup such as sinigang na baboy (pork sinigang) or a clear bulalo, with the souring agent — tamarind, guava, calamansi, or kamias — providing the acidity that Filipino palates expect alongside fatty meats.

Lechon sinigang

Leftover lechon meat and bones are also cooked directly into sinigang, giving the broth a smoky depth; the crisp skin, however, is kept aside, since simmering would soften it. Restaurants serving Cebu lechon commonly offer sinigang or the Visayan sour soup sinuglaw-adjacent broths as accompaniments.
