Lechon in Philippine cuisine

Lechon is the centrepiece dish of Filipino celebrations and arguably the most emblematic preparation in Philippine cuisine. Derived from the Spanish word for suckling pig, the Filipino lechon is a whole pig stuffed with lemongrass, garlic, onions and spices, then roasted slowly over charcoal on a bamboo spit until the skin turns amber-crisp and the meat stays juicy.

Role in Filipino food culture

No major fiesta, wedding, or town festival is considered complete without a lechon. Cebu is famed for its version — seasoned so thoroughly that it is traditionally eaten without liver sauce — while Manila-style lechon is served with a sweet liver-based sarsa. Leftovers are rarely wasted: they are chopped and simmered into paksiw na lechon in vinegar and liver sauce, or sizzled as lechon kawali and sisig.

The dish exemplifies the Filipino layering of influences — pre-colonial roasting, Spanish nomenclature and occasion, and local aromatics. In 2009, the travel writer Anthony Bourdain famously called Filipino lechon "the best pig ever," cementing its international reputation.
