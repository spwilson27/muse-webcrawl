Tagalog language and Philippine cuisine

The Tagalog language, basis of the national language Filipino, supplies much of the everyday vocabulary of Philippine cuisine. Dish names across the country — adobo (from Spanish, but naturalised in Tagalog usage), sinigang, pinakbet (borrowed from Ilocano), ulam (viand), kanin (cooked rice), sawsawan (dipping sauce) — circulate nationally through Tagalog-based media centred on Manila.

Food vocabulary

Tagalog encodes the structure of the Filipino meal: almusal (breakfast), tanghalian (lunch), hapunan (dinner) and merienda (snacks, from Spanish). Cooking verbs are precise — gisa (sauté in garlic and onion), paksiw (cook in vinegar), sigang (to sour a broth, root of sinigang), ihaw (grill) — and flavour words like maasim (sour), maalat (salty) and matamis (sweet) define the cuisine's flavour axes.

Loanwords and exchange

Tagalog food terms preserve the cuisine's history: Hokkien loans (pancit, lumpia, taho, siopao), Spanish loans (lechon, relleno, merienda), Nahuatl words carried by the galleon trade (tsokolate, kamote from camotl via Spanish), and Malay cognates shared with the wider Austronesian world (suka vinegar, gata coconut milk).
