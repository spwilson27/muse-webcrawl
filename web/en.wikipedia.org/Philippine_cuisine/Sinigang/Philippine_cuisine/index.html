Sinigang in Philippine cuisine

Sinigang is one of the most beloved dishes of Philippine cuisine and is often ranked alongside adobo as a candidate national dish. It is a sour, savoury soup or stew characterised by its broth soured with tamarind or other fruits, filled with meat or seafood and vegetables such as kangkong (water spinach), labanos (radish), okra, eggplant and tomatoes.

Souring agents

The classic souring agent is sampalok (tamarind), but regional variants use guava (for sinigang sa bayabas), calamansi, kamias, batwan, santol, or unripe mango. The dish's pronounced sourness — prized in its own right, not as a background note — is considered a hallmark of the indigenous Filipino palate, predating Spanish colonisation.

Variants

Sinigang na baboy (pork) is the most common form, followed by shrimp (sinigang na hipon), milkfish, salmon head, and beef. The Visayan sinigang family overlaps with tinola and the sour fish soup paklay-adjacent preparations in Mindanao. In a typical meal sinigang provides the soup course and a foil to fried or roasted mains such as lechon.
