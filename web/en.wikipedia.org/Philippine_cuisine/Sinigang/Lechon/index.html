Lechon (in relation to sinigang)

Lechon, the whole roasted pig of Filipino fiestas, is most often eaten alongside sour preparations — pre-eminently sinigang — because Filipino meal composition demands a sour, liquid counterpoint to rich roasted meat. From the perspective of sinigang, lechon is its richest partner: the soup's tamarind acidity refreshes the palate between bites of fatty pork and crackling skin.

Balance of flavours

Filipino food writers describe the pairing as an expression of the national taste for contrast: tamis (sweet), asim (sour), alat (salty) and fat in deliberate balance. A bowl of sinigang na baboy or a clear sour broth is served at lechon feasts much as pickles accompany barbecue elsewhere; vinegar-based dipping sauces for the lechon itself extend the same logic to the plate level.

Second lives

The relationship is also sequential: lechon leftovers re-enter the kitchen as paksiw or simmered in sinigang-style broths, while lechon bones flavour soups. In Cebu, where lechon is seasoned to stand alone, sour soups and suka (spiced vinegar) remain the standard accompaniments.
