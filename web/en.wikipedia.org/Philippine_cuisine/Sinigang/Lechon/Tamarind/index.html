Tamarind in sinigang

Tamarind (sampalok) is the classic souring agent of sinigang, the sour soup of Philippine cuisine. The unripe fruit, and sometimes the young leaves, are simmered in the broth to give sinigang its characteristic tart flavour, distinguishing it from blander boiled dishes and from adobo, which is soured with vinegar.

Use in cooking

In traditional preparation, fresh unripe tamarind pods are boiled until soft, then mashed and strained so the sour pulp flavours the broth, which typically contains pork, shrimp or milkfish together with tomatoes, onion, labanos (daikon), kangkong (water spinach), sitaw (long beans) and eggplant or okra. Regional and household variants substitute other souring fruits, including guava, kamias, calamansi, santol and green mango. Modern households frequently use powdered sinigang mixes based on tamarind extract.

Cultural significance

Sinigang is frequently cited as one of the most beloved Filipino comfort foods, and its pronounced sourness is often held up as a defining taste of Philippine cooking.
