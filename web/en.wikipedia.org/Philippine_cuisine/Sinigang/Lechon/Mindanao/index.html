Lechon in Mindanao

Lechon in Mindanao refers to the traditions of whole-roasted pig cookery in the southern Philippines, where the dish is a centrepiece of fiestas, weddings and other celebrations among the island's Christian communities. Like the lechon of the Visayas and Luzon, a whole pig is seasoned, skewered on a bamboo pole and roasted slowly over charcoal until the skin becomes crisp and golden.

Regional character

In cities such as Cagayan de Oro and Davao, lechon is commonly served with a vinegar-based dipping sauce rather than the sweet liver-based sauce popular in Luzon, and stuffings of lemongrass, garlic, onions and local herbs are typical. Iligan City is noted for its lechon, often accompanied by dinuguan (pork blood stew) and rice cooked for large gatherings.

Cultural notes

Because Mindanao also has a large Muslim population, for whom pork is prohibited, lechon is associated specifically with Christian and lumad festivities, while Muslim Mindanaoan cuisine centres on dishes such as pater, rendang-style beef and seafood. Lechon remains one of the most recognisable celebratory foods in the Philippines.
