Sinigang

TypeSoup or stew
Place of originPhilippines
Serving temperatureHot
Main ingredientsMeat or seafood, tamarind, tomatoes, onion, radish, water spinach, taro

Sinigang is a Filipino soup or stew characterised by its sour and savoury taste. It is most often associated with tamarind (sampalok), although other souring agents such as guava, calamansi, bilimbi (kamias), green mango or santol may be used. It is one of the most popular dishes in Philippine cuisine and is frequently cited as a national dish of the Philippines.

Preparation

Sinigang is typically made with pork, beef, shrimp, milkfish (bangus) or salmon, simmered in the sour broth with tomatoes, onion, garlic, labanos (daikon radish), gabi (taro), kangkong (water spinach), sitaw (yardlong beans), okra, eggplant and siling haba (long green chillies). It is seasoned with fish sauce (patis) and served hot with steamed rice. The sourness is adjusted to taste; regional and household versions vary widely. Modern cooks commonly use commercial tamarind soup base mixes, although fresh souring fruits are preferred by traditionalists.

Variants

Well-known variants include sinigang na baboy (pork), sinigang na hipon (shrimp), sinigang na bangus (milkfish), sinigang sa bayabas (soured with guava), sinigang sa miso (with fermented soybean paste) and the Visayan sinigang na isda. A similar sour soup, sinampalukan, uses tamarind leaves, while the Kapampangan buridibud and Ilocano dishes reflect regional takes on sour broths.

Cultural significance

Sinigang regularly ranks among the Philippines' favourite comfort foods, and in 2021 the food website TasteAtlas rated the vegetable version among the world's best vegetable soups. Its refreshing sourness is considered well suited to the tropical climate, stimulating the appetite in hot weather.
