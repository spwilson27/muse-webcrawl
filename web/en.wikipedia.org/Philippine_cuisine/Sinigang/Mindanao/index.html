Mindanao

LocationSouthern Philippines
Area97,530 km² (approx.)
RankSecond-largest island of the Philippines; 19th largest in the world
Largest cityDavao City

Mindanao is the second-largest island in the Philippines, after Luzon, and the southernmost major island group of the archipelago. It is nicknamed the "Land of Promise" for its fertile land and rich natural resources. Its food traditions — including sour soups related to sinigang — reflect the island's Malay and Muslim heritage.

Geography

Mindanao is mountainous and irregularly shaped, dominated by volcanic ranges and the country's highest peak, Mount Apo (2,954 m). Large river systems, including the Rio Grande de Mindanao and the Agusan River, drain broad fertile plains. The island is relatively spared from the typhoons that strike northern Philippines and is a major producer of bananas, pineapples, coconuts, rice, and corn.

People and culture

The island is home to Christian settlers, indigenous Lumad peoples, and Muslim Moro groups such as the Maranao, Maguindanao, and Tausug, whose sultanates resisted Spanish colonization for centuries. Moro cuisine makes prominent use of turmeric, coconut, and spices in dishes such as tiyula itum and rendang-influenced beef stews, distinct from the sour-and-vinegar profile of northern Filipino cooking.

History

Islam arrived in the fourteenth century, and the Sultanates of Sulu and Maguindanao became regional powers. Spain established footholds only late, and the United States assumed control after 1898. The twentieth century saw large-scale Christian migration, land disputes, and a long Moro insurgency addressed in part by the creation of the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao in 2019.
