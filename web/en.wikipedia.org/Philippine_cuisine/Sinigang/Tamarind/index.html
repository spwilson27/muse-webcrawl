Tamarind

KingdomPlantae
FamilyFabaceae
GenusTamarindus
SpeciesT. indica

Tamarind (Tamarindus indica) is a leguminous tree bearing edible fruit that is indigenous to tropical Africa and widely naturalized in Asia and Latin America. The fruit pulp is a defining souring agent in many cuisines; in the Philippines, where it is called sampalok, it is the classic base of the soup sinigang.

Description

The tamarind tree grows slowly to heights of up to about 24 metres, with feathery evergreen foliage. Its fruit is a brown, pod-like indehiscent legume containing a soft, sticky pulp around hard seeds. Unripe pods are intensely sour and green; ripe pulp becomes sweeter and paste-like and is used in drinks, candies, sauces, and chutneys.

Culinary uses

Tamarind pulp flavors Worcestershire sauce, South Asian curries and chutneys, Mexican aguas frescas and candies, and Southeast Asian dishes such as pad Thai. In Philippine cooking, fresh green tamarind or tamarind leaves sour sinigang na baboy (pork), hipon (shrimp), or isda (fish), and commercial tamarind soup mixes are a household staple. The young leaves and flowers are also cooked as vegetables.

Other uses

Tamarind wood is durable and used for furniture and carving; the pulp has traditional medicinal uses as a laxative and for fevers, and tamarind seed polysaccharide has industrial applications in food and pharmaceuticals.
