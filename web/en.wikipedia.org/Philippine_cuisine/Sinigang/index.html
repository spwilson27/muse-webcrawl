Sinigang

Sinigang
TypeSoup / stew
Place of originPhilippines
Serving temperatureHot
Main ingredientsMeat or seafood, tamarind, tomatoes, onions, vegetables

Sinigang is a sour soup or stew native to the Philippines, characterized by its sour and savory flavor, most often associated with tamarind (sampalok), which gives the broth its distinctive tang. It is one of the most popular dishes in Filipino cuisine and is frequently cited as a national dish of the Philippines.

Ingredients and preparation

Sinigang is typically made with pork, beef, shrimp, milkfish (bangus), or salmon, simmered with tomatoes, onions, garlic, and a souring agent. Vegetables commonly include water spinach (kangkong), daikon radish (labanos), eggplant, okra, string beans, and taro (gabi), which thickens the broth. Fish sauce (patis) and finger chilies are added for seasoning.

Souring agents

Although tamarind is the classic souring agent, regional and modern variations use guava, calamansi, green mango, santol, bilimbi (kamias), or watermelon (sinigang sa pakwan). Commercial sinigang mixes have made the dish easy to prepare year-round.

Variants

Regional variants include sinigang na baboy (pork), sinigang na hipon (shrimp), and the Visayan linarang. The related dish sinampalukan uses tamarind leaves, while sinigang sa miso adds fermented soybean paste. In 2021 the food guide TasteAtlas rated sinigang among the world's best-rated vegetable soups.
