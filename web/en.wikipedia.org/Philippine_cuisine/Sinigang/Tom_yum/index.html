Tom yum

Tom yum (Thai: ต้มยำ) is a type of hot and sour Thai soup, usually cooked with shrimp — tom yum goong — and flavored with lemongrass, galangal, kaffir lime leaves, lime juice, fish sauce, and crushed chili peppers. It is one of the best-known dishes of Thai cuisine and is frequently compared with the sour soups of neighboring cuisines, such as the Filipino sinigang, which uses tamarind as its souring agent.

Preparation

The broth is built on a fragrant base of lemongrass, galangal, and makrut lime leaves simmered in stock, then seasoned with lime juice (added off the heat to preserve its brightness), fish sauce, and chilies. The two common styles are the clear broth (nam sai) and the creamy version (nam khon) enriched with evaporated milk or coconut milk and chili paste. Straw mushrooms and tomatoes are typical additions.

Varieties

Named by protein, the soup appears as tom yum goong (prawn), tom yum gai (chicken), tom yum pla (fish), and mixed seafood versions. Instant tom yum flavoring has spread to snacks, noodles, and fusion dishes across Southeast Asia.

Cultural significance

Tom yum goong is promoted as a national dish of Thailand and became emblematic of the country's culinary diplomacy. During the 1997 Asian financial crisis — which began in Thailand — the crisis was nicknamed the "Tom Yum Goong crisis" in Thai media.
