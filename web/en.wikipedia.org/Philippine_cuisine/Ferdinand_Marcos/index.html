Philippine cuisine in the Ferdinand Marcos era

The presidency of Ferdinand Marcos (1965–1986) shaped Philippine food culture through state banquets, agricultural policy, and the promotion of Filipino cuisine as an instrument of national identity. Marcos, an Ilocano, was known for personally favouring simple Ilocano dishes such as pinakbet and dinengdeng, even as official entertaining at Malacañang Palace showcased elaborate Filipino and international fare for visiting heads of state.

State promotion of Filipino food

First Lady Imelda Marcos presided over grand banquets intended to display Philippine culture, while government cultural programs elevated regional dishes as national heritage. The era's nutrition campaigns promoted rice substitutes during shortages, including the controversial promotion of rice–corn mixes and the "Nutribun" bread distributed in public schools under a government feeding program to combat child malnutrition.

Agriculture and food security

The Marcos administration's "Masagana 99" program pushed high-yield rice varieties from the International Rice Research Institute in Los Baños, briefly achieving rice self-sufficiency in the mid-1970s. Coconut and sugar — pillars of Filipino cooking — became instruments of crony-controlled monopolies under the regime, shaping prices and rural livelihoods until the government's fall in 1986.
