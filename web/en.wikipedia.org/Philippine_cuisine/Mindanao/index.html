Mindanao in Philippine cuisine

Mindanao, the southernmost major island of the Philippines, contributes some of the country's most distinctive foodways, shaped by its Moro (Muslim Filipino) peoples, Lumad indigenous communities, and settler populations from Luzon and the Visayas. Unlike the pork-centred cooking of the Christian north, traditional Moro cuisine is halal and built on beef, goat, chicken and seafood, flavoured with turmeric, chillies and toasted coconut.

Moro cuisines

The Maranao of Lanao are known for palapa — a fiery condiment of sakurab (native scallions), ginger and chilli pounded with toasted coconut — and for dishes like piaparan chicken cooked in coconut milk. The Tausug of Sulu serve tiyula itum, a blackened beef soup coloured with burnt coconut, while Maguindanaon cooking features pastil, rice topped with shredded chicken or fish wrapped in banana leaf.

Other traditions

Davao is celebrated for durian, pomelo and mangosteen; General Santos and Sarangani supply much of the country's tuna, grilled as panga (collar) in eateries nationwide. Zamboanga's cuisine shows Malay and Spanish colonial inflections, including its own curacha crab in Alavar sauce.
