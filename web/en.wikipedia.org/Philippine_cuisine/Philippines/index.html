The Philippines, officially the Republic of the Philippines, is an archipelagic country in Southeast Asia, situated in the western Pacific Ocean. It consists of about 7,641 islands grouped into three main geographical divisions: Luzon, Visayas, and Mindanao. Its capital is Manila. With a population of over 110 million, it is one of the most populous countries in Asia.

History

The archipelago was inhabited by Austronesian peoples for millennia before Ferdinand Magellan's expedition arrived in 1521. Spain colonized the islands from 1565, naming them after King Philip II, and ruled for more than 300 years. The Philippine Revolution and the Spanish–American War led to American rule in 1898. The Commonwealth of the Philippines was established in 1935, and full independence followed in 1946 after the Second World War, during which the islands were occupied by Japan. The martial law era under Ferdinand Marcos (1972–1986) ended with the People Power Revolution.

Geography

The Philippines lies on the Pacific Ring of Fire and experiences frequent seismic and volcanic activity, as well as an average of about twenty typhoons a year. The terrain is mostly mountainous, with narrow coastal lowlands. The climate is tropical maritime, with a wet season from June to November.

Economy and culture

The economy is driven by services, remittances from millions of overseas Filipino workers, electronics manufacturing, and a large business process outsourcing sector. Filipino culture blends Malay, Spanish, American, and Chinese influences; Catholicism, introduced by Spain, is the predominant religion. Filipino cuisine features dishes such as adobo, sinigang, and lechon.
