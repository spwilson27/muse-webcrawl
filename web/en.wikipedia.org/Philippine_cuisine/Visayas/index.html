Visayas in Philippine cuisine

The Visayas, the central island group of the Philippines, holds a central place in Philippine cuisine. Cebu is regarded as the home of the country's finest lechon; Bacolod in Negros is the birthplace of chicken inasal; and Iloilo is known for La Paz batchoy and pancit Molo. Visayan cooking tends to be simpler in seasoning than Kapampangan food, relying on fresh seafood, grilled meats and souring agents such as batwan.

Regional specialties

Cebu lechon is stuffed with lemongrass, spring onions and spices and eaten with puso (rice boiled in woven coconut leaves). Bacolod inasal is chicken marinated in lemongrass, calamansi and annatto oil, grilled over charcoal and served with chicken oil rice. Iloilo contributes batchoy noodle soup and KBL (kadyos, baboy, langka — pigeon peas, pork and jackfruit). Bohol and the eastern Visayas add kinilaw traditions and the sour soup linarang.

Sweetness and sugar

Negros, long the country's sugar bowl, developed a notable sweet tooth and a repertoire of desserts and kakanin, while Cebu is known for dried mangoes exported worldwide.
