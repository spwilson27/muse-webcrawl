Luzon in Philippine cuisine

Luzon, the largest island of the Philippines, is home to several of the country's most influential regional cuisines. Filipino food writers often divide Luzon cooking into distinct traditions: the frugal, vegetable- and fermented-fish-based cooking of the Ilocos; the rich, festive cuisine of Pampanga, long called the culinary capital of the Philippines; the coconut-milk and chilli dishes of Bicol; and the diverse food of Metro Manila.

Regional traditions

Ilocos cooking favours bagoong (fermented fish paste), bitter vegetables in pinakbet and dinengdeng, and crisp bagnet pork. Pampanga gave the country sisig, bringhe, and elaborate fiesta cookery influenced by Spanish and Mexican trade through the galleons. Bicol is known for cooking in gata (coconut milk) with sili, as in Bicol express and laing. The Tagalog regions around Manila contributed adobo variants, sinigang, and countless rice cakes (kakanin).

Staples

Rice from the Central Luzon plain — historically the country's rice granary — anchors every meal, supplemented by upland corn in the north and root crops in mountain provinces such as the Cordilleras, where pinikpikan and smoked etag pork are traditional.
