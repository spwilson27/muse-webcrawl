Tamarind in Philippine cuisine

Tamarind (Tamarindus indica), called sampalok in Tagalog, is the signature souring agent of Philippine cuisine. Its tart pulp flavours the national sour soup sinigang, seasons marinades and sauces, and is eaten as a sweet-sour snack — green tamarind with salt, or ripe tamarind preserved with sugar.

Uses

For sinigang, unripe or ripe tamarind fruit is boiled and mashed through a strainer to extract the sour liquor; powdered instant mixes have largely supplemented the fresh fruit in modern households. Young tamarind leaves are added to soups in some regions, and the fruit also flavours paksiw and certain Kapampangan dishes. Candied sampalok balls — pulp rolled in sugar and salt — are a classic Filipino childhood treat sold in sari-sari stores.

Alternatives and relatives

Where tamarind is unavailable, cooks substitute kamias, calamansi, guava, batwan or santol, each giving sinigang a different regional character. The tamarind tree itself, likely introduced from India in pre-colonial trade, is a common shade tree across the lowlands of Luzon and the Visayas.
