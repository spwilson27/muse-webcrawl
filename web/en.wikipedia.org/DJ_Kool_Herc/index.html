DJ Kool Herc

DJ Kool Herc (born Clive Campbell, 16 April 1955) is a Jamaican-American DJ widely credited as a founder of hip hop music. In the Bronx, New York, in the early 1970s, he developed the breakbeat DJing technique that became the musical foundation of hip hop culture.

Early life

Campbell was born in Kingston, Jamaica, and emigrated with his family to the Bronx in 1967. Nicknamed "Hercules" at school for his size and strength — later shortened to Herc — he brought with him the Jamaican tradition of powerful sound systems and toasting over records.

The birth of breakbeat DJing

At parties in the recreation room at 1520 Sedgwick Avenue — most famously a back-to-school party hosted with his sister Cindy Campbell on 11 August 1973, a date often cited as the birthday of hip hop — Herc used two turntables and two copies of the same record to extend the percussion-only "break" sections of funk songs indefinitely. He called the technique the "Merry-Go-Round". Dancers who performed during these breaks were dubbed b-boys and b-girls, and Herc's announcements over the music fed into the development of rapping.

Legacy

Herc's innovations were taken up and elaborated by Afrika Bambaataa and Grandmaster Flash, and his sound system, the Herculords, set the standard for Bronx party culture. In 2023 he was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame.
