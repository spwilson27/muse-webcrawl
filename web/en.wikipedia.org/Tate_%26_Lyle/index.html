Tate & Lyle

Tate & Lyle PLC is a British multinational food ingredients company headquartered in London. Founded in 1921 from the merger of two sugar refining businesses — Henry Tate & Sons and Abram Lyle & Sons — the company was for most of the twentieth century one of the world's best-known sugar producers. In the twenty-first century it transformed itself into a supplier of speciality food ingredients, including sweeteners, texturants, and stabilisers, and developed the zero-calorie sweetener sucralose, marketed as Splenda.

History

Henry Tate, a Liverpool sugar refiner who popularised sugar cubes in Britain, and Abram Lyle, whose Greenock refinery produced the famous Lyle's Golden Syrup (sold in green-and-gold tins featuring a lion, among the world's oldest brands), merged their businesses in 1921. The combined firm dominated British sugar refining, with refineries at Silvertown in London and in Liverpool. Its Thames Refinery was long one of the largest sugar refineries in the world.

Transformation

From the 1980s the company expanded into starches and sweeteners in the United States and added sucralose, discovered in 1976 by researchers working with the company. In 2010 Tate & Lyle sold its historic sugars business, including the rights to the Tate & Lyle retail brand and Lyle's Golden Syrup (licensed), to American Sugar Refining, ending its direct involvement in cane sugar refining in order to concentrate on food and beverage ingredients. In the 2020s it combined with CP Kelco to expand its speciality ingredients portfolio.

Heritage

Henry Tate's fortune funded the Tate Gallery (now Tate Britain) in London; he was a major art collector who donated his collection and the gallery building to the nation.
