Sir William Timothy Gowers, FRS (/ˈɡaʊərz/; born 20 November 1963) is a British mathematician. He is Professeur titulaire of the Combinatorics chair at the Collège de France, and a fellow of Trinity College, Cambridge. In 1998, he received the Fields Medal for research connecting the fields of functional analysis and combinatorics.

Education and career

Gowers attended King's College School, Cambridge, and Eton College as a King's Scholar. He read mathematics at Trinity College, Cambridge, and completed his PhD in 1990 under Béla Bollobás, with a thesis on symmetric structures in Banach spaces. He was Rouse Ball Professor of Mathematics at Cambridge from 1998 until 2020, when he took up the combinatorics chair at the Collège de France while retaining a research professorship at Cambridge.

Work

Gowers's early work was in Banach space theory, where he solved several long-standing problems, including the unconditional basic sequence problem and (with Bernard Maurey) a counterexample to the hyperplane problem. In combinatorics he gave a new proof of Szemerédi's theorem on arithmetic progressions using higher-order Fourier analysis, introducing the "Gowers norms" that have become central tools in additive combinatorics. He also proved results on quasirandom groups and on the counting of arithmetic progressions of length four in the primes (with Ben Green and Terence Tao's related work).

Outreach and open science

Gowers wrote the popular book Mathematics: A Very Short Introduction (2002) and edited The Princeton Companion to Mathematics (2008). He founded the Polymath Project (2009), a pioneering experiment in massively collaborative mathematics conducted via his blog.

In 2012 he announced he would no longer submit to or referee for journals of the publisher Elsevier, helping to launch the "Cost of Knowledge" boycott that drew thousands of researchers' signatures. He later founded the overlay journal Discrete Analysis, and was named one of Nature's "Ten people who mattered this year" in 2012. He was knighted in 2012 for services to mathematics.
