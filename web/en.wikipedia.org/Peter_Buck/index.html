Peter Buck

Peter Lawrence Buck (born December 6, 1956) is an American musician and songwriter who was the co-founder and lead guitarist of the alternative rock band R.E.M. Throughout his career with the band, which lasted from 1980 until its dissolution in 2011, Buck was known for his distinctive chiming, arpeggiated guitar style, heavily influenced by The Byrds and built around open strings and a Rickenbacker guitar.

Career

Buck met singer Michael Stipe while working at Wuxtry Records in Athens, Georgia; together with Mike Mills and Bill Berry they formed R.E.M. in 1980. Buck co-wrote much of the band's catalogue and helped define the sound of American alternative rock on albums such as Murmur (1983), Document (1987), Out of Time (1991) and Automatic for the People (1992). His mandolin playing features on the hit single "Losing My Religion".

Other projects

Outside R.E.M., Buck has been prolific: he is a member of The Minus 5 (with Scott McCaughey), the instrumental group Tuatara, and has recorded and toured with Robyn Hitchcock and The Venus 3. Since 2012 he has released several solo albums and worked as a producer for bands including Uncle Tupelo and The Decemberists. Buck is also an avid record collector and music historian.
