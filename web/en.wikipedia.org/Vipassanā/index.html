Vipassanā

Vipassanā (Pāli) or vipaśyanā (Sanskrit) literally means "insight" or "clear seeing" — to see things as they really are. In Buddhism it is one of two qualities of mind developed in meditation (bhāvanā), the other being samatha (calm abiding). Vipassanā is traditionally cultivated together with samatha and is directed toward insight into the three marks of existence: impermanence (anicca), suffering (dukkha) and non-self (anattā).

Practice

Techniques associated with vipassanā include contemplation of the body, feelings, mind and mental objects as set out in the Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta, scanning of bodily sensations, and noting practices. The Burmese monk Mahasi Sayadaw systematised a "noting" method that became highly influential, while S. N. Goenka popularised a body-scanning technique in ten-day residential courses worldwide from the 1970s onward.

Modern movement

The 19th- and 20th-century revival of vipassanā in Burma, Thailand and Sri Lanka — sometimes called the Vipassanā movement — laid the groundwork for the secular mindfulness movement in the West, including Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction. Ledi Sayadaw, U Ba Khin and Mahasi Sayadaw are among its key figures.
