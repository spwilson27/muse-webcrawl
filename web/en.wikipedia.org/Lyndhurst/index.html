Lyndhurst

Lyndhurst, capital of the New Forest

County: Hampshire, England

Population: ~3,000

District: New Forest

Lyndhurst is a large village and civil parish in the New Forest National Park in Hampshire, England. Often styled the "Capital of the New Forest", it has been the administrative centre of the forest since the Middle Ages: the Verderers' Court still meets at the Queen's House in the village, hearing matters relating to the commoners and their animals. The village lies about 14 km southwest of Southampton.

History

The name Lyndhurst is first recorded in the Domesday Book of 1086 as "Linhurst", probably meaning a wooded hill growing with lime trees. Successive monarchs used the Queen's House (formerly the King's House) as a hunting lodge when visiting the New Forest. The village grew as a coaching and market centre, and Victorian visitors arrived with the railway age; the parish church of St Michael and All Angels, rebuilt in the 1860s, contains a famous fresco by Frederic Leighton and the grave of Alice Liddell — the "Alice" of Lewis Carroll's Alice's Adventures in Wonderland — who lived nearby at Cuffnells.

The village today

Lyndhurst's High Street retains many independent shops, pubs, and tea rooms serving the large numbers of visitors to the Forest, alongside the New Forest Heritage Centre. Free-roaming ponies, cattle, and donkeys wander the village streets under commoners' rights of pasture. The surrounding forest offers waymarked walks from Bolton's Bench, a well-known viewpoint hill east of the village.
