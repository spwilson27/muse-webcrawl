Kelp forest

Kelp forests are underwater ecosystems of dense large brown algae (kelps, order Laminariales) growing in shallow, nutrient-rich cold waters along temperate coasts, notably the Pacific coasts of North and South America, southern Australia, South Africa and the North Atlantic.

Giant kelp (Macrocystis pyrifera) can grow 30 cm a day and reach over 45 metres, forming a canopy that shelters fish, invertebrates, sea otters and marine mammals. Kelp forests are among the most productive ecosystems on Earth, but are threatened by warming waters, marine heatwaves and overgrazing by sea urchins when predators such as sea otters decline.
