The Empire of Austria (German: Kaisertum Österreich) was a multinational European great power from 1804 to 1867, created by proclamation out of the realms of the Habsburg Monarchy. Its hereditary emperor was Francis II, who had reigned as the last Holy Roman Emperor from 1792 to 1806; he assumed the title Emperor of Austria in anticipation of Napoleon's pressure, and in 1806 dissolved the Holy Roman Empire altogether.

History

The empire fought in the later Napoleonic Wars and, under the diplomacy of Chancellor Klemens von Metternich, emerged from the Congress of Vienna (1815) as the presiding power of the German Confederation and the dominant conservative force in Central Europe and Italy. The "Metternich system" sought to suppress liberalism and nationalism throughout Europe.

The revolutions of 1848 shook the empire: Ferdinand I abdicated in favour of his eighteen-year-old nephew Franz Joseph, Hungarian independence was proclaimed and then crushed with Russian help in 1849, and a period of neo-absolutist centralisation followed. Defeats by France and Piedmont in 1859 and by Prussia in the Austro-Prussian War of 1866 — ending with the Peace of Prague and Austria's exclusion from German affairs — forced reform. The Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867 transformed the empire into the dual monarchy of Austria-Hungary.

Geography and peoples

The empire encompassed the Austrian hereditary lands, Bohemia, Moravia and Austrian Silesia, Galicia, Hungary with Croatia and Transylvania, Lombardy–Venetia in Italy (until 1859/1866) and Dalmatia and Bukovina. It was a mosaic of peoples — Germans, Hungarians, Czechs, Poles, Ruthenians (Ukrainians), Romanians, Croats, Slovaks, Serbs, Slovenes and Italians — whose competing national aspirations were the empire's perennial problem.
