V8 engine

V8 engine
Configuration: Eight cylinders in two banks of four, forming a V
Typical use: Automobiles, trucks, boats
First production use: 1910 (De Dion-Bouton); popularized by Cadillac (1914)

A V8 engine is an eight-cylinder piston engine in which two banks of four cylinders share a common crankshaft and are arranged in a V configuration. V8s are prized for their power, smoothness, and compactness relative to straight-eight designs, and for their distinctive exhaust note.

Design

Most automotive V8s use a 90° bank angle, enabling even firing intervals and good balance, especially with cross-plane crankshafts; flat-plane designs, common in racing and some European sports cars, rev higher with a sharper sound. Valvetrains range from overhead-valve pushrod layouts (as in classic American small-block engines) to double overhead cams.

History

French maker De Dion-Bouton offered an early V8 in 1910 and Cadillac introduced the first mass-produced V8 in 1914. Ford's flathead V8 (1932) brought the layout to affordable cars. Though downsizing and electrification have reduced their share, V8s remain common in trucks, muscle cars, and motorsport.
