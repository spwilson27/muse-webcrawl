The Wealth of Nations

An Inquiry into the Nature and Causes of the Wealth of Nations, usually shortened to The Wealth of Nations, is a 1776 book by Scottish economist and moral philosopher Adam Smith. It is regarded as the founding work of classical economics and one of the most influential books ever written on political economy.

Content

The book examines the division of labour, productivity, free markets and the sources of national prosperity. Smith argued that individuals pursuing their own self-interest in competitive markets are guided, as if by an "invisible hand", to promote the public good. The work is organized into five books covering labour and wages, capital, the history of economic growth, competing systems of political economy (including a critique of mercantilism), and the revenue of the sovereign.

Influence

Published in the year of the American Revolution, Smith's work shaped generations of economists and policymakers, informing debates over free trade, taxation and the role of government that continue today. See also Adam Smith and Invisible hand.
