Titus Quinctius Flamininus


Gold stater of Flamininus, c. 196 BC

Titus Quinctius Flamininus (c. 228 BC – c. 174 BC) was a Roman politician and general who led Roman forces in the Second Macedonian War against Philip V of Macedon. His victory and the settlement that followed made Rome the dominant power in Greece.

Flamininus, a member of the gens Quinctia, served as a military tribune under Marcellus and as propraetor at Tarentum. Elected consul for 198 BC at an unusually young age, he took command in Greece. After a campaign along the Aous river, he defeated Philip decisively at the Battle of Cynoscephalae in 197 BC.

At the Isthmian Games of 196 BC, Flamininus dramatically proclaimed the "freedom of the Greeks", announcing that the Greek cities would be free, without garrisons, and subject to no tribute — a declaration that caused immense celebration and made him a hero in Greece. He remained in the east until 194 BC, negotiating with the Spartan tyrant Nabis and organising the settlement of Greece before returning to Rome to celebrate a triumph.

Back in Rome he served as censor in 189 BC and was later sent as an envoy to Prusias I of Bithynia in 183 BC to demand the surrender of Hannibal, who had taken refuge there; Hannibal took poison rather than be handed over. Flamininus's genuine philhellenism, expressed in dedications at Delphi and elsewhere, marked a new phase in Rome's engagement with the Greek world.
