Viticulture

A vineyard trained on trellises

Focus: Cultivation of grapevines

Primary species: Vitis vinifera

Viticulture (from the Latin for "vine") is the cultivation and harvesting of grapes, a branch of horticulture. It encompasses the study and practice of growing grapevines, principally for winemaking, but also for table grapes, raisins, juice, and other products. The duties of a viticulturist include monitoring and controlling pests and diseases, fertilizing, irrigation, canopy management, monitoring fruit development, and deciding when to harvest.

History

Grape growing dates back thousands of years: the earliest evidence of wine production comes from Georgia around 6000 BC, and viticulture spread through the ancient Near East, Egypt, Greece, and the Roman Empire. Roman writers such as Pliny the Elder documented grape varieties and training methods. In the 19th century, the phylloxera louse devastated European vineyards, which were saved by grafting European vines onto resistant American rootstocks — still standard practice today.

Practices

Site selection is crucial: vines need well-drained soils, adequate sun, and a suitable climate, summarized in the concept of terroir. Growers choose varieties and clones, plant and train vines on trellis systems, prune annually to control yield and quality, manage the canopy for light and airflow, and time the harvest by sugar, acid, and flavour ripeness. Modern viticulture increasingly uses precision agriculture, cover crops, and integrated pest management to reduce chemical inputs.
