Educational inequality

Educational inequality is the unequal distribution of academic resources and opportunities among socially distinct groups — including by socioeconomic status, race, gender and geography — and the resulting disparities in educational outcomes such as achievement, attainment and literacy.

Causes

Inequalities arise from differences in school funding, teacher quality, facilities, class sizes and access to tutoring, technology and early-childhood education. Family background, parental education and cultural capital strongly predict outcomes, as documented by the 1966 Coleman Report in the United States, which found family factors outweighed school factors in explaining achievement differences.

Effects

Educational inequality contributes to wider income inequality and reduced social mobility, since educational attainment is strongly linked to earnings and employment. Gaps that appear early in schooling tend to persist and widen over time, a phenomenon sometimes called the Matthew effect.

Responses

Policy responses include compensatory funding formulas, early intervention programs such as Head Start, desegregation, affirmative action, and international efforts under the Sustainable Development Goal 4 (quality education for all).
