Stephen Cole Kleene (1909–1994) was an American mathematician who helped lay the foundations of theoretical computer science. A student of Alonzo Church at Princeton, he worked on the lambda calculus and recursion theory.

Contributions

Kleene developed the theory of recursive functions and, with Emil Post, the arithmetical hierarchy. His name is attached to the Kleene star (denoting zero or more repetitions in regular expressions), Kleene's theorem equating regular expressions and finite automata, and the Kleene–Rosser paradox. His 1952 book Introduction to Metamathematics became a standard text in mathematical logic and helped spread intuitionistic and formalist ideas.

Later life

Kleene spent most of his career at the University of Wisconsin–Madison, where he also served as dean. An avid mountaineer, he remained active in logic research into old age; the Kleene Award for student papers at the LICS conference is named in his honour.
