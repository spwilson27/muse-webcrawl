Sir Isaac Vivian Alexander Richards KNH, OBE (born 7 March 1952) is an Antiguan retired cricketer who represented the West Indies cricket team between 1974 and 1991. Widely regarded as one of the greatest batsmen of all time, Richards was known for his dominant, aggressive batting, famously playing without a helmet throughout his career.

Career

Richards made his Test debut against India in 1974 and played 121 Tests, scoring 8,540 runs at an average of 50.23, including 24 centuries. In One Day Internationals he scored 6,721 runs in 187 matches at an average of 47.00, holding the record for the fastest ODI century at the time. He was a member of the West Indies teams that won the Cricket World Cup in 1975 and 1979, and captained the side from 1985 to 1991, never losing a Test series as captain.

In county cricket he played for Somerset, where he became a folk hero, and in 1976 he scored 1,000 first-class runs before the end of May. His 189 not out against England in 1984 is remembered as one of the greatest ODI innings.

Honours

Richards was knighted in 1999 for services to cricket, named one of five Wisden Cricketers of the Century in 2000, and inducted into the ICC Cricket Hall of Fame in 2009. The Sir Vivian Richards Stadium in Antigua is named after him.
