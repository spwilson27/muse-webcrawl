Command and Control (book)

AuthorEric Schlosser
Published2013 (Penguin Press)
Pages632
SubjectNuclear weapons safety

Command and Control: Nuclear Weapons, the Damascus Accident, and the Illusion of Safety is a 2013 non-fiction book by Eric Schlosser, author of Fast Food Nation. It examines the history of nuclear weapons safety in the United States, focusing on the tension between the military's desire for weapons that would always detonate when commanded and the need to ensure they would never detonate accidentally.

Content

The narrative centres on the 1980 Damascus Titan missile explosion in Arkansas, in which a dropped socket punctured the fuel tank of a Titan II intercontinental ballistic missile armed with the most powerful warhead in the American arsenal, the W-53. Schlosser reconstructs the accident in detail, drawing on interviews and declassified documents, and interweaves it with a broader history of "broken arrow" accidents, near misses, and the development (and shortcomings) of safety mechanisms and permissive action links.

Reception

The book was widely praised and was a finalist for the Pulitzer Prize for History. It was later adapted into a 2016 documentary film for the PBS series American Experience.
