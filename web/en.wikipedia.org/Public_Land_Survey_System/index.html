Public Land Survey System

The Public Land Survey System (PLSS) is the surveying method developed and used in the United States to plat, or divide, real property for sale and settling. Created by the Land Ordinance of 1785, it divides land into square townships six miles on a side, each subdivided into 36 sections of one square mile (640 acres), which can be further divided into quarter sections and smaller aliquot parts.

Structure

Surveys are referenced to initial points where a principal meridian (north–south) intersects a baseline (east–west). Townships are numbered north or south of the baseline and ranges east or west of the meridian. Correction lines accommodate the convergence of meridians, producing occasional irregular sections along township edges.

History and use

The system began with the Seven Ranges in Ohio and was applied across most states west of the original thirteen colonies, excepting areas surveyed under French, Spanish or metes-and-bounds systems. PLSS descriptions remain the standard legal description of land in much of the United States and underpin the grid pattern of roads and fields visible across the Midwest.
