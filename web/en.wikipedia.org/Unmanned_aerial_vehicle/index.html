Unmanned aerial vehicle

An MQ-9 Reaper, a large military UAV used for surveillance and strike missions.

An unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV), commonly known as a drone, is an aircraft without any human pilot, crew, or passengers on board. UAVs are controlled remotely by a human operator, by onboard computers, or by a combination of both. They range from hand-launched quadcopters weighing under 250 grams to jet-powered aircraft the size of airliners.

History

Early UAVs included pilotless target aircraft of the First World War era, the radio-controlled de Havilland Queen Bee of the 1930s (from which the name "drone" derives), and reconnaissance drones of the Vietnam War. The 1990s saw the rise of the MQ-1 Predator, which combined long-endurance surveillance with satellite control links, and its armed successor, the MQ-9 Reaper, became emblematic of 21st-century warfare.

Classification and applications

Military UAVs perform intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance (ISR), electronic warfare, and strike roles; loitering munitions ("kamikaze drones") have become prominent in recent conflicts. Civilian and commercial uses include aerial photography and filmmaking, agriculture (crop monitoring and spraying), infrastructure inspection, search and rescue, wildlife conservation, and delivery trials. Consumer quadcopters from companies such as DJI popularized drone photography worldwide.

Regulation

Aviation authorities such as the FAA in the United States and EASA in Europe regulate UAVs by weight and use, requiring registration, remote identification, and restrictions around airports and crowds. Privacy, safety, and military ethics — particularly around autonomous weapons — remain subjects of public debate.
