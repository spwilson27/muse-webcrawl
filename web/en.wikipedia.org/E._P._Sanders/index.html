E. P. Sanders, New Testament scholar.

Ed Parish Sanders (18 April 1937 – 21 November 2022) was an American New Testament scholar and a leading figure in historical Jesus research. He is best known for pioneering the "New Perspective on Paul", a major scholarly movement that reassessed the apostle Paul's views on Judaism and the Jewish law.

Life and career

Sanders was born in Grand Prairie, Texas, and studied at Texas Wesleyan College and Perkins School of Theology before completing his doctorate at Union Theological Seminary in New York in 1966. He taught at McMaster University in Canada and at the University of Oxford, where he held the post of Dean Ireland's Professor of the Exegesis of Holy Scripture, before moving to Duke University in North Carolina, where he was Arts and Sciences Professor of Religion until his retirement.

Scholarship

His 1977 book Paul and Palestinian Judaism compared patterns of religion in ancient Judaism and in Paul's letters, arguing that Second Temple Judaism was not a religion of works-righteousness, as much Christian scholarship had assumed, but one based on God's covenant — a pattern Sanders called "covenantal nomism". The work reshaped Pauline studies. His later books include Jesus and Judaism (1985), which won several awards, The Historical Figure of Jesus (1993), and Paul: The Apostle's Life, Letters, and Thought (2015).

Legacy

Sanders's work prompted decades of debate, notably with scholars such as James D. G. Dunn and N. T. Wright, who developed the New Perspective further, and with traditional Lutheran interpreters of Paul who disputed his conclusions. He was elected a Fellow of the British Academy in 1990.
