Acid–base homeostasis

Acid–base homeostasis is the homeostatic regulation of the pH of the body's extracellular fluid (ECF). The proper balance between the acids and bases (i.e. the pH) in the ECF is crucial for the normal physiology of the body—and for cellular metabolism. The pH of the intracellular fluid and the extracellular fluid need to be maintained at a constant level.

In humans, arterial blood pH is normally tightly regulated between about 7.35 and 7.45. Regulation depends on chemical buffer systems (especially the bicarbonate buffer system), respiratory control of carbon dioxide excretion by the lungs, and renal regulation of bicarbonate and hydrogen-ion excretion. Disturbances of the balance are classified as acidosis or alkalosis, each of respiratory or metabolic origin.
