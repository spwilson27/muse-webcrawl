Captain Nemo (from Latin nemo, "no one") is a fictional character created by the French novelist Jules Verne. He is the captain of the submarine Nautilus and the central figure of Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas (1870), later revealed in The Mysterious Island (1875) to be Prince Dakkar, an Indian rajah's son who lost his family in the Indian Rebellion of 1857 and withdrew from the world to live beneath the sea.

Character

Nemo is a scientific genius and polyglot who funds revolutionary movements while waging a private war on imperial oppression, sinking warships that cross him. Verne originally conceived him as a Polish nobleman avenging Russia's suppression of the January Uprising, but his publisher objected and his nationality was obscured in the first novel.

Legacy

Nemo has become one of literature's most enduring anti-heroes, portrayed in film by actors including James Mason in the 1954 Disney adaptation, and the name Nautilus was later given to the world's first nuclear submarine.
