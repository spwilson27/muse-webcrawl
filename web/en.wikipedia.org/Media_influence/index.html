Media influence

Media influence, studied within media effects and communication theory, concerns how mass media and social media affect audiences' attitudes, beliefs and behaviour, individually and collectively.

Theories

Early "hypodermic needle" models imagined powerful, direct effects. Later research developed agenda-setting theory (media tell audiences what to think about), cultivation theory (long-term exposure shapes perceptions of reality), framing, priming, and the spiral of silence. Uses-and-gratifications research emphasised active audiences.

Evidence and debate

Effects are documented in political persuasion, advertising, body image, and prosocial behaviour, while claims of strong effects of violent media remain debated. The internet era brought filter bubbles, echo chambers, and viral misinformation as new research focuses.
