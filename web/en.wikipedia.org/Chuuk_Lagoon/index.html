Chuuk Lagoon

Chuuk Lagoon, previously Truk Atoll, is an atoll in the central Pacific Ocean, part of Chuuk State in the Federated States of Micronesia. The circular lagoon, roughly 60 kilometres across, is enclosed by a barrier reef and contains a number of mountainous volcanic islands, the largest of which is Weno, the state capital. Its sheltered waters made it one of the finest natural anchorages in the Pacific.

History

Chuuk was part of the Spanish East Indies, then sold to Germany in 1899, and came under Japanese control as a League of Nations mandate after the First World War. Japan developed Truk into its principal naval base in the central Pacific — sometimes called the "Gibraltar of the Pacific". In February 1944, the United States Navy attacked the base in Operation Hailstone, sinking over 50 Japanese ships and destroying hundreds of aircraft in one of the most devastating carrier raids of the Pacific War.

Wreck diving

The sunken fleet left by Operation Hailstone — including the freighter Fujikawa Maru and the tanker Shinkoku Maru — has made Chuuk Lagoon one of the world's most famous wreck-diving destinations, often described as an underwater museum. The wrecks are protected, and concerns remain about oil leaking from ageing fuel tanks.
