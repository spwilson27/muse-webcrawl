World Athletics Championships

The World Athletics Championships, known as the World Championships in Athletics until 2019, are a biennial athletics competition organized by World Athletics (formerly the IAAF). Along with the Olympic Games, they represent the highest level of senior international outdoor track and field competition.

History

The first World Championships were held in Helsinki, Finland, in 1983, following earlier one-off world championship events in 1976 and 1980 for disciplines not contested at the Olympics. The championships were initially held every four years, then became biennial from 1991. Editions have been hosted by cities including Stuttgart, Athens, Edmonton, Paris, Osaka, Berlin, Daegu, Moscow, Beijing, London, Doha, Eugene and Budapest, with Tokyo hosting in 2025.

Events

The programme includes sprints, middle- and long-distance races, hurdles, steeplechase, relays, the marathon and race walking on the track and road, and field events such as long jump, high jump, pole vault, shot put, discus, javelin and hammer throw, plus the decathlon and heptathlon. The United States has been the most successful nation in the championships' history. Notable records include Usain Bolt's world records in the 100 m and 200 m set at Berlin 2009.
