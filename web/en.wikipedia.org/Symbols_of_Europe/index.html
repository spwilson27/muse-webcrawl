Symbols of Europe

A number of symbols of Europe have emerged since antiquity, notably the mythological figure of Europa. In modern times, symbols have been created by the Council of Europe and the European Union to represent the continent and European integration. The most widely recognised symbols are the European flag, the anthem, Europe Day, the motto "United in diversity", and—for the twenty member states that use it—the euro currency.

Flag

The flag of Europe consists of a circle of twelve golden stars on an azure background. Designed by Alsatian artist Arsène Heitz and Belgian official Paul M. G. Lévy, it was adopted by the Council of Europe in 1955 and by the European Community in 1985. The number twelve was chosen as a symbol of completeness and perfection, invariable regardless of the number of member states.

Anthem

The European anthem is based on the "Ode to Joy", the final movement of Ludwig van Beethoven's Ninth Symphony, with Friedrich Schiller's poem celebrating brotherhood and unity. An instrumental arrangement by Herbert von Karajan was adopted by the Council of Europe in 1972 and later by the European Union; officially it is performed without lyrics.

Europe Day and the motto

Europe Day is celebrated on 9 May, the anniversary of the 1950 Schuman Declaration, within the European Union, while the Council of Europe marks 5 May, its founding date. The EU motto, In varietate concordia ("United in diversity"), was adopted in 2000 and expressed in all of the Union's official languages.

Europa

The personification of the continent is Europa, the Phoenician princess of Greek mythology who was abducted by Zeus in the form of a bull. Her image has appeared on coins, stamps, and artworks for centuries; the European Central Bank's Europa series of euro banknotes carries her portrait in the watermark and hologram.

Euro

The euro sign (€) was designed by a team working for the European Commission; its form was inspired by the Greek letter epsilon, referencing the cradle of European civilisation, with two parallel lines signifying stability. The currency was launched in 1999 and entered circulation in 2002.
