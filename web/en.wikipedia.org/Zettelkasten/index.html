Zettelkasten

A Zettelkasten (German for "slip box") is a personal knowledge management and note-taking method consisting of many individual notes, each with a unique identifier, stored in a box (originally slips of paper in a physical box) and connected to one another through links and metadata. The system emphasises writing notes in one's own words and building a web of connections rather than a fixed hierarchy of categories.

Niklas Luhmann

The method is most associated with the German sociologist Niklas Luhmann, who built a Zettelkasten of some 90,000 cards over his career and credited it as a "communication partner" in producing his many books and articles, including his systems theory of society. Luhmann used an alphanumeric numbering scheme (Folgezettel) that allowed notes to branch indefinitely, so that new slips could be inserted in sequence after related ones.

Principles

Key principles of the method include atomicity (one idea per note), autonomy (each note understandable on its own), dense linking between notes, and writing notes with future retrieval and unexpected connections in mind. Luhmann distinguished his main slip box from a bibliographic box holding references and brief notes on literature.

Digital implementations

The method has been widely adopted in digital form, in personal knowledge software using plain-text files, backlinks and graph views; the approach underlies the workflows of many modern note-taking applications.
