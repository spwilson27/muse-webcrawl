Billie Holiday

BornEleanora Fagan, 7 April 1915, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, U.S.
Died17 July 1959, New York City, U.S.
GenresJazz, swing, blues
OccupationSinger

Billie Holiday (born Eleanora Fagan; 7 April 1915 – 17 July 1959) was an American jazz and swing music singer. Nicknamed "Lady Day" by her friend and musical partner Lester Young, Holiday had a profound influence on jazz singing and pop vocals with her distinctive phrasing and emotional delivery.

Career

Holiday was discovered singing in Harlem clubs and made her first recordings in 1933 with Benny Goodman. She went on to record with Teddy Wilson, Count Basie, and Artie Shaw, and became famous for songs including "What a Little Moonlight Can Do," "God Bless the Child," and "Strange Fruit" (1939), a searing protest against lynching that became her signature song.

Later life

Holiday's later years were marked by drug and alcohol problems, legal troubles, and declining health, though she continued to perform and record, including the acclaimed album Lady in Satin (1958). She died in New York City in 1959 at the age of 44.

Legacy

Holiday is regarded as one of the greatest jazz vocalists of all time. She was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame and the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame, and her recordings continue to influence singers across genres.
