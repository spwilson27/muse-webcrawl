National Museum of Anthropology (Mexico City)

The National Museum of Anthropology (Spanish: Museo Nacional de Antropología) is a national museum in Mexico City, located in Chapultepec Park. It is the largest and most visited museum in Mexico, housing the world's most important collection of pre-Hispanic Mexican art and archaeology.

Architecture

The museum opened on 17 September 1964 and was designed by architect Pedro Ramírez Vázquez. Its vast central courtyard is covered by a huge concrete umbrella (el paraguas) supported by a single pillar carved with mythological imagery, from which a curtain of water falls. Exhibition halls ring the courtyard on two levels: archaeology on the ground floor and ethnography above.

Collection

Highlights include the Aztec Sun Stone (Calendar Stone), the colossal Olmec stone heads, treasures from the Maya tomb of Pakal at Palenque, the Aztec Calendar's companion pieces, and models of the Templo Mayor and the market at Tlatelolco. Halls are devoted to each major culture — Olmec, Teotihuacan, Toltec, Mexica (Aztec), Oaxaca, Maya and others.
