Earthly Branches

The Earthly Branches (Chinese: 地支; pinyin: dìzhī) are a set of twelve terms used in traditional Chinese calendrical and astronomical systems. Together with the ten Heavenly Stems, they form the sexagenary cycle of sixty combinations used to record years, months, days, and hours.

The twelve branches

Each branch is associated with one of the twelve animals of the Chinese zodiac, a double-hour of the day, and a compass direction. In order they are: Zǐ (子, rat), Chǒu (丑, ox), Yín (寅, tiger), Mǎo (卯, rabbit), Chén (辰, dragon), Sì (巳, snake), Wǔ (午, horse), Wèi (未, goat), Shēn (申, monkey), Yǒu (酉, rooster), Xū (戌, dog), and Hài (亥, pig).

Uses

The branches were historically used to divide the day into twelve double-hours, to mark directions (with the rat branch pointing north), and as ordinal numbers in dates. In the sexagenary cycle, each branch pairs with a stem in sequence, producing the sixty-year cycle still used for naming years in the traditional calendar.
