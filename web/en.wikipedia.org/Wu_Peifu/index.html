Wu Peifu, the "Scholar Warlord" of the Zhili clique.

Wu Peifu (April 22, 1874 – December 4, 1939) was a Chinese warlord and a major figure of the Warlord Era. As the leading general of the Zhili clique, he dominated northern and central China in the early 1920s and was widely regarded — including by foreign observers, who put him on the cover of Time magazine in 1924 as China's "strongest man" — as the warlord most likely to reunify the country.

Early life

Born in Penglai, Shandong, Wu passed the imperial examinations and earned the xiucai degree before turning to a military career, an unusual combination that earned him the nickname "Scholar Warlord." He rose through the Beiyang Army under Cao Kun and distinguished himself campaigning in Hunan during the Constitutional Protection War.

Zhili wars and ascendancy

Wu led Zhili forces to victory in the Zhili–Anhui War (1920) and the First Zhili–Fengtian War (1922), making his faction dominant in Beijing. He championed the restoration of the 1912 constitution and national unification by force. In February 1923 his suppression of a strike on the Beijing–Hankou railway — the February 7 massacre — badly damaged his reputation among workers and intellectuals. Defeat in the Second Zhili–Fengtian War (1924), after Feng Yuxiang's betrayal in the Beijing Coup, destroyed his power base in the north.

Later years

Wu rebuilt a base in central China but was defeated by the Kuomintang's Northern Expedition in 1926–1927 and retired from military life. He lived quietly in Beiping, studying Buddhism and poetry. After the Japanese invasion of 1937 he refused collaborationist overtures to head a puppet government; his death in 1939, officially from septicaemia following dental surgery, was widely suspected to have been a Japanese-engineered poisoning.
