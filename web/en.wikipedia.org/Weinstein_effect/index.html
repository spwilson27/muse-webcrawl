Weinstein effect

The Weinstein effect is a global trend in which allegations of sexual abuse — initially against film producer Harvey Weinstein — led to a wave of public accusations against powerful men in entertainment, media, politics and other industries, and to many of their removals from positions of power. The phenomenon amplified the broader Me Too movement, which spread virally as a hashtag in October 2017.

Background

On 5 October 2017, The New York Times published an investigation by Jodi Kantor and Megan Twohey reporting decades of sexual harassment allegations against Weinstein; The New Yorker followed days later with Ronan Farrow's reporting. Weinstein was fired from his company, expelled from the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences, and later convicted of rape and sexual assault in New York (2020) and Los Angeles (2022), though his New York conviction was overturned on appeal in 2024.

Impact

Within weeks, dozens of prominent men faced allegations and consequences, resignations swept legislatures and newsrooms, and the phrase "Weinstein effect" entered wide use for the breaking of silence around entrenched abusers. The movement inspired the Time's Up legal defence fund and prompted reforms in workplace harassment law and corporate practice in many countries, alongside debate over due process and the permanence of professional exile.
