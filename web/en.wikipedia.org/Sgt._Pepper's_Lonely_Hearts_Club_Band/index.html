Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band is the eighth studio album by the English rock band the Beatles. Released on 26 May 1967, it spent 27 weeks at number one on the UK albums chart and 15 weeks at number one on the US Billboard Top LPs chart. It is widely regarded as one of the greatest and most influential albums of all time.

Conception and recording

After retiring from touring in 1966, the Beatles adopted the fictional Sgt. Pepper band as an alter ego, freeing them to experiment. Produced by George Martin and engineered by Geoff Emerick, the album exploited the four-track facilities of EMI Studios with techniques including varispeed recording, artificial double tracking, orchestral overdubs, tape loops, and direct injection. Songs include "With a Little Help from My Friends", "Lucy in the Sky with Diamonds", "Getting Better", "When I'm Sixty-Four", "Lovely Rita", "Being for the Benefit of Mr. Kite!", and the closing orchestral crescendo of "A Day in the Life".

Artwork and legacy

The cover, designed by Peter Blake and Jann Haworth, shows the band in satin military costumes before a collage of life-sized cardboard cut-outs of famous figures, beside waxworks of themselves — a landmark of pop art that won the Grammy Award for Best Album Cover. The album embraced the Summer of Love aesthetic, helped popularize the concept album and psychedelic rock, and elevated the LP as an art form. It won four Grammy Awards in 1968, including Album of the Year, the first rock album so honoured. In 2003 the Library of Congress added it to the National Recording Registry.
