Goalkeeper

In many team sports that involve scoring goals, the goalkeeper (sometimes termed goalie, netminder, keeper or custodian) is a designated player charged with directly preventing the opposing team from scoring by blocking or intercepting opposing shots. The position exists in association football, ice hockey, handball, water polo, lacrosse, field hockey and other sports.

In association football

The goalkeeper is the only player permitted to handle the ball, and then only within their own penalty area. They wear a jersey of a different colour from both teams and the officials, and commonly wear gloves. Goalkeepers are expected to save shots, claim crosses, organise the defence, and distribute the ball to start attacks, either by throwing or by kicking. The 1992 back-pass rule prohibited goalkeepers from handling deliberate passes from teammates, greatly increasing the importance of their foot skills and leading to the development of the "sweeper-keeper", who plays high up the pitch behind an advanced defensive line.

Other sports

In ice hockey the goaltender wears extensive protective equipment and defends a small net against a hard puck. In handball and water polo, goalkeepers defend from close range against fast throws and shots, relying on reflexes and positioning. Across these sports the goalkeeper is usually a specialist who plays the position for an entire career.
