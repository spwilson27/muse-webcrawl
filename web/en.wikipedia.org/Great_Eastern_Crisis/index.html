The Great Eastern Crisis of 1875–1878 was a series of uprisings, wars and diplomatic confrontations in the Balkans that engaged all the Great Powers of Europe and reshaped the map of south-eastern Europe. It began with the Herzegovina uprising against Ottoman rule in 1875 and culminated in the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–78 and the Congress of Berlin.

Course of the crisis

Rebellion in Herzegovina and Bosnia (1875) spread as Serbia and Montenegro declared war on the Ottoman Empire in 1876. In Bulgaria the April Uprising of 1876 was crushed with massacres that outraged European opinion — notably in Britain, where Gladstone's pamphlet The Bulgarian Horrors and the Question of the East galvanised public debate. The Constantinople Conference failed to impose reforms, and Russia declared war in April 1877, advancing with Romanian help through Bulgaria to the outskirts of Constantinople.

Settlement

The Treaty of San Stefano (March 1878) created a large autonomous Bulgaria and recognised the independence of Romania, Serbia and Montenegro. Britain and Austria-Hungary, alarmed at Russian gains, forced a revision at the Congress of Berlin (June–July 1878): Bulgaria was divided and reduced, Austria-Hungary occupied Bosnia-Herzegovina, and Britain took Cyprus. The settlement embittered Bulgaria and Russia and left grievances that fed the Balkan Wars and the road to 1914.
