Cytopathology

A Pap-stained cervical smear; cytopathologists screen such preparations for precancerous change.

Cytopathology is the branch of pathology concerned with the diagnosis of disease by examining individual cells and small clusters of cells obtained from tissues and body fluids, rather than intact tissue architecture (the province of histopathology). It is used above all for cancer screening and diagnosis, because samples can often be obtained by minimally invasive means.

Sample types

Exfoliative cytology

Cells shed naturally or scraped from a surface are collected: the cervical Pap test, sputum, urine, and fluids from the pleural, peritoneal and cerebrospinal cavities. The Pap test, developed by Georgios Papanikolaou in the 1920s–40s, transformed cervical cancer prevention by detecting precancerous cells years before invasion.

Fine-needle aspiration

In fine-needle aspiration (FNA) cytology, a thin needle samples masses in the thyroid, breast, lymph nodes, salivary glands, lung and pancreas, often under imaging guidance. FNA provides rapid, low-cost triage of nodules, though some diagnoses require tissue architecture and histology.

Interventional and ancillary techniques

Cell blocks prepared from cytology material allow immunohistochemistry and molecular testing — for example, mutation and fusion studies on lung cancer aspirates that guide targeted therapy.

Reporting systems

Standardised terminology improves reproducibility: the Bethesda System for cervical cytology and for thyroid FNA, the Paris System for urinary cytology, and the Milan System for salivary gland cytology each define diagnostic categories linked to estimated risk of malignancy and recommended management.

Practice

Cytopathologists are physicians (usually anatomic pathologists with fellowship training), supported by cytotechnologists who screen and triage cases. Digital imaging, liquid-based preparations and, increasingly, machine-learning screening aids are reshaping the specialty, even as widespread HPV vaccination and primary HPV testing reduce the volume of conventional Pap screening in many countries.
