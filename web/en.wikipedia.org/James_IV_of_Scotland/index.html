James IV of Scotland

James IV (17 March 1473 – 9 September 1513) was King of Scotland from 11 June 1488 until his death at the Battle of Flodden in 1513. He inherited the throne at the age of fifteen following the death of his father, James III, at the Battle of Sauchieburn. Generally regarded as the most successful of the Stewart monarchs, he was the last king in Scotland to be killed in battle.

Reign

James was a Renaissance prince: educated, fluent in several languages including Gaelic, and a patron of poets such as William Dunbar and Gavin Douglas. He patronised music and architecture, rebuilt royal palaces, and founded a printing press in Scotland. He took a practical interest in medicine and dentistry and is said to have paid patients to experiment on them.

Government

James subdued the Highlands and Islands, ending the semi-independent Lordship of the Isles in 1493. He built up a navy, including the great carrack Great Michael, at the time the largest ship in Europe. In 1503 he married Margaret Tudor, daughter of Henry VII of England, under the Treaty of Perpetual Peace — the marriage from which the later union of the crowns would derive.

Invasion of England and death

When Henry VIII invaded France in 1513, James honoured the Auld Alliance and invaded England. His army was defeated at Flodden Field in Northumberland, and James was killed along with much of the Scottish nobility. He was succeeded by his infant son, James V.
