Filibuster

A filibuster is a political procedure in which one or more members of a legislative body prolong debate to delay or prevent a vote. It is most associated with the United States Senate, where debate is generally unlimited and ending it requires a cloture vote of 60 senators under Rule XXII.

The term derives from the Spanish filibustero and Dutch vrijbuiter (freebooter/pirate). Modern filibusters often require no continuous speaking; exceptions ("nuclear option" precedents in 2013 and 2017) removed it for executive and judicial nominations.
