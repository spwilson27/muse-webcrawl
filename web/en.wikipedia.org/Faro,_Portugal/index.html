Faro is a city and municipality in southern Portugal, the capital of the Algarve region and of the district of the same name. It is the southernmost city in mainland Portugal and serves as the main gateway to the Algarve, chiefly through Faro Airport, one of the country's busiest international airports.

Geography

Faro lies on the shore of the Ria Formosa, a protected lagoon system of salt marshes, barrier islands and tidal channels stretching some 60 kilometres along the eastern Algarve coast. The lagoon is an important habitat for migratory birds and shellfish, and its barrier islands — including Ilha da Armona and Ilha da Culatra — shelter the city from the open Atlantic. The historic centre, the Cidade Velha, sits on a low rise above the marina and is enclosed by medieval walls.

History

The site was settled in Phoenician and Roman times, when it was known as Ossonoba, an important port and trading post. Under Moorish rule from the 8th century it became known as Santa Maria and later Faro, and it was reconquered for Portugal by King Afonso III in 1249, the last major stronghold in the Algarve to fall. The city suffered in the 1755 Lisbon earthquake, which destroyed much of the old town, though the cathedral, begun in the 13th century, survived in altered form.

Landmarks

Principal sights include Faro Cathedral (Sé), the Arco da Vila gateway, the Carmo Church famous for its Capela dos Ossos (Chapel of Bones), the Municipal Museum housed in the former Convent of Our Lady of the Assumption, and the Milreu Roman ruins a few kilometres away near Estoi. The University of the Algarve, founded in 1979, has its main campuses in the city.
