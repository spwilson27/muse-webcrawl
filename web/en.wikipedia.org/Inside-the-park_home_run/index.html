Inside-the-park home run

An inside-the-park home run is a play in baseball in which a batter rounds all the bases and scores on a hit that does not leave the field of play — that is, the ball is not hit over the outfield fence. Unlike a conventional home run, the batter must outrun the fielders' attempts to retrieve the ball and throw him out, making it a race between the runner and the defence.

How it happens

Inside-the-park home runs typically result from a ball hit into a deep gap or corner of the outfield, a ball that takes an unusual bounce off a wall or fence, or a defensive misplay — although if the scorer rules the misplay an error, a home run is usually not credited. Fast runners and large outfields increase the likelihood. In some cases a fielder is injured or falls while chasing the ball, giving the batter time to circle the bases.

History and frequency

The play was much more common in the dead-ball era, when outfields were vast and balls rarely left the park; Jesse Burkett and Sam Crawford were among its noted practitioners, and Crawford holds the career record with 51. As ballparks shrank and the lively ball era arrived, inside-the-park homers became rare novelties — typically only a handful per Major League Baseball season. They remain famous for their drama: notable examples include inside-the-park home runs in All-Star Games and the World Series, such as Alcides Escobar's leadoff homer in Game 1 of the 2015 World Series.
