Anticosti Island

Anticosti Island (French: Île d'Anticosti) is an island in the province of Quebec, Canada, located at the outlet of the Saint Lawrence River into the Gulf of Saint Lawrence. With an area of about 7,941 square kilometres (3,066 sq mi), it is the 90th largest island in the world and the 20th largest island in Canada.

Geography

The island is about 217 kilometres (135 mi) long and up to 48 kilometres (30 mi) wide. It is sparsely populated, with most residents living in the village of Port-Menier on the western tip. The island is known for its limestone cliffs, canyons such as the Vauréal Canyon, and abundant wildlife, including a large population of white-tailed deer introduced in the 1890s.

History

For thousands of years, Indigenous peoples used the island as hunting grounds. Jacques Cartier sighted the island in 1534. In 1895 it was purchased by French chocolate maker Henri Menier, who developed Port-Menier and introduced deer and other game animals. The island was later sold and eventually acquired by the Government of Quebec in 1974. Portions of the island are protected as part of Anticosti National Park, established in 2001. In 2023, Anticosti was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List for its exceptionally complete fossil record of the Ordovician–Silurian extinction event.
