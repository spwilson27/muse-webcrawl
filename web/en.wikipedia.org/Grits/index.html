Grits

TypePorridge
Place of originSouthern United States
Main ingredientGround corn (maize), typically dent corn
VariationsHominy grits; stone-ground grits; instant grits
Official statusState prepared food of Georgia

Grits are a porridge made from ground corn (maize), boiled in water or milk, and a signature dish of the cuisine of the Southern United States. They descend from Native American corn preparations — the word derives from the Old English grytt, "coarse meal" — adapted by settlers and enslaved Africans into a staple of Southern cooking.

Preparation and types

Grits are milled from dent corn. Stone-ground grits, milled whole between stones, are coarse, flavourful and slow-cooking. Hominy grits are made from corn treated with an alkali (nixtamalisation), as in hominy. Quick and instant grits, processed to cook in minutes, dominate supermarket sales but are considered inferior by connoisseurs. Properly cooked grits are thick, creamy and eaten with butter, salt, cheese or gravy — almost always savoury rather than sweet.

Serving

Grits anchor the classic Southern breakfast alongside eggs and bacon, and headline Lowcountry dishes such as shrimp and grits, a Charleston-area speciality that spread nationally. Cheese grits are a common side with fried fish and barbecue. Georgia designated grits its official prepared state food in 2002, and South Carolina has its own statutory homage.

Similar dishes

Grits are kin to Italian polenta (usually from flint corn, milled finer or coarser by region), Romanian mămăligă, and African maize porridges such as ugali — a global family of boiled ground-corn dishes.
