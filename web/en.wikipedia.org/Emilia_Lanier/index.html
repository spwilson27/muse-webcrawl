Emilia Lanier

Emilia Lanier (1569 – 1645), also spelled Aemilia Lanyer, was the first Englishwoman to assert herself publicly as a professional poet. Born Aemilia Bassano into a family of Venetian court musicians, she was for a time the mistress of Henry Carey, 1st Baron Hunsdon, the Lord Chamberlain.

In 1611 she published Salve Deus Rex Judaeorum ("Hail, God, King of the Jews"), a volume of religious verse framed by dedications to aristocratic women and including a defence of women that retells the Fall with Eve's apology. The collection also contains "The Description of Cooke-ham", one of the earliest country-house poems in English.

Lanier has been proposed by some writers, controversially, as a candidate for the "Dark Lady" of Shakespeare's sonnets. After her husband Alphonso Lanier's death she ran a school in St. Giles-in-the-Fields and was embroiled in lawsuits over her late brother-in-law's music patent. She died in London in 1645.
