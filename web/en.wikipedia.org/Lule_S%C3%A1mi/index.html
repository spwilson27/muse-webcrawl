Lule Sámi

Lule Sámi (julevsámegiella)
Native to: Norway, Sweden
Speakers: about 1,000–2,000
Family: Uralic › Sámi › Western Sámi
Writing: Latin script

Lule Sámi (Lule Sami: julevsámegiella) is a Sámi language spoken in the Luleå area of Sweden and in parts of Nordland county in Norway, notably around Tysfjord. It is the second most widely spoken Sámi language after Northern Sámi.

Geographic distribution

The traditional Lule Sámi area stretches from the coast near Tysfjord in Norway across the mountains to the Lule River valley in Swedish Norrbotten, including Jokkmokk and Gällivare municipalities.

Status and writing system

Lule Sámi has an official written standard, developed from the orthography of Karl Bernhard Wiklund and revised in 1983. The Árran Lule Sámi centre in Tysfjord promotes the language, which is nevertheless considered seriously endangered as intergenerational transmission has weakened.

Grammar

Like other Sámi languages, Lule Sámi is agglutinative, has a rich case system for nouns, consonant gradation, and no grammatical gender. Verbs conjugate for person, number, tense and mood.
