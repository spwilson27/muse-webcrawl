Dastgāh

Dastgāh (Persian: دستگاه) is the standard melodic system of traditional Persian art music, also used in Azerbaijani mugham and related traditions. A dastgāh is not merely a scale but a collection of melodic fragments (gusheh) organised around a tonal centre, from which musicians improvise and compose.

Organisation

The classical repertoire, the radif, is organised into twelve dastgāhs: seven primary dastgāhs — Shur, Segāh, Chahārgāh, Homāyun, Māhur, Rāstpanjgāh and Navā — and five derivative ones (āvāz) traditionally grouped under Shur and Homāyun. Within each dastgāh, gushehs move through characteristic melodic development, progressing from low to high registers and back.

Performance

A performance typically begins with an introductory section (darāmad), explores successive gushehs, and returns to the home mode, often concluding with a rhythmic composed piece such as a reng. The radif of Mirza Abdollah, transcribed in the 20th century, is the most influential version and was inscribed by UNESCO in 2009 on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
