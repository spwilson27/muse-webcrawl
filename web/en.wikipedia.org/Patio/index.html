Patio

A patio (from Spanish: patio, meaning courtyard or forecourt) is an outdoor space generally used for dining or recreation that adjoins a residence and is typically paved. In Australia the term is expanded to include structures resembling a verandah, which are roofed and often enclosed. In the United States, the term is also used for paved areas adjoining houses that may or may not be roofed.

A residential patio used for outdoor dining and relaxation.

History

The patio derives from the courtyard houses of Spain and Spanish America, where an open central court — in Andalusia often shared with traditions inherited from Roman and Islamic architecture — provided light, air, privacy, and a cool outdoor room. The famous patios of Córdoba, decorated with flowers and fountains, are celebrated in an annual festival and recognized as cultural heritage. In the American Southwest and California, Spanish colonial and revival architecture made the patio a standard feature of domestic design.

Construction

Patios are usually constructed from concrete, stamped concrete, brick, stone pavers, tiles, or gravel laid over a prepared base, are typically a few steps below or level with the adjoining floor, and may incorporate pergolas, shade sails, fire pits, outdoor kitchens, and planting. Unlike a deck, a patio rests directly on the ground and does not require railings.
