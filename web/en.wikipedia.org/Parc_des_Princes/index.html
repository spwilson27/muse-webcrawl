Parc des Princes

Parc des Princes Location: 16th arrondissement, Paris, France
Opened: 1897 (original); current stadium 1972
Capacity: 47,929
Tenants: Paris Saint-Germain
Architect: Roger Taillibert

The Parc des Princes (officially Stade du Parc des Princes) is an all-seater football stadium in Paris, France, in the 16th arrondissement, near the Stade Jean-Bouin and across from Roland Garros. It is the home of the Ligue 1 club Paris Saint-Germain, who have played there since 1974. With seating for 47,929 spectators, it is the fifth-largest football stadium in France.

History

A stadium has stood on the site since 1897, when it opened as a velodrome; the Tour de France finished there until 1967. The second stadium hosted the 1938 FIFA World Cup and served as the French national stadium for football and rugby before the opening of the Stade de France in 1998. The present stadium, designed by architect Roger Taillibert with its distinctive precast concrete ribs, was inaugurated in 1972 and hosted the finals of Euro 1984 (won by France), the 1998 FIFA World Cup third-place play-off, and Euro 2016 matches.

Paris Saint-Germain

PSG moved to the ground in 1974 and the stadium became synonymous with the club's ultras, notably the Kop of Boulogne and the Auteuil end. Plans under the club's Qatari ownership have variously proposed expanding the Parc des Princes to around 60,000 seats or building a new stadium elsewhere in the Paris region; delays in negotiations with the City of Paris, which owns the stadium, have kept the club's long-term home an open question, though the Parc remains its ground.

Other events

Beyond football, the Parc des Princes hosted the 1954 and 1972 Rugby League World Cup finals, French rugby union internationals, major boxing bouts, and concerts by artists including Michael Jackson, the Rolling Stones, and Prince, after whom the stadium is not named — the name refers to the princes (of the royal family) who used the surrounding woodland as hunting grounds in the 18th century.
