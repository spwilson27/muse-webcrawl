Legalism (Chinese philosophy)

Legalism (fajia, 法家, "School of Law") is a school of Chinese philosophy of the Warring States period that emphasised strict, clearly promulgated laws, administrative technique and the power of the ruler, rather than Confucian virtue and ritual.

Thinkers and ideas

Its major figures include Shang Yang, Shen Buhai, Shen Dao and Han Fei, whose Han Feizi synthesised the school's three pillars: fa (law), shu (method) and shi (positional power).

Influence

Legalist reforms in the state of Qin underpinned its unification of China in 221 BC; the harshness of Qin rule made the school unpopular under the Han, though its administrative practices endured in imperial government.
