Moscow Helsinki Group

The Moscow Helsinki Group (Russian: Московская Хельсинкская группа) was a Soviet human rights organization founded in Moscow on 12 May 1976 by the physicist Yuri Orlov and other dissidents. Its full name was the Public Group to Promote the Implementation of the Helsinki Accords in the USSR. It monitored Soviet compliance with the human-rights provisions of the 1975 Helsinki Final Act, which the Soviet Union had signed.

Activities and repression

The group collected documentation on violations such as the persecution of religious believers, restrictions on emigration, abuse of psychiatry, and the treatment of national minorities, and passed its reports to Western journalists and governments. Its founding and example inspired similar Helsinki watch groups in Ukraine, Lithuania, Georgia, and Armenia, and later Helsinki Watch (now part of Human Rights Watch) in the United States.

The Soviet authorities responded with arrests, trials, exile, and forced emigration. Orlov was sentenced to a labour camp and internal exile; other members, including Elena Bonner and Anatoly Shcharansky, were imprisoned or persecuted. By 1982 only a few members remained at liberty, and the group announced the suspension of its work under duress.

Revival

The group resumed activity in 1989 during perestroika, chaired later by the veteran dissident Lyudmila Alexeyeva, and continued to monitor human rights in post-Soviet Russia, despite pressure under laws on "foreign agents".
