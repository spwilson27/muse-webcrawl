Harpy eagle

The harpy eagle (Harpia harpyja) is a large neotropical species of eagle found in the tropical lowland rainforests of Central and South America. It is among the largest and most powerful raptors in the Americas, preying chiefly on tree-dwelling mammals such as sloths and monkeys.

Scientific classification
Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Chordata
Class: Aves
Order: Accipitriformes
Family: Accipitridae
Genus: Harpia
Species: H. harpyja
Conservation status: Vulnerable

Description

Females, larger than males, can weigh 6–9 kg and measure over a metre long, with a wingspan of about 2 m. The upperparts are slate-black, the underparts white, and the head pale grey with a distinctive double crest. The tarsus is thick and the rear talons can reach 13 cm — comparable to a grizzly bear's claws.

Ecology and conservation

Harpy eagles nest in the crowns of emergent trees such as kapoks, raising a single eaglet over roughly two years, giving them one of the slowest reproductive rates of any raptor. Habitat loss and shooting have caused declines across much of the range, and the species is listed as Vulnerable by the IUCN. It is the national bird of Panama.
