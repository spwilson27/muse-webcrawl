Cuthbert (Old English: Cuðberht, Latin: Cuthbertus; c. 634 – 20 March 687) was an Anglo-Saxon saint of the early English Church in the Celtic tradition. He was a monk, bishop, and hermit associated with the monasteries of Melrose and Lindisfarne in the Kingdom of Northumbria. He is one of the most venerated saints of early medieval England.

Life

Cuthbert entered Melrose Abbey after a vision on the night of St Aidan's death in 651. He served as guest-master at Ripon, returned to Melrose as prior, and later became prior of Lindisfarne. Seeking greater solitude, he withdrew to the Farne Islands, living as a hermit. Reluctantly, he accepted election as Bishop of Lindisfarne in 685, but returned to his hermitage before his death in 687.

Veneration and relics

When his tomb was opened eleven years after his death, his body was found incorrupt, greatly boosting his cult. Fleeing Viking attacks, his community carried his coffin around Northumbria for years before it came to rest at Durham, where Durham Cathedral was built over his shrine. The Lindisfarne Gospels are traditionally associated with his cult. The Venerable Bede wrote both a verse and a prose life of Cuthbert, the most important sources for his biography.
