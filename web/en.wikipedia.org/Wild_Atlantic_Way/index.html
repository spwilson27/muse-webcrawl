The Wild Atlantic Way (Irish: Slí an Atlantaigh Fhiáin) is a tourism trail on the west coast, and on parts of the north and south coasts, of Ireland. The 2,500 km (1,600 mi) driving route passes through nine counties and three provinces, from the Inishowen Peninsula in County Donegal in the north to the town of Kinsale, County Cork, in the south. It is marketed by Fáilte Ireland as one of the longest defined coastal touring routes in the world.

Route

The route follows existing roads along the Atlantic seaboard, passing landmarks including the Cliffs of Moher, the Burren, Killarney National Park, the Ring of Kerry, the Dingle Peninsula, Galway city, Connemara, Westport, the Céide Fields and Malin Head, the most northerly point of mainland Ireland. Fifteen "Signature Discovery Points" anchor the itinerary, with a further set of discovery points and embarkation points along the way.

Development and impact

The Wild Atlantic Way was launched in 2014 after a development phase involving Fáilte Ireland, local authorities and tourism businesses, with signposting installed along the entire route. It was designed to revitalise tourism in the west of Ireland, spread visitors beyond traditional hotspots, and lengthen stays. The brand has since been extended with walking and greenway projects, and is credited with significant growth in visitor numbers and revenue in the region.

Related routes

The Wild Atlantic Way is one of several touring brands in Ireland, alongside Ireland's Ancient East and the Causeway Coastal Route in Northern Ireland.
