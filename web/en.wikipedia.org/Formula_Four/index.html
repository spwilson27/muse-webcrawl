Formula 4

Formula 4 (also known as FIA Formula 4 or F4) is an open-wheel racing car category intended for junior drivers. Devised by the FIA and launched in 2014, it forms the first step of the FIA Global Pathway from karting to Formula One, sitting below Formula Three. There is no single global championship; instead, individual nations and regions host their own series to a common technical framework.

Origins

The category was conceived by former Formula One driver Gerhard Berger, then president of the FIA's Single-Seater Commission, to simplify the ladder between karting and Formula Three, which had fragmented into numerous expensive national series. The first championship, the Italian F4 Championship, began in 2014.

Technical regulations

Each national series chooses an approved chassis (from constructors such as Tatuus and Mygale) and a 1.4- to 2.0-litre engine producing about 160 horsepower. Costs are capped, tyres are controlled, and drivers must be at least 15 years old. Second-generation cars introduced from 2022 added the halo cockpit protection device.

Championships

F4 series run in Italy, Germany, Britain, Spain, France, Japan, the United States, the United Arab Emirates and many other countries, awarding Super Licence points to their champions. Graduates include Lando Norris, who won the inaugural British F4 title in 2015.
