Ghost kitchen

A ghost kitchen, also known as a cloud kitchen, dark kitchen, or virtual kitchen, is a commercial cooking facility that produces food exclusively for delivery, with no dine-in area, storefront, or walk-in customer service. Orders are placed through online platforms or apps and are collected by delivery couriers.

Business model

Ghost kitchens allow restaurateurs to operate delivery brands at lower cost than a conventional restaurant, since they need no prime retail location, dining room, or front-of-house staff. A single facility may house many separate "virtual brands", sometimes operated by the same staff from one kitchen, and large facilities rent kitchen space to multiple operators. The model expanded rapidly in the late 2010s and during the COVID-19 pandemic, when demand for food delivery surged.

Criticism

The model has drawn criticism on several grounds. Customers may not realise that several differently named brands on a delivery app share one kitchen, or that a listing imitates a well-known local restaurant. Concerns have also been raised about food-safety oversight, the quality of delivery-only food, the commissions charged by delivery platforms, and the effect on independent restaurants. Some cities have considered regulating the facilities, which are often located in industrial or warehouse districts.

Following the pandemic boom, several prominent ghost kitchen companies contracted or closed as delivery demand normalised and the economics of the model proved difficult.
