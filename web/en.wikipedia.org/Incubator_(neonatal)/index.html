Incubator (neonatal)

A neonatal incubator is an apparatus used to maintain environmental conditions suitable for a newborn baby, especially one born prematurely or ill. It provides a controlled environment of warmth, humidity and, where needed, oxygen, while protecting the infant from infection and allowing observation.

Design

A modern incubator is a transparent enclosed cot with servo-controlled heating that adjusts air temperature in response to the baby's skin temperature. Ports in the walls let staff handle the infant with minimal disturbance, and built-in monitors track temperature, oxygen and weight.

History

Incubators for infants were developed in late nineteenth-century France, adapting poultry-warming designs, and were famously exhibited to the public at fairs — exhibitions that, controversially, helped fund early neonatal care.

Use

Incubators are a central technology of the neonatal intensive care unit (NICU), used until a premature baby can maintain body temperature and feed independently.
