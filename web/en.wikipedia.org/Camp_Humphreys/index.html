Camp Humphreys (Korean: 캠프 험프리스), also known as United States Army Garrison Humphreys (USAG-H), is a United States Army garrison located near Pyeongtaek, South Korea. Following a major expansion completed in the late 2010s, it became the largest overseas U.S. military installation in the world.

History

The site began as a Japanese airfield built in 1919 and was used by U.S. forces after the Korean War; it was named for Chief Warrant Officer Benjamin K. Humphreys, a helicopter pilot killed in an accident in 1961. Under the Yongsan Relocation Plan and Land Partnership Plan, most U.S. forces in Seoul and north of the Han River relocated to Camp Humphreys, with the headquarters of United States Forces Korea and United Nations Command moving there in 2018.

Facilities

The expanded garrison hosts tens of thousands of soldiers, civilians and family members, with schools, a hospital, commissaries, housing towers and Desiderio Army Airfield, one of the busiest military airfields in Asia.
