Richard Steven Valenzuela (13 May 1941 – 3 February 1959), better known as Ritchie Valens, was an American guitarist, singer and songwriter. A rock and roll pioneer and a forefather of the Chicano rock movement, he recorded his hits during a career lasting only eight months.

Career

Valens was signed by Bob Keane of Del-Fi Records in 1958 and renamed to shorten his surname and broaden his appeal. His first single, "Come On, Let's Go", was a modest hit, followed by the self-penned ballad "Donna", which reached No. 2 on the Billboard Hot 100. Its B-side was "La Bamba", his adaptation of a Mexican folk song sung entirely in Spanish, which became his signature recording and one of the most influential songs in rock history.

Death

In the early hours of 3 February 1959, Valens was killed in a plane crash near Clear Lake, Iowa, along with fellow musicians Buddy Holly and J. P. "The Big Bopper" Richardson and pilot Roger Peterson. The date was later called "The Day the Music Died" in Don McLean's song "American Pie". Valens was 17 years old.

Legacy

Valens was the first Latino rock star and proved that Spanish-language material could succeed in the American market. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2001. His life was dramatised in the 1987 film La Bamba, starring Lou Diamond Phillips.
