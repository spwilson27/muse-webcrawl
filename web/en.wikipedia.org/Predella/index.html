Predella

The predella is the lowermost register of an altarpiece, painted with small narrative scenes.

In art history, a predella is the platform or step on which an altarpiece rests — in practice, the term refers to the painting or sculpture running along the frame at the bottom of a polyptych altarpiece. Predella panels typically carry small-scale narrative scenes related to the subject of the main panels above: episodes from the life of Christ, the Virgin, or the saints depicted in the principal registers.

Description

Predellas were a hallmark of Italian Gothic and Renaissance altarpieces from the 13th to the 16th century. Because they sat close to the viewer's eye level, artists filled them with lively, detailed storytelling, sometimes with more freedom and experimentation than the formal main panels allowed. Famous examples include the predella of Duccio's Maestà (1308–1311), the scenes beneath Gentile da Fabriano's Adoration of the Magi (1423), and those of Fra Angelico's altarpieces. Many predella panels were later separated from their frames and dispersed among museums, and reconstructing their original sequences is a notable branch of connoisseurship.
