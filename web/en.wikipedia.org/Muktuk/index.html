Muktuk


Type: Traditional food
Region: Arctic (Alaska, northern Canada, Greenland, Chukotka)
Main ingredients: Whale skin and blubber (beluga, bowhead, narwhal)

Muktuk (also spelled maktak or muktuk in various Inuit and Yupik languages) is a traditional food of Arctic Indigenous peoples consisting of frozen whale skin and blubber. It is most commonly made from beluga whale, and also from bowhead whale and narwhal.

Preparation and consumption

Muktuk is usually eaten raw and frozen, cut into small cubes; the skin has a rubbery, chewy texture and the attached blubber is soft and rich. It may also be diced, breaded, deep-fried, or pickled. It is traditionally shared at community feasts and celebrations such as whaling festivals in Alaska (for example Nalukataq among the Iñupiat).

Nutrition

Muktuk is a rich source of vitamin C — historically an important defence against scurvy in Arctic diets — as well as vitamin D and omega-3 fatty acids. The skin contains more vitamin C per weight than some citrus fruits.

Health concerns

Because blubber concentrates pollutants, muktuk from some regions can contain mercury and persistent organic pollutants such as PCBs, which accumulate in marine food webs. Beluga from certain areas has at times shown contaminant levels prompting dietary advice, and foodborne botulism has been associated with fermented or improperly stored marine mammal products. Subsistence hunting of whales for muktuk is regulated and remains culturally and nutritionally central to many Arctic communities.
