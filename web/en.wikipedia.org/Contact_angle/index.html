Contact angle

The contact angle is the angle, conventionally measured through the liquid, where a liquid–vapour interface meets a solid surface. It quantifies the wettability of a solid by a liquid via the Young equation, which balances the interfacial tensions at the three-phase contact line.

Young equation

For an ideal smooth surface, Young's equation relates the equilibrium contact angle θ to the solid–vapour (γSV), solid–liquid (γSL) and liquid–vapour (γLV) surface tensions: γSV = γSL + γLV cos θ. Angles below 90° indicate good wetting (hydrophilic surfaces for water); angles above 90° indicate poor wetting (hydrophobic), and angles above 150° are termed superhydrophobic.

Hysteresis and real surfaces

Real surfaces are rough and chemically heterogeneous, so the advancing and receding contact angles differ — a phenomenon called contact angle hysteresis, described by the Wenzel and Cassie–Baxter models. Hysteresis explains why droplets can cling to tilted surfaces.

Measurement and applications

Contact angles are measured with goniometers using the sessile drop method or, for fibres and powders, the Wilhelmy plate method. The measurement is central to coatings, printing, adhesives, microfluidics, enhanced oil recovery, and the design of self-cleaning and anti-fouling surfaces.
