The Yuan dynasty, officially the Great Yuan, was a Mongol-led imperial dynasty of China and a successor state to the Mongol Empire following its fragmentation. Founded by Kublai Khan, a grandson of Genghis Khan, it ruled China from the proclamation of the dynastic name in 1271 and the conquest of the Southern Song in 1279 until its overthrow by Zhu Yuanzhang's forces in 1368.

History

Kublai moved the Mongol capital to Khanbaliq (modern Beijing) and adopted Chinese-style imperial institutions while maintaining a hierarchy that privileged Mongols and their Central and West Asian allies over northern and southern Chinese. The Yuan completed the conquest of the Song in 1279, unifying China proper after centuries of division, but its expeditions against Japan (1274 and 1281) and Vietnam failed. The dynasty maintained vast trade networks across Eurasia under the Pax Mongolica; the Venetian traveller Marco Polo famously served at Kublai's court.

Society and culture

Yuan rule saw advances in astronomy, medicine, and cartography, the development of Chinese drama (the zaju plays of Guan Hanqing) and landscape painting, and the widespread use of paper money, whose over-issue contributed to inflation. Population was classified into a four-tier hierarchy: Mongols, Semu (various western and central Asian peoples), Han (northern Chinese), and Southerners.

Decline and fall

From the 1340s, floods, plague, inflation, and fiscal crisis provoked widespread rebellion. Zhu Yuanzhang emerged victorious from the rebel movements, captured Dadu in 1368, and founded the Ming dynasty; the Yuan court fled north to Mongolia, where it continued as the Northern Yuan.
