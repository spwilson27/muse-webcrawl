The Tabernacle Choir at Temple Square, formerly known as the Mormon Tabernacle Choir, is an American choir affiliated with the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. It has performed in the Salt Lake Tabernacle for over 100 years. The choir is composed of 360 volunteer singers and is accompanied by the 11,623-pipe Tabernacle Organ. Its weekly broadcast, Music & the Spoken Word, is the longest-running continuous network broadcast in radio history.

History

The choir was organized shortly after the arrival of the Mormon pioneers in the Salt Lake Valley in 1847, when a small choir sang at a conference of the church in August of that year. As the Salt Lake Tabernacle was completed in 1867, the choir took up residence there, and its size and reputation grew steadily. By the late 19th century it had become one of the largest choirs in the United States and undertook concert tours, including an appearance at the World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago in 1893.

On 15 July 1929, the choir began its weekly radio broadcast, Music & the Spoken Word, which continues to this day and is carried by hundreds of radio and television stations worldwide. The choir won a Grammy Award in 1960 for its recording of "The Battle Hymn of the Republic" with the Philadelphia Orchestra under Eugene Ormandy.

Name change

In October 2018, following guidance from church president Russell M. Nelson emphasizing the full name of the church, the choir's name was changed from the "Mormon Tabernacle Choir" to the "Tabernacle Choir at Temple Square". The change aligned the choir's identity with its home at Temple Square in Salt Lake City.

Recordings and performances

The choir has made more than 200 recordings, including albums that have achieved gold and platinum status. It has performed at the inaugurations of several United States presidents, at world's fairs, and in concert halls around the world. The choir regularly tours internationally and performs with its companion ensemble, the Orchestra at Temple Square, founded in 1999.
