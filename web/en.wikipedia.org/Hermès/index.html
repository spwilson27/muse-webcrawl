Hermès International, commonly known as Hermès, is a French luxury goods company founded in 1837 by Thierry Hermès as a harness and saddle workshop in Paris. Still controlled by the founding family, it is renowned for its handcrafted leather goods, silk scarves, and iconic handbags such as the Kelly and the Birkin.

History

The house won acclaim for its saddlery, supplying European royalty and winning prizes at the Paris Expositions of 1855 and 1867. Under Charles-Émile Hermès and his successors, the firm expanded into leather handbags, introducing the zip fastener to France and, in the 1920s, luggage and clothing. The silk carré scarf debuted in 1937, and the brand's signature orange packaging became standard in the 1940s.

Iconic products

The Kelly bag, named for Grace Kelly, and the Birkin bag, created in 1984 for actress Jane Birkin, are among the most sought-after accessories in the world, with waiting lists and strong resale values. Hermès is distinctive in the luxury industry for its artisanal production model — most leather items are made by a single craftsman in French ateliers — and for resisting licensing and mass production.

Company

Hermès remains majority-owned by descendants of the founder through a family holding structure, which successfully resisted a creeping takeover attempt by LVMH in the early 2010s. The group operates hundreds of stores worldwide and trades on Euronext Paris.
