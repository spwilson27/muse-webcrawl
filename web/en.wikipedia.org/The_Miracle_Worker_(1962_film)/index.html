The Miracle Worker (1962 film)

The Miracle Worker
Directed by: Arthur Penn
Screenplay by: William Gibson
Based on: The Miracle Worker (play) by William Gibson
Starring: Anne Bancroft, Patty Duke
Release date: July 28, 1962
Running time: 106 minutes

The Miracle Worker is a 1962 American biographical film directed by Arthur Penn, based on William Gibson's 1959 play of the same name. The play and film are based on Helen Keller's 1903 autobiography The Story of My Life and dramatize the relationship between Keller, a blind and deaf child, and her teacher Anne Sullivan, who breaks through Helen's isolation and teaches her to communicate.

Plot

In 1880s Alabama, Captain and Kate Keller despair over their daughter Helen, blind and deaf since infancy and given to violent tantrums. They hire Anne Sullivan, a young, half-blind graduate of the Perkins School for the Blind, as governess. Sullivan's fierce discipline collides with the family's indulgence, culminating in a prolonged physical struggle at the breakfast table. Given two weeks alone with Helen in a garden house, Sullivan teaches her the manual alphabet; the breakthrough comes at the water pump, when Helen finally connects the spelled word "water" with the substance flowing over her hand and eagerly demands the names of everything around her.

Cast and production

Anne Bancroft and Patty Duke reprised their Broadway roles as Sullivan and Keller. Director Arthur Penn had also directed the stage production. The film's famous nine-minute breakfast-table fight scene was shot over five days and required padding under the actresses' costumes.

Reception and awards

The film was a critical and commercial success. At the 35th Academy Awards, Bancroft won Best Actress and Duke, at sixteen, won Best Supporting Actress, then the youngest competitive Oscar winner. The film is regularly included on lists of the most inspiring American movies.
