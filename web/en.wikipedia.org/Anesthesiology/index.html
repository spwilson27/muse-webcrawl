Anesthesiology

FocusPerioperative care; anesthesia; pain medicine; critical care
SpecialistAnesthesiologist (anaesthetist)
Key techniquesGeneral, regional and local anesthesia; airway management

Anesthesiology (or anaesthesiology in Commonwealth English) is the medical specialty concerned with the total perioperative care of patients before, during and after surgery. It encompasses anesthesia, intensive care medicine, critical emergency medicine and pain medicine.

Scope of practice

Anesthesiologists assess patients before surgery, devise and deliver anesthetic plans — general, regional (such as spinal or epidural blocks) or local — monitor vital functions during operations, manage the airway and ventilation, and supervise recovery. Many also work in intensive care units, chronic pain clinics, obstetric anesthesia and emergency medicine.

History

The specialty began with the public demonstration of ether anesthesia by William T. G. Morton in Boston in 1846, followed by chloroform in Britain under John Snow. Advances in pharmacology, monitoring (pulse oximetry, capnography) and training transformed anesthesia from a technical task into a full medical discipline over the twentieth century.

Training

In the United States, anesthesiologists complete medical school and a four-year residency; in the United Kingdom, anaesthetists undertake a long postgraduate training programme under the Royal College of Anaesthetists. In some settings, nurse anesthetists and anesthesiologist assistants deliver care within physician-led teams.
