Gandhāran Buddhist texts

RegionGandhāra (northwest Pakistan / eastern Afghanistan)
LanguageGāndhārī Prakrit
ScriptKharoṣṭhī
MaterialBirch bark scrolls; palm leaf; schist
Date range1st century BCE – c. 4th century CE

The Gandhāran Buddhist texts are the oldest Buddhist manuscripts yet discovered, dating from about the 1st century BCE to the 4th century CE. They were produced in the ancient region of Gandhāra in northwest South Asia and are written in the Gāndhārī language using the Kharoṣṭhī script, mostly on birch bark. The corpus represents the literature of the Dharmaguptaka school and possibly other early Buddhist schools.

Discovery

Major finds emerged in the 1990s, including the British Library scrolls (acquired in 1994, some 80 fragments from 13 scrolls found in clay pots near Hadda, Afghanistan), the Senior scrolls, and the Bajaur collection. Further manuscripts, such as a Gāndhārī version of the Perfection of Wisdom from Split Rock and scrolls held in the Schøyen and Hirayama collections, continue to surface.

Contents and significance

The texts include sutras paralleling parts of the Pali Canon, commentaries, avadānas, abhidharma treatises, and early Mahāyāna works such as a Gāndhārī Prajñāpāramitā — among the oldest Mahāyāna manuscripts known. Because they predate most surviving Sanskrit and Chinese witnesses, the Gandhāran texts have reshaped scholarship on how Buddhist literature was composed, transmitted, and translated.
