SNCF Réseau is the French railway infrastructure manager, a subsidiary of the SNCF Group responsible for the operation, maintenance and development of the national rail network (réseau ferré national) of France. It manages access to the network for railway undertakings, allocates train paths, sets infrastructure charges, and oversees traffic management and signalling across roughly 28,000 kilometres of line.

SNCF Réseau maintains the French national rail network, including the LGV high-speed lines.

History

Under European railway legislation requiring the separation of infrastructure management from train operations, France created Réseau Ferré de France (RFF) in 1997 as owner of the network, while SNCF retained day-to-day maintenance under contract. The arrangement was widely criticised as inefficient, and the railway reform law of 4 August 2014 reintegrated infrastructure management within the SNCF group. On 1 January 2015 RFF, SNCF Infrastructure and the traffic management directorate were merged to form SNCF Réseau. A further reform in 2020 converted the SNCF group into a société anonyme structure with SNCF Réseau as one of its principal subsidiaries.

Activities

SNCF Réseau is responsible for maintaining track, catenary, signalling and level crossings, managing rail traffic in real time, and planning the regeneration and modernisation of the network, including the rollout of the European Rail Traffic Management System (ERTMS). It is financed by track access charges paid by train operators, public subsidies and its own borrowing, and carries a large debt historically associated with network investment.
