Special Boat Service

Special Boat Service
Abbreviation: SBS
Branch: Royal Navy / Royal Marines
Role: Special forces (maritime)
Motto: "By Strength and Guile"
Base: Poole, Dorset

The Special Boat Service (SBS) is the special forces unit of the United Kingdom's Royal Navy. Formed during the Second World War from the Army Special Boat Section and Royal Marines raiding parties, it specialises in maritime special operations — reconnaissance, raiding, counter-terrorism and operations in littoral and riverine environments. Together with the Special Air Service (SAS), Special Reconnaissance Regiment and Special Forces Support Group, it forms part of United Kingdom Special Forces (UKSF).

History

The SBS traces its origins to 1940, performing canoe-borne raids along the Mediterranean and Aegean coasts and in the Far East. In the post-war period it fought in Korea, Borneo during the Indonesia–Malaysia confrontation, and Oman. SBS teams conducted reconnaissance before the amphibious landings in the Falklands War of 1982 and operated in the Gulf War, Sierra Leone, Afghanistan and Iraq; they also maintain a maritime counter-terrorism role, famously retaking the container ship Grande Tema from stowaways in the Thames Estuary in 2018.

Selection and organisation

Recruitment is drawn mainly from the Royal Marines. Candidates attend the joint UKSF selection course alongside SAS candidates, with SBS aspirants additionally qualifying as Swimmer Canoeists. The unit is organised into squadrons including maritime counter-terrorism and general-purpose squadrons, based at Poole in Dorset.
