Wovoka

Wovoka (c. 1856 – September 20, 1932), also known by his adopted name Jack Wilson, was a Northern Paiute religious leader who founded the Ghost Dance movement of 1889–1890. His vision of a peaceful renewal of the world spread rapidly among Native American peoples of the western United States.

Life

Wovoka was born in the Smith Valley area of western Nevada, the son of a medicine man known as Tavibo. After his father's death, the young Wovoka worked for a rancher named David Wilson, from whom he took the name Jack Wilson and encountered Christianity, knowledge that influenced his later teachings.

The vision and the Ghost Dance

During a solar eclipse on January 1, 1889, while ill with fever, Wovoka experienced a vision in which he was taken to heaven, saw God and the spirits of the dead, and was shown a renewed earth. He taught that if Native peoples lived honestly, worked, avoided fighting and performed a round dance for five days, the dead would return, game would be restored, and the world would be made whole — by many later accounts a peaceful transformation, though some followers, notably among the Lakota, interpreted the message in more militant terms and adopted "ghost shirts" said to stop bullets.

Delegations from dozens of tribes visited Wovoka and carried the dance across the Plains. Alarmed officials moved to suppress the movement among the Lakota, contributing to the killing of Sitting Bull and the Wounded Knee Massacre of December 29, 1890. Wovoka himself preached non-violence throughout, and survived; he lived quietly in Nevada until his death in 1932, while Ghost Dance practice persisted in some communities.
