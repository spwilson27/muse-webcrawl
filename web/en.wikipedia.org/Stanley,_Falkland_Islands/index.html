Stanley, Falkland Islands

Stanley, capital of the Falkland Islands, on East Falkland.

Stanley (also known as Port Stanley, and in Spanish as Puerto Argentino) is the capital city of the Falkland Islands, a British Overseas Territory in the South Atlantic Ocean. It is located on the island of East Falkland, on a north-facing slope in one of the wettest parts of the archipelago, and is home to the majority of the islands' population, numbering roughly 2,500 people.

History

The settlement was founded by the British in 1843 and formally became the islands' capital in 1845, named after Edward Smith-Stanley, 14th Earl of Derby, then Secretary of State for War and the Colonies. Stanley grew as a deep-water port serving ships transiting Cape Horn, and as a base for whaling and sealing. In 1982 it was occupied by Argentine forces during the Falklands War and renamed Puerto Argentino; it was returned to British control at the end of the war.

Features

Stanley is home to the Falkland Islands Museum, Government House, Christ Church Cathedral (notable for its whalebone arch), and the islands' government offices. Its economy is based on government services, fishing, tourism, and servicing visiting ships and cruise passengers.
