Kawasaki Heavy Industries

Kawasaki Heavy Industries Ltd. (KHI) is a Japanese public multinational corporation manufacturing motorcycles, engines, heavy equipment, aerospace and defense equipment, rolling stock and ships, headquartered in Chūō, Kobe and Minato, Tokyo.

Founded in 1896 by Shōzō Kawasaki as a shipyard, the company diversified into steel, railway cars, aircraft and motorcycles. Its motorcycle division is known for the Ninja sport bikes. KHI is one of Japan's major heavy-industry groups, supplying liquefied hydrogen carriers, submarines for the Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force, helicopters, and components for Boeing and Airbus aircraft.

History

Shōzō Kawasaki established the Kawasaki Tsukiji Shipyard in Tokyo in 1878; it was incorporated as Kawasaki Dockyard Co., Ltd. in 1896. The company expanded through the Russo-Japanese War and both world wars, later merging its rolling stock, aircraft and engine operations into a single heavy industries group in 1969.

Divisions

Principal businesses include Aerospace Systems, Energy Solution & Marine Engineering, Precision Machinery and Robot, Rolling Stock, and Powersports & Engine.
