Beylik

A beylik (Turkish: beylik, beglik; Ottoman Turkish: بكلك) is a small Turkish principality governed by a bey, roughly equivalent to an emirate or petty lordship. The term is used primarily for two waves of small states in medieval Anatolia, and also for the regencies in North Africa (Algiers, Tunis, and Tripoli) under loose Ottoman suzerainty.

First period

The first beyliks appeared in the 11th century along the frontier (the Uc) between the Seljuk Turks and the Byzantine Empire, as Turkish warriors settled borderlands captured from Byzantium. Beyliks such as those of the Danishmendids, the Mengujekids, the Saltukids, and the Artuqids issued their own coinage and patronized early Anatolian Turkish culture.

Second period: the Anatolian beyliks

After the Battle of Köse Dağ (1243) placed the Seljuk Sultanate of Rûm under Mongol domination, and especially after the sultanate's collapse around 1308, dozens of independent beyliks sprang up across Anatolia. Among the most important were the Karamanids, the Germiyanids, the Aydınids, the Sarukhanids, the Menteshe, the Jandarids (Candaroğlu), the Dulkadirids, the Ramadanids — and the small frontier beylik of Osmanoğlu, the Ottomans, in northwestern Anatolia.

Over the 14th and 15th centuries the Ottomans gradually absorbed or annexed the other beyliks, a process completed under Mehmed II and Selim I, unifying Anatolia under the Ottoman Empire.
