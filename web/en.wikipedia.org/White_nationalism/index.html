White nationalism

White nationalism is a type of nationalism which espouses the belief that white people are a race and should maintain a white ethnostate with a dominant white demographic and culture. Its proponents identify with and advocate a "white nation", and it overlaps substantially with white supremacy and white separatism. Critics and scholars generally describe white nationalist beliefs as rooted in racism and pseudoscientific claims about race.

Beliefs

White nationalists typically argue that immigration, multiculturalism and declining white birth rates threaten white populations — a set of claims associated with the "white genocide" conspiracy theory and the related "Great Replacement" theory. Mainstream scholars reject the premise that races form a hierarchy or that the policies advocateded follow from mainstream science, and monitoring organizations such as the Southern Poverty Law Center classify many white nationalist groups as hate groups.

History

The ideology draws on 19th- and 20th-century racial nationalism, eugenics, and movements such as the Ku Klux Klan and apartheid-era politics. In the 2010s, white nationalist ideas gained visibility through the alt-right and online radicalization, and adherents have been responsible for acts of mass violence, including the 2011 Norway attacks and the 2019 Christchurch mosque shootings, whose perpetrators cited replacement theories.
