Natal chart

A natal chart — also called a birth chart or nativity — is an astrological chart or diagram representing the positions of the Sun, Moon, planets and astrological aspects at the exact time of an individual's birth, as seen from the place of birth. In Western astrology it is the foundation of the horoscope from which an astrologer interprets personality and life events.

Components

The chart is drawn as a circle divided into twelve signs of the zodiac and twelve houses. Key features include the Ascendant (rising sign) on the eastern horizon, the Midheaven (MC) at the top, the positions of the planets in signs and houses, and the aspects — angular relationships such as conjunction, opposition, trine and square — between them. An ephemeris or computer software is used to calculate positions for the date, time and coordinates of birth.

Interpretation

Astrologers interpret the chart as a symbolic map of character: the Sun sign is said to describe core identity, the Moon sign emotional life, and the Ascendant outward manner. House placements indicate the life areas where planetary influences are said to fall, while aspects describe how the "energies" interact.

Criticism

Scientists and sceptics regard natal astrology as a pseudoscience: controlled tests, notably Shawn Carlson's 1985 double-blind study published in Nature, found astrologers unable to match natal charts to personality profiles better than chance.
