Alkali


Sodium hydroxide pellets, a common alkali.

In chemistry, an alkali is a basic, ionic salt of an alkali metal or an alkaline earth metal. An alkali can also be defined as a base that dissolves in water. A solution of a soluble base has a pH greater than 7.0. The adjective alkaline is commonly used in English as a synonym for basic, especially for bases soluble in water.

Etymology and history

The word "alkali" comes from the Arabic al qalīy meaning "calcined ashes", referring to the original source of alkaline substances: the ashes of burned plants, rich in sodium or potassium carbonates. Historically, alkalis such as potash and soda ash were essential to soap, glass, and textile manufacture.

Properties and examples

Alkalis feel slippery in solution (because they saponify fats on the skin), turn red litmus blue, and neutralize acids. Common examples include sodium hydroxide (caustic soda), potassium hydroxide (caustic potash), calcium hydroxide (slaked lime), and aqueous ammonia. Strong alkalis are corrosive and must be handled with care.

Uses

Alkalis are used in soap and detergent production, paper making, biodiesel, food processing (such as nixtamalization and pretzel browning), water treatment, and as chemical reagents. Alkali soils and alkali lakes are named for their high content of such salts.
