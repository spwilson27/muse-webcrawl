Acid–base reaction


Neutralization of an acid by a base.

An acid–base reaction is a chemical reaction that occurs between an acid and a base. It can be used to determine pH via titration. Several theoretical frameworks provide alternative conceptions of the reaction mechanisms and their application to related problems; these include the Arrhenius, Brønsted–Lowry, and Lewis theories of acids and bases.

Common formulations

In the Arrhenius formulation, acids release hydrogen ions and bases release hydroxide ions in water, and their reaction — neutralization — produces water and a salt. In the Brønsted–Lowry formulation, the reaction is a transfer of a proton from the acid to the base, producing conjugate base and conjugate acid partners. In the Lewis formulation, the base donates an electron pair to the acid, forming a coordinate covalent bond in an adduct.

Neutralization and titration

When an acid and a base react in stoichiometrically equal amounts, the resulting solution may be neutral (pH 7) if the acid and base are both strong; salts of weak acids or bases hydrolyze to give mildly basic or acidic solutions. Titration uses a solution of known concentration, added gradually with an indicator or pH meter, to determine the concentration of an unknown acid or base, with the equivalence point marking complete reaction.

Importance

Acid–base reactions underlie buffer solutions, antacid medicines, soil treatment, industrial synthesis, and the regulation of blood pH in physiology.
