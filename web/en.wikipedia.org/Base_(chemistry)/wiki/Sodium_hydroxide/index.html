Sodium hydroxide


Sodium hydroxide pellets.

Sodium hydroxide, also known as lye and caustic soda, is an inorganic compound with the formula NaOH. It is a white solid ionic compound consisting of sodium cations Na+ and hydroxide anions OH−. Sodium hydroxide is a highly corrosive base and alkali that decomposes lipids and proteins at ambient temperatures and may cause severe chemical burns. It is highly soluble in water, and readily absorbs moisture and carbon dioxide from the air.

Production

Most sodium hydroxide is produced by the chloralkali electrolysis of sodium chloride brine, which also yields chlorine gas and hydrogen. Historically it was made by treating sodium carbonate with calcium hydroxide (causticization), and before that lye was obtained from wood ashes.

Uses

It is one of the highest-volume industrial chemicals. Uses include the manufacture of pulp and paper, textiles, drinking water treatment, soaps and detergents (via saponification of fats), biodiesel production, drain cleaners, and food processing, including curing olives, making pretzels and lye rolls, and processing cocoa. In the laboratory it is a standard strong base for titrations and synthesis.

Safety

Sodium hydroxide is severely corrosive to skin and eyes; contact can cause deep burns, and solutions feel slippery as they dissolve skin oils. It reacts exothermically with water and acids and attacks some metals such as aluminium, releasing hydrogen.
