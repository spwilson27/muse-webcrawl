Buffer solution


Buffers maintain pH despite added acid or base.

A buffer solution is an aqueous solution consisting of a mixture of a weak acid and its conjugate base, or vice versa. Its pH changes very little when a small amount of strong acid or base is added to it. Buffer solutions are used as a means of keeping pH at a nearly constant value in a wide variety of chemical applications.

Principles of buffering action

When a small amount of strong acid is added to a buffer, the conjugate base component reacts with the added hydrogen ions to form the weak acid, limiting the fall in pH. When a strong base is added, the weak acid component donates protons to neutralize hydroxide ions. The relationship between buffer pH and the concentrations of the two components is described by the Henderson–Hasselbalch equation: pH = pKa + log([A−]/[HA]). A buffer works best when the two components are present in comparable amounts, near the pKa of the acid.

Buffer capacity

Buffer capacity is a quantitative measure of the resistance of a buffer solution to changes in pH upon addition of an acid or base. It depends on the total concentration of the buffering components.

Applications

Common laboratory buffers include acetate, phosphate, and Tris buffers. In nature, the bicarbonate buffer system regulates blood pH, seawater is buffered by carbonate and borate systems, and buffers are used in fermentation, pharmaceuticals, and calibration of pH meters.
