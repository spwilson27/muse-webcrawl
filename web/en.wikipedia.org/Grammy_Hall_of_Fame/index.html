The Grammy Hall of Fame is a hall of fame established in 1973 by the Recording Academy (then the National Academy of Recording Arts and Sciences) to honour recordings of lasting qualitative or historical significance. Unlike the annual Grammy Awards, which recognise current releases, the Hall of Fame celebrates works from the entire history of recorded music.

Selection

Honorees are selected annually by a special member committee of eminent and knowledgeable professionals from all branches of the recording arts, with final approval by the Recording Academy's Board of Trustees. Recordings of all genres — singles and albums alike — are eligible once they are at least 25 years old. Inducted recordings span from early 20th-century discs to landmark works of jazz, blues, country, rock, classical, and spoken word.

Inductees

More than a thousand recordings have been inducted since the Hall's founding, including works such as "Strange Fruit" by Billie Holiday, Kind of Blue by Miles Davis, "Respect" by Aretha Franklin, and Abbey Road by the Beatles. The full collection of inducted recordings is maintained by the Grammy Museum in Los Angeles, which displays Hall of Fame exhibits as part of its permanent presentation of recording history.
