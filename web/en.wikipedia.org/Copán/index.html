Copán

Copán is an archaeological site of the Maya civilisation in western Honduras, near the border with Guatemala. It was the capital city of a major Classic-period Maya kingdom from the 5th to the 9th centuries AD.

History

The dynasty of Copán was founded in 426 by K'inich Yax K'uk' Mo'. The city reached its height under rulers such as Uaxaclajuun Ub'aah K'awiil ("18 Rabbit"), who commissioned many of the famous carved stelae, and Waxaklajuun Ub'aah K'awiil's successors, who built the Hieroglyphic Stairway—the longest known Maya hieroglyphic text, with over 2,000 glyphs. The kingdom declined in the 9th century amid dynastic crisis and environmental stress.

The site

Highlights include the Great Plaza with its portrait stelae, the Acropolis, a large ball court, and the Rosalila temple preserved beneath later construction. Copán was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1980, and its emblem glyph identifies the polity in inscriptions across the Maya region.
