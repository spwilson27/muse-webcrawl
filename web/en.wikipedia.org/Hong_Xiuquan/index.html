Hong Xiuquan

Hong Xiuquan (1 January 1814 – 1 June 1864) was a Hakka Chinese revolutionary who led the Taiping Rebellion against the Qing dynasty. Believing himself to be the younger brother of Jesus Christ, he founded the Taiping Heavenly Kingdom, which at its height ruled much of southern China.

Early life and visions

Hong was born into a Hakka family in Guangdong and repeatedly failed the imperial civil-service examinations. After his fourth failure in 1843 he read a Christian pamphlet he had received years earlier and interpreted an earlier feverish vision as a visit to Heaven, where God the Father revealed that Hong was Jesus's younger brother, charged with destroying the demons — identified with the Qing rulers.

The Taiping Rebellion

Preaching in Guangxi, Hong gathered converts into the God Worshipping Society. In 1851 he proclaimed the Taiping Heavenly Kingdom and rose in the Jintian Uprising. Taiping armies captured Nanjing in 1853 and made it their capital, Tianjing. The movement preached a radical programme: communal property, equality of men and women in principle, and bans on opium, foot-binding and the queue.

Decline and death

Internal purges, notably the Tianjing Incident of 1856, decapitated the leadership. Qing forces under Zeng Guofan's Xiang Army, aided by foreign-officered units, ground the kingdom down; Hong died in June 1864, weeks before Nanjing fell. The rebellion was among the deadliest conflicts in history, with deaths commonly estimated at 20–30 million.
