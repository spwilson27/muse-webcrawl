Country: India

Capital: Dispur

Largest city: Guwahati

Area: 78,438 km²

Official language: Assamese

Assam is a state in northeastern India, situated south of the eastern Himalayas along the Brahmaputra and Barak river valleys. It is known for Assam tea, Assam silk, petroleum resources, and rich biodiversity, including the one-horned Indian rhinoceros of Kaziranga National Park.

Geography

The state is dominated by the floodplain of the Brahmaputra River, one of the world's largest rivers, whose seasonal flooding shapes agriculture and settlement. Assam borders Bhutan and Arunachal Pradesh to the north, Nagaland and Manipur to the east, Meghalaya, Tripura, and Mizoram to the south, and West Bengal and Bangladesh to the west.

History

The region corresponds in part to the ancient kingdom of Kamarupa. From the 13th century the Ahom kingdom ruled much of the Brahmaputra valley for about six hundred years, famously defeating repeated Mughal invasions, including at the Battle of Saraighat in 1671. Assam came under British rule by the Treaty of Yandabo in 1826, after Burmese invasions, and its tea industry was developed during the colonial period. After Indian independence in 1947 the state's territory was progressively reorganised, giving rise to several other northeastern states.

Economy and culture

Assam is one of the world's largest tea-producing regions, and its economy also rests on rice, oil and natural gas, and handloom weaving. The spring festival of Bihu is the state's principal cultural celebration, tied to the agricultural cycle.
