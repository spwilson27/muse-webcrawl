Giacomo Puccini

Giacomo Antonio Domenico Michele Secondo Maria Puccini (22 December 1858 – 29 November 1924) was an Italian composer known primarily for his operas. Regarded as the greatest proponent of Italian opera after Giuseppe Verdi, he carried the tradition of Italian opera in the style of the late 19th century into the 20th, and his works — including La bohème, Tosca, Madama Butterfly, and Turandot — are among the most frequently performed in the standard repertory.

Life and career

Puccini was born in Lucca into a family of church musicians. After seeing Verdi's Aida in Pisa, he resolved to compose opera and studied at the Milan Conservatory, where his teachers included Amilcare Ponchielli. His first mature success, Manon Lescaut (1893), was followed by La bohème (1896, conducted at its premiere by Arturo Toscanini), Tosca (1900), and Madama Butterfly (1904), whose disastrous La Scala premiere he revised into a triumph. Working with librettists Luigi Illica and Giuseppe Giacosa, he combined verismo drama with sumptuous orchestration and memorable melody.

Later works

La fanciulla del West premiered at the Metropolitan Opera in New York in 1910, followed by Il trittico (1918), a triptych of one-act operas including Gianni Schicchi. At his death from throat cancer in Brussels in 1924, Puccini left Turandot unfinished; it was completed by Franco Alfano and premiered in 1926. Its aria "Nessun dorma" became one of the best-known pieces of operatic music worldwide.
