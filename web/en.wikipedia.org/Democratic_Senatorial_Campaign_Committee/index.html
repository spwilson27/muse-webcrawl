The Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee (DSCC) is the Democratic Hill committee for the United States Senate. It is the only organization solely dedicated to electing Democrats to the U.S. Senate and, more broadly, to the goal of a Democratic majority in the chamber. The DSCC's current chair, appointed by Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer for the 2026 cycle, is Senator Kirsten Gillibrand of New York.

Role

The committee recruits candidates, raises funds, provides campaign infrastructure and research, and makes independent expenditures and coordinated spending in competitive Senate races. Together with its Republican counterpart, the National Republican Senatorial Committee (NRSC), it is one of the party "Hill committees," alongside the Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee (DCCC) for the House of Representatives. Fundraising by the DSCC and NRSC typically runs into the hundreds of millions of dollars per two-year election cycle.

History

Senatorial campaign committees date to the era after the Seventeenth Amendment introduced direct election of senators in 1913. The modern DSCC grew in importance with the professionalization of Senate campaigns in the later 20th century. Notable recent cycles include 2006, when the DSCC under Chuck Schumer helped Democrats retake the Senate majority, and 2020–2022, when the committee under Gary Peters defended and then expanded a narrow Democratic majority.

List of chairs

Chairs are Democratic senators appointed by the party's Senate leader; recent chairs include Robert Menendez (2009–2011), Patty Murray (2011–2013), Michael Bennet (2013–2015), Jon Tester (2015–2017), Chris Van Hollen (2017–2019), Catherine Cortez Masto (2019–2021), and Gary Peters (2021–2025).
