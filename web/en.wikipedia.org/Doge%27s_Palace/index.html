Doge's Palace

LocationSt Mark's Square, Venice, Italy
Construction started1340 (present building)
Architectural styleVenetian Gothic
Current useMuseum

The Doge's Palace (Italian: Palazzo Ducale) is a palace built in Venetian Gothic style, and one of the main landmarks of the city of Venice in northern Italy. The palace was the residence of the Doge of Venice, the supreme authority of the former Venetian Republic, and housed the Republic's government offices, courtrooms, and prisons.

History

A first palace stood on the site from the 9th century; the present building was constructed principally from 1340 onwards, with the waterfront façade and the Porta della Carta following in the 15th century. Fires in 1574 and 1577 destroyed major interiors, which were rebuilt with decoration by Tintoretto, Veronese, and other masters. After the fall of the Republic in 1797 the palace served administrative functions under French and Austrian rule.

Interior

Highlights include the vast Sala del Maggior Consiglio (Hall of the Great Council), decorated with Tintoretto's Paradise, among the largest oil paintings in the world, and the sequence of state rooms and the Doge's apartments. The palace connects to the New Prison across the Rio di Palazzo by the Bridge of Sighs.

Museum

The palace became a museum in 1923 and is now part of the network of Venice civic museums, visited by well over a million people each year.
