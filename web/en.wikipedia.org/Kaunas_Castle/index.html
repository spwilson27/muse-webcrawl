Kaunas Castle

Kaunas Castle (Lithuanian: Kauno pilis) is a medieval castle in Kaunas, Lithuania. Located at the confluence of the Nemunas and Neris rivers, it is considered the oldest stone castle in Lithuania and one of the oldest buildings in the city.

History

Archaeologists estimate that the castle was built in the middle of the 14th century in the Gothic style. The fortress was constructed to resist the attacks of the Teutonic Knights, who sought to control the Nemunas river valley and expand into Lithuanian territory. In 1362 the castle was besieged and destroyed by the Teutonic Order after a three-week siege; it was the first major castle taken by the Order in Lithuania.

The castle was rebuilt in the early 15th century during the reign of Vytautas the Great. After the Battle of Grunwald (1410) removed the Teutonic threat, the castle lost its military importance and was repurposed for administrative and residential functions. In the late 16th century it was partially rebuilt, but floods from the Neris and subsequent wars damaged the structure, and by the 18th century much of it had fallen into ruin.

Present day

Today roughly a third of the castle survives, including its distinctive round tower. The castle houses a museum and hosts cultural events; the surrounding Santaka Park and the nearby old town make it one of Kaunas's principal landmarks. A bronze statue of Vytis, the Lithuanian knight, stands near the castle grounds.
