Posterior interosseous nerve

The posterior interosseous nerve (or dorsal interosseous nerve) is a nerve in the forearm. It is the deep branch (continuation) of the radial nerve after it passes through the supinator muscle. It supplies motor function to the extensor muscles of the forearm and contributes articular and sensory fibres to the wrist joint, though it carries no cutaneous sensation.

FromRadial nerve (deep branch), C7–C8
Clinical signFinger drop without wrist drop in PIN syndrome

Course

The deep branch of the radial nerve pierces the supinator (passing beneath the arcade of Frohse), winds around the neck of the radius, and emerges in the posterior compartment of the forearm as the posterior interosseous nerve. It descends on the interosseous membrane, accompanied by the posterior interosseous artery, and ends in a pseudoganglion on the back of the wrist.

Muscles supplied

As it travels, it supplies the extensor digitorum, extensor digiti minimi, extensor carpi ulnaris, abductor pollicis longus, extensor pollicis brevis, extensor pollicis longus, and extensor indicis — the muscles that extend the fingers and thumb.

Clinical significance

Posterior interosseous nerve syndrome is an entrapment neuropathy, most often at the arcade of Frohse. Unlike a proximal radial nerve palsy, it causes weakness of finger and thumb extension while wrist extension (and the radial-innervated brachioradialis and extensor carpi radialis longus) is preserved, so there is no complete wrist drop. It should be distinguished from radial tunnel syndrome, which produces pain without motor loss.
