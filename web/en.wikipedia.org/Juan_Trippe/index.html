Juan Trippe

Juan Terry Trippe
Born: June 27, 1899, Sea Bright, New Jersey, U.S.
Died: April 3, 1981, New York City, U.S.
Occupation: Airline entrepreneur
Known for: Founding Pan American World Airways

Juan Terry Trippe (June 27, 1899 – April 3, 1981) was an American commercial aviation pioneer and entrepreneur, best known as the founder of Pan American World Airways (Pan Am), one of the most prominent airlines of the twentieth century. Under Trippe's leadership, Pan Am became the United States' principal international air carrier and a symbol of the Jet Age.

Early life

Trippe was born in Sea Bright, New Jersey, the son of a banker. Despite his Spanish-sounding first name, he was of Northern European descent; he was named after Juanita, a Venezuelan relative by marriage. He attended Yale University, where he was a member of the St. Anthony Hall society and played on the golf team. During World War I he served as a pilot in the United States Navy.

Career

After the war, Trippe worked briefly on Wall Street before returning to aviation. He founded Colonial Air Transport in 1923, and in 1927 he became head of the Aviation Corporation of the Americas, whose operating arm, Pan American Airways, won the first United States airmail contract to Cuba. Trippe aggressively expanded the airline's routes throughout Latin America, often with the backing of the United States government, which relied on Pan Am as its "chosen instrument" for international aviation.

In the 1930s Pan Am pioneered transoceanic flying-boat services, including the famous China Clipper flights across the Pacific (beginning in 1935) and transatlantic service to Europe (beginning in 1939). During and after World War II the airline built the airport infrastructure of much of the developing world.

Jet Age

Trippe was instrumental in ushering in the Jet Age. In 1958 Pan Am inaugurated Boeing 707 jet service between New York and Paris, dramatically reducing transatlantic travel times and fares. In the mid-1960s Trippe pressed Boeing to build an aircraft far larger than the 707, an insistence that led directly to the development of the Boeing 747, which entered Pan Am service in 1970. Trippe stepped down as president in 1968 but remained on the board until his death.

Legacy

Trippe's vision of mass international air travel helped create the modern global airline industry. He died in New York City in 1981, a decade before Pan Am itself ceased operations in 1991.
