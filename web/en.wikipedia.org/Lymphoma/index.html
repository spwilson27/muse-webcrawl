Lymphoma

Lymphoma is a group of blood and lymph tumours that develop from lymphocytes. The name typically refers to the cancerous versions rather than all such tumours.

Signs and classification

Signs may include enlarged lymph nodes, fever, drenching sweats, unintended weight loss, itching and fatigue. The two main categories are Hodgkin lymphoma (about 10%) and non-Hodgkin lymphoma (about 90%), with dozens of subtypes classified by the World Health Organization.

Causes and treatment

Risk factors include Epstein–Barr virus infection, autoimmune disease, HIV/AIDS and immunosuppression. Treatment may involve chemotherapy, radiation therapy, immunotherapy such as rituximab, targeted therapy and stem cell transplantation. Prognosis varies widely by subtype; Hodgkin lymphoma is among the most curable cancers.
