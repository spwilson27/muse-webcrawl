Pirfenidone, sold under the brand name Esbriet among others, is a medication used for the treatment of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF). It works by reducing lung fibrosis through downregulation of the production of growth factors and procollagens I and II.

It was first approved in Japan in 2008 for the treatment of IPF under the brand name Pirespa, developed by Shionogi. It was approved for use in the European Union in 2011, in Canada in 2012, and in the United States in October 2014, where it was developed by InterMune and later marketed by Genentech (Roche).

Medical uses

In the European Union, pirfenidone is indicated for the treatment of mild-to-moderate idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis in adults. In the United States it is approved for IPF without the mild-to-moderate restriction. It is generally reserved for people with a diagnosis confirmed by high-resolution computed tomography or lung biopsy and multidisciplinary discussion.

Mechanism of action

Although the mechanism of action is not fully established, pirfenidone has demonstrated antifibrotic and anti-inflammatory properties in vitro and in animal models. It reduces fibroblast proliferation, the production of profibrotic cytokines such as transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β), and the synthesis and accumulation of extracellular matrix collagen.

Clinical evidence

Three phase III trials—the two CAPACITY studies and the ASCEND trial—showed that pirfenidone significantly reduced the decline of forced vital capacity (FVC) compared with placebo over one year. A pooled analysis suggested a reduction in all-cause mortality, though individual trials were not powered for mortality as a primary endpoint.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include nausea, rash, photosensitivity, fatigue, dyspepsia, dizziness, and elevated liver enzymes. Patients are advised to avoid sun exposure and to use sunscreen while taking the drug. Dose titration over two weeks is used to improve gastrointestinal tolerability, and the medication should be taken with food.

History

The compound was patented in the 1970s by Solomon Margolin and developed initially by Marnac, Inc. InterMune acquired rights and conducted the large clinical development program in IPF. Roche acquired InterMune in 2014 for approximately US$8.3 billion, principally for Esbriet.

Research

Pirfenidone has been investigated in other fibrotic conditions, including hepatic fibrosis, renal fibrosis, systemic sclerosis-associated interstitial lung disease, and unclassifiable progressive fibrosing interstitial lung diseases.
