History of printing

The history of printing starts as early as 3500 BCE, when the proto-Elamite and Sumerian civilizations used cylinder seals to certify documents written in clay tablets. Other early forms include block seals, hammered coinage, pottery imprints and cloth printing. Initially a method of printing patterns on cloth such as silk, woodblock printing for texts on paper originated in Tang-dynasty China by the 7th century CE, and later spread throughout East Asia.

East Asia

Woodblock printing on paper was developed in China; the world's earliest dated printed book is the Diamond Sutra of 868 CE. Around 1040, Bi Sheng invented movable type using ceramic pieces, and metal movable type was later used in Korea, where the Jikji was printed in 1377.

Gutenberg and the European press

Around 1450, Johannes Gutenberg of Mainz combined movable metal type, oil-based ink and a screw press adapted from wine-making. His printed Bible (c. 1455) demonstrated that books could be produced far more cheaply and quickly than by hand. Printing spread rapidly across Europe — by 1500 presses had produced millions of books — reshaping religion, science and literacy during the Renaissance and Reformation.

Industrial and digital eras

Steam-driven presses (from 1814), rotary presses, linotype composition and offset printing industrialised newspaper and book production in the 19th and 20th centuries. Phototypesetting, desktop publishing and digital printing followed in the late 20th century, and print-on-demand and e-books have transformed publishing since.
