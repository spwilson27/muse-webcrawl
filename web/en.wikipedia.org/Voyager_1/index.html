Voyager 1 is a space probe launched by NASA on September 5, 1977, as part of the Voyager program to study the outer Solar System and the interstellar medium beyond the Sun's heliosphere. It was launched 16 days after its twin, Voyager 2. Voyager 1 is the most distant human-made object from Earth and the first spacecraft to cross into interstellar space.

Planetary encounters

Voyager 1 flew past Jupiter in March 1979, discovering volcanic activity on the moon Io—the first active volcano observed beyond Earth—and obtaining detailed images of the Great Red Spot and Jupiter's rings. At Saturn in November 1980 it made a close approach to the moon Titan, which revealed a thick, opaque nitrogen atmosphere. The Titan encounter bent the spacecraft's trajectory out of the ecliptic plane, ending its planetary tour but sending it "above" the Solar System.

The Pale Blue Dot

On February 14, 1990, from a distance of about 6 billion kilometres, Voyager 1 turned its camera back toward the inner Solar System and captured the "Family Portrait" series of images, including the famous Pale Blue Dot photograph, in which Earth appears as a tiny crescent of light in a scattered band of sunlight. The cameras were then switched off permanently to conserve power.

Interstellar mission

Voyager 1 crossed the termination shock in December 2004 and in August 2012 crossed the heliopause—the boundary where the Sun's solar wind gives way to the interstellar medium—at about 121.6 astronomical units from the Sun, becoming the first human-made object in interstellar space. Its instruments continue to measure cosmic rays, magnetic fields, and plasma directly. Power from its three RTGs declines by about 4 watts per year, and instruments are being shut down one by one; contact is expected to be maintainable into the late 2020s or early 2030s. Radio signals from the probe take more than 22 hours to reach Earth at light speed.

The Golden Record

Like its twin, Voyager 1 carries the Voyager Golden Record, a phonograph record containing sounds and images selected to portray the diversity of life and culture on Earth, intended for any extraterrestrial intelligence that might find it.
