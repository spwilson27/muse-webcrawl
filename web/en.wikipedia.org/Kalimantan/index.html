Kalimantan is the Indonesian portion of the island of Borneo, comprising about 73 percent of the island's area. The non-Indonesian parts of Borneo are Brunei and East Malaysia. In Indonesia, "Kalimantan" refers both to the island's Indonesian territory and sometimes to the whole island.

Geography

Kalimantan is divided into five provinces: West Kalimantan, Central Kalimantan, South Kalimantan, East Kalimantan, and North Kalimantan (established in 2012). The terrain is dominated by lowland rainforest and peat swamp, crossed by long rivers such as the Kapuas — Indonesia's longest — the Barito, and the Mahakam. The interior is mountainous and sparsely populated, home to many Dayak groups, while the coasts have Malay, Banjarese, Bugis, and other communities.

Economy and environment

The regional economy depends on timber, palm oil, coal mining, oil and gas (around Balikpapan and Bontang), rubber, and rattan. Kalimantan's forests are among the most biodiverse on Earth, habitat for orangutans, proboscis monkeys, and clouded leopards, but have suffered extensive deforestation and peat fires. Conservation areas include Tanjung Puting and Betung Kerihun national parks.

New capital

In 2019 President Joko Widodo announced the relocation of Indonesia's capital from Jakarta to a site in East Kalimantan. The new city, Nusantara, is being built between Balikpapan and Samarinda and was inaugurated in stages from 2024, with the planned transfer of government functions phased over subsequent years.
