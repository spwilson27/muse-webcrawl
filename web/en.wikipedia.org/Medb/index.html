Medb

Medb (Old Irish), later spelled Meadhbh, Méabh or Maeve, is queen of Connacht in the Ulster Cycle of Irish mythology. Her name is thought to mean "she who intoxicates", cognate with "mead". She is the central antagonist of the epic Táin Bó Cúailnge ("The Cattle Raid of Cooley"), in which she leads a great army to seize the prize bull of Ulster.

Mythology

Medb ruled from Cruachan (Rathcroghan) in present-day County Roscommon. The Táin begins with a famous "pillow talk" between Medb and her husband Ailill, in which they compare their wealth and find them equal except for Ailill's great bull Finnbhennach; Medb therefore seeks the Donn Cúailnge, the Brown Bull of Cooley, to outdo him. Her forces fight the Ulster hero Cú Chulainn, who holds them off single-handedly.

Character and legacy

Medb is frequently interpreted as a sovereignty goddess figure, whose successive husbands become king by marrying her, though in the tales she is also depicted as wilful and promiscuous by medieval Christian redactors. Her burial is traditionally placed in a great cairn on Knocknarea in County Sligo. She remains a popular figure in modern Irish culture and literature.
