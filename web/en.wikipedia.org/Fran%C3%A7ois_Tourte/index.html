François Xavier Tourte
Born1747, Paris, France
Died25 April 1835, Paris, France
OccupationBow maker (archetier)
Known forThe modern violin bow

François Xavier Tourte (1747–1835) was a French bow maker, often called the "Stradivari of the bow," who developed the modern design of the violin bow that remains standard today.

Innovations

Working in Paris, Tourte standardised the bow at a length of about 74–75 cm and introduced the concave-curved stick of pernambuco wood, a "hatchet" style head with an ivory face, an ebony frog with a pearl eye, and a metal ferrule and spreader wedge that keep the hair ribbon flat. He also refined the screw-and-button mechanism for adjusting hair tension.

Significance

The Tourte bow gave players far greater control over articulation, dynamic range, and techniques such as spiccato, enabling the virtuoso string playing of the Romantic era. Original Tourte bows, most of them unsigned, are among the most valuable in the world, fetching prices in the hundreds of thousands of dollars.

Family

Tourte came from a family of luthiers and bow makers; his father Nicolas Pierre Tourte and his brother Nicolas Léonard Tourte also made bows, and distinguishing their work from François's is a specialist task.
