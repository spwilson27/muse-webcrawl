Free Church of Scotland (1843–1900)

The Free Church of Scotland was a Scottish denomination founded in 1843 by a large withdrawal — the Disruption of 1843 — from the established Church of Scotland amid the decade-long controversy over patronage and the spiritual independence of the church. Led by Thomas Chalmers, some 450 ministers, roughly a third of the ministry, left their churches, manses and stipends to form the new church.

Development

The Free Church rapidly built hundreds of new churches, schools and a theological college (New College, Edinburgh), funded by voluntary giving through Chalmers's Sustentation Fund. It became a major force in Scottish education, missions and social life, and was strongly evangelical and Presbyterian in polity, holding to the Westminster Confession of Faith.

Union

In 1900 the majority of the Free Church united with the United Presbyterian Church to form the United Free Church of Scotland. A minority, nicknamed the "Wee Frees", refused the union and continued as the Free Church of Scotland, later winning legal recognition as the continuing church and — in the famous litigation settled by Parliament in 1904 — much of the original church's property.
