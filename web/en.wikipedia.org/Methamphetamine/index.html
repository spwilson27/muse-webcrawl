Methamphetamine (contracted from N-methylamphetamine) is a potent central nervous system stimulant that is mainly used as a recreational drug and less commonly as a treatment for attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) and obesity. It was discovered in 1893 by Japanese chemist Nagai Nagayoshi, who synthesised it from ephedrine; the crystalline form was developed by Akira Ogata in 1919.

Effects and risks

Methamphetamine increases the release and blocks the reuptake of dopamine, norepinephrine, and serotonin, producing euphoria, increased wakefulness and energy, and decreased appetite. Recreational use is associated with high addiction potential, neurotoxicity, cardiovascular damage, severe dental problems ("meth mouth"), skin lesions, psychosis, and cognitive decline. Withdrawal and dependence are major public health problems in many countries, and the drug is controlled internationally under the Convention on Psychotropic Substances; illicit production has shifted between pseudoephedrine-based methods and industrial-scale synthesis in several regions.

Medical use

In the United States, methamphetamine hydrochloride is approved under the brand name Desoxyn for ADHD and short-term treatment of obesity, but is rarely prescribed because of its risks. Amphetamine, its close relative, is far more commonly used therapeutically.
