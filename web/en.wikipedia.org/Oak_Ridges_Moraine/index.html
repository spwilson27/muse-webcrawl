The Oak Ridges Moraine is a geological landform in southern Ontario, Canada, running roughly 160 kilometres from Caledon and the Niagara Escarpment in the west to the Trent River in the east, north of Lake Ontario. It is one of the most significant landforms of its kind in southern Ontario, formed from sediments deposited between two ice lobes during the retreat of the Laurentide Ice Sheet.

Hydrology and ecology

The moraine's permeable sands and gravels absorb rainfall and feed the headwaters of more than 60 rivers and streams flowing north to Lake Simcoe and Georgian Bay and south to Lake Ontario. It also recharges groundwater aquifers used for drinking water. Its forests, wetlands and kettle lakes support diverse wildlife and form a key part of Ontario's Greenbelt.

Conservation

Development pressure from the growing Toronto region led to the Oak Ridges Moraine Conservation Act (2001) and the Oak Ridges Moraine Conservation Plan, which restrict land use across most of the moraine. The landform remains a focus of debate between conservationists and developers in the Greater Toronto Area.
