Dinka language

Native toSouth Sudan
SpeakersSeveral million
FamilyNilo-Saharan? — Nilotic — Western Nilotic

The Dinka language (Thuɔŋjäŋ) is a Nilotic language cluster spoken by the Dinka people, the largest ethnic group in South Sudan. It consists of several mutually intelligible dialects, including Padang, Rek, Agaar, Bor and Twic.

Phonology

Dinka is notable for its rich vocalic system, with distinctions of vowel length, voice quality (breathy versus modal) and tone, giving it one of the larger phoneme inventories among Nilotic languages. It has an unusually large number of consonant contrasts as well.

Writing

Dinka is written in an extended Latin alphabet developed in the early 20th century, with additional letters such as ŋ, ɣ, and ɛ. Literacy materials, including Bible translations, have been produced in the major dialect groups.

Status

Dinka is widely used in radio broadcasting, primary education and church life in South Sudan, alongside English, the official language, and Juba Arabic.
