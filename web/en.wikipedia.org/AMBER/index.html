AMBER Alert

An AMBER Alert (also styled Amber Alert) is a child abduction emergency alert system that originated in the United States in 1996. When law enforcement confirms a child abduction meeting certain criteria, alerts are disseminated through radio, television, highway signs, and wireless emergency alerts to mobile phones to enlist the public's help in recovering the child quickly.

Origin

The system is named after Amber Hagerman, a nine-year-old girl abducted and murdered in Arlington, Texas, in 1996. "AMBER" is also a backronym for "America's Missing: Broadcast Emergency Response." Similar alert plans now operate in all 50 US states and in other countries, including Canada and Mexico.

Criteria and effectiveness

US Department of Justice guidelines recommend that an alert be issued only when there is reasonable belief an abduction occurred, the child is in imminent danger of serious injury or death, and enough descriptive information exists about the child, abductor, or vehicle. By the early 2020s, AMBER Alerts had contributed to the recovery of more than 1,000 children in the United States, though researchers continue to debate the programme's broader effectiveness and the risk of alert fatigue.
