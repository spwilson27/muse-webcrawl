Local anesthetic

A local anesthetic is a medication that causes reversible absence of pain sensation in a specific part of the body without loss of consciousness. Local anesthetics are widely used in dentistry, minor surgery and obstetrics.

Mechanism

These drugs block voltage-gated sodium channels in nerve membranes, preventing action potentials. Small, myelinated pain fibres are blocked earlier than larger motor fibres.

Types

They divide chemically into esters (procaine, benzocaine) and amides (lidocaine, bupivacaine, ropivacaine). Techniques include topical application, infiltration, peripheral nerve blocks, spinal and epidural anesthesia.

History

Cocaine was the first local anesthetic, used in the 1880s; safer synthetic agents followed in the 20th century.

Risks

Overdose can cause local anesthetic systemic toxicity, affecting the heart and brain; adding vasoconstrictors such as epinephrine prolongs action and reduces absorption.
