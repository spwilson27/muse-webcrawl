Noto fonts

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Noto is a font family comprising over 100 individual fonts, designed to cover all the scripts encoded in the Unicode standard. Developed by Google and released under the SIL Open Font License, the Noto fonts are intended as a comprehensive solution so that text in any language can be displayed without missing-glyph boxes — the problem from which the family takes its name: "Noto" derives from "no tofu," referring to the white rectangular placeholder characters (jokingly called tofu) shown when a system lacks a glyph for a character.

Design and coverage

The family includes serif and sans-serif designs for Latin, Greek, and Cyrillic scripts (Noto Serif and Noto Sans), and dedicated designs for scripts of Asia, Africa, the Middle East, and the Americas, as well as historical scripts, musical notation, and symbols. The CJK (Chinese, Japanese, Korean) editions, produced with Adobe and known in Adobe's version as Source Han Sans and Source Han Serif, are among the largest fonts ever made, covering tens of thousands of ideographs.

Noto fonts are the default typefaces on Android and ChromeOS devices, and they are widely bundled with Linux distributions and web services.

Development

Google commissioned the family from foundries including Monotype, Adobe, and volunteer contributors, and development continues as new scripts are added to Unicode. Related projects include Noto Color Emoji, the colour emoji font used on Android, and Noto Sans Symbols. In 2020 the variable-font editions of Noto Sans and Noto Serif were released, allowing a continuous range of weights and widths from a single font file.
