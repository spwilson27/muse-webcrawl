Sejm

The Sejm (Polish: [sɛjm]) is the name of the lower house of the bicameral parliament of Poland, and historically of the general parliament of the Kingdom of Poland and the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth. In the Commonwealth it comprised the King, the Senate, and the Chamber of Deputies — the three "sejming estates."

Historical Sejm

From the Nihil novi act (1505), no new law could pass without the Sejm's consent. Deputies were elected by local sejmiks of the szlachta and bound by their instructions. Ordinary Sejms met every two years for six weeks under the Henrician Articles. The unanimity principle produced the liberum veto. Great reforming assemblies included the Convocation Sejm of 1573 and the Four-Year Sejm (1788–1792), which enacted the Constitution of 3 May.

Modern Sejm

Revived in 1919 and again after 1989, today's Sejm has 460 deputies elected by proportional representation for four-year terms, sitting with the 100-member Senate. It elects governments, passes laws and budgets, and is led by its Marshal, second in presidential succession.
