The Vandal Kingdom (Latin: Regnum Vandalum) was a kingdom in North Africa ruled by the Germanic Vandals and their Alan allies, established after they crossed from Iberia in 429 AD under King Genseric and seized Carthage in 439.

The Vandal Kingdom at its greatest extent.

History

From Carthage the Vandals built one of the most powerful fleets in the Mediterranean, conducting raids as far as Greece. In 455 they famously sacked Rome for two weeks — an orderly but thorough plundering that gave rise to the modern word "vandalism". The kingdom controlled Sicily (temporarily), Sardinia, Corsica, and the Balearic Islands.

The Vandals were Arians and intermittently persecuted the Nicene Catholic church of Roman Africa, generating enduring hostility in the sources. Internally, succession followed a rule of seniority among male agnates devised by Genseric.

Fall

In 533–534 the Byzantine general Belisarius, sent by Emperor Justinian I, defeated the Vandals at Ad Decimum and Tricamarum; the last king, Gelimer, surrendered in 534, and North Africa became the Byzantine Praetorian Prefecture of Africa.
