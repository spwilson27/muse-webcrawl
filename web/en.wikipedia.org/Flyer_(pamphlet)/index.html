Flyer (pamphlet)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A flyer (or flier), also called a handbill or leaflet, is a form of paper advertisement intended for wide distribution, typically handed out in public places, posted on noticeboards, distributed door-to-door, or sent by mail. Unlike a pamphlet, a flyer is usually a single unfolded sheet, printed on one or both sides, designed to be read quickly and discarded.

History

Cheap printed handbills date to the early centuries of the printing press and were staples of fairs, elections, and theatrical promotion. In wartime, propaganda leaflets were dropped from aircraft in enormous numbers — a practice of both World Wars and the Korean and Vietnam wars. In the 1980s and 1990s, flyers were the primary medium of rave and club culture, with distinctive photocopied and later digitally designed styles; punk and hip-hop scenes likewise relied on flyers to publicise shows.

Production and use

Flyers advertise events, businesses, products, political campaigns, and lost items. Common sizes are A5, A6, and US letter or half-letter. Desktop publishing and digital printing made short-run full-colour flyers inexpensive, while digital flyers distributed via social media have partly replaced paper for event promotion. Many jurisdictions regulate flyposting and unsolicited distribution because discarded handbills contribute to litter.
