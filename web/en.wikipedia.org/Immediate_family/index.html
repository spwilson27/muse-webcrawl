Immediate family

The immediate family is a person's smallest family unit, typically taken to include their spouse or partner, parents, siblings, and children. The precise definition varies by context: laws, workplace policies, insurance contracts, and immigration rules each define the term for their own purposes, and the concept overlaps with but is distinct from the nuclear family and the extended family.

Usage in law and policy

The term is used in contexts such as bereavement leave, hospital visitation, conflict-of-interest and nepotism rules, and eligibility for family-based immigration. In United States immigration law, "immediate relatives" is a specific category covering spouses, unmarried children under 21, and parents of U.S. citizens, who are exempt from numerical quotas. Travel and insurance policies often extend or limit coverage to immediate family members, sometimes including in-laws or domestic partners.

Related concepts

Definitions in many legal systems are based on degrees of consanguinity (blood relationship) and affinity (relationship by marriage). The immediate family is generally contrasted with extended kin such as grandparents, grandchildren, aunts, uncles, and cousins, although some definitions include grandparents and grandchildren.
