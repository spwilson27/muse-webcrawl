The Netherlands national football team (Dutch: Het Nederlands Elftal) represents the Netherlands in international men's association football and is governed by the Royal Dutch Football Association (KNVB). Nicknamed Oranje after their orange home shirts — the colour of the Dutch royal House of Orange-Nassau — the team has long been regarded as one of the world's strongest, renowned for the philosophy of "Total Football" developed in the 1970s.

History

The Netherlands played its first international in 1905, a 4–1 win over Belgium. Under coach Rinus Michels and captain Johan Cruyff, the Total Football team reached the World Cup finals of 1974 and 1978, losing to hosts West Germany and Argentina respectively. The Dutch won their first major trophy at UEFA Euro 1988, beating the Soviet Union 2–0 in the final — remembered for Marco van Basten's volley from a tight angle. They reached a third World Cup final in 2010, losing to Spain in extra time, and finished third in 2014. The team has also reached the semi-finals of the European Championship several times and finished as runners-up in the inaugural UEFA Nations League in 2019.

Style and legacy

Dutch football is identified with fluid positional interchange, high pressing, and technical, attacking play rooted in the academy systems of clubs such as Ajax and Feyenoord. Illustrious Dutch internationals include Johan Cruyff, Marco van Basten, Ruud Gullit, Dennis Bergkamp, and Arjen Robben. Despite the country's small population, the Netherlands has repeatedly produced world-class players, though the World Cup remains the major trophy that has eluded it.
