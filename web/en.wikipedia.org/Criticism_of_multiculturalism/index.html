Criticism of multiculturalism

Criticism of multiculturalism encompasses arguments against policies and philosophies that promote the recognition and accommodation of diverse cultures within a society. Critics come from across the political spectrum, and their arguments differ considerably.

Arguments

Conservative and nationalist critics argue that multiculturalism undermines social cohesion and national identity, discourages integration, and can permit practices that conflict with liberal values such as gender equality and freedom of expression. In 2010–2011, leaders including Angela Merkel, David Cameron, and Nicolas Sarkozy declared state multiculturalism a failure in their countries.

From the left, critics such as Kenan Malik argue that official multiculturalism treats cultures as fixed blocs, empowers conservative community gatekeepers, and substitutes identity recognition for political and economic equality. Feminist critics have raised concerns about cultural defences of practices harmful to women.

Responses

Defenders respond that multicultural policies in countries such as Canada are associated with successful integration outcomes, and that critics conflate multiculturalism as demographic fact, as philosophy, and as specific government programmes.
