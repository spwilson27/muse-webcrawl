Constellation diagram

A constellation diagram is a representation of a signal modulated by a digital modulation scheme such as quadrature amplitude modulation (QAM) or phase-shift keying (PSK). It displays the signal as a two-dimensional scatter plot in the in-phase/quadrature (I/Q) plane: each point, or symbol, corresponds to a possible transmitted symbol, and the axes represent the in-phase and quadrature components.

Interpretation

In a clean signal, the received samples cluster tightly around the ideal constellation points. Noise spreads the clusters; gain and phase errors rotate or scale the constellation; and nonlinear distortion warps it. Engineers use the diagram, together with the error vector magnitude (EVM), to diagnose impairments in transmitters and receivers.

Examples

BPSK shows two points on the in-phase axis; QPSK shows four points; 16-QAM shows a 4×4 grid; and 64-QAM an 8×8 grid, trading noise tolerance for higher data rates per symbol.
