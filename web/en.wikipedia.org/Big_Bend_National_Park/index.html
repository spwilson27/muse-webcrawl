Big Bend National Park

Big Bend National Park
Location: Brewster County, Texas, US
Area: 801,163 acres (3,242 km²)
Established: 12 June 1944

Big Bend National Park is a national park of the United States located in West Texas, bordering Mexico. The park has national significance as the largest protected area of Chihuahuan Desert topography and ecology in the United States, and was named after a large bend in the Rio Grande/Río Bravo.

Geography and geology

The park protects about 2,600 km of the Rio Grande, which forms the international boundary with Mexico. Elevations range from about 550 m along the river to 2,387 m at Emory Peak in the Chisos Mountains. The geology records volcanic activity, uplift, and marine deposits from a Cretaceous seaway; Santa Elena Canyon cuts 460 m deep through limestone.

Ecology

Despite its arid climate, the park supports more than 1,200 plant species, over 450 bird species, and mammals including cougars, black bears, javelinas, and coyotes. It is designated an International Dark Sky Park, noted for some of the darkest night skies in the continental United States.

History and recreation

The area was home to Native American peoples for thousands of years and later to ranchers and miners. Texas acquired the land in the 1930s and 1940s, and the park was established in 1944; a proposed international park with Mexico has been discussed since the 1930s. Visitors use the Ross Maxwell Scenic Drive, the Chisos Basin, and river trips through the canyons.
