Leiden University

Leiden University (Dutch: Universiteit Leiden) is a public research university in Leiden, Netherlands. Founded in 1575 by William, Prince of Orange, it is the oldest university in the Netherlands and one of the oldest in Europe. The university has seven faculties and over 30,000 students, with a campus in The Hague as well as Leiden. Its motto is Praesidium Libertatis ("Bastion of Freedom").

Established: 1575
Type: Public research university
Location: Leiden and The Hague, Netherlands
Students: c. 33,000

History

The university was founded during the Dutch Revolt as a reward to the city of Leiden for its defence against Spanish attacks in 1574. It quickly became a leading centre of learning, attracting scholars such as Justus Lipsius, Joseph Scaliger, and later Herman Boerhaave, Jacobus Gronovius, and the physicists Heike Kamerlingh Onnes, Hendrik Lorentz and Pieter Zeeman. Its alumni and faculty include many Nobel laureates and members of the Dutch royal family. The Leiden University Library holds millions of volumes and internationally significant special collections.

Faculties

The university comprises the faculties of Archaeology, Governance and Global Affairs, Humanities, Law, Medicine (Leiden University Medical Center), Science, and Social and Behavioural Sciences. It is a member of the League of European Research Universities (LERU) and the Coimbra Group.
