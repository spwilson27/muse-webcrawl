Public holidays in Japan

Public holidays in Japan are established by the Public Holiday Law of 1948. Japan currently observes 16 national holidays, including New Year's Day (1 January), Coming of Age Day (second Monday of January), Foundation Day (11 February), the Emperor's Birthday (23 February), the equinox days, Constitution Memorial Day (3 May), Greenery Day (4 May), Children's Day (5 May), Marine Day (third Monday of July), Mountain Day (11 August), Respect for the Aged Day (third Monday of September), Sports Day (second Monday of October), Culture Day (3 November) and Labour Thanksgiving Day (23 November).

Rules

When a holiday falls on a Sunday, the following Monday becomes a substitute holiday. A working day sandwiched between two holidays becomes a "citizens' holiday". The cluster of holidays in late April and early May forms Golden Week, one of Japan's busiest travel periods. The "Happy Monday System" moved several holidays to Mondays to create long weekends.

Special holidays

One-off holidays have been legislated for imperial events, such as the enthronement ceremonies of Emperor Naruhito in 2019, and holidays were rearranged in 2020 and 2021 for the Tokyo Olympic Games.
