Symmetric graph

Symmetric graph
Field: Graph theory
Property: Arc-transitive automorphism group
Examples: Petersen graph, complete graphs, Foster census graphs

In graph theory, a symmetric graph (or arc-transitive graph) is a graph whose automorphism group acts transitively on its arcs — that is, for any two ordered pairs of adjacent vertices there is an automorphism mapping one pair to the other. Symmetric graphs are necessarily both vertex-transitive and edge-transitive.

Properties

A connected symmetric graph of degree (valency) three or more is regular and highly structured. Every connected symmetric graph is vertex- and edge-transitive, though the converse — a vertex- and edge-transitive graph — need not be arc-transitive; such exceptions are called half-transitive. Symmetric graphs of odd order cannot exist for certain valencies, and a theorem of Tutte showed that cubic symmetric graphs fall into restricted families determined by their girth-related "s-arc" parameter.

Examples and census

Famous symmetric graphs include the Petersen graph, the Heawood graph, complete graphs, and complete bipartite graphs. The Foster census, begun by Ronald Foster and completed by later researchers, catalogues all connected symmetric cubic graphs up to many thousands of vertices.
