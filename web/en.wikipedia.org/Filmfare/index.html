Filmfare

Filmfare is an Indian English-language fortnightly magazine published by Worldwide Media, a subsidiary of The Times Group. First published in 1952, it is one of India's oldest and most popular entertainment magazines, devoted primarily to Hindi cinema (Bollywood) — its stars, films, music, and industry news — with editions and coverage extending to regional film industries.

The Filmfare Awards

The magazine is best known for the Filmfare Awards, inaugurated in 1954, among the oldest and most prestigious film awards in India, honouring artistic and technical excellence in Hindi-language cinema. Winners receive the statuette popularly called the "Black Lady". A separate ceremony, the Filmfare Awards South, has honoured Telugu, Tamil, Malayalam, and Kannada cinema since 1954, and regional editions also exist for Bengali, Marathi, and Punjabi films.

Content and history

Filmfare was launched on 7 March 1952 and edited for many years by figures such as B. K. Karanjia. At its peak it was the definitive chronicle of the Bombay film industry, known for star interviews, photo features, and film reviews. The magazine later expanded into digital media, the Filmfare OTT Awards for streaming content, and international editions.

Reception

The awards have periodically faced criticism over commercial influence and popular-choice voting, but remain a benchmark of mainstream recognition in Indian cinema, with records such as Dilip Kumar and Shah Rukh Khan's eight Best Actor wins frequently cited in industry coverage.
