Podocyte

Podocytes are cells in the Bowman's capsule in the kidneys that wrap around capillaries of the glomerulus. Podocytes form the epithelial lining of Bowman's capsule, the third layer through which filtration of blood takes place. The Bowman's capsule filters the blood, retaining large molecules such as proteins, and passing through small molecules such as water, salts, and glucose that are similar in size to proteins but smaller.

The long processes of podocytes — the pedicels, also called foot processes — wrap around the glomerular capillaries and leave slits between them. Blood is filtered through these slits, each known as a slit diaphragm or filtration slit. Several proteins are required for the pedicels to wrap around the capillaries and function, including nephrin and podocin.

Structure

Podocytes have a large cell body with primary processes that branch into interdigitating secondary foot processes. The foot processes adhere to the glomerular basement membrane and are separated by filtration slits bridged by the slit diaphragm, a zipper-like junction containing the proteins nephrin and Neph1. The podocyte cell body protrudes into the urinary space of Bowman's capsule.

Function

Podocytes form the final barrier to protein loss in the urine, contributing to the size- and charge-selectivity of the glomerular filtration barrier. They also help maintain the structure of the glomerular capillary tuft and regulate glomerular filtration. Because podocytes are terminally differentiated and proliferate poorly, their loss cannot easily be replaced.

Clinical significance

Podocyte injury and loss ("podocytopathies") underlie many proteinuric kidney diseases, including minimal change disease, focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS), and diabetic kidney disease. Mutations in genes encoding slit-diaphragm and foot-process proteins such as NPHS1 (nephrin) and NPHS2 (podocin) cause inherited forms of nephrotic syndrome.
