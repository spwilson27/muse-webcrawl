The Second Turkic Khaganate (682–744), also known as the Second Göktürk Khaganate, was a nomadic empire of the Ashina clan of the Göktürks that succeeded the First Turkic Khaganate after an interregnum of Tang-dynasty domination. At its height it stretched across the eastern Eurasian steppe from Manchuria to the fringes of Central Asia.

Founding

In 682 Kutlug, a descendant of the Ashina royal house, rebelled against the Tang dynasty and rallied the Türk tribes, taking the title Ilterish Khagan ("gatherer of the people"). With the able adviser Tonyukuk at his side, he rebuilt the khaganate from its sacred base in the Ötüken forest and Orkhon Valley in present-day Mongolia.

Expansion and height

Ilterish's brother and successor Qapaghan Khagan (691–716) expanded the empire aggressively, defeating the Türgesh at Bolchu in 711 and campaigning deep into Transoxiana and toward China. After his death fighting rebels in 716, a coup brought Ilterish's sons Bilge Khagan and Kul Tigin to power. Their reign (716–734), recorded proudly in the Orkhon inscriptions, combined military consolidation with pragmatic diplomacy toward Tang China.

Decline and fall

Bilge Khagan was poisoned in 734, and the khaganate dissolved into succession struggles. A coalition of Uyghurs, Karluks and Basmyls overthrew the last khagan in 744; the Uyghur Khaganate succeeded it as the dominant steppe power, while many Oghuz Turks migrated westward.

Legacy

The khaganate's Orkhon inscriptions are the earliest extensive texts in a Turkic language and a foundational source for Turkic history, deciphered in 1893 by Vilhelm Thomsen.
