James Buchanan "Bucky" Barnes is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Originally introduced as the sidekick of Captain America, the character was created by Joe Simon and Jack Kirby and first appeared in Captain America Comics #1 (cover-dated March 1941). Decades after his apparent death, the character was revived as the brainwashed assassin known as the Winter Soldier.

Publication history

Bucky Barnes debuted in 1941 as the teenage mascot and partner of Captain America during World War II. When Marvel revived Captain America in The Avengers #4 (1964), writer Stan Lee established that Bucky had died in the explosion of a drone plane near the end of the war, an event that haunted Captain America for years. In 2005, writer Ed Brubaker retconned the death, revealing that Barnes had survived, lost an arm, and been captured by the Soviet Union, which fitted him with a bionic arm and brainwashed him into becoming the assassin codenamed the Winter Soldier.

Fictional character biography

James Barnes was an orphan raised at Camp Lehigh in Virginia, where he became the camp's mascot and befriended Private Steve Rogers. After discovering Rogers's secret identity, Barnes became his costumed partner, Bucky. The two fought together against the Axis powers until 1945, when both were caught in an explosion over the English Channel. Rogers fell into the Arctic waters and was preserved in ice, while Barnes was presumed dead.

In reality, Barnes was recovered by Soviet agents, given a cybernetic arm, and programmed as an assassin. Kept in cryogenic stasis between missions, he carried out covert killings for decades, including, in the Marvel Cinematic Universe continuity, the assassination of Howard and Maria Stark. After a confrontation with Captain America in the present day, Barnes gradually recovered his memories and sought redemption, eventually taking the codename White Wolf during a period in Wakanda.

In other media

Sebastian Stan portrays Bucky Barnes in the Marvel Cinematic Universe, beginning with Captain America: The First Avenger (2011) and continuing through Captain America: The Winter Soldier (2014), Captain America: Civil War (2016), and the Disney+ series The Falcon and the Winter Soldier (2021).
