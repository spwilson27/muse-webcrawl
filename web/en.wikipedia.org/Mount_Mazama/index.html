Mount Mazama

Mount Mazama is a complex volcano in the Cascade Range of southern Oregon, best known for its catastrophic eruption about 7,700 years ago, in which its summit collapsed to form the caldera now occupied by Crater Lake.

The climactic eruption

Before the eruption, Mazama stood roughly 3,600 m high. The climactic event, among the largest eruptions of the last 10,000 years, expelled some 50 km³ of magma — dozens of times the volume of the 1980 eruption of Mount St. Helens — and the emptied chamber collapsed, leaving a caldera nearly 1,000 m deep. The lake that filled it is the deepest in the United States (594 m) and famed for its clarity and blue colour. Later eruptions built Wizard Island within the lake. Klamath oral tradition preserves an account of the eruption as a battle between the sky god Skell and Llao, god of the underworld.
