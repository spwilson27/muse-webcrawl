The Constitution of Indonesia, formally the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia (Indonesian: Undang-Undang Dasar Negara Republik Indonesia Tahun 1945, abbreviated UUD 1945), is the supreme law of Indonesia. It was drafted in the final months of the Japanese occupation and adopted the day after the proclamation of Indonesian independence.

History

The Investigating Committee for Preparatory Work for Independence (BPUPK) drafted the constitution in mid-1945, drawing on the ideas of Soepomo and Sukarno's Pancasila speech of 1 June 1945. The Preparatory Committee for Indonesian Independence (PPKI) ratified it on 18 August 1945. It was replaced by a federal constitution in 1949 and a provisional constitution in 1950, but President Sukarno's decree of 5 July 1959 restored the 1945 Constitution.

Reform era amendments

Following the fall of Suharto in 1998, the People's Consultative Assembly (MPR) amended the constitution in four stages (1999–2002). The amendments introduced term limits for the president, direct presidential elections, a bicameral legislature including a Regional Representative Council, a Constitutional Court, stronger human-rights provisions, and restrictions on the MPR's former supremacy.

Contents

The constitution opens with a preamble containing the Pancasila, the five principles of the Indonesian state: belief in one God, a just and civilised humanity, Indonesian unity, democracy guided by wisdom in consultation, and social justice. The body of the text covers state form and sovereignty, the legislature, the presidency, state institutions, regional government, citizens' rights, religion, defence, education, the economy and the flag, language and symbols.
