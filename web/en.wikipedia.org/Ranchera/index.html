Ranchera

Ranchera, or canción ranchera, is a genre of traditional music of Mexico. It dates to before the years of the Mexican Revolution, and developed in the theatres and cabarets of Mexico City in the early twentieth century. Ranchera today is closely associated with the mariachi groups that usually accompany the singers, and with the image of the charro, the traditional Mexican horseman.

Characteristics

The word ranchera derives from rancho, a ranch, reflecting the genre's rural themes: the countryside, lost love, patriotism, and the lives of common people. Songs are typically performed in a variety of metres, including waltz time and polka, and are sung with dramatic vibrato and emotional intensity. The characteristic grito mexicano — a drawn-out shout of "ay ay ay" — punctuates many performances.

Notable performers

Among the most celebrated ranchera singers are Jorge Negrete, Pedro Infante, Javier Solís, José Alfredo Jiménez (also the genre's most famous composer), Lola Beltrán, and Vicente Fernández, long known as the "King of Ranchera Music". Singers such as Juan Gabriel and Rocío Dúrcal brought the genre to new audiences in the late twentieth century.

Cultural role

Ranchera became a symbol of Mexican national identity, popularised worldwide through the golden age of Mexican cinema in the 1930s–1950s. The genre remains a staple of radio, festivals and family celebrations throughout Mexico and the Mexican diaspora.
