Arbois

Arbois is a commune in the Jura department in the Bourgogne-Franche-Comté region of eastern France. It lies on the river Cuisance at the edge of the Revermont, and has a population of around 3,200. The town styles itself the wine capital of the Jura.

Wine

Arbois gives its name to the Arbois AOC, the largest and oldest appellation of the Jura wine region (granted in 1936). Local vineyards produce reds from Poulsard and Trousseau, whites from Chardonnay and Savagnin, and the distinctive long-aged oxidative vin jaune ("yellow wine"), matured under a veil of yeast for over six years.

Louis Pasteur

Arbois was the childhood home and later retreat of Louis Pasteur, whose house and laboratory are preserved as a museum. Pasteur conducted experiments on fermentation using local wines here, work fundamental to the germ theory of fermentation and to the process of pasteurisation.

Sights

Landmarks include the church of Saint-Just, the Tour Gloriette, the Château Pécauld, and the waterfalls (cascades des Tufs) on the Cuisance nearby.
