Metta

Mettā (Pali) or maitrī (Sanskrit) means benevolence, loving-kindness, friendliness, amity, goodwill and active interest in others. It is one of the four divine abidings (brahmavihāra) in Buddhism, along with compassion (karuṇā), sympathetic joy (muditā), and equanimity (upekkhā), and one of the ten perfections (pāramī) in Theravāda Buddhism.

Metta meditation

Mettā bhāvanā, or loving-kindness meditation, is a popular form of Buddhist meditation in which the practitioner cultivates benevolent wishes toward themselves, a benefactor, a friend, a neutral person, a difficult person, and ultimately all beings. A common formula is: "May I (you/all beings) be happy, may I be healthy, may I be safe, may I live with ease."

Texts

The Mettā Sutta (part of the Sutta Nipāta) is a well-known discourse on loving-kindness, frequently chanted in Theravāda countries. The 5th-century commentary Visuddhimagga by Buddhaghosa gives detailed instructions for its cultivation.

In other traditions

Maitrī also appears in Hinduism and Jainism; in Jainism it is one of the four meditations on friendliness toward all creatures, closely tied to the doctrine of ahimsa (non-violence).
