Kingdom:Animalia
Phylum:Arthropoda
Class:Arachnida
Order:Araneae
Family:Sparassidae Bertkau, 1872
Common name: Huntsman spiders
Diversity: c. 99 genera, over 1,300 species

Sparassidae is a family of spiders known commonly as huntsman spiders, so called because of their speed and mode of hunting prey rather than building webs to trap it. The family contains more than 1,300 described species in around 99 genera, distributed worldwide in warm and temperate regions, and includes some of the largest spiders by leg span, notably the giant huntsman spider (Heteropoda maxima) of Laos, whose leg span may reach 30 cm (12 in).

Description

Huntsman spiders are characterised by legs that bend forwards — the joints twist so that the legs extend laterally like those of crabs — an adaptation for living in narrow crevices, under bark and in leaf litter. They are generally drab brown, grey or mottled for camouflage, with flattened bodies. Their eight eyes are arranged in two rows; vision is modest, and they rely on vibration and speed to ambush insects and other invertebrates.

Behaviour and venom

Sparassids are active nocturnal hunters. Many species shelter by day in a silk retreat. Some, such as the social huntsman Delena cancerides of Australia, live in family groups. Females guard their egg sacs fiercely. Bites to humans occur when spiders are trapped against skin but are rarely medically serious, typically causing local pain and swelling, though the badge huntsman (Neosparassus) has been implicated in more significant symptoms.

Genera

Well-known genera include Heteropoda (including the cane huntsman), Holconia, Polybetes, Delena, Olios and Micrommata, the sole European representative of note being the green huntsman Micrommata virescens.
