Bruce Greyson (born October 1946) is an American psychiatrist and researcher best known for his scientific study of near-death experiences (NDEs). He was the Chester F. Carlson Professor of Psychiatry and Neurobehavioral Sciences at the University of Virginia, where he directed the Division of Perceptual Studies, and is considered one of the founding figures of the modern academic field of near-death studies.

Career

Greyson trained in psychiatry after studying at Cornell University and the State University of New York Downstate Medical Center. Early in his residency, an encounter with a patient who reported an out-of-body experience while unconscious prompted his decades-long research interest. He later taught at the University of Michigan and the University of Connecticut before joining the University of Virginia.

Research

Greyson developed the Greyson Near-Death Experience Scale, a standardised 16-item questionnaire widely used to quantify NDE features for research. He has published hundreds of papers and book chapters on the phenomenology, aftereffects and possible explanations of NDEs, served as editor of the Journal of Near-Death Studies, and co-edited The Handbook of Near-Death Experiences (2009). His 2021 book After: A Doctor Explores What Near-Death Experiences Reveal About Life and Beyond summarised his research for a general audience. Greyson emphasises a scientific, agnostic approach, noting features of NDEs — such as enhanced mentation during cardiac arrest — that he argues are difficult to explain with current neuroscience.
