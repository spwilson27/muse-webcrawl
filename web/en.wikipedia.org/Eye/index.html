Eye

An eye is a sensory organ that allows an organism to perceive visual information. It detects light and converts it into electro-chemical impulses in neurons (neurones). It is part of an organism's visual system. In higher organisms, the eye is a complex optical system that collects light from the surrounding environment, regulates its intensity through a diaphragm, focuses it through an adjustable assembly of lenses to form an image, converts this image into a set of electrical signals, and transmits them to the brain through neural pathways.

Overview

Eyes vary widely in form and capability across the animal kingdom. Simple eyes, sometimes called ocelli, do little more than detect whether the surroundings are light or dark; complex eyes form images and provide acute vision. Most complex eyes have a roughly spherical shape, as the shape is optically favourable and works well with a camera-like lens. The eyes of cephalopods (such as octopuses), fish, amphibians and snakes usually have a lens that focuses by moving rather than by changing shape, while the vertebrate lens changes shape through accommodation.

Types

There are ten different arrangements of eyes known: pit eyes, pinhole eyes, spherical lensed eyes, multiple lens eyes, refracting cornea eyes, reflector (mirror) eyes, and compound eyes of several designs, among others. Compound eyes, found in arthropods, consist of many individual units called ommatidia. Despite this diversity, the genetic toolkit for eye development — including the Pax6 gene — is deeply conserved, suggesting a common origin.

The human eye

The human eye is an organ of the sensory nervous system that reacts to visible light and allows the use of visual information for various purposes including seeing things, keeping balance, and maintaining circadian rhythm. Light enters through the cornea and the pupil (whose size is controlled by the iris), is focused by the lens onto the retina, where photoreceptor cells — about 120 million rods and 6–7 million cones — convert light into signals sent via the optic nerve to the visual cortex. The eye is moved by the extraocular muscles.

Evolution

Charles Darwin described the eye as an apparent challenge to evolution by natural selection, but concluded that it could have evolved gradually from a light-sensitive spot, each intermediate stage being useful. Photoreceptor cells are thought to have a single origin, and eyes have since evolved independently many times — estimates suggest eyes originated between 50 and 100 separate times — while camera-type eyes evolved independently in vertebrates and cephalopods, a textbook example of convergent evolution.
