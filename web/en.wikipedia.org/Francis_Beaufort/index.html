Sir Francis Beaufort (1774–1857) was an Irish hydrographer and Royal Navy officer, creator of the Beaufort scale for indicating wind force and, as Hydrographer of the Navy, the architect of the world's leading chart-making service.

Naval career

Beaufort went to sea at thirteen and served through the French Revolutionary and Napoleonic Wars, being severely wounded in action. In 1810–1812 he surveyed the southern coast of Anatolia (Karamania), producing charts and a celebrated memoir before his expedition was attacked and he was again wounded.

Hydrographer of the Navy

Appointed Hydrographer in 1829, Beaufort directed the Admiralty's chart production for over 25 years, overseeing great oceanic surveys and sending out expeditions including those associated with Darwin's voyage on HMS Beagle. In 1805 he had devised his wind force scale, a 13-point scale linking wind speed to observable effects at sea and on land, which was adopted by the Royal Navy and remains in use.

Legacy

The Beaufort Sea in the Arctic Ocean is named after him. He was knighted in 1848 and was a fellow of the Royal Society and a founder figure in British scientific and geographical societies.
