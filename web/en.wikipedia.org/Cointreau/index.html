Cointreau

Cointreau is a French orange-flavoured liqueur, a type of triple sec, produced in Saint-Barthélemy-d'Anjou, near Angers. It is made from the dried peels of sweet and bitter oranges and is bottled at 40% alcohol by volume. The brand is part of the Rémy Cointreau group.

History

The Cointreau brothers, Adolphe and Édouard-Jean, founded their distillery in Angers in 1849, originally making fruit liqueurs. Édouard Cointreau created the signature orange liqueur in 1875, selling it in the distinctive square bottle that remains in use today. The product won international prizes and became a global brand in the 20th century.

Production and use

Orange peels are macerated and distilled in copper pot stills, then blended with sugar and water. Cointreau is drunk as an apéritif and digestif and is an essential ingredient in classic cocktails including the Margarita, Cosmopolitan, Sidecar, and White Lady.
