Drone warfare

An unmanned aerial vehicle on a combat mission (placeholder image)

Drone warfare is a form of warfare using unmanned combat aerial vehicles (UCAVs) or weaponized commercial unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs). It encompasses the use of remotely piloted aircraft, loitering munitions ("kamikaze drones"), and increasingly autonomous systems to conduct surveillance, reconnaissance and strikes without a human operator on board the aircraft.

History

Military drones were used for reconnaissance throughout the late 20th century, but armed drones emerged prominently after 2001, when the United States began operating the MQ-1 Predator armed with Hellfire missiles in Afghanistan. Its successor, the MQ-9 Reaper, became the workhorse of US counterterrorism campaigns in Pakistan, Yemen, Somalia and elsewhere.

Expansion

From the 2010s onward, drone technology proliferated widely. Turkey's Bayraktar TB2 was used effectively by Azerbaijan in the 2020 Nagorno-Karabakh war and by Ukraine following Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022. The Russo-Ukrainian war saw mass use of first-person view (FPV) drones, loitering munitions such as the Shahed-136, and naval drones, transforming tactics at every level from strategic strikes to trench warfare.

Types

Modern drone warfare includes large long-endurance UCAVs, medium tactical drones, loitering munitions that hover before diving onto a target, small quadcopters adapted to drop munitions, FPV racing drones flown directly into targets, and unmanned surface vessels used against ships and ports.

Legal and ethical issues

Drone strikes have raised persistent questions about civilian casualties, the legality of targeted killings outside declared war zones, accountability for remote killing, and the future prospect of lethal autonomous weapons systems that select targets without meaningful human control. International humanitarian law applies to drone operations as it does to other means of warfare.
