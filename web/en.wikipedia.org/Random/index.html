Special: Random

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Special:Random is a special page of Wikipedia that redirects the visitor to an article chosen at random from the encyclopedia's main article namespace. It is linked from the sidebar of every page as "Random article" and can be reached at the address en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special:Random. The feature exists to encourage serendipitous reading, to help editors discover articles in need of improvement, and simply for entertainment.

Load a random article

How it works

When the page is loaded, the MediaWiki software selects a page uniformly at random from the encyclopedia's article space, using a random index maintained for each page in the database, and issues a redirect to it. Redirect pages themselves can be excluded so that readers land on substantive content. Variants of the special page restrict the draw to particular namespaces — for example, random files, categories, templates, or draft pages — which editors use for maintenance tasks such as checking images or reviewing drafts.

Uses

  • Serendipitous browsing — readers with no specific topic in mind can dip into the encyclopedia anywhere.
  • Editing and cleanup — editors patrol random articles to fix errors, add references, and tag problems; informal community challenges such as "random article" improvement drives build on this.
  • Games and education — the feature underpins games in which players navigate from one random article to a target article using only internal links, and classroom exercises in critical reading.

Statistical notes

Because the selection is uniform, long or famous articles are no more likely to appear than obscure stubs; in practice a random draw overwhelmingly lands on lesser-known topics — biographies of minor figures, small settlements, species, and local landmarks — which together make up the bulk of the encyclopedia. A related feature, "Random featured article", limits the selection to featured content.
