Sexy prime

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In number theory, sexy primes are prime numbers that differ from each other by 6. For example, the numbers 5 and 11 are both sexy primes because 11 − 5 = 6. The term "sexy prime" is a pun stemming from the Latin sex, meaning six.

If p and p + 6 are both prime and p + 2 and p + 4 are also prime, then the four primes form a prime quadruplet. Sexy primes also occur in triplets when p, p + 6, and p + 12 are all prime but p + 18 is not.

Examples

The sexy prime pairs below 500 include (5, 11), (7, 13), (11, 17), (13, 19), (17, 23), (23, 29), (31, 37), (37, 43), (41, 47), (47, 53), (53, 59), (61, 67), (67, 73), (73, 79), (83, 89), (97, 103), and many more. As of recent searches, the largest known sexy prime pair has tens of thousands of digits.

Related families

Sexy primes belong to the broader study of prime gaps and prime constellations. Pairs differing by 2 are twin primes, by 4 cousin primes. It is unknown whether there are infinitely many sexy prime pairs, just as the twin prime conjecture remains open, though results on bounded gaps between primes (following Yitang Zhang's 2013 breakthrough) guarantee infinitely many prime pairs with some bounded difference.
