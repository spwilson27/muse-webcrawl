Bank for International Settlements (BIS)
Founded: 17 May 1930
Headquarters: Basel, Switzerland
Membership: 63 central banks

The Bank for International Settlements (BIS) is an international financial institution owned by central banks that fosters international monetary and financial cooperation and serves as a bank for central banks. Founded in 1930, it is the world's oldest international financial organisation and is based in Basel, Switzerland.

History

The BIS was established by the Hague Agreements of 1930, initially to handle reparations payments imposed on Germany by the Treaty of Versailles. It soon broadened into a forum for central bank cooperation. It survived a controversial wartime period and a call for its dissolution at the 1944 Bretton Woods conference, later serving as agent for European payments arrangements and as a hub for monetary cooperation.

Functions

The BIS hosts regular meetings of central bank governors, provides banking services for central banks and international organisations, and conducts economic research. It hosts the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision, which sets global bank capital standards (the Basel Accords), and the Financial Stability Board's secretariat. Its Annual Economic Report and statistics on international banking are widely followed.
