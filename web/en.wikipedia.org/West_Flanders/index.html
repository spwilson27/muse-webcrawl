West Flanders (Dutch: West-Vlaanderen) is a province of Belgium, in the Flemish Region on the North Sea coast. Its capital is Bruges, and it is the westernmost province of the country, bordering the Netherlands to the northeast, the Belgian provinces of East Flanders and Hainaut to the east and southeast, France to the south, and the North Sea to the northwest.

Geography

The province consists of a flat coastal plain of polders behind a line of dunes and beaches, giving way inland to a gently rolling landscape of farmland and small towns. The principal rivers are the Yser (IJzer) and the Leie (Lys). The coastline, about 65 kilometres long, includes the resorts of Knokke-Heist, Blankenberge, Ostend and De Panne.

History

West Flanders roughly corresponds to the western part of the historic County of Flanders. During the First World War, the Yser Front ran through the province, and the area around Ypres (Ieper) became one of the most fought-over landscapes of the Western Front; the town's Menin Gate and the cemeteries maintained by the Commonwealth War Graves Commission remain major memorials.

Economy

Tourism along the coast, agriculture and food processing, textiles around Kortrijk, and the ports of Ostend and Zeebrugge are central to the provincial economy. Bruges attracts millions of visitors each year for its preserved medieval centre, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
