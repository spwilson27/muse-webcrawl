Cuttlebone

Cuttlebone, also known as cuttlefish bone, is a hard, brittle internal structure found in all cuttlefish, a family of marine molluscs in the order Sepiida. Unlike other cephalopod shells, it is a chambered, gas-filled internal shell used for buoyancy control.

Structure and function

The cuttlebone is composed mainly of aragonite, arranged in chambers separated by thin septa and supported by pillars. By varying the ratio of gas to liquid within the chambers (via the siphuncle), the cuttlefish adjusts its buoyancy to hover at different depths. In the common cuttlefish the bone withstands considerable pressure, though it limits the animal to depths above roughly 200 metres, beyond which it would implode.

Human uses

Cuttlebones are widely sold in pet shops as a calcium supplement and beak-conditioning perch for caged birds. Historically, powdered cuttlebone was used as a polishing agent and antacid, and silversmiths used whole bones as moulds for casting small metal objects, exploiting their heat resistance and softness for carving.
