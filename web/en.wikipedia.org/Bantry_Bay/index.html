Bantry Bay

Bantry Bay is a large bay in County Cork, south-west Ireland, extending about 35 km from the Atlantic between the Beara and Sheep's Head peninsulas. It is one of the longest inlets in Ireland and a deep-water harbour.

Geography

The bay contains Whiddy Island, site of a major oil terminal, and Bere Island near its mouth. The town of Bantry sits at its head, with Glengarriff and Castletownbere among settlements on its shores.

History

In 1796 a French expedition under General Hoche, carrying Wolfe Tone, attempted to land in Bantry Bay to support Irish rebellion, but storms scattered the fleet — an episode commemorated in the song tradition and in Bantry's Wolfe Tone Square. In 1979 the tanker Betelgeuse exploded at the Whiddy Island terminal, killing 50 people.

Economy

The bay supports mussel and salmon aquaculture, fishing, and tourism along the Wild Atlantic Way.
