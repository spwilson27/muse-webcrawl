The Shire

The Shire is a region of Middle-earth in J. R. R. Tolkien's fictional works, the homeland of the Hobbits. Located in the northwest of Middle-earth in Eriador, it is an inland realm settled by Hobbits in the Third Age and largely untouched by the wars of the wider world until the events of The Lord of the Rings.

Description

The Shire is divided into four Farthings — North, South, East and West — with the Three-Farthing Stone at their meeting point. Its settlements include Hobbiton, Bywater, Tuckborough and Michel Delving, the chief town. The Brandywine River forms its eastern border, with Buckland beyond.

Society

Hobbits of the Shire live in hobbit-holes and houses, farm the land, and govern themselves loosely through the Thain, the Mayor of Michel Delving, and Shirriffs. Tolkien modelled the Shire on the rural England of his childhood in the West Midlands.

History in the legendarium

Granted to the Hobbits by King Argeleb II of Arthedain in T.A. 1601, the Shire was invaded briefly during the Fell Winter and, in The Lord of the Rings, occupied and industrialised by Saruman's men — the "Scouring of the Shire" — before being restored by Frodo, Sam, Merry and Pippin.
