Diarrhea

Diarrhea results from excess water in the stool.

Diarrhea (also spelled diarrhoea) is the condition of passing loose or liquid stools at least three times a day. It occurs when the intestine absorbs too little water from, or secretes too much water into, the bowel contents. Acute diarrhea, usually caused by infection, is one of the leading causes of death in young children in low-income countries, responsible for roughly half a million deaths of children under five each year.

Causes

Infectious diarrhea (gastroenteritis) is caused by viruses such as rotavirus and norovirus, bacteria such as Escherichia coli, Salmonella, Campylobacter, and Vibrio cholerae (see cholera), and parasites such as Giardia. Non-infectious causes include irritable bowel syndrome, inflammatory bowel disease, coeliac disease, lactose intolerance, hyperthyroidism, and drugs such as antibiotics and metformin.

Mechanisms

Diarrhea is classified mechanistically as osmotic (unabsorbed solutes retain water in the gut), secretory (active secretion of electrolytes and water, as in cholera), inflammatory/exudative (mucosal damage), and motility-related.

Treatment and prevention

The mainstay of treatment is oral rehydration therapy — a solution of salts and glucose whose uptake drives water absorption — which has saved tens of millions of lives since its introduction. Zinc supplementation shortens episodes in children. Antibiotics are reserved for specific infections such as cholera and dysentery. Prevention rests on clean water, sanitation, hand washing, breastfeeding, and rotavirus vaccination.
