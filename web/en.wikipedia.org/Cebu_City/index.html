Cebu City

Cebu City, officially the City of Cebu (Cebuano: Dakbayan sa Sugbo), is a highly urbanized city in the Central Visayas region of the Philippines. According to the 2020 census, it has a population of 964,169 people, making it the sixth-most populated city in the country and the most populous in the Central Visayas Region.

The city is the capital of Cebu province and the main domestic shipping port of the Philippines. Located on the mid-eastern coast of Cebu island, it is the center of Metro Cebu, the second largest metropolitan area in the country. Cebu City was the first Spanish settlement and the oldest city in the Philippines.

History

The settlement was established by Rajah Humabon, whose baptism as a Christian by Ferdinand Magellan's expedition in 1521 began the Spanish colonial period. After Humabon's successor Rajah Tupas lost to Miguel López de Legazpi in 1565, the town became the first Spanish capital of the Philippines, named Villa de San Miguel de Cebú. Fort San Pedro, built in 1565, remains the oldest triangular bastion fort in the country. Magellan's Cross and the Basilica del Santo Niño, housing the image of the Santo Niño de Cebú, are major landmarks of the city's Spanish heritage.

Geography

Cebu City has a land area of 315 square kilometres, bounded by Mandaue City and Consolacion to the north, Talisay City to the south, and Balamban and Toledo to the west. Its terrain rises from the coastal plain to the mountain barangays of the interior.

Economy

The city is a hub for trade, commerce, education and industry in the Visayas. The Cebu Business Park and Cebu IT Park host business process outsourcing firms, while Mactan island across the channel holds export processing zones and resorts. Cebu's port handles the majority of domestic shipping in the Philippines.

Culture

The Sinulog festival, held every third Sunday of January in honour of the Santo Niño, draws millions of visitors. The city is known for lechon, dried mangoes, and its guitar-making industry in nearby Lapu-Lapu City.
