Operation Paget

Operation Paget
Launched: 2004, Metropolitan Police
Led by: Lord Stevens of Kirkwhelpington
Subject: Death of Diana, Princess of Wales
Report published: 14 December 2006

Operation Paget was a British police investigation led by former Metropolitan Police Commissioner Lord Stevens into the conspiracy theories surrounding the death of Diana, Princess of Wales on 31 August 1997 in the Pont de l'Alma tunnel in Paris.

Background

Diana, her companion Dodi Fayed and their driver Henri Paul died after their Mercedes crashed while being pursued by paparazzi. A French judicial investigation (1999) and a British coroner's inquest were complicated by persistent allegations, promoted especially by Dodi's father Mohamed Al-Fayed, that the couple had been murdered by British intelligence on the orders of Prince Philip, that Diana had been pregnant, and that the couple were about to announce their engagement. Because such allegations amounted to claims of criminal conspiracy, the Metropolitan Police opened Operation Paget in 2004 to examine them.

Findings

The 832-page report, published on 14 December 2006, addressed 814 allegations and concluded that there was no evidence of any conspiracy to murder Diana or Fayed, no evidence of pregnancy, and no evidence of a forthcoming engagement announcement. It confirmed the French findings: Henri Paul had been drinking and driving at excessive speed, none of the occupants wore seatbelts, and Paul's impairment and the pursuing vehicles contributed to the crash. The subsequent inquest (2007–08), at which the Paget evidence was presented, returned a verdict of unlawful killing through grossly negligent driving.
