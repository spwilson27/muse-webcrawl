Guru Granth Sahib

LanguagePunjabi, Braj, Sanskrit, Persian and others (Gurmukhi script)

The Guru Granth Sahib (Punjabi: ਗੁਰੂ ਗ੍ਰੰਥ ਸਾਹਿਬ) is the central holy scripture of Sikhism, regarded by Sikhs as the eternal, living Guru and the final sovereign authority of the faith. It is a collection of hymns (shabad) composed by the Sikh Gurus and by Hindu and Muslim saints, set to musical ragas.

Compilation

The first recension, the Adi Granth, was compiled by Guru Arjan, the fifth Guru, in 1604, with Bhai Gurdas as scribe, and installed in the Golden Temple in Amritsar. Guru Gobind Singh, the tenth Guru, later added the hymns of his father Guru Tegh Bahadur and, shortly before his death in 1708, declared that there would be no further human Gurus: the scripture itself would be the Guru for all time.

Contents

The standard printed edition has 1,430 pages (angs) and contains hymns by six Sikh Gurus and fifteen bhagats and other holy men, including Kabir, Namdev, Ravidas and the Sufi Sheikh Farid — a strikingly interfaith compilation. The hymns are arranged by raga (musical mode) and are written mainly in Punjabi and related languages in the Gurmukhi script. Its opening composition is the Japji Sahib of Guru Nanak, beginning with the Mool Mantar.

Veneration

In a gurdwara the Guru Granth Sahib is enthroned on a raised platform under a canopy, fanned with a chaur, ceremonially put to rest at night and awakened in the morning. A random reading (hukamnama) is taken daily for guidance. Non-Sikhs as well as Sikhs bow before it as a mark of respect.
