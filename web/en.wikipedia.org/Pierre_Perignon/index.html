Pierre Pérignon

Dom Pierre Pérignon (1638–1715) was a French Benedictine monk who made important contributions to the production of champagne wine at the abbey of Saint-Pierre d'Hautvillers in the Champagne region of France.

Life

Appointed cellarer of Hautvillers Abbey in 1668, a post he held until his death, Pérignon worked to improve the quality of the abbey's wines. He refined techniques of blending grapes from different vineyards, gentle pressing to keep white wine from black grapes, and the use of strong bottles and cork stoppers to contain the secondary fermentation that makes champagne sparkling.

Legacy

Popular legend credits Pérignon with inventing champagne and exclaiming "Come quickly, I am tasting the stars!" — stories largely created by later marketing. His real achievement lay in raising the standard of Champagne winemaking. The prestige cuvée Dom Pérignon, produced by Moët & Chandon, bears his name.
