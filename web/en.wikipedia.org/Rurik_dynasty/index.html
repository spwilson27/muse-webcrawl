The Rurik dynasty, also known as the Rurikids, was the ruling dynasty of Kievan Rus' and its successor principalities, and later of the Grand Duchy of Moscow and the Tsardom of Russia, for more than seven centuries. According to the Primary Chronicle, the dynasty was founded in 862 by Rurik, a Varangian prince invited to rule at Novgorod.

History

Rurik's successors — Oleg, Igor, Olga and Sviatoslav — consolidated power in Kyiv, and under Volodymyr the Great the Rus' adopted Christianity in 988. Succession by lateral rota among brothers and cousins fragmented the realm into appanage principalities, a system that weakened Rus' before the Mongol invasions of the 13th century. The Moscow branch of the dynasty, descending from Alexander Nevsky through Daniil of Moscow, gradually gathered the Rus' lands; Ivan III threw off Mongol suzerainty, and Ivan IV ("the Terrible") was crowned the first Tsar in 1547.

End of the dynasty

The main line ended with the death of the childless Feodor I in 1598, precipitating the Time of Troubles. Prince Vasili Shuisky, from the Shuisky branch of the Rurikids, reigned as Vasili IV from 1606 to 1610, the last Rurikid on the Russian throne. Numerous princely families of Russia and Poland–Lithuania (including the Shuiskys, Dolgorukovs and Repnins) descend from the dynasty's cadet branches.
