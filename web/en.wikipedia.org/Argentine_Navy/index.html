Argentine Navy

The Argentine Navy (Spanish: Armada de la República Argentina) is the naval force of Argentina and one of the three branches of the Armed Forces of the Argentine Republic, together with the Army and the Air Force. Its origins date to the May Revolution of 1810 and the Argentine War of Independence, in which the first Argentine naval squadron, commanded by figures such as William Brown, fought against Spanish forces.

History

The navy played an important role in 19th-century conflicts, including the Cisplatine War with Brazil and the War of the Triple Alliance. By the early 20th century, an arms race with Chile and Brazil led Argentina to acquire dreadnought battleships such as Rivadavia and Moreno. The navy later operated aircraft carriers, including ARA Veinticinco de Mayo.

During the 1982 Falklands War (Guerra de las Malvinas) against the United Kingdom, the Argentine Navy deployed its carrier and fleet units; the sinking of the cruiser ARA General Belgrano by the British submarine HMS Conqueror, with the loss of 323 lives, was one of the war's most significant events. After the cruiser's sinking, the Argentine surface fleet largely remained in port for the rest of the conflict. The submarine ARA San Luis conducted patrols against the British task force.

Modern force

Today the navy is headquartered at Puerto Belgrano Naval Base near Bahía Blanca, with additional bases including Mar del Plata and Ushuaia. It operates destroyers, frigates, corvettes, patrol vessels, submarines, and a naval aviation arm, and participates in Antarctic campaigns and multinational exercises. In November 2017 the submarine ARA San Juan was lost with all 44 crew in the South Atlantic; its wreck was located a year later.
