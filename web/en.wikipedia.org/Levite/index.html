Levite

Levites (or Levi) are Jewish males who claim patrilineal descent from the Tribe of Levi. The Tribe of Levi descended from Levi, the third son of Jacob and Leah. The Levites, including the Kohanim (priests), were set apart for service in the Tabernacle and later the Temple in Jerusalem.

Role in the Hebrew Bible

According to the Torah, the Levites were chosen to assist the priests, care for the Tabernacle and its vessels, and serve as teachers and judges. Unlike the other tribes of Israel, the Levites received no territorial inheritance in the Land of Israel; instead they were given forty-eight Levitical cities and supported by tithes.

Temple service

Levites performed a variety of functions: gatekeepers, singers and musicians, treasurers, and assistants in the sacrificial service. The Levitical singers, organised into courses, provided the music of the Temple, including the singing of the Psalms.

Modern practice

Today, Levites retain a distinct status in traditional Jewish ritual: they are called to the Torah second, after the Kohen, and ritually wash the hands of the Kohanim before the Priestly Blessing. See also Tribe of Levi.
