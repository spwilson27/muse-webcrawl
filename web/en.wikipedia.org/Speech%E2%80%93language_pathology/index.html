Speech–language pathology

FieldHealthcare, rehabilitation
PractitionersSpeech-language pathologists (SLPs)
ConditionsAphasia, apraxia, dysphagia, stuttering, articulation disorders

Speech–language pathology is a healthcare and academic discipline concerned with the assessment, diagnosis, and treatment of communication and swallowing disorders. Its practitioners, speech-language pathologists (also called speech therapists), work with people of all ages, from infants with feeding difficulties to adults recovering from stroke.

Scope

The field covers speech sound production, language comprehension and expression, voice and resonance, fluency (including stuttering), cognition and social communication, and swallowing (dysphagia). Common caseloads include children with developmental language disorder or autism, and adults with aphasia, dysarthria, or traumatic brain injury.

Settings

Speech-language pathologists work in schools, hospitals, rehabilitation centres, private practice, and research. In most countries the profession requires a master's degree and clinical certification or licensure.
