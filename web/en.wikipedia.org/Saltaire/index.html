Saltaire

Salts Mill and workers' houses in the model village of Saltaire.

Saltaire is a Victorian model village in Shipley, West Yorkshire, England, built between 1851 and 1871 by the industrialist Sir Titus Salt to house the workers of his textile mill, Salts Mill, away from the pollution of Bradford. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, inscribed in 2001.

History

Salt, a manufacturer of alpaca and mohair cloth, commissioned architects Lockwood and Mawson to build a mill and an entire self-contained village: stone houses with running water and outside toilets, a school, hospital, church (now Saltaire United Reformed Church), institute, almshouses and Roberts Park along the River Aire. Notably, the village had no pub, reflecting Salt's temperance beliefs. The name combines "Salt" with the River Aire.

Salts Mill today

The mill closed in 1986 and was bought by entrepreneur Jonathan Silver, who transformed it into a cultural and commercial centre housing one of the largest collections of works by Bradford-born artist David Hockney, along with shops, restaurants and tech businesses.

Transport

Saltaire railway station, reopened in 1984 on the Airedale line, and the Leeds and Liverpool Canal and the River Aire all serve the village. The nearby Shipley Glen Tramway connects Saltaire to Shipley Glen.
