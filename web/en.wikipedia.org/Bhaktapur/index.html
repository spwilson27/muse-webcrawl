Bhaktapur

Bhaktapur (Nepali: भक्तपुर), also known as Khwopa in the Newar language and historically as Bhadgaon, is a city in the east corner of the Kathmandu Valley in Nepal, about 13 kilometres from Kathmandu. It was the capital of the Malla confederacy and the largest of the three royal cities of the valley until the second half of the 15th century. Its well-preserved medieval centre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the Kathmandu Valley listing.

History and heritage

Bhaktapur flourished under the Malla kings, especially Bhupatindra Malla (reigned 1696–1722), who built much of its celebrated Durbar Square. Landmarks include the 55-Window Palace, the five-storey Nyatapola Temple (1702) — the tallest pagoda temple in Nepal — the Bhairavnath Temple, and Taumadhi and Dattatreya squares. The city is renowned for Newar art, woodcarving, pottery and its yoghurt, juju dhau ("king curd"). Bhaktapur was severely damaged by the 1934 Nepal–Bihar earthquake and again by the April 2015 Gorkha earthquake, after which major restoration campaigns rebuilt many monuments.

Culture

Bhaktapur is famous for its festivals, above all Biska Jatra, the New Year festival in mid-April involving chariot processions and a ceremonial tug-of-war. Entry fees paid by visitors fund much of the city's conservation, making it a noted model of heritage-funded preservation in South Asia.
