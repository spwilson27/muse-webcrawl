Hypermarket

A hypermarket is a large retail store that combines a supermarket and a department store under one roof. The result is an expansive retail facility carrying a wide range of products under one roof, including full groceries lines and general merchandise, typically in a warehouse-like setting with extensive parking.

History

The concept is credited to Carrefour, which opened the first hypermarket in 1963 at Sainte-Geneviève-des-Bois near Paris. The format spread across Europe in the following decades. In the United States, Fred Meyer pioneered a similar one-stop format earlier, and chains such as Meijer, Walmart (with its Supercenters) and Target developed the superstore format from the 1980s onward.

Characteristics

Hypermarkets typically occupy 5,000 to over 20,000 square metres of selling space and stock tens of thousands of product lines, roughly a third of them food. Their business model relies on high volume and low margins, self-service, and destination shopping by car. Since the 2010s the format has faced pressure from discounters, convenience stores and e-commerce, leading several operators to reduce store sizes or add online fulfilment.
