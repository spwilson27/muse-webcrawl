Cultural identity

Cultural identity is the identity of belonging to a group. It is part of a person's self-conception and self-perception and is related to nationality, ethnicity, religion, social class, generation, locality, or any kind of social group that has its own distinct culture. In this way, cultural identity is both characteristic of the individual but also of the culturally identical group of members sharing the same cultural identity or upbringing.

Cultural identity is a dynamic process: it is formed through socialization within families, schools, peer groups, and media, and it changes over the course of a person's life, especially in contexts of migration, diaspora, or intercultural contact. Scholars such as Stuart Hall have argued that cultural identity should be understood not as a fixed essence but as a "production" that is always in process.

Components

Cultural identity typically involves shared elements such as language, religion, cuisine, social habits, music, and arts. A person's cultural identity may differ from the identity others ascribe to them, and individuals may hold multiple, overlapping cultural identities simultaneously — for example, a regional identity, a national identity, and an ethnic or religious identity.

Formation and change

Identity formation is an important part of adolescent development. Acculturation — the process by which individuals adopt elements of another culture while retaining their own — can produce strategies of integration, assimilation, separation, or marginalization. Globalization and mass migration have made questions of cultural identity increasingly prominent in politics and public debate, including debates over multiculturalism and national identity.
