Port of Galveston

The Port of Galveston is the port of the city of Galveston, Texas, located on the upper Texas Gulf Coast at the mouth of Galveston Bay. Established by a proclamation issued by the Congress of the Republic of Texas on December 17, 1839, it is the oldest port in the Gulf of Mexico west of New Orleans. The port occupies facilities on Galveston Island and Pelican Island, connected by the Galveston Ship Channel to the Gulf of Mexico.

Operations

Today Galveston is one of the busiest cruise ports in the United States, ranking among the top homeports in North America, with terminals serving lines such as Carnival Cruise Line, Royal Caribbean International, and Disney Cruise Line. In addition to cruises, the port handles roll-on/roll-off cargo, wind turbine components and other project cargo, containers, bulk and breakbulk goods, and supports offshore energy and lay-up vessels. The port is operated by the Board of Trustees of the Galveston Wharves, a city-owned entity, and its activities generate a significant share of the Galveston County economy.

History

In the 19th century Galveston was Texas's principal port and one of the great cotton-exporting ports of the world; its wharves and the surrounding "Wall Street of the Southwest" commercial district flourished until the devastating hurricane of 1900, after which commerce shifted toward Houston and its new ship channel. The port rebuilt, modernized its wharves, and reinvented itself in the late 20th century as a cruise and specialty cargo port.
