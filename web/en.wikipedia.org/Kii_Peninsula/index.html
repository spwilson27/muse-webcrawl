Kii Peninsula

The Kii Peninsula (紀伊半島) is the largest peninsula on Honshū, the main island of Japan. It projects south into the Pacific Ocean from the Kansai region, spanning Mie, Nara and Wakayama prefectures, and takes its name from the ancient Kii Province.

Geography and significance

Mountainous and heavily forested, the peninsula receives some of Japan's highest rainfall. It is sacred geography: the "Sacred Sites and Pilgrimage Routes in the Kii Mountain Range" — including Mount Kōya, the Kumano shrines and Yoshino — are a UNESCO World Heritage Site, long walked by pilgrims on the Kumano Kodō trails. The Nachi Falls and the temple Seiganto-ji are emblematic landmarks.
