Kochi (Malayalam: കൊച്ചി), formerly known as Cochin, is a major port city on the Malabar Coast of India, in the state of Kerala. Bordering the Laccadive Sea (Arabian Sea), it is part of the district of Ernakulam and forms the largest urban agglomeration in Kerala. Kochi has been ranked among India's most important seaports and is often called the commercial and financial capital of Kerala.

History

Kochi rose to prominence after the great flood of the Periyar River in 1341 silted up the harbour at nearby Muziris (Kodungallur) and created a natural harbour at Kochi. The port became a centre of the Indian spice trade, attracting Arab, Chinese and Jewish merchants; the distinctive cantilevered Chinese fishing nets still used along the shore are traditionally said to date from this period.

In 1498 Vasco da Gama reached India, and in 1500 Pedro Álvares Cabral arrived at Kochi, where the Raja allowed the Portuguese to build a trading post. Fort Emmanuel, erected in 1503, was the first European fort in India. The city subsequently passed to the Dutch (1663) and then to the British (1795). Mattancherry Palace, built by the Portuguese and remodelled by the Dutch, and the Paradesi Synagogue (1568) in Jew Town testify to the city's layered cosmopolitan past.

Kochi was a princely state under British paramountcy until Indian independence, merging with Travancore in 1949 to form Travancore-Cochin, and becoming part of Kerala state in 1956.

Geography and climate

The city spreads across a cluster of islands and peninsulas — including Fort Kochi, Mattancherry, Willingdon Island, Vypeen and Bolgatty Island — on the shores of Vembanad Lake, where several rivers meet the sea. Kochi has a tropical monsoon climate, with heavy rainfall during the southwest monsoon (June–September) and relatively small seasonal temperature variation.

Economy

Cochin Port, one of India's major ports, handles containers at the International Container Transshipment Terminal at Vallarpadam. The city is home to Cochin Shipyard, India's largest shipbuilding and repair facility, as well as petrochemical and fertiliser industries, a naval base (headquarters of the Indian Navy's Southern Naval Command), the Kochi Refineries, and a growing information technology sector centred on Infopark and Kakkanad. Tourism — centred on the heritage areas of Fort Kochi and Mattancherry, Kerala backwater cruises, and Kathakali performance traditions — is also significant.

Transport

Cochin International Airport at Nedumbassery, among India's busiest airports for international traffic, serves the city; it became the world's first airport fully powered by solar energy. The Kochi Metro, opened in 2017, links Aluva with the city centre and beyond. National highways and the railway stations Ernakulam Junction and Ernakulam Town connect Kochi with the rest of India, and ferries link the islands of the harbour.

Culture

Kochi is known as a centre for contemporary art through the Kochi-Muziris Biennale, India's largest contemporary art exhibition, first held in 2012. The city hosted India's first European church (St. Francis Church, 1503), where Vasco da Gama was originally buried, and preserves communities shaped by centuries of trade: Konkani, Gujarati, Anglo-Indian, and the historic Cochin Jews.
