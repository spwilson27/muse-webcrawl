Bard

In medieval Gaelic and Welsh society, a bard was a professional poet and storyteller employed by a patron to compose praise poetry, preserve genealogy and history, and entertain the court. Bards formed a highly trained, hereditary caste in Ireland and Wales.

Traditions

Irish bards (fili at the highest grade) underwent years of training in strict metres; Welsh bards (beirdd) served the princes of the Welsh kingdoms, a tradition celebrated in the works associated with Taliesin and Aneirin. In Scotland the bard served Highland chiefs, alongside the harpist and piper.

Decline and revival

The collapse of Gaelic lordship in the 17th century ended the bardic order. The title was revived culturally: William Shakespeare is known as "the Bard of Avon", and in Wales the Gorsedd of the Bards, founded by Iolo Morganwg in 1792, still crowns bards at the National Eisteddfod.

In fantasy

The bard appears as a character class in role-playing games such as Dungeons & Dragons, a musician whose magic flows from song and lore.
