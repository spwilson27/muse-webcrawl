Children's television series


Puppetry is a longstanding feature of children's television

Children's television series (or children's television shows) are television programmes designed specifically for children. They are typically characterised by easy-going content devoid of sensitive issues, and come in many variants, including animated series, live-action programmes, puppet shows and educational programming. Children's television is usually broadcast during the morning or afternoon (after school) hours, or at weekends.

History

Children's television dates to the earliest years of the medium. In the United Kingdom, the BBC's For the Children began in 1937, and Watch with Mother (1950) catered to pre-school viewers. In the United States, Howdy Doody (1947) and Kukla, Fran and Ollie were early successes. A landmark came in 1969 with Sesame Street, produced by the Children's Television Workshop, which combined entertainment with a researched educational curriculum and demonstrated measurable learning benefits for its young audience.

Genres and formats

Programming is usually targeted at specific age bands: pre-school (ages 2–5), younger children (6–8) and older children (9–12), as well as tweens. Common formats include animated cartoons, educational series teaching literacy, numeracy or social skills, puppet and costumed-character shows, magazine programmes mixing segments with studio presenters, drama series, game shows and science or factual programmes. Since the 2000s, dedicated children's channels and, increasingly, streaming services have transformed how such programmes are distributed, with on-demand viewing displacing fixed broadcast schedules in many markets.

Regulation

Children's programming is regulated in many countries, particularly regarding advertising. Rules may limit advertising during children's programmes, prohibit certain product categories, or require minimum amounts of domestically produced children's content. In the United States, the Children's Television Act of 1990 required broadcasters to air educational programming for children as a condition of licence renewal.
