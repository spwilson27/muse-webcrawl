Treaty of London (1604)

The Treaty of London was a peace treaty signed on 28 August 1604 (Old Style) at Somerset House in London between England, under King James I, and Spain, under Philip III, formally ending the Anglo-Spanish War of 1585–1604. Negotiations, known as the Somerset House Conference, had begun after James succeeded Elizabeth I in 1603 and were commemorated in a famous group portrait of the two delegations.

Terms

Spain recognized the Protestant James as king of England, renounced support for Catholic plots and invasion, and opened the Spanish Netherlands' ports to English shipping, while England ended official support for Dutch rebels and privateering raids on Spanish commerce, and opened English ports to Spanish ships. Notably, England did not secure Spanish toleration for the Dutch Republic or freedom of trade with the Indies, grievances that persisted. The peace lasted until 1625 and eased the path for English colonization ventures in the Americas, including the founding of Jamestown in 1607.
