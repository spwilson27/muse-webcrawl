Plankalkül

Plankalkül ("plan calculus") is a programming language designed by the German engineer Konrad Zuse between 1942 and 1945. It was the first high-level programming language to be designed for a computer, though it was not implemented during Zuse's lifetime and was published in full only in 1972.

Design

The language was intended for Zuse's Z4 computer and, more generally, as a general "calculus of plans" for expressing computational procedures. It included advanced concepts for its time: structured data types, arrays and records, conditional statements, iteration, subroutines, and assertions, as well as operations on chess positions in Zuse's sample programs.

History and implementation

Zuse's 1945 manuscript remained largely unknown outside Germany for decades. The first compiler for Plankalkül was implemented only in 1998, with a further implementation for a dissertation in 2000, more than fifty years after the language was designed.
