Cyberstalking

Cyberstalking is the use of the internet or other electronic means to stalk or harass an individual, group, or organisation. It may include false accusations, defamation, slander and libel, monitoring, identity theft, threats, vandalism, solicitation for sex, doxing, or gathering information that may be used to threaten, embarrass, or harass the victim.

Definition and behaviour

Cyberstalking is generally defined as a repeated course of conduct carried out online that would cause a reasonable person to feel fear or substantial emotional distress. Perpetrators may send threatening messages, post false information, install stalkerware on a victim's devices, track their location, or encourage others to join the harassment. Motives include revenge, jealousy, control, and obsession, and offenders are often former intimate partners.

Prevalence and impact

Surveys in several countries indicate that millions of people, disproportionately women and young people, experience cyberstalking. Victims report anxiety, depression, sleep disturbance, and changes to their daily routines and online behaviour; in some cases online stalking escalates to offline violence.

Legal responses

Many jurisdictions have enacted anti-stalking laws that expressly cover electronic communications. In the United States, federal law prohibits using interstate communications to stalk, and states such as California were early to adopt specific cyberstalking statutes. Enforcement is complicated by perpetrators' anonymity, cross-border conduct, and the involvement of online platforms, whose reporting and blocking tools are a common first line of defence for victims.
