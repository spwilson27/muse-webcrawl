Instrument of Surrender (1971)

Instrument of Surrender
Signed16 December 1971
LocationRamna Race Course, Dhaka
SignatoriesLt. Gen. A. A. K. Niazi (Pakistan); Lt. Gen. Jagjit Singh Aurora (India)

The Pakistani Instrument of Surrender was a written agreement that enabled the surrender of 93,000 Pakistani soldiers to the joint forces of the Indian Army and the Mukti Bahini of Bangladesh, ending the Bangladesh Liberation War and the Indo-Pakistani War of 1971. It was signed on 16 December 1971 at the Ramna Race Course in Dhaka.

The surrender

Lieutenant General Amir Abdullah Khan Niazi, commander of the Pakistan Eastern Command, signed the instrument before Lieutenant General Jagjit Singh Aurora, commander of the Indian Eastern Command and the joint Indo-Bangladeshi forces. The surrender marked the largest capitulation of a military force since the Second World War and confirmed the independence of Bangladesh.

Aftermath

The Pakistani prisoners of war were repatriated under the Delhi Agreement of 1973 and the Simla Agreement. In Bangladesh, 16 December is celebrated as Victory Day; the site in Dhaka is now the Suhrawardy Udyan.
