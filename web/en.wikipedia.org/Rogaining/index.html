Rogaining

Rogaining is an orienteering sport of long-distance cross-country navigation in which teams of two to five people visit as many checkpoints as possible within a set time limit. Classic championship events run for 24 hours; shorter formats of 3, 6, or 12 hours are also common. Strategy, route planning, and endurance matter as much as navigation skill, because checkpoints carry different point values and teams choose their own routes.

History

The sport originated in Australia in the 1970s, growing out of twenty-four-hour walks organised by the Melbourne University Mountaineering Club from 1947 onward. The name is traditionally derived from the names of three founders — Rod, Gail, and Neil Phillips — or as an acronym for "Rugged Outdoor Group Activity Involving Navigation and Endurance." The first World Rogaining Championships were held in Victoria, Australia, in 1992, and the sport has since spread widely, especially in New Zealand, Europe, and North America.

Rules

Teams receive maps shortly before the start and plan their routes. During the event they navigate on foot using only map and compass (GPS use is prohibited), visiting checkpoints marked by orange-and-white flags and recording their visits with punches or electronic tags. Points are deducted for late return, and the team with the highest score wins. Competitors carry food, water, and safety gear and may return to a central "hash house" for hot meals during long events.
