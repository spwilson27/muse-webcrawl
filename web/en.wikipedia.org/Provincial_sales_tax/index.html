Provincial sales tax

In several provinces, sales tax appears as separate federal and provincial lines.

Provincial sales taxes (PST) are consumption taxes levied by provinces of Canada on the sale of goods and certain services. Together with the federal Goods and Services Tax (GST), they form the sales tax paid by consumers in most of the country, though the structure varies by province.

Structures across Canada

In Ontario, Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, and Prince Edward Island, the provincial tax has been harmonised with the GST into a single Harmonized Sales Tax (HST) administered by the federal government. British Columbia adopted the HST in 2010 but returned to a separate GST + PST system in 2013 after a referendum. Saskatchewan, Manitoba (where the tax is called the Retail Sales Tax, RST) and British Columbia levy a standalone PST alongside the GST, each with its own base and rules. Quebec operates the Quebec Sales Tax (QST), administered by Revenu Québec and largely harmonised with the GST base. Alberta and the three territories levy no provincial sales tax at all.

Features

Traditional PSTs are single-stage retail taxes that can "cascade" when businesses pay tax on their inputs — one reason economists favoured harmonisation into value-added taxes like the GST/HST, which credit business inputs. PST rates have generally ranged from about 6 to 10 per cent, applied on top of (and in some cases compounded with) the 5 per cent GST.
