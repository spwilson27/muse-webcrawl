Idiophone

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An idiophone is any musical instrument that creates sound primarily by the vibration of the instrument itself, without the use of air flow, strings, membranes, or electronic amplification. Idiophones form one of the five main categories of the Hornbostel–Sachs classification of musical instruments.

Classification

Hornbostel and Sachs divided idiophones according to how they are set in vibration: struck idiophones (such as xylophones, gongs, and bells), plucked idiophones (such as the Jew's harp and mbira), friction idiophones (such as the glass harmonica and singing bowl played with a wet finger), and blown idiophones (rare devices such as the aeolsklavier). Struck idiophones are further divided into concussion instruments (cymbals, castanets) and percussion idiophones struck with a beater (triangle, tubular bells).

Materials and examples

Idiophones are made of wood, metal, stone, bamboo, clay, and glass. They include some of the oldest known instruments; lithophones (sounding stones) and slit drums are found worldwide. Familiar orchestral idiophones include the glockenspiel, vibraphone, marimba, tubular bells, cymbals, and triangle, while bells of all kinds—church bells, handbells, and carillons—are among the largest musical instruments ever made.
