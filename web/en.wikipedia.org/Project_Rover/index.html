Project Rover

Project Rover was a United States project to develop a nuclear thermal rocket, conducted at Los Alamos National Laboratory from 1955 to 1972. It produced the Kiwi and Phoebus reactor designs and provided the basis for the NERVA engine programme with NASA.

Programme

Nuclear thermal propulsion heats liquid hydrogen propellant in a nuclear reactor and expels it through a nozzle, roughly doubling the specific impulse of chemical rockets. The Rover team built and test-fired a series of reactors at the Nevada Test Site: the low-power Kiwi-A (1959), progressively more powerful Kiwi-B models, and the Phoebus series, of which Phoebus-2A (1968) reached about 4,200 megawatts, among the most powerful nuclear reactors ever operated. Tests also included the Nuclear Furnace and the XE engine tests under NERVA.

End of the programme

Project Rover and NERVA were cancelled in 1972–1973 as post-Apollo budgets fell and a crewed Mars mission, the main use case, receded. The technology remains a reference point for renewed interest in nuclear thermal propulsion for deep-space missions.
