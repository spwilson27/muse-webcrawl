Zermatt is a municipality in the canton of Valais in southern Switzerland, famous as a mountaineering and ski resort beneath the Matterhorn, whose pyramid peak (4,478 m) towers over the village. Zermatt is car-free; visitors arrive by rail and move about on foot, by horse-drawn carriage, or in small electric vehicles.

History

The village grew from an Alpine farming community at the head of the Mattertal valley. Its transformation began with the golden age of alpinism: the first ascent of the Matterhorn by Edward Whymper's party in 1865 — which ended with four climbers falling to their deaths on the descent — made Zermatt world-famous. The Gornergrat railway opened in 1898, and the Visp–Zermatt line linked the village to the Swiss rail network in 1891.

Skiing and mountaineering

Zermatt's ski area, shared with Cervinia in Italy, includes the Klein Matterhorn — reached by cable car to 3,883 m, among the highest lift-served points in Europe — and offers glacier skiing year-round. The Gornergrat cog railway provides panoramic views of Monte Rosa and the Gorner Glacier, the second-largest glacier in the Alps.

Transport

The Matterhorn Gotthard Bahn connects Zermatt with Brig and the Glacier Express route to St. Moritz. Combustion-engine cars are prohibited in the village; drivers park at Täsch and take a shuttle train.
