The Co-operative Commonwealth Federation (CCF; French: Fédération du Commonwealth coopératif) was a federal democratic socialist and social-democratic political party in Canada. It was founded in Calgary in 1932 as a coalition of socialist, agrarian, co-operative and labour groups, and adopted its founding programme, the Regina Manifesto, in 1933.

History

Under its first leader, J. S. Woodsworth, the CCF grew out of the protest movements of the Great Depression, uniting western farmers' parties with labour and socialist groups in Ontario and elsewhere. The Regina Manifesto called for the public ownership of key industries, social welfare programmes, and the replacement of the capitalist system — closing with the famous pledge that "no CCF Government will rest content until it has eradicated capitalism."

The party's greatest breakthrough came in Saskatchewan, where in 1944 Tommy Douglas led the provincial CCF to power, forming the first socialist government in North America. The Saskatchewan CCF introduced public hospital insurance and medical care insurance that became the models for Canada's national Medicare system. Federally, the CCF pressed the Liberal government toward the welfare state, though it never formed a federal government; its best federal result came in 1945.

Legacy

In 1956 the party moderated its platform in the Winnipeg Declaration, and in 1961, together with the Canadian Labour Congress, it dissolved to form the New Democratic Party (NDP). The CCF's legacy includes Medicare, unemployment insurance advocacy, old-age pensions, and the normalization of social-democratic ideas in Canadian politics.
