Initial


A decorated initial at the start of a chapter in an illuminated manuscript.

In a written work, an initial is a letter at the beginning of a text, a chapter or a paragraph that is larger than the rest of the text and often decorated or ornamental. Decorated initials were a prominent feature of medieval illuminated manuscripts, where they could be historiated — containing a small picture or scene — or inhabited by figures, animals and foliage. In modern typesetting the most common form is the drop cap (or dropped initial), a large capital letter that descends below the baseline of the first line of a paragraph, spanning several lines of text.

History

Enlarged initials appear in late antique manuscripts such as the Vergilius Vaticanus (5th century), where they helped readers navigate largely unpunctuated text. Insular and Carolingian scribes developed them into major artistic elements, and in Romanesque and Gothic manuscripts initials could fill much of a page, combining interlace, beasts and biblical scenes. With the arrival of printing, spaces were initially left blank for initials to be added by hand rubricators; printers soon supplied woodcut and then cast-metal initials instead.

Terminology

An initial is sometimes called a versal, especially one drawn in a contrasting colour. In German typographic usage, initials set into the text block are distinguished from those standing in the margin. A related device is the lombardic capital, a simple, rounded decorated letter used for minor divisions.
