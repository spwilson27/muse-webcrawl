Édouard Lucas

Édouard Lucas
Born4 April 1842, Amiens, France
Died3 October 1891, Paris
FieldsNumber theory, recreational mathematics

François Édouard Anatole Lucas (1842–1891) was a French mathematician known for his study of the Fibonacci sequence — to which he gave its name — and for the Lucas sequences, Lucas numbers, and the Lucas primality test. The related sequence of Lucas numbers and the Lucas–Lehmer test for Mersenne primes bear his name.

Work

Lucas developed methods for testing the primality of Mersenne numbers; in 1876 he proved by hand that 2127−1 is prime, the largest known prime for many years. He studied the Tower of Hanoi puzzle, which he invented and marketed under an anagrammatic pseudonym, and devised the "Dames Anglaises" and other recreational mathematics published in his Récréations mathématiques. He also worked on umbral calculus and designed plans (never built) for a mechanical computer to perform arithmetic.

Death

Lucas died in unusual circumstances: at a banquet, a dropped plate shattered and a fragment cut his cheek; he died days later of erysipelas (a skin infection), aged 49.
