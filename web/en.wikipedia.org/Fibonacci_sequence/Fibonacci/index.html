Fibonacci

Bornc. 1170, Pisa, Republic of Pisa
Diedc. 1250 (aged about 80), Pisa
Known forLiber Abaci; Fibonacci sequence; popularizing Hindu–Arabic numerals in Europe

Fibonacci (c. 1170 – c. 1250), also known as Leonardo Bonacci, Leonardo of Pisa, and Leonardo Bigollo Pisano, was an Italian mathematician from the Republic of Pisa, considered by many to be the most talented Western mathematician of the Middle Ages.

Life

Leonardo was born in Pisa, the son of Guglielmo Bonacci, a customs official who directed a trading post in Bugia (Béjaïa) in present-day Algeria. Traveling with his father and later around the Mediterranean, Leonardo learned the Hindu–Arabic numeral system and recognized its superiority over Roman numerals for calculation. He studied under Arab teachers and traveled in Egypt, Syria, Greece, and Sicily before returning to Pisa.

Liber Abaci

In 1202 Fibonacci published the Liber Abaci ("Book of Calculation"), which introduced the Hindu–Arabic place-value system and its arithmetic to European merchants and scholars. The book demonstrated practical uses in bookkeeping, currency conversion, and interest calculation. It also contained the rabbit-breeding problem whose solution generated the sequence now called the Fibonacci sequence: 0, 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, …, in which each term is the sum of the two preceding ones. The sequence had been known earlier to Indian mathematicians but became attached to Fibonacci's name through this work.

Other works and legacy

Fibonacci also wrote the Liber Quadratorum ("Book of Squares", 1225), dedicated to Emperor Frederick II, dealing with number theory including congrua and Pythagorean triples, and was honored at the imperial court. His works helped trigger the gradual adoption of Arabic numerals in Europe, a transformation fundamental to later commerce and science. The Fibonacci sequence acquired cultural fame for its connections with the golden ratio and supposed appearances in nature and art.
