Liber Abaci

Liber Abaci ("The Book of Calculation"), also spelled Liber Abbaci, is a Latin treatise on arithmetic by Leonardo of Pisa, posthumously known as Fibonacci. Published in 1202 and revised in 1228, it was one of the first Western books to describe the Hindu–Arabic numeral system and to advocate its use in European commerce.

Contents

The book is divided into fifteen chapters. The first introduces the nine Indian figures 9 8 7 6 5 4 3 2 1 and the sign 0, "which in Arabic is called zephir". Subsequent chapters cover multiplication and division of integers and fractions, the extraction of roots, the rule of three, currency conversion, barter, and problems of the type later called algebra and almucabala.

The rabbit problem

The third section contains the famous problem of the growth of a population of rabbits under idealised conditions: beginning with a single pair, each mature pair produces a new pair every month. The resulting sequence of pair counts — 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, … — is the Fibonacci sequence, although the sequence had been described earlier by Indian mathematicians in connection with poetic metre.

Influence

Liber Abaci demonstrated the practical advantages of the Hindu–Arabic numeral system for merchants, bankers, and surveyors. It remained a standard authority on arithmetic and algebra for two centuries, and its problems were copied into Italian abbaco schools that trained generations of commercial arithmeticians.
