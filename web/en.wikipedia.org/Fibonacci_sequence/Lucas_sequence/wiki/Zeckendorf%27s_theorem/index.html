Zeckendorf's theorem

Zeckendorf's theorem, named after Belgian amateur mathematician Edouard Zeckendorf, states that every positive integer can be represented uniquely as the sum of one or more distinct Fibonacci numbers in such a way that the sum does not include any two consecutive Fibonacci numbers. Such a sum is called a Zeckendorf representation.

Example

For example, 64 = 55 + 8 + 1, and 100 = 89 + 8 + 3. In each case the summands are non-consecutive Fibonacci numbers, and the representation is unique.

Proof sketch

Existence is proved by a greedy algorithm: repeatedly subtract the largest Fibonacci number not exceeding the remainder. Uniqueness follows by induction, using the fact that the sum of any set of non-consecutive Fibonacci numbers below Fk is less than Fk+1 − 1.

Applications

Zeckendorf representations give rise to the Fibonacci coding used in data compression, and to the Zeckendorf game studied in combinatorial game theory.
