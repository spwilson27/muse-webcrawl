Lucas sequence

In mathematics, the Lucas sequences U_n(P, Q) and V_n(P, Q) are certain constant-recursive integer sequences that satisfy the recurrence relation

x_n = P·x_{n−1} − Q·x_{n−2}

where P and Q are fixed integers. Any sequence satisfying this recurrence can be expressed as a linear combination of the two Lucas sequences. The sequences are named after the French mathematician Édouard Lucas, who studied them in the 19th century.

Examples

The Fibonacci sequence is U_n(1, −1): 0, 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, … and the sequence of Lucas numbers is the companion sequence V_n(1, −1): 2, 1, 3, 4, 7, 11, 18, 29, … Other well-known examples include the Pell numbers, Jacobsthal numbers, and Mersenne numbers, each corresponding to particular choices of P and Q.

Explicit formulas

Let a and b be the roots of the characteristic equation x² − Px + Q = 0. Then Un = (aⁿ − bⁿ)/(a − b) and Vn = aⁿ + bⁿ. For the Fibonacci case this yields Binet's formula involving the golden ratio φ = (1 + √5)/2, the same ratio that appears in the geometry of the Penrose tiling.

Properties and applications

Lucas sequences satisfy numerous divisibility properties analogous to those of the Fibonacci numbers, and they underlie the Lucas primality test and the Lucas–Lehmer primality test used to identify Mersenne primes. They also appear in cryptography and in the theory of Diophantine equations.
