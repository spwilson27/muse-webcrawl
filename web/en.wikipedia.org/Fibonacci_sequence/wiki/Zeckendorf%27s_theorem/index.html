Zeckendorf's theorem

Zeckendorf's theorem, named after Belgian mathematician Edouard Zeckendorf (1901–1983), states that every positive integer can be represented uniquely as the sum of one or more distinct Fibonacci numbers in such a way that the sum does not include any two consecutive Fibonacci numbers.

Statement

For any positive integer N there exist positive integers ci with no two consecutive, such that N equals the sum of the corresponding Fibonacci numbers Fci, where F2 = 1 and F3 = 2.

History

Although named after Zeckendorf, who published the result in 1972, the theorem had been stated earlier by Lekkerkerker; Zeckendorf's paper contains the first published proof.

Related concepts

Analogous results hold for Lucas numbers, giving the representation of integers as sums of non-consecutive Lucas numbers. The theorem underlies Fibonacci coding, a universal code for integers.
