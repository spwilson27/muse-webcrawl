Édouard Lucas

Born: 4 April 1842, Amiens, France
Died: 3 October 1891, Paris, France
Known for: Lucas sequences; Lucas primality test; Tower of Hanoi

François Édouard Anatole Lucas (4 April 1842 – 3 October 1891) was a French mathematician. He is best known for his study of the Fibonacci sequence and the related Lucas sequences, for devising a primality test for Mersenne numbers, and for inventing the Tower of Hanoi puzzle.

Work

Lucas studied generalizations of the Fibonacci sequence now called Lucas sequences, Un(P,Q) and Vn(P,Q); the Lucas numbers (2, 1, 3, 4, 7, 11, 18, ...) are one instance. Using these sequences he developed the Lucas primality test, and in 1876 he verified by hand calculations that the Mersenne number 2127 − 1 is prime — it remained the largest known prime for 75 years and is still the largest ever verified by hand computation.

Puzzles and recreation

Lucas published extensively on recreational mathematics in his four-volume Récréations mathématiques. He invented the Tower of Hanoi puzzle (1883), marketed under the anagrammatic pseudonym "N. Claus de Siam", and the game of Dots and Boxes (La Pipopipette). His unusual death — from an infection after a shard of a dropped plate cut his cheek at a banquet — is frequently remarked upon in mathematical lore.
