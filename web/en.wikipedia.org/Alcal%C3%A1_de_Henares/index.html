Alcalá de Henares

Alcalá de Henares is a Spanish city in the Community of Madrid, located about 35 kilometres (22 miles) northeast of Madrid on the banks of the Henares River. It is best known as the birthplace of Miguel de Cervantes, author of Don Quixote, and as the home of one of Europe's oldest universities, the University of Alcalá, founded in 1499 by Cardinal Cisneros. Its historic centre was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1998.

History

The area was settled in ancient times; the Romans founded the town of Complutum nearby. The name Alcalá derives from the Arabic al-qalʿa, meaning "the citadel", built on the Henares during Muslim rule. After the Christian reconquest in 1118, the town grew around its church and later its university. Cardinal Francisco Jiménez de Cisneros founded the Complutense University in 1499, which produced the famous Polyglot Bible. The university later moved to Madrid, and a new University of Alcalá was established in 1977.

Landmarks

Notable sites include the Cervantes Birthplace Museum, the Cathedral of Saints Justus and Pastor, the Archbishop's Palace, and the Calle Mayor, one of the longest porticoed streets in Spain. The city celebrates the Semana Cervantina each October around the anniversary of Cervantes's baptism.

Demographics

Alcalá de Henares has a population of roughly 200,000, making it one of the largest municipalities in the Community of Madrid outside the capital itself.
