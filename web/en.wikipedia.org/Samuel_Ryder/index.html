Samuel Ryder (1858–1936) was an English businessman who transformed the seed trade by selling "penny packets" of seeds by mail order, and who is best remembered as the founder and trophy donor of the Ryder Cup, the biennial golf match between Europe and the United States.

Business career

Ryder built a successful seed business in St Albans, Hertfordshire, packaging small quantities of seeds in penny packets for home gardeners, an innovation that made him wealthy.

Golf

Ryder took up golf at about the age of fifty for his health and became an enthusiastic patron of the professional game. After an unofficial match between British and American professionals in 1926, he commissioned and donated the gold trophy — topped with a figure modelled on his friend and coach Abe Mitchell — for the first official Ryder Cup match in 1927 at Worcester Country Club, Massachusetts.

Death and legacy

Ryder died in 1936. The Ryder Cup has grown into one of the most prestigious team events in world sport.
