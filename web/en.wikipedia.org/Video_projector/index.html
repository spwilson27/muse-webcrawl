Video projector

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A video projector is an image projector that receives a video signal and projects the corresponding image onto a projection screen using a lens system. Video projectors use very bright ultra-high-performance lamps, xenon arc lamps, metal-halide lamps, LEDs or solid-state lasers to provide the illumination required to project the image.

Technologies

Several display technologies are used to create the image:

  • CRT projectors use three small cathode-ray tubes (red, green and blue) whose images are converged on the screen; once dominant in high-end installations, they are now obsolete.
  • LCD projectors pass light through three liquid-crystal panels, one per primary colour.
  • DLP projectors use a Texas Instruments digital micromirror device — a chip covered with tiny tilting mirrors — with a colour wheel or separate chips for each colour.
  • LCoS projectors (including D-ILA and SXRD variants) reflect light off liquid-crystal-on-silicon panels, combining high resolution with good contrast.
  • Laser and LED projectors replace lamps with solid-state light sources for longer life and instant switching.

Characteristics

Key specifications include native resolution (from SVGA through 4K UHD), brightness measured in ANSI lumens, contrast ratio, and throw ratio, which relates projection distance to image width; "short throw" and "ultra-short throw" models produce large images close to the screen. Projectors are used in home cinema, classrooms, business presentations, houses of worship, simulators and large venues such as cinemas — digital cinema projectors being the highest-performance class. Performance is greatly affected by ambient light and screen material.

History

Video projection developed from earlier film and overhead projectors. Henry Kloss's Advent VideoBeam of the 1970s popularized home projection television; the Eidophor system served large venues earlier. Affordable LCD and DLP data projectors spread through the 1990s and 2000s, and miniaturized "pico" projectors were later integrated even into mobile phones.
