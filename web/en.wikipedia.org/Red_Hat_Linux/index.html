Red Hat Linux

Red Hat Linux was a widely used commercial open-source Linux distribution created by Red Hat, Inc. First released in 1994, it was one of the oldest and most popular Linux distributions, and it introduced the RPM Package Manager for installing and managing software.

History

Red Hat Linux grew from a distribution founded by Marc Ewing and later developed by the company he co-founded with Bob Young. It was sold in boxed retail editions and widely used for servers and workstations. In 2003 Red Hat discontinued the consumer Red Hat Linux line, splitting its efforts between the enterprise-focused Red Hat Enterprise Linux and the community-supported Fedora project.

Legacy

Numerous distributions were based on Red Hat Linux, and its conventions — including RPM packaging and the "Red Hat family" of distributions such as CentOS, Oracle Linux, and Rocky Linux — remain influential in enterprise computing.
