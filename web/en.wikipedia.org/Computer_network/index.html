Computer network

A computer network is a set of computers sharing resources located on or provided by network nodes. The computers use common communication protocols over digital interconnections to communicate with each other. These interconnections are made up of telecommunications network technologies based on physically wired, optical, and wireless radio-frequency methods that may be arranged in a variety of network topologies.

Types by scale

Networks are classified by their geographic scope: personal area networks (PANs) span a few metres, local area networks (LANs) cover a building or campus, metropolitan area networks (MANs) cover a city, and wide area networks (WANs) span countries and continents. The Internet is the largest WAN, an internetwork of networks using the TCP/IP protocol suite.

Protocols and architecture

Communication is organised in layers, described by the OSI model and the simpler TCP/IP model. At each layer, protocols govern addressing, routing, error control, and data format. Key devices include switches, which forward traffic within a LAN, and routers, which forward packets between networks.

History

Modern networking descends from the ARPANET, begun in 1969, which pioneered packet switching. Ethernet, developed in the 1970s, became the dominant LAN technology, and the commercialisation of the Internet in the 1990s made networking ubiquitous.
