George Roger Waters (born 6 September 1943) is an English musician and singer-songwriter. In 1965 he co-founded the rock band Pink Floyd as its bassist. Following the departure of Syd Barrett in 1968, Waters became the band's lyricist, co-lead vocalist and conceptual leader.

Career

Waters was the primary creative force behind Pink Floyd albums including The Dark Side of the Moon (1973), Wish You Were Here (1975), Animals (1977), The Wall (1979) and The Final Cut (1983). He left the band in 1985 and, after a legal dispute over the name, pursued a solo career with albums such as The Pros and Cons of Hitch Hiking, Amused to Death and Is This the Life We Really Want? His tours of The Wall (2010–2013) were among the highest-grossing of all time. Waters is also known for outspoken political activism.
