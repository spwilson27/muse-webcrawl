Federal Department for Media Harmful to Young Persons

The Federal Department for Media Harmful to Young Persons (German: Bundeszentrale für Kinder- und Jugendmedienschutz, BzKJ; until 2021 the Bundesprüfstelle für jugendgefährdende Medien, BPjM) is a German federal authority responsible for the protection of minors in the media. Based in Bonn, it is best known for maintaining the list — commonly called "the Index" — of media deemed harmful to young persons, which restricts how those works may be sold or advertised in Germany.

History

The review board was founded in 1954 as the Bundesprüfstelle für jugendgefährdende Schriften (Federal Review Board for Publications Harmful to Young Persons), initially covering printed publications. Its remit expanded over the decades to films, videos, computer games, and online content. In May 2021, under a revised Protection of Young Persons Act, it was restructured and renamed the Federal Department for Media Harmful to Young Persons.

The Index

Media placed on the Index may not be sold, rented, or displayed to minors, and generally may not be publicly advertised in Germany. "Indexing" is not an outright ban: adults retain legal access to the works through restricted channels such as mail order with age verification or adult-only sections of shops. Works may be removed from the list after 25 years or upon review.

Over the decades the Index has included notorious horror films (many of them British "video nasties"), violent video games, and music recordings. Indexed games were a major reason why international publishers released specially censored German versions of games, with green blood or removed content; Germany's recognition of the USK rating system and later legal changes have reduced this practice.

Criticism

Critics have long debated the institution's role, arguing that indexing amounts to state censorship and that listed works become attractive "forbidden fruit" to young people. Supporters respond that the system merely restricts marketing to minors while leaving adult access intact, and that it is a proportionate tool of youth protection.
