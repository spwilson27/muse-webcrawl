Colonization of Mars

The colonization of Mars is the hypothetical permanent settlement of humans on the planet Mars. Proposals have been advanced by space agencies, private companies and advocacy groups such as the Mars Society, motivated by the survival of humanity as a multiplanetary species, scientific research and the exploitation of resources.

Conditions

Mars is the most Earth-like planet in the Solar System, with a day of about 24.6 hours, seasons, polar ice caps and abundant water ice. However, its atmosphere is less than one percent as dense as Earth's and composed mainly of carbon dioxide; surface temperatures average around −60 °C; there is no protective magnetosphere, so radiation exposure is high; and the soil contains toxic perchlorates. Settlers would need pressurised habitats, radiation shielding (possibly from regolith or underground lava tubes), and closed-loop life support.

Approaches

Most plans depend on in-situ resource utilisation: producing oxygen, water and methane propellant from local water ice and atmospheric CO2, growing food in greenhouses, and eventually manufacturing building materials on site. Robert Zubrin's "Mars Direct" plan proposed sending habitats and return vehicles ahead of crews. SpaceX has developed the Starship vehicle explicitly with Mars settlement as a goal. Longer-term proposals discuss terraforming — warming the planet and thickening its atmosphere — though the feasibility of doing so with available CO2 is disputed. Ethical and legal questions include planetary protection, the status of settlers under the Outer Space Treaty, and the governance of any colony.
