Agrigento (Italian: [aɡriˈdʒɛnto]; Sicilian: Girgenti) is a city on the southern coast of Sicily, Italy, and capital of the province of Agrigento. It was founded around 582–580 BC by Greek colonists from Gela as Akragas, and grew rapidly into one of the richest and most famous cities of Magna Graecia — the poet Pindar called it "the most beautiful city of mortals". At its height it may have had some 200,000 inhabitants.

History

Under the tyrant Theron (488–472 BC), who won the Battle of Himera alongside Syracuse in 480 BC, Akragas raised its great Doric temples. The city was sacked by Carthage in 406 BC, recovered under Timoleon, and passed to Rome in the First Punic War (262 BC), becoming Agrigentum. It later passed through Byzantine, Arab (as Kerkent/Girgenti) and Norman hands; the medieval town grew on the hill of Girgenti, while the ancient city site below remained largely abandoned.

Valley of the Temples

South of the modern city lies the Valle dei Templi (Valley of the Temples), one of the most outstanding examples of ancient Greek art and architecture, designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1997. Its best-preserved monuments include the Temple of Concordia (c. 440–430 BC), among the finest surviving Doric temples anywhere; the Temple of Juno (Hera Lacinia); the Temple of Olympian Zeus, one of the largest Doric temples ever attempted; and the Temples of Heracles, Asclepius and the Dioscuri.

Notable people

Agrigento was the birthplace of the pre-Socratic philosopher Empedocles and, in the modern era, the dramatist and Nobel laureate Luigi Pirandello, born in the nearby district of Caos (1867).
