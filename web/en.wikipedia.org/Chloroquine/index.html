Chloroquine

Chloroquine
Class: Antimalarial (4-aminoquinoline)
Uses: Malaria treatment and prophylaxis; autoimmune disease
WHO: On the List of Essential Medicines

Chloroquine is a medication used to prevent and treat malaria in areas where the parasite remains sensitive to it, and also for amoebic liver abscess and autoimmune conditions such as rheumatoid arthritis and lupus. It was synthesised in 1934 and came into wide use after the Second World War.

Malaria

Chloroquine was once the mainstay against Plasmodium falciparum, but widespread resistance has limited its use for that species; it remains effective for P. vivax, P. ovale and P. malariae in many regions. Hydroxychloroquine is a related, less toxic derivative.

Safety

Side effects include itching, gastrointestinal upset and, with long-term high doses, retinal damage requiring eye screening. Overdose is dangerous, particularly to the heart. During the COVID-19 pandemic, large trials found no benefit from chloroquine or hydroxychloroquine.
