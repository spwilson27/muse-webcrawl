State of Origin series

The State of Origin is Australian sport's greatest rivalry.

The State of Origin series is an annual Australian rugby league series contested between two state representative sides, the New South Wales Blues and the Queensland Maroons. Regarded as Australian sport's greatest rivalry, it is one of the country's premier sporting events, attracting huge television audiences and sell-out crowds.

Format and history

The series is usually played as a best-of-three over three matches in the middle of the NRL season. Before 1980, interstate matches were played under residency rules, which favoured New South Wales because most elite Queensland players played in the Sydney competition. From 1980, players were selected according to their state of origin — where they were born or first played senior football. The first Origin match at Brisbane's Lang Park in 1980, featuring Arthur Beetson leading Queensland, is considered the birth of the modern series.

Rivalry and records

The series is famous for its intensity — summed up in the motto "mate against mate, state against state" — and for iconic moments such as Wally Lewis's dominance for Queensland and the Blues' drought-breaking wins. Queensland enjoyed a record dynasty from 2006 to 2013, winning eight consecutive series. A Women's State of Origin is also contested between the same states.
