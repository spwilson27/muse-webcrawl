The lux (symbol: lx) is the unit of illuminance in the International System of Units (SI). It measures the luminous flux per unit area, equal to one lumen per square metre.

Illuminance quantifies how much luminous flux is spread over a given area — in effect, how brightly a surface is lit as perceived by the human eye. One lux is one lumen per square metre: lux = lm/m². In photometry, illuminance is the total luminous flux incident on a surface, per unit area, weighted according to the luminosity function, a standard model of the brightness sensitivity of the human eye.

Typical values

Direct sunlight on a clear day reaches roughly 100,000 lux; an overcast day is about 1,000 lux; a typical office is lit to around 300–500 lux; and full moonlight on a clear night is only about 0.1 lux. Recommended illuminance levels are specified in building codes and lighting standards for different tasks.

Relation to other units

The lux is related to the lumen and the candela: a uniform point source of one candela emits one lumen per steradian, and a surface one metre away receiving that flux perpendicular is illuminated at one lux. The corresponding CGS unit, the phot, equals 10,000 lux. Video camera sensitivity is often specified in lux.
