Yama

Yama, lord of death and justice, riding his buffalo

Yama (Sanskrit: यम, romanised: Yama) is the Hindu deity of death and justice, responsible for the dispensation of law and punishment of sinners in his abode, Naraka. He is often identified with Dharmadeva, the embodiment of Dharma. He is the twin brother of Yamuna, the goddess of the eponymous river, and the son of Surya, the sun god, and his wife Saranyu (or Sanjna).

In the Vedas and epics

Yama is among the earliest deities in the Vedic pantheon: in the Rigveda he is the first mortal to die, who thereby found the way to the celestial abodes, and so became the ruler of the departed. In later Hindu tradition he is a Lokapala, guardian of the south, depicted green or dark-skinned, wearing red garments, carrying a noose (pasha) and mace, and riding a water buffalo. His scribe Chitragupta records the deeds of every human being, by which Yama judges the dead. The Katha Upanishad features the dialogue between Yama and the boy Nachiketa on the nature of death and the self.

In other traditions

Figures derived from Yama spread across Asia with Buddhism and Hinduism: he appears as Yama in Buddhist cosmology as a dharmapala and judge of the dead, as Yanluo Wang (King Yama) in Chinese religion, as Enma in Japan, and as Yom-raja in Southeast Asian traditions.

Festivals and worship

Yama is honoured during festivals such as Yamadeepdaan on Diwali and Bhai Dooj (Yama Dwitiya), which celebrates the bond between Yama and his sister Yamuna.
