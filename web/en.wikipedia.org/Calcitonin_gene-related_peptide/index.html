Calcitonin Gene-Related Peptide

Calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) is a member of the calcitonin family of peptides, produced in both peripheral and central neurons. It is a potent peptide vasodilator and can function in the transmission of nociception (pain).

Structure and forms

Human CGRP exists in two forms, α-CGRP and β-CGRP, each 37 amino acids long. α-CGRP is generated by alternative splicing of the calcitonin gene (CALCA) in nervous tissue, while calcitonin itself is produced from the same gene in the thyroid.

Function

CGRP is released from trigeminal nerve endings and dilates cerebral and meningeal blood vessels. It plays a central role in migraine pathophysiology and cardiovascular regulation, and modulates inflammation and wound healing.

Clinical significance

Drugs targeting the CGRP pathway transformed migraine treatment from 2018: monoclonal antibodies (erenumab, fremanezumab, galcanezumab, eptinezumab) and small-molecule gepants (rimegepant, ubrogepant, atogepant) block CGRP or its receptor for prevention and acute treatment of migraine.
