L.A. Confidential is a 1997 American neo-noir crime film directed and produced by Curtis Hanson, based on James Ellroy's 1990 novel of the same name, the third book in his L.A. Quartet series. Set in 1950s Los Angeles, it follows three very different LAPD officers — the politically ambitious Ed Exley (Guy Pearce), the brutal Bud White (Russell Crowe) and the celebrity-obsessed Jack Vincennes (Kevin Spacey) — whose paths converge as they investigate a mass shooting at the Nite Owl coffee shop.

Plot

After the Christmas Eve "Bloody Christmas" scandal and the jailing of mob boss Mickey Cohen, a massacre at the Nite Owl diner draws the three detectives into a web of police corruption, a high-class call-girl ring whose women are surgically altered to resemble film stars — including Lynn Bracken (Kim Basinger), styled after Veronica Lake — and a power struggle over Cohen's heroin empire.

Reception and awards

The film premiered at Cannes and was widely acclaimed as one of the finest films of the 1990s. Nominated for nine Academy Awards, it won two: Best Supporting Actress for Basinger and Best Adapted Screenplay. In 2015 it was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry. It is also credited with launching the Hollywood careers of Crowe and Pearce.
