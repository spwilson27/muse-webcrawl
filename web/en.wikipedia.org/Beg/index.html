Beg


Calligraphic ornament of the Ottoman period.

Beg (also bey, Turkish: bey; Persian: beg or beig) is a Turkic title for a chieftain, commander or local ruler, traditionally used across Central Asia, the Middle East and the Balkans. The feminine equivalent for a noblewoman is begum. The title appears in many personal names and place names throughout the former Ottoman Empire and Persianate world.

Etymology and history

The word derives from the Old Turkic beg, meaning a tribal leader or lord. In the Ottoman Empire, bey ranked below a pasha and was applied to provincial governors, military commanders and the rulers of small principalities known as beyliks. In Anatolia, beys founded dynasties such as the Karamanids and Germiyanids before Ottoman consolidation.

Usage

In modern Turkey the word bey survives as a polite form of address equivalent to "Mr." or "sir", placed after a man's first name. In South Asia, Baig or Beg is a common surname among families claiming Mughal or Central Asian descent, and begum is an honorific for Muslim women of high rank.
