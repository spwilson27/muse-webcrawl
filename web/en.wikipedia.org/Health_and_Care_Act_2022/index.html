The Health and Care Act 2022 restructured the NHS in England around integrated care systems.

The Health and Care Act 2022 (c. 31) is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that reformed the organisation of the National Health Service in England. Introduced under Health Secretary Sajid Javid in the government of Boris Johnson, it received royal assent on 28 April 2022, with its main structural provisions taking effect on 1 July 2022.

Provisions

The Act placed integrated care systems (ICSs) on a statutory footing: 42 integrated care boards (ICBs) replaced clinical commissioning groups as the bodies responsible for planning and funding most NHS services in their areas, working with integrated care partnerships involving local government. It abolished NHS Improvement as a separate structure, merging Monitor and the NHS Trust Development Authority into NHS England, and gave the Secretary of State new powers of direction over NHS England and intervention in service reconfigurations.

The Act also removed some compulsory competitive tendering requirements introduced by the Health and Social Care Act 2012, enabled a new provider selection regime, made provision for the regulation of social care through the Care Quality Commission's assessments of local authorities, established the Health Services Safety Investigations Body (HSSIB) as a statutory body, and contained measures on public health (including restrictions on advertising of less healthy food), professional regulation, and information sharing. Provisions on a cap on adult social care costs were later postponed and abandoned.

Debate

Supporters argued the reforms encouraged collaboration and integration after the COVID-19 pandemic; critics, including some health unions and opposition parties, warned about increased ministerial control, potential private-sector involvement in ICB boards, and inadequate attention to workforce shortages and social care funding.
