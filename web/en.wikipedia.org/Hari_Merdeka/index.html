Hari Merdeka

Hari Merdeka (Independence Day), also known as Hari Kebangsaan (National Day), is the official independence day of Malaysia. It commemorates the Malayan Declaration of Independence of 31 August 1957, and is defined in Article 160 of the Constitution of Malaysia. The day is marked by official and unofficial celebrations and observances across the country.

History

The effort for independence was spearheaded by Tunku Abdul Rahman, the chief minister of the Federation of Malaya. Following negotiations in London, it was agreed that the Federation of Malaya would gain independence from the British Empire on 31 August 1957. On the night of 30 August 1957, crowds gathered at Merdeka Square (Dataran Merdeka) in Kuala Lumpur to witness the lowering of the Union Flag. On the morning of 31 August, at Stadium Merdeka, Tunku Abdul Rahman read the Proclamation of Independence, followed by seven chants of "Merdeka!" — the Malay word for independence — with the crowd joining in.

Celebrations

Hari Merdeka

Observed by: Malaysia

Date: 31 August

Significance: Marks the independence of the Federation of Malaya from the United Kingdom in 1957

The main national-level celebration is traditionally held at Merdeka Square in Kuala Lumpur, featuring a parade attended by the Yang di-Pertuan Agong, members of the Cabinet, and other dignitaries, with contingents from the armed forces, police, government agencies, schools and private companies. Since the 1990s the celebration has occasionally been hosted by other cities. Each year a theme is chosen for the celebration, with slogans such as "Malaysia Madani: Tekad Perpaduan, Penuhi Harapan" used in recent years. Schools, offices and homes across the country display the national flag, the Jalur Gemilang, during the Merdeka month.

Distinction from Malaysia Day

Hari Merdeka commemorates the independence of Malaya in 1957. Malaysia Day, celebrated on 16 September, marks the formation of the Federation of Malaysia in 1963, when Malaya, North Borneo (Sabah), Sarawak and Singapore (which later separated in 1965) joined together. Malaysia Day became a federal public holiday in 2010.
