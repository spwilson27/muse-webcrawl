Compagnie d'Aérostiers
Country: French Republic
Active: 1794–1799
Type: balloon observation company
First use: Battle of Fleurus, 1794

The Compagnie d'Aérostiers was the first air force unit in history: a French Revolutionary company created in 1794 to operate hydrogen observation balloons for reconnaissance. Its balloon l'Entreprenant observed Austrian movements at the Battle of Fleurus.

Creation

France's Committee of Public Safety ordered the company in April 1794, under Captain Jean-Marie-Joseph Coutelle, with the physicist Charles Coutelle and chemist Nicolas-Jacques Conté devising methods to produce hydrogen in the field and varnish balloon envelopes.

Operations

From a tethered balloon at Fleurus (26 June 1794), observers signalled enemy dispositions for some nine hours, contributing to the French victory and unnerving Austrian troops. A second company was raised, and balloons later served at sieges including Mainz and during the Egyptian campaign, where its equipment was lost at sea.

Disbandment

Napoleon, unimpressed, disbanded the companies in 1799. Military ballooning nevertheless spread to other armies in the following century, culminating in the balloon and airship services of the First World War.
