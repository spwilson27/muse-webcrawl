Women's health

Women's health refers to the health of women, which differs in many unique ways from the health of men. It forms part of the field of population health and includes the study and care of conditions affecting the female reproductive system, maternal health, menopause, and diseases that occur in women more often or differently than in men, such as breast cancer and autoimmune disorders.

Reproductive and maternal health

Major topics include contraception, pregnancy and childbirth, and efforts to reduce maternal mortality, which remains a key global health indicator. Access to antenatal care, skilled birth attendants, and family planning services are central targets of international development programmes.

Cancer screening

Screening programmes such as mammography for breast cancer and Pap tests for cervical cancer aim to detect disease early. Cervical cancer is largely preventable through HPV vaccination and screening.

Research and advocacy

The women's health movement, growing from the 1960s and 1970s, pressed for women's inclusion in clinical trials and for research into conditions historically understudied. Initiatives such as the U.S. Women's Health Initiative (launched 1991) examined hormone therapy, diet, and cardiovascular disease in postmenopausal women.
