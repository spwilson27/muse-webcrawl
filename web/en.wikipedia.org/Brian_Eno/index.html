Brian Eno

Brian Peter George Eno (born 15 May 1948) is an English musician, composer, record producer, and visual artist best known for pioneering ambient music and for his work in rock, electronic, and generative music. Describing himself as a "non-musician," Eno brought conceptual strategies from art school into the recording studio, treating the studio itself as a compositional tool.

Roxy Music and solo work

Eno rose to prominence in the early 1970s as the synthesizer player and sonic experimenter in Roxy Music, leaving after two albums amid friction with singer Bryan Ferry. His solo records of the 1970s — Here Come the Warm Jets (1974), Taking Tiger Mountain (By Strategy) (1974), Another Green World (1975), and Before and After Science (1977) — blended pop songwriting with avant-garde techniques, including the chance-based instruction cards he devised with Peter Schmidt, known as Oblique Strategies.

Ambient music

After being bedridden following a car accident, Eno conceived of music that could be "as ignorable as it is interesting." Discreet Music (1975) and Ambient 1: Music for Airports (1978) founded ambient as a genre. His collaborations with David Bowie on the "Berlin Trilogy" (Low, "Heroes", and Lodger) and with Robert Fripp, Harold Budd, and Cluster were equally influential.

Producer and later work

As a producer, Eno shaped landmark albums by Talking Heads (including Remain in Light), Devo, U2 (including The Joshua Tree and Achtung Baby, with Daniel Lanois), and Coldplay. He composed the Windows 95 startup sound, created generative music applications, and has pursued visual art installations and political activism throughout his career.
