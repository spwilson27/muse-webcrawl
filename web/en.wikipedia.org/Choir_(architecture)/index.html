Choir (architecture)

A choir, in ecclesiastical architecture, is the part of a church or cathedral situated between the nave and the sanctuary, reserved historically for the clergy and the choir singers who performed the sung liturgy. It is typically located near the east end of the building, around or beyond the crossing.

Layout

The choir is usually furnished with rows of wooden choir stalls facing each other across a central aisle, often under an organ loft. It is frequently separated from the nave by a screen — a rood screen, pulpitum, or iconostasis — and from the ambulatory and chapels beyond the high altar. In many larger churches the choir sits within the chancel, an area that also includes the sanctuary.

History

In early Christian basilicas the singers stood near the altar; over the medieval period the choir became a distinct architectural volume, elaborated in Gothic cathedrals with enclosed stalls, misericords, and canopies. Monastic churches had especially large choirs to accommodate monks singing the daily offices.
