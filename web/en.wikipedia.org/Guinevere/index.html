Guinevere

Guinevere (Welsh: Gwenhwyfar) is the queen consort of King Arthur in Arthurian legend. Famous for her love affair with Arthur's chief knight, Sir Lancelot, she is one of the central figures of the Matter of Britain.

Early appearances

Guinevere first appears in Geoffrey of Monmouth's Historia Regum Britanniae (c. 1136), where she is of noble Roman birth and is entrusted to Arthur's nephew Mordred while the king campaigns abroad; she breaks her marriage vows and joins Mordred. Welsh tradition knew Gwenhwyfar as Arthur's wife, and the Welsh Triads mention three queens of that name.

The Lancelot affair

Chrétien de Troyes introduced her abduction by Meleagant and her rescue by Lancelot in Lancelot, the Knight of the Cart. In the later Vulgate Cycle and in Thomas Malory's Le Morte d'Arthur, her adultery with Lancelot becomes a principal cause of the fall of Camelot: exposed by Mordred and Agravaine, she is condemned to burn at the stake, is rescued by Lancelot, and is eventually restored to Arthur, but the fellowship of the Round Table is destroyed. After Arthur's death she traditionally retires to a convent at Amesbury and dies there.
