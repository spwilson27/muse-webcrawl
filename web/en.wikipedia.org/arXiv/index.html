arXiv (pronounced as "archive") is an open-access archive for over two million scholarly articles in the fields of physics, mathematics, computer science, quantitative biology, quantitative finance, statistics, electrical engineering and systems science, and economics. Materials on this site are not peer-reviewed by arXiv. It was founded in 1991 by physicist Paul Ginsparg at Los Alamos National Laboratory (as xxx.lanl.gov) and moved with him to Cornell University in 2001.

History and operation

arXiv began as an email- and FTP-based distribution system for high-energy physics preprints and quickly became central to scholarly communication in physics, where results often appear on arXiv months before journal publication. Submissions are screened by volunteer moderators and an endorsement system for new authors, but are not formally peer reviewed. In 2007 a new identifier scheme (e.g. arXiv:0704.0001) was introduced. In 2022 Ginsparg received recognition for the archive's role in transforming scientific publishing; notable papers posted to arXiv include Grigori Perelman's proof of the Poincaré conjecture (2002–2003), which was never published in a traditional journal.

Influence

arXiv helped pioneer the preprint culture later adopted by other disciplines, inspiring subject repositories such as bioRxiv (biology) and medRxiv (medicine). It is funded by Cornell, member institutions and the Simons Foundation.
