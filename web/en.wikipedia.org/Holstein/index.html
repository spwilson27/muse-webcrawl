Holstein

Holstein is the region between the rivers Elbe and Eider in northern Germany. It is the southern half of the present-day state of Schleswig-Holstein, bordering Denmark's historic sphere to the north and Hamburg to the south.

History

Holstein was a county in the early medieval period, raised within the Holy Roman Empire, and later a duchy held in personal union by the kings of Denmark for centuries, even while remaining part of the Empire and later the German Confederation. The Schleswig-Holstein question over its status fueled wars in 1848-1851 and 1864.

After 1864

Following the Second Schleswig War and the Austro-Prussian War, Holstein became part of Prussia and then the German Empire. In 1946 it was joined with Schleswig to form the West German state of Schleswig-Holstein.

Other uses

The name is also known from Holstein cattle, a dairy breed originating in the wider region.
