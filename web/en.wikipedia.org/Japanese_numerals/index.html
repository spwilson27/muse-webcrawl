Japanese numerals

The Japanese numerals are the number names used in the Japanese language. The system is based on the Chinese numerals, grouped by myriads (units of 10,000) rather than by thousands as in Western systems.

Powers of ten

Large numbers are formed in multiples of 10,000: 万 (man, 104), 億 (oku, 108), 兆 (chō, 1012), 京 (kei, 1016) and so on. Thus 10,000,000 is expressed as 千万 (senman, "one thousand myriads") rather than by a single word for ten million.

Usage

In modern Japanese, Arabic numerals are common in horizontal writing, while kanji numerals persist in vertical writing, formal documents and traditional contexts. A formal financial variant of the numerals, daiji, is used in legal and banking documents to prevent alteration. Numbers are combined with counter words that depend on the kind of object being counted.
