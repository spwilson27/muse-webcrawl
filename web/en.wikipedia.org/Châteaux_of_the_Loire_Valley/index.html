Châteaux of the Loire Valley

The Châteaux of the Loire Valley are part of the architectural heritage of the historic towns of Amboise, Angers, Blois, Chinon, Orléans, Saumur and Tours, along the Loire River in France. More than 300 châteaux — from fortified keeps of the 10th century to splendid residences of the Renaissance and later — are located in the region, which UNESCO inscribed on its World Heritage List in 2000 as an exceptional cultural landscape.

History

The Loire became the heartland of the French monarchy in the 15th and 16th centuries. Charles VII and Louis XI held court there, and Francis I transformed the region with Renaissance building, inviting Italian artists including Leonardo da Vinci, who spent his last years at Clos Lucé in Amboise. Fortresses were converted into pleasure palaces surrounded by gardens, while nobles and financiers built residences throughout the valley. As political power shifted back to Paris and Versailles in the 17th century, the Loire châteaux retained their prestige as country estates.

Notable châteaux

Among the most celebrated are Chambord, the largest, with its double-helix staircase; Chenonceau, spanning the river Cher and shaped by Catherine de' Medici and Diane de Poitiers; Amboise; Blois; Azay-le-Rideau, reflected in the Indre; Villandry, famous for its reconstructed Renaissance gardens; and the medieval fortresses of Chinon and Angers, the latter housing the Apocalypse Tapestry.
