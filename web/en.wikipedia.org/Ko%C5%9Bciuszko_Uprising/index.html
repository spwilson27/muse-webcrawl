The Kościuszko Uprising was a national revolt of 1794 led by Tadeusz Kościuszko against the Russian Empire and Prussia, launched after the Second Partition of Poland and the imposition of a Russian protectorate over the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth. It was the Commonwealth's last armed attempt to preserve its independence.

Course of the uprising

Kościuszko proclaimed the insurrection in Kraków's market square on 24 March 1794 and won an early victory at Racławice, where peasant scythemen (kosynierzy) played a celebrated role. His Połaniec Manifesto promised reforms and personal liberty to peasants who joined. Uprisings also broke out in Warsaw and Wilno (Vilnius), expelling Russian garrisons.

Defeat

Despite determined resistance, the insurgents were outmatched. Kościuszko was wounded and captured at the Battle of Maciejowice in October 1794, and in November Russian forces under Alexander Suvorov stormed the Warsaw suburb of Praga with great loss of life. The uprising collapsed, and in 1795 Russia, Prussia, and Austria carried out the Third Partition, erasing Poland from the map for 123 years.

Legacy

The uprising became a foundational symbol of Polish resistance, and Kościuszko, a veteran of the American Revolutionary War, is honored as a national hero in Poland, Lithuania, Belarus, and the United States.
