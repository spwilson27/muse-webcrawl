Durbin–Watson statistic

FieldStatistics, econometrics
Introduced byJames Durbin and Geoffrey Watson (1950–51)
Tests forFirst-order autocorrelation of regression residuals

In statistics, the Durbin–Watson statistic is a test statistic used to detect the presence of autocorrelation at lag 1 in the residuals (prediction errors) from a regression analysis. It is named after James Durbin and Geoffrey Watson, who introduced it in papers of 1950 and 1951.

Definition

The statistic is computed as the sum of squared differences between successive residuals divided by the sum of squared residuals. Its value always lies between 0 and 4: a value near 2 indicates no autocorrelation, values approaching 0 indicate positive autocorrelation, and values toward 4 indicate negative autocorrelation.

Testing

The Durbin–Watson test has upper and lower critical bounds that depend on the number of observations and regressors; values falling between the bounds leave the test inconclusive. The test is invalid when a lagged dependent variable is among the regressors, in which case Durbin's h-test or the Breusch–Godfrey test is preferred.

Use

The statistic is routinely reported by regression software in econometrics, since autocorrelated residuals violate the assumptions of ordinary least squares and lead to inefficient estimates and misleading standard errors.
