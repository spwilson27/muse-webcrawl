Moment-generating function

In probability theory and statistics, the moment-generating function (MGF) of a real-valued random variable X is an alternative specification of its probability distribution, defined as the expected value of e raised to the power tX, for real t in a neighbourhood of zero: M(t) = E[e^{tX}].

Properties

When the MGF exists in an open interval around zero, it uniquely determines the distribution, and its derivatives at zero give the moments of X: the n-th derivative evaluated at t = 0 equals E[X^n], the n-th raw moment. From these come the mean, variance, skewness, and kurtosis. The MGF of a sum of independent random variables is the product of their individual MGFs, which makes it a powerful tool for deriving distributions of sums — for instance, showing that sums of independent normal variables are normal.

Related transforms

The MGF is closely related to the characteristic function (which always exists, using complex exponentials), the probability-generating function for non-negative integer variables, and the Laplace transform of the distribution. Some heavy-tailed distributions, such as the log-normal, have no MGF for t > 0 even though all their moments are finite.
