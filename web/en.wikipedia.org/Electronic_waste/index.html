Electronic waste

Electronic waste (or e-waste) describes discarded electrical or electronic devices. Used electronics destined for refurbishment, reuse, resale, salvage recycling, or disposal are also considered e-waste. Informal processing of e-waste in developing countries can lead to adverse human health effects and environmental pollution.

The world generated about 62 million tonnes of e-waste in 2022, of which only around a fifth was documented as formally recycled.

Composition and hazards

E-waste contains valuable materials such as gold, silver, copper, and rare-earth elements, as well as hazardous substances including lead, mercury, cadmium, and brominated flame retardants. Open burning and acid baths used in informal recycling release toxic pollutants.

Global flows

Large quantities of used electronics are exported from developed countries to places such as Agbogbloshie in Ghana and Guiyu in China, despite restrictions under the Basel Convention. The European Union's WEEE Directive requires producers to finance collection and recycling.

Management

Strategies include extended producer responsibility, design for disassembly, take-back schemes, and urban mining to recover metals. Extending product lifetimes through repair and reuse generally offers greater environmental benefit than recycling alone.
