Moundsville is a city in and the county seat of Marshall County, West Virginia, United States, situated along the Ohio River in the state's Northern Panhandle. It was formed in 1866 by the merger of the towns of Elizabethtown and Mound City.

History and the Grave Creek Mound

The city takes its name from the Grave Creek Mound, one of the largest conical burial mounds built by the Adena culture, constructed roughly between 250 and 150 BCE and standing about 19 m (62 ft) high. The mound is preserved as Grave Creek Mound Archaeological Complex, a National Historic Landmark with an adjacent museum. A small inscribed sandstone tablet reportedly found in the mound in 1838, the "Grave Creek Stone", was the subject of long-running controversy and is now generally regarded by scholars as a hoax.

Landmarks

Moundsville is also known for the former West Virginia Penitentiary, a Gothic-style prison that operated from 1876 to 1995 and is now a tourist attraction and training facility, famous for tours, its history of riots and executions, and paranormal tourism. Nearby attractions include the Marx Toy Museum (closed 2016) honouring the Marx toy company that once manufactured in nearby Glen Dale, and the Fostoria Glass and Moundsville economic legacy of glass, chemical, and toy manufacturing along the river.
