Pixel

In digital imaging, a pixel (abbreviation of picture element) is the smallest addressable element in a raster image, or the smallest point in an all-points-addressable display device. In most digital display devices, pixels are the smallest unit that can be manipulated through software. Each pixel is a sample of an original image; more samples typically provide more accurate representations.

Enlarged detail of a raster image showing individual pixels and their subpixels.

Colour representation

A colour pixel is commonly represented by three components — red, green, and blue — corresponding to subpixels in display panels and to the colour model used in image files. The number of bits per pixel (colour depth) determines how many colours can be represented: 24-bit "true colour" allows about 16.7 million colours. Grayscale pixels use a single intensity value, and some systems include an alpha channel for transparency.

Resolution and density

Display resolution is measured in pixels across and down, such as 1920 × 1080. Pixel density, measured in pixels per inch (PPI), affects sharpness; high-density "retina" displays aim to make individual pixels indistinguishable at normal viewing distances. In digital cameras, sensor resolution is marketed in megapixels (millions of pixels).

Related concepts

A pixel is distinct from a voxel (a volumetric pixel in three dimensions) and a texel (a texture element used in computer graphics). In image processing, neighbouring pixels are combined by filters for operations such as blurring, sharpening, and edge detection — processes also used in anti-aliasing to smooth the appearance of diagonal lines.
