The Nanny is an American television sitcom that aired on CBS from 1993 to 1999. Created by and starring Fran Drescher, together with her then-husband Peter Marc Jacobson, the series follows Fran Fine, a flamboyant Jewish-American woman from Flushing, Queens, who — after losing her job at a bridal shop — becomes the nanny of the three children of Maxwell Sheffield, a wealthy widowed Broadway producer.

Premise and characters

The show's comedy arises from the clash between Fran's working-class Queens background — her nasal voice, flashy outfits, and scheming mother Sylvia and grandmother Yetta — and the stiff upper-class world of the Sheffield household, served by the acerbic butler Niles. A long-running romantic tension between Fran and "Mr. Sheffield," and the sparring between Niles and Maxwell's business partner C.C. Babcock, drove much of the series. In the final seasons Fran and Maxwell marry, and Niles and C.C. also wed.

Production and reception

Loosely inspired by Drescher's own upbringing in Queens, the show was a ratings success for CBS, widely syndicated internationally, and credited with renewing interest in Jewish-American comic heroines on television. Drescher received two Emmy Award nominations and two Golden Globe nominations. The format was adapted in numerous countries, most famously the Russian version My Fair Nanny.

Legacy

The series' fashion, catchphrases, and theme song ("The Nanny Named Fran," written and performed by Ann Hampton Callaway) remain fixtures of 1990s pop culture. A 2020 virtual table read of the pilot by the original cast drew wide attention, and a stage musical adaptation has been developed.
