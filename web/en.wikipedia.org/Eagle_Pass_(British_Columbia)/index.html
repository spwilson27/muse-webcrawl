Eagle Pass (British Columbia)

Eagle Pass
Elevation: 550 m (1,804 ft)
Location: British Columbia, Canada
Range: Monashee Mountains
Traversed by: Trans-Canada Highway, Canadian Pacific Railway

Eagle Pass is a mountain pass through the Monashee Mountains in British Columbia, Canada, lying between Revelstoke and Sicamous. It carries the main line of the Canadian Pacific Railway and the Trans-Canada Highway between the Columbia River valley and Shuswap Lake.

Eagle Pass carries the Trans-Canada Highway and CPR main line through the Monashees.

History

The pass was discovered for European use in 1865 by Walter Moberly, a surveyor and engineer searching for a route for a wagon road, who is said to have shot an eagle or followed eagles through the valley of the Eagle River — giving the pass its name. Two decades later it became the route of the Canadian Pacific Railway.

At Craigellachie, just west of the summit of Eagle Pass, the last spike of the Canadian Pacific Railway was driven on 7 November 1885 by Donald A. Smith (later Lord Strathcona), completing Canada's first transcontinental railway. A monument at Craigellachie commemorates the event.
