Bas Cornelis van Fraassen (born 5 April 1941) is a Dutch-American philosopher of science known for developing constructive empiricism, an anti-realist account of scientific theories. He has been a leading figure in the philosophy of science since the publication of his book The Scientific Image (1980).

Constructive empiricism

In The Scientific Image, van Fraassen argued that the aim of science is not truth about unobservable entities but empirical adequacy — that a theory save all the observable phenomena. To accept a theory, on his view, is only to believe that it is empirically adequate, not that its claims about electrons or other unobservables are literally true. The book helped revive empiricist philosophy of science and set the terms of the scientific realism debate for decades.

Other work

Van Fraassen has also written on the semantics of physical theories, quantum mechanics, probability and decision theory, free logic, and the philosophy of religion. His later books include Laws and Symmetry (1989), Quantum Mechanics: An Empiricist View (1991), and The Empirical Stance (2002), in which he developed a broader account of empiricism as a stance rather than a dogma.

Career

Born in the Netherlands and raised in Canada, van Fraassen earned his PhD from the University of Pittsburgh. He taught at Yale, the University of Toronto, the University of Southern California, and Princeton University, where he is emeritus professor.
