Sandia Peak Tramway

LocationAlbuquerque, New Mexico, US
Opened7 May 1966
Length4.3 km (2.7 mi)
Longest span2,328 m (7,720 ft)
Top elevation3,163 m (10,378 ft)

The Sandia Peak Tramway is an aerial tramway in Albuquerque, New Mexico, carrying passengers from the northeastern edge of the city up the western face of the Sandia Mountains to the crest at Sandia Peak. With a total length of about 4.3 kilometres, it was for decades promoted as the longest aerial tramway in the world.

Construction

The tram was the project of Albuquerque businessman Robert Nordhaus and ski-area founder Ben Abruzzo, built to give skiers access from the city side to the Sandia Peak Ski Area on the mountain's eastern slope. Constructed by the Swiss firm Bell Engineering, it opened on 7 May 1966. Building it required more than 5,000 helicopter trips to pour foundations and raise its two support towers on the rugged, roadless mountainside.

Features

The system's second span, between the second tower and the summit terminal, is about 2,328 metres long — one of the longest clear spans of any aerial tramway. The cars climb some 1,200 metres in about 15 minutes, rising from the desert foothills through life zones that range from cactus to spruce-fir forest. The summit terminal includes observation decks and a restaurant, TEN 3, named for its elevation of 10,300 feet, and offers views extending, on clear days, over a huge area of New Mexico.

New tram cars were installed in 1986 and again in 2016 for the tram's 50th anniversary. The tram carries skiers, hikers and sightseers year-round and is one of New Mexico's best-known tourist attractions.
