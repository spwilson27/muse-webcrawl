Roadster (car)

A traditional two-seat roadster

A roadster (also spider, spyder) is an open two-seat car with emphasis on sporting appearance or character. Initially an American term for a basic, open-top, two-seater car, the term is now used internationally.

History

In the 19th century the word "roadster" described a horse suitable for travel. By the 1910s American makers applied it to stripped-down open cars. Early roadsters lacked roll-up windows and fixed roofs, offering only a folding top and side curtains as weather protection.

After the Second World War, British sports cars such as the MG TC and later the Triumph TR series popularised the type. The 1989 Mazda MX-5 revived the classic lightweight roadster formula and became the best-selling two-seat convertible sports car in history.

Modern usage

Manufacturers have applied the name to cars with fixed roofs or extra seats for marketing reasons, but the traditional definition remains an open, two-seat, rear-wheel-drive sports car. Notable examples include the BMW Z4, the Porsche Boxster and the Tesla Roadster, an all-electric sports car produced from 2008 to 2012.
