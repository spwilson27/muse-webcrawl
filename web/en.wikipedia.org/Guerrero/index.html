Guerrero
Capital: Chilpancingo
Largest city: Acapulco
Population: about 3.5 million
Named for: Vicente Guerrero

Guerrero is a state in southwestern Mexico, bordering the Pacific Ocean. Its capital is Chilpancingo and its largest city the port and resort of Acapulco. The state is named after Vicente Guerrero, a hero of the Mexican War of Independence and the country's second president.

Geography

Guerrero is a mountainous state crossed by the Sierra Madre del Sur, with a narrow Pacific coastal plain. Its best-known destinations are Acapulco, once Mexico's premier beach resort, the silver-mining town of Taxco, and the resort development of Ixtapa-Zihuatanejo. The Balsas River forms much of its northern border.

History

The state was created in 1849 from territory of the states of Mexico, Puebla and Michoacán. During the War of Independence, José María Morelos and Vicente Guerrero campaigned in the region; the first Mexican congress met at Chilpancingo in 1813 and issued the Solemn Act of the Declaration of Independence of Northern America.

Society and challenges

Guerrero is one of Mexico's poorest states and home to significant Nahua, Mixtec, Tlapanec and Afro-Mexican populations. It has suffered serious violence linked to organised crime and drug production in its mountain regions; the 2014 disappearance of 43 students from the Ayotzinapa teacher-training college near Iguala drew international attention and protest.
