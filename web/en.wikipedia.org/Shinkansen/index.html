The Shinkansen, known in English as the bullet train, is a network of high-speed railway lines in Japan. Initially built to connect distant regions with Tokyo, the capital, the network has expanded across Honshu and into Kyushu and Hokkaido. The Shinkansen is celebrated for its speed, punctuality and safety record: in more than half a century of operation it has carried over 10 billion passengers without a single fatal crash or derailment.

History

The first line, the Tōkaidō Shinkansen between Tokyo and Shin-Ōsaka, opened on 1 October 1964, just before the Tokyo Olympics, with 0 Series trains running at 210 km/h — then the fastest in the world. The project was championed by Japanese National Railways president Shinji Sogō and chief engineer Hideo Shima. Subsequent lines reached Hakata (1975), the Tōhoku region, and eventually Kagoshima and Shin-Hakodate-Hokuto.

Technology

Shinkansen lines use standard gauge track, dedicated rights of way, no level crossings, and electric multiple unit trains with distributed traction. The digital ATC signalling system, seismic detection, and rigorous maintenance contribute to average delays measured in seconds per train per year. Successive train generations, from the 0 Series to the E5 and E7 Series, pushed service speeds to 320 km/h. A superconducting maglev line, the Chūō Shinkansen, is under construction between Tokyo and Nagoya.
