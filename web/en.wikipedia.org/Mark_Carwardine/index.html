Mark Carwardine

Mark Carwardine (born 1959) is a British zoologist, conservationist, wildlife photographer, author and broadcaster. He is widely known for his work on whales and dolphins and for co-writing Last Chance to See with Douglas Adams, chronicling expeditions to find endangered species.

Career

Carwardine worked for conservation organisations including the World Wide Fund for Nature and the United Nations Environment Programme before becoming a freelance wildlife writer and photographer. He has written more than fifty books, including the influential Whales, Dolphins and Porpoises field guide and handbooks on wildlife watching. He co-presents wildlife programmes and writes the long-running "Discover Wildlife" column in BBC Wildlife Magazine.

Last Chance to See

In 1989 Carwardine travelled with Douglas Adams for a BBC radio series and book, visiting species on the brink of extinction including the aye-aye, the Komodo dragon, the kakapo and the Yangtze river dolphin (baiji), which was later declared functionally extinct. A 2009 television sequel with Stephen Fry revisited the same species and included a celebrated encounter with Sirocco the kakapo.
