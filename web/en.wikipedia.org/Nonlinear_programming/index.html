In mathematics, nonlinear programming (NLP) is the process of solving an optimization problem in which some of the constraints or the objective function are nonlinear. The problem takes the form of maximizing or minimizing a real-valued function subject to equality and inequality constraints, where at least one of these functions is not affine. Nonlinear programming generalizes linear programming and encompasses most optimization problems arising in engineering design, economics, machine learning, and operations research.

A nonlinear objective function optimized over a curved feasible region

Formulation

A typical NLP problem is: minimize f(x) subject to gᵢ(x) ≤ 0 and hⱼ(x) = 0. If the objective is convex and the feasible region is convex, the problem is a convex optimization problem, in which every local minimum is global and efficient algorithms exist. Non-convex problems may have many local optima and are in general NP-hard to solve globally.

Optimality conditions

For problems with inequality constraints, the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) conditions provide necessary conditions for a local optimum under constraint qualifications, generalizing the method of Lagrange multipliers. They combine stationarity of the Lagrangian, primal and dual feasibility, and complementary slackness. For convex problems with a constraint qualification such as Slater's condition, the KKT conditions are also sufficient.

Methods

Algorithms include gradient descent and Newton's method for unconstrained problems; penalty and barrier (interior-point) methods that convert constraints into modified objectives; sequential quadratic programming, which solves a series of quadratic approximations; and augmented Lagrangian methods. For non-convex and global optimization, heuristic and metaheuristic methods such as simulated annealing, genetic algorithms, and branch-and-bound schemes are used. Widely used solvers include IPOPT, KNITRO, and SNOPT.

Applications

NLP underlies portfolio optimization with nonlinear risk measures, structural and aerodynamic design, chemical process engineering, optimal control of robots and trajectories, and the training of many machine-learning models, where minimizing a non-convex loss function is a large-scale nonlinear program.
