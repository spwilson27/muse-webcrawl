Strathspey

The strathspey is a type of dance tune in 4/4 time, and the dance performed to it, originating in the Strathspey valley of the River Spey in north-east Scotland. It is slower and more stately than a reel and is one of the principal tune types of Scottish fiddle music and Scottish country dancing.

Characteristics

Strathspeys are distinguished by dotted rhythms and the "Scotch snap", a short accented note followed by a longer one (a reversed dotting), which gives the music its crisp, jerky drive. In Scottish country dance programs, strathspeys alternate with jigs and reels; in solo Highland dancing, the strathspey is followed without pause by a reel in the strathspey-and-reel combination. Fiddler-composers such as William Marshall and James Scott Skinner, "the Strathspey King", produced classic tunes in the form.
