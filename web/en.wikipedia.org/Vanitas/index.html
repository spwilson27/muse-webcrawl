Vanitas

Vanitas (Latin for "vanity" or "emptiness") is a genre of still-life painting, especially associated with the Dutch Golden Age of the 17th century, in which symbolic objects remind the viewer of the transience of life, the futility of pleasure and the certainty of death.

Symbolism

Common motifs include skulls, extinguished candles, hourglasses, wilting flowers, rotting fruit, soap bubbles, watches and overturned glasses, contrasted with symbols of wealth and earthly achievement such as jewels, coins, books, musical instruments and scientific instruments. The theme derives from the biblical book of Ecclesiastes: "Vanity of vanities, all is vanity."

Development

The genre emerged in Leiden in the 1620s and flourished in the Netherlands, Flanders, France and Spain. Artists such as Pieter Claesz, Willem Claesz Heda, Harmen Steenwijck and David Bailly produced vanitas still lifes of great technical refinement. A related genre is the memento mori.

Legacy

Vanitas imagery persisted in later art, from 18th-century examples to contemporary works that use skulls and luxury goods to comment on mortality and consumerism.
