Rahab is a figure in the Hebrew Bible, described in the Book of Joshua as a woman (often identified as a prostitute or innkeeper) in Jericho who hid two Israelite spies sent by Joshua and helped them escape, in exchange for the safety of her family when the city was conquered.

Biblical account

In Joshua 2, Rahab hides the spies on her roof under stalks of flax and misdirects the king of Jericho's pursuers. She professes faith in the God of Israel and asks the spies to spare her household; they instruct her to hang a scarlet cord from her window and gather her family inside. In Joshua 6, after the fall of Jericho, Joshua orders that Rahab and her family be spared, and she is said to live among Israel "to this day".

Later tradition

In the New Testament, Rahab is named in the genealogy of Jesus in Matthew 1:5 and is praised as an example of faith (Hebrews 11:31) and of works (James 2:25). Rabbinic tradition praises her as a righteous convert, while some modern scholars debate the translation of the Hebrew term describing her occupation.
