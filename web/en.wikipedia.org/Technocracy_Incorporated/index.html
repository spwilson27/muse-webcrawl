Technocracy Incorporated

Technocracy Incorporated is an American organisation founded in 1933 to promote technocracy — the proposed replacement of monetary economics and elected politicians with an energy-based accounting system administered by scientists and engineers. It grew out of the Technical Alliance and the Committee on Technocracy at Columbia University associated with Howard Scott.

History

The technocracy movement attracted wide attention during the Great Depression, when faith in the price system collapsed. Howard Scott's 1932–33 lecture tours drew large audiences, but the movement faded quickly as the New Deal advanced and Scott's personal credentials came under scrutiny. Technocracy Incorporated was registered in New York in 1933 and continued to publish its proposals for a "Technate" of North America, governed by a functional sequence of experts and using "energy certificates" instead of money.

Later years

The organisation went into decline after the Second World War and Scott's death in 1970, though a small membership continued to maintain headquarters (later in Ferndale, Washington), publish material, and use the movement's distinctive Monad symbol and grey uniforms — customs dating to its 1930s "grey fleet" motorcades.
