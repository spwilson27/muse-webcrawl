Scallion


Image placeholder: Scallion

Scallions (also known as green onions and spring onions) are edible vegetables of various species in the genus Allium. Scallions generally have a milder taste than most onions, and are eaten both raw and cooked. Their hollow tubular green leaves and small, incompletely developed white bulbs distinguish them from mature bulb onions.

Types and uses

Several species and forms are sold as scallions, including Allium cepa harvested young before the bulb swells, the Welsh onion Allium fistulosum which never forms a true bulb, and tree onions; in Britain and Ireland the term "spring onion" covers similar produce, while "scallion" usage varies regionally in North America. Scallions are a staple garnish and ingredient in East Asian cuisines — finely sliced in Chinese, Japanese and Korean cooking, and used in scallion pancakes (cong you bing) — and appear widely in salads, soups, salsas and stir-fries worldwide.

Cultivation

Scallions are fast-growing and can be harvested within weeks of sowing; home gardeners often regrow them from the white root ends in soil or water. Major commercial production occurs in China, Japan, Korea and Mexico.
