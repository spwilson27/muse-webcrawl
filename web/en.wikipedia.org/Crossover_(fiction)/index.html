Crossover (fiction)

Illustration: Crossover (fiction)

A crossover is the placement of two or more otherwise discrete fictional characters, settings, or universes into the context of a single story. Crossovers can occur across media, including comic books, films, television series, video games, and novels, and are often used to explore interactions between characters who would not normally meet.

History

Early crossovers appeared in literature and comics; the shared superhero universes developed by Marvel Comics and DC Comics in the mid-20th century made large-scale crossovers a staple of popular fiction. In Japan, manga and anime frequently hold crossover specials between series in the same magazine or franchise.

Types

Crossovers may be canonical, forming part of an official shared continuity, or non-canonical promotional events. They can take the form of guest appearances, full ensemble stories, or versus narratives in which characters from different works compete or fight.

Legal issues

Because characters are typically owned by different rightsholders, crossovers usually require licensing agreements, which is why most occur within a single publisher's catalogue or between collaborating companies.
