The Battle of Cedar Creek, fought on October 19, 1864, was the culminating battle of the Valley Campaigns of 1864 during the American Civil War. Confederate Lt. Gen. Jubal Early launched a surprise attack against the encamped Union Army of the Shenandoah under Maj. Gen. Philip Sheridan, driving it back in the morning fog — but Sheridan, arriving from Winchester, rallied his men in the famous "Sheridan's Ride" and counterattacked, winning a decisive Union victory.

Background

Sheridan had beaten Early at Winchester (Third Winchester) and Fisher's Hill in September and devastated the Shenandoah Valley's farms ("the Burning") so it could no longer supply Confederate armies. Nevertheless, secretly reinforced, Early resolved on one more attack.

Battle

Before dawn, Confederate troops crossed Cedar Creek and the North Fork of the Shenandoah River in dense fog and struck the Union left, overrunning the VIII Corps camps and capturing some 1,300 prisoners and about 20 guns; by mid-morning the Confederates had driven the Union army back several miles. But the attack stalled — hungry Confederate soldiers stopped to plunder the captured camps — and the Union XIX and VI Corps formed a new line.

Sheridan, who had spent the night at Winchester, rode the roughly 10–12 miles to the field (immortalized in Thomas Buchanan Read's poem "Sheridan's Ride"), restored order, and at about 4 p.m. launched a general counterattack. Union cavalry swept both flanks, and Early's army collapsed, losing all its captured guns and more — some 43 cannon in total — and fleeing up the Valley.

Consequences

Cedar Creek ended major Confederate resistance in the Shenandoah Valley and, coming shortly before the 1864 presidential election, strengthened Abraham Lincoln's re-election. The battle is preserved at Cedar Creek and Belle Grove National Historical Park.
