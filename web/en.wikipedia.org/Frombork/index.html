Frombork

Frombork (German: Frauenburg) is a town in northern Poland, on the Vistula Lagoon, in Warmian-Masurian Voivodeship, with a population of roughly 2,300. It is best known as the place where the astronomer Nicolaus Copernicus lived as a canon of the Warmia cathedral chapter for much of his life, wrote De revolutionibus orbium coelestium, died in 1543, and is buried.

History

The town grew up below the fortified cathedral hill (the Wzgórze Katedralne), where a cathedral was built between 1329 and 1388. Copernicus lived there from about 1510 until his death, conducting observations from the defensive tower that now bears his name. The town passed between Polish, Teutonic, and (after 1466) Polish royal rule as part of Royal Prussia, then to Prussia in the First Partition of 1772. Heavily damaged in the Second World War — about 80 percent of the town was destroyed in 1945 — Frombork was transferred to Poland and rebuilt, with much restoration work done by Polish scouts in the 1960s and 1970s.

Sights

The cathedral complex includes the Gothic Archcathedral Basilica of the Assumption of the Blessed Virgin Mary and St. Andrew, the Copernicus Tower, and the Nicolaus Copernicus Museum. In 2005 archaeologists located what was identified as Copernicus's grave in the cathedral; his remains were reburied with ceremony in 2010.
