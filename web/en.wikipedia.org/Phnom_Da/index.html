Phnom Da

Phnom Da is a hill and archaeological site in Angkor Borei district, Takéo Province, in southern Cambodia, near the Vietnamese border. Rising from the flat Mekong floodplain, the hill preserves remains from two eras of Khmer history: the early kingdom of Funan, whose capital region this was, and the later Angkorian period.

The temple

On the summit stands a temple tower built in the eleventh century, during the Angkorian period, under the reign associated with Udayadityavarman II. Constructed of laterite, brick and sandstone and dedicated to Shiva, the tower replaced or succeeded earlier shrines on the same sacred hill. Caves cut into the hillside contain carved beds and channels used in ritual, and early inscriptions found here are among the oldest in Cambodia.

Phnom Da style sculpture

The site gives its name to the Phnom Da style of early Khmer sculpture, dated to the sixth and early seventh centuries. Masterpieces in this style — including celebrated statues of Vishnu, Rama and Balarama, notable for their naturalistic modelling and smooth, sensuous forms influenced by Gupta-period Indian art — were recovered from the hill and are now housed in the National Museum of Cambodia in Phnom Penh and the Musée Guimet in Paris. Together with the nearby ancient city of Angkor Borei, Phnom Da is a key site for understanding the Funan kingdom and the beginnings of Khmer art. See also the related highland sanctuary of Phnom Kulen, associated with the succeeding Chenla period.
