The Politburo of the Chinese Communist Party, formally the Political Bureau of the Central Committee of the Communist Party of China, is the principal decision-making body of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) between sessions of the Central Committee. It is composed of the party's top leaders — currently 24 members — and effectively functions as one of the highest organs of state power in the People's Republic of China, since the CCP directs the government, the legislature, and the armed forces.

Selection and composition

Formally, the Politburo is elected by the Central Committee at its first plenary session following a National Congress, held every five years. In practice, membership is determined by negotiation among the incumbent top leadership and retired elders, reflecting factional balance, patronage, seniority, and informal norms such as age limits (the "seven up, eight down" convention, under which officials aged 67 may stay and those 68 must retire at a congress). Members typically hold concurrent top state or party posts: the General Secretary, the Premier, the chairman of the National People's Congress Standing Committee, the heads of the United Front and discipline-inspection apparatuses, the executive vice premier, senior military officers of the Central Military Commission, and the party secretaries of key provinces and municipalities such as Beijing, Shanghai, and Guangdong.

Powers and functioning

The Politburo meets roughly monthly and decides major policy questions: economic strategy, foreign policy, military affairs, appointments, and ideological direction. Its decisions are transmitted to state organs for implementation. Real power is concentrated in the smaller Politburo Standing Committee (PSC), which meets weekly and currently has seven members headed by the General Secretary. The General Secretary convenes both bodies and presides over the Secretariat, which handles day-to-day party business. Under Xi Jinping, who became General Secretary in 2012, authority has been further centralised through party "leading small groups" and commissions chaired by the General Secretary, and the informal retirement norms were set aside at the 20th Congress in 2022, when Xi took a third term.

History

The first Political Bureau was established in 1927, following the model of the Soviet Communist Party's Politburo. Under Mao Zedong the body's formal role was often eclipsed by the paramount leader's personal authority, particularly during the Cultural Revolution. In the reform era under Deng Xiaoping and his successors, collective leadership conventions were strengthened — fixed terms, retirement ages, and consensus decision-making — before the recentralisation of the Xi era.
