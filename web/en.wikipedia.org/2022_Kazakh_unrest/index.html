The 2022 Kazakh unrest, known in Kazakhstan as "Bloody January" (Qandy Qantar), was a wave of mass protests and violent unrest that began on 2 January 2022 after the government lifted a price cap on liquefied petroleum gas, causing fuel prices to double overnight. The protests, starting in the oil town of Zhanaozen, spread nationwide and broadened into demands over economic inequality, corruption and the continuing influence of former president Nursultan Nazarbayev.

Escalation

By 4–5 January, demonstrations in Almaty, the largest city, turned violent: protesters stormed and burned the city administration building, the presidential residence and other government sites, and briefly seized the international airport. President Kassym-Jomart Tokayev dismissed the government, imposed a state of emergency, and — characterising the unrest as a foreign-backed "terrorist" attack — appealed to the Russian-led Collective Security Treaty Organization for troops, the first CSTO deployment in its history. On 7 January Tokayev announced he had authorised security forces to "shoot to kill without warning".

Aftermath

Order was largely restored by 11 January and CSTO forces withdrew within two weeks. Kazakh authorities put the death toll at 238, including security personnel; around 12,000 people were detained, and human rights groups documented widespread allegations of torture in custody. In the political settlement that followed, Tokayev removed Nazarbayev from his remaining posts, stripped his family's privileges, arrested former security chief Karim Massimov on treason charges, and pursued a programme of constitutional reform — including restoring the capital's name from Nur-Sultan back to Astana in September 2022.
