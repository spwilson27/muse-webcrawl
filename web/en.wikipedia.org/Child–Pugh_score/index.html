Child–Pugh score

The Child–Pugh score is a clinical measure used to assess the prognosis of chronic liver disease, mainly cirrhosis. It scores five measures — bilirubin, albumin, INR, ascites and hepatic encephalopathy — each 1 to 3 points.

Totals place patients in class A (5–6, least severe), B (7–9) or C (10–15, most severe), correlating with survival and used in decisions about surgery and transplantation, alongside the MELD score.
