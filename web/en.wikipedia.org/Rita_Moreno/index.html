Rita Moreno (born Rita Dolores Alverío; December 11, 1931) is a Puerto Rican actress, dancer, and singer. With a career spanning more than eight decades, she is one of the few performers to win an Emmy, a Grammy, an Oscar, and a Tony Award (EGOT), as well as the first Latin American woman to win an Academy Award.

Career

Born in Humacao, Puerto Rico, Moreno moved to New York as a child and was performing on Broadway by her teens. Under contract at MGM she appeared in Singin' in the Rain (1952) and The King and I (1956). Her performance as Anita in West Side Story (1961) won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress. She later won a Tony for The Ritz (1975), a Grammy for The Electric Company album, and two Emmys, achieving EGOT status in 1977.

Later work

Later roles include Sister Peter Marie in HBO's Oz, Lydia in the sitcom One Day at a Time (2017–2020), and Valentina in Steven Spielberg's 2021 remake of West Side Story, in which she also served as executive producer. She received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2004 and Kennedy Center Honors in 2015.
