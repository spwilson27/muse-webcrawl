Double taxation


Double taxation can arise when corporate profits and dividends are both taxed.

Double taxation is the levying of tax by two or more jurisdictions on the same income, asset, or transaction in the same period. International double taxation occurs when a person or company is taxed by both their country of residence and the country where income arises. A second, economic form of double taxation arises within a single country, as when corporate profits are taxed at the company level and again as dividends in shareholders' hands.

Relief mechanisms

States mitigate double taxation through bilateral tax treaties, most following the OECD or UN model conventions, which allocate taxing rights and provide methods such as the foreign tax credit or exemption. Unilateral relief is also common: many countries credit foreign taxes paid against domestic liabilities. Rules on residence, source, and permanent establishment determine which methods apply.

Critiques

Critics argue double taxation discourages cross-border investment and saving; conversely, aggressive treaty use can enable double non-taxation through loopholes, a target of the OECD's base erosion and profit shifting (BEPS) project.
