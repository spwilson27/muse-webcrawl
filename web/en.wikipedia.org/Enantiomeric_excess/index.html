Enantiomeric excess

Enantiomeric excess
Symbolee
Range0% (racemic) to 100% (enantiopure)
MeasurementChiral chromatography, polarimetry, NMR

Enantiomeric excess is a measurement of purity used for chiral substances. It reflects the composition of a sample in terms of the relative quantities of its two enantiomers. A completely racemic sample (a 50:50 mixture of enantiomers) has an enantiomeric excess of 0%, while a single completely pure enantiomer has an enantiomeric excess of 100%.

Definition

Enantiomeric excess is defined as the absolute difference between the mole fractions of each enantiomer: ee = |FR − FS| × 100%. A sample with 70% of the R enantiomer and 30% of the S enantiomer has an enantiomeric excess of 40% in favour of R. The related quantity, optical purity, was historically calculated from optical rotation but is not always equal to ee, because rotation can vary nonlinearly with composition.

Measurement

Modern methods determine enantiomeric composition by chiral high-performance liquid chromatography or gas chromatography, or by NMR spectroscopy using chiral shift reagents or derivatising agents. Polarimetry alone is no longer considered reliable because impurities also contribute to rotation.

Importance

Because enantiomers can differ dramatically in biological activity — one may be a drug while the other is inactive or harmful — ee is a critical specification in pharmaceutical chemistry and in asymmetric synthesis, where catalytic methods are judged by the ee they achieve.
