Peel Sessions

HostJohn Peel
StationBBC Radio 1
Years1967–2004
Recording venueBBC Maida Vale Studios, London

The Peel Sessions were a feature of the British broadcaster John Peel's BBC Radio 1 shows, in which bands and artists were invited to record several tracks in a single day at the BBC's Maida Vale Studios for broadcast on Peel's programme. Between 1967 and Peel's death in 2004, more than 4,000 sessions were recorded by over 2,000 acts.

Format

Sessions typically consisted of four songs recorded and mixed in one day, giving them a raw, energetic quality distinct from studio album versions. Because musicians' union rules and needle-time restrictions limited the amount of commercially released music the BBC could broadcast, sessions were a vital way for Peel to feature new music. Many bands received their first national exposure through a Peel session, and an invitation was widely regarded as a mark of credibility.

Notable sessions

Acts that recorded sessions include Led Zeppelin, Pink Floyd, David Bowie, The Smiths, Joy Division, Nirvana, Pulp, and The Fall, who recorded 24 sessions—more than any other act. Some sessions, such as the only session by The Smiths' contemporaries and the celebrated 1991 Nirvana session, became commercially released albums in their own right.

Legacy

Many session recordings have been released on the Strange Fruit record label and by the BBC itself. Peel's personal archive of sessions and records was catalogued after his death, and the sessions remain an important documentary record of British popular and underground music across four decades.
