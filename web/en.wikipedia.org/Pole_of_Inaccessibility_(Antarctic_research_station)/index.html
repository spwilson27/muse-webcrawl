Pole of Inaccessibility (Antarctic research station)

LocationInterior East Antarctica, about 82°06′ S 55°04′ E
Established14 December 1958 (USSR)
ElevationAbout 3,800 m
StatusAbandoned; buried under snow

The Pole of Inaccessibility research station is a defunct Soviet research station in Kemp Land/Princess Elizabeth Land, Antarctica, at the southern pole of inaccessibility — the point on the Antarctic continent most distant from the Southern Ocean.

History

The station was established on 14 December 1958 by the 3rd Soviet Antarctic Expedition, led by Yevgeny Tolstikov. It was equipped for a small team with a hut on a sled, a radio room, and supplies, and operated for about two weeks of meteorological and glaciological observations before being left as a shelter for future expeditions.

The Lenin bust

The site is famous for a bust of Vladimir Lenin mounted on the roof of the station building, facing Moscow. Over the decades the building has been buried by accumulating snow, leaving only the bust visible above the surface — an iconic image of Antarctic exploration. The site has been designated a Historic Site and Monument under the Antarctic Treaty System.

Later visits

Expeditions have occasionally reached the site, including a 2007 British-New Zealand team that dug down to the station and the Lenin bust, and travellers using kite-ski and tractor traverses.
