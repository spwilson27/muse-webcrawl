Titan is the largest moon of Saturn and the second-largest natural satellite in the Solar System, bigger than the planet Mercury. It is the only moon known to have a dense atmosphere and the only object besides Earth where clear evidence of stable surface liquid has been found — lakes and seas of liquid methane and ethane near its poles.

Atmosphere and climate

Titan's atmosphere is mainly nitrogen with about 5% methane, shrouded in an orange photochemical haze. Its surface temperature of about 94 K allows methane to play the role water plays on Earth: it evaporates, condenses into clouds, and falls as rain, carving river channels and filling lakes such as Kraken Mare and Ligeia Mare.

Exploration

Titan was discovered by Christiaan Huygens in 1655. It was visited by Pioneer 11, Voyager 1 and 2, and studied extensively by the Cassini orbiter (2004–2017), which deployed the European Huygens probe; Huygens landed on Titan's surface on 14 January 2005, the most distant landing ever made. NASA's Dragonfly rotorcraft mission is planned to explore Titan in the 2030s.

Interior

Measurements of Titan's gravity and rotation suggest a global subsurface ocean of liquid water beneath its icy crust, making Titan a target of astrobiological interest.
