NEPTUNE

NEPTUNE (North-East Pacific Time-series Undersea Networked Experiments) is an ocean observatory operated by Ocean Networks Canada from the University of Victoria. Located off the west coast of Vancouver Island, British Columbia, it forms the world's first regional-scale cabled ocean observatory, connected to shore by an 812-kilometre loop of fibre-optic and power cable.

Description

Commissioned in 2009, NEPTUNE's ring topology carries electricity and two-way Internet communication to instrumented nodes at depths from the coast down to 2,660 metres in the abyssal plain. Its sites span key environments of the Juan de Fuca Plate, including Barkley Canyon, the Cascadia Basin, and the Endeavour hydrothermal vent field.

Science

Hundreds of sensors stream data continuously and in real time to researchers and the public: hydrophones record whale calls and shipping noise, seismometers and tsunameters feed earthquake and tsunami early-warning work, and cameras and chemical sensors observe methane seeps, gas hydrates, deep-sea ecosystems, and climate-driven changes in ocean chemistry and temperature.
