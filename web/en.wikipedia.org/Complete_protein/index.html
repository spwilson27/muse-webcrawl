Complete protein

Eggs, dairy products, meat and soy are sources of complete protein

A complete protein or whole protein is a food source of protein that contains an adequate proportion of each of the nine essential amino acids necessary in the human diet. By contrast, an incomplete protein is deficient in one or more of these essential amino acids relative to human requirements.

Sources

Nearly all animal-derived foods — meat, poultry, fish, eggs, milk, and cheese — are complete proteins. Plant sources that are complete or nearly complete include soy products (tofu, edamame, tempeh), quinoa, buckwheat, amaranth, hemp seeds, and chia seeds. Most other plant proteins are limiting in one amino acid: grains tend to be low in lysine, while legumes are low in methionine.

Protein combining

Because different incomplete proteins complement one another, eating a varied diet across the day supplies all essential amino acids; traditional combinations include rice and beans, and hummus with pita. Earlier dietary advice held that complementary proteins must be eaten at the same meal, but major dietetic associations now state that a varied diet over the course of the day is sufficient for healthy adults.

Protein quality measures

Protein quality is quantified by measures such as the protein digestibility-corrected amino acid score (PDCAAS) and its successor, the digestible indispensable amino acid score (DIAAS), which incorporate both amino acid composition and digestibility.
