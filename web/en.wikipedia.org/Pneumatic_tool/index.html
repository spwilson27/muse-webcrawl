Pneumatic tool

A pneumatic tool, air tool, air-powered tool or pneumatic-powered tool is a type of power tool driven by compressed air supplied by an air compressor. Pneumatic tools can also be driven by compressed carbon dioxide stored in small cylinders, allowing for portability.

Advantages

Compared with equivalent electric tools, pneumatic tools are generally lighter, since the air motor is smaller and simpler than an electric motor, and the power source (the compressor) is external and shared among many tools. They cannot overheat, produce no sparks (valuable in explosive atmospheres), and are durable in dirty or wet environments. Their main disadvantages are the need for a compressor and air hose, and often considerable exhaust noise.

Common types

Examples include jackhammers and rock drills, impact wrenches, ratchets, grinders, sanders, nail guns, staplers, spray guns, air blow guns, riveters, and chipping hammers. Air tools are standard in automotive repair, construction, assembly lines, and shipyards.

Air supply

Most air tools operate at about 90 pounds per square inch (6.2 bar). Air preparation typically includes filtration, pressure regulation, and (for some tools) lubrication through an in-line oiler.
