The Structure of Scientific Revolutions

The Structure of Scientific Revolutions is a 1962 book by philosopher of science Thomas S. Kuhn. It is one of the most influential works in the history and philosophy of science, arguing that science does not progress by the steady accumulation of facts but through periodic "paradigm shifts" in which one framework of theory and practice displaces another.

Key concepts

Kuhn introduced or popularized several concepts: the paradigm, a constellation of beliefs, values and techniques shared by a scientific community; normal science, puzzle-solving conducted within an accepted paradigm; anomalies, results that resist explanation; crisis, when anomalies accumulate; and scientific revolutions, in which a new paradigm replaces the old. Kuhn also argued that rival paradigms may be incommensurable, lacking a common measure for direct comparison.

Influence

The book popularized the phrase "paradigm shift" far beyond the philosophy of science, entering business, political theory and everyday language. It has been cited tens of thousands of times and remains a landmark in debates about scientific rationality, relativism and the sociology of knowledge. See also Philosophy of science.
