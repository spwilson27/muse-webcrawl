Vedic Sanskrit is the ancient Indo-Aryan language of the Vedas, the oldest texts of Hinduism. It is an archaic form of Sanskrit, distinct from the later Classical Sanskrit codified by the grammarian Pāṇini in his Ashtadhyayi (c. 5th–4th century BCE).

A Vedic verse showing the pitch accent notation.

Features

  • Pitch accent: Vedic had a musical pitch accent (udatta, anudatta, svarita), largely lost in Classical Sanskrit.
  • Rich verbal system: more moods and tenses than Classical Sanskrit, including the subjunctive and injunctive, and aorist and perfect systems fully alive.
  • Phonology: it retained the voiced retroflex lateral approximant and the syllabic ḭl, which Classical Sanskrit lost or restricted.

Development

Scholars divide Vedic Sanskrit into stages: Rigvedic (oldest), the language of the later Samhitas and Brahmanas, and late Vedic of the early Upanishads, shading into the classical language. Its closest relative is Avestan, the language of Zoroastrian scripture, reflecting a common Indo-Iranian origin.
