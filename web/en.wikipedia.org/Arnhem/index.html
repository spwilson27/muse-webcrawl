Arnhem

Arnhem is a city and municipality in the eastern Netherlands, the capital of the province of Gelderland. It lies on the banks of the Nederrijn (Lower Rhine) and has a population of roughly 160,000.

History

Arnhem received city rights in 1233. It grew as a trading town on the Rhine and joined the Hanseatic League. In September 1944 the city was the objective of Operation Market Garden, the Allied airborne operation intended to seize a bridgehead over the Rhine; the Battle of Arnhem ended in a German victory, and the city's population was evacuated, returning to a devastated city after liberation in April 1945.

Landmarks

Landmarks include the John Frost Bridge, named for the British airborne commander who held the road bridge in 1944, the Eusebius Church, the open-air Netherlands Open Air Museum, and Burgers' Zoo. The nearby Hoge Veluwe National Park and the Kröller-Müller Museum lie just north of the city.

Transport

Arnhem Centraal station is a major rail hub linking the Randstad with Germany. The city is also known for one of the few trolleybus networks remaining in Western Europe.
