Liability insurance

Liability insurance (also called third-party insurance) is a part of the general insurance system of risk financing to protect the purchaser (the "insured") from the risks of liabilities imposed by lawsuits and similar claims, and protects the insured if the purchaser is sued for claims that come within the coverage of the insurance policy.

Coverage

Liability policies typically cover legal defence costs and damages the insured becomes legally obligated to pay to third parties for bodily injury or property damage. Common forms include general liability insurance for businesses, professional liability (errors and omissions) insurance, directors and officers liability, and the liability component of motor insurance, which is compulsory in many jurisdictions.

Claims-made versus occurrence

Policies may be written on an occurrence basis, covering events that occur during the policy period whenever claimed, or on a claims-made basis, covering claims filed during the period. Liability insurance does not cover intentional wrongdoing. It is a key mechanism by which the financial risk of tort liability is spread.
