cgroups

cgroups (abbreviated from control groups) is a Linux kernel feature that limits, accounts for and isolates the resource usage (CPU, memory, disk I/O, network, etc.) of a collection of processes. It is a foundational technology behind containers such as those managed by Docker and Kubernetes.


The cgroup v2 unified hierarchy

History

Engineers at Google, principally Paul Menage and Rohit Seth, began work on the feature in 2006 under the name "process containers"; it was merged into the Linux kernel in version 2.6.24 (2008) and renamed cgroups. A major redesign, cgroup v2, introduced a single unified hierarchy and was declared stable with kernel 4.5 in 2016.

Features

Resource controllers ("subsystems") manage individual resources: the cpu controller throttles or weights processor time, the memory controller limits RAM use and can trigger out-of-memory handling per group, and io, pids, cpuset and devices controllers manage their respective resources. Processes are assigned to cgroups through a pseudo-filesystem interface, typically managed by systemd or container runtimes. Unlike virtual machines, cgroups provide isolation and limits without a separate kernel, and are combined with namespaces to build containers.
