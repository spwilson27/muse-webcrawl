Stormwater

Stormwater runoff entering a storm drain

Stormwater, also written storm water, is water that originates from precipitation (storm) events, including heavy rain and meltwater from hail and snow. Stormwater can soak into the soil (infiltrate) and become groundwater, be stored on depressed land surface in ponds and puddles, evaporate back into the atmosphere, or contribute to surface runoff. Most runoff is conveyed directly to nearby streams, rivers, or other water bodies (surface water) without treatment.

Runoff and pollution

In developed areas, impervious surfaces such as pavements and roofs prevent precipitation from naturally soaking into the ground, greatly increasing runoff volumes and speeds. Urban runoff picks up pollutants — oil, heavy metals, fertilisers, sediment, and litter — and is a leading cause of water quality impairment in receiving waters.

Management

Stormwater management uses storm drains, detention and retention ponds, and green infrastructure such as rain gardens, permeable pavement, constructed wetlands, and bioswales to slow, store, infiltrate, and filter runoff. Approaches such as low-impact development and water-sensitive urban design aim to mimic natural hydrology. In many cities, older combined sewers can overflow during storms, discharging untreated sewage.

Regulation

In the United States, stormwater discharges are regulated under the Clean Water Act's National Pollutant Discharge Elimination System, requiring permits for municipal and industrial discharges.
