Speculative fiction

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Speculative fiction spans science fiction, fantasy and horror

Speculative fiction is an umbrella term for fiction with supernatural, fantastic or futuristic elements, encompassing the genres of science fiction, fantasy, horror, and various hybrids and subgenres such as alternate history, utopian and dystopian fiction, apocalyptic fiction, and superhero fiction. The term is used to distinguish these works from realistic or mimetic fiction.

Terminology

Although earlier uses exist, the term was popularised in its modern sense by science-fiction author Robert A. Heinlein in 1947, and later championed by writers such as Margaret Atwood, who used it for works that extrapolate plausibly from known conditions rather than featuring impossibilities. Some writers use the abbreviation "SF" to stand for speculative fiction rather than science fiction.

Characteristics

Speculative fiction typically asks "what if" questions — about other worlds, alternative histories, future technologies, or supernatural premises — and uses these estranging settings to explore social, philosophical and scientific ideas. It ranges from "hard" science fiction grounded in known science to epic fantasy built on invented mythologies.
