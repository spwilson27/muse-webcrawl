APG III system

The APG III system of flowering plant classification is the third version of a modern, mostly molecular-based system of plant taxonomy published in 2009 by the Angiosperm Phylogeny Group, six years after the publication of the APG II system (2003) and eleven years after the initial APG system (1998). It was published in the Botanical Journal of the Linnean Society together with a formal classification of orders and families.

Overview

APG III recognizes 59 orders and 415 families of flowering plants. It continued the APG philosophy of basing classification on monophyletic groups supported by molecular phylogenetic evidence, using the informal clade names magnoliids, monocots, eudicots, core eudicots, rosids (with fabids and malvids), and asterids (with lamiids and campanulids).

Changes from APG II

APG III abandoned the APG II practice of listing "bracketed" alternative families, instead adopting broader family circumscriptions in many groups: for example, Amaryllidaceae was expanded to include the former Agapanthaceae and Alliaceae, and Asparagaceae absorbed families such as Hyacinthaceae and Ruscaceae. Several new orders were recognized, including Berberidopsidales, Buxales, Chloranthales, Escalloniales, Huerteales, Picramniales, Trochodendrales, Vitales, and Zygophyllales. The arrangement of families of uncertain position was reduced as phylogenetic evidence improved, though a small number of genera, such as Gumillea and Petenaea, remained unplaced.

Influence and succession

The APG III classification became the standard reference for the taxonomy of flowering plants in floras, databases such as Plants of the World Online, and herbarium arrangement. It was succeeded in 2016 by the APG IV system, which recognized 64 orders and 416 families and refined placements using larger genomic data sets.
