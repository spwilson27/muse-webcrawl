John McGeoch

Born: John Alexander McGeoch, 25 August 1955, Greenock, Scotland
Died: 4 March 2004 (aged 48), Launceston, Cornwall, England
Occupation: Guitarist
Associated acts: Magazine; Siouxsie and the Banshees; Visage; The Armoury Show; Public Image Ltd

John Alexander McGeoch (25 August 1955 – 4 March 2004) was a Scottish guitarist who played with several bands of the post-punk era, including Magazine, Siouxsie and the Banshees, Visage, the Armoury Show and Public Image Ltd. He is regarded as one of the most innovative and influential guitarists of his generation, noted for his flanged arpeggios, harmonics and use of chorus effects; Radiohead's Jonny Greenwood and the Red Hot Chili Peppers' John Frusciante are among those who have cited him as an influence.

Career

McGeoch grew up in Fort William and studied fine art at Manchester Polytechnic, where he formed Magazine in 1977 with Howard Devoto, formerly of Buzzcocks. His playing defined the band's early albums Real Life (1978) and Secondhand Daylight (1979), particularly on the single "Shot by Both Sides". In 1980 he left Magazine and joined Siouxsie and the Banshees, contributing to Kaleidoscope (1980), Juju (1981) and A Kiss in the Dreamhouse (1982); his guitar work on singles such as "Happy House", "Israel" and "Spellbound" is among the most celebrated of the era.

A nervous breakdown during the Banshees' 1982 tour led to his departure. He later played with the Armoury Show alongside former Skids members, and from 1987 to 1992 was a member of Public Image Ltd, appearing on the albums Happy?, 9 and That What Is Not. He also qualified and worked for a time as a nurse.

Death and legacy

McGeoch died in his sleep in 2004, aged 48. In 1996 Mojo magazine ranked him among the 100 greatest guitarists of all time, and his style — melodic, textural and technically inventive — remains a touchstone for alternative rock guitarists.
