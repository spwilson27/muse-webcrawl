Oat milk

Oat milk is a plant milk derived from whole oat grains by extracting the plant material with water. It has a creamy texture and a mild, oatmeal-like flavour, and is manufactured in plain and sweetened varieties with flavours such as chocolate and vanilla. It is consumed as an alternative to dairy milk and is popular among vegans and people with lactose intolerance or cow's-milk allergy.

History

Plant milks have been consumed for centuries, but oat milk is a recent invention. It was developed in the early 1990s by the Swedish scientist Rickard Öste, founder of the company Oatly, who was researching alternatives to milk for people with lactose intolerance. Oat milk grew rapidly in popularity in the late 2010s, particularly in coffee culture, because it froths well and is often considered environmentally favourable relative to other milks.

Production

Commercial production typically involves milling soaked oats with water, using enzymes (such as amylases) to break down oat starch and prevent a slimy texture, removing insoluble fibre, and homogenising the liquid, often with added vegetable oil, salt, stabilisers, and fortifying vitamins (D, B12) and calcium. Homemade versions are made by blending rolled oats with water and straining.

Nutrition and environment

Unsweetened oat milk typically provides about 40–60 kcal per 100 ml, contains beta-glucan soluble fibre, and is naturally free of lactose, soy, and nuts, though it usually has less protein than cow's or soy milk. Life-cycle assessments generally find oat milk has lower greenhouse-gas emissions and land and water use than dairy milk, and typically lower water use than almond milk.
