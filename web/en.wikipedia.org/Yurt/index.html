Yurt

A yurt (ger) in Mongolia

Yurt
RegionCentral Asian steppe
NamesGer (Mongolian), üy (Turkic)
MaterialsWood lattice, felt, rope

A yurt (from the Turkic languages) or ger (in Mongolian) is a portable, round tent covered with felt or fabric, used as a dwelling by nomadic and semi-nomadic peoples of the steppes of Central Asia, including Mongols, Kazakhs, Kyrgyz and Turkmen. Its design combines a collapsible lattice wall, roof poles, a central compression ring and tension bands, giving a structure that is warm in winter, cool in summer, and can be erected or packed onto animals or a truck within an hour or two.

Structure

The wall (khana) is a criss-cross lattice of wooden laths; roof poles (uni) run from the top of the wall to the central crown ring (toono), which also serves as a smoke vent above the stove. Layers of sheep-wool felt are tied over the frame and traditionally covered with a decorative outer cloth. A wooden door (khaalga), often painted orange with ornaments, faces south in Mongolia.

History and use

Felt tents of this type have been used on the steppe for well over a thousand years and were the standard dwelling during the Mongol Empire. Despite urbanisation, gers remain widespread: a large share of Mongolia's population, including many residents of Ulaanbaatar's ger districts, still lives in them. The traditional craftsmanship of the Kyrgyz and Kazakh yurt is inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Modern yurts

Adaptations with canvas, insulation and wooden floors are used worldwide for camping ("glamping"), classrooms and eco-tourism.
