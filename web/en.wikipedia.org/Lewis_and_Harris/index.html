Lewis and Harris (Scottish Gaelic: Leòdhas agus na Hearadh) is the largest island in Scotland and the third largest in the British Isles after Great Britain and Ireland. It forms the northern part of the Outer Hebrides archipelago. Although referred to as if it were two islands, Lewis (in the north) and Harris (in the south) are a single landmass, divided by a range of hills rather than by water.

The boundary between Lewis and Harris follows a line from Loch Resort to Loch Seaforth. Historically the two parts belonged to different counties — Lewis to Ross and Cromarty and Harris to Inverness-shire — a division that preserved the fiction of two separate islands.

Geography

Lewis is comparatively low-lying, a plateau of peat moorland scattered with lochans, while Harris is mountainous, with the Clisham rising to 799 metres. The west coast of Harris is celebrated for beaches such as Luskentyre and Scarista, among the largest and most striking in the United Kingdom. The main town and ferry port is Stornoway in Lewis.

Culture and economy

The island is a stronghold of Scottish Gaelic, spoken by a majority of the population, and of Presbyterian observance — particularly in Lewis, where Sabbatarian traditions remain influential. The island's best-known product is Harris Tweed, a handwoven woollen cloth protected by the Harris Tweed Act 1993 and the Orb trademark; by law it must be handwoven by islanders in the Outer Hebrides from pure virgin wool dyed and spun there. Other industries include fishing, tourism, and the manufacture of the famous Lewis chessmen replicas — the original 12th-century walrus-ivory pieces were found on the island in 1831.

History

Archaeological sites include the Callanish Stones, a Neolithic stone circle in Lewis dating from around 2900 BC. The islands passed between Norse and Scottish control before being ceded to Scotland by the Treaty of Perth in 1266. The traditional clan territories were held by the MacLeods in Lewis and Harris and the Morrisons in northern Lewis.
