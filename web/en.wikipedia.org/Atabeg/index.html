Atabeg

Atabeg, atabek, or atabey (from Turkic ata, "father," and beg, "lord, leader") is a hereditary title of nobility for a Turkic vassal ruler, indicating a subordinate who acted as governor of a nation or province and tutor of a young prince. The title was widely used in the Seljuk Empire and across the medieval Middle East.

History

The first atabegs were appointed by the Seljuk sultans as guardians and regents for young princes sent to govern provinces. As Seljuk power declined in the 12th century, many atabegs became effectively independent rulers, founding dynasties of their own.

Atabeg dynasties

Notable atabegates included the Eldiguzids (atabegs of Azerbaijan), the Zengids of Mosul and Aleppo — whose founder Zengi led the first major Muslim counterattacks against the Crusader states — the Salghurids of Fars, and the Ahmadilis of Maragha. The title later spread to Georgia and was used in various forms into the early modern period.
