Hudson Bay is a large body of saltwater in northeastern Canada with a surface area of about 1.23 million km², making it one of the world's largest bays. It drains a vast watershed covering much of central and northern Canada through rivers including the Nelson, Albany and Moose, and connects to the Atlantic via Hudson Strait and to the Arctic Ocean via Foxe Basin.

Geography and climate

The bay is relatively shallow and lies over the Canadian Shield; it was scoured by the Laurentide Ice Sheet, and the land around it is still rebounding isostatically. It is frozen for much of the year (roughly mid-December to mid-June). James Bay forms its southern extension. Its shores border Ontario, Quebec, Manitoba and Nunavut, and it is home to beluga whales, polar bears around Churchill, and major migratory bird habitat.

History

Named for Henry Hudson, who explored it in 1610–11 and was set adrift by mutineers, the bay became the commercial centre of the fur trade under the Hudson's Bay Company (chartered 1670), whose vast territory, Rupert's Land, was transferred to Canada in 1870. The port of Churchill, linked by rail, remains its only deep-water Arctic port.
