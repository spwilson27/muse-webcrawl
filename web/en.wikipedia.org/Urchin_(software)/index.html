Urchin was a web statistics analysis program developed by the Urchin Software Corporation of San Diego, California. It analysed web server log files and produced traffic reports, and in its later versions also used page-tag cookies for hybrid data collection. In 2005 the company was acquired by Google, and Urchin's technology and its Urchin Traffic Monitor (UTM) parameters became the basis of Google Analytics.

The Urchin reporting interface, showing visitor traffic statistics.

History

Urchin was founded in the mid-1990s by Paul Muret and Scott Crosby, who had built the product out of web-hosting work in San Diego; the software was publicly available by 1998. It was notable for processing large log files very quickly and for an interface that made analytics accessible to marketers as well as system administrators. Google acquired Urchin Software Corporation in April 2005 (announced in March 2005) for a reported price of about $30 million. In November 2005 Google launched the free, hosted Google Analytics service based on Urchin 6, while continuing to sell Urchin as a self-hosted product for enterprises, intranets and users who needed log-file analysis.

Features

Urchin could analyse logs from load-balanced and clustered web servers, track e-commerce transactions and campaign performance, and combine log data with JavaScript page tags. Its UTM (Urchin Traffic Monitor) campaign parameters — utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign and related tags appended to URLs — survived into Google Analytics and became a de facto standard for campaign tracking on the web. Successive releases culminated in Urchin 7 in 2010, with a redesigned interface resembling Google Analytics.

Discontinuation

In January 2012 Google announced that it would stop selling and developing Urchin, ending sales to new customers in March 2012 and directing users to Google Analytics. The decision reflected the shift of web analytics from log-file analysis toward hosted, tag-based services. A number of web hosts had bundled Urchin, notably through the cPanel control panel, and its removal led many hosting providers to substitute alternatives such as AWStats.
