Lahore Resolution

Minar-e-Pakistan, Lahore, built on the site of the 1940 session

The Lahore Resolution, later popularly called the Pakistan Resolution, was a political statement adopted by the All-India Muslim League at its annual session in Lahore on 23 March 1940. Moved by the Bengal leader A. K. Fazlul Huq and drafted with input from figures including Chaudhry Khaliquzzaman and Sikander Hayat Khan, it became the foundational demand for the creation of Pakistan.

Content

The resolution declared that no constitutional plan would be acceptable to Indian Muslims unless contiguous Muslim-majority regions in the north-western and eastern zones of India were grouped "to constitute Independent States in which the constituent units shall be autonomous and sovereign", and demanded adequate safeguards for minorities in both those states and the rest of India. Notably, the text used the plural "states" and never mentioned the word "Pakistan" — a name coined in 1933 by Choudhry Rahmat Ali — yet the Hindu press and later tradition dubbed it the Pakistan Resolution. At its Delhi convention in April 1946, the League clarified its demand to a single sovereign state of Pakistan.

Legacy

The resolution is commemorated in Pakistan on 23 March as Pakistan Day (now Republic Day), and the Minar-e-Pakistan tower was erected in Iqbal Park, Lahore, at the site of its adoption. The document's text is displayed at the monument's base. It remains central to the "two-nation theory" — the claim, championed by Muhammad Ali Jinnah, that Hindus and Muslims of the subcontinent constituted two separate nations entitled to separate homelands.
