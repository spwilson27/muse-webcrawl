The People's Party, also known as the Populist Party or simply the Populists, was a left-wing agrarian populist political party in the United States in the late 19th century. The Populist Party emerged out of the Farmers' Alliance, a network of agrarian protest organizations, and was organized formally in 1891–1892, holding its first national convention in Omaha, Nebraska, in July 1892.

The Omaha Platform

The party's Omaha Platform of 1892 demanded the free and unlimited coinage of silver, a graduated income tax, government ownership of railroads, telegraphs and telephones, the direct election of U.S. senators, a shorter working day, and restrictions on immigration. Many of these proposals were later adopted by the major parties during the Progressive Era. The Populist presidential candidate in 1892, James B. Weaver, won over one million votes and 22 electoral votes, carrying several western states.

Fusion and decline

In 1896 the Populists fused with the Democratic Party behind William Jennings Bryan, whose "Cross of Gold" speech championed free silver. Bryan's defeat weakened the party, which declined rapidly after 1896 and effectively dissolved by 1909, though it left a lasting legacy on American reform politics and on the vocabulary of "populism" itself.
