Southeast Asian cuisine

Southeast Asian cuisine encompasses the diverse food traditions of the countries of Southeast Asia, including Thailand, Vietnam, Malaysia, Indonesia, the Philippines, Singapore, Cambodia, Laos, Myanmar, and Brunei. While each nation has distinct dishes, the region shares core ingredients and techniques shaped by a tropical climate, monsoon agriculture, and centuries of trade with China, India, and Europe.

Common elements

Rice is the staple, eaten at most meals and made into noodles, cakes, and fermented products. Signature aromatics include lemongrass, galangal, turmeric, chilli, garlic, shallots, and fresh herbs such as Thai basil, mint, and coriander. Fermented fish sauces and shrimp pastes provide umami depth, while coconut milk, tamarind, lime, and palm sugar balance curries and salads among sweet, sour, salty, spicy, and bitter notes.

Regional varieties

Thai cuisine is famous for dishes such as tom yum, green curry, and pad thai; Vietnamese cooking for phở, bánh mì, and fresh spring rolls; Malaysian and Singaporean food blends Malay, Chinese, and Indian influences in nasi lemak, laksa, and satay; Indonesian staples include rendang, nasi goreng, and gado-gado; Filipino cuisine combines indigenous, Spanish, and Chinese elements in adobo, sinigang, and lechon.
