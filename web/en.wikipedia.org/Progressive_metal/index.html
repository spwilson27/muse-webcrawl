Progressive metal

Progressive metal (often shortened to prog metal) is a broad fusion music genre melding heavy metal with progressive rock. It combines the loud, guitar-driven aggression of metal with the experimental song structures, unusual time signatures, long compositions, and instrumental virtuosity of progressive rock bands such as Rush, Yes, and King Crimson.

Characteristics

Progressive metal songs frequently exceed ten minutes, alternate between quiet and crushing passages, use odd meters such as 7/8 and 5/4 alongside rapid meter changes, and showcase highly technical playing — sweep-picked guitar solos, two-handed tapping, slap bass, and polyrhythmic drumming. Concept albums and lyrical themes drawn from science fiction, philosophy, and psychology are common. The genre's musicianship is a central attraction, and many of its performers are celebrated as virtuosos.

History

The genre crystallised in the mid-1980s with American bands Fates Warning, Queensrÿche, and Watchtower, and reached wider success with Dream Theater, whose 1992 album Images and Words and single "Pull Me Under" brought prog metal to mainstream rock radio. In the 1990s and 2000s bands such as Tool, Opeth, Symphony X, Porcupine Tree, Pain of Salvation, Ayreon, and Meshuggah pushed the style in heavier, darker, and more experimental directions. Meshuggah's downtuned, polymetric approach spawned the "djent" subgenre of the 2010s, carried by bands such as Periphery, Animals as Leaders, and Tesseract, often using seven- and eight-string guitars.

Subgenres and influence

Progressive metal overlaps with technical death metal, progressive rock, math rock, and metalcore. Its emphasis on extended-range instruments, home-produced albums distributed online, and virtuoso technique has made it a durable niche within modern metal.
