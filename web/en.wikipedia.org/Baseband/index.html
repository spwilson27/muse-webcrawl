Baseband


A spectrum centered near zero frequency: the baseband.

In telecommunications and signal processing, baseband is the range of frequencies occupied by a signal that has not been modulated to higher frequencies. Baseband signals typically originate from a transducer that converts some variable into an electrical signal; for example, the output of a microphone or a video camera. A baseband signal may have frequency components going approximately down to zero, or at least close to zero, with no frequency components higher than some intended maximum.

Baseband transmission

A signal at baseband can be transmitted directly over a suitable medium — baseband transmission — as in Ethernet over copper cable, serial cables, and composite video links. Because a baseband channel can carry only one signal at a time, sharing requires multiplexing in time.

Comparison with passband

A passband signal is a baseband signal shifted up to much higher frequencies, typically with a carrier wave through modulation. Passband transmission allows many signals to share one medium through frequency-division multiplexing, as in radio broadcasting and cable television. Digital baseband signals are often described by an equivalent low-pass (complex baseband) representation used in simulations of modulated systems.

Usage in mobile devices

In smartphones the "baseband processor" is the chip that handles radio communication functions such as cellular voice and data, running its own real-time operating system and firmware, separate from the applications processor.
