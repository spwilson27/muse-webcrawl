Lancaster is a town in Coös County, New Hampshire, United States, and serves as the county seat. Situated on the Connecticut River in the state's North Country, it styles itself the "Gateway to the Great North Woods".

History

Chartered in 1763 and named for Lancaster, Massachusetts, the town grew as an agricultural and trading centre for the upper Connecticut valley. It was the home of Jared Sparks, historian and president of Harvard, and its nineteenth-century Main Street, with the Coös County Courthouse, reflects its role as a regional hub.

Geography

The town lies at the junction of the Connecticut and Israel rivers, beneath the Pilot Range of the White Mountains. U.S. Routes 2 and 3 meet here, making Lancaster a crossroads for travellers to the White Mountains and Vermont. Mount Prospect and Weeks State Park, with the summer estate of conservationist John Wingate Weeks, overlook the town.
