Arthur Balfour

Arthur James Balfour, 1st Earl of Balfour (1848–1930), was a British statesman and Conservative politician who served as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom from 1902 to 1905. He was later Foreign Secretary, in which role he issued the Balfour Declaration of 1917.

Early career

Born at Whittingehame, East Lothian, and educated at Eton and Trinity College, Cambridge, Balfour entered Parliament in 1874. As Chief Secretary for Ireland under his uncle, Lord Salisbury, he combined land reform with coercion of nationalist agitation, earning the nickname "Bloody Balfour". He succeeded Salisbury as prime minister in 1902.

Premiership and later career

Balfour's government passed the Education Act 1902 and concluded the Entente Cordiale with France, but split over tariff reform, and he resigned in December 1905. As Foreign Secretary in Lloyd George's coalition from 1916, he issued the Balfour Declaration, expressing British support for a "national home for the Jewish people" in Palestine. He later represented Britain at the Washington Naval Conference and gave his name to the Balfour Declaration of 1926 on Commonwealth equality. He was also a published philosopher, author of A Defence of Philosophic Doubt.
