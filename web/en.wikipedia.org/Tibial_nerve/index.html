Tibial nerve

Details
FromSciatic nerve (L4, L5, S1, S2, S3)
InnervatesPosterior compartment of leg, intrinsic muscles of sole of foot
BranchesMedial plantar nerve, lateral plantar nerve, medial calcaneal nerve, sural nerve contribution

The tibial nerve is the larger of the two terminal branches of the sciatic nerve, the other being the common fibular (peroneal) nerve. The tibial nerve derives its fibres from the ventral branches of the L4, L5, S1, S2, and S3 spinal nerves. It supplies motor and sensory innervation to the posterior compartment of the leg and the sole of the foot.

Structure

The tibial nerve descends through the popliteal fossa, passing superficial to the popliteal vessels, then runs deep to the soleus muscle down the back of the leg alongside the posterior tibial vessels. On its way it gives muscular branches to the gastrocnemius, soleus, plantaris, popliteus, tibialis posterior, flexor digitorum longus, and flexor hallucis longus muscles.

Behind the medial malleolus at the ankle, the nerve passes through the tarsal tunnel, deep to the flexor retinaculum, and divides into its terminal branches: the medial plantar nerve and the lateral plantar nerve, which supply the intrinsic muscles and skin of the sole. Before dividing, it gives off the medial calcaneal nerve, which supplies sensation to the heel.

Function

Motor functions of the tibial nerve include plantar flexion of the foot at the ankle, flexion of the toes, and inversion of the foot. Its sensory branches supply the posterolateral calf (via the medial sural cutaneous contribution to the sural nerve), the sole of the foot, and the heel.

Clinical significance

Entrapment of the tibial nerve in the tarsal tunnel (tarsal tunnel syndrome) causes pain, numbness, and tingling in the sole of the foot. Injury to the tibial nerve, though less common than injury to the common fibular nerve, results in loss of plantar flexion and toe flexion and a characteristic "claw foot" posture from unopposed action of the foot's extensors and intrinsic muscle loss.
