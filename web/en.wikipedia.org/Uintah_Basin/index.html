Uintah Basin

LocationNortheastern Utah, United States
CountiesUintah, Duchesne, Carbon, Emery, Grand
Major townsVernal, Roosevelt, Duchesne, Price
Known forOil, natural gas, gilsonite, oil shale

The Uintah Basin, also spelled Uinta Basin, is a physiographic section of the larger Colorado Plateaus province and an oil field in northeastern Utah in the western United States. The basin stretches roughly 130 kilometres east to west and contains the headwaters of tributaries of the Green River. Its principal communities include Vernal, Roosevelt, Duchesne, and Price.

Geology

The basin is a structural depression formed during the Laramide orogeny some 50–70 million years ago, when the Uinta Mountains were uplifted to the north. During the Eocene epoch the basin held a large lake, Lake Uinta, whose organic-rich sediments became the Green River Formation oil shales. The basin also contains gilsonite — a glossy black hydrocarbon found nowhere else in commercial quantities — as well as tar sands and conventional oil and gas deposits.

Economy

Energy extraction dominates the basin's economy. Oil was discovered near Vernal in the 1940s, and the basin has since been a significant producer of crude oil and natural gas, including unique waxy crude transported by heated trucks. Oil shale development has been attempted repeatedly during periods of high prices without reaching sustained commercial production.

Inhabitants

The basin is home to the Uintah and Ouray Reservation of the Ute Indian Tribe, headquartered at Fort Duchesne. The region is also the source of the "Uintah Basin" reports of unusual phenomena, including Skinwalker Ranch, which have attracted popular attention.
