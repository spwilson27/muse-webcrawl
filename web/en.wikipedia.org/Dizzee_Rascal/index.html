Dylan Kwabena Mills (born 18 September 1984), better known by his stage name Dizzee Rascal, is a British rapper, songwriter and record producer from Bow, East London. A pioneering figure in grime music, he is widely credited with bringing the genre to mainstream attention. His debut album, Boy in da Corner (2003), won the Mercury Prize, making him the first rapper to receive the award, and is frequently cited among the greatest British albums of all time.

Early life and career

Mills was born in London to a Nigerian mother and a Ghanaian father who died when he was young. Raised in Bow by his mother, he was expelled from several schools before a music teacher encouraged his talent for production and rapping. He began performing on pirate radio stations such as Deja Vu 97.7 as a teenager and was a member of the Roll Deep crew before going solo.

Music

Boy in da Corner (2003), recorded when Mills was eighteen, combined abrasive electronic production with vivid depictions of inner-city life; its singles included "I Luv U" and "Fix Up, Look Sharp". Follow-up albums Showtime (2004) and Maths + English (2007) consolidated his critical reputation. With Tongue n' Cheek (2009) he moved toward dance and pop sounds, producing UK number-one singles including "Dance Wiv Me", "Bonkers", "Holiday" and "Dirtee Disco". He also had a 2010 hit with "Shout", recorded for England's World Cup campaign. Later albums include The Fifth (2013), Raskit (2017), which returned to grime, E3 AF (2020) and Don't Gas Me (2018, EP) and Don't Take It Personal (2024). In 2020 he was appointed MBE for services to music.

Legal issues

In 2022 Mills was convicted of assaulting his former fiancée; he lost an appeal against the conviction in 2023. The case received extensive media coverage in the United Kingdom.
