Nasi Padang

Nasi Padang is a Minangkabau-style steamed rice served with a variety of pre-cooked side dishes originating from Padang, West Sumatra, Indonesia. It is one of the most popular ways of eating across Indonesia, Malaysia and Singapore, and is renowned for its rich, spicy flavours built on coconut milk, chilli and aromatic spices.

Serving styles

In a Padang restaurant (rumah makan Padang), diners choose between two services. In pesan (order) style, common for solo diners, the customer selects dishes at a display window and is served an individual plate. In hidang (serve) style, the waiter stacks and carries a dozen or more small dishes at once and covers the table with them; diners pay only for the dishes they actually eat from, and the remainder are returned to the display.

Typical dishes

Signature dishes include rendang (slow-braised beef in spiced coconut milk), ayam pop (skinless fried chicken), gulai (curries of chicken, fish, offal or vegetables), dendeng balado (crispy beef with chilli), sambal ijo (green chilli sambal), perkedel (potato fritters) and sayur singkong (cassava leaves in coconut milk), all accompanied by steamed rice and sambal.

Spread

Minangkabau migrants — practising the matrilineal tradition of merantau (sojourning) — carried Padang cooking across the archipelago, making the Padang restaurant a fixture of Indonesian towns everywhere.
