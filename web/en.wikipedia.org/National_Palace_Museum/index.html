National Palace Museum

The National Palace Museum in Taipei, Taiwan, houses one of the world's largest collections of Chinese art and artifacts, with nearly 700,000 pieces spanning 8,000 years, from Neolithic pottery to paintings, calligraphy, jades, and bronzes of the imperial collections.

Established1925 (Beijing); 1965 (Taipei)
LocationShilin District, Taipei
Collection size~700,000 items

History

Founded in the Forbidden City in Beijing in 1925 after the expulsion of the last Qing emperor, the collection was moved south in the 1930s to protect it from the Japanese invasion, then shipped to Taiwan in 1948–49 during the Chinese Civil War. The Taipei museum opened in 1965; a southern branch opened in Chiayi in 2015.

Famous pieces

Celebrated treasures include the Jadeite Cabbage, the Meat-Shaped Stone, the Mao Gong Ding bronze, and masterpieces of Song dynasty painting and calligraphy. Only a fraction can be displayed at once, and exhibitions rotate regularly.
