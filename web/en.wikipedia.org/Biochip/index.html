Biochip

A biochip is a collection of miniaturised test sites (microarrays) arranged on a solid substrate that permits many tests to be performed at the same time on a single sample in order to achieve higher throughput and speed. Typically, a biochip's surface area is no larger than a fingertip. The best-known examples are DNA microarrays, used to measure gene expression levels or detect mutations in thousands of genes simultaneously; protein microarrays and lab-on-a-chip microfluidic devices are also classed as biochips.

Technology

Like a computer chip that can perform millions of mathematical operations, a biochip can perform thousands of biological reactions in a few seconds. Known samples of DNA, proteins, or antibodies are spotted or synthesised at defined positions on glass, silicon, or polymer substrates. When the chip is exposed to a target sample, binding events are read out, usually by fluorescence, and analysed computationally.

Applications

Biochips are used in genomics, drug discovery, clinical diagnostics (including infectious disease and cancer testing), toxicology, and food and environmental testing. Implantable chips for identification of animals, and experimental glucose-monitoring chips for humans, are also sometimes described as biochips.
