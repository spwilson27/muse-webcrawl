Handbook of South American Indians

EditorJulian H. Steward
PublisherSmithsonian Institution, Bureau of American Ethnology
Published1946–1959 (Bulletin 143)
Volumes7 (6 + index)
SubjectEthnology of Indigenous peoples of South America

The Handbook of South American Indians is a seven-volume encyclopedia of the ethnology and archaeology of the Indigenous peoples of South America, published by the Smithsonian Institution's Bureau of American Ethnology as its Bulletin 143 between 1946 and 1959. It was edited by anthropologist Julian H. Steward and assembled contributions from dozens of specialists.

Contents

The first four volumes are organised by culture area: Volume 1 covers the Marginal Tribes; Volume 2, the Andean Civilizations; Volume 3, the Tropical Forest Tribes; and Volume 4, the Circum-Caribbean Tribes. Volume 5 addresses the comparative anthropology of South American Indians, including physical anthropology and linguistics, while Volume 6 covers geography, environmental background and maps. A seventh volume, published in 1959, is a bibliography and index.

Significance

The Handbook was the most comprehensive synthesis of South American Indigenous cultures produced to its date and remained a standard reference for decades. Steward's culture-area framework and his typology of levels of sociocultural integration, set out in the Handbook, strongly influenced mid-twentieth-century anthropology and his development of cultural ecology. Some classifications and terminology are now dated, and the work has been criticised for its period-bound assumptions, but it remains a baseline source of ethnographic data on many groups.
