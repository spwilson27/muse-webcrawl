Liu Yiqing (Chinese: 劉義慶; 403–444) was a prince of the Liu Song dynasty and one of the most celebrated compilers of Chinese anecdotal literature. He is best known as the compiler of Shishuo Xinyu (A New Account of the Tales of the World), a collection of anecdotes and vignettes about scholars, artists, and eccentrics of the late Han dynasty and the Wei–Jin period.

Life

A nephew of Emperor Wu of Song, the dynasty's founder, Liu Yiqing was enfeoffed as Prince of Linchuan. Talented and fond of literature, he gathered a circle of scholars at his court. According to his biography, he withdrew from political ambition — a dangerous asset for an imperial clansman — and devoted himself to letters and Buddhism. He held regional posts including governor of Jiangzhou and Jingzhou before dying in 444 at the age of forty-one.

Works

Shishuo Xinyu

The Shishuo Xinyu contains over a thousand brief anecdotes arranged in thirty-six categories, from "Virtuous Conduct" to "Stinginess." It captures the era of "pure conversation" (qingtan), wit, and romantic individualism among the Wei–Jin elite, and it remains an indispensable source for the period's culture and personalities — figures such as the Seven Sages of the Bamboo Grove. Liu Xiaobiao's later commentary added extensive quotations from now-lost sources.

Other compilations

Liu Yiqing is also credited with Youming lu (Records of the Hidden and Visible Worlds), a collection of supernatural tales in the zhiguai tradition, surviving only in fragments quoted in later encyclopedias.
