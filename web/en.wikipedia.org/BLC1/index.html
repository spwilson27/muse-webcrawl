BLC1

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

BLC1 (Breakthrough Listen Candidate 1) was a candidate radio signal detected in April and May 2019 by the Parkes radio telescope in Australia during observations of Proxima Centauri, the nearest star to the Sun. Announced in December 2020, the narrowband signal at about 982 MHz attracted attention because it appeared to come from the direction of Proxima Centauri, which hosts at least two planets, one in the habitable zone. Subsequent analysis concluded that BLC1 was almost certainly human-generated radio-frequency interference, not an extraterrestrial transmission.

Detection

The signal was found by the Breakthrough Listen technosignature search in data taken during a 26-hour observation campaign targeting Proxima Centauri. It appeared only in on-source pointings in its initial presentation, drifted slightly in frequency, and persisted for hours — features that made it the project's most intriguing candidate to that date, and reporting of it leaked to the press before analysis was complete.

Analysis

Follow-up studies in 2021 showed that BLC1 had look-alike signals at harmonically related frequencies present in off-source data, generated by intermodulation of local interference in the receiver system. The signal was never re-detected, and the Breakthrough Listen team concluded it was terrestrial interference. BLC1 nevertheless became a widely used case study in how technosignature candidates must be verified, and in designing pipelines that screen out human technology.
