Rich Communication Services

Rich Communication Services (RCS) is a communication protocol between mobile phone carriers and between phone and carrier, aiming to replace SMS messages with a richer text-message system. Developed under the auspices of the GSMA, RCS provides features long common in over-the-top messaging apps, including read receipts, typing indicators, group chat, and high-resolution media sharing.

History

The Rich Communication Suite industry initiative was formed in 2007. The GSMA adopted the programme in 2008, and the first RCS specifications were published in 2012. Adoption was slow until Google promoted the Universal Profile, a single interoperable specification released in 2016, and began rolling out RCS through its Messages app on Android. In 2024, Apple added RCS support to iOS 18, greatly expanding interoperability.

Features

RCS supports one-to-one and group messaging, file transfer, voice and video calls, location sharing, and verified business messaging. Unlike SMS, messages travel over IP networks, over Wi-Fi or mobile data.

Encryption

The base RCS standard does not mandate end-to-end encryption; Google's implementation added end-to-end encryption for one-to-one and later group conversations using the Signal Protocol. Cross-platform encrypted RCS has been a subject of ongoing standardisation work.
