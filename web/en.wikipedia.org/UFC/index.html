The Ultimate Fighting Championship (UFC) is an American mixed martial arts (MMA) promotion company based in Las Vegas, Nevada. It is the largest MMA promotion in the world, producing events worldwide that showcase many of the top-ranked fighters in the sport. The UFC is owned and operated by TKO Group Holdings, a majority-owned subsidiary of Endeavor Group Holdings.

History

The UFC was founded in 1993 by Art Davie, Rorion Gracie and others, with its first event, UFC 1, held in Denver, Colorado on 12 November 1993. Conceived as a one-night tournament to determine the most effective martial art, the early events had few rules and no weight classes; Brazilian jiu-jitsu practitioner Royce Gracie won three of the first four tournaments, demonstrating the importance of grappling and ground fighting.

The spectacle of the early events attracted criticism from politicians, most prominently Senator John McCain, who called the sport "human cockfighting". Cable pay-per-view providers dropped the events, and many US states banned them. In response, the UFC introduced stricter rules, weight divisions and cooperation with state athletic commissions, a process of regulation that laid the groundwork for the modern sport.

In 2001, the struggling promotion was bought for $2 million by Zuffa, LLC, a company formed by casino executives Frank and Lorenzo Fertitta, with Dana White installed as president. Under Zuffa the UFC was sanctioned in Nevada, launched the reality series The Ultimate Fighter in 2005 — whose first-season finale between Forrest Griffin and Stephan Bonnar is credited with saving the company — and grew into a mainstream global sport. In 2016 Zuffa sold the UFC to a group led by Endeavor (then WME-IMG) for about $4 billion. In 2023 the UFC was combined with WWE under the new TKO Group Holdings.

Rules and competition

UFC bouts take place in an eight-sided fenced enclosure known as the Octagon. Fights follow the Unified Rules of Mixed Martial Arts: three rounds of five minutes for regular bouts and five rounds for title fights and main events. Fighters compete in weight divisions from strawweight (115 lb) through heavyweight (265 lb), in separate men's and women's divisions. Fouls include eye-gouging, groin strikes, strikes to the back of the head, and kneeing a downed opponent in the head. Bouts can end by knockout, technical knockout, submission, judges' decision, or disqualification.

Champions and notable fighters

Notable champions and stars in UFC history include Anderson Silva, Georges St-Pierre, Jon Jones, Demetrious Johnson, Amanda Nunes, Ronda Rousey, Conor McGregor, Khabib Nurmagomedov, José Aldo, Daniel Cormier, Stipe Miocic and Islam Makhachev.

Business

The UFC produces dozens of events per year, including numbered pay-per-view cards and Fight Night events, and broadcasts in over 170 countries. Its Las Vegas UFC APEX facility hosts smaller events, and the promotion runs feeder programming such as Dana White's Contender Series.
