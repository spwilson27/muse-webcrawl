Chuck Berry

Charles Edward Anderson Berry (18 October 1926 – 18 March 2017) was an American singer, guitarist, and songwriter who was one of the pioneers of rock and roll music. With songs such as "Maybellene" (1955), "Roll Over Beethoven" (1956), "Rock and Roll Music" (1957), and "Johnny B. Goode" (1958), Berry refined elements of rhythm and blues into the distinctive sound of rock and roll, centered on electric guitar riffs, showmanship — including his famous "duckwalk" — and lyrics aimed at teenage life and consumer culture.

Early life and breakthrough

Berry was born into a middle-class African American family in St. Louis. He served a term in a reform school for armed robbery as a teenager, worked in factories and as a hairdresser, and played in local clubs. In 1955 he traveled to Chicago, met Muddy Waters, and was directed to Leonard Chess of Chess Records. His reworking of the country song "Ida Red" as "Maybellene" became a major hit, crossing over to white audiences.

Career and influence

A string of hits followed, chronicling cars, school, dancing, and rock music itself. Berry's career was interrupted by a prison sentence in the early 1960s under the Mann Act, yet his records profoundly influenced the British Invasion bands: the Beatles and the Rolling Stones both covered his songs, and his guitar intros became a template for rock guitarists. After leaner years he scored his only US number one with "My Ding-a-Ling" in 1972. He was in the first group inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1986, and "Johnny B. Goode" was included on the Voyager Golden Record as an emblem of Earth's music. Berry performed monthly in St. Louis into his eighties and died in 2017; his final album, Chuck, appeared posthumously.
