Fender Jazzmaster

The Fender Jazzmaster, introduced in 1958, was designed for jazz guitarists but found its audience in surf and alternative rock.

The Fender Jazzmaster is an electric guitar designed as a more expensive sibling to the Fender Stratocaster and first introduced at the 1958 NAMM Show. It was initially marketed to jazz guitarists, but found favour among surf rock guitarists in the early 1960s, and later among punk, new wave, and indie rock musicians.

The Jazzmaster features an offset-waist body, a 25.5-inch scale length, wide single-coil pickups with large flat coils (often mistaken for P-90s), and a unique dual-circuit electronics system: a "lead" circuit with conventional controls and a darker "rhythm" circuit engaging only the neck pickup, switchable via controls on the upper bout. Its floating tremolo system with a locking button was designed to produce smooth pitch variation.

Legacy

Although jazz guitarists largely ignored it, the Jazzmaster was played by surf acts, adopted by Elvis Costello and Tom Verlaine in the 1970s, and became a defining guitar of 1980s–90s alternative and shoegaze bands such as Sonic Youth, Dinosaur Jr., and My Bloody Valentine. Discontinued in 1980, it returned as a Japanese reissue in 1984 and has remained in production in various forms since, with signature models for J Mascis, Jim Root, and others.
