Bolt action

TypeManual firearm action
OperationBolt handle lifted, pulled back, pushed forward, locked down
Famous examplesMauser Gewehr 98; Lee–Enfield; Mosin–Nagant; Remington 700

Bolt action is a type of manual firearm action in which the weapon's bolt is operated by hand, usually via a small handle. The shooter lifts the handle to unlock the bolt, pulls it rearward to eject the spent cartridge, pushes it forward to chamber a new round, and rotates it down to lock before firing.

History

The first bolt-action rifle was produced in 1824 by Johann Nikolaus von Dreyse, whose needle gun entered Prussian service in 1841. Metallic cartridges and smokeless powder made the bolt action the dominant military rifle mechanism from the 1880s through the Second World War, with designs such as the Mauser 98, Lee–Enfield, and Mosin–Nagant arming the major armies.

Characteristics

Bolt actions are simple, strong, and accurate: the rigid lockup makes them ideal for powerful cartridges, and they remain the standard action for precision and sniper rifles, as well as for much hunting and target shooting. Their disadvantage is a slower rate of fire than semi-automatic actions.

Modern use

Although superseded in general military service by self-loading rifles, bolt actions dominate long-range precision shooting and remain widespread among hunters and sport shooters worldwide.
