Mountains of the Moon (Africa)

The Mountains of the Moon (Latin: Montes Lunae) are a legendary mountain range of East Africa, reported in Greco-Roman geography as the source of the Nile. They appear in the writings of Ptolemy, who placed two great lakes south of them fed by their snows, and they were drawn on maps of Africa for more than a thousand years while remaining unlocated by Europeans.

The legend

The story traces to reports brought by the merchant Diogenes around 100 CE and codified in Ptolemy's Geography: beyond the lakes of Ethiopia rose snow-covered mountains whose meltwater fed the Nile's sources. Medieval and Renaissance cartographers reproduced them faithfully across the heart of Africa even as the surrounding geography was revised.

Identification

The mountains are now most convincingly identified with the Rwenzori Mountains on the Uganda–Congo border — equatorial, glacier-capped peaks whose streams feed the White Nile system. The explorer Henry Morton Stanley, who in 1888 became the first European to confirm the range's snowy summits, argued for the identification. Kilimanjaro and Mount Kenya have also been proposed, but the Rwenzori combine the requisite snow, lakes and position best. In 1906 the Duke of the Abruzzi's expedition mapped the range and named most of its peaks.
