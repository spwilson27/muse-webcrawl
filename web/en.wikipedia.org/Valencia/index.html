Valencia (Valencian: València) is the capital of the autonomous community of Valencia and the third-most-populous municipality in Spain, with around 800,000 inhabitants in the city and roughly 1.6 million in its metropolitan area. Located on the Mediterranean coast at the mouth of the Turia river, it is a major port city, an industrial and cultural centre, and the historic home of paella.

History

Valencia was founded by the Romans in 138 BC as Valentia Edetanorum. It later passed through Visigothic and Muslim rule — under Islam it flourished as Balansiya, a centre of agriculture, paper and silk production — before being conquered briefly by El Cid in 1094 and definitively for the Crown of Aragon by James I in 1238. The fifteenth century was a golden age: Valencia became one of the largest cities in Europe, and its silk exchange, La Lonja de la Seda, is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Geography and landmarks

After a catastrophic flood in 1957, the Turia river was diverted south of the city and its old riverbed converted into the Turia Gardens, a long urban park. At its eastern end stands the City of Arts and Sciences, a complex of futuristic buildings by Santiago Calatrava including an opera house, science museum and the Oceanogràfic aquarium. The old town contains the cathedral — which claims to hold the Holy Chalice — the Torres de Serranos and Quart gates, and the Central Market.

Culture

Valencia's great festival is Las Fallas, held each March, in which enormous satirical sculptures (fallas) are displayed and then burned on the night of 19 March amid fireworks; it is inscribed on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list. The region is the birthplace of paella, traditionally cooked outdoors over orange-wood fires with rice, rabbit, chicken and beans. Valencia CF plays at the Mestalla stadium, and the city hosts a Formula 1–era street circuit legacy and the Valencia Open tennis traditions.
