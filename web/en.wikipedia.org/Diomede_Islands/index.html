Diomede Islands

The Diomede Islands are two rocky islands in the middle of the Bering Strait between mainland Alaska and Siberia. They consist of Big Diomede Island (Russian: остров Ратманова), belonging to Russia, and Little Diomede Island (Inupiaq: Iŋalik), belonging to the United States. The islands are separated by about 3.8 km (2.4 mi) of water.

The Diomede Islands: Little Diomede (front) and Big Diomede (behind).

Geography and the date line

The International Date Line passes between the two islands, so Big Diomede is almost a day ahead of Little Diomede—21 hours in winter—leading to the nicknames "Tomorrow Island" and "Yesterday Island". The islands were named by the Danish explorer Vitus Bering in 1728 for Saint Diomede, on whose feast day they were sighted, though Indigenous Iñupiat communities had lived there for centuries.

History and people

Under the 1867 Alaska Purchase treaty, the boundary between Russia and the United States was drawn between the islands. During the Cold War the strait became known as the "Ice Curtain", and the Inupiaq population of Big Diomede was relocated to mainland Siberia; Little Diomede retains a small village, Diomede, with a population of around one hundred. In 1987 Lynne Cox swam between the islands in a gesture of Cold War thaw.
