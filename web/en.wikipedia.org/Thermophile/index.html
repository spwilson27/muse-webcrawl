Thermophile

Thermophiles produce some of the bright colours found in hot springs such as the Grand Prismatic Spring in Yellowstone.

Growth range45–122 °C (113–252 °F)
DomainBacteria, Archaea, some fungi
HabitatsHot springs, hydrothermal vents, compost, deep subsurface

A thermophile is an organism—a type of extremophile—that thrives at relatively high temperatures, between 41 and 122 °C (106 and 252 °F). Many thermophiles are archaea, though some are bacteria and fungi. Thermophilic eubacteria are suggested to have been among the earliest bacteria.

Thermophiles are found in various geothermally heated regions of the Earth, such as hot springs like those in Yellowstone National Park and deep sea hydrothermal vents, as well as decaying plant matter, such as peat bogs and compost.

Classification

Thermophiles can be classified in various ways. One classification sorts these organisms according to their optimal growth temperatures:

  • Moderate thermophiles: optimal growth between 50 and 64 °C.
  • Extreme thermophiles: optimal growth between 65 and 79 °C.
  • Hyperthermophiles: optimal growth at 80 °C or above; some can survive at temperatures above 100 °C.

Adaptations

Thermophiles survive at high temperatures through several adaptations. Their proteins are more heat-stable than those of mesophiles, often with increased numbers of salt bridges and tighter hydrophobic packing. Their cell membranes contain more saturated fatty acids (or, in archaea, ether-linked lipids), which remain stable and less permeable at high temperature. Many thermophiles also possess the enzyme reverse gyrase, which introduces positive supercoils into DNA and increases its thermal stability.

Importance

Thermophiles are a source of heat-stable enzymes used in biotechnology. The best known is Taq polymerase, isolated from Thermus aquaticus, which made the polymerase chain reaction (PCR) practical and revolutionised molecular biology. Thermostable enzymes are also used in detergents, food processing and biofuel production. Because early Earth was hot, thermophiles have also been studied as models for early life and for possible life on other planets.
