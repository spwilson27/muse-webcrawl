Frigyes Riesz

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Frigyes Riesz (22 January 1880 – 28 February 1956) was a Hungarian mathematician who made fundamental contributions to functional analysis. Together with his brother Marcel Riesz and contemporaries such as Stefan Banach, he helped create the modern theory of function spaces and linear operators.

Career

Riesz studied in Budapest, Zürich, and Göttingen, earning his doctorate in Budapest in 1902. He taught at Kolozsvár (Cluj) before becoming professor at the University of Szeged in 1920, where, with Alfréd Haar, he founded the journal Acta Scientiarum Mathematicarum and made Szeged a centre of analysis. In 1945 he moved to Budapest.

Contributions

The Riesz representation theorem (1909) identifies the continuous linear functionals on a Hilbert space with inner products against fixed vectors, a cornerstone of functional analysis and quantum mechanics. With Ernst Fischer, he proved the Riesz–Fischer theorem on the completeness of Lp spaces, showing that the Lebesgue space L2 is a complete metric space. Riesz also developed the theory of compact operators (the Riesz–Schauder theory), introduced Riesz spaces (vector lattices), and proved results in ergodic theory, Fourier series, and subharmonic functions. His book Leçons d'analyse fonctionnelle (1952), written with Béla Szőkefalvi-Nagy, became a classic text.

Legacy

Numerous concepts bear the Riesz name, including the Riesz potential, the Riesz transform, and the Riesz lemma. The asteroid 3033 Riesz is named after him and his brother.
