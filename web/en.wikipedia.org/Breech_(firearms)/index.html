Breech (firearms)

The breech of a howitzer, showing the chamber into which the projectile and charge are loaded.

The breech of a firearm is the part of the weapon at the rear of the barrel, adjoining the chamber, through which the weapon is loaded in a breech-loading design. More broadly, the term refers to the rear portion of the barrel and its associated mechanism — the breechblock or bolt — that seals the chamber during firing and supports the cartridge against the pressure of the burning propellant.

Function

When a gun fires, the breech must withstand the full pressure of the propellant gases driving the projectile forward. The breech mechanism therefore locks securely before firing, seals the chamber (aided by the cartridge case expanding against the chamber walls, called obturation), and then opens after firing to extract the spent case and allow reloading. In artillery, interrupted-screw and sliding-wedge breechblocks are common; in small arms, rotating bolts, tilting bolts, blowback and roller-delayed systems are among the many locking methods.

History

Early firearms were loaded from the muzzle; breech-loading designs existed from the 14th century in the form of removable chambers for cannon, but reliable breech-loading became practical only in the 19th century with metallic cartridges and better machining. Rifles such as the Dreyse needle gun (adopted by Prussia in the 1840s), the Sharps, and the Snider and Martini-Henry conversions demonstrated the military advantages of breech-loading — faster reloading and the ability to load while prone — and by the 1870s–1880s breech-loading rifles and artillery had displaced muzzleloaders worldwide. Terms such as "breech birth" and the mining of a wall's "breach" share the same Germanic root meaning the rear or a break.

Related terms

The breechblock is the component that closes the breech; in many pistols and rifles this function is performed by a bolt or the rear of the slide. A breechloader is any firearm loaded through the breech, as distinct from a muzzleloader. "Breech pressure" denotes the gas pressure at the chamber during firing, a key design parameter.
