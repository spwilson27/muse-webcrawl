Snoopy


Snoopy lying on top of his doghouse

Snoopy is an anthropomorphic beagle in the comic strip Peanuts by Charles M. Schulz. He can also be found in all of the Peanuts films and television specials. Since his debut on October 4, 1950, Snoopy has become one of the most recognizable and iconic characters in the comic strip and is considered, with Charlie Brown, the main character. The original drawings of Snoopy were inspired by Spike, one of Schulz's childhood dogs.

Characteristics

Snoopy is a loyal, imaginative, and confident beagle who is prone to imagining fantasy lives. His best-known alter egos include the Flying Ace, a World War I flying ace battling the Red Baron from atop his doghouse (which he imagines as a Sopwith Camel), and "Joe Cool," a laid-back college student leaning against a wall. He is also a writer whose typewritten novels invariably begin "It was a dark and stormy night." Snoopy's thoughts are conveyed in thought balloons; he does not speak aloud.

Snoopy's best friend is Woodstock, a small yellow bird, and his owner is Charlie Brown, whom he frequently refers to (in thought) as "that round-headed kid." He has several siblings, including Spike, who lives in the desert near Needles, and Belle. Snoopy sleeps on top of his red doghouse rather than inside it, and is famous for his "happy dance" and his appetite—he is devoted to root beer and pizza, and his suppertime arrival is a highlight of his day.

History

Snoopy first appeared two days after the strip's debut, on October 4, 1950, initially as a relatively ordinary dog. Over the 1950s and 1960s he progressively took on human characteristics, walking upright, wearing sunglasses, and developing his rich fantasy life. Schulz intended the name Snoopy for a character he called Sniffy, but changed it on learning the name was used elsewhere; "Snoopy" was a name his mother had suggested the family should have given a dog.

Cultural legacy

Snoopy is among the most popular cartoon characters in the world, appearing on merchandise, balloons in the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade, and as the safety mascot of NASA, which named the Apollo 10 lunar module "Snoopy." A star bearing his name is on the Hollywood Walk of Fame, alongside that of Schulz.
