Siegfried Line campaign

The Siegfried Line campaign was a phase in the Western European campaign of the Second World War, fought from 26 August 1944 until the end of December 1944. It comprised the Allied advance from Paris to Germany's western fortifications, the Siegfried Line or Westwall.

Course of the campaign

After the breakout from Normandy, Allied armies raced across France but outran their supplies. Attempts to outflank the line — notably Operation Market Garden in September 1944 — failed, and the autumn was spent in costly fighting for the Channel ports, the Scheldt estuary, Aachen, and the Hürtgen Forest, the longest battle the U.S. Army fought in the war.

Key battles

Major operations included the capture of Aachen (the first German city to fall), the Battle of the Scheldt clearing the approaches to Antwerp, the Battle of Metz, and the grinding Battle of Hürtgen Forest. By mid-December the Allies stood on or within the Westwall along much of the frontier.

Conclusion

The campaign ended as Germany launched its Ardennes offensive, the Battle of the Bulge, in December 1944, temporarily halting the Allied advance into Germany until early 1945.
