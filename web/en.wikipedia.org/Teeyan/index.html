Teeyan

Teeyan (Punjabi: ਤੀਆਂ), also called Teej in parts of Punjab, is a monsoon festival celebrated by women in the Indian and Pakistani Punjab. It takes its name from the month of Teej (Sawan) in the traditional calendar, when the rains arrive.

Celebration

During Teeyan, married and unmarried women gather, traditionally under trees in village groves, to swing on swings (peengh) hung from branches, sing folk songs, dance the gidha, and share festive foods. The festival celebrates marital happiness, the wellbeing of husbands and children, and female kinship and friendship; newly married women traditionally return to their parents' homes for the celebrations. Fairs and organised Teeyan gatherings are now also held in cities and in the Punjabi diaspora in Canada, the United Kingdom and elsewhere.

Songs and customs

The folk songs sung at Teeyan are a rich record of rural women's lives, treating themes of love, separation, in-laws and the longing for the parental home. Traditional dress, henna, glass bangles and special dishes such as kheer and malpura accompany the festivities.
