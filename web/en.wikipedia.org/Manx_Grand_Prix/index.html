Manx Grand Prix

Venue: Snaefell Mountain Course, Isle of Man
First held: 1923 (as the Manx Amateur Road Races)
Usually held: Late August
Course length: 37.73 miles (60.72 km)

The Manx Grand Prix is a motorcycle road race held annually on the Isle of Man, traditionally in late August, over the Snaefell Mountain Course — the same 37.73-mile public-road circuit used by the Isle of Man TT races.

History

The event began in 1923 as the Manx Amateur Road Races, created to give amateur competitors an equivalent of the professional TT. It took the name Manx Grand Prix in 1930. Historically it served as a proving ground for riders who later succeeded at the TT, and classes included Newcomers, Junior, Senior, Lightweight and Classic races for historic machinery.

Modern format

In 2013 the meeting was restructured as the Isle of Man Festival of Motorcycling: the classic races were rebranded as the Classic TT while the Manx Grand Prix continued for modern machinery and newcomers. A further review shortened the programme from 2022, with the festival typically running over the August bank holiday period with fewer race days.

The course

Riders start at the TT Grandstand in Douglas and lap through landmarks including Ballacraine, Kirk Michael, Ramsey Hairpin, the Mountain section and Governor's Bridge, on closed public roads lined with walls, houses and hedges.
