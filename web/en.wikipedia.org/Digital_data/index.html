Digital data

Digital data, in information theory and information systems, is information represented as a string of discrete symbols, each of which can take on one of only a finite number of values from some alphabet, such as letters or digits. The most common example is a binary digit representation using bits (0 and 1).

Characteristics

Digital data contrasts with analog data, which is represented by a continuous physical quantity. Because digital values are discrete, they can be copied, transmitted and stored with very low error rates, and error-correcting codes can detect and repair corruption.

Representation

Text, numbers, images, audio and video can all be encoded digitally. Characters are represented by codes such as ASCII and Unicode; images by arrays of pixels; sound by sampled waveforms. The unit of digital information is the bit, grouped into bytes of eight bits.

Storage and transmission

Digital data is stored on media including magnetic disks, optical discs, flash memory and solid-state drives, and transmitted over networks using protocols that packetise and route the data between systems.
