Lake Zurich (German: Zürichsee) is a lake in Switzerland, extending southeast of the city of Zurich. It lies across the cantons of Zurich, Schwyz, and St. Gallen, and is fed chiefly by the Linth River, which has been diverted through Lake Walen and the Linth Canal since the early nineteenth century.

Geography

The lake is long and narrow, about 40 kilometres in length, and is divided near its middle by the Hurden peninsula and the Seedamm causeway between Rapperswil and Pfäffikon; the smaller upper lake beyond is called the Obersee. The island of Ufenau, the largest island in a Swiss lake, lies in the lower basin. The lake drains at its northwestern end into the Limmat, which flows through Zurich.

Use

Passenger boats of the Zürichsee-Schifffahrtsgesellschaft have plied the lake since the nineteenth century, including historic paddle steamers. The shores are densely settled with prosperous towns and villages, and lakeside "Gold Coast" and "Silver Coast" districts are among the most expensive residential areas in Switzerland. In exceptionally cold winters, such as 1962–63, the lake has frozen completely, an event known locally as Seegfrörni.
