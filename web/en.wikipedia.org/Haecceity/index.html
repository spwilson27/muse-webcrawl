Haecceity

Haecceity (from the Latin haecceitas, which translates as "thisness") is a term from medieval scholastic philosophy, first coined by followers of Duns Scotus to denote a concept of individuation.

Concept

Haecceity is a non-qualitative property of a substance that accounts for its being this particular individual — what makes Socrates Socrates, beyond all the qualities he shares with others. Scotus proposed it as the principle of individuation (principium individuationis) that contracts a common nature to a singular instance.

Later use

The term was revived by Charles Sanders Peirce and used by Gilles Deleuze and Félix Guattari, who broadened "haecceity" to modes of individuation not tied to persons — a season, a wind, an hour of the day.
