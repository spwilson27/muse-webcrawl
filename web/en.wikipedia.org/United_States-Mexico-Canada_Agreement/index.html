United States–Mexico–Canada Agreement

The United States–Mexico–Canada Agreement (USMCA; known as CUSMA in Canada and T-MEC in Mexico) is a free trade agreement between the United States, Mexico, and Canada. It replaced the North American Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA) and entered into force on 1 July 2020.

Negotiation and ratification

The agreement resulted from the renegotiation of NAFTA demanded by the administration of US President Donald Trump. A deal was announced on 30 September 2018 and signed on 30 November 2018; a modified version, adjusting provisions on labour, environment, and pharmaceuticals, was signed in December 2019 and ratified by all three countries in early 2020.

Provisions

The USMCA retains largely duty-free trade among the three countries while introducing stricter automotive rules of origin (75% North American content and a labour-value requirement tied to higher wages), increased US access to Canada's dairy market, a chapter on digital trade, strengthened labour and environmental obligations, currency provisions, and a 16-year term with periodic review — the first review scheduled for 2026.
