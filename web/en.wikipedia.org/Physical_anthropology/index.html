Physical anthropology

Physical anthropology, now usually called biological anthropology, is the branch of anthropology studying human evolution, biological variation, primates and the fossil record of hominins.

Biological anthropology
Topics: Palaeoanthropology, primatology, human variation, forensics

Subfields

Palaeoanthropology studies fossil hominins (Australopithecus, Homo erectus, Neanderthals); primatology studies non-human primates; human biology and bioarchaeology examine adaptation, growth, disease and skeletal remains; forensic anthropology identifies remains for legal purposes.

History

Early work was entangled with racial typology and craniometry. Franz Boas showed head form changes with environment, and post-war researchers such as Sherwood Washburn refocused the field on evolution and adaptation, integrating genetics.
