Neva

The Neva is a river in northwestern Russia flowing from Lake Ladoga through the Leningrad Oblast to the Neva Bay of the Gulf of Finland. Despite its modest length of 74 km, it is the third-largest river in Europe by average discharge, after the Volga and the Danube.

Geography

The Neva flows westwards from Lake Ladoga, passing Shlisselburg, and divides into a delta at Saint Petersburg, which Peter the Great founded at its mouth in 1703. Its many distributaries, including the Bolshaya Neva and the Fontanka, form the canal-laced centre of the city.

History

The river was a vital trade route between the Baltic and the Volga basin. The Battle of the Neva in 1240, in which Alexander Nevsky defeated a Swedish expedition, took place near its banks. The Neva is notorious for flooding in Saint Petersburg, most disastrously in 1824; a protective dam complex across the Neva Bay was completed in 2011.
