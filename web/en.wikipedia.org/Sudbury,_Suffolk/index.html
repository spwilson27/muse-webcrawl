Sudbury, Suffolk

Sudbury is a market town in the county of Suffolk, England, on the River Stour near the Essex border, about 60 miles (97 km) north-east of London. It has a population of roughly 13,000. Sudbury is best known as the birthplace of the artist Thomas Gainsborough, and the surrounding Stour Valley landscape is closely associated with the painter John Constable.

History

Sudbury's history dates to the Anglo-Saxon period, and it is recorded in the Domesday Book of 1086. In the medieval period the town prospered from the wool and cloth trade; Flemish weavers settled here in the 14th century. Silk weaving later became important, and the coming of the railway branch line from Marks Tey in 1849 — the Gainsborough line — linked the town to the main network.

Gainsborough and Constable country

Gainsborough's House, the artist's birthplace in Gainsborough Street, is now a museum and national centre for Thomas Gainsborough. Constable painted extensively in the Stour valley around Sudbury, Flatford, and Dedham, an area marketed as "Constable Country". The town's water meadows beside the Stour are among the oldest continuously grazed pasture in England.

Landmarks

Notable buildings include the churches of St Gregory, St Peter (now a cultural venue), and All Saints; the Corn Exchange (now the library); and the Mill Hotel on the site of a former water mill. The town is also the birthplace of Simon of Sudbury, Archbishop of Canterbury, beheaded in the Peasants' Revolt of 1381.
