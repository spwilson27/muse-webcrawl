Pneumatics

Pneumatics (from Greek πνεῦμα pneuma, "wind, breath") is a branch of engineering that makes use of gas or pressurized air. Pneumatic systems used in industry are commonly powered by compressed air or compressed inert gases. A centrally located, electrically powered compressor powers cylinders, air motors, and other pneumatic devices.

Comparison with hydraulics

Pneumatics uses compressible gas, while hydraulics uses relatively incompressible liquid such as oil. Pneumatic systems are typically lower-force, cleaner (leaks do not contaminate in the same way), and faster-responding, but less precise and less stiff than hydraulic systems because gas compresses under load. Hydraulics is preferred for very high forces, such as presses and excavators.

Components

A typical pneumatic circuit includes a compressor, receiver tank, filters, regulators and lubricators (FRL units), control valves (directional, flow, and pressure), actuators (linear cylinders and rotary air motors), and connecting tubing. Logic control may be wholly pneumatic or electronic via solenoid valves and PLCs.

Applications

Pneumatics is used in factory automation, dentist's drills, pneumatic tube delivery systems, air brakes on trains and trucks, pipe organs, and air tools.

History

The study of pneumatics in antiquity includes the inventions of Ctesibius and Hero of Alexandria; the word was used in the 17th century for the science of air, and Otto von Guericke's air pump and Magdeburg hemispheres demonstrated atmospheric pressure dramatically.
