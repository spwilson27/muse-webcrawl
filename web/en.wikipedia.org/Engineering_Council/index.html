The Engineering Council (formerly the Engineering Council UK) is the United Kingdom's regulatory body for the engineering profession. Holding a Royal Charter, it sets and maintains the standards of professional competence and conduct for engineers and technicians in the UK, and licenses professional engineering institutions to assess candidates for registration.

Registration

The Council maintains the national register of engineers and technicians. Its principal titles are Chartered Engineer (CEng), Incorporated Engineer (IEng), Engineering Technician (EngTech) and ICT Technician (ICTTech). Registrants must demonstrate competence and commitment against the UK Standard for Professional Engineering Competence (UK-SPEC), commit to a code of professional conduct, and maintain continuing professional development. Registration is voluntary in most branches of UK engineering but carries international recognition through accords such as the Washington Accord.

History

The Council was established by Royal Charter in 1981 following recommendations of the Finniston Report (1980) on the engineering profession. It succeeded earlier coordinating bodies such as the Council of Engineering Institutions, and in 2002 the engineering profession's representative functions passed to the Engineering and Technology Board (now EngineeringUK), leaving the Council as a purely regulatory body.
