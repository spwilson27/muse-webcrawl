Bird of prey

Birds of prey, also known as raptors, are hypercarnivorous birds that actively hunt and feed on other vertebrates (mainly mammals, reptiles and smaller birds). They are characterised by keen vision for detecting prey at a distance, strong feet with sharp talons for grasping or killing, and powerful hooked beaks for tearing flesh.

Groups

The traditional raptor groups include the eagles, hawks, kites, harriers, buzzards and Old World vultures (Accipitridae); the osprey; the secretarybird; the falcons and caracaras (Falconidae); and the owls (Strigiformes), which are mostly nocturnal hunters. New World vultures are also commonly included. Modern phylogenetics places falcons closer to parrots and passerines than to hawks and eagles.

Behaviour

Many raptors are apex predators in their ecosystems; some, like the peregrine falcon — capable of exceeding 320 km/h in a stoop — are famed for speed, while vultures specialise in scavenging carrion. Raptors have long been used by humans in falconry and serve as sensitive indicators of environmental health, famously declining under the pesticide DDT before recovering after its ban.
