Avalokiteśvara is a bodhisattva in Mahayana Buddhism who embodies the compassion of all Buddhas. The name is commonly interpreted as "the Lord who looks down (in compassion)". Avalokiteśvara is among the most widely venerated bodhisattvas in East and Central Asia.

Forms across Asia

In China, Avalokiteśvara became Guanyin, usually depicted as female, a transformation completed by the Song dynasty partly through the legend of Princess Miaoshan. In Japan the bodhisattva is Kannon, in Korea Gwaneum, in Vietnam Quan Âm, and in Tibet Chenrezig, regarded as the patron deity of Tibet; the Dalai Lamas are considered emanations of Chenrezig. Regional forms include the thousand-armed, eleven-headed manifestation symbolising all-encompassing compassion.

Scriptures and mantra

The Lotus Sutra's twenty-fifth chapter, often circulated independently as the Guanyin Sutra, promises that Avalokiteśvara rescues devotees who call the bodhisattva's name from fire, flood, demons and other perils. The Heart Sutra is framed as Avalokiteśvara's teaching on emptiness. The six-syllable mantra oṃ maṇi padme hūṃ is especially associated with this bodhisattva and is ubiquitous in Tibetan Buddhism.

Iconography

Common attributes include a vase of nectar, a willow branch, a lotus, and an image of Amitābha Buddha in the crown. Famous pilgrimage sites include Mount Putuo in China, dedicated to Guanyin.
