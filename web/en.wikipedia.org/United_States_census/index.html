The United States census is a constitutionally mandated decennial count of the population of the United States, conducted every ten years since 1790 by the U.S. Census Bureau. Its primary constitutional purpose is the apportionment of seats in the House of Representatives among the states; the data also guide the distribution of hundreds of billions of dollars in federal funding and provide the demographic basis for redistricting and research.

Constitutional basis

Article I, Section 2 of the Constitution requires an "actual Enumeration" of the population every ten years. The original text counted enslaved persons as three-fifths of free persons for apportionment purposes — the Three-fifths Compromise — a provision superseded by the Fourteenth Amendment, which bases apportionment on the whole number of persons in each state.

History

The first census, taken in 1790 under Secretary of State Thomas Jefferson, counted 3,929,214 people. Assistant U.S. marshals conducted the count on horseback for over a century. The 1880 census took eight years to tabulate, prompting Herman Hollerith to develop a punched-card tabulating machine for the 1890 census — the foundation of the company that became IBM. A permanent Census Bureau was created in 1902/1903 within the Department of Commerce.

Confidentiality of individual responses was progressively strengthened; Title 13 prohibits disclosure of identifiable information for 72 years, after which schedules are released by the National Archives (the 1950 census schedules were released in 2022).

The 2020 census

The 24th census, with Census Day on 1 April 2020, was the first to invite responses primarily by internet and telephone, alongside paper forms and door-to-door enumeration. It counted 331,449,281 residents. Apportionment results shifted House seats: Texas gained two seats; Colorado, Florida, Montana, North Carolina, and Oregon gained one each; California, Illinois, Michigan, New York, Ohio, Pennsylvania, and West Virginia each lost one. The count was conducted amid the COVID-19 pandemic and litigation over a proposed citizenship question, which the Supreme Court blocked in 2019 on administrative-law grounds.

Between censuses

The Census Bureau publishes annual population estimates and conducts the American Community Survey, which replaced the census "long form" after 2000, providing detailed social and economic data on a rolling basis.
