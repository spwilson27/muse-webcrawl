Turkish (Turkish: Türkçe) is the most widely spoken of the Turkic languages, with around 80 million native speakers. It is the official language of Turkey and one of the official languages of Cyprus. The standard language is based on the dialect of Istanbul.

Characteristics

Turkish is an agglutinative language: grammatical relations are expressed by long chains of suffixes added to a stem, so a single word such as evlerinizden ("from your houses") contains several morphemes. It exhibits vowel harmony, has subject–object–verb word order, no grammatical gender, and a phonemic inventory of eight vowels. Its alphabet, adopted in 1928 as part of Atatürk's reforms in place of the Ottoman Turkish Arabic-based script, has 29 letters including ç, ş, ğ, ı, i, ö and ü, and is almost perfectly phonemic.

History

Ottoman Turkish, the administrative language of the Ottoman Empire, contained extensive Arabic and Persian vocabulary and was largely unintelligible to the rural population. The republican language reform, guided by the Turkish Language Association (founded 1932), replaced many loanwords with coinages from Turkic roots, dramatically reshaping the written language within a generation.

Related languages

Turkish is closely related to Azerbaijani and Gagauz, and more distantly to Turkmen, Uzbek, Kazakh and other Turkic languages of Central Asia; mutual intelligibility is high with Azerbaijani.
