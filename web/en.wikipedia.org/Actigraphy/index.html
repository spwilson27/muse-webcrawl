Actigraphy

Actigraphy is a non-invasive method of monitoring human rest and activity cycles. A small actigraph unit, also called an actimetry sensor, is worn for a week or more to measure gross motor activity, usually on the wrist of the non-dominant arm. The unit continually records movement, and the data are later analysed to estimate sleep and wake patterns.

Uses

Actigraphy is used in sleep medicine and research to estimate parameters such as sleep onset, total sleep time, sleep efficiency, and wake after sleep onset over many days in a person's normal environment. It is particularly useful for studying circadian rhythm sleep disorders, insomnia, and excessive sleepiness, and for monitoring treatment response. It complements polysomnography, the laboratory gold standard, which measures brain activity directly but is more expensive and intrusive.

Limitations

Because actigraphy infers sleep from movement, it can misclassify quiet wakefulness as sleep and is less accurate in populations with movement disorders. Consumer fitness trackers and smartwatches use similar accelerometers to estimate sleep, though their algorithms vary in validation.
