The National Association for the Advancement of Colored People (NAACP) is a civil rights organization in the United States, founded in 1909. One of the oldest and most influential civil rights groups in the country, its mission is to ensure the political, educational, social, and economic equality of rights of all persons and to eliminate race-based discrimination. For much of the twentieth century its strategy centred on impact litigation against segregation and disenfranchisement, culminating in the Supreme Court's school-desegregation decision in Brown v. Board of Education (1954).

Founding

The NAACP was founded by an interracial group of activists — including the scholar W. E. B. Du Bois, journalist Ida B. Wells-Barnett, Archibald Grimké, and the white reformers Mary White Ovington, Oswald Garrison Villard, and William English Walling — in response to the 1908 race riot in Springfield, Illinois, and the continuing lynching and disenfranchisement of Black Americans. The founding call was issued on Abraham Lincoln's birthday in 1909, and the organization drew partly on Du Bois's Niagara Movement of 1905. Du Bois edited the NAACP's magazine, The Crisis, which became a leading voice of the Harlem Renaissance.

Legal campaigns

Under legal director Charles Hamilton Houston and his protégé Thurgood Marshall, the NAACP Legal Defense Fund mounted a decades-long litigation strategy against Jim Crow: Guinn v. United States (1915) struck down grandfather clauses in voting, anti-lynching campaigns publicised racial terror, and a series of graduate-education cases preceded Brown, in which Marshall argued that segregated public schools were inherently unequal. The organization lobbied for the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the Voting Rights Act of 1965 and has continued litigation and advocacy on voting rights, criminal justice, education, housing, health, and economic opportunity, operating through state conferences and local branches across the country.

Programs and honours

The NAACP presents the Image Awards for achievement in entertainment and the Spingarn Medal, awarded since 1915 for outstanding achievement by an African American. Its youth and college divisions and the ACT-SO academic competition ("Afro-Academic, Cultural, Technological and Scientific Olympics") support young people. In 2023 the association issued a travel advisory for Florida over state policies it characterised as hostile to African Americans and other minorities, drawing national attention.
