Officer (armed forces)

An officer is a member of an armed force or uniformed service who holds a position of responsibility and command authority derived from a commission granted by the head of state. In its broadest sense the term has come to be used for anyone who holds any form of authority, but in its military sense it denotes the leadership cadre of an armed force.

Commissioned officers

Commissioned officers are the managers, leaders, and decision-makers of a military organization. They are trained at military academies or officer candidate schools and hold ranks from lieutenant (or ensign) through field grades to general officers. Their authority flows from a commission issued in the name of the sovereign or president.

Non-commissioned and warrant officers

Non-commissioned officers (NCOs) are enlisted personnel promoted into supervisory ranks such as sergeant and corporal; they provide the experienced technical and disciplinary backbone of units. Warrant officers, used in some forces, are specialists appointed by warrant rather than commission.

History

The modern officer corps developed in early modern Europe as standing armies replaced feudal levies; commanders from the armies of Octavian to the forces of Oda Nobunaga mark stages in the professionalization of military leadership. For the general concept, see officer.
