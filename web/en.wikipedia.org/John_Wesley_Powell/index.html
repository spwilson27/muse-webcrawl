John Wesley Powell (March 24, 1834 – September 23, 1902) was an American geologist, explorer of the American West and ethnologist. He led the first documented expedition down the Green and Colorado rivers through the Grand Canyon in 1869, despite having lost his right arm in the Civil War.

Exploration

Powell served as a Union artillery officer and lost his forearm at the Battle of Shiloh. In 1869 he led ten men in wooden boats from Green River, Wyoming, through the largely unmapped canyons of the Colorado; a second expedition in 1871–72 mapped the region systematically. His Exploration of the Colorado River of the West (1875) became a classic.

Scientific career

Powell founded and directed the Bureau of Ethnology at the Smithsonian Institution and in 1881 became the second director of the U.S. Geological Survey. His Report on the Lands of the Arid Region (1878) warned that western settlement must adapt to scarce water — advice largely ignored at the time and since recognized as prescient.

Legacy

Lake Powell, the reservoir behind Glen Canyon Dam, is named for him, as are numerous peaks and institutions in the American West.
