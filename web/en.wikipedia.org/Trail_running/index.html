Trail running

Trail running is a sport-activity that combines running and hiking, done on unpaved surfaces such as dirt trails, forest paths, and mountain routes, rather than on roads or tracks. It is similar to mountain running and fell running, and often involves significant ascents and descents.

Events

Races range from short cross-country-style events to ultramarathons of 100 miles or more, such as the Western States Endurance Run and the Ultra-Trail du Mont-Blanc. Runners carry water, food, and safety gear in hydration packs, and use shoes with aggressive tread and rock protection.

Governing bodies

The International Trail Running Association (ITRA), founded in 2013, works with World Athletics to define and promote the discipline, which has grown rapidly in participation since the early 2000s.
