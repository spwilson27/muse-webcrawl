MI5

Thames House, MI5 headquarters since 1994

Legal name: Security Service
Formed: 1909
Headquarters: Thames House, London
Director General: Ken McCallum (since 2020)
Staff: ~5,000

MI5, officially the Security Service, is the United Kingdom's domestic counter-intelligence and security agency. It is responsible for protecting the UK, its citizens, and its interests from threats to national security, including terrorism, espionage, and proliferation of weapons of mass destruction. MI5 operates under the authority of the Home Secretary and is distinct from MI6 (the Secret Intelligence Service), which collects foreign intelligence, and GCHQ, responsible for signals intelligence.

History

The service began in 1909 as the Home Section of the Secret Service Bureau, founded by Vernon Kell in response to fears of German espionage. During the Second World War it ran the Double Cross System, turning German agents in Britain into double agents who fed deception material to Berlin. In the Cold War it countered Soviet espionage, including the Cambridge Five spy ring. Since the 1990s, and especially after the 2005 London bombings, counter-terrorism has dominated its work; it took the lead on Irish republican terrorism in Great Britain in 1992 and leads domestic investigations of Islamist and right-wing extremist plots.

Organisation and oversight

MI5's functions were placed on a statutory basis by the Security Service Act 1989. It has no police powers of arrest; its officers work with police, who make arrests. It is overseen by the Intelligence and Security Committee of Parliament and judicial commissioners under the Investigatory Powers Act. Its headquarters are at Thames House in London, with a significant presence in Northern Ireland.
