The Hertzsprung–Russell diagram (abbreviated H–R diagram) is a scatter plot of stars showing the relationship between their absolute magnitudes or luminosities and their stellar classifications or effective temperatures. Created independently by astronomers Ejnar Hertzsprung and Henry Norris Russell in the early 1910s, it is one of the most important tools in the study of stellar evolution.

Structure

Most stars lie along a prominent diagonal band, the main sequence, running from hot, luminous stars at upper left to cool, faint stars at lower right; the Sun sits near the middle of this band. Above the main sequence lie the giants and supergiants—large, luminous, evolved stars—while below and to the left lie the white dwarfs, the hot but tiny remnants of low- and medium-mass stars.

Uses

Because a star's position on the diagram changes predictably as it ages, H–R diagrams of star clusters reveal the cluster's age from the point where its stars begin to leave the main sequence. The diagram also underpins the classification of stars by spectral type (O, B, A, F, G, K, M) and luminosity class.
