Black Square (also known as The Black Square or Malevich's Black Square) is an iconic painting by Russian artist Kazimir Malevich, first exhibited in 1915. It consists of a black square painted on a white background, and is regarded as a landmark of abstract art and the founding work of Malevich's Suprematist movement.

History

The work was first shown at the Last Futurist Exhibition of Paintings 0,10 in Petrograd in December 1915, where it was hung high across a corner of the room — the position traditionally reserved for a Russian Orthodox icon — making a provocative statement about the new art. Malevich painted several later versions of the square between the late 1910s and early 1930s.

Interpretation

Malevich described the square as "the face of the new art" and a form of pure feeling, freed from representation of the visible world. He wrote that in transforming himself "in the zero of form," he had reached the "zero point" of painting. The work is widely considered one of the most influential paintings of the twentieth century; X-ray analysis in 2015 revealed earlier compositions and a handwritten inscription beneath the black surface.
