Groundnut stew

Groundnut stew, also called peanut stew, is a stew made with groundnuts (peanuts) that is common in West African cuisine. Variants include maafe (Wolof: mafé), widely eaten in Mali, Senegal, Guinea and the Gambia, and Ghanaian nkatenkwan.

Ingredients and preparation

The base of the stew is peanut paste or ground roasted peanuts combined with tomatoes, onions, chillies and stock. Meat such as chicken, beef or lamb is commonly added, as are vegetables including sweet potato, okra, carrots and cabbage. The stew is simmered until the oil from the peanuts rises to the surface and the sauce thickens.

Serving

Groundnut stew is typically served over rice or with starchy staples such as fufu. In Ghana, groundnut soup (nkatenkwan) is a Sunday staple served with fufu or rice balls. Versions of the dish travelled with the African diaspora and influenced peanut-based stews in other cuisines.

History

Although peanuts themselves were introduced to Africa from South America by Portuguese traders in the sixteenth century, West African cooks incorporated them into an older tradition of seed-based sauces, producing a dish that is now regarded as a national dish in several countries, including Mali.
