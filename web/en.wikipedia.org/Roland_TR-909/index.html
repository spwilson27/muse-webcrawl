The Roland TR-909 Rhythm Composer is a drum machine introduced by the Roland Corporation in 1983. A hybrid design, it combines analogue synthesis for its kick drum, snare, toms, rimshot, clap and other voices with short digital samples for the hi-hats and cymbals. It was among the first Roland instruments equipped with MIDI. Though it sold modestly on release, the 909 became — together with its sibling the TR-808 — a foundational instrument of house, techno, and rave music, and its punchy kick drum remains the standard four-on-the-floor pulse of dance music.

Development

The 909 succeeded the all-analogue TR-808, with Tadao Kikumoto again leading design. Roland sought more realistic cymbal sounds than analogue circuits could produce and turned to 6-bit samples for the hats, crash, and ride, while retaining analogue circuits for the drums so users could shape them with tuning and decay controls. The sequencer expanded on the 808's, adding shuffle (swing) and flam, and MIDI allowed the machine to synchronise with the emerging generation of synthesisers and computers. Only about 10,000 units were built before production ended in 1985, partly because musicians of the day preferred fully sampled machines such as the LinnDrum.

Adoption by dance music

Like the 808, the discontinued 909 became cheap second-hand equipment and was taken up in the mid-1980s by producers in Chicago and Detroit. Frankie Knuckles and the Chicago house scene, and Detroit techno pioneers Juan Atkins, Derrick May, and Jeff Mills, made its driving kick, crisp hats, and ride cymbal the rhythmic signature of their records. The 909's sound then spread through European rave, hardcore, trance, and mainstream dance-pop in the 1990s; Daft Punk and many others built signature tracks around it. Unlike the softer, booming 808 kick, the 909 kick is hard and punchy, and the two machines are often described as the twin pillars of electronic dance music.

Reissues

Original 909s command high prices on the vintage market. Roland revisited the design in the Aira TR-8 and TR-8S (which model its sounds digitally), the Boutique TR-09 module (2016), and software versions in Roland Cloud. September 9 ("909 Day") has been used by Roland as a marketing date for product launches.
