Governor general

This article covers the office as styled "governor general" without a hyphen, the form used in several official styles and translations. For the hyphenated form, see Governor-general.

Governor general is a title and office of a representative of a head of state, used in a number of present and former states. Written without a hyphen, the form "Governor General" (often capitalised) is the official style in several countries—including Canada, where the officeholder styles the title in both official languages—and appears in English renderings of equivalent offices in other states, such as the governor general of Korea under Japanese rule and the governors general of Taiwan.

Usage in Canada

In Canada, the Governor General is the federal viceregal representative of the monarch, appointed by the sovereign on the advice of the Canadian prime minister for a term of about five years. The office was created at Confederation in 1867; the first holder was Lord Monck. The Governor General summons Parliament, grants royal assent, reads the Speech from the Throne, and serves as commander-in-chief of the Canadian Armed Forces. Since 1952 the office has alternated between anglophone and francophone Canadians, beginning with Vincent Massey, the first Canadian-born holder.

Other usages

The unhyphenated style also appears in historical contexts: the Governor General of India (the title borne by the viceroy until 1947 and by the head of state of the Dominion of India until 1950), the Governor-General of the Philippines under American rule, and translations of offices in the Russian Empire and other states, where a governor general administered a large territorial unit (a governorate-general) on behalf of the sovereign.

Functions

Whether hyphenated or not, the office's functions are broadly comparable: representing the head of state, exercising executive authority on ministerial advice, and performing ceremonial duties such as presenting honours and credentials. The distinction between "governor-general" and "governor general" is one of orthography and local official style rather than of substance.
