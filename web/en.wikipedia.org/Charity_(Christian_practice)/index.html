In Christian theology, charity (Latin caritas) is considered the greatest of the three theological virtues, alongside faith and hope. In the New Testament, the Greek word agape — denoting selfless, sacrificial love — was translated as caritas in the Latin Vulgate and as "charity" in the King James Version. Paul's hymn to love in 1 Corinthians 13 concludes that "the greatest of these is charity."

Theological understanding

Thomas Aquinas described charity as the friendship of human beings with God, and as the form of all the virtues. Charity expresses itself in love of God above all things and love of neighbour as oneself, traditionally practised through the corporal and spiritual works of mercy, including feeding the hungry, sheltering the homeless, and visiting the sick and imprisoned.

Practice

Almsgiving has been a central Christian duty since the early church. Religious orders, hospitals, confraternities, and later charitable organisations institutionalised the practice, and it remains foundational to Christian social teaching and to many modern relief agencies founded by churches.
