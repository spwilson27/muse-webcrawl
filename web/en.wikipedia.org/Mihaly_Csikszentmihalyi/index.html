Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi

Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi
Born: 29 September 1934, Fiume, Kingdom of Italy
Died: 20 October 2021 (aged 87), Claremont, California, U.S.
Known for: Flow theory; positive psychology

Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi (29 September 1934 – 20 October 2021) was a Hungarian-American psychologist best known as the architect of the concept of flow, the highly focused mental state of complete absorption in an activity, and as a co-founder of the field of positive psychology.

Life and career

Born in Fiume (now Rijeka, Croatia) to a Hungarian diplomatic family, Csikszentmihalyi witnessed the devastation of the Second World War as a child — an experience he later said turned his attention to the question of how people find meaning and happiness amid disorder. He emigrated to the United States at 22, studied psychology at the University of Chicago (PhD 1965), taught at Lake Forest College, and returned to Chicago as a professor before moving in 1999 to Claremont Graduate University, where he co-founded the first doctoral programme in positive psychology.

Flow

In research begun in the 1970s, interviewing artists, chess players, rock climbers and surgeons, Csikszentmihalyi described "optimal experiences" in which people are so absorbed that self-consciousness and the sense of time disappear. In his 1990 bestseller Flow: The Psychology of Optimal Experience he systematised the conditions: a balance of challenge and skill, clear goals, immediate feedback, concentration, a sense of control, and the merging of action and awareness. His Experience Sampling Method, using pagers to beep participants at random moments, became a standard research tool.

Positive psychology

With Martin Seligman, Csikszentmihalyi helped launch positive psychology in the late 1990s, redirecting the discipline toward strengths, creativity and flourishing rather than only pathology. His other books include Creativity: Flow and the Psychology of Discovery and Invention (1996) and Finding Flow (1997).
