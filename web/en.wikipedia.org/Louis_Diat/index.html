Louis Diat

Louis Felix Diat (May 5, 1885 – August 29, 1957) was a French-American chef, best known as the inventor of vichyssoise, the chilled leek and potato soup. Born in Montmarault, France, he trained in Paris and at the Ritz hotels in Paris and London under the Escoffier tradition.

Diat became chef at the Ritz-Carlton Hotel in New York from its opening in 1910, remaining for over four decades and becoming one of America's most respected chefs. He created vichyssoise in 1917, inspired by the leek and potato soup of his childhood, serving it cold with cream and chives. He also wrote influential cookbooks and a long-running column in Gourmet magazine, helping introduce French culinary technique to American home cooks.
