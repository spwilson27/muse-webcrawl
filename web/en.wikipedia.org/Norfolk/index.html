Norfolk

Norfolk is a ceremonial county in the East of England and East Anglia. It borders Lincolnshire and The Wash to the north-west, the North Sea to the north and east, Cambridgeshire to the west, and Suffolk to the south. The largest settlement is the city of Norwich.

The Norfolk Broads, a network of navigable rivers and lakes

Geography

Norfolk is largely rural and low-lying, known for the Norfolk Broads, a network of rivers and broads (lakes) in the east of the county that is a popular boating destination and protected as a national park in status. The north coast features sandy beaches, salt marshes, and resorts such as Cromer, Sheringham, and Hunstanton. The Brecks and the Fens occupy parts of the west.

History

The county was part of the territory of the Iceni tribe, whose queen Boudica led a revolt against Roman rule in AD 60-61. In Anglo-Saxon times Norfolk formed part of the kingdom of East Anglia; its name derives from the "north folk" of that kingdom (the "south folk" gave Suffolk its name). Norwich became one of England's largest cities in the medieval period, enriched by the wool trade.

Economy

Agriculture and food production are major employers; Norfolk is a leading producer of barley, sugar beet, and turkeys. Tourism, offshore energy (including North Sea gas and wind power from Great Yarmouth and Lowestoft), and the research institutes of the Norwich Research Park are also significant.
