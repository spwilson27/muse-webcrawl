Colour space

A colour space (color space) is a specific organization of colors. In combination with an associated mapping function and a reference white, it allows reproducible representations of colour in both analogue and digital systems.

Examples

sRGB is the standard space for the web and most displays. Adobe RGB and ProPhoto RGB offer wider gamuts for photography and printing. CIE 1931 XYZ is the foundational device-independent space derived from human vision experiments; CIELAB and CIELUV are perceptually more uniform derivatives.

Gamut and conversion

The range of colours a space or device can represent is its gamut. Colours outside a destination gamut must be mapped using rendering intents defined by ICC profiles. Video uses spaces such as Rec. 709 and Rec. 2020.

History

Early systems by Munsell and Ostwald organised colour by perception; the CIE system of 1931 placed colour science on a quantitative, standard-observer basis.
