iPLEDGE


Isotretinoin packaging with a warning against use during pregnancy.

iPLEDGE is a mandatory risk evaluation and mitigation strategy (REMS) program in the United States, managed by the manufacturers of isotretinoin under the direction of the Food and Drug Administration (FDA). Its purpose is to prevent pregnancies in patients taking isotretinoin and to prevent pregnant patients from taking the drug, because isotretinoin is a known teratogen that can cause severe birth defects — a condition known as fetal retinoid syndrome / isotretinoin embryopathy.

Requirements

The program, which replaced the earlier SMART system, has been in effect since 1 March 2006. It requires registration of all parties involved in the use of isotretinoin (brand names have included Accutane, Absorica, Claravis, Amnesteem and others):

  • Prescribers must register and document counseling and negative pregnancy tests.
  • Patients who can become pregnant must commit to two forms of contraception (or abstinence), take monthly pregnancy tests, and complete monthly comprehension questions in the iPLEDGE system before each prescription can be filled.
  • Pharmacies must be registered and may dispense only with system authorization, within a limited window after the pregnancy test.

Criticism

iPLEDGE has been criticized as burdensome for patients, prescribers and pharmacists, with system errors and strict dispensing windows causing interruptions in treatment. Critics also note that despite the program, pregnancies in patients taking isotretinoin have continued to occur. In December 2021 the program moved to a new platform and removed binary sex-based patient categories, requiring instead categorization by pregnancy potential.
