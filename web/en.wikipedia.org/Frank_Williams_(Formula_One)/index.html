Frank Williams (Formula One)

BornApril 16, 1942, South Shields, England
DiedNovember 28, 2021 (aged 79)
OccupationFounder and team principal of Williams Grand Prix Engineering

Sir Francis Owen Garbett Williams (16 April 1942 – 28 November 2021) was a British businessman, racing car driver, and the founder of the Williams Formula One team. He was the team principal from its founding in 1977 until 2020, making him one of the longest-serving and most successful team principals in the sport's history.

Early career

After a brief career as a racing driver and mechanic, Williams founded Frank Williams Racing Cars in 1966, running cars for other drivers in Formula Two and Formula One. The operation struggled financially through the 1970s; after a partnership with Canadian oil magnate Walter Wolf ended, Williams started afresh in 1977 with engineer Patrick Head, founding Williams Grand Prix Engineering.

Success with Williams

From modest beginnings in Didcot, Oxfordshire, the team rose rapidly: Alan Jones won its first World Championship in 1980. Under Williams and Head, the team won nine Constructors' Championships and seven Drivers' Championships, with champions including Jones, Keke Rosberg, Nelson Piquet, Nigel Mansell, Alain Prost, Damon Hill, and Jacques Villeneuve. In 1986 Williams was seriously injured in a road accident in France, which left him tetraplegic; he returned to lead the team from a wheelchair within months. Ayrton Senna's fatal crash in a Williams at Imola in 1994 led to a protracted manslaughter case in Italy, from which Williams was ultimately cleared.

Later years

Williams was knighted in 1999. The team declined in competitiveness in the 2000s and 2010s, and the Williams family sold the team to Dorilton Capital in 2020, ending over four decades of family ownership. Sir Frank died in November 2021, aged 79.
