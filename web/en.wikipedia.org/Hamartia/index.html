Hamartia

Hamartia is a term used by Aristotle in his Poetics for a mistake or error in judgment by the protagonist of a tragedy, which leads to a chain of actions culminating in their downfall.

Interpretation

The Greek word ἁμαρτία means "to miss the mark" or "to err". Aristotle writes that the tragic hero's reversal of fortune should arise not from vice or depravity but from some hamartia. Translators have debated whether it denotes an intellectual error, a moral failing, or a "tragic flaw" of character — as in Oedipus's rashness or Hamlet's indecision.

Usage

The term is also used in Christian theology for sin, and in literary criticism generally for the fatal flaw that brings about a hero's ruin.
