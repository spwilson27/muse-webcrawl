Curcumin

Curcumin is a bright yellow chemical produced by plants of the Curcuma longa species, a member of the ginger family (Zingiberaceae). It is the principal curcuminoid of turmeric, the spice ground from the plant's rhizome, and gives turmeric and curry powder their characteristic yellow colour.

Chemistry

Curcumin is a diarylheptanoid with the chemical formula C21H20O6. It exists in keto and enol tautomeric forms, is poorly soluble in water, and dissolves in organic solvents such as ethanol and dimethyl sulfoxide. It is used as a food colouring (E100), in cosmetics, and traditionally in Ayurvedic medicine.

Research

Curcumin has been extensively studied in laboratory and clinical research for possible anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects. However, it is chemically unstable, poorly bioavailable, and known to interfere with many laboratory assays, which has complicated interpretation of results. As of recent assessments by regulators and reviewers, no medical use of curcumin has been confirmed by good clinical evidence, and it is not an approved drug for any condition. In 2023 the editor of a major journal retraction review highlighted widespread problems in the curcumin literature.

Safety

Consumed in food, turmeric is generally regarded as safe. High-dose supplements may cause gastrointestinal effects and can interact with medications; some turmeric products have also been found adulterated with lead chromate added to enhance colour.
