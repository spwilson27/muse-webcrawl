Horizontal gene transfer

The three main mechanisms of horizontal gene transfer in bacteria

Horizontal gene transfer (HGT) or lateral gene transfer (LGT) is the movement of genetic material between organisms other than by the ("vertical") transmission of DNA from parent to offspring through reproduction. HGT is an important factor in the evolution of many organisms, especially bacteria, where it allows rapid sharing of advantageous traits such as antibiotic resistance.

Mechanisms

In bacteria, three classical mechanisms mediate horizontal transfer. Transformation is the uptake of free DNA from the environment. Transduction occurs when a bacteriophage carries DNA from one cell to another. Conjugation transfers DNA, often on a plasmid, through direct cell-to-cell contact. Mobile genetic elements such as transposons and integrons further shuffle genes within and between genomes.

Significance

Horizontal transfer complicates the picture of a simple branching "tree of life," particularly for prokaryotes, whose evolution is better represented as a network. Clinically, HGT spreads genes for antibiotic resistance and virulence among pathogens. In eukaryotes, HGT is rarer but documented, including transfers from endosymbionts to host nuclei — a process central to the evolution of mitochondria and chloroplasts.
