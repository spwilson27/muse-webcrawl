Lake Nokoue

Location: southern Benin
Type: lagoon lake
Coordinates: 6°26′N 2°27′E (approx.)
Length: 20 km
Width: 11 km

Lake Nokoue (Lac Nokoué) is a large shallow lake in the southern part of Benin, West Africa. It lies immediately north of Cotonou, the country's economic capital, and connects to the Atlantic Ocean through the Cotonou channel.

The lake, about 20 km long and 11 km wide, is fed by the Ouémé and Sô rivers. Water levels and salinity vary with the seasons, as fresh river water floods in during the rainy season and seawater enters through the channel in the dry season.

Ganvie

On the lake stands Ganvie, often called the "Venice of Africa": a village of thousands of stilt houses founded, according to tradition, by the Tofinu people in the 16th–17th centuries as a refuge from slave raiders of the Fon kingdom, whose religious taboos forbade warriors to cross water. The village is a notable site for tourism and is on Benin's tentative list for UNESCO World Heritage status.

Fishing

Lake Nokoue supports an important artisanal fishery, including the traditional acadja method, in which bundles of branches are planted in the lakebed to attract fish. Pollution from Cotonou and overfishing are major environmental concerns.
