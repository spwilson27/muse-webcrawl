Prasad

Prasad (Sanskrit: प्रसाद, prasāda, "grace, favour, offering") is a religious offering in Hinduism as well as in some other Indian religions such as Sikhism and Jainism. Most often prasada is vegetarian food especially cooked for devotees after praise and thanksgiving to a god or deity, or it is first offered to the deity and then distributed to devotees as a token of divine blessing.

Practice

Prasada is closely linked to the term naivedya, the food offered to a deity in a temple or home shrine. After the offering ritual (puja), the food is believed to be infused with the deity's grace and is shared among worshippers. Common prasada items include fruits, sweets such as laddu and peda, coconut, and cooked dishes. Famous temple prasadas include the Tirupati laddu of the Venkateswara Temple and the Mahaprasad of the Jagannath Temple in Puri.

In Sikhism

In Sikh tradition, karah parshad — a sweet flour-based food — is served to the congregation at gurdwaras, symbolising equality and shared blessing. The langar meal likewise is regarded as blessed food shared by all.
