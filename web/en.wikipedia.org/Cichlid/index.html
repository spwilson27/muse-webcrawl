Cichlids are fishes from the family Cichlidae in the order Cichliformes. Cichlids were traditionally classed in a suborder, the Labroidei, along with the wrasses (Labridae), in the order Perciformes, but molecular studies have contradicted this grouping. On the basis of fossil evidence, it first appeared in Argentina during the Early Eocene epoch, about 48 million years ago, and was diversifying by the Late Cretaceous period.

The family Cichlidae includes more than 1,300 described species, with hundreds more undescribed, making it one of the largest vertebrate families. New species are discovered annually, and many species remain undescribed. Cichlids span a wide range of body sizes, from species as small as 2.5 cm (1 in) in length (e.g., female Neolamprologus multifasciatus) to much larger taxa approaching 1 m (3 ft) in length (including the giant cichlid Boulengerochromis microlepis and the speckled peacock bass Cichla temensis).

Distribution and habitat

Cichlids are distributed mainly in Africa, Madagascar, South America, Central America, and the Caribbean, with a few species in southern Asia and North America. The greatest diversity is found in the great lakes of East Africa — Lake Malawi, Lake Tanganyika, and Lake Victoria — where spectacular adaptive radiations have produced hundreds of endemic species. The rift-lake radiations are among the most celebrated examples of rapid speciation in vertebrates.

Behaviour

As a group, cichlids exhibit a diverse array of body shapes and behaviours. Many are highly territorial, especially during breeding. Cichlids are renowned for their advanced parental care: most species guard their eggs and fry, and many are mouthbrooders, in which one parent (usually the female) incubates the eggs and shelters the young in the mouth. This care can last for several weeks.

Feeding

Cichlids employ a wide variety of feeding strategies. Some are herbivores grazing algae from rocks (the mbuna of Lake Malawi), others are predators of other fish, snail-crushers with powerful pharyngeal jaws, or planktivores. A second set of jaws in the throat — the pharyngeal jaw apparatus — is thought to have been a key innovation enabling their dietary diversification.

In the aquarium

Many cichlids are popular aquarium fish, valued for their colour, behaviour, and personalities. Popular groups include angelfish, discus, oscars, and the African rift-lake cichlids. Some species, such as tilapias, are also important food fish farmed worldwide.
