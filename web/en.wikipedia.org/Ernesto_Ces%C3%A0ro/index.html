Ernesto Cesàro

Ernesto Cesàro (1859–1906), Italian mathematician known for Cesàro summation.

Ernesto Cesàro (12 March 1859 – 12 September 1906) was an Italian mathematician who worked in the fields of differential geometry and the theory of infinite series. He is remembered chiefly for Cesàro summation, a method of assigning sums to some divergent series by averaging the partial sums.

Life

Cesàro was born in Naples. He studied briefly at the mining school in Liège, Belgium, where his brother taught mineralogy and crystallography, before returning to Italy; he received his doctorate from the University of Rome in 1887 on the strength of work already published, having never formally completed undergraduate examinations in the usual way. He held professorships at Palermo and Naples, and was a member of the Accademia dei Lincei. He died in Torre Annunziata in 1906, after an accident while attempting to rescue his eldest son, who was drowning.

Work

His 1894 Lezioni di geometria intrinseca set out methods of intrinsic differential geometry that influenced the subject. In analysis he developed the summability theory associated with his name: the Cesàro mean of a sequence, and its generalisations of higher order, underpin results such as Fejér's theorem on Fourier series. His name is also attached to the Cesàro equation for plane curves, the Cesàro curve (a fractal related to the Lévy C curve), and the theorem that the probability that two randomly chosen integers are coprime tends to 6/π².
