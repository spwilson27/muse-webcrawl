Francisco Varela

Francisco Varela (1946–2001), co-developer of the theory of autopoiesis.

Francisco Javier Varela García (7 September 1946 – 28 May 2001) was a Chilean biologist, philosopher and neuroscientist who, together with his mentor Humberto Maturana, developed the concept of autopoiesis — the self-producing organisation characteristic of living systems. He was a leading figure in cognitive science and a pioneer of the enactive approach to cognition.

Life and work

Varela studied biology at the University of Chile and earned his PhD in biology at Harvard University in 1970. Returning to Chile, he and Maturana published De máquinas y seres vivos (1972), introducing autopoiesis, and later the popular The Tree of Knowledge (1987). After the 1973 coup he worked abroad, eventually settling in Paris, where he directed research at the CNRS and taught at the École Polytechnique.

With Evan Thompson and Eleanor Rosch he wrote The Embodied Mind (1991), which argued that cognition is not the manipulation of internal representations but the enactment of a world through a history of embodied action, drawing connections between cognitive science, phenomenology and Buddhist thought. Varela himself was a practising Buddhist and a founding figure of the Mind and Life Institute dialogues between scientists and the Dalai Lama. In the 1990s he proposed neurophenomenology, a programme for studying consciousness that combines first-person accounts of experience with neuroscience.
