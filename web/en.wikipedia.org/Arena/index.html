Arena

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An arena is a large enclosed platform, often circular or oval-shaped, designed to showcase theatre, musical performances, or sporting events. It is composed of a large open space surrounded on most or all sides by tiered seating for spectators, and may be covered by a roof. The key feature of an arena is that the event space is the lowest point, allowing maximum visibility. The word derives from Latin harēna, "sand" — the sand spread on the floor of Roman amphitheatres to absorb blood.

History

The archetype is the Roman amphitheatre, of which the Colosseum in Rome (dedicated AD 80) is the largest surviving example, built for gladiatorial combats, wild beast hunts, and public spectacles. Similar venues across the Roman world hosted the spectacles of the empire; surviving arenas at Arles, Nîmes, Verona, and Pula are still used for concerts and, in southern France, bullfighting. The circus and hippodrome served related functions for chariot racing.

Modern arenas

The modern indoor arena developed in the late 19th and 20th centuries for ice hockey, basketball, boxing, and indoor concerts. Madison Square Garden in New York (in its successive incarnations since 1879) is the most famous example. Typical capacities range from a few thousand to over 20,000 for basketball and hockey; the largest indoor arenas, such as the Philippine Arena (55,000), exceed this for concerts and assemblies. Convertible designs use retractable seating and interchangeable floors — ice rink, court, or concert stage — to host many event types. Since the 1990s, arenas have commonly carried corporate naming rights and anchored urban entertainment districts.

Terminology

"Arena" is distinguished from "stadium" chiefly by being enclosed (usually roofed) and smaller: stadiums host field sports such as football and athletics, generally outdoors. Usage varies by region — in North America "arena" usually means an indoor venue, while open-air bowls built for bullfighting (plaza de toros) and ancient venues retain the name. Figuratively, "arena" denotes any sphere of activity or contest, as in "the political arena".
