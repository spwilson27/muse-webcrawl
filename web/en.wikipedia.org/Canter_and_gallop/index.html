Canter and gallop

Canter and gallop
Fast gaits of the horse
Canter: three-beat; gallop: four-beat

The canter and gallop are variations of the fastest gait that can be performed by a horse or other equine. The canter is a controlled three-beat gait, while the gallop is a faster four-beat variation in which all four feet leave the ground during suspension.

Canter

In the canter, one hind leg strikes first, followed by the diagonal pair, then the remaining foreleg (the "lead"). Speeds average 16–27 km/h depending on the length of stride.

Gallop

The gallop is the fastest gait, reaching 40–48 km/h in short bursts. Racehorses gallop with a transverse or rotary footfall sequence; the cheetah-like rotary gallop is used by fast mammals.
