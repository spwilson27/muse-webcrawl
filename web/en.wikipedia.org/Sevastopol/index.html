Sevastopol

Sevastopol Bay

Sevastopol is the largest city in Crimea and a major port on the Black Sea. Founded in 1783 after the Russian Empire annexed the Crimean Khanate, it has served as the home port of the Russian Black Sea Fleet for most of its history. The city is internationally recognised as part of Ukraine, though it has been administered by Russia since the annexation of Crimea in 2014.

History

The site was first settled by Greeks, who founded the colony of Chersonesus in the 5th century BC; its ruins lie within the modern city. In 1783, following Catherine the Great's annexation of Crimea, a fortress and naval base were established at the superb natural harbour of Akhtiar Bay, renamed Sevastopol — Greek for "venerable city."

Crimean War

Sevastopol is best known for the Siege of Sevastopol (1854–1855) during the Crimean War, when British, French, Ottoman, and Sardinian forces besieged the city for eleven months. The siege featured the Charge of the Light Brigade at Balaclava and the pioneering war nursing of Florence Nightingale. The city fell in September 1855, and the Treaty of Paris demilitarised the Black Sea.

Second World War and after

In 1941–42, Sevastopol withstood a 250-day siege by German and Romanian forces, one of the most intense of the Eastern Front, before falling in July 1942; Soviet forces recaptured it in May 1944. The city was almost completely destroyed and was rebuilt in Stalinist neoclassical style. After 1991 the status of the Black Sea Fleet was negotiated between Ukraine and Russia under a lease agreement. Russia annexed Crimea in 2014, a move condemned by the UN General Assembly, and Sevastopol became a federal city of Russia. Since 2022, Ukrainian drone and missile strikes during the Russo-Ukrainian War have repeatedly targeted the fleet in Sevastopol.

Geography and economy

Sevastopol occupies the southwestern tip of the Crimean Peninsula, with numerous bays indenting its coastline. Its economy centres on the naval base, ship repair, fishing, wine production from the surrounding vineyards, and tourism to sites such as the Panorama Museum depicting the 1855 siege and the ancient ruins of Chersonesus, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
