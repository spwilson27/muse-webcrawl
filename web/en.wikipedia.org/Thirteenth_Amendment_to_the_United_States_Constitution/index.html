Thirteenth Amendment to the United States Constitution

Thirteenth Amendment
Proposed31 January 1865
Ratified6 December 1865
Declared adopted18 December 1865
SubjectAbolition of slavery and involuntary servitude
Part ofReconstruction Amendments

The Thirteenth Amendment (Amendment XIII) to the United States Constitution abolished slavery and involuntary servitude, except as punishment for a crime. It was passed by the Senate in April 1864 and by the House of Representatives on 31 January 1865, and was ratified by the required number of states on 6 December 1865 — the first of the three Reconstruction Amendments adopted following the American Civil War.

Text

Section 1. Neither slavery nor involuntary servitude, except as a punishment for crime whereof the party shall have been duly convicted, shall exist within the United States, or any place subject to their jurisdiction.

Section 2. Congress shall have power to enforce this article by appropriate legislation.

Adoption

President Abraham Lincoln's Emancipation Proclamation of 1863 had freed enslaved people only in rebellious states and rested on war powers, so abolitionists pressed for a constitutional guarantee. Lincoln made the amendment a central plank of his 1864 re-election and lobbied lame-duck Democrats to secure its passage through the House. Georgia became the 27th state to ratify on 6 December 1865, and Secretary of State William Seward proclaimed the amendment adopted on 18 December.

Interpretation and legacy

The amendment ended legal chattel slavery for roughly four million people, but its punishment exception was exploited through convict leasing and Black Codes in the Jim Crow South. The Supreme Court has read Section 2 — the enforcement clause — as empowering Congress to legislate against the "badges and incidents of slavery", a doctrine applied in Jones v. Alfred H. Mayer Co. (1968) to uphold federal bans on racial discrimination in housing. Debates continue over the amendment's application to prison labour and modern trafficking, addressed in part by the Trafficking Victims Protection Act.
