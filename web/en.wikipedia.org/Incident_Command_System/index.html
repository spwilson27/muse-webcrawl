Incident Command System

The Incident Command System (ICS) is a standardized approach to the command, control, and coordination of emergency response, providing a common hierarchy within which responders from multiple agencies can work effectively together. It is used for incidents ranging from single-vehicle accidents to major disasters and planned events.

Origins

ICS was developed in California in the 1970s by the FIRESCOPE program after disastrous wildfires exposed problems of unclear authority, incompatible terminology, and overloaded supervisors among responding agencies. It was subsequently adopted across the US fire service and, after the September 11 attacks, was mandated nationally as part of the National Incident Management System (NIMS) in 2004.

Structure

An Incident Commander has overall authority, supported by command staff (public information, safety, and liaison officers) and general staff sections: Operations, Planning, Logistics, and Finance/Administration. Key principles include management by objectives, a modular organization that expands or contracts with the incident, manageable spans of control (typically three to seven subordinates), common terminology, and unified command when multiple jurisdictions are involved.

Adoption

Variants of ICS are used in Canada, Australia (as AIIMS), the United Kingdom, and by the United Nations and private-sector emergency managers.
