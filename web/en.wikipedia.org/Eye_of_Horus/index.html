Eye of Horus

The Eye of Horus, also known as the wadjet (or wedjat, udjat) eye, is an ancient Egyptian symbol of protection, royal power, and good health. It is depicted as the stylized eye of the falcon god Horus, combining a human eye with the distinctive cheek markings and brow of a falcon.

The Eye of Horus (wedjat eye)

Mythology

In Egyptian mythology, Horus fought his uncle Set to avenge the murder of his father Osiris. During the struggle, Set tore out or damaged Horus's left eye. The eye was magically restored, according to different versions of the myth, by the goddess Hathor or by the god Thoth. Because the eye was restored, it became a symbol of healing and wholeness; the word "wedjat" means "whole" or "healthy".

Symbolism and use

The Eye of Horus was one of the most common motifs in Egyptian art. It appeared on amulets, jewellery, funerary objects, and the prows of boats, where a pair of eyes was painted to allow the vessel to "see" its way. In funerary contexts, wedjat amulets were placed on mummies to protect the deceased in the afterlife. The symbol was also used in mathematics: the parts of the eye represented fractional quantities in the Egyptian system of measurement, and in medicine the symbol is sometimes linked to the origin of the ℞ prescription sign.
