LCD Soundsystem is an American rock band from Brooklyn, New York, formed in 2002 by musician and producer James Murphy, co-founder of the DFA Records label. The band blends dance music and punk with influences from disco, krautrock, post-punk and electronic music, and became one of the defining acts of 2000s indie culture.

History

Murphy launched the project with the self-deprecating single "Losing My Edge" (2002), followed by "Yeah" (2004). The self-titled debut album (2005) earned two Grammy nominations and critical acclaim; its follow-up, Sound of Silver (2007), was widely ranked among the best albums of the decade and included the much-loved elegy "All My Friends" and "Someone Great". This Is Happening (2010) became the band's first US top-ten album.

In 2011 Murphy dissolved the band with a celebrated farewell concert at Madison Square Garden, documented in the film Shut Up and Play the Hits (2012) and the live album The Long Goodbye (2014). The retirement proved temporary: LCD Soundsystem reunited in 2015, released the chart-topping album American Dream (2017) — whose single "Tonite" won the Grammy Award for Best Dance Recording — and have continued touring and holding residencies in the years since.

Style and influence

Murphy's songs pair propulsive live drums, analogue synthesisers and cowbell-driven percussion with wry, self-conscious lyrics about ageing, scenes and authenticity. The band is credited with bridging indie rock and club culture and with popularising the dance-punk sound alongside acts such as The Rapture and !!!.
