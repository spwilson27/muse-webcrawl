Tonic (music)

In music theory, the tonic is the first scale degree (the note "do") of the diatonic scale and the tonal center or final resolution tone of a piece. The triad formed on the tonic note, the tonic chord, is the most significant chord in tonal music, symbolized by the Roman numerals I in major and i in minor.

Function

All harmonic and melodic tension in a tonal work is conventionally measured against the tonic: departures from it create expectation, and returns to it create closure. A piece typically begins and ends on the tonic, and other scale degrees and chords are described by their relationship to it — supertonic, mediant, subdominant, dominant, submediant, and leading tone. The tonic of the relative key differs, although the two keys share the same pitches.

Solfège and usage

In movable-do solfège, the tonic is always sung as "do" regardless of key. In Indian classical music, the tonic (Sa) plays an analogous role as the reference pitch of the raga. The word "tonic" is also used more loosely in popular music to mean the home key or root note of a scale used for improvisation.
