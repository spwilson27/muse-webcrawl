Parallel key

In music theory, a parallel key is the major or minor key that shares the same tonic (keynote) as another key. For example, C major and C minor are parallel keys, as are G major and G minor.

Distinctions

Parallel keys should not be confused with relative keys: relative major and minor keys share the same key signature but have different tonics (for example, C major and A minor), whereas parallel keys share the tonic but differ in key signature — the parallel minor has three more flats (or fewer sharps) than its parallel major. In German musical terminology the relationship is described with the term Paralleltonart for relative keys, while parallel keys are called Varianttonarten, a source of occasional confusion.

Usage

Moving between parallel keys is a common modulation technique. Pieces in a minor key frequently end on the parallel major tonic chord (the tierce de Picardie or Picardy third), and composers often shift between parallel major and minor within a work for expressive contrast, a practice known as modal mixture or borrowed chords in analysis.
