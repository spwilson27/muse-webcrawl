A tortilla chip is a snack food made from corn tortillas, which are cut into triangles and then fried or baked. Corn tortillas themselves are made from nixtamalized corn, vegetable oil, salt, and water. Tortilla chips are considered a Mexican food, though mass production in Los Angeles in the late 1940s helped popularize them throughout the United States.

History

Triangular tortilla chips were popularized by Rebecca Webb Carranza in the 1940s, when she began frying the misshapen tortillas rejected by the automated line at her Los Angeles tortilla factory and selling them as a snack. In Mexico, the traditional fried tortilla pieces known as totopos long predated the commercial chip.

Production

Commercial chips are made by cutting nixtamalized corn masa into wedges, baking them briefly, then deep-frying them until crisp, and salting them while hot. Variants include blue corn chips, lime-flavored chips, and chips seasoned with chili powder or cheese flavoring.

Uses

Tortilla chips are most commonly served with salsa, guacamole, or cheese dips. They are the base of nachos, which add melted cheese and toppings, and are crushed over dishes such as chilaquiles and migas.
