Peenemünde Army Research Center

The Peenemünde Army Research Center (German: Heeresversuchsanstalt Peenemünde) was founded in 1936 as one of five military proving grounds of the German Army Ordnance Office. Located at Peenemünde on the island of Usedom in the Baltic Sea, it was the main centre for the development of German guided missiles and rockets during the Second World War, most notably the V-2 rocket.

Development work

Under the military direction of Walter Dornberger and the technical leadership of Wernher von Braun, Peenemünde developed the Aggregat series of rockets, culminating in the A-4, later renamed the V-2. The first successful V-2 launch took place on 3 October 1942, when a rocket reached the edge of space—the first human-made object to do so. The nearby Luftwaffe test site, Peenemünde-West, worked on the V-1 flying bomb and other weapons.

Operation Hydra

On the night of 17–18 August 1943, the Royal Air Force attacked Peenemünde in Operation Hydra, with nearly 600 bombers targeting the site in the first deliberate raid against the German rocket programme. Production of the V-2 was subsequently moved underground to the Mittelwerk facility near Nordhausen, where concentration camp labour was used with great loss of life.

Postwar legacy

After the war, Peenemünde personnel and technology were acquired by both the United States (Operation Paperclip) and the Soviet Union, seeding their respective space and missile programmes. The site is now home to the Peenemünde Historical Technical Museum.
