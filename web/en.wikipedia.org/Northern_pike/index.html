The northern pike (Esox lucius) is a species of carnivorous fish of the genus Esox (the pikes). It is a circumpolar freshwater species of the Northern Hemisphere, native to North America and Eurasia, found in lakes, slow rivers and brackish coastal waters of the Baltic Sea. The species is also known simply as "pike" in Britain, Ireland and much of Europe.

Description

Northern pike have a long, flattened, duckbill-shaped snout and an elongated body, olive-green in colour with light, bean-shaped spots along the flanks and a white belly. The jaws carry many sharp, backward-pointing teeth. Average adults weigh 1–4 kg and measure 40–55 cm, but exceptional specimens exceed 1.2 m and 20 kg; females grow larger than males. Pike are identifiable by the fully scaled cheek and only partly scaled gill cover, distinguishing them from muskellunge and other pikes.

Behaviour and diet

Pike are ambush predators: they lie motionless among weeds and accelerate explosively to seize fish, frogs, small mammals and waterfowl, sometimes including prey a third of their own size. They spawn in early spring in shallow, vegetated water over flooded meadows. Pike are generally solitary and territorial, and larger individuals are cannibalistic.

Fishing

The species is a prized sport fish throughout its range, taken on lures and deadbaits, and is also fished commercially in parts of Europe and Canada. Its flesh is white and flaky but very bony. Pike are the state fish of several northern U.S. states and appear in heraldry and folklore across northern Europe.
