Leiria

Leiria is a city in central Portugal, on the river Lis between Lisbon and Porto, and capital of the Leiria District. Its medieval castle, rebuilt by King Denis, overlooks the old town, and the surrounding pine forest of Leiria — planted in the Middle Ages — supplied timber for Portuguese shipbuilding.

History

Leiria was retaken from the Moors by Afonso Henriques in 1135 and received its charter in 1142. King Denis made it a favoured residence and is credited with planting the great Pinhal de Leiria. The city was sacked during the Peninsular War.

Economy and culture

Today Leiria is a regional centre for plastics moulding, glass and food industries, with a polytechnic institute and a football stadium built for Euro 2004. Nearby lie the monasteries of Batalha and Alcobaça, both World Heritage Sites.
