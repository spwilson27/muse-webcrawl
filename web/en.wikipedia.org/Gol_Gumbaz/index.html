Gol Gumbaz

Gol Gumbaz (lit. "round dome") is a 17th-century mausoleum located in Bijapur, Karnataka, India. It houses the remains of Mohammed Adil Shah, the seventh sultan of the Adil Shahi dynasty, and some of his relatives. The structure, begun in 1626 and completed in 1656, is famous for its enormous dome and its remarkable acoustic properties.

Architecture

The mausoleum is built of dark grey basalt in a cube measuring about 47.5 metres (156 ft) on each side, capped by a hemispherical dome roughly 44 metres (144 ft) in external diameter — one of the largest domes built before the modern era. At each corner stands a seven-storey octagonal tower topped by a small dome.

The dome is supported by a system of intersecting arches forming pendentives, an advanced structural technique for its time. Inside, a circular gallery runs around the base of the dome — the famous whispering gallery, where even the faintest whisper can be heard clearly on the opposite side some 37 metres away, and sounds echo multiple times.

History

Mohammed Adil Shah commissioned the tomb early in his reign; construction began in 1626 and was still under way at his death in 1656, when the building was hastily completed. The architect is traditionally named as Yaqut of Dabul. The sultan's cenotaph rests on a raised platform in the centre of the hall, with the actual grave below.

The complex

The tomb stands within a walled complex that includes a mosque, a dharamshala (rest house), and other structures. The site is maintained by the Archaeological Survey of India and is one of the best-known monuments of Deccan Sultanate architecture.
