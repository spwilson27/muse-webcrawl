Yukos was a Russian oil and gas company created in 1993 from state assets and privatised in the mid-1990s, coming under the control of businessman Mikhail Khodorkovsky. By the early 2000s it was Russia's largest oil producer and one of the world's biggest oil companies.

Downfall

In 2003 Khodorkovsky was arrested on fraud and tax charges, widely viewed internationally as politically motivated. The Russian government assessed enormous back-tax claims against Yukos; its main production unit, Yuganskneftegaz, was auctioned in 2004 and acquired by the state company Rosneft. Yukos was declared bankrupt in 2006 and struck off the company register in 2007.

Litigation

Former shareholders pursued claims for years in international tribunals; in 2014 an arbitral tribunal in The Hague awarded them $50 billion, finding the company had been expropriated, though the award was subsequently contested and partially set aside and re-litigated in Dutch courts for years afterwards.
