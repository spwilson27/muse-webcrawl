The 2000 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XXVII Olympiad and commonly known as Sydney 2000, were held from 15 September to 1 October 2000 in Sydney, New South Wales, Australia. They were the second Summer Games hosted in Australia, after Melbourne 1956, and were widely praised for their organisation; IOC president Juan Antonio Samaranch famously described them as the "best Olympic Games ever".

Highlights

At the opening ceremony, Aboriginal sprinter Cathy Freeman lit the Olympic cauldron; ten days later she won the women's 400 metres before an enormous home crowd. Australian swimmer Ian Thorpe won three gold and two silver medals. British rower Steve Redgrave won gold at his fifth consecutive Games. The United States topped the medal table with 93 medals, ahead of Russia and China; host nation Australia finished fourth with 58 medals, its best performance to that date.

Sports and venues

Most events were centred on Sydney Olympic Park at Homebush Bay, built around Stadium Australia. New sports and events included taekwondo, triathlon, trampolining, women's water polo and synchronised diving. A record 199 nations took part, including a team of four athletes from East Timor competing under the Olympic flag as Individual Olympic Athletes.

Legacy

The Games left Sydney with major new sporting facilities and transport infrastructure, and are remembered as a high point of Australian public life at the start of the new millennium.
