Joseph Kabila

Born: Joseph Kabila Kabange, June 4, 1971, Hewa Bora, South Kivu, Zaire
Office: President of the Democratic Republic of the Congo (2001–2019)
Predecessor: Laurent-Désiré Kabila
Successor: Félix Tshisekedi
Party: People's Party for Reconstruction and Democracy (PPRD)

Joseph Kabila Kabange (born 4 June 1971) is a Congolese politician and military officer who served as President of the Democratic Republic of the Congo from January 2001 until January 2019. He took office ten days after the assassination of his father, President Laurent-Désiré Kabila, during the Second Congo War, and was elected to two terms in the country's first democratic elections of the post-Mobutu era.

Early life

Kabila was born in Hewa Bora, in the Fizi territory of South Kivu, the son of Laurent-Désiré Kabila, a veteran rebel leader, and Sifa Mahanya. He spent much of his childhood in exile in Tanzania, where he attended school, and later underwent military training in Rwanda and at the National Defence University in Beijing, China. During the First Congo War (1996–1997) he served in the rebel Alliance of Democratic Forces for the Liberation of Congo that overthrew Mobutu Sese Seko, and afterwards became commander of the Congolese land forces.

Presidency

After his father's assassination on 16 January 2001, Kabila was sworn in as president at the age of 29. He moved to negotiate an end to the Second Congo War, culminating in the Sun City Agreement and the Pretoria Accord of 2002, which withdrew foreign troops and established a transitional government. A new constitution was approved by referendum in 2005, and in 2006 Kabila won the country's first free multi-party presidential election in over four decades, defeating Jean-Pierre Bemba in a run-off. He was re-elected in 2011 in a poll criticized by international observers.

Later years in office and departure

Kabila's second term was marked by continuing conflict in the eastern provinces, including the M23 rebellion of 2012–2013, and by disputes over his remaining in power after his constitutional term ended in December 2016. Following a delayed election held on 30 December 2018, he handed power to Félix Tshisekedi in January 2019 — the first peaceful transfer of power in the DRC's history. He retained influence as a senator for life and through his Common Front for Congo coalition, though his relations with Tshisekedi later broke down, and in 2025 he returned to public politics from exile amid renewed conflict in the east.
