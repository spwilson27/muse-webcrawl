São Paulo Art Biennial

Bienal de São Paulo
Founded: 1951
Venue: Ciccillo Matarazzo Pavilion, Ibirapuera Park, São Paulo
Founder: Francisco "Ciccillo" Matarazzo Sobrinho

The São Paulo Art Biennial (Portuguese: Bienal de São Paulo) is an international contemporary art exhibition held every two years in São Paulo, Brazil. Founded in 1951 by the industrialist Francisco Matarazzo Sobrinho, it is the second-oldest art biennial in the world after the Venice Biennale, on which it was modelled.

Since 1957 the exhibition has been held in the Ciccillo Matarazzo Pavilion in Ibirapuera Park, a vast modernist building designed by a team led by Oscar Niemeyer. The Biennial was instrumental in introducing modern and contemporary international art to Brazil, showing works by artists such as Picasso, Kandinsky and the Abstract Expressionists in its early editions, and it remains one of the most significant art events in the Southern Hemisphere.

History

The first edition in 1951 attracted 1,800 works from 23 countries. During Brazil's military dictatorship, several editions were marked by censorship and an international boycott (1969–1970s). From the 1980s onwards the Biennial embraced large thematic exhibitions, and in 2010 its 29th edition drew roughly a million visitors.

Organisation

The event is organised by the Fundação Bienal de São Paulo. Entry has been free of charge since 2004, a policy credited with greatly broadening its audience.
