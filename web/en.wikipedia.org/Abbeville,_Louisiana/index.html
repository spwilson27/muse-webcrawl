Abbeville is a city in and the parish seat of Vermilion Parish, Louisiana, United States, located on the Vermilion River in the Acadiana region of southern Louisiana. It is a centre of Cajun culture, agriculture (notably rice and sugarcane), and the seafood industry of the Gulf Coast.

History

The town was founded in 1843 by Père Antoine Désiré Mégret, a Capuchin missionary from Abbeville, France, who purchased the land and laid out the settlement around a chapel; residents originally called it La Chapelle. Modelled on a French village, the original plan centred on a square with the church, courthouse and public buildings. Abbeville was incorporated in 1850 and grew as a trading point for the surrounding prairies and marshes. The town was badly damaged by Hurricane Rita in 2005, which drove storm surge up the Vermilion River and flooded much of the parish.

Culture

Abbeville is famous for its Giant Omelette Festival (Festival de l'Omelette Géante), held each November, in which chefs cook an omelette of thousands of eggs in a giant skillet as a celebration of French heritage shared with sister cities in France, Canada and elsewhere. The city also bills itself as a centre of Cajun cuisine, and its oyster industry is celebrated in the Louisiana Cattle Festival and other local events.
