Academy Award for Best Original Song

The Academy Award for Best Original Song is one of the awards given annually by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences (AMPAS) to the songwriters of the best song written specifically for a film. The category was introduced at the 7th Academy Awards in 1934, honoring films of that year; the first winner was "The Continental" from The Gay Divorcee, written by Con Conrad (music) and Herb Magidson (lyrics).

Rules

To be eligible, a song must be original and written specifically for the film, and must be heard during the film or as the first music of the ending credits. A film may submit a limited number of songs, and the Academy's music branch votes to determine the nominees, typically five. The award goes to the songwriters rather than the performer, unless the performer also contributed to writing the song.

Notable winners

Famous winning songs include "Over the Rainbow" (The Wizard of Oz, 1939), "White Christmas" (1942), "Moon River" (Breakfast at Tiffany's, 1961), "My Heart Will Go On" (Titanic, 1997), "Lose Yourself" (8 Mile, 2002) — the first hip-hop song to win — and "Let It Go" (Frozen, 2013). Songwriters such as Alan Menken, Tim Rice, Elton John and Billie Eilish have won in the category; performers traditionally sing the nominated songs live at the Oscar ceremony.

Multiple winners

Alan Menken holds the record for the category's parent fields of film songwriting with four Best Original Song wins, for "Under the Sea", "Beauty and the Beast", "A Whole New World" and "Colors of the Wind". The songwriters Sammy Cahn and Jimmy Van Heusen also won four Oscars for songs across their careers.
