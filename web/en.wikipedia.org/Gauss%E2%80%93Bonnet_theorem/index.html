Gauss–Bonnet theorem

The Gauss–Bonnet theorem is a central result in differential geometry linking the geometry of a surface to its topology. For a compact two-dimensional Riemannian manifold without boundary, the integral of Gaussian curvature over the surface equals 2πχ(M), where χ(M) is the Euler characteristic.

Statement

In symbols, ∫M K dA = 2π χ(M). For a sphere the total curvature is 4π; for a torus it is 0; for a closed orientable surface of genus g it is 4π(1 − g).

Surfaces with boundary

For a compact surface with boundary, terms for geodesic curvature along the boundary and exterior angles at corners are added. Special cases include the formula for the angle sum of a geodesic triangle on a curved surface.

History and generalisations

The result developed from work by Carl Friedrich Gauss and Pierre Ossian Bonnet in the nineteenth century. Higher-dimensional generalisations include the Chern–Gauss–Bonnet theorem, connecting curvature integrals to Euler characteristic in even dimensions.
