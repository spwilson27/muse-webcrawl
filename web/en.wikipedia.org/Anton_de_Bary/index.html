Heinrich Anton de Bary (1831–1888) was a German surgeon, botanist, microbiologist, and mycologist, regarded as a founding father of plant pathology (phytopathology) and of modern mycology. He held chairs at Freiburg, Halle, and Strasbourg, where he attracted students from around the world, including the Russian biologist Sergei Winogradsky.

Work

De Bary experimentally elucidated the complex life histories of many fungi. In 1853 he showed that rust and smut fungi are parasitic causes—not consequences—of plant disease, challenging prevailing views that fungi merely grew on already-diseased tissue. He worked out the life cycle of Phytophthora infestans, the organism responsible for the Irish potato blight, confirming Miles Berkeley's hypothesis that the fungus caused the disease. His comparative anatomy of fungi and his studies of slime moulds and algae were foundational textbooks.

In 1879 de Bary introduced the term symbiosis as "the living together of unlike organisms", using lichens—which he recognised as dual organisms of fungus and alga—as a key example. The fungal family name de Bary's contributions are commemorated in numerous species epithets (e.g. debaryanus).

Legacy

De Bary's insistence on experimental life-cycle studies established plant pathology as a science and influenced the germ theory era of biology. His slogan "the fungus causes the disease" summarised a turning point in how plant diseases were understood.
