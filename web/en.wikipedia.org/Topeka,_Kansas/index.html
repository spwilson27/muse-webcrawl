Topeka is the capital city of the U.S. state of Kansas and the county seat of Shawnee County. It is situated along the Kansas River in the north-eastern part of the state, in the valley of the Kansas River. The 2020 census recorded a population of 126,587, making it the fifth-most populous city in Kansas. Topeka is best known nationally as the origin of the landmark school desegregation case Brown v. Board of Education.

History

The area was long inhabited by Indigenous peoples, most recently the Kansa (Kaw) and Pawnee. In the 1840s a ferry crossing of the Kansas River used by Oregon Trail wagon trains operated near the present city. Topeka was founded in 1854 by antislavery settlers associated with the New England Emigrant Aid Company, and its name derives from a Kansa word meaning "place where we dig potatoes". After Kansas became a state in 1861, Topeka was chosen as the capital, and construction of the Kansas State Capitol began in 1866; the building was completed in 1903.

Topeka grew as a railroad town — the Atchison, Topeka and Santa Fe Railway was founded there in 1859 and kept its headquarters and major shops in the city — and as a centre of state government, education and insurance.

Brown v. Board of Education

In 1951, Oliver Brown and other Topeka parents challenged the city's segregated elementary schools. Their case, combined with four others, reached the U.S. Supreme Court as Brown v. Board of Education of Topeka, which ruled in 1954 that state-sponsored segregation in public schools was unconstitutional. The Monroe Elementary School in Topeka is preserved as the Brown v. Board of Education National Historical Park.

Geography and economy

Topeka lies in an area of rolling plains drained by the Kansas River. Government is the city's largest employment sector, followed by healthcare, education, transportation and manufacturing; major employers include the State of Kansas, BNSF Railway (successor to the Santa Fe), Stormont Vail Health, and Hill's Pet Nutrition. The city was struck by a destructive F5 tornado on June 8, 1966, which caused extensive damage — it was among the costliest tornadoes in U.S. history at the time.
