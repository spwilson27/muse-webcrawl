Leghemoglobin

Leghemoglobin is an oxygen-carrying protein found in the root nodules of leguminous plants such as soybeans, peas and clover. Like the hemoglobin of animal blood, it contains a heme group with an iron atom and gives active nodules their characteristic pink colour.

Function

Inside nodules, symbiotic rhizobium bacteria fix atmospheric nitrogen into ammonia the plant can use. Nitrogenase, the fixing enzyme, is destroyed by free oxygen, so leghemoglobin buffers oxygen at a low but steady level — enough for bacterial respiration, never enough to poison fixation.

Structure and evolution

Leghemoglobins are monomeric proteins closely related to animal globins, an example of convergent recruitment of an ancient protein family to a new task. The genes are expressed only in nodule tissue.

Applications

Because it is a plant-made heme protein, leghemoglobin produced by engineered yeast is used to give plant-based meat substitutes a meaty colour and flavour.
