Ergosterol

Formula: C28H44O

Molar mass: 396.65 g/mol

Melting point: 160 °C

Occurrence: Fungal cell membranes

Ergosterol (ergosta-5,7,10(19),22-tetraen-3β-ol) is a sterol found in the cell membranes of fungi and some protozoa, where it performs the same membrane-stabilising role that cholesterol plays in animal cells. It takes its name from ergot, the fungus from which it was first isolated.

Role in fungi

Ergosterol regulates membrane fluidity, permeability and the activity of membrane-bound enzymes, and is essential for fungal growth. Because it is absent from animal cell membranes, the ergosterol biosynthesis pathway is a prime target for antifungal drugs: azole antifungals such as fluconazole and itraconazole inhibit the enzyme lanosterol 14α-demethylase, while polyene antibiotics such as amphotericin B bind ergosterol directly and form pores that kill the cell.

Vitamin D precursor

When exposed to ultraviolet light, ergosterol undergoes a photochemical reaction to form ergocalciferol, or vitamin D2. This reaction is exploited commercially: irradiated yeast and mushrooms are major dietary sources of vitamin D2, used in food fortification and supplements.

Detection

Because ergosterol occurs almost exclusively in fungi, its concentration in soil, dust and grain is widely used as a chemical marker for fungal biomass and mould contamination.
