Meze

Meze (from Turkish meze, from Persian maza, "taste, snack") is a selection of small dishes served as appetisers or a light meal in cuisines of the Eastern Mediterranean, the Balkans and the Middle East, including Greek, Turkish, Lebanese, Syrian, Cypriot and Balkan traditions. In some regions — particularly Greece, Cyprus, Turkey and Lebanon — a meze spread can be an elaborate meal in itself, unfolding over hours with drinks, conversation and successive waves of cold and hot dishes.

Composition

A typical spread begins with bread (pita or flatbread) and dips, then salads, cheeses and cold dishes, followed by hot items and sometimes grilled meats or seafood. Common meze include:

  • Dips — hummus, tzatziki, taramasalata, baba ghanoush (moutabal), muhammara, haydari, skordalia
  • Salads and vegetables — Greek salad, tabbouleh, fattoush, dolmades (stuffed vine leaves), olives, pickles, marinated beans
  • Cheeses — feta, halloumi (often grilled or fried as saganaki), kasseri, beyaz peynir
  • Hot dishes — falafel, kibbeh, calamari, spanakopita, börek, grilled octopus, keftedes, souvlaki skewers, sigara böreği

Regional traditions

In Greece, meze accompanies ouzo or tsipouro in an ouzeri; in Cyprus, a "meze" in a taverna denotes a set banquet of twenty or more small courses. In Turkey, meze is the opening act of a rakı table, in meyhanes accompanied by music and melons in season. In the Levant, mezze is the classic beginning (or totality) of a festive meal, with the table covered edge to edge. Serbian and other Balkan cuisines use meze for assortments of cured meats, kajmak and cheeses served with rakija. The word and custom spread widely with the Ottoman Empire, whose centuries of rule shared ingredients and techniques across the region.
