Formula for primes

In number theory, a formula for primes is a formula generating the prime numbers, exactly and without exception. No easily computable polynomial formula is known that produces only primes, and it is known that no non-constant polynomial with integer coefficients can take prime values for all integer inputs; nevertheless, many remarkable formulas exist.

Polynomial formulas

Euler's polynomial n² − n + 41 yields primes for n = 0, …, 40, a celebrated near-miss. A system of Diophantine equations in 26 variables (Jones, Sato, Wada, and Wiens, 1976) has the property that its positive values, as the variables range over non-negative integers, are exactly the prime numbers—an outcome of work connected with the MRDP theorem on Hilbert's tenth problem.

Other formulas

Willans's formula expresses the n-th prime in terms of Wilson's theorem, and Mills's theorem shows there is a constant A such that ⌊A^(3^n)⌋ is prime for all n; both are of theoretical interest rather than computational use. Formulas using the floor function and the prime-counting function can enumerate primes but essentially presuppose knowledge of them. In practice, primes are found with sieves and primality tests, not closed formulas.
