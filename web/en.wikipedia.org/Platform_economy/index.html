Platform economy

A platform mediates transactions between multiple groups of users.

The platform economy is economic and social activity facilitated by digital platforms — online frameworks, usually websites or smartphone apps, that mediate interactions and transactions between two or more groups of users. Typical examples include Amazon for retail, Uber for ride-hailing, Airbnb for accommodation, and app stores and social media services for digital goods and content. The platform economy encompasses the related concepts of the gig economy, the sharing economy, and the on-demand economy.

Characteristics

Platforms profit from network effects: the value of the service increases as more users join on each side of the market, which can produce winner-take-most dynamics. Rather than owning the underlying assets — cars, rooms, inventory — platform firms own the digital infrastructure, the matching algorithms, the reputation systems (such as star ratings), and the data generated by transactions. Prices may be set dynamically by algorithm, as in surge pricing.

Labour and regulation

Platform work has been controversial. Companies typically classify workers who provide services through apps as independent contractors, which critics argue denies them employment protections such as minimum wages, sick pay, and pensions. Legal disputes and legislation — including the UK Supreme Court's 2021 ruling that Uber drivers are "workers", California's Proposition 22, and the European Union's Platform Work Directive — have sought to define the status of platform labour. Regulators have also addressed competition, taxation, data protection, and the impact of short-term rental platforms on housing markets.

Development

Scholars trace the platform model to earlier two-sided markets such as credit cards and shopping malls, but its scale expanded dramatically with the spread of smartphones and mobile internet after 2007. The COVID-19 pandemic accelerated reliance on delivery and remote-work platforms. Economists continue to debate whether platform firms represent a new organisational form or an extension of outsourcing and franchising, and how platform revenues and value should be measured in national accounts.
