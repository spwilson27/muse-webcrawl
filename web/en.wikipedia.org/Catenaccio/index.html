Catenaccio

Catenaccio (Italian for "door-bolt") is a tactical system in association football characterised by a strong, highly organised defence and a man-to-man marking scheme, with a sweeper (libero) positioned behind the line of defenders to clear loose balls and cover beaten teammates. It is most closely associated with Italian football of the 1960s.

Origins

The system's roots lie in the verrou ("lock") developed by Austrian coach Karl Rappan with Servette and the Swiss national team in the 1930s, which introduced the verrouilleur, a spare defender behind the back line. In Italy the system was adopted and refined by Nereo Rocco at Triestina and later AC Milan, and most famously by Helenio Herrera, whose Internazionale side — the "Grande Inter" — won three Serie A titles and two consecutive European Cups (1964, 1965) playing catenaccio with Giacinto Facchetti as an attacking full-back and Armando Picchi as libero.

Style and criticism

Catenaccio teams defended in depth, allowed opponents possession in harmless areas, and struck on the counter-attack through fast forwards. Critics called it negative and destructive; admirers praised its discipline and efficiency, and it shaped Italy's defensive tradition for decades. The classic form of the system faded as offside traps, zonal marking and rule changes (notably the back-pass law) transformed defending, but its emphasis on compactness and a spare defender influenced later tactics worldwide.
