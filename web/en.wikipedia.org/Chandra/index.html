Chandra

For the X-ray telescope named after the physicist Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar, see Chandra X-ray Observatory.

Chandra (Sanskrit: चन्द्र, Candra, "shining, moon") is the Hindu god of the Moon, and a major deity among the Navagraha (the nine celestial bodies of Hindu astrology). He is also known as Soma, Indu and Nishakara. Chandra is described as young and beautiful, fair and two-armed, holding a club and a lotus, riding a chariot drawn by ten white horses or by antelopes across the night sky. As Soma he is identified with the sacred ritual drink and the plant that produced it, and presides over the lunar calendar, tides, vegetation and the mind in Hindu astrology.

Mythology

In the Purāṇas Chandra is the son of the sage Atri and Anasuya, born from Atri's eye. He married the 27 daughters of Daksha — the Nakshatras (lunar mansions) — but favoured Rohini above the rest; Daksha, angered by the neglect of his other daughters, cursed Chandra to waste away. Shiva mitigated the curse so that the moon wanes for a fortnight and waxes for another, explaining the lunar phases; Chandra's crescent adorns Shiva's hair (whence Shiva's epithet Chandrashekhara). In another celebrated tale, Chandra abducted Tara, wife of Brihaspati (Jupiter); the ensuing war of the gods (Tārakāmaya) ended only when Brahma intervened, and Tara bore Chandra the son Budha, god of Mercury, ancestor of the Lunar dynasty (Chandravamsha) of kings to which the heroes of the Mahābhārata traced their line.

Worship and astrology

Monday (Somavāra) is sacred to Chandra; devotees, especially women, fast on Mondays. In Jyotiṣa (Hindu astrology) Chandra rules the mind, emotions, mother and water, and his sign (Cancer) and period (daśā) are held to shape temperament and fortune. The Moon's nodes Rāhu and Ketu, which cause eclipses, complete the Navagraha with him. Temples dedicated to Chandra include the Navagraha temple at Thingalur (Kailasanathar Temple) in Tamil Nadu, where he is worshipped facing Surya.

The name

Chandra and its derivatives (Chandran, Chandrasekhar "moon-crested") are common personal names in India. The word also names India's lunar exploration programme, Chandrayaan ("moon craft"), whose third mission made India, in August 2023, the first country to land near the lunar south pole.
