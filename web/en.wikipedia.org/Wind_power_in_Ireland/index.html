Wind power in Ireland

[Image: Wind power in Ireland]

Wind power in Ireland is the largest source of renewable electricity in the Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland. The island of Ireland has one of the best wind resources in Europe, and wind farms regularly supply around a third of the island's electricity demand, with instantaneous wind generation frequently exceeding half of demand.

History and development

Ireland's first commercial wind farm was built at Bellacorick, County Mayo, in 1992. Capacity grew rapidly in the 2000s and 2010s, mostly from onshore turbines; by the early 2020s installed wind capacity on the island exceeded 4.5 gigawatts in the Republic of Ireland alone. The Irish and Northern Irish grids are operated as a single all-island system (EirGrid and SONI), with interconnectors to Great Britain (the Moyle and East–West interconnectors) and planned links to France.

Policy and challenges

The Republic of Ireland's climate plans target 80 percent renewable electricity by 2030, with major expansion of offshore wind in the Irish and Celtic Seas. Challenges include grid constraints, curtailment of wind output when generation exceeds system limits, planning objections, and the need for storage and interconnection.
