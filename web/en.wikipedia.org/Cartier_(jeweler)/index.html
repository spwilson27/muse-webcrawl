Cartier (French: [kaʁtje]), formally Cartier International SNC, is a French luxury-goods company that designs, manufactures, distributes, and sells jewellery, leather goods, and watches. Founded by Louis-François Cartier in Paris in 1847, the company remained under family control until 1964. It is part of the Swiss Richemont group and operates more than 200 stores in 125 countries.

History

Louis-François Cartier took over the workshop of his master Adolphe Picard in Paris in 1847. His grandsons Louis, Pierre, and Jacques expanded the business internationally in the early twentieth century, opening boutiques in London (1902) and New York (1909) and making Cartier a jeweller to royalty; King Edward VII of the United Kingdom referred to Cartier as "the jeweller of kings and the king of jewellers." In 1904, Louis Cartier created one of the first wristwatches, the Santos, for the aviator Alberto Santos-Dumont.

Products

Cartier is known for iconic designs including the Panthère motif (introduced in 1914), the Trinity ring (1924), the Love bracelet (1969), and the Juste un Clou collection, as well as famous pieces such as the necklace made for Bhupinder Singh, the Maharaja of Patiala. Its watch lines include the Santos, Tank, Ballon Bleu, and Pasha models.
