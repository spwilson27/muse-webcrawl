Virginals

The virginals were a type of harpsichord, a rectangular keyboard instrument popular in northern Europe during the Renaissance and early Baroque periods. Unlike the wing-shaped grand harpsichord, virginals have strings running parallel to the keyboard, giving them a compact rectangular or polygonal case.

Types and repertoire

Two main types were made in Flanders: the muselar virginals, with the keyboard to the right and a rich, flute-like tone, and the spinet virginals, with the keyboard to the left. The name's origin is uncertain — possibly from Latin virga (rod, referring to the jacks) or from their association with young women players.

The English "virginalists" — William Byrd, John Bull, Orlando Gibbons and Giles Farnaby — produced a rich repertoire preserved in collections such as the Fitzwilliam Virginal Book and Parthenia (1612/13), the first printed music for the instrument.
