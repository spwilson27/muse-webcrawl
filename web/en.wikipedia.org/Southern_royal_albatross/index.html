Southern royal albatross

The southern royal albatross (Diomedea epomophora) is a large seabird of the albatross family. At an average wingspan of about 3 metres (9.8 ft), it is one of the two largest species of albatross, rivalling the wandering albatross. It was once considered conspecific with the northern royal albatross as the "royal albatross"; the two are now usually treated as separate species.

Description and distribution

Adults are mostly white with black upperwings that whiten with age, a large pink bill with a black cutting edge to the upper mandible, and a wingspan among the greatest of any living bird. The species breeds almost entirely on Campbell Island, New Zealand, with a few pairs on the Auckland Islands, and ranges across the southern oceans foraging over the Patagonian Shelf, the waters off Chile and Argentina, and around New Zealand.

Life history and conservation

Royal albatrosses are biennial breeders, laying a single egg in November; incubation and chick-rearing take nearly a year, and successful pairs usually breed every second year. Young birds spend years at sea before returning to breed at around eight years or older. The species is listed as Vulnerable by the IUCN, threatened mainly by bycatch in longline and trawl fisheries and by changes at its restricted breeding sites.
