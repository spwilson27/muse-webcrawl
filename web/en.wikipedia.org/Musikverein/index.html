Musikverein

The Großer Musikvereinssaal (Golden Hall)

The Musikverein in Vienna, Austria, is the concert hall of the Gesellschaft der Musikfreunde (Society of Friends of Music), founded in 1812. Opened in 1870, the building was designed by the Danish architect Theophil Hansen in a neoclassical style.

The Golden Hall

The main auditorium, the Großer Musikvereinssaal or "Golden Hall", seats about 1,744 people with standing room for several hundred more. It is celebrated worldwide for its acoustics — a long, narrow, high shoebox room with a coffered ceiling and gilded caryatids along the walls — and is considered one of the finest concert halls in the world. It is the home of the Vienna Philharmonic Orchestra.

Traditions

Each year on 1 January the hall hosts the Vienna New Year's Concert of waltzes and polkas by the Strauss family, broadcast to tens of millions of viewers worldwide. The building also contains several smaller halls, including the Brahms-Saal, and the society's archive holds major collections of manuscripts by Haydn, Mozart, Beethoven, Schubert, and Brahms.
