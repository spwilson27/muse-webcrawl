Iceni

The Iceni (also rendered Icēnī) were an ancient British tribe of eastern Britain during the Iron Age and early Roman era. Their territory, corresponding roughly to modern Norfolk and parts of Suffolk and Cambridgeshire, bordered the lands of the Corieltauvi to the west, the Catuvellauni and Trinovantes to the south, and the North Sea to the east and north. Their capital was at Venta Icenorum, near modern Caistor St Edmund.

Boudica's revolt

The Iceni are most famous for the revolt of AD 60–61 led by their queen Boudica. After the death of her husband, the client king Prasutagus, Roman officials annexed the kingdom, flogged Boudica and assaulted her daughters. In response the Iceni, joined by the Trinovantes, rose in revolt, burning the Roman towns of Camulodunum (Colchester), Londinium (London) and Verulamium (St Albans) before being defeated by Suetonius Paulinus at the Battle of Watling Street.

Culture and coinage

The Iceni produced fine metalwork, including the great gold torcs of the Snettisham Hoard, and minted their own inscribed coinage. Archaeology reveals wealthy oppida, temples and burials across their territory.
