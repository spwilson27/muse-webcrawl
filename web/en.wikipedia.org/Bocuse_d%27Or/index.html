Bocuse d'Or

The Bocuse d'Or is a biennial world chef championship created by French chef Paul Bocuse in 1987. Held in Lyon, France, it is often described as the culinary equivalent of the Olympic Games.

Format

National teams—each comprising a chef and a commis—must first qualify through continental selections. In the final, teams prepare elaborate meat and fish dishes within about five and a half hours before a jury of leading chefs, scored on taste, presentation, technique and organisation.

Winners

France, Norway, Denmark and the United States are among the nations to have won the gold trophy; Norway is the most successful country overall by medal count.
