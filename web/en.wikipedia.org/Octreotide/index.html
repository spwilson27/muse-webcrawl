Octreotide

Octreotide, sold under the brand name Sandostatin among others, is a synthetic octapeptide analogue of the natural hormone somatostatin. It mimics somatostatin pharmacologically but has a much longer half-life, and it inhibits the release of growth hormone, glucagon, insulin, and many gastrointestinal hormones.

ClassSomatostatin analogue
RoutesSubcutaneous, intramuscular (depot), intravenous, oral
Half-life~1.5 hours (immediate release)
FormulaC49H66N10O10S2
First approved1988

Octreotide is a cyclic octapeptide modelled on somatostatin-14.

Medical uses

Octreotide is used to treat acromegaly (excess growth hormone), symptoms of neuroendocrine tumours including carcinoid syndrome and VIPomas, and acute bleeding from oesophageal varices. Long-acting depot formulations (octreotide LAR) are given monthly. It is also used off-label for chylothorax, cluster headache, and refractory diarrhoea, and in nuclear medicine, labelled with indium-111 or gallium-68, for imaging neuroendocrine tumours (octreotide scan / DOTATATE PET).

Mechanism

The drug binds with high affinity to somatostatin receptor subtypes 2 and 5 (SSTR2, SSTR5), suppressing adenylate cyclase and hormone secretion, and reducing splanchnic blood flow.

Adverse effects

Common adverse effects include gastrointestinal upset, gallstones (from inhibited gallbladder motility), injection-site pain, and disturbances of glucose regulation — both hypoglycaemia and hyperglycaemia occur. Long-term use may cause hypothyroidism and vitamin B12 deficiency.
