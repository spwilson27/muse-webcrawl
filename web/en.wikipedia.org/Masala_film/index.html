Masala film

Masala films are a genre of Indian cinema that blend multiple genres into one work. Typically these films freely mix action, comedy, romance, and drama, along with musical numbers and song-and-dance sequences, into a single crowd-pleasing package. The term "masala" — a mixture of spices in Indian cooking — describes this mixing of genres.

Characteristics

Masala films usually have runtimes of around three hours, an intermission, larger-than-life heroes, melodrama, comic subplots often featuring a dedicated comedian character, romantic subplots, and villains whose defeat restores family and moral order. Stock plots include lost-and-found family separations, twins separated at birth, and revenge stories.

History

The style coalesced in the 1970s in Hindi (Bollywood) cinema, notably in the films of Manmohan Desai, such as Amar Akbar Anthony (1977), and the blockbusters written by Salim–Javed such as Sholay (1975), often cited as the definitive masala film. The format became dominant across Indian regional industries, especially Telugu and Tamil cinema, and remains a template for mainstream commercial filmmaking in India.

Influence

The masala format influenced later Indian cinema and was echoed in works aimed at the global diaspora. Directors abroad have cited masala films as inspirations for genre-blending commercial cinema.
