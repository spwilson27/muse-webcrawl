Canonization is the process by which a Christian church declares a deceased person to be a saint, upon which declaration the person is included in the "canon," or list, of recognized saints. Originally, saints were recognized by popular acclamation and local episcopal approval; over time the process in the Catholic Church became centralized under the authority of the pope.

A canonization Mass celebrated in St Peter's Square, Vatican City.

History

In the early church, martyrs and confessors were venerated locally, and bishops approved the cult of saints within their dioceses. The first recorded papal canonization was that of Ulrich of Augsburg by Pope John XV in 993. In 1234, Pope Gregory IX reserved canonization to the papacy in the Decretals. Urban VIII further tightened the rules in the seventeenth century, forbidding public veneration before formal approval. The process was substantially streamlined by Pope John Paul II's apostolic constitution Divinus Perfectionis Magister (1983).

The Catholic process

The modern Catholic process proceeds through stages. A cause is opened at the diocesan level, where the candidate's life, writings, and reputation for holiness are investigated; during this stage the person is titled Servant of God. If the Congregation (now Dicastery) for the Causes of Saints accepts the findings and the pope declares the person to have lived a life of heroic virtue, the candidate is called Venerable. Beatification — permitting limited public veneration — normally requires proof of one miracle attributed to the candidate's intercession (martyrs are exempt). Canonization generally requires a second miracle occurring after beatification, and results in the saint being inscribed in the Roman Martyrology and permitted universal veneration.

Other traditions

The Eastern Orthodox Church also canonizes saints (often termed "glorification"), generally through recognition by a synod of bishops following popular veneration. The Anglican Communion maintains calendars of saints, adding figures by decisions of its provinces. Some other Christian bodies recognize saints without a formal judicial process.

Record numbers

Pope John Paul II canonized 482 saints, more than all his predecessors combined in the previous several centuries, and Pope Francis surpassed that total, canonizing among others the 813 Martyrs of Otranto in a single ceremony in 2013.
