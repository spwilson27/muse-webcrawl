The Toei Subway (都営地下鉄, Toei chikatetsu, literally "metropolitan-managed subway") is one of two rapid transit networks serving Tokyo, Japan, the other being the Tokyo Metro. The Toei Subway lines were originally licensed to the Teito Rapid Transit Authority (predecessor of Tokyo Metro) but were built by the Tokyo Metropolitan Government and are operated by the Tokyo Metropolitan Bureau of Transportation (Toei).

Lines

  • Asakusa Line (A, vermilion red) – Nishi-Magome to Oshiage, 18.3 km; opened 1960 as the first government-operated line, with through services to Keikyū and Keisei lines, including to both Narita and Haneda airports.
  • Mita Line (I, blue) – Meguro to Nishi-Takashimadaira, 26.5 km; opened 1968.
  • Shinjuku Line (S, lime green) – Shinjuku to Moto-Yawata, 23.5 km; opened 1978, with through service to the Keiō New Line.
  • Ōedo Line (E, magenta) – Tochōmae via Shinjuku-Nishiguchi, Daimon and Higashi-Shinjuku to Hikarigaoka, 40.7 km; opened in stages 1991–2000. It is a deep-level line built with linear-motor trains, and its name ("Great Edo") was selected by a public competition involving novelist and governor Shintarō Ishihara's administration.

History

The Toei Subway began with the Asakusa Line in 1960, built to relieve congestion and to connect the Keisei and Keikyū networks across central Tokyo. Construction of the Ōedo Line, Tokyo's most expensive subway, was completed in 2000. Together with Tokyo Metro's nine lines, the Toei network forms the core of one of the most heavily used urban rail systems in the world.

Fares and passes

Toei fares are distance-based and separate from Tokyo Metro fares, though discounted transfer tickets exist at shared stations. Both networks accept the Pasmo and Suica IC cards, and Toei sells one-day economy passes covering its lines.
