Cornbread

Cornbread is a quick bread made with cornmeal, native to the cuisines of the Americas. Indigenous peoples of North America ground maize (corn) into meal for millennia, and corn-based breads became a staple of Southern United States cooking after European colonisation.

History

Native American peoples made many forms of corn cakes and breads. Settlers adopted corn as a staple grain because wheat grew poorly in much of the early South, and cornmeal breads — pone, hoecake, johnnycake, hushpuppies — proliferated. The introduction of chemical leaveners such as baking soda and baking powder in the nineteenth century produced the risen loaf recognised today. In the twentieth century, the spread of industrially milled, steel-ground cornmeal and the wider availability of wheat flour changed regional preferences.

Regional varieties

A longstanding culinary divide separates Southern cornbread — traditionally made with white or yellow cornmeal, little or no sugar or wheat flour, and baked in a preheated cast-iron skillet with bacon drippings or lard — from Northern cornbread, which is sweeter, cakier, and typically includes substantial sugar and flour. Mexican pan de elote is a moist, sweet corn cake made with fresh kernels.

Preparation

A basic batter combines cornmeal, flour (optional), baking powder, salt, eggs, milk or buttermilk, and fat. It is baked in a skillet, muffin tin or baking dish until golden. Cornbread is served as an accompaniment to beans, greens, barbecue and chili, and crumbled cornbread is the base of Southern cornbread dressing and stuffing.
