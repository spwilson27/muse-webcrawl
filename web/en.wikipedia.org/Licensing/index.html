Licensing

Licensing is the practice by which the owner of property or rights (the licensor) grants another party (the licensee) permission to use that property under defined conditions, in exchange for payment (royalties or a fee), while retaining ownership. Licences are used widely for intellectual property — patents, trademarks, copyrights, and trade secrets — as well as for real property and regulated activities.

Intellectual property licensing

A patent licence allows the licensee to make, use, or sell a patented invention that would otherwise infringe. Trademark licensing lets another business sell goods or services under a brand, common in franchising and merchandising. Copyright licences govern the reproduction and distribution of creative works; open-content licences such as Creative Commons and software licences such as the GNU General Public License grant broad permissions in advance.

Terms and types

Licences may be exclusive (only one licensee), sole, or non-exclusive; they may be limited by territory, duration, and field of use. A cross-licence is an exchange of rights between two patent holders. Compulsory licences, imposed by governments — for example for pharmaceuticals in public-health emergencies — allow use of a patent without the owner's consent, subject to compensation.

Licensing as regulation

Separately, governments issue licences as regulatory permissions: driving licences, professional licences, broadcasting licences, and liquor licences certify that the holder meets statutory requirements. Operating without a required licence can result in fines or criminal penalties.
