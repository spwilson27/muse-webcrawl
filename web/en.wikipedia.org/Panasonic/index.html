Panasonic Holdings Corporation, formerly Matsushita Electric Industrial Co., Ltd., is a major Japanese multinational electronics company headquartered in Kadoma, Osaka. Founded in 1918 by Kōnosuke Matsushita, it grew from a small maker of electric lamp sockets into one of the world's largest producers of electronic products.

History

Matsushita began business selling an improved attachment plug he had designed, then expanded into bicycle lamps, radios, and household appliances. After the Second World War the company expanded internationally, selling products under brands including National, Panasonic, Technics, and Quasar. The Panasonic brand, created in 1955 for export products, eventually became the company's global name, and in 2008 the company itself was renamed Panasonic Corporation; in 2022 it adopted a holding-company structure as Panasonic Holdings.

Business

Panasonic's operations span consumer electronics and appliances, housing and HVAC equipment, automotive infotainment and batteries, aviation electronics, and factory automation. It is a major manufacturer of lithium-ion batteries for electric vehicles, notably through its partnership with Tesla at Gigafactory Nevada. The company was a long-standing Olympic sponsor and is known for its Lumix cameras, Technics audio equipment, and Let’s Note laptops in Japan.
