Electoral integrity

Electoral integrity refers to the quality of elections and the degree to which they conform to international standards and global norms for democratic elections. It encompasses every stage of the electoral cycle, from the legal framework, electoral boundaries, and voter registration to campaign finance, media access, voting, counting, and the resolution of disputes.

Free, fair, and well-administered voting is the core of electoral integrity.

Concept

The concept was developed by scholars such as Pippa Norris and by practitioner bodies including International IDEA. Rather than a simple distinction between "free and fair" elections and rigged ones, electoral integrity treats quality as a continuum and identifies many forms of malpractice, including gerrymandering, voter suppression, vote buying, biased media coverage, fraud, and failures of election administration.

Measurement

The Electoral Integrity Project, founded in 2012, surveys election experts worldwide to produce the Perceptions of Electoral Integrity (PEI) index, which rates national elections on a 100-point scale. Countries such as Denmark, Finland, and Norway typically rank highest, while authoritarian states rank lowest. Such indices complement the reports of citizen and international election monitors.

Threats

Contemporary threats to electoral integrity include disinformation and social-media manipulation, foreign interference, cyberattacks on election infrastructure, declining trust in results, and legal changes perceived as partisan. Protecting integrity involves independent election management bodies, transparent counting, audits such as risk-limiting audits, and effective courts.
