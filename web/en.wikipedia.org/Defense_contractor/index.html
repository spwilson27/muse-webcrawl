Defense contractor

A defense contractor is a business organisation or individual that provides products or services to a military or intelligence department of a government. Products typically include military aircraft, ships, vehicles, weapons, and electronic systems, while services range from logistics and maintenance to training and, controversially, armed security provided by private military companies.

Industry

The modern defence industry is dominated by large firms such as Lockheed Martin, RTX, Northrop Grumman, Boeing, General Dynamics, and BAE Systems. The United States is the largest market; its largest contractors derive most of their revenue from government contracts, often awarded on a cost-plus basis for development programmes.

Criticism

The sector is criticised for the "revolving door" between government and industry, for cost overruns on major programmes, and for its political influence — concerns summarised in President Eisenhower's 1961 warning about the "military–industrial complex". Supporters argue that private industry provides expertise, surge capacity, and innovation that governments cannot maintain in-house.
