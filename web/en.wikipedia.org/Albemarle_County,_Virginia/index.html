Albemarle County, Virginia

Albemarle County is a county located in the Piedmont region of the Commonwealth of Virginia. Its county seat is Charlottesville, which is an independent city entirely enclosed within the county. As of the 2020 census, the county's population was about 112,000.

History

Albemarle County was created in 1744 from the western portion of Goochland County and named in honour of Willem Anne van Keppel, 2nd Earl of Albemarle. The county was the birthplace and home of Thomas Jefferson, whose estate Monticello stands on a hilltop southeast of Charlottesville, and also home to James Monroe, whose Highland estate adjoined Monticello. The University of Virginia, founded by Jefferson in 1819, lies within the county and the city.

Geography

The county covers roughly 726 square miles, rising from rolling Piedmont farmland in the east to the Blue Ridge Mountains in the west, part of which lies within Shenandoah National Park. The Rivanna River flows through the county past Charlottesville.

Economy and culture

The county is known for its farms, orchards, and a substantial wine industry along the Monticello Wine Trail. Monticello and the University of Virginia together form a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
