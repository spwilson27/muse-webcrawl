A crossover, also called a crossover SUV or CUV (crossover utility vehicle), is a type of automobile with an increased ride height that is built on a unibody car platform rather than the body-on-frame chassis of a traditional sport utility vehicle. Crossovers combine features of passenger cars — fuel efficiency, ride comfort and handling — with the tall seating position, cargo space and styling of SUVs.

History

Early examples included the AMC Eagle (1980), often credited as the first mass-market crossover. The segment took off in the mid-1990s with the Toyota RAV4 (1994) and Honda CR-V (1995), followed by models such as the Subaru Forester and the luxury Lexus RX (1998). By the 2010s crossovers had overtaken sedans as the best-selling vehicle type in the United States, and the format now dominates line-ups from subcompact to three-row family sizes.

Characteristics

Most crossovers offer optional all-wheel drive, hatchback cargo access, and seating for five to eight. Critics note their popularity has raised average vehicle size and weight, while regulators in several markets classify many crossovers as light trucks.
