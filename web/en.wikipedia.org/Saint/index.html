Saint

A saint is a person who is considered exceptionally holy or virtuous and is recognised as such within a religion, typically Christianity. In the broadest sense the word translates the Latin sanctus, "holy", and in the New Testament it is used of all believers; in later Christian usage it came to denote especially those, often martyrs and confessors, formally honoured by the Church and believed to be in heaven.

In Catholicism and Orthodoxy

The Roman Catholic Church recognises saints through canonisation, a process culminating in a declaration by the Pope that a deceased person lived a life of heroic virtue (or died a martyr) and is in heaven and worthy of veneration. The usual path runs through diocesan investigation, beatification, and proof of miracles attributed to the candidate's intercession. Saints have feast days, serve as patrons of places, professions and causes, and are invoked in prayer; their relics are venerated. The Eastern Orthodox Church glorifies saints through recognition by synods, often following popular devotion, with similar roles as intercessors and exemplars.

In Protestant traditions

Most Protestant churches, following New Testament usage, use "saint" for all Christians and reject the invocation of saints; Anglican and Lutheran traditions nevertheless commemorate notable figures of faith, including martyrs and teachers, as examples.

In other religions

Analogous figures exist elsewhere: in Islam, wali ("friends of God") are venerated especially in Sufi tradition; Hinduism honours saints and gurus (the word sant is related to the same root); Sikhism's Bhagat and Sant poets, Buddhism's arhats and bodhisattvas, and Judaism's tzadikim occupy partially comparable roles as models of holiness.

Veneration and culture

Devotion to saints shaped medieval and early modern culture: churches and cities took patrons, pilgrims travelled to shrines, and hagiography — lives of the saints such as the Golden Legend — was among the most popular literature of the age. Thousands of places worldwide bear saints' names (St Albans, San Francisco, Saint Petersburg). In everyday speech, "saint" is used figuratively for any person of exceptional patience or virtue.
