Military working dog

A military working dog (MWD) is a dog trained for use by the armed forces in roles such as detection, tracking, search and rescue, sentry duty, and patrol. Common breeds include the German Shepherd, Belgian Malinois, and Labrador Retriever.

Roles

Detection dogs locate explosives, narcotics, and other materials using their acute sense of smell. Patrol dogs are trained in controlled aggression, tracking dogs follow human scent trails, and specialized dogs search for weapons caches, mines, and survivors.

History

Dogs have served in war since antiquity as guards, messengers, and attackers. Formal programs expanded greatly in the Second World War; the US military's principal training center is at Lackland Air Force Base in Texas.

Welfare and status

Handlers often form strong bonds with their dogs. Legislation such as Robby's Law (2000) in the United States made it easier for retired military dogs to be adopted, frequently by their former handlers.
