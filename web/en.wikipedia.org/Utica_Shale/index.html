Utica Shale

Drilling activity in the Utica Shale play.

The Utica Shale is a unit of Upper Ordovician sedimentary rock in the Appalachian Basin of the eastern United States and Canada. It underlies parts of Ohio, Pennsylvania, West Virginia, New York, Quebec and Ontario, lying several thousand feet below the better-known Marcellus Shale. Named for outcrops near Utica, New York, the formation consists largely of organic-rich black shale interbedded with limestones.

Natural gas and oil

Beginning around 2010, advances in horizontal drilling and hydraulic fracturing made the Utica a major unconventional play. Its Point Pleasant sub-unit in Ohio proved especially productive of natural gas, natural gas liquids, and oil, and the formation's extent into Quebec and New York prompted moratoria on high-volume fracking in those jurisdictions. Total estimated resources run to tens of trillions of cubic feet of gas, though estimates have been revised substantially over time.

Geology

The shale was deposited about 450 million years ago in a deep foreland basin formed during the Taconic orogeny. Its organic content derives from marine plankton preserved in oxygen-poor bottom waters.
