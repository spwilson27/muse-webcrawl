The Iowa class was a class of six fast battleships ordered by the United States Navy in 1939 and 1940, of which four were completed: Iowa, New Jersey, Missouri and Wisconsin. They were the last battleships commissioned by the US Navy and the only ones armed with 16-inch guns to be fast enough — at 33 knots — to escort the new Essex-class aircraft carriers.

Design

The class combined the heavy armament and armour of the preceding South Dakota class with a lengthened hull and much more powerful machinery, producing the fastest battleships ever built. Their main battery of nine 16-inch/50-calibre guns could fire 2,700-pound armour-piercing shells to a range of about 24 miles.

Service history

All four ships served in the Pacific Theater of the Second World War; Missouri hosted the Japanese surrender ceremony in Tokyo Bay on 2 September 1945. They shelled targets during the Korean War, and New Jersey served off Vietnam. All four were reactivated in the 1980s under President Reagan's 600-ship Navy plan, refitted with Tomahawk and Harpoon missiles, and saw final combat in the Gulf War of 1991.

Retirement and preservation

The ships were decommissioned by 1992 as too expensive to operate in the post–Cold War era. A serious turret explosion aboard Iowa in 1989 killed 47 sailors. All four survive as museum ships: Iowa at Los Angeles, New Jersey at Camden, Missouri at Pearl Harbor and Wisconsin at Norfolk.
