The Southern Ute Indian Tribe is a federally recognized tribe of Ute people with a reservation in southwestern Colorado, headquartered in Ignacio. The tribe descends primarily from the Mouache and Capote bands of Utes, who ranged across Colorado, Utah and northern New Mexico. The Southern Ute Reservation spans roughly 1,059 square miles (about 680,000 acres) in a checkerboard pattern in La Plata, Archuleta and Montezuma counties.

History

Treaties in 1863 and 1868 recognised Ute title to much of western Colorado. The Brunot Agreement of 1873 and subsequent legislation reduced Ute lands, and under the Ute Removal Act of 1880 most Utes were removed to Utah after the Meeker incident; the Mouache and Capote bands remained in southern Colorado. The reservation was allotted under the Dawes-era policy, creating the present checkerboard of tribal, allotted and non-Indian land. The tribe organised under the Indian Reorganization Act of 1934 and adopted a constitution in 1936.

Government and economy

The tribe is governed by an elected Tribal Council chaired by a chairman. Energy development — especially natural gas from the northern San Juan Basin coalbed methane fields — transformed tribal finances from the 1990s; the tribe's Red Willow Production Company and its Growth Fund made the Southern Ute one of the wealthiest tribes in the United States, with top-tier credit ratings. Tribal enterprises include the Sky Ute Casino Resort in Ignacio and interests in energy, real estate and finance.

Culture

The tribe holds the annual Southern Ute Tribal Fair and powwow, maintains the Southern Ute Cultural Center and Museum in Ignacio, and works to revitalise the Ute language. The neighbouring Ute Mountain Ute Tribe (descended largely from the Weeminuche band) is a separate federally recognized tribe centred at Towaoc.
