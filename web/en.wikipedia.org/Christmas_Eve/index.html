Christmas Eve

Christmas Eve is the evening or the entire day before Christmas Day, the festival commemorating the birth of Jesus Christ. It falls on December 24 in the Gregorian calendar and is widely observed around the world as a full or partial holiday, often the high point of the Christmas season for family gatherings, religious services, and gift-giving.

Families gather on Christmas Eve in many countries to open gifts and share a festive meal

Religious observances

Many Christians attend evening or midnight church services on Christmas Eve, including the Midnight Mass of the Roman Catholic Church and candlelight services in Protestant traditions; Christingle services are popular in Britain. In liturgical reckoning Christmas begins at sundown on December 24, since the ecclesiastical day traditionally begins with vespers.

Customs

In much of continental Europe — including Germany, Poland, and Scandinavia — Christmas Eve, rather than Christmas morning, is when the main celebration occurs: families share a festive dinner and exchange gifts, said to be brought by the Christkind, St. Nicholas, or local equivalents such as Joulupukki in Finland. In many English-speaking countries, children hang stockings on Christmas Eve for Santa Claus to fill during the night, leaving out milk, cookies, or mince pies. Other customs include the Polish Wigilia supper, the Italian Feast of the Seven Fishes, Christmas markets, carol singing, and the reading of Clement Clarke Moore's poem "A Visit from St. Nicholas" (1823).
