Taos, New Mexico

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Taos is a town in Taos County in the north-central region of New Mexico, in the Sangre de Cristo Mountains. Initially founded in 1615, it was intermittently occupied until its formal establishment in 1795 by Nuevo México Governor Fernando Chacón to act as a fortified plaza and trading outpost for the neighbouring Native American Taos Pueblo and Hispano communities. The town was incorporated in 1934 and has a population of roughly 6,500.

History

Taos takes its name from the nearby Taos Pueblo, an ancient Native American community whose multi-storey adobe dwellings have been continuously inhabited for about a thousand years and are now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. In 1847 Taos was the scene of a revolt against the newly installed United States government of New Mexico, during which Governor Charles Bent was killed.

Art colony

From the early twentieth century Taos became a renowned art colony. Painters such as Bert Geer Phillips and Ernest L. Blumenschein founded the Taos Society of Artists in 1915, drawn by the quality of the desert light, and later figures — Georgia O'Keeffe, Ansel Adams, D. H. Lawrence and Mabel Dodge Luhan — consolidated the town's artistic reputation. The town remains a centre for galleries, museums and the "Taos hum", a low-frequency sound reported by some residents.

Geography and tourism

Taos lies at about 6,969 feet (2,124 m) elevation. Nearby attractions include the Taos Ski Valley, the Rio Grande Gorge Bridge, the Earthship community, and the historic San Francisco de Asís Mission Church, photographed by Adams and painted by O'Keeffe.
