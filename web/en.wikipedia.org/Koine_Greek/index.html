Koine Greek

Koine Greek (from Greek koinē, "common") was the common supra-regional form of Greek spoken and written from about 300 BC to about AD 600, spanning the Hellenistic period, the Roman Empire and the early Byzantine world. It developed chiefly from Attic Greek, with Ionic and other dialect influences, spread by the conquests of Alexander the Great.

Use

Koine was the lingua franca of the eastern Mediterranean and Near East, the language of administration, trade and literature. It is the language of the Septuagint translation of the Hebrew Bible, of the New Testament, and of writers such as Polybius, Epictetus and Plutarch in part.

Features

Compared with Classical Greek, Koine simplified inflection and syntax, levelled many irregular forms, changed the pronunciation of several vowels and diphthongs (iotacism), and absorbed loanwords. It is the ancestor of Medieval and Modern Greek.
