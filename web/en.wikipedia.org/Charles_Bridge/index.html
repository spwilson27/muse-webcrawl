The Charles Bridge (Czech: Karlův most) is a medieval stone arch bridge crossing the Vltava River in Prague, Czech Republic. It connects the Old Town with the Lesser Town (Malá Strana) and Prague Castle, and is one of the city's most famous landmarks.

History

Construction began in 1357 under King Charles IV, replacing the earlier Judith Bridge, which had been destroyed by a flood in 1342. Legend holds that the foundation stone was laid at a date and time chosen by royal astrologers (9 July 1357 at 5:31) for its auspicious palindrome of numerals. The bridge, originally called simply the Stone Bridge or Prague Bridge, was renamed Charles Bridge in 1870. It was the only bridge over the Vltava in Prague until 1841 and the only means of crossing the river for centuries.

Statues

The bridge is lined with 30 statues and statuaries, mostly Baroque works erected between 1683 and 1714, including the celebrated statue of St John of Nepomuk (1683). Most have since been replaced by replicas, with originals preserved in the National Museum. The bridge is closed to vehicles and is thronged with pedestrians, artists, and musicians.
