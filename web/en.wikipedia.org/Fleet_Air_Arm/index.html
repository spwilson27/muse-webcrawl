Fleet Air Arm

The Fleet Air Arm (FAA) is the naval aviation component of the United Kingdom's Royal Navy, responsible for the operation of aircraft from naval vessels and shore bases. It was formed on 1 April 1924 as the Fleet Air Arm of the Royal Air Force, from the Royal Naval Air Service elements retained after the First World War, and passed to full Admiralty control on 24 May 1939, on the eve of the Second World War — a date still celebrated by the FAA as its birthday. Its motto is Per Mare, Per Terras, Per Caelum ("By Sea, By Land, By Air").

Second World War

Despite starting the war with largely obsolete aircraft, the FAA achieved notable successes. In November 1940, Fairey Swordfish torpedo bombers from HMS Illustrious attacked the Italian fleet at Taranto, disabling three battleships in the first major all-aircraft naval strike — a model studied by the Japanese before Pearl Harbor. In May 1941, Swordfish from HMS Ark Royal crippled the steering of the German battleship Bismarck, enabling her destruction. FAA aircraft flew from fleet, light, and escort carriers in the Atlantic, Mediterranean, Arctic (convoy protection), and, from 1944, with the British Pacific Fleet against Japan; aircraft included the Seafire (naval Spitfire), Corsair, Hellcat, Avenger, Barracuda, and Firefly, many supplied by the United States under Lend-Lease.

Post-war and modern role

Post-war, the FAA operated jet fighters from carriers, pioneered the angled flight deck, steam catapult, and mirror landing sight (all British naval aviation inventions), and flew Sea Harriers with distinction in the 1982 Falklands War, where its air defence of the task force was decisive. The Sea Harrier was retired in 2006 and the Harrier GR force in 2010, leaving a period without fixed-wing carrier aviation until the arrival of the Queen Elizabeth-class carriers and the F-35B Lightning II, flown jointly by the FAA and RAF from 2018. Today the FAA also operates large helicopter forces — Merlin and Wildcat in anti-submarine, airborne surveillance, and commando support roles (the Commando Helicopter Force) — from principal bases at RNAS Yeovilton (HMS Heron) and RNAS Culdrose (HMS Seahawk).
