Hornet

Hornets are the largest of the eusocial wasps and belong to the genus Vespa, in the family Vespidae. There are about 22 recognised species, distributed mainly across Asia and Europe, with some introduced elsewhere. The best-known species include the European hornet (Vespa crabro) and the Asian giant hornet (Vespa mandarinia), the world's largest hornet, whose queens can exceed 5 cm in length.

Biology

Hornets build papery nests from chewed wood fibres, often in hollow trees, wall cavities, or underground. A colony is founded in spring by a single fertilised queen and grows through the summer, producing workers and, in autumn, new queens and males. Hornets are predators of other insects and also feed on ripe fruit and tree sap. Adults capture prey such as bees, flies, and caterpillars to feed to their larvae.

Stings

Hornets can sting repeatedly in defence of their nest. Despite their fearsome reputation, most hornets are not especially aggressive away from the nest, and their venom is broadly comparable in potency to that of other wasps, although the larger volume delivered by big species can make stings more painful. As with other wasp stings, the main danger is an allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) in sensitive individuals.

Asian giant hornet

The Asian giant hornet attracted worldwide attention when it was detected in North America in 2019; eradication efforts in Washington State and British Columbia removed the known nests, and the species was declared eradicated from the United States in 2024. Because hornets prey on honey bees, they can pose a threat to apiculture where they become established.
