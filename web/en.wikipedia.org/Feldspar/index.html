Feldspar

General
Category: Tectosilicate minerals
Formula: KAlSi3O8 – NaAlSi3O8 – CaAl2Si2O8
Hardness: 6.0–6.5 (Mohs)
Crystal system: Monoclinic / triclinic

Feldspar is a group of rock-forming aluminium tectosilicate minerals, also containing other cations such as sodium, calcium, potassium, or barium. The most common members of the feldspar group are the plagioclase (sodium-calcium) feldspars and the alkali (potassium-sodium) feldspars. Feldspars make up about 60% of the Earth's crust and 41% of the Earth's continental crust by weight, making them the most abundant mineral group in the crust.

Composition and structure

Feldspars crystallize from magma as both intrusive and extrusive igneous rocks and are also present in many types of metamorphic rock. Their structures are based on a three-dimensional framework of corner-sharing SiO4 and AlO4 tetrahedra, with alkali or alkaline-earth cations in the cavities. Chemically, feldspar compositions fall within the ternary system KAlSi3O8 (orthoclase) – NaAlSi3O8 (albite) – CaAl2Si2O8 (anorthite). The name derives from the German Feldspat, a compound of Feld ("field") and Spat ("a rock that does not contain ore").

Varieties

The alkali feldspars include orthoclase, microcline, sanidine, and anorthoclase; gem varieties include moonstone and amazonite. The plagioclase series runs from albite through oligoclase, andesine, labradorite, and bytownite to anorthite; labradorite is prized for its iridescent play of colour (labradorescence), and sunstone is a variety containing reflective inclusions.

Uses

Feldspars are an important raw material in the manufacture of glass and ceramics, where they act as fluxing agents that lower melting temperatures and promote vitrification. They are also used as fillers in paints, plastics, and rubber, and as mild abrasives. Feldspar mining is carried out in many countries, with large deposits exploited by open-pit methods.
