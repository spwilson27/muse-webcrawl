Viola

Viola
Type: bowed string instrument
Tuning: C3–G3–D4–A4 (fifths, a fifth below the violin)
Clef: alto clef (primarily)
Range: C3 upwards, about three and a half octaves

The viola is a bowed string instrument of the violin family, larger and lower-pitched than the violin. It is tuned in fifths to C–G–D–A, a fifth below the violin and an octave above the cello, and its music is written mainly in the alto clef. The viola supplies the inner voices of the orchestra and string quartet.

Sound and repertoire

The viola's tone is darker and warmer than the violin's, owing to its body size being acoustically small for its pitch. Long overshadowed as a solo instrument, it gained major works in the 20th century: Walton's and Bartók's viola concertos, Hindemith's sonatas (he was himself a violist), and Berlioz's Harold in Italy. Lionel Tertis and William Primrose pioneered its solo career.

Construction

Violas vary in body length from about 38 to 46 cm (15–18 in) with no fixed standard, unlike the violin. They are played held under the chin, like the violin.
