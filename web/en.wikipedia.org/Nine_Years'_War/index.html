Nine Years' War

The Nine Years' War (1688–1697), also called the War of the League of Augsburg or the War of the Grand Alliance, was a major conflict between France under Louis XIV and a European coalition including England, the Dutch Republic, the Holy Roman Empire, Spain and Savoy. In North America its theatre is known as King William's War, and in Ireland as the Williamite War.

Course

The war began with France's invasion of the Rhineland. Fighting spread to the Spanish Netherlands, Piedmont, Ireland — where William III defeated the Jacobites at the Boyne (1690) and Aughrim (1691) — and the high seas, where the French won at Beachy Head but lost at La Hogue. Major land battles included Fleurus, Steenkerque, Landen and Namur. Exhaustion and famine pushed both sides to negotiate.

Peace of Ryswick

The Treaty of Ryswick (1697) restored most conquests, with France keeping Strasbourg and gaining Saint-Domingue, while Louis recognised William III as king of England. The settlement left the Spanish succession unresolved, setting the stage for the next great war.
