Aryabhata (476–550 AD) was an Indian mathematician and astronomer, the first of the major mathematician-astronomers of the classical age of Indian mathematics and astronomy. His principal work, the Aryabhatiya, written in 499 AD when he was 23, is a compendium of mathematics and astronomy that profoundly influenced later Indian and Islamic learning.

Mathematics

Aryabhata worked with the place-value system and a form of alphabetic numeral notation. He gave an approximation of pi as 3.1416, provided a table of sines, solved linear indeterminate equations by the kuṭṭaka (pulverizer) method, and gave formulas for areas of triangles and volumes of spheres.

Astronomy

Aryabhata proposed that the Earth rotates on its axis daily, explaining the apparent westward motion of the stars, and correctly explained eclipses as caused by shadows rather than by the demon Rahu. He estimated the Earth's circumference with remarkable accuracy and gave a sidereal year close to the modern value.

Legacy

His work was developed by commentators such as Bhaskara I and transmitted to the Islamic world through translations. India's first satellite, launched in 1975, was named Aryabhata in his honour.
