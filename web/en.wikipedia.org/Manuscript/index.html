Manuscript

Manuscript
Meaning"Written by hand" (Latin manu scriptus)
MaterialsPapyrus, parchment, vellum, paper
FormatsScroll, codex, loose sheets

A manuscript is any document written by hand, as opposed to one printed or reproduced by other mechanical means. The term also survives in publishing, where an author's typed or word-processed draft submitted for publication is still called a manuscript.

History

Before the printing press, every book was a manuscript, copied one at a time by scribes. Writing surfaces evolved from clay tablets and papyrus scrolls in the ancient world to parchment and vellum codices in medieval Europe, where monasteries maintained scriptoria for copying religious and classical texts. Skilled illuminators decorated important volumes with miniatures and gold leaf. Paper, invented in China and transmitted through the Islamic world, gradually replaced parchment in late medieval Europe. In many cultures the manuscript tradition long outlasted Gutenberg: Chinese woodblock printing, Islamic calligraphy, and South Asian palm-leaf manuscripts each represent rich scribal cultures of their own.

Study and preservation

The study of handwriting and manuscripts is called palaeography; the physical study of books as objects is codicology. Major libraries — such as the Vatican Library, the British Library, and the Bibliothèque nationale de France — hold vast manuscript collections that are now being digitised for preservation and access. Scholars use variant readings across manuscript copies to reconstruct the original text of ancient works, a discipline known as textual criticism.
