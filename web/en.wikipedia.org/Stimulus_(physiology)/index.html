Stimulus (physiology)

Stimulus
DefinitionA detectable change in the environment of an organism
Detected bySensory receptors
ResponseAction potential; reflex; behaviour

In physiology, a stimulus (plural stimuli) is a detectable change in the physical or chemical structure of an organism's internal or external environment. The ability of an organism or organ to respond to external stimuli is called sensitivity (excitability). Sensory receptors receive stimuli and convert them into electrochemical signals through a process called transduction.

Types of stimuli

Stimuli are classified by modality: mechanical (touch, pressure, vibration, sound), electromagnetic (light), thermal (temperature), and chemical (odorants, tastants, blood gases, pH). They may also be classed as external — originating outside the body — or internal, such as blood pressure or hormone levels detected by interoceptors.

Threshold and response

For a stimulus to be detected it must exceed the receptor's threshold. Below threshold, no response occurs; above it, receptor potentials are generated and, if sufficient, trigger action potentials in sensory neurons. The strength of a stimulus is encoded by the frequency of action potentials and the number of receptors recruited. With continued constant stimulation, receptors adapt: phasic receptors respond mainly to changes, while tonic receptors signal stimulus intensity for its duration. Responses range from simple reflex arcs, such as withdrawal from a painful stimulus, to complex behaviour coordinated by the central nervous system.
