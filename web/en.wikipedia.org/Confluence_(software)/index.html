Confluence is a web-based corporate wiki and team workspace developed by the Australian company Atlassian. Launched in 2004, it allows teams to create, organise, and collaboratively edit pages of documentation, meeting notes, project plans, and knowledge bases, and integrates closely with Atlassian's other products, especially the issue tracker Jira.

Features

Content in Confluence is organised into spaces containing hierarchies of pages. Users edit with a rich-text editor, comment on and mention colleagues, attach files, embed tables, calendars, and roadmaps, and control access through page- and space-level permissions. Templates and blueprints standardise recurring documents such as retrospectives and product requirements, while a marketplace of apps extends the platform with diagramming, workflow, and formatting tools. Search and page analytics support its use as a company intranet and knowledge management system.

Deployment and editions

Confluence was originally offered as self-hosted Server and Data Center software; Atlassian ended sales of new Server licences in 2021 and support in February 2024, steering customers to Confluence Cloud, its hosted subscription service. A free tier for small teams is available alongside paid Standard, Premium, and Enterprise plans.

Reception and security

Confluence is one of the most widely used enterprise wikis, particularly in software organisations that also use Jira. Its popularity has made it a target for attackers, and several critical vulnerabilities in self-hosted instances have been widely exploited, prompting urgent patch advisories from Atlassian and national security agencies.
