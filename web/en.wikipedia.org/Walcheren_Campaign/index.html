Walcheren Campaign

The Walcheren Campaign was an unsuccessful British expedition to the Netherlands in 1809 during the Napoleonic Wars. The largest British expeditionary force assembled up to that time — some 40,000 troops — landed on the island of Walcheren in the Scheldt estuary intending to destroy French naval power at Antwerp and Flushing and to aid Austria, but the campaign collapsed amid delay and epidemic disease.

Course of the campaign

British forces landed on 30 July 1809 and, after bombardment, took Flushing (Vlissingen) in mid-August. However, slow progress up the Scheldt gave the French time to reinforce Antwerp, and the fleet could not force the river defenses. On Walcheren, "Walcheren fever" — a combination of malaria, typhoid, and dysentery endemic to the marshy polders — ravaged the army: over 4,000 men died of disease and more than 11,000 were sick, against only about 100 killed in action.

Aftermath

The expedition was evacuated in December 1809 having achieved little beyond the temporary occupation of Flushing. The fiasco caused a political storm in London, a parliamentary inquiry, and a notorious quarrel between the army and navy commanders, summed up in a contemporary lampoon about Lord Chatham and Sir Richard Strachan each expecting the other to act.
