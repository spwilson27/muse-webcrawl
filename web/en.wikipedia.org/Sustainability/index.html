Sustainability is a social goal for people to co-exist on Earth over a long time. Definitions of this term are disputed and have varied with literature, context, and time. Sustainability usually has three dimensions (or pillars): environmental, economic, and social. Many definitions emphasize the environmental dimension. This can include addressing key environmental problems, such as climate change and biodiversity loss.

The most widely used definition comes from the 1987 Brundtland Report, which describes sustainable development as development that "meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs."

Dimensions

Environmental sustainability concerns the integrity of ecosystems and the Earth's carrying capacity — climate stability, clean air and water, fertile soils, and biodiversity. Social sustainability addresses equity, health, education, and community cohesion. Economic sustainability concerns livelihoods, resource efficiency, and economic systems that can endure without degrading the other two pillars.

Measurement and policy

Sustainability is pursued through international frameworks such as the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (2015), national regulations, corporate reporting standards, and indicators ranging from carbon footprints to the Human Development Index. Scholars debate the tension between "weak" sustainability, which allows substitution between natural and human-made capital, and "strong" sustainability, which treats critical natural capital as irreplaceable.
