Impression management

Impression management is a conscious or subconscious process in which people attempt to influence the perceptions of other people about a person, object or event by regulating and controlling information in social interaction. It was first conceptualised by sociologist Erving Goffman in The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life (1959).

Goffman's dramaturgy

Goffman compared social life to a theatre: people perform roles on a "front stage" for an audience, while relaxing and preparing "back stage". Managing one's appearance, manner and setting helps sustain a desired definition of the situation.

Tactics

Common tactics include ingratiation, self-promotion, exemplification, intimidation and supplication, as well as excuses and apologies when an impression is threatened.

Applications

The concept is used in social psychology, organisational behaviour, job-interview research and studies of social media self-presentation.
