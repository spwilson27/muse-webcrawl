Île-de-France is the most populous of the eighteen regions of France and the political, economic and cultural heart of the country. Centred on Paris, its capital, it covers only about 2 per cent of metropolitan France's territory yet contains roughly a fifth of its population and generates around a third of national GDP.

Geography

The region is a low sedimentary basin drained by the Seine and its tributaries, the Marne and the Oise. Paris and the densely built inner suburbs (the Petite Couronne) are surrounded by the Grande Couronne of newer towns, farmland and forests such as Fontainebleau and Rambouillet.

History

The name, first attested in the fourteenth century, originally described the royal domain between the Seine, Marne and Oise — an "island" of France at the core of the early Capetian kingdom. The modern administrative region was created in 1976, replacing the former Région Parisienne.

Economy and transport

Île-de-France hosts the headquarters of most major French companies, the La Défense business district, two international airports (Charles de Gaulle and Orly), and Europe's busiest urban rail network, including the Métro, RER and the expanding Grand Paris Express. Major sights such as Versailles, Saint-Denis and Disneyland Paris draw tens of millions of visitors a year.
