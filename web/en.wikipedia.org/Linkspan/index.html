Linkspan

A linkspan or link-span is a type of drawbridge used mainly to move vehicles on and off a roll-on/roll-off (RO-RO) ferry or railroad ferry. Linkspans are found at ferry terminals where a ship uses a bow, stern or side door to load or unload cars, trucks, semi-trailer trucks or railroad cars. They bridge the gap between the shore and the vessel, and can be raised or lowered to accommodate the ship's freeboard at different states of load and tide.

Design

A linkspan typically consists of a bridge structure hinged at the shore end, supported at the sea end by hydraulic rams, counterweights, or lifting towers, so its gradient can be adjusted during loading. Some ferry terminals have two-tier linkspans allowing simultaneous loading of two decks of a ferry. The vessel end of the linkspan rests on the ship, or is closely aligned with its ramp, so vehicles can drive directly between ship and shore. Linkspans are essential infrastructure at busy RO-RO ports such as Dover, Calais, Holyhead and the inter-island terminals of Scotland and New Zealand.
