Flatcar

A flatcar (also flat wagon or flatbed rail car) is a piece of railway rolling stock consisting of an open, flat deck mounted on a pair of bogies (trucks). With no sides or roof, it carries loads that do not require weather protection or that are loaded by crane from above.

Uses

Flatcars carry machinery, vehicles, lumber, steel products, rails, and large structures. Specialised variants include centrebeam cars for lumber, depressed-centre ("well") cars for tall or heavy loads, and spine cars and well cars built for intermodal containers and piggyback trailers.

History

The flatcar is among the oldest freight car types, descending from the simple platform wagons of early railways. Modern North American flatcars are commonly 60 or 89 feet (18 or 27 m) long and rated for loads of 70 to 100 tons.
