European Science Foundation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The European Science Foundation (ESF) is an independent, non-governmental organisation dedicated to promoting collaboration in scientific research across Europe. Founded in 1974 and based in Strasbourg, France, it brought together national funding agencies, research-performing organisations, and academies from around thirty European countries.

History and role

The ESF was established to help coordinate European research and provide a common platform for its member organisations. For decades it ran networking instruments such as EUROCORES programmes, research networking programmes, exploratory workshops, and forward looks, and it hosted expert boards and committees in fields including marine sciences (the Marine Board), space sciences, radio-astronomy frequencies, nuclear physics (NuPECC), and polar research.

Transition to Science Connect

Following the creation of Science Europe in 2011, which took over the science-policy advocacy role of the ESF's member organisations, the ESF progressively wound down its traditional programme activities. It reoriented itself towards providing research-support and grant-evaluation services through its successor structure, Science Connect, offering peer review, programme management, and evaluation expertise to funding bodies and institutions.
