Awadh (also spelled Oudh), is a historical region in northern India, now constituting the central part of the state of Uttar Pradesh. It is bounded by the Ganges to the south and the foothills of the Himalayas to the north. Its principal city is Lucknow, the state capital.

History

Awadh was one of the ancient kingdoms of India, traditionally identified with Kosala, whose capital was Ayodhya. In the Mughal period it became a subah (province). After Mughal decline, Saadat Ali Khan founded the semi-autonomous Nawabi state in 1722, ruled by the Nawabs of Awadh first from Faizabad and then, from 1775, from Lucknow. The nawabs' court became renowned for Urdu poetry, music, cuisine, and the composite "Ganga-Jamuni" culture.

Annexation and revolt

The British East India Company annexed Awadh in 1856 under Lord Dalhousie, deposing Nawab Wajid Ali Shah on grounds of alleged misgovernment. The annexation, combined with grievances among sepoys recruited from the region, made Awadh a heartland of the Indian Rebellion of 1857, led locally by figures such as Begum Hazrat Mahal.
