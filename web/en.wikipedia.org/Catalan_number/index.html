In combinatorial mathematics, the Catalan numbers are a sequence of natural numbers that occur in various counting problems, often involving recursively defined objects. They are named after the Franco-Belgian mathematician Eugène Charles Catalan (1814–1894), though they were known earlier to the Chinese mathematician Mingantu and to Leonhard Euler.

The nth Catalan number can be expressed directly in terms of binomial coefficients:

Cn = (1/(n+1)) · (2n choose n) = (2n)! / ((n+1)! n!)

Monotonic paths that never cross the diagonal, counted by Catalan numbers

Applications

The Catalan numbers count, among many other objects: the number of expressions containing n pairs of correctly matched parentheses; the number of ways a convex polygon with n+2 sides can be triangulated; the number of full binary trees with n+1 leaves; the number of monotonic lattice paths from (0,0) to (n,n) that never rise above the diagonal; and the number of stack-sortable permutations of length n.

History

The sequence was described by Mingantu in the 1730s in the context of series expansions. Euler found the numbers while studying triangulations of polygons and communicated them to Goldbach in 1751. Catalan's name became attached to the sequence after he wrote on the parenthesis-counting problem in 1838.

Properties

The Catalan numbers satisfy the recurrence C0 = 1 and Cn+1 = Σ CiCn−i. Their generating function is (1 − √(1−4x)) / 2x. The only odd Catalan numbers are those where n is one less than a power of two.
