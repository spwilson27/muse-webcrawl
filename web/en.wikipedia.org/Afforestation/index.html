Afforestation

Afforestation is the establishment of a forest or stand of trees in an area where there was no recent tree cover. It differs from reforestation, which restores previously forested land.

Purposes

Afforestation is undertaken for timber production, erosion control, restoration of degraded land, and carbon sequestration to mitigate climate change. Large-scale examples include China's efforts to hold back desert expansion and shelterbelt projects such as the "Three-North" programme begun in 1978.

Criticisms

Poorly planned afforestation can harm native grassland ecosystems, reduce water availability, and — when done with single-species plantations — support little biodiversity. Critics argue protecting existing ecosystems is often more effective.
