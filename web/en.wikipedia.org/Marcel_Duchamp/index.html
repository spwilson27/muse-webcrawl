Marcel Duchamp (28 July 1887 – 2 October 1968) was a French-American painter, sculptor, chess player and writer whose work is associated with Cubism, Dada and conceptual art. He is commonly regarded as one of the most influential artists of the 20th century, whose work helped shape the direction of post-war avant-garde art in Europe and the United States.

Fountain (1917), the most famous of Duchamp's readymades.

Early work

Duchamp's Nude Descending a Staircase, No. 2 (1912), fusing Cubist and Futurist devices, caused a sensation at the 1913 Armory Show in New York. Increasingly sceptical of "retinal" painting, he turned to the readymades—ordinary manufactured objects presented as art—beginning with Bicycle Wheel (1913) and culminating in Fountain (1917), a urinal submitted under the name "R. Mutt" to an open exhibition and rejected.

Later life

His monumental glass work The Bride Stripped Bare by Her Bachelors, Even (the Large Glass) occupied him from 1915 to 1923, when he declared it "definitively unfinished". Thereafter he devoted much of his time to chess, reaching near-master strength, while secretly constructing his final assemblage, Étant donnés, revealed only after his death. Under the female alter ego Rrose Sélavy he produced photographs (with Man Ray), puns and objects. Duchamp became a U.S. citizen in 1955 and died in Neuilly-sur-Seine in 1968.

Influence

By privileging the idea over craft, Duchamp is often called the father of conceptual art; his readymades profoundly influenced Pop art, Minimalism, Fluxus and postmodern practice.
