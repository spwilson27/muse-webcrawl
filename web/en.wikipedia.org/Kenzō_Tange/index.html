Kenzō Tange (丹下 健三, Tange Kenzō, 4 September 1913 – 22 March 2005) was a Japanese architect and a winner of the 1987 Pritzker Architecture Prize. Combining traditional Japanese styles with modernism, he designed major buildings on five continents and was a leading figure of the Metabolist movement.

Early works

Tange graduated from the University of Tokyo in 1938. His career advanced when he won the competition for the Hiroshima Peace Memorial Park and Museum in 1949, a landmark of post-war reconstruction that combined modernist planning with references to traditional Japanese architecture.

Major projects

His design for the Yoyogi National Gymnasium, built for the 1964 Tokyo Olympics, with its sweeping suspended roof, brought him international fame. Other major works include St. Mary's Cathedral in Tokyo, the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building, the Kagawa Prefectural Government Building, and the master plan for the 1970 Osaka Expo. He also prepared planning schemes for cities including Skopje after its 1963 earthquake.

Legacy

Tange taught at the University of Tokyo and mentored a generation of Japanese architects, including Fumihiko Maki and Arata Isozaki. His office, Tange Associates, continued his practice after his death in 2005.
