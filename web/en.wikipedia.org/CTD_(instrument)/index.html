CTD (instrument)

A CTD is an oceanographic instrument used to measure the electrical conductivity, temperature, and pressure (from which depth can be calculated) of seawater. The name is an initialism of the three measurements: conductivity, temperature, and depth. CTD casts are a principal tool of physical oceanography, used on research vessels worldwide.

MeasuresConductivity, temperature, pressure
Derived quantitiesSalinity, density, depth
Used inOceanography

Operation

The instrument is lowered from a ship on a conducting cable, often mounted within a rosette frame carrying water-sampling bottles that can be closed at chosen depths. Sensors record profiles of conductivity and temperature against pressure as the package descends and is raised, typically at about one metre per second.

Derived properties

Salinity is computed from conductivity, temperature, and pressure using standard seawater equations. Combined with the equation of state of seawater, these measurements yield density profiles, water-mass identification, and estimates of ocean circulation and stratification. Modern CTDs often carry auxiliary sensors for dissolved oxygen, fluorescence, turbidity, and pH.

Variants

Related instruments include expendable bathythermographs, moored profilers, gliders, and Argo floats, which carry CTD sensors on autonomous platforms to collect profiles throughout the world ocean.
