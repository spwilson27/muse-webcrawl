Artemis 1, officially Artemis I, was an uncrewed Moon-orbiting mission and the first integrated flight test of NASA's Orion spacecraft and Space Launch System (SLS) rocket. It launched on 16 November 2022 from Launch Complex 39B at Kennedy Space Center, following several delays caused by technical issues, a lightning strike and hurricanes.

Mission profile

Orion entered a distant retrograde orbit around the Moon, travelling farther from Earth than any previous crew-capable spacecraft — about 432,000 km at maximum distance. The mission tested Orion's heat shield, the most powerful in service, which endured temperatures of about 2,760 °C during high-speed re-entry. The capsule splashed down in the Pacific Ocean near Guadalupe Island on 11 December 2022.

Ten CubeSats were deployed as secondary payloads, including the BioSentinel yeast experiment, the Japanese OMOTENASHI lander (which failed), the NEA Scout solar-sail demonstrator, and LunaH-Map. The mission also carried radiation-measuring mannequins ("Moonikins") to assess conditions for future crews.

Significance

Artemis 1 was the debut of the Artemis programme, NASA's effort to return humans to the Moon, culminating in a crewed lunar landing planned for Artemis III. Its success cleared the way for Artemis II, the first crewed flight around the Moon.
