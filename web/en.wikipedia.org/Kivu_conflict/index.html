The Kivu conflict is an ongoing armed conflict in the Kivu provinces of North and South Kivu in the eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo. Beginning in 2004 as an offshoot of the Second Congo War, it has pitted the Congolese army and UN peacekeepers against a shifting array of rebel groups — many backed at times by Rwanda or Uganda — and has caused mass displacement, widespread sexual violence and a persistent humanitarian emergency.

Background and phases

The conflict grew out of the aftermath of the 1994 Rwandan genocide, when Hutu militias (later the FDLR) fled into the Kivus and Rwanda intervened repeatedly. Laurent Nkunda’s Tutsi-led CNDP rebellion (2004–09) ended with his arrest and a peace deal, but its successor, the M23 movement, seized Goma in 2012 and was defeated in 2013 by the FARDC and the UN Force Intervention Brigade — the first UN unit mandated for offensive operations.

Resurgence

M23 re-emerged in 2021–22, again accused by the UN and Kinshasa of Rwandan support, capturing large areas of North Kivu including Goma in early 2025, while dozens of local Mai-Mai and CODECO-style militias, the Islamist ADF in the north, and competition over coltan, gold and other minerals keep the region unstable. Millions have been displaced over the conflict’s course, making it one of the world’s longest-running humanitarian crises.
