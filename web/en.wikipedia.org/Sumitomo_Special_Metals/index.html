Sumitomo Special Metals

Sumitomo Special Metals Co., Ltd. was a Japanese materials company belonging to the Sumitomo Group, specialising in magnetic materials, powder metallurgy products, and electronic components. In 2004 it merged with Hitachi Metals, Ltd., becoming part of Hitachi Metals.

Neodymium magnets

Sumitomo Special Metals is best known as the original developer of the neodymium magnet: in 1982–1984, Masato Sagawa and colleagues at the company invented the sintered neodymium–iron–boron (Nd2Fe14B) permanent magnet, the strongest type of magnet commercially available, independently of the melt-spun material developed at General Motors. The invention revolutionised hard disk drives, electric motors, and many consumer electronics. Sumitomo held key patents on sintered NdFeB magnets and licensed production widely.

Products

Besides rare-earth and ferrite magnets, the company produced powder metallurgy parts, amorphous and nanocrystalline soft magnetic materials, semiconductors packages, and rolls for steel mills, supplying automotive, electronics, and heavy industries.

History

The company's roots lay in Sumitomo's special steel operations in Osaka. As Sumitomo Special Metals, it grew into one of the world's leading producers of high-performance magnets before its absorption into Hitachi Metals in 2004; the magnet business continues within Hitachi Metals (now Proterial).
