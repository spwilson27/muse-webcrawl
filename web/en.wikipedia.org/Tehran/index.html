Tehran is the capital and largest city of Iran, located in the north of the country at the foot of the Alborz mountain range, which separates it from the Caspian Sea. It is Iran's political, economic, and cultural center and one of the largest metropolitan areas in Western Asia.

History

The ancient city of Rhages (modern Rey), now a southern suburb, was a major center of medieval Persia, but Tehran itself was a modest town until Agha Mohammad Khan Qajar made it the capital in 1796. Under the Qajar and Pahlavi dynasties it expanded rapidly, and in the 20th century it grew into a sprawling metropolis. Tehran hosted the 1943 Tehran Conference of Allied leaders during the Second World War, and was the stage of the 1979 Iranian Revolution and the hostage crisis at the U.S. embassy.

Geography and landmarks

The city slopes upward from about 1,100 m in the south to 1,800 m in the affluent northern districts near the mountains. Landmarks include the Golestan Palace (a UNESCO World Heritage Site), the Azadi Tower, the Milad Tower — among the world's tallest telecommunications towers — and the Grand Bazaar, one of the largest covered markets in the world.

Challenges

Tehran suffers from severe air pollution, traffic congestion, and water stress, and it sits near active earthquake faults. The Tehran Metro, universities such as the University of Tehran, and a large share of Iran's industry and finance are concentrated in the capital region.
