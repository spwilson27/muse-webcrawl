Intuitive Surgical, Inc. is an American corporation that develops, manufactures and markets robotic products designed to improve clinical outcomes of patients through minimally invasive surgery, most notably the da Vinci Surgical System. Founded in 1995 and headquartered in Sunnyvale, California, the company grew out of research at SRI International and is a constituent of the Nasdaq-100 and S&P 500 indices.

History

The technology behind the da Vinci system originated in the late 1980s at SRI International, where researchers under contract with the United States Army developed a surgical telepresence system intended to let surgeons operate on wounded soldiers remotely. Intuitive Surgical was founded in 1995 to commercialise the technology, and the da Vinci system received FDA clearance in 2000 for general laparoscopic surgery. In 2003 Intuitive merged with its principal rival, Computer Motion, consolidating the surgical robotics market. Successive generations — da Vinci S, Si, Xi and the single-port da Vinci SP — expanded indications in urology, gynaecology, general, thoracic and head-and-neck surgery.

Products and business model

The da Vinci platform consists of a surgeon console, a patient-side cart with wristed instrument arms, and a 3D high-definition vision system; the surgeon controls the instruments remotely, with the system translating hand movements into scaled, tremor-filtered motions. The company's revenue model combines system sales and leases with recurring sales of limited-use instruments and accessories and service contracts. Its Ion endoluminal system extends robotic assistance to minimally invasive peripheral lung biopsy.

Criticism and competition

Intuitive has faced scrutiny over the cost of its systems, aggressive marketing, adequacy of surgeon training, and litigation concerning injuries during robot-assisted procedures. Since the late 2010s it has faced growing competition from platforms developed by Medtronic, Johnson & Johnson and other entrants.
