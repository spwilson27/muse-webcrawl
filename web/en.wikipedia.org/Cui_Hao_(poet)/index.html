Cui Hao (Chinese: 崔顥; Wade–Giles: Ts'ui Hao; c. 704–754) was a Chinese poet of the Tang dynasty. He passed the imperial examination in 723 and held minor official posts, but his reputation rests on his poetry — above all on the seven-syllable regulated verse "Yellow Crane Tower", often judged the finest poem of its form in the language.

Yellow Crane Tower

The poem recalls the legend of a sage who flew away from the tower in Wuhan on a yellow crane, moving from vanished immortals to the poet's homesickness as he gazes over the Yangtze toward his home. Tradition holds that the great Li Bai, visiting the tower and reading Cui's lines already inscribed there, laid down his brush, exclaiming that he could not write after such a scene and such a poem. About forty of Cui Hao's poems survive, including vigorous frontier ballads from his travels in the north and a group of much-discussed poems on women.

Life

Cui's personal life was turbulent — he married and divorced several times and was demoted for his conduct — but later anthologists, notably Yan Yu, ranked his best work at the summit of High Tang poetry.
