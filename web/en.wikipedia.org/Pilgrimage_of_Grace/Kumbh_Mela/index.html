Kumbh Mela is a major Hindu pilgrimage and festival, the largest peaceful gathering in the world, attracting tens of millions of devotees. It is celebrated in a cycle of approximately twelve years at four river-bank sites: Prayagraj (the confluence of the Ganges, Yamuna and the mythical Saraswati), Haridwar, Nashik and Ujjain.

Mythology

The festival's mythology derives from the Samudra Manthana, the churning of the ocean of milk, during which a pot (kumbha) of the nectar of immortality was spilled at the four festival sites. Bathing in the rivers at the astrologically determined moments is believed to cleanse pilgrims of sin and aid liberation from the cycle of rebirth.

Scale

On the principal bathing days, processions of naga sadhus and ascetic orders take part in the royal bath (shahi snan). The 2013 Maha Kumbh at Prayagraj drew an estimated 100–120 million visitors over its duration. UNESCO inscribed Kumbh Mela on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2017.
