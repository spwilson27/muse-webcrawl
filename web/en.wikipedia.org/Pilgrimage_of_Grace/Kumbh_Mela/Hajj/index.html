Hajj

The Hajj is the pilgrimage to Mecca required of able Muslims once in a lifetime. Together with festivals such as the Kumbh Mela, it ranks among the largest religious gatherings on the planet, with annual attendance of roughly two to three million pilgrims in recent years.

TypeIslamic pilgrimage
LocationMecca and environs, Saudi Arabia
StatusOne of the Five Pillars of Islam

Observance

During the days of Dhu al-Hijjah, pilgrims circle the Kaaba, run between Safa and Marwah, spend the Day of Arafah in prayer, stone the pillars at Mina and conclude with Eid al-Adha. Quotas allocate places by country, and Saudi authorities manage one of the world's most demanding crowd-logistics operations.

Comparison

While the Hajj is an individual obligation performed within a week, the Hindu Kumbh Mela is a cyclical festival of mass bathing; both demonstrate the enduring power of pilgrimage across faiths.
