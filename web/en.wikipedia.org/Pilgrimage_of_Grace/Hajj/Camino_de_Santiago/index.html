Camino de Santiago

The Camino de Santiago (Way of St James) is a network of pilgrims' ways leading to the shrine of the apostle Saint James the Great in the Cathedral of Santiago de Compostela in Galicia, northwestern Spain. Alongside the Hajj, it is one of the world's great pilgrimages and a cornerstone of religious tourism.

Routes and practice

The most popular route, the Camino Francés, crosses the Pyrenees from Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port and traverses northern Spain. Pilgrims carry a credencial stamped along the way, stay in albergues, and on arrival may attend the Pilgrims' Mass and receive the Compostela certificate if they have walked at least 100 km.

History

The pilgrimage flourished from the 9th century, guided by the 12th-century Codex Calixtinus. After decline in the early modern period, it revived dramatically from the late 20th century; hundreds of thousands now complete a Camino each year.
