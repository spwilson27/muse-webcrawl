Kumbh Mela

Kumbh Mela is a major Hindu pilgrimage festival centred on ritual bathing in sacred rivers. It rotates among four sites in India – Prayagraj, Haridwar, Nashik and Ujjain – and is the largest peaceful gathering in the world; the 2019 Prayagraj Ardh Kumbh drew around 150 million visitors over its course, and the 2025 Maha Kumbh drew still greater crowds.

TypeHindu pilgrimage festival
SitesPrayagraj; Haridwar; Nashik; Ujjain
Cycle12 years per site; Maha Kumbh every 144 years
RecognitionUNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage (2017)

Mythology and practice

The festival celebrates the myth of the churning of the ocean of milk, during which drops of the nectar of immortality fell at the four sites. On astrologically determined days, millions bathe at dawn; processions of naga sadhus and the akharas (monastic orders) lead the royal baths (shahi snan).

Scale

Comparable in devotional scale to the Hajj, Kumbh Mela requires a temporary megacity with bridges, hospitals and sanitation for tens of millions – often described as the largest public gathering visible from space.
