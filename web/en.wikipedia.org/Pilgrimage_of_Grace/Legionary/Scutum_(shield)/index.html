Scutum (shield)

The scutum was the large body shield of the Roman legionary. The classic Imperial scutum was a curved rectangle – effectively a section of a cylinder – about 1.2 m tall and 0.6 m wide, built like plywood from laminated strips of wood, covered in canvas and hide, with an iron boss (umbo) protecting the central handgrip.

TypeBody shield
UsersRoman legionaries
Dimensionsc. 1.2 m × 0.6 m (Imperial)
Weightc. 10 kg

Use

The scutum's curve wrapped around the legionary's left side, and its boss could be punched forward to unbalance an opponent before a gladius thrust. Overlapping scuta formed the testudo formation, proof against arrows. Decoration typically included the legion's emblem and Jupiter's eagle and lightning bolts.

Evolution

Earlier scuta were oval; the rectangular curved form is characteristic of the early Empire. From the 3rd century AD flat oval and round shields replaced it as armies and tactics changed.
