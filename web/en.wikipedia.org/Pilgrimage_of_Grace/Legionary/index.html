The Roman legionary was a professional heavy infantryman of the Roman army. Legionaries had to be Roman citizens and served long terms — under Augustus, a standard term of 20 years plus five in reserve — after which they received a discharge bonus of land or cash.

Equipment and training

A legionary carried the curved rectangular scutum shield, wore a metal helmet and body armour (mail, and later the articulated plate lorica segmentata), and was armed with the gladius short sword and two pila. On the march he carried a pack of entrenching tools, rations and stakes, earning soldiers the nickname "Marius's mules". Legionaries were also engineers, building roads, bridges, siege works and a fortified camp every night.

Life and reward

Pay was modest but regular, supplemented by donatives and plunder. Service was one of the surest routes of advancement for ordinary citizens, and auxiliary troops who completed service earned citizenship, a privilege many provincials sought.
