Roman infantry tactics

Roman infantry tactics were the battlefield methods of the Roman legions, built around the legionary's combination of heavy javelin, large shield (scutum) and short sword. Flexibility, discipline and relentless close-order fighting distinguished them from the phalanxes and warbands they defeated.

Battle sequence

Legions typically deployed in a triple line with intervals, screened by light troops. After skirmishing, the heavy infantry hurled their pila at close range, then charged with shields and gladii. Fresh lines relieved the front ranks, giving Roman armies unusual stamina in prolonged combat.

Formations

The testudo (tortoise) locked shields overhead and on all sides against missiles; the wedge concentrated force for breakthrough; the orbis formed an all-round defence. Marching camps, built nightly, underpinned Roman operational staying power.
