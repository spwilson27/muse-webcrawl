Religious tourism

Religious tourism, or faith tourism, is travel undertaken for pilgrimage, missionary or leisure purposes connected with religion. It is one of the oldest forms of travel: routes such as the Camino de Santiago in Spain, the Hajj to Mecca and the Kumbh Mela in India each draw millions of travellers annually.

Major destinationsMecca; Santiago de Compostela; Jerusalem; Varanasi; Vatican City
FormsPilgrimage; faith travel; mission trips

Scale

Hundreds of millions of people travel each year wholly or partly for religious reasons. The Hajj brings two to three million pilgrims to Mecca; the Kumbh Mela can draw over a hundred million across its duration; and hundreds of thousands walk the Camino de Santiago annually, receiving the Compostela on completion.

Management and impact

Religious tourism sustains whole local economies along pilgrimage routes, but also poses challenges in crowd safety, conservation and the commercialisation of sacred places.
