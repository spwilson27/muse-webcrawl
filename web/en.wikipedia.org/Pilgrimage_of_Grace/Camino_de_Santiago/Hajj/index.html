Hajj

The Hajj is the annual Islamic pilgrimage to Mecca, Saudi Arabia, and one of the Five Pillars of Islam. Every physically and financially able Muslim is expected to perform it at least once in a lifetime. Like other great pilgrimages such as the Camino de Santiago, it is a defining act of religious tourism, drawing around two to three million pilgrims each year.

TypeIslamic pilgrimage
LocationMecca, Saudi Arabia
Dates8th–13th Dhu al-Hijjah
Attendancec. 2–3 million annually

Rites

Pilgrims in the state of ihram circumambulate the Kaaba (tawaf), walk between the hills of Safa and Marwah (sa'i), stand in prayer at Arafat, stone the pillars at Mina, and celebrate Eid al-Adha. The rites commemorate Abraham, Hagar and Ishmael.

Significance

The Hajj is among the largest recurring gatherings on Earth and a profound expression of Muslim unity and equality; the pilgrim who completes it bears the honorific Hajji.
