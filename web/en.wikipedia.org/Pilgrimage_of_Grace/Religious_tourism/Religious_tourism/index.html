Religious tourism

Religious tourism, also called faith tourism or pilgrimage tourism, is a form of travel in which people journey, individually or in groups, for pilgrimage, missionary, or leisure purposes connected with their religion. It is one of the oldest forms of tourism, with roots in ancient pilgrimages to sacred sites.

Forms and destinations

Major destinations include Mecca, the destination of the Islamic Hajj, which draws millions of pilgrims annually; Jerusalem and Bethlehem for Jews, Christians and Muslims; Rome and the Vatican; the Hindu Kumbh Mela festival in India, among the largest gatherings on Earth; Bodh Gaya, associated with the enlightenment of the Buddha; and routes such as the Camino de Santiago in Spain.

Scale and impact

Religious travel accounts for hundreds of millions of journeys each year and brings substantial economic benefit to destination communities, while also posing challenges in crowd management, conservation of sacred sites, and balancing devotional use with commercial tourism.
