Kumbh Mela

The Kumbh Mela is a major pilgrimage and festival in Hinduism, widely regarded as the largest peaceful gathering of people in the world. It is celebrated in a cycle of approximately 12 years at four river-bank sites: Prayagraj (Allahabad), Haridwar, Nashik, and Ujjain. The festival is marked by ritual bathing in sacred rivers, which devotees believe cleanses them of sins and helps attain liberation (moksha).

Kumbh Mela
Type: Hindu pilgrimage festival
Frequency: Every ~12 years (full cycle)
Locations: Prayagraj, Haridwar, Nashik, Ujjain
Significance: Ritual bathing for purification

Mythology

The festival's origins lie in the Hindu legend of Samudra Manthana, the churning of the ocean of milk by gods and demons to obtain amrita, the nectar of immortality. During the struggle over the kumbha (pot) containing the nectar, drops fell at the four sites where the Kumbh Mela is now held.

Types and cycle

Several grades of the festival are recognised: the Maha Kumbh Mela, held once every 12 cycles (about 144 years) at Prayagraj; the Purna (full) Kumbh Mela every 12 years; and the Ardh (half) Kumbh Mela every six years. The 2013 Prayagraj Maha Kumbh drew an estimated 120 million visitors over its duration, with up to 30 million on a single peak day.

Rituals

The central rite is the shahi snan, or royal bath, in which akharas of ascetics — including Naga sadhus — process ceremonially to the river at astrologically determined moments. Pilgrims also attend religious discourses, mass feedings of monks and the poor, and devotional singing.

Recognition

In 2017 the Kumbh Mela was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
