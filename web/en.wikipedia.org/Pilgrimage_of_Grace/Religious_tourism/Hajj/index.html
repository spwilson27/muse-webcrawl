Hajj

The Hajj is the annual pilgrimage to Mecca, performed during Dhu al-Hijjah by Muslims from every country. As the largest recurring event in religious tourism, it combines fixed ritual obligations with an immense travel operation of charter flights, tent cities at Mina and purpose-built transport.

TypeIslamic pilgrimage
LocationMecca, Mina, Arafat, Muzdalifah
Attendancec. 2–3 million (quota-managed)
ObligationOnce in a lifetime for the able

Ritual journey

Pilgrims perform tawaf around the Kaaba, sa'i between Safa and Marwah, the standing at Arafat, the stoning at Mina and the farewell tawaf. The journey retraces the steps of Abraham's family and of Muhammad's Farewell Pilgrimage.

Organisation

Saudi Arabia allocates quotas of roughly one pilgrim per thousand Muslims per country, and has invested heavily in the Grand Mosque, the Jamarat Bridge and the Mashair railway to move millions safely through a handful of ritual days.
