Religious tourism, also known as faith tourism, is a form of tourism in which people travel individually or in groups for pilgrimage, missionary or leisure purposes to places of religious significance. It is one of the oldest forms of travel, with pilgrimages attested in ancient Egypt, Greece and India.

Major destinations

The largest religious gatherings in the world include the Hajj to Mecca, which draws some two to three million pilgrims each year, and the Hindu Kumbh Mela in India, the largest single gathering on Earth. Christian destinations include the Vatican, Lourdes, Fatima, Jerusalem and the Camino de Santiago; Buddhist sites include Bodh Gaya and Lumbini.

Impact

Religious tourism is a major economic sector for host cities and regions, supporting transport, lodging and guiding industries, but it also poses challenges of crowd management, heritage preservation and environmental pressure at sacred sites.
