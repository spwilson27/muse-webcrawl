The scutum (Latin for "shield") was the large body shield of the Roman legionary. The Imperial-era scutum was a curved rectangle of laminated wood — essentially an early form of plywood — about 1.2 m (4 ft) tall, which wrapped around the soldier's body and provided excellent protection while weighing roughly 10 kg (22 lb).

Construction and use

Three layers of wood strips were glued at right angles, faced with canvas and hide, and reinforced with a central iron boss (umbo) and metal edging. The legionary gripped it by a horizontal handle behind the boss and could punch forward with the boss as an offensive weapon. Overlapping scuta formed the testudo formation, and the shields were often painted with the legion's insignia, such as Jupiter's eagle and lightning bolts.

Development

The scutum evolved from earlier Italian and Celtic oval shields; by late antiquity it gave way to the flat oval or round shields of late Roman infantry.
