Roman infantry tactics

Roman infantry tactics were the battle strategies and formations used by the heavy infantry of ancient Rome, from the manipular legions of the Republic to the cohort-based armies of the Empire. Their hallmarks were discipline, flexibility, engineering skill and the ability to combine missile, shield and sword work in close order.

Roman infantry tactics
Used by: Roman Republic, Roman Empire
Key units: Legionaries, auxilia
Key weapons: Pilum, gladius, scutum
Famous formation: Testudo

Manipular system

By the Middle Republic the legion fought in three lines of maniples: the younger hastati in front, the principes behind them, and the veteran triarii as a final reserve, with light-armed velites screening the front. The checkerboard (quincunx) arrangement allowed lines to relieve one another mid-battle.

Cohort system

After the Marian reforms the cohort — about 480 men — became the standard tactical unit, and legionaries were uniformly equipped. In battle the legionary threw his pila at close range to disable shields and men, then advanced behind the large scutum to fight with the gladius, rotating fresh ranks forward to sustain pressure.

Formations and engineering

Roman infantry could form the wedge, the orb, and the testudo (tortoise), in which shields overlapped on all sides against missiles. Just as important was the fortified marching camp built every night, which gave Roman armies a secure base in hostile territory.
