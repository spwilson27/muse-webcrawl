Legionary

A legionary (Latin: legionarius) was a professional heavy infantryman of the Roman army after the Marian reforms of 107 BC. Legionaries were Roman citizens who signed up for a term of service — eventually fixed at 25 years — and formed the backbone of the legions that conquered and defended the Roman Empire.

Legionary
Branch: Roman army
Role: Heavy infantry
Service term: 25 years (under Augustus)
Equipment: Gladius, pilum, scutum

Recruitment and service

Under the Principate, legionaries were recruited from citizens, often provincials, and trained intensively in marching, camp-building, swimming and weapons drill. Pay was modest but regular, supplemented by donatives and a retirement bonus of land or cash (the praemia). Legionaries were forbidden to legally marry during service until the reign of Septimius Severus.

Equipment

The legionary's characteristic kit included the gladius short sword, two heavy javelins (pila), the large curved rectangular scutum shield, a helmet (galea), and segmented or mail armour (lorica segmentata or lorica hamata). On the march he carried a pack of tools, rations and stakes, earning the nickname "Marius's mules".

Role in battle

In Roman infantry tactics the legionary typically hurled his pila to disrupt the enemy formation, then closed with shield and gladius in tight order. After discharge, veterans often settled in colonies and became an influential social class.
