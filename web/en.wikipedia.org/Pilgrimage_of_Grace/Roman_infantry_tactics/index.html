Roman infantry tactics refers to the deployment, manoeuvre and battle methods of Roman heavy infantry from the early Republic to the late Empire. Roman success rested less on individual heroics than on discipline, relief of lines, engineering and the systematic rotation of fresh troops into the fight.

The triple line

In the manipular legion of the middle Republic, troops deployed in three lines — hastati, principes and veteran triarii — with light-armed velites screening the front. Engagements began with a volley of pila, followed by sword combat, and the rear lines could relieve or reinforce the first. In the Imperial cohort legion, larger cohorts deployed in one or two lines with reserves.

Formations

Special formations included the testudo (tortoise), in which shields overlapped on all sides and overhead to protect against missiles during sieges, the wedge for breaking enemy lines, and the anti-cavalry formation with pila planted in the ground. Soldiers were trained to fight in position, thrusting with the gladius from behind the scutum rather than swinging wildly.
