Milk

Composition (cow)Water ~87%, fat ~3–4%, protein ~3.3%, lactose ~4.8%
Main producersIndia, United States, Pakistan, China, Brazil

Milk is a white liquid food produced by the mammary glands of lactating mammals. It is the primary source of nutrition for young mammals (including breastfed human infants) before they are able to digest solid food. Milk contains lactose, proteins such as casein and whey, fats, and minerals including calcium. As an agricultural product, milk is collected from farm animals, most commonly dairy cattle, and also from goats, sheep, buffalo and camels.

Production and processing

Global milk production exceeds 900 million tonnes annually. India is the largest producer, where buffalo milk accounts for about half the output. Commercial cow's milk is typically pasteurised — heated to kill pathogens — and homogenised to prevent the cream from separating. The ability of adults to digest lactose (lactase persistence) evolved independently in several pastoralist populations, and roughly two-thirds of the world's adults have some degree of lactose intolerance.

Uses

Milk is consumed as a drink and used in cooking and in producing dairy products: cream, butter, yogurt, cheese, ice cream and milk powder. Plant-based alternatives such as soy, oat and almond drinks are marketed as milk substitutes. Milk has also held religious and symbolic significance in many cultures, from Hindu reverence for the cow to its use as a symbol of purity and abundance.
