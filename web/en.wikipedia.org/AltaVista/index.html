AltaVista

Launched: 15 December 1995
Closed: 8 July 2013
Creator: Digital Equipment Corporation
Known for: First full-text web search; Babel Fish translation

AltaVista was an early and influential web search engine established by researchers at Digital Equipment Corporation. Launched in December 1995, it was the first search engine to index the full text of a large portion of the web — about 16 million pages at debut — and to allow natural-language queries and advanced search features such as phrase searching and Boolean operators. It quickly became one of the most popular search sites of the late 1990s.

History

AltaVista was developed at DEC's Network Systems Laboratory in Palo Alto, principally by Paul Flaherty, Louis Monier, and Michael Burrows. Its crawler, Scooter, and its fast indexing on Alpha servers showcased DEC's hardware. The service also launched Babel Fish in 1997, the first widely used free machine-translation website. After DEC was acquired by Compaq in 1998, AltaVista was spun off and later sold, passing through CMGI and Overture before being acquired by Yahoo! in 2003.

Decline and shutdown

Losing ground to Google's cleaner interface and better ranking, AltaVista's index was folded into Yahoo! Search, and Yahoo! shut the service down on 8 July 2013, redirecting its domain to Yahoo!'s main site.
