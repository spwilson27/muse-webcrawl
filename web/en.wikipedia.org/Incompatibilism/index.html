Incompatibilism

Incompatibilism is the view that a deterministic universe is logically incompatible with free will, and hence that if determinism is true, human beings do not have free will. It is opposed to compatibilism, which holds that free will and determinism can coexist.

Variants

Incompatibilists divide according to their response to the dilemma. Libertarians (in the metaphysical sense) accept free will and therefore reject determinism, often invoking agent causation or indeterministic processes. Hard determinists accept determinism and conclude that free will does not exist. Hard incompatibilists, such as Derk Pereboom, argue that free will is incompatible with both determinism and certain kinds of indeterminism, and is therefore impossible or at least not instantiated.

Arguments

A central argument is the consequence argument, developed by Peter van Inwagen: if determinism is true, our actions are consequences of laws of nature and events in the remote past, over which we have no control, so we have no control over their consequences either. Compatibilists reply by analysing free will in terms of reasons-responsiveness or the absence of coercion rather than alternative possibilities.
