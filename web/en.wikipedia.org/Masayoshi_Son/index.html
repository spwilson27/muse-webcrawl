Masayoshi Son

Masayoshi Son (born August 11, 1957) is a Japanese billionaire technology entrepreneur and investor, founder and CEO of SoftBank Group. Born in Tosu, Saga Prefecture, to a Korean-Japanese (Zainichi) family, he studied economics and computer science at the University of California, Berkeley.

Career

Son founded SoftBank in 1981 as a software distributor. An early, spectacularly profitable investment in Alibaba made him one of the world's richest investors; at the peak of the dot-com bubble in 2000 he was briefly among the wealthiest people alive, before losing much of that fortune in the crash. He rebuilt SoftBank into a global telecommunications and investment conglomerate, acquiring Sprint in the United States and the chip designer ARM Holdings in 2016.

Vision Fund

In 2017 Son launched the SoftBank Vision Fund, at nearly $100 billion the largest technology investment fund ever raised, taking large stakes in companies including Uber, WeWork, and DoorDash. The fund's losses on WeWork and the volatility of his aggressive, debt-funded strategy have drawn both admiration and criticism.
