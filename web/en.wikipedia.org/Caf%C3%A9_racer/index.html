Café racer

A café racer is a genre of sport motorcycle that originated among British motorcycle enthusiasts of the early 1960s in London. Riders — notably the "Rockers" subculture — modified standard production bikes for speed and handling, racing between transport cafés, and developed a distinctive minimalist style.

TypeSport motorcycle style
OriginLondon, England
Era1960s; revived 2000s–2010s

Characteristics

Typical café racer modifications included low, narrow clip-on handlebars, a single racing seat with a rear cowl, swept-back exhaust pipes, rear-set footpegs, and the removal of unnecessary bodywork to reduce weight. Engines were tuned toward achieving "the ton" — 100 mph — a celebrated benchmark among café racers.

History

The style grew up around cafés such as the Ace Cafe on London's North Circular Road, where young riders gathered and raced to other cafés and back before a jukebox record finished playing. British machines from Triumph, Norton, and BSA were the usual basis, sometimes combined as hybrids such as the Triton (Triumph engine in a Norton frame).

Revival

From the 2000s, café racers enjoyed a major revival in custom motorcycle culture worldwide, and manufacturers released factory models styled after the genre, including the Triumph Thruxton and Moto Guzzi V7 Racer.
