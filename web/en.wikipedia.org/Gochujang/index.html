Gochujang (고추장) is a savory, sweet, and spicy fermented condiment popular in Korean cooking. It is made from gochugaru (red chili powder), glutinous rice, meju (fermented soybean) powder, yeotgireum (barley malt powder), and salt. The sweetness comes from the starch of cooked glutinous rice, cultured with saccharifying enzymes during the fermentation process.

History

Chili peppers, native to the Americas, reached Korea in the late 16th century via Japan and China, following the Columbian exchange. Early Korean pepper pastes are mentioned in texts such as the 1766 Jeungbo sallim gyeongje. Traditionally, gochujang was fermented outdoors in earthenware pots called onggi, often for months or years; the village of Sunchang in North Jeolla Province is especially famed for its gochujang.

Uses

Gochujang is a staple seasoning used in dishes such as bibimbap, tteokbokki, stews (jjigae), soups, marinades for grilled meat, and dipping sauces such as ssamjang and chogochujang. Its heat level is graded in Korea by the Gochujang Hotness Unit (GHU) scale, and commercial products are commonly sold in grades from mild to extremely hot.

Nutrition

Gochujang contains capsaicin from chili peppers along with protein, sugars, and fermentation products. It is energy-dense and high in sodium, and is typically consumed in small quantities as a seasoning.
