Gérard Genette (7 June 1930 – 11 May 2018) was a French literary theorist and a leading figure of structuralism, widely regarded as one of the founders of narratology, the systematic study of narrative structure. He taught at the Sorbonne and the École pratique des hautes études and was associated with the journal Tel Quel and the journal Poétique, which he co-founded.

Narrative Discourse

Genette's most influential work, Figures III (1972), translated as Narrative Discourse, took Marcel Proust's In Search of Lost Time as its test case and gave narratology a durable technical vocabulary: the distinction between story, narrative, and narrating; analyses of tense (order, duration, frequency — including analepsis and prolepsis), mood (focalization: zero, internal, external), and voice (heterodiegetic and homodiegetic narrators, narrative levels such as extradiegetic and metadiegetic).

Transtextuality and paratexts

In Palimpsests (1982), Genette mapped "transtextuality" — all the relations a text bears to other texts — dividing it into intertextuality, paratextuality, metatextuality, architextuality, and hypertextuality (the relation of a text to an earlier hypotext, as in parody or sequel). Seuils (1987; Paratexts) analysed the thresholds that mediate a text — titles, prefaces, dedications, blurbs, interviews — coining terms now standard in book studies. His later works turned to aesthetics, including The Work of Art and Codicille (2009), a memoir-like coda.
