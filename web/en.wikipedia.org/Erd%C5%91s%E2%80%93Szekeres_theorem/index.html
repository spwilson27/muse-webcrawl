Erdős–Szekeres theorem

Erdős–Szekeres theorem

Field
Combinatorics; Ramsey theory
Proved by
Paul Erdős and George Szekeres, 1935

The Erdős–Szekeres theorem is a result in combinatorics, proved by Paul Erdős and George Szekeres in 1935, asserting that any sequence of distinct real numbers of length (r − 1)(s − 1) + 1 contains either a monotonically increasing subsequence of length r or a monotonically decreasing subsequence of length s.

Proof idea

Assign to each term the pair (i, d), where i is the length of the longest increasing subsequence ending at that term and d is the length of the longest decreasing subsequence ending there. Distinct terms receive distinct pairs, so if all i < r and all d < s, only (r − 1)(s − 1) pairs are available — a contradiction for a longer sequence.

Context

The theorem is an early landmark of Ramsey theory, showing that complete disorder is impossible in sufficiently large structures. The 1935 paper also introduced the Erdős–Szekeres convex polygon problem (the "happy ending problem"), asking for the smallest number of points in general position that must contain a convex n-gon; the problem acquired its nickname because it led to the marriage of Szekeres and Esther Klein.
