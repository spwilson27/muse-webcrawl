Belmont Stakes

The Belmont Stakes is an American Grade I stakes race for Thoroughbred horses held at Belmont Park in Elmont, New York. It is the third and final leg of the American Triple Crown, following the Kentucky Derby and the Preakness Stakes.

History

First run in 1867, the Belmont is the oldest of the three Triple Crown races. It is traditionally run over 1½ miles (2,414 m) on dirt, earning it the nickname "The Test of the Champion" because of its demanding distance.

Records

Secretariat set the still-standing world record for 1½ miles on dirt in 1973, winning by 31 lengths in 2:24 flat to complete the Triple Crown. Other famous winners include Man o' War, Citation, Seattle Slew, Affirmed and American Pharoah.

Traditions

The race is held on a Saturday in early June, five weeks after the Kentucky Derby. The winning horse is draped with a blanket of white carnations, and the official drink is the Belmont Jewel.
