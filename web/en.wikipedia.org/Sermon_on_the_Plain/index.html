Sermon on the Plain

In Christianity, the Sermon on the Plain refers to a set of teachings by Jesus in the Gospel of Luke, given on a level place (Luke 6:17–49) after Jesus had spent the night in prayer and selected the Twelve Apostles.

Content

The sermon includes the Beatitudes in their Lucan form—four blessings ("Blessed are you who are poor", "who hunger now", "who weep now", "when people hate you") balanced by four woes against the rich, the full, those who laugh, and the well-spoken-of. It also contains the commands to love one's enemies, the Golden Rule, teachings on judging others, and the parable of the wise and foolish builders.

Comparison with the Sermon on the Mount

Many scholars regard the Sermon on the Plain as Luke's version of Matthew's longer Sermon on the Mount, both drawing on a common source of Jesus's sayings, though others hold that Jesus delivered similar sermons on multiple occasions.
