ANSI art

ANSI art is a computer art form that was widely used at one time on BBSes. It is similar to ASCII art, but constructed from a larger set of 256 letters, numbers, and symbols — often referred to as extended ASCII — arranged with ANSI escape sequences that modify the foreground and background colours and cursor position.

Overview

ANSI art is considerably more flexible than ASCII art, because the particular character set it uses contains symbols intended for drawing, such as a variety of box-drawing characters and block characters that dither the colour. The popularity of ANSI art encouraged the creation of a powerful shareware package called ACiDDraw, which made it easy to create elaborate ANSI artwork.

TheDraw and editors

TheDraw, released in 1986 by Ian Davis of TheDraw Development Tools, was the first ANSI editor to support fonts and animations. Later editors such as ACiDDraw and PabloDraw allowed artists to create detailed screens for bulletin board systems throughout the late 1980s and early 1990s.

Scene

Art groups such as ACiD Productions, iCE Advertisements, and later Blocktronics released monthly artpacks collecting the work of ANSI artists. The form declined as BBSes gave way to the World Wide Web, but it survives in retro-computing communities.
