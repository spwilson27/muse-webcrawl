William Tell

OccupationMarksman, folk hero
Known forShooting an apple off his son's head; resisting Habsburg rule
RegionCanton of Uri, Switzerland

William Tell (German: Wilhelm Tell) is a legendary folk hero of Switzerland, said to have lived in the canton of Uri in the early 14th century. His story became a founding myth of the Swiss Confederacy and a universal symbol of resistance to tyranny.

The legend

According to the tale, the Habsburg bailiff Albrecht Gessler placed his hat on a pole in Altdorf and demanded that passers-by bow to it. Tell refused and was arrested; Gessler ordered him to shoot an apple off his young son's head with his crossbow, promising freedom if he succeeded. Tell split the apple with one bolt, having hidden a second bolt to kill Gessler had he harmed the boy. Later escaping custody, Tell ambushed and shot Gessler, sparking a rebellion.

In culture

The legend inspired Friedrich Schiller's play Wilhelm Tell (1804) and Gioachino Rossini's opera Guillaume Tell (1829), whose overture became famous worldwide.
