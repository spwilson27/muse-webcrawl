Poyang Lake

Poyang Lake (Chinese: 鄱阳湖; pinyin: Póyáng Hú) is the largest freshwater lake in China, located in Jiujiang, in the northern part of Jiangxi Province. The lake is fed by the Gan, Xin, and Xiu rivers and drains into the Yangtze River through a channel at its northern end.

Geography

The surface area of Poyang Lake fluctuates dramatically with the seasons. In the wet season it can reach roughly 3,210 to 4,400 square kilometres, but in the dry season it shrinks to less than 1,000 square kilometres, exposing vast mudflats and grasslands and a network of smaller residual lakes. This fluctuation is driven by monsoon rainfall and the water level of the Yangtze; since the completion of the Three Gorges Dam, altered flow regimes and sand dredging have been implicated in lower dry-season water levels.

Ecology

Poyang Lake is an internationally important wetland, designated a Ramsar site. Its wintering grounds host hundreds of thousands of migratory waterbirds, including about 98 per cent of the world's critically endangered Siberian cranes, as well as white-naped cranes, swan geese and many duck species. The lake historically supported the Yangtze finless porpoise; the baiji (Yangtze river dolphin) was declared functionally extinct in 2006. Fishing bans, including a ten-year moratorium on fishing in the Yangtze basin that began in 2020, have been introduced to aid ecological recovery.

History

In 1363 the Battle of Lake Poyang, one of the largest naval battles in history, was fought on the lake between the rebel forces of Zhu Yuanzhang and Chen Youliang during the collapse of the Yuan dynasty; Zhu's victory paved the way for his founding of the Ming dynasty. The lake and its wetlands have long supported fishing communities and the famous birdwatching area around Duchang and the Poyang Lake Nature Reserve.
