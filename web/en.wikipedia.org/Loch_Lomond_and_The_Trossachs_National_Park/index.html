Loch Lomond and The Trossachs National Park

Loch Lomond and The Trossachs National Park (Scottish Gaelic: Pàirc Nàiseanta Loch Laomainn is nan Tròisichean) is a national park in Scotland centred on Loch Lomond, including the hills and glens of the Trossachs and surrounding areas.

Establishment

It was the first of Scotland's two national parks, established by the Scottish Parliament in 2002 under the National Parks (Scotland) Act 2000, and is managed by a national park authority whose aims cover conservation, sustainable use, public enjoyment and sustainable economic development of the area.

Geography

The park covers about 1,865 square kilometres and straddles the Highland Boundary Fault. It includes 21 Munros (mountains over 3,000 feet), 19 Corbetts, two forest parks and the Falls of Dochart, as well as lochs including Lomond, Katrine, Venachar and Lubnaig. The Trossachs — a wooded glen and hills area between Callander and Aberfoyle — was popularised by Sir Walter Scott's poem The Lady of the Lake and is sometimes called the Highlands in miniature.

Access and recreation

Close to Glasgow and Stirling, the park is one of Scotland's most visited countryside areas, popular for hillwalking, climbing, boating, cycling and camping; a camping management byelaw system operates in busy shoreline zones.
