Connecticut

Connecticut is a state in the New England region of the northeastern United States. It is bordered by Rhode Island to the east, Massachusetts to the north, New York to the west, and Long Island Sound to the south. Its capital is Hartford and its most populous city is Bridgeport. Connecticut was one of the original Thirteen Colonies and the fifth state to ratify the United States Constitution, on 9 January 1788.

History

Dutch traders explored the Connecticut River valley from 1614, and English settlers from Massachusetts founded the river towns of Windsor, Wethersfield and Hartford in the 1630s. The Fundamental Orders of 1639, adopted by those towns, are sometimes described as the first written constitution in America, giving Connecticut its nickname, the "Constitution State". The colony's 1662 charter was so generous that, legend holds, it was hidden in a Hartford oak tree — the Charter Oak — to keep it from a royal governor in 1687.

Geography and economy

Connecticut is the third-smallest state by area but among the most densely populated, with about 3.6 million residents. The Connecticut River bisects the state from north to south. Historically a centre of manufacturing — firearms, clocks, brass and machine tools — its modern economy rests on finance and insurance (Hartford is a longtime insurance capital), healthcare, education and defence manufacturing, including submarines built at Groton. Yale University, founded in 1701 in New Haven, is one of the state's best-known institutions.
