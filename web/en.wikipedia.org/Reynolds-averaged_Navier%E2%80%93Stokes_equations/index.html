Reynolds-averaged Navier–Stokes equations

The Reynolds-averaged Navier–Stokes equations (RANS equations) are time-averaged equations of fluid motion used in computational fluid dynamics to model turbulent flow. Named after Osborne Reynolds, who proposed decomposing flow variables into mean and fluctuating components in 1895, they remain the workhorse of industrial CFD.

Reynolds decomposition

Each instantaneous variable is split into a time-averaged part and a fluctuation: ui = Ui + u′i. Averaging the Navier–Stokes equations produces terms involving correlations of the fluctuations, the Reynolds stresses −ρu′iu′j, which are unknown — the turbulence closure problem.

Closure models

The Boussinesq hypothesis relates the Reynolds stresses to the mean strain rate through an eddy viscosity. Popular closures include the one-equation Spalart–Allmaras model and the two-equation k-ε and k-ω models, which solve transport equations for turbulent kinetic energy and its dissipation or specific dissipation.

Applications

RANS methods are used throughout aerospace, automotive, marine and process engineering because they are far cheaper computationally than direct numerical simulation or large eddy simulation, at the cost of modelling error in complex separated flows.
