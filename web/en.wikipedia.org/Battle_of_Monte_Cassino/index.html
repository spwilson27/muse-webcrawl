Battle of Monte Cassino

The destroyed Benedictine abbey of Monte Cassino, spring 1944.

Date17 January – 18 May 1944
LocationMonte Cassino, Italy
ResultAllied victory
BelligerentsAllied forces vs. Nazi Germany

The Battle of Monte Cassino was a series of four costly assaults by the Allies against the German Winter Line in Italy, fought between January and May 1944. The monastery hill of Monte Cassino, crowned by a Benedictine abbey founded in 529 AD, anchored the German Gustav Line and blocked the road to Rome through the Liri Valley.

The first battles

The first assault in January 1944, by American, British and French forces under the U.S. Fifth Army, gained footholds across the Rapido and Garigliano rivers but failed to take the heights. The second battle, in February, was preceded by the controversial Allied bombing of the abbey on 15 February, which reduced it to rubble — rubble that German paratroopers of the 1st Parachute Division then turned into an even stronger defensive position. A third assault in March, involving New Zealand and Indian troops, destroyed the town of Cassino but again failed to dislodge the defenders.

Operation Diadem

The fourth battle, Operation Diadem, began on 11 May 1944 as part of a general Allied spring offensive. While British and American forces attacked along the coast and through the Liri Valley, French Expeditionary Corps troops under General Juin broke through the supposedly impassable Aurunci Mountains, and the Polish II Corps under General Władysław Anders stormed the monastery heights from the north. On 18 May, Polish troops raised their flag over the ruins of the abbey.

Aftermath

The fall of Monte Cassino opened the road to Rome, which was liberated on 4 June 1944. The four battles cost the Allies some 55,000 casualties and the Germans around 20,000. The abbey was rebuilt after the war and reconsecrated in 1964; war cemeteries of many nations, including a large Polish cemetery on the slope below the monastery, commemorate the dead.
