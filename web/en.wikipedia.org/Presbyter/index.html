Presbyter

Presbyter is an honorific title for Christian clergy, from the Greek word presbyteros (πρεσβύτερος), meaning "elder". In the New Testament, presbyters appear as leaders of local Christian congregations, and the office is closely associated with that of the bishop (episkopos, "overseer").

Etymology and early usage

The term derives from the Greek comparative of presbys, "old man". In the Jewish communities of the ancient Mediterranean world, elders (presbyters) formed councils that governed congregations; the early Christian churches adopted similar structures. The Book of Acts and the pastoral epistles refer to presbyters appointed in churches, and early Christian writings such as the Didache and the letters of Ignatius of Antioch describe their role alongside bishops and deacons.

Development of the office

As the episcopate developed into a distinct office above the presbyterate in the second century, presbyters came to be understood as the second order of ordained ministry: the priesthood. In the Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox and Anglican traditions, the word is now generally equivalent to "priest", and ordination to the presbyterate is ordination to the priesthood.

Presbyterian usage

In Presbyterian and other Reformed churches, the term presbyter is applied to both teaching elders (ministers) and ruling elders, reflecting a polity ("presbyterian polity") in which churches are governed by assemblies of presbyters rather than by bishops. The word "Presbyterian" itself comes from this usage, and "presbytery" denotes both a regional governing body and, in Catholic usage, the residence of a parish priest.
