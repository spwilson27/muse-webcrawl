Kyklos

Key author: Polybius, Histories Book VI
Concept: Cycle of constitutions (anacyclosis)

The kyklos (Greek: κύκλος, "cycle") is a term used by ancient Greek political thinkers, notably Polybius, for the cycle of political change through which constitutions pass. Polybius set out the theory in Book VI of his Histories.

The cycle

In Polybius's scheme (anacyclosis), government begins as monarchy, which degenerates into tyranny; tyranny is overthrown by aristocracy, which degenerates into oligarchy; oligarchy gives way to democracy, which degenerates into ochlocracy (mob rule), from which a single ruler again emerges, restarting the cycle.

Influence

Polybius argued that Rome escaped the cycle's instability through its mixed constitution, combining consuls (monarchic), Senate (aristocratic), and assemblies (democratic). The theory influenced later thinkers including Machiavelli and the framers of modern constitutional systems. Kyklos is also the name of an international journal of social sciences founded in 1947.
