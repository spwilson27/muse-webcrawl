Aspergillus flavus

Aspergillus flavus is a saprotrophic and pathogenic fungus with a cosmopolitan distribution. It is best known for its colonisation of cereal grains, legumes, and tree nuts. It is also a human and animal pathogen, causing aspergillosis of the lungs and producing the potent carcinogen aflatoxin.

Description

The fungus grows as a yellow-green mould with rough, spiny conidiophores bearing chains of conidia from both metulae and phialides. It thrives in warm, humid conditions and frequently infects maize and peanut crops in the field, as well as stored grain after harvest.

Aflatoxins

Many strains produce aflatoxins, among the most potent naturally occurring carcinogens known. Aflatoxin contamination of food causes acute aflatoxicosis and chronic liver disease and is a major food-safety concern in tropical countries. Outbreaks have caused hundreds of deaths, notably in Kenya in 2004.

Medical significance

A. flavus is the second most common cause of invasive aspergillosis after Aspergillus fumigatus, and it frequently infects the paranasal sinuses and skin. Control in agriculture uses atoxigenic strains of the fungus as a biocontrol to outcompete toxigenic ones.
