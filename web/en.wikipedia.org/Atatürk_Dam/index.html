Atatürk Dam


The Atatürk Dam is the centrepiece of Turkey's Southeastern Anatolia Project.

The Atatürk Dam is a zoned rock-fill dam with a central clay core on the Euphrates River in southeastern Turkey, near Bozova in Şanlıurfa Province. Completed in 1990 and named for Mustafa Kemal Atatürk, it is the largest of the 22 dams of the Southeastern Anatolia Project (GAP) and among the largest dams in the world by fill volume.

Characteristics

The dam is 166 metres high and 1,819 metres long; its reservoir, Lake Atatürk, covers about 817 square kilometres and stores some 48.7 billion cubic metres of water. Its power station, with eight turbines totalling 2,400 MW, produces around 8.9 billion kWh annually, a significant share of Turkey's hydroelectric output. Water from the reservoir feeds the Şanlıurfa irrigation tunnels, among the world's largest, supplying the Harran plain and other irrigation schemes.

Impact

Filling the reservoir displaced tens of thousands of people and submerged the town of Samsat and numerous villages and archaeological sites. The dam's control of the Euphrates has been a recurring source of tension with downstream Syria and Iraq over water flows.
