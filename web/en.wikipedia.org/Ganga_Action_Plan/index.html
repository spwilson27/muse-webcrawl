Ganga Action Plan

The Ganga Action Plan (GAP) was a programme launched by the Government of India in 1985 to reduce the pollution of the Ganges river. It was one of India's first large river-cleaning initiatives and sought to improve the river's water quality by intercepting, diverting, and treating municipal sewage that flowed into it from the cities along its banks.

Background

By the early 1980s the Ganges, although sacred to Hindus and the lifeline of hundreds of millions of people, had become heavily polluted by untreated domestic sewage, industrial effluents, and the disposal of bodies and waste along its banks. The Central Pollution Control Board identified the discharge of untreated municipal wastewater as the largest source of pollution.

Phases

The first phase of the plan, launched in 1985 under Prime Minister Rajiv Gandhi, covered 25 major towns in Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, and West Bengal and concentrated on building sewage interception works and treatment plants. A second phase, approved in the 1990s, extended the approach to tributaries including the Yamuna, Gomti, and Damodar, and to additional towns. The National River Conservation Directorate oversaw implementation.

Assessment and legacy

The programme built considerable sewage treatment capacity, but evaluations found that it fell short of its water-quality goals: treatment capacity lagged behind the growth of urban wastewater, many plants suffered from power cuts and poor maintenance, and industrial and diffuse pollution were incompletely addressed. The Ganga Action Plan was later absorbed into the National River Conservation Plan and, in 2014, succeeded in ambition by the Namami Gange programme, which adopted a broader basin-level approach.
