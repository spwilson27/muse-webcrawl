Sgùrr Alasdair is the highest peak of the Black Cuillin range and of the Isle of Skye in Scotland, rising to 992 metres (3,255 ft) above sea level. It is classified as a Munro and, because Skye is an island, its prominence equals its height, making it the most prominent peak on Skye and one of the most prominent in the British Isles. The Gaelic name means "Alasdair's peak", honouring Sheriff Alexander Nicolson of Husabost, who made the first recorded ascent in 1873.

Geography and geology

The mountain stands at the head of Coire Lagan on the southern side of the main Cuillin ridge, overlooking Loch Coruisk and the sea loch of Loch Brittle. Like the rest of the Black Cuillin, it is formed of gabbro, a rough, dark igneous rock that gives excellent grip for scramblers and climbers and contains iron compounds that can disturb compasses. The summit is a narrow, rocky point reached only by scrambling, and the peak is usually climbed as part of the greater Cuillin ridge traverse, widely regarded as one of the finest mountaineering expeditions in Britain.

Ascent routes

The normal route starts from Glen Brittle and ascends Coire Lagan, then climbs the Great Stone Chute — a long scree gully infamous among walkers — which leads directly to the summit ridge a short distance from the top. A more sporting alternative follows the southwest ridge. Despite its modest height, Sgùrr Alasdair demands care: the Cuillin weather is notoriously changeable, route-finding on the ridge is intricate, and the descent of the Stone Chute is loose and tiring.
