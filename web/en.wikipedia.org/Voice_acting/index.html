Voice acting

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Voice acting is the art of performing a character or providing information to an audience via one's voice. Performers are called voice actors or voice artists. Examples of voice work include animated, off-stage and off-screen characters and narration for television, film, radio, audiobooks, video games, dubbing of foreign-language productions, and automated announcements.

History

The craft grew with radio drama in the 1920s–30s, when performers such as Orson Welles achieved fame by voice alone, and with animation: Mel Blanc, "the man of a thousand voices", defined the voices of Bugs Bunny, Daffy Duck and many other Warner Bros. characters. Reginald Fessenden's 1906 Christmas Eve broadcast is sometimes cited as the first voice transmission intended as entertainment. In Japan, voice acting (seiyū) developed into a major celebrity profession tied to the anime industry.

Techniques and work

Voice actors work from scripts in sound studios, often recording alone and relying on direction, "wild lines" and multiple takes. Roles demand control of pitch, timbre, accent, pacing and characterisation, and performers frequently play several characters in one production. Dubbing into other languages requires matching lip movements and performance intent — an area that became contentious in the 2020s as AI voice synthesis entered commercial use, prompting union action such as the 2024 SAG-AFTRA video game strike over AI protections.
