Restriction of Hazardous Substances Directive

The Restriction of Hazardous Substances Directive (RoHS), Directive 2002/95/EC, is a European Union directive restricting the use of specific hazardous materials in electrical and electronic equipment. It took effect on 1 July 2006 and was recast as RoHS 2 (Directive 2011/65/EU).

Adopted2003 (effective 2006)
RecastRoHS 2, 2011/65/EU
ScopeElectrical and electronic equipment in the EU

Restricted substances

The directive limits, by weight in homogeneous materials, lead (0.1%), mercury (0.1%), cadmium (0.01%), hexavalent chromium (0.1%), polybrominated biphenyls (PBB, 0.1%) and polybrominated diphenyl ethers (PBDE, 0.1%). RoHS 2 added four phthalates (DEHP, BBP, DBP, DIBP) from 2019. Certain applications are exempt, such as lead in some solders and alloys where substitution is impractical.

Impact

RoHS drove the electronics industry's shift to lead-free solder and is closely linked with the WEEE Directive on waste electrical equipment. Similar "China RoHS" and other national rules followed.
