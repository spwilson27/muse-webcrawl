Zachary Taylor

Zachary Taylor (November 24, 1784 – July 9, 1850) was an American military leader and statesman who served as the 12th president of the United States, in office from 1849 until his death in 1850. Taylor was a career officer in the United States Army, rising to the rank of major general and becoming a national hero through victories in the Mexican–American War, particularly at Palo Alto, Monterrey and Buena Vista.

Despite his vague political beliefs, voters turned to the Whig Party's popular general at the polls; Taylor won the 1848 election. As president, his top priority was preserving the Union against threats of secession over the issue of slavery in the territories. He died suddenly of a stomach disease after sixteen months in office, and was succeeded by his vice president, Millard Fillmore.

Military career

Taylor received his first commission in 1808 and served in the War of 1812, the Black Hawk War, and the Second Seminole War. In 1846, after the annexation of Texas, President James K. Polk ordered Taylor to the Rio Grande, where fighting with Mexico opened the war. Taylor's victory at the Battle of Buena Vista in February 1847, against a much larger Mexican force, made him a national figure and a leading presidential contender.

Presidency

Taylor took office in March 1849. He urged settlers in New Mexico and California to bypass the territorial stage and draft state constitutions, hoping to avoid the slavery question; the resulting crisis contributed to the debates that produced the Compromise of 1850, passed after his death. Taylor fell ill after a Fourth of July ceremony in 1850 and died on July 9; the exact cause was long debated, and a 1991 exhumation found no evidence of poisoning.
