Write-off

A write-off is a reduction of the recognised value of something, in accounting the removal or reduction of an asset's book value when it has become worthless or uncollectable, with the loss charged against earnings.

Uses

Businesses write off bad debts that customers will not pay, obsolete or damaged inventory, and impaired assets whose recoverable amount has fallen. Banks write off loans after exhausting collection efforts, though borrowers may still legally owe the debt. A write-down is a partial reduction; a write-off removes the value entirely.

Tax

Tax systems generally allow deductions for genuine business write-offs, subject to rules proving the asset's loss of value. In everyday usage, a vehicle damaged beyond economical repair is called an insurance write-off.
