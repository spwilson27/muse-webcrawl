Crete (Greek: Κρήτη, Kríti) is the largest and most populous of the Greek islands, the 88th largest island in the world and the fifth largest island in the Mediterranean Sea, after Sicily, Sardinia, Cyprus and Corsica. It bounds the southern border of the Aegean Sea, with the Sea of Crete to the north and the Libyan Sea to the south.

History

Crete was the centre of the Minoan civilization (c. 2700–1420 BCE), the earliest known civilization in Europe, whose palace complexes at Knossos, Phaistos, Malia and Zakros are major archaeological sites. The island subsequently passed through Mycenaean, classical Greek, Roman, Byzantine, Venetian and Ottoman rule before unifying with Greece in 1913. In May 1941, Crete was the scene of the Battle of Crete, the first major airborne invasion in history, when German paratroopers captured the island after fierce fighting against Allied and Greek forces and Cretan resistance fighters.

Geography

The island is elongated, stretching 260 km from east to west, with three mountain ranges including the White Mountains (Lefka Ori) and Mount Ida, the island's highest peak. Deep gorges cut through the ranges, most famously the Samaria Gorge, one of the longest in Europe. The Messara Plain in the south is a major agricultural region, growing olives, grapes and citrus.

Economy and culture

Tourism is the dominant industry, alongside agriculture — Crete is a major producer of olive oil. The island is known for its distinctive cuisine (see Cuisine), mantinades (rhyming couplets), and dances such as the pentozali. Heraklion, Chania, Rethymno and Agios Nikolaos are its principal cities.
