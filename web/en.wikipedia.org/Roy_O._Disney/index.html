Roy O. Disney

Roy O. Disney
Born: June 24, 1893, Chicago, Illinois
Died: December 20, 1971, Burbank, California
Occupation: Businessman, studio executive
Known for: Co-founding The Walt Disney Company

Roy Oliver Disney (1893–1971) was an American businessman who, together with his younger brother Walt Disney, co-founded the Disney Brothers Studio, today The Walt Disney Company. While Walt supplied the creative vision, Roy managed the company's finances and business affairs throughout its formative decades.

Role in the company

Roy served as the studio's financial chief from its founding in 1923, arranging the loans and deals that sustained it through the Depression and beyond, and later became chief executive and chairman. He generally avoided the spotlight, describing himself as the brother who made sure the money was there.

Walt Disney World

After Walt's death in 1966, Roy postponed retirement to supervise completion of the Florida project, insisting it be named Walt Disney World in his brother's honour. He dedicated the resort at its opening on October 1, 1971, and died less than three months later.
