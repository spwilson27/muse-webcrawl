History of art

The history of art is the study of visual art — painting, sculpture, architecture, and related media — across time and cultures. It encompasses the earliest known images of the Paleolithic era, the art of the ancient civilisations, the medieval, Renaissance and modern periods in Europe, and the rich independent traditions of Asia, Africa, the Islamic world, Oceania and the Americas, as well as contemporary global art.

Prehistoric and ancient art

The oldest known art includes the cave paintings of Sulawesi and of European sites such as Chauvet (c. 36,000 years old) and Lascaux, and small sculptures like the Venus figurines. With settled agriculture came monumental art: the pyramids and temples of Egypt, Mesopotamian palaces and ziggurats, the sculpture of Greece — which developed from Archaic stiffness to Classical naturalism — and Rome, which spread portraiture and engineering across an empire. In parallel, ancient China produced bronzes, jade and calligraphy; India created Buddhist and Hindu sculpture; and Mesoamerican civilisations raised pyramids and carved stelae.

Medieval art

After Rome's fall, Byzantine art developed icons and mosaics, Islamic art flourished in calligraphy, geometry and architecture, and early medieval Europe produced Insular illuminated manuscripts such as the Book of Kells. Romanesque and then Gothic styles transformed church architecture, while in Asia Tang and Song painting, and later Japanese woodblock traditions, reached high levels of refinement.

Renaissance to the nineteenth century

The Italian Renaissance revived classical ideals with perspective, anatomy and humanism (Masaccio, Leonardo, Michelangelo, Raphael), spreading north to Dürer, Bruegel and the Dutch Golden Age painters such as Rembrandt and Vermeer. The Baroque (Caravaggio, Bernini, Rubens) gave way to Rococo, then to Neoclassicism and Romanticism as reactions to the Enlightenment and revolution. In the nineteenth century Realism, Impressionism and Post-Impressionism broke with academic tradition and began the modern trajectory.

Modern and contemporary art

The twentieth century saw an accelerating succession of movements — Fauvism, Cubism, abstraction (Kandinsky, Mondrian, Malevich), Dada, Surrealism, Abstract Expressionism, Pop art, Minimalism and Conceptual art — alongside the Bauhaus's fusion of art and design. Photography and film became major art forms. Since the later twentieth century, art has become increasingly global and plural, engaging installation, performance, video and digital media, while art history as a discipline — founded by figures such as Vasari, Winckelmann and Panofsky — has expanded beyond its traditional Western canon to a worldwide perspective.
