José Venustiano Carranza de la Garza (29 December 1859 – 21 May 1920) was a Mexican landowner, politician, and revolutionary leader who served as President of Mexico from 1917 to 1920. As "First Chief" of the Constitutionalist Army, he led the faction that triumphed in the Mexican Revolution, and his government promulgated the Constitution of 1917, which remains Mexico's fundamental law.

Early life and career

Carranza was born into a wealthy landowning family in Cuatro Ciénegas, Coahuila. He entered politics under the regime of Porfirio Díaz, serving as mayor, state deputy, senator, and interim governor of Coahuila. He supported Francisco I. Madero's anti-reelectionist movement and, after Madero became president in 1911, was elected governor of Coahuila.

Mexican Revolution

When General Victoriano Huerta overthrew and murdered Madero in February 1913, Carranza refused to recognize the new regime and issued the Plan de Guadalupe, calling for Huerta's removal and the restoration of constitutional order. As First Chief of the Constitutionalist Army, he coordinated forces including Álvaro Obregón in the northwest and, uneasily, Pancho Villa in Chihuahua and Emiliano Zapata in the south. After Huerta fell in 1914, Carranza's relations with Villa and Zapata collapsed into civil war among the revolutionaries; Obregón's victories at Celaya in 1915 secured Constitutionalist supremacy. The United States extended de facto recognition to Carranza's government in 1915, and he resisted the U.S. Punitive Expedition of 1916–1917 sent after Villa's raid on Columbus, New Mexico.

Presidency

A constituent congress at Querétaro produced the Constitution of 1917, which went beyond Carranza's comparatively conservative draft to include sweeping provisions on land reform (Article 27), labor rights (Article 123), anticlerical restrictions, and national ownership of subsoil resources. Carranza was elected president and took office on 1 May 1917. His administration faced continuing insurgency, fiscal weakness, and diplomatic friction, particularly over oil. Conservative in temperament, he implemented the constitution's social provisions only haltingly.

Death

In 1920 Carranza attempted to impose a civilian successor, Ignacio Bonillas, over the ambitions of Obregón. Sonora's leaders issued the Plan de Agua Prieta, and Carranza fled Mexico City by train toward Veracruz. Abandoning the train, he was murdered on the night of 20–21 May 1920 at Tlaxcalantongo, Puebla, by forces under Rodolfo Herrero. He was buried in Mexico City, and his remains were later moved to the Monument to the Revolution.
