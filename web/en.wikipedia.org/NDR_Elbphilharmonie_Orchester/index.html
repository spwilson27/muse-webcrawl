The NDR Elbphilharmonie Orchestra (German: NDR Elbphilharmonie Orchester) is a German radio orchestra. Based in Hamburg, it is affiliated with the Norddeutscher Rundfunk (NDR), the public broadcaster for northern Germany. Since January 2017 the orchestra has been the resident orchestra of the Elbphilharmonie concert hall in Hamburg's HafenCity.

History

The orchestra was founded in 1945 as the symphony orchestra of Radio Hamburg (NWDR), the broadcasting service established under British occupation. Its first chief conductor was Hans Schmidt-Isserstedt, who led the ensemble for more than two decades and shaped its reputation for clarity and precision. Günter Wand served as chief conductor from 1982 to 1990 and remained honorary conductor for life; his recordings of Bruckner and Brahms with the orchestra are widely admired.

Later chief conductors included John Eliot Gardiner, Herbert Blomstedt, Christoph Eschenbach, Christoph von Dohnányi, and Thomas Hengelbrock. In 2016, ahead of the move into the Elbphilharmonie, the orchestra was renamed from NDR Sinfonieorchester to its present name. Alan Gilbert became chief conductor in 2019.

Activities

The orchestra performs subscription concerts in Hamburg and throughout the NDR broadcast region, tours internationally, and makes recordings. Under the Elbphilharmonie's opening in January 2017 — with a premiere by Wolfgang Rihm — the orchestra gained a large new international audience, and it regularly appears at festivals such as the Schleswig-Holstein Musik Festival.
