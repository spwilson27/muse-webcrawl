Parliament House, Canberra

Parliament House is the meeting place of the Parliament of Australia, located in Canberra, the capital of Australia. The building, designed by the firm Mitchell/Giurgola & Thorp and opened on 9 May 1988 by Queen Elizabeth II, sits atop Capital Hill and replaced the Provisional Parliament House (now Old Parliament House), which had served since 1927. At the time of its construction it was one of the most expensive buildings in the world, costing more than A$1.1 billion.

Design

The building is largely built into the hill itself, so that the summit of Capital Hill is preserved; visitors can walk on the grassed roof, a deliberate symbol that the people stand above their representatives. Its plan follows two boomerang-shaped walls enclosing the House of Representatives chamber on the east and the Senate chamber on the west, separated by the Members' Hall. Above the hall rises an 81-metre stainless-steel flag mast flying an Australian flag about the size of a double-decker bus. The interior incorporates Australian timbers, stone, and commissioned artworks throughout, and the building contains some 4,700 rooms.

Chambers and functions

The House of Representatives chamber is decorated in eucalyptus green and seats 151 members; the Senate chamber is in ochre red, reflecting the colours of the older Westminster-derived chambers, and seats 76 senators. The Great Hall, hung with a large tapestry after a painting by Arthur Boyd, hosts state banquets and major national events. The building also contains the prime minister's office, ministerial suites, the press gallery, and committee rooms, and it is open to the public most days.
