Tomb of Cyrus

Tomb of Cyrus
Location: Pasargadae, Fars Province, Iran
Coordinates: 30°11′38″N 53°10′2″E
Material: Limestone
Built: c. 530 BC

The Tomb of Cyrus (Persian: آرامگاه کوروش بزرگ, Ārāmgāh-e Kūroš-e Bozorg) is a monument thought to be the tomb of Cyrus the Great, the founder of the Achaemenid Empire. It is located in Pasargadae, an archaeological site in the Fars Province of Iran, roughly 90 km (56 mi) to the northeast of the modern city of Shiraz.

History

According to historical sources, following Cyrus's death in 530 BC, his body was embalmed and placed in a golden coffin within the tomb. The tomb was visited by Alexander the Great after his conquest of Persia in 330 BC; Alexander ordered its restoration after finding it had been looted, an account recorded by the Greek historian Arrian.

The mausoleum is thought to have been built shortly after Cyrus's death. The structure consists of a stepped base of six receding tiers surmounted by a rectangular chamber with a gabled roof — a design that some scholars link to earlier Anatolian or Mesopotamian traditions, executed in the fine limestone masonry characteristic of Pasargadae.

Description

The tomb stands 11.1 m (36 ft) high. The burial chamber, measuring approximately 3.17 m by 2.11 m, is reached by a narrow doorway and once contained the sarcophagus. The building rests on a plinth of six steps. The monument's austere geometric form has made it one of the most iconic surviving structures of the Achaemenid world.

Modern significance

The tomb is a site of Iranian national pride and a popular pilgrimage destination, particularly during Nowruz celebrations. It forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site of Pasargadae, inscribed in 2004. Nearby stand the remains of Cyrus's palace complex and the citadel known as Tall-e Takht.
