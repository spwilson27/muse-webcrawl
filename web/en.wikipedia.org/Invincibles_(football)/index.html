Invincibles (football)

In association football, the Invincibles is a nickname applied to teams that complete a league season undefeated. The term was first used for Preston North End, winners of the inaugural Football League season in 1888–89, and is most famously associated with the Arsenal team of 2003–04.

Preston North End, 1888–89

Preston North End, managed by William Sudell, won the first Football League championship without losing a match (18 wins, 4 draws) and also won the FA Cup that season without conceding a goal — an unbeaten "double" that earned them the name "The Invincibles" (and "The Old Invincibles").

Arsenal, 2003–04

Arsène Wenger's Arsenal won the 2003–04 Premier League with 26 wins and 12 draws, becoming the only team to go through a 38-game English top-flight season unbeaten. The side, captained by Patrick Vieira and featuring Thierry Henry, Robert Pires, and Dennis Bergkamp, extended its unbeaten league run to 49 matches before losing to Manchester United in October 2004. The Premier League commissioned a special gold version of its trophy to mark the achievement.

Other unbeaten teams

Other celebrated unbeaten league seasons include Celtic's 2016–17 "Invincible treble" under Brendan Rodgers in Scotland, Juventus's 2011–12 Serie A campaign, Ajax in 1994–95, Milan in 1991–92, and Bayer Leverkusen's 2023–24 Bundesliga season under Xabi Alonso.
