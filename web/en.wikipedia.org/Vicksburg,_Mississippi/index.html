Vicksburg is a city in and the county seat of Warren County, Mississippi, United States. Located on bluffs above the Mississippi River near its confluence with the Yazoo River, it was a major river port and commercial centre in the 19th century. The population was 21,573 at the 2020 census.

History

The site was occupied by Indigenous peoples for centuries and was part of the territory of the Natchez and later the Choctaw. The city traces its founding to the Methodist minister Newitt Vick, who established a mission and laid out a town in 1819 on land he had purchased in 1811; the town, incorporated in 1825, took his name after his death.

Vicksburg grew wealthy on cotton shipped down the Mississippi, becoming one of the most commercially important cities in the South.

Civil War

During the American Civil War, Vicksburg's position on a hairpin turn of the Mississippi made it a Confederate fortress guarding the river — the "Gibraltar of the Confederacy". Union general Ulysses S. Grant's Vicksburg campaign culminated in a 47-day siege; the city surrendered on 4 July 1863, the day after the Confederate defeat at Gettysburg. Together the two victories marked a turning point of the war, and the fall of Vicksburg gave the Union control of the Mississippi River, splitting the Confederacy in two. The battlefield is preserved as Vicksburg National Military Park, one of the city's major attractions.

Geography and economy

The city sits atop the loess bluffs of the Yazoo basin. In 1876 the Mississippi River changed course, leaving Vicksburg on the Yazoo until the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers completed the Yazoo Diversion Canal in 1903, restoring river access. The Corps' Engineer Research and Development Center is a major employer. Riverboat casinos, tourism centred on Civil War history, and the port contribute to the local economy. Vicksburg is also remembered as the place where Coca-Cola was first bottled, in 1894, an event commemorated by the Biedenharn Coca-Cola Museum.
