Fourth Geneva Convention

The Geneva Conventions were adopted on 12 August 1949 in Geneva, Switzerland.

The Fourth Geneva Convention — formally the Geneva Convention relative to the Protection of Civilian Persons in Time of War — is one of the four treaties of the Geneva Conventions of 1949. Adopted on 12 August 1949 and in force from 21 October 1950, it defines humanitarian protection for civilians in and around a war zone, including in occupied territory. It has been ratified by 196 states and forms a core part of international humanitarian law.

Background

Earlier conventions had protected wounded soldiers, shipwrecked combatants and prisoners of war, but the mass suffering of civilians during the Second World War — occupation, deportation and extermination — demonstrated the need for a dedicated civilian convention. The Fourth Convention was the first treaty of its kind devoted principally to civilians.

Key provisions

"Protected persons" are civilians who find themselves in the hands of a party to the conflict or occupying power of which they are not nationals. The Convention guarantees humane treatment without adverse distinction and prohibits murder, torture, corporal punishment, mutilation, medical experiments, and any other brutality. It forbids collective punishment, reprisals against protected persons and their property, the taking of hostages, and the individual or mass forcible transfer or deportation of protected persons from occupied territory. Article 49 also prohibits an occupying power from transferring parts of its own civilian population into the territory it occupies.

Further articles govern the treatment of internees, require that children, hospital and safety zones receive special protection, and set out the rights of civilians to food, medical supplies and relief consignments. Serious violations defined in the treaty are termed "grave breaches" and include wilful killing, torture or inhuman treatment, and extensive destruction of property not justified by military necessity.

Common Article 3 and later development

Common Article 3, shared by all four Conventions, extends minimum humanitarian standards to armed conflicts "not of an international character." The Fourth Convention was supplemented by Additional Protocol I of 1977, which further developed the protection of civilians in international armed conflict.
