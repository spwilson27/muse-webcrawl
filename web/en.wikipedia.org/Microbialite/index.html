Microbialite

A microbialite is a sedimentary deposit made of carbonate mud that is formed by the mediation of microbes, especially benthic microbial communities of cyanobacteria and other microorganisms. Microbialites are the oldest evidence of life on Earth, with examples dating back more than 3 billion years.

Types

Microbialites are classified by their internal structure. Stromatolites have layered (laminated) internal structures; thrombolites have a clotted structure; dendrolites have a branching, tree-like structure; and leiolites are largely structureless.

Distribution

Modern microbialites are found in places such as Shark Bay in Western Australia, the Bahamas, Lake Salda in Turkey, the Cuatro Ciénegas Basin in Mexico, and Pavilion Lake in Canada. They tend to thrive in environments with few grazing animals, which in the Precambrian were absent, allowing microbialites to dominate shallow marine ecosystems.

Significance

Microbialites dominate the fossil record of the Precambrian. Their decline near the end of the Proterozoic is linked to the rise of grazing and burrowing animals during the Cambrian explosion.
