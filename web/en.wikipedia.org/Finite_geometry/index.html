Finite geometry

Finite geometry is a geometry with only a finite number of points. The smallest example is the Fano plane, with seven points and seven lines. Finite geometries are studied through finite projective and affine planes, block designs, and their connections to combinatorics and coding theory.

Finite planes

A finite projective plane of order n has n² + n + 1 points, with n + 1 points on each line. Such planes are known to exist for prime-power orders, constructed from finite fields; the Bruck–Ryser–Chowla theorem rules out many other orders, and order 10 was excluded by a famous computer search.

Applications

Finite geometries underpin error-correcting codes, experimental design, cryptography and the construction of symmetric block designs such as the Kirkman schoolgirl problem arrangements.
