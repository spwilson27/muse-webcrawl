Skeletal dysplasia

FieldMedical genetics, orthopaedics
Number of typesMore than 450 recognised conditions
CauseGenetic variants affecting bone and cartilage growth
ExamplesAchondroplasia, osteogenesis imperfecta, thanatophoric dysplasia

Skeletal dysplasia is an umbrella term for a group of more than 450 hereditary disorders in which bone and cartilage develop abnormally, generally resulting in disproportionate short stature (dwarfism) and other skeletal abnormalities. The term osteochondrodysplasia is also used. Collectively these conditions occur in roughly 1 in 5,000 births.

Classification

The international nosology of skeletal dysplasias groups conditions by their molecular basis and radiographic features. Well-known examples include achondroplasia, the most common form of short-limbed dwarfism, caused by variants in the FGFR3 gene; osteogenesis imperfecta, characterised by fragile bones and usually caused by variants in type I collagen genes; and thanatophoric dysplasia, a severe form lethal in the newborn period.

Clinical features

Depending on the condition, features may include shortened limbs or trunk, bowed legs, spinal curvature, joint laxity or stiffness, a large head, and abnormalities of the chest, hands and feet. Some dysplasias involve other organs, including the eyes, ears and teeth. Diagnosis combines clinical assessment, skeletal X-rays and genetic testing.

Management

There is no cure for most skeletal dysplasias; care is supportive and multidisciplinary. It may involve orthopaedic surgery (including limb-lengthening procedures), physical therapy, hearing and vision monitoring, and management of complications such as spinal stenosis. The growth-hormone-receptor antagonist vosoritide has been approved for children with achondroplasia in several countries.
