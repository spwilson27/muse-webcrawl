Rush–Bagot Treaty

Signed28–29 April 1817
LocationWashington, D.C.
PartiesUnited States; United Kingdom (British North America)
NegotiatorsRichard Rush; Charles Bagot

The Rush–Bagot Treaty or Rush–Bagot Agreement was a treaty between the United States and Great Britain limiting naval armaments on the Great Lakes and Lake Champlain, following the War of 1812. It was exchanged in April 1817 between Acting US Secretary of State Richard Rush and the British minister in Washington, Charles Bagot, and ratified by the US Senate in 1818.

Terms

Each side was permitted only a handful of small armed vessels — on Lake Ontario one vessel of no more than 100 tons mounting a single 18-pounder cannon; on the upper lakes, two such vessels; and on Lake Champlain, one — with all other armed vessels dismantled and no new ones built. No limit was placed on land forces or fortifications.

Significance

Although the treaty covered only naval forces, it became a symbol of the demilitarisation of the US–Canadian border and of the peaceful relations that followed between the United States and British North America, later Canada. The agreement remains technically in force, having been adapted by later exchanges of notes — notably during the Second World War and the Cold War — to permit training and construction of vessels under specified conditions.
