Mobile broadband

Mobile broadband is wireless Internet access delivered through mobile telephone networks, allowing users to connect portable devices — smartphones, tablets, laptops with modems, and mobile hotspots — to the Internet at broadband speeds while stationary or in motion.

Generations and technologies

  • 3G (UMTS/HSPA and CDMA2000/EV-DO) introduced packet-switched data at speeds from hundreds of kilobits to tens of megabits per second in the 2000s.
  • 4G LTE and LTE-Advanced, standardized by 3GPP in the 2010s, brought all-IP networks with typical speeds of tens to hundreds of megabits per second.
  • 5G NR, rolled out from 2019, adds much higher peak throughput, lower latency, and greater capacity using wider channels, massive MIMO, and millimeter-wave spectrum, and is used both for phones and fixed wireless access.

Competing or complementary systems have included WiMAX and dedicated mobile satellite services. Devices connect via embedded modems, USB dongles, or tethering and portable Wi-Fi hotspots.

Use and significance

Mobile broadband is the primary or only Internet connection for much of the world's population, especially in developing countries where fixed infrastructure is sparse. Subscriptions number in the billions worldwide. Regulators and providers contend with questions of spectrum allocation, rural coverage, data caps and pricing, and network neutrality.
