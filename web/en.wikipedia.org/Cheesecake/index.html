Overview

Cheesecake is a dessert made with a soft fresh cheese — typically cream cheese, cottage cheese or ricotta — eggs and sugar. It usually has a base made from crushed biscuits (often digestive biscuits or graham crackers), pastry or sponge cake. Cheesecake may be baked or set without baking in a refrigerator, and is often topped with fruit, chocolate, whipped cream or fruit sauce.

History

Cheese-based cakes were known in ancient Greece; a form of cheesecake was reportedly served to athletes at the first Olympic Games in 776 BC, and the Roman writer Cato the Elder recorded a recipe for a cheese cake called libum. Medieval and early-modern European versions, such as the English cheesecake described in the 14th-century Forme of Cury, used curd cheese and pastry. Modern American cheesecake depends on cream cheese, developed in 1872 in Chester, New York, and popularised as Philadelphia cream cheese.

Styles

New York-style cheesecake is dense and rich, made with cream cheese, cream and egg yolks, baked in a springform pan. Chicago-style cheesecake is firmer outside and softer within; Pennsylvania Dutch and Philadelphia styles are lighter. Basque "burnt" cheesecake, created in San Sebastián in 1990, is crustless and deliberately caramelised on top at high temperature. Japanese soufflé cheesecake is light and jiggly, made by folding beaten egg whites into the batter. No-bake versions are set with gelatine.
