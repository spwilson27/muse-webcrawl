Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau

Abbreviation: TTB
Formed: 24 January 2003
Parent agency: U.S. Department of the Treasury
Headquarters: Washington, D.C.

The Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau, statutorily named the Tax and Trade Bureau and frequently shortened to TTB, is a bureau of the United States Department of the Treasury that regulates and collects taxes on the manufacture and sale of alcohol, tobacco, and — historically — firearms and explosives within the United States.

History

TTB was created by the Homeland Security Act of 2002, which split the former Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco and Firearms (ATF) into two agencies. The law-enforcement functions of ATF moved to the Department of Justice, while the revenue and trade-regulation functions remained in the Treasury Department as the new TTB, effective 24 January 2003.

Functions

TTB administers the Federal Alcohol Administration Act and the Internal Revenue Code provisions on alcohol and tobacco excise taxes. Its responsibilities include issuing permits to breweries, wineries, distilleries, and importers; approving beverage alcohol labels through the Certificate of Label Approval (COLA) process; defining American Viticultural Areas for wine; collecting billions of dollars annually in excise taxes; and regulating advertising and trade practices in the alcohol industry. The bureau also administers the federal health warning statement required on alcoholic beverage containers.

Organization

The bureau is headed by an Administrator and operates field offices, laboratories, and the National Revenue Center in Cincinnati, Ohio, which processes permits and tax returns.
