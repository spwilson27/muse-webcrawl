President of the European Commission

President of the European Commission
Incumbent: Ursula von der Leyen (since 1 December 2019)
Term: Five years, renewable
First holder: Walter Hallstein (1958)
Seat: Berlaymont building, Brussels

The Berlaymont in Brussels is the Commission's headquarters.

The president of the European Commission heads the European Commission, the executive branch of the European Union. The president sets the Commission's policy agenda, allocates portfolios among the commissioners (one from each member state), represents the EU at G7 and G20 summits, and proposes the legislation over which the Commission holds a near-monopoly of initiative.

Selection

Under the Treaty of Lisbon, the European Council, "taking into account" the results of the European Parliament elections, proposes a candidate by qualified majority; the Parliament then elects the president by absolute majority. In 2014 this produced the Spitzenkandidaten system, in which Jean-Claude Juncker, lead candidate of the winning party group, was chosen; the practice lapsed in 2019 when the Council instead nominated Ursula von der Leyen, a former German defence minister not previously a lead candidate, who was confirmed by a narrow parliamentary margin. She was re-elected for a second term in 2024.

Notable presidents

Early holders included Walter Hallstein, the Commission's first president (1958–1967). Jacques Delors (1985–1995) is widely credited with relaunching European integration through the Single European Act and the path to monetary union; later presidents include Romano Prodi and José Manuel Barroso.
