Nodule (geology)

In geology, a nodule is a small, roughly rounded mass of mineral matter that differs in composition from the surrounding rock or sediment in which it is embedded. Nodules typically form by precipitation of minerals around a nucleus during diagenesis.

Types

Common examples include flint and chert nodules in chalk and limestone, ironstone nodules in shales, and septarian nodules with internal crack networks. On the deep ocean floor, polymetallic nodules (manganese nodules) rich in manganese, nickel, copper, and cobalt grow extremely slowly around fragments on abyssal plains; they have attracted commercial interest for deep-sea mining, alongside environmental concern about disturbing poorly known ecosystems.

Formation

Nodules grow concentrically as dissolved minerals precipitate from pore waters. Because their composition records local chemical conditions, nodules are useful indicators of depositional environments and diagenetic history.
