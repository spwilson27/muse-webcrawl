Video on demand (VOD)
Models: SVOD (subscription), TVOD (transactional), AVOD (advertising), NVOD (near video on demand)

Video on demand (VOD) is a media distribution system that allows users to access films and television programmes when they choose, rather than at a scheduled broadcast time. It developed from cable-television pay-per-view services and came of age with broadband internet streaming in the 2000s.

Models

Subscription VOD (SVOD), exemplified by Netflix and Amazon Prime Video, offers a catalogue for a flat fee. Transactional VOD (TVOD) rents or sells individual titles, as on Apple TV or YouTube Movies. Advertising-supported VOD (AVOD) is free to viewers. Catch-up TV services offered by broadcasters, such as BBC iPlayer, form another large category.

Technology

Modern VOD relies on adaptive bitrate streaming protocols such as HLS and MPEG-DASH, content delivery networks, and digital rights management. Download-to-own and download-to-rent variants allow offline viewing.
