Nuclear propulsion

Nuclear propulsion comprises a large variety of propulsion methods that use some form of nuclear reaction as their primary power source. The use of nuclear power for propulsion covers a broad range of applications, from naval vessels and submarines to spacecraft and proposed aircraft.

Naval propulsion

Nuclear marine propulsion powers most of the world's nuclear submarines and the largest aircraft carriers. A pressurized water reactor heats steam to drive turbines and generators, giving vessels very long endurance without refuelling. The first nuclear-powered submarine, USS Nautilus, was launched in 1954. Nuclear propulsion is also used in some Russian icebreakers and was used in a small number of civilian cargo ships such as NS Savannah.

Space propulsion

Concepts for spacecraft include nuclear thermal rockets, in which a reactor heats a propellant such as hydrogen, and nuclear electric propulsion, in which a reactor powers ion thrusters. The United States tested nuclear thermal rocket engines under the NERVA program between the 1950s and 1970s. Radioisotope thermoelectric generators power deep-space probes but provide electricity rather than thrust.

Aircraft

Nuclear-powered aircraft were studied during the Cold War — the American Aircraft Nuclear Propulsion program and the Soviet Tupolev Tu-95LAL experiment — but no operational nuclear aircraft was produced, owing to shielding weight and safety concerns.
