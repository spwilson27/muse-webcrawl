Treaty of Turkmenchay

Signed22 February 1828
LocationTorkamanchay, Persia
PartiesRussian Empire; Qajar Persia

The Treaty of Turkmenchay was a treaty between Qajar Persia and the Russian Empire signed on 22 February 1828 at the village of Torkamanchay near Tabriz. It concluded the Russo-Persian War of 1826–1828 and confirmed the losses Persia had suffered in the earlier Treaty of Gulistan of 1813.

Terms

Persia ceded the Erivan and Nakhchivan khanates — roughly modern Armenia and the Nakhchivan exclave of Azerbaijan — to Russia, and the Aras River became the frontier between the two empires. Persia also paid a large indemnity, recognised Russian navigation rights on the Caspian Sea, and granted capitulatory rights to Russian subjects. The treaty was negotiated for Russia by Alexander Griboyedov, who was killed the following year in an attack on the Russian mission in Tehran.

Legacy

Together with Gulistan, Turkmenchay fixed the modern boundary between Iran and the South Caucasus states and remains a symbol in Iranian memory of territorial loss to European imperial pressure.
