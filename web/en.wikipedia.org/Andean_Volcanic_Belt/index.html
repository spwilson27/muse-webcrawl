Andean Volcanic Belt

The Andean Volcanic Belt is a major volcanic arc along the western coast of South America, formed by the subduction of the Nazca Plate and the Antarctic Plate beneath the South American Plate. It extends through Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, Bolivia, Chile, and Argentina.

Volcanic zones

The belt is divided into four main zones separated by gaps where subduction is too shallow to generate volcanism: the Northern Volcanic Zone (Colombia and Ecuador, including Cotopaxi and Galeras), the Central Volcanic Zone (southern Peru, Bolivia, northern Chile and Argentina, home to many of the world's highest volcanoes and the Altiplano–Puna volcanic complex), the Southern Volcanic Zone (central and southern Chile, including Villarrica and Llaima), and the Austral Volcanic Zone (Patagonia).

Activity

The belt contains hundreds of volcanoes, dozens of them active. Major eruptions include Huaynaputina (1600), the largest historical eruption in South America, and modern events at Chaitén (2008), Puyehue-Cordón Caulle (2011), and Villarrica (2015).
