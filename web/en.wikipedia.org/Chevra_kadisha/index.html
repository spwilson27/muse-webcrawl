Chevra kadisha

A chevra kadisha (Aramaic: חֶבְרָה קַדִּישָׁא, "holy society") is a Jewish burial society, usually a group of volunteers — men and women — who prepare the bodies of deceased Jews for burial according to Jewish tradition and protect them until interment. Their work is considered a profound act of kindness (chesed shel emet, "true kindness"), because it can never be repaid by the recipient.

Duties

The society's tasks include shemirah (watching over the body from death until burial), taharah (ritual washing and purification of the body, accompanied by prayers and psalms), dressing the deceased in plain white shrouds (tachrichim), and placing the body in a simple coffin. The society may also maintain cemeteries and assist bereaved families.

History

Formalised burial societies became a fixture of Jewish communal life in early modern Europe; the Prague chevra kadisha, founded in 1564, is a famous example. Nearly every Jewish community today organises or is served by such a society, and members traditionally fast and study on the society's annual day, often the 7th of Adar, the traditional date of Moses' death.
