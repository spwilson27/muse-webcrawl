Kʼicheʼ people

The Kʼicheʼ (also spelled Quiché) are an Indigenous people of Mesoamerica and a Maya ethnic group native to the highlands of Guatemala. They speak the Kʼicheʼ language, the most widely spoken Mayan language, with well over a million speakers.

History

Before the Spanish conquest, the Kʼicheʼ kingdom of Qʼumarkaj was the most powerful state in the Guatemalan highlands, having expanded from its capital near modern Santa Cruz del Quiché during the 15th century under the king Quicab. In 1524 the conquistador Pedro de Alvarado defeated the Kʼicheʼ armies and executed their lords, bringing the kingdom under Spanish rule.

Culture and literature

The Kʼicheʼ are the authors of the Popol Vuh, the celebrated book of Maya mythology, cosmology and dynastic history written in the Kʼicheʼ language shortly after the conquest. Kʼicheʼ culture is also known for colourful textiles, market towns such as Chichicastenango, and the dance drama Rabinal Achí, recognised by UNESCO.
