McDonnell Douglas F-15 Eagle

The McDonnell Douglas F-15 Eagle is an American twin-engine, all-weather tactical fighter aircraft designed by McDonnell Douglas (now part of Boeing). First flown in July 1972 and entering service with the United States Air Force in 1976, the F-15 was conceived as a dedicated air superiority fighter — its motto during development was "not a pound for air-to-ground". It is among the most successful modern fighters, with over 100 aerial combat victories and no losses to enemy air-to-air fire, most of them scored by the Israeli Air Force.

Design and development

Lessons from Vietnam, where large, missile-armed fighters suffered against agile opponents, drove the F-X programme. The resulting F-15 combines a large, lightly loaded wing, two powerful Pratt & Whitney F100 turbofan engines giving a thrust-to-weight ratio greater than 1:1, a bubble canopy for visibility, and the Hughes APG-63 pulse-Doppler radar able to look down and shoot down targets below. The single-seat F-15A and F-15C are air-defence variants; the two-seat F-15E Strike Eagle (developed in the 1980s) added precision ground-attack capability while retaining full air-to-air performance, and its derivatives — including the F-15EX Eagle II — remain in production.

Operational history

Israeli F-15s scored the type's first kills in 1979 and dominated Syrian aircraft over Lebanon in 1982. In the 1991 Gulf War, USAF F-15Cs claimed 34 Iraqi aircraft; F-15Es flew deep strike missions. Saudi, Japanese, South Korean, Singaporean, Qatari and Israeli variants have followed. NASA also famously repaired and flew a damaged Israeli F-15 that landed safely after losing an entire wing in a 1983 collision. With successive upgrades, the Eagle is expected to serve into the 2040s.
