Heinrich Brüning

Heinrich Aloysius Maria Elisabeth Brüning (26 November 1885 – 30 March 1970) was a German politician of the Centre Party who served as Chancellor of Germany from March 1930 to May 1932, during the Weimar Republic. A trained economist and First World War veteran, he was the longest-serving chancellor of the republic's later years.

Chancellorship

Appointed by President Paul von Hindenburg after the collapse of the grand coalition, Brüning governed largely by emergency decree under Article 48 of the Weimar constitution when the Reichstag refused his budgets. Confronted with the Great Depression, he pursued a deflationary policy of spending cuts and tax increases, partly to demonstrate Germany's inability to pay reparations and secure their cancellation — achieved in effect at the Lausanne Conference of 1932, shortly after his fall. His austerity deepened unemployment, which reached six million, and contributed to the radicalisation that benefited the Nazi and Communist parties. Hindenburg dismissed him in May 1932 and replaced him with Franz von Papen.

Later life

Brüning fled Germany in 1934 as the Nazis consolidated power, eventually settling in the United States, where he taught at Harvard University. He returned to Germany after the war and held a professorship at the University of Cologne, remaining a controversial figure in debates over whether his policies, or his resort to presidential government, hastened the Weimar Republic's collapse.
