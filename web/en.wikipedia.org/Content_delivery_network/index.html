Content delivery network

A CDN serves content from edge servers near the user.

A content delivery network (CDN), or content distribution network, is a geographically distributed network of proxy servers and their data centers. The goal is to provide high availability and performance by distributing the service spatially relative to end users. CDNs came into existence in the late 1990s — Akamai Technologies, founded in 1998, is widely credited as the first — as a means for alleviating the performance bottlenecks of the Internet of the time.

Operation

When a user requests content, the request is routed — via DNS redirection, Anycast, or HTTP redirects — to a nearby edge node. If the object is cached there, it is served directly; otherwise the edge server fetches it from the origin server. CDNs deliver web pages, images, streaming video (via protocols such as HLS and MPEG-DASH), software downloads and other assets, and also provide services such as DDoS mitigation and web application firewalls.

Providers

Major providers include Akamai, Cloudflare, Amazon CloudFront, Fastly and Google Cloud CDN; large content companies such as Netflix (Open Connect) and Meta operate their own private CDNs. By the 2020s, CDNs carried a majority of all Internet traffic.
