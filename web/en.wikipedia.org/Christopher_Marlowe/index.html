Christopher Marlowe

Christopher Marlowe, also known as Kit Marlowe (baptised 26 February 1564 – 30 May 1593), was an English playwright, poet, and translator of the Elizabethan era. Marlowe is among the most famous of the Elizabethan playwrights. Based upon the "many imitations" of his play Tamburlaine, modern scholars consider him to have been the foremost dramatist in London in the years just before his mysterious early death. Some scholars also believe that he greatly influenced William Shakespeare, who was baptised in the same year as Marlowe and later succeeded him as the pre-eminent dramatist of the Elizabethan age. Marlowe was the first to achieve critical reputation for his use of blank verse.

Life

Marlowe was born in Canterbury, the son of a shoemaker, and educated at the King's School and Corpus Christi College, Cambridge, where he took his MA in 1587—only after the university, suspecting him of intending to defect to the Catholic seminary at Reims, was assured by the Privy Council that he had performed services for the government. This episode, together with later evidence, has led to the widespread belief that Marlowe worked as an intelligencer or spy for Sir Francis Walsingham's network.

In May 1593 Marlowe was arrested on charges arising from accusations of atheism and blasphemy by his former roommate Thomas Kyd and the informer Richard Baines. Released on bail, he was killed ten days later, on 30 May 1593, stabbed above the right eye by Ingram Frizer during an argument in a house in Deptford, in the company of two other men connected with the intelligence world. The official inquest recorded a quarrel over the bill—the "reckoning"—but theories of assassination have persisted ever since.

Works

Marlowe's plays include Dido, Queen of Carthage; Tamburlaine the Great (Parts 1 and 2), whose conquering shepherd-hero transformed the London stage; The Jew of Malta; Edward II, a pioneering history play; The Massacre at Paris; and Doctor Faustus, his most famous work, in which a scholar sells his soul to the devil for twenty-four years of knowledge and power. His poems include the erotic pastoral "The Passionate Shepherd to His Love," the unfinished epic Hero and Leander, and translations of Ovid's Amores and Lucan's Pharsalia.

Influence

Marlowe's "mighty line" of unrhymed iambic pentameter liberated English drama from rhymed verse and set the course for Shakespeare and later playwrights. His protagonists—Tamburlaine, Faustus, Barabas, Edward—are driven by aspiration, ambition, and desire that exceed social and theological limits, making Marlowe a central figure in the study of Renaissance individualism.
