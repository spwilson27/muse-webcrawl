Foundation (nonprofit)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A foundation is a type of nonprofit organization or charitable trust that typically provides funding and support for other charitable organizations through grants, while also possibly engaging directly in charitable activities. Foundations include public charitable foundations, which derive funding from the general public, and private foundations, which are typically endowed by an individual or family.

TypeNonprofit organization / charitable trust
PurposeGrant-making and charitable activity
ExamplesGates Foundation, Ford Foundation, Rockefeller Foundation, Wellcome Trust

Types

Private foundations

Private foundations usually have a single major source of funding — an individual, a family, or a corporation — and most of their activities are charitable grants to other organizations. In the United States they are generally subject to a requirement to distribute about five percent of their investment assets annually for charitable purposes.

Community and public foundations

Community foundations pool donations from many donors within a geographic area and make grants locally. Public foundations (or grant-making public charities) raise funds from the general public and from other sources.

Operating foundations

Operating foundations use the bulk of their income to run their own charitable programs — such as museums, research institutes or schools — rather than making grants to outside organizations.

Legal status and governance

Foundations are governed by boards of directors or trustees under the charity law of their home jurisdiction. Many civil-law countries, such as Germany (Stiftung) and the Netherlands (stichting), have long traditions of foundations as asset-holding legal persons dedicated to a stated purpose. Foundations are typically supervised by a charity regulator or attorney general and must report on their finances and grant-making.

History

Philanthropic foundations in the modern sense emerged in the early twentieth century in the United States, with the Rockefeller Foundation (1913) and the Carnegie Corporation (1911) among the pioneers of scientific, professionally managed grant-making. The form subsequently spread worldwide; very large foundations such as the Gates Foundation (founded 2000) now rival official aid agencies in fields like global health.
