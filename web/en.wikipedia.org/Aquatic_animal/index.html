Aquatic animal

An aquatic animal is any animal, whether vertebrate or invertebrate, that lives in water for all or most of its lifetime. Aquatic animals generally breathe through gills or directly through their skin, although many (such as whales, dolphins and frogs) have lungs and must surface periodically to breathe air. Aquatic animals may live in freshwater habitats, in salt water (marine animals), or in brackish environments such as estuaries.

Types

Aquatic animals include fish, marine mammals such as whales and dolphins, molluscs such as octopuses and squid, crustaceans such as crabs and shrimp, echinoderms such as starfish, and cnidarians such as jellyfish. Some animals are semi-aquatic, spending part of their lives in water and part on land; examples include amphibians, penguins, seals, and many aquatic insects in their larval stages.

Adaptations

Aquatic animals display a wide range of adaptations to life in water: streamlined body shapes and fins or flippers for swimming, gills for extracting dissolved oxygen, blubber or other insulation in cold water, and specialised sensory systems such as the lateral line in fish or echolocation in toothed whales. Air-breathing aquatic animals include the marine mammals, sea turtles, and some aquatic snakes and insects that carry air with them or surface regularly.

The term "aquatic animal" can be applied to animals that live in either fresh water or salt water. However, the adjective marine is most commonly used for animals that live in saltwater, i.e. in oceans, seas, etc.

Conservation

Many aquatic animals are threatened by overfishing, habitat destruction, pollution (including plastic pollution and nutrient runoff), and climate change, which warms and acidifies oceans and alters freshwater flows.
