Liquid smoke

Liquid smoke, or smoke flavouring, is a water-soluble yellow to red liquid used as a flavouring to add a smoky taste to foods without cooking over fire. It is produced by condensing smoke from burning wood and dissolving the condensate in water, then filtering out tar and some carcinogenic compounds.

Ernest H. Wright commercialised the process in 1895. Liquid smoke is applied to meats, cheeses, sauces and vegetarian dishes, and is regulated as a flavouring in many jurisdictions.
