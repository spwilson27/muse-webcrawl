Cairngorms National Park

Location: Scottish Highlands, Scotland
Area: 4,528 km²
Established: 2003 (extended 2010)
Highest point: Ben Macdui, 1,309 m

The Cairngorms National Park (Scottish Gaelic: Pàirc Nàiseanta a' Mhonaidh Ruaidh) is a national park in northeast Scotland, established in 2003 and extended in 2010. It is the largest national park in the British Isles, covering about 4,528 square kilometres of the Cairngorms mountain range and surrounding hills across parts of five local authority areas. The park contains a quarter of Scotland's native woodland and is home to a quarter of the United Kingdom's threatened species.

Geography

The park is centred on the Cairngorms massif, a high granite plateau with five of the six highest mountains in Scotland, including Ben Macdui (1,309 m), the second-highest peak in the United Kingdom, and Cairn Gorm itself (1,245 m). The plateau's arctic-alpine environment supports snowbeds that can persist through summer. Rivers including the Spey, Dee, and Don rise in the park.

Nature and wildlife

The ancient Caledonian pinewoods of Rothiemurchus, Abernethy, and Glenmore shelter red squirrels, Scottish crossbills, capercaillie, and wildcats, while the high plateau hosts ptarmigan, dotterel, and mountain hares. Reintroduction and conservation projects have included work on beavers in the wider Spey catchment and long-standing protection of nesting ospreys at Loch Garten.

Recreation and settlements

Towns and villages within the park include Aviemore, Kingussie, Grantown-on-Spey, Ballater, and Braemar. The Cairngorm Mountain ski area near Aviemore and the Cairngorm funicular railway are major attractions, alongside walking, climbing, and the long-distance routes of the Lairig Ghru pass. About 18,000 people live within the park boundary, and its economy depends heavily on tourism.
