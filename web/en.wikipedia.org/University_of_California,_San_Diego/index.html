The University of California, San Diego (UC San Diego or UCSD) is a public land-grant research university in San Diego, California, United States. Established in 1960, it sits near the Pacific Ocean in the La Jolla neighbourhood of San Diego and has grown into one of the most selective and research-intensive public universities in the United States.

History

UC San Diego grew out of the Scripps Institution of Oceanography (founded 1903), whose director Roger Revelle championed a new science-focused campus. The first undergraduates arrived in 1964 at what became Revelle College. The university adopted a residential college system modelled loosely on Oxford and Cambridge; it now has eight undergraduate colleges, each with its own general-education requirements and traditions.

Academics and research

The university is organized into schools including the Jacobs School of Engineering, the Rady School of Management, the School of Medicine, and the Scripps Institution of Oceanography, a world leader in earth and marine science. Its central landmark is the Geisel Library, an eight-storey brutalist structure named for Theodor "Dr. Seuss" Geisel. UCSD researchers helped pioneer study of the greenhouse effect through the Keeling Curve record of atmospheric carbon dioxide maintained by Scripps scientists.

Athletics

The UC San Diego Tritons compete in NCAA Division I as members of the Big West Conference, having completed the transition from Division II in 2024.
