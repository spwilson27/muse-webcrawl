The President of Italy (Italian: Presidente della Repubblica) is the head of state of the Italian Republic. The role is defined by the Constitution of 1948 and is largely representative and guarantee-oriented: the President represents national unity, promulgates laws, accredits ambassadors, ratifies international treaties, and acts as the formal commander-in-chief of the Armed Forces.

Election

The President is elected for a seven-year term by an electoral college consisting of both houses of the Parliament in joint session together with delegates from the twenty regions (three per region, except Valle d'Aosta, which sends one). A two-thirds majority is required in the first three ballots; an absolute majority suffices thereafter. Candidates must be Italian citizens aged fifty or over in full possession of their civil and political rights.

Powers and duties

Although executive power rests with the Prime Minister and the Council of Ministers, the President appoints the Prime Minister after consulting the parliamentary groups, and may dissolve Parliament after hearing its presidents. The President also appoints five senators for life, five of the fifteen judges of the Constitutional Court, and presides over the High Council of the Judiciary. Every act of the President must be countersigned by the proposing minister, who assumes responsibility for it; a limited set of acts is exempt from countersignature.

History

The office emerged from the institutional referendum of 2 June 1946, in which Italians voted to abolish the monarchy of the House of Savoy and establish a republic. Enrico De Nicola served as provisional head of state before becoming the first President under the new constitution in 1948. Subsequent presidents have included Luigi Einaudi, Giovanni Gronchi, Antonio Segni, Giuseppe Saragat, Giovanni Leone, Sandro Pertini, Francesco Cossiga, Oscar Luigi Scalfaro, Carlo Azeglio Ciampi, Giorgio Napolitano — the first to be re-elected, in 2013 — and Sergio Mattarella, re-elected in 2022.
