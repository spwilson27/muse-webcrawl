Semantic dementia

Semantic dementia (SD), also known as semantic variant primary progressive aphasia, is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder characterised by the gradual loss of semantic memory—the knowledge of words, objects, and concepts—while other aspects of cognition are initially preserved.

Clinical features

Patients develop fluent but increasingly empty speech, severe anomia, and impaired word comprehension, often asking the meaning of familiar words. Surface dyslexia, in which irregularly spelled words are pronounced by rule, is common. The condition is associated with asymmetric atrophy of the anterior temporal lobes, usually worse on the left, and is most often caused by TDP-43 pathology within the frontotemporal lobar degeneration spectrum.

History

The syndrome was delineated in the 1970s–1990s by Elizabeth Warrington and by John Hodges and colleagues, and its contrasting profile with Alzheimer's disease helped establish the organisation of semantic memory in the brain.
