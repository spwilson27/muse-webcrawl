V bomber

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: An Avro Vulcan in flight — placeholder]

The "V bombers" were the Royal Air Force (RAF) aircraft during the 1950s and 1960s that comprised the United Kingdom's strategic nuclear strike force. The three models were the Vickers Valiant, the Handley Page Victor, and the Avro Vulcan; all were named with words beginning with "V". The force reached its peak in June 1964, with 50 Valiants, 70 Vulcans, and 39 Victors in service.

Development

After the Second World War, Britain sought an independent nuclear deterrent. The Air Ministry's Specification B.35/46 called for a jet bomber able to carry a 10,000 lb bomb load to targets at long range. Vickers' conservative design (Valiant) entered service first in 1955; the more advanced crescent-wing Victor and delta-wing Vulcan followed, armed initially with the Blue Danube free-fall bomb and later with Yellow Sun weapons and the Blue Steel stand-off missile.

Operations

V-bombers formed the backbone of Britain's deterrent until the Royal Navy's Polaris submarines took over the strategic role in 1969. Valiants dropped bombs during the Suez Crisis of 1956 and suffered early retirement due to metal fatigue. Victors excelled as aerial refuelling tankers, and Vulcans flew the famous Black Buck raids on the Falkland Islands in 1982 — the longest bombing missions then flown. The Vulcan's delta wing and howling engines made it a beloved airshow performer long after retirement in 1984.
