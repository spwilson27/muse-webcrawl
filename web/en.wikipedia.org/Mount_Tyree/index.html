Mount Tyree (78°24′S 85°55′W) is a mountain in Antarctica about 13 kilometres (8.1 mi) northwest of Vinson Massif (4,892 m (16,050 ft)), the highest peak on the continent. At 4,852 metres (15,919 ft) it is the second highest mountain in Antarctica. Mount Tyree sits in the Sentinel Range of the Ellsworth Mountains.

The mountain was discovered in January 1958 during an IGY reconnaissance flight from Byrd Station. It was named for Rear Admiral David M. Tyree, commander of the U.S. Naval Support Force in Antarctica in the late 1950s. Its steep, icy faces and extreme, unpredictable weather make it one of the most technically demanding of the continental high points, and it is climbed far less often than nearby Vinson Massif.

Climbing history

Mount Tyree was first climbed in 1967 by Barry Corbet and John Evans, members of the American Antarctic Mountaineering Expedition that also made the first ascent of Vinson. Because the peak requires true technical climbing in severe cold rather than the largely non-technical ascent of Vinson, only a small number of expeditions have succeeded on it. It is sometimes included among the "Second Seven Summits", the second-highest mountains of each continent.

Geography

The mountain rises above the Patton and Cervellati glaciers. Its southern face drops steeply to the ice cap. Access is by ski-equipped aircraft from Union Glacier camp, followed by a flight to a landing near Vinson Base Camp.
