London Zoo
Opened: 27 April 1828
Location: Regent's Park, London
Operated by: Zoological Society of London (ZSL)
Animals: approx. 14,000+
Species: 750+

London Zoo, previously known as ZSL London Zoo, is the world's oldest scientific zoo. It was opened in London on 27 April 1828, initially as a collection for scientific study, and opened to the public in 1847. It is located at the northern edge of Regent's Park, on the boundary between the City of Westminster and Camden.

History

Run by the Zoological Society of London (founded 1826 by Sir Stamford Raffles and others), the zoo pioneered many firsts: the first reptile house (1849), aquarium (1853), insect house (1881) and children's zoo (1938). Charles Darwin was a frequent visitor. Famous residents have included Jumbo the elephant, Guy the gorilla, and Winnie the black bear, who inspired Winnie-the-Pooh.

Exhibits

Major exhibits include Gorilla Kingdom, Land of the Lions, Tiger Territory, Penguin Beach, the Reptile House (a filming location for Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone), Rainforest Life, and the Snowdon Aviary, now a monkey walk-through. ZSL also runs Whipsnade Zoo in Bedfordshire and conducts conservation work worldwide.
