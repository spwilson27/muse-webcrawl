The Mahavidyas (Sanskrit: महाविद्या, "Great Wisdoms") are a group of ten aspects of the Divine Mother in Hinduism, particularly in Shaktism and Tantra. They are all forms of the supreme Goddess (Adi Parashakti), who is considered the sum total of all energies in the universe. The ten goddesses are usually named in the following sequence: Kali, Tara, Tripura Sundari (Shodashi), Bhuvaneshvari, Bhairavi, Chhinnamasta, Dhumavati, Bagalamukhi, Matangi and Kamala.

Origin and mythology

According to the Devi Bhagavata Purana and other texts, the Mahavidyas arose from Sati, who, insulted that her husband Shiva had been excluded from her father's sacrifice, multiplied herself into ten fierce forms that blocked Shiva's every escape route, compelling him to let her attend. Each form embodies a particular cosmic function and stage of consciousness, from time and death (Kali, Dhumavati) to beauty and liberation (Tripura Sundari, Kamala).

The ten goddesses

  • Kali – goddess of time, destruction and ultimate power
  • Tara – the saviouress and guide
  • Tripura Sundari (Shodashi) – beauty of the three worlds
  • Bhuvaneshvari – the world-mother, space itself
  • Bhairavi – the fierce goddess of decay and radiance
  • Chhinnamasta – the self-decapitated goddess of sacrifice and renewal
  • Dhumavati – the widow goddess of smoke, loss and detachment
  • Bagalamukhi – she who paralyses enemies and speech
  • Matangi – goddess of inner thought and the arts
  • Kamala – the lotus goddess of prosperity, akin to Lakshmi

Worship

The Mahavidyas are central to Tantric practice, especially in eastern India (Bengal, Assam, Odisha), where their worship is said to grant both worldly powers (siddhis) and liberation. Their worship is traditionally undertaken under the guidance of a guru.
