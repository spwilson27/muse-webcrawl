Mary Vetsera

Baroness Marie Alexandrine "Mary" von Vetsera (1871–1889) was an Austrian noblewoman and mistress of Crown Prince Rudolf of Austria. Her death alongside Rudolf at the Mayerling hunting lodge on 30 January 1889 — in what is generally believed to have been a murder-suicide — shocked Europe and remains famous as the Mayerling incident.

Life

Mary was the daughter of Baron Albin von Vetsera, a diplomat, and his wife Helene Baltazzi. Moving in Viennese court circles, the seventeen-year-old met Rudolf, the only son and heir of Emperor Franz Joseph I, in 1888, reportedly through Princess Marie Larisch. Their affair quickly became intense; farewell letters later published indicate Mary went willingly to her death with the Crown Prince.

Mayerling and aftermath

The imperial court attempted a cover-up, first announcing that Rudolf had died of a heart attack. Mary's body was smuggled out of Mayerling and buried secretly at Heiligenkreuz Abbey. The tragedy left Archduke Franz Ferdinand as heir presumptive. In 2015, Mary's farewell letters, long kept in a bank vault, were deposited in the Austrian National Library, confirming the suicide pact interpretation.
