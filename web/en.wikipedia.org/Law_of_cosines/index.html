Law of cosines

In trigonometry, the law of cosines relates the lengths of the sides of a triangle to the cosine of one of its angles. For a triangle with sides a, b, c and angle C opposite side c:

c² = a² + b² − 2ab·cos C

Applications

The law generalises the Pythagorean theorem (which is the special case C = 90°, where cos C = 0). It is used to find a third side given two sides and the included angle, or to find all angles given three sides, in surveying, navigation and physics. Equivalent forms exist in spherical and hyperbolic geometry.

History

Propositions in Euclid's Elements (Book II) amount to a geometric version; the explicit trigonometric form was developed by medieval Islamic mathematicians such as al-Kashi, after whom it is named in French (théorème d'Al-Kashi).
