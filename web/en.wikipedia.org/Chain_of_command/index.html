Chain of command

A chain of command is an unbroken line of authority extending from the top of an organization to the lowest echelon, along which orders are passed down and reports are passed up. The term comes from the military, where it defines the succession of commanding officers from a superior to a subordinate through which command is exercised.


Hierarchy of command

Military usage

In armed forces, orders flow from senior officers through intermediate commanders to the troops, and each member is accountable to the person immediately above them. The principle of unity of command holds that a subordinate should receive orders from one superior only. Modern "mission command" philosophies modify strict top-down control by giving subordinates intent and latitude, but the chain of command remains the formal structure of accountability.

In management

Businesses, emergency services and government agencies use similar hierarchical reporting lines. Management theorists such as Henri Fayol described the "scalar chain" as one of the principles of administration, while noting the need for lateral communication shortcuts (the "gang plank") to avoid delay.
