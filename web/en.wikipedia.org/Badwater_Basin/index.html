Badwater Basin is an endorheic basin in Death Valley National Park, Death Valley, Inyo County, California, noted as the lowest point in North America and the United States, with a depth of 282 ft (86 m) below sea level. Mount Whitney, the highest point in the contiguous 48 states, is only 84.6 miles (136 km) to the northwest.

Geography

The site consists of a small spring-fed pool of "bad water" next to the road in a sink; the accumulated salts of the surrounding basin make the water undrinkable, hence the name. The pool itself is not the lowest point; the actual lowest point is a broad salt flat several miles to the west, whose exact location shifts as the salt crust changes. After heavy rains the basin floor may flood temporarily, forming a shallow lake known informally as Lake Manly — as occurred after the remnants of Hurricane Hilary in 2023 flooded the valley floor.

History and name

According to legend, the name derives from an early prospector's mule refusing to drink from the pool, marking the water as "bad." The basin lies within the ancestral lands of the Timbisha Shoshone. A sign on the cliff face above the basin, 282 feet up, marks sea level and is a popular photographic landmark. The site is also the start of the Badwater Ultramarathon, a 135-mile footrace to Whitney Portal held in midsummer heat, and it lies near some of the highest reliably recorded air temperatures on Earth.
