Real-time strategy

Typical RTS interface with base building and unit control

Real-time strategy (RTS) is a subgenre of strategy video games in which the game does not progress in discrete turns but continuously, with all players acting simultaneously. Players typically position and manoeuvre units and structures under their control to secure areas of a map, gather resources, build bases and technology, and destroy the assets of opposing players.

Gameplay

Characteristic elements include resource gathering, base building, technology trees, and direct control of military units in combat, combining long-term strategic planning with rapid tactical decisions and fast mouse and keyboard "micro-management". The genre contrasts with turn-based strategy, where players act in sequence, and with the related MOBA genre, which developed from RTS custom maps such as Aeon of Strife and Defense of the Ancients.

History

The genre was established in its modern form by Westwood Studios' Dune II (1992), and was popularised by Command & Conquer (1995) and Blizzard's Warcraft series. StarCraft (1998) became a landmark competitive title, especially in South Korea, and a foundation of professional esports. Other major series include Age of Empires, Total Annihilation and Company of Heroes. The genre's popularity on personal computers peaked around the early 2000s, though titles such as StarCraft II (2010) sustained large competitive scenes.
