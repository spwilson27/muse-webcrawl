Prokop the Great

Prokop the Great (Czech: Prokop Veliký; c. 1380 – 30 May 1434) was a Czech Hussite general and the leading commander of the radical Taborites during the later Hussite Wars. After the death of Jan Žižka in 1424, Prokop became the principal military leader of the Hussite forces, leading them to a series of remarkable victories against crusading armies.

Life

Little is certain about Prokop's early life. He was probably born around 1380, possibly in Prague, and was educated for the priesthood, becoming a Utraquist priest. He travelled in his youth and may have been influenced by reformist preaching. By the early 1420s he had joined the Taborite community, the most radical wing of the Hussite movement, and rose quickly through its ranks.

Military career

Prokop led the Taborites and the united Hussite armies in a string of victories over the crusades launched against Bohemia. At the Battle of Aussig (Ústí nad Labem) in 1426 he destroyed a large crusading army; at Tachov in 1427 and famously at Domažlice in 1431, crusader forces fled at the approach of the Hussites, whose singing of the hymn "Ktož jsú boží bojovníci" ("Ye Who Are Warriors of God") was said to terrify the enemy. Prokop also led offensive "beautiful rides" (spanilé jízdy) deep into Germany, Silesia, Hungary, and as far as the Baltic coast, spreading Hussite influence and collecting supplies and ransoms.

Diplomacy and death

Prokop led the Hussite delegation to the Council of Basel in 1433, arguing the Hussite case before the assembled churchmen. As the moderate Utraquists moved toward a settlement with the council and Emperor Sigismund, relations with the radicals deteriorated into civil war. Prokop was defeated and killed at the Battle of Lipany on 30 May 1434, fighting against a combined force of Utraquists and Catholics — a defeat that effectively ended the Hussite Wars.
