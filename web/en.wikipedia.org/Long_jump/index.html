The long jump is a track and field event in which athletes combine speed, strength and agility in an attempt to leap as far as possible from a take-off point. It has been contested at the modern Olympic Games since 1896 for men and 1948 for women, and was one of the events of the ancient Greek pentathlon.

Technique

The jumper sprints down a runway of at least 40 m, takes off from a wooden board 20 cm wide, and lands in a sand-filled pit. Distance is measured from the take-off line to the nearest mark made in the sand. Flight techniques include the hang, the hitch-kick and the simple sail, all aimed at countering forward rotation and presenting the feet ahead of the body's centre of mass at landing.

History

Bob Beamon's leap of 8.90 m at the 1968 Mexico City Olympics shattered the world record by 55 cm and stood for nearly 23 years, until Mike Powell jumped 8.95 m at the 1991 World Championships in Tokyo, defeating Carl Lewis in one of the event's most famous duels. Lewis himself won four consecutive Olympic long jump titles (1984–1996).
