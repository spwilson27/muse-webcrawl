Pelé

Edson Arantes do Nascimento (23 October 1940 – 29 December 2022), better known by his nickname Pelé, was a Brazilian professional footballer who played as a forward. Widely regarded as one of the greatest players of all time, he was among the most successful and popular sports figures of the 20th century. In 1999, he was named Athlete of the Century by the International Olympic Committee.

Club career

Pelé began playing for Santos at age 15 and the Brazil national team at 16. With Santos (1956–1974) he won numerous state and national titles and two Copa Libertadores and Intercontinental Cups (1962, 1963). He finished his career with the New York Cosmos (1975–1977), helping to popularise soccer in North America.

International career

Pelé is the only player to win three FIFA World Cups, with Brazil in 1958, 1962 and 1970. He scored 77 goals in 92 official matches for Brazil, a record for the national team shared in later years. His performances at the 1958 World Cup as a 17-year-old, including a hat-trick in the semi-final and two goals in the final, made him a global star.

Style and legacy

Pelé was renowned for his dribbling, passing, heading, flair and prolific goalscoring, including spectacular overhead (bicycle) kicks. FIFA labels him "the greatest" and he was awarded the FIFA Ballon d'Or Prix d'Honneur in 2014.

Later life

After retirement Pelé served as Brazil's Minister of Sport and as a global ambassador for football and UNESCO. He died in São Paulo in December 2022, prompting worldwide tributes.
