Lysis
From Greek λύσις, "loosening, dissolution"
Meaning: breakdown of a cell's membrane
Related: autolysis, cytolysis, haemolysis, lytic cycle

Lysis is the breaking down of the membrane of a cell, often by viral, enzymatic or osmotic mechanisms, and the subsequent release of the cell's contents.

Types

Cytolysis occurs when a cell bursts because of osmotic imbalance, as when a cell in a hypotonic solution takes in too much water. Haemolysis is the rupture of red blood cells. In the lytic cycle, a bacteriophage replicates inside a bacterium and destroys the host cell to release new virus particles.

Laboratory use

Cell lysis is a routine laboratory technique for extracting proteins, DNA, RNA and organelles; methods include detergents, freeze–thaw cycles, bead beating and sonication. Lysozyme and other lysins attack bacterial cell walls and are used in both research and medicine.
