Dim sum

Dim sum (traditional Chinese: 點心; simplified Chinese: 点心; Cantonese Yale: dímsām) is a large range of small Chinese dishes that are traditionally enjoyed in restaurants for brunch, together with tea. Most modern dim sum dishes are commonly associated with Cantonese cuisine, although dim sum dishes also exist in other Chinese cuisines.

Steamed dim sum served in bamboo baskets.

History

Dim sum developed along the Silk Road, where teahouses served travelers small snack dishes to accompany tea, a custom known in Cantonese as yum cha ("drinking tea"). In Guangzhou and later Hong Kong, dim sum restaurants became a central social institution, serving dishes from carts wheeled between tables, from which diners select items charged by the plate or steamer.

Dishes

Common dim sum items include har gow (steamed shrimp dumplings with translucent rice-wheat wrappers), siu mai (open-topped pork and shrimp dumplings), char siu bao (barbecue pork buns), rice noodle rolls (cheung fun), turnip cake, chicken feet (fung zao), congee, and egg tarts for dessert. Dishes may be steamed, pan-fried, deep-fried, or baked.
