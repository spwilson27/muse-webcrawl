Lochaber

Ben Nevis, Britain's highest mountain, rises above Lochaber

Lochaber (Scottish Gaelic: Loch Abar) is a district in the western Scottish Highlands, centred on the town of Fort William. It is one of the traditional districts of Scotland and gives its name to the famous Lochaber axe, a type of polearm, and to the Lochaber No More lament.

Geography

Lochaber encompasses the area around Loch Linnhe and the lower reaches of the Great Glen, including Ben Nevis (1,345 m), the highest mountain in the British Isles, and the hills of Glen Nevis and Glen Coe's eastern fringes. The district includes the Ardnamurchan peninsula, the most westerly point of mainland Britain, and communities such as Ballachulish, Kinlochleven, Arisaig and Mallaig.

History

Historically a lordship within the medieval Kingdom of Scotland, Lochaber was a stronghold of the MacDonald and Cameron clans. The area saw action in the Jacobite risings; the standard of Charles Edward Stuart was raised at Glenfinnan in 1745. In the 20th century, the British Aluminium Company's works at Kinlochleven and later the Lochaber aluminium smelter at Fort William harnessed hydroelectric power from the surrounding hills, and the region's wartime Commando training centre at Achnacarry earned it the name "Commando Country."

Economy

Today Lochaber brands itself the "Outdoor Capital of the UK," with tourism, hillwalking, mountain biking at the Nevis Range, and hydroelectric and aluminium industries central to the local economy. The area is administered as part of the Highland council area.
