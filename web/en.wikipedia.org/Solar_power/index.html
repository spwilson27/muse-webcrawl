Solar power

Solar power, also known as solar electricity, is the conversion of energy from sunlight into electricity, either directly using photovoltaics (PV) or indirectly using concentrated solar power. Solar power plants use one of two technologies: photovoltaic systems use solar panels, either on rooftops or in ground-mounted solar farms, converting sunlight directly into electric power; concentrated solar power systems use mirrors or lenses to focus sunlight onto a receiver that heats a fluid to drive a turbine.

Growth

Solar is one of the fastest-growing sources of electricity worldwide, driven by steep declines in the cost of photovoltaic modules — by roughly 90% during the 2010s — and by supportive policies in China, the United States, Europe, India and elsewhere. Global cumulative PV capacity passed 1 terawatt in 2022 and continued to grow rapidly, with China the largest installer and manufacturer. Rooftop solar on homes and businesses, combined with battery storage, has become common in countries such as Australia and Germany.

Characteristics

Solar generation is variable, producing nothing at night and less on cloudy days, so its large-scale integration requires grid flexibility, storage or interconnection. In return it produces no emissions or fuel costs during operation and can be deployed at scales from a single panel to power stations covering many square kilometres. Solar thermal technologies also supply heat for water and space heating.
