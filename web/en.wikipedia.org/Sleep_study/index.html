Sleep study

A sleep study, formally polysomnography, is a diagnostic test used in sleep medicine to diagnose sleep disorders. It records brain waves (EEG), blood oxygen level, heart rate, breathing, eye movements and leg movements while the subject sleeps, typically overnight in a laboratory or with a home testing device.

Sleep study

Synonym: Polysomnography
Purpose: Diagnosis of sleep disorders
Measures: EEG, EOG, EMG, airflow, SpO2, ECG

Indications

Sleep studies are most commonly ordered to evaluate suspected obstructive sleep apnoea, but they also help diagnose narcolepsy, REM sleep behaviour disorder, periodic limb movement disorder and unexplained insomnia. A multiple sleep latency test, conducted the following day, measures how quickly the patient falls asleep in naps and supports a narcolepsy diagnosis.

Types

Type I studies are attended in-laboratory polysomnography; Types II–IV are progressively simpler unattended home tests. Results are scored in 30-second epochs and summarised by indices such as the apnoea–hypopnoea index (AHI), which grades sleep apnoea severity.
