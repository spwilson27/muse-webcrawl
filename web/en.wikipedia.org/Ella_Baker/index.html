Ella Baker

Ella Josephine Baker (December 13, 1903 – December 13, 1986) was an American civil rights and human rights activist. Working largely behind the scenes, she was a key organiser of the NAACP, the Southern Christian Leadership Conference (SCLC) and the Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee (SNCC), and a mentor to a generation of younger activists.

Early life

Baker was born in Norfolk, Virginia, and raised in Littleton, North Carolina. She graduated as valedictorian from Shaw University in Raleigh in 1927 and moved to New York City, where she worked as a journalist and organiser, co-founding the Young Negroes Cooperative League and writing for African-American newspapers.

NAACP and SCLC

In 1940 Baker began working for the NAACP, travelling the South as a field secretary and later as director of branches, building local membership and leadership. In 1957 she moved to Atlanta to help organise Martin Luther King Jr.'s SCLC, serving as its first staff member and interim executive director, though she chafed at its clergy-led, top-down structure.

SNCC and participatory democracy

After the Greensboro sit-ins of 1960, Baker convened the conference at Shaw University that founded SNCC, urging students to build an independent organisation committed to group-centred leadership rather than charismatic figureheads. Her philosophy of participatory democracy shaped SNCC's voter-registration work, including Freedom Summer in Mississippi in 1964.

Legacy

Baker continued activism into her eighties and died in New York on her 83rd birthday. The Ella Baker Center for Human Rights, founded in Oakland in 1996, carries her name and approach.
