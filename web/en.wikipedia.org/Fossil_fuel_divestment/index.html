Fossil fuel divestment or divestment from fossil fuels is the removal of investment assets, including stocks, bonds, and investment funds, from companies involved in extracting fossil fuels. The movement seeks to reduce carbon emissions and combat climate change by stigmatizing the fossil fuel industry, reducing its political influence, and in some cases reducing the risk of "stranded assets" — fossil fuel reserves that cannot be burned if the world is to meet climate targets.

History

The campaign took off in the early 2010s, led by the environmental organization 350.org and writer Bill McKibben, whose 2012 article "Global Warming's Terrifying New Math" argued that most known fossil fuel reserves must remain in the ground. Student campaigns pressed universities to divest their endowments, echoing earlier divestment movements against apartheid South Africa and the tobacco industry.

Scope and debate

By the 2020s, institutions controlling tens of trillions of dollars in assets — including pension funds, religious organizations, sovereign wealth funds, and universities — had made full or partial divestment commitments. Supporters argue divestment shifts social norms and raises the cost of capital for polluters; critics argue selling shares merely transfers ownership to less scrupulous investors and has little direct financial effect. The movement is generally considered part of the broader environmental, social, and governance (ESG) investing trend.
