Sunday roast

A Sunday roast is a British meal traditionally served on Sunday, consisting of roast meat, roast or mashed potatoes, and accompaniments such as Yorkshire pudding, gravy, roasted root vegetables, and greens. It is a cultural institution in Britain and Ireland and is widely served in pubs as a "carvery" or Sunday lunch.

Components

The centrepiece is a roasted joint — beef (traditionally served with Yorkshire pudding, horseradish, and mustard), lamb with mint sauce, pork with crackling and apple sauce, or chicken with stuffing. Potatoes roasted in fat, parsnips, carrots, and Brussels sprouts are typical, all brought together with gravy made from the meat juices.

History

The meal is associated with the Sunday post-church gathering; in an era when bread ovens were shared, families would leave a joint to cook while attending church. Industrial-era working patterns made Sunday the one day for a substantial midday family meal, cementing the tradition.
