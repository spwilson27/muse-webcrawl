Pacific Plate

Pacific Plate
Type: Major
Area: approximately 103,300,000 km²
Movement: north-west
Speed: roughly 7–11 cm/year
Features: Pacific Ocean, Hawaii, New Zealand (part), California (part)

The Pacific Plate is a oceanic tectonic plate that lies beneath the Pacific Ocean. At 103 million km² (40 million sq mi), it is the largest tectonic plate on Earth. The Pacific Plate contains an interior hot spot forming the Hawaiian Islands.

Hillis and Müller consider it to be moving generally northwest at a speed of 7 to 11 cm/yr (2.8 to 4.3 in/yr). The plate is almost entirely oceanic crust, with only small slivers of continental crust along its margins, such as parts of California west of the San Andreas Fault and part of New Zealand's South Island and North Island.

Boundaries

The northeastern side is a divergent boundary with the Explorer Plate, the Juan de Fuca Plate, and the Cocos Plate, forming the Explorer Ridge, the Juan de Fuca Ridge, and the East Pacific Rise respectively. In the middle of the eastern side is a transform boundary with the North American Plate along the San Andreas Fault and a boundary with the Cocos Plate. The southeastern side is a divergent boundary with the Nazca Plate forming the East Pacific Rise. The southern side is a divergent boundary with the Antarctic Plate forming the Pacific–Antarctic Ridge. The western side is bounded by the Okhotsk Plate at the Kuril–Kamchatka Trench and the Japan Trench, and forms convergent boundaries subducting under the Philippine Sea Plate. The southwestern side forms a complex boundary with the Indo-Australian Plate.

History

The Pacific Plate originated roughly 190 million years ago at the triple junction of the ancient plates of Farallon, Phoenix, and Izanagi. Over time it consumed much of the Farallon and Izanagi plates through subduction, growing to dominate the Pacific basin — the remnant of the ancient Panthalassic Ocean.
