Hot sauce

Hot sauce is a type of condiment, seasoning, or salsa made from chilli peppers and other ingredients. Many commercial varieties of mass-produced hot sauce exist, and the heat level is measured on the Scoville scale, which quantifies the capsaicin concentration.

Types

Louisiana-style hot sauces, such as Tabasco and Crystal, are made from fermented chilli peppers, vinegar, and salt. Mexican sauces include salsa roja, salsa verde, and bottled sauces such as Cholula and Valentina. Sriracha, a Thai-style sauce of chillies, garlic, vinegar, sugar, and salt, became globally popular through the version produced by Huy Fong Foods in the United States. Peri-peri sauce uses African bird's eye chillies; Caribbean sauces often use Scotch bonnet or habanero peppers, sometimes with fruit. Extremely hot "extract" sauces add concentrated capsaicin oleoresin.

Production

Many sauces are produced by mashing chillies into a mash, sometimes fermenting it in barrels for weeks or years, then blending with vinegar and other ingredients. Fermentation mellows the peppers and adds complexity. The global hot sauce market has grown steadily, driven by interest in spicy foods and craft and small-batch sauces.
