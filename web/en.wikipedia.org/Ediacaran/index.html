Ediacaran

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Members of the Ediacara biota, among the earliest known complex multicellular life.

The Ediacaran Period (from about 635 to 538.8 million years ago) is the final geological period of the Neoproterozoic Era and of the Proterozoic Eon. It follows the Cryogenian and precedes the Cambrian, the first period of the Phanerozoic. Ratified by the International Union of Geological Sciences in 2004, it was the first new geological period declared in 120 years. It is named after the Ediacara Hills of South Australia, where its characteristic fossils were found.

The Ediacara biota

The period is renowned for the Ediacara biota — enigmatic, mostly soft-bodied multicellular organisms preserved as impressions in sandstone at sites such as the Flinders Ranges, Mistaken Point in Newfoundland, and the White Sea coast of Russia. Forms include the quilted glider Dickinsonia, the frond-shaped Charnia, the disc-like Cyclomedusa, and the segmented Spriggina; whether these were animals, fungi-like organisms, or an extinct experiment in multicellular life remains debated.

Setting and climate

The Ediacaran began at the end of the Marinoan "Snowball Earth" glaciation, whose cap carbonates mark the period's base. Rising oxygen levels in the oceans are thought to have enabled the large body sizes of the biota. The Avalon, White Sea and Nama assemblages document successive evolutionary radiations.

End of the period

Most Ediacaran forms disappeared near the boundary with the Cambrian, in what some researchers count as the first mass extinction of animals, possibly driven by the evolution of burrowing and predatory animals. The base of the Cambrian is defined by the first appearance of the trace fossil Treptichnus pedum.
