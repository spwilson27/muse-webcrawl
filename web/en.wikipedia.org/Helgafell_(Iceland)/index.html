Helgafell ("holy mountain") is a small volcanic mountain, about 73 metres high, on the Snæfellsnes peninsula in western Iceland, near the town of Stykkishólmur. Despite its modest height, it offers wide views over Breiðafjörður and the surrounding countryside, and it holds an important place in Icelandic saga tradition and folklore.

Saga history

Helgafell was a sacred site in the early settlement period. According to Eyrbyggja saga, the settler Þórólfur Mostrarskegg built a temple to Thor at Helgafell and regarded the mountain as so holy that no one was allowed to look at it unwashed, and nothing — man or animal — was to be killed on it. The settlers believed that they would enter the mountain after death. The site later became an Augustinian monastery in the twelfth century and a centre of learning; the saga hero Guðrún Ósvífrsdóttir of Laxdæla saga is said to have lived and been buried there, and a grave traditionally identified as hers lies by the church.

Folklore

A local tradition holds that visitors who climb Helgafell in silence — without looking back or speaking — and follow the customary ritual at the remains of a small chapel on the summit may make three wishes, which will be granted if the wishes are for good purposes, made facing east, and never revealed to anyone.
