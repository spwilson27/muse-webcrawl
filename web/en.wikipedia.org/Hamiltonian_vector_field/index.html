Hamiltonian vector field

In mathematics and physics, a Hamiltonian vector field on a symplectic manifold is a vector field defined from the differential of a smooth function, the Hamiltonian, using the symplectic form. Its flow consists of symplectomorphisms and, in classical mechanics, describes the time evolution of a physical system in phase space.

Definition

Let (M, ω) be a symplectic manifold and H a smooth function on M. The Hamiltonian vector field XH is defined by ω(XH, ·) = dH, equivalently by contracting ω with XH to give dH. In canonical coordinates (q, p) this recovers Hamilton's equations: dq/dt = ∂H/∂p and dp/dt = −∂H/∂q.

Properties

The Hamiltonian is constant along its own flow, reflecting conservation of energy. The Lie bracket of two Hamiltonian vector fields corresponds to the Poisson bracket of their Hamiltonians. Hamiltonian flows preserve the symplectic form and hence, by Liouville's theorem, phase-space volume.

Examples

For a particle in a potential, H = p²/2m + V(q) generates ordinary Newtonian motion in phase space. The harmonic oscillator gives closed elliptical orbits in the (q, p) plane.
