Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer is a 1964 American stop-motion animated Christmas television special produced by Videocraft International, Ltd. (later known as Rankin/Bass Productions) and currently distributed by Universal Television. It first aired Sunday, December 6, 1964, on the NBC television network in the United States. The special was based on the 1949 Johnny Marks song "Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer", itself derived from the 1939 poem by Marks's brother-in-law, Robert L. May.

Plot

Rudolph, a young reindeer born with a glowing red nose, is shunned by the other reindeer and his own father. He befriends Hermey, an elf who wants to be a dentist, and Yukon Cornelius, a prospector searching for silver and gold. Together they flee the Abominable Snow Monster, visit the Island of Misfit Toys, and return when Rudolph realises his friends are in danger. During a violent snowstorm on Christmas Eve, Santa Claus asks Rudolph to guide his sleigh with his nose, and Rudolph accepts.

Production

The special was shot in Rankin/Bass's "Animagic" stop-motion process in Japan under the supervision of Tadahito Mochinaga. Burl Ives narrates as Sam the Snowman and sings several songs. It has aired every Christmas season since 1964, making it the longest continuously running Christmas TV special in the United States.

Legacy

The special's characters have become fixtures of American holiday culture. Its puppets were long thought lost; surviving Rudolph and Santa figures surfaced in the 2000s and were restored. See also stop motion and Rankin/Bass productions.
