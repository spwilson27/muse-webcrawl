Rankin/Bass Productions, Inc. was an American production company founded in 1960 by Arthur Rankin Jr. and Jules Bass, originally as Videocraft International. It is best remembered for its holiday television specials produced in "Animagic" stop-motion animation, many of which have been broadcast annually for decades and become fixtures of American popular culture.

Animagic specials

Working with Japanese studio MOM Productions (later Topcraft), Rankin/Bass produced beloved stop-motion specials including Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer (1964) — the longest-running holiday special in television history — The Little Drummer Boy (1968), Frosty the Snowman (1969, in cel animation), Santa Claus Is Comin' to Town (1970) and The Year Without a Santa Claus (1974). The specials were noted for their original songs by Maury Laws and Jules Bass and narration by stars such as Burl Ives, Fred Astaire and Jimmy Durante.

Cel animation and features

In traditional animation the studio made the features The Hobbit (1977) and The Return of the King (1980), The Last Unicorn (1982) and The Flight of Dragons (1982), and the syndicated action series ThunderCats (1985–1989) and SilverHawks (1986). Production ceased in 1987; the company's library passed through several owners and its pre-1974 works are now generally distributed in association with DreamWorks Classics, while Warner Bros. holds much of the later catalogue.
