Baku (Azerbaijani: Bakı) is the capital and largest city of Azerbaijan, as well as the largest city on the Caspian Sea and in the Caucasus region. It lies on the Absheron Peninsula on the western shore of the Caspian, 28 metres below sea level, making it the lowest-lying national capital in the world. Baku is Azerbaijan's political, economic and cultural centre and its principal port.

History

The area around Baku was known in antiquity for its natural oil seeps and "eternal fires" associated with Zoroastrian worship. The city developed under the Shirvanshahs, whose palace and the Maiden Tower survive in the walled Old City (Icheri Sheher), now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Baku passed between Persian, Ottoman and local control before being annexed by the Russian Empire in the early 19th century.

Oil boom

In the late 19th century Baku became one of the world's first great oil centres; by 1901 it produced more than half of the world's oil. Entrepreneurs such as the Nobel brothers and the Rothschilds built refineries and pipelines, and the city grew into a cosmopolitan industrial hub. Oil again made Baku strategically vital in the Second World War and remains the backbone of Azerbaijan's economy, with offshore Caspian fields exported via the Baku–Tbilisi–Ceyhan pipeline.

Modern city

Soviet-era Baku was a major industrial and educational centre. Since independence in 1991, an oil-funded construction boom has added landmarks such as the Flame Towers, the Heydar Aliyev Center (designed by Zaha Hadid) and the Crystal Hall. Baku hosted the Eurovision Song Contest in 2012, the first European Games in 2015, the Azerbaijan Grand Prix of Formula One from 2016, and matches of UEFA Euro 2020. The city is nicknamed the "City of Winds" for its frequent strong winds, the khazri and gilavar.
