The Holland Torpedo Boat Company was an American shipbuilding company founded in 1899 by Irish-American inventor John Philip Holland to build submarines of his design for the United States Navy and foreign customers. It is regarded as the first modern submarine production company, and it became the nucleus of the Electric Boat Company, one of the principal submarine builders for the U.S. Navy throughout the twentieth century.

History

John Philip Holland had experimented with submarine designs since the 1870s, backed at times by the Fenian Brotherhood, for whom he built the small Fenian Ram in 1881. After a series of Navy design competitions in the 1890s, Holland's company built the Holland VI at Crescent Shipyard in Elizabeth, New Jersey. Following successful trials, the United States Navy purchased the boat on 11 April 1900 and commissioned her as USS Holland (SS-1), the Navy's first submarine.

To finance production, Holland's backers organised the Electric Boat Company in February 1899, with the Holland Torpedo Boat Company as its operating subsidiary. The company soon received orders for additional boats of the improved Plunger (A-class) design, and licensed Holland designs abroad; submarines derived from his patents were built for the navies of the United Kingdom, Japan, Russia, and the Netherlands. The Royal Navy's first submarines, the Holland-class boats built by Vickers at Barrow-in-Furness, were constructed under such a licence.

Legacy

The Holland Torpedo Boat Company's designs established the basic configuration of the modern submarine: a cylindrical pressure hull, combined diesel-electric propulsion on later boats, horizontal rudders (hydroplanes) for depth control, and torpedo armament. Through Electric Boat — later General Dynamics Electric Boat — the company's lineage continues in the construction of U.S. Navy nuclear submarines at Groton, Connecticut.
