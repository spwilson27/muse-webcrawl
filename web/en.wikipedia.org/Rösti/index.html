Rösti

Rösti (Swiss German: [ˈrøːʃti]) is a Swiss dish of grated potatoes, formed into a flat cake and fried until crisp and golden. Originally a breakfast dish eaten by farmers in the canton of Bern, it is now widely regarded as a national dish of Switzerland and is served throughout the country and in Swiss restaurants worldwide.

A rösti of grated potato fried to a crisp golden crust.

Preparation

Rösti is made of coarsely grated potatoes, either raw or parboiled, seasoned with salt and fried in butter or fat. The grated potato is pressed into a round cake, typically in a frying pan, and cooked until both sides are crisp. It requires no binding ingredients, though egg or flour is sometimes added. Additional ingredients such as bacon, onion, cheese, herbs or apple slices are common regional variations.

Berner Rösti

The Bernese style, Berner Rösti, is among the best known regional variants. It is made with parboiled potatoes, milk, and often streaky bacon, giving it a richer texture than versions prepared with raw potato. In Valais, tomatoes, cheese and paprika may be added to make Rösti à la valaisanne.

Cultural significance

Rösti gave its name to the Röstigraben ("rösti ditch"), the humorous term for the cultural and political divide between German-speaking and French-speaking Switzerland. The dish is traditionally served as a side dish with meals such as Zürcher Geschnetzeltes (sliced veal in cream sauce), eggs, or spinach.
