Breusch–Pagan test

In statistics, the Breusch–Pagan test, developed in 1979 by Trevor Breusch and Adrian Pagan, is used to test for heteroscedasticity in a linear regression model. It tests whether the estimated variance of the residuals depends on the values of the independent variables, in which case heteroscedasticity is present.

Procedure

After fitting a regression by ordinary least squares, the squared residuals are regressed on the original regressors. The test statistic — half the explained sum of squares in the original formulation, or nR² in the Koenker variant — is asymptotically chi-squared distributed under the null hypothesis of homoscedasticity.

Assumptions and variants

The original test assumes normally distributed errors. Koenker's studentized version relaxes this assumption and is the form implemented in most statistical software. Related procedures include the White test and the Goldfeld–Quandt test.
