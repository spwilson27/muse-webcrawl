Sonography (writing)

TypeShorthand / phonetic writing system, unimplemented
CreatorCharles Kenneth Scott Moncrieff (attributed proposal)
PurposePhonetic transcription analogous to ultrasound imaging

This article is about a proposed phonetic writing system. For the medical imaging technique, see Medical ultrasound.

Sonography, in the context of writing systems, refers to proposed methods of representing speech sounds visually in a way analogous to how medical sonography renders images from sound waves. The term is occasionally used for experimental phonetic scripts designed so that the shape of each symbol reflects the acoustic or articulatory properties of the sound it denotes.

Concept

Unlike alphabetic systems, whose letter shapes are arbitrary, sonographic proposals aim at an iconic notation: vowels might be graphed by their formant frequencies, and consonants by features of place and manner of articulation. Such systems are related to "visible speech" notations of the 19th century, such as Alexander Melville Bell's Visible Speech (1867), which encoded articulatory gestures in stylised symbols and was used in deaf education and elocution teaching.

Applications and status

No sonographic writing system has achieved general adoption. The ideas remain of interest in phonetics pedagogy, speech technology interfaces, and discussions of how spectrograms — themselves often called sonograms — might serve as readable representations of speech.
