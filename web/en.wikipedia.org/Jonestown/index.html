Jonestown

Official name: Peoples Temple Agricultural Project
Location: Barima-Waini, Guyana
Founded: 1974 (settlement from 1977)
Founder: Jim Jones / Peoples Temple
Destroyed: 18 November 1978 — deaths of 918 people

Jonestown was the informal name for the Peoples Temple Agricultural Project, a remote settlement in northwestern Guyana established by the Peoples Temple, an American religious movement under the leadership of Jim Jones. It became internationally notorious on November 18, 1978, when a total of 918 people died there and in related incidents in Georgetown and at a nearby airstrip — the great majority by cyanide poisoning in an event Jones had rehearsed as a "revolutionary suicide".

Origins

The Peoples Temple, founded by Jones in Indiana in the 1950s and later based in California, combined Pentecostal Christianity with socialist and racial-integrationist ideals, attracting a largely African American congregation. In 1974 the Temple leased nearly 4,000 acres of jungle in Guyana's Barima-Waini region, promoting the settlement as a socialist paradise free of American racism and economic pressure. From 1977 hundreds of Temple members emigrated there; at its peak Jonestown held around 1,000 residents, who endured long agricultural workdays, armed guards, restrictions on leaving, and Jones's marathon amplified sermons.

November 18, 1978

Concerned relatives and media reports of abuse prompted U.S. Congressman Leo Ryan to lead a fact-finding delegation to Jonestown. After a visit on November 17–18, during which a number of residents asked to leave, Temple gunmen ambushed the party at the Port Kaituma airstrip, killing Ryan, three journalists and a defector — the only U.S. congressman ever assassinated in the line of duty. That evening, Jones ordered the community to drink a grape-flavoured drink laced with cyanide and sedatives; parents gave it to their children, and armed guards surrounded the pavilion. 909 people died at Jonestown itself, including 276 children; applying the phrase "drinking the Kool-Aid" to later usage, though the drink was mainly Flavor Aid.

Aftermath

The deaths were the largest single loss of American civilian life in a deliberate act until the September 11 attacks. Investigations concluded that Jones and his inner circle had planned the deaths, staged rehearsals called "White Nights", and accumulated the poison in advance. The settlement was abandoned and reclaimed by jungle; survivors, defectors and families continue to contest the memory and meaning of the event, and memorials listing all the victims were dedicated at Oakland, California.
