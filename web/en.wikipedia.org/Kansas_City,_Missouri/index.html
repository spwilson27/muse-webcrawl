Kansas City (abbreviated KC or KCMO) is the most populous city in the U.S. state of Missouri. It straddles the confluence of the Kansas and Missouri rivers and forms, with Kansas City, Kansas, across the state line, the core of the Kansas City metropolitan area. The city is famous for its barbecue, its jazz heritage, and its more than 200 fountains, which earn it the nickname "City of Fountains".

History

Founded in the 1830s as a river landing for travellers on the Santa Fe, California and Oregon trails, the Town of Kansas was incorporated in 1850 and renamed Kansas City in 1889. It grew into a great rail and livestock centre — its stockyards were second only to Chicago's — and a major hub for meatpacking. During the Prohibition era, under political boss Tom Pendergast, Kansas City became a wide-open town whose clubs incubated Kansas City jazz, associated with Count Basie, Charlie Parker and big-band swing. Postwar development included the Country Club Plaza, the first suburban shopping centre built for automobiles.

Culture and economy

The city's cultural landmarks include the Nelson-Atkins Museum of Art, the National World War I Museum and Memorial at the Liberty Memorial, the American Jazz Museum and the Negro Leagues Baseball Museum in the historic 18th and Vine district. Kansas City–style barbecue, cooked low and slow and served with thick sweet sauce, is celebrated at more than a hundred restaurants. Major employers include health care, finance, government and Hallmark Cards, founded in the city. The Kansas City Chiefs (NFL) and Royals (MLB) play at the Truman Sports Complex in the city.

Geography

Kansas City covers 127 square miles of rolling terrain at the junction of the Missouri and Kansas rivers. Its climate is humid continental, with hot summers and cold winters.
