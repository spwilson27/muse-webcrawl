Moderna


Type: Public (Nasdaq: MRNA)
Founded: 2010
Headquarters: Cambridge, Massachusetts, US
Industry: Biotechnology
Key people: Stéphane Bancel (CEO)

Moderna, Inc. is an American pharmaceutical and biotechnology company based in Cambridge, Massachusetts, that focuses on RNA therapeutics, primarily mRNA vaccines. These vaccines use a copy of a molecule called messenger RNA (mRNA) to carry instructions for making proteins that trigger an immune response.

History

Moderna was founded in 2010 by Flagship Pioneering, building on work by Derrick Rossi on reprogramming stem cells with modified mRNA. The company spent its first decade developing its mRNA platform without a commercial product, raising substantial venture and partnership funding and going public in 2018 in what was then the largest biotechnology IPO in history.

COVID-19 vaccine

In 2020, Moderna developed the COVID-19 vaccine mRNA-1273 (later branded Spikevax) in partnership with the US National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases. The vaccine received emergency use authorization from the US Food and Drug Administration in December 2020, days after the Pfizer–BioNTech vaccine, and became the company's first commercial product, generating tens of billions of dollars in revenue in 2021 and 2022. An Omicron-adapted bivalent booster followed in 2022.

Pipeline

Moderna's pipeline includes mRNA vaccines against influenza, respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) — its vaccine mRESVIA was approved in 2024 — cytomegalovirus, Epstein–Barr virus, HIV and Zika, as well as individualized cancer therapies developed with Merck, including the melanoma candidate mRNA-4157/V940.
