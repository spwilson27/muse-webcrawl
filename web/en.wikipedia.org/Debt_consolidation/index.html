Debt consolidation

Debt consolidation is a form of debt refinancing in which several existing debts are combined into a single, larger loan, ideally with more favorable terms — such as a lower interest rate or a single fixed monthly payment. It is used by individuals, companies, and governments to simplify repayment and reduce borrowing costs.

Types

For consumers, common methods include personal consolidation loans, balance-transfer credit cards charging low introductory rates, home equity loans, and 401(k) loans. Student loan consolidation, offered for example through the U.S. federal Direct Consolidation Loan program, merges multiple education loans into one. For companies and sovereign borrowers, consolidation may involve issuing new bonds to retire several outstanding obligations.

Advantages and risks

Combining debts can lower the total interest paid, convert variable rates to fixed ones, and reduce the chance of missed payments. However, extending the repayment term may increase the total cost despite lower monthly payments, secured consolidation loans put collateral such as a home at risk, and borrowers who do not change spending habits may accumulate new debts on top of the consolidation loan. Financial advisers generally recommend comparing fees and total repayment costs and addressing the causes of the original borrowing.
