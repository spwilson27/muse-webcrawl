Wicker

Wicker is a technique for making products woven from any one of a variety of pliable plant materials, and also the resulting lightweight woven furniture and other goods themselves. The word derives from a Scandinavian term, wika, meaning "to fold" or "bend." Materials used include rattan, willow, reed, bamboo, and cane, as well as modern synthetic fibres that imitate the natural product.

History

Wickerwork is among the oldest furniture-making techniques: wicker baskets and furniture survive from ancient Egypt, and woven pieces were common in Roman and medieval households. The craft reached a popular peak in the Victorian era, when wicker furniture was prized for porches, conservatories, and gardens. Documented wicker basketry dates to ancient Egypt, where woven pieces were placed in tombs.

Materials and manufacture

The stout frame of a wicker piece is typically made from thicker plant stalks such as rattan poles, while the weave itself uses more pliable material. Willow is traditional in Europe; in North America, commercial production expanded in the 19th century, notably through Cyrus Wakefield's rattan company in Massachusetts. Since the late 20th century, resin and polyethylene "all-weather wicker" have become common for outdoor furniture because they resist moisture and ultraviolet damage.

Uses

Wicker is used for chairs, tables, baskets, bassinets, hot-air balloon baskets, and decorative objects. Its combination of lightness and durability made it the standard material for early aircraft and bicycle seating as well.
