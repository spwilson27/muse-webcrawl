Beautiful Festival of the Valley

The Beautiful Festival of the Valley was an ancient Egyptian festival held annually in Thebes (modern Luxor) during the second month of the season of Shemu. It was one of the most important celebrations in the Theban calendar, centred on the visit of the god Amun of Karnak to the mortuary temples and necropolis on the west bank of the Nile, and on families honouring and feasting with their deceased ancestors.

The festival

The festival is attested from the Middle Kingdom and reached its height in the New Kingdom. The cult image of Amun, accompanied by Mut and Khonsu (the Theban Triad), was carried in a splendid processional barque from Karnak Temple, crossed the Nile by boat, and visited the royal mortuary temples on the west bank, culminating at the temple of the reigning king — most famously the terraced temple of Hatshepsut at Deir el-Bahari, beside which a chapel of Hathor stood. The procession was greeted by crowds, musicians and dancers.

Feasting with the dead

Uniquely, ordinary Thebans participated by holding banquets in the courtyards and chapels of their family tombs in the Theban Necropolis, presenting food, drink, flowers and music to their ancestors. Tomb paintings — notably in the tombs of Nakht and Rekhmire — depict these joyful gatherings, which blurred the boundary between the living and the dead and reaffirmed family bonds. The celebrations lasted at least a full day and night, and the festival gave its name to the month in which it was held.
