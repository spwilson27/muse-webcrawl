The American Civil Liberties Union (ACLU) is an American nonprofit organisation founded in 1920 "to defend and preserve the individual rights and liberties guaranteed to every person in this country by the Constitution and laws of the United States." The ACLU works through litigation, lobbying, and community organising, and has been involved in many landmark United States Supreme Court cases concerning free speech, racial equality, reproductive rights, LGBTQ rights, privacy, and criminal justice.

History

The ACLU grew out of the National Civil Liberties Bureau, formed during the First World War to defend conscientious objectors and opponents of the Espionage Act. Under its first director, Roger Baldwin, the new organisation defended radicals, labour organisers, and pacifists during the Palmer Raids era. Early landmark cases included the Scopes trial (1925), in which ACLU lawyers defended a Tennessee teacher's right to teach evolution, and Gitlow v. New York (1925), which began the incorporation of the Bill of Rights against the states.

During the civil rights era the ACLU supported cases such as Brown v. Board of Education (1954). Its defence of the American Nazi Party's right to march in Skokie, Illinois (1977–78) — defending the speech of a group it opposed — caused thousands of members to resign but became a defining statement of its content-neutral free-speech philosophy. More recent major litigation includes challenges to post-9/11 surveillance, the USA PATRIOT Act, abortion restrictions, voting laws, and immigration enforcement policies.

Organisation

The ACLU comprises two separate but closely affiliated nonprofits: the ACLU itself, a 501(c)(4) social welfare group, and the ACLU Foundation, a 501(c)(3) engaged in litigation and education. It has affiliates in every state plus Puerto Rico and Washington, D.C., and reports over a million members and supporters.

Positions

The ACLU supports freedom of speech, press, assembly and religion; separation of church and state; abortion rights; marriage equality; voting rights; reform of the criminal legal system; an end to mass incarceration and the death penalty; and protections against discrimination. It has also opposed campaign finance limits on First Amendment grounds, a stance that has drawn criticism from some political allies.
