Mahabhashya

The Mahābhāṣya ("Great Commentary"), attributed to Patañjali, is a commentary on selected rules of Sanskrit grammar from Pāṇini's Aṣṭādhyāyī, written in roughly the 2nd century BCE. Together with the Aṣṭādhyāyī itself and Kātyāyana's Vārttika, it forms the foundational "three sages" (trimuni) tradition of Sanskrit grammar (vyākaraṇa).

Content

Written in clear, conversational prose, the Mahābhāṣya discusses roughly 1,700 of Pāṇini's sūtras, weighing Kātyāyana's criticisms and establishing authoritative interpretations. It begins with the famous Paspaśāhnika chapter, which considers the purposes of grammar and the nature of words. Patañjali's discussion of language — including the permanence of words (śabda) and the relation of word and meaning — made the work influential in Indian philosophy of language.

Authorship

Tradition sometimes identifies the grammarian Patañjali with the Patañjali of the Yoga Sūtras, though most modern scholars treat them as distinct figures of possibly the same period.
