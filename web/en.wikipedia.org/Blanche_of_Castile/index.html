Blanche of Castile (4 March 1188 – 27 November 1252) was Queen of France by marriage to Louis VIII. She acted as regent of France during the minority of her son Louis IX from 1226 until 1236, and again during his absence on the Seventh Crusade from 1248 until her death. Renowned for her political skill, she is regarded as one of the most capable rulers of medieval France.

Early life and marriage

Blanche was the third daughter of Alfonso VIII of Castile and Eleanor of England, daughter of Henry II of England and Eleanor of Aquitaine. Under the Treaty of Le Goulet (1200), she was betrothed to the future Louis VIII; her grandmother Eleanor escorted her to France, and the marriage was celebrated on 23 May 1200 at Port-Mort in Normandy.

Regency

When Louis VIII died in November 1226, the twelve-year-old Louis IX succeeded, and Blanche became regent. She faced down a coalition of rebellious barons, secured the Treaty of Paris (1229) ending the Albigensian Crusade and bringing much of Languedoc under royal control, and arranged her son's marriage to Margaret of Provence. Criticised as a foreign woman exercising power, she nevertheless preserved and strengthened the Capetian monarchy.

Second regency and death

When Louis departed on crusade in 1248, Blanche again governed France, suppressing the Pastoureaux revolt of 1251. She fell ill and died in Paris in November 1252; her son, still in the Holy Land, learned of her death the following year.

Patronage

Blanche was a notable patron of architecture and manuscripts, associated with the Cistercian abbeys of Royaumont and Maubuisson and with moralised Bibles produced at the Parisian court.
