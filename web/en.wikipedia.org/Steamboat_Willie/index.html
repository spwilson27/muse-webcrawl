Steamboat Willie is a 1928 American animated short film directed by Walt Disney and Ub Iwerks. It is considered the debut of Mickey Mouse and his girlfriend Minnie Mouse, and was one of the first cartoons with synchronized sound — the first to feature a fully post-produced soundtrack — making it a landmark in animation history.

Plot

Mickey is a deckhand on the steamboat Podunk, captained by Pete. Whistling the tune "Steamboat Bill", Mickey helps load cargo and picks up Minnie at the dock; when she falls behind, he hoists her aboard with a crane. The two then improvise a musical performance of "Turkey in the Straw" using the livestock on board as instruments, until the captain catches Mickey, orders him below deck, and puts him to work peeling potatoes.

Production and reception

The cartoon parodied Buster Keaton's Steamboat Bill, Jr. (1928). Although two Mickey cartoons, Plane Crazy and The Gallopin' Gaucho, were made before it as silent films, Disney chose Steamboat Willie for release first because of its synchronized sound, following the success of The Jazz Singer. It premiered at New York's Colony Theater on November 18, 1928, and was an immediate sensation, launching Mickey's popularity and the Disney studio's rise.

Copyright and legacy

The film's copyright history was central to debates over copyright extension in the United States, culminating in the Copyright Term Extension Act of 1998. Steamboat Willie entered the public domain in the United States on January 1, 2024, while Disney retains trademark rights in the characters. The opening whistling scene is used as the logo sequence of Walt Disney Animation Studios.
