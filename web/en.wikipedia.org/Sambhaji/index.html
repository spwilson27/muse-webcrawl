Sambhaji (14 May 1657 – 11 March 1689), also known as Chhatrapati Sambhaji Maharaj, was the eldest son of Shivaji, founder of the Maratha Empire, and its second ruler, reigning from 1681 to 1689. His reign was dominated by war with the Mughal emperor Aurangzeb, whose vast Deccan campaign he resisted until his capture and execution.

Early life

Sambhaji lost his mother, Saibai, in infancy and was raised largely by his grandmother Jijabai. Well educated, he was proficient in Sanskrit, Persian and other languages and wrote poetry (the Budhbhushanam is attributed to him). As a boy he accompanied Shivaji to Agra in 1666 and escaped with him from Mughal house arrest. His early career was turbulent — he briefly defected to the Mughal commander Diler Khan in 1678 before returning — but he succeeded his father in 1681 after defeating a rival court faction.

Reign and wars

Sambhaji continued Maratha expansion, attacking the Portuguese at Goa and sacking Burhanpur. He sheltered Aurangzeb's rebellious son Akbar, provoking the emperor's full-scale invasion of the Deccan in 1682. For eight years Sambhaji's mobile forces withstood the Mughal war machine, though he suffered setbacks, including the loss of territory to the Siddis of Janjira and internal dissension among Maratha sardars.

Capture and death

In February 1689 Sambhaji was captured at Sangameshwar by the Mughal general Muqarrab Khan, reportedly while distracted by festivities. Taken before Aurangzeb, he refused offers of clemency conditioned on conversion and surrender of his forts, and after weeks of torture was executed at Tulapur on 11 March 1689. His death galvanised Maratha resistance under his brother Rajaram and later his widow's regency, turning the war into a long struggle that drained the Mughal Empire. He is venerated in Maharashtra as a martyr — Dharmaveer, "protector of dharma".
