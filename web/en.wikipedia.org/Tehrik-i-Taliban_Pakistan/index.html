Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan

Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan (TTP), commonly known as the Pakistani Taliban, is an umbrella organization of Islamist militant groups based in the tribal areas along the Afghan–Pakistani border. It was founded in December 2007 under the leadership of Baitullah Mehsud, bringing together disparate militant factions with the stated aims of resisting the Pakistani state, enforcing its interpretation of sharia law, and expelling Pakistani and coalition forces from the region.

History

The TTP emerged amid Pakistani military operations in the Federally Administered Tribal Areas following the 2007 Lal Masjid siege. It is organisationally distinct from the Afghan Taliban, though the groups share ideology and historical ties. The TTP has been held responsible for numerous attacks inside Pakistan, including the 2009 attack on the Sri Lanka national cricket team, the 2012 shooting of Malala Yousafzai, and the 2014 Peshawar school massacre, in which more than 140 people, mostly children, were killed. Pakistan responded with major military operations, notably Operation Zarb-e-Azb (2014), which displaced the group from its strongholds in North Waziristan.

Leadership and status

Successive TTP leaders — Baitullah Mehsud, Hakimullah Mehsud and Mullah Fazlullah — were killed in US drone strikes. After Fazlullah's death in 2018, Noor Wali Mehsud became emir and sought to reunify splinter factions. The group is designated a terrorist organisation by Pakistan, the United States and the United Nations. Following the Afghan Taliban's return to power in 2021, TTP attacks inside Pakistan increased, straining relations between Islamabad and Kabul.
