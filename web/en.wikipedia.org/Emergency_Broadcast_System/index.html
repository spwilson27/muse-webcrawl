Emergency Broadcast System

The Emergency Broadcast System (EBS), sometimes called the Emergency Action Notification System (EANS), was an emergency warning system used in the United States from 1963 to 1997. It was initiated under President John F. Kennedy and replaced the CONELRAD system. The EBS was designed to provide the President with a rapid method of communicating with the American public in the event of war, threat of war, or grave national crisis.

Operation

Stations that received an Emergency Action Notification were required to interrupt regular programming and broadcast presidential messages. The system was never activated for a national emergency, though it was used thousands of times for local weather warnings and civil emergencies. A mistaken activation in 1971, when an incorrect tape was transmitted, revealed weaknesses that led to procedural reforms. Viewers knew the EBS chiefly through its regular tests, announced by the familiar words: "This is a test. This station is conducting a test of the Emergency Broadcast System."

Successor

The EBS was replaced by the Emergency Alert System (EAS) in 1997, which uses digital SAME protocol headers to automate activation and target alerts geographically.
