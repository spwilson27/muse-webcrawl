Nasserism

Ideology: Arab nationalism, Arab socialism, pan-Arabism, anti-imperialism

Key figure: Gamal Abdel Nasser

Region: Egypt and the Arab world

Nasserism (Arabic: التيار الناصري, at-Tayyār an-Nāṣirī) is an Arab nationalist and socialist political ideology based on the thinking and policies of Gamal Abdel Nasser, the second president of Egypt, who led the country from 1956 until his death in 1970. It was one of the most influential political currents in the Arab world during the 1950s and 1960s, shaping the politics of Egypt and inspiring movements across the Middle East and North Africa.

Principles

Nasserism combined Arab nationalism with a form of socialism adapted to Egyptian and Arab conditions. Its central tenets included pan-Arab unity, the liberation of Arab lands from foreign domination, opposition to imperialism and Zionism, land reform, state-led industrialisation, and social justice. Nasser's philosophy was articulated in his book The Philosophy of the Revolution (1954), in which he described Egypt's role within three circles: the Arab circle, the African circle, and the Islamic circle.

Policies

Under Nasser, Egypt nationalised the Suez Canal in 1956, triggering the Suez Crisis, in which Egypt withstood a joint British, French and Israeli invasion and emerged with its nationalisation intact — an event that made Nasser a hero across the Arab world. Domestically, the regime carried out sweeping land reforms, expanded free education and healthcare, built the Aswan High Dam, and pursued state-led industrialisation through the Arab Socialist Union, the sole legal political party from 1962.

Influence and decline

Nasserist movements appeared in many Arab countries, and Egypt briefly united with Syria as the United Arab Republic (1958–1961). The ideology's prestige suffered severely after Egypt's defeat in the Six-Day War of 1967. After Nasser's death, his successor Anwar Sadat reversed many Nasserist policies through the infitah economic opening and the "Corrective Revolution." Nevertheless, Nasserist parties and movements continue to exist in Egypt and elsewhere in the Arab world.
