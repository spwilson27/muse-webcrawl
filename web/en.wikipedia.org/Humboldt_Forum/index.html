Humboldt Forum

The Humboldt Forum is a museum and cultural complex in the centre of Berlin, Germany, located on Museum Island in the reconstructed Berlin Palace (Berliner Schloss). Named in honour of the brothers Alexander and Wilhelm von Humboldt, it houses the non-European collections of the Berlin State Museums — principally the Ethnological Museum and the Museum of Asian Art — together with exhibitions on the history of Berlin and spaces for events and temporary shows.

The site

The palace site has been the symbolic centre of Berlin since the 15th century. The baroque Berlin Palace, residence of the Hohenzollern electors and kings, was badly damaged in the Second World War and demolished in 1950 by the East German government, which built its Palace of the Republic (Palast der Republik) there in 1976. After German reunification, that building was in turn demolished (2006–2008), and after long debate the Bundestag voted to rebuild the palace with three reconstructed baroque facades and a modern eastern facade and interior, designed by Italian architect Franco Stella. Construction ran from 2013 to 2020.

Opening and exhibitions

The Humboldt Forum opened digitally in December 2020 during the COVID-19 pandemic, with physical exhibitions phased in from July 2021 and the ethnological collections' main galleries opening in September 2022. Covering about 30,000 square metres of exhibition space, it displays art and artefacts from Africa, Asia, Oceania and the Americas, alongside the "Berlin Global" city exhibition and Humboldt University displays.

Controversy

The project has been controversial from several directions: critics objected to demolishing the Palace of the Republic and to reconstructing an imperial palace, while its ethnological collections have been at the centre of Germany's restitution debates. Particular attention has focused on the Benin Bronzes looted by British forces in 1897 and held in Berlin; Germany agreed in 2022 to transfer ownership of more than 1,000 bronzes to Nigeria, with many remaining on loan display. Provenance research and questions of colonial context remain ongoing public issues for the institution.
