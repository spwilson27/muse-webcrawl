Scripps Research

Founded: 1924

Location: La Jolla, California, United States

Type: Nonprofit biomedical research institute

Scripps Research (previously The Scripps Research Institute, TSRI) is an American nonprofit medical research facility based in La Jolla, California, that focuses on research in the biomedical sciences. It is one of the largest independent nonprofit biomedical research organisations in the world.

History

The institute traces its origins to the Scripps Metabolic Clinic founded in 1924 by philanthropist Ellen Browning Scripps. It developed into a research institute under the leadership of Frank J. Dixon and others, and was renamed The Scripps Research Institute in 1991; the shorter name Scripps Research was adopted in 2018.

Research

Scripps Research employs several thousand scientists, staff and graduate students working in chemistry, biology, immunology, neuroscience and translational medicine. Its researchers have developed approved medicines including the cancer drug bortezomib-related advances, and its Skaggs Graduate School of Chemical and Biological Sciences trains doctoral students. The affiliated translational arm Calibr works on drug discovery.

Notable faculty

Faculty have included Nobel laureates K. Barry Sharpless and Ardem Patapoutian, the latter recognised for discoveries of receptors for temperature and touch.
