The Imperial Universities (Kyūjitai: 帝國大學, Shinjitai: 帝国大学, Teikoku Daigaku) were founded by the Empire of Japan between 1886 and 1939 as the nation's premier institutions of higher education and research. There were nine Imperial Universities: seven in Japan, one in Korea, and one in Taiwan.

History

The first was Tokyo Imperial University, established in 1886 under the Imperial University Order. Kyoto followed in 1897, then Tohoku (1907), Kyushu (1911), Hokkaido (1918), Osaka (1931), and Nagoya (1939), along with Keijō Imperial University in Seoul (1924) and Taihoku Imperial University in Taipei (1928).

Legacy

After the Second World War the "Imperial" designation was dropped; the seven Japanese institutions survive as leading national universities, while the Seoul and Taipei institutions became Seoul National University and National Taiwan University.
