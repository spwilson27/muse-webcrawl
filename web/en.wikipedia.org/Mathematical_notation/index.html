Mathematical notation

Mathematical notation consists of using symbols for representing operations, unspecified numbers, relations, and other mathematical objects, and assembling them into expressions and formulas. Mathematical notation is widely used in mathematics, science, and engineering for representing complex concepts and properties in a concise, unambiguous, and accurate way.

History

Early mathematics was largely rhetorical, with problems stated in words. Symbolic algebra emerged in the work of François Viète in the late sixteenth century, and René Descartes introduced conventions such as using x, y, z for unknowns and a, b, c for known quantities. The signs + and − appeared in print in the fifteenth century; Robert Recorde introduced the equals sign = in 1557. Leibniz and Newton developed notations for calculus, and Leonhard Euler popularized many conventions, including f(x) for functions, e for the base of natural logarithms, and i for the imaginary unit.

Modern notation

Modern notation includes the summation and product signs (Σ and Π), set-theoretic symbols such as ∈ and ⊂, quantifiers ∀ and ∃ from logic, and specialized systems in fields such as tensor analysis and category theory. Notation continues to evolve, and its design affects how easily ideas can be manipulated and communicated.
