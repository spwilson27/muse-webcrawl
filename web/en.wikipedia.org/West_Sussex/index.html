West Sussex is a ceremonial county in South East England, bordered by Hampshire to the west, Surrey to the north, East Sussex to the east, and the English Channel to the south. Its county town is the cathedral city of Chichester, and its largest town is Crawley. The county was created in 1974 when the historic county of Sussex was divided into East and West Sussex for administrative purposes.

Geography

The county is dominated by the chalk hills of the South Downs, much of which lie within the South Downs National Park, and by the wooded Weald in the north. The coastal plain hosts the seaside towns of Worthing, Littlehampton, and Bognor Regis. London Gatwick Airport, the UK's second-busiest airport, lies in the north of the county near Crawley.

History

Sussex was one of the kingdoms of early Anglo-Saxon England, founded, according to tradition, by Ælle in 477 AD. The county's history includes the Norman Conquest — William the Conqueror landed nearby at Pevensey in 1066 — and it was long administered as a single county with rapes as subdivisions. The Local Government Act 1972 split Sussex into the two present counties from 1 April 1974.

Settlements

Major towns include Chichester, Crawley, Worthing, Horsham, Bognor Regis, East Grinstead, Haywards Heath, Burgess Hill, and Shoreham-by-Sea. The city of Brighton and Hove, formed from towns historically in East and West Sussex, is administered separately as a unitary authority but remains part of the ceremonial county of East Sussex and West Sussex respectively by portion.
