Home theater

A home theater (or home cinema) is an entertainment system designed to reproduce the experience of a movie theater in a private home, combining a large video display — typically a television or a projector and screen — with multichannel surround sound audio, comfortable seating, and controlled lighting.

Components

A typical system includes a display, an AV receiver that decodes audio formats such as Dolby TrueHD and DTS-HD Master Audio, source devices such as Blu-ray players and streaming boxes, and a speaker array. Common configurations are 5.1 and 7.1 surround, with the ".1" denoting a subwoofer channel dedicated to low-frequency effects. Object-based formats such as Dolby Atmos and DTS:X add overhead or height speakers for three-dimensional sound.

History

Home cinema grew from the hi-fi hobbyist culture of the 1970s and 1980s. The arrival of VHS with stereo sound, LaserDisc with digital audio, and Dolby Surround processing brought cinema-style sound into homes. DVD in the late 1990s, with Dolby Digital and DTS 5.1 soundtracks, made surround sound mainstream, and high-definition discs and streaming services in the 2000s and 2010s pushed resolutions to 4K and beyond.

Dedicated rooms and media rooms

Enthusiasts distinguish the "dedicated theater" — a room designed solely for movies, with acoustic treatment, soundproofing, tiered seating and total light control — from the more casual media room, where the system shares space with everyday living. Acoustic calibration systems such as Audyssey, Dirac Live and YPAO measure the room and adjust speaker levels, distances and equalization automatically.
