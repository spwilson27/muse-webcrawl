DTS:X is a multi-dimensional, object-based audio codec developed by DTS and launched in 2015. In a home theater it competes with Dolby Atmos: instead of assigning every sound to a fixed loudspeaker channel, a DTS:X mix treats sounds as independent objects positioned and moved in three-dimensional space, and the playback system renders them to whatever speaker layout is installed.

Technology

DTS:X is built on the open Multi-Dimensional Audio (MDA) platform and is carried within a DTS-HD Master Audio bitstream, which keeps it backwards compatible with existing DTS-HD equipment. The format does not mandate a specific speaker layout; free configurations from a conventional 5.1 system up to layouts with height speakers (such as 7.1.4) are supported, and the decoder maps objects to the available speakers. A notable feature is dialogue control, which can allow the listener to raise or lower dialogue level independently of the rest of the mix on suitably authored content.

Consumer rollout

The first DTS:X-enabled AV receivers reached the market in 2015 via firmware updates and new models from manufacturers including Denon, Marantz, Yamaha, and Onkyo. The format appeared on Ultra HD Blu-ray and Blu-ray titles beginning the same year, and an IMAX Enhanced variant later brought DTS:X to selected streaming and disc releases. A headphone virtualization version, DTS Headphone:X, applies related object rendering to stereo headphones.
