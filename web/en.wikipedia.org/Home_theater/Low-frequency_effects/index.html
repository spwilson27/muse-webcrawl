The low-frequency effects (LFE) channel is the dedicated bass channel in surround sound systems. In a home theater it supplies the deep, physical impact of a film soundtrack — the rumble of an explosion or the lowest notes of a score — and is reproduced by one or more subwoofers. It is the ".1" in 5.1 and 7.1 channel configurations.

The LFE channel carries only low frequencies, band-limited to about 120 Hz, and is authored separately from the main channels. Sound mixers place effects into the LFE track deliberately; ordinary bass in music and dialogue stays in the main channels and is redirected to the subwoofer only by the receiver's bass management settings. Because the LFE track is recorded with 10 dB of extra headroom, decoders apply a corresponding 10 dB boost on playback.

In modern formats

Both of the lossless Blu-ray audio formats, Dolby TrueHD and DTS-HD Master Audio, carry a discrete LFE channel. Object-based formats such as Dolby Atmos and DTS:X retain the LFE channel as the foundation of their bass reproduction.
