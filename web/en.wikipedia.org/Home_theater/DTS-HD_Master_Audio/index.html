DTS-HD Master Audio is a multi-channel, lossless audio codec developed by DTS as an extension of the DTS-HD format family. In a home theater, it is one of the two principal lossless surround formats found on Blu-ray Disc, the other being Dolby TrueHD.

The format is structured as a core-plus-extension bitstream: a conventional lossy DTS core (at up to 1.5 Mbit/s) is embedded so that older equipment can still play the track, while the Master Audio extension carries the additional data needed to reconstruct the original audio bit-for-bit. On Blu-ray, DTS-HD Master Audio supports up to 7.1 discrete channels at sampling rates up to 96 kHz, or two channels at up to 192 kHz, with constant or variable bit rates that can exceed 24 Mbit/s.

Use in home theater

For home cinema playback, a DTS-HD Master Audio track is typically transported from a Blu-ray player to an AV receiver over HDMI, either decoded in the player to multichannel PCM or bitstreamed for decoding in the receiver. The format became the dominant lossless soundtrack format on Blu-ray releases, in part because of its scalable core design and broad support among receiver manufacturers.

Successors

DTS later introduced DTS:X, an object-based audio format built on the DTS-HD framework, which allows sounds to be placed in three-dimensional space rather than being tied to fixed channels.
