Dolby TrueHD is a lossless multi-channel audio codec developed by Dolby Laboratories. It is a mainstay of the modern home theater, serving as one of the primary high-definition soundtrack formats on Blu-ray Disc and Ultra HD Blu-ray.

TrueHD is based on Meridian Lossless Packing (MLP), the same technology used for DVD-Audio. It supports up to 24-bit/192 kHz audio, with channel configurations up to 7.1 on Blu-ray, at variable bit rates that can reach roughly 18 Mbit/s. Because the compression is lossless, decoding reproduces the studio master exactly. On Blu-ray, a TrueHD track is accompanied by a conventional lossy Dolby Digital track for compatibility with older equipment.

Dolby Atmos

Dolby Atmos object-based mixes on disc are carried inside a Dolby TrueHD container: the channel-based TrueHD core plays on legacy systems, while additional metadata and extension data let an Atmos-capable receiver render height and positional objects. This arrangement made TrueHD the delivery format for most Atmos home releases.

Playback

TrueHD can be decoded in a player and sent to a receiver as multichannel PCM over HDMI, or bitstreamed for decoding in the receiver — the latter is required for Atmos rendering. The format competed with, and coexists alongside, DTS-HD Master Audio in the home market.
