Parti Québécois
Founded: 1968
Ideology: Quebec nationalism, sovereignty

The Parti Québécois (PQ) is a sovereignist and social democratic provincial political party in Quebec, Canada. It advocates national sovereignty for Quebec, a predominantly French-speaking province, and the defence and promotion of the French language.

History

The party was founded in 1968 by René Lévesque through the merger of his Mouvement Souveraineté-Association with other nationalist groups. It first won power in the 1976 provincial election, forming the first sovereignist government in Quebec's history.

Referendums

PQ governments held two referendums on sovereignty: in 1980, when the proposal was defeated with about 60 percent voting No, and in 1995, when the Yes side lost narrowly, 49.4 percent to 50.6 percent.

Policies and legacy

The party enacted the Charter of the French Language (Bill 101) in 1977 and has been a central force in Quebec politics, alternating in power with the Quebec Liberal Party for much of its history, though its fortunes declined in the 2010s and 2020s.
