Carlo Gambino

Carlo Gambino, boss of the Gambino crime family

Carlo Gambino (Italian: [ˈkarlo ɡamˈbiːno]; August 24, 1902 – October 15, 1976) was a Sicilian-born American crime boss who was the leader and namesake of the Gambino crime family of New York City. Following the Apalachin meeting in 1957, he assumed control of the family and later became the boss of the Commission of the American Mafia.

Early life

Gambino was born in Palermo, Sicily, on August 24, 1902, and immigrated to the United States in 1921, arriving as a stowaway. He joined his cousins in Brooklyn and soon became involved with associates of the Mafia. During Prohibition he made a fortune in bootlegging.

Rise to power

Gambino rose through the ranks of the Mangano crime family, becoming a capo under boss Vincent Mangano. In 1951, Mangano disappeared and his underboss Albert Anastasia took control, making Gambino his underboss. In October 1957, Anastasia was murdered in a Manhattan barbershop, and Gambino took over the family, which was thereafter known by his name.

Unlike many of his contemporaries, Gambino kept a low profile and avoided displays of wealth and power. He expanded the family's influence through alliances, deals in narcotics trafficking, loan-sharking, gambling, and labour racketeering, and was widely regarded as the most powerful Mafia boss in America. He was arrested few times and spent almost no time in prison.

Death and legacy

Gambino died of a heart attack in 1976. He designated his brother-in-law Paul Castellano as his successor. The character of Vito Corleone in The Godfather is often said to be partly based on Gambino.
