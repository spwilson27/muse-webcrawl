Chautauqua County is the westernmost county in the U.S. state of New York. As of the 2020 census, its population was 127,657. The county seat is Mayville, and the largest city is Jamestown. The county takes its name from Chautauqua Lake, whose name derives from a Seneca (Erie) word whose meaning is debated — commonly rendered "bag tied in the middle," describing the lake's shape, or "place where fish are taken out." The county borders Lake Erie to the northwest and Pennsylvania to the south.

History

The land was part of the Holland Purchase. Chautauqua County was created from Genesee County in 1808 and fully organised in 1811. In the summer of 1874 the Chautauqua Assembly was founded on the lake's shore at what became the Chautauqua Institution, giving the county's name to the national Chautauqua movement in adult education. Jamestown, at the lake's southern end, grew into a major furniture and metalworking centre, drawing many Swedish immigrants; it is the birthplace of Lucille Ball, commemorated by the Lucille Ball Desi Arnaz Museum and the National Comedy Center.

Geography

According to the U.S. Census Bureau, the county covers about 1,500 square miles, of which roughly 1,060 are land and 440 — most of them Lake Erie — are water. Chautauqua Lake, about 17 miles long, is one of the largest inland lakes in New York and entirely within the county. The terrain rises from the Lake Erie plain to the wooded hills of the Allegheny Plateau in the south. Principal communities include Jamestown, the city of Dunkirk and adjacent village of Fredonia on Lake Erie, Mayville, Westfield, and Lakewood.

Economy and attractions

Tourism centres on Chautauqua Lake and the Chautauqua Institution's summer season. Concord grapes — the county, with neighbouring Erie County, Pennsylvania, forms the largest Concord grape-growing region in the world, home to Welch's processing plants around Westfield — dominate agriculture along the Lake Erie shore. Higher education includes SUNY Fredonia and Jamestown Community College. Interstate 90 and the Southern Tier Expressway (I-86/NY-17) cross the county.

Notable people

  • Lucille Ball, actress and comedian, born in Jamestown
  • Roger Tory Peterson, naturalist and field-guide author, born in Jamestown
  • Robert H. Jackson, U.S. Supreme Court justice and Nuremberg prosecutor, from Frewsburg
  • Salman Rushdie, novelist, who survived an attack while lecturing at the Chautauqua Institution in 2022
