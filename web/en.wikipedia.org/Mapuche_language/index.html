Mapuche language

Mapuche or Mapudungun is an Araucanian language spoken in south-central Chile and west-central Argentina by the Mapuche people. It is traditionally considered a language isolate, though it is grouped with the closely related and nearly extinct Huilliche language in the Araucanian family. Estimates of active speakers range from about 140,000 to 250,000, concentrated in the regions of Biobío, La Araucanía, and Los Lagos in Chile.

History and status

Mapudungun was described by Jesuit missionaries in the seventeenth century (grammars by Luis de Valdivia, 1606, and Andrés Febrés, 1765). It lacks official status in Chile, though intercultural bilingual education programs exist, and the language is endangered, with transmission to children declining as Spanish dominates schooling and media.

Features

The language has six vowels (including a high central unrounded vowel written ü) and a moderately complex consonant system. It is polysynthetic and agglutinative: verbs encode subject, object, direction, and evidential-like distinctions, and noun incorporation is frequent. Several competing alphabets — including the Ragileo, Unified, and Azümchefi systems — are used to write it in Latin script.
