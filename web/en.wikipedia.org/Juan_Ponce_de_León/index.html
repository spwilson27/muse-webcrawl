Juan Ponce de León

Juan Ponce de León
Born: 1474, Santervás de Campos, Castile
Died: July 1521, Havana, Cuba
Known for: First governor of Puerto Rico; exploring Florida

Juan Ponce de León (1474 – July 1521) was a Spanish explorer and conquistador known for leading the first official European expedition to Puerto Rico and Florida. He served as the first governor of Puerto Rico (1508–1511).

Career

Ponce de León sailed with Columbus's second voyage in 1493, helped conquer Hispaniola and the Taíno of Puerto Rico, founded settlements including Caparra, and accumulated wealth from gold and plantations.

Florida

In 1513 he explored the coast of a land he named La Florida, landing near present-day St. Augustine, and encountered the Gulf Stream. Legend later linked his voyage to a search for the Fountain of Youth, a story historians regard as largely mythical. On a colonising return in 1521 he was wounded by a Calusa arrow and died in Havana.

Legacy

The city of Ponce in Puerto Rico and Ponce de León Inlet in Florida bear his name.
