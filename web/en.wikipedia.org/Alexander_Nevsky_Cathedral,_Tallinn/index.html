Alexander Nevsky Cathedral, Tallinn

The Alexander Nevsky Cathedral is an Eastern Orthodox cathedral on Toompea Hill in Tallinn, Estonia. Built in the Russian Revival style between 1894 and 1900, during the period when Estonia was part of the Russian Empire, it is Tallinn's largest Orthodox church.

LocationToompea, Tallinn, Estonia
DenominationEstonian Orthodox Church of the Moscow Patriarchate
Built1894–1900
ArchitectMikhail Preobrazhensky

History

The cathedral was built on a site in front of Toompea Castle, opposite the Estonian parliament, and dedicated to Saint Alexander Nevsky, commemorating his victory in the Battle on the Ice in 1242. Designed by Mikhail Preobrazhensky, it features five onion domes and houses Tallinn's largest bell, part of a set of eleven bells.

So disliked was the cathedral by many Estonians as a symbol of Russification that its demolition was seriously debated in the 1920s and again in the late 1930s, but it was never carried out. Neglect followed under Soviet rule; the building was restored after Estonian independence in 1991.

Architecture

The richly decorated interior contains mosaics and iconostases, and the cathedral follows a three-part basilica plan typical of Russian church design of the period.
