Admiral Sir Henry Harwood Harwood, KCB, OBE (19 January 1888 – 9 June 1950) was a Royal Navy officer who won fame for his role in the Battle of the River Plate in December 1939, the first major naval battle of the Second World War.

Early life and career

Harwood was born in London and educated at Stubbington House School before joining the Royal Navy as a cadet in 1903. He specialised in torpedoes and served during the First World War. Between the wars he held a variety of sea and staff appointments, including service on the South America station, where he became familiar with the waters in which he would later fight his most famous action.

Battle of the River Plate

At the outbreak of the Second World War Harwood was a commodore commanding the South America Division, consisting of the heavy cruiser HMS Exeter and the light cruisers HMS Ajax and HMS Achilles. Anticipating that the German pocket battleship Admiral Graf Spee would head for the busy shipping lanes off the River Plate estuary, Harwood concentrated his force there. On 13 December 1939 his cruisers engaged the more heavily armed German ship. Although Exeter was badly damaged and Ajax and Achilles were also hit, Harwood's aggressive tactics drove Graf Spee into Montevideo harbour, where her captain, Hans Langsdorff, scuttled her on 17 December rather than face what he believed was a superior force. Harwood was promoted to rear admiral and knighted for the victory.

Later service

Harwood later served as a Lord Commissioner of the Admiralty and as Commander-in-Chief, Mediterranean Fleet, in 1942, overseeing naval operations connected with the North African campaign. Ill health forced his retirement in 1945. He died at Goring-on-Thames in 1950.
