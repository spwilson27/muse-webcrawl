The Araucanía Region (Spanish: Región de La Araucanía) is one of Chile's sixteen first-order administrative divisions, located in the south of the country between the Biobío Region to the north and the Los Ríos Region to the south. Its capital is Temuco. The region comprises the provinces of Cautín and Malleco. It takes its name from the historical territory of Araucanía, home of the Mapuche people, who resisted Spanish and later Chilean expansion for centuries during the Arauco War.

Geography

The region spans the Pacific coast, the Chilean Central Valley, and the Andes, where snow-capped volcanoes such as Llaima, Villarrica, and Lanín rise above lakes and ancient araucaria (monkey puzzle) forests. National parks including Conguillío, Huerquehue, Nahuelbuta, and Villarrica protect this landscape. Major rivers include the Imperial, Toltén, and the upper Biobío.

History and demography

Following the Chilean state's "Occupation of Araucanía" (1861–1883), Mapuche land was drastically reduced and the region was opened to Chilean and European settlement. Today Araucanía has the highest proportion of Indigenous (Mapuche) population of any Chilean region — roughly a third of its inhabitants — and also the country's highest poverty rates. A long-running land-rights conflict between Mapuche communities, forestry companies, and the state continues in the region, which is also a leading center for forestry, agriculture, and tourism.
