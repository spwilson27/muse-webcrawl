Sir Clive Hubert Lloyd (born 31 August 1944) is a Guyanese-British former cricketer who captained the West Indies to become the dominant force in world cricket between 1974 and 1985. A tall, powerfully built left-handed batsman recognisable by his thick-rimmed glasses, Lloyd led the West Indies to victory in the first two Cricket World Cups, in 1975 and 1979, and to a record run of 27 Test matches without defeat, including 11 consecutive victories.

Playing career

Lloyd made his Test debut against India in 1966, scoring 82 and 78 not out. In county cricket he spent two decades with Lancashire, whom he also captained, becoming one of the most popular overseas players in England. As a batsman he scored 19 Test centuries, including a famous 242 not out against India in 1975, and he was a brilliant cover fielder in his younger days before a back injury slowed him.

Captaincy

Appointed West Indies captain in 1974, Lloyd built a team around a battery of four fast bowlers — Roberts, Holding, Garner, and Marshall among them — and outstanding batsmen such as Viv Richards and Gordon Greenidge. After defeat in the 1975–76 series in Australia he resolved that his side would match Australian aggression; the result was a decade of dominance that made the West Indies a symbol of Caribbean pride. Lloyd's team also reached the 1983 World Cup final, losing to India.

Later life

After retiring in 1985, Lloyd worked as a match referee for the International Cricket Council and in West Indies cricket administration. He was knighted in 2020 for services to cricket and is a member of the ICC Cricket Hall of Fame.
