Gaston Maurice Julia (3 February 1893 – 19 March 1978) was a French mathematician who devised the formula for the Julia set, one of the foundational objects of fractal geometry and the theory of complex dynamics. His 1918 memoir on the iteration of rational functions won the Grand Prix of the French Academy of Sciences and made him famous in his twenties; the work was largely eclipsed until computer graphics in the 1980s revealed the beauty of Julia sets and the related Mandelbrot set.

Life

Julia was born in Sidi Bel Abbès in French Algeria and grew up in a family of Catalan origin. A brilliant student, he won a scholarship to the Lycée Janson-de-Sailly in Paris and entered the École Normale Supérieure in 1911. His studies were interrupted by the First World War: called up in 1914, he was gravely wounded in the face during a German attack in January 1915, losing his nose; he wore a leather strap over the injury for the rest of his life and refused further reconstructive surgery after early failed operations.

Work

Working in hospital between operations, Julia wrote his great memoir Mémoire sur l'itération des fonctions rationnelles, published in the Journal de Mathématiques Pures et Appliquées in 1918. It described, for an iterated rational map of the complex plane, the set of points whose orbits remain bounded — now called the Julia set — and its chaotic properties; Pierre Fatou had independently developed similar results. Julia went on to work on functional analysis and Hilbert spaces (Les Espaces de Hilbert), and held chairs at the Sorbonne and the École Polytechnique. He was elected to the Académie des Sciences in 1934 and ran an influential seminar in the 1930s, in which the Bourbaki group formed.

Legacy

Benoit Mandelbrot, whose uncle Szolem had been Julia's colleague, revisited the iteration theory with computers; the Mandelbrot set (1980) — a map of which Julia sets are connected — turned Julia's abstract constructions into iconic images and revived scholarly interest in his work. Julia died in Paris in 1978, just before that revival.
