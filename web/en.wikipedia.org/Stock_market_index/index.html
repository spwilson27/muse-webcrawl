Stock market index

A stock market index is a measurement of the performance of a section of the stock market, computed from the prices of a selected basket of stocks. Indices are used by investors and financial media as benchmarks of market and economic performance, and as the basis for index funds and derivatives.

Weighting methods

Indices differ chiefly in how constituents are weighted. In price-weighted indices, such as the Dow Jones Industrial Average, each stock influences the index in proportion to its price per share. In market-capitalization-weighted indices, such as the S&P 500 and the FTSE 100, companies are weighted by their total market value, often adjusted for free float. Equal-weighted indices give each constituent the same weight.

Major indices

Widely followed indices include the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite in the United States, the Nikkei 225 in Japan, the DAX in Germany, the Hang Seng in Hong Kong, and global indices such as the MSCI World. Sector, regional, bond and commodity indices cover other market segments.

Uses

Indices serve as benchmarks against which portfolio performance is measured, as underlyings for futures and options, and as the tracked targets of passive investment products. Because most indices are not directly investable, funds replicate them by holding the constituent shares.
