Grand Inquisitor (Spanish: Inquisidor General) was the lead official of the Spanish Inquisition. The Grand Inquisitor headed the Council of the Supreme and General Inquisition (Suprema), appointed inquisitors to regional tribunals, issued edicts and instructions, and answered directly to the Spanish Crown, which nominated him subject to papal confirmation.

History

The office was created in 1483 when Tomás de Torquemada, confessor to Isabella I of Castile, was appointed inquisitor general of Castile and soon of Aragon as well. Torquemada organised the Inquisition into an effective royal instrument directed mainly at conversos — converted Jews suspected of secretly practising Judaism — and his name became synonymous with religious persecution. Later holders included Cardinal Francisco Jiménez de Cisneros and Fernando de Valdés, who issued the 1559 Index of prohibited books.

In literature

"The Grand Inquisitor" is also the title of a famous chapter of Fyodor Dostoevsky's novel The Brothers Karamazov (1880), a prose poem told by Ivan Karamazov in which a cardinal of Seville imprisons a returned Christ and argues that the Church has corrected his work by accepting the temptations of miracle, mystery, and authority that Christ rejected.
