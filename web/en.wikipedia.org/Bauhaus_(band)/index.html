Bauhaus (band)

Bauhaus are an English rock band formed in Northampton in 1978 by vocalist Peter Murphy, guitarist Daniel Ash, drummer Kevin Haskins and bassist David J. Named after the German art school, they are widely regarded as pioneers of gothic rock, combining post-punk with glam, dub and dark theatrical imagery.

History

Their debut single, "Bela Lugosi's Dead" (1979), a nine-minute dub-inflected track, became a gothic anthem. After signing to 4AD and then Beggars Banquet they released the albums In the Flat Field (1980), Mask (1981), The Sky's Gone Out (1982) — which reached the UK top five — and Burning from the Inside (1983). Their cover of David Bowie's "Ziggy Stardust" was a UK top-20 hit.

Later years

The band split in 1983; members went on to Tones on Tail and Love and Rockets, and Murphy pursued a solo career. Bauhaus reunited for tours in 1998 and 2005–2008, releasing the album Go Away White in 2008, and re-formed again in 2019 before a further split in 2022. The single "Drink the New Wine" (2022) was their first new music in 14 years.

Legacy

Bauhaus's bleak, bass-driven sound and Murphy's baritone stage persona profoundly influenced goth subculture and later acts in dark alternative music.
