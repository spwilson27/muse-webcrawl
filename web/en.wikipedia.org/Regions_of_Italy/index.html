Regions of Italy

The twenty regions of the Italian Republic.

Italy is divided into 20 regions (Italian: regioni), the first-level constituent entities of the Republic under the Constitution of 1948. Five regions — Aosta Valley, Friuli-Venezia Giulia, Sardinia, Sicily and Trentino-Alto Adige/Südtirol — enjoy special autonomous status, largely to protect linguistic minorities and island territories. The remaining fifteen have ordinary statutes.

Government

Each region has an elected regional council (legislature) and a president who heads the regional government (giunta). Regions legislate on matters not reserved to the state, including health care, which is delivered largely through regional health services. Ordinary regions were not actually instituted until 1970, despite the constitutional provision of 1948.
