Augusta Treverorum

Location: Modern Trier, Rhineland-Palatinate, Germany

Founded: c. 16 BC

Region: Gallia Belgica, Roman Empire

Augusta Treverorum (Latin for "City of Augustus among the Treveri") was a Roman city on the Moselle River, from which modern Trier descends. Founded around 16 BC under the emperor Augustus, it is considered Germany's oldest city. It was established among the Treveri, a Celtic tribe, and grew into a major administrative and commercial centre of the province of Gallia Belgica.

Imperial residence

In the late Roman Empire, Augusta Treverorum became one of the capitals of the Western Empire and a residence of the Tetrarchy; Constantine the Great resided there before becoming sole emperor. The city boasted monumental structures including the Porta Nigra — the largest surviving Roman city gate — the Imperial Baths, the Basilica of Constantine (Aula Palatina), a large amphitheatre, and the Roman bridge over the Moselle, whose piers are still in use.

Decline and legacy

The city suffered during the invasions of the 5th century and passed to the Franks, but retained importance as an episcopal see; tradition held its bishopric to have been founded by companions of Saint Peter. The Roman monuments of Trier, including those of the ancient Augusta Treverorum, are now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
