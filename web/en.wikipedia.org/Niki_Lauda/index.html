Niki Lauda

Andreas Nikolaus "Niki" Lauda (22 February 1949 – 20 May 2019) was an Austrian racing driver and aviation entrepreneur. He was a three-time Formula One World Drivers' Champion, winning in 1975 and 1977 with Ferrari and in 1984 with McLaren. He is regarded as one of the greatest drivers in the history of the sport.

Racing career

Born in Vienna into a wealthy family that disapproved of racing, Lauda borrowed money to buy his way into Formula One with March and BRM before joining Ferrari in 1974. He won his first championship in 1975. On 1 August 1976, during the German Grand Prix at the Nürburgring, Lauda crashed and his car burst into flames; he suffered severe burns and inhaled toxic gases, and was widely expected to die. In one of sport's most famous comebacks, he returned to racing only six weeks later, finishing fourth in Italy despite his injuries, and lost the 1976 title to rival and friend James Hunt by a single point after withdrawing from the rain-soaked finale in Japan.

Lauda won the 1977 title, left Ferrari, raced for Brabham, and retired in 1979 to found the airline Lauda Air. He returned with McLaren in 1982 and won his third championship in 1984 by half a point over teammate Alain Prost — still the closest margin in F1 history. After retiring for good in 1985, he served as a consultant to Ferrari and later as non-executive chairman of the Mercedes team, playing a key role in its dominant era.

Legacy

Lauda's rivalry with Hunt was dramatised in the 2013 film Rush. He died in Zurich in 2019, aged 70.
