Las Vegas Strip

The Las Vegas Strip is a stretch of Las Vegas Boulevard South in Clark County, Nevada, internationally known for its concentration of resort hotels and casinos. The Strip is approximately 4.2 miles (6.8 km) long and lies in the unincorporated towns of Paradise and Winchester, just south of the Las Vegas city limits. Many of the world's largest hotel, casino, and resort properties are located on the Strip, including the MGM Grand, Caesars Palace, Bellagio, The Venetian, Luxor, and Mandalay Bay.

History

The Strip's development began in the 1940s with El Rancho Vegas (1941) and the Flamingo (1946), built by mobster Bugsy Siegel. Successive eras brought mob-financed resorts of the 1950s (the Sands, the Desert Inn), corporatization after Howard Hughes's buying spree in the late 1960s, and the megaresort era launched by Steve Wynn's Mirage in 1989, followed by themed giants such as Bellagio (1998) with its famous fountains. The famous "Welcome to Fabulous Las Vegas" sign, erected in 1959, stands in the median near the Strip's southern end.

Today

The Strip hosts tens of millions of visitors annually and features headline residencies, conventions, championship boxing, and, since 2023, the Las Vegas Grand Prix on the street circuit incorporating the boulevard. As of the 2020s it remains the symbolic center of the American gambling and entertainment industry.
