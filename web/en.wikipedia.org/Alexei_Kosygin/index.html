Alexei Kosygin

Alexei Nikolayevich Kosygin (21 February 1904 – 18 December 1980) was a Soviet statesman who served as Premier of the Soviet Union (Chairman of the Council of Ministers) from 1964 to 1980. Together with Leonid Brezhnev he led the USSR after the removal of Nikita Khrushchev, and he is associated with attempts at economic reform, most notably the 1965 "Kosygin reform", which sought to introduce profit and sales incentives into Soviet planning.

Alexei Kosygin, Premier of the Soviet Union 1964–1980.

Early career

Born in Saint Petersburg, Kosygin fought in the Russian Civil War, studied textile engineering, and rose through the Leningrad party and industrial administration. During the Second World War he helped organise the evacuation of industry and supplies during the siege of Leningrad. After the war he became a full member of the Politburo under Stalin.

Premiership

After Khrushchev's ouster in October 1964, Kosygin became head of government while Brezhnev led the party. His 1965 reforms gave enterprises more autonomy and judged them by sales and profitability rather than gross output quotas; the reforms produced some gains before being rolled back after the Prague Spring of 1968. Kosygin was also a prominent diplomatic figure, mediating the Tashkent Declaration between India and Pakistan in 1966 and pursuing détente with the West. He resigned in October 1980, weeks before his death.
