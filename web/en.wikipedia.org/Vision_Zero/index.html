Vision Zero

Vision Zero is a multi-national road traffic safety project that aims to achieve a highway system with no fatalities or serious injuries involving road traffic. It started in Sweden and was approved by the Swedish parliament in October 1997. Its core principle is that "it can never be ethically acceptable that people are killed or seriously injured when moving within the road transport system", shifting responsibility from road users alone to the designers of the system.

Principles

Vision Zero rests on a "safe system" approach: humans make mistakes and the human body has limited tolerance to crash forces, so roads, vehicles, and speeds must be designed so that mistakes are not fatal. Typical measures include separating vulnerable users from motor traffic, roundabouts in place of signalised junctions, median barriers, speed limits set by biomechanical tolerance (for example, 30 km/h where pedestrians mix with cars), and stricter vehicle safety standards.

Adoption and results

Sweden cut road deaths by more than half in the two decades after adoption, achieving among the lowest rates in the world. The philosophy spread to Norway (whose capital, Oslo, recorded years with zero pedestrian and cyclist deaths), the Netherlands' related "Sustainable Safety" programme, the European Union, and dozens of cities in the United States beginning with New York City in 2014. Results in adopting US cities have been mixed, and critics caution that slogans without funding, enforcement, and street redesign accomplish little — but the ethical framing has reshaped traffic-safety policy worldwide.
