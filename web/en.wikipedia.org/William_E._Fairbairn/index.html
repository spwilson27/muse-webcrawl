William Ewart Fairbairn (28 February 1885 – 20 June 1960) was a British Royal Marines officer and police officer. He developed hand-to-hand combat methods for the Shanghai Municipal Police and for British commandos during the Second World War, and with Eric A. Sykes designed the Fairbairn–Sykes fighting knife.

Career

Fairbairn joined the Royal Marine Light Infantry in 1901 and later served with the Shanghai Municipal Police from 1907, rising to Assistant Commissioner. Working in one of the world's most violent ports, he studied jujutsu, Chinese martial arts and boxing, and distilled what he learned into a practical system of close combat he called Defendu. He organised and trained the force's Riot Squad and reserve unit, and wrote manuals including Defendu (1926) and Scientific Self-Defence (1931).

Second World War

Returning to Britain in 1940, Fairbairn was commissioned into the General List and, with Sykes, trained British Commandos, the Special Operations Executive and other Allied units in silent killing, unarmed combat and close-quarter shooting at training centres including Lochailort in Scotland. His wartime books Get Tough! (1942) and Hands Off! spread his methods widely. After the war he advised police and military forces in Cyprus and Singapore.

Legacy

Fairbairn's methods influenced modern military close-quarters combat, and the knife he co-designed remains in production and is depicted on the insignia of several special forces units.
