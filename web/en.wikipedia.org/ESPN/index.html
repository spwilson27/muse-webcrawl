ESPN

ESPN (originally the Entertainment and Sports Programming Network) is an American international cable sports television network owned by ESPN Inc., a joint venture between the Walt Disney Company (80%) and Hearst Communications (20%). Founded by Bill Rasmussen with his son Scott and Ed Eagan, it launched on 7 September 1979 and is headquartered in Bristol, Connecticut. Its flagship programme, SportsCenter, has been broadcast since the network's first day.

History

ESPN began as a low-budget operation broadcasting niche sports before securing rights that built its audience, including NCAA basketball, the NFL draft and, from 1987, Sunday night NFL games. It expanded into a family of channels — ESPN2 (1993), ESPNews, ESPN Classic, ESPNU and others — as well as ESPN Radio, ESPN.com, ESPN The Magazine, international channels, the ESPN+ streaming service (2018) and a direct-to-consumer flagship streaming service launched in 2025 under the ESPN name. ESPN holds major US broadcast rights partnerships with the NFL, NBA, MLB, NHL, college football playoffs and many other properties.

Criticism and influence

ESPN has been credited with transforming sports coverage and the economics of televised sport, and criticised for the size of the carriage fees it charges cable operators, its influence over the sports it covers, and declining subscriber numbers amid cord-cutting, which have led to restructurings and layoffs since the mid-2010s.
