Right to protest

The right to protest is the internationally recognised freedom of people to gather and express dissent against governments, policies or other powers. It is not usually enumerated as a single right but is protected through a cluster of human rights: freedom of speech, freedom of peaceful assembly and freedom of association.

Peaceful assembly is protected under international human rights law.

Legal basis

Article 20 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and Article 21 of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights protect peaceful assembly. Regional instruments such as the European Convention on Human Rights (Article 11) and national constitutions—including the First Amendment in the United States—provide similar guarantees. Rights may be restricted only by measures that are lawful, necessary and proportionate to a legitimate aim such as public safety.

Forms of protest

Protest encompasses marches, rallies, pickets, sit-ins, boycotts and strikes, as well as symbolic and artistic expression. Civil disobedience—the deliberate, nonviolent breach of a law considered unjust—has been influential in movements from Indian independence to the American civil rights movement, though it occupies a more contested legal position than lawful assembly.

Contemporary issues

Modern disputes concern police tactics such as kettling, surveillance of demonstrators, permit regimes, anti-protest legislation, and the policing of online organisation. Human rights bodies stress that the right covers spontaneous assemblies and that states have positive obligations to facilitate peaceful protest.
