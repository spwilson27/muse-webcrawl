Mount Ararat

Mount Ararat is a snow-capped dormant volcanic mountain in eastern Turkey, near the borders with Armenia, Iran, and Azerbaijan's Nakhchivan exclave. It consists of two cones: Greater Ararat, at 5,137 metres the highest peak in Turkey, and Little Ararat, at 3,896 metres.

Geography

Ararat is a stratovolcano formed by lava flows and pyroclastic material. It has no recorded eruptions in historical times, though a phreatic eruption and mudflow may have occurred in 1840. Its summit carries a permanent ice cap that has been shrinking since the mid-20th century.

Cultural significance

In the biblical Book of Genesis, the mountains of Ararat are named as the resting place of Noah's Ark after the flood. Ararat is a national symbol of Armenia, appearing on its coat of arms, although the mountain itself lies within Turkey.

Climbing

The first recorded ascent was made in 1829 by Friedrich Parrot and Khachatur Abovian. Today the mountain is climbed mainly from the southern side via Doğubayazıt, with permits required.
