The Spanish Empire, sometimes called the Hispanic Monarchy or the Catholic Monarchy, was a colonial empire governed by Spain between 1492 and 1976. Beginning with Columbus's voyage of 1492 and the conquest of the Aztec and Inca empires, it became the first truly global empire — under Philip II it was said that "the sun never set" on it. At its height it ruled most of the Americas south of the present-day United States, the Philippines, and territories in Europe, Africa and Oceania, spreading the Spanish language and Catholicism across them.

Rise

The marriage of Isabella of Castile and Ferdinand of Aragon united Spain's crowns; the conquest of Granada and Columbus's landfall both came in 1492. Hernán Cortés conquered the Aztec Empire (1519–1521) and Francisco Pizarro the Inca Empire (1532–1533), aided by steel, horses, alliances and Old World diseases that devastated Indigenous populations. Silver from Potosí and Zacatecas flowed through the treasure fleets to Seville and onward to Manila via the galleon trade linking Acapulco to the Philippines (colonised from 1565).

Administration and society

The empire was governed through the Council of the Indies and viceroyalties — New Spain, Peru, later New Granada and the Río de la Plata — and a caste hierarchy distinguishing peninsulares, criollos, mestizos and Indigenous and African peoples. Encomienda and mita labour systems, missions, and the Catholic Church shaped colonial society, alongside fierce debates about Indigenous rights led by figures such as Bartolomé de las Casas.

Decline

Spain's European power waned through the 17th century; the Bourbon Reforms tried to tighten control in the 18th. Napoleon's invasion of Spain in 1808 triggered independence wars that by 1825 had ended Spanish rule on the American mainland. Cuba, Puerto Rico and the Philippines were lost in the Spanish–American War of 1898; the last African possessions were relinquished by 1976.
