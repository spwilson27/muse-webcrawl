Associated bundle


An associated bundle is built from a principal bundle and a group action on a fibre.

In mathematics, an associated bundle is a fibre bundle constructed from a principal bundle and an action of its structure group on a space. The construction associates to a principal G-bundle P over a base B, and a left action of G on a space F, a new bundle whose fibres are copies of F twisted together in the same way as P.

Construction

Given the principal bundle P and the action of G on F, the associated bundle is the quotient of the product P × F by the equivalence relation identifying (pg, f) with (p, g·f) for every group element g. The natural projection onto B makes the quotient into a fibre bundle with fibre F.

Examples

The most important examples arise when F is a vector space and the action is a representation of G; the result is an associated vector bundle. The frame bundle of a vector bundle is a principal bundle, and the original vector bundle can be recovered from it as an associated bundle. Tensor bundles, determinant bundles and bundles of linear maps are all obtained this way, as are the spinor bundles of spin geometry.
