The Rodin Museum (French: Musée Rodin) in Paris is a museum devoted to the works of the French sculptor Auguste Rodin. It occupies the Hôtel Biron, an elegant 18th-century mansion — built in the era when Rococo taste still shaped Parisian interiors — where Rodin himself lived and worked from 1908 until his death in 1917. A second site, Rodin's home and studio at Meudon, is also part of the museum.

History

Rodin rented rooms in the Hôtel Biron from 1908, joining tenants such as Henri Matisse and Rainer Maria Rilke, who served briefly as his secretary. In 1916 he reached an agreement with the French state: he would donate his entire collection — his sculptures, drawings, antiquities and his works by other artists, including paintings by Van Gogh and Monet — in return for the state buying the Hôtel Biron and converting it into a museum of his work. The museum opened in 1919, two years after his death.

Collection and gardens

The museum holds the world's richest Rodin collection: some 6,600 sculptures in marble, bronze, plaster and terracotta, about 8,000 drawings, and extensive photographic and personal archives. Masterpieces on view include The Thinker, The Kiss, The Burghers of Calais, the monumental Gates of Hell and the statue of Balzac. Major bronzes are displayed in the three-hectare garden, one of the most celebrated sculpture gardens in Europe, within sight of Les Invalides. Works by Rodin's pupil and lover Camille Claudel are also exhibited.

Other Rodin museums

A separate Rodin Museum in Philadelphia, opened in 1929 with the collection of Jules Mastbaum, houses the largest Rodin collection outside Paris; significant holdings also exist in museums worldwide, authorised by the Paris museum's right to cast posthumous bronzes from Rodin's original moulds.
