Arihant (Jainism)

Arihant (Sanskrit: अरिहन्त, "conqueror") is a term in Jainism for a soul that has conquered inner passions such as anger, pride, deceit, and greed, and has thereby destroyed the karma that obscures omniscience. Having attained kevala jnana (infinite knowledge), an Arihant is a living, omniscient being who preaches the path of liberation. Arihants occupy the highest place in the Jain hierarchy of revered beings and are the first of the five supreme beings (Pancha-Parameshthi) saluted in the Namokar Mantra.

Attainment

According to Jain doctrine, when a soul destroys the four destructive (ghati) karmas — those that delude, obscure knowledge, obscure perception, and obstruct energy — it attains omniscience while still embodied. Such a being is an Arihant. Those Arihants who go on to establish a fourfold religious community (monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen) and revive the teaching in their era are called Tirthankaras; there are 24 Tirthankaras in the present time cycle. An Arihant who is not a Tirthankara is called a Kevalin.

Final liberation

At death, an Arihant sheds the remaining non-destructive (aghati) karmas and becomes a Siddha, a liberated soul residing eternally at the top of the universe. Jain temples depict Arihants and Tirthankaras in serene meditative postures, and their lives are traditionally described through five auspicious events (kalyanakas), from conception to liberation.
