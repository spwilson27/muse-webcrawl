Racial discrimination
Definition: Differential treatment based on race or ethnicity
Key treaty: International Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Racial Discrimination (1965)

Racial discrimination is any distinction, exclusion, restriction, or preference based on race, colour, descent, or national or ethnic origin that impairs the equal enjoyment of human rights and fundamental freedoms. It can be direct (explicit differential treatment) or indirect (neutral rules with disparate effects), and can occur interpersonally or institutionally, as in housing, employment, education, policing, and lending.

International law

The International Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Racial Discrimination (ICERD), adopted by the United Nations in 1965 and in force from 1969, obliges states parties to prohibit and eliminate racial discrimination and guarantees equality before the law. Most states also ban racial discrimination constitutionally or by statute, such as the U.S. Civil Rights Act of 1964.

Forms and study

Scholars distinguish individual prejudice from structural or systemic discrimination embedded in institutions and historical disadvantage — for example, redlining in housing markets. Measurement approaches include audit studies, in which matched testers of different races apply for jobs or housing to detect unequal treatment.
