Mygale is a historical name applied to the large, hairy spiders of the infraorder Mygalomorphae — the group that includes the tarantulas, trapdoor spiders, and funnel-web spiders. In French, the word mygale remains the ordinary common name for a tarantula.

Taxonomic history

The name was used by early arachnologists, including Walckenaer and Latreille in the early 19th century, for a genus grouping the largest spiders then known. As spider taxonomy developed, the constituent species were redistributed among many genera of the family Theraphosidae and other mygalomorph families, and Mygale fell out of use as a formal genus name.

Characteristics of mygalomorphs

Mygalomorph spiders are distinguished by chelicerae that strike downward, parallel to the body's long axis, unlike the pincer-like fangs of most other spiders. Most are heavy-bodied, long-lived burrowers; females of some tarantula species may live for several decades.
