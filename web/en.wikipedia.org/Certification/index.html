Certification

Certification is the formal attestation or confirmation of certain characteristics of an object, person, or organization. This confirmation is often, but not always, provided by some form of external review, education, assessment, or audit. Accreditation is a producer's certification process. According to the U.S. National Council on Measurement in Education, a certification test is a credentialing test used to determine whether individuals are knowledgeable enough in a given occupational area to be labeled "competent to practice" in that area.

Types of certification

Certifications fall into several broad categories. Professional certifications attest that an individual has met standards of knowledge and competence in an occupation, such as medicine, law, accounting, or information technology. Product certification confirms that a product meets specified requirements, often relating to safety or performance. Process and system certification, such as ISO 9001 quality management certification, evaluates an organization's management systems rather than its individual outputs.

Certification process

A typical certification process involves application, examination or audit, evaluation against published criteria, and a formal decision by the certifying body. Certified individuals or organizations are usually required to maintain their status through continuing education, periodic re-examination, or surveillance audits. Failure to meet ongoing requirements can result in suspension or withdrawal of the certification.

Accreditation

Certification bodies themselves are often accredited by national accreditation bodies, which assess their competence and impartiality against international standards such as ISO/IEC 17011. This layered structure — sometimes called the certification pyramid — is intended to ensure that certificates are trustworthy across borders and industries.

Criticism

Certification schemes have been criticized for encouraging credential inflation, for conflicts of interest when certifiers are paid by those they assess, and for measuring test-taking ability rather than real-world competence. Nevertheless, certification remains a widespread mechanism for signaling quality and protecting the public.
