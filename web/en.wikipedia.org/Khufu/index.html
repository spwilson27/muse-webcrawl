Khufu

Khufu (Ancient Egyptian: Ḫw.f-wj; Greek: Χέοψ, Cheops) was an ancient Egyptian monarch who was the second pharaoh of the Fourth Dynasty, ruling during the first half of the 26th century BC, in the Old Kingdom period. He succeeded his father Sneferu and was succeeded by his son Djedefre.

Reign

Khufu is widely accepted as the builder of the Great Pyramid of Giza, the largest Egyptian pyramid and the only one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World still in existence. Very little is known about Khufu's reign with certainty; estimates of its length range from roughly 23 to over 60 years. His full name, Khnum-Khufu, means "the god Khnum protects me."

The Great Pyramid

The Great Pyramid originally stood about 146.6 metres tall and was the tallest human-made structure in the world for more than 3,800 years. It is estimated to contain some 2.3 million stone blocks. Within and around the pyramid complex were subsidiary pyramids for Khufu's queens, boat pits, and in 1954 the discovery of the intact Khufu ship — a full-size solar barque now displayed at Giza.

Depictions

Despite the enormity of his monument, the only complete three-dimensional depiction of Khufu known today is a small ivory statuette, about 7.5 cm tall, found at Abydos and now in the Egyptian Museum in Cairo. Greek historians such as Herodotus portrayed Khufu as a cruel and oppressive tyrant who closed temples and forced his subjects to labour, though Egyptologists regard these accounts as later distortions.

Family

Khufu's mother was Queen Hetepheres I, whose treasure-filled shaft tomb was found near the Great Pyramid. His sons included Djedefre and Khafre, both of whom became pharaohs, and the prince Kawab; his daughters included Hetepheres II and Meritites II.
