Desert pavement

A desert pavement, also called reg (in the western Sahara), serir or gibber plain, is a desert surface covered with closely packed, interlocking angular or rounded rock fragments.

Formation

Explanations include deflation — wind removing fine material and leaving stones behind — and the upward migration of stones through cycles of wetting, drying and dust accretion beneath them. Stones often develop a dark desert varnish.

Significance

Pavements protect underlying sediment from erosion and can preserve archaeological material on their surfaces for millennia.
