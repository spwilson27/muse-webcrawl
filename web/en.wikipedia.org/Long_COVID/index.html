Long COVID

Long COVID affects multiple organ systems

Long COVID, also known as post-COVID-19 syndrome or post-acute sequelae of COVID-19 (PASC), is a condition characterised by long-term health problems persisting or emerging after an initial infection with SARS-CoV-2. The World Health Organization defines it as symptoms usually beginning within three months of infection, lasting at least two months, and not explained by an alternative diagnosis.

Signs and symptoms

More than 200 symptoms have been reported. The most common include fatigue, post-exertional malaise, shortness of breath, cognitive dysfunction ("brain fog"), chest pain, palpitations, muscle and joint pain, and loss or distortion of smell and taste. Symptoms may persist from the initial illness, relapse and remit, or appear after apparent recovery.

Causes and risk factors

Proposed mechanisms include viral persistence, immune dysregulation and autoimmunity, microvascular clotting and endothelial dysfunction, reactivation of latent viruses such as Epstein–Barr virus, and tissue damage from the acute infection. Risk is higher after severe acute illness, but long COVID also follows mild infections; women and middle-aged adults appear at greater risk. Vaccination and antiviral treatment during acute illness appear to reduce the risk.

Epidemiology and management

Estimates vary widely; large studies suggest a substantial minority of infected people experience prolonged symptoms, amounting to tens of millions worldwide. As of the mid-2020s there is no single validated treatment; care is multidisciplinary and symptom-focused, with research trials ongoing into antivirals, immunomodulatory drugs and rehabilitation approaches.
