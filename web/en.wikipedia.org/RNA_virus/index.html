RNA virus

Structure of a typical RNA virus, with its ribonucleic acid genome enclosed in a protein capsid and, in many groups, a lipid envelope.

An RNA virus is a virus that has ribonucleic acid (RNA) as its genetic material. The nucleic acid is usually single-stranded RNA (ssRNA) but it may be double-stranded (dsRNA). Notable human diseases caused by RNA viruses include influenza, COVID-19, the common cold, measles, Ebola virus disease, HIV/AIDS, rabies and hepatitis C.

The International Committee on Taxonomy of Viruses (ICTV) classifies RNA viruses as those that belong to Group III, Group IV or Group V of the Baltimore classification system. Viruses with RNA genomes that replicate via a DNA intermediate, such as retroviruses, are placed in Group VI.

Characteristics

RNA viruses generally have very high mutation rates compared to DNA viruses. This is because viral RNA-dependent RNA polymerases lack the proofreading ability of DNA polymerases, so copying errors accumulate rapidly. These high mutation rates, combined with short generation times and large population sizes, give RNA viruses high genetic variability and a strong capacity for rapid evolution, including the evasion of host immune responses.

Replication

Because host cells do not possess enzymes that copy RNA from an RNA template, RNA viruses must encode their own RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (RdRp) or carry it within the virion. Positive-sense single-stranded RNA viruses have genomes that can serve directly as mRNA and be translated by host ribosomes. Negative-sense viruses carry the complement of the coding sequence, so their genomes must first be transcribed into positive-sense RNA by a packaged polymerase. Double-stranded RNA viruses transcribe mRNA from within the core particle, shielding the dsRNA from host immune sensors.

Classification

RNA viruses infect all forms of cellular life, including animals, plants, fungi and bacteria. Major human pathogens include the influenza viruses, coronaviruses, picornaviruses such as poliovirus and rhinoviruses, flaviviruses such as dengue and Zika viruses, and the retroviruses HIV-1 and HIV-2. Plant RNA viruses, including tobacco mosaic virus — the first virus ever discovered — cause major agricultural losses.
