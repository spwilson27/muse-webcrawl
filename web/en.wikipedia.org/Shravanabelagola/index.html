Shravanabelagola

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: the Gommateshwara statue]

Shravanabelagola is a town in Hassan district, Karnataka, India, and one of the most important pilgrimage centres of Jainism. Its name refers to the "white pond of the ascetic" (belagola) between its two hills, Chandragiri and Vindhyagiri.

Dominating the town is the 17.4-metre (57-foot) monolithic statue of Gommateshwara, depicting the Jain saint Bahubali standing in meditation. Carved from a single block of granite around 981–983 CE under the patronage of the Western Ganga minister Chavundaraya, it is among the largest free-standing monolithic statues in the world. Every twelve years, hundreds of thousands of devotees attend the Mahamastakabhisheka festival, during which the statue is anointed with milk, saffron, sandalwood paste, and other offerings poured from scaffolding above its head.

History

Tradition associates Chandragiri hill with the Maurya emperor Chandragupta, who is said to have abdicated and fasted to death (sallekhana) here with the monk Bhadrabahu around 298 BCE — a tradition supported by inscriptions that make Shravanabelagola a centre of Jain learning for over two millennia. Its many basadis (temples) and more than 800 inscriptions document the patronage of the Ganga, Rashtrakuta, Hoysala, and Vijayanagara dynasties.
