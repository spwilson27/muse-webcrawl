Nationality law

Nationality law is the law of a sovereign state, and of each of its jurisdictions, that defines how a national of that state is acquired or lost. It governs the legal relationship between a person and a state, distinct from but closely related to citizenship.

Acquisition

Nationality is most commonly acquired at birth through jus sanguinis (by descent from a national parent) or jus soli (by birth within the territory), or a combination of both. It may also be acquired later through naturalization, usually after a period of residence and subject to conditions such as language knowledge and good character.

Loss and multiple nationality

Nationality may be lost by voluntary renunciation, by deprivation for fraud or certain offences, or automatically under some systems upon acquiring another nationality. States differ widely on whether they permit multiple (dual) nationality.

International law

International conventions, such as the 1961 Convention on the Reduction of Statelessness, seek to prevent statelessness. The International Court of Justice's Nottebohm case (1955) addressed the "genuine link" between a person and a state for purposes of diplomatic protection.
