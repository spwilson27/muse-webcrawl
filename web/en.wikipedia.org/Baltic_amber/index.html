Baltic amber is amber from the region of the Baltic Sea, formed from the fossilized resin of Eocene conifer forests about 44 million years ago. Chemically it is distinguished as succinite, containing 3–8% succinic acid.

Formation and deposits

The largest deposit lies on the Samland Peninsula in Russia's Kaliningrad Oblast, where amber is mined at Yantarny in open pits said to hold around 90% of the world's extractable amber. Material is also washed ashore along the coasts of Poland, Lithuania and Germany after storms.

History

Amber has been traded from the Baltic since prehistoric times along the Amber Road to the Mediterranean; it was prized by the Romans and later worked by guilds in Gdańsk and Königsberg. The famous Amber Room, made of amber panels, was created in the 18th century.

Inclusions

Baltic amber frequently preserves insects, spiders and plant fragments, providing an important record of Eocene forest ecosystems.
