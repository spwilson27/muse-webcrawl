Tokyo subway

The Tokyo subway system consists of two interconnected rapid transit networks in Tokyo, Japan: the nine lines of Tokyo Metro and the four lines of the Toei Subway, operated by the Tokyo Metropolitan Bureau of Transportation. Together they comprise 13 lines, over 290 km of route and 285 stations, and carry around 8–9 million passengers per day in normal years, making them one of the most heavily used subway systems in the world.

History

Tokyo's first subway, a section of today's Ginza Line between Ueno and Asakusa, opened in 1927 under the Tokyo Underground Railway — the first subway in East Asia. Post-war expansion created the two-network structure: the Teito Rapid Transit Authority (Eidan, privatized as Tokyo Metro in 2004) and the municipal Toei. Through services with suburban private railways and JR lines extend subway trains far into the suburbs of Chiba, Saitama, Kanagawa and western Tokyo.

Operation

Trains run about 05:00 to 01:00. Fares are distance-based, with transfers between the two networks discounted; IC cards such as Suica and PASMO are accepted. Stations are identified by letter-and-number codes, and multilingual signage (Japanese, English, Chinese, Korean) is standard. Notable features include platform screen doors on many lines, women-only cars during morning peaks, and famously punctual operations with staff ("oshiya") assisting boarding on the busiest services in earlier decades.
