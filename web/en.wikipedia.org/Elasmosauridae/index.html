Elasmosauridae is a family of plesiosaurs — marine reptiles of the Mesozoic era — characterised by extremely long necks containing more cervical vertebrae than almost any other known animal. They flourished in seas worldwide from the Jurassic until the end-Cretaceous mass extinction 66 million years ago.

Anatomy

Elasmosaurids combined a relatively small head with a neck that could make up half of the total body length; Elasmosaurus had 72 neck vertebrae, and Albertonectes had 76, the most of any known vertebrate. The body was broad and rigid, propelled by four large paddle-like flippers. Their teeth were slender and conical, suited to catching fish and cephalopods, and gastroliths (swallowed stones) are often found with their skeletons.

Discovery and the Bone Wars

The family was established by Edward Drinker Cope in 1869 for Elasmosaurus platyurus from Kansas. Cope famously reconstructed the skeleton with the head at the end of the tail, an error pointed out by Joseph Leidy and gleefully exploited by Cope's rival Othniel Charles Marsh during the "Bone Wars".

Classification and genera

Well-known elasmosaurids include Elasmosaurus, Styxosaurus, Thalassomedon, Hydrotherosaurus and the Southern Hemisphere Aristonectes, the last placed in its own subfamily Aristonectinae, which shows adaptations suggesting a different feeding style. Elasmosaurids belong to the superfamily Plesiosauroidea within the order Plesiosauria; they were not dinosaurs, but diapsid reptiles that returned to the sea.
