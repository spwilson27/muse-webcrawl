Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi (born Ibrahim Awad Ibrahim al-Badri, 1971–2019) was an Iraqi militant who led the Islamic State (IS) terrorist organization. In June 2014, after IS seized Mosul, he proclaimed a "caliphate" from the pulpit of the Great Mosque of al-Nuri, taking the regnal name Caliph Ibrahim.

Rise

Al-Badri earned a doctorate in Islamic studies in Baghdad and was detained by U.S. forces in 2004 at Camp Bucca. He became leader of the Islamic State of Iraq in 2010, expanded it into Syria in 2013 against the wishes of al-Qaeda's leadership, and broke with al-Qaeda in 2014. Under his leadership IS conquered large parts of Iraq and Syria, perpetrated mass atrocities including the genocide of the Yazidis, and inspired or directed attacks worldwide. The United States offered a reward of up to $25 million for information leading to his capture.

Death

On 26–27 October 2019, U.S. special operations forces raided his compound in Barisha, Idlib province, Syria; cornered in a tunnel, al-Baghdadi detonated a suicide vest, killing himself and two children. The Islamic State named Abu Ibrahim al-Hashimi al-Qurashi as his successor.
