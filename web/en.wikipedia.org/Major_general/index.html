Major general

Major general is a senior military rank used in many armies. It is typically a two-star general officer rank (NATO code OF-7), senior to brigadier (or brigadier general) and junior to lieutenant general. A major general often commands a division or holds senior staff appointments.

History

The rank derives from the older title "sergeant major general," the third-in-command of an army in the 16th and 17th centuries, later shortened to major general. Despite the name, a major general outranks a lieutenant but is outranked by a lieutenant general, a relic of this historical ordering.

By country

In the United States, major general is a permanent two-star rank in the Army, Air Force, Marine Corps and Space Force, normally held by division commanders and senior staff officers. In the British Army, major general is likewise a two-star rank. Equivalent naval ranks are usually rear admiral (upper half).

Insignia

Insignia commonly feature two stars, or in Commonwealth armies a pip above a crossed sword and baton.
