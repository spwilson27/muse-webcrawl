Dark Waters is a 2019 American legal thriller film directed by Todd Haynes and written by Mario Correa and Matthew Michael Carnahan. It stars Mark Ruffalo as environmental attorney Robert Bilott and is based on the 2016 New York Times Magazine article "The Lawyer Who Became DuPont's Worst Nightmare" by Nathaniel Rich.

Plot

Cincinnati corporate defence lawyer Robert Bilott is approached by West Virginia farmer Wilbur Tennant, whose cattle are dying near a DuPont landfill in Parkersburg. Bilott's investigation uncovers that DuPont has for decades knowingly contaminated the local water supply with PFOA (C8), a "forever chemical" used in Teflon production. His lawsuit expands into a long-running class action and a landmark health study of some 70,000 residents.

Background

The real case led to DuPont and Chemours settling thousands of personal-injury claims for more than $670 million in 2017, and the film drew wide attention to PFAS contamination. Ruffalo, who also produced, became an activist on the issue.
