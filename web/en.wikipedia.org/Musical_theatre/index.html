Musical theatre

Musical theatre is a form of theatrical performance that combines songs, spoken dialogue, acting, and dance. The story and emotional content are communicated through words, music, movement, and the technical aspects of entertainment as an integrated whole.

History

Musical theatre has roots in ancient Greek drama, comic opera, vaudeville, and music hall. The modern book musical is often dated to Show Boat (1927), and the form matured with Rodgers and Hammerstein's Oklahoma! (1943), in which songs and dances advanced the plot.

Golden age and beyond

The mid-20th century "Golden Age" produced classics such as My Fair Lady, West Side Story, and The Sound of Music. Later decades brought rock musicals, the megamusicals of Andrew Lloyd Webber such as The Phantom of the Opera, and contemporary hits including Hamilton.

Venues

Major centres of musical theatre include Broadway in New York and the West End in London, with touring productions and regional theatres worldwide.
