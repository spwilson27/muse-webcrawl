Pioneer plaque

The Pioneer plaque, designed by Carl Sagan and Frank Drake and drawn by Linda Salzman Sagan

The Pioneer plaques are a pair of gold-anodized aluminum plaques that were placed on board the 1972 Pioneer 10 and 1973 Pioneer 11 spacecraft, featuring a pictorial message, in case either spacecraft is intercepted by intelligent extraterrestrial life. The plaques show the nude figures of a human male and female along with several symbols that are designed to provide information about the origin of the spacecraft.

The Pioneer spacecraft were the first human-built objects to achieve escape velocity from the Solar System, and the plaques were the first physical artifacts sent into interstellar space. The idea was proposed by journalist Eric Burgess and developed by astronomers Carl Sagan and Frank Drake; the artwork was drawn by Sagan's wife, Linda Salzman Sagan. The plaques are mounted on the spacecrafts' antenna support struts, where they are shielded from interstellar dust.

Symbolism

At the top left is a schematic of the hyperfine transition of hydrogen, used to define units of time and length for the rest of the message. To the right stand the human figures, drawn to scale against an outline of the spacecraft; the man's hand is raised in greeting, and binary numbers beside the woman give her height in units of the hydrogen wavelength. At the bottom is a diagram of the Solar System showing the spacecraft's trajectory from Earth, the third planet. Across the middle, a radial "pulsar map" of 14 long-period galactic pulsars, with binary frequencies, triangulates the Sun's position and the time of launch; a fifteenth line extends to the galactic center.

Reception

The plaque generated both praise and controversy: some criticized the nudity of the figures, leading newspapers to retouch the image, while others objected that the message was anthropocentric and might be incomprehensible. The concept was expanded for the Voyager Golden Record launched in 1977. The plaques remain attached to Pioneer 10 and Pioneer 11, now billions of kilometres from Earth and heading toward the stars Aldebaran and into the constellation Aquila respectively; they are expected to outlast the Earth itself.
