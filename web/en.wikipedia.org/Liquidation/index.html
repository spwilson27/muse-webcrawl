Liquidation
Assets of a liquidated business are sold off

In law and business, liquidation is the process by which a company is brought to an end: its assets are collected and sold ("liquidated"), the proceeds are distributed to creditors and, if any surplus remains, to shareholders, and the company is formally dissolved. In the United Kingdom the technical term is winding up; in the United States, liquidation is principally governed by Chapter 7 of the Bankruptcy Code, in contrast to reorganisation under Chapter 11.

Types

Compulsory liquidation

A court orders the winding up of a company, usually on the petition of a creditor, on the ground that the company is unable to pay its debts. A liquidator — in England and Wales initially the Official Receiver — takes control of the company's assets and investigates its affairs.

Voluntary liquidation

Shareholders may resolve to wind up a company themselves. In a members' voluntary liquidation the directors swear that the company is solvent and can pay its debts in full within 12 months; where it cannot, the process becomes a creditors' voluntary liquidation, in which creditors play the dominant role in appointing the liquidator.

Process and priorities

The liquidator realises the company's assets, settles legal claims, and distributes the proceeds in a statutory order: secured creditors (from their security), the expenses of the liquidation, preferential creditors (including certain employee claims and, in the UK, HMRC for some taxes), floating-charge holders, unsecured creditors, and finally shareholders. Unsecured creditors rank pari passu — equally with one another. Transactions before the liquidation may be challenged as preferences or transactions at an undervalue, and directors may face liability for wrongful or fraudulent trading.

Effects

Once liquidation begins, the directors' powers cease, employees are typically dismissed, and the company exists only for the purpose of being wound up. At the end of the process the company's name is struck off the register and it ceases to exist.
