Fractional ownership

Fractional ownership is a method in which several unrelated parties can share in, and mitigate the risk of, ownership of a high-value tangible asset, such as a private jet, a yacht, or a vacation property. Each owner holds a share proportional to their investment and is allotted a corresponding fraction of the asset's use.

How it works

A management company typically sells shares in the asset, schedules owners' usage, and handles maintenance, insurance, and storage. Owners pay an initial purchase price for their fraction plus ongoing monthly management fees and hourly usage charges where applicable.

Applications

The model was popularised in business aviation by NetJets, founded in 1986, which sells fractional shares in private aircraft. Fractional ownership is also common for vacation homes, where it differs from timeshare in that owners hold an actual equity interest in the property rather than merely a right to use it. More recently the concept has been extended to art, collectibles, and other alternative assets.

Advantages and drawbacks

Fractional ownership lowers the entry cost of expensive assets and spreads fixed costs among owners. Drawbacks include limited scheduling flexibility, resale constraints, and dependence on the management company's solvency.
