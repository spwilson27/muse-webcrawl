Black Friday

Black Friday is the name given to the day after Thanksgiving in the United States, traditionally the start of the Christmas shopping season. Since 2005 it has routinely been the busiest shopping day of the year, with retailers offering heavily promoted sales at discounted prices.

Origin of the term

The term was used by Philadelphia police in the 1950s and 1960s to describe the heavy pedestrian and vehicle traffic that clogged the city centre the day after Thanksgiving. Retailers later promoted a more positive explanation: that the day is when shops move "into the black" (profit) from "the red" (loss) in their accounts.

Spread and criticism

The custom has spread to many other countries, often detached from Thanksgiving. Sales increasingly begin on Thanksgiving evening or stretch across a "Black Friday week", and online equivalents such as Cyber Monday compete for spending. The day has drawn criticism for consumerism and for occasional violence among shoppers competing for bargains.

Other uses

"Black Friday" has also been applied to days of disaster, including the 1869 collapse of the United States gold market and several notorious stock-market crashes.
