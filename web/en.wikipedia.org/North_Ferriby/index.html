North Ferriby is a village and civil parish in the East Riding of Yorkshire, England. It lies on the north bank of the Humber Estuary about 8 miles (13 km) west of the centre of Kingston upon Hull, at the southern end of the Yorkshire Wolds. The village is served by Ferriby railway station on the Hull and Selby Railway line.

History

The village takes its name from the ferry that once crossed the Humber to South Ferriby in Lincolnshire. North Ferriby is internationally noted for the Ferriby Boats: the remains of three Bronze Age sewn-plank boats discovered in the estuary mud by Ted Wright from 1937 onwards. Dating to roughly 2000–1700 BC, they are among the oldest known boats in Europe and evidence of early seafaring and cross-Channel links.

The parish church of All Saints dates largely from the nineteenth century and serves a village that has grown as a residential area for Hull commuters while retaining a rural character along the Humber bank.

Sport

The village is home to North Ferriby United Association Football Club, founded in 1934, which won the FA Trophy at Wembley in 2015 and reached the National League, the fifth tier of English football, before financial difficulties led to the club's liquidation in 2019; a phoenix club, North Ferriby F.C., was formed the same year.
