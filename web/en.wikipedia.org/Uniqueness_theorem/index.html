Uniqueness theorem

A uniqueness theorem is a theorem in mathematics or physics asserting that, under stated conditions, there is at most one solution to a given problem. Uniqueness results usually complement existence theorems: together they show that a problem has exactly one solution and that "the" solution is well defined.

Examples

In electrostatics, the uniqueness theorem states that the solution of Poisson's equation in a region is uniquely determined by the charge distribution and the boundary conditions (for example, the potential or its normal derivative on the boundary). In ordinary differential equations, the Picard–Lindelöf theorem guarantees that an initial value problem with a Lipschitz-continuous right-hand side has a locally unique solution. In fluid mechanics and potential theory, analogous uniqueness results hold for harmonic functions satisfying Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions.

Significance

Uniqueness is physically important because it guarantees that a mathematical model makes a definite prediction: any method that produces a solution satisfying the conditions must produce the true one.
