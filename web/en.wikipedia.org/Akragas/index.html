Akragas

Location: Agrigento, Sicily, Italy

Founded: c. 580 BCE

Founded by: Greek settlers from Gela

Modern name: Agrigento

Akragas (Ancient Greek: Ἀκράγας) was a major ancient Greek city on the south coast of Sicily, at the site of modern Agrigento. Founded around 580 BCE by colonists from Gela, it grew into one of the richest and most powerful cities of Magna Graecia, famed for its temples and for the philosopher Empedocles.

History

Under the tyrant Theron (r. 488–472 BCE), Akragas expanded and, allied with Syracuse, defeated Carthage at the Battle of Himera in 480 BCE. The city's great building programme produced the temples of the ridge now known as the Valley of the Temples, including the exceptionally well-preserved Temple of Concordia and the colossal, unfinished Temple of Olympian Zeus. The poet Pindar called Akragas "the most beautiful city of mortals."

In 406 BCE Carthage besieged and sacked the city. Akragas revived under Timoleon in the later 4th century BCE, and passed to Rome during the First Punic War, when its Latin name became Agrigentum. The archaeological area of Agrigento, including the Valley of the Temples, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Empedocles

Akragas was the home of the pre-Socratic philosopher Empedocles (c. 494 – c. 434 BCE), who proposed the four classical elements — earth, air, fire, and water — combined and separated by the forces of Love and Strife.
