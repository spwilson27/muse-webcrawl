The International Telecommunication Union (ITU) is a specialized agency of the United Nations responsible for matters related to information and communication technologies. Founded in Paris in 1865 as the International Telegraph Union, it is one of the oldest international organizations in operation.

Mandate

The ITU coordinates the shared global use of the radio spectrum, promotes international cooperation in assigning satellite orbits, works to improve telecommunication infrastructure in developing countries, and develops worldwide technical standards. Its standardization sector (ITU-T) issues the ITU-T Recommendations, while the radiocommunication sector (ITU-R) manages spectrum and satellite orbit resources.

History

The ITU became a United Nations specialized agency in 1947 and moved its headquarters to Geneva. It helped organize the World Summit on the Information Society and publishes global statistics on connectivity, including measures of Internet access and mobile subscriptions.
