The Clay Mathematics Institute

The Clay Mathematics Institute (CMI) is a private, non-profit foundation dedicated to increasing and disseminating mathematical knowledge. Formerly based in Peterborough, New Hampshire, the corporate address is now in Denver, Colorado. CMI's scientific activities are managed from the President's office in Oxford, United Kingdom.

It gives out various awards and sponsorships to promising mathematicians. The institute was founded in 1998 through the sponsorship of Boston businessman Landon T. Clay and his wife, Lavinia D. Clay. Harvard mathematician Arthur Jaffe was its first president.

Millennium Prize Problems

The institute is best known for the Millennium Prize Problems, a list of seven important unsolved mathematical problems announced in 2000. The problems were selected by CMI's Scientific Advisory Board, and each carries a prize of one million US dollars for the first correct solution. The seven problems are:

  • Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture
  • Hodge conjecture
  • Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness
  • P versus NP problem
  • Riemann hypothesis
  • Yang–Mills existence and mass gap
  • Poincaré conjecture

Of these, only the Poincaré conjecture has been solved: Grigori Perelman proved it in 2003, but declined both the prize money and the associated Fields Medal. The list was modelled in part on Hilbert's problems of 1900 and on the example of earlier prize problems in mathematics.

Other activities

CMI runs summer schools, workshops and fellowships, supports postdoctoral Clay Research Fellows, and awards the Clay Research Award annually for major breakthroughs in mathematical research. It also publishes monographs and maintains the online edition of the works of several mathematicians.
