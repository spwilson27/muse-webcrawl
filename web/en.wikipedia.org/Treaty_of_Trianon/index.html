Treaty of Trianon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Treaty of Trianon (French: Traité de Trianon; Hungarian: Trianoni békeszerződés) was a peace agreement signed in 1920 between Hungary and the Allied Powers, formally ending Hungary's participation in the First World War. It was signed on 4 June 1920 at the Grand Trianon palace in Versailles.

Terms

The treaty redrew Hungary's borders drastically. The Kingdom of Hungary lost about 72% of its territory and 64% of its population: Transylvania and other eastern territories went to Romania; Slovakia and Carpathian Ruthenia to Czechoslovakia; Croatia-Slavonia, Vojvodina and other southern lands to the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes; and Burgenland (mostly) to Austria. Roughly 3.3 million ethnic Hungarians found themselves outside the new borders. Hungary's army was capped at 35,000 men and reparations were imposed.

Legacy

Trianon remains a defining trauma in Hungarian political memory and a recurring theme in Hungarian politics, while neighbouring states view the settlement as the recognition of their national self-determination. Partial revisions under the Vienna Awards (1938, 1940) were annulled after the Second World War by the Paris Peace Treaties of 1947.
