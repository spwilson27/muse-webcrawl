In-flight fire

An in-flight fire is a fire occurring aboard an aircraft while it is in flight. In-flight fires are among the most serious emergencies in aviation: they can escalate within minutes, producing toxic smoke, damaging flight-critical systems, and leaving flight crews very little time to land. Fires may originate in engines, cargo holds, electrical systems, cabin materials, or — increasingly — in lithium batteries in passengers' electronic devices.

Notable accidents

Several major disasters have been attributed to in-flight fires, including South African Airways Flight 295 (1987), ValuJet Flight 592 (1996), in which oxygen generators ignited in the cargo hold, and Swissair Flight 111 (1998), brought down by a fire in wiring above the cockpit. These accidents prompted stricter rules on hazardous cargo, improved fire detection and suppression in cargo compartments, and changes to wiring and insulation standards.

Prevention and response

Aircraft are equipped with smoke detectors, built-in extinguishers in cargo holds and lavatories, portable extinguishers, and fire containment bags for battery incidents. Crew procedures for smoke or fire emphasize immediate diversion and landing as soon as possible, since a hidden fire may make the aircraft uncontrollable in as little as 15–20 minutes.
