Grey market

Also known asParallel market; parallel imports
GoodsGenuine, sold outside authorised distribution channels
LegalityVaries by jurisdiction and product
Contrasted withBlack market (illegal goods); counterfeit goods

A grey market (or gray market) is the trade of genuine, non-counterfeit goods through distribution channels that are legal but unintended or unauthorised by the original manufacturer or trademark holder. Grey-market goods are often called parallel imports.

How it arises

Grey markets arise when the same product is priced differently in different countries, or subject to different availability. Arbitrageurs buy in a low-price market — exploiting exchange rates, regional pricing or tax differences — and resell in a high-price market, undercutting authorised dealers. Common categories include cars, cameras and electronics, watches, pharmaceuticals, textbooks, video games and cosmetics.

Consequences

Consumers benefit from lower prices and access to models not officially sold locally, but grey-market goods may lack local warranty support, come with foreign-language manuals or incompatible plugs and standards, and miss region-specific approvals. Manufacturers object that parallel trade undermines authorised service networks, complicates recalls and damages brand control; they may refuse warranty service on grey imports, though competition authorities in some jurisdictions restrict such refusals.

Legal status

Legality turns largely on intellectual-property exhaustion doctrine. Under international exhaustion (e.g. largely in Japan and Switzerland for some goods), first sale anywhere exhausts the right-holder's control; under regional exhaustion in the European Economic Area, rights are exhausted only by a first sale within the region. In the United States, courts have allowed parallel imports of genuine goods unless the goods are materially different from domestic versions (the Lever Brothers rule), and the Supreme Court's Kirtsaeng v. John Wiley decision (2013) extended first-sale doctrine to copyrighted works lawfully made abroad.
