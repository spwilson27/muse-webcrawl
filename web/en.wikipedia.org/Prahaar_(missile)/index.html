Prahaar ("Strike" in Hindi) is an Indian solid-fuel road-mobile tactical ballistic missile developed by the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO). Prahaar is expected to replace the Prithvi short-range ballistic missile in the Indian Army service.

Design

Prahaar is a quick-reaction, all-weather, all-terrain, highly accurate battlefield support tactical weapon system. It uses a single-stage solid propellant and is carried on an 8×8 transporter erector launcher with six missiles in canisters, allowing salvo launches. The missile can carry conventional warheads of different types and uses inertial navigation with terminal guidance for accuracy.

Development

The missile was first successfully test-fired on 21 July 2011 from the Integrated Test Range at Chandipur, Odisha, hitting its target at a range of 150 km. A longer-range derivative, Pranash, with a range of around 200 km or more, has also been reported in development for export and service use.
