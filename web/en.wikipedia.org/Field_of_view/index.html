Field of view

Measured inDegrees (°)

The field of view (FOV) is the extent of the observable world that is seen at any given moment, expressed as an angular measurement. In humans and primates the term is usually used for the effective field from which visual input is taken, while in optics and photography it describes the extent of a scene imaged by a lens or sensor.

In humans

The typical human binocular field of view is about 114 degrees horizontally, with roughly 60–70 degrees of overlap between the two eyes; the total visual field approaches 200–220 degrees when peripheral vision of each eye is counted, and about 135 degrees vertically.

In optics and photography

For a camera, FOV is determined by the focal length of the lens and the size of the image sensor: shorter focal lengths give wider fields of view. The angle of view is related to these by trigonometry through the sensor dimension.

Other uses

The term is used in video games, where FOV settings affect how much of the game world is visible, in remote sensing for satellite swaths, and in astronomy for the apparent extent seen through a telescope eyepiece.
