Oracle Corporation is an American multinational computer technology company headquartered in Austin, Texas. Founded in 1977 by Larry Ellison, Bob Miner and Ed Oates as Software Development Laboratories, Oracle built its business on the first commercially available relational database management system based on SQL, and grew — largely through aggressive acquisitions — into one of the world's largest enterprise software and cloud computing companies.

History

Inspired by Edgar F. Codd's relational model and a CIA project codenamed "Oracle", the company released Oracle Version 2 in 1979, among the first commercial SQL databases. Renamed Oracle Systems Corporation, it went public in 1986 and weathered a near-collapse in 1990 amid accounting problems. Under Ellison, Oracle expanded from databases into enterprise applications (ERP, CRM, supply-chain and human-capital software), both by development and by acquiring rivals including PeopleSoft (2005), Siebel (2006), BEA Systems (2008), Sun Microsystems (2010, bringing Java and MySQL), NetSuite (2016) and health-records giant Cerner (2022). In 2020 the company moved its headquarters from Redwood Shores, California, to Austin, Texas.

Products and cloud

Oracle Database remains a flagship product, alongside the Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI) platform, the Fusion and NetSuite cloud applications, and stewardship of the Java programming language. From the mid-2010s Oracle pivoted to cloud computing, competing with Amazon, Microsoft and Google; in the 2020s surging demand for AI infrastructure made OCI one of its fastest-growing businesses, including major capacity deals with AI companies.

Legal matters

Oracle's decade-long copyright suit against Google over Java APIs in Android ended in 2021, when the US Supreme Court ruled Google's copying was fair use. The company has also been a vigorous enforcer of its software licensing terms, a practice that has drawn criticism from customers and regulators.
