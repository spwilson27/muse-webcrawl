Achalasia

Achalasia
Specialty: Gastroenterology
Symptoms: Dysphagia, regurgitation, weight loss
Cause: Loss of esophageal neurons

Achalasia is a rare disorder of the esophagus in which the lower esophageal sphincter fails to relax properly and coordinated peristalsis is lost, making it difficult for food and liquid to pass into the stomach.

Signs and symptoms

The main symptoms are difficulty swallowing both solids and liquids, regurgitation of undigested food, chest pain, heartburn, and weight loss. Symptoms typically develop gradually.

Cause and diagnosis

Achalasia results from degeneration of nerve cells in the myenteric plexus of the esophagus; the trigger is unknown but may involve autoimmune or viral factors. Diagnosis is by esophageal manometry, supported by barium swallow showing a dilated esophagus with a narrowed "bird's beak" outlet, and endoscopy to exclude other causes.

Treatment

Treatments aim to reduce sphincter pressure: pneumatic balloon dilation, Heller myotomy, peroral endoscopic myotomy (POEM), botulinum toxin injection, and medications such as calcium channel blockers.
