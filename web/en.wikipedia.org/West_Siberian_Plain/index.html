The West Siberian Plain (Russian: Западно-Сибирская равнина, Zapadno-Sibirskaya ravnina) is a large plain that occupies the western portion of Siberia, between the Ural Mountains in the west and the Yenisei River in the east, and the Altai Mountains and Kazakh uplands in the southeast. Covering roughly 2.6 to 2.7 million square kilometres — about one third of Siberia — it is one of the largest areas of uninterrupted lowland terrain in the world.

Geography

The plain extends some 2,500 km from the Arctic Ocean in the north to the foothills of the Altai in the south, and about 1,500 km from the Urals to the Yenisei. Much of it lies less than 200 metres above sea level, and its surface is exceptionally flat: the Ob River falls only a few dozen metres over thousands of kilometres. Because the great rivers — the Ob, Irtysh and Yenisei — flow northward and their mouths remain icebound while their upper reaches thaw in spring, enormous areas are flooded seasonally. The Vasyugan Swamp, among the largest wetlands on Earth, covers much of the southern-central plain.

Geology

The plain corresponds largely to the West Siberian Basin, a vast sedimentary basin filled with Mesozoic and Cenozoic deposits several kilometres thick overlying an older basement. These sediments host some of the world's largest oil and natural gas fields, including the giant gas fields of the Yamal Peninsula and the Nadym-Pur-Taz region, and the oil fields around Samotlor near the middle Ob. Quaternary glaciations and permafrost have shaped the northern portions of the plain.

Climate and vegetation zones

From north to south the plain passes through bands of Arctic tundra, forest-tundra, the coniferous taiga — the largest forest belt on Earth — mixed forest, forest-steppe and steppe. The climate is strongly continental, with very cold winters and warm summers; temperatures below −50 °C have been recorded in the north-east of the plain.

Population and economy

The southern plain contains major cities including Novosibirsk, Omsk, Tyumen, Barnaul, Tomsk and Chelyabinsk, served by the Trans-Siberian Railway. The economy is dominated by oil and gas extraction in the Khanty-Mansi and Yamalo-Nenets autonomous okrugs, agriculture on the southern steppes, and forestry. Indigenous peoples of the north include the Khanty, Mansi, Nenets and Selkup.
