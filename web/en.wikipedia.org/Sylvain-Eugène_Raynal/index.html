Sylvain-Eugène Raynal (6 March 1867 – 13 January 1939) was a French military officer best remembered as the commander of Fort Vaux during the Battle of Verdun in the First World War. His garrison's desperate defence of the fort in June 1916 became one of the emblematic episodes of the battle.

Defence of Fort Vaux

From 2 June 1916, Raynal — then a major (commandant) — led some 600 men in the underground galleries of Fort Vaux against repeated German assaults. The garrison endured intense shelling, flame-thrower and gas attacks, and hand-to-hand fighting in the corridors, while suffering extreme thirst as the fort's water supplies were destroyed. Raynal communicated with French headquarters by carrier pigeon; the last bird, named Vaillant, delivered his final message despite being gassed, and was posthumously cited in orders.

On 7 June 1916, with his men incapacitated and out of water, Raynal surrendered the ruined fort. He was received with military honours by the German Crown Prince Wilhelm and presented with tokens of respect for the defence, before spending the rest of the war as a prisoner. Repatriated after the armistice, Raynal was promoted and decorated; streets and schools in France still bear his name.

Legacy

Raynal's account of the siege appeared in his memoir Le drame du Fort de Vaux. The defence of Fort Vaux, alongside Fort Douaumont, remains a central symbol of French endurance at Verdun.
