Murad II

Reign: 1421–1444; 1446–1451
Born: c. 1404, Amasya
Died: 3 February 1451, Edirne
Successor: Mehmed II
Predecessor: Mehmed I

Murad II (c. 1404 – 3 February 1451) was the sultan of the Ottoman Empire from 1421 until 1451, with an interruption in 1444–1446 when he abdicated in favour of his young son Mehmed II. His reign consolidated the empire after the interregnum and saw decisive victories in the Balkans that left the Ottomans the dominant power in southeastern Europe.

Accession and early reign

Murad succeeded his father Mehmed I in 1421 but immediately faced a rival claimant, his uncle Mustafa Çelebi, backed by Byzantium; Mustafa was defeated and executed. Murad then besieged Constantinople in 1422 without success, put down further revolts in Anatolia, and campaigned to bring the Anatolian beyliks back under Ottoman control. In Europe he captured Thessalonica from Venice in 1430 and campaigned repeatedly in the Balkans.

Wars in the Balkans

The Hungarian commander John Hunyadi led crusading armies into Ottoman territory in the "Long Campaign" of 1443–44, winning several engagements. Murad negotiated the Peace of Szeged in 1444 and, weary of rule, abdicated to his twelve-year-old son Mehmed. When the crusaders broke the peace and marched again, the Janissaries' unrest forced Murad to resume the throne; at the Battle of Varna on 10 November 1444 he crushed the crusader army and killed King Władysław III of Poland and Hungary. In 1448 he defeated Hunyadi again at the Second Battle of Kosovo.

Death and legacy

Murad died at Edirne in 1451 and was succeeded by Mehmed II, who would conquer Constantinople two years later. Murad was a patron of scholars, poets and Sufis, founded charitable complexes in Bursa and Edirne, and his reign marked a flourishing of early Ottoman culture as well as the military expansion that made the conquest of Constantinople possible.
