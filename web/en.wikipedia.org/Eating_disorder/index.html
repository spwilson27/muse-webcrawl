Eating disorder

An eating disorder is a mental disorder defined by abnormal eating behaviours that adversely affect a person's physical or mental health. The main types include anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa, binge eating disorder, pica, rumination disorder and avoidant/restrictive food intake disorder (ARFID).

Types

Anorexia nervosa involves restriction of food intake leading to significantly low body weight, an intense fear of gaining weight and disturbance in body image. Bulimia nervosa is characterised by cycles of binge eating followed by purging or other compensatory behaviour. Binge eating disorder involves recurrent binge episodes without regular purging, and is the most common eating disorder in the United States.

Causes

No single cause is known. Twin studies indicate substantial heritability; risk is increased by perfectionism, anxiety and obsessive traits, and by sociocultural pressures emphasising thinness or muscularity. Dieting is a common trigger in vulnerable individuals.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis follows criteria in the DSM-5 or ICD-11 and requires assessment of medical complications such as electrolyte disturbance, bradycardia and bone loss. Treatment is multidisciplinary: weight restoration and medical monitoring, family-based treatment for adolescents, and cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT-E) for adults.
