Polycythemia vera

Polycythemia vera (PV) is a slow-growing blood cancer, classified as a myeloproliferative neoplasm, in which the bone marrow produces too many red blood cells. It may also cause overproduction of white blood cells and platelets. The excess cells thicken the blood, increasing the risk of blood clots, stroke, and heart attack. Most cases are caused by a mutation in the JAK2 gene, identified in about 95 percent of patients.

Signs and symptoms

Many people with polycythemia vera have no symptoms, and the condition is discovered on a routine blood test. When symptoms occur they may include headaches, dizziness, itching — classically after a warm shower or bath (aquagenic pruritus) — redness of the face, fatigue, blurred vision, and a burning sensation in the hands and feet. An enlarged spleen is common. Gout and peptic ulcers occur at increased rates.

Cause

PV arises from a genetic mutation in a hematopoietic stem cell. The JAK2 V617F mutation, in which valine is replaced by phenylalanine at position 617, causes the JAK-STAT signaling pathway to be constitutively active, driving production of blood cells independently of erythropoietin. A smaller number of patients have mutations in exon 12 of JAK2. The disease is not ordinarily inherited.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on blood counts (elevated hemoglobin and hematocrit), low serum erythropoietin, bone marrow findings, and testing for the JAK2 mutation. There is no cure, but treatment aims to reduce the risk of thrombosis and control symptoms. Standard management includes phlebotomy to keep hematocrit below 45 percent, low-dose aspirin, and, for higher-risk patients, cytoreductive drugs such as hydroxyurea or the JAK inhibitor ruxolitinib. PV can progress over years to myelofibrosis and, in a small proportion of cases, to acute leukemia.
