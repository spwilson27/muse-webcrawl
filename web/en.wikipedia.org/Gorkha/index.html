Gorkha


Gorkha Durbar, the palace from which Prithvi Narayan Shah launched the unification of Nepal.

Gorkha is a municipality in Gorkha District in Gandaki Province of Nepal, created on 18 May 2014 by merging the former Village development committees Gorkha, Phinam, Nareshwor and Ranishwara. The place is historically significant as the birthplace of King Prithvi Narayan Shah, who founded the Shah dynasty and began the unification of the Kingdom of Nepal in the 18th century. The name gives its name to the Gurkha soldiers of Nepal, famous for their service in the British and Indian armies.

History

The small hill principality of Gorkha was one of the many petty kingdoms (the Baise and Chaubisi rajya) of the Nepal hills. The Shah dynasty there claimed descent from rulers of Lamjung and, by legend, from Rajput princes of Chittor. Drabya Shah founded the kingdom in 1559. His descendant Prithvi Narayan Shah (r. 1743–1775) launched a decades-long campaign that conquered the Kathmandu Valley in 1768–69 and built the much larger Gorkha kingdom — the nucleus of modern Nepal, whose Nepali language was long called Gorkhali. Under the 1816 Treaty of Sugauli, after the Anglo-Nepalese War, Nepal lost a third of its territory, but British respect for Gorkhali soldiers in that war began the tradition of Gurkha recruitment.

Geography and sites

The town lies on a hillside about 140 km west of Kathmandu, at roughly 1,135 metres elevation. Its principal sights are the Gorkha Durbar — a combined palace, fort, and temple complex crowned by the Gorakhnath cave and the temple of the goddess Gorakhkali — and the Tallo Durbar below. The district was near the epicentre of the devastating April 2015 earthquake (the "Gorkha earthquake"), which destroyed much of the old palace complex and many houses; reconstruction has been under way since.

The Gurkha connection

"Gurkha" is the Anglicised name for soldiers recruited from Nepal — originally from Gorkha and surrounding hill peoples such as the Magar, Gurung, Rai, and Limbu. Gurkha regiments have served with distinction in both world wars and many campaigns since, winning numerous Victoria Crosses, and remain in the British Army (the Brigade of Gurkhas), the Indian Army, and Singapore's police Gurkha Contingent.
