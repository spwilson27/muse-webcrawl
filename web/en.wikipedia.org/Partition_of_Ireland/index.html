Partition of Ireland

The partition of Ireland was the process by which the Government of the United Kingdom, under the Government of Ireland Act 1920, divided the island of Ireland into two self-governing polities: Northern Ireland and Southern Ireland.

Background

The partition grew out of the long-standing conflict between Irish nationalists, who sought independence or home rule, and unionists, concentrated in the northeast, who wished to remain in the United Kingdom. The Irish War of Independence and the Anglo-Irish Treaty of 1921 led to the creation of the Irish Free State in 1922.

Implementation

Southern Ireland was short-lived, superseded by the Irish Free State, which later became the Republic of Ireland. Northern Ireland, comprising six of the nine counties of Ulster, remained within the United Kingdom. A Boundary Commission's recommendations were shelved in 1925, leaving the border essentially as drawn in 1920.

Legacy

Partition shaped the politics of the island for a century, including the Troubles in Northern Ireland and the 1998 Good Friday Agreement, which affirmed the principle of consent regarding Northern Ireland's status.
