Lyrical Ballads

Title page of the 1798 first edition

Authors: William Wordsworth and Samuel Taylor Coleridge

Published: 1798 (2nd ed. 1800, with Preface)

Movement: Romanticism

Lyrical Ballads, with a Few Other Poems is a collection of poems by William Wordsworth and Samuel Taylor Coleridge, first published in 1798 and generally considered to mark the beginning of the English Romantic movement in literature. The volume grew out of the poets' friendship and walks in the Quantock Hills of Somerset, and from a plan to finance a trip to Germany with its proceeds.

Contents and method

The collection opens with Coleridge's supernatural ballad The Rime of the Ancyent Marinere and closes with Wordsworth's reflective Tintern Abbey. Between them stand poems on humble and rustic life — The Idiot Boy, We Are Seven, Lines Written in Early Spring — deliberately written in what Wordsworth called "the real language of men", rejecting the ornate poetic diction of the 18th century. The two poets divided their labour: Coleridge was to treat supernatural subjects so as to procure willing suspension of disbelief, Wordsworth to give the charm of novelty to everyday things.

The Preface

The second edition (1800), published under Wordsworth's name alone, added the celebrated Preface, expanded in 1802, which became the manifesto of English Romanticism. It defines poetry as "the spontaneous overflow of powerful feelings" recollected in tranquillity, and argues for incidents from common life described in ordinary language — a programme attacked by some reviewers but transformative for English poetry.
