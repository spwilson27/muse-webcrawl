Durango City

Durango Cathedral in the historic centre (placeholder image)

Durango City (Spanish: Durango, officially Victoria de Durango) is the capital and largest city of the Mexican state of Durango. It stands in the Guadiana Valley in north-central Mexico at an elevation of about 1,890 metres (6,200 ft), and had a population of roughly 600,000 in recent censuses, with a larger metropolitan area around it.

History

The city was founded on 8 July 1563 by the Basque explorer Francisco de Ibarra, who named it after the town of Durango in Biscay, Spain. It grew as a colonial centre for mining — the nearby Cerro de Mercado was one of Mexico's great iron deposits — and for missions and ranching in the north. The name "Victoria" honours Guadalupe Victoria, the first president of Mexico, who was born in the state.

Geography and climate

Durango has a semi-arid climate moderated by its altitude, with warm days, cool nights and most rain falling in summer. The Sierra Madre Occidental rises west of the city.

Culture

The historic centre preserves colonial churches and mansions, including the cathedral on the Plaza de Armas. The surrounding deserts and sierras have served as locations for many films, especially Westerns, earning the state the nickname "land of cinema" (tierra del cine). The city is also known for its scorpion population, a local emblem — Durango is famous in Mexico for its alacranes, which appear in its folklore and crafts.
