Music of Indonesia

The music of Indonesia reflects the archipelago's extraordinary ethnic and cultural diversity — hundreds of distinct traditions across thousands of islands — and centuries of influence from India, the Islamic world, China, and Europe through trade and colonisation. Its best-known form worldwide is the gamelan.

Gamelan

Gamelan ensembles, developed in the courts of Java and Bali, are built around tuned bronze percussion: metallophones, kettle gongs, large hanging gongs, drums, and often flutes, bowed strings and voices. Javanese gamelan is meditative and layered, organised in great repeating cycles marked by the deepest gong; Balinese gamelan is faster, brighter and more explosive, with interlocking parts (kotekan). The five-tone sléndro and seven-tone pélog tuning systems are central to the music's identity. In 2021 UNESCO inscribed Indonesian gamelan on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Regional traditions

Other major traditions include the angklung bamboo rattles of West Java, the kroncong music of Portuguese-influenced coastal communities, the sung poetry and dance-theatre of Sumatra and Sulawesi, Sasando music from Rote, and the vocal and instrumental arts accompanying wayang shadow-puppet theatre. Gamelan has profoundly influenced Western composers from Debussy, who heard it at the 1889 Paris Exposition, to twentieth-century minimalists.

Popular music

Modern Indonesian popular music includes dangdut — a hugely popular blend of Malay, Indian film, Arabic and rock elements, embodied by stars such as Rhoma Irama — as well as Indonesian pop, rock and hip-hop industries centred in Jakarta, and regional campursari fusions. Religious music, from Islamic qasidah to Christian choral traditions in eastern Indonesia, is also prominent.
