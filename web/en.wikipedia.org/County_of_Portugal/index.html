County of Portugal

First county: 868–1071

Second county: 1095–1139

Capital: Guimarães (first); Braga/Guimarães (second)

Outcome: Became the Kingdom of Portugal

The County of Portugal (Portuguese: Condado de Portugal) was a medieval political entity in the northwest of the Iberian Peninsula, from which the Kingdom of Portugal emerged. There were two successive counties: the first, founded in 868 by Vímara Peres after the reconquest of Portucale (Porto), and the second, established in 1095 by Henry of Burgundy.

First county

The first county was a fief of the Kingdom of Asturias, later of Galicia and León. It grew southward with the Reconquista but was absorbed into the Kingdom of Galicia and then León in 1071 after Count Nuno Mendes was defeated and killed at the Battle of Pedroso.

Second county

In 1095 Alfonso VI of León granted the county to Henry of Burgundy, who had married the king's illegitimate daughter Teresa. After Henry's death in 1112, Teresa ruled as regent for their son Afonso Henriques. Afonso defeated his mother's forces at the Battle of São Mamede in 1128, took control of the county, defeated the Moors at Ourique in 1139, and proclaimed himself king; Portugal's independence was recognised by León in the Treaty of Zamora (1143) and by the papacy in 1179.
