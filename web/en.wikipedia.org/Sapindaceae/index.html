Sapindaceae

Lychee (Litchi chinensis), a well-known member of the family.

The Sapindaceae are a family of flowering plants in the order Sapindales, commonly known as the soapberry family. It comprises about 138 genera and roughly 1,850 accepted species, distributed mainly in tropical and subtropical regions worldwide, with some temperate representatives.

Description

Members of the family are trees, shrubs, herbs, and lianas, including tendril-bearing climbers such as Cardiospermum. Leaves are usually alternate and often compound. Flowers are small, typically unisexual, with four or five sepals and petals and a characteristic nectary disk. Fruits are varied: capsules, berries, drupes, samaras, and schizocarps.

Notable members

Under the modern APG classification, the family includes the maples (Acer) and horse chestnuts (Aesculus), formerly placed in their own families. Economically important tropical fruits include lychee, rambutan, longan, and ackee. The soapberries (Sapindus) contain saponins and have been used as a natural soap. Guarana, a source of caffeine, and the timber and shade trees koelreuteria and golden rain tree are also members.

Classification

Molecular phylogenetic work has reorganised the family into subfamilies including Sapindoideae, Hippocastanoideae (maples and horse chestnuts), Dodonaeoideae, and Xanthoceroideae. The family belongs to the order Sapindales alongside Rutaceae (citrus) and Anacardiaceae (mango and cashew).
