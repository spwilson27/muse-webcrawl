Drakensberg

CountriesSouth Africa, Lesotho
Highest peakThabana Ntlenyana (3,482 m)
Length~1,000 km

The Drakensberg (Afrikaans for "Dragon's Mountain") is the eastern portion of the Great Escarpment, which encloses the central Southern African plateau. It forms the border between South Africa and Lesotho and contains the highest peaks in southern Africa, including Thabana Ntlenyana (3,482 metres), the highest point south of Kilimanjaro.

The range was formed by basalt lava flows overlying sandstone, the result of volcanic activity about 180 million years ago during the break-up of Gondwana. Erosion has carved dramatic cliffs, spires, and amphitheatres into the escarpment.

Geography and ecology

The Drakensberg is the source of major rivers, including the Tugela, which drops over the Amphitheatre cliff in one of the world's tallest waterfalls. The mountains protect a rich variety of habitats and endemic species. The uKhahlamba-Drakensberg Park is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, noted both for its natural beauty and for its extraordinary concentration of San rock art — some 40,000 paintings, the largest group in Africa south of the Sahara.

Tourism

The range is a major destination for hiking, climbing, and mountain biking. Popular areas include Royal Natal National Park, Cathedral Peak, Giant's Castle, and the Sani Pass road into Lesotho.
