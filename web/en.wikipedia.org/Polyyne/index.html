Polyyne

A polyyne is an organic compound with alternating single and triple bonds, of the general form H–(C≡C)n–H for chains terminated by hydrogen, with n greater than 1. Polyynes are a class of compounds related to cumulenes and are of interest as models for the hypothetical carbon allotrope carbyne.

Occurrence

Naturally occurring polyynes are produced by plants, fungi, and marine organisms, and some exhibit antibiotic, antifungal, or cytotoxic activity. Long carbon chains with conjugated triple bonds have also been detected in interstellar clouds and in the atmosphere of Titan.

Synthesis and stability

Long polyynes are increasingly unstable and prone to explosive decomposition and cross-linking. Stabilization strategies include end-capping with bulky groups such as tris(trimethylsilyl)silyl or wrapping the chain inside a rotaxane sheath.

Properties

Polyynes show strong Raman scattering and unusual electronic properties, making them candidates for molecular electronics research.
