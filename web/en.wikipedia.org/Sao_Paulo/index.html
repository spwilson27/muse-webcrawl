Sao Paulo

Skyline of Sao Paulo, Brazil's largest city.

Sao Paulo (Portuguese: São Paulo) is the largest city in Brazil and the capital of the state of the same name. With more than 11 million inhabitants in the municipality and over 22 million in its metropolitan area, it is the most populous city in the Southern Hemisphere and one of the largest cities in the world. It is Brazil's financial, industrial, and cultural centre.

History

The city was founded on 25 January 1554 by Jesuit priests, including Manuel da Nóbrega and José de Anchieta, who established a mission, the Pátio do Colégio, between the Tamanduateí and Anhangabaú rivers. For centuries a modest colonial outpost and a base for the bandeirantes who explored the interior, Sao Paulo grew explosively in the late 19th and early 20th centuries on the back of the coffee economy, attracting millions of immigrants from Italy, Japan, Germany, Lebanon, and elsewhere. Industrialisation after the 1930s made it the engine of the Brazilian economy.

Geography and climate

The city sits on a plateau at roughly 760 metres above sea level, some 70 km from the Atlantic port of Santos. Its climate is humid subtropical, with mild winters and warm, rainy summers.

Economy and culture

Sao Paulo hosts the headquarters of most of Brazil's major corporations and banks, and the B3 stock exchange. Avenida Paulista is its financial and cultural spine. The city is home to the University of São Paulo, the São Paulo Museum of Art (MASP), the São Paulo Biennial, and a renowned gastronomic scene. It hosts the Brazilian Grand Prix at Interlagos and several major football clubs, including Corinthians, Palmeiras, and São Paulo FC.

Challenges

Rapid growth produced vast peripheral districts and favelas, severe traffic congestion, and air pollution. The city operates one of the largest urban helicopter fleets in the world, a by-product of both affluence and congestion.
