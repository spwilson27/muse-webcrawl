Year of the Five Emperors

Year of the Five Emperors
Date: 193 AD
Claimants: Pertinax, Didius Julianus, Pescennius Niger, Clodius Albinus, Septimius Severus
Outcome: Severan dynasty founded

The Year of the Five Emperors was 193 AD, a year of civil war in the Roman Empire during which five men claimed the imperial throne following the murder of Commodus. The phrase parallels the later Year of the Four Emperors (69 AD).

Sequence of events

Commodus was strangled on the last day of 192. His successor, the capable city prefect Pertinax, tried to restore discipline and finances but was murdered by the Praetorian Guard after only 86 days. The Guard then notoriously auctioned the empire to the wealthy senator Didius Julianus, an outrage that provoked the provinces: the Danubian legions proclaimed Septimius Severus, Syria acclaimed Pescennius Niger, and Britain's governor Clodius Albinus was recognised as Caesar, then Augustus.

Severus marched on Rome first: Julianus was abandoned and executed after 66 days, in June 193. Severus then turned east, defeating Niger at Issus in 194, and west, destroying Albinus at Lugdunum (Lyon) in February 197 in one of the largest battles of Roman civil warfare.

Consequences

Severus founded the Severan dynasty, which ruled (with interruptions) until 235. His victory entrenched the principle that the throne went to the commander of the strongest army, disbanded and rebuilt the Praetorian Guard from legionaries, and set patterns for the military monarchy of the third century.
