Nymphaeaceae

Nymphaeaceae is a family of flowering plants, commonly called water lilies. They live as rhizomatous aquatic herbs in temperate and tropical climates around the world. The family contains five genera with about 70 known species. Water lilies are rooted in soil in bodies of water, with leaves and flowers floating on or emergent from the surface.

Taxonomy

The Nymphaeaceae have been investigated systematically for decades. Several studies have indicated that the family, along with Amborellaceae and Austrobaileyales, forms a grade of early-diverging flowering plants near the base of the angiosperm tree. The five genera are Barclaya, Euryale, Nuphar, Nymphaea, and Victoria, following the merger of the former Cabombaceae into the family in some classifications.

Ecology

Water lilies provide habitat and shade for aquatic animals, and their floating leaves reduce algae growth by limiting light penetration. Many species are pollinated by beetles; some flowers are thermogenic, generating heat that volatilizes scent compounds.

Uses

Members of the family are widely grown as ornamentals in ponds and aquaria. Some species have edible seeds and rhizomes and have been used in traditional medicine.
