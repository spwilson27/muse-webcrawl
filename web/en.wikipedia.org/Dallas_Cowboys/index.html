The Dallas Cowboys are a professional American football team based in the Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex. They compete in the National Football League (NFL) as a member of the National Football Conference (NFC) East division. Founded in 1960, the Cowboys are among the most valuable sports franchises in the world and are popularly known as "America's Team."

History

Under coach Tom Landry, who led the team from 1960 to 1988, the Cowboys became a perennial contender, winning Super Bowls VI and XII behind quarterback Roger Staubach. After Jerry Jones bought the team in 1989, coach Jimmy Johnson rebuilt the roster; with the "Triplets" of Troy Aikman, Emmitt Smith, and Michael Irvin, Dallas won three Super Bowls in four years (XXVII, XXVIII, XXX) during the 1990s.

Stadiums

The Cowboys played at the Cotton Bowl (1960–1971), Texas Stadium in Irving (1971–2008), and since 2009 at AT&T Stadium in Arlington, a retractable-roof venue famous for its enormous center-hung video board.

Rivalries

The Cowboys' chief divisional rivals are the Philadelphia Eagles, Washington Commanders, and New York Giants. Historic non-divisional rivalries include the San Francisco 49ers and Pittsburgh Steelers.
