The Battle of Lake Erie, sometimes called the Battle of Put-in-Bay, was fought on 10 September 1813 during the War of 1812. A United States Navy squadron under Master Commandant Oliver Hazard Perry defeated and captured a Royal Navy squadron under Commander Robert Heriot Barclay, securing American control of Lake Erie for the remainder of the war.

Background

Control of Lake Erie determined supply lines for the war in the Old Northwest. Through 1813 Perry supervised construction of a squadron at Presque Isle (Erie, Pennsylvania), including the brigs Lawrence and Niagara, while Barclay's smaller squadron at Amherstburg was chronically short of men, guns and supplies because Detroit's garrison depended on the lake route.

The battle

Perry engaged the British line on the morning of 10 September. His flagship Lawrence, bearing his battle flag inscribed "Don't Give Up the Ship", took the brunt of British long-gun fire and was reduced to a wreck with most of her crew killed or wounded. Perry famously rowed through fire to the undamaged Niagara, took command, and broke the British line, raking the British flagships Detroit and Queen Charlotte, which had collided. Barclay, already wounded, surrendered his entire squadron — the first time in history a whole British naval squadron was captured. Perry's laconic dispatch to General William Henry Harrison became famous: "We have met the enemy and they are ours."

Consequences

With the lake secure, Harrison's army crossed into Canada, retook Detroit and defeated the British and Tecumseh's confederacy at the Battle of the Thames in October 1813, ending British power in the Northwest.
