Standing wave ratio

In radio engineering and telecommunications, standing wave ratio (SWR) is a measure of impedance matching of loads to the characteristic impedance of a transmission line or waveguide. Imperfect matching causes a portion of the transmitted wave to be reflected, and the interference between the forward and reflected waves produces a standing wave along the line.

Definition

The SWR is defined as the ratio of the maximum to the minimum amplitude of the standing wave along the line:

SWR = |Vmax| / |Vmin|

It is related to the magnitude of the reflection coefficient Γ by SWR = (1 + |Γ|) / (1 − |Γ|). A perfectly matched load gives an SWR of 1:1 (no reflected power). Higher values indicate more reflected power; an SWR of 2:1 corresponds to about 11% of the power being reflected. Return loss and mismatch loss are closely related quantities used to describe the same phenomenon.

Measurement

SWR is measured with an SWR meter or a directional coupler that samples forward and reflected power. A slotted line can be used to probe the standing wave directly in laboratory work. In practice, radio transmitters often include protection circuits that reduce output power when the SWR exceeds a safe threshold, since reflected power can damage the output stage and cause losses and voltage peaks in the feed line.

Practical implications

High SWR in an antenna feed system does not by itself mean the antenna radiates poorly, but it increases feed-line loss and can stress the transmitter. Antenna tuners (matching networks) are used to transform the load impedance seen by the transmitter to an acceptable SWR.
