Fylgja

A fylgja (Old Norse: [ˈfylɣjɑ], plural fylgjur) is a supernatural being or spirit in Norse mythology which accompanies a person in connection to their fate or fortune. The word means "one who accompanies". Fylgjur can appear in animal form or as women; they are sometimes described as guardian spirits attached to individuals or families, and their appearance could portend death or great fortune.

Animal fylgjur

In animal form, the fylgja often reflects the character or social standing of the person it accompanies: kings and chieftains might have bears or eagles, warriors wolves, and others foxes, goats, or boars. Animal fylgjur appear in dreams and visions, and to see one's own fylgja clearly awake was often taken as an omen of impending death.

Female fylgjur and dísir

Female fylgjur were associated with families and inherited luck, connected to the dísir — ancestral female powers. These guardian women could protect their descendants in battle and were honoured with sacrifices such as the dísablót.

In the sagas

Fylgjur appear in numerous sagas, including Njáls saga, Hallfreðar saga, and the legendary sagas. In Njáls saga, the appearance of a billy-goat fylgja drenched in blood foreshadows the death of Þórðr.
