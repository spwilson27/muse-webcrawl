Selkie


A selkie leaves the sea

Selkies are mythological beings capable of therianthropy, changing from seal to human form by shedding their skin. They are found primarily in the folklore of the British Isles and Scandinavia — the Northern Isles of Scotland (Orkney and Shetland), Ireland, Iceland, and the Faroe Islands. The word derives from early Scots selich, from Old English seolh meaning "seal".

Legend

In the typical tale, a female selkie is captured by a fisherman who steals and hides her sealskin while she is in human form on shore; unable to return to the sea, she marries him and bears children, but years later finds the hidden skin and immediately returns to the ocean, sometimes taking or visiting her children. Male selkies were said to be handsome in human form, with powers over storms and the ability to contact mortal women dissatisfied with their lives. Similar seal-people appear in Norse and Sámi legend, and the theme parallels swan maiden and other animal-bride tales worldwide.

In modern culture

Selkies feature in the film The Secret of Roan Inish (1994), the animated film Song of the Sea (2014), and the novel The Brides of Rollrock Island. The Faroese legend of the seal woman of Mikladalur is commemorated by the Kópakonan statue erected on Kalsoy in 2014.
