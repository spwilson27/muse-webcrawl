Peleus

Peleus (Ancient Greek: Πηλεύς, Pēleús) was a hero of Greek mythology, king of the Myrmidons of Phthia in Thessaly, husband of the sea goddess Thetis, and father of Achilles.

Myth

Peleus was the son of Aeacus, king of Aegina. Exiled with his brother Telamon for the murder of their half-brother Phocus, he was purified in Phthia, where he married Antigone, daughter of King Eurytion — whom he later accidentally killed during the Calydonian boar hunt, requiring a second exile and purification at Iolcus. He sailed with the Argonauts and took part in Heracles' expedition against Troy.

His most famous exploit was winning Thetis as his bride. Because a prophecy foretold that Thetis would bear a son greater than his father, Zeus arranged for her to marry a mortal. On Chiron's advice, Peleus seized Thetis and held fast while she transformed into fire, water, a lion, and a serpent, until she yielded. Their wedding on Mount Pelion was attended by the gods — but Eris, uninvited, threw the Apple of Discord among the guests, setting in motion the Judgment of Paris and the Trojan War. Their son Achilles was, when Peleus was too old to fight, the greatest warrior of the Greeks at Troy.
