iTunes

iTunes is a media player, media library, Internet radio broadcaster, mobile device management utility, and the client app for the iTunes Store. It was developed by Apple Inc. and released on January 9, 2001, for use on personal computers running macOS and Windows. It was used to play, download, and organize digital multimedia files, including music and video, and to manage content on iPod, iPhone, and iPad devices.

History

iTunes originated from SoundJam MP, an MP3 player application Apple acquired in 2000 from Casady & Greene. Apple reworked the software into iTunes, launched alongside the original iPod later in 2001. The iTunes Music Store opened on April 28, 2003, selling songs at 99 cents each with digital rights management; it became the largest music vendor in the United States by 2008 and the largest in the world by 2010, and sold its 25 billionth song in 2013. Features added over the years included video (2005), podcasts, the App Store for iPhone apps (2008), Genius playlists, Ping (a short-lived social network), iTunes Match, and Apple Music streaming integration (2015).

Features

iTunes maintained a media library database, supported ripping CDs, playlists and smart playlists, metadata editing, AirPlay streaming to other devices, Home Sharing, and synchronizing and backing up iOS devices. Purchased media was protected at first by FairPlay DRM; DRM-free "iTunes Plus" tracks became standard for music in 2009. Songs such as Whitney Houston's version of "I Will Always Love You" were among the store's perennial bestsellers.

Replacement

With macOS Catalina (10.15) in October 2019, Apple split iTunes into separate Music, Podcasts, and TV apps, and moved device management into Finder. The iTunes application continued on Windows until Apple began replacing it with separate Apple Music, Apple TV, and Apple Devices apps, rolled out in 2024, after which iTunes on Windows remained mainly for podcasts and audiobooks. The iTunes Store brand continues for media purchases on iOS.
