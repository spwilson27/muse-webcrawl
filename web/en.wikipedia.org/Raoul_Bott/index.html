Raoul Bott (24 September 1923 – 20 December 2005) was a Hungarian-American mathematician known for numerous fundamental contributions to geometry and topology. He spent most of his career at Harvard University, where he was a professor from 1959 to 1999.

Work

Bott was initially trained in electrical engineering at McGill University before turning to mathematics at the Carnegie Institute of Technology, where he wrote a thesis on electrical network theory. His most famous result is the Bott periodicity theorem (1957), which describes a repeating pattern in the homotopy groups of the classical Lie groups and became a cornerstone of topological K-theory. With Michael Atiyah he developed the Atiyah–Bott fixed-point theorem and worked on Yang–Mills theory over Riemann surfaces; his generalisation of Morse theory to Morse–Bott functions remains standard.

Students and honours

Bott supervised many doctoral students who became leading mathematicians, including Stephen Smale, Robert MacPherson and Daniel Quillen. He received the National Medal of Science in 1987 and shared the Wolf Prize in Mathematics in 2000 with Jean-Pierre Serre.

Later life

Born in Budapest and raised in Slovakia, Bott emigrated to Canada in 1938. He was renowned as a teacher and expositor; his lecture notes and books, including Differential Forms in Algebraic Topology (with Loring Tu), are classics of the subject.
