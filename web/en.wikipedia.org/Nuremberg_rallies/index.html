Nuremberg rallies

The Nuremberg rallies were a series of mass propaganda rallies held by the Nazi Party in Nuremberg, Germany, between 1923 and 1938. The best known were the annual Reich Party Congresses of the 1930s, staged on the vast rally grounds designed by Albert Speer, featuring choreographed parades of hundreds of thousands of uniformed participants.

Staging and purpose

The rallies projected an image of national unity and the Führer cult around Adolf Hitler, with set-piece elements such as the "cathedral of light" formed by anti-aircraft searchlights. Leni Riefenstahl's film Triumph of the Will (1935) recorded the 1934 rally and became a landmark of propaganda cinema. The 1935 rally was the occasion for announcing the Nuremberg Laws, which stripped Jews of citizenship.

Legacy

The partially completed rally grounds survive as a memorial and documentation centre, a major site for the study of Nazi propaganda and spectacle.
