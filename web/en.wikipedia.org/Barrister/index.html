Barrister

A barrister is a type of lawyer in common law jurisdictions. Barristers mostly specialise in courtroom advocacy and litigation. Their tasks include arguing cases in higher courts, drafting legal pleadings, researching the law and giving expert legal opinions.

Regulation

In England and Wales barristers are regulated by the Bar Standards Board and must belong to one of the four Inns of Court. They are usually instructed by solicitors rather than directly by clients, though direct access has expanded.

Distinction from solicitors

In jurisdictions with a split legal profession, solicitors handle most client-facing and preparatory work, while barristers are engaged for advocacy and specialist advice.

Other jurisdictions

Barristers practise in Ireland, Northern Ireland, Scotland (as advocates), parts of Australia, and other Commonwealth countries.
