Acronauplia

The Acronauplia (Greek: Ακροναυπλία, Akronafplia) is a rocky promontory and castle situated near the midpoint of the southern side of the Palamidi hill in Nafplio, Greece, overlooking the Argolic Gulf. It is the oldest part of the city of Nafplio and served as its acropolis and fortified port defence from antiquity until the development of the Palamidi fortress in the early 18th century.

History

The site was fortified from prehistoric times and was the acropolis of ancient Nauplia. Walls surviving in part include polygonal masonry of the ancient period, with substantial additions from the Byzantine era, the Frankish and Venetian occupations of the Morea, and the Ottoman period. During the first Venetian occupation (1388–1540) the castle was strengthened; the Ottomans held it from 1540 until the Venetians recaptured Nafplio in 1685, after which Venice built the much larger Palamidi fortress above the town between 1711 and 1714.

During and after the Greek War of Independence, Acronauplia's position made it a key stronghold in the struggle for Nafplio, which briefly served as the first capital of modern Greece. In the 20th century the castle area was used for a time as a prison, and later a hotel complex — the Nafplia Palace — was built within the site. The remaining fortifications and the clock tower are now a preserved archaeological site and a landmark of the town.
