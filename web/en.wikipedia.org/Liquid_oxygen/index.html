Liquid oxygen

Boiling point: 90.19 K (−182.96 °C)
Appearance: Pale blue liquid
Abbreviation: LOX

Liquid oxygen, abbreviated LOX, is the liquid form of molecular oxygen. It was first produced by Polish scientists Zygmunt Wróblewski and Karol Olszewski in 1883. Liquid oxygen has a pale blue colour and is strongly paramagnetic.

Production

It is produced industrially by fractional distillation of liquefied air and is one of the most common cryogenic oxidizers.

Uses

Liquid oxygen is the standard oxidizer in liquid-propellant rockets, combined with fuels such as liquid hydrogen, kerosene (RP-1) or methane. It is also used in medicine, in submarines and spacecraft for breathing gas, in steelmaking and in oxy-fuel welding and cutting.

Hazards

Materials soaked in liquid oxygen can become explosively reactive, and asphalt and organic materials may detonate on impact in its presence.
