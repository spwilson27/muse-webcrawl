Ad blocking

Ad blocking or ad filtering is a type of software that can remove or alter advertising content from a web page, website, or mobile app. Ad blockers are available as browser extensions, standalone browsers, operating-system features, and network-level tools. They typically work by comparing requests against filter lists of known advertising and tracking domains and resources, and preventing matching content from loading or being displayed.

Benefits and motivations

Users adopt ad blocking to improve page load speed and reduce data consumption, remove intrusive or malicious advertising (malvertising), protect privacy by blocking trackers, and reduce visual clutter. Studies have repeatedly found that a substantial fraction of web users — often estimated between a quarter and a third — use an ad blocker.

Effects on publishers

Because much web content is funded by advertising, widespread ad blocking reduces publisher revenue and has prompted countermeasures: anti-adblock scripts that detect blockers and restrict access, "acceptable ads" whitelisting programmes, subscription and paywall models, and native advertising. Some publishers argue ad blocking amounts to copyright interference or breach of contract; courts in Germany repeatedly ruled in favour of the legality of Adblock Plus in suits brought by publishers.

Technology

Filter lists such as EasyList define blocking rules maintained by volunteer communities. Blocking can occur at the browser level (extensions such as uBlock Origin), at the DNS level (for example Pi-hole on a home network), or through hosts-file modification. Content-blocking APIs in browsers such as Safari and later versions of Chrome's extension platform (Manifest V3) have shaped what blockers can do.
