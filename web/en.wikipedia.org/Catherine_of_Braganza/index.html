Catherine of Braganza

Catherine of Braganza
Born: November 25, 1638, Vila Viçosa, Portugal
Died: December 31, 1705, Lisbon
Spouse: Charles II of England
Queen consort: 1662–1685

Catherine of Braganza (Portuguese: Catarina de Bragança; 25 November 1638 – 31 December 1705) was Queen of England, Scotland and Ireland as the wife of King Charles II, whom she married in 1662. She was a Portuguese infanta, daughter of King John IV of Portugal.

Marriage and dowry

The marriage sealed an alliance between England and Portugal. Catherine's dowry brought England the ports of Tangier and Bombay — the latter later leased to the East India Company and foundational to British India — along with trading privileges and a large cash payment. Her Catholicism made her unpopular in Protestant England, and she was falsely implicated in the fabricated Popish Plot of 1678, though Charles protected her.

Later life

The marriage produced no surviving children. After Charles II's death in 1685 Catherine remained in England for several years before returning to Portugal in 1692, where she served twice as regent for her brother, Peter II. She is often credited with popularising tea drinking in England, and Queens in New York is traditionally said to be named for her.
