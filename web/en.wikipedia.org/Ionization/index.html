Ionization

Ionization (or ionisation) is the process by which an atom or molecule acquires a positive or negative electric charge by gaining or losing electrons. The resulting charged particles are called ions: negatively charged anions and positively charged cations. The minimum energy needed to remove an electron from a gaseous atom in its ground state is its ionization energy.

Mechanisms

Ionization can occur through collisions with energetic particles, absorption of electromagnetic radiation (photoionization), strong electric fields (field ionization), heating, or chemical reactions. In solution, molecules may ionize by dissociation — for example, acids releasing protons — governed by equilibrium constants such as pKa.

Ionizing radiation

Radiation with enough energy to ionize atoms — ultraviolet light, X-rays, gamma rays, and energetic particles — is called ionizing radiation. Its ability to break chemical bonds makes it both useful (radiotherapy, sterilisation, smoke detectors using americium-241) and hazardous to living tissue, where ionization can damage DNA.

Applications

Controlled ionization underpins mass spectrometry, gas-discharge lamps and neon signs, plasma processing in semiconductor manufacture, ion thrusters for spacecraft, and air ionizers. The degree of ionization of a gas determines whether it behaves as a plasma.
