Kiheitai

The Kiheitai (Japanese: 奇兵隊, "irregular troop") was a volunteer militia formed in 1863 in Chōshū Domain, Japan, by Takasugi Shinsaku. It broke with samurai tradition by recruiting men regardless of social status — samurai, farmers, townspeople and even Buddhist monks — and equipping and drilling them in Western fashion.

History

Founded after Shimonoseki was threatened by Western warships, the Kiheitai fought in the defence of Chōshū, the domain's civil war of 1864–1865 and the Boshin War that overthrew the Tokugawa shogunate and brought about the Meiji Restoration. Its success demonstrated the effectiveness of mixed-status, rifle-armed units and influenced the creation of the Imperial Japanese Army. Discontented former members later rose in rebellion in 1876.
