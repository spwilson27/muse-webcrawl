Atlin Lake and the Coast Mountains

Atlin Lake is a large mountain lake in northwestern British Columbia, Canada, with its northern tip extending into Yukon. It is regarded as the source of the Yukon River, since its waters flow through a short chain of lakes and rivers into the Yukon system. The name Atlin comes from a Tlingit word, Aa Tlein, meaning "big body of water".

Geography

The lake is long and narrow, set in a glacial valley of the Coast Mountains and fed at its southern end by the Llewellyn Glacier. The small community of Atlin, British Columbia, sits on its eastern shore. Atlin Lake drains northward via the Atlin River into Tagish Lake, thence through Bennett Lake's outlet waters toward the Yukon River.

History

Gold was discovered in the Atlin district in 1898, near the end of the Klondike Gold Rush, when Fritz Miller and Kenny McLaren found gold on Pine Creek. The resulting Atlin Gold Rush drew thousands of prospectors, and the town of Atlin grew rapidly, supplied by steamboats crossing the lake. Today the area is known for fishing (including lake trout), boating, and wilderness tourism, and much of the lake's eastern shore is protected within Atlin Provincial Park and Recreation Area.
