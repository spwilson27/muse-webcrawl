Madchester

Madchester was a British music and cultural scene that developed in Manchester in the late 1980s and early 1990s. It fused alternative rock with acid house, rave culture and psychedelia, producing a distinctive baggy sound and fashion — loose clothes, bucket hats and flared jeans — that briefly made Manchester the centre of British youth culture. The term is a portmanteau of "mad" and "Manchester", popularised by the Happy Mondays and Factory Records' Tony Wilson.

Origins

Manchester had a rich musical heritage from the post-punk era of Joy Division and The Smiths. In the mid-1980s the Haçienda nightclub, owned by Factory Records and New Order, struggled as a live venue but was transformed after 1986 by DJs such as Graeme Park and Mike Pickering playing Chicago house and Detroit techno, alongside the arrival of ecstasy. Club nights like Nude and Hot turned the Haçienda into the epicentre of the acid house explosion and the 1988 "Second Summer of Love".

The bands

The scene's defining bands combined indie guitar music with dance rhythms:

  • The Stone Roses, whose 1989 debut album is routinely ranked among the greatest British albums ever, blending jangling guitars with funk-inflected rhythms.
  • Happy Mondays, led by Shaun Ryder, whose loose, groove-heavy sound and hedonistic image embodied the scene; their records were produced by Paul Oakenfold and others from the dance world.
  • Inspiral Carpets, The Charlatans, James, 808 State, A Guy Called Gerald and Northside also enjoyed success.

Decline and legacy

By 1991 the moment had passed: the Stone Roses became embroiled in a legal dispute with their label, the Haçienda was plagued by gang violence and financial losses, and rave culture fragmented. Yet Madchester reshaped British music by breaking down the barrier between rock and dance culture, paved the way for Britpop — whose champions Oasis came from Manchester — and left an enduring image of the city as England's music capital of the north.
