The Northwest Caucasian languages, also called Abkhaz–Adyghe or Circassian, are a language family spoken in the northwestern Caucasus, chiefly in the Russian republics of Adygea, Kabardino-Balkaria and Karachay-Cherkessia, in Abkhazia, and in a large diaspora in Turkey and the Middle East.

Languages

The family divides into an Abkhaz–Abaza branch and a Circassian (Adyghe–Kabardian) branch. Ubykh, the best-known third branch, became extinct in 1992 with the death of its last native speaker, Tevfik Esenç.

Features

Northwest Caucasian languages are famous for their enormous consonant inventories — Ubykh had over 80 consonants — paired with minimal vowel systems, sometimes analysed as just two or three vowels. They are polysynthetic and ergative, with verbs agreeing with subject, object and indirect object.

History

Speaker communities were drastically reduced by the Russian conquest of the Caucasus and the mass expulsion of Circassians in 1864, which created the large diaspora communities where many speakers now live.
