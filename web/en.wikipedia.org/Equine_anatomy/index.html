Equine anatomy

Points of a horse: the external anatomy

Equine anatomy encompasses the gross and microscopic anatomy of horses, ponies and other equids, including donkeys, mules and zebras. While all anatomical features of equids are described in the same terms as for other animals by the Nomina Anatomica Veterinaria, there are many horse-specific colloquial terms used by equestrians.

External anatomy

The points of the horse include the muzzle, nostrils, eyes, ears, poll, crest, mane, withers, back, loin, croup, dock, and tail. The forelimb consists of the shoulder, forearm, knee (carpus), cannon bone, fetlock, pastern, coronet, and hoof; the hindlimb consists of the hip, stifle, gaskin, hock, cannon, fetlock, pastern, and hoof. The withers, where the neck meets the back, are the standard point at which a horse's height is measured in hands.

Skeletal system

The horse's skeleton has about 205 bones. Distinctive features include the fusion and reduction of the side toes — the modern horse stands on a single digit (the third) equivalent to the human middle finger — and a long skull housing large teeth adapted for grazing. The vertebral column consists of 7 cervical, 18 thoracic, 6 lumbar, 5 fused sacral, and variable caudal vertebrae.

Hoof

The hoof is the horny covering of the toe, composed of the wall, sole, and frog, growing continuously from the coronet. Inside lie the coffin bone (distal phalanx), navicular bone, and associated tendons and ligaments of the stay apparatus, which allows horses to rest standing with minimal muscular effort.

Digestive system

Horses are hindgut fermenters with a relatively small stomach and a greatly enlarged caecum and colon where microbial fermentation breaks down fibrous forage. They cannot vomit, making them vulnerable to colic, a major cause of death. An adult horse's intestine may exceed 25 metres in length.

Teeth and ageing

Horses have hypsodont (high-crowned), continuously erupting teeth suited to grinding abrasive grass. The traditional practice of "ageing" a horse examines tooth eruption and wear patterns; mature males typically have 40 teeth and mares 36.
