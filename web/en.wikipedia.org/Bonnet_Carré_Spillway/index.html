Bonnet Carré Spillway

Location: St. Charles Parish, Louisiana, US

Completed: 1931

Operator: US Army Corps of Engineers

Capacity: 250,000 cu ft/s (7,100 m³/s)

Purpose: Flood control for New Orleans

The Bonnet Carré Spillway is a flood control structure on the Mississippi River near Norco, Louisiana, about 45 kilometres upriver from New Orleans. Completed in 1931 in response to the Great Mississippi Flood of 1927, it diverts excess river water northward into Lake Pontchartrain, sparing New Orleans from flooding when the river runs dangerously high. It is operated by the United States Army Corps of Engineers as part of the Mississippi River and Tributaries Project.

Design and operation

The spillway consists of a concrete weir about 2.3 kilometres long containing 350 gated bays, fronted by a guide levee system. Timber needles are lifted from the bays by cranes mounted on the spillway bridge, allowing up to 250,000 cubic feet per second (about a quarter of the river's flood flow) to pass through a floodway roughly 9 kilometres long to Lake Pontchartrain. The floodway is crossed by rail lines and by US Highway 61 and Interstate 10.

Openings

The spillway was first opened in 1937 and has operated a limited number of times since, including 1973, 1997, 2011, 2016, and 2018. In 2019 it was opened twice in one year for the first time, for a total of more than 120 days across the two openings. Openings send huge volumes of fresh water and sediment into the brackish lake and onward to the Mississippi Sound, causing algal blooms and oyster and fish kills that have prompted litigation from Mississippi coastal communities.

Recreation and land use

When dry, the spillway grounds serve as a public recreation area for fishing, hunting, off-road vehicles, and model-aircraft flying, and clay is excavated from the floodway for levee construction.
