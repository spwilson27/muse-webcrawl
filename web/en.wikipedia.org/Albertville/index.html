Albertville

Albertville (French pronunciation: [albɛʁvil]; Arpitan: Arbèrtvela) is a commune in the Savoie department in the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region in southeastern France, with a population of roughly 20,000. It is best known internationally as the host city of the 1992 Winter Olympics.

History

The town was founded in 1836 by King Charles Albert of Sardinia, who united the medieval town of Conflans with the villages of the plain below. It stands at the confluence of the Isère and Arly rivers, at the gateway to the Tarentaise valley and its famous ski resorts, including Courchevel, Méribel, and Val d'Isère.

1992 Winter Olympics

Albertville hosted the XVI Olympic Winter Games in February 1992, the last Winter Games held in the same year as the Summer Games. Events were spread across the surrounding Savoie resorts; Albertville itself hosted the opening and closing ceremonies and the skating events.

Geography and economy

Situated at about 345 metres above sea level, the town serves as a commercial and transport hub for the upper Isère valley. The medieval quarter of Conflans, on its hill above the modern town, preserves ramparts and old houses.
