Kingstown

Kingstown Harbour, Ireland, the mail packet port for Dublin.

Kingstown is the former name of the port town of Dún Laoghaire in County Dublin, Ireland, and also the name of the capital city of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines in the Caribbean.

Kingstown, Ireland

The Irish town was renamed in 1821 in honour of King George IV, whose visit that year coincided with the construction of a great new asylum harbour — the largest artificial harbour in the world at the time of its completion — to serve as the mail packet station for Dublin and a refuge for sailing ships driven onto the dangerous Leinster coast. A railway from Dublin, opened in 1834, was one of Ireland's first. For a century the town was officially Kingstown; in 1920 the town council restored the older Irish name Dún Laoghaire ("fort of Laoghaire"), after the 5th-century High King Lóegaire mac Néill. The harbour remains a major ferry port, and the town is known for its two granite piers, the National Maritime Museum and the annual Festival of World Cultures it once hosted.

Kingstown, Saint Vincent

Kingstown, founded by French colonists around 1722 and later developed by the British, is the capital, largest city and main port of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines. Nicknamed the "City of Arches" for its many stone arcades, it lies on the island's south-west coast and is noted for its botanical gardens, among the oldest in the Western Hemisphere (established 1765).
