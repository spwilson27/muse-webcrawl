Bamyan

Bamyan, also spelled Bamiyan, is the capital of Bamyan Province in central Afghanistan, in the high valley of the Hindu Kush at an elevation of about 2,550 metres. The town is best known for the two monumental statues of the Buddha carved into the cliff face in the 6th century, which were destroyed by the Taliban in March 2001.

History

Bamyan lay on the Silk Road between Balkh and the Indian subcontinent and was a flourishing Buddhist pilgrimage centre from the 2nd century CE. The Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang described its monasteries and giant statues in the 7th century. The valley later came under Islamic rule and was devastated by Genghis Khan in 1221. Its population is predominantly Hazara.

Heritage

The empty niches of the Buddhas, the surrounding cave murals, the ruins of Shahr-e Gholghola, and the lakes of Band-e Amir to the west form a UNESCO World Heritage cultural landscape, listed in 2003 and simultaneously placed on the list of World Heritage in Danger.
