Sacred Valley

The Sacred Valley of the Incas (Spanish: Valle Sagrado de los Incas; Quechua: Willka Qhichwa) is a valley in the Andes of Peru, roughly 20 kilometres (12 mi) at a direct distance north of the Inca capital of Cusco. It is located in the present-day Peruvian region of Cusco.

Geography

The valley stretches along the Urubamba River (also called the Vilcanota or Willkamayu River) from the town of Písac in the east to Ollantaytambo and beyond in the west. Its moderate climate and fertile plains made it a key agricultural area of the Inca Empire, particularly for maize production.

Sites

Major archaeological sites in the valley include the terraced ruins and citadel at Písac, the fortress and temple complex at Ollantaytambo, the concentric circular terraces of Moray, and the salt pans of Maras. The town of Chinchero and the valley's markets are also noted stops on the way to Machu Picchu, reached through the valley.

History

The valley was appreciated by the Incas for its climate and rich plains, and formed the heartland route toward the jungle and Machu Picchu. Colonizing cultures lived in the area long before the Incas, including the Chanapata (700–400 BCE) and Qotacalla (500–900 CE) cultures.
