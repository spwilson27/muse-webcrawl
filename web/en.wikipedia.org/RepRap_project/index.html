RepRap project

FounderAdrian Bowyer
Started2005, University of Bath
TypeOpen-source self-replicating 3D printer
LicenceGNU General Public License

The RepRap project is an open-source initiative to develop a low-cost 3D printer that can print most of its own components—a self-replicating machine. The name is short for replicating rapid prototyper. Founded in 2005 by Adrian Bowyer, a senior lecturer in mechanical engineering at the University of Bath, RepRap is widely credited with igniting the desktop 3D printing revolution by making fused filament fabrication printers affordable and freely reproducible.

Designs

The first RepRap printer, Darwin, was released in 2007; its successor Mendel (2009) simplified the frame into a triangular structure. Later influential designs included the Prusa Mendel, developed by Josef Průša, whose i3 design became one of the most copied printer architectures in the world. RepRap machines print in thermoplastics such as PLA and ABS and typically print the plastic parts of the next machine, while standard "vitamin" parts (motors, electronics, rods, fasteners) are bought in.

Philosophy and impact

All RepRap designs are released under the GNU General Public License, allowing anyone to build, modify, and sell them. Bowyer envisioned self-replication as a way to distribute manufacturing wealth—the machines, in his phrase, give "the means of production" to individuals. The project spawned a large community and many companies, including MakerBot (originally built on RepRap designs) and Prusa Research, and drove the steep fall in consumer 3D printer prices in the early 2010s.
