Valhalla

In Norse mythology, Valhalla (Old Norse: Valhǫll, "hall of the slain") is a majestic hall located in Asgard and ruled over by the god Odin. Warriors who die in combat are chosen by Odin and brought by the valkyries to Valhalla, where they become einherjar — spirits who feast and fight daily in preparation for Ragnarök.

Description

The Prose Edda describes Valhalla as a vast hall roofed with golden shields, its rafters made of spears, with 540 doors, through each of which 800 warriors can march abreast. The einherjar spend their days fighting one another, killing and being killed, and each evening those slain rise whole again to feast on the boar Sæhrímnir, which is cooked each day and restored each night, and drink mead that flows from the udders of the goat Heiðrún.

Half of those who die in battle go to Valhalla; the other half go to Fólkvangr, the field of the goddess Freyja. At Ragnarök, the einherjar will march out of Valhalla's doors to fight alongside the gods against the forces of chaos.

Modern influence

Valhalla's name has been given to a temple-like commemoration hall near Regensburg in Germany, to a large impact structure on Jupiter's moon Callisto, and to countless works of popular culture.
