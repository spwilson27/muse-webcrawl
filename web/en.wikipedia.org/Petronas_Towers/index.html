Petronas Towers

The Petronas Towers, also known as the Petronas Twin Towers and colloquially the KLCC Twin Towers, are a pair of 88-storey supertall skyscrapers in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia, standing at 451.9 metres (1,483 feet). Between 1998 and 2004, they were officially designated the tallest buildings in the world, a title they held until surpassed by Taipei 101. They remain the tallest twin buildings in the world and are a major landmark of Kuala Lumpur, standing beside the Kuala Lumpur City Centre (KLCC) development and Suria KLCC shopping mall.

History

The towers were designed by Argentine-American architect César Pelli and completed in 1998. Their design was inspired by Islamic art and architecture, reflecting Malaysia's predominantly Muslim culture: the floor plan is based on the Rub el Hizb, an eight-pointed star, with circular sectors added to increase floor space. Construction involved a deep foundation of a 4.6-metre-thick raft supported by 104 barrette piles driven up to 114 metres into the ground. The buildings are constructed largely of high-strength reinforced concrete, with a steel and glass façade.

Features

The two towers are joined at the 41st and 42nd floors by a double-decker skybridge, 170 metres above the ground and 58 metres long — the highest two-storey bridge in the world. The skybridge is not rigidly attached to the towers, allowing it to slide in and out as the buildings sway in the wind. Below the towers lies Suria KLCC, a large shopping mall, and the 50-acre KLCC Park, designed by Brazilian landscape architect Roberto Burle Marx. Tower One is occupied by Petronas, the Malaysian state oil and gas company, while Tower Two hosts multinational companies.

Tenants and tourism

The towers are a major tourist attraction; tickets are sold for visits to the skybridge and the observation deck on the 86th floor. In September 2009, French climber Alain Robert scaled Tower Two using only his bare hands and feet.
