Pancytopenia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Pancytopenia is a medical condition in which there is a reduction in the number of all three major types of blood cells: red blood cells (anaemia), white blood cells (leukopenia) and platelets (thrombocytopenia). It is not a disease in itself but a finding that results from many different disorders affecting the bone marrow or the peripheral blood.

Causes

Pancytopenia arises broadly from three mechanisms: decreased production of blood cells by the bone marrow, increased destruction or sequestration of cells, or a combination of both. Causes include:

  • Bone marrow failure: aplastic anaemia, Fanconi anaemia and other inherited marrow failure syndromes
  • Marrow infiltration: leukaemias, lymphomas, multiple myeloma, myelofibrosis and metastatic cancer
  • Nutritional deficiency: severe vitamin B12 or folate deficiency (megaloblastic anaemia)
  • Drugs and toxins: chemotherapy, some antibiotics, alcohol, benzene exposure and radiation
  • Infections: HIV, hepatitis viruses, Epstein–Barr virus, tuberculosis and visceral leishmaniasis
  • Immune and other causes: systemic lupus erythematosus, paroxysmal nocturnal haemoglobinuria, and hypersplenism from an enlarged spleen trapping blood cells

Symptoms and diagnosis

Symptoms reflect the three cytopenias: fatigue and pallor from anaemia, recurrent infections from leukopenia, and bruising or bleeding from thrombocytopenia. Diagnosis begins with a full blood count and blood film; bone marrow aspiration and biopsy are usually performed to establish the underlying cause, alongside tests for nutritional deficiencies, infections and autoimmune disease.

Treatment

Treatment is directed at the underlying cause — for example, withdrawal of an offending drug, vitamin replacement, treatment of infection, immunosuppression for aplastic anaemia, or haematopoietic stem cell transplantation in suitable patients. Supportive care with blood and platelet transfusions and prompt treatment of infections is often required.
