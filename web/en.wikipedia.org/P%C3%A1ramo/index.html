Páramo

Páramo is a neotropical high mountain ecosystem located in the Andes between the upper forest line (about 3,100 metres elevation) and the permanent snow line (about 5,000 metres). It consists mainly of grassland with rosette plants, shrubs, and wetlands, and is sometimes described as an "evolutionary hot spot" for its rapid speciation and many endemic species.

Distribution

Páramos occur chiefly in Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, and Venezuela, with outliers in Costa Rica and Panama. Colombia holds the largest share, including the Sumapaz páramo near Bogotá, considered the world's largest.

Ecology and water

Characteristic plants include giant rosettes of the genus Espeletia (frailejones), tussock grasses, and cushion plants adapted to intense ultraviolet radiation, cold nights, and daily freeze–thaw cycles. Páramo soils and vegetation store and slowly release water, supplying rivers and drinking water to millions of people in Andean cities.

Threats

Agriculture, grazing, burning, mining, and climate change threaten páramo ecosystems; several Andean nations have adopted legal protections restricting development in them.
