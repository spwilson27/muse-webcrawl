Kota Kinabalu (Malay pronunciation: [ˈkota kinaˈbalu]), often abbreviated to KK, is the state capital of Sabah, Malaysia, on the northwest coast of the island of Borneo facing the South China Sea. It is named after nearby Mount Kinabalu, the highest peak in Malaysia. The city is a major gateway for tourism to Sabah's islands, rainforests and mountain parks, and a regional hub for trade, education and administration.

History

The modern city began as Jesselton, a trading outpost established in 1899 by the British North Borneo Chartered Company and named after its vice-chairman, Charles Jessel. Chosen for its sheltered deep-water harbour, it replaced an earlier settlement on Gaya Island that had been burned during Mat Salleh's rebellion in 1897. Jesselton was almost completely destroyed by Allied bombing during the Second World War, when Sabah was under Japanese occupation (1942–1945).

After the war, British North Borneo became a Crown Colony and Jesselton was chosen as its capital over ruined Sandakan. When North Borneo joined the Federation of Malaysia in 1963 as Sabah, the city remained the capital; in 1968 it was renamed Kota Kinabalu. It was granted city status on 2 February 2000.

Geography and climate

KK lies on a narrow coastal plain between the Crocker Range and the sea, just south of the equator, with a tropical rainforest climate — hot, humid and wet year-round. Offshore, the Tunku Abdul Rahman National Park protects five islands (Gaya, Sapi, Manukan, Mamutik and Sulug) popular for snorkelling and diving.

Economy and attractions

Tourism is a mainstay: visitors come for the islands, the sunset over Tanjung Aru beach, seafood restaurants and markets such as the Filipino Market and the Sunday Gaya Street Fair, and as a base for climbing Mount Kinabalu in Kinabalu Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Landmarks include the Atkinson Clock Tower (1905), one of the few structures to survive the war, the Sabah State Mosque, the Tun Mustapha Tower and the Signal Hill Observatory. Kota Kinabalu International Airport is Malaysia's second busiest.
