Bill Walton

William Theodore Walton III (November 5, 1952 – May 27, 2024) was an American professional basketball player and television sportscaster. He played college basketball for the UCLA Bruins under coach John Wooden, winning three consecutive national college player of the year awards (1972–1974) and leading UCLA to NCAA championships in 1972 and 1973 and an 88-game winning streak.

Professional career

Walton was the first overall pick in the 1974 NBA draft by the Portland Trail Blazers. Despite a career plagued by foot injuries, he led Portland to its only NBA championship in 1977, winning the Finals MVP award, and was named the league's Most Valuable Player in 1978. He later played for the San Diego/Los Angeles Clippers and won a second championship with the Boston Celtics in 1986, when he was named the NBA Sixth Man of the Year. He was inducted into the Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame in 1993 and named to the NBA's 50th and 75th anniversary teams.

Broadcasting and life

After retiring, Walton overcame a stutter to become a popular, exuberant basketball commentator known for his hyperbolic style and love of the Grateful Dead. He won an Emmy Award for his broadcasting work. Walton died of cancer in 2024 at the age of 71.
