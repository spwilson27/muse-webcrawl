Privacy law

FieldLaw; information privacy
Landmark instrumentsGDPR (EU, 2016); CCPA (California, 2018); OECD Privacy Guidelines (1980)
Foundational articleWarren & Brandeis, "The Right to Privacy" (1890)

Privacy law is the body of law that regulates the collection, storage, use, and disclosure of personal information and protects individuals against intrusions into their private lives. It spans constitutional provisions, statutes, common-law torts, and sector-specific regulation of records such as health, financial, and communications data.

History

The modern field is often traced to Samuel Warren and Louis Brandeis's 1890 Harvard Law Review article "The Right to Privacy," which argued for a legal right "to be let alone." In the United States, William Prosser later classified the privacy torts as intrusion upon seclusion, public disclosure of private facts, false light, and appropriation of likeness. Constitutional privacy protections were recognised in cases such as Griswold v. Connecticut (1965).

Data protection

From the 1970s, computerisation shifted attention to information privacy and data protection. The European Union's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR, applicable from 2018) established a comprehensive regime of lawful bases for processing, data-subject rights, breach notification, and heavy fines, influencing legislation worldwide, including California's Consumer Privacy Act and Brazil's LGPD. Privacy law must balance individual rights against freedom of expression, public safety, and law-enforcement interests.
