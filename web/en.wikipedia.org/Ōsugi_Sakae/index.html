Ōsugi Sakae

Born17 January 1885, Marugame, Kagawa, Japan
Died16 September 1923 (aged 38), Tokyo, Japan
OccupationAnarchist writer and activist

Ōsugi Sakae (大杉 栄, January 17, 1885 – September 16, 1923) was a prominent Japanese anarchist. He published numerous anarchist periodicals, translated Western anarchist works by writers such as Peter Kropotkin and Errico Malatesta into Japanese, and helped shape the anarchist and free-love movements of Taishō-era Japan.

Life

Born in Marugame, the son of an army officer, Ōsugi was expelled from military school and turned to radical politics after encountering socialism as a student in Tokyo. He was repeatedly imprisoned, including for his part in the Red Flag Incident of 1908. He advocated free love and lived openly with several partners, most famously the feminist writer Itō Noe.

Death

In the chaos following the Great Kantō earthquake of 1923, Ōsugi, Itō Noe, and Ōsugi's six-year-old nephew were arrested by military police and beaten to death on 16 September 1923 — the Amakasu Incident, named after the officer responsible, who received only a light sentence. Ōsugi remains the best-known figure of Japanese anarchism.
