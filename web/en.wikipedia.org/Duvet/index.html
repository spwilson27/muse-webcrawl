Duvet

A duvet (from the French duvet, meaning "down") is a type of bedding consisting of a soft, flat bag filled with down, feathers, wool, cotton, or a synthetic alternative, protected by a removable cover similar to a pillow and pillow case. Duvets originated in rural Europe and were made from the down feathers of the eider duck.

Characteristics

A duvet is generally used alone, in place of a combination of sheets, blankets and quilts, and is standard bedding across much of Europe. Warmth is commonly rated in togs in the United Kingdom: a light summer duvet may be 4.5 tog, while a winter duvet can be 13.5 tog or more, and all-season sets combine two duvets of different weights.

Fillings

Natural fillings include goose and duck down and feathers, prized for their loft and insulation. Synthetic fillings, such as hollowfibre polyester, are cheaper and hypoallergenic. The fill is held in place by stitched pockets — either sewn-through or baffle-box construction — which prevent cold spots.

Duvet covers

The removable cover protects the duvet insert from washing wear; the system as a whole is sometimes called a "duvet set". In North America the terms comforter and duvet are often confused, as a comforter is typically used without a cover.
