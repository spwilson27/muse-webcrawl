Aisha

Born: c. 614 CE, Mecca

Died: c. 678 CE, Medina

Spouse: Muhammad

Father: Abu Bakr

Mother: Umm Ruman

Aisha bint Abi Bakr (c. 614 – c. 678 CE) was the third and youngest wife of the Islamic prophet Muhammad and the daughter of Abu Bakr, the first caliph. In Islamic tradition she is known as "Mother of the Believers" and is one of the most important female figures in Islam, credited with narrating a large number of hadith (sayings and actions of Muhammad).

Life

Aisha was born in Mecca to Abu Bakr and Umm Ruman. She married Muhammad in Mecca around 620 CE, shortly before the Hijra (migration) to Medina. After Muhammad's death in 632 CE, she played a prominent role in early Islamic history, including supporting the caliphate of her father and of Uthman.

During the First Fitna (first Islamic civil war), Aisha opposed the caliph Ali ibn Abi Talib and participated in the Battle of the Camel in 656 CE near Basra, where her forces were defeated. She then retired to Medina, where she spent her remaining years teaching and transmitting religious knowledge. She died around 678 CE and was buried in Al-Baqi cemetery.

Legacy

Aisha is credited with narrating over two thousand hadith in the canonical Sunni collections and was consulted by senior companions on matters of law, ritual, and Qur'anic interpretation. Sunni Muslims hold her in high esteem as a scholar and teacher; her role in the civil war is a point of contention in Shia tradition, which views her critically. Her age at marriage has been the subject of historical and modern debate.
