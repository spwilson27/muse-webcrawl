Megatsunami

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A megatsunami is a very large wave created by a sudden, massive displacement of material into a body of water. Unlike ordinary tsunamis, which are typically caused by undersea earthquakes, megatsunamis are usually generated by giant landslides, volcanic flank collapses or impact events, and can reach wave heights far exceeding those of normal tsunamis.

Lituya Bay, 1958

The best-documented megatsunami occurred in Lituya Bay, Alaska, on 9 July 1958, when an earthquake triggered a rockslide of about 30 million cubic metres into the bay. The resulting wave reached a run-up height of 524 metres (1,719 ft), the highest ever recorded, stripping trees from the opposite shore.

Other examples

Prehistoric events inferred from geological evidence include waves from landslides in Norwegian fjords, the Storegga Slide, and collapses of volcanic islands such as Fogo in Cape Verde. A megatsunami is also hypothesised to have followed the Chicxulub impact at the end of the Cretaceous.

Hazard

Megatsunamis are rare but can be locally devastating; unlike ocean-wide tsunamis, their extreme heights are typically confined to enclosed or nearby waters.
