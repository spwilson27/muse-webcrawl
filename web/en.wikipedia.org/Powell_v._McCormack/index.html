Powell v. McCormack

Powell v. McCormack, 395 U.S. 486 (1969), is a landmark United States Supreme Court case concerning the power of the U.S. House of Representatives to exclude a duly elected member. The Court held that in judging the qualifications of its members under Article I, Section 5 of the Constitution, the House is limited to the qualifications prescribed in Article I, Section 2—age, citizenship, and inhabitancy—and may not impose additional qualifications.

Background

Adam Clayton Powell Jr., the representative from Harlem, New York, had been the subject of controversy over alleged misuse of travel funds and other misconduct, and had been stripped of his committee chairmanship. After he was re-elected in 1966, the House voted in March 1967 to exclude him from the 90th Congress rather than seat him. Powell sued the Speaker, John William McCormack, and other House officials, claiming his exclusion was unconstitutional and seeking his salary.

Decision

The Supreme Court, in an opinion by Chief Justice Earl Warren, held that the House had acted unconstitutionally. The Framers intended the qualifications in the Constitution to be exclusive and fixed; permitting the House to add qualifications would undermine the people's right to choose their representatives. The Court distinguished exclusion from expulsion: the House could still expel a seated member by a two-thirds vote under its disciplinary powers, but Powell's exclusion had not been conducted as an expulsion. The Court also held that the case was not moot despite Powell's subsequent seating in 1969, because his claim for back salary remained, and that the Speech or Debate Clause did not bar the suit.

Significance

The decision is a foundational precedent on congressional membership and the separation of powers, affirming judicial review of congressional action that exceeds constitutional bounds. Powell was re-elected in a special election and eventually seated in 1969, though the House fined him and stripped his seniority; the Court left those internal sanctions undisturbed.
