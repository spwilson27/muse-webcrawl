Thomas John Watson Sr. (17 February 1874 – 19 June 1956) was an American businessman who served as the chairman and chief executive officer of IBM from 1914 to 1956. He oversaw the company's growth into an international force, building it from a maker of tabulating machines into the dominant company of the punched-card era and laying the foundations of the modern computer industry.

Early career

Watson was born in Campbell, New York, the son of a lumber dealer. After working as a travelling salesman, he joined the National Cash Register Company (NCR) in Buffalo, rising to become its general sales manager under John Henry Patterson. He left NCR in 1913 amid an antitrust conviction that was later overturned on appeal.

Building IBM

In 1914 Watson became general manager of the Computing-Tabulating-Recording Company (CTR), a holding company whose businesses included Herman Hollerith's tabulating machines. In 1924 he renamed it International Business Machines — IBM. Watson cultivated a distinctive corporate culture summed up in his slogan "THINK", insisted on dress codes and rigorous sales training, and developed generous employee benefits unusual for the era. He favoured renting rather than selling machines, a practice that produced steady income and helped IBM survive the Great Depression while expanding into electric typewriters and calculating machines.

Later years

During the Second World War IBM's plants produced war matériel and its tabulators were used throughout the Allied war effort. Watson steered the company into early electronic computing, supporting projects that led to the IBM 701. In May 1956, shortly before his death, he handed control to his son, Thomas Watson Jr. Watson also served as a trustee of Columbia University and was a prominent figure in international business organisations, serving as president of the International Chamber of Commerce in the late 1930s.

His son Thomas J. Watson Jr. succeeded him and led IBM through the mainframe era. The Watson Research Center and IBM's Watson artificial-intelligence system are named in his honour.
