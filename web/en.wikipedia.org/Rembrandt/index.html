Rembrandt Harmenszoon van Rijn (1606–1669), usually simply known as Rembrandt, was a Dutch Golden Age painter, printmaker, and draughtsman. He is generally considered one of the greatest visual artists in the history of art and the most important in Dutch art history, renowned for his mastery of light and shadow (chiaroscuro) and his unflinching examination of the human condition.

Career

After training in Leiden and a brief apprenticeship in Amsterdam, Rembrandt achieved early success as a portraitist and history painter. In Amsterdam he painted the group portraits The Anatomy Lesson of Dr. Nicolaes Tulp (1632) and his most famous work, The Night Watch (1642), properly a civic-guard company portrait notable for its dramatic movement and lighting. He was also a prolific and innovative etcher, producing some 300 etchings.

Life and self-portraits

Rembrandt married Saskia van Uylenburgh in 1634; her death in 1642 began a period of personal and financial difficulty, ending in insolvency in 1656. Throughout his life he painted, drew, and etched around a hundred self-portraits, an unmatched visual autobiography ranging from youthful bravado to the grave late portraits. His late style — rough, expressive paint handling and profound psychological depth — was little appreciated in his day but is now prized above all.
