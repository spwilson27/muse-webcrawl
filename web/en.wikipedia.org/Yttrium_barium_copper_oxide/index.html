Yttrium barium copper oxide

Chemical formulaYBa2Cu3O7−x
AppearanceBlack to dark green crystalline solid
Superconducting Tc93 K (−180.2 °C)
StructurePerovskite-derived (orthorhombic)
AliasesYBCO, Y123

Yttrium barium copper oxide (YBCO) is a family of crystalline chemical compounds famous for displaying high-temperature superconductivity. It includes the first material ever discovered to become superconducting above the boiling point of liquid nitrogen (77 K), making superconductivity far cheaper to reach. Its chemical formula is YBa2Cu3O7−x, often abbreviated Y123.

History

YBCO was discovered in 1987 by teams at the University of Alabama in Huntsville and the University of Houston, building on the cuprate superconductors identified by Bednorz and Müller in 1986. Maw-Kuen Wu, Paul Chu and colleagues showed that the compound's critical temperature reached 93 K — the breakthrough that ignited the "high-Tc revolution" in physics.

Properties

Superconductivity in YBCO depends sensitively on oxygen content: fully oxygenated YBa2Cu3O7 is an orthorhombic superconductor, while oxygen-depleted samples become tetragonal insulators. Its layered perovskite-like structure contains copper-oxide planes believed to be essential to its superconductivity. YBCO is a type-II superconductor and a brittle ceramic, which complicates making wires; it is typically used in thin films and coated conductors (tapes) for magnets, power cables, fault-current limiters, and magnetic levitation demonstrations.

Applications

REBCO coated conductors (using YBCO and related rare-earth compounds) are now produced commercially and used in high-field magnets, compact fusion experiments, and experimental power-grid installations.
