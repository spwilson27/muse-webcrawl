Pier

A pier is a raised structure that rises above a body of water and usually juts out from shore, typically supported by piles or pillars. Piers serve as working platforms for loading and unloading ships, as landing places for boats and ferries, and — especially in seaside resorts — as promenades and entertainment venues.

Working piers

Working piers in ports are equipped with cranes, warehouses, rail lines and utilities, and allow vessels to berth alongside for cargo handling. In North American usage, "pier" often denotes such a structure, while "wharf" refers to a platform parallel to the shore.

Pleasure piers

Pleasure piers flourished in Victorian Britain and later in North America, offering theatres, pavilions, amusement rides and fishing decks. Notable examples include the piers of Blackpool and Brighton in England and the Santa Monica Pier in California. Fire and storms have destroyed many, and several surviving piers are protected heritage structures.

Construction

Piers are commonly built on timber, steel or concrete piles driven into the seabed, with a deck of timber or concrete. Their design must withstand waves, tides, ship impacts and corrosion.
