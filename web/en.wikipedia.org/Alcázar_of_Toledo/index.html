Alcázar of Toledo

The Alcázar of Toledo is a stone fortification at the highest point of Toledo, Spain. Originally a Roman palace and later a medieval fortress, the present building was largely constructed in the 16th century under Charles V, with Renaissance courtyards designed by Alonso de Covarrubias and Juan de Herrera. It is best known internationally for the Siege of the Alcázar during the Spanish Civil War.

History

The site was fortified by the Romans in the 3rd century and used by Visigothic and Muslim rulers before Alfonso VI captured Toledo in 1085. Charles V rebuilt the alcázar as a royal residence, though the court's move to Madrid reduced its importance. It later housed a military academy (the Toledo Infantry Academy). The building was badly damaged by fire in 1710 and again in 1887, being rebuilt each time.

Siege of the Alcázar

From July to September 1936, a Nationalist force of about 1,000 under Colonel José Moscardó, together with civilian family members, held the alcázar against Republican forces. Relieved by Franco's Army of Africa on 27 September, the siege became a celebrated symbol in Nationalist propaganda; the ruins were subsequently preserved and the building rebuilt in its heroic image.

Today

The restored Alcázar houses the library of Castile-La Mancha and, since 2010, Spain's National Museum of the Army (Museo del Ejército), transferred from Madrid.
