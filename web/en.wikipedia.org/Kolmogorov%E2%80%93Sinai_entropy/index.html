Kolmogorov–Sinai entropy

In dynamical systems theory, Kolmogorov–Sinai entropy (also called metric entropy or measure-theoretic entropy) quantifies the rate at which a measure-preserving dynamical system produces information, generalizing Shannon entropy to trajectories of a system over time.

Definition

For a partition of the phase space, one computes the Shannon entropy of the refined partitions generated by iterating the dynamics, takes the rate per unit time, and then the supremum over all finite partitions. Andrey Kolmogorov introduced the invariant in 1958 and Yakov Sinai refined it in 1959.

Significance

The entropy is an isomorphism invariant of measure-preserving systems. Positive entropy signals chaotic behaviour; by Pesin's theorem it equals the sum of the positive Lyapunov exponents for smooth systems.
