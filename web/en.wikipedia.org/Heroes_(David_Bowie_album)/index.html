"Heroes" is the twelfth studio album by English singer-songwriter David Bowie, released on 14 October 1977 by RCA Records. It is the second instalment of Bowie's "Berlin Trilogy", recorded with producer Tony Visconti and musician Brian Eno at Hansa Tonstudio in West Berlin, within sight of the Berlin Wall.

Recording and style

The album blends art rock songs on its first side with ambient instrumental pieces on the second, continuing the experiments of its predecessor Low (1977) but with a somewhat more positive tone. Robert Fripp contributed distinctive guitar work, recorded in a single day. The title track, "'Heroes'", tells of two lovers meeting at the Berlin Wall; it was inspired by Bowie seeing Visconti embracing backing singer Antonia Maaß beside the Wall. Co-produced with Visconti and written with Eno, the song's swelling vocal performance was captured using a multi-latch microphone gating system set up across the studio.

Track listing

Side one: "Beauty and the Beast", "Joe the Lion", "'Heroes'", "Sons of the Silent Age", "Blackout". Side two: "V-2 Schneider", "Sense of Doubt", "Moss Garden", "Neuköln", "The Secret Life of Arabia".

Reception and legacy

Although it reached number 3 in the UK, "Heroes" was initially a modest commercial success, and the title track was not a hit single on release. In later decades the album and its title song came to be regarded as among Bowie's greatest works; "'Heroes'" grew into one of his signature songs, performed at Live Aid in 1985 and used widely in film and advertising. The album cover, photographed by Masayoshi Sukita, shows Bowie in a pose inspired by Erich Heckel's painting Roquairol.
