Dendroclimatology

Dendroclimatology is the science of determining past climates from trees, primarily from properties of the annual growth rings. Tree rings are wider when conditions favour growth and narrower when times are difficult.

Principles

By cross-dating ring-width series from living trees, dead wood and historic timbers, researchers build chronologies extending back centuries or millennia. Ring width, maximum latewood density and isotope ratios correlate with temperature and precipitation.

Applications

Dendroclimatology has reconstructed droughts, volcanic cooling events and long-term temperature trends, contributing to debates about the Medieval Climate Anomaly and the Little Ice Age. It is a branch of dendrochronology alongside dendroecology and dendroarchaeology.

Limitations

Growth is affected by many factors besides climate — age, competition, disturbance and carbon dioxide fertilisation — so careful standardisation is needed to extract the climatic signal.
