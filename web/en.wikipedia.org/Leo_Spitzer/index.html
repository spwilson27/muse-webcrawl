Leo Spitzer (7 February 1887 – 16 September 1960) was an Austrian Romance philologist and literary historian, widely regarded as a founder of modern stylistics. His method of moving from minute linguistic details of a text to its author's inner state of mind and world view — the so-called "philological circle" — influenced generations of literary scholars.

Life

Born in Vienna, Spitzer studied Romance philology under Wilhelm Meyer-Lübke and received his doctorate in 1910. He habilitated at Bonn and held chairs at Marburg and Cologne. Dismissed from his Cologne professorship by the Nazi regime in 1933 because of his Jewish ancestry, he emigrated to Istanbul, where he helped build the university's modern languages programme, and in 1936 moved to the United States, joining Johns Hopkins University in Baltimore, where he remained until his retirement.

Work

Spitzer's early work concerned word-formation, wordplay, and the language of advertising in French and Italian. His major contribution was a stylistics grounded in philology: beginning with a striking deviation from standard usage in a text — a "click" of intuition — the critic works outward to the writer's characteristic "etymon," the root mental pattern expressed in the style.

His books include Stilstudien (1928), Romanische Stil- und Literaturstudien (1931), and the English-language collection Linguistics and Literary History (1948), which introduced his method to anglophone readers. Together with Erich Auerbach, his Istanbul colleague, Spitzer exemplified the great tradition of German Romance scholarship in exile.
