The Oromo people (Oromo: Oromoo) are a Cushitic ethnic group native to the Horn of Africa, primarily inhabiting the Oromia Region of Ethiopia and northern Kenya. They are the largest single ethnic group in Ethiopia and in the Horn of Africa overall, numbering roughly 45 million.

History

The Oromo homeland is generally placed in the southern highlands of present-day Ethiopia and northern Kenya, around Lake Turkana. From the 16th century, Oromo groups expanded northward in a series of migrations that reshaped the Ethiopian region; some groups founded kingdoms such as the Gibe states, while others were incorporated into the Ethiopian Empire. In the late 19th century, under Menelik II, Oromo territories were conquered and incorporated into the modern Ethiopian state, a process accompanied by land expropriation that remains central to Oromo political memory. The Oromo language was long suppressed in public life under imperial and Derg governments; since 1991 Oromia has been a federal regional state.

Society and culture

Traditionally, Oromo society was organised by the Gadaa system, in which men pass through age grades every eight years, with elected leaders (Abbaa Gadaa) holding office for a single eight-year term — a system often cited as an indigenous African democratic institution. The traditional religion, Waaqeffanna, centres on the creator god Waaqa; today most Oromo are Muslims or Christians (Orthodox, Protestant or Catholic). The Irreechaa thanksgiving festival, held at Hora Arsedi lake in Bishoftu and in Addis Ababa, draws millions of participants.

Language

Oromo is an Afroasiatic Cushitic language, the fourth most widely spoken language in Africa. It is written in the Qubee Latin alphabet, adopted in 1991, and is the medium of instruction and administration in Oromia.
