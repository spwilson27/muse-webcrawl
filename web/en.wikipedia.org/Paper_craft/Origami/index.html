Origami

Origami is the Japanese art of paper folding, from oru ("to fold") and kami ("paper"). The goal is to transform a flat sheet into a finished sculpture through folding and sculpting techniques, traditionally without cuts or glue.

History

Paper folding developed in Japan after paper arrived from China in the 6th century; the traditional crane (orizuru) became its most famous model. In the 20th century Akira Yoshizawa systematised diagramming and popularised creative origami worldwide.

Mathematics and applications

Origami has a rich mathematical theory — including the Huzita–Hatori axioms — and inspires engineering designs such as airbags, stents and space-telescope solar arrays, notably the Miura fold.
