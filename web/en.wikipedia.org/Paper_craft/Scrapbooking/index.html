Scrapbooking

Scrapbooking
PurposePreserving and presenting photographs, memorabilia and family history
Typical materialsAlbums, cardstock, patterned paper, stickers, die-cuts, journaling

Scrapbooking is a method of preserving, presenting and arranging personal and family history in the form of a book, box or card. Typical memorabilia include photographs, printed media and artwork, arranged on decorated pages together with captions and journaling that record the stories behind the images.

History

Commonplace books and friendship albums were kept in Europe from the 15th century onward. In the 19th century, printed "scraps" — colourful die-cut images sold for pasting into albums — gave the hobby its name. Mark Twain patented a self-pasting scrapbook in 1873. The modern craft took off in the United States in the 1980s and 1990s, when acid-free, lignin-free materials made long-term photo preservation possible and a large retail industry grew up around decorative papers and embellishments.

Digital scrapbooking

Digital scrapbooking uses graphics software to assemble pages from scanned photographs and digital embellishments, which may then be printed as photo books or shared online. It has become increasingly popular as printing costs have fallen.
