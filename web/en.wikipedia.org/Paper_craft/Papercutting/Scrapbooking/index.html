Scrapbooking

Scrapbooking is a method of preserving, presenting, and arranging personal and family history in the form of a book, box, or card. Typical memorabilia include photographs, printed media, and artwork. Scrapbook albums are often decorated and contain extensive journal entries or written descriptions. Scrapbooking as a hobby grew out of the commonplace books and albums of printed clippings kept since the 19th century.

History

Keeping scraps of printed material in albums became popular in the 19th century, aided by chromolithography and mass printing; Mark Twain even patented a self-pasting scrapbook in 1873. The modern hobby took off in the 1980s–1990s, particularly in the United States, when companies such as Creative Memories promoted archival-quality, photo-safe materials and home-based scrapbooking parties. At its peak in the early 2000s, the industry was estimated at over two billion dollars annually in the US.

Materials and techniques

Scrapbookers emphasise preservation: acid-free and lignin-free paper, page protectors, and safe adhesives to slow photo deterioration. Layouts combine photos with patterned paper, stickers, die-cuts, stamps, ribbons, and journaling. Popular formats include 12×12 inch and 8.5×11 inch pages. Digital scrapbooking, using graphics software and printable or online albums, emerged in the 2000s and now coexists with traditional paper crafting.
