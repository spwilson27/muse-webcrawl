Papercutting

Papercutting
MediumPaper, scissors or craft knife
OriginsChina, 4th–6th century
Related artsOrigami, silhouette portraiture

Papercutting or paper cutting is the art of cutting designs from paper using scissors or a sharp knife. It is one of the oldest decorative folk arts, with surviving traditions on every continent. Designs range from simple snowflake-like folds to elaborate multi-layered scenes depicting landscapes, animals, figures and lettering.

History

Paper was invented in China during the Han dynasty, traditionally credited to Cai Lun in 105 CE. Cut-paper decoration followed soon after; the oldest surviving fragment, a symmetrical circle cut from paper, dates to the sixth century and was found in Xinjiang. Chinese papercuts became associated with festivals, weddings and the Lunar New Year, when red paper designs are pasted on windows and doors for luck and prosperity.

The art travelled along trading routes to Central Asia, the Middle East and Europe. In Jewish tradition, intricate papercuts decorated marriage contracts and festival walls. German and Swiss immigrants carried Scherenschnitte to Pennsylvania, where it became a hallmark of Pennsylvania Dutch folk art, often combined with Fraktur calligraphy. Polish wycinanki, Mexican papel picado and Japanese kirigami are other well-known regional forms.

Techniques

Artists may work freehand or transfer a drawn design onto the paper before cutting. Folded cutting produces radial or bilateral symmetry, while layered cutting builds colour by stacking sheets. Modern practitioners also use laser cutters, but hand-cut work remains prized for its delicacy.
