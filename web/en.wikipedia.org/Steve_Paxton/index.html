Steve Paxton

BornFebruary 21, 1939, Phoenix, Arizona, U.S.
DiedFebruary 20, 2024 (aged 84)
OccupationDancer, choreographer
Known forContact Improvisation

Steve Paxton (February 21, 1939 – February 20, 2024) was an American dancer and choreographer best known as the developer of Contact Improvisation, a form of dance improvisation based on shared weight, rolling, falling, and the physical laws of momentum between moving bodies.

Career

Paxton trained in gymnastics and the martial art aikido, whose rolling and falling techniques profoundly influenced his dance practice. In the 1960s he danced with José Limón and Merce Cunningham, and became a founding member of the experimental Judson Dance Theater in New York, where he created works such as Proxy (1961) and Flat (1964) that treated ordinary movement as dance. He was also a founding member of the improvisational group Grand Union.

Contact Improvisation

In 1972, working with students at Oberlin College, Paxton developed Contact Improvisation out of explorations of the "small dance" — the subtle postural adjustments of the standing body — and partnered work drawing on aikido and wrestling. The form spread worldwide through workshops, jams, and the journal Contact Quarterly, which Paxton co-founded. His later research included the "Material for the Spine" system, analyzing the mechanics of the back and pelvis in movement.

Later life

Paxton lived for many years in Vermont, where he also farmed and wrote about dance. He received numerous honors, including a Bessie Award and recognition from the French Ministry of Culture. He died in February 2024, one day before his 85th birthday.
