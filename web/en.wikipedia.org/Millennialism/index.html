Millennialism

Millennialism (or chiliasm, from the Greek equivalent) is the belief that, before or after the Second Coming of Christ, there will be a Golden Age — the Millennium — in which Christ and his saints will reign on earth for a thousand years. The doctrine is based principally on Revelation 20:1–6, which describes Satan bound for a thousand years while the martyrs reign with Christ.

Millennialism is a specifically Christian eschatological doctrine; the broader comparative concept covering analogous movements in other cultures is millenarianism.

Varieties

Interpreters have divided over when the Millennium occurs relative to Christ's return and whether it is literal or symbolic:

Premillennialism holds that Christ will physically return before the Millennium and establish a literal thousand-year reign. It was common in the early church and was revived in the nineteenth century, becoming central to dispensationalism and to much modern evangelicalism, often combined with belief in a rapture of believers before a period of tribulation.

Postmillennialism holds that Christ will return after a long age in which the gospel gradually transforms the world — the Millennium being the triumph of Christian ethics rather than a literal reign beginning with the parousia. It flourished in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries and motivated many social-reform movements.

Amillennialism interprets the thousand years symbolically, as the present age of the church between Christ's first and second comings; it has been the dominant view in Roman Catholic, Orthodox and many mainline Protestant traditions since Augustine of Hippo argued for it in The City of God.

History

Early church writers such as Papias, Justin Martyr and Irenaeus expected a literal earthly kingdom. Expectation of an imminent end repeatedly surfaced in later centuries — around the year 1000, among the radical reformers of the sixteenth century, and in seventeenth-century England. In the nineteenth-century United States, William Miller's prediction of Christ's return in 1843–44 — and its failure, the "Great Disappointment" — gave rise to Adventist denominations including the Seventh-day Adventist Church. Jehovah's Witnesses likewise teach that Christ's thousand-year reign will follow Armageddon.

Influence

Beyond theology, millennial expectation has shaped political movements, communal experiments and popular culture, and the turn of each millennium (1000 and 2000 CE) has been accompanied by public speculation about the end of the age.
