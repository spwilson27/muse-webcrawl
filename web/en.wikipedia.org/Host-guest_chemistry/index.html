Host–guest chemistry

Host–guest chemistry is a branch of supramolecular chemistry in which a molecular host binds a guest molecule or ion through non-covalent interactions, forming a host–guest complex. Binding is typically reversible and selective, driven by a combination of hydrogen bonding, hydrophobic effects, van der Waals forces, and electrostatic interactions.

Hosts and guests

Important host molecules include crown ethers, cryptands, cyclodextrins, calixarenes, cucurbiturils, and cyclophanes. Crown ethers, discovered by Charles J. Pedersen, selectively bind alkali metal cations according to the match between cavity size and ion radius. The field's foundational work was recognised by the 1987 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, awarded to Pedersen, Donald J. Cram, and Jean-Marie Lehn.

Applications

Host–guest systems are used in chemical sensors, drug delivery (for example cyclodextrin formulations that solubilise drugs), catalysis, separations, and molecular machines.
