Income distribution

Income distribution describes how a country's total income is distributed amongst its population. It is a central topic in economics and social policy and is closely related to, but distinct from, the distribution of wealth.

Measurement

Common measures include the Gini coefficient, which summarises inequality in a single number between 0 (perfect equality) and 1, and the Lorenz curve, which plots the cumulative share of income against the cumulative share of population. Shares accruing to each quintile or decile are also widely reported.

Factors

Income distribution is shaped by education, technology, globalisation, labour-market institutions, tax and transfer systems and the distribution of asset ownership.

Trends

Within many advanced economies inequality rose from the 1980s onward, while global inequality between countries has tended to fall as large developing economies have grown.
