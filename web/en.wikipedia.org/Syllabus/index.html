Syllabus

Syllabus
Type: Educational document
Purpose: Course outline and expectations
Contents: Topics, schedule, assessment, policies

A syllabus (plural syllabuses or syllabi) is a document that communicates information about an academic course or class and defines expectations and responsibilities. It is generally prepared by the instructor and given to students at the start of a term.

Contents

A typical syllabus lists course objectives and learning outcomes, a schedule of topics and readings, required materials, assignment and examination dates, grading criteria, and policies on attendance, academic integrity, and accommodations. In some contexts — for example in school systems in South Asia — "syllabus" refers to the entire prescribed curriculum for a subject or examination board rather than a single teacher's course plan.

Function

The syllabus serves as a contract-like reference between teacher and students, a planning tool for the instructor, and evidence of course content for accreditation and transfer-credit decisions. The word derives from a Latin misreading of a Greek term for a parchment label or table of contents.
