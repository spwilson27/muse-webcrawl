Georges J. F. Köhler

Georges Jean Franz Köhler (17 April 1946 – 1 March 1995) was a German biologist. Together with César Milstein, he shared the 1984 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with Niels Kaj Jerne, "for theories concerning the specificity in development and control of the immune system and the discovery of the principle for production of monoclonal antibodies".

Work

As a postdoctoral researcher in Milstein's laboratory in Cambridge, Köhler developed the hybridoma technique in 1975: fusing antibody-producing B cells with myeloma cells to create immortal cell lines secreting monoclonal antibodies of a single specificity. The technique revolutionized diagnostics, research, and therapeutics. Köhler later directed the Max Planck Institute of Immunobiology in Freiburg.
