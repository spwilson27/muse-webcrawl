Diepkloof Rock Shelter

Diepkloof Rock Shelter is an archaeological site in the Western Cape province of South Africa, overlooking the Verlorenvlei River about 180 km north of Cape Town. It contains one of the longest and richest Middle Stone Age sequences in southern Africa.

Archaeology

Excavations have revealed deposits spanning roughly 130,000 to 45,000 years ago, including assemblages associated with the pre-Still Bay, Still Bay and Howiesons Poort industries.

The site is famous for fragments of ostrich eggshell engraved with geometric patterns — cross-hatched and parallel-line motifs — dated to around 60,000 years ago. These are among the earliest evidence of symbolic communication and graphic design by early modern humans, suggesting the eggshells were used as marked containers for water or other goods.

Significance

Together with sites such as Blombos Cave, Diepkloof provides key evidence that anatomically modern humans in southern Africa developed complex symbolic behaviour tens of thousands of years earlier than once believed. In 2024 the site was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as part of the "Pleistocene Occupation Sites of South Africa" listing.
