TKS (spacecraft)

TKS
Type: Crewed / cargo spacecraft
Country: Soviet Union
First flight: 1977 (uncrewed)
Launch vehicle: Proton
Design bureau: TsKBM (Chelomey)

The TKS spacecraft (Russian: Транспортный корабль снабжения, Transportnyi Korabl Snabzheniya, "Transport Supply Spacecraft") was a Soviet spacecraft conceived in the late 1960s by Vladimir Chelomey's design bureau as a large supply and crew vehicle for the military Almaz space stations. At nearly 18 tonnes, it was far larger than the Soyuz it was meant to complement.

The TKS consisted of a functional cargo block (FGB) joined to a VA capsule, a reusable conical crew return vehicle that had been developed for Almaz. Although the TKS was flown several times from 1977 onwards, it never carried a crew; its flights were made uncrewed, docked to Salyut stations as heavy supply ships.

Legacy

The design had a lasting afterlife: TKS-derived FGB modules formed the basis of several Mir space station modules (Kvant-2, Kristall, Spektr and Priroda), and the Zarya module of the International Space Station was likewise derived from the TKS functional cargo block.

Flights

Free-flying TKS missions flew under the designations Kosmos 929 (1977), Kosmos 1267 (1981, docked to Salyut 6), Kosmos 1443 (1983, Salyut 7) and Kosmos 1686 (1985, Salyut 7). The VA capsules returned cargo and film to Earth, demonstrating systems intended for crews.
