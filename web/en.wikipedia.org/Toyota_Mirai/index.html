The Toyota Mirai (Japanese for "future") is a mid-size hydrogen fuel cell electric vehicle (FCEV) manufactured by Toyota, first launched in Japan in December 2014. It was one of the first hydrogen fuel cell vehicles to be sold commercially to retail customers, alongside the Hyundai Nexo and the Honda Clarity Fuel Cell.

Technology

The Mirai stores compressed hydrogen in carbon-fibre tanks at 700 bar. In the fuel cell stack, hydrogen reacts with oxygen from the air to generate electricity, which powers an electric motor; the only tailpipe emission is water vapour. A small battery buffers power for acceleration and regenerative braking.

Generations

The first-generation Mirai (2014–2020) was a front-wheel-drive sedan with a range of about 500 km (312 miles EPA). The second generation, launched for 2021, moved to Toyota's rear-wheel-drive GA-L platform shared with the Lexus LS, with sleeker coupe-like styling, increased range of up to about 650 km, and five seats.

Market

Sales have been limited by the scarcity of hydrogen refuelling infrastructure, concentrated in Japan, California and parts of Europe. The Mirai served as an official vehicle of the Tokyo 2020 Olympic Games, reflecting Toyota's promotion of a "hydrogen society".
