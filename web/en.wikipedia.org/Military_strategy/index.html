Military strategy

Military strategy is a set of ideas implemented by military organizations to pursue desired strategic goals. Derived from the Greek strategos ("general"), it deals with the planning and conduct of campaigns, the movement and disposition of forces, and the deception of the enemy.

Levels of war

Strategy sits between national policy (grand strategy) and tactics, the conduct of individual engagements. The intermediate operational level links strategic aims to tactical actions within a theatre of war.

Principles

Classical theorists such as Sun Tzu, Carl von Clausewitz, Antoine-Henri Jomini, and Basil Liddell Hart emphasized principles including concentration of force, economy of effort, surprise, and the political nature of war — Clausewitz famously called war "the continuation of politics by other means".

Modern strategy

Modern strategy encompasses nuclear deterrence, sea, air, space, and cyber power, joint operations, and coalition warfare, and is formulated in national strategy documents by general staffs and defense ministries.
