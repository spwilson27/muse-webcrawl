Wernicke's area

Wernicke's area is one of the two parts of the cerebral cortex traditionally linked to language, the other being Broca's area. Located in the posterior part of the superior temporal gyrus in the (usually left) dominant hemisphere — roughly Brodmann area 22 — it is involved in the comprehension of written and spoken language.

Function

The area is named after the German neurologist Carl Wernicke, who in 1874 described patients whose speech remained fluent but incomprehensible following damage to this region, a condition now called Wernicke's aphasia (receptive aphasia). Such patients produce fluent but often meaningless speech and have severe difficulty understanding language.

Modern view

Modern neuroimaging has refined the classical Wernicke–Geschwind model: language comprehension involves a broader network across the temporal and parietal lobes, and the precise boundaries and role of "Wernicke's area" remain debated. It is connected to Broca's area by the arcuate fasciculus.
