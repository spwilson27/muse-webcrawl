Linji school

The Linji school (Chinese: 臨濟宗) is a school of Chan Buddhism founded by the Tang-dynasty master Linji Yixuan (d. 866/867). Together with the Caodong school it became one of the two surviving and most influential houses of Chan; in Japan it is known as the Rinzai school of Zen Buddhism.

Characteristics

Linji teaching emphasises sudden awakening and direct, often forceful master–student interaction: shouts, blows, and the kōan (gong'an) method, in which students meditate on paradoxical cases to break through conceptual thinking. In the Song dynasty, Dahui Zonggao systematised "kanhua Chan" — contemplation of the critical phrase (huatou) of a kōan — which became characteristic of the school.

History

The school flourished through the Five Dynasties and Song periods. During the Kamakura period, the monk Eisai transmitted Linji (Rinzai) Zen to Japan, where it patronised by the shogunate and associated with the samurai culture and arts. In modern times, Chinese, Korean (as part of the Jogye order), Vietnamese, and Japanese lineages all trace descent through Linji.
