Sulfide mineral

The sulfide minerals are a class of minerals containing sulfide (S2−) or disulfide (S22−) as the major anion. Some sulfide minerals are economically important as metal ores. The sulfide class also includes the selenides, tellurides, arsenides, antimonides, bismuthinides, sulfarsenides, and sulfosalts.

Properties

Sulfide minerals generally have a metallic or sub-metallic lustre, high density, and moderate hardness, and are opaque in thin section, though some, such as sphalerite and cinnabar, can be translucent. Many are semiconductors. Because sulfur forms covalent bonds with many metals, sulfide structures differ markedly from ionic oxide structures.

Important examples

  • Pyrite (FeS2) — "fool's gold"
  • Chalcopyrite (CuFeS2) — the most important copper ore
  • Galena (PbS) — the chief ore of lead
  • Sphalerite (ZnS) — the chief ore of zinc
  • Cinnabar (HgS) — the chief ore of mercury
  • Pentlandite — an important nickel ore
  • Molybdenite (MoS2) — the chief ore of molybdenum

Occurrence and weathering

Sulfides form in igneous, sedimentary, and hydrothermal environments; many of the world's great ore deposits are hydrothermal sulfides. Exposed to air and water, sulfides oxidise readily, producing acidic, metal-rich drainage — acid mine drainage — a major environmental problem at mine sites. The oxidation of pyrite in massive sulfide deposits can form colourful secondary minerals and gossans at the surface.
