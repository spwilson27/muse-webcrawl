African forest elephant

Scientific nameLoxodonta cyclotis
HabitatRainforests of Central and West Africa
StatusCritically Endangered (IUCN)

The African forest elephant (Loxodonta cyclotis) is one of two living species of African elephant. Native to the humid rainforests of West Africa and the Congo Basin, it is smaller than the African bush elephant, with a shoulder height of about 2.4–3 metres, rounder ears, and straighter, downward-pointing tusks with harder, pinker ivory.

Ecology

Forest elephants are important seed dispersers for many rainforest trees and maintain forest clearings called bais. They live in smaller, looser family groups than savanna elephants and communicate with low-frequency rumbles.

Conservation

Long treated as a subspecies, it was recognized as a distinct species following genetic studies in the 2000s–2010s. Populations fell by an estimated 62% between 2002 and 2011 due to ivory poaching and habitat loss, and the species was listed as Critically Endangered in 2021.
