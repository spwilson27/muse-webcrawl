Substitution effect

Substitution effect
FieldMicroeconomics, consumer theory
Companion conceptIncome effect
Key formalismsSlutsky equation; Hicksian decomposition

The substitution effect is the change in consumption of a good associated with a change in its price, holding the consumer's level of utility constant. When the price of a good falls, consumers substitute toward that good and away from relatively more expensive alternatives; when its price rises, they substitute away from it. The substitution effect is always negative with respect to price: a price increase reduces the quantity demanded through this channel alone.

Decomposition of price changes

Economists decompose the total effect of a price change into the substitution effect and the income effect (the change in consumption caused by the change in real purchasing power). Two classical approaches exist:

  • The Hicksian decomposition holds utility constant, compensating the consumer to remain on the original indifference curve.
  • The Slutsky decomposition holds purchasing power constant, compensating the consumer enough to afford the original bundle.

The relationship is summarised in the Slutsky equation, which expresses the total price derivative of demand as the substitution term minus the income term scaled by the quantity consumed.

Giffen goods

For normal goods, the substitution and income effects reinforce each other. For inferior goods they oppose; in the rare case of a Giffen good, the income effect outweighs the substitution effect, so a price rise increases quantity demanded. Substitution with a cross-price change between substitutes and complements is measured by cross-price elasticity of demand.

Applications

The substitution effect underlies analysis of labour supply (substituting leisure for work as wages change), taxation (distortions as consumers substitute away from taxed goods), and the construction of cost-of-living indices, where substitution bias arises when a fixed basket fails to account for consumers switching to cheaper alternatives.
