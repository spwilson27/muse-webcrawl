EMV

EMV is a payment method based on a technical standard for smart payment cards and the terminals and automated teller machines that accept them. EMV stands for "Europay, Mastercard, Visa", the three companies that created the standard. EMV cards store data on integrated circuit chips instead of, or in addition to, magnetic stripes.

How it works

During a transaction the chip creates a unique cryptogram for each purchase, making skimmed card data far less useful to fraudsters than static magnetic-stripe data. Cardholder verification may be by PIN ("chip and PIN", standard in most of Europe) or signature. EMV also underpins contactless payments via near-field communication, where the same cryptography is used over a radio link.

Adoption

The standard is managed by EMVCo, owned by the major card networks. Europe migrated in the 2000s under liability-shift rules that moved fraud costs to the party with weaker technology; the United States adopted a liability shift in October 2015, accelerating the replacement of swipe-only terminals.

Security

Chip adoption sharply reduced counterfeit card fraud in face-to-face transactions, though fraud partly migrated to card-not-present (online) channels. Known attacks, including pre-play and relay attacks, have led to continuing refinements of the specification.
