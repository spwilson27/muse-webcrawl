Ancient Greek philosophy

Ancient Greek philosophy arose in the Greek-speaking world of the 6th century BCE and continued through the Hellenistic period and into the Roman Empire. Beginning with the Pre-Socratic thinkers of Ionia, who sought natural explanations of the cosmos, it developed through Socrates, Plato, and Aristotle into the foundational traditions of Western philosophy, encompassing metaphysics, ethics, logic, politics, and natural science.

Periods

The Pre-Socratics — including Thales, Heraclitus, Parmenides, Democritus, and Pythagoras — debated the fundamental nature of reality. The classical period centred on Athens, where Socrates' dialectical method, Plato's theory of Forms and Academy, and Aristotle's Lyceum and system of logic set the agenda for centuries. Hellenistic schools — Stoicism, Epicureanism, Skepticism, and Cynicism — emphasized ethics and the pursuit of tranquillity.

Legacy

Transmitted through Roman, Byzantine, and Islamic scholarship, Greek philosophy shaped medieval scholasticism and the Renaissance and remains the starting point of the Western philosophical canon. Later movements such as Neoplatonism, founded by Plotinus, influenced Christian and Islamic theology profoundly.
