Pâté

Pâté (French for "paste") is a paste, pie or loaf of finely or coarsely ground seasoned meat, offal, fish or vegetables, often combined with fat, herbs and spirits such as cognac. It is usually served cold or at room temperature, sliced, as a first course or spread.

Varieties

Pâté en croûte is baked in a pastry crust; pâté en terrine is cooked in a mould and unmoulded. Pâté de campagne is a rustic country-style preparation, typically of pork and liver. The most luxurious form is pâté de foie gras, made from the fattened liver of a goose or duck, whose production is controversial on animal-welfare grounds and banned in some jurisdictions. Related preparations exist across Europe — German Leberwurst, Scandinavian leverpostej — and in Vietnamese cuisine, where a pork pâté is a key filling of the bánh mì sandwich, a legacy of French colonial cooking.
