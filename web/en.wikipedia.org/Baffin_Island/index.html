Baffin Island

Baffin Island (Inuktitut: ᕿᑭᖅᑖᓗᒃ, Qikiqtaaluk), in the Canadian territory of Nunavut, is the largest island in Canada, the second-largest island in the Americas (behind Greenland), and the fifth-largest island in the world. Its area is 507,451 km² (195,928 sq mi) with a population density of 0.03/km²; the population was 13,039 according to the 2021 Canadian census. It is also home to the capital of Nunavut, Iqaluit (population 7,429 in 2021).

History

Baffin Island has been inhabited for at least 3,000 years, first by the pre-Inuit Dorset culture and later by the Thule people, ancestors of the modern Inuit. Norse explorers likely reached the island around 1000 CE. The island was visited by Martin Frobisher in the 1570s and named for the English navigator William Baffin, who charted surrounding waters in 1616. The Royal Canadian Mounted Police post and later the DEW Line stations brought permanent non-Inuit settlement in the 20th century.

Geography

The island is deeply indented by fjords; Frobisher Bay cuts into its southeastern end, and Foxe Basin lies to the west. The Cumberland Sound and Davis Strait separate it from Greenland. The eastern coast is mountainous, forming part of the Arctic Cordillera, with Mount Odin (2,147 m) the highest point. The Barnes Ice Cap, a remnant of the Laurentide Ice Sheet, lies in the interior.

Fauna

Wildlife includes polar bears, caribou, Arctic wolves, Arctic foxes, and lemmings; the waters harbour narwhals, belugas, bowhead whales, and seals. Auyuittuq National Park protects dramatic glacial scenery on the Cumberland Peninsula.

Settlements

Communities include Iqaluit, Pangnirtung, Kinngait (Cape Dorset), Pond Inlet, Clyde River, Arctic Bay, Qikiqtarjuaq, Kimmirut, and Iglulik. The Mary River iron mine, roughly north of Pond Inlet, is one of the world's richest iron ore deposits.
