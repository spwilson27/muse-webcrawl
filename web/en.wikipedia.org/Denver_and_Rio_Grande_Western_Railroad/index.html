The Denver and Rio Grande Western Railroad (reporting mark DRGW), often shortened to the Rio Grande, was an American Class I railroad company. Founded in 1870 as the Denver & Rio Grande Railway, it built a famed network of mountain routes through the Colorado Rockies and the Wasatch Range in Utah, operating under the motto "Through the Rockies, not around them."

History

General William Jackson Palmer founded the line to build south from Denver toward Mexico City, but financial struggles and competition with the Atchison, Topeka and Santa Fe Railway led it instead to turn west, reaching Salt Lake City in 1883 via the Royal Gorge, Marshall Pass and the Black Canyon of the Gunnison. The railroad began as a narrow-gauge system and gradually converted its main lines to standard gauge.

Routes

Its best-known routes included the line over Tennessee Pass, the Moffat Tunnel route under the Continental Divide, and the route through Glenwood Canyon. The Rio Grande operated the famous passenger train California Zephyr in partnership with other railroads.

Decline and merger

In 1988 the Rio Grande's parent company purchased the Southern Pacific Railroad and merged the two under the Southern Pacific name; in 1996 the combined system was acquired by Union Pacific.
