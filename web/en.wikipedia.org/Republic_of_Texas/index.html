Republic of Texas

Existed: 2 March 1836 – 29 December 1845
Capital: Houston (1837–39); Austin (1839–45)
Presidents: Sam Houston; Mirabeau B. Lamar; Anson Jones
Currency: Texas dollar

The Republic of Texas (Spanish: República de Tejas) was a sovereign state in North America that existed from March 2, 1836, to December 29, 1845. It bordered Mexico to the west and southwest, the Gulf of Mexico to the southeast, and the United States to the east and northeast. Formed as a breakaway republic from Mexico during the Texas Revolution, it claimed a vast territory including present-day Texas and parts of New Mexico, Oklahoma, Kansas, Colorado, and Wyoming, though its effective control was far smaller.

The Republic of Texas was an independent nation for nearly ten years.

History

Texas declared independence from Mexico on 2 March 1836, amid the Texas Revolution; after the Texian defeat at the Alamo and the massacre at Goliad, Sam Houston's army destroyed Santa Anna's force at the Battle of San Jacinto on 21 April 1836, securing de facto independence. The United States recognized the republic in 1837, followed by France, Britain, and other powers, though Mexico never did and periodically invaded, briefly recapturing San Antonio in 1842. The republic struggled with public debt, conflicts with Native American nations — including Lamar's aggressive expulsion of the Cherokee and wars against the Comanche, culminating in the Council House Fight and Great Raid of 1840 — and a small navy that campaigned off the Yucatán coast.

Annexation

Annexation to the United States was the central political question of the republic's existence. After a failed treaty in 1844, a joint resolution of the US Congress in 1845 offered annexation on terms allowing Texas to retain its public lands and divide into up to five states. A Texas convention accepted, and Texas became the 28th US state on 29 December 1845 — a step that helped precipitate the Mexican–American War the following year.
