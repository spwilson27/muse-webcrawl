Ruthenian language

A page from a Ruthenian chancery document.

Ruthenian (also called Old Belarusian or Old Ukrainian in some contexts) was the written East Slavic language used in the Grand Duchy of Lithuania and later the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth from the 14th to the 18th centuries. It served as a chancery and legal language of the Grand Duchy and was used in its Statutes of 1529, 1566 and 1588.

Characteristics and decline

Ruthenian developed from Old East Slavic with strong influences from local Belarusian and Ukrainian dialects, Polish and Church Slavonic. Written in Cyrillic, it is regarded as the historical predecessor of modern Belarusian and, in its southern varieties, Ukrainian. After the Union of Lublin (1569) and the growing Polonisation of the Commonwealth's elite, Ruthenian was progressively displaced by Polish; in 1696–1697 its use in official documents of the Commonwealth was formally abolished.
