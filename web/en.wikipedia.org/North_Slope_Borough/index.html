The North Slope Borough is the northernmost borough in the United States state of Alaska and, by area, the largest county or county-equivalent in the country, covering 94,796 square miles — larger than the state of Utah and comparable in size to the United Kingdom. Its borough seat is Utqiagvik, formerly known as Barrow, the northernmost city in the United States.

Geography

The borough occupies the Alaska North Slope, the region draining north from the Brooks Range to the Arctic Ocean. It is bordered by the Chukchi Sea to the west and the Beaufort Sea to the north and east. It includes Point Hope, Point Lay, Wainwright, Atqasuk, Utqiagvik, Prudhoe Bay, Deadhorse, Kaktovik, Nuiqsut and Anaktuvuk Pass. Much of the National Petroleum Reserve–Alaska and part of the Arctic National Wildlife Refuge lie within it.

History and government

The borough was incorporated in 1972, shortly after the discovery of oil at Prudhoe Bay in 1968, in large part so that the predominantly Iñupiat population could tax oil infrastructure and fund local services. Oil revenues transformed the region, financing water and sewer systems, schools, clinics and roads in villages that had previously lacked them.

Demographics

The population at the 2020 census was 11,031. A majority of residents are Iñupiat Alaska Natives, and the Iñupiaq language remains widely spoken. The workforce is swelled by thousands of non-resident oilfield workers at Prudhoe Bay, who are not counted as borough residents.
