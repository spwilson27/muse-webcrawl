The Politburo Standing Committee (PSC), officially the Standing Committee of the Central Political Bureau of the Communist Party of China, is a committee consisting of the top leadership of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP). It is the most powerful decision-making body in China, whose members simultaneously hold the senior offices of the state.

Composition and role

Historically the PSC has had five to eleven members; since the 18th Party Congress in 2012 it has had seven. Its members are formally elected by the Central Committee at the first plenum following a Party Congress, though in practice the leadership line-up is settled beforehand through consultation among incumbents and retired elders. The committee is headed by the CCP General Secretary, who also chairs the Central Military Commission and serves as President of the People's Republic of China; other members typically include the Premier, the chair of the National People's Congress Standing Committee, the chair of the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference, the secretary of the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection, and the executive secretary of the Central Secretariat.

Functioning

The PSC meets regularly, traditionally weekly, and rules by consensus, though its internal deliberations are secret. Decisions taken by the committee direct the State Council, the legislature and the judiciary, since China is a party-state in which the CCP stands above state institutions. Membership is governed by informal norms including retirement-age conventions, which have been applied inconsistently in recent years.
