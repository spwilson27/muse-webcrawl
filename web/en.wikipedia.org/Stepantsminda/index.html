Country: Georgia
Region: Mtskheta-Mtianeti
Elevation: about 1,740 m
Former name: Kazbegi (1925–2007)

Stepantsminda (Georgian: სტეფანწმინდა) is a small town in the Mtskheta-Mtianeti region of north-eastern Georgia. Historically and ethnographically part of the Khevi province, it lies in the Greater Caucasus mountains along the Georgian Military Road, about 157 km north of Tbilisi, at an elevation of around 1,740 metres (5,710 ft). The town was known as Kazbegi from 1925 until 2007.

Landmarks

Stepantsminda is famous for its scenic location beneath Mount Kazbek (5,047 m), one of the highest peaks of the Caucasus. Above the town, at 2,170 m, stands the 14th-century Gergeti Trinity Church, whose isolated silhouette against the mountain has become one of the most photographed views in Georgia. The town is a base for mountaineering, trekking to the Gergeti Glacier, and trips toward the Darial Gorge and the Russian border.

Name

The name means "Saint Stephen", after a Georgian Orthodox monk who, according to tradition, built a hermitage on the site. The Soviet-era name Kazbegi honoured Alexander Kazbegi, a Georgian writer born in the town; the original name was officially restored in 2007. The Alexander Kazbegi Museum occupies the writer's family house in the town.
