Cellulose fiber
Material: Cellulose
Sources: Wood pulp, cotton, plants
Uses: Insulation, textiles, paper

Cellulose fibers are fibers made with ethers or esters of cellulose obtained from bark, wood, leaves or other plant material. In building contexts, cellulose fiber insulation is made from recycled paper treated with borates for fire and pest resistance.

Types

Natural cellulose fibers include cotton, flax, hemp and jute. Regenerated fibers such as rayon, modal and lyocell are produced by dissolving pulp and spinning new filaments. Acetate and triacetate are cellulose esters.

Applications

Beyond textiles and paper, cellulose fiber is blown into walls and attics as insulation, used in composites, filtration and hygiene products, and valued for absorbency and biodegradability.
