California Master Plan for Higher Education

The California Master Plan for Higher Education of 1960 is the principal document guiding California's public higher education system. Drafted under University of California president Clark Kerr, it was enacted as the Donahoe Higher Education Act.

Three segments

The plan differentiated three public systems: the University of California (research university drawing from the top eighth of graduates, exclusive right to doctoral degrees), the California State University (top third, bachelor's and master's degrees), and the California Community Colleges (open to all able to benefit, transfer and vocational education).

Principles

It promised a tuition-free place for every qualified Californian, transfer pathways from community colleges, and governance by separate boards. Fees later eroded the tuition-free ideal, though resident tuition long remained low.

Legacy

The plan became a model worldwide for mass public higher education, though it has been criticised for rigidity and for eligibility pools that no longer reflect state demographics.
