Jerk pork

Jerk pork is a Jamaican dish in which pork is seasoned with jerk spice and slow-cooked over pimento wood. Pork is regarded by many as the original jerk meat: the Maroons of Jamaica's Cockpit Country and Blue Mountains traditionally jerked wild boar, an abundant game animal in the island's interior.


Jerk pork over pimento wood

History

The Taíno people cooked meat on wooden frames over slow fires. When African Maroons escaped into the mountains, they adopted and refined the technique, seasoning wild boar with peppers, allspice (pimento) and salt, and cooking it in earth pits so the smoke would not reveal their location. This method of preserving and flavouring pork became the foundation of jerk cooking.

Preparation

Pork shoulder or leg is scored and rubbed with a marinade of Scotch bonnet peppers, allspice berries, thyme, scallions, garlic and other seasonings, then left to absorb the flavours for several hours or overnight. It is grilled slowly over pimento wood until tender and smoky, then chopped and served — often with festival dumplings, hard-dough bread or rice and peas.
