Microstate

A microstate or ministate is a sovereign state having a very small population or very small land area, usually both. However, the meanings of "state" and "very small" are not well-defined in international law. Some scholars accept the definition used by the Commonwealth Secretariat and the European Union, which limits microstates to countries with a population of no more than about one million people.

Examples

Europe has several historic microstates. Monaco, on the French Riviera, covers about 2 km², while Liechtenstein and San Marino are landlocked enclaves in the Alps and Apennines respectively. Vatican City, an enclave within Rome, is the smallest state in the world by both area and population. In Oceania, island nations such as Nauru — the world's smallest island nation at 21 km² — and Tuvalu are classified as microstates.

Characteristics

Microstates often face distinctive constraints: limited natural resources, small domestic markets, vulnerability to economic shocks and natural disasters, and limited administrative capacity. Many rely on niche strategies such as tourism, offshore finance, selling citizenship or passports, issuing postage stamps and coins for collectors, or licensing their internet country-code domains. Tuvalu, for example, earns significant revenue from its .tv domain.

International relations

Despite their size, microstates are sovereign equals in the United Nations General Assembly, where each member state has one vote. This gives microstates influence disproportionate to their size and has at times made their votes and diplomatic recognition valuable to larger powers. Many microstates delegate defence and some administrative functions to larger neighbours through free-association or treaty arrangements.

Debates over viability

Small states were long considered unviable, and the wave of decolonisation after the Second World War produced debate over minimum sizes for statehood. In practice, microstates have generally prospered, and several — including Singapore, sometimes classified as a microstate by population-era definitions — rank among the world's wealthiest countries per capita.
