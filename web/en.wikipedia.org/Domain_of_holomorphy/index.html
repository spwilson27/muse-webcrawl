Domain of holomorphy

In mathematics, in the area of complex analysis, a domain of holomorphy is a domain, i.e. an open connected subset of the n-dimensional complex space (complex coordinate space) Cn, which is the natural domain of a maximally extended holomorphic function. Informally, a domain D is a domain of holomorphy if there exists a holomorphic function on D which cannot be analytically continued to any strictly larger domain containing D.

Definition

Let D be an open subset of Cn. D is a domain of holomorphy if there does not exist a nonempty open set U in Cn and an open set V in D such that U is not contained in D, U is connected to D through V, and for every holomorphic function f on D there exists a holomorphic function g on U that agrees with f on V.

Equivalently, D is a domain of holomorphy if there is a holomorphic function on D that cannot be holomorphically extended to a larger domain.

One variable versus several variables

In one complex variable the situation is simple: every open subset of C is a domain of holomorphy, because for any domain D one can construct a holomorphic function on D with a natural boundary along every boundary point, for instance by using a series with singularities dense in the boundary.

In several complex variables (n ≥ 2) the situation is strikingly different, owing to the Hartogs phenomenon: a holomorphic function on certain domains automatically extends to a larger domain. For example, if D is a domain in Cn, n ≥ 2, and K is a compact subset such that D \ K is connected, then every holomorphic function on D \ K extends holomorphically to all of D. Hence domains such as a spherical shell are never domains of holomorphy.

Characterisations

For domains in Cn, the following conditions are equivalent:

  • D is a domain of holomorphy.
  • D is holomorphically convex, meaning that the holomorphic hull of every compact subset of D is again compact in D.
  • D is pseudoconvex.
  • D satisfies the Levi problem condition: D is a domain of holomorphy precisely when it is pseudoconvex.

The equivalence of pseudoconvexity and being a domain of holomorphy is the solution of the Levi problem, first solved by Kiyoshi Oka and later extended by Hans Grauert and Lars Hörmander to Stein manifolds and general settings.

Envelope of holomorphy

For an arbitrary domain D in Cn, there exists a largest domain to which all holomorphic functions on D simultaneously extend; this is called the envelope of holomorphy of D. Unlike in one variable, the envelope may not be a subset of Cn at all: it can be a multi-sheeted Riemann domain over Cn. Such multi-valuedness is a fundamental feature of the theory of functions of several complex variables.
