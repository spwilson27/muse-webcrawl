Bottle rocket

A bottle rocket is a small skyrocket firework. It consists of a paper tube filled with gunpowder or flash powder, topped with a small payload, and attached to a long thin stick that stabilizes its flight. The name comes from the traditional practice of launching them from a glass bottle, which serves as a convenient stand.

Operation

When the fuse is lit, the propellant charge burns and produces thrust that propels the rocket into the air along the guide stick. At the end of the flight the head typically bursts with a report, a flash, or a spray of sparks or stars, depending on the design. Larger variants are sometimes called skyrockets.

Regulation and safety

Bottle rockets are banned or restricted in many jurisdictions because their erratic flight can start fires or cause eye injuries. The term also refers to a homemade water rocket made from a plastic soda bottle partly filled with water and pressurized with air, a popular science-classroom demonstration of rocketry principles.
