Western Alps

Western Alps
Highest peak: Mont Blanc (4,808 m)
Countries: France, Italy, Switzerland, Monaco
Part of: Alps

The Western Alps are the western part of the Alpine range, running roughly from the Mediterranean coast near Nice north and east to the Great St Bernard Pass, the Simplon area, and Lake Geneva. The conventional dividing line between the Western and Eastern Alps runs from Lake Constance down the Rhine and over the Splügen Pass to Lake Como.

Geography

The Western Alps contain the range's highest summits, including Mont Blanc (4,808 m), Mont Blanc de Cheilon, and the great Pennine peaks such as Monte Rosa, the Matterhorn, and the Weisshorn. Subdivisions include the Maritime Alps, Cottian Alps, Dauphiné Alps, Graian Alps, Savoy Alps, and Pennine Alps. The region is drained by the Rhône, the upper Rhine, the Po tributaries such as the Dora Baltea, and the Durance.

Passes and tourism

Historic crossings include the Great St Bernard, Little St Bernard, Mont Cenis, Montgenèvre, and Simplon passes, now complemented by major road and rail tunnels. The Western Alps host some of Europe's most famous ski resorts — Chamonix, Courchevel, Zermatt, and Verbier — and several national parks including Écrins, Vanoise, and Gran Paradiso.
