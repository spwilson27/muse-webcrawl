Board of Control for Cricket in India

The Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI) is the national governing body for cricket in India. Founded in December 1928 and headquartered at the Wankhede Stadium in Mumbai, it is the richest cricket board in the world, organising domestic competitions such as the Ranji Trophy and the Indian Premier League (IPL), and administering the India national cricket teams.

History

The BCCI was formed in 1928 and affiliated to the Imperial Cricket Conference (now the International Cricket Council). India played its first Test match at Lord's in 1932. For decades the board operated on modest means, but satellite television rights from the 1990s onward transformed its finances, and the launch of the IPL in 2008 made it the economic centre of world cricket, generating billions of dollars in media-rights sales.

Competitions

Domestic tournaments run by the BCCI include the Ranji Trophy, Duleep Trophy, Vijay Hazare Trophy, Syed Mushtaq Ali Trophy and the Irani Cup, alongside women's domestic cricket and age-group tournaments. The IPL, a franchised Twenty20 league, is among the most valuable sports leagues in the world per match.

Governance and controversies

The board is an autonomous society of state associations, historically criticised for opacity and conflicts of interest. Following the 2013 IPL betting and spot-fixing scandal, the Supreme Court of India appointed the Lodha Committee, whose reform recommendations led to a new constitution, and a Committee of Administrators ran the BCCI from 2017 until elected office-bearers returned in 2019.
