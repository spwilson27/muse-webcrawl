Mango

A mango is an edible stone fruit produced by the tropical tree Mangifera indica. It originated in the region between northwestern Myanmar, Bangladesh, and northeastern India, and has been cultivated in South Asia for thousands of years. The mango is the national fruit of India, Pakistan and the Philippines.

Description

Mango trees grow to 30–40 metres tall, with a dense evergreen canopy. The fruit is a drupe, varying greatly in size, shape and colour; ripe mangoes are generally sweet, with skin that may be yellow, orange, red or green. Inside is a single large, flattened seed surrounded by the edible flesh.

Cultivation and cultivars

Hundreds of named cultivars exist, including 'Alphonso', 'Kesar', 'Haden', 'Tommy Atkins', 'Kent' and 'Ataulfo'. India is the world's largest producer, accounting for roughly half of global output, though most of its crop is consumed domestically.

Cuisine and nutrition

Mangoes are eaten fresh, juiced, dried, pickled and used in chutneys, curries, sorbets and desserts such as mango lassi and mango sticky rice. They are a good source of vitamin C and vitamin A.
