The Erdős–Stone theorem is an asymptotic result in extremal graph theory, proved by Paul Erdős and Arthur H. Stone in 1946. It determines, to first order, the maximum number of edges in an n-vertex graph that does not contain a given forbidden subgraph H.

Statement

For any graph H with chromatic number χ(H) = r + 1, the extremal number ex(n; H) equals (1 − 1/r + o(1)) · n²/2. Thus the extremal number is governed to first order by Turán's theorem: graphs avoiding H cannot have asymptotically more edges than the complete r-partite Turán graph, regardless of the detailed structure of H.

Significance

The theorem is often called the fundamental theorem of extremal graph theory. Its proof introduced the technique of embedding a large complete multipartite "blow-up" in any sufficiently dense graph. Later refinements, such as the Erdős–Simonovits stability theorem, sharpen the picture of near-extremal graphs.
