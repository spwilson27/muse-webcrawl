Dark triad

The dark triad is a psychological theory of personality positing three distinct but overlapping socially undesirable traits: narcissism, Machiavellianism, and psychopathy. The construct was introduced by Delroy L. Paulhus and Kevin M. Williams in 2002.

All three traits are associated with callousness, manipulativeness, and a tendency to prioritize one's own interests over those of others. Research on the dark triad examines these traits in subclinical populations — that is, in people who do not meet clinical diagnostic thresholds.

The traits

  • Narcissism is characterized by grandiosity, pride, egotism, and a lack of empathy.
  • Machiavellianism is characterized by manipulation, deceit, and a cynical disregard for morality in the pursuit of self-interest.
  • Psychopathy is characterized by impulsivity, thrill-seeking, low empathy, and antisocial behavior.

Measurement

Common instruments include the Dirty Dozen, a twelve-item questionnaire developed by Jonason and Webster, and the Short Dark Triad (SD3), a 27-item measure. More recently, researchers have proposed a "dark core" or D factor underlying the traits, and an expanded "dark tetrad" that adds everyday sadism.

Applications

Dark triad research has been applied to workplace behavior, leadership, online trolling, mate selection, and criminal behavior. Critics question the overlap between Machiavellianism and psychopathy and the construct validity of brief self-report measures.
