Sir Thomas Malory (c. 1415–1418 – 14 March 1471) was an English writer, the author of Le Morte d'Arthur, the classic English-language prose account of the Arthurian legend. Malory, a knight and member of Parliament from Warwickshire, compiled and translated French and other sources into a single narrative while spending much of his later life in prison.

Life

Malory served in France during the Hundred Years' War and sat as MP for Warwickshire. From 1451 he was repeatedly imprisoned on charges including theft, extortion, and ambush — though never clearly convicted — and he apparently wrote much of his book during these confinements, identifying himself in the text as a "knight prisoner."

Le Morte d'Arthur

Completed around 1469–70 and printed by William Caxton in 1485, the work recounts the birth of King Arthur, the Knights of the Round Table, the Grail quest, and the love of Lancelot and Guinevere, ending with the fall of the kingdom. It became the principal source for later Arthurian literature, from Tennyson to T. H. White.
