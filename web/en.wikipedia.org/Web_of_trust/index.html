Web of trust

A web of trust is a concept used in PGP, GnuPG, and other OpenPGP-compatible systems to establish the authenticity of the binding between a public key and its owner, without relying on a central certificate authority. Instead, users sign each other's keys to attest to their validity, creating a decentralized network of endorsements.

How it works

Each user generates a key pair and collects signatures ("certifications") from people who have verified their identity, often at key-signing parties. Software computes an owner's trust from chains of signatures: a key is considered valid if it is signed by someone the user trusts fully, or by several people trusted marginally. This contrasts with the hierarchical X.509 model used by TLS, where trust flows from certificate authorities.

Limitations

Critics note the burden placed on users to manage trust decisions, difficulties exposing forgery, and social-graph privacy issues raised by public key servers. The concept has influenced other decentralized trust systems, including reputation networks and some blockchain identity proposals.
