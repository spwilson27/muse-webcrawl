A national nature reserve (NNR) is a designation for protected natural areas in several countries. In the United Kingdom, national nature reserves are areas of national importance for wildlife or geology, established to protect some of the most significant habitats and species and to provide opportunities for research and public enjoyment.

United Kingdom

The designation derives from the National Parks and Access to the Countryside Act 1949. In England, Natural England declares NNRs; there are over 220, including The Lizard in Cornwall and the great wetland reserves of the Somerset Levels. Scotland's NNRs, managed by NatureScot and partners, include Beinn Eighe — the first declared in Britain, in 1951 — and the island of Rum. Welsh reserves are declared by Natural Resources Wales. Many UK NNRs are also Sites of Special Scientific Interest and may form part of Natura 2000 or Ramsar sites.

Other countries

China operates a large system of national nature reserves protecting ecosystems such as Wolong's giant panda habitat. Similar national-level designations exist in many countries, differing in legal force and management from local nature reserves.
