Obesity is most commonly defined by a body mass index of 30 or greater.

Obesity is a medical condition in which excess body fat accumulates to an extent that it may have a negative effect on health. People are classed as obese when their body mass index (BMI) — body mass divided by the square of height — is 30 kg/m² or greater, with the range 25–30 defined as overweight. Some East Asian countries use lower thresholds. Obesity is a major cause of disability and is correlated with various diseases, particularly type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease, obstructive sleep apnoea, certain types of cancer, and osteoarthritis.

Causes

At the individual level, obesity results from consuming more calories than are expended, but the condition is shaped by a complex interplay of genetic susceptibility, endocrine and hypothalamic regulation of appetite, medications, sleep, psychological factors, and an "obesogenic" environment characterised by energy-dense processed foods, sugar-sweetened beverages, large portion sizes, and sedentary work and transport. Genetics accounts for a substantial share of variation in susceptibility; rare single-gene disorders such as leptin deficiency cause severe early-onset obesity. Socioeconomic and cultural factors strongly influence prevalence.

Health effects

Obesity increases the likelihood of numerous conditions, including metabolic syndrome, type 2 diabetes, hypertension, coronary heart disease, stroke, fatty liver disease, gallstones, gastro-oesophageal reflux, infertility, and at least thirteen types of cancer. It is associated with reduced life expectancy and is one of the leading preventable causes of death worldwide. Excess weight also carries stigma and is linked to depression and reduced quality of life.

Management

First-line treatment is dietary change and increased physical activity, though long-term maintenance of weight loss through lifestyle measures alone is difficult for many people. Anti-obesity medications — historically including orlistat and, more recently, GLP-1 receptor agonists such as semaglutide and the dual agonist tirzepatide — produce substantially larger average weight losses. For severe obesity, bariatric (metabolic) surgery, including Roux-en-Y gastric bypass and sleeve gastrectomy, is the most effective durable intervention and can induce remission of type 2 diabetes.

Epidemiology

Worldwide obesity has nearly tripled since 1975. The World Health Organization estimated that in 2022 more than one billion people — roughly one in eight — were living with obesity, including over 160 million children and adolescents. Prevalence is highest in the Americas and parts of the Pacific and Middle East, and is rising fastest in low- and middle-income countries, where obesity increasingly coexists with undernutrition.
