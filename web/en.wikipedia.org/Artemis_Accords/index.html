Artemis Accords

Signed: 13 October 2020

Location: Washington, D.C. (virtual)

Named for: Artemis program

Lead agency: NASA

The Artemis Accords are a series of non-binding multilateral arrangements between the United States government and other world governments participating in the Artemis program, an American-led effort to return humans to the Moon by the late 2020s and ultimately to Mars. The Accords establish a framework for cooperation in the peaceful civil exploration and use of the Moon, Mars, comets and asteroids.

History

The Accords were announced by NASA administrator Jim Bridenstine in May 2020 and signed on 13 October 2020 by the eight founding nations: Australia, Canada, Italy, Japan, Luxembourg, the United Arab Emirates, the United Kingdom and the United States. Many additional countries have signed since, bringing the total to several dozen signatories.

Principles

The Accords affirm the importance of the 1967 Outer Space Treaty and address topics including peaceful purposes, transparency, interoperability, emergency assistance, registration of space objects, release of scientific data, preserving outer space heritage, utilisation of space resources, deconfliction of space activities and the mitigation of orbital debris. Signatories affirm that extraction of space resources is not inherently national appropriation under the Outer Space Treaty.

Reception

Supporters describe the Accords as an important step toward common norms for lunar exploration. Critics, including Russia and China, which are not signatories, have described them as too centred on American interests; Russia and China instead pursue the separate International Lunar Research Station project.
