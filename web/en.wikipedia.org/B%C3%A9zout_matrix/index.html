Bézout matrix

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, a Bézout matrix (or Bézoutian) is a square matrix associated with two polynomials. It is named after the French mathematician Étienne Bézout. Bézout matrices arise in the theory of resultants and are applied in several areas, including control theory (stability criteria) and polynomial root-finding.

FieldAlgebra, control theory

Definition

Given polynomials f and g of degree at most n, the Bézout matrix B(f, g) is the symmetric n × n matrix whose entries are defined through the generating identity: the difference quotient (f(x)g(y) − f(y)g(x)) / (x − y) is a polynomial in two variables whose coefficients form the matrix.

Properties and applications

The determinant of the Bézout matrix equals the resultant of the two polynomials, so it vanishes exactly when they have a common root (or common factor); the rank reveals the degree of their greatest common divisor. In control theory, the signature of a Bézout matrix built from the even and odd parts of a characteristic polynomial counts how many roots lie in the left half-plane, giving a stability test related to the Routh–Hurwitz criterion. Bézoutians also appear in algorithms for polynomial GCD computation and in studying the inertia of matrices.
