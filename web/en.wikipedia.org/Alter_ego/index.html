Alter ego

An alter ego (Latin for “other I”) means an alternate self, which is believed to be distinct from a person's normal or original personality. A person who has an alter ego is said to lead a double life. The term was coined in the early 19th century when schizophrenia and dissociative identity disorder were first described by psychologists.

In literature and popular culture

The alter ego became a staple of fiction: Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde in Robert Louis Stevenson's 1886 novella dramatize the divided self. In superhero comics, alter egos are the civilian identities of heroes, such as Clark Kent for Superman and Bruce Wayne for Batman. Performers also adopt alter egos, including David Bowie's Ziggy Stardust, Beyoncé's Sasha Fierce, and Marshall Mathers's Slim Shady.

In psychology and law

Cicero used the idea of the “second self”—a trusted friend—in his writings on friendship, and the phrase entered legal usage for a corporation or entity treated as indistinguishable from its owner (the alter ego doctrine, used in piercing the corporate veil). In psychology it is used more loosely for a secondary, contrasting personality a person presents or inhabits.
