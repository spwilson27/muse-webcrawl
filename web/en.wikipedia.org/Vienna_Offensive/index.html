Vienna Offensive

Date: 16 March – 15 April 1945
Location: Vienna and eastern Austria
Result: Soviet victory

The Vienna Offensive was a major offensive operation launched by the Soviet Union's 2nd and 3rd Ukrainian Fronts against Nazi German forces in and around Vienna, Austria, in the closing weeks of the Second World War in Europe. It lasted from 16 March to 15 April 1945, and ended with the capture of Vienna.

Background

After the fall of Budapest in February 1945, Soviet forces advanced westward into Austria. Germany's last major counteroffensive, Operation Spring Awakening around Lake Balaton in Hungary, failed in mid-March 1945, after which Marshal Fyodor Tolbukhin's 3rd Ukrainian Front, supported by Rodion Malinovsky's 2nd Ukrainian Front and the Bulgarian First Army, drove toward Vienna.

The battle

Soviet forces crossed into Austria in late March, taking Wiener Neustadt, Eisenstadt, and Neunkirchen. The assault on Vienna itself began in early April; German defenders included elements of the 6th SS Panzer Army. Street fighting lasted about a week, and Soviet troops captured the city centre by 13 April 1945, while other forces bypassed and encircled the city from the west and north. Fighting in Austria continued until mid-April as Soviet forces advanced toward Linz and Graz and linked up with American forces.

Aftermath

Vienna suffered heavy damage and thousands of civilian deaths. Austria was subsequently divided into Allied occupation zones, with Vienna itself split among the four powers, until the Austrian State Treaty of 1955 restored full sovereignty. The battle marked the end of organized German resistance in Austria.
