Little Rann of Kutch

The Little Rann of Kutch is a salt marsh in the Kutch district of Gujarat, India, covering about 5,000 square kilometres. Together with the larger Great Rann to the northwest, it forms part of the Rann of Kutch, a seasonally flooded salt desert between the Gulf of Kutch and the mouth of the Indus.

Ecology

The area contains the Indian Wild Ass Sanctuary, the last refuge of the Indian wild ass (khur), whose population has recovered to several thousand. In the monsoon the flats flood with a mixture of seawater and river water, attracting flamingos, pelicans and other waterbirds; a notable "flamingo city" breeding colony forms on raised mud islands. Other wildlife includes the desert fox, nilgai, wolf and caracal.

Salt production

The Agariya community works seasonal salt pans across the dry flats, producing a large share of India's inland salt under harsh conditions. Traditional livelihoods also include fishing and grazing on the grassy bets (islands) that rise above the marsh.
