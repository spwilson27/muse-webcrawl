Papua New Guinea (PNG) is a country in Oceania occupying the eastern half of the island of New Guinea and numerous offshore islands, directly north of Australia. It is one of the most culturally and linguistically diverse nations on earth, with more than 800 living languages and only a small share of its population living in urban centres.

Geography

The country's core is a mountainous spine of highlands rising above 4,500 metres at Mount Wilhelm, cut by large river systems such as the Sepik and Fly. The terrain, tropical rainforest and swamps isolate communities from one another, which accounts for the extraordinary diversity of languages and customs. PNG lies on the Pacific Ring of Fire and experiences earthquakes and volcanic eruptions.

History

Humans settled New Guinea at least 40,000–50,000 years ago, and its highland peoples developed agriculture independently around 7,000–10,000 years ago. In the late 19th century the island was divided between Germany (north-east), Britain (south-east, later transferred to Australia as Papua) and the Netherlands (west). After the two world wars Australia administered both eastern territories; self-government came in 1973 and full independence on 16 September 1975.

Economy and society

The economy rests on mining (gold, copper, oil and liquefied natural gas), timber and agriculture — coffee, cocoa, copra and palm oil. Most Papua New Guineans practise subsistence farming and live under customary land tenure, which covers about 97% of the land area. A decade-long civil conflict on Bougainville (1988–1998) ended in a peace agreement granting autonomy and a 2019 referendum in which voters overwhelmingly chose independence.
