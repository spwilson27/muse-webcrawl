Appropriations bill

An appropriations bill is a legislative measure that provides legal authority for a government to spend money from its treasury for specified purposes and amounts. The plural form often refers to the set of regular spending bills that together fund the discretionary operations of government for a fiscal year.

Function

Authorisation laws establish programmes and agencies, but money can generally be spent only when an appropriation has been enacted. Appropriations bills therefore set the actual funding levels, frequently differing from both the executive's budget request and the amounts authorised. They may be regular (annual), supplemental (for emergencies or shortfalls during the year), or continuing (to extend funding temporarily at prior rates).

Practice

In the United States, the twelve annual appropriations bills are frequently combined into omnibus packages when individual passage stalls. Earmarks — provisions directing funds to specific projects — were long a controversial feature of appropriations legislation. In parliamentary systems, appropriations are typically presented as supply or estimates bills voted by the lower house.
