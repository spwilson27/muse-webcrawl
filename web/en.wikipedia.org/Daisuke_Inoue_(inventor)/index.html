Daisuke Inoue (inventor)

Daisuke Inoue
Born: 10 May 1940, Osaka, Japan
Occupation: Inventor, businessman
Known for: Inventing the karaoke machine

Daisuke Inoue (井上 大佑, Inoue Daisuke, born 10 May 1940) is a Japanese inventor and businessman best known for inventing the karaoke machine in 1971. A former backing musician in Kobe, Inoue built the "8 Juke" — a machine combining a car stereo, an amplifier and a coin box — after a client asked for a recording of his own playing so that he could sing along on a company trip.

Karaoke invention

Inoue leased out his machines to bars rather than selling them, an innovation that helped karaoke spread rapidly through Japan and then the world. Because he never patented the invention, he earned little from the multibillion-dollar karaoke industry that followed; a Filipino inventor, Roberto del Rosario, held a patent on a related sing-along system.

Recognition

In 1999 Time magazine named Inoue one of the most influential Asians of the 20th century. In 2004 he was awarded the Ig Nobel Peace Prize for inventing karaoke, thereby "providing an entirely new way for people to learn to tolerate each other".
