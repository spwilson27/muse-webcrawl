Corporate manslaughter

Corporate manslaughter is a criminal offence in which a corporation is held responsible for a death resulting from a gross breach of a duty of care, typically in workplace or public safety contexts.

United Kingdom

The Corporate Manslaughter and Corporate Homicide Act 2007, in force from 2008, replaced the common-law identification doctrine that had made prosecutions of large companies difficult after disasters such as the Herald of Free Enterprise sinking. The offence focuses on senior management failure; penalties are unlimited fines and publicity orders.

Other jurisdictions

Canada created corporate criminal liability for workplace deaths after the Westray mine disaster (Bill C-45, 2004). Australia and other common-law countries have industrial manslaughter offences in several states.
