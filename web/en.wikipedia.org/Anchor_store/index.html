Anchor store

An anchor store, also called an anchor tenant or key tenant, is a major retail store — typically a large department store or big-box retailer — that serves as the principal draw for customers to a shopping mall or shopping center. Anchor stores occupy the largest spaces, usually at the ends of a mall's wings, and their drawing power benefits the smaller in-line shops between them.

Role in shopping centers

Because anchors generate the foot traffic on which the whole center depends, they historically paid little or no rent, or received generous terms and purpose-built premises, while smaller tenants paid premium rents. Developers design malls around their anchors (the "dumbbell" layout places anchors at opposite ends so shoppers pass the smaller stores between them). Traditional anchors included department-store chains such as Sears, Macy's, Dillard's, and JCPenney in the United States.

Decline and adaptation

The contraction of department-store chains and the closure of once-dominant anchors such as Sears have left many malls with vacant anchor spaces, contributing to the "dead mall" phenomenon. Owners have increasingly re-let anchor boxes to nontraditional tenants — gyms, entertainment venues, grocery stores, medical facilities, offices, and fulfillment uses — or demolished them for mixed-use redevelopment. In power centers, the analogous role is played by big-box "category killer" stores.
