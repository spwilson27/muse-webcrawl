Set (also Seth, Sutekh, or Setekh) is a god of deserts, storms, disorder, violence, and foreigners in ancient Egyptian religion. He is usually depicted with the head of the mysterious "Set animal" — a creature with a curved snout, tall squared ears, and a forked tail that matches no single known animal. Set was the brother and murderer of Osiris and the great rival of Horus, yet he was not purely evil: Egyptians also honoured him as the strong defender who stood in the prow of Ra's barque and speared the serpent Apep.

Mythology

In the Osiris myth, the jealous Set murdered his brother Osiris, dismembered his body, and usurped the throne. Osiris's son Horus contested the succession, and their struggle — the "Contendings of Horus and Set," preserved in a New Kingdom papyrus — lasted eighty years before the divine tribunal awarded the kingship to Horus, though Set was compensated with a place in the sky as god of storms and the desert. Set also figured in the cycle of the sun god Ra, fighting chaos nightly.

Worship and changing reputation

Set was one of the oldest Egyptian gods, worshipped since predynastic times at Ombos (Naqada). He was the patron of the Hyksos rulers, who identified him with the Canaanite Baal, and of the Ramesside pharaohs — whose names (Seti, "man of Set") honoured him. In later periods, as Osiris worship grew dominant, Set was increasingly demonized as the embodiment of evil and disorder, his images defaced and his role taken over by other gods in some myths. The Greeks identified him with Typhon. See also Osiris, Horus, and ancient Egyptian religion.
