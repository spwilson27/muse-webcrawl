Land degradation

Land degradation is a process in which the value of the biophysical environment is affected by a combination of human-induced processes acting upon the land. It involves the temporary or permanent decline in the productive capacity of land — through soil erosion, nutrient depletion, salinisation, desertification, deforestation, pollution and loss of biodiversity, among other processes.

Causes

Major causes include unsustainable agriculture (over-cultivation, overgrazing and poor irrigation), deforestation and forest degradation, urbanisation and infrastructure, mining, pollution and waste disposal, and climate change, which intensifies drought and extreme rainfall. Poverty and insecure land tenure can drive short-term exploitation.

Consequences

Degraded land produces less food and stores less carbon and water, contributing to food insecurity, poverty, migration and conflict, and to climate change through released soil carbon. Global assessments have estimated that a large share of the world's soils are already degraded and that billions of people are affected.

Responses

Responses include sustainable land management — conservation agriculture, agroforestry, terracing, restoration of degraded ecosystems — and policies under the United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD), whose Land Degradation Neutrality target is part of Sustainable Development Goal 15.3.
