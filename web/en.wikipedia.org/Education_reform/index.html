Education reform

Education reform is the goal of changing public education in order to improve its quality, equity, accessibility or efficiency. Historically, reforms have concerned the purposes of schooling, curriculum content, teaching methods, governance and funding.

Historical reforms

In the 19th century, figures such as Horace Mann in the United States championed free, compulsory common schooling. The 20th century saw reforms following events such as the launch of Sputnik (which spurred science and mathematics education in the US) and reports such as A Nation at Risk (1983).

Common themes

Recurring reform movements include standards-based reform, school choice (charter schools and vouchers), small schools, bilingual and multicultural education, inclusion of students with disabilities, literacy and numeracy initiatives, and the integration of educational technology. International assessments such as PISA have influenced reform agendas in many countries.

Criticism and debate

Reforms are frequently contested. Critics argue that successive waves of reform often fail for lack of sustained funding, teacher involvement or realistic timelines, while supporters contend that structural change is necessary to adapt education to economic and social change.
