Morris Minor

[Image: Morris Minor 1000 two-door saloon]

The Morris Minor is a British small family car produced by Morris Motors between 1948 and 1971. Designed by Alec Issigonis, it debuted at the Earls Court Motor Show in 1948 and was the first British car to sell over a million units, a milestone reached in 1960. Combining unitary construction, torsion-bar independent front suspension, rack-and-pinion steering and small wheels, the Minor was praised for its handling and economy and became an emblem of post-war British motoring.

Models

The original MM series (1948-1953) used a side-valve engine derived from the pre-war Morris Eight. The Series II (1952-1956) adopted the Austin A-series overhead-valve engine after the Morris-BMC merger. The Minor 1000 (Series III, 1956-1971) received a 948 cc engine, later enlarged to 1098 cc, a single-piece windscreen and other refinements. Body styles included two- and four-door saloons, a convertible (Tourer), the wood-framed Traveller estate car, and van and pick-up commercial versions.

Legacy

Production ended in 1971 after more than 1.6 million cars had been built, with assembly also taking place in Australia and New Zealand. The Minor remains one of Britain's most beloved classic cars, supported by an extensive owners' club network and parts supply, and Issigonis went on to apply its space-efficient philosophy to the Mini.
