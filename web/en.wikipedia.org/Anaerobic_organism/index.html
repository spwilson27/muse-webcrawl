Anaerobic organism

An anaerobic organism or anaerobe is any organism that does not require molecular oxygen for growth. It may react negatively or even die if free oxygen is present. In contrast, an aerobic organism (aerobe) is an organism that requires an oxygenated environment.

TypesObligate anaerobes, facultative anaerobes, aerotolerant organisms
Energy metabolismFermentation or anaerobic respiration
ExamplesClostridium, Bacteroides, methanogens

Classifications

For practical purposes, there are three categories of anaerobes. Obligate anaerobes, which are harmed by the presence of oxygen, lack defenses such as superoxide dismutase and catalase that detoxify reactive oxygen species. Aerotolerant organisms cannot use oxygen for growth but tolerate its presence. Facultative anaerobes can grow without oxygen but use oxygen if it is present, typically growing better with it.

Energy metabolism

Anaerobes obtain energy through fermentation, in which organic compounds serve as both electron donors and acceptors, or through anaerobic respiration, using terminal electron acceptors such as nitrate, sulfate, or carbon dioxide. Methanogens, a group of archaea, reduce carbon dioxide to methane. Fermentation yields far less ATP per glucose molecule than aerobic respiration.

Significance

Anaerobes dominate environments such as deep sediments, the animal gut, and waterlogged soils. Several are important human pathogens: Clostridium tetani causes tetanus, Clostridioides difficile causes antibiotic-associated colitis, and Bacteroides species are frequent causes of abscesses. In industry, anaerobic fermentation produces ethanol, biogas, and fermented foods.
