Popular Front of Latvia

The Popular Front of Latvia (Latvian: Latvijas Tautas fronte, LTF) was a political organisation in the Latvian Soviet Socialist Republic and, after 1990, in the restored Republic of Latvia. Founded on 9 October 1988 during the period of perestroika, it became the principal vehicle of the Latvian national movement — the "Singing Revolution" — and led the country to the restoration of independence from the Soviet Union in 1990–1991.

History

The Front grew out of the environmental and cultural protests of 1986–1988 and the mobilisation around the anniversary of the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact. Its founding congress in October 1988, chaired by the journalist Dainis Īvāns, initially called for greater autonomy within a reformed Soviet federation, but by its second congress in 1989 the Front had adopted the goal of full independence. At its peak it claimed more than 200,000 members and it organised, together with the Estonian and Lithuanian movements, the Baltic Way human chain of 23 August 1989, in which some two million people joined hands across the three republics.

In the March 1990 elections to the Latvian Supreme Soviet the Front won a two-thirds majority, and on 4 May 1990 the parliament it dominated adopted the Declaration on the Restoration of Independence. After independence was fully restored in August 1991, the Front fragmented; it performed poorly in the 1993 Saeima election, failed to win seats, and was dissolved later that decade. Many of its activists went on to found or join other Latvian parties.
