Flavius Josephus (Greek: Ἰώσηπος, c. AD 37 – c. 100), born Yosef ben Matityahu (Hebrew: יוסף בן מתתיהו), was a Jewish historian of priestly and royal ancestry who recorded Jewish history, with special emphasis on the first century AD and the First Jewish–Roman War (AD 66–70), including the siege of Masada. His works are the chief source for the history of Judaea in the period and provide valuable extra-biblical testimony about figures such as Jesus, John the Baptist and James the Just.

Life

Born in Jerusalem to a priestly family claiming Hasmonean descent, Josephus was educated in Jerusalem and at 26 travelled to Rome to plead for imprisoned priests, winning the favour of Empress Poppaea. Returning to Judaea as revolt broke out in AD 66, he was appointed commander of Galilee, fortified its towns, and in AD 67 was captured by the Romans at Jotapata after a suicide pact among the defenders left him among the last survivors. Brought before Vespasian, he prophesied that the general would become emperor; when the prophecy came true in AD 69, Vespasian freed him, and Josephus took the imperial family name Flavius. He accompanied Titus during the siege of Jerusalem in AD 70 and spent the rest of his life in Rome under Flavian patronage, writing his histories.

Works

The Jewish War (c. AD 75) narrates the revolt from the Maccabees to the fall of Masada. The twenty-book Antiquities of the Jews (AD 93/94) retells Jewish history from Creation to the eve of the revolt, modelled on Dionysius of Halicarnassus, and contains the famous Testimonium Flavianum passage about Jesus, whose authenticity is partly debated. His apologetic treatise Against Apion defends Judaism against anti-Jewish writers, and his Life answers critics of his conduct in Galilee.

Influence

Josephus's writings were preserved by Christians, for whom they illuminated the world of the New Testament, and have profoundly shaped understanding of Second Temple Judaism. The "Josephus problem" in mathematics (a counting-out puzzle) takes its name from the legend of Jotapata.
