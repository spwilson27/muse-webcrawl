Kingdom of the Isles

The Kingdom of the Isles, also known as the Kingdom of Man and the Isles, was a Norse-Gaelic kingdom of the Irish Sea and Scotland's western seaboard, comprising the Isle of Man, the Hebrides and the islands of the Clyde, between the 9th and 13th centuries. Its kings, of the Crovan dynasty and the descendants of Somerled, ruled a maritime realm of galleys (birlinns) owing loose allegiance to Norway until the Treaty of Perth (1266) transferred the Isles to Scotland.

History

Viking raids from the 790s turned into settlement of Man, the Hebrides and the Northern Isles. Irish annals call the rulers ríg Innse Gall — kings of the Isles of the Foreigners. In 1079 the Manx hero-king Godred Crovan, victor at Skyhill, founded the Crovan dynasty ruling Man and the North Isles from Dublin-linked ports. His descendants' realm was split by the rise of Somerled (d. 1164), whose sons founded Clan Donald, Clan Dougall and other lordships of the southern Hebrides; Somerled's heirs styled themselves ri Innse Gall, kings of the Isles, and later Lords of the Isles.

End

King Magnus III of Man submitted to Edward I of England in the 1290s' precursors — but the decisive change came in 1263, when Haakon IV of Norway's great expedition failed after the indecisive Battle of Largs. By the Treaty of Perth (1266) Magnus VI of Norway sold Man and the Hebrides to Alexander III of Scotland for 4,000 marks and an annual payment. The Crovan line in Man ended in 1265; its successor regime under Scots, then English, rule became the Kingdom of Man as a lordship, while the MacDonald Lordship of the Isles preserved much of the sea-kingdom's culture until 1493.
