Zacatecas (Spanish pronunciation: [sakaˈtekas]) is the capital city of the Mexican state of Zacatecas. Founded in 1546 after the discovery of rich silver deposits, it became one of the great mining cities of colonial New Spain, supplying a large share of Spain's silver. Its well-preserved historic centre, built of pink cantera stone in a narrow valley, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1993.

History

Spanish prospectors led by Juan de Tolosa found silver at the Cerro de la Bufa in 1546, and the mining camp grew rapidly despite its remote, arid site and conflicts with the Chichimeca peoples. Silver from Zacatecas travelled the Camino Real de Tierra Adentro to Mexico City and helped finance the Spanish Empire. During the Mexican Revolution, the Battle of Zacatecas in June 1914 — a decisive victory for Pancho Villa's División del Norte — was one of the bloodiest engagements of the war.

Sights

Landmarks include the baroque Zacatecas Cathedral, the former San Agustín church, the Rafael Coronel and Pedro Coronel museums, and the El Edén mine, now a museum reached by an underground train. A cable car crosses from the Cerro del Grillo to the Cerro de la Bufa, which offers views over the city and monuments to the 1914 battle.

Culture

The city hosts the Festival Cultural Zacatecas and an international folklore festival, and its Marcha de Zacatecas is one of Mexico's best-known marches, often called the country's second national anthem.
