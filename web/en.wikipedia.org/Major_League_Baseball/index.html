Major League Baseball

Major League Baseball (MLB) is a professional baseball organisation in North America and the oldest of the major professional sports leagues in the United States and Canada. It comprises 30 teams — 29 in the United States and one in Canada (Toronto) — divided equally between the National League (founded 1876) and the American League (founded 1901). The season champion is decided by the World Series, played since 1903.

Structure and season

Each league has three divisions (East, Central, West) of five teams. Clubs play a 162-game regular season, followed by a postseason of Wild Card series, best-of-five Division Series, best-of-seven League Championship Series, and the best-of-seven World Series. Six teams per league qualify for the playoffs under the format introduced in 2022. The All-Star Game is played in mid-July. MLB also oversees Minor League Baseball, whose affiliated farm system develops players.

History

Professional baseball dates to the Cincinnati Red Stockings (1869); the National League was founded in 1876 and the American League declared major status in 1901. The modern era includes the dead-ball era, the Black Sox scandal (1919) and the appointment of Commissioner Kenesaw Mountain Landis, Babe Ruth's home-run revolution, Jackie Robinson breaking the colour barrier with the Brooklyn Dodgers in 1947, westward expansion, free agency after 1975, and the steroid era of the 1990s–2000s, followed by drug-testing regimes. Landmark rules changes in 2023 — a pitch clock, larger bases, and limits on defensive shifts — shortened game times significantly.

Economics and audience

MLB revenues exceed US$10 billion annually from media rights, ticketing, and sponsorship; total attendance across the regular season is around 70 million. The New York Yankees are the most successful franchise with 27 World Series titles, and the league's iconic records include Barry Bonds's 762 career home runs and Joe DiMaggio's 56-game hitting streak.
