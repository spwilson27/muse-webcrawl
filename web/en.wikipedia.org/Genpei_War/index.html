Genpei War

Genpei War
Date: 1180–1185
Location: Japan
Result: Minamoto victory; destruction of the Taira clan
Belligerents: Minamoto clan (Genji) vs. Taira clan (Heike)

The Genpei War (源平合戦, Genpei Kassen) was a national civil war between the Taira and Minamoto clans during the late Heian period of Japan. It resulted in the downfall of the Taira and the establishment of the Kamakura shogunate under Minamoto no Yoritomo, who appointed himself as shōgun in 1192, ushering in the Kamakura period. The name "Genpei" comes from the on'yomi readings of the characters for Minamoto (源, Gen) and Taira (平, Hei or Pei), the rival clans also being known as the Genji and the Heike.

Background

The war followed decades of growing rivalry between the two great samurai clans. Taira no Kiyomori had risen to dominate the imperial court in Kyoto, placing his own grandson, Antoku, on the throne in 1180. In response, the retired Emperor Go-Shirakawa's son, Prince Mochihito, issued a call to arms urging the Minamoto to rise against the Taira. Minamoto no Yorimasa was defeated at the Battle of Uji in June 1180, but Yoritomo raised an army in the Kantō and established his base at Kamakura.

Course of the war

Early fighting included the Battle of Fujigawa (1180), where the Taira army retreated after mistaking a flock of waterbirds for a night attack, and the Battle of Kurikara (1183), where Minamoto no Yoshinaka drove oxen bearing torches into the Taira camp. Yoshinaka seized Kyoto but alienated the court and was destroyed by Yoritomo's forces in 1184.

Command then passed to Yoritomo's half-brothers, Minamoto no Noriyori and the brilliant young general Minamoto no Yoshitsune. Yoshitsune won a string of victories: at Ichi-no-Tani (1184), a major Taira stronghold near modern Kobe; at Yashima (1185) on Shikoku; and finally at the naval Battle of Dan-no-ura in the Shimonoseki Strait on 25 April 1185. In that final battle the Taira were annihilated, and the child Emperor Antoku drowned with his grandmother, along with the loss of the imperial sword Kusanagi no Tsurugi.

Aftermath

The Minamoto victory ended Taira rule and confirmed the ascendancy of the warrior class. Yoritomo's Kamakura shogunate became the first of the military governments that would rule Japan, in the emperor's name, until 1868. Yoshitsune's later falling-out with Yoritomo and his death in 1189 became one of Japan's most famous tragedies. The conflict is immortalized in the epic The Tale of the Heike, whose opening lines on the impermanence of all things remain among the best-known passages in Japanese literature.
