Open science

Open science is a movement and policy framework aiming to make scientific research, data and dissemination accessible to all levels of society, amateur as well as professional. UNESCO's 2021 Recommendation on Open Science defined it as an inclusive construct combining open access, open data, open methods and infrastructure, citizen science and open evaluation.

Practices

Open science encompasses publishing research openly (open access), sharing data according to the FAIR principles (findable, accessible, interoperable, reusable), openly documenting methods and code, preregistering study hypotheses and analyses to reduce publication bias, conducting open peer review, and engaging the public through citizen science projects such as Galaxy Zoo. During the COVID-19 pandemic, preprint servers such as medRxiv and bioRxiv accelerated the sharing of results.

Rationale

Advocates argue that transparency improves reproducibility — a major concern after the "replication crisis" in psychology and other fields — speeds discovery, prevents duplication of effort, and improves public trust and the return on publicly funded research. Funders including the European Commission and Wellcome have adopted open research requirements, and universities increasingly reward open practices in hiring and promotion.

Criticism

Debates concern the career risks of sharing data early, the costs of article processing charges shifting barriers from readers to authors, privacy and dual-use concerns with sensitive data, and whether mandates adequately respect disciplinary and regional differences.
