Vincentian cuisine
Country: Saint Vincent and the Grenadines
National dish: roasted breadfruit and fried jackfish
Key ingredients: breadfruit, callaloo, arrowroot, seafood, plantain, coconut

Vincentian cuisine is the cooking of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines in the Caribbean, blending African, Indigenous Kalinago and Carib, European and East Indian influences. It relies on locally grown root vegetables, breadfruit, tropical fruit and freshly caught fish.

Staple foods and dishes

The national dish is roasted breadfruit served with fried jackfish. Breadfruit — the island grows many varieties, and St Vincent hosts a Breadfruit Festival — is also boiled, baked or made into flour. Other staples include callaloo soup, ducuna (a sweet potato and coconut dumpling), madungo bakes, saltfish with roasted breadfruit, pelau, and souse. Arrowroot, once a major export grown around the Owia area, is still used as a starch and thickener.

Drinks

Local drinks include mauby, sorrel and ginger beer, golden apple juice, sea moss drinks, and the island's Sunset rum. Hairoun is the national lager.
