Mapai

Founded: 1930
Dissolved: 1968 (merged into the Israeli Labor Party)
Ideology: Labor Zionism, social democracy
Notable leader: David Ben-Gurion

Mapai (Hebrew: מפא"י, an acronym for Mifleget Poalei Eretz Yisrael, "Workers' Party of the Land of Israel") was a democratic socialist political party in Mandatory Palestine and Israel. It was the dominant force in Israeli politics from its founding in 1930 until its merger into the Israeli Labor Party in 1968.

History

Mapai was formed in 1930 by the merger of Ahdut HaAvoda and Hapoel Hatzair, the two main Labor Zionist parties of the Yishuv. Under David Ben-Gurion it came to lead the Histadrut trade union federation, the Jewish Agency and, after 1948, the government of the new State of Israel. Mapai led every Israeli government from independence until 1977, through its own rule and later through the Alignment, overseeing state-building, mass immigration and the establishment of Israel's welfare and defence institutions.

Ideology and splits

Mapai combined Zionism with social-democratic economics, though in practice it grew increasingly centrist and establishment-oriented. Tensions produced splits: in 1944 the left wing left to form what became Mapam, and in 1965 Ben-Gurion himself broke away to found Rafi after disputes with Prime Minister Levi Eshkol.

Merger

In 1968 Mapai merged with Ahdut HaAvoda and Rafi to form the Israeli Labor Party, ending its independent existence while its political tradition continued within Labor and the Alignment.
