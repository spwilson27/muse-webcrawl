Cyber-physical system

A cyber-physical system (CPS) is a system in which a mechanism is controlled or monitored by computer-based algorithms, tightly integrated with the internet and its users. In cyber-physical systems, physical and software components are deeply intertwined, able to operate on different spatial and temporal scales, exhibit multiple behavioural modalities, and interact with each other in ways that change with context.

Characteristics

Examples include smart grids, autonomous vehicle systems, medical monitoring, industrial control systems, robotics, and automatic pilot avionics. A CPS typically involves a feedback loop in which sensors measure the physical process, embedded computation decides on a response, and actuators affect the process, often communicating over networks. The term was coined around 2006 by Helen Gill at the US National Science Foundation.

Challenges

Designing cyber-physical systems requires jointly reasoning about computation and physical dynamics: timing constraints, safety, security, and reliability must all be guaranteed together. Because such systems control infrastructure and vehicles, failures or cyberattacks can have direct physical consequences, as demonstrated by attacks on industrial control systems.

Relation to other concepts

Cyber-physical systems overlap with embedded systems and the Internet of Things, but emphasise the closed loop between computation and the physical world and the engineering challenge of integrating the two. The concept underlies Industry 4.0 and the idea of digital twins, in which a software model mirrors a physical asset in real time.
