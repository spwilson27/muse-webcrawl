Strait of Hormuz

The Strait of Hormuz is a strait between the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman. It provides the only sea passage from the Persian Gulf to the open ocean and is one of the world's most strategically important chokepoints. On the north coast lies Iran, and on the south coast lies the Musandam Peninsula, shared by the United Arab Emirates and the Musandam Governorate, an exclave of Oman.

Geography

The strait is about 167 kilometres (104 mi) long, with a width varying from about 97 km (60 mi) to 39 km (24 mi). The traffic separation scheme for shipping is about 3.2 km (2 mi) wide in each direction, and the shipping lanes lie within Omani territorial waters near the Musandam coast.

Oil transit

A very large share of the world's seaborne oil passes through the strait — on the order of a fifth of global petroleum consumption, including most of the exports of Saudi Arabia, Iran, Iraq, Kuwait, Qatar and the UAE — as well as a large share of the world's liquefied natural gas from Qatar. This makes the strait one of the most closely watched energy chokepoints in the world; Iran has periodically threatened to close it in response to sanctions, and the United States and other navies maintain a presence in the region to keep it open.

Incidents

The strait was a focus of the "Tanker War" phase of the Iran–Iraq War in the 1980s, during which shipping was attacked by both sides, and of Operation Praying Mantis (1988), the largest US naval surface engagement since the Second World War. In 1988, USS Vincennes shot down Iran Air Flight 655 over the strait, killing all 290 people aboard.
