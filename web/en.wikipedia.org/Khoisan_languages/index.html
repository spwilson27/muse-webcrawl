Khoisan languages

The Khoisan languages are a group of African languages originally spoken by the Khoekhoe and San peoples of southern Africa, famous for their click consonants. The term, coined by Joseph Greenberg, grouped several families now considered unrelated.

Families

Modern scholarship distinguishes the Khoe-Kwadi, Kx'a and Tuu families, plus isolates such as Sandawe and Hadza in Tanzania. Many Khoisan languages are endangered; the largest, Khoekhoegowab (Nama/Damara), is spoken in Namibia.
