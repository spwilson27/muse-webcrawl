Ten Tragic Days

The Ten Tragic Days (Spanish: Decena Trágica) was a series of events during the Mexican Revolution from 9 to 19 February 1913, culminating in a coup d'état in Mexico City that overthrew President Francisco I. Madero.

Events

Rebel generals led by Félix Díaz (nephew of Porfirio Díaz) and Bernardo Reyes seized positions in the capital; Reyes was killed on the first day in an attack on the National Palace. General Victoriano Huerta, ostensibly defending the government, negotiated secretly with Díaz and with the United States ambassador Henry Lane Wilson. Under the "Pact of the Embassy", Huerta betrayed Madero, who was forced to resign on 19 February and, with Vice-President José María Pino Suárez, was murdered a few days later while being transferred to prison.

Aftermath

Huerta's seizure of power provoked renewed revolution under Venustiano Carranza and others, and the United States under President Woodrow Wilson refused to recognize Huerta's government.
