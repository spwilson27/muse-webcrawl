Fanconi anemia

SpecialtyHaematology, medical genetics
InheritanceMostly autosomal recessive
Named afterGuido Fanconi

Fanconi anemia (FA) is a rare genetic disease characterised by progressive bone marrow failure, congenital abnormalities, and a greatly increased risk of cancer, particularly acute myeloid leukaemia and solid tumours of the head, neck, and anogenital regions.

Cause

FA results from mutations in any of more than 20 genes (FANCA through FANCW) whose proteins cooperate in the repair of DNA interstrand crosslinks. FANCA mutations are the most common. Inheritance is usually autosomal recessive, though some forms are X-linked or autosomal dominant. Cells from patients show chromosomal breakage when exposed to crosslinking agents such as diepoxybutane, a diagnostic test.

Management

Treatment includes androgens and blood transfusions for marrow failure; haematopoietic stem cell transplantation is the only curative therapy for the haematological manifestations, though it does not remove the elevated solid-tumour risk, requiring lifelong surveillance. The condition is named after Swiss paediatrician Guido Fanconi, who described it in 1927.
