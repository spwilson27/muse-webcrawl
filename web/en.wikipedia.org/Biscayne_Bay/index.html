Biscayne Bay

Biscayne Bay is a lagoon with characteristics of an estuary located on the Atlantic coast of South Florida, United States. It extends roughly 35 miles (56 km) from North Miami Beach south to the upper Florida Keys, separating Miami and Miami Beach from the mainland barrier islands.

Geography

The northern part of the bay is the most urbanised, flanked by the Miami skyline and crossed by several causeways linking the mainland with Miami Beach. The central bay is shallower and more open, while the southern portion, much of it protected within Biscayne National Park, contains the Safety Valve, a series of shallow sand flats and tidal channels, and the northernmost Florida Keys.

Ecology

The bay supports seagrass meadows, mangrove shorelines, and hardbottom communities that shelter manatees, sea turtles, crocodiles, and large numbers of fish and wading birds. Stormwater runoff, dredging, and algal blooms have periodically stressed the ecosystem, prompting restoration efforts.

History

The bay was named for a Basque mariner or for the Bay of Biscay. The Tequesta people lived around its shores before European contact. Juan Ponce de León visited in 1513. Stiltsville, a group of houses built on stilts in the bay, became a local landmark.
