Tallinn Manual

The Tallinn Manual on the International Law Applicable to Cyber Warfare is an academic, non-binding study of how existing international law applies to cyber conflicts and cyber warfare. It was produced by an international group of legal experts invited by the NATO Cooperative Cyber Defence Centre of Excellence (CCDCOE) in Tallinn, Estonia, and published by Cambridge University Press in 2013.

Development

Work began in 2009 under the direction of Professor Michael N. Schmitt of the United States Naval War College. The project was prompted in part by the 2007 cyberattacks on Estonia, which raised questions about whether and how the law of armed conflict applies to hostile operations in cyberspace.

Contents

The original Manual contains 95 black-letter rules addressing sovereignty, state responsibility, the jus ad bellum (including when a cyber operation constitutes a "use of force" or an "armed attack" triggering self-defence), and the jus in bello (such as distinction, proportionality, and the protection of civilians during cyber operations in armed conflict).

Tallinn Manual 2.0

An expanded second edition, Tallinn Manual 2.0 on the International Law Applicable to Cyber Operations, was published in 2017 with 154 rules, extending the analysis to peacetime legal regimes including human rights law, air and space law, and diplomatic and consular law. A third revision process ("Tallinn Manual 3.0") was launched in 2021.

Reception

Although not an official NATO position, the Manual has been highly influential in shaping government legal positions and academic debate on state behaviour in cyberspace.
