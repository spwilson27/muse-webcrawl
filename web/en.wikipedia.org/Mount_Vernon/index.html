Mount Vernon

Mount Vernon is the former residence and plantation of George Washington, a Founding Father, commander of the Continental Army, and the first president of the United States, and his wife, Martha Washington. It is located on the banks of the Potomac River in Fairfax County, Virginia, near Alexandria and about 15 miles (24 km) south of Washington, D.C. The estate is owned and maintained as a museum by the Mount Vernon Ladies' Association, which purchased it in 1858 and opened it to the public in 1860. It is one of the most visited historic houses in the United States.

History

The land was granted to Washington's great-grandfather in 1674. George Washington's father, Augustine Washington, built a modest house on the site around 1734, and George began leasing the property from his half-brother Lawrence's widow in 1754, inheriting it in 1761. Over the following decades he greatly enlarged the house — adding wings, a piazza overlooking the Potomac, and the cupola — and expanded the plantation to some 8,000 acres divided into five farms. Its crops included tobacco and later wheat and corn, worked by more than 300 enslaved people whom Washington held in bondage; his will provided for the emancipation of those he owned outright after his wife's death, making him the only slaveholding Founding Father president to do so.

The mansion and grounds

The wooden mansion, finished with rusticated pine boards painted and sanded to imitate stone, contains 21 rooms including the large dining room ("New Room") and Washington's study and bedchamber, where he died on 14 December 1799. Outbuildings include a kitchen, slave quarters, a smokehouse, stables, a greenhouse, and a working distillery and gristmill reconstructed on their original sites. Washington designed the formal grounds, with a bowling green, upper and lower gardens, and a four-acre demonstration farm. His tomb, built to his instructions in 1831, stands on the estate, as does a memorial to the enslaved community and their burial ground.

Preservation

After the deaths of George and Martha Washington (1802), the estate passed through family hands and declined. Ann Pamela Cunningham founded the Mount Vernon Ladies' Association in 1853 and raised funds to buy the mansion and 200 acres in 1858 — the first nationwide historic preservation effort in the United States and an early example of a major civic undertaking led by women. The Association still owns and operates Mount Vernon privately, receiving no regular government funding, and welcomes roughly a million visitors a year.
