The Spanish Republic (Spanish: República española), historiographically referred to as the First Spanish Republic, was the political regime in Spain from 11 February 1873 to 29 December 1874. It was proclaimed after King Amadeo I abdicated amid political crisis, and the National Assembly voted to establish a republic.

History

The Republic was beset from the start by instability and overlapping crises: the Third Carlist War in the north, the Ten Years' War in Cuba, and the Cantonal rebellion, in which federalist radicals proclaimed autonomous cantons in the south and east. Four presidents succeeded one another in eleven months — Estanislao Figueras, Francisco Pi y Margall, Nicolás Salmerón, and Emilio Castelar — each struggling to hold together a coalition divided between unitary and federal republicans. A draft federal constitution was proposed but never enacted.

Castelar, granted extraordinary powers, governed increasingly by decree and suspended constitutional guarantees to fight the Carlists and cantons. On 3 January 1874, General Manuel Pavía dissolved the Cortes in a coup. A government under General Serrano followed, but on 29 December 1874 General Arsenio Martínez Campos pronounced for Alfonso, son of Isabella II, at Sagunto, bringing about the Bourbon Restoration and ending the Republic.

Legacy

The First Republic, lasting less than two years, became a byword for political fragmentation, yet it also embodied Spain's first experiment with republican and federal government and abolished slavery in Puerto Rico. The later Second Spanish Republic (1931–1939) consciously positioned itself as its successor.
