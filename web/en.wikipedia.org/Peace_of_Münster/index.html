Peace of Münster

Signed30 January 1648
LocationMünster, Westphalia
PartiesDutch Republic; Spanish Empire
ContextEighty Years' War; part of the Peace of Westphalia

The Peace of Münster was a treaty signed on 30 January 1648 between the Dutch Republic and the Spanish Empire in the Westphalian city of Münster. It formally ended the Eighty Years' War (1568–1648) between Spain and its former provinces in the Low Countries, and Spain recognised the independence of the Dutch Republic, which had declared itself free of Spanish rule in the Act of Abjuration of 1581. The treaty was part of the broader Peace of Westphalia negotiations that also concluded the Thirty Years' War in Germany with the Treaty of Münster (October 1648) between France, the Emperor, and their allies, and the Treaty of Osnabrück.

Background

By the 1640s both sides were exhausted. Spain, fighting France since 1635 and facing revolts in Catalonia and Portugal, sought to detach the Dutch from their French alliance, while Dutch merchants—especially in Holland—wanted peace to protect trade, even as Zeeland and the House of Orange leaned toward continuing the war. Negotiations at Münster, conducted with mediators including the papal nuncio Fabio Chigi (the future Pope Alexander VII), dragged on for years as Spain conceded point after point.

Terms

Spain recognised the United Provinces as free and sovereign. The Dutch retained their conquests in the East and West Indies, the closure of the Scheldt river—which throttled Antwerp's trade to Amsterdam's benefit—was confirmed, and territory in Flanders, Brabant, and Limburg ("Generality Lands") stayed in Dutch hands. Dutch merchants received favourable trading terms in Spanish ports, and the treaty effectively freed Spain to concentrate on its war with France, which continued until the Treaty of the Pyrenees in 1659.

Significance

The Peace of Münster marks the international legal recognition of the Dutch Republic, which had become a global commercial and colonial power during its long war of independence. Gerard ter Borch's painting of the swearing of the oath on 15 May 1648, when the treaty was ratified in Münster's town hall, is one of the most famous images of early modern diplomacy.
