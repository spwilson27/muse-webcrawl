The Constitution of Iraq is the supreme law of the Republic of Iraq. It was drafted in 2005 by members of the Iraqi National Assembly, approved in a nationwide referendum on 15 October 2005, and came into force with the formation of the first permanent post-invasion government in 2006.

Drafting and referendum

After the 2003 invasion of Iraq and the transitional Law of Administration for the State of Iraq (the Transitional Administrative Law), a 55-member Constitutional Committee produced a draft in the summer of 2005. Negotiators bargained intensely over federalism, the role of Islam, Kirkuk, women's rights and control of oil revenues. Sunni Arab negotiators were added late to the process, and last-minute amendments were promised to secure passage. In the referendum the constitution was approved nationally, although two provinces rejected it by more than two-thirds — just short of the three-province veto threshold.

Structure and principles

Article 1 declares Iraq a single, independent federal state with a republican, representative, parliamentary and democratic system. Islam is the official religion and a foundation source of legislation; no law may contradict the established provisions of Islam, the principles of democracy, or the rights guaranteed in the constitution. Arabic and Kurdish are official languages.

Federalism and disputes

The constitution recognises the Kurdistan Region and allows other governorates to form regions. Contentious provisions on the status of Kirkuk (Article 140) and the management of oil and gas were deferred to later legislation and remain among the most disputed parts of the document.
