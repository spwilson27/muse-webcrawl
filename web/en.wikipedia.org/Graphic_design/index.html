Graphic design

[ Image placeholder: A collage of posters, type specimens and layouts illustrating graphic design ]

Graphic design is a profession, academic discipline and applied art whose activity consists in projecting visual communications intended to transmit specific messages to social groups, with specific objectives. Graphic design is an interdisciplinary branch of design and of the fine arts. Its practice involves creativity, innovation and lateral thinking using manual or digital tools, where it is usual to use text and graphics to communicate visually.

History

The term "graphic design" was coined by William Addison Dwiggins in 1922, but the practice is far older, spanning illuminated manuscripts, heraldry, and the printing trades. Movements such as the Arts and Crafts movement, Bauhaus, Swiss Style (International Typographic Style) and postmodernism successively reshaped the field, and the arrival of desktop publishing in the 1980s transformed its tools and workflow.

Applications

Graphic designers create visual identities and branding, editorial design for books and magazines, advertising, packaging, signage and wayfinding, environmental graphics, motion graphics, and user interfaces for websites and applications. Core elements include typography, colour, layout, imagery and grid systems; key principles include balance, contrast, emphasis, hierarchy and rhythm.
