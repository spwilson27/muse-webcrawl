Congressional Budget Office

AgencyUnited States Congress
Formed1974
HeadquartersFord House Office Building, Washington, D.C.

The Congressional Budget Office (CBO) is a federal agency within the legislative branch of the United States government that provides budget and economic information to Congress. It was established in 1974 by the Congressional Budget and Impoundment Control Act and began operating in 1975; its first director was Alice Rivlin.

Role

The CBO produces independent, nonpartisan analyses of budgetary and economic issues, including cost estimates for nearly every bill reported by congressional committees, long-term budget outlooks, and baseline projections of federal revenues and spending. Its estimates of the cost of proposed legislation — commonly called "scores" — play a central role in congressional debate.

Organisation

The CBO's director is appointed jointly by the Speaker of the House and the President pro tempore of the Senate, after considering recommendations from the budget committees, for a four-year term. The agency employs roughly 270 people, mostly economists and public policy analysts, and is organised into divisions covering budget analysis, macroeconomics, health, taxation and other areas.

Independence

By law and by practice the CBO makes no policy recommendations and its staff are chosen without regard to political affiliation. Its projections of major legislation, such as health care and tax bills, are frequently politically consequential, reinforcing its institutional emphasis on transparency about methods and assumptions.
