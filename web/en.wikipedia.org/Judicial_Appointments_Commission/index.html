Judicial Appointments Commission

The Judicial Appointments Commission (JAC) is an independent non-departmental public body in the United Kingdom responsible for selecting candidates for judicial office in England and Wales, and for certain United Kingdom-wide tribunals. It was established under the Constitutional Reform Act 2005 and began work on 3 April 2006, taking over judicial selection from the Lord Chancellor in order to strengthen the independence of the appointments process and public confidence in the judiciary.

Functions

The Commission runs selection exercises for posts ranging from lay magistrates and tribunal members to High Court judges. It advertises vacancies, assesses candidates against published competencies through applications, tests, and interviews, and recommends one candidate per vacancy to the Lord Chancellor, who may accept or (subject to limited powers) reject or ask the Commission to reconsider. The JAC is under a statutory duty to select solely on merit and to have regard to the need to encourage diversity among applicants.

Composition

The Commission consists of a chair, who is a lay member, and fourteen other commissioners drawn from the judiciary, the legal professions, tribunal members, magistrates, and lay members, appointed through open competition. The first chairman was Baroness Usha Prashar. The JAC is accountable to Parliament through the Ministry of Justice, and its annual reports and selection statistics are published openly.

Background

Before 2006, judges in England and Wales were appointed by the Lord Chancellor, in practice often by invitation and confidential soundings — a system criticised as opaque and unrepresentative. The Constitutional Reform Act 2005 also created the Supreme Court of the United Kingdom and reformed the office of Lord Chancellor; judicial appointments commissions with similar functions exist for Scotland (the Judicial Appointments Board for Scotland) and Northern Ireland (the Northern Ireland Judicial Appointments Commission). Separate selection processes, involving the JAC, apply to senior posts including the senior judiciary and the Supreme Court justices.
