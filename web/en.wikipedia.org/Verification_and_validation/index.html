Verification and validation

Verification"Are we building the product right?"
Validation"Are we building the right product?"
Applied inEngineering, software, quality management

Verification and validation (V&V) are the processes by which a product, service, or system is checked against requirements and intended use. Verification confirms that a product was built correctly according to its specifications, while validation confirms that the right product was built — that it fulfils the purpose and needs of its users.

Distinction

The classic formulation is attributed to Barry Boehm: verification asks "Are we building the product right?" and validation asks "Are we building the right product?" Verification typically involves reviews, inspections, and testing against specified requirements; validation involves demonstrating performance in the intended operational environment, often with end users.

Software engineering

In software, verification includes static analysis, code review, and unit and integration testing; validation includes acceptance testing and beta use. Independent verification and validation (IV&V), in which a third party performs the checks, is used for safety-critical systems such as spacecraft and medical devices, notably by NASA.

Models and simulation

In computational science, verification of a model means confirming the equations are solved correctly (code and calculation verification), while validation means assessing how accurately the model represents the real world, by comparison with experiments.
