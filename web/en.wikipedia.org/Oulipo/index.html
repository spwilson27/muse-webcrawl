Oulipo

Oulipo (short for French: Ouvroir de littérature potentielle, roughly "workshop of potential literature") is a loose gathering of French-speaking writers and mathematicians who seek to create works using constrained writing techniques.

History

The group was founded on 24 November 1960 by writer Raymond Queneau and mathematician François Le Lionnais, initially as a seminar under the patronage of the Collège de 'Pataphysique. Oulipo members — who style themselves "rats who build the labyrinth from which they plan to escape" — devise formal constraints, such as lipograms and palindromes, as triggers for creativity.

Techniques and works

Famous Oulipian works include Queneau's Cent mille milliards de poèmes (1961), a book of ten sonnets whose interchangeable lines allow 1014 possible poems, and Georges Perec's novel La Disparition (1969), written entirely without the letter "e", and his La Vie mode d'emploi (1978), structured by a knight's tour of a Paris apartment block. Other notable members include Italo Calvino, Marcel Duchamp, Harry Mathews, and Jacques Roubaud. Well-known constraints include the N+7 method (replacing each noun with the seventh noun after it in a dictionary), snowballs, and univocalics.

Legacy

Oulipo continues to meet monthly in Paris and has inspired sister groups devoted to constrained painting, music, comics, and cinema (the "Ou-x-po" family). Its playful fusion of mathematics and literature has influenced experimental writing worldwide.
