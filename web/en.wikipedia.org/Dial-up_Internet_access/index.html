Dial-up Internet access

Dial-up Internet access is a form of Internet access that uses the public switched telephone network to establish a connection to an Internet service provider by dialing a telephone number on a conventional telephone line with a modem.

Technology

A dial-up connection converts computer data into audio signals sent over a voice line. Early modems ran at 300 baud; standards improved through V.32 (9.6 kbit/s) to V.90 and V.92, which reached nominal 56 kbit/s downstream. Connection is temporary — the user dials in, the modems negotiate ("handshake") with a characteristic sequence of tones, and the line is occupied for the duration of the session, preventing simultaneous voice calls on the same line.

History

Dial-up was the dominant means of home Internet access through the 1990s, provided by companies such as AOL, CompuServe, and local ISPs using protocols such as SLIP and PPP. From the early 2000s, broadband technologies (DSL, cable, fibre) displaced it in most developed countries. Dial-up survives in some rural areas and niche uses, and major providers have progressively shut down their services.
