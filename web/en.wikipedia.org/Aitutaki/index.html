Aitutaki

Aitutaki, traditionally known as Ararau, is one of the Cook Islands in the South Pacific Ocean. Sometimes described as a "half-atoll", it consists of a main volcanic island partly surrounded by a triangular barrier reef enclosing a turquoise lagoon dotted with small uninhabited islets (motu). It lies about 264 kilometres north of Rarotonga, the main island of the Cook Islands.

Geography

The main island covers about 16.8 km², rising to 123 metres at Maunga Pu. The lagoon, roughly 50 km², is renowned for its clarity and marine life and is a centrepiece of the island's tourism. One Foot Island (Tapuaetai) in the lagoon is famous for its beach passport stamp, often cited among the world's most beautiful beaches.

History and population

Polynesian tradition holds that the island was settled by voyagers from Raiatea; Captain William Bligh sighted it in 1789 shortly before the mutiny on the Bounty. Missionaries arrived in 1821, making Aitutaki an early centre of Christianity in the Cook Islands. The population is around 1,800. During the Second World War, American forces built the island's airstrip, which still serves Air Rarotonga flights.

Economy

Tourism centred on the lagoon is the mainstay, supplemented by fishing and small-scale agriculture. The island has appeared in reality television, including series of Survivor and Shipwrecked filmed on its motu.
