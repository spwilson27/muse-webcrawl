The European Space Agency (ESA) is an intergovernmental organisation of European states dedicated to the exploration of space. It was established in 1975 and has its headquarters in Paris, France. ESA's space flight programme includes human spaceflight, mainly through participation in the International Space Station; the launch and operation of unmanned exploration missions to other planets and the Moon; Earth observation; science; and telecommunication.

ESA maintains the Guiana Space Centre at Kourou, French Guiana, as its primary launch site. Its main technical centre, ESTEC, is in Noordwijk, the Netherlands, and mission operations (ESOC) are in Darmstadt, Germany.

History

ESA was created by merging the European Launcher Development Organisation (ELDO) and the European Space Research Organisation (ESRO). Early successes included the Ariane rocket family and the Spacelab module flown on the Space Shuttle. Notable missions include Giotto (Halley's Comet, 1986), Hipparcos and Gaia (astrometry), Rosetta (the first comet orbiter and lander, 2014), Planck (cosmic microwave background), ExoMars, and Juice, launched in 2023 to study Jupiter's icy moons.

Member states and funding

ESA's funding comes from contributions of its member states, scaled roughly by GDP, plus funds from the European Union for programmes such as Galileo (navigation) and Copernicus (Earth observation). ESA is independent of the EU, though the two cooperate closely.

Programmes

  • Human and robotic exploration — ISS participation, European astronauts, ExoMars
  • Earth observation — Copernicus Sentinels, meteorological satellites
  • Navigation — Galileo global navigation satellite system
  • Science — astronomy and Solar System missions (Juice, Euclid, Solar Orbiter)
  • Launchers — Ariane 6 and Vega C

Facilities

Key establishments: HQ (Paris), ESTEC (Noordwijk), ESOC (Darmstadt), ESAC (near Madrid), ESRIN (Frascati, Italy), EAC astronaut centre (Cologne), and the Guiana Space Centre.
