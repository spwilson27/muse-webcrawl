Misotheism

Misotheism is the "hatred of God" or "hatred of the gods" (from the Greek misos, "hatred", and theos, "god"). It is a term describing a belief stance in which a person believes in one or more deities but hates them, resents them, or wishes to rebel against them, often because of the problem of evil — the suffering that an omnipotent and benevolent god would presumably not permit.

Relation to other positions

Misotheism differs from atheism, which denies the existence of gods, and from dystheism, the belief that a god is not wholly good or is outright evil. A misotheist may regard a deity as worthy of hatred rather than worship, a stance sometimes expressed in literature through characters who defy or curse God.

In literature and culture

Themes resembling misotheism appear in works such as William Blake's prophetic books, the writings of the Marquis de Sade, and in Fyodor Dostoevsky's characters who rail against God's ordering of the world. The term was also used by Thomas De Quincey and appears in discussions of "maltheism".
