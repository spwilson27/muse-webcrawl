Jean de Joinville

Jean de Joinville (c. 1224 – 24 December 1317) was one of the great chroniclers of medieval France. His Life of Saint Louis, a biography of King Louis IX written from Joinville's own close experience of the king, is a principal source for the Seventh Crusade and for the character of the sainted monarch.

Life

Joinville was born into a noble family of Champagne and became seneschal of the county, a hereditary office. In 1241 he attended the court at Saumur where he first encountered the young King Louis IX. He inherited the lordship of Joinville and married Alix de Reynel, and later Alix de Risnel.

The Seventh Crusade

In 1248 Joinville sailed with Louis on the Seventh Crusade to Egypt. He fought at the capture of Damietta and at the disastrous Battle of Mansurah (1250), where he was captured along with the king. After their ransom he accompanied Louis to the Holy Land for four years, becoming one of the king's intimates. His vivid first-person account describes battles, storms at sea, negotiations with the Saracens, and the king's personal piety and justice — including the famous image of Louis dispensing judgement beneath an oak at Vincennes.

The Life of Saint Louis

Commissioned by Queen Joan of Navarre, Joinville completed his Vie de Saint Louis in 1309, drawing on memories recorded decades earlier. Part biography, part crusade memoir, the work is prized for its lifelike dialogue and candid portraiture. Joinville, who had refused to join Louis's fatal second crusade of 1270, later testified to the king's sanctity in the canonisation process. He died at the age of about 93, having outlived nearly all his contemporaries.
