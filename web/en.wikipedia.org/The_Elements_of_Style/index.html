The Elements of Style


The fourth edition (2000), by William Strunk Jr. and E. B. White.

The Elements of Style is an American style guide for writing that was originally created by William Strunk Jr., a professor of English at Cornell University. Strunk privately printed the book in 1918 for his students; it was published commercially in 1920. In 1957 the writer E. B. White, Strunk's former student, praised it in The New Yorker and then revised and expanded it for a new edition in 1959. The Strunk and White version — often called simply "Strunk & White" — became one of the most influential and best-selling books on English usage and composition.

Contents

The guide is organized around elementary rules of usage, principles of composition, matters of form, and a list of words and expressions commonly misused. Its maxims include the celebrated "Omit needless words", "Use the active voice", "Make the paragraph the unit of composition", and "Use definite, specific, concrete language". White added an introductory chapter and a final chapter, "An Approach to Style", setting out his own views on writing.

Editions and reception

Subsequent editions appeared in 1972, 1979, and 2000, the last with an afterword by White's stepson Roger Angell; the 50th-anniversary fourth edition was issued with illustrations by Maira Kalman in 2005. The book has sold more than ten million copies. It has also attracted criticism: linguist Geoffrey Pullum attacked the fourth edition in 2009 as containing misleading advice about grammar and the passive voice, and other critics have questioned some of its prohibitions. It nevertheless remains a classroom standard and a touchstone of plain, vigorous prose.
