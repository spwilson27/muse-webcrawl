Germany–United States relations

Germany–United States relations cover the diplomatic, military and economic ties between the Federal Republic of Germany and the United States of America. The relationship has passed through phases of mass emigration and cultural exchange, two world wars in which the countries were enemies, and a close Cold War and post-Cold War alliance.

Early relations and emigration

German-speaking immigrants formed one of the largest ancestry groups in the United States, arriving from colonial times through the great 19th-century waves after the failed revolutions of 1848. The United States broke with Imperial Germany by entering the First World War in 1917, and declared war on Nazi Germany in December 1941.

Cold War alliance

After 1945 the United States occupied western Germany and supported the founding of the Federal Republic (West Germany) in 1949. The Berlin Airlift of 1948–49, Marshall Plan aid, and West Germany's accession to NATO in 1955 anchored a partnership in which tens of thousands of American troops were stationed in Germany. The two governments cooperated closely through German reunification in 1990, which the United States strongly supported.

Contemporary relations

Germany and the United States are among each other's most important trading partners and allies within NATO. Relations have periodically strained — over the 2003 Iraq War, surveillance revelations in 2013, and trade and defence-spending disputes — but both governments describe the partnership as a cornerstone of transatlantic security. The United States maintains major bases in Germany, including Ramstein Air Base.
