Dzogchen

Dzogchen (Tibetan: རྫོགས་ཆེན་, "Great Perfection" or "Great Completion") is a tradition of teachings in Indo-Tibetan Buddhism and Bön aimed at discovering and continuing to remain in the natural, primordial state of mind, known as rigpa. It is considered the highest teaching within the Nyingma school of Tibetan Buddhism.

Teachings

Dzogchen emphasises direct introduction to the nature of mind by a qualified teacher, followed by practices such as trekchö ("cutting through") and tögal ("direct crossing") that work with visionary appearances of light. Central texts include the Seventeen Tantras and the works of Longchenpa (1308–1364), the great systematiser of the tradition.

History

The teachings are traditionally traced to Garab Dorje and were transmitted in Tibet from the 8th century by figures such as Padmasambhava and Vimalamitra. The heart-essence (snying thig) lineages developed from the 11th century onward and became central to the Nyingma school.

Practice

Preliminary practices (ngöndro) usually precede Dzogchen training. The goal is recognition of rigpa — awareness that is spontaneously present and self-perfected — and its integration into all activities, ultimately culminating, in some accounts, in the "rainbow body" at death.
