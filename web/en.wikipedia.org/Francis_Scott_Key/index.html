Francis Scott Key

Francis Scott Key (August 1, 1779 – January 11, 1843) was an American lawyer, author, and poet from Frederick, Maryland, best known as the author of the text of the American national anthem, "The Star-Spangled Banner".

The Star-Spangled Banner

During the War of 1812, Key travelled to Baltimore to negotiate the release of a prisoner, Dr. William Beanes, aboard a British vessel. Detained overnight during the British bombardment of Fort McHenry on September 13–14, 1814, he witnessed at dawn that the American flag — an enormous garrison flag — still flew over the fort, and began writing the poem "Defence of Fort M'Henry" on the back of a letter. Set to the tune of "To Anacreon in Heaven", the song grew in popularity and was officially adopted as the national anthem of the United States in 1931.

Legal career

Key practised law in Frederick and Georgetown and served as United States Attorney for the District of Columbia from 1833 to 1841. He was also a slaveholder who later manumitted some enslaved people, represented enslaved petitioners in some freedom suits, and was a founding figure of the American Colonization Society — a record that has drawn modern scrutiny.
