Philip IV (Spanish: Felipe IV; 8 April 1605 – 17 September 1665) was King of Spain from 1621 to his death and (as Philip III) King of Portugal from 1621 to 1640. He is remembered for Spain's role in the Thirty Years' War, the long conflict with the Dutch Republic and France, and for his patronage of the arts, most famously of the painter Diego Velázquez.

Reign

Philip came to the throne at sixteen. For the first two decades of his reign, power was exercised largely by his favorite, Gaspar de Guzmán, Count-Duke of Olivares, who pursued an ambitious program of reform and sought to sustain Spanish predominance in Europe. The burdens of simultaneous wars against the Dutch, France, and rebels in Catalonia and Portugal strained the monarchy severely. Portugal regained its independence in 1640, ending the Iberian Union, and the Peace of Westphalia in 1648 recognized Dutch independence. The war with France continued until the Treaty of the Pyrenees in 1659, which marked the decline of Spanish hegemony in Europe.

Patronage of the arts

Philip IV was one of the great art patrons of the seventeenth century. His court painter Velázquez produced many of the king's most famous portraits, and the royal collection grew into one of Europe's finest, including works by Rubens and Titian. The Buen Retiro Palace in Madrid was built during his reign as a center of court culture, theater, and spectacle.

Death and succession

Philip died in 1665 and was succeeded by his four-year-old son Charles II, the last Habsburg king of Spain, whose reign began under a regency.
