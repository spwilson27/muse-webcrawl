Thomas Stevenson (22 July 1818 – 8 May 1887) was a pioneering Scottish civil engineer, lighthouse designer and meteorologist, and the father of the author Robert Louis Stevenson. A member of the famous Stevenson family of engineers, he worked for the Northern Lighthouse Board and, with his brother David, designed many of the lighthouses around the coasts of Scotland and beyond.

Engineering career

Stevenson was born in Edinburgh, the youngest son of engineer Robert Stevenson, builder of the Bell Rock Lighthouse. Educated at the Royal High School and the University of Edinburgh, he was apprenticed to his half-brother Alan Stevenson and in 1843 became a partner in the family firm. Together with David Stevenson he built lighthouses at sites including Whalsay Skerries, Muckle Flugga (the most northerly lighthouse in Britain, completed 1858 amid severe controversy about building in such seas), Davaar, and Chickens Rock off the Isle of Man. In Japan, his designs were carried forward by his protégé Richard Henry Brunton, the "father of Japanese lighthouses".

Scientific work

Stevenson made important contributions to optics and meteorology. He developed the "Stevenson screen", the standard ventilated shelter still used worldwide to house meteorological instruments, and devised methods for measuring wave force and for the illumination of lighthouses, improving lens and lighting arrangements. His papers on the relation between wind and waves and on harbour engineering influenced coastal engineering practice.

Family

In 1847 he married Margaret Isabella Balfour. Their only surviving child, Robert Louis Stevenson, disappointed his father's hope that he would continue the family engineering firm, choosing literature instead — though the lighthouses and engineering feats of the Stevensons recur throughout his essays and fiction. Thomas Stevenson died in Edinburgh in 1887.
