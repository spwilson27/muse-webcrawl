Learning

Learning is the process of acquiring new understanding, knowledge, behaviours, skills, values, attitudes, and preferences. The ability to learn is possessed by humans, animals, and some machines; there is also evidence for some kind of learning in certain plants. Some learning is immediate, induced by a single event, but much skill and knowledge accumulates from repeated experiences. The changes induced by learning often last a lifetime, and it is hard to distinguish learned material that seems to be "lost" from that which cannot be retrieved.

Types

Learning may occur as part of education, personal development, schooling, or training, and may be goal-oriented, aided by motivation, or incidental. Major categories include non-associative learning (habituation and sensitisation), associative learning (classical conditioning and operant conditioning), observational learning, enculturation, and play. Formal learning takes place within teacher–student relationships, while non-formal and informal learning occur in everyday settings.

Theories

Learning theory developed through the twentieth century around three broad schools: behaviourism, which focuses on observable behaviour change; cognitivism, which emphasises mental processes such as memory and problem solving; and constructivism, which holds that learners actively build new knowledge on the basis of prior understanding. In neuroscience, learning is associated with synaptic plasticity, notably the mechanisms of long-term potentiation and depression.
