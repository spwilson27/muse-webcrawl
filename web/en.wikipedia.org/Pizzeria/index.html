Pizzeria

A pizzeria is a restaurant focusing on pizza. In addition to pizzas, many pizzerias also offer other Italian dishes such as pasta, salads, and desserts. Pizzerias range from small family-run establishments with wood-fired ovens to large international delivery chains.

History

The first establishment generally recognised as a pizzeria, Antica Port'Alba in Naples, began serving customers in 1830, having operated as a stand for travellers since 1738. Naples remained the centre of pizza making, and the Associazione Verace Pizza Napoletana, founded in 1984, certifies pizzerias that follow traditional Neapolitan methods. Italian emigration spread the pizzeria worldwide; Lombardi's, opened in New York City in 1905, is often cited as the first pizzeria in the United States.

Types

Pizzerias may specialise in a regional style — Neapolitan, New York, Chicago deep-dish, Sicilian, or Roman — and in a service model: dine-in restaurants, takeaway counters, and delivery-focused shops using pizza boxes to transport orders. Some operate as fast-casual chains where customers assemble their own pizzas along a counter.

Operations

Key equipment includes a deck, conveyor, or wood-fired oven; dough preparation and refrigeration areas; and, for delivery operations, insulated transport bags. The pizzaiolo is the specialist pizza maker responsible for dough handling and baking.
