Operation Python

Operation Python
Date: Night of 4/5 June and 8/9 June 1971
Location: Karachi, Pakistan
Part of: Indo-Pakistani War of 1971
Result: Indian success; heavy damage to Karachi fuel storage

Operation Python was the codename of a naval attack launched by the Indian Navy on the port city of Karachi, Pakistan, during the Indo-Pakistani War of 1971. It was carried out on the night of 8/9 December 1971 by a strike group of the Western Naval Command, following the successful Operation Trident four nights earlier.

The attack

The strike group included the missile boat INS Vinash and two frigates, INS Talwar and INS Trishul. Indian missile boats fired Styx missiles at ships and shore installations near Karachi, striking the fleet tanker PNS Dacca and setting ablaze the oil storage tanks at Keamari, whose fires burned for days. Bad weather had delayed the operation by a day, and in the confusion of the first night's attempt a merchant vessel was mistakenly engaged.

Aftermath

The two strikes on Karachi effectively neutralized the Pakistan Navy in the west for the remainder of the war, leaving Indian naval forces in control of the Arabian Sea approaches. Pakistan moved much of its fleet and relied on dispersing merchant shipping, while Karachi's fuel reserves suffered severe losses.
