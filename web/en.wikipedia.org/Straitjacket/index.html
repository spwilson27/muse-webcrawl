Straitjacket

A straitjacket (also spelled strait-jacket) is a garment of strong canvas with long sleeves that can be tied behind the wearer's back, used to restrain the arms of a person who may otherwise harm themselves or others. It became emblematic of nineteenth- and early twentieth-century psychiatric asylums.

History

Restraint garments were described in the eighteenth century and widely adopted in asylums as a supposedly more humane alternative to chains and manacles. Their use declined sharply from the mid-twentieth century with the advent of antipsychotic medication, changing standards of patient rights, and modern restraint regulations; in most jurisdictions their use is now rare and tightly restricted.

In culture

Escape artists, most famously Harry Houdini, made escaping from a straitjacket — sometimes while suspended upside down — a signature feat; escapologists use dislocated shoulders and slack in the sleeves to work free. The word is also used metaphorically for anything that severely restricts freedom of action ("a regulatory straitjacket").
