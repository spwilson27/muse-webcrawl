Cheese curd

Cheese curds
RegionQuebec, Canada; Upper Midwest, US
Main ingredientsCurdled milk
TextureSpringy, squeaky when fresh

Cheese curds are moist pieces of curdled milk, eaten either alone as a snack or as an ingredient in dishes such as poutine. They are produced during cheesemaking when the curd is separated from the whey but before it is pressed and aged into hard cheese.

Characteristics

Fresh curds have a mild, salty flavour and a springy, rubbery texture. Their most famous trait is the "squeak" heard when bitten, caused by the elastic protein network of fresh casein rubbing against tooth enamel; the squeak fades within a day or so as the curds age and absorb moisture. In Quebec, curds (fromage en grains) are a canteen staple, and in Wisconsin and Minnesota they are sold at fairs and creameries, often breaded and deep-fried.

Poutine

Curds are an essential component of poutine, the Quebec dish of fries topped with cheese curds and brown gravy, which emerged in rural Quebec in the 1950s. The hot gravy slightly melts the curds. Outside Quebec, mozzarella is sometimes substituted, but purists insist on proper squeaky curds.
