Opéra Bastille

Location: Place de la Bastille, Paris, France

Opened: 13 July 1989

Architect: Carlos Ott

Capacity: 2,723 (Grand Salle)

Company: Paris Opera

The Opéra Bastille is a modern opera house in Paris, France. Inaugurated in 1989 as part of President François Mitterrand's Grands Projets, it is the main facility of the Paris Opera, the other being the historic Palais Garnier. Located at the Place de la Bastille in the 12th arrondissement, on the site of the former Gare de la Bastille railway station, the building seats 2,723 in its Grand Salle and is noted for its monumental modern architecture, its technical stage facilities, and its integrated workshops for scenery and costumes.

History

In 1982 President Mitterrand decided to build a new, "modern and popular" opera house in Paris, intended to relieve the Palais Garnier and broaden opera's audience. An international competition in 1983 selected the design of Uruguayan-born architect Carlos Ott. Construction began in 1984; the house was inaugurated on 13 July 1989, the eve of the bicentenary of the storming of the Bastille, with a gala concert conducted by Georges Prêtre, although regular opera performances began in 1990 with Berlioz's Les Troyens.

Architecture and facilities

The building is dominated by a curved façade of glass and stone tiles. Behind the Grand Salle lie an amphitheatre of about 450 seats, a studio theatre (Salle Gounod), and the vast backstage "opera factory" — workshops for scenery, costumes, and wigs, with stage-sized rehearsal spaces that allow entire productions to be assembled and moved on tracks. The main stage can hold nine sets simultaneously on mobile platforms. The stone cladding of the façade has been troubled by falling tiles, which led to safety netting and later renovation works.

The Paris Opera today

The Paris Opera divides its repertoire between its two houses: large-scale opera productions are generally staged at the Bastille, while the Palais Garnier hosts mainly ballet. The Bastille's size and acoustics, sometimes criticised as cold, allow it to mount the grandest Romantic and modern repertory, and the house remains one of the largest opera stages in the world.
