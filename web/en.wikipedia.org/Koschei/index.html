Koschei


Koschei the Deathless, a villain of Russian folklore whose death is hidden outside his body.

Koschei (Russian: Коще́й, Koshchey), often given the epithet "the Deathless" or "the Immortal", is a stock villain in Russian and Slavic folklore — typically a gaunt, ugly old man of great magical power who abducts the hero's beloved.

The hidden death

Koschei cannot be killed by ordinary means because his soul (or death) is separated from his body and hidden: in the best-known tales it is inside a needle, which is inside an egg, which is inside a duck, which is inside a hare, which is inside an iron chest buried beneath an oak tree on the island of Buyan. Only by finding the needle and breaking it can the hero destroy him. Tales featuring Koschei include "The Death of Koschei the Deathless", featuring Prince Ivan and Marya Morevna, and he also appears in stories connected with the Firebird.

In later culture

The figure has inspired works including Stravinsky's ballet The Firebird, Rimsky-Korsakov's opera Kashchey the Deathless, and many modern fantasy novels, games and films, where "Koschei the Deathless" frequently appears as an archetypal lich-like sorcerer.
