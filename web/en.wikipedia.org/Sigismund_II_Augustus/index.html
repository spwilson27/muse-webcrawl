Sigismund II Augustus (1520–1572) was King of Poland and Grand Duke of Lithuania from 1548 until his death, and the last male member of the Jagiellonian dynasty. His reign saw the Union of Lublin (1569), which created the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth.

Reign

Son of Sigismund I the Old and Bona Sforza, he was crowned vivente rege in 1530. He contended with the executionist movement of the Sejm demanding reform, navigated the Reformation's spread in Poland, and maintained relative religious tolerance. His secret marriage to Barbara Radziwiłł caused a major political crisis.

Union of Lublin and Livonian War

To secure Lithuania's defence against Muscovy in the Livonian War and settle succession, he pressed through the 1569 union, transferring Ukrainian territories from Lithuania to the Polish Crown. He also built a Baltic naval presence and annexed Livonia.

Legacy

A Renaissance patron famed for his tapestry collection at Wawel, he died childless at Knyszyn; the Commonwealth then became an elective monarchy.
