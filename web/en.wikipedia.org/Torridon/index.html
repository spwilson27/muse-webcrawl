Torridon (Scottish Gaelic: Toirbheartan) is a small village in the Northwest Highlands of Scotland. The village lies on the shore of Loch Torridon at the head of the sea loch, in Wester Ross, and the name is also applied to the area of dramatic mountain scenery around it. The Torridon Hills — including Liathach, Beinn Eighe and Beinn Alligin — are among the most celebrated mountains in Britain, built from ancient Torridonian sandstone resting on even older Lewisian gneiss.

Geology

The Torridon area is of major significance in British geology. The mountains consist largely of Torridonian sandstone, a thick succession of red-brown sedimentary rocks laid down by rivers roughly a billion years ago in the Proterozoic, resting unconformably on Lewisian gneiss basement approaching three billion years old — some of the oldest rock in Europe. The sandstone weathers into distinctive terraced, castellated peaks separated by deep glens.

Mountains and conservation

Liathach (1,055 m), with its pinnacled main ridge, and Beinn Alligin (986 m) are both Munros, while Beinn Eighe (1,010 m) was designated in 1951 as Britain's first National Nature Reserve, protecting one of the largest remnants of ancient Scots pinewood at Coille na Glas Leitire. The northern part of the area lies within the Beinn Eighe and Loch Maree Islands National Nature Reserve and the Wester Ross National Scenic Area. The Torridon estate, including the village, is managed in part by the National Trust for Scotland, which runs a countryside centre there.

The village

The village developed as a crofting and estate settlement and now serves walkers, climbers and tourists travelling the North Coast 500 route along the A896 road. It contains a youth hostel, hotel, community hall and the Torridon Countryside Centre. Nearby settlements include Shieldaig, Annat, Inveralligin and Diabaig, the last reached by a minor road noted for its steep gradients and views.
