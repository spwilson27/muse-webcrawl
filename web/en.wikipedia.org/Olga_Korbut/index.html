Olga Korbut

Olga Valentinovna Korbut
Born: 16 May 1955, Grodno, Byelorussian SSR
Height: 4 ft 11 in (1.50 m)
Country: Soviet Union
Sport: Artistic gymnastics

Olga Valentinovna Korbut (born 16 May 1955) is a retired Soviet gymnast who won four gold medals and two silver medals at the 1972 and 1976 Summer Olympics. Nicknamed the "Sparrow from Minsk," she revolutionised gymnastics with her daring acrobatic skills and charismatic performances, popularising the sport worldwide.

Career

Coached by Renald Knysh in Grodno, Korbut first gained attention at the 1969 USSR Championships. At the 1972 Munich Olympics, aged 17, she won gold in the team event, balance beam and floor exercise, and silver on the uneven bars. Her repertoire included the Korbut flip — a backflip performed standing on the high bar of the uneven bars, catching the bar again — and a backflip-to-catch on the balance beam. The Korbut flip on bars was later banned because standing on the high bar was prohibited.

At the 1976 Montreal Olympics she won team gold and a silver on the balance beam, finishing in the shadow of Nadia Comăneci. She retired in 1977.

Later life

Korbut emigrated to the United States in 1991 and became a US citizen in 2000. She worked as a coach and motivational speaker. In 1988 she was among the first inductees into the International Gymnastics Hall of Fame. In later interviews she spoke about abuse within the Soviet gymnastics system, and in 2017 she auctioned several of her medals.
