Athletics (sport)

Track and field is the core discipline of athletics.

Athletics is a group of sporting events that involves competitive running, jumping, throwing, and walking. The most common types of athletics competitions are track and field, road running, cross-country running, and racewalking. In North American usage, "athletics" often refers to any sport in general, while track and field is the more common term for the disciplines described here.

History

Organised athletics traces its origins to the Ancient Olympic Games, first recorded in 776 BC in Olympia, Greece. The modern sport took shape in nineteenth-century England, and the International Association of Athletics Federations (IAAF) was founded in 1912; the body renamed itself World Athletics in 2019. Athletics has been a core part of the Summer Olympic Games since the first modern Games in 1896.

Events

Track events include sprints (100 m, 200 m, 400 m), middle- and long-distance races, hurdles, steeplechase, and relays. Field events comprise jumps (long jump, triple jump, high jump, pole vault) and throws (shot put, discus, hammer, javelin). Combined events are the decathlon (men) and heptathlon (women). Road events include the marathon.
