Disintegration is the eighth studio album by English rock band the Cure, released on 2 May 1989 by Fiction Records. Co-produced by band member Robert Smith and David M. Allen, the album features the return to the introspective gothic rock style the band had established in the early 1980s. As he neared the age of 30, Smith felt pressured to follow up on the band's pop successes with a more enduring work.

Background and recording

Following the commercial success of Kiss Me, Kiss Me, Kiss Me (1987), Smith deliberately moved away from pop material, returning to the atmospheric soundscapes of earlier albums such as Pornography (1982). The album was recorded at Hookend Studios in Oxfordshire. Keyboardist Roger O'Donnell joined as a full member, while founding member Lol Tolhurst was dismissed during recording due to alcohol problems.

Music and lyrics

The album is characterised by layered synthesisers, reverberant guitars, slow tempos and melancholic lyrics dealing with love, loss and mortality. Lead single "Lovesong" became the band's biggest hit in the United States, reaching number two on the Billboard Hot 100. Other singles included "Pictures of You", "Fascination Street" and "Lullaby".

Reception and legacy

Disintegration peaked at number three on the UK Albums Chart and number 12 on the US Billboard 200. It has sold more than three million copies worldwide and is frequently ranked among the greatest albums of all time. A deluxe edition was released in 2010, and the album was performed in full at concerts marking its 30th anniversary in 2019.
