Fabry–Pérot interferometer

In optics, a Fabry–Pérot interferometer (FPI) or etalon is an optical cavity made from two parallel reflecting surfaces (i.e. thin mirrors). Optical waves can pass through the optical cavity only when they are on resonance with it. It is named after Charles Fabry and Alfred Pérot, who developed the instrument in 1899.

Theory

The transmission of an etalon as a function of wavelength follows an Airy function: peaks are narrow and regularly spaced in frequency, giving the device high spectral resolution. The resolving power depends on the reflectivity of the mirrors and the spacing between them.

Applications

Fabry–Pérot interferometers are used in spectroscopy, lasers (as the resonant cavity), wavelength-division multiplexing filters, and in gravitational-wave detectors such as LIGO, where Fabry–Pérot cavities in the arms greatly increase the effective optical path length.
