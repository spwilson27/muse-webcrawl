Muslim conquest of the Levant

The conquest of Byzantine Syria, 634–638

The Muslim conquest of the Levant (Arabic: فَتْحُ الشَّام), also known as the conquest of Syria, was the invasion of Byzantine Syria and Palestine by the armies of the Rashidun Caliphate between 634 and 638 AD, one of the opening campaigns of the early Muslim conquests.

Background

The Byzantine Empire and Sasanian Persia had exhausted each other in the war of 602–628, and plague and religious tensions had weakened Byzantine control of the Levant. Following the death of Muhammad in 632, Caliph Abu Bakr and his successor Umar dispatched armies north under commanders including Khalid ibn al-Walid, Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah, and Amr ibn al-As.

Course of the conquest

Muslim forces entered southern Palestine in 634, defeated the Byzantines at Ajnadayn, and took Damascus in 635. The decisive Battle of Yarmouk in August 636 destroyed the main Byzantine field army, after which Jerusalem surrendered to Umar in 637/638 and the remaining coastal cities fell by about 640.

Aftermath

The Levant became the province of Bilad al-Sham, later the heartland of the Umayyad Caliphate with its capital at Damascus. The conquest permanently removed the region from Byzantine rule and began its gradual Arabisation and Islamisation.
