Trans-Neptunian object

A trans-Neptunian object (TNO) is any minor planet or dwarf planet in the Solar System that orbits the Sun at a greater average distance than Neptune, whose orbit lies at about 30 astronomical units (AU). The region beyond Neptune contains the Kuiper belt, the scattered disc and the remote Oort cloud. Thousands of TNOs have been catalogued, including the dwarf planets Pluto, Eris, Haumea, Makemake and Sedna-like detached objects.

Classification

  • Classical Kuiper belt objects (cubewanos) — in low-eccentricity orbits between about 42 and 48 AU, subdivided into "hot" and "cold" populations.
  • Resonant objects — locked in orbital resonances with Neptune; the 3:2 resonance group (plutinos, including Pluto itself) is the most populous.
  • Scattered-disc objects — highly eccentric orbits extending to hundreds of AU, scattered by Neptune; Eris is the largest known.
  • Detached objects — perihelia too distant for Neptune's influence, such as Sedna; their origins may implicate a hypothetical Planet Nine or stellar encounters.

Discovery and exploration

Pluto, found by Clyde Tombaugh in 1930, was the only known TNO until the discovery of (15760) Albion in 1992 by David Jewitt and Jane Luu, which revealed the Kuiper belt. Large surveys since then have found thousands of bodies, and the colour and binary studies of TNOs illuminate the early Solar System's composition. NASA's New Horizons spacecraft flew past Pluto in 2015 and the small classical object Arrokoth in 2019 — the most distant object ever visited.
