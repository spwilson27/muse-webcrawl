Electronic speed control

An electronic speed control (ESC) is an electronic circuit that controls and regulates the speed of an electric motor. It may also provide reversing of the motor and dynamic braking.

Function

Miniature ESCs are used in electrically powered radio-controlled models; full-size electric vehicles also have systems termed speed controllers. For brushed motors the ESC varies the average voltage by pulse-width modulation; for brushless motors it additionally sequences power to the motor's phases, sensing rotor position from back-EMF or Hall sensors.

Features

Many ESCs include a battery eliminator circuit supplying power to the receiver and servos, programmable throttle curves, low-voltage cutoffs to protect lithium batteries, and thermal protection. ESCs are rated by the maximum current they can handle.

Applications

ESCs are essential to drones and multirotors — where each rotor has its own controller responding hundreds of times per second — as well as model aircraft, boats, and cars.
