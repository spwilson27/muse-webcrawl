Robert F. Wagner Jr.

Robert F. Wagner Jr., Mayor of New York City 1954–1965

Robert Ferdinand Wagner Jr. (April 20, 1910 – February 12, 1991) was an American diplomat and Democratic politician who served as the 102nd mayor of New York City from 1954 to 1965. He was the son of Senator Robert F. Wagner, author of the National Labor Relations Act.

Early life and career

Born in Manhattan, Wagner was educated at Loyola School, Phillips Exeter Academy, Yale University and Yale Law School. He served in the New York State Assembly, as a city tax commissioner, and in the Army Air Forces during the Second World War, before winning election as Manhattan borough president in 1949.

Mayor of New York

Elected mayor in 1953 in a break with the Tammany Hall machine, Wagner won three terms. His administration granted collective bargaining rights to city employees, expanded public housing and university provision, and oversaw major construction. He also faced rising crime, fiscal strain and criticism over urban renewal programmes led by Robert Moses, whose power Wagner curtailed.

Later life

After leaving office Wagner served as United States Ambassador to Spain (1968–1969) and as a personal envoy of President Jimmy Carter to the Vatican. The Robert F. Wagner Graduate School of Public Service at New York University is named in his honour.
