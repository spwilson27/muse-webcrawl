Epic of King Gesar

The Epic of King Gesar is an epic cycle of Central and East Asia, celebrated in Tibet, Mongolia, and among peoples of the Himalayas and Inner Asia. It concerns the fearless lord Gesar, a divine hero sent from heaven to defeat demons and monsters, subdue the enemies of the Buddhist teaching, and establish peace. Believed to have developed over many centuries of oral transmission, it is often described as the longest epic in the world, with some versions estimated at more than a million verses.

King Gesar depicted in a traditional Tibetan thangka

Content

The hero is born miraculously, is exiled as a boy, wins a horse race that makes him king of the kingdom of Ling, and then undertakes a series of campaigns against demon kings in the four directions, descending even to the underworld. Episodes vary greatly between regional traditions and individual storytellers, and new chapters were added over the centuries.

Transmission

The epic survives primarily as an oral tradition performed by trained bards, some of whom claimed to recite episodes received in dreams. Written versions exist in Tibetan and Mongolian, including woodblock prints from the 18th century. In 2009 the tradition was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
