Odesa

Country: Ukraine
Oblast: Odesa Oblast
Founded: 1794
Population: approx. 1 million

Odesa (Ukrainian: Одеса) is the third most populous city and municipality in Ukraine and a major seaport and transport hub located in the south-west of the country, on the northwestern shore of the Black Sea. The city is the administrative centre of Odesa Oblast and of Odesa Raion. Historically known for its cosmopolitan population, Odesa is often called the "Pearl of the Black Sea".

History

The site was an ancient Greek colony and later the Tatar and Ottoman settlement of Khadjibey. After Russia captured the area in the Russo-Turkish War, Catherine the Great founded Odesa in 1794, naming it after the ancient colony of Odessos. As a free port from 1819, the city grew rapidly into the Russian Empire's great grain-exporting harbour and a famously multi-ethnic city of Ukrainians, Russians, Jews, Greeks, Italians and others. Odesa featured prominently in the Revolution of 1905, when the mutiny aboard the battleship Potemkin was later immortalised in Sergei Eisenstein's film.

Modern city

Landmarks include the Potemkin Stairs, the Opera and Ballet Theatre, and Deribasivska Street. The historic centre was inscribed on UNESCO's World Heritage List in 2023, simultaneously being placed on the list of sites in danger because of the Russian invasion of Ukraine, during which the city has been repeatedly struck by missiles and drones while remaining Ukraine's principal working seaport for grain exports.

Economy

The Port of Odesa and nearby Chornomorsk and Pivdennyi form Ukraine's largest port complex, handling grain, metals and containers, alongside ship repair, food processing and tourism.
