Gecko

InfraorderGekkota
Families7 recognised families
SpeciesOver 1,500 described species
RangeWorldwide in warm climates, all continents except Antarctica

Geckos are small, mostly carnivorous lizards of the infraorder Gekkota, found on every continent except Antarctica. Most geckos are nocturnal and range in length from about 1.6 cm to 60 cm. They are famous for their ability to climb smooth vertical surfaces and even walk across ceilings.

Adhesive toe pads

Many gecko species have specialised toe pads covered with millions of microscopic hair-like structures called setae, which branch into hundreds of nanoscale spatulae. These create strong adhesion through van der Waals forces, letting a gecko support many times its own body weight on a single toe. The adhesion is dry and self-cleaning, and has inspired synthetic adhesives and climbing robots.

Characteristics

Unlike most lizards, geckos cannot blink: their eyes are protected by a transparent scale that they clean with their tongues. Many species are vocal, producing chirps, clicks and barks used in social interaction — the name "gecko" itself imitates the call of the Southeast Asian tokay gecko. Most geckos lay small, hard-shelled eggs, though a few bear live young. Geckos can shed their tails (autotomy) to escape predators and later regenerate them. Several species, such as the mourning gecko, reproduce by parthenogenesis.

Distribution and human relations

Geckos inhabit warm climates worldwide, from rainforests and deserts to houses, where house geckos are welcomed for eating insects. Many species, including leopard geckos and crested geckos, are popular in the pet trade.
