Sukhavati (Sanskrit: Sukhāvatī, "the Blissful [Land]"; Chinese: 極樂, Jílè; Japanese: Gokuraku) is the western Pure Land of the Buddha Amitābha in Mahayana Buddhism. It is described in the Larger and Shorter Sukhāvatīvyūha Sūtras and the Amitayurdhyana Sutra as a land of immeasurable beauty, created by the fulfilled vows of Amitabha, where beings experience no suffering and can progress toward enlightenment without falling back — hence it is a central focus of Pure Land Buddhism.

Description in the sutras

According to the sutras, Sukhavati lies countless buddha-fields to the west. Its ground is level and made of gold; it is adorned with jewelled trees, lotus ponds of fragrant water, and celestial music. Beings are reborn there from lotus buds, suffer no pain or old age, and hear the Dharma from birds, trees, and Amitabha himself. Lifespans there are immeasurably long, and rebirth in Sukhavati guarantees eventual Buddhahood, since conditions for regression are absent.

Path to rebirth

Devotees aspire to rebirth in Sukhavati through faith in Amitabha's Primal Vow — his promise that any being who sincerely entrusts themselves to him and calls his name even ten times will be born in his land. Practices include recitation of the Buddha's name (nianfo in Chinese, nembutsu in Japanese), visualisation, and merit-making. Schools such as Chinese Pure Land, Japanese Jōdo-shū and Jōdo Shinshū are centred on this aspiration.
