No-hitter

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A no-hitter is a baseball game in which a team fails to record a single hit. In Major League Baseball, an official no-hit game occurs when a pitcher (or pitchers) allows no hits during the entire course of a game of at least nine innings. Base runners may still reach base via walks, errors, hit batsmen or other means, so a no-hitter is not necessarily a perfect game, in which no batter reaches base at all.

History and notable achievements

More than 300 no-hitters have been thrown in Major League history. Nolan Ryan holds the record with seven in his career, ahead of Sandy Koufax with four. Johnny Vander Meer is the only pitcher to throw no-hitters in consecutive starts, doing so in June 1938. Don Larsen pitched the only World Series no-hitter — a perfect game — for the New York Yankees against the Brooklyn Dodgers in 1956. Roy Halladay threw a second postseason no-hitter in the 2010 National League Division Series. Combined no-hitters, in which multiple pitchers from one team share the feat, have become increasingly common with modern bullpen usage.

Definition and near misses

Under a 1991 redefinition by MLB's Committee for Statistical Accuracy, games shortened by weather and games in which a team is held hitless through nine innings but records a hit in extra innings no longer count as official no-hitters, a ruling that removed several dozen previously recognized feats. A pitcher can lose a no-hitter, as when runs score without a hit, and can even throw a no-hitter in a losing effort in a shortened road game.
