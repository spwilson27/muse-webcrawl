Benedict of Nursia

Benedict of Nursia (c. 480 – c. 547), often known as Saint Benedict, was an Italian Christian monk and abbot, founder of the Benedictine order and author of the Rule of Saint Benedict, the most influential rule for Western monasticism.

Life

Born in Nursia (modern Norcia) in Umbria, Benedict studied in Rome before withdrawing to a cave at Subiaco to live as a hermit. He later founded twelve monasteries and, around 529, the great abbey of Monte Cassino.

The Rule

His Rule balanced prayer, work and reading — summarised as ora et labora — under an abbot, with vows of stability, obedience and conversion of life. It became the standard for Western monasteries for centuries.

Legacy

Benedict is a patron saint of Europe. His feast day is 11 July in the Roman calendar.
