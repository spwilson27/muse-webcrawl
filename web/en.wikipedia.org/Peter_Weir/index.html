Peter Weir

Peter Lindsay Weir (born 21 August 1944) is an Australian film director. A leading figure of the Australian New Wave of the 1970s, he achieved international success with films blending mystery, atmosphere and philosophical themes. He was nominated six times for the Academy Awards and received an Academy Honorary Award in 2022.

Australian films

After work in Australian television, Weir came to prominence with the eerie mystery Picnic at Hanging Rock (1975), followed by The Last Wave (1977), the war epic Gallipoli (1981), starring Mel Gibson, and the romantic thriller The Year of Living Dangerously (1982), set in Indonesia during the 1965 upheaval.

Hollywood career

Weir's American films include Witness (1985) with Harrison Ford, Dead Poets Society (1989) with Robin Williams, the romantic comedy Green Card (1990), the satire The Truman Show (1998) with Jim Carrey — now regarded as a prescient classic about reality television and surveillance — and the seafaring epic Master and Commander: The Far Side of the World (2003). His final film was The Way Back (2010), about escapees from a Siberian gulag.

Style and honours

Weir's work is noted for its contemplative pacing, striking imagery, and recurring explorations of isolated communities, cultural collision and the numinous. He announced his retirement from directing in the 2010s; the Academy Honorary Award of 2022 recognised his lifetime contribution to cinema.
