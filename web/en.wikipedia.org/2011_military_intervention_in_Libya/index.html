2011 military intervention in Libya

The 2011 military intervention in Libya was a multistate military intervention in the Libyan Civil War, carried out under United Nations Security Council Resolution 1973, adopted on 17 March 2011. The resolution authorised member states to establish a no-fly zone over Libya and to take "all necessary measures" to protect civilians.

Background

Protests against the rule of Muammar Gaddafi began in February 2011 as part of the Arab Spring and escalated into civil war. As government forces advanced on the rebel-held city of Benghazi, the Security Council authorised intervention.

Operations

Coalition operations began on 19 March 2011 with French air strikes and US and British Tomahawk missile strikes against Libyan air defences. NATO assumed command of the operation, named Operation Unified Protector, on 31 March 2011. Air and missile strikes targeted Gaddafi's forces throughout the campaign.

Outcome

Rebel forces captured Tripoli in August 2011. Gaddafi was captured and killed in Sirte on 20 October 2011. NATO formally ended the operation on 31 October 2011.

Reactions and criticism

The intervention was praised by supporters as preventing a massacre in Benghazi, but critics argued it exceeded the civilian-protection mandate and contributed to instability in Libya after the war.
