House music

House is a genre of electronic dance music characterised by a repetitive "four-on-the-floor" beat, typically 120–130 beats per minute, with a bass drum on every beat, off-beat hi-hats, and synthesised basslines. It originated in Chicago in the early 1980s and became the first mass form of electronic dance music, spawning countless subgenres worldwide.

Origins

House is named after the Warehouse club, where resident DJ Frankie Knuckles — the "Godfather of House" — blended disco, soul, and European electronic records for a predominantly Black and gay audience. Cheap drum machines and synthesisers such as the Roland TR-808, TR-909, and TB-303 let local producers make their own tracks: Mr. Fingers' deep, jazzy productions and Phuture's "Acid Tracks" (1987), which invented acid house, were foundational.

Spread and subgenres

House crossed to Britain and Europe in the late 1980s, fuelling the rave scene and the 1988 "Second Summer of Love." Subgenres include deep house, tech house, progressive house, garage house, and French house. The genre repeatedly fed mainstream pop, and its four-on-the-floor template underlies most modern EDM.
