Mount Tai (Chinese: Tàishān) is a mountain of historical and cultural significance in Shandong province, China, just north of the city of Tai'an. Its highest point, the Emperor Jade Peak, rises 1,545 metres above sea level. It is the foremost of the Five Great Mountains of China.

History

Mount Tai has been a place of worship for at least 3,000 years. Emperors of successive dynasties climbed the mountain to perform the Feng and Shan sacrifices, declaring their mandate heaven-approved; 72 emperors are said to have ascended. Over 1,800 stone inscriptions and cliff carvings cover the mountain, including calligraphy by emperors and poets.

Ascent

The eastern route climbs more than 6,000 stone steps from Dai Temple in Tai'an past the Midway Gate to Heaven (Zhongtianmen) to the South Gate to Heaven (Nantianmen) near the summit, where temples, guesthouses and the Jade Emperor Temple stand. Sunrise from the summit is a traditional high point of the visit.
