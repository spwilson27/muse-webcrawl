Somerset House Conference


The Somerset House Conference, 1604, depicting the English and Spanish delegations

The Somerset House Conference was a diplomatic meeting held in London in 1604 between representatives of England and Spain, together with delegates from the Spanish Netherlands. The negotiations produced the Treaty of London, signed on 28 August 1604 (New Style), which ended the nineteen-year Anglo-Spanish War (1585–1604).

Background

The war, fought during the reigns of Elizabeth I of England and Philip II of Spain, had continued inconclusively since the Spanish Armada of 1588. After Elizabeth's death in 1603, her successor James I sought peace, as did Philip III of Spain, both crowns being financially exhausted.

Negotiations

The conference met at Somerset House on the Strand. The English delegation included Thomas Sackville, Earl of Dorset; Charles Howard, Earl of Nottingham; and Robert Cecil. The Spanish and Flemish delegation included the Constable of Castile, Juan Fernández de Velasco, and Charles de Ligne, Count of Arenberg. A group portrait of the two delegations seated at a long table survives and is attributed to Juan Pantoja de la Cruz.

Terms

Under the treaty, Spain abandoned plans to restore Catholicism in England by force and recognised the Protestant monarchy, while England ended its support for the Dutch Revolt and its privateering attacks on Spanish shipping. Trade between the two kingdoms was reopened, and English merchants were permitted to operate in Spain without molestation by the Inquisition for religious reasons, provided they caused no scandal.
