Weight stigma

Weight stigma, also known as weight bias or weight discrimination, is the social devaluation and denigration of individuals because of their excess body weight. It manifests as negative stereotypes — such as assumptions of laziness or lack of willpower — and as unfair treatment in employment, healthcare, education and interpersonal relationships.

Prevalence

Studies in the United States and other Western countries find weight stigma to be common and, unlike some other forms of prejudice, socially acceptable in many settings. Reported rates of weight discrimination have increased alongside obesity prevalence.

Effects

Experiencing weight stigma is associated with psychological distress, depression, anxiety, low self-esteem, disordered eating, avoidance of medical care, and physiological stress responses. Internalized weight bias — applying negative stereotypes to oneself — is linked to poorer mental and physical health outcomes, and stigma itself may contribute to further weight gain through stress-related pathways.

Responses

Researchers and advocacy groups have called for anti-bullying policies, training for healthcare providers, and legal protections; a small number of jurisdictions prohibit weight discrimination explicitly. Public-health guidance increasingly emphasizes behaviour and metabolic health rather than blame.
