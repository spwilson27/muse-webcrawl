Ernest Renan

Born: 27 February 1823, Tréguier, France
Died: 2 October 1892, Paris, France
Occupation: Philosopher, historian, philologist

Joseph Ernest Renan (27 February 1823 – 2 October 1892) was a French orientalist and semitic scholar, expert of Semitic languages and civilisations, philosopher, historian, and writer. He is best known for his influential historical works on early Christianity and his political theories, especially concerning nationalism.

Life of Jesus

Renan's Vie de Jésus (1863), the first volume of his History of the Origins of Christianity, portrayed Jesus as a wholly human figure and became a bestseller, provoking fierce controversy in Catholic France. His scholarly career nevertheless flourished; he held the chair of Hebrew at the Collège de France and was elected to the Académie française.

"What Is a Nation?"

In his celebrated 1882 Sorbonne lecture "Qu'est-ce qu'une nation?" ("What Is a Nation?"), Renan argued that nationhood rests not on race, language, or geography but on shared memory and the will to live together — a "daily plebiscite". The lecture remains a classic text in the study of nationalism.

Legacy

Renan's work influenced generations of historians and political theorists, while aspects of his writings on race have been widely criticised.
