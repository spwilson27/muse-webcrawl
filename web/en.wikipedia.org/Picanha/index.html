Picanha

Type
Beef cut
Origin
Brazil
Also known as
Rump cap, sirloin cap, coulotte

Picanha is a cut of beef first popularized in Brazil, taken from the top of the rump (sirloin cap) and covered by a thick layer of fat. In the United States it is known as the sirloin cap or coulotte; it corresponds to the biceps femoris muscle and its fat covering.

Preparation

In Brazilian churrasco, picanha is typically seasoned only with coarse salt, skewered in a crescent shape on a sword-like spit, and roasted over charcoal, with the fat cap rendering and basting the meat. Slices are carved directly from the skewer at the table in rodízio service. Because the muscle is relatively tender, it is usually cooked to medium-rare and cut against the grain.

Characteristics

A whole picanha generally weighs about 1 to 1.5 kg (2–3 lb); larger pieces include meat from beyond the rump cap that is less prized. The cut gained international popularity in the 2010s and 2020s through Brazilian steakhouses and online grilling culture.
