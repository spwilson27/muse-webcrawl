Edmonton is the capital city of the Canadian province of Alberta. Situated on the North Saskatchewan River in central Alberta, it is the northernmost major city in North America with a population over one million, and serves as a gateway to Canada's northern resource industries. Edmonton is a cultural, governmental and educational centre, home to the University of Alberta and Canada's largest mall by area for many years, West Edmonton Mall.

History

The area has been inhabited by Indigenous peoples for thousands of years. In 1795 the Hudson's Bay Company established Fort Edmonton near the present city, naming it after Edmonton, London, the birthplace of deputy governor Sir James Winter Lake. The settlement grew as a fur-trading post and staging point; the Klondike Gold Rush of 1897–98 brought thousands of prospectors through. Edmonton was incorporated as a city in 1904 and became Alberta's capital when the province was created in 1905. The discovery of oil at Leduc in 1947 transformed its economy.

Culture and sport

Edmonton hosts more than thirty annual festivals, including the Edmonton International Fringe Festival — the largest fringe theatre festival in North America — the Folk Music Festival and the Heritage Festival. The city is home to the Edmonton Oilers of the NHL, five-time Stanley Cup champions in the 1980s, and hosted the 1978 Commonwealth Games and the 2001 World Championships in Athletics. Its river valley park system is the largest urban parkland in Canada.
