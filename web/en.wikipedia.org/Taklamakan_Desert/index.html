Taklamakan Desert

The Taklamakan Desert (also Taklimakan) is a desert in northwest China, occupying most of the Tarim Basin in southwestern Xinjiang. At about 337,000 km² it is the world's second-largest shifting sand desert, after the Rub' al Khali.

Geography and climate

Ringed by high mountains, the Taklamakan receives almost no rain; summers are hot and winters bitterly cold. Dunes reach up to about 300 m high. Its name is popularly glossed in Uyghur/Turkic as "place of no return" — "you go in and you don't come out."

Silk Road and exploration

The Silk Road split around it: a northern route through Turpan and a southern route through Hotan and Kashgar, joining at the Pamirs. Explorers Sven Hedin, Aurel Stein and others recovered manuscripts and art from buried oasis cities such as Niya and Loulan. Modern highways and the Hotan–Ruoqiang railway now cross or encircle it; oil and gas are extracted in its interior.
