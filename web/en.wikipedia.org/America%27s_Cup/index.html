America's Cup

The America's Cup is a sailing competition and the oldest international competition still operating in any sport. First contested in 1851 around the Isle of Wight, it is a match race between a defender yacht club and a challenging club, governed by the terms of the Deed of Gift of the America's Cup.

America's Cup
First held: 1851
Organizer: defender's yacht club under the Deed of Gift
Type: Match race sailing
Trophy: Auld Mug

History

The trophy originated as the "Royal Yacht Squadron Cup" won by the schooner America in a race around the Isle of Wight in 1851. The trophy was donated under a deed of gift to the New York Yacht Club, which successfully defended it for 132 years — the longest winning streak in sport — until Australia II won in 1983, ending American dominance.

Modern era

Subsequent contests have seen the Cup held by teams from Australia, the United States, Switzerland, and New Zealand. Recent editions have used high-performance foiling catamarans and monohulls (such as the AC75 class) reaching speeds above 50 knots, with the competition format including challenger selection series such as the Louis Vuitton Cup.
