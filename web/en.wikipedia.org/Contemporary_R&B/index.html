Contemporary R&B

Contemporary R&B blends soul, funk, pop and hip hop influences.

Contemporary R&B (or simply R&B) is a popular music genre that combines rhythm and blues with elements of pop, soul, funk, hip hop and electronic music. The genre emerged after the decline of disco in the late 1970s and early 1980s, with artists such as Michael Jackson, Luther Vandross and Whitney Houston popularising a polished, synthesiser-driven sound characterised by smooth vocals, lush production and drum-machine rhythms.

Development

In the late 1980s, new jack swing — pioneered by producers Teddy Riley and Jimmy Jam and Terry Lewis — fused R&B with hip hop beats. Through the 1990s, acts including Boyz II Men, Mariah Carey, TLC and Mary J. Blige (dubbed the "Queen of Hip-Hop Soul") kept the genre at the centre of pop. The 2000s saw the rise of crunk-influenced R&B and stars such as Usher, Beyoncé and Alicia Keys, while from the 2010s "alternative R&B" artists like Frank Ocean and The Weeknd pushed toward moodier, more experimental production.

Characteristics

Contemporary R&B typically features melismatic singing, tight vocal harmonies, and production built on drum machines, synthesisers and samples, distinguishing it from the live-band sound of classic rhythm and blues.
