Pyotr Ufimtsev

Born: 1931, Altai Krai, Soviet Union
Field: Electromagnetism, diffraction theory
Known for: Physical theory of diffraction

Pyotr Yakovlevich Ufimtsev (Russian: Пётр Яковлевич Уфимцев; born 1931) is a Soviet and Russian physicist and mathematician known as the founder of the physical theory of diffraction (PTD), a high-frequency method for predicting the scattering of electromagnetic waves. His work, little recognised in the Soviet Union at the time, became a theoretical foundation of modern stealth technology after it was applied by engineers at Lockheed in the design of the F-117 Nighthawk.

Work

Building on the physical optics approximation, Ufimtsev showed that the total field scattered by an object can be calculated as the sum of the elementary edge waves produced by "nonuniform" or fringe currents flowing near the edges and discontinuities of the scattering body. He developed the theory in the 1950s and 1960s while working in Moscow, publishing it in his 1962 book Method of Edge Waves in the Physical Theory of Diffraction. Because Soviet authorities judged the work to have no military significance, it was published openly and translated into English by the US Air Force's Foreign Technology Division in 1971.

Influence on stealth

In 1975 Lockheed engineer Denys Overholser recognised that Ufimtsev's equations made it possible to compute the radar cross-section of faceted shapes accurately with the computers then available. This insight led to the Have Blue demonstrator and then the F-117, whose flat angled surfaces were shaped to deflect radar energy away from its source. Ufimtsev later emigrated to the United States, where he taught at UCLA and worked in industry, and he received honours including the Leroy Randle Grumman Medal for his contributions.
