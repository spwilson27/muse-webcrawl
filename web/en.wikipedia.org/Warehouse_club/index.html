A warehouse club (also called a wholesale club) is a retail store, usually selling a wide variety of merchandise, in which customers pay annual membership fees in order to shop. The clubs are able to keep prices low due to the no-frills format of the stores: goods are displayed on pallets or in plain steel shelving, staffing and fixtures are minimal, and most products are sold in large, bulk quantities.

History

The warehouse club concept was pioneered by Sol Price, who opened the first Price Club in San Diego, California, in 1976, initially serving small businesses. The model proved popular with ordinary households as well. Sam Walton's Walmart opened the first Sam's Club in Midwest City, Oklahoma, in 1983, and BJ's Wholesale Club followed in 1984. Price Club merged with Costco in 1993 to form PriceCostco, which later became simply Costco. The format spread internationally, with clubs operating in Canada, Mexico, Australia, Japan, South Korea, Spain, and elsewhere.

Business model

Warehouse clubs typically carry only a few thousand stock-keeping units — far fewer than a supermarket — concentrating on fast-turning items purchased at large scale. Margins on merchandise are deliberately thin; the bulk of profits comes from membership fees, which also fund the low prices and create customer loyalty. Clubs often operate ancillary businesses such as food courts (famous for inexpensive hot dogs and pizza), optical departments, pharmacies, tyre centres, and petrol stations, which act as traffic drivers.

Membership is usually tiered, with higher-priced executive tiers offering annual rebates. Some clubs restrict membership categories — BJ's, for example, began by serving employees of particular companies — and some countries' clubs sell to businesses only.

Major operators

Costco is the largest warehouse club operator in the world and one of the largest retailers overall. Sam's Club, a division of Walmart, is the second-largest in the United States. BJ's Wholesale Club operates mainly on the U.S. East Coast. Regional and international chains include City Club and PriceSmart in Latin America and Makro (cash and carry) in Europe, South America, and Asia.

Comparison with other formats

Unlike cash-and-carry wholesalers, warehouse clubs sell to individuals as well as businesses. Unlike big-box discount stores such as Walmart or Target, clubs require membership, stock far fewer items, and emphasize bulk packaging, which can produce lower per-unit prices but higher total ticket sizes.
