Minimal polynomial (field theory)

In field theory, the minimal polynomial of an element α of a field extension E/F is the unique monic polynomial of lowest degree over the base field F that has α as a root. An element possessing such a polynomial is called algebraic over F; otherwise it is transcendental.

Definition and properties

The minimal polynomial is always irreducible over F, and it divides every other polynomial over F that vanishes at α. Its degree equals the degree of the extension F(α)/F — the dimension of the smallest subfield of E containing both F and α. For example, the minimal polynomial of √2 over the rationals is x² − 2, and that of the imaginary unit i is x² + 1.

Applications

Minimal polynomials underpin the classical impossibility proofs for doubling the cube and trisecting the angle with compass and straightedge: the relevant lengths have minimal polynomials of degree 3, while constructible numbers must have degrees that are powers of two. They also appear in the theory of finite fields, where primitive polynomials generate multiplicative groups.
