Carbon-fiber-reinforced polymer

Carbon-fiber-reinforced polymer (CFRP)
TypeComposite material
ConstituentsCarbon fibres in a polymer (usually epoxy) matrix
PropertiesVery high strength-to-weight ratio, stiff, corrosion-resistant
UsesAerospace, motorsport, sporting goods, civil engineering

Carbon-fiber-reinforced polymer (CFRP) is an extremely strong and light fiber-reinforced plastic that contains carbon fibers embedded in a polymer resin matrix, commonly epoxy. It has one of the highest strength-to-weight ratios of any structural material and is used where weight saving justifies its high cost.

Manufacture

Parts are made by laying up woven carbon-fiber fabric or unidirectional tape in a mould, impregnating it with resin — often as pre-preg — and curing it under heat and pressure, frequently in an autoclave. Fibres are typically derived from polyacrylonitrile (PAN) precursor.

Applications

CFRP is used in aircraft structures (much of the Boeing 787 and Airbus A350 airframes), Formula One monocoques, bicycle frames, wind turbine blades, and strengthening of concrete structures. Drawbacks include brittleness under impact, difficulty of repair and recycling, and galvanic corrosion when in contact with aluminium.
