Uruguay River

Native nameRío Uruguay (Spanish); Rio Uruguai (Portuguese)
LengthAbout 1,838 km (1,140 mi)
SourceSerra do Mar, Brazil
MouthRío de la Plata
CountriesBrazil, Argentina, Uruguay

The Uruguay River (Spanish: Río Uruguay; Portuguese: Rio Uruguai) is a major river in South America. It flows from north to south and forms parts of the boundaries between Brazil and Argentina, and between Argentina and Uruguay. Its name, from the Guaraní language, is usually interpreted as "river of the painted birds".

Course

The river rises in the Serra do Mar in southern Brazil, formed by the confluence of the Canoas and Pelotas rivers, and flows west, then southwest, then south for about 1,838 kilometres. Along its middle course lie the Moconá (Yucumã) Falls, which run parallel to the river rather than across it. Below its confluence with the Paraná Bravo it joins the Paraná River to form the Río de la Plata estuary. Important cities on its banks include Salto and Paysandú in Uruguay, and Concordia and Concepción del Uruguay in Argentina.

Use and disputes

The river is navigable for oceangoing vessels as far as Paysandú and for smaller craft much further. The binational Salto Grande Dam, completed in 1979, provides hydroelectric power to Argentina and Uruguay. Bridges such as the Salto Grande bridge and the General Artigas Bridge link the two banks. Between 2005 and 2010 the river was at the centre of the "pulp mill dispute" between Argentina and Uruguay over a cellulose plant at Fray Bentos, which was taken to the International Court of Justice — the Court found in 2010 that Uruguay had breached procedural obligations but allowed the mill to keep operating.
