William Dickson

William Dickson may refer to several notable people. The best known is William Kennedy Laurie Dickson (3 August 1860 – 28 September 1935), a Scottish-French inventor and photographer who, while employed by Thomas Edison, played a central role in the invention of motion pictures. Dickson designed the Kinetograph camera and the Kinetoscope viewer, standardised 35 mm film with sprocket holes, and directed some of the earliest films ever made at the Black Maria studio in West Orange, New Jersey.

William Kennedy Laurie Dickson

Born in Le Minihic-sur-Rance, Brittany, to Scottish parents, Dickson joined Edison's laboratory in 1883 as a photographer and mining engineer. Tasked by Edison with combining sound and moving images, Dickson and his assistant William Heise produced experimental films by 1891; the public Kinetoscope parlours of 1894 showed 50-foot loops such as Fred Ott's Sneeze and the Blacksmith Scene. After disputes over credit and patents pushed him out of Edison's organisation, Dickson co-founded the American Mutoscope and Biograph Company in 1895, becoming a leading producer-director in Britain, where he filmed extensively during the Boer War. His 1899 history The Biograph in Battle is an early account of war cinematography. He died in Twickenham, England, in 1935.

Other William Dicksons

The name is also borne by Canadian lawyer and land speculator William Dickson (1769–1846), founder of Galt, Ontario; by British abolitionist writer William Dickson (1756–1823), author of Letters on Slavery (1789); and by numerous politicians, soldiers and sportsmen in Britain, Canada, Australia and the United States.
