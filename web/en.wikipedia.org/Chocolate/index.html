Chocolate

Chocolate is a food made from roasted and ground cacao beans that can be consumed as a liquid, paste, or solid block, or used as a flavoring ingredient. Cacao has been consumed in some form since at least the Olmec civilization of Mesoamerica (19th–11th century BCE), and the majority of Mesoamerican peoples made chocolate beverages, including the Maya and Aztecs. The seeds of the cacao tree (Theobroma cacao) have an intensely bitter taste and must be fermented to develop their flavor.

Dark, milk, and white chocolate

History

Chocolate was first developed as a beverage in Mesoamerica, where cacao drinks were consumed by elites and used in rituals; cacao beans also served as currency. After the Spanish conquest of the Aztec Empire, chocolate was introduced to Europe in the 16th century, initially as a drink sweetened with sugar. It remained an aristocratic luxury until industrial processes in the 19th century — including the Dutch press of Coenraad van Houten, the conche of Rodolphe Lindt, and Daniel Peter's milk chocolate — made solid eating chocolate widely affordable.

Production

After harvest, cacao seeds and pulp are fermented, dried, cleaned, and roasted. The roasted beans are cracked and winnowed to produce nibs, which are ground into cocoa mass (chocolate liquor). Cocoa mass can be pressed to separate cocoa butter from cocoa solids. Chocolate is produced by combining cocoa mass and cocoa butter in varying proportions with sugar, and for milk chocolate, milk powder or condensed milk. The mixture is refined, conched, and tempered before molding.

Types

The principal types are dark chocolate, milk chocolate, and white chocolate (which contains cocoa butter but no cocoa solids). Blonde chocolate, made from caramelized white chocolate, and ruby chocolate, introduced in 2017, are more recent varieties. Regulations in many jurisdictions specify minimum cocoa contents for each designation.

Nutrition and effects

Chocolate is energy-dense, rich in fat and sugar. Cocoa contains stimulants including caffeine and theobromine, as well as flavanols that have been studied for cardiovascular effects. Theobromine is toxic to dogs and some other animals.
