European Atomic Energy Community

Abbreviation: EAEC / Euratom
Established: 25 March 1957 (Treaty of Rome), in force 1 January 1958
Members: The 27 member states of the European Union

The European Atomic Energy Community (EAEC or Euratom) is an international organisation established by the Euratom Treaty of 1957 with the original purpose of creating a specialist market for nuclear power in Europe, developing nuclear energy, and distributing it to member states while selling the surplus to non-member states.

History

Euratom was one of the three European Communities founded in the 1950s, alongside the European Coal and Steel Community and the European Economic Community, with which it shared its institutions following the Merger Treaty of 1967. Unlike the other communities, which were eventually absorbed into the European Union, Euratom retains a distinct legal personality, although it shares the EU's institutions and its membership is identical to the EU's. The United Kingdom left Euratom as part of Brexit in 2020.

Activities

Euratom coordinates member states' research in nuclear energy through framework programmes, operates safeguards to ensure nuclear materials are used for declared peaceful purposes, sets uniform safety standards for radiation protection, and supplies nuclear fuels through the Euratom Supply Agency. It is also the European partner in the ITER international fusion project.
