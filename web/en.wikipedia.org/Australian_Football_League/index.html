Australian Football League

The AFL Grand Final is traditionally held at the Melbourne Cricket Ground.

The Australian Football League (AFL) is the pre-eminent professional competition in the sport of Australian rules football. It is Australia's most attended and most watched sporting league, and is run by the AFL Commission. The league consists of 18 teams spread across Australia's five mainland states.

The competition was founded in 1897 as the Victorian Football League (VFL), a breakaway of eight clubs from the Victorian Football Association. It was renamed the Australian Football League in 1990 as it expanded into a national competition.

History

Australian rules football was codified in Melbourne in 1859 by Tom Wills and others, making it one of the oldest football codes in the world. The VFL began in 1897 with Carlton, Collingwood, Essendon, Fitzroy, Geelong, Melbourne, St Kilda and South Melbourne. Over the following century the league expanded beyond Victoria: South Melbourne relocated to Sydney in 1982, and new clubs joined from Western Australia, South Australia, Queensland and New South Wales, including the Gold Coast Suns and Greater Western Sydney Giants in 2011 and 2012.

Format

The season runs from March to September. Teams play a home-and-away season of about 23 rounds, after which the top eight teams contest a finals series culminating in the AFL Grand Final, usually held on the last Saturday in September or the first in October. The premiership is one of the most watched sporting events in Australia.

Clubs and records

Carlton, Collingwood and Essendon are among the most successful clubs, each with 16 premierships. The women's competition, AFL Women's (AFLW), was launched in 2017 and has expanded to include all 18 clubs. Notable individual honours include the Brownlow Medal for the fairest and best player, the Coleman Medal for the leading goalkicker, and the Norm Smith Medal for the best player in the Grand Final.
