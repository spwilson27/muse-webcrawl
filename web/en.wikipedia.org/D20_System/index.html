The d20 System is a role-playing game system published in 2000 by Wizards of the Coast, originally developed for the third edition of Dungeons & Dragons. The system is named after the 20-sided die (d20) that is central to its core resolution mechanic: actions succeed when a d20 roll plus applicable modifiers equals or exceeds a target number (Difficulty Class or armour class).

Mechanics

Characters are defined by six ability scores (Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom, Charisma), whose modifiers feed into attack rolls, saving throws, and skill checks, all resolved with the same d20 roll. The system also uses feats, skills, classes, levels, and hit points. Compared with earlier editions of D&D it unified many disparate mechanics into a single consistent core rule.

Open gaming and influence

Wizards of the Coast released the system under the Open Game License, allowing other publishers to produce compatible games and supplements using the d20 System Trademark License. This triggered a boom in third-party products in the early 2000s, including Mutants & Masterminds, Spycraft, and countless D&D supplements, followed by a glut and bust around 2003–2004. The System Reference Document (SRD) also enabled the later Pathfinder Roleplaying Game (2009), which continued the 3.5-edition rules after D&D moved to a new edition.
