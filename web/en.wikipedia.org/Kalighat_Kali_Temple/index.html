Kalighat Kali Temple is a Hindu temple in Kalighat, Kolkata, West Bengal, India, dedicated to the goddess Kali. It is one of the 51 Shakti Peethas in Shaktism, where it is believed that the toes of the right foot of the goddess Sati fell. The temple is one of the most important pilgrimage sites in Kolkata, and the goddess is worshipped here as a manifestation of the city's presiding deity — the name "Kolkata" is often said to derive from "Kalikkhetra", the land of Kali.

History and legend

The site on the bank of the Adi Ganga (the old course of the Hooghly River) is mentioned in 15th- and 17th-century Bengali devotional literature. The present temple was built under the patronage of the Sabarna Roy Chowdhury family and completed in 1809, in the Bengal temple style with a distinctive three-spired roof. The stone image of Kali, with its protruding gold tongue and four arms, was carved by two saints, Atmaram Brahmachari and Brahmananda Giri.

Worship

The temple draws hundreds of thousands of devotees, especially on Tuesdays, Saturdays and during Kali Puja. The surrounding Kalighat area, one of Kolkata's oldest neighbourhoods, is also known for its patachitra paintings — the Kalighat school of painting produced by the patuas of the temple bazaar in the 19th century.
