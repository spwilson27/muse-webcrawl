Fab lab

A fab lab (fabrication laboratory) is a small-scale workshop offering (personal) digital fabrication. A fab lab is generally equipped with an array of flexible computer-controlled tools that cover several different length scales and various materials, with the aim to make "almost anything". This includes technology-enabled products generally perceived as limited to mass production.

History

The fab lab concept grew out of the "How To Make (almost) Anything" class (MAS.863) at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, led by Professor Neil Gershenfeld of the Center for Bits and Atoms. The first fab lab outside MIT was established in inner-city Boston in 2002 with National Science Foundation support; the idea spread to community settings in India, Norway, Ghana, and beyond. The global network grew to more than two thousand labs in over a hundred countries, coordinated through the Fab Foundation and annual FAB conferences.

Equipment and charter

While fab labs have competed with hackerspaces and makerspaces, they share a common core inventory designed so that processes and projects can be shared between labs worldwide: a laser cutter, a large CNC router, a precision milling machine, a vinyl cutter, 3D printers, and an electronics workstation with components for circuit production. The Fab Charter sets out principles: open public access, shared tools and knowledge, responsibility for safety and for one's own projects, and commercial activities permitted so long as they do not conflict with open access.

Projects and impact

Fab labs have been used for education (including the Fab Academy distributed diploma programme), local problem-solving such as low-cost prosthetics and agricultural tools, and rapid prototyping during crises — notably face shields and ventilator parts during the COVID-19 pandemic. Critics note challenges in sustaining funding and in converting access into broader economic outcomes.
