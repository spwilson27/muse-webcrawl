Tehching Hsieh

Tehching Hsieh (Chinese: 謝德慶; pinyin: Xiè Déqìng; born December 31, 1950, in Nanzhou, Pingtung, Taiwan) is a Taiwanese-born American performance artist. He is known for a series of five One Year Performances carried out between 1978 and 1986, followed by a thirteen-year piece, in which extreme durational constraints structured his life. Performance artist Marina Abramović has called him a "master" of performance art.

Early life

Hsieh was born in Taiwan, one of fifteen children. He trained as a painter, served in Taiwan's compulsory military service, and in 1974 arrived in the United States, where he lived for years as an undocumented immigrant working in restaurants while developing his art in New York City.

One Year Performances

Between 1978 and 1986, Hsieh completed five year-long performances. In Cage Piece (1978–79) he lived for a year locked in a wooden cage in his studio. In Punch Clock Piece (1980–81) he punched a time clock every hour, on the hour, for a year. In Outdoor Piece (1981–82) he remained outside for a year without entering any building or shelter. In Rope Piece (1983–84), with artist Linda Montano, the two were tied together by an eight-foot rope for a year without touching each other. In No Art Piece (1985–86) he abstained from making, viewing, or discussing art for a year.

Thirteen Year Plan

From 1986 to 1999, Hsieh undertook the Tehching Hsieh 1986–1999 (Thirteen Year Plan), during which he made art but did not show it publicly. At its conclusion he announced that he had "kept himself alive" for the period. Afterwards he declared he no longer makes art, though his historical works are exhibited internationally, including representing Taiwan at the 2017 Venice Biennale.
