The 1962 FIFA World Cup was the seventh FIFA World Cup, the quadrennial international football championship for men's national teams. It was held in Chile from 30 May to 17 June 1962. Brazil successfully defended the title they had won four years earlier in Sweden, defeating Czechoslovakia 3–1 in the final at the Estadio Nacional in Santiago.

Chile was awarded the tournament in 1956, narrowly defeating West Germany and Argentina in the bidding. Preparations were badly disrupted by the 1960 Valdivia earthquake, the most powerful earthquake ever recorded, which devastated much of southern Chile. Under the leadership of organiser Carlos Dittborn, the country rebuilt its infrastructure in time, giving rise to the famous phrase "because we have nothing, we want to do everything" (porque no tenemos nada, queremos hacerlo todo).

Format and group stage

Sixteen teams qualified for the finals and were divided into four groups of four. The top two teams from each group advanced to the quarter-finals. The tournament saw the last appearance of the great Brazilian side of Pelé, Garrincha, Didi and Vavá, although Pelé was injured in the second group match against Czechoslovakia and missed the rest of the tournament.

The Battle of Santiago

The group match between hosts Chile and Italy on 2 June became notorious as the "Battle of Santiago", a violent and ill-tempered encounter refereed by Englishman Ken Aston. Aston later invented the yellow and red card system partly in response to the difficulties of controlling such matches. Chile won 2–0 and advanced, while Italy were eliminated in the group stage.

Knockout stage

In the semi-finals, Brazil defeated Chile 4–2 and Czechoslovakia beat Yugoslavia 3–1. The final, played on 17 June before 68,679 spectators, saw Czechoslovakia take an early lead through Josef Masopust, but goals from Amarildo, Zito and Vavá gave Brazil a 3–1 victory and their second consecutive World Cup title.

Top scorers

Six players finished as joint top scorers with four goals each: Garrincha and Vavá of Brazil, Leonel Sánchez of Chile, Dražan Jerković of Yugoslavia, Valentin Ivanov of the Soviet Union and Florian Albert of Hungary. Garrincha was widely regarded as the outstanding player of the tournament in Pelé's absence.

Legacy

The tournament was the last World Cup final won by a team retaining the trophy until Argentina's repeat bids in later decades; Brazil's back-to-back titles of 1958 and 1962 remain one of the defining achievements of the Brazil national team. Tactically, the tournament accelerated the decline of the old W-M formation in favour of the 4-3-3 and 4-2-4 systems.
