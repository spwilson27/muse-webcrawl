Hōkūleʻa

TypeWaʻa kaulua (double-hulled voyaging canoe)
Launched8 March 1975
Length62 ft (18.9 m)
OperatorPolynesian Voyaging Society

Hōkūleʻa is a performance-accurate replica of an ancient Polynesian double-hulled voyaging canoe (waʻa kaulua). Her name means "star of gladness" in Hawaiian — the Hawaiian name for Arcturus, a zenith star used by navigators over Hawaiʻi. She was built by the Polynesian Voyaging Society and launched in 1975 at Kaneʻohe Bay, Oʻahu.

The 1976 voyage to Tahiti

In 1976 Hōkūleʻa sailed from Hawaiʻi to Tahiti using only traditional non-instrument navigation, guided by the Micronesian master navigator Mau Piailug of Satawal. The voyage demonstrated that the settlement of Polynesia could have been achieved by deliberate, navigated voyaging, and it became a landmark event in the Hawaiian cultural renaissance.

Later voyages

Tragedy struck in 1978 when the canoe capsized in the Molokaʻi Channel; crew member Eddie Aikau was lost attempting to paddle for help. Under navigator Nainoa Thompson, Hōkūleʻa completed further landmark voyages, including the 1985–87 Voyage of Rediscovery throughout Polynesia, the 1992 voyage to Rarotonga, and voyages to Easter Island (Rapa Nui) in 1999 and Japan in 2007. From 2013 to 2019 she undertook the Mālama Honua Worldwide Voyage, a circumnavigation of roughly 42,000 nautical miles promoting environmental stewardship. In 2023 she began the Moananuiākea Voyage around the Pacific.
