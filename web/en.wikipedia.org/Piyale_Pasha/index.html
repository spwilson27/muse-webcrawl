Piyale Pasha

Piyale Pasha
Born: c. 1515, Croatia (Habsburg monarchy)
Died: 21 January 1578, Istanbul, Ottoman Empire
Allegiance: Ottoman Empire
Rank: Kapudan Pasha (Grand Admiral)

Piyale Pasha (c. 1515 – 21 January 1578) was an Ottoman grand admiral (Kapudan Pasha) and vizier during the reign of Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent and his son Selim II. Of Croatian origin, he was taken into Ottoman service as a boy and educated in the palace school system. He commanded the Ottoman fleet for some fourteen years, from 1554 to 1568, and was one of the most successful Ottoman naval commanders of the sixteenth century.

Naval career

Piyale Pasha won a major victory over a combined Spanish and Italian fleet at the Battle of Djerba in 1560, one of the most decisive Ottoman naval triumphs of the era. In 1565 he transported the Ottoman army to Malta for the Great Siege, though disagreements with the land commander Mustafa Pasha contributed to the siege's failure. In 1570–1571 his fleet supported the conquest of Cyprus, capturing Nicosia and Famagusta.

After the Ottoman fleet was destroyed at the Battle of Lepanto in 1571, Piyale Pasha oversaw its rapid rebuilding in a single winter, famously declaring to the Venetian ambassador that the loss of Cyprus was like the loss of an arm to Venice, while the loss of the fleet was only like a shaved beard to the Ottomans — it would grow back stronger.

Family and legacy

He married Gevherhan Sultan, a granddaughter of Suleiman the Magnificent. In Istanbul he commissioned the Piyale Pasha Mosque in the Kasımpaşa district, designed by the imperial architect Mimar Sinan. He died in 1578 and was buried at that mosque.
