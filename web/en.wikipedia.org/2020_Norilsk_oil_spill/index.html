2020 Norilsk oil spill

The Norilsk oil spill was an industrial disaster near Norilsk, in Russia's Krasnoyarsk Krai, that began on 29 May 2020, when a fuel storage tank at a power plant operated by the Norilsk-Taimyr Energy Company (a subsidiary of Norilsk Nickel) collapsed and released about 21,000 tonnes of diesel oil — roughly 150,000 barrels — into local rivers. It was one of the largest oil spills in Russian history and the worst ecological disaster in the Russian Arctic.

Diesel-stained waters of the Ambarnaya River, June 2020.

The spill

The reserve diesel tank at Thermal Power Plant No. 3 collapsed as its support pillars sank into ground softened by thawing permafrost; a car passing nearby was set ablaze by the escaping fuel. Around 6,000 tonnes of diesel soaked into the soil and some 15,000 tonnes entered the Daldykan and Ambarnaya rivers, turning long stretches red and flowing toward Lake Pyasino and ultimately the Kara Sea. Containment booms on the Ambarnaya limited further spread, but cleanup in the roadless tundra was slow and difficult.

Response and criticism

Local officials learned of the disaster from social media two days after the collapse; President Vladimir Putin publicly rebuked the Krasnoyarsk governor and company management, declared a federal state of emergency on 3 June, and ordered an investigation. Greenpeace Russia compared the scale of the spill to the 1989 Exxon Valdez disaster. Hundreds of responders recovered fuel from the river through the summer, and Norilsk Nickel claimed the bulk of the river-borne diesel was collected by autumn.

Consequences

Russia's environmental regulator Rosprirodnadzor assessed damages at 147.8 billion rubles (about US$2.1 billion) — the largest environmental damage claim in Russian history. In February 2021 a Krasnoyarsk court ordered Norilsk Nickel's subsidiary to pay 146 billion rubles, which the company accepted. The disaster intensified scrutiny of Arctic industrial infrastructure built on degrading permafrost, a hazard expected to grow with climate warming; the tank's supports were later found to have deteriorated over decades without adequate inspection.
