Leo Joseph Ryan Jr. (May 5, 1925 – November 18, 1978) was an American teacher and politician who served as a U.S. Representative from California from 1973 until his assassination in Guyana in 1978. He is the only member of the United States Congress ever killed in the line of duty (a later representative, Jo Cox of the United Kingdom aside, he remains the sole U.S. congressman assassinated while on an official fact-finding mission).

Early life and career

Ryan was born in Lincoln, Nebraska, and served in the United States Navy during the Second World War. He became a schoolteacher in California and entered politics, serving as mayor of South San Francisco and then in the California State Assembly from 1963 to 1973. Known for experiential investigation, he famously taught in an inner-city school and arranged to be held as an inmate at Folsom Prison to study conditions there.

Congressional service

Elected to Congress in 1972, Ryan was a liberal Democrat noted for his work on government oversight and his opposition to unchecked intelligence-agency power; he co-authored the Hughes–Ryan Amendment of 1974, requiring the president to report covert CIA operations to Congress.

Jonestown and assassination

In November 1978 Ryan led a fact-finding delegation to Jonestown, the Guyanese settlement of the Peoples Temple led by Jim Jones, after constituents reported relatives held against their will. On November 18, as the delegation prepared to depart the Port Kaituma airstrip with several defectors, Temple gunmen opened fire, killing Ryan, three journalists, and a defector. Hours later, Jones directed the mass murder-suicide of more than 900 of his followers at Jonestown.

Ryan was posthumously awarded the Congressional Gold Medal in 1983.
