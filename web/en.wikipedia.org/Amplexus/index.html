Amplexus

Amplexus (Latin "embrace") is a type of mating behavior exhibited by some externally fertilizing species, chiefly amphibians and horseshoe crabs, in which the male grasps the female with his front legs as part of the mating process. It positions the male so that sperm can be released over the eggs as the female lays them, maximizing the chance of fertilization.

In amphibians

In most frogs and toads, amplexus is the standard mating posture. The male mounts the female from behind and clasps her either behind the forelimbs (axillary amplexus, typical of toads and many frogs) or in front of the hindlimbs (inguinal amplexus, considered the ancestral condition). Males of many species develop roughened nuptial pads on their thumbs to improve their grip. The pair may remain in amplexus for hours or even days before spawning.

Females in amplexus carry the male to the spawning site and deposit eggs — often in strings or clusters — which the male fertilizes externally. Competition among males can be intense: rival males may attempt to displace a clasping male, and mistaken amplexus with other species, males, or even inanimate objects is common.

In other animals

A comparable clasping behavior occurs in horseshoe crabs, where the smaller male attaches to the female's shell during spawning on beaches. The term is also applied loosely to similar mating grasps in some other arthropods.
