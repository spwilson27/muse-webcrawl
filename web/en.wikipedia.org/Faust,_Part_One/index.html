Faust, Part One

Faust, Part One (1808) by Johann Wolfgang von Goethe is regarded as one of the greatest works of German literature.

Faust, Part One (German: Faust. Eine Tragödie) is the first part of Johann Wolfgang von Goethe's two-part tragedy Faust. It was first published in 1808; a preliminary version, the Urfaust, was written between 1772 and 1775. Part One is considered one of the greatest works of German literature.

The play tells the story of Heinrich Faust, a dissatisfied scholar who makes a pact with the devil, represented by Mephistopheles. In exchange for his soul, Faust gains unlimited knowledge and worldly pleasures. Mephistopheles leads Faust through a series of adventures, among them the seduction of the innocent Gretchen (Margarete), whose downfall — the death of her mother and brother, the murder of her illegitimate child, and her own execution — forms the tragic core of Part One, though Gretchen is ultimately saved by a voice from above.

Structure

Part One begins with a "Prelude in the Theatre" and a "Prologue in Heaven," in which the Lord wagers with Mephistopheles over Faust's soul, echoing the Book of Job. The action then moves to Faust's study, his pact with the devil, Auerbach's Cellar in Leipzig, the Witch's Kitchen, and the Gretchen tragedy.

Goethe worked on Faust across his whole life; Part Two was completed shortly before his death in 1832 and published posthumously. The work has inspired countless adaptations, including operas by Gounod, Boito, and Berlioz.
