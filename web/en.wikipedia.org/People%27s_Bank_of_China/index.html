The People's Bank of China (PBOC; Chinese: 中国人民银行) is the central bank of the People's Republic of China, responsible for carrying out monetary policy as determined by the State Council and regulating financial institutions in mainland China. It manages the world's largest foreign-exchange reserves.

History

The bank was founded on 1 December 1948 in Shijiazhuang from the merger of the Huabei Bank, the Beihai Bank and the Xibei Farmer Bank. It became the country's sole bank, combining central and commercial functions, until reforms in the 1980s spun off commercial banking into state-owned banks and the PBOC took on a pure central-banking role, formalized by law in 1995.

Functions

The PBOC issues the renminbi, sets benchmark interest rates and reserve requirements, conducts open-market operations, manages the exchange-rate regime, and oversees payment systems and anti-money-laundering rules. Its monetary policy committee advises on policy but ultimate authority rests with the State Council.
