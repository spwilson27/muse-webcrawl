Fetchmail

Fetchmail
DeveloperMatthias Andree (maintainer); originally Eric S. Raymond

Written inC
TypeMail retrieval agent
LicenseGPL

Fetchmail is a free, open-source software utility for POSIX systems used to retrieve email from a remote mail server (POP3, IMAP, and related protocols) and forward it to the local system's delivery agent (SMTP or a local mail delivery agent) for reading with ordinary mail user agents. It typically runs as a daemon polling servers at intervals.

History

Fetchmail grew out of Carl Harris's popclient program and was developed and popularized by Eric S. Raymond, who used its development as the principal example in his influential essay The Cathedral and the Bazaar (1997) on open-source development methodology. Maintenance later passed to other developers. Because fetchmail operates over a stored-credentials model, it has occasionally been the subject of security advisories, and users are advised to use encrypted (TLS) connections.

Usage

Configuration is stored in a user's .fetchmailrc file, defining servers, credentials, and delivery options. Fetchmail is commonly combined with procmail for filtering and a local IMAP server such as Dovecot for access from multiple devices. Related alternatives include getmail and mpop.
