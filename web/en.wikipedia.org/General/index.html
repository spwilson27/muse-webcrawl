General officer

A general officer is an officer of high rank in the armies, air forces, or marines of many nations. The term general is used in two ways: as the generic title for all grades of general officer, and as a specific rank. Generals command large formations — brigades, divisions, corps and field armies — and occupy the senior staff and leadership posts of their services.

Etymology and history

The word derives from the Latin generalis ("pertaining to a whole class"), via the French capitaine général — an officer appointed to command an entire army rather than a single regiment. The rank system crystallised in the armies of 17th- and 18th-century Europe, and the adjectival grades (lieutenant general, major general) reflect the original chain of command beneath the captain general.

Grades

In the United States and many allied forces the grades are brigadier general (one star), major general (two stars), lieutenant general (three stars) and general (four stars); a wartime five-star grade, General of the Army, ranks above them. In the British Army the sequence is brigadier (technically a senior field rank), major-general, lieutenant-general and general, with field marshal as the highest grade. The naval equivalent of a general officer is a flag officer (admiral).

Role

Generals exercise operational and strategic command, advise governments on military matters, and head service staffs — the senior officer of a service is often its chief of staff. Promotion to general rank is typically by selection from colonels and brigadiers, subject in many countries to political confirmation.
