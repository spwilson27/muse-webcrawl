Tragédie en musique

A tragédie en musique, also called a tragédie lyrique, is a genre of French Baroque opera developed by Jean-Baptiste Lully and his librettist Philippe Quinault in the late 17th century, beginning with Cadmus et Hermione (1673).

Structure

The form typically comprises a prologue celebrating the monarch and five acts, combining recitative modelled on the declamation of French classical theatre, airs, choruses, and extensive ballet divertissements. Subjects were drawn from classical mythology and chivalric epic; the overture in the French style — a slow dotted section followed by a faster fugal section — became a model across Europe.

Legacy

Lully's successors included Marin Marais, André Campra, and Jean-Philippe Rameau, whose Hippolyte et Aricie (1733) renewed the genre. The tragédie en musique dominated French serious opera until the era of Gluck's reforms in the 1770s.
