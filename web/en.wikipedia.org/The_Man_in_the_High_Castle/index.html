The Man in the High Castle

The Man in the High Castle is an alternative history novel by American author Philip K. Dick, first published in 1962. Set in an alternate 1962 in which the Axis powers won the Second World War, the novel depicts the United States partitioned between Nazi Germany and Imperial Japan, and follows several characters whose lives intersect around a banned novel-within-the-novel, The Grasshopper Lies Heavy, which imagines an Allied victory.

Plot

In the novel's timeline, President Franklin D. Roosevelt was assassinated in 1933, prolonging the Great Depression and weakening the American war effort. Germany and Japan defeated the Allies, and by 1962 the former United States is divided into the Japanese-administered Pacific States of America, a Nazi-dominated east, and a neutral buffer zone in the Rocky Mountains. Characters including antiques dealer Robert Childan, jeweller Frank Frink, and Japanese trade official Nobusuke Tagomi consult the I Ching as they navigate the occupied world, while Juliana Frink travels to meet Hawthorne Abendsen, the reclusive "man in the high castle" who wrote the forbidden book.

Themes and reception

The novel explores authenticity and forgery, the nature of historical reality, and the role of chance, with the I Ching used by Dick himself to plot the story. It won the Hugo Award for Best Novel in 1963 and is regarded as one of the foundational works of the alternative history genre. An Amazon television adaptation, The Man in the High Castle, ran from 2015 to 2019.
