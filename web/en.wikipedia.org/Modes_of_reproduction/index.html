Modes of reproduction


Main modes: Sexual, asexual
Animal strategies: Oviparity, ovoviviparity, viviparity

Animals, plants and other organisms reproduce through a variety of modes of reproduction, broadly divided into sexual reproduction, which involves the fusion of gametes from two parents, and asexual reproduction, in which a single parent produces genetically identical offspring without gamete fusion.

Sexual reproduction

In sexual reproduction, offspring inherit genetic material from two parents, producing genetic variation on which natural selection can act. Fertilization may be external, as in most fish and amphibians, or internal, as in reptiles, birds, and mammals. Animals are further classified by where development takes place: oviparous animals lay eggs that develop outside the body; viviparous animals give birth to live young nourished inside the body; and ovoviviparous animals retain eggs inside the body until they hatch.

Asexual reproduction

Asexual modes include binary fission (in bacteria and protists), budding (as in hydra and yeasts), fragmentation, vegetative propagation in plants, sporogenesis, and parthenogenesis, in which females produce offspring from unfertilized eggs — a mode found in some insects, reptiles, and fish. Asexual reproduction is fast and requires no mate, but produces clones with little genetic diversity.

Alternation of generations

Many plants and algae alternate between a haploid gametophyte generation and a diploid sporophyte generation. Some organisms, such as aphids and many parasites, switch between sexual and asexual modes depending on environmental conditions, combining the advantages of both.
