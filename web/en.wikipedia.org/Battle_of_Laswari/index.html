The Battle of Laswari was fought on 1 November 1803 in India during the Second Anglo-Maratha War. A British force under General Gerard Lake defeated the Maratha regular infantry of Daulat Rao Scindia, commanded for the most part by European-trained battalions.

Background

Following the capture of Delhi and Agra, Lake pursued Scindia's remaining regular brigades, which had retreated toward Laswari. The Maratha force, though abandoned by its French commander, was well equipped with artillery and took up a strong position along a stream.

The battle

Lake attacked after a long march, and his cavalry and infantry endured heavy artillery fire before breaking the Maratha line in hard fighting. The Maratha battalions fought with notable discipline and suffered very heavy losses, with some 7,000 killed and wounded and 72 guns captured, while British casualties were also severe, around 800.

Aftermath

Laswari destroyed the last effective field army of Scindia in Hindustan and, together with Wellington's victories in the Deccan at Assaye and Argaon, forced Scindia to conclude the Treaty of Surji-Anjangaon in December 1803.
