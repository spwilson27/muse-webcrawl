Schwannoma

Micrograph of a schwannoma, showing characteristic Antoni A and B patterns.

A schwannoma (also known as a neurilemmoma) is a benign nerve-sheath tumour composed of Schwann cells, which normally produce the insulating myelin sheath covering peripheral nerves. Schwannomas are typically slow-growing and encapsulated, and they displace rather than invade the nerve from which they arise.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms depend on the nerve involved. The most common schwannoma is the vestibular schwannoma (acoustic neuroma), which arises on the vestibulocochlear nerve and causes hearing loss, tinnitus, and balance problems. Schwannomas elsewhere in the body may present as a painless lump or cause pain and paraesthesia if they compress adjacent structures.

Cause

Most schwannomas are sporadic. They are associated with inactivation of the NF2 tumour suppressor gene, which encodes the protein merlin. Multiple schwannomas occur in the genetic conditions neurofibromatosis type II and schwannomatosis.

Treatment

Treatment is usually surgical removal, which can often be accomplished while preserving nerve function because the tumour is encapsulated. Small or asymptomatic tumours may be monitored with imaging, and stereotactic radiosurgery is an option for some intracranial tumours.
