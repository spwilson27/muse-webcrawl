John Daniel Singleton (January 6, 1968 – April 28, 2019) was an American film director, screenwriter and producer. His debut feature, Boyz n the Hood (1991), written while he was still a student, made him the youngest person and the first African American nominated for the Academy Award for Best Director.

Early life

Singleton was born and raised in South Central Los Angeles. He studied filmic writing at the University of Southern California's School of Cinematic Arts, where his script for Boyz n the Hood won awards before he graduated and was bought by Columbia Pictures.

Career

Boyz n the Hood, starring Ice Cube, Cuba Gooding Jr. and Laurence Fishburne, portrayed youth violence in Los Angeles and became a landmark of New Black Cinema. Singleton followed it with Poetic Justice (1993), Higher Learning (1995), Rosewood (1997), the remake Shaft (2000), Baby Boy (2001), 2 Fast 2 Furious (2003) and Four Brothers (2005). In television he co-created the crime drama Snowfall (2017).

Death and legacy

Singleton suffered a stroke in April 2019 and died aged 51. His films are credited with bringing authentic stories of Black American life to mainstream cinema and with opening doors for a generation of filmmakers.
