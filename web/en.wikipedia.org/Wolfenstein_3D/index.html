Wolfenstein 3D is a first-person shooter video game developed by id Software and published by Apogee Software, released for MS-DOS on May 5, 1992. The player controls Allied spy William "B.J." Blazkowicz as he escapes from the Nazi stronghold Castle Wolfenstein and battles through its levels during World War II. Inspired by the 1981 game Castle Wolfenstein, it popularised the first-person shooter genre and the shareware distribution model.

Gameplay and technology

The game renders maze-like levels from a first-person perspective using ray casting, a technique refined by programmer John Carmack to run smoothly on the PCs of the time. Walls are texture-mapped at right angles, and the player collects weapons, ammunition, health, and treasure while fighting guards, dogs, SS troopers, and bosses across episodes of ten levels each. The first episode was distributed free as shareware, with further episodes sold commercially; a prequel, Spear of Destiny, followed in 1992.

Impact

Wolfenstein 3D was a major commercial success, selling hundreds of thousands of copies and licensing its engine to other developers. It established conventions — fast-paced gunplay, maze levels, secret push walls — that id's Doom (1993) would build upon, and it launched the long-running Wolfenstein series. Its depiction of Nazi imagery led to confiscation in Germany, where it was placed on the national index of media harmful to minors until restrictions were relaxed decades later.
