Rarotonga Treaty

The Treaty of Rarotonga, formally the South Pacific Nuclear Free Zone Treaty, establishes a nuclear-weapon-free zone in the South Pacific. It was signed in Rarotonga, Cook Islands, on 6 August 1985 — the 40th anniversary of the Hiroshima bombing — and entered into force on 11 December 1986.

Provisions

Parties undertake not to manufacture, acquire, possess, or control nuclear explosive devices; not to station such devices in their territories; and not to test them or dump radioactive waste at sea within the zone. The zone extends across the South Pacific from Latin America's Treaty of Tlatelolco region to the boundary with the Southeast Asian zone, and includes the territories of the Pacific Islands Forum members.

Protocols and parties

Three protocols are open to the nuclear-weapon states and to France, the United Kingdom, and the United States as territorial powers. France, the UK, Russia, and China have ratified relevant protocols; the United States signed the protocols in 1996 but has not ratified them. The treaty responded to decades of French testing at Moruroa and US and British tests in the region.
