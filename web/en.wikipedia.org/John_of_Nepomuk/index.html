John of Nepomuk

John of Nepomuk (Czech: Jan Nepomucký; German: Johannes Nepomuk; Latin: Ioannes Nepomucenus; c. 1345 – 20 March 1393) is the most famous saint of Bohemia. According to tradition, he was drowned in the Vltava River in Prague on the orders of King Wenceslaus IV for refusing to break the seal of confession.

Life and martyrdom

Jan was born in Pomuk (later renamed Nepomuk) in Bohemia and studied at the University of Prague and at Padua. He became vicar-general of the Archdiocese of Prague under Archbishop Jan of Jenštejn, whose conflicts with Wenceslaus IV were partly political. In 1393 the king, enraged by the confirmation of an abbot against his wishes, had John tortured and thrown from the Charles Bridge into the Vltava. Later tradition made him the Queen's confessor who refused to reveal her secrets.

Veneration

John was canonized by Pope Benedict XIII in 1729. His tomb in St. Vitus Cathedral is a masterpiece of silver work, and his statue on the Charles Bridge (1683) became the model for countless bridge statues across Catholic Europe. He is the patron saint of Bohemia, of confessors, and against floods and drowning, and is typically depicted with five stars around his head, said to have appeared over the river where his body was found.
