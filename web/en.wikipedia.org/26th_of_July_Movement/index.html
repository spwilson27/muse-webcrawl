26th of July Movement

The 26th of July Movement (Spanish: Movimiento 26 de Julio; M-26-7) was a Cuban revolutionary organization founded in 1955 by Fidel Castro and his supporters. It took its name from the failed attack on the Moncada Barracks in Santiago de Cuba on 26 July 1953, and it led the guerrilla war that overthrew the government of Fulgencio Batista in 1959.

Origins

Castro organised the movement after his release from prison in 1955 under an amnesty, uniting the survivors of the Moncada attack with other opponents of Batista. In Mexico, Castro, his brother Raúl and the Argentine Ernesto "Che" Guevara trained and prepared an expedition to Cuba.

Guerrilla war

The yacht Granma landed in Cuba in December 1956 with 82 expeditionaries; the few survivors regrouped in the Sierra Maestra mountains. From there, the movement combined rural guerrilla warfare with an urban underground, gaining strength as Batista's regime lost legitimacy.

Victory and legacy

Batista fled Cuba on 1 January 1959, and the movement's forces entered Havana. After the revolution, the movement merged with other revolutionary organisations, ultimately forming the Communist Party of Cuba in 1965. July 26 is commemorated in Cuba as the Day of the National Rebellion.
