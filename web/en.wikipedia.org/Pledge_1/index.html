Pledge 1%

Founded2014
FoundersSalesforce, Atlassian, Rally Software
TypeCorporate philanthropy movement
The Pledge 1% model of corporate philanthropy.

Pledge 1% is a corporate philanthropy movement that encourages companies to donate 1% of their equity, 1% of their product, 1% of their profit, and 1% of their employees' time to charitable causes. It was launched in December 2014 by Salesforce, Atlassian, and Rally Software, building on the "1-1-1 model" of integrated philanthropy pioneered by Salesforce founder Marc Benioff from the company's founding in 1999.

Model

Member companies make a public pledge, typically setting aside equity at an early stage so that, if the company succeeds, a meaningful sum flows to charity — equity pledges are often held through community foundations or donor-advised funds until a liquidity event such as an IPO or acquisition. Companies may adopt one or more of the four components (equity, product, profit, time) according to their stage and means. Employee time is commonly implemented as paid volunteer time off.

Growth and impact

Thousands of companies in dozens of countries have joined the movement, including technology firms and startups of all sizes. High-profile exits by member companies have generated substantial charitable proceeds; Atlassian's and other members' equity pledges alone have accounted for hundreds of millions of dollars committed to philanthropic purposes. Pledge 1% is administered as an initiative associated with Salesforce and Tides, working with community foundation partners, and operates alongside related movements such as Founders Pledge and the Giving Pledge for individuals.

The approach is sometimes discussed under the broader headings of corporate social responsibility and stakeholder capitalism: embedding giving into a company's founding documents is intended to make philanthropy structural rather than discretionary, and to align employees, customers, and communities with the company's success.
