Emmy Noether

Amalie Emmy Noether (March 23, 1882 – April 14, 1935) was a German mathematician who made fundamental contributions to abstract algebra and theoretical physics. Albert Einstein described her as the most important woman in the history of mathematics.

Working at the University of Göttingen despite being denied a paid position for years because of her sex, Noether revolutionised the theory of rings, fields and algebras; the term "Noetherian" honours her ascending chain condition. In 1918 she published Noether's theorem, which proves that every differentiable symmetry of a physical system corresponds to a conservation law — linking time-translation symmetry to conservation of energy, for example.

Dismissed by the Nazi regime in 1933, she emigrated to the United States, taking a post at Bryn Mawr College, where she died two years later following surgery. Her students, the "Noether boys", carried her approach into modern algebra worldwide.
