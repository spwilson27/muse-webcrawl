Fast food worker strikes

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The fast food worker strikes are a series of strikes and protests in the United States, beginning in 2012, concerning the minimum wage and union representation for workers in the fast food industry. The movement became known as the Fight for $15, demanding a minimum wage of US$15 per hour and the right to form a union without retaliation. The strikes were largely organised with the support of the Service Employees International Union (SEIU) and community groups.

History

The first strike took place on 29 November 2012, when about 200 workers at fast food outlets in New York City walked out. The action spread rapidly: in 2013 strikes reached dozens of cities, and on 4 December 2014 coordinated protests and civil disobedience occurred in some 190 US cities, joined by home-care and airport workers. Demonstrations also spread internationally, with actions in more than 30 countries in May 2014.

Impact

Although few fast food workers have been unionised as a direct result, the campaign is widely credited with shifting the politics of the minimum wage in the United States. Seattle, San Francisco, Los Angeles and New York State adopted phased $15 minimum wages beginning in 2014–2016, and California later enacted a sectoral law setting a $20 minimum wage for large fast food chains (effective 2024) with a Fast Food Council to set standards. The movement also influenced national politics, making a $15 federal minimum wage a mainstream policy proposal. Economists and industry groups continue to debate its effects on employment, prices and automation in the sector.
