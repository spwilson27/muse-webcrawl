Proportionality

Directly proportional quantities form a straight line through the origin.

Proportionality is a relationship between two quantities. Two quantities are directly proportional if they vary so that one equals a constant multiple of the other: y = kx, where k is the constant of proportionality. Their graph is a straight line through the origin, and the ratio y/x is constant. They are inversely proportional if their product is constant, xy = k, so that when one doubles the other halves.

Applications

Proportional relationships underlie much of physics and everyday reasoning: distance travelled at constant speed is proportional to time, the circumference of a circle is proportional to its diameter (with constant π), and Ohm's law states that current is proportional to voltage at fixed resistance. Inverse proportionality appears in Boyle's law relating gas pressure and volume.

Other meanings

The term is also used in law and ethics — proportionality is a principle in constitutional and international law requiring that measures be suitable and no more restrictive than necessary — and in politics, where proportional representation allocates legislative seats in proportion to votes cast.
