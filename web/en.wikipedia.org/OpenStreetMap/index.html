OpenStreetMap

OpenStreetMap (OSM) is a collaborative, open geographic database and digital map. It is built by a community of mappers who contribute and maintain data about roads, trails, cafés, railway stations and much more, all over the world. OpenStreetMap was founded by Steve Coast in the United Kingdom in 2004. The data is released under the Open Database License (ODbL) and is freely reusable, which has made it a foundation for countless applications and services.

How it works

Volunteers survey their surroundings with GPS receivers and smartphones, trace features from aerial imagery that providers have made available for the purpose, and import free government datasets. The database is composed of nodes (points), ways (lines and polygons) and relations, each carrying free-form tags (key=value pairs) describing what the feature is. Editors include the web-based iD editor, the advanced JOSM desktop editor, and mobile apps such as StreetComplete and OsmAnd.

Uses

OSM data powers maps and routing in apps and websites from Facebook, Apple and Snapchat to humanitarian relief systems, cycling and hiking guides, logistics tools and research. The Humanitarian OpenStreetMap Team coordinates mapping of vulnerable regions, famously after the 2010 Haiti earthquake and subsequent disasters, where up-to-date maps aided responders.

History

Coast started the project because most map data in the UK was encumbered by restrictive licensing, despite being produced with public money. The OpenStreetMap Foundation was established in 2006. Yahoo! and later Bing allowed tracing from their aerial imagery, greatly accelerating coverage; GPS maker TomTom and mapping firms have used and contributed to OSM. The project passed one million registered users in 2013 and ten million around 2023.
