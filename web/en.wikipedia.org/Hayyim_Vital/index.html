Hayyim Vital

Born1542, Safed, Ottoman Palestine
Died1620, Damascus
Known forPrincipal disciple and scribe of Isaac Luria; author of Etz Chaim

Hayyim ben Joseph Vital (1542–1620) was a rabbi and Kabbalist in Safed and later Damascus, and the foremost disciple of Isaac Luria ("the Ari"), the founder of Lurianic Kabbalah. Almost everything known of Luria's teachings comes through Vital's writings, since Luria himself published nothing.

Life

Born in Safed, Vital studied Talmud and Kabbalah, including with Moses Cordovero. When Luria arrived in Safed in 1570, Vital became his chief student for the roughly two years until Luria's death in 1572. Vital spent the rest of his life recording, organising and guarding Luria's doctrines, insisting that he alone was authorised to transmit them.

Works

His magnum opus, Etz Chaim ("Tree of Life"), sets out the Lurianic system — the contraction of God (tzimtzum), the shattering of the vessels, and cosmic repair (tikkun). Vital jealously restricted access to his manuscripts, but copies circulated and were eventually printed, making Lurianic Kabbalah the dominant form of Jewish mysticism. He also wrote works on alchemy and practical Kabbalah and kept a dream diary, published as Sefer ha-Hezyonot.
