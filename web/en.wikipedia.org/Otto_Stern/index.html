Otto Stern

Born17 February 1888, Sohrau, German Empire (now Żory, Poland)
Died17 August 1969 (aged 81), Berkeley, California, U.S.
Known forStern–Gerlach experiment; molecular beam method; magnetic moment of the proton
AwardsNobel Prize in Physics (1943)

Otto Stern (1888–1969) was a German-American physicist who won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1943 for his contribution to the development of the molecular beam method and his discovery of the magnetic moment of the proton. He was nominated for the Nobel Prize a record 82 times.

Work

After studies at Breslau and work with Albert Einstein in Prague and Zurich, Stern developed the molecular beam method at Frankfurt. In 1922, with Walther Gerlach, he performed the Stern–Gerlach experiment: a beam of silver atoms passed through an inhomogeneous magnetic field split into two, providing striking evidence for space quantization and, in retrospect, for electron spin. As director of the Institute for Physical Chemistry in Hamburg, he measured the magnetic moments of the proton and deuteron (1933), finding values at odds with theory. Fleeing Nazi Germany in 1933, he joined the Carnegie Institute of Technology in Pittsburgh, where he remained until his retirement to Berkeley.
