Marseille (French: Marseille; Occitan: Marselha) is a city in southern France, the prefecture of the Bouches-du-Rhône department and of the Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur region. Situated on the Mediterranean coast, it is the second-most populous city in France after Paris and the country's oldest city, founded around 600 BC by Greek settlers from Phocaea who named it Massalia. Marseille is France's largest port for freight and a major cruise ship terminal; its metropolitan population is about 1.9 million.

History

Greek Massalia became a flourishing independent port trading wine, tin, and pottery with the Celtic interior, and founded colonies such as Nice and Antibes. It allied with Rome against Carthage but lost its independence to Julius Caesar in 49 BC after siding with Pompey. Marseille joined the Kingdom of France in 1481. Its merchants prospered from Levantine trade, and its volunteers marched to Paris in 1792 singing the song that became the French national anthem, La Marseillaise. The 19th century brought explosive growth with steam navigation and the opening of the Suez Canal; the city was the gateway to France's colonial empire, receiving immigrants from Italy, Spain, Armenia, North Africa, and the Comoros, which shaped its famously cosmopolitan character.

Geography and landmarks

The city centres on the Vieux-Port (Old Port), guarded by the forts of Saint-Jean and Saint-Nicolas. Landmarks include the hilltop basilica of Notre-Dame de la Garde, the cathedral (Cathédrale de la Major), the 17th-century Hôtel de Ville, Le Corbusier's Cité Radieuse (1947–52), and the MuCEM museum (opened 2013, when Marseille was European Capital of Culture). Offshore lie the Frioul archipelago and the Château d'If, the island prison of Dumas's The Count of Monte Cristo. To the east, the fjord-like inlets of the Calanques form a national park.

Culture

Marseille is known for bouillabaisse, its saffron fish stew, for pastis, for its football club Olympique de Marseille (European champions in 1993), and as a birthplace of French hip-hop. The writer Marcel Pagnol and the painter Paul Cézanne (in nearby Aix-en-Provence) are among its celebrated cultural figures.
