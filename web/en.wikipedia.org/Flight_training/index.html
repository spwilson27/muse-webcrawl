Flight training

Flight training is a course of study used when learning to pilot an aircraft. The overall purpose of primary and intermediate flight training is the acquisition and honing of basic airmanship skills. Flight training can be conducted under a structured accredited syllabus with a flight instructor at a flight school, or on a less structured basis with a freelance instructor.

Licences and syllabi

Student pilots study both ground school subjects — aerodynamics, air law, meteorology, navigation, and human factors — and practical flying with an instructor in dual instruction, progressing through exercises such as take-offs and landings, stalls, steep turns, navigation cross-country flights and emergency procedures. The first solo flight is a major milestone. For a private pilot licence, regulators typically require a minimum of about 40–45 hours of flight time, including solo and cross-country experience, followed by written examinations and a practical flight test (checkride) with an examiner.

Further qualifications build on the private licence: an instrument rating permits flight in cloud and poor visibility by reference to instruments; a multi-engine rating, a commercial pilot licence, and ultimately the airline transport pilot licence are required for professional flying.

Training devices

Much training, particularly for instrument flying and airline operations, takes place in flight simulators and flight training devices, which are cheaper and safer than aircraft and allow practice of failures and emergencies. Military flight training proceeds through primary, basic and advanced phases on progressively faster aircraft before conversion to an operational type.
