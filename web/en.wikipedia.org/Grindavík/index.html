Grindavík is a fishing town on the southern coast of Iceland's Reykjanes Peninsula. It is one of the few towns in Iceland with a harbour on the south coast and has long been an important centre for fishing and fish processing. The nearby Blue Lagoon geothermal spa is one of Iceland's most visited attractions.

History

Grindavík is one of the oldest recorded settlements in Iceland, mentioned as a trading and fishing station from the 10th century. In 1627, Barbary pirates raided the town in the events known as the Turkish Abductions. The town grew with the fishing industry in the 20th century; most of its residents historically worked in fishing-related industries.

2023–2024 volcanic activity

In November 2023, intense earthquake swarms and ground deformation led to the complete evacuation of Grindavík. A series of eruptions of the Sundhnúkur crater row began on 18 December 2023, and lava flows later reached defensive barriers around the town; some houses were destroyed by lava and fissures in January 2024. Further eruptions occurred through 2024 and into 2025, keeping most residents displaced and the town largely closed, though some fishing operations and day access resumed intermittently.

Sport

The town's sports club, Knattspyrnudeild UMFG, fields football teams known locally as Grindavík. The Icelandic Saltfish Museum is located in the town.
