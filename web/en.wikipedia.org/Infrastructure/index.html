Infrastructure

Infrastructure is the set of fundamental facilities and systems that support the sustainable functionality of households and firms. It typically includes roads, bridges, tunnels, water supply, sewers, electrical grids, telecommunications networks (including internet connectivity and broadband access), and so on. Serving a country, city, or other area, these facilities are essential to its economy and quality of life.

Types

Infrastructure is commonly divided into "hard" infrastructure — the physical networks necessary for the functioning of a modern industrial nation — and "soft" infrastructure, the institutions and services that maintain the economic, health, social, and cultural standards of a population, such as education, healthcare, law enforcement, and financial systems. Some classifications also recognise green infrastructure, which uses natural systems (wetlands, forests, green roofs) to provide services such as stormwater management.

Economic aspects

Economists regard infrastructure as a form of public capital with strong positive externalities: investment in transport or communications networks benefits users far beyond those who pay directly. Because of natural-monopoly characteristics and high upfront costs, infrastructure has traditionally been financed and provided by governments, though public–private partnerships and regulated private utilities are common in many sectors.

History

The word entered English from French in the early twentieth century, originally in a military and railway context. Large-scale infrastructure building characterised much of the nineteenth and twentieth centuries — canals, railways, electrification, interstate highways — and remains a major focus of development policy in emerging economies, where gaps in basic infrastructure constrain growth.

Challenges

Ageing infrastructure in developed countries, maintenance backlogs, vulnerability to natural disasters and climate change, and the challenge of financing renewal are recurring policy issues. Professional bodies such as the American Society of Civil Engineers periodically grade infrastructure condition and warn of the economic costs of underinvestment.
