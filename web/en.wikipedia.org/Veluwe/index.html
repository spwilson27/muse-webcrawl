Veluwe

The Veluwe is a forest-rich ridge of hills in the province of Gelderland in the Netherlands. Covering about 1,100 km², it is the largest lowland nature area in northwestern Europe, composed of woodland, heathland, drift sands and lakes. The region's terrain was shaped by the Saalian glaciation, which pushed up ridges of sand and gravel (push moraines) rising to over 100 metres — high by Dutch standards.

Nature and recreation

The Veluwe contains two national parks: De Hoge Veluwe National Park, which also hosts the Kröller-Müller Museum with its large Van Gogh collection, and Veluwezoom National Park, the oldest national park in the Netherlands. The area is popular for walking and cycling, and animals such as red deer, wild boar, mouflon and — since 2019 — wolves can be found there.

Towns

Apeldoorn, with the royal palace Het Loo, lies on the eastern edge of the Veluwe. Other settlements include Ede, Harderwijk, Putten and Nunspeet.
