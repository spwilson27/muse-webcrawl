Traffic light

A traffic light, or traffic signal, is a signalling device positioned at road intersections, pedestrian crossings and other locations to control flows of traffic. Signals normally show three colours — red, amber (yellow) and green — following a standard sequence codified internationally by the Vienna Convention on Road Signs and Signals.

History

The first traffic signal, a gas-lit manually operated device, was installed outside the Houses of Parliament in London in 1868; it exploded less than a month later. Electric signals appeared in Cleveland, Ohio, in 1914, and the three-colour signal was introduced in Detroit in 1920. Garrett Morgan received a 1923 patent for an improved design.

Operation

Signals may run on fixed timing plans, respond to vehicle detectors, or be coordinated along corridors and adapted by central control systems. Separate aspects serve turning traffic, trams, buses and pedestrians; flashing modes indicate caution or signal malfunction in some jurisdictions.
