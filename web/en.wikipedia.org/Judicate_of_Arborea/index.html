The Judicate of Arborea (Sardinian: Judicadu de Arbaree; Italian: Giudicato di Arborea) was one of the four kingdoms (judicates) into which the island of Sardinia was divided in the Middle Ages. Occupying the west-central part of the island, with its capital first at Tharros and later at Oristano, Arborea was the longest-lived of the judicates and the last bastion of Sardinian independence against Pisan, Genoese and Aragonese encroachment.

Origins

The judicates arose as Sardinia slipped from effective Byzantine control; by the eleventh century Arborea was governed by hereditary judges (judices) of the Lacon-Gunale and later Bas (de Baux) dynasties. The judges ruled with a council (corona de logu) in a constitutional order whose customs were codified in written law.

High point under Marianus IV and Eleanor

Under Marianus IV (r. 1347–1376), Arborea allied at times with and at times fought against the Crown of Aragon, which had invaded Sardinia from 1323, and nearly drove the Aragonese from the island. His daughter Eleanor of Arborea (r. 1383–1404), regent for her sons and one of the most celebrated figures in Sardinian history, continued the war and promulgated the Carta de Logu (c. 1392), a comprehensive legal code in the Sardinian language that regulated agriculture, fire prevention, and civil and criminal matters; it remained in force in much of Sardinia until 1827.

Decline and extinction

After Eleanor's death from plague in 1404, and the defeat of her grandson William III of Narbonne at the Battle of Sanluri (1409) by Martin I of Sicily, the judicate's power collapsed. In 1420 William sold his rights to Alfonso V of Aragon for 100,000 gold florins, formally ending the judicate, though the later revolt of Leonardo Alagon, Marquis of Oristano — defeated at Macomer in 1478 — briefly revived Arborean claims.

Legacy

Arborea and Eleanor's Carta de Logu occupy a central place in Sardinian regional identity; Eleanor is commemorated by a statue in Oristano's Piazza Eleonora.
