LocationPincian Hill, Rome, Italy

The Villa Borghese gardens are a large landscape garden in the English style in Rome, containing a number of villas, museums and attractions. Covering about 80 hectares on and around the Pincian Hill, it is one of the largest public parks in the city and a favourite place for walking, rowing and cycling.

History

The estate was created in the early 17th century for Cardinal Scipione Borghese, nephew of Pope Paul V, around the Casino Borghese designed by Flaminio Ponzio. The gardens were redesigned in the natural English landscape style in the late 18th century. In 1903 the Italian state purchased the property and gave it to the city of Rome as a public park.

Attractions

The park contains the Galleria Borghese, one of Rome's great art museums, housed in the original casino; the Villa Medici; the Bioparco (zoo); the Casa del Cinema; the Museo Pietro Canonica; and the Pincio terrace overlooking Piazza del Popolo. A small lake with the Temple of Aesculapius offers rowing boats, and the Piazza di Siena hosts international show-jumping competitions.
