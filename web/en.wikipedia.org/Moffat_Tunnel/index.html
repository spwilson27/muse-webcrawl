Moffat Tunnel

Moffat Tunnel

Location: Colorado, United States

Length: 6.2 miles (10.0 km)

Opened: February 1928 (railroad tunnel)

Carries: Union Pacific Railroad; water (pioneer bore)

The Moffat Tunnel is a railroad and water tunnel that cuts through the Continental Divide in north-central Colorado. Named after Colorado railroad pioneer David Moffat, the tunnel opened in 1928 and, at 6.2 miles (10.0 km) long, was one of the longest railroad tunnels in the United States. It lies under James Peak, with its east portal near Rollinsville and its west portal at Winter Park.

History

David Moffat's Denver, Northwestern and Pacific Railway had crossed the divide over Rollins Pass at 11,660 feet (3,554 m), a route plagued by snow and steep grades. Moffat sought financing for a tunnel for years but died in 1911 before construction began. After voters approved the Moffat Tunnel Improvement District, construction started in 1923. A smaller "pioneer tunnel" was bored alongside to assist excavation and later became a water tunnel, carrying water from the Fraser River basin under the divide to Denver's system.

The first train passed through on 26 February 1928. The tunnel shortened the Denver–Salt Lake City route by 176 miles relative to the Rollins Pass route it replaced.

Operations

The tunnel is now owned by the State of Colorado and used by Union Pacific Railroad freight trains and Amtrak's California Zephyr. A ventilation system with large doors and fans clears exhaust between trains. Because the bore is single-track, capacity is limited to roughly a few dozen trains per day. Seasonal ski trains between Denver and Winter Park also use the tunnel.

Water tunnel

The pioneer bore delivers water eastward as part of Denver Water's Moffat Collection System, one of the transbasin diversions supplying the Denver metropolitan area.
