Macapá

Macapá is a city in Brazil, located on the northern shore of the Amazon River near its mouth on the Atlantic Ocean. It is the capital and largest city of the state of Amapá. The city lies almost exactly on the Equator — the Marco Zero monument and the Zerão football stadium are bisected by the equatorial line — and it is the only Brazilian state capital that cannot be reached by road from the rest of the country, being accessible only by boat or aeroplane.

History

The settlement grew around the Fortaleza de São José de Macapá, a Portuguese colonial fortress built between 1764 and 1782 to defend the mouth of the Amazon. Macapá became the capital of the Federal Territory of Amapá in 1944 and of the state of Amapá when it was created by the 1988 constitution.

Geography and economy

The municipality covers about 6,400 square kilometres and had roughly half a million inhabitants in recent census estimates. Its economy rests on public administration, commerce, fishing, and the extraction of manganese, timber, and açaí. The port of Santana serves the metropolitan area.
