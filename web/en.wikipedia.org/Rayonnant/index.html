Rayonnant

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Rayonnant (French for "radiant") is the term for the middle phase of French Gothic architecture, flourishing roughly from 1240 until about 1350. The style takes its name from the radiating spokes of its great rose windows, and is characterised by the dissolution of solid wall into enormous expanses of stained glass divided by slender bar tracery. Where Early Gothic had still treated windows as openings punched in a wall, Rayonnant architects replaced the wall with a glazed cage of glass supported by an elegant stone skeleton, achieving interiors flooded with coloured light. The style originated in royal Paris and spread across Europe, influencing Gothic design in the Holy Roman Empire, England and Spain.

Characteristics

The key innovation was bar tracery — thin stone bars forming window divisions, developed in the rebuilding of Reims Cathedral and perfected at Amiens — replacing the heavier plate tracery of earlier Gothic. Armed with this technique, architects enlarged clerestory and triforium windows until the upper walls were almost entirely glass; the transept façades of Notre-Dame de Paris, rebuilt by Jean de Chelles and Pierre de Montreuil, display enormous rose windows framed by fields of glazing. In interiors, shafts were refined into bundles of thin colonnettes, mouldings became finer, and ornament such as gables, pinnacles and blind tracery proliferated over every surface.

Major monuments

The supreme monument of the style is the Sainte-Chapelle in Paris (consecrated 1248), built by Louis IX to house the relic of the Crown of Thorns: its upper chapel is essentially a cage of glass, 15 windows each 15 metres high, holding one of the most complete surviving ensembles of 13th-century stained glass. Other landmarks include the Rayonnant choir of Amiens Cathedral, the nave of Saint-Denis, Saint-Urbain de Troyes, and the choir of Cologne Cathedral, which exported the style into the Empire. In England, Rayonnant ideas fed into the Decorated style (as at Exeter Cathedral), while in Spain they shaped León Cathedral.
