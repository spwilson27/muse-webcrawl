The New York Times (often abbreviated NYT) is an American daily newspaper based in New York City. Founded in 1851, it is one of the most influential newspapers in the world and has won more Pulitzer Prizes — more than 130 — than any other news organisation. Its motto, "All the News That's Fit to Print," has appeared on the front page since 1897.

History

The paper was founded as the New-York Daily Times by journalist and politician Henry Jarvis Raymond and banker George Jones, and shortened its name to The New York Times in 1857. After a period of decline, it was purchased in 1896 by Adolph S. Ochs, publisher of the Chattanooga Times, whose emphasis on sober, fact-centred reporting rebuilt its reputation; control passed to his son-in-law Arthur Hays Sulzberger, and the paper has remained under the stewardship of the Ochs-Sulzberger family since.

Landmark episodes in its history include publication of the Pentagon Papers in 1971, which led to the Supreme Court's landmark ruling in New York Times Co. v. United States affirming freedom of the press; its coverage of the September 11 attacks; and the 2003 Jayson Blair plagiarism scandal, which prompted major newsroom reforms.

Digital transition

In 2011 the Times introduced a metered paywall for its website, a model then considered risky that became the template for the subscription strategies of news publishers worldwide. Digital subscriptions grew into the millions, and the company crossed ten million subscribers in the mid-2020s, with growth driven also by products such as Cooking, Games (including Wordle, acquired in 2022), Wirecutter and The Athletic (acquired in 2022).

Influence and criticism

The Times is frequently described as the American newspaper of record. Its editorial page is broadly liberal, and the paper has faced recurring criticism from both the political right — over alleged bias — and the left — over coverage choices on issues including the Iraq War and transgender healthcare — as well as scrutiny of the separation between its news and opinion operations.
