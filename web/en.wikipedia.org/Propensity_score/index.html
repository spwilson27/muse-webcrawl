Propensity score

In the statistical analysis of observational data, the propensity score is the probability of a unit (e.g., a person) being assigned to a particular treatment given a set of observed covariates. Introduced by Paul Rosenbaum and Donald Rubin in 1983, propensity scores are used to reduce selection bias by equating groups based on these covariates, emulating some features of a randomized experiment.

Estimation and use

The propensity score is most often estimated using logistic regression of treatment status on observed covariates. Once estimated, it can be used in matching, stratification, inverse probability weighting, or as a covariate in regression adjustment. Conditional on the propensity score, the distribution of observed covariates is balanced between treated and untreated groups, a property known as the balancing property.

Limitations

Propensity score methods can only adjust for observed confounders; unmeasured confounding remains a threat to causal conclusions. The approach also depends on overlap (positivity) between treatment groups and on correct specification of the propensity model.
