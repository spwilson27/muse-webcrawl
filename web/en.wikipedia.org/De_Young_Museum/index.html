De Young Museum

The de Young Museum, formally the M. H. de Young Memorial Museum, is a fine arts museum located in Golden Gate Park in San Francisco, California. Together with the Legion of Honor it forms the Fine Arts Museums of San Francisco. The museum is named for newspaperman M. H. de Young, a founder of the San Francisco Chronicle.

History

The museum grew out of an Egyptian-style building erected for the California Midwinter International Exposition of 1894. De Young championed making the fair's art displays permanent, and the museum opened in 1895, expanding over the following decades. The original buildings were severely damaged in the 1989 Loma Prieta earthquake, and after years of political debate the museum was rebuilt on the same site.

Building and collections

The new building, designed by the Swiss architects Herzog & de Meuron together with San Francisco firm Fong & Chan, opened in October 2005. Clad in perforated and embossed copper that is intended to oxidise and blend with the park landscape, it includes a 44-metre twisting observation tower open to the public free of charge. The collections comprise American art from the seventeenth century to the present, international contemporary art, textiles and costumes, and major holdings of art from Africa, Oceania and the Indigenous Americas.
