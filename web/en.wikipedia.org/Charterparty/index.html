Charterparty

A charterparty (sometimes charter party) is a maritime contract between a shipowner and a "charterer" for the hire of either a ship for the carriage of passengers or cargo, or a yacht for pleasure purposes. The word derives from the French charte partie, a "divided charter" — historically a contract written in duplicate on one sheet, torn or cut in two, with each party keeping a half whose matching edges proved authenticity.

Types of charter

There are three principal types. A demise charter (or bareboat charter) transfers possession and control of the vessel to the charterer, who provides crew and fuel and is treated as the owner for the charter period. A voyage charter hires the ship, with crew, for a single voyage between specified ports for an agreed freight. A time charter hires the vessel for a set period; the shipowner retains nautical management while the charterer directs the ship's employment and pays hire, bunkers and port charges.

Terms

Charterparties are usually concluded on standard printed forms published by bodies such as BIMCO — for example the GENCON form for voyage charters and the NYPE form for time charters — negotiated and fixed by shipbrokers. Key terms include laytime (the time allowed for loading and discharge), demurrage (liquidated damages for exceeding laytime), despatch (a reward for early completion), and the allocation of seaworthiness, deviation and lien rights. In the tramp trade a charter may be recorded simply by a fixture note and an exchange of recaps before the formal charterparty is signed.
