Hacı I Giray

Reign1441–1466
TitleKhan of Crimea
DynastyGiray dynasty (founder)
Died1466, Bakhchysarai

Hacı I Giray (died 1466) was the founder of the Crimean Khanate and of the Giray dynasty, which ruled Crimea for more than three centuries. Claiming descent from Genghis Khan through the line of Jochi, he carved an independent state out of the disintegrating Golden Horde in the Crimean peninsula.

Rise to power

After years of exile and struggle — including time spent in Lithuania under the protection of Grand Duke Casimir IV — Hacı Giray seized power in Crimea around 1441 with Lithuanian support, minting his own coins and defeating rival claimants backed by the Great Horde. He made his capital at Qırq Yer and later at Salacıq, near the site of Bakhchysarai, which his son Meñli I Giray would develop into the khanate's capital.

Reign

Hacı Giray balanced relations between Lithuania, Muscovy, the Genoese colonies on the Crimean coast and the rising Ottoman Empire, defeating an invasion by the Great Horde under Sayid Ahmad I in 1465. His reign established the essential structures of the khanate: a Chinggisid khan ruling with the great clan beys (notably the Shirin), and an economy sustained by agriculture, trade and slave-raiding into the steppe frontier. After his death in 1466, his sons contested the succession, and within a decade the khanate accepted Ottoman suzerainty (1478), under which the Girays nonetheless retained their throne until the Russian annexation of 1783.
