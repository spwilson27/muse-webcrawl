McDonnell Douglas F/A-18 Hornet

Role: Multirole fighter
Manufacturer: McDonnell Douglas / Boeing; Northrop
First flight: 18 November 1978
Introduced: 7 January 1983

The McDonnell Douglas F/A-18 Hornet is an all-weather supersonic, twin-engine, carrier-capable, multirole combat aircraft, designed as both a fighter and attack aircraft (hence the F/A designation). Designed by McDonnell Douglas and Northrop, the F/A-18 was derived from Northrop's YF-17 that lost against the F-16 in the USAF lightweight fighter programme. It is the first US aircraft designed from the start for both air-to-air and air-to-ground missions, and has proven versatile throughout its career, having performed fighter escort, fleet air defence, suppression of enemy air defences, air interdiction, close air support, and aerial reconnaissance.

Development and variants

The Hornet entered service with the United States Navy and Marine Corps and has been operated by the air forces of Canada, Australia, Spain, Finland, Switzerland, Malaysia and Kuwait. Improved C and D models succeeded the original A/B, with enhanced avionics and weapons. The substantially larger F/A-18E/F Super Hornet and EA-18G Growler electronic-warfare variant followed.

Operational history

The F/A-18 first saw combat in the 1986 strikes on Libya and the 1991 Gulf War, and served in every major US air campaign since. The type is flown by the US Navy's Blue Angels demonstration team since 2021 in its Super Hornet form.
