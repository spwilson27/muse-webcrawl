Section 504


The 1977 sit-in demanding Section 504 regulations

Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973 is a United States federal civil rights law that prohibits discrimination based on disability in any program or activity receiving federal financial assistance. Its key provision states that no otherwise qualified individual with a disability in the United States shall, solely by reason of his or her disability, be excluded from participation in, be denied the benefits of, or be subjected to discrimination under any program or activity receiving federal financial assistance.

History

The Rehabilitation Act was signed in 1973, but regulations implementing Section 504 were not issued for four years, prompting disability rights activists to stage protests nationwide. In April 1977, demonstrators occupied federal buildings, including a 28-day sit-in at the San Francisco office of the Department of Health, Education, and Welfare — the longest sit-in at a federal building to that date — after which Secretary Joseph Califano signed the regulations. Section 504 was the first federal civil rights protection for people with disabilities and a model for the Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990.

Scope

Section 504 applies to federal agencies, federal contractors and grantees, and programs receiving federal funds, including public schools, universities, and hospitals. In education, it requires schools to provide a free appropriate public education to qualified students with disabilities through accommodations set out in a "504 plan," covering conditions that do not qualify for an individualized education program under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act.

Definition of disability

The Act defines an individual with a disability as a person with a physical or mental impairment that substantially limits one or more major life activities, a person with a record of such an impairment, or a person regarded as having such an impairment. The ADA Amendments Act of 2008 broadened the interpretation of this definition for both statutes.

Enforcement

Compliance is overseen by the Office for Civil Rights in each federal agency, and individuals may file complaints or bring private lawsuits.
