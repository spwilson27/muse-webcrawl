The 1980 Damascus Titan missile explosion was a "Broken Arrow" incident at a United States Air Force Titan II intercontinental ballistic missile site near Damascus, Arkansas. On 18 September 1980 a technician dropped a socket from a wrench, puncturing the missile's first-stage fuel tank; the leaking fuel vapour exploded early the following morning, killing one airman and blowing the missile's 9-megaton nuclear warhead out of the silo. The warhead's safety devices prevented any nuclear detonation or radioactive contamination.

Incident

During routine maintenance on the evening of 18 September 1980, Senior Airman David Livingston dropped a ratchet socket while working on a pressurised Titan II in Launch Complex 374-7. The socket fell about 24 metres (80 feet), bounced off the thrust mount and punctured the missile's skin, breaching the first-stage tank of toxic Aerozine 50 fuel. The complex was evacuated as a fuel vapour cloud built up inside the silo.

At about 3:00 a.m. on 19 September, a two-man team, including Livingston and Sergeant Jeff Kennedy, re-entered the area to activate an exhaust fan. When Livingston switched on the fan, an electrical arc apparently ignited the accumulated fuel vapour. The explosion blew off the silo's 740-ton blast door, ejected the second stage, and threw the W-53 warhead some 30 metres from the complex, where it was later found intact by security forces. Twenty-one personnel were injured; Livingston died of his injuries the next day.

Aftermath

The site, part of the 308th Strategic Missile Wing based at Little Rock Air Force Base, was destroyed and never rebuilt; cleanup cost millions of dollars and the pit was filled with gravel and concrete. The incident intensified public debate about the safety of the ageing Titan II system, which was retired later in the decade. Investigative reporting on the accident, including David Livingston's posthumous recognition, contributed to his being awarded the Air Force Cross decades later. The event is the subject of Eric Schlosser's book Command and Control (2013) and a 2016 PBS American Experience documentary.
