Fictional universe

A fictional universe, or fictional world, is the setting for one or many works of fiction, which may be self-consistent with its own distinct set of natural laws, history, geography, cultures, and characters. Fictional universes may span novels, films, television, comics, and games.

Characteristics

A fictional universe differs from a simple setting in its internal consistency and depth: it typically has a worked-out history, fictional languages, mythology, and rules that constrain what can happen within it. Well-known examples include J. R. R. Tolkien's Middle-earth, the Star Wars galaxy, and the Marvel Universe.

Shared universes

A shared universe is one in which works by multiple authors or studios take place, each contributing to an overarching continuity. Maintaining consistency across such works often requires detailed reference material, sometimes called a "series bible".

Worldbuilding

The craft of constructing fictional universes is called worldbuilding. Creators may design maps, calendars, economies, and ecosystems to give the world verisimilitude, even where only a fraction of the material appears in the published work.
