Qiantang River

The Qiantang tidal bore

The Qiantang River (Chinese: 钱塘江) is a river in eastern China flowing through Zhejiang province to Hangzhou Bay on the East China Sea. About 459 kilometres (285 mi) long, it passes the provincial capital Hangzhou. The river is famous for its tidal bore, the largest in the world.

The tidal bore

The funnel shape of Hangzhou Bay amplifies the incoming tide into a bore that travels upstream against the river's current, reaching heights of up to about 9 metres (30 ft) and speeds of up to 40 km/h, and audible from far away. Tide-watching on the Qiantang has been a tradition for over two thousand years, centred on the Mid-Autumn Festival (the 18th day of the 8th lunar month), when crowds gather at vantage points such as Yanguan in Haining to watch the bore. The phenomenon is dangerous: spectators have been swept away, and extensive sea walls — the great stone seawall along the north bank — have been maintained since ancient times to protect farmland from the bore and storm tides.

Course and bridges

The river rises in Anhui and western Zhejiang, is known in its upper reaches as the Xin'an River, and becomes the Qiantang below Hangzhou. The Qiantang River Bridge, designed by Mao Yisheng and completed in 1937, was the first modern double-deck road-and-rail bridge built by Chinese engineers; several large modern crossings, including the Hangzhou Bay Bridge further downstream, now span its estuary.
