Firehole River

Firehole River
Location: Yellowstone National Park, Wyoming
Length: approx. 21 mi (34 km)
Mouth: Madison River
Notable features: Geothermal inflow, Firehole Falls

The Firehole River is a tributary of the Madison River in Yellowstone National Park, Wyoming, famed for flowing through several of the park's major geyser basins. Rising at Madison Lake on the Continental Divide, it runs about 21 miles north to join the Gibbon River and form the Madison.

Geothermal influence

Hot springs and geysers discharge into the river in the Upper, Midway and Lower Geyser Basins, raising its temperature significantly — in summer by as much as 10 °C in places. Landmarks along its course include Kepler Cascades, Firehole Falls and the Grand Prismatic Spring's Excelsior Geyser runoff.

Wildlife and fishing

The warm water supports rainbow and brown trout and is a noted, if challenging, fly-fishing stream; in winter bison and elk gather along its banks, and trumpeter swans overwinter on its ice-free stretches.
