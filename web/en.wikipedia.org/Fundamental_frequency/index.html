Fundamental frequency

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The fundamental frequency, often referred to simply as the fundamental and abbreviated f0 or F0, is defined as the lowest frequency of a periodic waveform. In music, the fundamental is the musical pitch of a note that is perceived as the lowest partial present. The fundamental is the first component of the harmonic series; the higher components are called overtones or harmonics.

In acoustics

For a vibrating string fixed at both ends, the fundamental mode has a wavelength twice the string length, so its frequency is v/2L, where v is the wave speed on the string. In wind instruments the fundamental corresponds to the lowest playable note of a given tube length and fingering. Because a periodic sound's period T is the reciprocal of the fundamental frequency, f0 = 1/T, the fundamental also sets the repetition rate that the ear chiefly uses to judge pitch.

Missing fundamental

Listeners perceive the pitch of the fundamental even when that component is absent or very weak — the phenomenon of the missing fundamental (or residue pitch). The brain infers f0 from the spacing of the higher harmonics. This is why small loudspeakers that cannot reproduce low bass still convey a sense of the bass note, and why the pitch of a telephone voice (band-limited to frequencies above about 300 Hz) is still recognised.

Speech and electronics

In speech analysis, F0 is the rate of vocal-fold vibration: typical values are around 85–155 Hz for adult men and 165–255 Hz for adult women, and its contour carries intonation and tone. In signal processing and electrical engineering, the fundamental is the lowest-frequency component of a complex waveform, such as the 50 or 60 Hz component of mains electricity, with distortion products appearing at its harmonics.
