SNCF

The Société nationale des chemins de fer français (SNCF; "National Company of the French Railways") is France's national state-owned railway company. Founded in 1938 from the nationalisation of the country's main railway companies, it operates nearly all rail traffic in France and Monaco, including the high-speed TGV network, the Paris region's Transilien services, and regional TER trains, as well as freight through subsidiaries such as Geodis and Keolis.

A TGV, SNCF's high-speed train, the backbone of French long-distance rail.

History

SNCF was created on 1 January 1938, merging the major private and state railway networks. During the Second World War the company was compelled to transport deportees to Nazi concentration camps, a role formally acknowledged by the company in 2010. After the war SNCF drove electrification and speed records: in 2007 a modified TGV reached 574.8 km/h (357.2 mph), a world record for conventional rail. The TGV network, inaugurated in 1981 between Paris and Lyon, made France a pioneer of high-speed rail. A 2018 reform converted SNCF into a state-owned joint-stock group; since 2020 the infrastructure manager SNCF Réseau and operator SNCF Voyageurs have been subsidiaries of the SNCF group holding company.

Services

Passenger services include TGV inOui and low-cost Ouigo high-speed trains, Intercités long-distance trains, regional TER services ordered by the French regions, and Transilien commuter trains in Île-de-France. SNCF also has stakes in international services such as Eurostar (to London via the Channel Tunnel) and Lyria (to Switzerland).
