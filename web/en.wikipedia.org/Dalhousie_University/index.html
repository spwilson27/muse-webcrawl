Dalhousie University (commonly called Dal) is a public research university in Halifax, Nova Scotia, Canada. Founded in 1818, it is one of Canada's oldest universities and the largest in Atlantic Canada, with around 20,000 students across thirteen faculties. It is a member of the U15 group of Canadian research-intensive universities.

History

The university was founded by George Ramsay, 9th Earl of Dalhousie and lieutenant governor of Nova Scotia, modelled on the University of Edinburgh and intended to be open to all regardless of religion — unusual for its time. Classes were interrupted repeatedly in the nineteenth century by funding disputes before a permanent reorganisation in 1863. In 1997 Dalhousie amalgamated with the Technical University of Nova Scotia, adding engineering and computer science strengths, and in 2012 it merged with the Nova Scotia Agricultural College in Truro to form its Faculty of Agriculture.

Campuses and faculties

Dalhousie operates four campuses: the Studley and Carleton campuses in Halifax's south end (arts, science, law, management, and the health faculties including medicine and dentistry), the Sexton campus downtown (engineering and architecture), and the Agricultural Campus in Bible Hill near Truro. Its Schulich School of Law, founded in 1883, is the oldest university-based common law school in Canada, and the university is notable for oceanography and ocean research through the Ocean Frontier Institute.

Student life and athletics

The Dalhousie Tigers compete in Atlantic University Sport, while the Dalhousie Rams represent the Agricultural Campus. The university's colours are black and gold, and its student union, founded in 1869, is among Canada's oldest. Notable alumni include three prime ministers of Canada and Nobel laureate Arthur B. McDonald.
