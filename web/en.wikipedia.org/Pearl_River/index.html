Pearl River

The Pearl River (Chinese: 珠江, Zhū Jiāng) is an extensive river system in southern China, named for the Pearl Island (海珠) that once lay in its course through Guangzhou. It is formed by the confluence of the Xi (West), Bei (North), and Dong (East) rivers, and flows into the Pearl River Delta and the South China Sea between Hong Kong and Macau.

Geography

The Pearl River is about 2,400 kilometres (1,500 mi) long when measured along its longest tributary, the Xi River, making it the third-longest river in China after the Yangtze and the Yellow River. Its drainage basin of roughly 453,700 km² covers most of Guangdong and Guangxi provinces, along with parts of Yunnan, Guizhou, Hunan, and Jiangxi, and a small portion of northeastern Vietnam.

The river's estuary, the Pearl River Delta, is one of the most densely urbanised and economically productive regions in the world, encompassing Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Dongguan, Foshan, Zhuhai, and the special administrative regions of Hong Kong and Macau. Major crossings include the Humen Pearl River Bridge and the Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macau Bridge, opened in 2018.

History

The Pearl River has been a centre of trade for centuries; Guangzhou (Canton) was China's principal port for foreign commerce during the Qing dynasty's Canton System. The delta's silt has progressively extended the coastline, and islands in the estuary such as Lantau have grown from deposits carried downstream.
