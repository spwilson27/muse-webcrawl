Marguerite Catherine Perey (19 October 1909 – 13 May 1975) was a French physicist and a student of Marie Curie. In 1939, Perey discovered the element francium by purifying samples of lanthanum that contained actinium. In 1962, she was the first woman to be elected to the French Académie des Sciences, an honor denied to her mentor Curie.

Early life and education

Perey was born in Villemomble, outside Paris. As a child she dreamed of becoming a doctor, but after her father's death the family was left in straitened circumstances. She studied at a technical school for girls and earned a diploma in chemistry, graduating at the top of her class in 1929. Though the diploma did not qualify her for university study, it allowed her to work as a chemistry technician.

Career

At the age of 19, Perey interviewed with Marie Curie and was personally hired as a laboratory assistant at the Institut du Radium in Paris. Working under André-Louis Debierne, she was entrusted with purifying actinium, a highly radioactive element. It was during this painstaking work that she detected an unexpected alpha radiation and identified a new element, which she named francium (symbol Fr, atomic number 87) after her native country. The discovery was announced in 1939 and filled the last remaining gap among the naturally occurring elements then being sought in the periodic table.

Perey eventually completed university studies, earning a doctorate from the Sorbonne in 1946. She became a professor at the University of Strasbourg, where she directed the nuclear chemistry laboratory and helped build a research program in radiochemistry.

Later life and health

Like many early radiation workers, Perey suffered severe health effects from prolonged exposure to radioactivity, including bone cancer, which ultimately caused her death in 1975. She spent her final years campaigning for greater safety precautions for scientists working with radioactive materials.

Honours

  • First woman elected to the Académie des Sciences (1962, as a corresponding member)
  • Officer of the Legion of Honour
  • Commander of the Ordre des Palmes académiques
  • The mineral pereyite-like naming legacy and the asteroid 6622 Perey are associated with her name
