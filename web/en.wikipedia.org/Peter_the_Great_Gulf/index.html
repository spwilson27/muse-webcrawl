The Peter the Great Gulf (Russian: Залив Петра Великого, Zaliv Petra Velikogo) is a gulf on the northwestern shore of the Sea of Japan in Primorsky Krai, Russia. It is the largest gulf in the Sea of Japan and contains the ports of Vladivostok and Nakhodka, two of Russia's most important Pacific harbours.

Peter the Great Gulf, with Vladivostok on the Muravyov-Amursky Peninsula

The gulf stretches for about 185 kilometres from Cape Povorotny in the east to the mouth of the Tumen River in the west, near the border with North Korea. Its shores are deeply indented, forming numerous secondary bays, among them the Amur Bay, Ussuri Bay, Nakhodka Bay, Vostok Bay and Possiet Bay. The Muravyov-Amursky Peninsula divides the gulf into the Amur Bay to the west and the Ussuri Bay to the east; the city of Vladivostok stands on this peninsula, and Russky Island lies just offshore, connected to the mainland by the Russky Bridge, one of the longest cable-stayed bridges in the world.

History

The gulf was named in honour of Peter the Great, founder of the Russian navy. Russian mariners surveyed its shores in the 1850s, shortly before the founding of Vladivostok in 1860. During the Cold War the gulf was a heavily militarised zone centred on the Soviet Pacific Fleet. In 2012 the area hosted the APEC summit on Russky Island, which prompted major infrastructure construction including the Russky Bridge and the Zolotoy Bridge across the Golden Horn Bay.

Geography and nature

More than thirty islands lie within the gulf, including Russky, Popov, Reyneke, Rikord and the Rimsky-Korsakov Archipelago. The southern part of the gulf falls within the Far Eastern Marine Reserve, established to protect the rich marine life of the area, which includes seals, whales and diverse fish species. The waters freeze in part during the severe winters, when ice may cover the northern bays from December to March.
