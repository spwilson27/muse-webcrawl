National Popular Vote Interstate Compact

The National Popular Vote Interstate Compact (NPVIC) is an agreement among a group of U.S. states and the District of Columbia to award all their electoral votes to the presidential candidate who wins the overall popular vote in the 50 states and the District of Columbia. The compact is designed to ensure that the candidate who receives the most popular votes nationwide is elected president through the Electoral College, without amending the U.S. Constitution.

Mechanism

Under the U.S. Constitution, each state may appoint its electors as its legislature directs, and nearly all states award them to the statewide popular-vote winner. Under the compact, member states instead award their electors to the winner of the national popular vote. The compact takes effect only when states controlling at least 270 electoral votes — the majority needed to elect a president — have joined.

Adoption

Maryland and New Jersey were the first states to enact the compact in 2007. By the mid-2020s, states including California, New York, Illinois, Washington, Massachusetts, Colorado, Oregon, Minnesota and Maine had joined, together controlling roughly 205–210 electoral votes — short of the 270 threshold, so the compact has not yet taken effect.

Debate

Supporters argue the compact would make every vote equally relevant and end the concentration of campaigning in a few swing states. Critics question its constitutionality under the Compact Clause, warn of recount and faithless-elector complications, and argue it diminishes the federal character of presidential elections.
