Off-road vehicle

An off-road vehicle (ORV), also called an off-highway vehicle or all-terrain vehicle, is any motorised vehicle designed or capable of travelling on unpaved, uneven, or natural terrain. The category includes four-wheel-drive SUVs and pickup trucks, all-terrain vehicles (ATVs), dirt bikes, dune buggies, and specialised military and agricultural machines.

Characteristics

Off-road vehicles typically feature high ground clearance, large all-terrain or mud-terrain tyres, four-wheel drive with a low-range transfer case, locking differentials, skid plates protecting the underside, and suspension with long travel. Approach, departure, and breakover angles determine a vehicle's ability to clear obstacles.

History

Early examples include the Citroën-Kégresse half-tracks of the 1920s and the military vehicles of the Second World War, notably the Willys MB "Jeep" and the Land Rover of 1948, which spawned civilian off-roaders. Recreational off-roading grew from the 1960s with dune buggies and organised trail riding.

Activities and concerns

Recreational uses include trail riding, rock crawling, mudding, desert racing (such as the Baja 1000 and Dakar Rally), and overlanding. Environmental groups have raised concerns about habitat damage, erosion, and noise from unmanaged off-road use, leading to designated trail systems and area restrictions in many countries.
