Flor

Flor (Spanish and Portuguese for "flower") in winemaking is a variety of yeast that forms a protective layer — often described as a film or veil — on the surface of certain wines, most famously the fino and manzanilla sherries of Jerez, Spain. The flor protects the wine from oxidation while imparting distinctive flavours, and the wines aged under it are said to undergo biological ageing.

Formation

Flor consists of strains of Saccharomyces cerevisiae distinct from those of ordinary fermentation. After fermentation is complete, the wine is fortified only lightly (to about 15–15.5% alcohol, a level the flor tolerates but which discourages acetic acid bacteria) and placed in partially filled barrels. The yeast floats, forming a wrinkled white layer, consuming oxygen, alcohol, and glycerol, and producing acetaldehyde, which gives fino sherry its characteristic almond and dough aromas.

Role in sherry production

In the solera system, wines are fractionally blended through tiers of barrels. Where flor survives year-round, as in the humid coastal town of Sanlúcar de Barrameda, the wine becomes manzanilla; where it dies back in summer, as in Jerez, the slightly more oxidised fino results. If the flor dies completely, the wine continues as an amontillado, ageing oxidatively. Flor also develops in the Jura's vin jaune (where it is called the voile) and in some other traditionally made wines.
