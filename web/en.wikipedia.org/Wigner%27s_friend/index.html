Wigner's friend

Wigner's friend is a thought experiment in quantum mechanics, proposed by physicist Eugene Wigner in 1961, extending the paradox of Schrödinger's cat. It probes the question of when a measurement occurs and whether quantum mechanics gives a consistent account when the observer is itself treated as a quantum system.

Proposed byEugene Wigner
Year1961
SubjectMeasurement problem; interpretations of quantum mechanics

The experiment

Wigner's friend performs a measurement on a quantum system inside a sealed laboratory and observes a definite outcome. Wigner, outside, describes the entire laboratory — friend included — as remaining in a superposition of "friend saw outcome A" and "friend saw outcome B" until he himself opens the lab and learns the result. The two descriptions seem inconsistent: the friend insists the outcome was definite before Wigner asked.

Interpretations and responses

Wigner originally suggested that consciousness causes the collapse of the wave function. Most modern responses instead depend on interpretation: many-worlds denies collapse altogether; QBism and relational quantum mechanics treat outcomes as relative to the observer; objective-collapse theories posit a physical threshold. Extended versions of the paradox (e.g. the Frauchiger–Renner argument of 2016) derive apparent contradictions by chaining several such observers, fuelling debate about whether quantum theory can consistently describe its own use.
