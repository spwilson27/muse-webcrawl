Chu Ci

The Chu Ci (Chinese: 楚辭, "Verses of Chu" or "Songs of Chu") is an anthology of Chinese poetry traditionally attributed mainly to Qu Yuan and Song Yu of the southern state of Chu, dating from the Warring States period through the Han dynasty. Together with the northern Shijing (Classic of Poetry), it forms one of the two foundational sources of classical Chinese poetry.

Contents

The anthology, compiled in its present form by the Han scholar Wang Yi in the second century AD, contains seventeen sections. Its most famous piece is Qu Yuan's long lyric "Li Sao" ("On Encountering Sorrow"), a fantastical autobiographical poem in which the poet, slandered and exiled, journeys among gods and spirits in search of an ideal ruler. Other celebrated works include the "Nine Songs", derived from shamanistic hymns of Chu; the "Heavenly Questions", a long series of riddling questions about myth and history; and the "Nine Pieces" and the "Summons of the Soul".

Style and influence

Unlike the terse four-character lines of the Shijing, Chu Ci verse uses longer, irregular lines with the exclamatory particle xi (兮), lavish catalogues of fragrant plants and goddesses, and motifs drawn from Chu shamanism and southern mythology. The anthology deeply influenced later Chinese poetry, especially the fu rhapsodies of the Han dynasty and the romantic tradition generally.
