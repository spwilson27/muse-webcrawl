Mainsail

A mainsail is a sail rigged on the main mast of a sailing vessel. On a square-rigged ship, the mainsail (or course) is the lowest and largest sail on the main mast. On a fore-and-aft-rigged vessel such as a modern sloop or cutter, the mainsail is the large triangular or quadrilateral sail set behind the main mast, attached to the mast along its luff and to the boom along its foot.

Fore-and-aft mainsails

The modern Bermuda-rig mainsail is controlled by the mainsheet, outhaul, halyard, boom vang and traveller, and its shape is adjusted for wind conditions; sail area is reduced in heavy weather by reefing or furling, including in-boom and in-mast systems. Parts of the sail include the head, tack, clew, leech, roach and battens.

Square-rig mainsails

On full-rigged ships, the mainsail hangs from the main yard beneath the main topsail, and worked together with the fore and mizzen courses to drive the ship.
