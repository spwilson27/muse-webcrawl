Fallingwater

Location: Mill Run, Pennsylvania, U.S.
Architect: Frank Lloyd Wright
Built: 1936–1939
Client: Edgar J. Kaufmann

Fallingwater is a house designed in 1935 by the American architect Frank Lloyd Wright for the family of Edgar J. Kaufmann, owner of Pittsburgh's Kaufmann's Department Store. Built between 1936 and 1939 near Mill Run, Pennsylvania, it famously stands partly over a waterfall on Bear Run — rather than facing the falls from below, as the Kaufmanns had expected, Wright placed the house directly above them so the family would live with the falls rather than merely look at them.

Design

A celebrated example of Wright's organic architecture, the house is organized around a central stone core with bold cantilevered concrete terraces that echo the rock ledges of the site. Its reinforced-concrete cantilevers were controversial and have required major structural restoration, completed in 2002, after decades of deflection. The interior features built-in furniture, a suspended staircase leading down to the stream, and extensive glass.

Legacy

Fallingwater revitalized Wright's career and is widely regarded as his masterpiece and one of the greatest works of American architecture. Donated to the Western Pennsylvania Conservancy by Edgar Kaufmann Jr. in 1963, it opened as a museum in 1964 and was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2019 as part of "The 20th-Century Architecture of Frank Lloyd Wright".
