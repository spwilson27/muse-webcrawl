Bombing of Darwin

Date19 February 1942
LocationDarwin, Northern Territory, Australia
AttackerImperial Japanese Navy and Army air units
CasualtiesApprox. 235–250 killed; 300–400 wounded

The Bombing of Darwin, also known as the Battle of Darwin, on 19 February 1942 was the largest single attack ever mounted by a foreign power on Australia. On that day, 242 Japanese aircraft, launched from four aircraft carriers and land bases in the Dutch East Indies, attacked the port and town of Darwin in two waves.

The attack

The first wave of 188 carrier aircraft struck at about 9:58 a.m., achieving near-total surprise; a second wave of 54 land-based bombers followed around noon. The raids sank or damaged numerous ships in the harbour — including the destroyer USS Peary — destroyed aircraft on the ground, and heavily damaged port and town infrastructure.

Aftermath

Australian defences were unprepared: anti-aircraft fire was ineffective and only ten American P-40 fighters opposed the first wave. Official investigations criticised the military administration, and much of Darwin's civilian population was evacuated. The attack was the first and largest of almost 100 Japanese air raids on northern Australia during 1942–43.

Commemoration

19 February is now commemorated annually in Darwin as Bombing of Darwin Day, and the raids remain a defining episode in Australian wartime memory.
