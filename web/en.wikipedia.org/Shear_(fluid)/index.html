Shear (fluid)


Planar shear flow between two parallel plates, one moving relative to the other.

In fluid mechanics, shear refers to the deformation of a fluid element caused by tangential (shear) stresses acting along its faces. When a fluid is subjected to a shear stress, it deforms continuously; the rate of this deformation is called the shear rate, and for many fluids it is proportional to the applied shear stress through the fluid's viscosity.

Definition

Consider a fluid layer between two large parallel plates separated by a small distance, with the upper plate moving at constant velocity while the lower plate is fixed. The fluid in contact with each plate sticks to it (the no-slip condition), so the fluid velocity varies linearly across the layer. The shear rate γ̇ at any point is the velocity gradient perpendicular to the flow direction:

γ̇ = du/dy

where u is the flow velocity and y is the distance from the fixed plate. The shear stress τ in a Newtonian fluid is then given by Newton's law of viscosity, τ = μ du/dy, where μ is the dynamic viscosity.

Shear thinning and thickening

Fluids whose apparent viscosity decreases with increasing shear rate are called shear thinning (or pseudoplastic); examples include blood, paint, and many polymer solutions. Fluids whose viscosity increases with shear rate are called shear thickening (dilatant), such as concentrated suspensions of cornstarch in water. A third class, Bingham plastics, behave as solids until a yield stress is exceeded, after which they flow.

Wall shear stress

The shear stress exerted by a flowing fluid on a solid boundary is called wall shear stress. It is of central importance in engineering because it determines frictional drag on pipes, ships, and aircraft, and in haemodynamics, where endothelial cells in blood vessels respond biologically to the wall shear stress produced by blood flow.
