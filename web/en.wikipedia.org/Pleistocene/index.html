Pleistocene

The Pleistocene is the geological epoch that lasted from about 2.58 million to 11,700 years ago, spanning the Earth's most recent period of repeated glaciations — popularly, the Ice Age. It is the first epoch of the Quaternary Period and follows the Pliocene; it is succeeded by the current Holocene epoch.

Climate

During Pleistocene glacial maxima, vast ice sheets covered much of North America, northern Europe and Asia, and global sea levels fell by up to about 120 metres, exposing land bridges such as Beringia between Siberia and Alaska and Doggerland in the North Sea. Glacial and interglacial cycles, paced by Milankovitch variations in Earth's orbit, lengthened from roughly 41,000 to about 100,000 years across the Mid-Pleistocene Transition.

Life

The epoch saw a distinctive megafauna including woolly mammoths, mastodons, sabre-toothed cats, giant ground sloths and woolly rhinoceroses, most of which vanished in the Quaternary extinction event near the epoch's end, likely through a combination of climate change and human hunting. The genus Homo evolved and diversified during the Pleistocene; anatomically modern humans spread across all continents, and Neanderthals and Denisovans disappeared around 40,000 years ago.
