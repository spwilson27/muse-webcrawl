Freedom

Freedom, generally, is the ability or right to change or act without constraint, or to possess the power and resources to carry out one's goals. It is often associated with liberty, autonomy, and free will. A person is said to have the freedom to do something if that person is not prevented from doing it by outside forces and has the resources and capacity to act.

Types of freedom

Political freedom concerns the absence of interference from a sovereign or other power in the sphere of individual action. Important distinctions are often drawn between negative liberty (freedom from interference) and positive liberty (the possession of the power and resources to act), a contrast famously elaborated by Isaiah Berlin in his 1958 lecture "Two Concepts of Liberty".

Freedom in philosophy

Philosophers have long debated the relation between freedom and determinism: whether human actions are causally determined and, if so, whether moral responsibility and free will remain possible. Compatibilists hold that freedom is compatible with determinism, while incompatibilists deny this, dividing into libertarians about free will and hard determinists.

Civil and political freedoms

Enshrined freedoms in modern constitutions commonly include freedom of speech, freedom of the press, freedom of religion, freedom of assembly, and freedom of association. Indices such as those published by Freedom House attempt to measure the degree of civil liberties and political rights in countries around the world.
