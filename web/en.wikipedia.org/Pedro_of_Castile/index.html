Peter of Castile

Peter (Spanish: Pedro; 30 August 1334 – 23 March 1369), known as Peter the Cruel (el Cruel) or Peter the Just (el Justiciero), was King of Castile and León from 1350 to 1369. His reign was dominated by civil war against his half-brother Henry of Trastámara and by Castile's entanglement in the Hundred Years' War.

Reign

Peter succeeded his father Alfonso XI at sixteen amid plague and noble factionalism. He favoured royal authority and the towns, and his treatment of rivals and of his queen, Blanche of Bourbon, earned him the epithet "the Cruel" from hostile chroniclers, while supporters praised his enforcement of justice.

Civil war

His half-brother Henry, backed by France, challenged him in the Castilian Civil War. Peter in turn allied with Edward, the Black Prince of England, winning the Battle of Nájera (1367), but Henry returned with Bertrand du Guesclin and defeated and killed Peter at the Battle of Montiel in 1369.
