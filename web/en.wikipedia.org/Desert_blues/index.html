Desert blues

Desert blues is a popular name for the guitar-based music of the Tuareg people and related groups of the Sahara and the Sahel, particularly in Mali, Niger, Algeria, and Libya. The style, locally rooted in traditions such as tishoumaren (in Tamashek), blends traditional West African melodies and pentatonic scales with electric guitar and elements of American blues and rock, producing a hypnotic, droning sound.

History

The music emerged in the refugee camps and exile communities of Tuareg (Kel Tamasheq) people during the droughts and rebellions of the 1970s-1990s, when young Tuareg men — the ishumar — took up electric guitars and sang of exile, resistance, and longing for home. Cassettes circulated hand to hand across the desert before the music reached international audiences.

Artists

The band Tinariwen, formed in 1979 and internationally discovered in the 2000s, is the genre's flagship act, winning a Grammy Award for their album Tassili. Other prominent artists include Ali Farka Touré (whose earlier work is often classed as a precursor), Bombino, Mdou Moctar, Songhoy Blues, Tamikrest, and Terakaft.

Festivals

The Festival au Désert, held near Timbuktu from 2001 until security concerns suspended it, became a celebrated meeting point of desert blues and world music.
