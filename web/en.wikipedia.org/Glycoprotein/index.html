Glycoprotein

A glycoprotein is a protein that contains oligosaccharide chains (glycans) covalently attached to amino acid side-chains. The carbohydrate is attached to the protein in a cotranslational or posttranslational modification known as glycosylation. Glycoproteins are important membrane proteins, where they play a role in cell–cell interactions, and secreted glycoproteins are key components of blood plasma and mucus.

Types of glycosylation

In N-linked glycosylation, a glycan is attached to the nitrogen atom of an asparagine residue within the Asn-X-Ser/Thr sequon, typically beginning in the endoplasmic reticulum and maturing in the Golgi apparatus. In O-linked glycosylation, sugars are attached to the oxygen atom of serine or threonine residues, mainly in the Golgi, with no single consensus sequence. Rarer forms include C-mannosylation and GPI anchors.

Functions

The glycans influence protein folding, stability, trafficking and half-life, and serve as recognition sites in immunity and fertilisation. Well-known glycoproteins include antibodies, the hormones FSH, LH and TSH, erythropoietin, mucins, the blood group antigens carried on cell-surface glycoproteins and glycolipids, and viral envelope proteins such as influenza haemagglutinin and HIV gp120, whose dense glycan shield helps evade the immune system. Altered glycosylation is a hallmark of many cancers and congenital disorders of glycosylation.
