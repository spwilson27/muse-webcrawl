South Korea, officially the Republic of Korea, is a country in East Asia occupying the southern half of the Korean Peninsula. It borders North Korea to the north — along the heavily fortified Demilitarised Zone — and is surrounded by the Yellow Sea, the East Sea (Sea of Japan) and the Korea Strait. Its capital and largest city is Seoul.

History

Following Japan's surrender in 1945, Korea was divided at the 38th parallel between Soviet and American occupation zones. The Republic of Korea was proclaimed in 1948 under Syngman Rhee; two years later North Korea invaded, beginning the Korean War (1950–1953), which ended in an armistice that remains in force. After decades of authoritarian rule, South Korea transitioned to democracy in 1987. The "Miracle on the Han River" transformed one of the world's poorest countries into a major industrial economy within a generation.

Economy and culture

South Korea has one of the world's largest economies, led by conglomerates (chaebol) such as Samsung, Hyundai, SK and LG, with strengths in semiconductors, shipbuilding, automobiles and consumer electronics. Korean popular culture — K-pop, television dramas and films, collectively the Korean Wave or Hallyu — has achieved global reach, exemplified by the group BTS and the film Parasite (2019), the first non-English-language film to win the Academy Award for Best Picture.
