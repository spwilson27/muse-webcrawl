Lajes Field (Portuguese: Base Aérea das Lajes, officially Air Base No. 4) is a Portuguese Air Force base on Terceira Island in the Azores, home to the United States Air Force's 65th Air Base Group and shared with the civil airport serving the island (IATA: TER, ICAO: LPLA). Its mid-Atlantic position, some 3,700 km from North America and 1,600 km from mainland Europe, has made it a strategic refuelling and staging point since the Second World War.

History

The airfield was constructed in 1943 after Portugal, though neutral, agreed under its ancient alliance with Britain to allow Allied use of the Azores; British forces arrived in October 1943, and the United States followed, building the long runway to support anti-submarine patrols and ferry flights across the Atlantic. During the Cold War Lajes supported NATO operations and transatlantic deployments; in 1973 it was the crucial staging base for Operation Nickel Grass, the US airlift to Israel during the Yom Kippur War, when most European allies refused landing rights. It later supported operations in the Gulf War, the Balkans, Iraq and Afghanistan, and served as an emergency landing and diversion field for transatlantic airliners and a designated alternate for space shuttle missions.

Present day

US personnel levels were reduced substantially in the 2010s, shifting the base toward a caretaker and "warm base" status, while Portuguese units and civil aviation continue to operate from the field. The base hosted the March 2003 Azores Summit of US, British, Spanish and Portuguese leaders on the eve of the Iraq War.
