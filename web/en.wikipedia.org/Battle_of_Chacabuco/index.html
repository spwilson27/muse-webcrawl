Battle of Chacabuco

Date12 February 1817
LocationChacabuco, near Santiago, Chile
ResultPatriot victory
BelligerentsArmy of the Andes (United Provinces and Chilean patriots) vs. Spanish royalists
CommandersJosé de San Martín, Bernardo O'Higgins vs. Rafael Maroto

The Battle of Chacabuco, fought on 12–14 February 1817 (the decisive engagement on 12 February), was a battle of the Chilean War of Independence in which the patriot Army of the Andes, commanded by General José de San Martín, defeated Spanish royalist forces and opened the way to the liberation of Santiago de Chile. It was the culmination of the celebrated Crossing of the Andes, one of the most daring logistical feats of the South American wars of independence.

Background

After the patriot disaster at Rancagua in 1814, Chile had returned to Spanish control during the Reconquista, and Chilean exiles gathered in Mendoza, Argentina, where San Martín organized the Army of the Andes. In January 1817 some 4,000–5,000 troops, with artillery dismantled and carried by mules, crossed the high passes of the Andes in summer columns, deceiving the royalists about the main axis of advance. The patriot army descended toward the Aconcagua valley and advanced on the Chacabuco estate north of Santiago, where the royalist governor's field force under Rafael Maroto took position.

Battle

San Martín divided his army into two divisions: one under Miguel Estanislao Soler was to make a wide flanking march, while Bernardo O'Higgins attacked frontally with the second division. O'Higgins, impatient, charged before Soler's division was fully in position and was initially repulsed, but San Martín committed his reserves and his cavalry, and Soler's arrival on the royalist flank decided the day. Royalist losses were heavy: around 600 killed and 600 captured out of some 1,500–2,000 men, while patriot casualties were comparatively light.

Aftermath

The patriots entered Santiago on 14 February 1817. San Martín declined the supreme directorship of Chile, which passed to O'Higgins. Royalist forces regrouped in the south, and the independence of Chile was not secured until the Battle of Maipú in April 1818, but Chacabuco broke the Reconquista and began the liberation of Chile — the essential precondition for San Martín's subsequent expedition to Peru.
