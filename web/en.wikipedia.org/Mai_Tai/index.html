Mai Tai

TypeCocktail
Base spiritRum
ServedOn the rocks
Standard garnishMint sprig, lime

The Mai Tai is a rum-based cocktail associated with tiki culture. Its name derives from the Tahitian maita'i, "good" or "excellent". The drink is traditionally made with rum, lime juice, orange curaçao (or orange liqueur), and orgeat syrup.

Origin

Victor "Trader Vic" Bergeron claimed to have created the Mai Tai in 1944 at his Oakland restaurant for Tahitian friends, one of whom reportedly exclaimed maita'i roa a'e — "out of this world". Donn Beach (Don the Beachcomber) also claimed an earlier similar drink. Rivalry over the recipe became a famous part of tiki lore and was litigated in court, where Bergeron's claim prevailed.

Variations

Numerous later versions add pineapple and other fruit juices, especially in Hawaii, where the drink became a staple of resort bars; purists distinguish these from the 1944 formula built on aged Jamaican rum.
