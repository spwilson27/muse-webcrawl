Barnacle

Barnacles are arthropods of the infraclass Cirripedia in the subphylum Crustacea — relatives of crabs and shrimp that live exclusively in marine waters. Adults are sessile suspension feeders encased in calcareous plates, cemented to rocks, ship hulls, and even whales and turtles.

Life cycle

Barnacles begin as free-swimming nauplius larvae, then settle as cyprid larvae, cementing themselves head-first to a surface and metamorphosing into the shelled adult, which feeds by sweeping feathery cirri through the water.

Darwin's studies

Charles Darwin spent eight years (1846–1854) monographing barnacles, work that honed his understanding of variation and classification before he published On the Origin of Species. Barnacles are mostly hermaphroditic; the parasitic rhizocephalans, such as Sacculina, radically modify their crab hosts.

Biofouling

Barnacle encrustation of hulls greatly increases drag and fuel use, making barnacles a major economic concern for shipping and a model for adhesives research.
