Group of Eight

Group of Eight (G8)
Members (at suspension): Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, Russia, United Kingdom, United States; European Union (participant)
Active as G8: 1997–2014
Reverted to: G7

The Group of Eight (G8) was an intergovernmental political forum from 1997 until 2014. It had formed from incorporating Russia into the Group of Seven (G7), and returned to its previous name after Russia was disinvited in 2014.

The forum originated with a 1975 summit hosted by France that brought together representatives of six governments: France, Germany, Italy, Japan, the United Kingdom, and the United States. Canada joined in 1976, forming the G7, and the European Union began participating in 1977. Russia joined in 1997, making it the G8, holding its first summit in 1998.

Suspension of Russia

On 24 March 2014, the G7 members cancelled the planned G8 summit in Sochi, Russia, and suspended Russia from the group in response to the annexation of Crimea. The other members met in Brussels instead as the G7. In 2017 Russia announced it would permanently leave the G8. The forum has operated as the G7 ever since.

Activities

The G8's annual summits of heads of government dealt with global economic governance, international security, energy, climate change, development, and health initiatives such as the fight against HIV/AIDS. Ministerial meetings in areas including finance, foreign affairs, and the environment prepared the summits. Decisions were politically influential but not legally binding, and the group was often criticized for excluding emerging economies and for the large protests that accompanied its summits.
