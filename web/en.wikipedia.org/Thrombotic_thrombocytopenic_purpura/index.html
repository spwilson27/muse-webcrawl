Thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura
SpecialtyHaematology
CauseSevere ADAMTS13 deficiency
TreatmentPlasma exchange, caplacizumab, rituximab

Thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP) is a rare blood disorder in which small blood clots form throughout the body, causing low platelet counts (thrombocytopenia), destruction of red blood cells (microangiopathic haemolytic anaemia), and damage to organs including the brain and kidneys. It is a medical emergency with high mortality if untreated.

Cause

TTP results from severe deficiency of ADAMTS13, an enzyme that cleaves von Willebrand factor. Without it, unusually large von Willebrand factor multimers cause spontaneous platelet clumping in small vessels. The deficiency is usually acquired through autoantibodies, and rarely inherited (Upshaw–Schulman syndrome).

Diagnosis and treatment

The classic pentad of fever, thrombocytopenia, haemolytic anaemia, renal dysfunction, and neurological symptoms is now rarely seen in full. Diagnosis is supported by schistocytes on the blood film and ADAMTS13 activity below 10 percent. Urgent plasma exchange transformed survival from around 90 percent mortality to about 80–90 percent survival; caplacizumab and immunosuppression such as rituximab are now also used.
