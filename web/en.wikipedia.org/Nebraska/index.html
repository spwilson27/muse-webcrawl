Nebraska

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Nebraska is a state in the Midwestern region of the United States. It is bordered by South Dakota to the north; Iowa to the east and Missouri to the southeast, both across the Missouri River; Kansas to the south; Colorado to the southwest; and Wyoming to the west. It is the only triply landlocked U.S. state. Its capital is Lincoln, and its largest city is Omaha, on the Missouri River. Nebraska was admitted to the Union as the 37th state on March 1, 1867.

Geography

The state consists of two major land regions: the Dissected Till Plains in the east, with rolling hills and most of the population, and the Great Plains in the west, including the unique Sandhills grasslands. The Platte River runs east across the state. Nebraska experiences hot summers and cold winters, with spring tornadoes common in "Tornado Alley."

Economy and culture

Agriculture — corn, soybeans, beef cattle, and hogs — dominates, supplemented by food processing, insurance, rail transport (Union Pacific is headquartered in Omaha), and the finance sector around investor Warren Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway. Nebraska has the nation's only unicameral (single-house) and officially nonpartisan state legislature. The University of Nebraska Cornhuskers are a cultural institution, and hundreds of thousands of sandhill cranes migrate through the Platte valley each spring. Indigenous peoples including the Omaha, Ponca, Pawnee, and Lakota have deep roots in the region. See also Omaha and Lincoln.
