New Mexico History Museum

The New Mexico History Museum, located on the Plaza in Santa Fe, New Mexico, is the state's flagship history museum and a division of the New Mexico Department of Cultural Affairs. Its campus includes the Palace of the Governors, built in 1610 as the seat of Spanish colonial government and recognised as the oldest continuously occupied public building in the United States. A modern museum building behind the Palace opened in May 2009, greatly expanding exhibition space.

Collections and exhibitions

The museum presents the history of New Mexico from Indigenous cultures through the Spanish colonial, Mexican and US territorial periods to statehood (1912) and beyond. Its permanent exhibition, Telling New Mexico: Stories from Then and Now, covers subjects from Pueblo societies and the Camino Real to the Santa Fe Trail, the Fred Harvey era, the Manhattan Project at Los Alamos and the civil-rights era. The museum also encompasses the Fray Angélico Chávez History Library, the Palace Print Shop & Bindery, and the Photo Archives, which hold hundreds of thousands of historical images. Under the portal of the Palace, Native American artisans sell their work through the long-running Native American Artisans Program.

The Palace of the Governors

The Palace, constructed in the early 17th century, served successive Spanish, Mexican and American administrations and is a National Historic Landmark. During the Pueblo Revolt of 1680 it was occupied by Pueblo peoples; it was retaken by Diego de Vargas in 1693. Restored in the early 20th century under archaeologist Edgar Lee Hewett, the Palace helped define the Pueblo Revival (Santa Fe) style of architecture and was home to the Museum of New Mexico's history collections from 1909 until the new building opened a century later.
