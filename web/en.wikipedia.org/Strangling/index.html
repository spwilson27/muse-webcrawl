Strangling

TypesLigature, manual (throttling), hanging
MechanismsVascular occlusion, airway obstruction, vagal reflex

Strangling is the compression of the neck that may lead to unconsciousness or death by depriving the brain of oxygen. It is distinguished by the means of compression: ligature strangling uses a cord or similar object, manual strangling (throttling) uses the hands or arms, and hanging involves suspension by a ligature tightened by body weight.

Physiology

Death or injury may result from occlusion of the carotid arteries and jugular veins, obstruction of the airway, or stimulation of the carotid sinus triggering a vagal reflex. Relatively modest pressures can occlude the jugular veins and carotids. Forensic signs may include petechial haemorrhages, conjunctival bleeding and fractures of the hyoid bone or thyroid cartilage, though strangling can leave few external marks. In many jurisdictions non-fatal strangulation in domestic abuse is treated as a specific serious offence because it is a strong predictor of later homicide.
