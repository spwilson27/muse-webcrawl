Nornickel

Nornickel (Russian: Норникель), formerly known as Norilsk Nickel, is a Russian mining and metallurgical company and the world's largest producer of palladium and high-grade nickel, and a major producer of platinum and copper. Its main operations are centred on the Siberian city of Norilsk, one of the largest industrial cities within the Arctic Circle.

Smelters in Norilsk, Siberia

History

Mining at Norilsk began in the 1930s under the Soviet Gulag system, which built the combine using forced labour. The company was privatized in the 1990s amid the controversial loans-for-shares auctions and came under the control of oligarchs Vladimir Potanin and Mikhail Prokhorov; Potanin remains its largest shareholder and president.

Environmental record

Nornickel's smelters have made Norilsk one of the most polluted places on Earth. In May 2020 a company fuel tank collapsed near Norilsk, spilling about 21,000 tonnes of diesel into Arctic rivers — one of Russia's worst environmental disasters — leading to a record fine of roughly 146 billion rubles (about US$2 billion). The company has since invested in sulphur-capture and clean-up programmes.
