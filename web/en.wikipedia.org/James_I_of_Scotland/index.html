James I of Scotland

James I (late July 1394 – 21 February 1437) was King of Scots from 1406 until his assassination in 1437. Captured by the English as a boy while travelling to France, he spent 18 years as a prisoner in England before returning to a Scotland that had been governed in his absence by the Albany Stewart regents.

Captivity

James, the younger son of Robert III, was seized at sea in 1406 and held — comfortably but firmly — at the courts of Henry IV and Henry V, receiving an excellent education and fighting alongside Henry V in France. He married Joan Beaufort, a niece of Henry IV, in 1424, and was released after a ransom of £40,000 was agreed.

Reign

On his return, James moved ruthlessly against the Albany Stewarts, executing the Duke of Albany and his sons, and set about strengthening royal authority, reforming taxation and justice, and curbing the Highland clans, famously arresting clan chiefs at the Inverness parliament of 1428. He is traditionally credited with the long poem The Kingis Quair ("The King's Book"), written during his captivity.

Assassination

James was murdered at the Blackfriars monastery in Perth by a conspiracy led by his uncle Walter Stewart, Earl of Atholl, and Sir Robert Graham, who hoped to place Atholl's line on the throne. The conspirators were captured and executed with legendary brutality. James was succeeded by his six-year-old son, James II.
