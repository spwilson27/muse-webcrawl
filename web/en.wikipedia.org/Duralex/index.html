Duralex

Founded1945
HeadquartersLa Chapelle-Saint-Mesmin, France
ProductsTempered glass tableware
Known productsGigogne, Picardie tumblers

Duralex is a French manufacturer of tempered glass tableware and kitchenware based in La Chapelle-Saint-Mesmin, near Orléans. Founded in 1945 by the industrial group Saint-Gobain, the company took its name from the Latin phrase dura lex, sed lex ("the law is harsh, but it is the law").

Products

Duralex is best known for its moulded tempered-glass tumblers, notably the Gigogne (launched 1946) and the Picardie (1954), whose faceted design became an icon of French café and school-canteen culture. Tempering makes the glass several times stronger than ordinary glass and causes it to shatter into small blunt fragments. The Picardie tumbler is in the collection of design museums and is widely imitated.

History

The company passed through several owners after Saint-Gobain sold it, and experienced repeated financial difficulties in the 2000s and 2020s, including receiverships and rescue buyouts; in 2024 the workforce backed a cooperative (SCOP) takeover of the plant. Duralex glassware has enjoyed revivals in export markets, particularly in the Middle East, and is celebrated as a symbol of robust French industrial design.
