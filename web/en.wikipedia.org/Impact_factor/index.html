Impact factor

The impact factor (IF) or journal impact factor (JIF) of an academic journal is a scientometric index calculated by Clarivate that reflects the yearly mean number of citations of articles published in the last two years in a given journal, as indexed by Clarivate's Web of Science. It is frequently used as a proxy for the relative importance of a journal within its field.

Calculation

In any given year, the two-year journal impact factor is the ratio between the number of citations received in that year for publications in the journal published in the two preceding years, and the total number of "citable items" published in that journal in the two preceding years. For example, a journal with an impact factor of 3 in 2023 had its 2021 and 2022 articles cited, on average, three times each in 2023.

History

The impact factor was devised by Eugene Garfield, the founder of the Institute for Scientific Information (ISI). Impact factors have been calculated yearly starting from 1975 for journals listed in the Journal Citation Reports (JCR).

Use and criticism

Impact factors are used by researchers, librarians, and institutions to evaluate journals, and they have controversially been used to assess individual researchers and institutions. Critics note that the metric can be gamed (for example through editorial self-citation policies and coercive citation), that citation distributions are highly skewed so the mean is a poor summary, that review articles inflate scores, and that the factor does not measure the quality of individual articles. The San Francisco Declaration on Research Assessment (DORA) recommends against using journal impact factors as a surrogate measure of the quality of individual research articles.
