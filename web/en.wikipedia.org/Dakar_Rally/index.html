The Dakar Rally or simply "The Dakar", formerly known as the Paris–Dakar Rally, is an annual rally raid organised by the Amaury Sport Organisation (ASO). Most events since the inception in 1978 have been staged from Paris, France, to Dakar, Senegal. Security threats in Mauritania led to the cancellation of the 2008 rally, and events from 2009 to 2019 were held in South America. Since 2020 the rally has been held in Saudi Arabia.

Format

The event is open to amateur and professional entrants, with amateurs typically making up about eighty percent of the participants. Vehicles compete in classes including motorcycles, quads, cars (from buggies to small SUVs), UTVs, and trucks. The race lasts about two weeks over roughly 8,000–9,000 kilometres, consisting of timed special stages over dunes, camel grass, rocks and erg desert, connected by road liaisons. Navigation by roadbook is a critical skill, and the marathon stages — where no outside servicing is allowed — are among the most demanding.

History

The rally was conceived by French motorcyclist Thierry Sabine after he got lost in the Libyan desert in 1977 and decided the desert would make a magnificent rally course. The first Paris–Dakar ran in 1978–79 with 182 vehicles. Sabine was killed in a helicopter crash during the 1986 rally. The event grew into the world's most famous off-road race despite persistent criticism over its environmental impact, safety record — numerous competitors and spectators have died — and its relationship with local communities along the route.

Notable champions include Stéphane Peterhansel, the most successful competitor (with titles on both motorcycles and in cars), Cyril Despres, Nasser Al-Attiyah, and Carlos Sainz. Manufacturers such as Mitsubishi, Volkswagen, Peugeot, KTM and Toyota have been dominant in their eras.

Moves to South America and Saudi Arabia

After the 2008 cancellation due to terrorist threats, the Dakar moved to South America from 2009, passing through Argentina, Chile, Peru, Bolivia, and Paraguay. In 2020 it moved again to Saudi Arabia under a multi-year agreement, where stages traverse the Empty Quarter and the mountains near the Red Sea.
