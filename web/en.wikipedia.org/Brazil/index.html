Rio de Janeiro

Brazil, officially the Federative Republic of Brazil, is the largest country in South America and the fifth-largest country in the world by both area and population. It covers nearly half of South America's landmass and borders every South American country except Ecuador and Chile. Its capital is Brasília, and its most populous city is São Paulo.

History

The land was inhabited by numerous indigenous peoples before the Portuguese explorer Pedro Álvares Cabral claimed it for Portugal in 1500. Brazil remained a Portuguese colony until independence in 1822, when it became the Empire of Brazil; a republic was proclaimed in 1889. The country experienced military rule from 1964 to 1985 and is today a federal presidential republic comprising 26 states and the Federal District.

Geography and biodiversity

Brazil contains most of the Amazon rainforest and the Amazon River system, the world's largest by discharge. It is considered a megadiverse country, holding the greatest biodiversity of any nation, with biomes including the Amazon, the Cerrado savanna, the Atlantic Forest, the Pantanal wetlands, and the Caatinga.

Culture

Brazil is the only Portuguese-speaking country in the Americas. Its culture blends Portuguese, African, and indigenous influences, expressed in music (samba, bossa nova), Carnival, capoeira, and football — Brazil has won the FIFA World Cup five times, more than any other nation.
