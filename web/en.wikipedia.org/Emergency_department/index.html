An emergency department (ED), also known as an accident and emergency department (A&E), emergency room (ER), or casualty department, is a medical treatment facility specializing in emergency medicine, the acute care of patients who present without prior appointment, either by their own means or by ambulance. Emergency departments are usually found in hospitals or other primary care centres and operate around the clock.

Triage

Because attendance is unscheduled and unpredictable, patients are sorted on arrival by a process called triage, in which a nurse or clinician assesses urgency so that those with life-threatening conditions are treated first. Standardized scales include the five-level Emergency Severity Index (ESI) in the United States, the Manchester Triage System in the United Kingdom, and the Canadian Triage and Acuity Scale (CTAS). Resuscitation cases — cardiac arrest, major trauma, airway compromise — go directly to a dedicated resuscitation area.

Work and facilities

Emergency staff stabilize patients, perform rapid diagnostics (laboratory tests, X-ray, CT scanning, point-of-care ultrasound), and either discharge patients with follow-up advice or admit them to inpatient wards, operating theatres, or intensive care. Many departments include specialized zones: pediatric areas, observation or clinical decision units, isolation rooms for infectious patients, and decontamination facilities. Major trauma centres receive patients triaged in the field by ambulance and helicopter services.

Overcrowding

Overcrowding is a persistent problem in many health systems, driven by rising demand, shortages of inpatient beds ("exit block"), and patients attending with conditions manageable in primary care. Consequences include long waits, patients leaving without being seen, ambulance delays, and worse outcomes for time-critical conditions such as stroke and heart attack. Interventions include fast-track streams for minor injuries, expanded urgent-care centres, and hospital-wide flow management.

History

Dedicated emergency care developed from wartime casualty clearing and urban "receiving" hospitals. In the United States, the first purpose-built emergency departments appeared in the 1960s, and emergency medicine became a recognized specialty in 1979. In the United Kingdom, the Platt Report of 1962 recommended reorganizing casualty services into modern accident and emergency departments.
