Metabolome

The metabolome refers to the complete set of small-molecule chemicals found within a biological sample — a cell, tissue, organ, biofluid or whole organism. These molecules, called metabolites, include amino acids, sugars, lipids, nucleotides, organic acids and many signalling and secondary compounds, together with exogenous chemicals derived from diet, drugs and the environment.

Metabolomics

The systematic study of the metabolome is called metabolomics. Because metabolites are the downstream products of gene expression and protein activity, the metabolome reflects the current physiological state of a system more directly than the genome or transcriptome. Unlike genes and proteins, metabolites are not encoded in a template, so their number and identity vary strongly with species, tissue and condition.

Measurement

The metabolome is measured chiefly by mass spectrometry, often coupled to liquid or gas chromatography, and by NMR spectroscopy. Resources such as the Human Metabolome Database catalogue tens of thousands of detected and expected human metabolites.

Applications

Metabolomics is applied in biomarker discovery, disease diagnostics, toxicology, nutrition, plant science and personalised medicine.
