Windsor is a city in southwestern Ontario, Canada, on the south bank of the Detroit River directly across from Detroit, Michigan. It is the southernmost city in Canada and the seat of Essex County.

History

French farmers settled the area from 1749, making it one of the oldest continuously inhabited European settlements in Canada west of Montreal. The town was named after Windsor, Berkshire. During the American Civil War and after, Windsor was a terminus of the Underground Railroad. In 1935 several border municipalities amalgamated into the modern city.

Economy

Windsor's economy has long centred on the automotive industry — it hosts a major Stellantis minivan plant and a large tool-and-die sector — as well as the Hiram Walker distillery, founded in 1858, producer of Canadian Club whisky. The Ambassador Bridge and the Detroit–Windsor Tunnel carry a large share of Canada–US trade; the Gordie Howe International Bridge is under construction to supplement them. The University of Windsor and Caesars Windsor casino are major employers.
