Jean-Claude Killy

Jean-Claude Killy

Born: 30 August 1943, Saint-Cloud, France

Occupation: Alpine skier, sports administrator

Olympic medals: 3 gold (1968)

Jean-Claude Killy (born 30 August 1943) is a French former alpine ski racer who dominated the sport in the late 1960s. He is best remembered for sweeping all three men's alpine events — downhill, giant slalom and slalom — at the 1968 Winter Olympics in Grenoble, a feat matched only by Toni Sailer before him.

Skiing career

Raised in the ski resort of Val-d'Isère, Killy won the first two overall World Cup titles in 1967 and 1968, collecting 12 World Cup race victories. At the 1966 World Championships in Portillo, Chile, he won the downhill and combined titles. His aggressive technique and meticulous preparation made him the defining skier of his generation.

Later life

After retiring in 1968, Killy pursued business and endorsement ventures, co-founded the resort of Tignes' expansion projects, and became a leading sports administrator. He served as co-president of the organizing committee for the 1992 Winter Olympics in Albertville and was a member of the International Olympic Committee from 1995 until his resignation in 2014.
