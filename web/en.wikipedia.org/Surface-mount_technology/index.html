Surface-mount technology

Surface-mount technology (SMT) is a method of manufacturing electronic circuits in which components are mounted directly onto the surface of printed circuit boards (PCBs). Devices so made are called surface-mount devices (SMDs). SMT largely replaced through-hole technology in mass-produced electronics from the 1980s onward.

Process

Solder paste is printed onto pads on the board through a stencil; automated pick-and-place machines position components at high speed; and the assembly passes through a reflow oven that melts the solder to form joints. Optical inspection and X-ray systems check the result.

Advantages and disadvantages

SMT components are much smaller than leaded equivalents, allow components on both sides of a board, and suit automation, enabling smaller, cheaper, and faster-assembling products. Downsides include harder manual prototyping and repair, and joints that are more vulnerable to certain mechanical and thermal stresses.
