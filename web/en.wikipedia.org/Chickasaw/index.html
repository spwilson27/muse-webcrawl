The Chickasaw are an Indigenous people of the Southeastern Woodlands, originally from present-day Mississippi, Alabama and Tennessee. They were one of the "Five Civilized Tribes" named by Anglo-European settlers. The federally recognised Chickasaw Nation is headquartered in Ada, Oklahoma.

History

The Chickasaw were renowned warriors who resisted Hernando de Soto's expedition in 1540–41 and, allied with the British, fought the French and their Choctaw allies in the 18th century, notably at the Battle of Ackia (1736). Their language belongs to the Muskogean family and is closely related to Choctaw. Under the Treaty of Pontotoc Creek (1832) the Chickasaw ceded their eastern lands and were removed to Indian Territory in 1837–38, initially settling within the Choctaw Nation before establishing their own government in 1856.

Chickasaw Nation today

The Chickasaw Nation has tens of thousands of enrolled citizens and operates extensive enterprises — including the WinStar World Casino, among the largest in the world — funding health, education and cultural programmes. The tribe works to revitalise the Chickasaw language, whose fluent first-language speakers are now few. Notable Chickasaw citizens include astronaut John Herrington, the first enrolled tribal member in space.
