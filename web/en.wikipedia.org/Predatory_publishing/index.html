Predatory publishing

Predatory publishing is an exploitative academic publishing business model in which journals or publishers charge authors publication fees without providing the legitimate editorial and publishing services—especially meaningful peer review—associated with reputable scholarly journals.

Characteristics

Common features include aggressive email solicitation, promises of unusually rapid publication, acceptance of low-quality or nonsensical manuscripts, opaque fee structures, fabricated or misleading impact metrics, editorial boards listing scholars without their consent, and journal titles designed to mimic established publications.

Beall's List

The librarian Jeffrey Beall coined the term "predatory" for such publishers and maintained a widely used list of suspect open-access publishers and journals from about 2010 until 2017. The list was influential but also criticised for potential bias against new, small or non-Western publishers. Archived and successor lists continue to circulate.

Impact

Predatory outlets pollute the scholarly record with unvetted research that may be cited in later work, systematic reviews and policy documents, and they divert research funds. Researchers—particularly early-career scholars and those under publication pressure—may publish in them unknowingly or, in some cases, deliberately. Initiatives such as "Think. Check. Submit." aim to help authors evaluate journals.
