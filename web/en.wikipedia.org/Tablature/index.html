Tablature

Tablature (or tabulature, shortened to tab) is a form of musical notation indicating instrument fingering rather than musical pitches. It was widely used in Europe for lute, vihuela, theorbo and keyboard music from the late Middle Ages through the Baroque era, and it remains the most common notation for popular guitar and bass music today.

Guitar tablature

In modern guitar tab, six horizontal lines represent the strings, and numbers indicate the frets to be fingered. Symbols convey techniques such as bends, slides, hammer-ons, pull-offs and palm muting. Unlike standard notation, tab shows exactly where on the instrument a note is played, but traditionally does not specify rhythm, which must be inferred from the recording or an accompanying staff.

History

The earliest surviving tablatures date from the fifteenth century. French lute tablature used letters on lines, German tablature used a more complex system of letters and symbols, and Spanish and Italian systems used numbers. Keyboard tablature combined a staff for the right hand with letters or numbers for the left.

Harmonica and other instruments

Tablature systems exist for harmonica (numbers for holes with breath direction), drums, and various folk instruments, and ASCII tablature became a staple of early internet guitar communities.
