The Second Battle of Panipat was fought on 5 November 1556 between the forces of the Mughal emperor Akbar, led in the field by his regent Bairam Khan, and the army of Hemu, the Hindu general and self-proclaimed ruler of Delhi. The Mughal victory secured Akbar's throne and restored Mughal rule in northern India after the interruption of the Suri interregnum.

Background

On the death of Humayun in January 1556, his thirteen-year-old son Akbar was proclaimed emperor at Kalanaur in Punjab, with the Turkoman noble Bairam Khan as regent. Meanwhile Hemu, the able general and chief minister of the Suri claimant Adil Shah, had won a string of victories across northern India and seized Delhi and Agra in October 1556, assuming the royal title Vikramaditya. The two armies met on the historic plain of Panipat, site of Babur's victory thirty years earlier.

The battle

Hemu's army, with a strong elephant corps and superior numbers, initially pressed the Mughal wings hard. At the height of the fighting, Hemu was struck in the eye by an arrow while riding his famous war elephant Hawai and fell unconscious. Leaderless, his army dissolved into flight. Hemu was captured and executed shortly afterwards.

Aftermath

The victory re-established Mughal authority over Delhi and Agra and opened the way for Akbar's long reign (1556–1605), during which the Mughal Empire expanded across most of the Indian subcontinent. Panipat would host a third famous battle in 1761, between the Marathas and Ahmad Shah Durrani. See also Akbar, First Battle of Panipat, and Third Battle of Panipat.
