BioWare

BioWare is a Canadian video game developer based in Edmonton, Alberta, best known for story-driven role-playing games (RPGs) including the Baldur's Gate, Neverwinter Nights, Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic, Mass Effect, and Dragon Age series. Founded in 1995 by physicians Ray Muzyka, Greg Zeschuk, and Augustine Yip, the studio has been a subsidiary of Electronic Arts (EA) since 2007.

History

BioWare's founders were medical doctors who developed simulation software before turning to games. Its first title, Shattered Steel (1996), was followed by Baldur's Gate (1998), a landmark computer RPG built on the Infinity Engine that revived the genre on PC. Baldur's Gate II: Shadows of Amn (2000) and Neverwinter Nights (2002) cemented the studio's reputation. Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic (2003) won numerous Game of the Year awards, and Jade Empire (2005) was its first original intellectual property console title.

In 2005 BioWare formed a partnership with Pandemic Studios backed by private equity, and in 2007 EA acquired both studios in a deal valued at about $860 million. The Mass Effect trilogy (2007–2012) and Dragon Age: Origins (2009) defined the studio's modern style: cinematic presentation, player choice, and companion characters. The MMORPG Star Wars: The Old Republic launched in 2011. Muzyka and Zeschuk left the company in 2012.

Later years

Subsequent releases had mixed fortunes: Dragon Age: Inquisition (2014) was a major success and Game of the Year winner, while Mass Effect: Andromeda (2017) and the online shooter Anthem (2019) were commercial and critical disappointments that led to restructuring, including the closure of BioWare Montreal. The studio later released the well-received Mass Effect Legendary Edition remaster (2021) and Dragon Age: The Veilguard (2024).
