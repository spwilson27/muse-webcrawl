Pennsylvania Station, commonly known as Penn Station, is the main intercity railroad station in New York City and the busiest transportation facility in the Western Hemisphere, serving more than 600,000 passengers per weekday as of 2019. Located in Midtown Manhattan, close to Herald Square, the Empire State Building, Koreatown, and the Chelsea neighborhood, it is served by the Amtrak Northeast Corridor, the Long Island Rail Road, and NJ Transit, as well as several New York City Subway stations.

The station is located beneath Pennsylvania Plaza, an office and entertainment complex between Seventh and Eighth Avenues and 31st and 33rd Streets, which includes Madison Square Garden. Because the station is underground, accessible only through the surrounding buildings, it lacks the street-level presence of New York's other major terminal, Grand Central Terminal.

History

The original Pennsylvania Station was a monumental Beaux-Arts building designed by the firm of McKim, Mead & White and completed in 1910. Inspired by the Baths of Caracalla and other classical structures, it occupied two city blocks and was considered a masterpiece of the Beaux-Arts style. The station enabled direct rail access to New York City from the south for the first time, via tunnels under the Hudson River.

Beginning in 1963, the original station building was demolished to make way for the construction of Pennsylvania Plaza and Madison Square Garden. The demolition sparked an international outcry and galvanized the modern historic preservation movement in the United States, leading to laws that ultimately saved landmarks such as Grand Central Terminal. The rail facilities continued operating underground, and the current station opened in 1968.

Moynihan Train Hall

In January 2021, the Moynihan Train Hall opened across Eighth Avenue in the renovated James A. Farley Building, the city's former main post office. The hall, named for Senator Daniel Patrick Moynihan, expanded Penn Station's concourse space and serves Amtrak and Long Island Rail Road passengers, relieving congestion in the original underground complex.

Services

Penn Station is the hub for Amtrak services along the Northeast Corridor, including the high-speed Acela trains to Boston and Washington, D.C., as well as long-distance trains to Chicago, Florida, and the South. NJ Transit operates trains to New Jersey suburbs and the Jersey Shore, and the Long Island Rail Road runs trains to Long Island. The station is among the busiest in North America and is a critical node in the regional rail network.
