Karuṇā

Karuṇā (Sanskrit and Pali: करुणा) is a central concept in Indian religions, generally translated as "compassion": the wish that beings be free from suffering, together with active sympathy and tender care for those who suffer. It is sometimes rendered "compassion and self-compassion".

In Buddhism

Karuṇā is one of the four divine abidings (brahmavihāras), along with loving-kindness (mettā), sympathetic joy (muditā), and equanimity (upekkhā). In Mahāyāna Buddhism it is paired with wisdom (prajñā) as the defining virtue of the bodhisattva, personified by Avalokiteśvara, the bodhisattva of compassion. Great compassion (mahākaruṇā) is said to arise spontaneously in one who perceives the emptiness of all phenomena.

In Hinduism and Jainism

In Hindu traditions karuṇā appears among the virtues enjoined in texts such as Patañjali's Yoga Sūtras, where it is cultivated toward the suffering. In Jainism, compassion for all living beings is closely tied to ahiṃsā (non-violence) and to practices of charity and protection of life.

Practice

Compassion is cultivated through meditation practices such as the Tibetan Buddhist tonglen (sending and receiving) and through acts of generosity and service. Modern research in psychology has adapted these practices into secular compassion training programs.
