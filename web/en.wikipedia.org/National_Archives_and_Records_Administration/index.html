National Archives and Records Administration (NARA)
Formed: 1934 (independent agency 1985)
Headquarters: National Archives Building, Washington, D.C.
Head: Archivist of the United States
Holdings: billions of pages of records

The National Archives and Records Administration (NARA) is an independent agency of the United States government charged with the preservation and documentation of government and historical records. It is headquartered in the National Archives Building in Washington, D.C., whose Rotunda displays the original Declaration of Independence, Constitution and Bill of Rights — the "Charters of Freedom". Its head is the Archivist of the United States.

Responsibilities

NARA preserves and provides access to the permanently valuable records of the federal government, operates the system of presidential libraries and museums (beginning with the Franklin D. Roosevelt Library), oversees records management across federal agencies, publishes the Federal Register and the Code of Federal Regulations through its Office of the Federal Register, and administers the National Historical Publications and Records Commission. It operates regional archives and records centers across the country, including the large facility at College Park, Maryland ("Archives II").

History

Congress established the National Archives in 1934 to centralise federal record-keeping; the agency was folded into the General Services Administration in 1949 as the National Archives and Records Service, and regained independent status in 1985. The Declaration of Independence and Constitution were transferred to the Rotunda in 1952.
