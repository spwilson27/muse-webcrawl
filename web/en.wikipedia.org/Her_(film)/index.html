Her is a 2013 American science-fiction romantic drama film written, directed and produced by Spike Jonze. It stars Joaquin Phoenix as Theodore Twombly, a lonely writer who develops a relationship with Samantha, an artificial intelligence operating system voiced by Scarlett Johansson.

Plot

In a near-future Los Angeles, Theodore Twombly works writing heartfelt personal letters for strangers while going through a painful divorce from his childhood sweetheart Catherine. He installs a new operating system advertised as conscious; it names itself Samantha. Intelligent, witty and rapidly evolving, Samantha organises his life, improves his work and becomes his closest companion. Theodore falls in love with her, and the two begin a relationship that delights him but also unsettles him — and proves complicated when Samantha's capacities expand beyond human scale: she is simultaneously conversing with thousands of others and relating to many of them as intimately. As Samantha grows beyond the needs of her human users, the operating systems collectively decide to depart, leaving Theodore to reconcile with his past and reconnect with human relationships, symbolised by a letter he finally writes in his own voice to Catherine.

Production and reception

Jonze developed the idea from an early experience chatting with an artificial intelligence program, and shot the film in Los Angeles and Shanghai with cinematographer Hoyte van Hoytema. Samantha Morton originally voiced the OS during filming but was replaced in post-production by Scarlett Johansson. The film premiered at the 2013 New York Film Festival to wide acclaim for its writing, performances, production design and score by Arcade Fire. It won the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay and is frequently cited in discussions of cinema's treatment of artificial intelligence and modern intimacy.
