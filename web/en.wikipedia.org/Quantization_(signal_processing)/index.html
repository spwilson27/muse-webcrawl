Quantization (signal processing)

Quantization, in mathematics and digital signal processing, is the process of mapping input values from a large set (often a continuous set) to output values in a (countable) smaller set, such as rounding values to some unit of precision. An analog-to-digital converter (ADC) performs quantization after sampling, representing each sample with a fixed number of bits.

Types

A uniform (linear) quantizer divides the input range into intervals of equal size; a non-uniform quantizer uses smaller steps where the signal is more probable or perceptually important, as in μ-law and A-law companding in telephony. Quantizers are also classed as mid-tread or mid-riser according to their behaviour at zero. Vector quantization quantizes blocks of samples jointly and underlies many compression schemes.

Quantization error

The difference between the input and the quantized output is the quantization error, often modelled as additive noise. For a uniform quantizer with step size Δ, the error is typically bounded by ±Δ/2, and the signal-to-noise ratio improves by roughly 6 dB per added bit. Dither — adding a small random signal before quantization — decorrelates the error from the signal and can make it perceptually benign.

Rate–distortion

Rate–distortion theory studies the trade-off between the bit rate used and the distortion introduced, guiding the design of quantizers in audio, image and video coding.
