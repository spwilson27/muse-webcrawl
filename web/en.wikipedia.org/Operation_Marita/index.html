Operation Marita (German: Unternehmen Marita) was the German invasion of Greece in April 1941, during the Second World War. It was launched together with the invasion of Yugoslavia (Operation 25) after a coup in Belgrade overthrew the Yugoslav government that had just joined the Axis Tripartite Pact. Together the operations constituted the Balkan campaign's decisive phase.

Background

Italy had attacked Greece from Albania in October 1940 and been driven back by Greek counteroffensives. Fearing that British forces in Greece would threaten the Romanian oilfields at Ploiești, Adolf Hitler issued Directive No. 20 in December 1940, ordering plans to occupy northern Greece, later extended to the whole country. The pro-Axis Yugoslav regent Prince Paul was overthrown on 27 March 1941, prompting Hitler to add Yugoslavia to the operation.

The invasion

On 6 April 1941, the German Twelfth Army under Field Marshal Wilhelm List attacked from Bulgaria. In the east, German forces assaulted the fortified Metaxas Line along the Greek-Bulgarian border; although the border forts resisted fiercely for several days, a thrust through Yugoslavia's Vardar valley outflanked the line and seized Thessaloniki. The Greek Army of Eastern Macedonia, cut off, surrendered on 9 April. German armour then drove south, breaking the Commonwealth and Greek positions around Mount Olympus. Athens fell on 27 April, and organised resistance on the mainland ended by 30 April, as British Commonwealth forces evacuated some 50,000 troops by sea, mainly to Crete—which Germany invaded in May in Operation Mercury.

Occupation

Greece was partitioned into German, Italian, and Bulgarian occupation zones. The occupation brought famine, mass reprisals against resistance, and the destruction of Greek Jewry; it also saw one of Europe's largest resistance movements. Greek forces in exile continued to fight with the Allies in North Africa and Italy.
