Natural resource

Natural resources are resources that are drawn from nature and used with few modifications. This includes the sources of valued characteristics such as commercial and industrial use, aesthetic value, scientific interest, and cultural value. On Earth, natural resources include sunlight, the atmosphere, water, land, all minerals along with all vegetation and animal life.

Classification

Resources are commonly divided into:

  • Renewable resources, replenished naturally on human timescales — sunlight, wind, water cycles, biomass, and sustainably harvested timber and fish
  • Non-renewable resources, formed over geological timescales and consumed faster than they form — fossil fuels (coal, petroleum, natural gas) and metal ores

They may also be classified as biotic (living) or abiotic (non-living), and as actual or potential resources depending on whether they are currently exploited.

Management and issues

Natural resource management concerns how societies allocate, conserve, and extract resources. Key issues include the tragedy of the commons, resource depletion, the "resource curse" linking mineral wealth to poor governance outcomes, and the protection of ecosystem services and biodiversity. Extractive industries remain central to many national economies, while transitions to renewable energy are reshaping demand for resources such as lithium, cobalt, and rare-earth elements.
