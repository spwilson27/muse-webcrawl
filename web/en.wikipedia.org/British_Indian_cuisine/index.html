British Indian cuisine

British Indian cuisine refers to the style of Indian food developed in the United Kingdom, where curry houses—many run by Sylheti Bangladeshis—became a major part of British food culture from the mid-20th century. Dishes were adapted to British tastes, typically with rich, mildly spiced sauces.

Signature dishes

Chicken tikka masala, often cited as Britain's national dish, is the best-known example, said to have been created in Glasgow in the 1970s. The balti, developed in Birmingham, and dishes such as madras, vindaloo and korma in their British forms are staples of the curry house menu, usually served with pilau rice, naan and poppadoms.
