Prospect Park is an urban park in the New York City borough of Brooklyn, covering 526 acres (213 ha) between the neighborhoods of Park Slope, Prospect Heights, Prospect-Lefferts Gardens, Flatbush, and Windsor Terrace. The park is operated by the New York City Department of Parks and Recreation (NYC Parks) in partnership with the Prospect Park Alliance. It was designed by Frederick Law Olmsted and Calvert Vaux, who considered it a refinement of their earlier work on Central Park.

History

The land was acquired by the city of Brooklyn in the 1860s; the park's name derives from Prospect Hill, near its northwestern corner, one of the highest points in Brooklyn. The site includes ground fought over during the Battle of Long Island in 1776. Construction began in 1866 under Egbert Viele's initial plan, but Olmsted and Vaux's revised design—organized around the Long Meadow, the Ravine, and the Lake—was adopted, and the park opened in stages from 1867, substantially completed by 1873. The park's Soldiers' and Sailors' Arch at Grand Army Plaza, designed by John H. Duncan, was dedicated in 1892.

Features

Prospect Park contains Brooklyn's last remaining natural forest and the borough's only lake. The 90-acre Long Meadow is one of the longest continuous meadows in an American urban park. Other features include the Prospect Park Zoo, the Prospect Park Bandshell (home of the Celebrate Brooklyn! festival), the Boathouse (a designated landmark housing an Audubon center), the Lefferts Historic House, the Carousel, athletic fields, and the Peristyle and Concert Grove. The park's watercourse—the Ravine, pools, and 60-acre Lake—was designed as a connected system recreating the scenery of the Adirondack Mountains.

Restoration

After decades of deterioration, the Prospect Park Alliance, founded in 1987, has led a major restoration effort, including the Lakeside project (2013), which added ice rinks and restored shoreline landscapes, and the restoration of the Endale Arch and the park's woodlands. The park is listed on the National Register of Historic Places and contains several New York City designated landmarks.
