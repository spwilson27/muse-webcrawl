Battle of Dorylaeum (1147)

The Battle of Dorylaeum took place on 25 October 1147 during the Second Crusade, near Dorylaeum in Anatolia. The crusading army of Conrad III of Germany was ambushed and largely destroyed by the Seljuk Turks of the Sultanate of Rum under Mesud I. It is sometimes distinguished as the Second Battle of Dorylaeum, the first having been fought in 1097 during the First Crusade.

Background

Following the fall of Edessa in 1144, Pope Eugenius III proclaimed a new crusade. Conrad III's German army, numbering perhaps 20,000 men including non-combatants, crossed Byzantine territory and entered Anatolia, intending to march across the plateau to Iconium and onward to Syria. Poor supplies, disease, and harassment by Turkish horse archers weakened the column as it advanced beyond Dorylaeum.

The battle

On 25 October 1147 the crusaders were attacked near Dorylaeum, in the same region where the First Crusade had won its victory fifty years earlier. The Seljuks used feigned retreats to draw out the crusader cavalry, then enveloped the army with swarms of horse archers. The German infantry, exhausted and short of water, broke apart under repeated attacks. Conrad himself was wounded by arrows. Only a fraction of the army—perhaps one in ten—survived to fall back on Nicaea.

Aftermath

Conrad retreated to Nicaea, where he joined forces with the French army of Louis VII, which had taken the coastal route. The survivors of the German crusade accompanied the French to Ephesus, from where Conrad returned by sea to Constantinople and eventually sailed to Palestine. The disaster at Dorylaeum severely diminished the strength of the Second Crusade, which ultimately failed in its attack on Damascus in 1148.
