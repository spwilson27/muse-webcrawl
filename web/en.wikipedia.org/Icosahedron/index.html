Icosahedron

A regular icosahedron, one of the five Platonic solids.

In geometry, an icosahedron is a polyhedron with 20 faces. The name comes from the Greek eíkosi, meaning twenty, and hédra, meaning seat or base. The most familiar form is the regular icosahedron, whose faces are 20 congruent equilateral triangles; it is one of the five Platonic solids, with 12 vertices and 30 edges. Five triangles meet at each vertex.

Properties

The regular icosahedron is dual to the regular dodecahedron: the vertices of one correspond to the faces of the other. Its symmetry group is the icosahedral group, which has 120 symmetries and is the largest of the finite rotation groups in three dimensions. The dihedral angle of the regular icosahedron is approximately 138.19 degrees. Icosahedra with lower symmetry, such as the gyroelongated pentagonal birotunda, are catalogued among the Johnson solids.

Occurrences

Icosahedral symmetry appears in nature in the capsids of many viruses, which assemble protein subunits into a roughly spherical twenty-faced shell, and in radiolarian skeletons described by Ernst Haeckel. The boron allotrope and the carborane molecule B12H122− adopt icosahedral structures. In popular culture, the twenty-sided die (d20) used in role-playing games such as Dungeons & Dragons is a regular icosahedron, and R. Buckminster Fuller's geodesic domes are typically derived from subdividing an icosahedron.
