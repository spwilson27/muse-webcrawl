James Smithson

James Smithson (c. 1765 – 27 June 1829) was an English chemist and mineralogist whose bequest founded the Smithsonian Institution in Washington, D.C., despite his never having visited the United States.

Life

Smithson was born in Paris, the illegitimate son of Hugh Smithson (later Hugh Percy, 1st Duke of Northumberland) and Elizabeth Hungerford Keate. Educated at Pembroke College, Oxford, he published papers on chemistry and geology, was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1787, and travelled widely in Europe. The mineral smithsonite (zinc carbonate) is named after him, following his identification of the distinct minerals among the "calamines."

The bequest

Smithson's will directed that if his nephew died without heirs, his estate should go "to the United States of America, to found at Washington, under the name of the Smithsonian Institution, an Establishment for the increase & diffusion of knowledge among men." The contingency occurred in 1835; after litigation in England, the United States received about £105,000 in gold sovereigns in 1838, and Congress established the Smithsonian in 1846. Why Smithson chose America remains unknown — speculations include sympathy with democratic and scientific ideals and resentment of the British class system that had stigmatised his birth.

Smithson died in Genoa, Italy; in 1904 Alexander Graham Bell, a Smithsonian regent, had his remains brought to Washington, where they rest in a crypt in the Smithsonian Castle.
