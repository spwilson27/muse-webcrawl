Central Otago

Central Otago is an area in the inland Otago region of New Zealand's South Island. It is the country's southernmost wine region and is noted for its arid continental climate, dramatic schist landscapes, orchards and 1860s gold-rush heritage.

Geography and climate

The region lies in the rain shadow of the Southern Alps, giving it hot summers, cold winters and some of the lowest rainfall in New Zealand. The Clutha River and its tributaries flow through broad basins surrounded by block mountain ranges such as the Dunstan and Old Man ranges.

Wine and horticulture

Central Otago is internationally recognised for pinot noir, which accounts for the majority of plantings around sub-regions including Bannockburn, Gibbston, Bendigo and Wanaka. The district is also one of New Zealand's main stone-fruit producing areas, especially cherries, apricots and peaches.

History

Gold discoveries in the 1860s, beginning with Gabriel Read's find at Gabriel's Gully, drew thousands of miners. Towns such as Cromwell, Clyde and Arrowtown retain stone buildings from the gold era, and old water races and sluicing sites remain visible in the landscape.
