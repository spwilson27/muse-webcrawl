Araucanian languages

Araucanian
RegionCentral Chile and west-central Argentina
Native speakersc. 260,000 (Mapudungun)
LanguagesMapudungun, Huilliche

The Araucanian languages are a small language family spoken in central Chile and neighbouring parts of Argentina by the Mapuche peoples. The family is generally considered to consist of two living languages: Mapudungun, by far the larger, and Huilliche, a highly divergent and moribund variety spoken south of the Mapudungun area; some scholars classify Huilliche as a dialect of Mapudungun rather than a separate language.

Classification

Proposals linking Araucanian to other South American families — such as Penutian or the "Andean" groupings of earlier scholars — have not been substantiated, and the family is usually treated as an isolate grouping with no demonstrated relatives. A possible extinct third member, the language of the Pehuenche, is poorly attested.

Features

Mapudungun is a polysynthetic language with complex verbal morphology, an inverse system marking person hierarchies, and a small consonant inventory notable for retroflex and interdental sounds. Historically it lacked a standard writing system; several competing alphabets are in use today, and the language is the object of revitalization efforts in Chile and Argentina, though the number of fluent speakers continues to decline.
