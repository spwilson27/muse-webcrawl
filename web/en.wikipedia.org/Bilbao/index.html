Bilbao (Basque: Bilbo) is a city in northern Spain, the largest city in the province of Biscay and in the Basque Country as a whole. It is also the largest city in northern Spain. Bilbao is the tenth largest city in Spain, with a population of around 340,000, and about one million in its metropolitan area.

History

Bilbao was founded as a town in 1300 by Diego López V de Haro. Its port on the Nervión estuary made it a centre for exporting iron and wool, and in the 19th century industrialisation driven by iron mining, steelmaking, and shipbuilding transformed it into one of Spain's richest cities. The decline of heavy industry in the late 20th century prompted a celebrated urban regeneration.

The "Bilbao effect"

The opening of the Guggenheim Museum Bilbao in 1997, a titanium-clad building designed by Frank Gehry, drew international attention and large visitor numbers. Together with a new metro designed by Norman Foster, the Abandoibarra redevelopment, and other projects, it made Bilbao a textbook case — the "Bilbao effect" — of culture-led urban regeneration.

Culture and sport

The Casco Viejo (old town) contains the cathedral of Santiago and the "Seven Streets". The city's football club, Athletic Bilbao, is famous for fielding only players of Basque origin or formation. Basque cuisine, including pintxos, is a major attraction.
