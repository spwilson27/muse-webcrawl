Apostolic Age

The Apostolic Age is the period of early Christianity traditionally dated from about 30 CE, beginning with the ministry and crucifixion of Jesus, until the death of the last of the Twelve Apostles, conventionally placed around 100 CE with the death of John the Apostle. It is the foundational period in which the Christian message spread from Jerusalem across the Roman world.

History

According to the Acts of the Apostles, the age began at Pentecost, when the disciples began preaching in Jerusalem. The Jerusalem church under James the Just remained an early centre, while Paul of Tarsus carried the message to Gentile communities around the Mediterranean. Key events include the Council of Jerusalem (c. 49 CE), which addressed whether Gentile converts must follow Jewish law, and the destruction of the Second Temple in 70 CE during the First Jewish–Roman War, which reshaped both Judaism and the young church.

Sources

The principal sources are the New Testament writings — the Gospels, Acts, and the epistles — supplemented by early works such as the Didache and the letters of Clement of Rome. The period is followed by the post-apostolic or Ante-Nicene period.
