Jeonju

Jeonju (Korean: 전주) is the capital and largest city of North Jeolla Province, South Korea. It is both urban and rural due to the closeness of Wanju County, which almost entirely surrounds Jeonju. The city is most famous for Jeonju bibimbap, a version of the Korean rice dish, its historic Hanok Village of traditional houses, and as the birthplace of the Joseon dynasty's Yi clan.


Jeonju Hanok Village

History

Jeonju has a long history dating to the Baekje kingdom, when the area was known as Wansanju. In the Later Baekje kingdom founded by Gyeon Hwon in 892, it served as a capital. Under the Joseon dynasty the city gained particular prestige because the royal Yi family originated there; the Gyeonggijeon shrine was built in 1410 to house a portrait of King Taejo, the dynasty's founder.

Culture and tourism

The Jeonju Hanok Village contains over 800 traditional Korean houses (hanok) and is one of the most popular tourist destinations in South Korea. Landmarks include Jeondong Cathedral, a Romanesque Catholic church; the Pungnammun gate, the south gate of the former city fortress; and the Jeonju Hanji Museum, celebrating traditional Korean paper. The Jeonju International Film Festival (JIFF), founded in 2000, is held annually.

Cuisine

Jeonju is celebrated as a centre of Korean gastronomy and was designated a UNESCO City of Gastronomy in 2012. Its signature dishes include bibimbap, bean sprout soup with rice (kongnamul gukbap), and makgeolli (rice wine) served with a large spread of side dishes.
