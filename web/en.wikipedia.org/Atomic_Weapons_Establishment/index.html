Atomic Weapons Establishment

Atomic Weapons Establishment (AWE)
Founded: 1987 (origins 1950, AWRE Aldermaston)
Headquarters: Aldermaston, Berkshire, England
Owner: UK Ministry of Defence
Role: Design, manufacture, and support of UK nuclear warheads

The Atomic Weapons Establishment (AWE) is responsible for the design, manufacture, and support of warheads for the United Kingdom's nuclear weapons programme, including the Trident missile system carried by the Royal Navy's Vanguard-class submarines. Its principal sites are at Aldermaston and Burghfield in Berkshire.

History

The establishment began in 1950 as the Atomic Weapons Research Establishment (AWRE) at the former RAF Aldermaston, under William Penney, to develop Britain's first atomic bomb, tested in Operation Hurricane in 1952. In 1987 AWRE merged with other facilities to form AWE. From 1993 AWE was operated by a contractor consortium; in 2021 the Ministry of Defence brought it back in-house as an executive agency.

Activities

Because the UK has observed a moratorium on explosive nuclear testing since 1991, AWE maintains the warhead stockpile through experiments, supercomputing simulation, and facilities such as the Orion laser. It also supports the replacement warhead programme for the Dreadnought-class submarines.
