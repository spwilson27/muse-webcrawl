Educational Testing Service

Educational Testing Service (ETS), founded in 1947, is the world's largest private nonprofit educational testing and assessment organization. It is headquartered in Princeton, New Jersey, on a large campus in neighboring Lawrence Township. ETS develops and administers more than 50 million tests annually — including the TOEFL, TOEIC, GRE, and Praxis tests — in more than 180 countries.

History

ETS was created in 1947 through the merger of the testing activities of the American Council on Education, the Carnegie Foundation for the Advancement of Teaching, and the College Entrance Examination Board, which transferred its SAT programme to the new organization. Its first president was the psychometrician Henry Chauncey. For decades ETS administered the SAT for the College Board; design and development responsibilities for the SAT shifted to the College Board itself in the 2010s, though ETS long remained involved in test production and scoring.

Products

ETS's principal tests include the TOEFL and TOEIC English-language assessments, the GRE General and Subject Tests for graduate admissions, the Praxis Series used for teacher licensure in the United States, and a range of K–12 and workforce assessments. It also conducts research in psychometrics, statistics, and educational measurement, and has developed automated scoring systems for constructed responses, such as the e-rater engine.

Criticism

ETS has faced criticism over test security breaches, scoring errors, fees, and the cultural fairness of standardized tests, as well as scrutiny of its nonprofit status given the scale of its revenues. Notable episodes include litigation over the Golden Rule settlement on item selection and controversies surrounding the administration of exams abroad.
