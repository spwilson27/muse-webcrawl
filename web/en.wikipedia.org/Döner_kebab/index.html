Doner kebab (Turkish: döner kebap, "rotating roast") is a dish of seasoned meat stacked in the shape of an inverted cone on a vertical rotisserie, slowly turned and roasted, from which crisped outer layers are shaved off with a long knife. It is commonly served in flatbread with salad and sauces, or on a plate with rice or fries.

History

Meat cooked on a vertical spit developed in the Ottoman Empire in the 19th century; the dish is traditionally associated with Bursa. Its relatives include Arab shawarma and Greek gyros. The modern sandwich form — meat in flatbread with salad — was popularised in West Berlin in the early 1970s by Turkish immigrant workers, notably Kadir Nurman, and became one of Germany's most popular fast foods before spreading worldwide.

Regional variations

In Turkey, döner is typically served on a plate (pilav üstü döner), in a wrap (dürüm), or as İskender kebap over pide with tomato sauce and browned butter. In Germany the sandwich form with a wide choice of salads and garlic or herb sauces predominates. Production of the seasoned meat cones is industrialised, and the dish has been the subject of EU quality-specification debates, including a Turkish application for Traditional Speciality Guaranteed status.
