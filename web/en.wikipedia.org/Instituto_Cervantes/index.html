The Instituto Cervantes is a non-profit organisation created by the Spanish government in 1991 to promote the Spanish language and the culture of Spain and other Spanish-speaking countries around the world. Named after the writer Miguel de Cervantes, author of Don Quixote, it is comparable to Germany's Goethe-Institut and France's Alliance française.

Activities

The institute runs Spanish language courses for all levels, trains teachers of Spanish as a foreign language, and administers the DELE (Diplomas de Español como Lengua Extranjera) examinations on behalf of Spain's Ministry of Education. Its centres host exhibitions, film screenings, concerts, lectures, and literary events, and maintain libraries of Spanish and Latin American material.

Organisation and reach

Headquartered in Madrid — in the former Banco Central building, known as the Edificio de las Cariátides — the institute operates a network of centres in dozens of cities across five continents. Its Aula Virtual de Español provides online courses, and its annual report El español en el mundo is a standard reference on the global state of the language, spoken by roughly 500 million people.
