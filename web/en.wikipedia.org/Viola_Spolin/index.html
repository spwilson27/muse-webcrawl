Viola Spolin

Viola Spolin (November 7, 1906 – November 22, 1994) was an American theatre educator and acting coach, widely regarded as the originator of improvisational theatre in the United States. Her "theater games" — exercises designed to free actors from self-consciousness and to build ensemble spontaneity — transformed actor training, and her 1963 book Improvisation for the Theater remains a foundational text.

Early work

Spolin studied at Neva Boyd's Recreational Training School in Chicago, where she absorbed Boyd's theories of group play and folk games as tools of learning. Working as a drama supervisor for the WPA Federal Theatre Project in Chicago from 1939 to 1941, she developed exercises to help immigrant children and adults communicate across language barriers. After moving to Los Angeles, she founded the Young Actors Company in 1946.

Theater games

In 1955 Spolin returned to Chicago to work with the Playwrights Theatre Club and later with her son Paul Sills and The Second City, the improvisational comedy troupe founded in 1959. Her games — including exercises in mirroring, gibberish, space objects and point of concentration — taught performers to solve theatrical problems through play rather than intellect. She emphasized direct experience, intuition, and freeing the body and voice from habitual tension.

Publications and legacy

Improvisation for the Theater (1963) codified her method and has been through several editions; she later published Theater Games for Rehearsal (1985) and Theater Games for the Lone Actor (2001, posthumous). Spolin's techniques underpin the training of improvisers worldwide and have influenced fields far beyond theatre, including education, therapy and business creativity training.
