Broth

A bowl of clear broth. (Image placeholder)

Broth, also known as bouillon, is a savoury liquid made of water in which meat, fish or vegetables have been simmered for a short period of time. It can be eaten alone, but it is most commonly used to prepare other dishes, such as soups, gravies and sauces.

Preparation

Broth is typically made by simmering meat, bones, seafood or vegetables in water, often with aromatics such as onion, carrot, celery, herbs and spices. Compared with stock, broth is usually made primarily from meat rather than bones, is simmered for a shorter time, and is seasoned so that it can be served on its own. Commercially prepared broths are available as cubes, powders and ready-to-use liquids.

Varieties

Common varieties include chicken broth, beef broth, fish broth and vegetable broth. In Japanese cuisine, dashi is a broth made from kombu and bonito flakes; in Vietnamese cuisine, the broth is the defining element of phở. Scotch broth is a hearty soup-broth made with barley, lamb or mutton and root vegetables.

Nutrition

Broth is low in calories and provides water, electrolytes and small amounts of protein and minerals extracted from the simmered ingredients. Bone broth, simmered for extended periods, contains collagen-derived gelatin and amino acids such as glycine and proline.
