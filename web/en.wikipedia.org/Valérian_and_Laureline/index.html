Valérian and Laureline

Valérian and Laureline (French: Valérian et Laureline), originally titled Valérian: Spatio-Temporal Agent (Valérian, agent spatio-temporel), is a French science fiction comics series, created by writer Pierre Christin and artist Jean-Claude Mézières. First published in Pilote magazine on 9 November 1967, the series focuses on the adventures of the dark-haired Valérian, a spatio-temporal agent, and his companion Laureline, a red-headed peasant girl whom he rescued from 11th-century France.

Overview

Working for the Spatio-Temporal Service based in Galaxity, the capital of future Earth, the pair travel through space and time to protect the timeline from threats. The series is noted for its inventive alien worlds, strong ecological and political themes, and the egalitarian partnership between its two leads — unusual for comics of its era, in which Laureline frequently proves the more resourceful of the two.

Influence

Valérian is considered one of the most influential European science fiction comics. Its visual designs are widely credited with influencing films such as Star Wars and The Fifth Element; Mézières later contributed concept art to the latter. Artist Jean Giraud (Moebius) and many others have cited the series as formative.

Adaptations

The series was adapted as the animated television series Time Jam: Valerian & Laureline (2007–2008) and as the film Valerian and the City of a Thousand Planets (2017), directed by Luc Besson and starring Dane DeHaan and Cara Delevingne. The main series concluded with its twenty-first album, The Time Opener (2010), though further specials and short stories have followed.
