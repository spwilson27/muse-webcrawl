Wembley Stadium

Location: Wembley, London, England
Capacity: 90,000
Opened: 9 March 2007
Owner: The Football Association
Architect: Foster + Partners, Populous

The new Wembley Stadium, distinguished by its 133 m (436 ft) arch.

Wembley Stadium (sometimes referred to as The New Wembley) is an association football stadium in Wembley, London. It opened in 2007 on the site of the original Wembley Stadium, which was demolished in 2002–2003. The stadium hosts major football matches including home matches of the England national football team, and the FA Cup final.

It also hosts other sporting events such as NFL International Series games, rugby league's Challenge Cup final, and major boxing matches, as well as large concerts. With 90,000 seats, it is the largest stadium in the United Kingdom and the second-largest in Europe.

Design

The stadium was designed by Foster + Partners and HOK Sport (now Populous) and built by Multiplex at a cost of £798 million. Its signature feature is a partially retractable roof and a 133-metre-tall illuminated arch, the world's longest single-span roof support structure, which replaced the old stadium's famous Twin Towers. A statue of Bobby Moore, captain of England's 1966 World Cup-winning team, stands outside.

The original stadium

The original Wembley Stadium, built for the 1924 British Empire Exhibition, hosted the 1948 Summer Olympics, the 1966 FIFA World Cup final, and the 1985 Live Aid concert. The new stadium has since hosted two UEFA Champions League finals (2011 and 2013), matches of the 2012 Olympics, the UEFA Euro 2020 final, and the final of the UEFA Women's Euro 2022.
