Credit bureau
Also called: Credit reporting agency
Major US bureaus: Equifax, Experian, TransUnion
Regulation: Fair Credit Reporting Act

A credit bureau, or credit reporting agency, is a company that collects and maintains credit information on individuals and businesses and sells it, typically as a credit report, to lenders, employers, landlords, and other businesses.

Operations

Bureaus receive data from creditors ("furnishers") about accounts, balances, and payment history, and compile public records such as bankruptcies. In the United States, the three nationwide bureaus are Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion. Their data feeds scoring models such as the FICO score.

Regulation and rights

The Fair Credit Reporting Act (1970) gives US consumers the right to free annual reports, to dispute inaccurate information, and to know when a report was used against them. Data breaches, notably the 2017 Equifax breach affecting some 147 million people, have driven debate over bureau accountability.
