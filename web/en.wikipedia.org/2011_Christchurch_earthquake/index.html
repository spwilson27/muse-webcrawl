The 2011 Christchurch earthquake struck the Canterbury Region of New Zealand's South Island at 12:51 p.m. on Tuesday 22 February 2011. Although its magnitude of 6.3 was moderate, its shallow depth and close proximity to Christchurch produced some of the strongest ground shaking ever recorded in an urban area.

Damage and casualties

185 people were killed, making it New Zealand's second-deadliest natural disaster. Most deaths occurred in the collapses of the Canterbury Television (CTV) building and the Pyne Gould Corporation building. Christchurch Cathedral was badly damaged, its spire toppling, and thousands of buildings were destroyed or later demolished. Severe soil liquefaction flooded eastern suburbs with silt.

Context and recovery

The earthquake was part of the aftershock sequence of the magnitude 7.1 Darfield earthquake of September 2010, which had caused damage but no deaths. The February quake prompted a National State of Emergency and a recovery effort lasting years, administered in part by the Canterbury Earthquake Recovery Authority. Much of the central city was cordoned off for over two years, and the rebuild reshaped Christchurch's economy and population.
