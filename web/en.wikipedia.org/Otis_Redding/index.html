Otis Redding

Born9 September 1941, Dawson, Georgia, U.S.
Died10 December 1967 (aged 26), Madison, Wisconsin, U.S.
GenresSoul, R&B, Southern soul
LabelsVolt / Stax, Atco
NicknamesKing of Soul; the Big O

Otis Ray Redding Jr. (1941–1967) was an American singer and songwriter, regarded as one of the greatest singers in the history of American popular music and a seminal artist in soul and rhythm and blues. His singing style, marked by emotional intensity and a powerful, raspy voice, influenced many later soul artists.

Career

Raised in Macon, Georgia, Redding recorded for Stax Records' Volt label in Memphis, backed by Booker T. & the M.G.'s and the Memphis Horns. His albums Otis Blue (1965) and singles such as "These Arms of Mine", "Pain in My Heart", "I've Been Loving You Too Long", "Respect" (later a signature hit for Aretha Franklin), and "Try a Little Tenderness" made him a star. A triumphant performance at the Monterey International Pop Festival in June 1967 won him a mainstream American audience.

Death and legacy

On 10 December 1967, Redding and most of his backing band, the Bar-Kays, were killed when their plane crashed into Lake Monona near Madison, Wisconsin. Days earlier he had recorded "(Sittin' On) The Dock of the Bay", co-written with Steve Cropper; released posthumously in 1968, it became his only number-one single on the Billboard Hot 100 and the first posthumous number-one in U.S. chart history. Redding was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1989 and received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award.
