No-observed-adverse-effect level

The no-observed-adverse-effect level (NOAEL) denotes the level of exposure of an organism, found by experiment or observation, at which there is no biologically or statistically significant increase in the frequency or severity of any adverse effects of the tested protocol. In other words, it is the highest dose or exposure level at which no harmful effects are observed in the exposed population when compared with its appropriate control.

Definition and use

The NOAEL is a standard concept in toxicology and risk assessment. When a NOAEL has not been demonstrated experimentally, the regulatory focus shifts to the lowest-observed-adverse-effect level (LOAEL), the lowest dose at which adverse effects are seen. Regulators such as the United States Environmental Protection Agency and the European Food Safety Authority use NOAEL values, divided by uncertainty (safety) factors, to derive reference doses and acceptable daily intakes for pesticides, food additives and other chemicals.

Relationship to other dose descriptors

The NOAEL sits within a family of dose-response descriptors that also includes the NOEL (no-observed-effect level, covering any effect, adverse or not), the LOAEL, and benchmark dose (BMD) approaches, which many toxicologists prefer because the NOAEL depends heavily on the particular doses chosen in a study and on sample size. A poorly designed study with few animals can paradoxically yield a higher NOAEL, rewarding less sensitive experiments.
