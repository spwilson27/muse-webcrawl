Kediri (historical kingdom)

Kediri (also known as Kadiri, Javanese: ꦏꦝꦶꦫꦶ) was a Hindu-Buddhist Javanese kingdom based in East Java from 1042 until 1222 (1293 in some accounts of a later revival). This kingdom was centred in the city of Kediri, near the modern city of the same name in East Java. It was one of the major states of pre-Islamic Indonesia and a major centre of Javanese literature.

Formation

The kingdom of Kediri emerged when Airlangga divided his kingdom of Kahuripan into two — Janggala and Kediri — in 1042, so that his two sons could each rule without fighting. He abdicated in 1045 and died in 1049. Kediri, known to the Chinese as part of She-po (Java), grew rich on the trade in spices passing along the Brantas River valley.

Golden age

The kingdom reached its height under King Jayabaya (reigned c. 1135–1157), whose name is attached to famous Javanese prophecies (the Jangka Jayabaya). Under his patronage the poets Mpu Sedah and Mpu Panuluh composed the Bharatayuddha, a kakawin version of the Mahabharata war, in 1157. Kediri's courts produced a flourishing of Old Javanese literature, including the Lubdhaka, the Wrtasancaya and the Smaradahana by Mpu Dharmaja.

Decline and fall

In 1222 the last king, Kertajaya, quarrelled with the Brahmin priesthood and was defeated at the Battle of Ganter by Ken Arok, a commoner who founded the kingdom of Singhasari and the Rajasa dynasty. A brief revival of Kediri under Jayakatwang of Gelang-gelang in 1292 was crushed the following year when Raden Wijaya, aided by Mongol forces of Kublai Khan, destroyed it and founded Majapahit.
