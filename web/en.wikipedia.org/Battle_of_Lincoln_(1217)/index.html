Battle of Lincoln (1217)

Battle of Lincoln (1217)
Date20 May 1217
LocationLincoln, Lincolnshire, England
ResultVictory for the supporters of Henry III
BelligerentsRoyalist forces of Henry III; rebel barons and French forces of Prince Louis
CommandersWilliam Marshal; Thomas, Count of Perche

The Battle of Lincoln, sometimes called the Battle of Lincoln Fair, was fought on 20 May 1217 during the First Barons' War. A royalist army commanded by the regent William Marshal relieved the city of Lincoln, whose castle remained in royalist hands under Nicola de la Haye, and defeated a combined force of rebel English barons and French troops supporting Prince Louis of France's claim to the English throne.

Marshal's men entered the city through a little-used gate, and fighting spread through the streets. The French commander, Thomas, Count of Perche, was killed after refusing to surrender, and the rebel force collapsed; many barons were captured. Because the victorious royalists then looted the city, chroniclers ironically dubbed the battle the "Lincoln Fair".

Aftermath

The defeat at Lincoln, followed by Hubert de Burgh's naval victory off Sandwich in August, ended Louis's hopes of conquering England. The Treaty of Lambeth in September 1217 brought the war to a close, and the young Henry III's government reissued Magna Carta, consolidating the settlement.
