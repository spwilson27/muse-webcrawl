The Cosworth DFV (Double Four Valve) is an internal combustion engine that was originally produced by Cosworth for Formula One motor racing. Funded by Ford and developed under Keith Duckworth, it is one of the most successful racing engines of all time: between 1967 and 1983, DFV-powered cars won 155 Grands Prix, 12 Drivers' Championships, and 10 Constructors' Championships.

The Cosworth DFV, the most successful Formula One engine in history.

History

Lotus founder Colin Chapman persuaded Ford to fund Cosworth's design for the new 3-litre Formula One formula of 1966. The engine debuted in the Lotus 49 at the 1967 Dutch Grand Prix, which Jim Clark won. From 1968 the DFV was made available for sale to other teams, becoming the standard engine for privateer entrants and enabling teams such as McLaren, Tyrrell, and Williams to win championships. Its reign ended with the rise of turbocharged engines in the early 1980s, though derivatives (DFY, DFZ, DFR) raced on, and detuned versions (DFL, DFV variants) powered sports cars and the CART IndyCar DFX.

Design

The DFV is a naturally aspirated 90-degree V8 with four valves per cylinder operated by double overhead camshafts, gear-driven. It was designed as a stressed member of the chassis, bolted directly to the monocoque and carrying the rear suspension—a philosophy that became standard in Formula One.
