Orestes

In Greek mythology, Orestes was the son of Agamemnon and Clytemnestra, and the brother of Electra and Iphigenia. He is the central figure of the Oresteia, the trilogy of tragedies by Aeschylus, and appears in plays by Sophocles and Euripides. His story—avenging his father's murder by killing his own mother, and then being pursued by the Furies—is one of the foundational myths of Greek tragedy and law.

Myth

When Agamemnon returned from the Trojan War, he was murdered by Clytemnestra and her lover Aegisthus. The young Orestes was smuggled to safety in Phocis. Grown to manhood, he consulted the oracle of Apollo at Delphi, which commanded him to avenge his father. Accompanied by his friend Pylades, Orestes returned to Argos, was reunited with Electra at Agamemnon's tomb, and killed Clytemnestra and Aegisthus.

Trial and aftermath

For the crime of matricide, Orestes was tormented by the Erinyes (Furies) and driven mad. In Aeschylus's Eumenides he is tried on the Areopagus in Athens—the mythic origin of the homicide court—where Athena's casting vote acquits him, and the Furies are transformed into the kindly Eumenides. In other versions Orestes is purified in Delphi, or sent to steal the image of Artemis from Tauris, where he is nearly sacrificed by his sister Iphigenia before the two escape together. He later became king of Mycenae and Argos and married Hermione, daughter of Menelaus and Helen.
