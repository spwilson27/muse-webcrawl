Wave–particle duality

Wave–particle duality is the concept in quantum mechanics that every particle or quantum entity may be described as either a particle or a wave. It expresses the inability of classical concepts to fully describe the behaviour of quantum-scale objects.

History

Debates over whether light is a wave or a stream of particles date to Newton and Huygens. Einstein's 1905 explanation of the photoelectric effect established that light arrives in discrete quanta (photons), while interference experiments showed wavelike behaviour. In 1924 Louis de Broglie proposed that matter also has wave properties, predicting a wavelength inversely proportional to momentum; electron diffraction confirmed this within a few years.

Double-slit experiment

In the double-slit experiment, electrons or photons fired one at a time build up an interference pattern characteristic of waves, yet are detected as individual particles — the emblematic demonstration of duality. Modern quantum field theory reframes the duality: particles are excitations of underlying quantum fields.
