Moqueca

Type: Stew
Place of origin: Brazil
Region: Bahia and Espírito Santo
Main ingredients: Fish or seafood, tomatoes, onions, garlic, coriander, coconut milk and/or palm oil

Moqueca (IPA: [moˈkɛkɐ] or [muˈkɛkɐ]) is a Brazilian seafood stew traditionally cooked in a clay pot. It is made with fish or other seafood, tomatoes, onions, garlic, lime juice, and coriander, and is one of the emblematic dishes of Brazilian coastal cuisine, particularly of the states of Bahia and Espírito Santo.

Regional varieties

Two famous regional versions exist. Moqueca baiana, from Bahia, reflects Afro-Brazilian influence and is cooked with coconut milk and dendê (red palm oil), giving it a rich colour and flavour, and is often accompanied by pirão, a sauce thickened with manioc flour. Moqueca capixaba, from Espírito Santo, uses olive oil instead of palm oil and no coconut milk, and is coloured with annatto (urucum); it is traditionally prepared in a handcrafted black clay pot made by the paneleiras of Goiabeiras. A friendly rivalry between the two states over the "true" moqueca is a well-known part of Brazilian food culture.

Preparation

The stew is assembled by layering seasoned fish or seafood with sliced tomatoes, onions, and peppers, then simmering gently in the clay pot without added water, so the ingredients cook in their own juices along with the added oils and coconut milk where used. It is typically served with white rice and farofa.

Origins

The dish's roots combine Indigenous Brazilian cooking techniques—seafood stewed in clay vessels—with Portuguese and African influences. The name is thought to derive from Indigenous Tupi terms related to cooking or stewing.
