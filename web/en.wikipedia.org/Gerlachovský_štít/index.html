Gerlachovský štít (Slovak pronunciation: [ˈɡerlaxɔʋskiː ˈʃciːt]; German: Gerlsdorfer Spitze; Hungarian: Gerlachfalvi-csúcs) is the highest peak in the High Tatra mountains and in the whole 1,500 km (930 mi) long Carpathian mountain chain, as well as the highest point of Slovakia. Rising to 2,654.4 metres (8,709 ft) above sea level, it dominates the skyline above the town of Gerlachov, from which it takes its name.

Geography

The mountain lies within Tatra National Park in northern Slovakia, near the border with Poland. Its massive, rugged summit pyramid — with faces rising above the Velická and Batizovská valleys — is one of the most recognisable profiles in the Tatras. Long thought to be lower than neighbouring Lomnický štít, Gerlach was confirmed as the range's highest summit by surveys in the nineteenth century.

Names and history

The peak has carried names reflecting the region's shifting rulers: it was called Franz-Joseph-Spitze under Austria-Hungary, and in the twentieth century was successively renamed for legionnaires, then as Stalin Peak (1949–1959) under Czechoslovakia's communist government, before reverting to Gerlachovský štít. The first recorded ascent was made in 1834 by Ján Still, a schoolteacher from Nová Lesná, with companions; the first winter ascent followed in 1875.

Climbing

Because the normal routes involve exposed scrambling and the summit area is a protected nature reserve, ascent of Gerlachovský štít is permitted only with a certified mountain guide, or for members of national mountaineering associations on specified routes. The most popular guided route ascends via the Velická valley and is secured in places with chains; other routes, such as the Tatarka and Martin routes, are classic Tatra climbs. On clear days the summit offers views across much of Slovakia and southern Poland.
