Hodge theory


The Hodge diamond of a K3 surface

Hodge theory is a branch of mathematics that studies the topology of smooth manifolds using differential forms and partial differential equations. Developed principally by W. V. D. Hodge in the 1930s and 1940s, it shows that on a compact oriented Riemannian manifold every de Rham cohomology class has a unique harmonic representative. For complex algebraic varieties, Hodge theory refines cohomology into a "Hodge structure", yielding powerful constraints linking topology, complex geometry and arithmetic.

Harmonic forms and the Hodge theorem

Given a Riemannian metric, one defines the Hodge star operator and the Laplacian Δ on differential forms. A form is harmonic if Δω = 0. The Hodge theorem states that each de Rham cohomology class on a compact oriented manifold contains exactly one harmonic representative, so cohomology is isomorphic to the space of harmonic forms. Consequences include finite-dimensionality of cohomology and Poincaré duality, via the pairing of harmonic forms. The theory extends to the Hodge–de Rham spectral sequence and, for Kähler manifolds, the ∂∂̄-lemma.

Hodge structures

On a compact Kähler manifold, complexified cohomology decomposes into pieces Hp,q of (p,q)-forms, giving the Hodge diamond and Hodge numbers hp,q. For smooth projective varieties over the complex numbers these numbers are topological invariants with deep geometric meaning: the Hodge conjecture, one of the Millennium Prize Problems, asserts that certain rational cohomology classes (Hodge classes) come from algebraic cycles. Period domains, variations of Hodge structure (developed by Phillip Griffiths) and mixed Hodge structures (Pierre Deligne) generalise the picture to families of varieties and singular or open varieties.

Applications

Hodge theory is central to algebraic geometry (transcendental methods for algebraic cycles, the theory of abelian varieties), complex geometry, mirror symmetry, and mathematical physics, where Hodge structures appear in string theory compactifications. Non-abelian Hodge theory relates Higgs bundles, representations of the fundamental group and harmonic maps (work of Hitchin, Simpson and Corlette).
