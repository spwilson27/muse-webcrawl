Sunset Strip

The Sunset Strip is the 1.5-mile (2.4 km) stretch of Sunset Boulevard that passes through the city of West Hollywood, California. It extends from West Hollywood's eastern border with Los Angeles to its western border with Beverly Hills and is famous for its nightclubs, rock venues, billboards and entertainment history.

History

Because the Strip lay in unincorporated Los Angeles County, outside the stricter jurisdiction of the Los Angeles Police Department, it became a haven for speakeasies, casinos and nightclubs during Prohibition, and in the 1930s and 1940s for glamorous clubs such as Ciro's and the Trocadero frequented by film stars. In the 1960s it became a centre of the counterculture and rock scene; the 1966 "Sunset Strip curfew riots" inspired the Buffalo Springfield song "For What It's Worth". Clubs such as the Whisky a Go Go, The Roxy and the Viper Room made it a hub of rock and heavy metal in the 1970s and 1980s.

Landmarks

Notable venues and landmarks include the Whisky a Go Go, The Roxy Theatre, the Rainbow Bar and Grill, the Comedy Store, the Chateau Marmont hotel (just off the Strip), and the giant hand-painted and digital billboards that are a hallmark of the street. The Strip is also associated with the television series 77 Sunset Strip (1958–64), which took its name from an address on the street.

Present day

Since West Hollywood's incorporation in 1984, the Strip has been governed by the city, and has seen cycles of decline and upscale redevelopment with hotels, restaurants and residential towers, while retaining its role as a centre of Los Angeles nightlife and the annual Sunset Strip Music Festival tradition.
