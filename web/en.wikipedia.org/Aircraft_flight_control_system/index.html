Aircraft flight control system

A conventional fixed-wing aircraft flight control system (AFCS) consists of flight control surfaces, the respective cockpit controls, connecting linkages, and the necessary operating mechanisms to control an aircraft's direction in flight. Aircraft engine controls are also considered flight controls as they change the speed.

Primary controls

The primary flight control surfaces are the ailerons (controlling roll), the elevator (controlling pitch), and the rudder (controlling yaw). Secondary controls include flaps, slats, spoilers and trim systems. In early aircraft, surfaces were moved by cables and pulleys directly connected to the pilot's controls; larger and faster aircraft introduced hydraulic power assistance.

Fly-by-wire

Modern airliners and military aircraft increasingly use fly-by-wire systems, in which pilot inputs are interpreted by computers that command actuators, allowing control laws that improve stability, protect the flight envelope and reduce pilot workload. The Airbus A320 pioneered digital fly-by-wire in commercial service in 1988.
