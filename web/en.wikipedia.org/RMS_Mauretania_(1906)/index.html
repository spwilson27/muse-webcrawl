RMS Mauretania was a British ocean liner designed by Leonard Peskett and built by Swan Hunter on the River Tyne for the Cunard Line, launched on 20 September 1906. Sister ship to RMS Lusitania, she was for two decades the fastest ship in the world and one of the most beloved liners ever built.

Speed records

Powered by steam turbines driving four propellers, Mauretania captured the Blue Riband for the fastest transatlantic crossing in 1909 and held it for 22 years, averaging nearly 26 knots on her record passages — a tenure unmatched by any other liner. Her turbine machinery proved far more reliable than her sister's and became a landmark in marine engineering.

War service and later career

During the First World War she served as a troopship and as the hospital ship HMHS Mauretania. Returning to civilian service in 1919, she was converted to oil fuel, set further records in her later years, and undertook cruises in the 1930s as passenger tastes changed. She was withdrawn in 1934 and scrapped at Rosyth in 1935, amid public lament; fixtures from the ship survive in buildings in Bristol and elsewhere.
