Lithium iron phosphate battery

Cathode materialLiFePO4
Nominal voltage3.2 V
Specific energy~90–160 Wh/kg
Cycle life2,000–7,000+ cycles
Common namesLFP battery

The lithium iron phosphate battery (LiFePO4 battery) or LFP battery is a type of lithium-ion battery using lithium iron phosphate as the cathode material and a graphitic carbon electrode as the anode. LFP batteries are known for their safety, long cycle life, and low cost, offset by lower energy density than cobalt- or nickel-based lithium-ion chemistries.

History

LiFePO4 was identified as a cathode material by John Goodenough's research group at the University of Texas at Austin in 1996. Its olivine structure gives strong phosphorus–oxygen bonds that resist releasing oxygen at high temperature, making the material intrinsically more thermally stable than layered oxide cathodes. Early drawbacks — poor electronic conductivity — were overcome by carbon coating and particle-size reduction, enabling commercialization in the 2000s by firms such as A123 Systems.

Characteristics

  • Safety: highly resistant to thermal runaway; tolerates full charge and abuse better than NMC or LCO cells.
  • Longevity: typically 2,000–7,000 cycles to 80% capacity, and a calendar life exceeding ten years.
  • Flat discharge curve: voltage stays near 3.2–3.3 V for most of the discharge, complicating state-of-charge estimation by voltage.
  • Cost and materials: uses abundant iron and phosphate, avoiding cobalt and nickel; cheaper per cycle though lower in energy density (~15–25% below NMC).
  • Performance: good power capability and tolerance of high charge rates; performance falls in very cold conditions.

Applications

LFP dominates stationary energy storage and has become the standard chemistry for standard-range electric vehicles from manufacturers including Tesla, BYD, and Ford, as well as electric buses, marine and RV house batteries, solar storage, and power tools. BYD's "Blade Battery" popularized cell-to-pack LFP designs that recover much of the energy-density gap at pack level.
