Rat

Rats
Family: Muridae
Genus: Rattus (64 species)
Notable: Brown rat (R. norvegicus), Black rat (R. rattus)

Rats are various medium-sized, long-tailed rodents. Species of rats are found throughout the order Rodentia, but stereotypical rats are in the genus Rattus. The best-known species, the brown rat (Rattus norvegicus) and the black rat (Rattus rattus), originated in Asia but have spread worldwide following human trade and settlement, and now live on every continent except Antarctica.

Biology and behaviour

True rats have slender bodies, pointed snouts, prominent ears and long, sparsely haired tails used for balance and thermoregulation. They are predominantly nocturnal, omnivorous and highly adaptable, with keen senses of smell and touch. Rats are social animals living in colonies with dominance hierarchies, and they are noted for intelligence: they learn mazes, cooperate, and display empathy-like behaviour in experiments. Females breed prolifically, producing litters of up to a dozen pups several times a year.

Rats and humans

Wild rats are significant pests, consuming stored grain and spreading diseases including leptospirosis; their fleas historically transmitted bubonic plague. Conversely, domesticated fancy rats, descended from brown rats, are popular pets, and laboratory rats have been indispensable research animals in medicine, genetics and psychology since the 19th century — the Wistar and Sprague Dawley strains are standard models. Rats also feature in the cultures of Asia, as the first animal of the Chinese zodiac and as the vehicle of the Hindu god Ganesha.
