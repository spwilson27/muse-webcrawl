The Zapatista Army of National Liberation (Spanish: Ejército Zapatista de Liberación Nacional, EZLN), often referred to as the Zapatistas, is a far-left political and militant group in Mexico that controls a substantial amount of territory in the state of Chiapas. It is named after Emiliano Zapata, the agrarian revolutionary of the Mexican Revolution, and its members are mainly Indigenous Maya peoples.

History

The EZLN was founded clandestinely in the Lacandon Jungle in 1983 by a small group of urban guerrillas and indigenous communities. It emerged publicly on 1 January 1994 — the day the North American Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA) took effect, which the Zapatistas called a "death sentence" for indigenous Mexicans — with an armed uprising that seized San Cristóbal de las Casas and other towns in Chiapas. After twelve days of fighting, a ceasefire was declared; the government negotiated but never fully implemented the San Andrés Accords on indigenous rights signed in 1996.

Autonomous communities

In response, the Zapatistas built de facto autonomous self-governing communities, reorganized in 2003 as Rebel Zapatista Autonomous Municipalities with regional Juntas de Buen Gobierno ("Good Government Councils"), providing their own education, healthcare, and justice systems under the principle of mandar obedeciendo — "to lead by obeying." In 2023 the EZLN announced a further restructuring of its autonomous structures in response to organized-crime violence in Chiapas.

Influence

The movement, and its eloquent, pipe-smoking spokesperson Subcomandante Marcos, became an icon of the global anti-globalization movement, pioneering the use of the early Internet to mobilize international solidarity. The EZLN has since renounced seeking state power, instead advocating autonomous, participatory democracy and indigenous self-determination, and has hosted international gatherings including the "Little School" (Escuelita) for supporters.
