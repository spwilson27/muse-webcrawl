Holiday

A holiday is a day set aside by custom or by law on which normal activities, especially business or work, are suspended or reduced. The word derives from the Old English hāligdæg, "holy day", and many holidays began as religious festivals.

Public holidays are marked by festivals, parades and time away from work.

Types of holidays

Public holidays are established by law — national days, days of remembrance and major religious feasts — and usually close government offices, schools and many businesses. Religious holidays include Christmas and Easter in Christianity, Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha in Islam, Passover and Yom Kippur in Judaism, Diwali in Hinduism and Vesak in Buddhism. Observances such as Valentine's Day or Halloween are widely celebrated without time off work.

Secular and national celebrations

States commemorate independence days, constitution days and founders' birthdays; examples include the Fourth of July in the United States and Bastille Day (14 July) in France. International days, such as International Workers' Day on 1 May, cross national boundaries.

Holiday as leisure

In British English, "holiday" also means a period of leisure travel — the sense rendered "vacation" in American English. Statutory paid leave is guaranteed in most countries; the European Union mandates at least four weeks per year, while the United States has no federal minimum.
