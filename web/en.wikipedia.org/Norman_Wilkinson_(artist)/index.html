Norman Wilkinson (24 November 1878 – 30 May 1971) was a British artist who usually worked in oils, watercolour and drypoint. He was primarily a marine painter, but was also an illustrator, poster artist, and creator of the dazzle camouflage scheme used on ships during the First World War.

Life and work

Wilkinson was born in Cambridge and trained at the Portsmouth School of Art. He contributed illustrations to the Illustrated London News and produced celebrated poster designs for railway and shipping companies, including the London and North Western Railway and the London Midland and Scottish Railway.

Dazzle camouflage

In 1917, serving with the Royal Naval Volunteer Reserve, Wilkinson proposed painting ships in bold, contradictory patterns of colour and geometry so as to confuse enemy submarine commanders about a vessel's course, speed and type. The scheme, known as dazzle painting or razzle dazzle, was widely adopted by the Royal Navy and the United States Navy.

Later years

Wilkinson became president of the Royal Institute of Painters in Water Colours and was appointed CBE. He recorded his life in the autobiography A Brush with Life (1969).
