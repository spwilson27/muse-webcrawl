Ceviche

Ceviche is a dish made from fresh raw fish or seafood cured in citrus juices, typically lime or lemon, and seasoned with chilli peppers, onions, salt, and herbs such as coriander. It is popular throughout coastal Latin America and is the national dish of Peru.

Origins and Peruvian ceviche

The dish has pre-Hispanic roots on the Peruvian coast, where fish was cured with local fruits and chilli. Modern Peruvian ceviche is usually prepared fresh to order with sea bass or similar fish, red onion, ají chilli, and the marinade known as leche de tigre, served with sweet potato and corn.

Regional variations

Ecuadorian ceviche is often made with shrimp in a tomato-rich broth; Mexican versions may include tomato, avocado, and olives; and variations throughout the Pacific coast and Caribbean use coconut milk or different seafood.
