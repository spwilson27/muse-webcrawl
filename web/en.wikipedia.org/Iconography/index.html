Iconography

Iconography, as a branch of art history, studies the identification, description and interpretation of the content of images: the subjects depicted, the particular compositions and details used to do so, and other elements distinct from artistic style. The word comes from the Greek eikōn ("image") and graphein ("to write" or "draw").

Method

Erwin Panofsky formalised a three-level method: pre-iconographical description (primary subject matter), iconographical analysis (conventional subject matter, such as identifying a figure as a specific saint by attributes), and iconological interpretation (the deeper cultural meaning). Iconography draws on texts, myths, religious doctrine and pattern books used by artists and patrons.

Applications

Iconographic analysis is applied to religious art (Christian icons, Hindu and Buddhist imagery), portraits (attributes, dress and symbols of status), political imagery and even modern media. Reference works such as the Iconclass system in the Netherlands catalogue iconographic subjects systematically.
