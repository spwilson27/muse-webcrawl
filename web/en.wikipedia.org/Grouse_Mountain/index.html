Grouse Mountain

Grouse Mountain is a mountain in the Pacific Ranges of the Coast Mountains in the District of North Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada, overlooking the city of Vancouver. With a summit elevation of about 1,231 metres (4,039 feet), it hosts one of the Lower Mainland's most popular ski areas and year-round tourist attractions, reached from the valley floor by the Grouse Mountain Skyride aerial tramway. It is nicknamed "The Peak of Vancouver".

History

The mountain was named by a party of hikers in 1894 after the sooty grouse found on its slopes. A toll road and lodge followed in the 1920s, and the first chairlift was built in 1949. The original Skyride gondola opened in 1966; the larger current tram, carrying 100 passengers, opened in 1976. The resort is privately owned and operates throughout the year.

Attractions

Summer attractions include lumberjack shows, a wildlife refuge for orphaned grizzly bears, the Eye of the Wind wind turbine with a viewing pod, and paragliding. The Grouse Grind is a steep 2.5-kilometre hiking trail climbing about 800 metres up the mountainside, sometimes called "Mother Nature's Stairmaster". In winter the mountain offers skiing, snowboarding, snowshoeing and ice skating, lit for night skiing and visible across Vancouver.

Access

The Skyride departs from Nancy Greene Way in North Vancouver, which is served by public transit from downtown Vancouver via the SeaBus and bus connections.
