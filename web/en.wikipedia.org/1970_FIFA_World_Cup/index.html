1970 FIFA World Cup

The 1970 FIFA World Cup was the ninth edition of the FIFA World Cup, held in Mexico from 31 May to 21 June 1970. It was the first World Cup held outside Europe and South America, and the first to be broadcast in colour. Brazil won the tournament, defeating Italy 4–1 in the final at the Estadio Azteca in Mexico City, and their attacking team — featuring Pelé, Jairzinho, Gérson, Tostão, and Rivellino — is widely regarded as one of the greatest in football history.

The Estadio Azteca, venue of the final.

Tournament summary

Sixteen teams competed. Brazil won all six of their matches, and Jairzinho scored in every game — a feat unmatched since. The semi-final between Italy and West Germany at the Azteca, won 4–3 by Italy after extra time with five goals in the added period, is known as the "Game of the Century". Gerd Müller of West Germany was the tournament's top scorer with 10 goals. The tournament introduced substitutions (two per team) and red and yellow cards, though no player was sent off; it was also the first World Cup to use a ball named for television, the Adidas Telstar.

Final

In the final on 21 June 1970, Pelé opened the scoring with a header; Roberto Boninsegna equalised for Italy. In the second half Gérson and Jairzinho put Brazil ahead, and captain Carlos Alberto finished a flowing team move for the fourth goal — one of the most celebrated in World Cup history. As three-time winners, Brazil permanently retained the Jules Rimet Trophy, which had to be replaced by the current FIFA World Cup Trophy in 1974.

Legacy

The 1970 tournament is remembered as a landmark in the globalisation of football: colour television brought the World Cup to a mass worldwide audience, and Brazil's style of play set a lasting benchmark for attacking football.
