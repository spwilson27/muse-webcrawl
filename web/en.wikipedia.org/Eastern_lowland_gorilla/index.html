The eastern lowland gorilla (Gorilla beringei graueri), also known as Grauer's gorilla, is a subspecies of the eastern gorilla endemic to the lowland tropical rainforests of the eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo. It is the largest of the four gorilla subspecies, with stockier build and broader hands than the mountain gorilla.

Description and behaviour

Adult males (silverbacks) may weigh around 200 kg in the wild, with jet-black coats that grey with age. Groups typically consist of a dominant silverback, several females and their offspring. The diet is mainly fruit and vegetation, varying seasonally, and groups range widely in search of food, building fresh nests each night.

Distribution and habitat

Important strongholds include Kahuzi-Biega and Maiko National Parks and the Itombwe Massif. The subspecies occupies lowland forest up to montane elevations, at densities lower than mountain gorillas in the Virungas.

Conservation

Surveys in the 1990s estimated around 17,000 individuals; by 2015 the population had fallen catastrophically — declines of more than 75% in a generation were reported in a 2016 assessment — leading the IUCN to list Grauer's gorilla as Critically Endangered. The collapse is attributed to poaching for bushmeat, habitat destruction and fragmentation linked to civil conflict and artisanal mining for coltan and gold in the region.
