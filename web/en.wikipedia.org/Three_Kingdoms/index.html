The Three Kingdoms (220–280 AD) was a tripartite division of China among the dynastic states of Cao Wei, Shu Han and Eastern Wu that succeeded the collapsed Han dynasty. It remains one of the most celebrated and romanticised eras in Chinese history.

History

As Han authority collapsed amid the Yellow Turban Rebellion and warlordism, the warlord Cao Cao unified the north; his rivals Liu Bei and Sun Quan held the southwest and southeast. The decisive Battle of Red Cliffs (208) prevented Cao Cao from conquering the south. In 220 Cao Pi forced the last Han emperor to abdicate and founded Wei; Liu Bei and Sun Quan proclaimed rival dynasties. Famed strategists and generals — Zhuge Liang, Sima Yi, Guan Yu, Zhou Yu — became legendary figures.

Reunification

Wei conquered Shu in 263; the Sima family then usurped Wei to found the Jin dynasty (266), which conquered Wu in 280, reuniting China after a century of division. Warfare and famine reduced the registered population drastically compared with Han census figures.

Cultural legacy

The period is immortalised in Chen Shou's historical Records of the Three Kingdoms and especially in Luo Guanzhong's 14th-century novel Romance of the Three Kingdoms, one of China's Four Great Classical Novels, which spawned countless operas, games, films and television series.
