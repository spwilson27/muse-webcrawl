Serjeant-at-arms

A serjeant-at-arms (or sergeant-at-arms) is an officer appointed by a deliberative body — such as a parliament, legislature, court or guild — to keep order, enforce its rules and carry out its orders, including arresting disorderly persons. In the United Kingdom, the Serjeant at Arms of the House of Commons carries the ceremonial mace and is responsible for security and access to the chamber.

History

The office dates to medieval England, where serjeants-at-arms formed a royal bodyguard established by Richard I. Parliamentary serjeants developed from the 15th century; the Commons' serjeant famously attempted, with the Speaker, to resist Charles I's arrest of the Five Members in 1642. Similar offices exist in the US Senate and House of Representatives and in Commonwealth legislatures.
