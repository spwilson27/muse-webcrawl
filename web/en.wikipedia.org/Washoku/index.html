Washoku

Washoku (Japanese: 和食) is the traditional dietary culture of the Japanese, centered on rice served with miso soup and a variety of seasonal side dishes. In 2013 it was inscribed on the UNESCO Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, recognized as a social practice based on a set of skills, knowledge, and traditions related to the production, preparation, and consumption of food.

Principles

Washoku is guided by principles of balance and harmony: the use of fresh, seasonal ingredients (shun); an emphasis on the natural flavors of ingredients; attention to color, texture, and presentation; and the standard meal structure of ichijū-sansai — one soup and three dishes served with rice and pickles. Fundamental seasonings include soy sauce, miso, sake, mirin, and dashi stock, which provides umami.

Cultural significance

The practice is closely tied to seasonal festivals and to New Year celebrations, when special dishes called osechi-ryōri are prepared and shared. UNESCO's inscription emphasizes washoku's role in strengthening social bonds and expressing respect for nature and the sustainable use of natural resources.
