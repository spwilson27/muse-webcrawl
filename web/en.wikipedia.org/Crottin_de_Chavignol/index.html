Crottin de Chavignol

Crottin de Chavignol is a French goat's cheese produced in and around the village of Chavignol in the Loire Valley, near Sancerre in the department of Cher. Made from raw goat's milk, it is one of the best known of the small cylindrical goat cheeses of the Berry region and has held Appellation d'Origine Contrôlée (AOC) status since 1976, now protected under the EU's PDO scheme.

Production and character

The cheese is made in small drums weighing about 60 grams. Young crottins, aged around ten days, are soft, white, and mild; with further maturing over several weeks they shrink, harden, and develop a wrinkled, sometimes blue-moulded rind and a stronger, nuttier, distinctly goaty flavour. The name "crottin" derives, somewhat unappetisingly, from a local word for horse or goat dung, a reference to the dark, hard appearance of well-aged cheeses.

Serving

Crottin de Chavignol is famously paired with the Sauvignon Blanc wines of Sancerre, produced in the same area — a classic example of local food-and-wine matching. It is eaten at every stage of maturity, and the firmer cheeses are often grilled and served warm on salads (salade de chèvre chaud).
