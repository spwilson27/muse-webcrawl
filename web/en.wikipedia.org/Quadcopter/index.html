Quadcopter

A quadcopter, also called a quadrotor helicopter or quadrotor, is a multirotor helicopter that is lifted and propelled by four rotors. Quadcopters are classified as rotorcraft, as opposed to fixed-wing aircraft, because their lift is generated by a set of rotors (vertically oriented propellers). Unlike most helicopters, quadcopters use two sets of identical fixed-pitch propellers; two clockwise (CW) and two counterclockwise (CCW). These use variation of RPM to control lift and torque.

Flight control

Control of vehicle motion is achieved by altering the rotation rate of one or more rotors, changing its torque load and thrust/lift characteristics. An onboard flight controller uses inertial sensors (gyroscopes and accelerometers) to stabilise the craft many times per second, making the quadcopter far easier to fly than an equivalent conventional helicopter. Early designs in the 1920s and 1950s were piloted full-size machines, but the configuration only became widespread with small unmanned aerial vehicles once lightweight electronic stabilisation became available.

Applications

Quadcopters are the most common configuration for small drones, used for aerial photography and videography, racing, research, agriculture, inspection and delivery experiments. Companies such as DJI popularised folding consumer camera quadcopters, while the same layout is used in classroom robotics and in swarm research. Limitations include short endurance from battery energy density and sensitivity to wind compared with fixed-wing designs.
