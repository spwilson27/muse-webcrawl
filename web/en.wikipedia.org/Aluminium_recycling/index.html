Aluminium recycling

Aluminium recycling is the process by which scrap aluminium is reprocessed into products for reuse. Recycling aluminium requires only about 5 percent of the energy needed to produce the metal from bauxite ore, making it one of the most economically and environmentally attractive materials to recycle. Aluminium can be recycled repeatedly without loss of quality.

Process

Scrap is collected and sorted, often using eddy-current separators and, increasingly, laser-induced breakdown spectroscopy to distinguish alloys. The sorted scrap is cleaned, shredded and decoated to remove paints and lacquers, then melted in reverberatory furnaces. The molten metal is treated to remove impurities and adjust alloy composition, then cast into ingots, rolling slabs or extrusion billets.

Advantages

Because primary aluminium production via the Hall–Héroult process is extremely electricity-intensive, recycling saves roughly 95 percent of that energy and avoids most associated carbon emissions and bauxite mining. Beverage cans are the most visibly recycled product: in efficient deposit systems, a can may return to shelves as a new can within about 60 days. Roughly three quarters of all aluminium ever produced is estimated to still be in use.
