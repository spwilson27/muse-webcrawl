Flat racing

Flat racing is a form of horse racing run on a level racecourse over a predetermined distance. It differs from jump racing, in which horses negotiate hurdles or fences, and from harness racing. Flat races are the most commercially significant branch of the sport worldwide.

The sport

Races are run over distances from about 5 furlongs (1,000 m) sprints to staying races of 2 miles (3,200 m) and beyond, on turf or synthetic ("all-weather") surfaces. Most flat racehorses are Thoroughbreds, and the sport underpins the global bloodstock breeding industry.

Major races

Famous flat races include the Epsom Derby, the Prix de l'Arc de Triomphe, the Kentucky Derby, the Melbourne Cup, and the Japan Cup. In Britain, the flat season traditionally runs from spring to autumn and includes the five "Classics" for three-year-olds.

Handicapping and weights

Horses carry weights assigned according to age, sex, and, in handicap races, their official ratings, with the aim of equalising each runner's chance. Jockeys must be light enough to ride at the assigned weights while retaining strength and balance.
