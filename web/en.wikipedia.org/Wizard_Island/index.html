Wizard Island

LocationCrater Lake, Oregon, United States
Elevation2,114 m (6,933 ft)
TypeCinder cone
Area315 acres (127 ha)

Wizard Island is a volcanic cinder cone that forms an island at the western end of Crater Lake in Crater Lake National Park, Oregon. Rising about 763 feet (233 m) above the lake's surface, it is the largest of the lake's islands and a prominent landmark visible from the caldera rim.

Geology

The island formed after the climactic eruption of Mount Mazama roughly 7,700 years ago created the caldera that filled with water to become Crater Lake. Wizard Island's cone is one of the youngest features in the caldera, built by eruptions that began within a few hundred years of the lake's formation; its crater, nicknamed the "Witch's Cauldron", is about 90 feet deep. Geologist William Gladstone Steel named it in 1885 for its resemblance to a wizard's hat.

Access

During the summer season, tour boats circle the lake and stop at the island, where visitors can hike a trail to the summit or fish. The island's slopes support stands of pine and fir that survived with limited human disturbance.
