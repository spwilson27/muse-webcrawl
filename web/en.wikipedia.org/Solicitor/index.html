Solicitor

A solicitor advising a client

A solicitor is a legal practitioner who traditionally deals with most of the legal matters in some jurisdictions, particularly those following the English common law tradition, such as the United Kingdom, Ireland, Australia, and Hong Kong. In these systems the legal profession is split: solicitors handle client-facing work — advice, conveyancing, wills, contracts, and litigation preparation — while barristers specialise in advocacy before the courts.

Role and work

Solicitors are usually a client's first point of contact with the law. They conduct much of their work outside court: drafting documents, negotiating settlements, administering estates, transferring property (conveyancing), and advising businesses. Where a case requires a court hearing, a solicitor may instruct a barrister to represent the client, although solicitors may also obtain higher rights of audience and act as solicitor advocates.

Qualification

In England and Wales, qualification is overseen by the Solicitors Regulation Authority. The route traditionally involved a law degree (or conversion course), the Legal Practice Course, and a two-year training contract; from 2021 the Solicitors Qualifying Examination (SQE) introduced a standardised assessment route. Solicitors are represented professionally by the Law Society.

Other uses of the term

In government, "solicitor" titles survive in offices such as the Solicitor General. In the United States the term is used mainly for municipal attorneys ("city solicitor") and for the federal Solicitor General, and historically in some states (e.g. South Carolina) for district prosecutors.
