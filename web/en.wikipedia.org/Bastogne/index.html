Bastogne (German and Luxembourgish: Bastnach; Dutch: Bastenaken) is a municipality and town in the province of Luxembourg in the Ardennes region of Wallonia, Belgium, near the border with Luxembourg. It lies on a ridge in the Ardennes plateau and is crossed by the important road network centred on its market square.

The Mardasson Memorial commemorates the American soldiers of 1944–45.

History

Bastogne became famous during the Second World War. In December 1944, during Germany's Ardennes offensive (the Battle of the Bulge), the town — a key road junction — was defended by the US 101st Airborne Division under Brigadier General Anthony McAuliffe, supported by elements of other units. Encircled and ordered to surrender on 22 December, McAuliffe replied with the famous one-word answer "NUTS!", and the defenders held until relieved by Patton's Third Army on 26 December. The Mardasson Memorial and the Bastogne War Museum commemorate the siege.

Sport

Bastogne is a regular fixture of the Liège–Bastogne–Liège cycling classic, one of the five Monuments of professional road cycling, in which the town is the turning point of the out-and-back course.
