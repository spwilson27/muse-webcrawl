Row and column spaces


The column space is the span of a matrix's columns

In linear algebra, the column space of a matrix A is the set of all possible linear combinations of its column vectors — equivalently, the image (or range) of the linear transformation defined by A. The row space is the span of the matrix's row vectors, and equals the column space of the transpose AT.

Definitions

Let A be an m × n matrix with columns v1, …, vn in Rm. The column space C(A) is span{v1, …, vn}, a subspace of Rm. A vector b lies in C(A) exactly when the system Ax = b has a solution, so the column space describes the set of attainable outputs of the transformation. The row space is a subspace of Rn spanned by the rows of A.

Rank

A fundamental theorem states that the dimensions of the row space and column space are always equal; this common dimension is the rank of the matrix. Together with the rank–nullity theorem — rank plus nullity equals the number of columns — this gives the complete picture of the four fundamental subspaces: the column space and left null space in Rm, and the row space and null space in Rn.

Bases

A basis for the column space is obtained by reducing A to row echelon form: the columns of the original matrix corresponding to pivot columns form a basis. Row operations preserve the row space, so the non-zero rows of the echelon form themselves form a basis for the row space. Row operations do not preserve the column space, however, which is why the original pivot columns must be used for its basis.
