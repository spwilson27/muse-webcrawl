Sclareol

Clary sage (Salvia sclarea), the principal source of sclareol.

Sclareol is a fragrant diterpene alcohol found in clary sage (Salvia sclarea), from which it takes its name. It is a white to pale powdery solid with a sweet, balsamic, amber-like odour. Sclareol is used in perfumery and is an important industrial starting material for the synthesis of ambergris-type fragrance ingredients.

Production and uses

Sclareol is extracted from clary sage concrete obtained from the flowering tops of the plant, grown commercially in France, Russia, and elsewhere. Its best-known use is as the precursor of Ambrox (ambroxide), a synthetic substitute for ambergris that is one of the most valuable components of modern fine fragrances. Sclareol is also used directly as a fixative and fragrance ingredient.

Chemistry

Sclareol is a labdane-type diterpenoid with the molecular formula C20H36O2. It contains two hydroxyl groups and two carbon–carbon double bonds; degradation of its side chain yields sclareolide, another widely used fragrance material.

Biological activity

Laboratory studies have reported antibacterial, antifungal, and cytotoxic activities for sclareol, and it has been investigated for potential anticancer effects, though no medical use has been established.
