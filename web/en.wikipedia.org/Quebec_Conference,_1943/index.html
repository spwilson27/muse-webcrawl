Quebec Conference, 1943

Dates17–24 August 1943
LocationQuebec City, Canada
ParticipantsFranklin D. Roosevelt, Winston Churchill, William Lyon Mackenzie King
CodenameQuadrant

The First Quebec Conference, codenamed Quadrant, was a highly secret military conference held during World War II by the British, Canadian, and American governments. It took place in Quebec City, Canada, from 17 to 24 August 1943, at the Citadelle and the Château Frontenac. The principal representatives were Winston Churchill, Franklin D. Roosevelt, and host William Lyon Mackenzie King.

Decisions

The Combined Chiefs of Staff agreed to intensify the strategic bombing offensive against Germany, to continue building up forces in Britain for the invasion of France — Operation Overlord was approved with a target date of 1 May 1944 under a still-to-be-named (ultimately American) supreme commander — and to increase pressure on Italy, whose government was collapsing after Mussolini's fall. In the Pacific, the conference adopted plans to bypass and neutralize Japanese strongholds such as Rabaul, and approved Lord Mountbatten's appointment to South East Asia Command and operations in Burma. Reporting on the atomic bomb project (the Manhattan Project) was coordinated through the secret Quebec Agreement, signed by Roosevelt and Churchill on 19 August 1943, which merged the British Tube Alloys program with the American effort and pledged that the weapon would not be used against each other or third parties without mutual consent.

Context

The conference followed the Casablanca Conference (January 1943) and the Trident Conference in Washington (May 1943), and it preceded the Cairo and Tehran conferences of late 1943, where Stalin would press the Western Allies on the timing of the Second Front. Discussions at Quebec also touched on operations in the Mediterranean and the Balkans, where British and American preferences diverged.

Second Quebec Conference

A second conference (codename Octagon) met in Quebec in September 1944, dealing with the final phase against Germany, the Morgenthau Plan, and British participation in the Pacific war.
