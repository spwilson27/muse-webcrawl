Veterinarian

A veterinarian (vet) is a medical professional who practices veterinary medicine: the diagnosis, treatment and prevention of disease, disorder and injury in non-human animals. Veterinarians work with a wide range of species, from companion animals such as dogs and cats, to livestock, horses, zoo animals and wildlife.

Training and roles

Veterinarians typically complete a professional degree — such as the DVM (Doctor of Veterinary Medicine) in North America or BVSc in the United Kingdom and Commonwealth — followed by licensure. Many undertake further specialist training in fields such as surgery, internal medicine, cardiology, oncology, dentistry, or exotic-animal practice.

Fields of work

Beyond clinical practice, veterinarians play central roles in food safety and inspection, control of zoonotic diseases that can pass between animals and humans, public health, biomedical research, animal welfare enforcement, and wildlife conservation. Their work underpins the "One Health" approach linking animal, human and environmental health.

History

The first veterinary school was founded in Lyon, France, in 1761 by Claude Bourgelat; the Royal Veterinary College in London followed in 1791. The profession developed alongside the military need to care for cavalry horses and the agricultural need to control cattle plagues.
