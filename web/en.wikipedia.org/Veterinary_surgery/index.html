Veterinary surgery

Veterinary surgery is surgery performed on animals by veterinarians, whereby the procedures fall into three broad categories: orthopaedics (bones, joints, muscles), soft tissue surgery (skin, body cavities, cardiovascular system, gastrointestinal, urogenital and respiratory tracts), and neurosurgery. Advanced surgical procedures such as joint replacement (total hip, knee and elbow replacement), fracture repair, stabilization of cranial cruciate ligament deficiency, oncologic (cancer) surgery, herniated disc treatment, complicated gastrointestinal or urogenital procedures, kidney transplant, skin grafts, complicated wound management, and minimally invasive procedures (arthroscopy, laparoscopy, thoracoscopy) are performed by veterinary surgeons, as registered by the applicable board or college.

Specialization

Most general practice veterinarians perform routine surgeries such as neutering and minor mass excisions; those wishing to specialize complete additional training, typically a one-year internship followed by a three-year residency program, and board certification examinations administered by bodies such as the American College of Veterinary Surgeons or the European College of Veterinary Surgeons.

Anaesthesia

Anaesthesia in animals has many similarities to human anaesthesia, but some differences as well. Local anaesthesia is primarily used for wound closure and removal of small tumours, while general anaesthesia is used for more invasive procedures. Monitoring of vital signs under anaesthesia is a critical part of modern veterinary surgical practice.
