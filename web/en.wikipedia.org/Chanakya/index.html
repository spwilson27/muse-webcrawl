Chanakya

Chanakya (Sanskrit: चाणक्य, c. 375 – c. 283 BCE), also known as Kautilya or Vishnugupta, was an ancient Indian philosopher, economist, jurist and royal advisor. He is traditionally credited with authoring the Arthashastra, a treatise on statecraft, economics and military strategy, and with engineering the rise of Chandragupta Maurya to found the Maurya Empire.

Life and legend

According to Jain, Hindu and Buddhist traditions, Chanakya was a scholar at Takshashila (Taxila). After being insulted at the court of the Nanda king Dhana Nanda, he vowed to destroy the dynasty. He identified the young Chandragupta, trained him, and through a combination of alliances, espionage and warfare overthrew the Nandas, crowning Chandragupta around 321 BCE. He then served as chief minister and helped consolidate the empire, also defeating the successors of Alexander the Great in the northwest.

Works

The Arthashastra discusses taxation, administration, espionage, diplomacy and war in remarkable detail and is often compared with Machiavelli's The Prince, leading some to call Chanakya the "Indian Machiavelli" — though his work predates Machiavelli by some 1,800 years. The Chanakya Niti, a collection of aphorisms on practical wisdom, is also attributed to him.

Legacy

Chanakya remains a celebrated figure in India; the diplomatic enclave in New Delhi is named Chanakyapuri after him, and numerous institutions bear his name.
