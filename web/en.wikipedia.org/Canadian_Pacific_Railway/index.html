The Canadian Pacific Railway (French: Chemin de fer Canadien Pacifique), reporting mark CP, was a Canadian Class I railway incorporated in 1881. The railway was owned by Canadian Pacific Railway Limited, which began operations as early as 1868. The famous "Last Spike" completing Canada's first transcontinental railway was driven at Craigellachie, British Columbia, on 7 November 1885, fulfilling a promise made to British Columbia when it joined Confederation in 1871.

History

The construction of the railway across the Canadian Shield and the Rocky Mountains was an immense engineering undertaking involving tens of thousands of workers, including many Chinese labourers whose contributions were long underacknowledged. Under general manager William Van Horne, the line was completed years ahead of schedule. The completed railway bound the young Dominion together, enabled settlement of the Prairies, and served as a critical supply line in both world wars.

Beyond rail, Canadian Pacific built an empire of hotels (including the Banff Springs Hotel and Château Frontenac), steamships on the Atlantic and Pacific, telegraph services, and the airline that became Canadian Airlines.

Canadian Pacific Kansas City

In 2021, Canadian Pacific agreed to acquire Kansas City Southern, the smallest Class I railroad in the United States. Following regulatory approval by the U.S. Surface Transportation Board, the two companies merged on 14 April 2023 to form Canadian Pacific Kansas City (CPKC), the first single-line railway network linking Canada, the United States, and Mexico.
