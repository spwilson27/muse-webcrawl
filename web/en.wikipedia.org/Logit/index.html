Logit

In statistics, the logit function is the quantile function associated with the standard logistic distribution. It is the natural logarithm of the odds: for a probability p, logit(p) = ln(p / (1 − p)).

Properties

The logit maps probabilities in (0, 1) onto the whole real line: a probability of 0.5 gives 0, probabilities below 0.5 give negative values, and probabilities above 0.5 give positive values. Its inverse is the logistic (sigmoid) function.

Applications

The logit is the canonical link function in logistic regression, where the log-odds of the outcome are modelled as a linear combination of predictors. Differences of logits are log odds ratios, widely used in medicine and the social sciences. The function was introduced by Joseph Berkson in 1944 as an alternative to the probit model.

Related functions

Generalised linear models use other link functions such as probit and complementary log-log; in machine learning, logits are the raw, unnormalised scores produced by a classifier before a softmax or sigmoid transformation.
