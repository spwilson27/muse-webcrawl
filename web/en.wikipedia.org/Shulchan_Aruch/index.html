Shulchan Aruch

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Shulchan Aruch (Hebrew: שֻׁלְחָן עָרוּךְ, literally "Set Table"), sometimes called the Code of Jewish Law, is the most widely consulted of the various legal codes in Judaism. It was authored in Safed by Rabbi Joseph Karo in 1563 and published in Venice two years later. Together with its commentaries, it is the most widely accepted code of Jewish law.

Structure

The Shulchan Aruch follows the structure of Karo's larger work, the Beit Yosef, and the earlier Arba'ah Turim of Jacob ben Asher, dividing Jewish law into four sections:

  • Orach Chayim — laws of prayer, synagogue, Sabbath, festivals and fasts;
  • Yoreh De'ah — laws of kashrut, mourning, charity, and other ritual matters;
  • Even Ha'ezer — laws of marriage and divorce;
  • Choshen Mishpat — civil, financial and court law.

The Rema's glosses

Karo's rulings generally followed Sephardic practice. Rabbi Moses Isserles (the Rema), of Kraków, wrote glosses known as the Mappah ("Tablecloth") incorporating Ashkenazic customs and rulings. Printed editions present Karo's text with the Rema's glosses interleaved, and in this combined form the work became the authoritative code for virtually all Orthodox Jewish communities.

Influence

The Shulchan Aruch generated hundreds of commentaries, including the Magen Avraham, the Turei Zahav (Taz), and the Siftei Kohen (Shach), as well as later abridgements such as the Kitzur Shulchan Aruch. It remains the standard reference point for halakhic practice.
