Sadhu

A sadhu (Sanskrit: साधु, "good, holy") is a Hindu religious ascetic or mendicant who has renounced worldly life in pursuit of liberation (moksha). Female ascetics are called sadhvi. Sadhus are a familiar sight in India and Nepal, estimated to number in the millions, recognisable by their saffron, ochre or white robes — or, in some orders, ash-covered nakedness.

Life and orders

Sadhus belong to many sects and lineages, principally Shaivite orders (such as the Dashanami, founded in the tradition of Adi Shankara, and the Nath yogis) and Vaishnavite orders (such as the Ramanandi). Practices include meditation, yoga, celibacy, fasting, pilgrimage and living on alms; some perform severe austerities. Great gatherings such as the Kumbh Mela draw millions of sadhus, including the ash-smeared Naga babas who process with tridents and swords.
