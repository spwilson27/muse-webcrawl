The Next Day is the twenty-fourth studio album by the English musician David Bowie, released on 8 March 2013 through his Iso label in collaboration with Columbia Records. Announced on Bowie's 66th birthday after a decade of public silence following a heart attack in 2004, the album was recorded in near-total secrecy over two years in New York City with producer Tony Visconti and longtime collaborators.

Background and release

Bowie had largely withdrawn from public life after the Reality Tour ended abruptly in 2004. Recording sessions for The Next Day began in 2010, with participants sworn to secrecy. The album was announced without warning on 8 January 2013 alongside the single "Where Are We Now?", a melancholic reflection on Bowie's Berlin years that immediately topped download charts. The cover, designed by Jonathan Barnbrook, famously defaces the artwork of Bowie's 1977 album "Heroes" with a white square bearing the new title.

Music and themes

The album's songs address violence, fame, war, and mortality, from the mediaeval murder ballad "The Next Day" to the school-shooting narrative "Valentine's Day" and the celebrity autopsy of "The Stars (Are Out Tonight)." Musically it revisits the angular art rock of Bowie's late-1970s work while incorporating contemporary production.

Reception

The Next Day debuted at number one in many countries and number two on the US Billboard 200, Bowie's highest-charting American album at the time. Critics hailed it as a remarkable comeback, and it was nominated for the Mercury Prize and for Best Rock Album at the Grammy Awards. A deluxe companion EP, The Next Day Extra, followed in November 2013. The album was Bowie's penultimate release before Blackstar (2016).
