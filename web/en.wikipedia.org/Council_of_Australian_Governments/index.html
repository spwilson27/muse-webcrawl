The Council of Australian Governments (COAG) was the primary intergovernmental forum in Australia from 1992 to 2020. Its members were the Prime Minister of Australia, the premiers of the six states, the chief ministers of the two mainland territories, and the president of the Australian Local Government Association.

Role

COAG was established to increase cooperation among governments on reforms requiring national coordination, including microeconomic reform, competition policy, health, education, water, and energy. It operated by consensus and was supported by a system of ministerial councils and intergovernmental agreements, such as the Intergovernmental Agreement on Federal Financial Relations.

Abolition

In May 2020, Prime Minister Scott Morrison announced that COAG would be replaced by a National Cabinet, which had been formed in March 2020 to coordinate the national response to the COVID-19 pandemic. The National Cabinet continued as the main forum for federal–state relations.
