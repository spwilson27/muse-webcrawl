Karl Benz

Born: 25 November 1844, Mühlburg (now Karlsruhe), Germany
Died: 4 April 1929 (aged 84), Ladenburg
Known for: Benz Patent-Motorwagen, co-founder of Benz & Cie.

Karl Friedrich Benz (25 November 1844 – 4 April 1929) was a German engine designer and automotive engineer, generally regarded as the inventor of the first practical modern automobile — the petrol-powered Benz Patent-Motorwagen of 1885 — and the founder of the manufacturer Benz & Cie.

Life

Benz was born Karl Friedrich Michael Vaillant in Mühlburg, near Karlsruhe, the son of a locomotive driver who died when Karl was two. He studied mechanical engineering at the Polytechnic University of Karlsruhe, graduating at nineteen. After working for various engineering firms, he founded his own workshops in Mannheim in 1871 with his fiancée Bertha Ringer, whose dowry sustained the business through its difficult early years.

The Patent-Motorwagen

In 1879 Benz patented a successful two-stroke engine. On 29 January 1886 he filed the landmark patent (DRP 37435) for a three-wheeled "vehicle powered by a gas engine" — a single-cylinder four-stroke machine with electric ignition, differential gearing, and a purpose-built chassis, conceived as an integrated whole rather than a motorised carriage. In August 1888 his wife Bertha famously drove a Motorwagen some 106 km from Mannheim to Pforzheim without his knowledge — the first long-distance automobile journey — demonstrating the vehicle's practicality and greatly boosting sales.

Legacy

Benz & Cie. became one of the largest automobile manufacturers of its era. In 1926 it merged with Daimler-Motoren-Gesellschaft to form Daimler-Benz, whose cars carry the Mercedes-Benz name. Benz is commemorated by the Bertha Benz Memorial Route in Germany.
