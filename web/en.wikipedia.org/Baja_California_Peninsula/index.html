The Baja California Peninsula (Spanish: Península de Baja California) is a peninsula in northwestern Mexico. It separates the Pacific Ocean from the Gulf of California (Sea of Cortez) and extends about 1,247 km (775 mi) from Mexicali in the north to Cabo San Lucas in the south.

Geography

The peninsula is divided between the Mexican states of Baja California in the north and Baja California Sur in the south. A mountainous spine, including the Sierra de Juárez, Sierra San Pedro Mártir and Sierra de la Giganta, runs its length, and much of the interior is desert.

History

Indigenous peoples including the Cochimí, Guaycura and Pericú inhabited the peninsula for millennia. European contact began in the 1530s; for a time California was mapped as an island. Jesuit missions founded from 1697 onward, beginning with Loreto, anchored Spanish colonization.

Flora and fauna

The peninsula hosts distinctive desert ecosystems, including the boojum tree and cardón cactus forests, and is a major corridor for gray whale migration along the Pacific coast.
