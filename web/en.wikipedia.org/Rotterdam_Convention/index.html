Rotterdam Convention

Conference of the Parties to the Rotterdam Convention
Signed:September 10, 1998, Rotterdam
Effective:February 24, 2004
Parties:More than 160

The Rotterdam Convention (formally, the Rotterdam Convention on the Prior Informed Consent Procedure for Certain Hazardous Chemicals and Pesticides in International Trade) is a multilateral environmental treaty that promotes shared responsibility and open exchange of information in the international trade of certain hazardous chemicals. It was adopted in Rotterdam on September 10, 1998, and entered into force on February 24, 2004.

Purpose and mechanism

The Convention establishes a Prior Informed Consent (PIC) procedure. Chemicals listed in Annex III—substances that have been banned or severely restricted for health or environmental reasons by parties, as well as certain severely hazardous pesticide formulations—may be exported only with the prior informed consent of the importing party. Exporting parties must ensure that shipments do not occur against the import decisions published under the Convention, and all exports of listed chemicals require labelling and accompanying safety information. The treaty does not itself ban any chemical; it guarantees importing countries, particularly developing countries, the information and the right to decide which listed chemicals they wish to receive.

Institutions and related treaties

The Convention is administered jointly by the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) and the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), with its secretariat based in Geneva (with part located at FAO headquarters in Rome). The Conference of the Parties decides on adding chemicals to Annex III, considering recommendations from the Chemical Review Committee; listings—including long-debated proposals for chrysotile asbestos—require consensus, which has on occasion stalled additions. The Rotterdam Convention is one of the three "chemicals and waste" conventions, alongside the Basel Convention on hazardous waste movements and the Stockholm Convention on persistent organic pollutants, with which it shares joint secretariat functions and synchronised meetings since the 2010s "synergies" process.
