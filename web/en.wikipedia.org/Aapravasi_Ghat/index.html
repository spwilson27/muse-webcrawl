Built1849
FunctionImmigration depot for indentured labourers
UNESCO World HeritageInscribed 2006

Aapravasi Ghat (Hindi for "Immigration Depot") is a building complex in Port Louis, Mauritius, that served from 1849 as the depot through which indentured labourers arriving from India were processed before being sent to work on the island's sugar estates, or re-embarked for Réunion, Australia, southern Africa and the Caribbean. It is one of the earliest surviving sites associated with the global indentured labour system that followed the abolition of slavery in the British Empire.

History

Britain abolished slavery in 1834, and Mauritius became the testing ground for the "Great Experiment" of replacing enslaved labour with contracted Indian workers. The first 36 indentured labourers arrived on 2 November 1834 aboard the Atlas, a date now commemorated as a public holiday in Mauritius. The depot at Trou Fanfaron was built in 1849 and expanded over the following decades with kitchens, hospitals, stables and sheds for new arrivals. Nearly half a million Indian indentured labourers passed through the site; their descendants form the majority of Mauritius's population today.

Heritage

Only part of the original complex survives, including the stone stairway of fourteen steps that arrivals climbed from the landing wharf, now symbolic of the immigrant journey. The site was declared a national monument and was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2006. Its interpretation centre, the Beekrumsing Ramlallah Interpretation Centre, opened in 2014 and presents the history of indenture using the depot's surviving archives.
