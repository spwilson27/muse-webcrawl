Constitutional Court of Korea

The Constitutional Court of Korea in Jongno, Seoul.

The Constitutional Court of Korea is a specialised court of South Korea, established in 1988 under the Constitution of the Sixth Republic, that exercises judicial review and adjudicates constitutional disputes. Modelled in part on the constitutional courts of Germany and Austria, it sits alongside, rather than above, the ordinary court system headed by the Supreme Court.

Composition

The Court is composed of nine justices serving renewable six-year terms. Three are appointed by the President, three are elected by the National Assembly, and three are designated by the Chief Justice of the Supreme Court; all are formally appointed by the President. The President of the Constitutional Court is chosen from among the justices with the consent of the National Assembly.

Jurisdiction

The Court decides on the constitutionality of statutes at the request of ordinary courts or through constitutional complaints filed directly by citizens who claim that their fundamental rights have been infringed by the exercise or non-exercise of public power. It also has final authority over impeachments of senior officials, the dissolution of political parties, competence disputes between state agencies, and certain election-related matters.

Notable decisions

The Court gained prominence through a series of high-profile rulings. In 2004 it rejected the impeachment of President Roh Moo-hyun, holding that the violations found were not grave enough to justify removal. In 2014 it ordered the dissolution of the Unified Progressive Party. In March 2017 it unanimously upheld the impeachment of President Park Geun-hye, removing her from office. The Court has also struck down laws on adultery, restrictions on internet speech, and aspects of the National Security Act, while upholding capital punishment and the ban on conscientious objection at various points in its history, before the ordinary courts and legislation moved toward recognising alternative service.
