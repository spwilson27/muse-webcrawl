Ptolemy I Soter

Ptolemy I Soter (Ancient Greek: Πτολεμαῖος Σωτήρ, "Ptolemy the Saviour"; c. 367 – 282 BC) was a Macedonian Greek general, historian, and ruler under Alexander the Great who became king of Egypt and founder of the Ptolemaic dynasty. He was one of the Diadochi, the rival generals who fought over Alexander's empire after his death in 323 BC.

Career under Alexander

Ptolemy was a childhood companion of Alexander and one of his seven bodyguards (somatophylakes). He distinguished himself in the campaigns in Afghanistan and India, and Alexander trusted him with important independent commands.

Satrap and king of Egypt

Appointed satrap of Egypt in the Partition of Babylon (323 BC), Ptolemy famously diverted Alexander's funeral cortege and buried the conqueror's body first at Memphis and later at Alexandria, legitimising his rule. He fended off the invasion of Perdiccas, annexed Cyrenaica and Cyprus, and in 305/304 BC assumed the royal title, founding a dynasty that would last nearly three centuries.

Works and patronage

Ptolemy wrote a history of Alexander's campaigns, now lost but used by Arrian and regarded as a sober, reliable source. He founded the Library of Alexandria and Mouseion, commissioned the great Pharos lighthouse, and promoted the cult of Serapis. He abdicated in favour of his son Ptolemy II in 285 BC and died in 282 BC, the only Diadoch to die peacefully in his bed.
