A Jyotirlinga (Sanskrit: ज्योतिर्लिङ्ग, "lingam of light") is a devotional representation of the Hindu god Shiva. The word is a Sanskrit compound of jyotis ("radiance") and linga ("sign"). There are twelve traditional Jyotirlinga shrines in India, each regarded as a place where Shiva appeared as a fiery column of light; they are among the most sacred sites of Shaivism, and pilgrims undertaking the Jyotirlinga yatra seek to visit all twelve.

The legend

According to the Shiva Purana, Brahma and Vishnu once argued over supremacy. To settle the dispute Shiva manifested as an infinite pillar of fire whose ends neither god could find: Brahma, flying upward as a swan, falsely claimed to have reached the top; Vishnu, digging downward as a boar, honestly admitted failure. Shiva cursed Brahma to have no temples of his own and blessed Vishnu. The sites where the light-pillar appeared became the Jyotirlingas.

The twelve shrines

  • Somnath — Gir Somnath, Gujarat
  • Mallikarjuna — Srisailam, Andhra Pradesh
  • Mahakaleshwar — Ujjain, Madhya Pradesh
  • Omkareshwar — Khandwa district, Madhya Pradesh
  • Kedarnath — Rudraprayag, Uttarakhand, high in the Himalayas
  • Bhimashankar — Pune district, Maharashtra
  • Vishvanatha — Varanasi, Uttar Pradesh (the Kashi Vishwanath Temple)
  • Trimbakeshwar — Nashik district, Maharashtra
  • Vaidyanatha — Deoghar, Jharkhand (location also claimed at Parli, Maharashtra)
  • Nageshvara — near Dwarka, Gujarat (location disputed)
  • Rameshvara — Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu
  • Grishneshwar — near Ellora, Maharashtra

Significance and worship

The Sanskrit Jyotirlinga Stotra lists the twelve names, and reciting it is believed to confer merit. The shrines draw millions of devotees, particularly during the month of Shravana and on Maha Shivaratri. Several — Kedarnath, Somnath, and Kashi Vishwanath — rank among the very holiest sites in Hinduism, and the Jyotirlingas overlap with other sacred geographies such as the Char Dham and the Sapta Puri. Each temple preserves its own sthala-purana (local legend) explaining Shiva's manifestation there.
