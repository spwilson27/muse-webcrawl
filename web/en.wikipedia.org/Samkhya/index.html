Samkhya

Samkhya is one of the oldest schools of Indian philosophy.

Samkhya (Sanskrit: सांख्य, Sāṃkhya) is one of the six orthodox (āstika) schools of Indian philosophy. It is a strongly dualist system that distinguishes between consciousness, or Purusha, and matter/nature, or Prakriti, and it aims at liberation (moksha) through discriminative knowledge of the difference between the two.

History

Samkhya ideas have roots in the Upanishads and the epic Mahabharata, and are traditionally traced to the sage Kapila. The oldest surviving systematic text is the Sāṃkhyakārikā of Īśvarakṛṣṇa (c. 4th–5th century CE), a verse summary of 72 stanzas that became the school's authoritative exposition. Classical Samkhya is generally non-theistic, though later commentaries differ on the role of God.

Metaphysics

Prakriti, the primal materiality, consists of three gunas — sattva (clarity, lightness), rajas (activity, passion) and tamas (inertia, darkness) — in equilibrium. When the balance is disturbed, Prakriti evolves into 23 further principles (tattvas), including intellect (buddhi), ego (ahaṃkāra), mind (manas), the senses, and the gross elements. Purusha is pure, passive consciousness; suffering arises from misidentifying the self with the evolutes of Prakriti.

Influence

Samkhya provided the theoretical framework for Yoga philosophy, with which it is traditionally paired (Samkhya–Yoga), and profoundly influenced Ayurveda, the Bhagavad Gita, and later Hindu thought. Its enumeration of principles and guna theory remain central categories in Indian intellectual history.
