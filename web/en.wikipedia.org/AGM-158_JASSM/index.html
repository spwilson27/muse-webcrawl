AGM-158 JASSM

The AGM-158 JASSM (Joint Air-to-Surface Standoff Missile) is an American low-observable, air-launched cruise missile developed by Lockheed Martin. Carrying a 450 kg (1,000 lb) penetrating warhead, it is designed to strike high-value, well-defended targets from long range while keeping launch aircraft out of harm's way.

Development and variants

The JASSM programme began in the mid-1990s, and the missile entered United States Air Force service in 2009. The baseline AGM-158A has a range of about 370 km (230 mi); the extended-range AGM-158B JASSM-ER reaches roughly 925 km (575 mi). The AGM-158C LRASM is an anti-ship derivative developed for the US Navy. Launch platforms include the B-1, B-2, B-52, F-15E, F-16, and F-35.

Operational use

JASSM's first combat use came in April 2018, when US B-1 bombers fired nineteen missiles at Syrian chemical-weapons facilities. JASSM-ER missiles were also used in the 2019 raid that killed the Islamic State leader Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi. Export operators include Australia, Finland, and Poland.
