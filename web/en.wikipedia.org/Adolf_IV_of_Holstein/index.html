Adolf IV (before 1205 – 8 July 1261) was a Count of Schauenburg (1225–1238) and of Holstein (1227–1238), of the House of Schaumburg. He is best remembered for his victory over the Danes at the Battle of Bornhöved in 1227, which ended Danish supremacy in northern Germany and secured Holstein's independence.

Life

Adolf succeeded his father, Adolf III, in Schauenburg. After his release from Danish captivity, he joined a coalition of north German princes and cities — including Lübeck and Hamburg — against King Valdemar II of Denmark. At Bornhöved on 22 July 1227 his forces decisively defeated the Danes; legend holds that Adolf vowed during the battle to found a monastery if granted victory.

Abdication and legacy

In 1238 Adolf abdicated in favor of his sons John I and Gerhard I and became a Franciscan friar, later being ordained a priest in Rome. He founded the city of Kiel around 1242 (the privilege is associated with his sons' era, though tradition credits him) and is linked with the founding of Itzehoe and Oldenburg in Holstein. He died in 1261 at the Franciscan monastery he had endowed in Kiel.
