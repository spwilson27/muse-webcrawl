Forecasting

Forecasting is the process of making predictions based on past and present data. Most commonly, analysis of trends is used. A commonplace example might be estimation of some variable of interest at some specified future date. Prediction is a similar concept, but is more general; forecasting usually refers to time-series methods applied to planning and decision-making in business, economics, meteorology, and other fields.

A forecast extrapolates a time series into the future, with uncertainty intervals.

Methods

Quantitative methods include time-series models (moving averages, exponential smoothing, ARIMA), causal/econometric models such as regression against explanatory variables, and machine-learning approaches. Qualitative methods—used when historical data are scarce—include the Delphi method, expert judgment, market research, and scenario building. Forecasts are typically evaluated by accuracy measures such as mean absolute error and mean absolute percentage error, and good practice attaches prediction intervals expressing uncertainty.

Applications

Demand forecasting drives production, inventory, and supply-chain planning; financial forecasting informs budgeting and investment; weather forecasting applies numerical models of the atmosphere; and technology forecasting attempts to anticipate technological change. Research on judgmental forecasting, including Philip Tetlock's studies of expert prediction, has led to interest in structured aggregation and "superforecasting."
