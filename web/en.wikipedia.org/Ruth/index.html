Ruth

Ruth gleaning in the fields of Boaz, a classic illustration of the biblical story.

Ruth is the title character of the Book of Ruth in the Hebrew Bible and the Old Testament. A Moabite woman who married into an Israelite family, she is remembered for her loyalty to her mother-in-law Naomi, her marriage to Boaz, and her place as an ancestor of King David and, in Christian tradition, of Jesus.

The story

After the deaths of her husband and father-in-law in Moab, Ruth refuses to leave Naomi, declaring "whither thou goest, I will go." Returning with Naomi to Bethlehem, she supports them by gleaning grain in the fields of Boaz, a wealthy kinsman of Naomi's late husband. Boaz, moved by her loyalty and character, treats her generously and, following the custom of the kinsman-redeemer, marries her after a nearer relative declines. Their son Obed becomes the grandfather of David.

Significance

Ruth is one of two women with books of the Bible named for them (the other being Esther). She is celebrated in Judaism as a righteous convert — the Book of Ruth is read at Shavuot — and in Christianity as one of the four women named in Matthew's genealogy of Jesus. Her story is frequently read as a meditation on faithfulness (hesed), kindness to strangers, and the inclusion of outsiders.
