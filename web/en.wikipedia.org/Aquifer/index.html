Aquifer

Aquifer
DefinitionAn underground layer of permeable rock, sediment or soil that yields useful quantities of groundwater
TypesUnconfined (water-table), confined (artesian), perched
Major examplesOgallala Aquifer, Great Artesian Basin, Nubian Sandstone Aquifer

An aquifer is an underground layer of water-bearing permeable rock, rock fractures or unconsolidated materials (gravel, sand or silt) from which groundwater can be extracted using a well. Aquifers occur at various depths and are a crucial source of fresh water: groundwater from aquifers supplies drinking water to a large share of the world's population and irrigates a substantial fraction of its farmland.

Characteristics

An aquifer must be both porous (containing interconnected spaces that hold water) and permeable (allowing water to flow). Rock types that make good aquifers include sandstone, conglomerate, fractured limestone and unconsolidated sands and gravels; clay, shale and unfractured igneous rock are poor aquifers and may act as barriers called aquitards or, if effectively impermeable, aquicludes. The upper surface of the saturated zone is the water table.

Types

An unconfined aquifer is bounded below by a low-permeability layer and above by the water table, and is directly recharged by rain and surface water percolating down. A confined aquifer lies between two impermeable layers; water within it is under pressure, so when pierced by a well the water may rise above the aquifer's top — and if it flows to the surface, the well is called artesian. A perched aquifer is a small localised saturated zone held above the regional water table by a lens of impermeable material.

Recharge and depletion

Aquifers are recharged where precipitation or surface water infiltrates in a recharge zone. If extraction exceeds recharge over long periods, water tables fall, wells dry up, land may subside, and coastal aquifers can suffer saltwater intrusion. Some aquifers, such as the Nubian Sandstone Aquifer System beneath the Sahara, contain "fossil water" deposited thousands of years ago and receive negligible modern recharge, making their use effectively mining. The Ogallala Aquifer beneath the Great Plains of the United States and Australia's Great Artesian Basin are among the largest known aquifer systems.

Use and management

Groundwater pumping supports cities, industry and agriculture worldwide. Sustainable management requires monitoring of water levels, licensing of withdrawals, protection of recharge zones from contamination, and sometimes artificial recharge, in which surface water is deliberately directed into an aquifer for storage.
