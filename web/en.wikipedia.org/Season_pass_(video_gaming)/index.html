Season pass (video gaming)

A season pass is a video game monetisation model in which players purchase, usually at a discount relative to buying separately, a bundle of current and future downloadable content (DLC) for a game. The term is adapted from season tickets in sports and entertainment. Season passes became widespread in the 2010s with the growth of digital distribution, and are related to the battle pass, in which rewards are unlocked by playing during a limited-time season.

Marketing artwork for a typical season pass bundle

History

One of the earliest prominent examples was the "Rockstar Pass" for L.A. Noire (2011), followed by season passes for series such as Call of Duty, Battlefield, Borderlands and Assassin's Creed, typically bundling map packs or story expansions released over the year following launch. Publishers use passes to increase launch-window revenue and retain players between releases.

Battle passes

Popularised by Dota 2's Compendium and by Fortnite Battle Royale (2017–18), the battle pass instead sells a track of cosmetic rewards earned through play during a season, often with a free tier and a premium tier. Because progress is time-limited, battle passes encourage regular engagement; they have largely supplanted map-pack season passes in multiplayer games, since free maps avoid splitting the player base.

Criticism

Season passes have been criticised for asking players to pay in advance for unannounced or unfinished content, for fragmenting games' content between editions, and for "fear of missing out" pressures in expiring battle passes. Some publishers have moved to giving away gameplay content free and monetising cosmetics instead.
