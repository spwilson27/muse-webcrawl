Montmartre Cemetery

The Montmartre Cemetery (French: Cimetière de Montmartre) is a famous cemetery in the 18th arrondissement of Paris, France. Opened in 1825 in former gypsum quarries beneath the Butte Montmartre, it covers about 11 hectares and contains some 20,000 burial plots, making it the third-largest cemetery within Paris after Père Lachaise and Montparnasse.

Montmartre Cemetery
Location: 18th arrondissement, Paris
Opened: 1825
Size: c. 11 hectares (27 acres)

Setting

The cemetery occupies a hollow below street level, reached by stairs descending from the Avenue Rachel. The Pont Caulaincourt, a road bridge carrying the Rue Caulaincourt, crosses directly over its western portion. Shaded avenues of maple, lime, and chestnut trees wind between dense rows of family vaults and chapels, and the site is known for its population of semi-feral cats.

Notable burials

Reflecting Montmartre's artistic history, the cemetery holds the graves of many celebrated figures, among them the composer Hector Berlioz, the writers Alexandre Dumas fils, Stendhal, and Émile Zola (whose remains were later transferred to the Panthéon), the painter Edgar Degas, the film director François Truffaut, the singer Dalida, the dancer Vaslav Nijinsky, the physicist André-Marie Ampère, and Jacques Offenbach, composer of the can-can. The composer Hector Berlioz lies beneath a monument near the cemetery's main avenue.
