Antyeshti

Antyeshti, the Hindu funeral rites, traditionally conclude with cremation

Antyeshti (Sanskrit: अन्त्येष्टि, "last sacrifice"), also called Antim Sanskar, is the Hindu funeral rites, one of the sixteen samskaras (sacraments) marking the stages of life. The rites typically involve cremation of the body, prayers for the soul of the deceased, and subsequent ceremonies, and are intended to help the departed spirit transition to the next life in accordance with beliefs in samsara (reincarnation) and moksha (liberation).

The rites

Traditionally the body is washed, dressed, and placed on a funeral pyre, often at a cremation ground near a sacred river such as the Ganges at Varanasi. The chief mourner—usually the eldest son—circumambulates the pyre and performs the kapala kriya and lighting of the fire, accompanied by Vedic mantras, including verses from the Rigveda addressed to Agni, the fire god. Offerings and rituals symbolically return the body to the five elements.

After cremation

The ashes are collected and, after a period of mourning, immersed in a holy river (asthi visarjan). Subsequent rites include the pinda daan (offerings of rice balls to ancestors) and the shraddha ceremonies performed periodically for the deceased. Practices vary considerably by region, caste, and family custom; burial is customary for some communities and for ascetics and young children.

Significance

Antyeshti rests on the doctrine that the soul (atman) is immortal while the body is transient, and that properly performed rites assist both the deceased and the bereaved family.
