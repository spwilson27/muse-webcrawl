The Grand Union Canal in England is part of the British canal system. Its main line, the longest canal in the United Kingdom, runs 137 miles (220 km) from London to Birmingham with 166 locks, passing through the Chilterns, Braunston, and the Blisworth Tunnel. The canal in its present form dates from 1929, when the Regent's Canal, Grand Junction Canal, and the Warwick and Leicester lines were merged into a single company under the Grand Union Canal Company.

Route and branches

From Brentford on the Thames, the canal climbs through west London — passing Little Venice via the Regent's Canal arm — to Braunston, where the Oxford Canal branches south, and continues to Birmingham's Gas Street Basin. Arms serve Leicester (via the old Grand Union line up the Foxton staircase locks), Northampton, Aylesbury, Wendover, and Slough. Structures include the Iron Trunk Aqueduct at Cosgrove and the 2,813-metre Blisworth Tunnel, the longest wide navigable tunnel in Britain.

History and present day

The merged company modernised the route in the 1930s with government support, widening locks and dredging for larger barges, but traffic declined sharply after the Second World War; the severe winter of 1962–63 effectively ended regular commercial carrying. Today the canal is a major leisure waterway maintained by the Canal & River Trust, popular with narrowboaters, walkers, and cyclists.
