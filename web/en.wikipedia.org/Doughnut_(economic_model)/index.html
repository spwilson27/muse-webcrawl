Doughnut (economic model)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Doughnut, or Doughnut economics, is a visual framework for sustainable development — shaped like a doughnut — combining the concept of planetary boundaries with the complementary concept of social boundaries. It was proposed by Oxford economist Kate Raworth, first in a 2012 Oxfam paper and developed in her 2017 book Doughnut Economics: Seven Ways to Think Like a 21st-Century Economist.

The framework

The inner ring of the doughnut is the social foundation: twelve dimensions derived from the UN Sustainable Development Goals, including food, water, health, education, housing, energy, equality and political voice. Falling inside the hole means human deprivation. The outer ring is the ecological ceiling: the nine planetary boundaries identified by Earth-system scientists, such as climate change, biodiversity loss and freshwater use. Overshooting it means environmental degradation. The ring between them is "the safe and just space for humanity", where all people can thrive within the means of the planet.

Application

Rather than pursuing endless GDP growth, Doughnut economics asks economies to be "growth agnostic" and regenerative and distributive by design. The city of Amsterdam formally adopted the model in 2020 as a guide for post-pandemic policy, and "city portraits" applying the framework have since been produced for many other places.
