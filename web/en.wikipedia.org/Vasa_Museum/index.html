The Vasa Museum (Swedish: Vasamuseet) is a maritime museum in Stockholm, Sweden. Located on the island of Djurgården, the museum displays the only almost fully intact 17th-century ship that has ever been salvaged, the 64-gun warship Vasa, which sank on her maiden voyage in 1628. It is the most visited museum in Scandinavia.

The ship

Vasa capsized and sank in Stockholm harbour on 10 August 1628, barely 1,300 metres into her maiden voyage, because she was top-heavy and lacked sufficient ballast. She was located in the late 1950s by amateur researcher Anders Franzén and salvaged in 1961, remarkably intact thanks to the brackish Baltic water, which is inhospitable to shipworm. After years of conservation — spraying with polyethylene glycol to replace water in the timbers — the ship was placed in the purpose-built museum, opened in 1990.

Exhibitions

Around the ship, exhibits cover 17th-century shipbuilding, life aboard, the salvage operation, and objects recovered from the wreck, including sculptures, weapons, tools, and the remains of crew members.
