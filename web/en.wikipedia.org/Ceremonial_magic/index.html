Ceremonial magic

Ceremonial magic (also called ritual magic or high magic) is a broad category of rituals found in many esoteric traditions, characterised by ceremony, prescribed tools, and often the invocation of angels, spirits, or deities. It draws on grimoires, Hermeticism, Kabbalah, and alchemy.

History

Medieval and Renaissance grimoires such as the Key of Solomon set out elaborate procedures for summoning spirits. In the nineteenth century, figures such as Éliphas Lévi revived occult ritual, and the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn systematised ceremonial magic, influencing Aleister Crowley and later Western esotericism.

Practice

Practitioners may use ritual tools such as wands, swords, cups, and pentacles, robes, circles, and banishing rituals such as the Lesser Banishing Ritual of the Pentagram. Modern currents include Thelema, chaos magic, and various neopagan traditions.
