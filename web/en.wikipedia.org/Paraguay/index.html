Paraguay

Paraguay, officially the Republic of Paraguay, is a landlocked country in South America, bordered by Argentina to the south and southwest, Brazil to the east and northeast, and Bolivia to the northwest. It has a population of around 6.8 million, of whom nearly 2.3 million live in the capital and largest city, Asunción, and its surrounding metropolitan area.

The Paraguay River divides the country into two regions: the fertile, more populous eastern region (Paraguay Oriental) and the vast, semi-arid Gran Chaco plain in the west. Paraguay is unusual in the Americas for the strength of its indigenous culture: Guaraní is an official language alongside Spanish and is spoken by the great majority of the population, including many of non-indigenous descent.

History

Spanish conquistadors founded Asunción in 1537. Paraguay's early colonial history was shaped by the Jesuit missions (reductions) among the Guaraní. Independence from Spain came in 1811. Under José Gaspar Rodríguez de Francia and the López family, Paraguay was devastated by the Paraguayan War (1864–1870) against the Triple Alliance of Argentina, Brazil, and Uruguay, losing a large share of its population and territory. In the twentieth century Paraguay fought Bolivia in the Chaco War (1932–1935) and endured the long dictatorship of Alfredo Stroessner (1954–1989).

Economy

Paraguay is a major exporter of soybeans, beef, and electricity. The Itaipú Dam, shared with Brazil on the Paraná River, is one of the largest hydroelectric power stations in the world, and Paraguay exports most of its share of the electricity generated.
