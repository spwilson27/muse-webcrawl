Bayesian game

A Bayesian game is a game, in the game-theoretic sense, in which players have incomplete information about the other players — for example, about their payoffs or available actions. The framework was introduced by John Harsanyi in 1967–68.

The Harsanyi transformation

Harsanyi modelled incomplete information by giving each player a set of possible "types," with a common prior probability distribution over type profiles. A player's type determines their payoffs and beliefs about others' types, converting a game of incomplete information into one of imperfect information that can be analysed with standard tools.

Equilibrium

The central solution concept is the Bayesian Nash equilibrium, in which each type of each player plays a best response to the strategies of the other types, weighted by their beliefs. Bayesian games underpin auction theory, mechanism design, and the economics of information.
