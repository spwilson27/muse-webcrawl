Prince Fumimaro Konoe (12 October 1891 – 16 December 1945) was a Japanese politician who served as Prime Minister of Japan from 1937 to 1939 and again from 1940 to 1941. An aristocrat of the ancient Fujiwara lineage, he presided over Japan's escalation of the war in China, the formation of the Axis alliance, and the slide toward war with the United States.

Premierships

Konoe's first cabinet took office weeks before the Marco Polo Bridge Incident of July 1937 triggered full-scale war with China, which he proved unable or unwilling to restrain. Returning as premier in 1940, he founded the Imperial Rule Assistance Association to unify political life, signed the Tripartite Pact with Germany and Italy, and authorised the occupation of French Indochina. In 1941 he sought a personal summit with President Franklin D. Roosevelt to avert war, but the negotiations collapsed, and he resigned in October 1941, replaced by General Hideki Tojo.

Final years and death

During the last months of the Pacific War, Konoe worked for a negotiated end to the conflict and advised Emperor Hirohito on surrender. After Japan's defeat he briefly served in the first postwar cabinet, but when the Allied occupation authorities moved to arrest him as a suspected war criminal, he took his own life with cyanide on 16 December 1945, hours before he was due to surrender himself.
