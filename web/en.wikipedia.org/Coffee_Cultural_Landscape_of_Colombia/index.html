Coffee Cultural Landscape of Colombia

The Coffee Cultural Landscape of Colombia (Spanish: Paisaje Cultural Cafetero de Colombia) is a UNESCO World Heritage Site inscribed in 2011. It covers a region of the Colombian Andes where coffee has been grown for over a century, principally in the departments of Caldas, Quindío, Risaralda and northern Valle del Cauca — an area often called the Eje Cafetero or Coffee Axis.

Description

The site comprises six designated areas including 47 municipalities and hundreds of coffee farmsteads. It recognises the adaptation of coffee cultivation to steep mountain terrain, the distinctive architecture of the settlements — built with bahareque (bamboo and earth) construction and tile or zinc roofs — and a living culture centred on coffee, with its festivals, music, dress and cuisine.

Coffee culture

Coffee arrived in the region in the 19th century, and smallholder family farms (fincas) came to dominate production of washed Arabica coffee. The landscape reflects generations of farming knowledge and has become emblematic of Colombian national identity, symbolised by the fictional coffee grower Juan Valdez.
