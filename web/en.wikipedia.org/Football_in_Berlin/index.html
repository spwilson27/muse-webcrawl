Football in Berlin

The Olympiastadion, home of Hertha BSC and venue of the DFB-Pokal final since 1985.

Football in Berlin, the capital of Germany, has a history shaped by the city's size, its division during the Cold War, and its many clubs. Berlin's two best-known professional clubs are Hertha BSC, based in the west of the city, and 1. FC Union Berlin, based in Köpenick in the east. Their meetings are known as the Berlin derby. Berlin is also home to the German Football Association's cup final, which has been held permanently at the Olympiastadion since 1985.

History

Organized football reached Berlin in the late nineteenth century, and Berlin clubs such as BFC Viktoria 1889 were among the founders of the German Football Association (DFB) in 1900. Viktoria and other Berlin teams won early German championships. During the Third Reich, Hertha BSC played at the Olympiastadion, built for the 1936 Summer Olympics, which also hosted the 1974 FIFA World Cup matches and the 2006 FIFA World Cup Final.

After the Second World War, Berlin's division split its football. West Berlin clubs, including Hertha, played in the West German league system, Hertha being a founding member of the Bundesliga in 1963, while East Berlin clubs such as BFC Dynamo and Union Berlin played in the DDR-Oberliga. BFC Dynamo won ten consecutive East German titles from 1979 to 1988 amid controversy over state support and refereeing. Berlin's isolation also produced oddities such as Tennis Borussia Berlin's Bundesliga seasons and the "ghost games" atmosphere of a front-line city.

Clubs after reunification

After German reunification in 1990, the eastern clubs entered the all-German pyramid, most starting in lower divisions. Hertha BSC established itself as the city's principal Bundesliga club, winning the DFB-Ligapokal in 2001 and 2002 but no major championship. 1. FC Union Berlin rose through fan-driven rebuilding of its Stadion An der Alten Försterei, reached the Bundesliga for the first time in 2019, and qualified for the UEFA Champions League in 2023. The first top-flight Berlin derby between Hertha and Union took place in November 2019. Other historic Berlin clubs include BFC Dynamo, FC Viktoria 1889 Berlin, Tennis Borussia Berlin, and Tasmania 1900 Berlin, the latter remembered for its record-poor 1965–66 Bundesliga season.

Stadiums

The Olympiastadion (capacity about 74,000) is Germany's largest stadium built for athletics and football and hosts the DFB-Pokal final and Hertha BSC. Union Berlin's Stadion An der Alten Försterei holds about 22,000 and is famous for its standing terraces. The Friedrich-Ludwig-Jahn-Sportpark in Prenzlauer Berg has hosted BFC Dynamo and cup finals of the GDR era.
