Pecorino Romano

Pecorino Romano is a hard, salty Italian cheese made from sheep's milk (the Italian word pecora means "sheep"). It is one of Italy's oldest cheeses, described in Roman-era agricultural writings, and was a staple ration of Roman legionaries.

Production

Pecorino Romano holds protected designation of origin (PDO) status. Although named for Rome, the great majority of production now takes place in Sardinia, with the remainder in Lazio and the Tuscan province of Grosseto. The curd is cooked, pressed into cylindrical moulds, salted repeatedly over several weeks, and aged for at least five months; cheese aged eight months or more is sold as a grating cheese, while younger cheese is softer and milder.

Culinary use

Its sharp, salty flavour makes Pecorino Romano essential to Roman pasta dishes such as cacio e pepe, pasta alla gricia, bucatini all'amatriciana, and carbonara (traditionally, often mixed with Parmigiano-Reggiano). It is more pungent and saltier than Parmesan, which is made from cow's milk.
