Course of the sciatic nerve down the back of the leg

The sciatic nerve, also called the ischiadic nerve, is a large nerve in humans and other animals that begins in the lower back and runs through the buttock and down the lower limb. It is the largest and longest peripheral nerve in the human body, measuring about 2 cm in width at its origin.

Structure

The nerve arises from the lumbosacral plexus, from the ventral rami of spinal nerves L4 through S3. It leaves the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen, usually passing below the piriformis muscle, descends between the greater trochanter and ischial tuberosity, and runs down the back of the thigh deep to the hamstring muscles. In the popliteal fossa it divides into its two terminal branches: the tibial nerve and the common fibular (peroneal) nerve, though the two components are in fact bundled together from the start.

Function

The sciatic nerve supplies motor innervation to the muscles of the posterior thigh and, through its branches, to all muscles of the leg and foot. It provides sensation to much of the leg and the foot.

Clinical significance

Irritation or compression of the sciatic nerve roots causes sciatica, pain radiating from the lower back down the leg, most often due to a herniated intervertebral disc. The nerve's superficial position in the buttock also makes it vulnerable to injury from misplaced intramuscular injections.
