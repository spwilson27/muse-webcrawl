János Kornai (21 January 1928 – 18 October 2021) was a Hungarian economist known for his analysis and criticism of the command economies of Eastern European communist states. He is regarded as the most influential analyst of the socialist economic system and of the post-socialist transition.

Work

His early book Overcentralization in Economic Administration (1957), based on his experience as an economic journalist, was one of the first critical analyses of central planning published from within a socialist country. His magnum opus, Economics of Shortage (1980), argued that the chronic shortages of socialist economies were not planning errors but systemic consequences of the "soft budget constraint": state firms that expected to be bailed out faced no real financial discipline, generating insatiable investment hunger and hoarding.

In The Socialist System: The Political Economy of Communism (1992) he set out a general theory of the system's workings. After 1989 he advised on transition policy, favouring gradual, institutionally grounded reform, and later warned about illiberal tendencies and a "U-turn" in Hungary in works such as Dynamism, Rivalry, and the Surplus Economy (2013).

Career

Kornai worked at the Institute of Economics of the Hungarian Academy of Sciences and was professor at Harvard University from 1987 to 2002, thereafter professor emeritus at Harvard and at Corvinus University of Budapest.
