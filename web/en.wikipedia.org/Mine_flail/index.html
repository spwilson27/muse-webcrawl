Mine flail

A mine flail is a vehicle-mounted device that makes a safe path through a minefield by deliberately detonating land mines in front of the vehicle that carries it. It consists of a horizontal rotating drum fitted with many lengths of chain, at the ends of which are attached steel balls or hammers that thrash the ground as the vehicle advances.

History

The concept was developed during the Second World War, most famously in the British Scorpion flail tanks fitted to Matilda and later Sherman tanks (the Sherman Crab). Flails were used during the Normandy landings and the advance across Europe to clear lanes through German minefields. Modern flail vehicles, including remote-controlled systems, remain in use for humanitarian demining.
