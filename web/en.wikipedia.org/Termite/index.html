Termite

Termites
Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Arthropoda
Class: Insecta
Order: Blattodea
Infraorder: Isoptera
Families: 12 (about 3,100 species)

Termites are a group of detritophagous eusocial insects in the infraorder Isoptera, within the order Blattodea (alongside cockroaches). Often called "white ants", they are not ants; termites evolved from cockroach-like ancestors and are most closely related to wood-eating cockroaches of the genus Cryptocercus.

Social structure

Termite colonies have a caste system with a king and queen, sterile workers and soldiers, and seasonal winged reproductives (alates). Unlike ants, termite workers may be male or female, and the king remains with the queen for life. Colonies of mound-building species in Africa and Australia can number millions and their mounds regulate temperature and humidity with remarkable precision.

Ecology and significance

Termites feed on dead plant material — wood, leaf litter and soil organic matter — digested with the aid of gut symbionts. They are major decomposers and ecosystem engineers in the tropics, but some species are serious pests of buildings, crops and timber, causing billions of dollars of damage annually.
