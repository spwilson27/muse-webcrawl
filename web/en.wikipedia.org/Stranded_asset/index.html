Stranded asset

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A coal-fired power station of the kind at risk of becoming a stranded asset.

Stranded assets are assets that have suffered unanticipated or premature write-downs, devaluations or conversion to liabilities. The term gained prominence in climate policy, where fossil-fuel reserves and infrastructure risk losing value if climate policy, technological change or shifting demand prevents their full exploitation.

Climate context

The concept is central to the "carbon bubble" argument developed by the think tank Carbon Tracker from 2011: to keep warming below 2 °C, a large fraction of proven fossil fuel reserves — often estimated at 60–80 per cent — is "unburnable", so companies valuing reserves on the assumption of full extraction may be overvalued. Coal mines, pipelines, power plants and oil fields can likewise become stranded if they cannot operate profitably for their intended lifetimes.

Wider usage

The term applies beyond energy: shopping centres undermined by e-commerce, water utilities facing drought, or machinery made obsolete by regulation can all be stranded. Central banks and regulators, including the Bank of England under Mark Carney, have warned that stranded assets pose risks to financial stability and have promoted climate-related financial disclosure.
