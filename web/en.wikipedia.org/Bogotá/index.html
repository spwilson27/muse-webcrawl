Bogotá (Spanish pronunciation: [boɣoˈta]), officially Bogotá, Distrito Capital, abbreviated Bogotá, D.C., and formerly known as Santa Fe de Bogotá, is the capital and largest city of Colombia. It is a territorial entity of the first order and the administrative centre of the country, home to the executive, legislative and judicial branches of government.

Bogotá stands in the centre of Colombia, in a high plateau known as the Bogotá savanna, part of the Altiplano Cundiboyacense in the Eastern Cordillera of the Andes. At an average elevation of 2,640 metres above sea level, it is the third-highest capital city in South America. Its climate is cool and springlike year-round.

History

The area was inhabited by the Muisca people before the Spanish conquest. Gonzalo Jiménez de Quesada founded the city on 6 August 1538, naming it Santa Fe. Bogotá became the capital of the New Kingdom of Granada and later of the Viceroyalty of New Granada. After independence in 1819 it was capital of Gran Colombia and then of the Republic of Colombia. On 9 April 1948 the assassination of Jorge Eliécer Gaitán triggered the Bogotazo riots, a formative event in modern Colombian history.

Geography and climate

The city spreads north–south along the savanna, bounded to the east by the Cerros Orientales (Eastern Hills). The Bogotá River flows along its western edge. The climate is classified as subtropical highland / oceanic, with average temperatures around 14 °C and two rainy seasons.

Culture and transport

Bogotá is Colombia's cultural heart: the Gold Museum (Museo del Oro), the Botero Museum, the historic La Candelaria district and the Monserrate sanctuary overlook the city. Its TransMilenio bus rapid transit system, opened in 2000, carries millions daily; a first metro line is under construction. The weekly Ciclovía closes major avenues to cars for cyclists and pedestrians.
