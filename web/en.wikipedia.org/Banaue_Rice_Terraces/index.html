Banaue Rice Terraces

Location: Banaue, Ifugao, Cordillera Administrative Region, Philippines

Built by: Ifugao people

Elevation: ~1,500 m above sea level

The Banaue Rice Terraces (Filipino: Hagdan-hagdang Palayan ng Banawe) are terraces carved into the mountains of Banaue, Ifugao, in the Philippines by the ancestors of the Igorot people. Often called the "Eighth Wonder of the World", the terraces were built largely by hand over centuries — local tradition and tourism literature commonly date them to about 2,000 years ago, though archaeological research suggests much of the system expanded in the last 500 years, partly in response to Spanish colonisation.

Description

Rising to about 1,500 metres above sea level, the terraces are irrigated by an intricate system drawing water from cloud forests above. Stone and mud retaining walls follow the contours of the Cordillera slopes. It is often said that, laid end to end, the terrace walls would stretch halfway around the Earth. Terraces at Batad, Bangaan, Hapao, Kiangan and Mayoyao form part of the wider Rice Terraces of the Philippine Cordilleras, inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1995 — five component sites that do not, strictly, include the Banaue viewpoint terraces themselves.

Threats and preservation

The terraces face erosion, giant earthworm damage, drought, and abandonment as younger Ifugao leave for wage work; the Cordillera terraces were listed as World Heritage in Danger from 2001 to 2012. Restoration programmes, community tourism and organic native rice (tinawon) initiatives now support their maintenance, and the terraces appear on the reverse of the Philippine 20-peso note.
