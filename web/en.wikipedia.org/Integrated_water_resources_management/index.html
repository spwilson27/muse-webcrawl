Integrated water resources management

Integrated water resources management (IWRM) is a process that promotes the coordinated development and management of water, land and related resources in order to maximise the resultant economic and social welfare in an equitable manner without compromising the sustainability of vital ecosystems. It emerged as an international policy concept at the 1992 Dublin and Rio conferences on water and environment.

Principles

The Dublin principles hold that fresh water is a finite and vulnerable resource; water development should be participatory, involving users, planners and policy-makers at all levels; women play a central part in water provision and management; and water has an economic value in all its competing uses. IWRM typically organises management by river basin rather than by administrative boundaries.

Implementation and criticism

IWRM has been adopted in national water laws and in Sustainable Development Goal target 6.5, which calls for its implementation at all levels by 2030. Critics argue that the concept is vague, difficult to implement in practice, and sometimes a donor-driven template that neglects local politics and power relations over water.
