Quintus Fabius Maximus Rullianus

A Roman bust of the Republican era

Quintus Fabius Maximus Rullianus (or Rullo) was a Roman statesman and general of the gens Fabia, five times consul and twice dictator during the Samnite Wars of the late 4th and early 3rd centuries BC. He was the most celebrated member of his family before his descendant Fabius Maximus Cunctator.

Early career

Fabius first rose to prominence as magister equitum (Master of the Horse) to the dictator Lucius Papirius Cursor in 325 BC. Ordered not to engage the Samnites in his commander's absence, he nevertheless fought and won a battle near Imbrinium, and narrowly escaped execution for disobedience through the pleas of the army and people.

Consulships

As consul in 322 BC and repeatedly thereafter, Fabius campaigned successfully in Samnium and Etruria. His most famous victory came in 295 BC at the Battle of Sentium, fought alongside Publius Decius Mus, where the Romans defeated a coalition of Samnites, Gauls, Etruscans, and Umbrians — one of the decisive battles of the Third Samnite War.

Legacy

Fabius also served as censor in 304 BC, where he reformed the tribal enrolment of the landless urban population. The historian Livy treats him as one of the great commanders of the middle Republic, and his cognomen Maximus, "the Greatest", was inherited by later generations of Fabii.
