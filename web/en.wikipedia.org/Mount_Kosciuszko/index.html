Mount Kosciuszko

Elevation: 2,228 m (7,310 ft)

Prominence: 2,228 m

Location: New South Wales, Australia

Range: Main Range, Snowy Mountains

Mount Kosciuszko is mainland Australia's tallest mountain, at 2,228 metres (7,310 ft) above sea level, located on the Main Range of the Snowy Mountains in Kosciuszko National Park, part of the Australian Alps National Parks and Reserves, in New South Wales, Australia.

History

The mountain was named by the Polish explorer Paweł (Paul) Edmund Strzelecki in 1840, in honour of the Polish-Lithuanian hero Tadeusz Kościuszko, because of its perceived resemblance to the Kościuszko Mound in Kraków. Strzelecki made the first recorded European ascent that year. The local Aboriginal Ngarigo people called the summit by names including Tar Gan Gil; dual naming has been a subject of ongoing discussion. A long-running surveying confusion — the peaks of Kosciuszko and neighbouring Mount Townsend were originally mislabelled — was resolved in the late 19th century, with names swapped so that the higher summit bore the Kosciuszko name.

Climbing

Mount Kosciuszko is one of the Seven Summits in the Bass list. It is among the easiest of them to climb: a chairlift from Thredbo and a raised metal walkway lead most of the way, and thousands of visitors walk to the summit each summer. In winter the approach is a serious alpine undertaking.
