Esthetician

OccupationLicensed skin care professional
ActivitiesFacials, waxing, chemical peels, body treatments, makeup
RegulationState cosmetology boards (US); varies by country
SettingsSpas, salons, dermatology offices, medical spas

An esthetician (also spelled aesthetician) is a licensed professional who specializes in the beautification and health of the skin. Estheticians provide treatments such as facials, superficial chemical peels, body wraps, waxing and other hair removal, pore extraction, eyelash and brow services, and makeup application. They also advise clients on skin care products and routines for home use.

Training and licensing

In the United States, estheticians must complete a state-approved program — commonly 300 to 1,500 hours of training depending on the state — and pass licensing examinations administered under state boards of cosmetology. Some states offer a higher-tier "master" or "medical" esthetician license requiring additional hundreds of hours covering advanced procedures such as laser treatments, lymphatic drainage, and medium-depth peels performed under medical supervision.

Scope of practice

Estheticians may work in day spas, salons, resorts, and cruise ships, or in clinical settings alongside dermatologists and plastic surgeons, where they help prepare patients' skin before and after medical procedures. Their scope of practice generally excludes diagnosing or treating skin diseases, administering injections, or performing procedures that penetrate living tissue, which remain the domain of medical professionals.
