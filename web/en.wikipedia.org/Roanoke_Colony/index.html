Roanoke Colony

The "CROATOAN" carving found at the deserted settlement

The Roanoke Colony was an attempt, initially funded by Sir Walter Raleigh, to found the first permanent English settlement in North America. The colony was founded in 1585, but when the supply ship returned in 1590 the settlement was abandoned and its fate remains unknown; it is often called the "Lost Colony".

First colony, 1585–1586

A first expedition under Sir Richard Grenville and Ralph Lane established a settlement on Roanoke Island, in present-day Dare County, North Carolina. Short of food and in conflict with local Native Americans, the colonists returned to England with Sir Francis Drake's fleet in 1586.

Second colony, 1587

A second group of 115 settlers under governor John White arrived in 1587. White's granddaughter Virginia Dare, born that August, was the first English child born in the Americas. White sailed back to England for supplies, but the Anglo-Spanish War and the Spanish Armada delayed his return for three years.

Disappearance

When White finally returned on 18 August 1590, he found the settlement deserted. The only clues were the word "CROATOAN" carved into a fort post and "CRO" carved into a tree. No sign of struggle or graves was found, suggesting the colonists may have moved to Croatoan Island (Hatteras) or assimilated with local peoples, but storms prevented White from searching further.

Theories and legacy

Hypotheses include relocation and assimilation with the Hatteras or other Algonquian peoples, death from disease or famine, and dispersal to the Chesapeake. Archaeological work at Bertie County and Hatteras has found English artefacts in Native contexts. The mystery has inspired books, plays including Paul Green's The Lost Colony (1937), and films.
