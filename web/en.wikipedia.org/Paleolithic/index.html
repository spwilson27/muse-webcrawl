Paleolithic

The Paleolithic or Palaeolithic (from Greek palaios, "old", and lithos, "stone") is a period in human prehistory characterised by the original development of stone tools. It spans the vast majority of human technological history, from the earliest known use of stone tools by hominins about 3.3 million years ago to the end of the Pleistocene, around 11,650 years ago.

Subdivisions

The Paleolithic is conventionally divided into the Lower (Early), Middle and Upper Paleolithic. The Lower Paleolithic includes the Oldowan and Acheulean industries of early Homo. The Middle Paleolithic, roughly 300,000 to 50,000 years ago, saw the Mousterian tools of Neanderthals and early anatomically modern humans. The Upper Paleolithic (c. 50,000–12,000 years ago) is marked by a flourishing of blade tools, art, jewellery and long-distance trade.

Way of life

Paleolithic humans lived in small bands of hunter-gatherers, subsisting on wild plants and animals. They controlled fire, built shelters, and produced some of the earliest known art, including the cave paintings of Chauvet, Altamira and Lascaux and portable figurines such as the Venus of Willendorf. Burial of the dead and evidence of ritual behaviour appear during this period.

End of the Paleolithic

The period ends with the close of the last Ice Age and the transition to the Mesolithic and then the Neolithic, when agriculture transformed human societies in the "Neolithic Revolution".
