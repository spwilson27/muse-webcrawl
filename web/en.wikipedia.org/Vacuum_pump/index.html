Vacuum pump

A vacuum pump is a device that draws gas molecules from a sealed volume in order to leave behind a partial vacuum. Vacuum pumps are used in industry, laboratories, refrigeration servicing, and many manufacturing processes.

Types

Positive-displacement pumps, such as rotary vane, diaphragm, and piston pumps, expand and seal off volumes of gas. Momentum-transfer (molecular) pumps, including turbomolecular and diffusion pumps, impart velocity to gas molecules to reach high and ultra-high vacuum. Entrapment pumps capture gases by condensation or adsorption, as in cryopumps and ion pumps.

Performance

Pumps are characterized by pumping speed and the ultimate pressure they can reach, which ranges from rough vacuum near atmospheric pressure down to below 10⁻⁹ pascals for ultra-high vacuum systems. The first vacuum pump, a suction pump, was demonstrated by Otto von Guericke around 1650.
