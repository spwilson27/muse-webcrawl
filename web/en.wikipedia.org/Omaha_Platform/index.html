Omaha Platform

AdoptedJuly 4, 1892
LocationOmaha, Nebraska
PartyPeople's Party (Populists)
Key authorIgnatius L. Donnelly (preamble)

The Omaha Platform was the party program adopted at the founding convention of the People's Party, held in Omaha, Nebraska, on July 4, 1892. It articulated the demands of the Populist movement, which drew support from farmers and laborers aggrieved by deflation, railroad monopolies, and the power of banks and corporations. The preamble, written by Minnesota author and politician Ignatius L. Donnelly, opened with the famous declaration that the nation stood "on the verge of moral, political, and material ruin."

Principal demands

The platform called for the free and unlimited coinage of silver at a ratio of 16 to 1 with gold, a graduated income tax, government ownership of railroads, telegraph, and telephone systems, a postal savings bank, and an increase in the money supply to at least $50 per capita. It also demanded the direct election of United States senators, the secret ballot, the initiative and referendum, a shorter working day, and restrictions on immigration and alien land ownership. Its "subtreasury plan" proposed federal warehouses where farmers could store crops and borrow against them at low interest.

Legacy

The Populist ticket of James B. Weaver won over a million votes and 22 electoral votes in 1892. Although the People's Party soon faded, many Omaha Platform proposals — including the income tax, direct election of senators, and regulation of railroads — were later enacted during the Progressive Era and the New Deal.
