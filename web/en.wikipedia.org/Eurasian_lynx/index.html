Eurasian lynx

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Eurasian lynx (Lynx lynx) is a medium-sized wild cat widely distributed from Northern, Central and Eastern Europe to Central Asia and Siberia, the Tibetan Plateau and the Himalayas. It is the largest of the four lynx species, standing about 55–75 cm at the shoulder and weighing 18–30 kg. It inhabits temperate and boreal forests with plenty of cover and ungulate prey.

Description

The Eurasian lynx has a short reddish or brown coat marked with black spots, turning longer and greyer in winter, and a short "bobbed" tail with an all-black tip. Its most distinctive features are the long black tufts on the tips of its ears and the prominent cheek ruffs framing its face. Its large, fur-covered paws act like snowshoes, distributing its weight on snow.

Behaviour and ecology

The lynx is a solitary, predominantly crepuscular and nocturnal predator. Its principal prey are small to medium-sized ungulates — it is the only lynx species that regularly kills deer, especially roe deer, chamois, and young red deer — supplemented by hares and rodents. An adult requires roughly 1–2 kg of meat per day. Females give birth to litters of one to four kittens in late spring; kittens stay with their mother for about ten months.

Distribution and conservation

Once exterminated from much of western Europe by hunting and habitat loss, the Eurasian lynx has been reintroduced to the Alps, Carpathians, Balkans, and parts of Germany, France, and Switzerland. The species as a whole is listed as Least Concern by the IUCN, thanks to its large Siberian population, though several European subpopulations — notably the Balkan lynx — are critically endangered.
