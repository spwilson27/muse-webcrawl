Relics associated with Buddha

Relics associated with Buddha are objects believed to derive from the body of Siddhartha Gautama, the Buddha, and to have been distributed after his cremation at Kushinagar. According to Buddhist tradition, the Buddha's cremated remains were divided into eight portions and enshrined in stupas by the peoples who claimed them. In the 3rd century BCE the Mauryan emperor Ashoka is said to have redistributed the relics into 84,000 stupas across his empire.

History

Early texts such as the Mahaparinibbana Sutta describe the division of the relics among eight kingdoms and clans. Archaeological finds, including the relic casket from Piprahwa on the India–Nepal border, have been proposed as genuine deposits, though their identification remains debated among scholars.

Famous relics

Among the most venerated are the Sacred Relic of the Tooth in Kandy, Sri Lanka, kept in the Temple of the Tooth; relics at Sanchi in India; and remains enshrined at sites in Myanmar, Thailand, and China. Relics are central to devotional practice, pilgrimage, and the legitimization of Buddhist kingship across Asia.

Significance

The veneration of relics is one of the oldest continuously practiced devotions in Buddhism. Stupas built to house them became focal points of monasteries and pilgrimage routes, and the circulation of relics helped spread Buddhism along trade routes into Central and East Asia.
