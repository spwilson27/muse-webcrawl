Morbidity

Morbidity is a term used to describe the state of being symptomatic or unhealthy due to a disease or condition. It is usually represented by the prevalence or incidence of a disease within a population. Morbidity statistics are used by public health officials, epidemiologists, and insurers to measure the burden of disease and to plan healthcare services.

FieldEpidemiology, public health
MeasuresPrevalence, incidence
ContrastMortality

Measures

Morbidity is commonly reported as incidence, the number of new cases arising in a period, or prevalence, the total number of cases present at a given time. Comorbidity refers to the presence of additional conditions co-occurring with a primary condition, and multimorbidity to the coexistence of two or more chronic conditions.

Data sources

Morbidity data come from disease registries, hospital discharge records, surveys such as national health interview surveys, and mandatory notification systems for infectious diseases.

Use

Together with mortality, morbidity informs measures such as disability-adjusted life years (DALYs), which combine years of life lost with years lived with disability.
