George V (George Frederick Ernest Albert; 3 June 1865 – 20 January 1936) was King of the United Kingdom and the British Dominions, and Emperor of India, from 6 May 1910 until his death in 1936.

Born during the reign of his grandmother Queen Victoria, George was the second son of the Prince and Princess of Wales (later King Edward VII and Queen Alexandra), and third in the line of succession to the British throne behind his father and his elder brother, Prince Albert Victor. From 1877 to 1892, George served in the Royal Navy, until his elder brother's unexpected death in January 1892 put him directly in line for the throne. George married his brother's fiancée, Princess Victoria Mary of Teck, the following year. Although they occasionally travelled the British Empire, George preferred to stay at home with his stamp collection and lived what later biographers would consider a dull life because of its conventionality.

Reign

George became king on his father's death in 1910. His reign saw the rise of socialism, communism, fascism, Irish republicanism, and the Indian independence movement, all of which radically changed the political landscape of the British Empire, which itself reached its territorial peak in the early 1920s. The Parliament Act 1911 established the supremacy of the elected House of Commons over the unelected House of Lords. As a result of the First World War (1914–1918), the empires of his first cousins Nicholas II of Russia and Wilhelm II of Germany fell, while the British Empire expanded to its greatest extent. In 1917, George became the first monarch of the House of Windsor, which he renamed from the House of Saxe-Coburg and Gotha as a result of anti-German public sentiment.

In 1924, George appointed the first Labour ministry and the 1931 Statute of Westminster recognised the Empire's dominions as separate, independent states within the British Commonwealth of Nations. He was plagued by illness throughout much of his later reign, and at his death was succeeded by his eldest son, Edward VIII.

Early life and naval career

George was born on 3 June 1865 at Marlborough House, London, and was christened George Frederick Ernest Albert. As a younger son expected never to reign, he was educated with his elder brother and then sent to the Royal Navy, serving as a cadet aboard HMS Britannia and later travelling the world, visiting many areas of the British Empire. He rose to the rank of commander before his brother's death changed his future.

Marriage and family

On 6 July 1893 George married Princess Victoria Mary of Teck at the Chapel Royal, St James's Palace. They had six children: Edward, Albert (later George VI), Mary, Henry, George, and John. The family lived mainly at York Cottage on the Sandringham estate, and George was a devoted if strict father.

Death

George V's health deteriorated through the 1930s, worsened by heavy smoking. He fell seriously ill in January 1936 and died at Sandringham on 20 January 1936. His physician, Lord Dawson of Penn, later admitted to hastening the King's death with injections of morphine and cocaine so that it could be announced in the morning edition of The Times rather than "less appropriate" evening journals — a disclosure that prompted lasting ethical debate. George was interred at St George's Chapel, Windsor Castle.

Legacy

George V is remembered for steadying the monarchy through war and constitutional change, for establishing the modern image of the British royal family (including the first royal Christmas broadcast, in 1932), and for his famous stamp collection, which formed the nucleus of the Royal Philatelic Collection. Towns, schools, and ships — including the battleship HMS King George V — were named in his honour.
