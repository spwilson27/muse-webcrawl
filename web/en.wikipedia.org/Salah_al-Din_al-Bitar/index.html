Salah al-Din al-Bitar (1912 – 21 July 1980) was a Syrian politician who co-founded the Arab Ba'ath Party with Michel Aflaq in the 1940s and served several times as Prime Minister of Syria in the 1960s. A Sunni Muslim from a Damascene merchant family, Bitar became the leading "moderate" figure of early Ba'athism before being driven into exile and ultimately assassinated.

Founding the Ba'ath

Educated as a teacher, Bitar met Michel Aflaq while both were students in Paris in the early 1930s. Back in Damascus they built a movement around the ideas of Arab unity, freedom, and socialism, holding the founding congress of the Arab Ba'ath Party in 1947. The party merged in 1952 with Akram al-Hawrani's Arab Socialist Party. Bitar served as foreign minister of the United Arab Republic during Syria's union with Egypt (1958–1961).

Prime minister and exile

After the Ba'athist coup of March 1963, Bitar became prime minister, heading several governments marked by rivalry between the party's civilian founders and its military committee. The radical coup of February 1966 ousted Bitar and Aflaq; Bitar was arrested, escaped, and lived thereafter in exile, chiefly in Paris, editing the journal al-Ihyaa al-Arabi and criticizing the regime of Hafez al-Assad. On 21 July 1980 he was shot dead outside his Paris office; his assassin was never conclusively identified, though suspicion fell on Syrian intelligence. See also Ba'ath Party and Michel Aflaq.
