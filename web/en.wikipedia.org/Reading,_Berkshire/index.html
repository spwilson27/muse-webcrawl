Reading (pronounced "RED-ing") is a town and borough in Berkshire, England, situated at the confluence of the River Thames and the River Kennet, about 36 miles (58 km) west of London. It is the county town of Berkshire and one of the largest urban areas in the United Kingdom without city status.

History

Reading grew around Reading Abbey, founded by Henry I in 1121, which became one of the richest and most influential monasteries in medieval England before its dissolution in 1539. The town was a major cloth centre and later became noted for the "three Bs"—beer (Simonds' brewery), bulbs (Sutton's seeds) and biscuits (Huntley & Palmers, once the world's largest biscuit factory). Oscar Wilde was imprisoned in Reading Gaol, where he wrote The Ballad of Reading Gaol.

Economy and transport

Reading is a major commercial centre in the Thames Valley, sometimes dubbed part of "Silicon Valley" UK for its concentration of information technology companies, including offices of Microsoft and Oracle. Reading station is one of the busiest rail interchanges in Britain, on the Great Western Main Line, and is the western terminus of the Elizabeth line. The town hosts the University of Reading, founded in 1892, and the Reading Festival, one of Britain's longest-running rock music festivals.
