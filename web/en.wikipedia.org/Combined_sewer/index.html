Combined sewer

A combined sewer is a type of gravity sewer with a system of pipes, tunnels, channel culverts, and other infrastructure that convey sewage and stormwater together in a single pipe system. They were the norm in older cities, including much of London, Paris, New York, Chicago, and Boston.

Overflow problem

In dry weather, combined flow goes to a treatment plant. In heavy rain, capacity is exceeded and combined sewer overflows (CSOs) discharge a mixture of stormwater and untreated sewage into rivers and coasts, harming water quality and public health. Regulators in the US and EU require CSO control plans.

Mitigation

Solutions include storage tunnels (such as London's Thames Tideway Tunnel), sewer separation, real-time control, green infrastructure to reduce runoff, and disinfection of overflows. Modern practice builds separate sanitary and storm sewers.

History

Victorian engineers such as Joseph Bazalgette built London's intercepting combined system after the Great Stink of 1858, dramatically improving public health despite the overflow legacy.
