Stream

TypeFlowing body of water
ComponentsSource, channel, banks, mouth
Related termsBrook, creek, river, tributary

A stream is a body of water flowing within a bed and banks across the surface of the land. Streams range from tiny headwater brooks and creeks to large rivers; the term is often used for smaller watercourses, though usage varies regionally. All streams are part of the hydrological cycle and drain a watershed toward larger streams, lakes or the sea.

Characteristics

A stream begins at a source such as a spring, lake or melting snow, flows downhill through a channel shaped by erosion and deposition, and ends at a mouth where it joins another water body. Flow velocity, discharge and sediment load vary with gradient, rainfall and season. Stream order systems, such as the Strahler system, classify streams by the hierarchy of their tributaries.

Ecology

Streams support rich ecosystems along their riparian zones and serve as corridors for wildlife; intermittent and ephemeral streams flow only part of the year.
