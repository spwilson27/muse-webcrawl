Siege

A siege is a military blockade of a city, fortified place or enemy position, intended to conquer by attrition, well-supported defence or by an assault on its fortifications. It is a fundamental form of constant, low-intensity conflict characterised by one party's possession of a strong, static, defensive position.

Methods

Besiegers have historically surrounded the target with lines of circumvallation, cut off supplies of food and water, bombarded defences with artillery, undermined walls by tunnelling, and employed siege engines such as battering rams, siege towers, trebuchets and ladders to storm the position. Starvation and disease were often deadlier than direct assault.

History

Sieges are recorded from the earliest cities of Mesopotamia and Egypt. Famous examples include the sieges of Tyre and Jerusalem, the long sieges of Constantinople culminating in 1453, and the siege of Leningrad during the Second World War, which lasted 872 days.

Modern era

Fortifications lost their dominance with modern artillery, aircraft and armour, but extended sieges of cities and strongpoints still occur, as at Stalingrad, Sarajevo and in more recent conflicts.
