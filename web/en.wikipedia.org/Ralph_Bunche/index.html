Ralph Bunche

Ralph Johnson Bunche (August 7, 1904 – December 9, 1971) was an American political scientist, diplomat and leading figure in the civil rights movement. A senior official of the United Nations for over two decades, he received the 1950 Nobel Peace Prize for mediating the 1949 Armistice Agreements between Israel and the Arab states — becoming the first African American and the first person of colour to receive the prize.

Early life and education

Bunche was born in Detroit, Michigan, and raised largely in Los Angeles after being orphaned. He graduated as valedictorian from UCLA in 1927 and earned a doctorate in political science from Harvard University in 1934, the first African American to receive a PhD in the field from an American university. He taught at Howard University and co-authored, with Gunnar Myrdal, research for An American Dilemma (1944) on race in America.

United Nations career

During the Second World War Bunche worked in the Office of Strategic Services and the State Department, helping plan the United Nations. He joined the UN Secretariat in 1946, working on trusteeship and decolonisation. After the assassination of mediator Folke Bernadotte in 1948, Bunche led the Rhodes armistice negotiations, securing agreements in 1949. He later served as Under-Secretary-General, supervising peacekeeping operations in Suez, the Congo, Yemen, Kashmir, Cyprus and elsewhere.

Civil rights

Bunche was an active supporter of the American civil rights movement, participating in the 1963 March on Washington and the 1965 Selma to Montgomery marches, and serving on the board of the NAACP for over two decades. He declined President Truman's offer of an assistant secretary of state post, citing segregation in Washington, D.C.

Legacy

Bunche died in New York in 1971. He is commemorated in the Ralph Bunche Park opposite the UN headquarters, the Bunche Center at UCLA, and numerous schools. His grandson and biographers have emphasised both his diplomatic achievements and his often cautious public stance on domestic racial politics.
