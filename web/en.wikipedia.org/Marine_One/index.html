Marine One

Marine One is the call sign of any United States Marine Corps aircraft carrying the president of the United States. As a specific reference, it most commonly denotes the presidential helicopters operated by Marine Helicopter Squadron One (HMX-1), the VH-3D Sea King and VH-60N White Hawk, painted in a distinctive green-and-white livery.

History

Helicopter transport of the president began in 1957, when Dwight D. Eisenhower used a Bell UH-13J. The South Lawn of the White House became the customary landing site, making the helicopter an iconic visual symbol of the presidency.

Operations

The helicopters typically fly in groups of up to five identical aircraft, one carrying the president and the others serving as decoys, changing formation in flight. Flights are short-range, ferrying the president between the White House, Camp David, Joint Base Andrews and other nearby locations, where Air Force One handles longer journeys.

Replacement

The Sikorsky VH-92A Patriot, based on the S-92, was developed under the VXX program to replace the ageing fleet and began presidential service in 2024.
