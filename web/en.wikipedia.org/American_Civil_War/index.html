American Civil War

The American Civil War (12 April 1861 – 26 May 1865) was a civil war in the United States between the Union and the Confederacy, formed by states that seceded over slavery. It remains the deadliest war in American history.

Date: 1861–1865
Location: United States
Result: Union victory; slavery abolished; Reconstruction began

Causes

Disputes over slavery's expansion into western territories, states' rights, and economic differences culminated after Abraham Lincoln's election in 1860. Seven Deep South states seceded, then four more after fighting began at Fort Sumter.

War

Major theatres included the East (Antietam, Gettysburg), the West (Shiloh, Vicksburg, Atlanta) and naval blockade. Lincoln issued the Emancipation Proclamation in 1863, making abolition a war aim and enabling mass enlistment of Black soldiers. Generals included Ulysses S. Grant and William T. Sherman for the Union, Robert E. Lee for the Confederacy.

End and legacy

Lee surrendered at Appomattox Court House on 9 April 1865. Lincoln was assassinated days later. The 13th Amendment abolished slavery; Reconstruction Amendments promised citizenship and voting rights, contested for a century thereafter. An estimated 620,000–750,000 soldiers died.
