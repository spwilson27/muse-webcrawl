The ASEAN Summit is a biannual meeting held by the members of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) in relation to economic, political, security and socio-cultural development of Southeast Asian countries. The summit has become the supreme policy-making body of ASEAN under the ASEAN Charter, and serves as a venue for the region's leaders to meet leaders of ASEAN's dialogue partners in related summits.

History

The first ASEAN Summit was held in Bali, Indonesia on 23–24 February 1976, attended by the leaders of the five founding members: Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore and Thailand. The summit produced the Declaration of ASEAN Concord and the Treaty of Amity and Cooperation in Southeast Asia. For many years summits were held only every few years, but they became annual in the 1990s and, following the entry into force of the ASEAN Charter in December 2008, the summit has been held twice a year.

Format

The chairmanship of ASEAN rotates annually among member states in alphabetical order, and the chair country hosts the summits. Around the plenary ASEAN Summit are held related meetings, including the ASEAN Plus Three summit (with China, Japan and South Korea) and the East Asia Summit, which also includes countries such as the United States, Russia, India, Australia and New Zealand. The summits are preceded by ministerial and senior officials' meetings that prepare declarations and agreements for leaders to adopt.

Key outcomes

Summits have launched major community-building initiatives, including the ASEAN Community (formally established in 2015, with its Political-Security, Economic and Socio-Cultural pillars) and agreements such as the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP), signed in 2020 on the margins of the 37th ASEAN Summit.
