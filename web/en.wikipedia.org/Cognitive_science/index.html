Cognitive science

Cognitive science is the interdisciplinary, scientific study of the mind and its processes. It examines the nature, tasks, and functions of cognition. Cognitive scientists study intelligence and behaviour with a focus on how nervous systems represent, process, and transform information. The field draws on psychology, neuroscience, linguistics, philosophy, anthropology, and artificial intelligence — often drawn as the vertices of a hexagon in textbook diagrams.

Scope

Core topics include perception, attention, memory, learning, language, reasoning, emotion, and consciousness. Cognitive science typically operates at Marr's three levels of analysis: the computational problem a system solves, the algorithm it uses, and the physical implementation in brains or machines.

Approaches

Historically, the field was dominated by symbolic approaches treating cognition as rule-based manipulation of representations, exemplified by early artificial intelligence and production systems. Connectionism offered an alternative based on artificial neural networks. More recent perspectives — embodied, embedded, enactive, and extended cognition — argue that thinking is shaped by the body and the environment, challenging purely brain-bound accounts. Dynamical systems and Bayesian models of cognition are also influential.

Institutions and methods

The Cognitive Science Society was founded in 1979 and its journal Cognitive Science began publication in 1977. Methods include behavioural experiments, computational simulation, brain imaging, lesion studies, and philosophical analysis. Cognitive science underpins much of modern AI research and human–computer interaction.
