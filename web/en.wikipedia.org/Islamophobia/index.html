Islamophobia

Islamophobia refers to fear, hatred of, or prejudice towards Islam and Muslims.

Islamophobia is the fear of, hatred of, or prejudice against the religion of Islam or Muslims in general. The term was popularised in the 1990s, notably by a 1997 report of the British think tank the Runnymede Trust, which characterised Islamophobia as "unfounded hostility towards Islam" and a set of "closed views" treating Islam as monolithic, separate, inferior and hostile.

Definitions and debate

Scholars and policymakers debate the term's scope: working definitions range from individual prejudice and discrimination to structural and institutional exclusion, while critics argue that the concept can be misused to shield religious doctrines from legitimate criticism. In 2019 the All-Party Parliamentary Group on British Muslims proposed defining Islamophobia as "rooted in racism and ... a type of racism that targets expressions of Muslimness or perceived Muslimness."

Manifestations

Reported manifestations include hate crimes and harassment (including against women wearing hijab), discrimination in employment and housing, hostile media representation, and restrictive policies targeting Muslim practices. Hate-crime statistics in several Western countries recorded sharp increases following terrorist attacks, notably after 11 September 2001.

Responses

Responses include anti-discrimination law, interfaith initiatives, police hate-crime monitoring, and in 2022 the United Nations General Assembly proclaimed 15 March the International Day to Combat Islamophobia.
