The Kaibiles (singular: Kaibil) are a special operations force of the Guatemalan military, specializing in jungle warfare, counter-insurgency, and commando operations. Founded in 1974 during Guatemala's civil war, the unit is renowned for one of the most gruelling selection and training regimes in the world and is distinguished by its maroon beret.

History

The Kaibil training centre was established on 5 December 1974 at Poptún in the Petén jungle, initially with instruction influenced by the United States Army Rangers and Green Berets. The unit's name derives from Kayb'il B'alam, a Mam indigenous leader who resisted the Spanish conquest in the sixteenth century. The Kaibil school later trained soldiers from many countries and the unit itself deployed on United Nations peacekeeping operations, notably in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, where eight Kaibiles were killed in 2005.

Training

The Kaibil course lasts about eight weeks of extreme physical and psychological pressure: sleep and food deprivation, forced marches, survival in the jungle, river crossings, demolitions, and hand-to-hand combat. Trainees must eat raw and unconventional foods and historically were required to raise and then kill a puppy to harden them — a practice the army says has been discontinued. Only a small fraction of candidates graduate; the course's credo is attributed to a saying of the unit's founder.

Human rights controversy

Kaibil units were implicated in some of the gravest abuses of the Guatemalan Civil War, most notoriously the Dos Erres massacre of December 1982, in which a Kaibil patrol murdered more than 200 villagers. Several perpetrators were convicted in Guatemalan and foreign courts decades later. Separately, some former Kaibiles have been recruited by Mexican drug cartels, and ex-Kaibil instructors were linked to the founding of Los Zetas.
