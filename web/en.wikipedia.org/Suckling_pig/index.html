Suckling pig

A suckling pig is a piglet fed on its mother's milk (i.e., a suckling) that is slaughtered young, typically between two and six weeks old, and traditionally cooked whole. The meat is pale, tender and mild, with gelatinous texture and a skin that roasts to crispness. The dish is found in many cuisines and is often a festive centrepiece.

Regional preparations

In Spain, cochinillo asado — especially of Segovia, where it has protected status — is roasted in a wood oven until the skin is glassy and crisp; tradition holds it should be tender enough to cut with the edge of a plate. Portugal's leitão assado of the Bairrada region is similarly celebrated. In the Philippines, lechón, a whole pig roasted over charcoal, is the centrepiece of fiestas and is commonly made with larger pigs, though sucklings are also used. Chinese siu yuk-style roast suckling pig features in Cantonese banquets, and a suckling pig has long been a feature of festive tables in Germany (Spanferkel), Italy (porchetta is usually a larger boned pig, but sucklings are roasted whole in Sardinia as porceddu), and the South Pacific.

History

Roast suckling pig is among the oldest festive dishes: it appears in Roman cookery, where Apicius gives recipes for roast piglet, and it has remained a banquet dish in Europe and Asia since. Its small size made it practical to cook whole in domestic ovens and village bread ovens after the day's baking.
