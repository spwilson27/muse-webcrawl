Vasily II of Moscow

Vasily II Vasilyevich (Russian: Василий II Васильевич; 10 March 1415 – 27 March 1462), nicknamed the Blind, was the Grand Prince of Moscow from 1425 until his death in 1462. His twenty-year struggle against his uncle and cousins in the Muscovite Civil War was the only major dynastic war in the history of medieval Muscovy.

The feudal war

Vasily succeeded his father at the age of ten. His right to the throne was contested by his uncle Yuri of Zvenigorod, who claimed succession under the older lateral principle. The dispute went before the Khan of the Golden Horde, who ruled for Vasily, but Yuri seized Moscow twice by force before his death in 1434. The conflict continued under Yuri's sons, Vasily Kosoy and Dmitry Shemyaka. Vasily II was captured by Tatars at the Battle of Suzdal in 1445 and ransomed; in 1446 Shemyaka had him blinded and exiled — hence his epithet. Supported by the church and the boyars, Vasily recovered the throne in 1447 and eventually subdued his rivals.

Church and state

In 1448, during Vasily's reign, a council of Russian bishops elected Jonah as Metropolitan of Moscow without Constantinople's consent, effectively establishing the autocephaly of the Russian Orthodox Church, a decision reinforced after Constantinople accepted union with Rome at Florence. Vasily also strengthened the central administration and reduced the appanages, preparing the ground for the unification completed by his son Ivan III.
