Target Corporation

Type: Public
Industry: Retail
Founded: June 24, 1902 (as Dayton Dry Goods Company); May 1, 1962 (first Target store)
Headquarters: Minneapolis, Minnesota, U.S.
Number of locations: ~1,900 stores

Target Corporation is an American retail corporation headquartered in Minneapolis, Minnesota. It is the seventh-largest retailer in the United States, and is known by its red bullseye logo and its positioning, sometimes described by the slogan "Expect More. Pay Less.", as an upscale discounter offering fashionable merchandise at discount prices. Target operates roughly 1,900 stores across the United States.

History

The company traces its origins to the Dayton Dry Goods Company, founded by George Dayton in Minneapolis in 1902, which became the Dayton Company and opened the first Target discount store in Roseville, Minnesota, in 1962. The Dayton-Hudson Corporation grew through acquisitions, and in 2000 the parent company renamed itself Target Corporation, reflecting the discount chain's dominance. The company sold its department-store divisions, including Marshall Field's, in 2004. An expansion into Canada in 2013 ended in failure two years later, and a 2013 data breach affecting tens of millions of customers led to a major overhaul of its payment security.

Operations

Target stores carry general merchandise including clothing, electronics, home goods, groceries and pharmacy items. Formats include standard Target stores and larger SuperTarget hypermarkets. The company has developed a large portfolio of private-label brands, such as Cat & Jack, Good & Gather, Threshold and Hearth & Hand, and has invested heavily in same-day fulfilment services including Drive Up and the Shipt delivery platform.
