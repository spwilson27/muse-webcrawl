Rukmini Devi Arundale

Rukmini Devi Arundale (née Shastri; 29 February 1904 – 24 February 1986) was an Indian theosophist, dancer and choreographer of the Indian classical dance form of Bharatanatyam, and an activist for animal welfare. She is credited with rescuing Bharatanatyam from disrepute and re-establishing it as a respected art, founding the Kalakshetra Foundation in Madras (Chennai) in 1936 to train dancers and musicians.

Life and work

Raised in the Theosophical Society at Adyar, Rukmini Devi married the English theosophist George Arundale in 1920, scandalising orthodox society. Encouraged by the Russian ballerina Anna Pavlova, whom she met on a voyage in the 1920s, she turned from ballet to India's own dance traditions and learned Bharatanatyam — then performed chiefly by hereditary devadasi temple dancers and regarded as disreputable — from Pandanallur Meenakshisundaram Pillai. Her public debut in 1935 and the founding of Kalakshetra the following year began a transformation: she systematised teaching, designed costumes and jewellery, composed dance-dramas drawn from the Ramayana and other epics, and brought the form to national and international stages.

Other work

Rukmini Devi served in the Rajya Sabha (1952–1962) — reportedly declining an offer of nomination for President of India in 1977 — and was a pioneering animal-welfare activist who chaired the Animal Welfare Board of India and promoted vegetarianism. Critics have debated how her reforms "sanitised" the devadasi tradition and displaced its hereditary practitioners, but her role in securing Bharatanatyam's prestige is undisputed. She received the Padma Bhushan in 1956 and the Sangeet Natak Akademi Fellowship.
