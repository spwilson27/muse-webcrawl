Hurricane Irma was an extremely powerful Cape Verde hurricane that struck the Caribbean and Florida in September 2017. It was the strongest Atlantic hurricane ever recorded outside the Gulf of Mexico and Caribbean Sea, and it maintained peak sustained winds of 185 mph (295 km/h) for 37 hours — longer than any other tropical cyclone worldwide.

Meteorological history

Irma developed from a tropical wave near Cape Verde on 30 August 2017. It intensified into a Category 5 hurricane on 5 September and made devastating landfalls on Barbuda, Saint Martin, and the British Virgin Islands on 6 September, then grazed Puerto Rico and struck Cuba as a Category 5. After weakening and re-strengthening over the Florida Straits, Irma hit the Florida Keys as a Category 4 and mainland Florida near Marco Island as a Category 3 on 10 September, before dissipating over the southeastern United States.

Impact

Irma caused catastrophic damage on several Caribbean islands, destroying most buildings on Barbuda and devastating Saint Martin. In Florida it produced storm surge flooding, widespread power outages affecting millions, and severe damage in the Keys. Total damage was estimated around $77 billion, making it among the costliest Atlantic hurricanes on record, and at least 134 deaths were attributed to the storm.

Aftermath

The name Irma was retired by the World Meteorological Organization after the season and replaced with Idalia. Recovery in Florida and the Caribbean took years, and the storm intensified debate about building codes, evacuation planning, and climate change.
