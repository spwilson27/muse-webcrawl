Piercing saw

Type
Hand saw
Used in
Jewellery making, metalwork
Other names
Jeweler's saw

A piercing saw, also called a jeweler's saw, is a small-frame saw used principally by jewelers and metalsmiths to cut intricate shapes and pierced designs in sheet metal. It consists of a lightweight adjustable frame, often of a deep "C" or "D" shape, holding a very fine blade under tension.

Use

Blades are graded by tooth count, from coarse grades for thicker stock to extremely fine grades (such as 8/0) for delicate work. To cut an internal shape, a small hole is drilled in the metal, the blade is threaded through and reattached to the frame, and the design is sawn out while the work rests on a V-shaped bench pin. The blade is lubricated with wax or cutting fluid to reduce friction and breakage.

Applications

Beyond jewellery, piercing saws are used in model making, watchmaking, and electronics for cutting thin brass, copper, silver, gold, and mild steel sheet with minimal waste and clean edges.
