Color term

A color term (or colour term) is a word or phrase used to designate a particular color. The ways in which different languages divide and name the color spectrum vary considerably, and the study of color terms has been an influential topic in linguistics, anthropology, and cognitive science.

Basic color terms

Brent Berlin and Paul Kay's 1969 study Basic Color Terms: Their Universality and Evolution proposed that languages develop basic color terms — monolexemic, commonly used, non-subordinate terms such as red or blue — in a predictable evolutionary sequence. Languages with only two basic terms distinguish roughly dark/cool from light/warm; red is typically the third term added, followed by green or yellow, then blue, then brown, and later purple, pink, orange, and grey. English has eleven basic color terms. The findings suggested universal perceptual tendencies underlying linguistic variation, and were later tested and refined by the World Color Survey.

Debate

Relativist critics argue that color categories are more culturally shaped than Berlin and Kay allowed, pointing to languages whose terms do not fit the scheme neatly — such as Russian's two basic blues, or languages distinguishing several greens. Experiments on categorical perception have shown that speakers perceive colors partly through the categories of their language, keeping the debate between universalism and linguistic relativity active.
