Port of Rotterdam

The Port of Rotterdam is the largest seaport in Europe, and the world's largest seaport outside of Asia, located in and near the city of Rotterdam, in the province of South Holland in the Netherlands. From 1962 until 2004, it was the world's busiest port by annual cargo tonnage. It was overtaken first in 2004 by the port of Singapore, and since then by Shanghai and other Chinese seaports.

Geography

The port stretches some 40 kilometres from the historic city harbours westward along the Nieuwe Waterweg and the Calandkanaal to the Maasvlakte reclamation at the North Sea. It sits at the mouth of the Rhine–Meuse delta, giving it unrivalled access to the European hinterland.

History

Rotterdam's port grew from medieval harbours on the Rotte river. The digging of the Nieuwe Waterweg (1866–1872), engineered by Pieter Caland, gave ocean-going ships direct access. After devastating bombing in 1940, the port was rebuilt and expanded massively with the Europoort and Maasvlakte projects in the post-war decades.

Operations

The port handles containers, liquid and dry bulk, breakbulk, and roll-on/roll-off traffic, and is a major hub for crude oil, petrochemicals, and LNG. Automation is extensive: terminals such as APM Terminals and Rotterdam World Gateway operate with remote-controlled cranes and automated guided vehicles. The Betuweroute freight railway links the port directly to Germany.
