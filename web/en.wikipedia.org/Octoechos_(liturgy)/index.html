Octoechos (liturgy)

The Octoechos (Greek: Ὀκτώηχος, "of eight tones") is the system of eight musical modes, and the liturgical book containing the hymns arranged by them, used in the Byzantine Rite of the Eastern Orthodox Church and Eastern Catholic Churches. The eight tones rotate weekly, so that the variable hymns of the daily services cycle through the full Octoechos in an eight-week period.

The eight tones

The system comprises four authentic tones and their four plagal counterparts. In Slavic practice they are called glas; in Greek, echos. Tone 1 begins on the Sunday of All Saints (the week after Pentecost in the cycle's reckoning varies by tradition) and the rotation continues through the year, interrupted by the hymns of the Triodion and Pentecostarion in their seasons.

The book

The Octoechos as a book contains the hymns for each day of the week — principally the resurrectional hymns for Saturday evening and Sunday — in each tone. Tradition attributes its compilation to St. John of Damascus in the 8th century, building on earlier hymnography from the monasteries of Palestine. A fuller version used in monasteries, the Parakletike (Great Octoechos), includes complete weekday services.
