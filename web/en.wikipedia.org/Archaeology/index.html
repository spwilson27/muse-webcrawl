Archaeology

Archaeology is the study of human activity through the recovery and analysis of material culture. Archaeologists study human prehistory and history, from the development of the first stone tools in eastern Africa 3.3 million years ago up to recent decades.

Scope and methods

The discipline involves surveying, excavation, and the analysis of artifacts, architecture, biofacts, and cultural landscapes. Because most human activity left no written record, archaeology is often the primary means of learning about prehistoric societies. Major methods include stratigraphy, radiocarbon dating, typology, and, increasingly, remote sensing and ancient DNA analysis.

Sub-disciplines

Archaeology is divided into sub-fields such as prehistoric archaeology, classical archaeology, Egyptology, underwater archaeology, and historical archaeology. It is related to anthropology, history, and the natural sciences.

History of the discipline

Antiquarian interest in ancient remains developed in the Renaissance, but scientific archaeology emerged in the 19th century with the establishment of stratigraphic excavation and the three-age system (Stone, Bronze, and Iron Ages) by Christian Jürgensen Thomsen.
