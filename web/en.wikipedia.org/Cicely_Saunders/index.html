Cicely Saunders

Born22 June 1918, Barnet, England
Died14 July 2005 (aged 87), London, England
Known forFounder of the modern hospice movement

Dame Cicely Mary Strode Saunders (22 June 1918 – 14 July 2005) was an English nurse, social worker, physician and writer. She is recognised as the founder of the modern hospice movement, which transformed the care of people approaching the end of life and laid the foundations of palliative care as a medical specialty.

Career

Saunders trained first as a nurse at St Thomas' Hospital, London, then, after a back injury ended her nursing career, as a medical social worker. Her work with dying patients, beginning in the late 1940s, convinced her that terminal pain could be controlled and that dying people needed care addressing what she called "total pain" — physical, emotional, social and spiritual suffering. She qualified as a doctor in 1957 to give her research a firmer footing.

St Christopher's Hospice

In 1967 Saunders founded St Christopher's Hospice in Sydenham, London, the first purpose-built modern hospice, combining clinical care, teaching and research. Its model — regular administration of pain relief, attention to the whole person, support for families, and care in the patient's home as well as in the hospice — spread worldwide. Saunders was made a Dame Commander of the Order of the British Empire in 1979 and was appointed to the Order of Merit in 1989. She died at St Christopher's in 2005.
