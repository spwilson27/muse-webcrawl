The Nabataean Kingdom was an ancient Arab kingdom centred on the city of Petra in present-day Jordan. The Nabataeans, originally nomadic Arabs, grew wealthy by controlling the caravan trade in frankincense, myrrh, and other luxury goods moving between southern Arabia, the Levant, and the Mediterranean world.

History

Nabataeans appear in historical records by the late fourth century BC, when they repelled attacks by the Diadochi general Antigonus. Their kingdom reached its greatest extent under Aretas IV (9 BC – AD 40), a prolific builder whose reign saw the construction of Petra's most famous monuments, including the Treasury (Al-Khazneh) and the theatre. The kingdom survived as a Roman client state until AD 106, when the emperor Trajan annexed it as the province of Arabia Petraea.

Culture and engineering

The Nabataeans wrote in a form of Aramaic whose cursive script contributed to the development of the Arabic alphabet. They were master hydraulic engineers, building dams, cisterns, and channels that made settled agriculture and urban life possible in the desert — a network later called the Nabataean water system. Their rock-cut architecture at Petra and Hegra (Madain Saleh, in modern Saudi Arabia) is inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.
