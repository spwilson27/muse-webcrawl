Tangent bundle

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In differential geometry, the tangent bundle of a differentiable manifold M is the disjoint union of the tangent spaces of M. That is, the tangent bundle TM is the set of all pairs (p, v) where p is a point of M and v is a tangent vector to M at p. Equipped with a natural topology and smooth structure, the tangent bundle is itself a differentiable manifold of twice the dimension of M, and is the prototypical example of a vector bundle.

Definition

There are two ways to define the tangent bundle: through the atlas of charts on M, using the Jacobian matrices of transition functions as the transition functions of the bundle, or through equivalence classes of curves through each point. The natural projection π : TM → M sends each tangent vector to the point at which it is based; the fibre over p is the tangent space TpM.

Vector fields and examples

A smooth section of the tangent bundle — a map assigning to each point a tangent vector, varying smoothly — is a vector field on M. The tangent bundle of a Euclidean space is trivial: Tℝn ≅ ℝn × ℝn. By contrast the tangent bundle of the 2-sphere is nontrivial, which underlies the hairy ball theorem: the sphere admits no nowhere-vanishing vector field. Tangential data such as position and velocity together form the phase space of Lagrangian mechanics, which is naturally modelled by the tangent bundle of the configuration space.
