Ryukyu Islands

Shuri Castle, the palace of the Ryukyu Kingdom, in Naha, Okinawa.

The Ryukyu Islands (Japanese: 琉球列島, Ryūkyū-rettō), also known as the Nansei Islands, are a chain of Japanese islands stretching southwest from Kyushu toward Taiwan, between the East China Sea and the Pacific Ocean. The largest island is Okinawa. The archipelago covers most of Okinawa Prefecture and part of Kagoshima Prefecture.

History

From the 15th century the islands were united under the Ryukyu Kingdom, a maritime trading state that paid tribute to China while maintaining its own monarchy and culture. The kingdom was invaded by the Satsuma Domain in 1609 and annexed by Meiji Japan in 1879 as Okinawa Prefecture. The islands were the scene of the Battle of Okinawa in 1945 and were administered by the United States until reversion to Japan in 1972.

Culture

The islands have a distinct culture and the endangered Ryukyuan languages, related to but distinct from Japanese, as well as traditional music, dance, karate (which originated in Okinawa), and the Gusuku castle sites, listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
