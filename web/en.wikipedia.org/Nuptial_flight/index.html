Nuptial flight

A nuptial flight is the mating flight of many ant, termite and some bee species, in which virgin queens and males (alates) leave their colonies, often in large synchronised swarms, mate in the air, and then disperse to found new colonies.

Process

Flights are often triggered by weather — warm, humid conditions after rain — and timed so that colonies across a region swarm simultaneously, overwhelming predators and promoting outbreeding. After mating, males die; fertilised queens land, shed their wings and excavate a first chamber, raising the founding brood on stored body reserves.

In popular awareness

In the United Kingdom, mass emergences of flying ants (mainly Lasius niger) on hot summer days are popularly known as "flying ant day", though they occur over several weeks and locations rather than a single day.
