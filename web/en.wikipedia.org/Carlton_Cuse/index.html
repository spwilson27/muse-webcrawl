Arthur Carlton Cuse (born March 22, 1959) is an American screenwriter, producer, and showrunner. He is best known as the co-showrunner, with Damon Lindelof, of the ABC drama series Lost (2004–2010), one of the most influential television series of its era.

Cuse co-ran Lost and later developed Bates Motel and The Strain.

Early life and education

Cuse was born in Mexico City to an American father who worked in Mexico for his own father; the family later moved to Boston and then to Orange County, California. He attended The Putney School in Vermont and Harvard University, where he rowed crew and began writing.

Career

Cuse began in film, working as an assistant and development executive, and co-wrote the screenplay for The Adventures of Ford Fairlane (1990). In television he co-created the series The Adventures of Brisco County, Jr. (1993) and Nash Bridges (1996), and developed Martial Law (1998).

He joined Lost in its first season and, with Lindelof, steered the show's mythology-driven storytelling, winning Emmy Awards and helping to popularize serialized network drama and transmedia storytelling. After Lost, Cuse co-created Bates Motel (2013–2017), a contemporary prequel to Psycho; co-created The Strain (2014–2017) with Guillermo del Toro and Chuck Hogan; and helped develop the Amazon series Jack Ryan (2018–2023) and the Netflix series Locke & Key (2020–2022).
