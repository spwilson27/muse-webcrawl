Cairn Gorm (Scottish Gaelic: An Càrn Gorm, "the blue cairn") is a mountain in the Scottish Highlands overlooking Strathspey and the town of Aviemore. At 1,245 metres it is the sixth-highest mountain in Scotland and the United Kingdom, and it gives its name to the whole Cairngorms mountain range and national park.

Geography and climate

The mountain is famous for its severe weather; its summit weather station records some of the strongest winds in Britain, including a gust of 173 mph measured in 1986. The northern corries, including Coire an t-Sneachda and Coire an Lochain, hold snow well into summer and are major centres for climbing and ski mountaineering. The broad summit plateau is an arctic-alpine environment noted for its rare plants and dotterel breeding habitat.

Skiing and the funicular

The Cairngorm Mountain ski area on the northern slopes was one of Scotland's first and remains among its largest ski resorts. The Cairngorm Mountain Railway, a funicular opened in 2001, is the highest railway in the United Kingdom, carrying visitors to the Ptarmigan station near the summit. The railway was closed for several years from 2018 owing to structural concerns before reopening after remedial works.

Ascent

The usual route of ascent begins at the ski area car park in Coire Cas and follows constructed paths to the summit cairn. Cairn Gorm is classified as a Munro and is frequently climbed in combination with neighbouring peaks across the plateau, in conditions that demand full navigation skills in poor visibility.
