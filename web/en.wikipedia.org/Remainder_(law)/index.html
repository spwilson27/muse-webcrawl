Remainder (law)

In property law, a remainder is a future interest given to a third party (the remainderman) that becomes possessory when a preceding estate — typically a life estate — naturally ends. For example, if property is granted "to A for life, then to B", B holds a remainder.

Types

A remainder is vested when it is given to an ascertained person, ready to take effect whenever the prior estate ends, and subject to no condition precedent. It is contingent when the taker is unascertained (for example, "A's children" before any exist) or when it depends on a condition other than the natural termination of the prior estate.

Related concepts

Remainders are distinguished from reversions, which return to the grantor, and from executory interests, which cut short a preceding estate rather than waiting for it to end. The classification of future interests historically carried consequences under doctrines such as the rule in Shelley's case and the doctrine of worthier title.
