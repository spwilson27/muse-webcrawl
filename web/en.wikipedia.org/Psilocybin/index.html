Psilocybin is a naturally occurring psychedelic compound produced by more than 200 species of fungi, commonly known as psilocybin mushrooms or "magic mushrooms". It is a prodrug: after ingestion, the body converts it to psilocin, which acts principally as an agonist at serotonin 5-HT2A receptors in the brain and is responsible for the psychedelic effects.

Psilocybin-containing mushrooms have been used in indigenous religious and healing rituals in Mesoamerica for centuries. They were introduced to Western audiences in the 1950s, notably through R. Gordon Wasson's 1957 Life magazine account, and the compound was isolated and identified by the Swiss chemist Albert Hofmann in 1958. Research in the 1950s and 1960s was curtailed when psilocybin was made a controlled substance in most countries around 1970.

Effects

Effects typically begin within 20–40 minutes of ingestion and last roughly 4–6 hours, depending on dose. They include altered perception of time and space, visual and auditory changes, emotional intensification, synesthesia, and experiences described as mystical-type. Adverse effects can include anxiety, panic, nausea, and, rarely, more prolonged psychological disturbance. Psilocybin has low physiological toxicity and is not considered addictive; tolerance develops rapidly with repeated use.

Research

Since the 2000s, clinical research has revived, with studies at institutions such as Johns Hopkins University and Imperial College London investigating psilocybin-assisted therapy for depression, anxiety associated with life-threatening illness, obsessive-compulsive disorder, and substance use disorders. Trials have reported durable reductions in depressive symptoms for some patients after one or two supervised doses. In 2018 and 2019, the US Food and Drug Administration granted "breakthrough therapy" designations to psilocybin therapy for treatment-resistant depression and major depressive disorder.

Legal status

Psilocybin is listed in Schedule I of the UN 1971 Convention on Psychotropic Substances and is illegal in most countries. Some jurisdictions have decriminalized possession or regulated therapeutic use; Oregon voters approved supervised psilocybin services in 2020, and Colorado followed with a regulated-access framework in 2022. In the Netherlands, psilocybin truffles are legally sold, while the mushrooms themselves are banned.
