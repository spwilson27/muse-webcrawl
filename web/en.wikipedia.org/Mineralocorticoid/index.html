Mineralocorticoid

Mineralocorticoids are a class of corticosteroid hormones that influence electrolyte and water balance, principally by regulating sodium retention and potassium excretion in the kidney. The primary mineralocorticoid is aldosterone.

Mineralocorticoid
Class: Corticosteroid
Main hormone: Aldosterone
Produced in: Zona glomerulosa of adrenal cortex
Receptor: Mineralocorticoid receptor

Function

Produced in the zona glomerulosa of the adrenal cortex, mineralocorticoids act on the distal tubules and collecting ducts of the kidney to increase sodium and water reabsorption and potassium and hydrogen ion excretion, thereby controlling blood volume and pressure. They are contrasted with glucocorticoids such as cortisol, which mainly affect metabolism and immunity.

Clinical aspects

Excess aldosterone causes hypertension and low potassium; deficiency contributes to salt wasting in Addison disease. Fludrocortisone is a synthetic replacement, while spironolactone and eplerenone block the mineralocorticoid receptor and are used in heart failure and hypertension.
