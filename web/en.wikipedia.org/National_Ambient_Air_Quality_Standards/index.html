National Ambient Air Quality Standards

Set byU.S. Environmental Protection Agency
Legal basisClean Air Act
Criteria pollutantsPM, ozone, CO, lead, SO2, NO2

The National Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS) are limits on outdoor air pollution in the United States, established by the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) under the Clean Air Act. They are the cornerstone of U.S. air quality regulation.

Criteria pollutants

The standards cover six "criteria" pollutants: particulate matter (PM2.5 and PM10), ground-level ozone, carbon monoxide, lead, sulfur dioxide and nitrogen dioxide. For each pollutant the EPA sets a primary standard at a level requisite to protect public health with an adequate margin of safety, and a secondary standard to protect public welfare, including visibility and ecosystems. By law the science must be reviewed at least every five years.

Implementation

States prepare State Implementation Plans to bring areas that exceed the standards ("nonattainment areas") into compliance, controlling emissions from power plants, vehicles and industry. The standards have been tightened repeatedly — for example, the annual PM2.5 standard was lowered to 9.0 µg/m³ in 2024 — and have driven substantial reductions in U.S. air pollution since the 1970s.
