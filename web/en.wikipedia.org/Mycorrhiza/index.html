Mycorrhiza

A mycorrhiza (from Greek mukēs, "fungus", and rhiza, "root"; plural mycorrhizae) is a mutualistic association between a fungus and a plant root. The fungus colonises the root and extends a network of fine hyphae far into the soil, greatly increasing the plant's access to water and mineral nutrients — especially phosphorus and nitrogen — while the plant supplies the fungus with sugars and lipids produced by photosynthesis. Mycorrhizal associations are ancient, dating back roughly 450 million years, and occur in some 80–90% of land plant species, making them one of the most important symbioses on Earth.

Fungal hyphae enveloping and penetrating plant roots extend the root system's reach through the soil.

Types

In arbuscular mycorrhizas, formed by fungi of the Glomeromycota with most herbaceous plants and many trees, fungal hyphae penetrate root cells and branch into tree-like structures called arbuscules where nutrients are exchanged; this is the oldest and most widespread type. In ectomycorrhizas, typical of pines, oaks, and birches, the fungus forms a sheath (mantle) around the root tip and a net of hyphae between — but not inside — the root cells; these fungi, many of them mushroom-forming basidiomycetes, also mobilise nutrients from organic matter. Other specialised types include ericoid mycorrhizas of heathers and the orchid mycorrhizas on which orchid seeds, lacking food reserves, entirely depend to germinate.

Ecological role

Shared fungal networks can link many plants of the same or different species, transferring nutrients and even chemical alarm signals between them — a phenomenon popularised as the "wood wide web," though the extent and adaptive significance of plant-to-plant transfers remain debated. Mycorrhizal fungi also improve soil structure through the glycoprotein glomalin, store substantial carbon underground, and can protect plants against pathogens and drought. In agriculture and forestry, seedlings are often inoculated with mycorrhizal fungi, while heavy fertilisation and tillage can suppress the symbiosis.
