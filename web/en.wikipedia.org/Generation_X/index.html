Generation X

Generation X (often shortened to Gen X) is the demographic cohort following the baby boomers and preceding the millennials. Researchers and popular media generally use the mid-1960s as its starting birth years and the late 1970s to early 1980s as its ending birth years; the Pew Research Center defines the cohort as those born from 1965 to 1980. The term was popularised by Douglas Coupland's 1991 novel Generation X: Tales for an Accelerated Culture.

Characteristics

Gen X came of age in the 1980s and 1990s, during the end of the Cold War, the rise of personal computing and MTV, and economic shifts including the decline of lifetime manufacturing employment. Because many grew up with two working parents or divorced parents, they were dubbed the "latchkey generation", and stereotyped in the media of the early 1990s as cynical, disaffected "slackers" — an image associated with grunge music and films such as Slacker (1990) and Reality Bites (1994). Research instead often describes the cohort as entrepreneurial and sceptical of institutions: Gen Xers founded a large share of the dot-com era's start-ups and, in midlife, have tended to report relatively high life satisfaction despite squeezed finances between boomer parents and millennial children.

Population

In the United States the cohort numbers roughly 65 million people, smaller than both the baby boomer and millennial generations because of lower birth rates after 1964. Equivalent cohorts exist in other Western countries, though formative events differ — in the United Kingdom, for example, Gen X childhoods spanned the Thatcher era.
