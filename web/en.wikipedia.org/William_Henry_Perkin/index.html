William Henry Perkin

Born12 March 1838, London, England
Died14 July 1907 (aged 69), Sudbury, England
Known forDiscovery of mauveine, the first synthetic dye
AwardsRoyal Medal; Davy Medal; knighted 1906

Sir William Henry Perkin (12 March 1838 – 14 July 1907) was a British chemist who, at age 18, accidentally discovered mauveine — the first synthetic organic dye — while attempting to synthesize the antimalarial drug quinine in 1856.

Discovery of mauve

Working in a home laboratory during the Easter holidays from his studies under August Wilhelm von Hofmann at the Royal College of Chemistry, Perkin oxidized aniline and obtained a purple substance that dyed silk brilliantly. Recognizing its commercial potential, he patented it, left the college, and with his father and brother built a factory at Greenford Green producing the dye marketed as "mauve."

Later career

Perkin went on to develop other synthetic dyes, synthesize coumarin (the first synthetic perfume), and make lasting contributions to organic chemistry before retiring from industry in 1874 to devote himself to research.
