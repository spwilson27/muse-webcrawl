The French car manufacturer Renault has a long and influential history in Formula One, as a constructor, engine supplier, and innovator. Renault's works team competed in several eras — 1977–1985, 2002–2011, and 2016–2020 — before the team was rebranded as Alpine in 2021.

Turbo pioneers (1977–1985)

Renault entered Formula One at the 1977 British Grand Prix with the RS01, the first car powered by a turbocharged engine. Initially derided as the "yellow teapot" for its frequent smoky failures, the 1.5-litre V6 turbo won its first race at the 1979 French Grand Prix with Jean-Pierre Jabouille, while René Arnoux took a famous wheel-to-wheel third place in the same race. Alain Prost came close to the championship in 1983. Although Renault never won a title in this era, the turbo concept it pioneered came to dominate the sport, and Renault engines later powered championship-winning cars of other teams.

Engine supplier success

Returning as an engine supplier with Williams in 1989, Renault engines powered a string of dominant cars: Nigel Mansell (1992), Alain Prost (1993), Michael Schumacher at Benetton (1995), Damon Hill (1996), and Jacques Villeneuve (1997) all won drivers' championships with Renault power, and Renault won six consecutive constructors' championships from 1992 to 1997. Renault engines returned to championship success powering Red Bull Racing's four consecutive titles with Sebastian Vettel in 2010–2013.

Works team and titles (2002–2011)

Renault bought the Benetton team in 2000 and rebranded it Renault F1 Team for 2002. Under Flavio Briatore, with Fernando Alonso, the team won both the constructors' and drivers' championships in 2005 and 2006, ending the Ferrari–Schumacher era. The period ended amid the 2008 "Crashgate" scandal, in which Nelson Piquet Jr. was ordered to crash deliberately at the Singapore Grand Prix to aid Alonso's victory; the affair led to bans for team management and Renault's sale of a majority stake, the team becoming Lotus Renault GP in 2011.

Return and Alpine era (2016– )

Renault reacquired the Enstone team and returned as a works constructor in 2016. Results were modest — its best finish was fourth in the constructors' standings — and for 2021 the team was rebranded Alpine, Renault's sports-car marque, with Renault remaining the engine supplier based at Viry-Châtillon.
