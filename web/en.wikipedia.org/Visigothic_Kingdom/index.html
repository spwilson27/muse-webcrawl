The Visigothic Kingdom, officially the Kingdom of the Visigoths (Latin: Regnum Visigothorum), was a kingdom that occupied what is now southwestern France and the Iberian Peninsula from the 5th to the 8th centuries. One of the Germanic successor states to the Western Roman Empire, it was originally created by the settlement of the Visigoths under King Wallia in the province of Gallia Aquitania in southwest Gaul by the Roman government in 418.

Kingdom of Toulouse

From their capital at Toulouse, the Visigoths expanded their rule, and under Euric (466–484) the kingdom became effectively independent of Rome and reached into Iberia. In 507, however, the Franks under Clovis I defeated the Visigoths at the Battle of Vouillé and killed King Alaric II; the Visigoths lost most of their Gallic territory, retaining only Septimania in the southeast.

Kingdom of Toledo

The kingdom's centre of gravity shifted to Hispania, with the capital eventually at Toledo. King Leovigild (568–586) subdued much of the peninsula, conquering the Suevic Kingdom of Galicia in 585. His son Reccared I converted from Arianism to Catholicism at the Third Council of Toledo in 589, aligning the monarchy with the Hispano-Roman majority. Recceswinth's Liber Iudiciorum (654) created a single territorial law code for Goths and Romans alike.

Fall

In 711, an Umayyad Muslim army under Tariq ibn Ziyad defeated King Roderic at the Battle of Guadalete. Within a few years most of the peninsula was under Muslim control, ending the Visigothic Kingdom; Christian resistance in the north eventually gave rise to the Kingdom of Asturias and the Reconquista.
