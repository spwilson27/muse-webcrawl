Sky island

A sky island is an isolated mountain or mountain range whose high-elevation habitats are separated from similar habitats by intervening lowlands with very different environments, acting as an “island” of cool, moist habitat in a “sea” of hot, dry terrain.

Characteristics

Sky islands are notable for endemism and for harbouring relict species left behind as climates warmed after the last glacial period, when woodland extended continuously across today's deserts. The best-known examples are the Madrean sky islands of southeastern Arizona, southwestern New Mexico, and northern Mexico, where ranges such as the Chiricahuas and Santa Catalinas rise from Sonoran and Chihuahuan desert. Other examples occur in the Great Basin, East Africa (such as Mount Mulanje), and Southeast Asia.

Biogeography

Because populations on separate sky islands evolve in isolation, they show patterns similar to oceanic island biogeography, including genetic divergence between neighbouring ranges. Sky islands are vulnerable to climate change, since warming pushes suitable habitat ever higher until it disappears from the summits, and to barriers such as highways that block the occasional dispersal between ranges.
