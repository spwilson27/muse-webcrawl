Paul Verhoeven

Paul Verhoeven (born 18 July 1938) is a Dutch filmmaker known for blending graphic violence, sexuality, and social satire. His films include the Dutch classics Turkish Delight (1973) and Soldier of Orange (1977), and the Hollywood hits RoboCop (1987), Total Recall (1990), Basic Instinct (1992), and Starship Troopers (1997).

Career

Born in Amsterdam, Verhoeven studied mathematics and physics at Leiden University before turning to film in the Royal Dutch Navy's film unit. In the Netherlands he became the country's most commercially successful director. Moving to Hollywood in the 1980s, he directed Flesh and Blood (1985) and then the satirical science-fiction hit RoboCop. Showgirls (1995) was a notorious flop later reappraised as a cult film.

Returning to Europe, he directed Black Book (2006) in the Netherlands and the French-language Elle (2016), which won the Golden Globe for Best Foreign Language Film, followed by Benedetta (2021).

Style

Verhoeven's work is marked by explicit content used to critique media, militarism, and commercialism, and by a career-long interest in the historical Jesus, the subject of his book Jesus of Nazareth (2007).
