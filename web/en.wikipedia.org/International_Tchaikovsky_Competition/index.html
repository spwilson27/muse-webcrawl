International Tchaikovsky Competition

The International Tchaikovsky Competition is a classical music competition held every four years in Moscow, Russia, for pianists, violinists, cellists and singers, with later additions including a junior competition and categories for wind and brass instruments. First held in 1958, it is named after the composer Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky and is widely regarded as one of the most prestigious competitions in classical music.

History

The competition was established amid the cultural thaw of the Soviet Union, with the pianist Emil Gilels chairing the organising committee and later the piano jury. The first piano prize in 1958 went, sensationally, to the young American Van Cliburn, whose victory at the height of the Cold War made him an international celebrity. Subsequent first-prize winners in piano have included Vladimir Ashkenazy (shared, 1962), Mikhail Pletnev (1978), Daniil Trifonov (2011) and Seong-Jin Cho was among the leading prizewinners in cello-era expansions; the cello competition's laureates include David Geringas and, as a shared winner in 1962, Natalia Shakhovskaya, while violin winners include Gidon Kremer (1970).

Format

The competition consists of preliminary auditions and three live rounds in Moscow, with finalists in the instrumental categories performing concertos with orchestra in the Grand Hall of the Moscow Conservatory. Categories since 2019 include piano, violin, cello, voice, woodwinds and brass. The competition is a member of the World Federation of International Music Competitions. After 2022, following the Russian invasion of Ukraine, the competition was suspended from that federation, and the 2023 (17th) edition proceeded with a largely Russian and allied-country field.
