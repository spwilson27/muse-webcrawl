Drill press

A drill press is a fixed machine tool used for drilling holes in workpiece materials, in which the rotating spindle and chuck are fed into the work with a lever-operated quill. Unlike a hand-held drill, the drill press holds the cutting tool precisely perpendicular to the work, allowing accurate, repeatable holes.

Construction

The machine consists of a heavy base, a column, an adjustable work table, and a head containing the motor, pulleys or electronic speed control, spindle, and chuck. The quill is advanced by rotating a feed handle, and a depth stop limits the travel. Belt-driven models change speed by moving the belt between stepped pulleys; variable-speed models use a dial control.

Types

Common forms include benchtop models, larger floor-standing models, radial drill presses whose head slides along an arm for large workpieces, and magnetic drill presses that attach directly to steel structures. Drill presses are used with twist drills, Forstner bits, hole saws, and other cutters, and can also perform operations such as countersinking, reaming, and tapping with suitable attachments.

Use and safety

Work is normally clamped or held in a drill press vise to prevent it spinning if the bit binds. Correct speed depends on bit diameter and material, and cutting fluids are used for many metals.
