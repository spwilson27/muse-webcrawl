Leif Erikson (Old Norse: Leifr Eiríksson; c. 970 – c. 1018–1025) was a Norse explorer from Iceland, the son of Erik the Red, founder of the first Norse settlements in Greenland. According to the Icelandic sagas, he led an expedition around the year 1000 that reached lands in North America he called Vinland — nearly five centuries before Christopher Columbus.

The Vinland voyages

The Saga of the Greenlanders and the Saga of Erik the Red give differing accounts: in one, Leif bought the ship of Bjarni Herjólfsson, who had sighted unknown lands to the west after being blown off course; in the other, Leif himself was blown off course returning from Norway. Both agree that his party landed at a stony land (Helluland, likely Baffin Island), a forested land (Markland, likely Labrador), and finally Vinland, where they built houses and overwintered, finding wild grapes or berries and abundant timber and fish.

Evidence and legacy

The Norse site at L'Anse aux Meadows in Newfoundland, excavated in the 1960s by Helge and Anne Stine Ingstad, is widely accepted as the archaeological remains of these voyages, confirming a pre-Columbian European presence in the Americas. Leif is commemorated by statues in Iceland, Greenland and the United States, and Leif Erikson Day is observed on 9 October in the US. His brother Thorvald and sister Freydís also feature in the saga accounts of subsequent voyages.
