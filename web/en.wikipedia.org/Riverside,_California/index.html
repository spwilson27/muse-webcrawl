Riverside is a city in, and the county seat of, Riverside County, California, in the Inland Empire metropolitan area. Named for its location beside the Santa Ana River, it is the most populous city in the Inland Empire and among the largest cities in California, with a population of about 315,000 at the 2020 census.

History

Riverside was founded in 1870 by John W. North as a planned utopian colony. Its prosperity was transformed by the citrus industry: in 1873 Eliza Tibbets planted two Brazilian navel orange trees — descendants of which still stand — and the Washington navel orange launched California's citrus boom. By the 1880s Riverside was among the wealthiest cities per capita in the United States. The eccentric Mission Inn, built in stages from 1876 and grown into a fantastical complex by Frank Miller, became a landmark hosting presidents and celebrities.

Today

The city is home to the University of California, Riverside, California Baptist University and La Sierra University, as well as the California Citrus State Historic Park, which preserves the legacy of the groves. Landmarks include Mount Rubidoux, the Fox Performing Arts Center and one of the largest collections of mission-revival architecture in the state. Riverside is served by Metrolink commuter rail and lies near March Air Reserve Base.
