The history of general anesthesia traces efforts from antiquity to render patients insensible for surgery. Ancient physicians used alcohol, opium, mandrake and cannabis, but these were unreliable and often dangerous.

The inhalational breakthrough

In 1842 Georgia physician Crawford Long used diethyl ether for a tumour removal but did not publish. Dentist Horace Wells experimented with nitrous oxide in 1844. On 16 October 1846, William T. G. Morton publicly demonstrated ether anesthesia at Massachusetts General Hospital — the famous "Ether Dome" demonstration — and news spread worldwide within months. In Scotland, James Young Simpson introduced chloroform in 1847, popular for obstetrics after John Snow administered it to Queen Victoria in 1853.

Professionalisation

The late 19th and 20th centuries added local anesthetics (cocaine, 1884), spinal anesthesia, intravenous agents such as thiopental (1934), muscle relaxants (curare introduced clinically in 1942), and modern volatile agents (halothane 1956, then isoflurane, sevoflurane and desflurane). Anesthesiology became a distinct specialty, with monitoring standards — pulse oximetry and capnography — dramatically improving safety from the 1980s.
