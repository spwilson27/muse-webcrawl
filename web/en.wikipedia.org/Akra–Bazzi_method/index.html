The Akra–Bazzi method, or Akra–Bazzi theorem, is used in the analysis of algorithms to determine the asymptotic behaviour of recurrences that arise in divide-and-conquer algorithms where the subproblems have unequal sizes. It is a generalisation of the master theorem, which requires equal subproblem sizes.

Formulation

The method applies to recurrences of the form:

T(x) = g(x) + Σi=1..k ai T(bi x + hi(x))

where the ai are positive constants, each bi lies strictly between 0 and 1, g(x) is a reasonably smooth driving function, and the perturbation terms hi(x) grow at most polynomially slower than x.

One first finds the unique real number p such that Σ ai bip = 1. The asymptotic behaviour is then:

T(x) = Θ( xp ( 1 + ∫1x g(u) / up+1 du ) )

Example

For the recurrence T(x) = (9/4) T(3x/4) + T(x/2) + x2, the exponent p satisfies (9/4)(3/4)p + (1/2)p = 1, giving p = 2, and evaluating the integral yields T(x) = Θ(x2 log x).

Significance

Unlike the master theorem, the Akra–Bazzi method tolerates unequal splits, floors and ceilings, and certain perturbations in the subproblem sizes, making it applicable to recurrences such as those produced by quickselect, integer multiplication variants and median-of-medians selection. The result was proved by Mohamad Akra and Louay Bazzi in 1998; a simplified proof was later given by Tom Leighton in course notes, and a rigorous justification appears in standard references on analytic combinatorics.
