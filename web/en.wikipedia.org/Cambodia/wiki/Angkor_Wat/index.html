Angkor Wat (Khmer: អង្គរវត្ត, "City Temple") is a Hindu-Buddhist temple complex in Cambodia and the largest religious monument in the world, covering an area of 162.6 hectares. It was built in the first half of the 12th century by the Khmer king Suryavarman II as his state temple and eventual mausoleum, dedicated to the Hindu god Vishnu.

Unlike most Angkorian temples, Angkor Wat is oriented to the west. It is admired for the grandeur and harmony of its architecture, its extensive bas-reliefs, and the numerous guardian spirits adorning its walls. The temple appears on the national flag of Cambodia and is the country's prime attraction for visitors.

Architecture

The temple is a representation of Mount Meru, home of the gods in Hindu mythology: the central towers symbolise its five peaks, the outer wall the mountains enclosing the world, and the moat the ocean beyond. The complex is surrounded by a moat 190 metres wide and more than 3.6 kilometres in perimeter, enclosing a rectangular wall 1.5 km by 1.3 km.

Three concentric rectangular galleries rise toward the central tower, which stands 65 metres above the ground. The outer gallery is famous for nearly 800 metres of bas-reliefs depicting scenes from the Ramayana and Mahabharata, historical processions, heavens and hells, and the Churning of the Sea of Milk.

History

Construction is estimated to have taken some 30 years, using roughly 300,000 workers and as many as 5 million sandstone blocks quarried at Mount Kulen, 40 km away, and floated down the Siem Reap River on rafts. After Suryavarman's death, the temple was sacked by the Cham in 1177, and the capital area was later rebuilt by Jayavarman VII.

In the 14th or 15th century the temple was gradually converted to Theravada Buddhist use, which continues today. Unlike other Angkor temples it was never completely abandoned, partly because its moat protected it from jungle encroachment. The Portuguese monk António da Madalena described it in 1586, and the French naturalist Henri Mouhot's accounts in the 1860s popularised it in the West.

Restoration and tourism

Conservation efforts began under the École française d'Extrême-Orient in the early 20th century and continue under Cambodia's APSARA Authority with international partners. Angkor was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1992. Tourism grew rapidly in the 2000s, reaching over two million visitors a year at its peak.
