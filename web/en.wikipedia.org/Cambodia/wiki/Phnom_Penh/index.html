Phnom Penh (Khmer: ភ្នំពេញ) is the capital and most populous city of Cambodia, located at the confluence of the Tonlé Sap, Mekong, and Bassac rivers. It has been the national capital since the French colonisation of Cambodia and has become the nation's economic, industrial, and cultural centre.

Once known as the "Pearl of Asia," Phnom Penh was considered one of the loveliest French-built cities in Indochina in the 1920s. The city suffered catastrophic harm during the Cambodian Civil War and was forcibly evacuated by the Khmer Rouge in 1975; it has since recovered and grown rapidly.

History

Legend attributes the founding to a wealthy widow named Penh, who in 1372 found a tree containing Buddha statues floating in the river and built a temple on an artificial hill (phnom) to house them — Wat Phnom, which still stands near the city centre. King Ponhea Yat moved the capital here from Angkor Thom in 1434.

Phnom Penh remained the capital through periods of decline, Siamese and Vietnamese incursions, and French protectorate rule from 1863. After independence in 1953, the city expanded under King Norodom Sihanouk's "New Khmer Architecture" movement. Civil war and the Khmer Rouge regime (1975–1979) emptied the city; repopulation began after 1979, and the 1990s brought reconstruction and rapid high-rise development.

Geography and climate

The city lies in south-central Cambodia on the banks of three rivers, in a low-lying floodplain that rises only slightly above river level. It has a tropical wet and dry climate, with a monsoon season from May to November.

Landmarks

Notable sites include the Royal Palace and Silver Pagoda, the National Museum, Wat Phnom, the Art Deco Central Market (Phsar Thmei), the Independence Monument, and the Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum and Choeung Ek memorial, which document the genocide of 1975–79. The annual Water Festival (Bon Om Touk) draws millions for boat races on the Tonlé Sap.

Economy and transport

Phnom Penh is Cambodia's commercial hub, with growing garment, tourism, finance, and real-estate sectors. It is served by Phnom Penh International Airport, river ports, and rail links to Sihanoukville and Poipet. Road traffic is dominated by motorbikes, tuk-tuks, and cyclos.
