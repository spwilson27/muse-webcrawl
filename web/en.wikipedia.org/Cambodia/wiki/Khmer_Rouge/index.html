The Khmer Rouge (Khmer: ខ្មែរក្រហម, "Red Khmer") is the name that was popularly given to members of the Communist Party of Kampuchea and by extension to the regime through which that party ruled Cambodia between 1975 and 1979. The movement was formed in 1968 as an offshoot of the Vietnam People's Army and fought a civil war against the US-backed Khmer Republic of Lon Nol.

After seizing Phnom Penh on 17 April 1975, the Khmer Rouge under Pol Pot attempted to transform Cambodia into a classless agrarian utopia. Cities were emptied, money and private property abolished, and the population forced into collective farms. The resulting Cambodian genocide claimed an estimated 1.5 to 2 million lives from execution, starvation, disease, and overwork — roughly a quarter of the population.

Origins

The Cambodian communist movement grew out of the Indochinese Communist Party founded in 1930. Many future leaders, including Pol Pot (Saloth Sâr), studied in Paris in the 1950s, where they were exposed to Stalinist and Maoist thought. The party's insurgency gained momentum after 1970, when Prince Norodom Sihanouk was deposed and Cambodia was drawn into the Vietnam War; massive US bombing of the countryside devastated communities and drove recruits to the movement.

Democratic Kampuchea

The regime proclaimed the abolition of markets, schools, pagodas, and currency, and declared 1975 "Year Zero." Citizens were divided into "base people" and urban "new people," the latter treated as suspect class enemies. Intellectuals, professionals, ethnic minorities (including Cham Muslims and Vietnamese), and former officials were targeted for elimination. The S-21 security prison at Tuol Sleng in Phnom Penh processed at least 14,000 prisoners, almost all of whom were executed at the Choeung Ek killing fields.

Fall and aftermath

Border clashes with Vietnam escalated into war; Vietnamese forces invaded in December 1978 and captured Phnom Penh on 7 January 1979, driving the Khmer Rouge to the Thai border. The movement waged a guerrilla war through the 1980s and formally dissolved in 1999 after Pol Pot's death in 1998. Beginning in 2006, the Extraordinary Chambers in the Courts of Cambodia tried senior leaders; Nuon Chea and Khieu Samphan were convicted of genocide and crimes against humanity.
