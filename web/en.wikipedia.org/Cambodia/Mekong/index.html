The Mekong or Mekong River is a transboundary river in East and Southeast Asia. Rising on the Tibetan Plateau in China's Qinghai province, it flows about 4,350 km (2,700 mi) through Myanmar, Laos, Thailand, Cambodia and Vietnam, emptying into the South China Sea through the Mekong Delta. It is the world's twelfth-longest river and one of its most biologically productive: the Lower Mekong Basin supports one of the largest inland fisheries on Earth and the livelihoods of tens of millions of people.

Course

In China the river is called the Lancang Jiang. Flowing south, it forms borders between Myanmar, Laos and Thailand — the "Golden Triangle" region — before entering Laos and Cambodia. Below Phnom Penh it splits into the Mekong and the Bassac, feeding the Tonlé Sap Great Lake system, whose flow actually reverses with the monsoon flood pulse. South of Cambodia, the river spreads into the vast, rice-growing Mekong Delta of Vietnam.

Ecology

The Mekong hosts exceptional biodiversity — over a thousand fish species, including the giant Mekong catfish and the critically endangered Irrawaddy dolphin — second only to the Amazon in richness. Its annual flood pulse drives the Tonlé Sap fishery, among the world's most productive.

Development issues

Hydropower dams on the mainstream and tributaries — notably in China (the Lancang cascade) and Laos (e.g. Xayaburi) — together with sand mining, climate change and delta subsidence, have made the Mekong a focus of regional tension and environmental concern. The Mekong River Commission (1995) coordinates among Cambodia, Laos, Thailand and Vietnam, with China and Myanmar as dialogue partners.
