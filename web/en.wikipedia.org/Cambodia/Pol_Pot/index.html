Pol Pot (born Saloth Sâr; 19 May 1925 – 15 April 1998) was a Cambodian revolutionary and dictator who led the Khmer Rouge from 1963 until his death in 1998. As leader of the Communist Party of Kampuchea, he ruled Cambodia as Prime Minister of Democratic Kampuchea from 1976 to 1979. His regime's radical attempt to create an agrarian socialist society resulted in the Cambodian genocide, in which an estimated 1.5 to 2 million people — roughly a quarter of Cambodia's population — died from execution, forced labour, starvation and disease.

Early life

Saloth Sâr was born to a prosperous farming family in Kompong Thom province and educated in Phnom Penh and, from 1949, in Paris, where he joined the French Communist Party and associated with other Cambodian radicals (the "Paris student group"). Returning to Cambodia in 1953, he became a leader of the clandestine communist movement, fleeing to the countryside in 1963 and building a guerrilla army, the Khmer Rouge.

Rule

After five years of civil war, the Khmer Rouge seized Phnom Penh on 17 April 1975. The regime immediately evacuated the cities, abolished money, markets, private property, religion and formal education, and forced the population into agricultural cooperatives under the secretive leadership known as Angkar ("the Organisation"). Waves of purges consumed party cadres and targeted ethnic minorities, intellectuals and former officials; the S-21 prison at Tuol Sleng processed at least 14,000 prisoners, almost all of whom were killed.

Fall and death

Border clashes with Vietnam provoked a Vietnamese invasion in December 1978; Phnom Penh fell on 7 January 1979, and Pol Pot fled to the Thai border, where the Khmer Rouge waged guerrilla war for two more decades. In 1997 the movement turned on him: he was placed under house arrest by his former comrade Ta Mok after ordering the murder of Son Sen. He died on 15 April 1998, reportedly of heart failure, just as arrangements were being made to hand him to an international tribunal. Senior Khmer Rouge leaders were later convicted of crimes against humanity by the UN-backed Extraordinary Chambers in the Courts of Cambodia.
