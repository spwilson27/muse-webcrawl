Cambodian genocide

The Cambodian genocide was the systematic persecution and killing of Cambodians by the Khmer Rouge under the leadership of Pol Pot, General Secretary of the Communist Party of Kampuchea. It resulted in the deaths of 1.5 million to 2 million people between 1975 and 1979, nearly a quarter of Cambodia's population in 1975, estimated at between 7 and 8 million.

Background

The Khmer Rouge came to power at the end of the Cambodian Civil War, capturing Phnom Penh on 17 April 1975 after years of conflict in which the United States had heavily bombed the Cambodian countryside. The new regime, Democratic Kampuchea, proclaimed "Year Zero" and set out to remake society completely along agrarian-communist lines, abolishing money, markets, religion, and private property.

Democratic Kampuchea

The population of Phnom Penh and other cities was forcibly evacuated to the countryside, where "new people" (urban evacuees) were set to work in collective farms under harsh conditions; starvation, disease, and overwork killed huge numbers. The regime purged perceived enemies — former officials, soldiers of the Lon Nol regime, intellectuals (classed as such for wearing glasses or speaking a foreign language), ethnic Vietnamese, Cham Muslims, and Buddhist monks — many of them executed in mass killings at sites known as "Killing Fields" or tortured to death in prisons such as S-21 (Tuol Sleng) in Phnom Penh, where of an estimated 14,000–17,000 prisoners only a handful survived.

Death toll

Estimates of the death toll range from about 1.5 million to 3 million. The most commonly cited scholarly figure is around 1.7 to 2 million deaths, from a 1975 population of roughly 7–8 million — between a fifth and a quarter of the country's people — from execution, starvation, disease, and forced labour.

Prosecution

The Khmer Rouge remained in the jungle as an insurgency after Vietnam's invasion drove them from power in January 1979. Pol Pot died in 1998 without facing trial. The Extraordinary Chambers in the Courts of Cambodia (Khmer Rouge Tribunal), established jointly with the United Nations, convicted senior leaders including "Duch" (Kaing Guek Eav), Nuon Chea, and Khieu Samphan of crimes against humanity and genocide.
