Small cap

In typography, small caps (short for "small capitals") are lowercase characters typeset as smaller versions of the capital letters. They are commonly used in text for acronyms, initialisms and certain headings, where full capitals would appear too emphatic, and in some styles for the first line or words of a chapter.

Design

Well-designed small caps are not simply scaled-down capitals: their stroke weights and proportions are adjusted so they harmonise with lowercase text, appearing slightly taller than the x-height. Many OpenType fonts include true small caps accessible via font features; where these are absent, software may synthesise "faux" small caps by shrinking capitals, with noticeably thinner strokes.

Usage

Small caps are used in bibliographies, legal documents for party names, in some style guides for terms such as BC and AD, and traditionally for the name of God (the LORD) in some Bible translations. In CSS they can be invoked with the font-variant property value small-caps.
