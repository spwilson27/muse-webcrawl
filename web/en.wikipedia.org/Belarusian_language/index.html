Belarusian language

Belarusian (беларуская мова, biełaruskaja mova) is an East Slavic language spoken by the Belarusians. It is one of the two official languages of Belarus, alongside Russian. It is also spoken by Belarusians in neighbouring countries, particularly Poland, Ukraine, Russia and Lithuania.

History

Modern Belarusian developed from the Ruthenian language used in the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, where it served as a chancery language. After the partitions of Poland, Belarusian was largely displaced by Russian and Polish in official use; it was revived in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, notably with the newspaper Nasha Niva, and codified in Branisłaŭ Taraškievič's 1918 grammar. A 1933 orthographic reform under Soviet rule brought the spelling closer to Russian; the pre-reform "classical" orthography (Taraškievica) remains in use by some speakers and publications.

Features

Belarusian is written in Cyrillic with the distinctive letters і and ў, and is characterised by akanye (the merger of unstressed vowels) and other phonetic features that are reflected in its phonetic spelling.
