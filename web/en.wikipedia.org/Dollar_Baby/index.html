Dollar Baby

A Dollar Baby (or Dollar Deal) is a short film adaptation of a short story by American author Stephen King, made under an informal arrangement by which King grants student and aspiring filmmakers permission to adapt certain of his short works for a fee of one dollar. The resulting films may be shown at festivals and used in showreels, but cannot be distributed commercially.

Origin

King began the practice in the late 1970s, reasoning that while he was unwilling to sell the film rights to his short stories outright, he was happy to let students experiment with them. The best known early Dollar Baby was Frank Darabont's adaptation of "The Woman in the Room" (1983), which impressed King and began a collaboration that led to The Shawshank Redemption, The Green Mile, and The Mist.

Terms and availability

The agreement is not a formal licence programme: King offers a list of stories not already under option, the filmmaker pays one dollar, and King retains all commercial rights. Because the films cannot be sold, most Dollar Babies circulate only at festivals or through occasional sanctioned collections and screenings. King himself keeps copies and has commented favourably on many of them.

Legacy

Dozens of Dollar Babies have been made, and the phenomenon has been documented in books and festival programmes devoted to the adaptations. In 2023 it was reported that King had ended the programme, honouring existing agreements but not accepting new applicants.
