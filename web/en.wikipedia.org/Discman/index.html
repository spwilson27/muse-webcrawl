Discman

Discman was a brand name used by Sony for its portable CD players, first introduced in 1984 with the model D-5 (D-50 in some markets). The name echoed Sony's successful Walkman cassette players, and "Discman" became a generic term for portable CD players generally.

History

The original D-5, released in November 1984, was the first portable CD player; it was roughly the size of two CD cases and cost about as much as a home CD player. Early models lacked effective shock protection and skipped when jostled. Through the 1990s, models gained electronic skip protection (marketed as ESP), longer battery life, and slimmer designs. Sony rebranded the line "CD Walkman" from 1997, though the Discman name remained in wide use.

Decline

Portable CD players peaked around the turn of the 2000s and were rapidly displaced by MP3 players—especially Apple's iPod from 2001—and later by smartphones. Production of portable CD players dwindled through the 2010s.
