Promontory is an area of high ground in Box Elder County, Utah, United States, about 51 km (32 miles) west of Brigham City and 107 km (66 miles) northwest of Salt Lake City. Rising to an elevation of about 1,470 m (4,900 ft) above sea level, it lies north of the Great Salt Lake, at the eastern end of the Promontory Mountains. The area is famous as the site of Promontory Summit, where the Union Pacific and Central Pacific railroads met on May 10, 1869, completing the first transcontinental railroad in the United States with the driving of the ceremonial Golden Spike.

History

The meeting point was fixed by compromise after the two companies, subsidized by the mile, graded parallel roadbeds past each other. On the day of the ceremony, Central Pacific president Leland Stanford and Union Pacific vice president Thomas Durant drove the final spike amid national celebration, linking Omaha to Sacramento by rail. The isolated site was never a substantial town, though a rough temporary settlement known as "Hell on Wheels" sprang up briefly. Through rail traffic was later rerouted over the Lucin Cutoff (1904), a trestle causeway across the Great Salt Lake, and the original line over the summit was eventually relegated to a backup route before its rails were removed in 1942 for wartime scrap.

Historic site

The location is preserved as the Golden Spike National Historical Park, administered by the National Park Service. The park features a visitor center, restored track, and working replicas of the two locomotives present at the ceremony — the Union Pacific No. 119 and the Central Pacific Jupiter — which are re-enacted nose-to-nose each year on the anniversary and during summer demonstrations.
