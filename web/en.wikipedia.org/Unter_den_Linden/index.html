Unter den Linden ("under the linden trees") is a boulevard in the central Mitte district of Berlin, the capital of Germany. It runs from the Brandenburg Gate at Pariser Platz eastward to the site of the Berlin Palace and Museum Island, and is one of the city's most famous and grandest avenues.

History

The street began as a bridle path from the royal palace to the hunting grounds of the Tiergarten. In 1647 Elector Frederick William ordered it planted with linden (lime) trees, giving the boulevard its name. Under Frederick the Great it was lined with monumental buildings, including the opera house, the royal library, and St. Hedwig's Cathedral. Heavily damaged in the Second World War, the street lay in East Berlin during the Cold War and was rebuilt after German reunification in 1990, regaining its role as a ceremonial and tourist axis.

Notable buildings

Landmarks along Unter den Linden include the equestrian statue of Frederick the Great, the Berlin State Opera, Humboldt University's main building, the Neue Wache memorial, the Zeughaus (now the German Historical Museum), the Russian embassy, and the Hotel Adlon near Pariser Platz. The boulevard is a traditional venue for parades, state ceremonies, and the annual Festival of Lights.
