The Wedding at Cana (Le nozze di Cana), by the Italian artist Paolo Veronese, is a representational painting that depicts the biblical story of the Wedding at Cana, at which Jesus miraculously converts water into wine (John 2:1–11). Executed in the Mannerist style, the large-format work (6.77 m × 9.94 m) is the largest painting in the collection of the Louvre Museum in Paris, where it hangs opposite the Mona Lisa.

Description

Veronese sets the biblical feast in the midst of a splendid sixteenth-century Venetian celebration. More than 130 figures crowd the scene around a U-shaped banqueting table beneath a classical colonnade, with a balustrade and Venetian skyline behind. Jesus and the Virgin Mary sit at the center of the table; contemporary notables, including — according to tradition — portraits of Eleanor of Austria, Francis I, Mary I of England, and the Ottoman Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent, are among the guests. A group of musicians in the foreground is said to include Veronese himself, Titian, Tintoretto, and Jacopo Bassano.

History

The painting was commissioned in 1562 by the Benedictine monks of the San Giorgio Monastery in Venice for their refectory, designed by Palladio. In 1797 it was seized by Napoleon's troops as war loot and taken to Paris. During the Second World War it was evacuated for safekeeping. A copy now hangs in the original refectory setting in Venice.
