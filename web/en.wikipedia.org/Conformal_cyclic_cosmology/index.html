Conformal cyclic cosmology

Conformal cyclic cosmology (CCC) is a cosmological model in the framework of general relativity, advanced by the theoretical physicist Roger Penrose in his 2010 book Cycles of Time: An Extraordinary New View of the Universe. In CCC, the universe iterates through infinite cycles, where the future timelike infinity of each previous cycle (or "aeon") is identified with the Big Bang singularity of the next.

The model

The identification is possible because, in the remote future, the universe is dominated by massless radiation, for which the notion of scale disappears, so the infinitely expanded future can be conformally rescaled to match the finitely small state at the Big Bang. Penrose argues that the model addresses the low entropy of the early universe, and predicts that traces of the previous aeon — such as concentric low-variance circles in the cosmic microwave background, and "Hawking points" from evaporated supermassive black holes — might be observable.

Reception

Claims of statistical evidence for such circles in WMAP and Planck satellite data have been disputed by other research groups, who find the features consistent with standard inflationary cosmology plus noise. CCC remains a minority, speculative proposal, though it has stimulated work on conformal geometry and the far future of the universe.
