Nuclear power

Nuclear power is the use of nuclear reactions to produce electricity. Nuclear power can be obtained from nuclear fission, nuclear decay and nuclear fusion reactions. Presently, the vast majority of electricity from nuclear power is produced by nuclear fission of uranium and plutonium in nuclear power plants.

History

The first commercial plants opened in the 1950s. Growth was rapid in the 1970s, then slowed after the Three Mile Island (1979), Chernobyl (1986) and Fukushima Daiichi (2011) accidents. Interest revived in the 2020s over climate change and energy security, including small modular reactors.

Technology

Most reactors are pressurised water or boiling water reactors. Heat from fission boils water, drives turbines, and generates electricity with low operational carbon emissions, but produces radioactive waste.

Debate

Supporters cite reliability and low emissions; critics cite cost, waste, proliferation and accident risk. France derives about two-thirds of its electricity from nuclear power, while Germany completed a phase-out in 2023.
