Tao

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Tao or Dao (Chinese: 道; pinyin: dào) is the natural order of the universe whose character one's intuition must discern to realize the potential for individual wisdom, as conceived in the context of East Asian philosophy, religion and related traditions. This seeing of life cannot be grasped as a concept; it is seen through actual living experience of one's everyday being.

In the older Wade–Giles romanization the word is spelled Tao, giving rise to the spelling "Taoism"; the modern pinyin spelling is Dao and "Daoism". The character 道 originally meant "way", "road" or "path", and by extension "method", "principle" or "doctrine".

In the Tao Te Ching

The concept is most famously expounded in the Tao Te Ching, attributed to Laozi: "The Tao that can be told is not the eternal Tao; the name that can be named is not the eternal name." The Tao is portrayed as the ineffable source and sustaining principle of the ten thousand things, acting without striving (wu wei) yet leaving nothing undone. The sage aligns himself with the Tao through simplicity, humility and non-contention.

Usage across traditions

The term was also used in Confucianism, where it denotes the right way of moral conduct and social order, and in Chinese Buddhism, where "dao" translated the Sanskrit bodhi and mārga. In Zhuangzi, the Tao is playful and perspectival, dissolving fixed distinctions between things.
