The Isley Brothers

The Isley Brothers are an American musical group originally from Cincinnati, Ohio, that began as a vocal trio consisting of the brothers O'Kelly Isley Jr., Rudolph Isley and Ronald Isley in the 1950s. With a career spanning over seven decades, the group has enjoyed one of the longest, most influential, and most diverse careers in popular music, with hit records in every decade from the 1950s to the 2000s — one of the few groups to chart on the Billboard Hot 100 in six consecutive decades.

Career

Their first hit was the call-and-response classic "Shout" (1959), followed by the Billboard top-20 hit "Twist and Shout" (1962, later covered by the Beatles) and the Motown hit "This Old Heart of Mine (Is Weak for You)" (1966). On their own T-Neck label they released the civil-rights-era funk anthem "It's Your Thing" (1969), which won the Grammy for Best R&B Vocal Performance by a Group.

In 1973 the group expanded to a six-member band with younger brothers Ernie (guitar) and Marvin (bass) and brother-in-law Chris Jasper (keyboards), launching a run of gold and platinum funk and quiet-storm albums including 3 + 3 (1973, featuring "That Lady" and their cover of "Summer Breeze"), The Heat Is On (1975, with "Fight the Power" and "For the Love of You"), and Harvest for the World (1976). Ronald and Ernie continued the group into the 1990s and 2000s, notably with the R. Kelly-written hit album Eternal (2001) and Ronald's "Mr. Biggs" persona.

Honours

The Isley Brothers were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1992, received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2014, and are noted as one of the most sampled acts in hip hop.
