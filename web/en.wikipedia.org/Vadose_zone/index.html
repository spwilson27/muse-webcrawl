Vadose zone

The vadose zone lies above the water table, below the saturated zone.

The vadose zone, also termed the unsaturated zone, is the part of Earth between the land surface and the top of the phreatic zone, the position at which groundwater is at atmospheric pressure. Hence the vadose zone extends from the top of the ground surface to the water table.

Water movement

Water in the vadose zone has a pressure head less than atmospheric pressure and is retained by adhesion, surface tension, and capillary action. Movement is generally slow and is described by Richards' equation, combining Darcy's law with mass conservation for unsaturated media. Water content varies with depth and time as precipitation infiltrates, plants transpire, and drainage recharges the aquifer below. The zone includes the soil-water belt, the intermediate belt, and the capillary fringe just above the water table.

Significance

The vadose zone governs how precipitation, irrigation water, fertilisers, and pollutants reach groundwater, making its study central to hydrology, agriculture, and contaminant remediation. It also matters in cave formation — vadose cave passages are carved by free-flowing water above the water table — and in engineering, where moisture conditions affect foundations and slope stability.
