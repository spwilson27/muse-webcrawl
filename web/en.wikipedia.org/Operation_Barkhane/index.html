Operation Barkhane was a French-led counter-insurgency operation in the Sahel region of Africa, launched on 1 August 2014 as the successor to Operation Serval in Mali and Operation Épervier in Chad. Named after a crescent-shaped dune (a barchan), it was headquartered in N'Djamena, Chad, and operated across the five G5 Sahel countries: Burkina Faso, Chad, Mali, Mauritania, and Niger.

Course of the operation

With an initial strength of about 3,000 troops, later rising to roughly 5,100, Barkhane targeted al-Qaeda-linked and Islamic State-linked groups including JNIM and the Islamic State in the Greater Sahara (ISGS). French forces killed several senior jihadist leaders, including ISGS leader Adnan Abu Walid al-Sahrawi in 2021. The mission was supported by the UN mission MINUSMA and, from 2020, by the European Task Force Takuba.

End

Relations with Mali collapsed after coups in 2020 and 2021 and the arrival of Russian Wagner Group mercenaries. France announced the operation's end on 9 November 2022, having withdrawn its forces from Mali in August of that year and repositioned around Niger. The operation cost the lives of more than 50 French soldiers and did not prevent the jihadist insurgency from spreading across the central Sahel.
