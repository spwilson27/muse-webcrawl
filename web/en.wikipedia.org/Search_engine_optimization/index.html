Search engine optimization

Search engine optimization (SEO) is the process of improving the quality and quantity of website traffic to a website or a web page from search engines. SEO targets unpaid traffic (known as "natural" or "organic" results) rather than direct traffic or paid traffic. Unpaid traffic may originate from different kinds of searches, including image search, video search, academic search, news search, and industry-specific vertical search engines.

History

Webmasters and content providers began optimizing websites for search engines in the mid-1990s, as the first search engines were cataloging the early Web. Initially, all webmasters only needed to submit the address of a page or URL to the various engines, which would send a web crawler to crawl that page. As search engines such as Google grew in importance, terms such as keyword stuffing and link farming emerged as attempts to manipulate rankings, prompting engines to refine their algorithms with regular updates.

Methods

Common techniques include improving content relevance, using descriptive title tags and meta descriptions, earning inbound links from other websites (backlinks), ensuring a site is crawlable and indexable, improving page loading speed, and making pages mobile-friendly. Practices are often divided into "white hat" techniques, which follow search engine guidelines, and "black hat" techniques, which attempt to manipulate rankings and risk penalties.

Relationship with search engines

Search engines provide guidelines for webmasters but retain control over their ranking algorithms, which are proprietary and frequently changed. SEO practitioners study ranking factors and test strategies, while search engines also offer paid placement through advertising programs, which are kept separate from organic results.
