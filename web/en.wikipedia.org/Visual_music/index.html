Visual music

Visual music
MediumFilm, light instruments, digital media
Key figuresOskar Fischinger, Mary Ellen Bute, John Whitney, Len Lye

Visual music, sometimes called colour music, refers to the creation of a visual analogue to musical form by adapting musical structures and concepts to visual imagery. It includes abstract film, performances on light instruments such as the colour organ, and computer-generated works synchronized with sound. Often the output is silent — the imagery alone is intended to evoke musical qualities such as rhythm, harmony, and counterpoint.

History

The idea dates at least to proposals for colour organs in the 18th century, such as Louis-Bertrand Castel's ocular harpsichord. In the early 20th century, painters and filmmakers including Viking Eggeling, Hans Richter, Walter Ruttmann, Len Lye, and Oskar Fischinger made abstract films structured like music; Fischinger's "Studies" of the late 1920s and 1930s synchronized drawn animation to popular and classical recordings. Mary Ellen Bute and later John and James Whitney extended the field, with John Whitney pioneering computer animation in the 1960s.

Modern practice

Visual music today encompasses VJ performance, music videos, audiovisual installations, and software that generates imagery from audio analysis. Walt Disney's Fantasia (1940), influenced by Fischinger, remains the most famous popularization of the concept.
