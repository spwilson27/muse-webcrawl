Northrop B-2 Spirit

B-2 Spirit
Manufacturer: Northrop (later Northrop Grumman)
First flight: 17 July 1989
Introduced: 1 January 1997
Role: Stealth strategic bomber
Number built: 21

The Northrop B-2 Spirit, also known as the Stealth Bomber, is an American heavy strategic bomber, featuring low observable stealth technology designed to penetrate dense anti-aircraft defenses. A subsonic flying wing with a crew of two, the plane was designed by Northrop (later Northrop Grumman) and produced from 1987 to 2000.

Design

The B-2's flying-wing configuration and radar-absorbent materials give it a very small radar cross-section, allowing it to attack heavily defended targets. It can carry up to 40,000 pounds (18,000 kg) of conventional or nuclear weapons in two internal bays, and has an unrefuelled range of about 6,000 nautical miles (11,000 km).

Operational history

The B-2 first saw combat in the Kosovo War in 1999, flying long missions from Whiteman Air Force Base in Missouri. It has since flown missions over Afghanistan, Iraq, and Libya. Only 21 aircraft were built, making the B-2 one of the most expensive aircraft ever produced; the fleet is based primarily at Whiteman AFB, with deployments to Guam and other forward locations.
