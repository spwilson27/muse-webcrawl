Bob Dylan (legally Robert Dylan; born Robert Allen Zimmerman, May 24, 1941) is an American singer-songwriter. Considered one of the greatest songwriters of all time, Dylan has been a major figure in popular culture over his nearly 70-year career. With an estimated 125 million records sold worldwide, he is one of the best-selling musicians of all time, and his work has been recognised with numerous awards, including the Nobel Prize in Literature in 2016.

Early life and career

Dylan was born in Duluth, Minnesota, and raised in Hibbing, where he formed bands in high school and was influenced by early rock and roll, blues and country music. After dropping out of the University of Minnesota, he moved to New York City in 1961 and became a fixture of the Greenwich Village folk scene. His self-titled debut album appeared in 1962, followed by The Freewheelin' Bob Dylan (1963), which included "Blowin' in the Wind" and "A Hard Rain's a-Gonna Fall" and established him as a leading voice of the folk revival and the civil rights era.

Going electric and peak fame

In 1965 Dylan controversially adopted electric instruments; the album Highway 61 Revisited and its single "Like a Rolling Stone" are landmarks of rock music. Together with Bringing It All Back Home (1965) and the double album Blonde on Blonde (1966), this period is widely regarded as one of the greatest runs in popular music. A motorcycle accident in 1966 led him to withdraw from touring for several years, during which he recorded the informal sessions later released as The Basement Tapes with the Band.

Later career

Dylan's career has moved through many styles, from the country of Nashville Skyline (1969) and the acclaimed Blood on the Tracks (1975) to a gospel period in the late 1970s. After a commercially quieter 1980s, albums such as Time Out of Mind (1997), Love and Theft (2001) and Modern Times (2006) were hailed as a late-career resurgence. Since 1988 he has toured almost continuously on what became known as the Never Ending Tour. His 39th studio album of original material, Rough and Rowdy Ways (2020), included the 17-minute "Murder Most Foul", his first song to top a Billboard chart.

Legacy and honours

Dylan's lyrics, drawing on folk, blues, literature and scripture, transformed the ambitions of popular songwriting. He has received ten Grammy Awards, an Academy Award (for "Things Have Changed", 2001), the Presidential Medal of Freedom (2012), and the Nobel Prize in Literature in 2016 "for having created new poetic expressions within the great American song tradition" — the first musician so honoured. He is also a painter and sculptor, and his memoir Chronicles: Volume One (2004) was a bestseller.
