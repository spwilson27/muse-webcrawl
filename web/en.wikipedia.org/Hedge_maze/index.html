Hedge maze

A hedge maze is an outdoor garden maze or labyrinth in which the "walls" or dividers between passages are made of vertical hedges. It is a type of puzzle garden, descended from the knot gardens and mazes of Renaissance Europe.

History

The most famous surviving hedge maze is at Hampton Court Palace in England, planted around 1700 for William III and occupying about a third of an acre; it is often described as the oldest surviving hedge maze in Britain. Other notable examples include the maze at Longleat House in Wiltshire, the labyrinth of the Villa Pisani in Italy, and the maze at Schönbrunn Palace in Vienna.

Hedge mazes are usually planted with yew, box, privet or beech, clipped to a height that prevents visitors from seeing over. Modern hedge mazes are also grown from maize ("corn mazes") as seasonal attractions.
