The Stedelijk Museum Amsterdam (Dutch: [ˈsteːdələk myˈzeːjʏm ɑmstərˈdɑm]; abbreviation Stedelijk) is a museum for modern art, contemporary art and design located in Amsterdam, Netherlands. The 19th-century building was designed by Dutch architect Adriaan Willem Weissman; the 21st-century wing, with a design by Benthem Crouwel Architects, is nicknamed "The Bathtub" for its smooth white form.

Collection

The collection comprises some 90,000 objects, one of the richest collections of modern and contemporary art in the world. It is especially strong in De Stijl (with major works by Piet Mondrian and Theo van Doesburg), Kazimir Malevich and Russian avant-garde art, German Expressionism, CoBrA, Pop art, and Dutch design and photography. Highlight works include paintings by Van Gogh, Matisse, Kandinsky, Chagall, Warhol and Lichtenstein.

History

The museum grew from a 1874 municipal initiative and opened in its neo-Renaissance home on the Museumplein in 1895, near the Rijksmuseum and the Van Gogh Museum. Under director Willem Sandberg (1945–1962) it became an international pioneer in exhibiting modern art and graphic design. After a long renovation, the museum reopened in September 2012 with the new Benthem Crouwel wing providing additional gallery and entrance space.
