Dresden Frauenkirche

The Dresden Frauenkirche (German: Dresdner Frauenkirche, "Church of Our Lady") is a Lutheran church in Dresden, the capital of the German state of Saxony. An earlier church building was Roman Catholic until it became Protestant during the Reformation. It is considered an outstanding example of Protestant sacred architecture, featuring one of the largest domes in Europe.

History

The old church was replaced in the 18th century by a larger Baroque Lutheran building with a distinctive bell-shaped dome, designed by architect George Bähr and completed in 1743. The church was destroyed in the bombing of Dresden during World War II: on 13 February 1945 the firestorm caused by Allied bombing heated the church's sandstone pillars until they gave way, and the building collapsed two days later.

The ruin was left as a war memorial in the city centre for decades under the East German government. After German reunification, reconstruction began in 1994 using original plans and salvaged stones: the darker, fire-blackened original blocks were visibly integrated into the new masonry. The rebuilt church was reconsecrated on 30 October 2005, the year before Dresden's 800th anniversary. The golden orb and cross on top of the dome were crafted in London and donated by the British people and the Dresden Trust.

Architecture

The Frauenkirche is built on a roughly square plan with a central, steep sandstone dome — nicknamed the Steinerne Glocke ("stone bell") — rising about 91 metres. Inside, the altar and the multi-storey galleries were reconstructed in polychrome Baroque style. The church's seven bells include one surviving from the original building.
