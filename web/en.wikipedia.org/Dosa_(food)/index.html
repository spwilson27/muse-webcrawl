Dosa (food)

A dosa (also spelled dosai or dose) is a thin, crisp pancake or crepe originating from South India, made from a fermented batter of ground black gram (urad dal) and rice. It is a staple of South Indian cuisine, commonly eaten for breakfast or dinner, and is typically served hot with sambar, coconut chutney, and other accompaniments.

Preparation

Rice and urad dal are soaked separately, ground to a smooth batter, mixed — typically in a ratio favouring rice — salted, and left to ferment overnight; the batter is leavened naturally by wild yeasts and bacteria, developing a slight sourness. A ladleful is spread in a thin spiral over a hot, lightly greased flat griddle (tawa), drizzled with oil or ghee, and cooked until golden and crisp, usually on one side only. It may be folded in half or rolled into a cone or cylinder for serving.

Variations

The best-known variation is the masala dosa, filled with a spiced potato mixture seasoned with mustard seeds, curry leaves, turmeric, and green chillies. Other varieties include the rava dosa, made with semolina batter and cooked without fermentation; the small, spongy set dosa of Karnataka; the lacy, watery neer dosa of coastal Karnataka; and pesarattu, a green-gram (moong dal) dosa from Andhra Pradesh. Modern restaurants offer many fusion fillings. The dosa is closely related to the thicker uttapam, topped with chopped vegetables, and the steamed idli, made from the same batter.

History and spread

Dosa-like preparations are mentioned in early Tamil literature, and the fermented rice-and-gram batter tradition is centuries old in the Tamil country and Karnataka; the modern restaurant dosa was popularised worldwide by Udupi restaurants in the 20th century. It is now served across India and in Indian restaurants globally, and is among India's most recognised dishes.

Nutrition

Dosa is a source of carbohydrates and moderate protein from the dal; fermentation improves digestibility and vitamin content. It is naturally gluten-free.
