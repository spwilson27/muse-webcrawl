Bernie Sanders

Bernard Sanders (born September 8, 1941) is an American politician and activist who has served as the senior United States senator from Vermont since 2007. A self-described democratic socialist, he is the longest-serving independent in U.S. congressional history but maintains a close relationship with the Democratic Party, having caucused with House and Senate Democrats throughout his career.

Early career

Sanders was born in Brooklyn, New York, the son of Polish Jewish immigrants. After graduating from the University of Chicago in 1964, he settled in Vermont, working as a carpenter and filmmaker and running unsuccessfully for office with the Liberty Union Party during the 1970s. In 1981 he was elected mayor of Burlington, the state's largest city, by ten votes, and served four terms, presiding over a self-consciously progressive municipal administration.

Congressional career

Elected to the U.S. House of Representatives in 1990, Sanders served sixteen years before winning election to the Senate in 2006. He chaired the Senate Veterans' Affairs Committee and later the Budget Committee. He is known for his opposition to economic inequality, his advocacy of universal single-payer health care ("Medicare for All"), tuition-free public college, a higher minimum wage, and campaigns against the influence of wealthy donors in politics.

Presidential campaigns

Sanders sought the Democratic presidential nomination in 2016, winning 23 primaries and caucuses and building a mass small-donor fundraising movement before losing to Hillary Clinton. He ran again in 2020, winning early contests including the Nevada caucuses, but suspended his campaign in April 2020 as Joe Biden consolidated support. Both campaigns moved issues such as single-payer health care and free public college toward the centre of Democratic debate.
