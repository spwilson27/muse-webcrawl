K-1

Founded: 1993

Founder: Kazuyoshi Ishii

Headquarters: Tokyo, Japan

Sport: Kickboxing

K-1 is a Japanese kickboxing promotion founded in 1993 by Kazuyoshi Ishii. It is best known for its heavyweight division, and during its peak in the 1990s and 2000s its K-1 World Grand Prix tournament finals at the Tokyo Dome drew some of the largest audiences in combat sports. The "K" derives from the initials of karate, kickboxing and kung fu, among other striking arts.

Rules and format

K-1 rules emphasise striking: punches, kicks and knee strikes are permitted, while elbows, throws and prolonged clinching are restricted. Bouts are typically three or five rounds. The classic World Grand Prix was a single-elimination tournament of eight fighters, preceded by qualification events around the world.

History

The first K-1 Grand Prix was held in 1993 and won by Branko Cikatić. Dutch fighters came to dominate the heavyweight tournament: Ernesto Hoost won four titles, Semmy Schilt four, and Peter Aerts and Remy Bonjasky three each. K-1 also launched the lighter-weight K-1 World MAX series in 2002. Financial troubles afflicted the original organisation (Fighting and Entertainment Group) around 2010–2011; the brand was sold and K-1 events have since been promoted in Japan by M-1 Sports Media under the K-1 World GP name.
