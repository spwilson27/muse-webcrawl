Free content

Free content, also called free cultural works, refers to text, images, sound, video, and other creative works that are published under terms granting everyone the freedoms to use, study, modify, and distribute them, with at most attribution and share-alike conditions. The concept adapts the principles of free software to works of culture, scholarship, and information.

Definition and licences

The Definition of Free Cultural Works (2006), associated with Erik Möller and the Wikimedia community, sets the standard: a free licence must permit free use, study, redistribution, and derivative works for any purpose, including commercial use. Widely used free licences include the Creative Commons Attribution (CC BY) and Attribution-ShareAlike (CC BY-SA) licences, and the GNU Free Documentation License; works in the public domain, including those released with CC0, also qualify. Creative Commons licences carrying NoDerivatives or NonCommercial restrictions are not considered free under the definition.

Examples and movement

Wikipedia and its sister projects are the best-known repositories of free content, published under CC BY-SA. Other examples include OpenStreetMap's map data, free-content textbooks and open-access scholarly publishing, and large photo collections released by museums and archives. Advocates argue free content enables education, remixing culture, and preservation; debates continue over non-commercial clauses, the rights of creators to earn a living, and the treatment of traditional cultural expressions.
