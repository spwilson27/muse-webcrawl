Screamadelica

Screamadelica is the third studio album by the Scottish rock band Primal Scream, released on 23 September 1991 by Creation Records in the United Kingdom and Sire Records in the United States. Fusing indie rock with the acid house and rave culture of the late 1980s, the album was a landmark crossover between guitar music and dance music and is widely regarded as one of the defining records of its era.

Background and recording

Primal Scream, fronted by Bobby Gillespie, had made two conventional rock albums before embracing the acid house scene. Producer Andrew Weatherall transformed their song "I'm Losing More Than I'll Ever Have" into the looping, euphoric single "Loaded" (1990), built around a sample of Peter Fonda's dialogue from the film The Wild Angels. Its success set the template for Screamadelica, produced mainly by Weatherall and Hugo Nicolson, with contributions from The Orb and others. Tracks move between house ("Come Together", "Higher Than the Sun"), gospel-tinged soul ("Movin' On Up"), dub, ambient textures and the country-rock ballad "Damaged", produced by Jimmy Miller.

Reception and legacy

The album reached number 8 on the UK Albums Chart and won the inaugural Mercury Prize in 1992. Its cover, a painted sun by Paul Cannell, became iconic. Critics have consistently ranked Screamadelica among the greatest albums of all time; it has appeared in numerous all-time lists, was selected for preservation in the United States National Recording Registry in 2020, and was celebrated with anniversary tours and reissues, including a 20th-anniversary edition in 2011 and live performances of the album in full.

Track listing highlights

Key tracks include "Movin' On Up", "Come Together", "Higher Than the Sun", "Loaded", "Don't Fight It, Feel It" (featuring vocals by Denise Johnson), "Inner Flight" and "Shine Like Stars". The album's sequencing, moving from euphoria to a comedown close, is often said to mirror the arc of a night in club culture.
