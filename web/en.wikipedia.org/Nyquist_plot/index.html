Nyquist plot

A Nyquist plot (or Nyquist diagram) is a graphical representation of the frequency response of a linear system; it is a parametric plot of the frequency response in which the Cartesian coordinates are the real and imaginary parts of the transfer function as the frequency varies. It is named after Harry Nyquist, a Swedish-American engineer who developed the associated stability criterion while working at Bell Laboratories.

Overview

The magnitude and phase of the frequency response are represented in a single plot: for each frequency ω from 0 to infinity, the point (Re[G(jω)], Im[G(jω)]) is plotted, so the plot is a curve in the complex plane traced as frequency increases. Unlike a Bode plot, a Nyquist plot does not explicitly show frequency, gain in decibels, or phase in degrees, but it displays the stability properties of a feedback system in a particularly direct way.

Nyquist stability criterion

The Nyquist stability criterion relates the open-loop frequency response of a system to the stability of the closed-loop system. It states that the number of encirclements of the critical point (−1, 0) by the Nyquist plot, combined with the number of unstable open-loop poles, determines the number of unstable closed-loop poles, via the argument principle of complex analysis.

Stability margins

Gain margin and phase margin can be read from the plot: the gain margin is related to the point where the curve crosses the negative real axis, and the phase margin to the point where the curve crosses the unit circle centred at the origin.
