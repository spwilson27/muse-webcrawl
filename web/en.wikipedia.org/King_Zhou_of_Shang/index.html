King Zhou of Shang

Reignc. 1075–1046 BC (traditional)
DynastyShang
Personal nameDi Xin

King Zhou of Shang (Chinese: 商紂王), personal name Di Xin, was the last king of the Shang dynasty of ancient China. Traditional histories portray him as a corrupt and tyrannical ruler whose cruelty—encouraged by his consort Daji—brought about the dynasty's downfall.

Downfall

According to the received accounts, Zhou indulged in extravagance, heavy taxation, and brutal punishments while neglecting state affairs. King Wu of Zhou defeated him at the Battle of Muye (c. 1046 BC), after which Zhou is said to have set fire to his palace and died, and the Zhou dynasty replaced the Shang. Later Confucian tradition made him the archetype of the bad last ruler; modern historians note that some accusations were likely exaggerated or invented by his Zhou successors, though oracle bone and bronze evidence broadly confirms the violent end of his reign.

He is a central figure in the Ming-dynasty novel Investiture of the Gods (Fengshen Yanyi).
