Homocysteine

HomocysteineFormula: C4H9NO2S
Type: non-proteinogenic amino acid
Related: methionine, cysteine

Homocysteine is a non-proteinogenic amino acid formed in the body during the metabolism of methionine. It sits at a metabolic crossroads: it can be remethylated back to methionine (a reaction requiring folate and vitamin B12) or converted via transsulfuration to cysteine (requiring vitamin B6).

Clinical significance

Elevated blood levels, known as hyperhomocysteinemia, are associated with an increased risk of cardiovascular disease, stroke and venous thrombosis, and during pregnancy with neural tube defects. Causes include genetic defects such as MTHFR polymorphisms, renal impairment, and deficiencies of folate, B12 or B6.

Homocystinuria

The classical inborn error homocystinuria, most often caused by cystathionine beta-synthase deficiency, produces very high homocysteine levels, with lens dislocation, skeletal abnormalities, intellectual disability and early vascular disease. It is treated with vitamin B6 in responsive cases, dietary methionine restriction and betaine.

Measurement and treatment

Total plasma homocysteine is measured by immunoassay or chromatography; typical reference ranges are roughly 5–15 µmol/L. Supplementation with folic acid and B vitamins lowers levels, though large trials have questioned whether lowering homocysteine reduces cardiovascular events.
