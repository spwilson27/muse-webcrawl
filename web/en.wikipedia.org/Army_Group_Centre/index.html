Army Group Centre

Army Group Centre (German: Heeresgruppe Mitte) was the name of two distinct German strategic army groups that fought on the Eastern Front in the Second World War. The first Army Group Centre was created in June 1941 as one of three army groups for Operation Barbarossa, the invasion of the Soviet Union, and was given the task of advancing through Belorussia toward Moscow.

Army Group Centre Country: Nazi Germany
Branch: Heer (Wehrmacht)
Active: 1941–1945
Engagements: Operation Barbarossa; Battle of Moscow; Operation Bagration

Operation Barbarossa

Commanded initially by Field Marshal Fedor von Bock, Army Group Centre comprised the 4th and 9th Armies and the 2nd and 3rd Panzer Groups. It won enormous encirclement victories at Białystok–Minsk and Smolensk in 1941, capturing hundreds of thousands of Soviet prisoners, but was halted before Moscow in December 1941 by Soviet counteroffensives and the winter. Thereafter it fought a series of defensive battles, including around Rzhev and in the aftermath of the German defeat at Kursk in 1943.

Destruction in Operation Bagration

In June 1944 the Soviet summer offensive, Operation Bagration, struck Army Group Centre in Belorussia. Within weeks the army group lost some 28 divisions and several hundred thousand men — a defeat comparable in scale to Stalingrad — and the Red Army advanced to the Vistula and the borders of East Prussia. The remnants were reorganised, and a second Army Group Centre was formed from the former Army Group North in January 1945; it fought in East Prussia and finally surrendered in Czechoslovakia in May 1945.
