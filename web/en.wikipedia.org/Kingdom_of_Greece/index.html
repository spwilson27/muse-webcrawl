The Kingdom of Greece (Greek: Βασίλειον τῆς Ἑλλάδος) was the Greek state established in 1832 following the Greek War of Independence and the London Conference, when the great powers installed the Bavarian prince Otto as its first king. Athens became the capital in 1834.

History

Otto was deposed in 1862 and replaced by George I of the House of Glücksburg, under whom Greece expanded to include the Ionian Islands, Thessaly, Epirus, Macedonia, Crete, and the Aegean islands through the Balkan Wars. The country endured the National Schism between King Constantine I and Prime Minister Eleftherios Venizelos, defeat in the Greco-Turkish War (1919–1922), and abolition of the monarchy in 1924. Restored in 1935, the kingdom passed through the Metaxas dictatorship, occupation in the Second World War, and civil war.

The monarchy ended after the 1967–1974 military junta: Constantine II fled after a failed counter-coup in 1967, and a referendum in 1974 confirmed the republic, a year after the junta had abolished the monarchy itself.
