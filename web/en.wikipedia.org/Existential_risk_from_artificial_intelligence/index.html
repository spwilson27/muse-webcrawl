Existential risk from artificial intelligence

The possibility that advanced AI systems could escape human control is debated among researchers and policymakers.

Existential risk from artificial intelligence refers to the idea that substantial progress in artificial general intelligence (AGI) could lead to human extinction or an irreversible global catastrophe. Proponents of the concern argue that a sufficiently capable AI system, if its goals were misaligned with human values, might resist shutdown, seize resources, or otherwise disempower humanity.

Arguments

Key arguments include the orthogonality thesis — that intelligence and final goals can vary independently — and instrumental convergence, the idea that agents with many different goals would pursue common sub-goals such as self-preservation and resource acquisition. Thinkers such as Nick Bostrom, in his 2014 book Superintelligence, and Eliezer Yudkowsky have popularised these arguments, while computer scientists including Geoffrey Hinton and Yoshua Bengio have publicly expressed concern. Critics such as Yann LeCun and Andrew Ng argue the risks are speculative and that present-day harms deserve priority.

Responses

The topic has attracted academic research programmes in AI alignment and AI safety, dedicated research institutes, and government attention, including international summits on AI safety and statements signed by researchers and executives calling for the risk of extinction from AI to be treated as a global priority.
