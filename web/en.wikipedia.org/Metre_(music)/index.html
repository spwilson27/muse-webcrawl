Metre (music)

In music, metre (American spelling: meter) refers to the regularly recurring patterns and accents, such as bars and beats, that structure a piece. It is the grouping of beats into regular measures, indicated in notation by a time signature, and is analogous to poetic metre in verse. Whereas rhythm describes the durations of individual notes, metre describes the background grid against which those rhythms are heard.

Types

Metres are classified by the number of beats per measure — duple (2), triple (3), quadruple (4) — and by how each beat subdivides: simple metres divide beats in two, compound metres in three, and complex or asymmetrical metres (such as 5/4 or 7/8) combine unequal groups. Marches are typically in duple metre, waltzes in triple, and most Western popular music in quadruple metre (common time, 4/4).

Perception and theory

Listeners infer metre from accents, harmonic change, and melodic grouping, and syncopation plays against it for expressive effect. At larger scales, groups of measures form hypermetre. Some traditions, such as Indian tala and Balkan folk dances, employ elaborate additive metres, and some 20th-century art music deliberately avoids regular metre altogether.
