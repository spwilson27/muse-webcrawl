E–Z notation

E–Z notation, also called the E/Z convention, is the IUPAC-preferred method of describing the stereochemistry of double bonds in organic chemistry. It extends the cis–trans convention, which only works unambiguously when a double bond has two simple substituent patterns, to cases where each carbon of the double bond bears two different groups.

Assignment rules

At each carbon of the double bond, the two substituents are ranked by the Cahn–Ingold–Prelog (CIP) priority rules, based on the atomic numbers of the atoms directly attached. If the two higher-priority groups lie on the same side of the double bond, the configuration is Z (from German zusammen, "together"); if they lie on opposite sides, it is E (from entgegen, "opposite"). The descriptor is written in italics, in parentheses, as a prefix to the compound's name, e.g. (E)-but-2-ene.

Relation to cis and trans

For simple alkenes, Z usually corresponds to cis and E to trans, but the correspondence fails when the visually "same-side" groups are not the CIP high-priority ones. Because CIP priorities depend on atomic number rather than size or identity of the whole group, a cis alkene can correctly be assigned E. For this reason, IUPAC recommends E/Z for precision.

Multiple bonds

When a molecule contains several stereogenic double bonds, each receives its own locant-tagged descriptor, as in (2E,4Z)-hexa-2,4-diene. The notation also applies to C=N bonds in imines and oximes and to other suitably substituted double bonds.
