Griot

RegionWest Africa (Mali, Senegal, Guinea, Gambia, and neighbours)
Local termsJeli (Mande), gewel (Wolof)
InstrumentsKora, balafon, ngoni
RolesHistorian, praise-singer, genealogist, mediator, musician

A griot (French pronunciation roughly "gree-oh") is a West African hereditary historian, storyteller, praise singer, poet and musician — a repository of oral tradition and one of the most important social institutions of the region. Among the Mandé peoples the griot is called jeli (pl. jelilu); in Wolof society, gewel.

Role in society

Griots belong to endogamous artisan castes and were traditionally attached to noble patrons and royal courts — a relationship already noted when the traveller Ibn Battuta described court jelis at the court of Mali in the fourteenth century. Their duties include preserving genealogies and epic history (such as the Epic of Sundiata, the founding narrative of the Mali Empire), composing praise songs for patrons, mediating disputes, advising rulers, performing at naming ceremonies, weddings and funerals, and conveying messages between families and villages.

The griot's authority rests on mastery of speech — the power of the word (nyama) — and on memory trained from childhood within griot families.

Music

Griot musicians are virtuosos of instruments including the kora, a 21-string harp-lute; the balafon, a wooden xylophone; and the ngoni lute, considered an ancestor in spirit of the banjo. In the twentieth and twenty-first centuries, griot musicians such as Toumani Diabaté, Ballaké Sissoko and the families of Guinea, Mali and Senegal carried the tradition to world stages, recording and collaborating widely, while female griottes became celebrated singers in their own right.

Modern era

The institution has adapted to radio, recording, politics and tourism; praise-singing for politicians and businesspeople is now a major livelihood, drawing both celebration and criticism for flattery. Alex Haley's novel Roots (1976), which traced his ancestry through a Gambian griot, brought the figure worldwide recognition.
