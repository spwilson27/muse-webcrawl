Sinseollo

Sinseollo (신선로) is a Korean royal court dish consisting of a complex hot pot of meat, seafood, mushrooms and vegetables cooked in a rich beef broth. It is also the name of the distinctive brass or silver vessel in which it is served: a wide, shallow pot with a central chimney funnel in which burning charcoal is placed to keep the dish simmering at the table.

Origins and preparation

The name derives from a legend that the dish was so delicious it was fit for a sinseon — a Taoist immortal or hermit sage; one story attaches it to the Joseon-era scholar Jeong Hui-ryang, who lived as a hermit and cooked vegetables in such a vessel. Originally called yeolguja-tang ("soup that delights the mouth"), it became a centrepiece of Joseon court cuisine.

A proper sinseollo may contain dozens of ingredients arranged in neat colour-grouped rows over a bed of radish and broth: beef, liver, tripe, meatballs (gyeongdan), seafood, abalone, mushrooms, egg garnish ribbons, pine nuts, gingko nuts, carrots and water parsley. The art lies as much in the arrangement as in the cooking, and the dish is now served mainly in traditional restaurants and on special occasions.
