The Hero with a Thousand Faces

The Hero with a Thousand Faces is a 1949 book by American mythologist Joseph Campbell. It introduces the theory of the monomyth, or hero's journey: a common pattern underlying hero stories across world mythologies.

The monomyth

Campbell summarizes the pattern: "A hero ventures forth from the world of common day into a region of supernatural wonder: fabulous forces are there encountered and a decisive victory is won: the hero comes back from this mysterious adventure with the power to bestow boons on his fellow man." He structures it as Departure, Initiation, and Return, with stages including the Call to Adventure, Supernatural Aid, Crossing the Threshold, Road of Trials, Atonement, and Master of Two Worlds.

Influence

The book profoundly influenced storytelling; George Lucas credited it in shaping Star Wars. Christopher Vogler's The Writer's Journey adapted it for screenwriters. Critics debate its universality and Campbell's selective examples. See also The Gulag Archipelago.
