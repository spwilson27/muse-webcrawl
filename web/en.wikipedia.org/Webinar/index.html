Webinar

EtymologyBlend of "web" and "seminar"
FormatLive or recorded online presentation
Typical featuresSlides, screen sharing, chat, polls, Q&A
Major platformsZoom, Microsoft Teams, Webex, GoToWebinar

A webinar (short for web seminar) is an online meeting or presentation held via the Internet in real time, or delivered as a recorded, on-demand session. Webinars are used for training, marketing, corporate communications, academic lectures and conferences, allowing participants in different locations to view slides and video, hear a presenter, and interact through chat, polls and question-and-answer tools.

Features

Webinar software typically provides registration pages, email reminders, screen and slide sharing, live video of speakers, text chat, audience polling, hand-raising, breakout discussions, and recording for later playback. Unlike ordinary videoconferences, webinars are usually presenter-led and one-to-many, with audience participation moderated through structured channels rather than open microphones. Vendors variously distinguish "webcasts" (streamed, minimal interaction) from interactive webinars.

History and growth

Early web conferencing tools appeared in the late 1990s, with products such as WebEx and PlaceWare (later Microsoft Live Meeting); the word "webinar" was trademarked in 1998 by Eric R. Korb, though the registration was later abandoned and the term became generic. Adoption grew through the 2000s as broadband spread, and exploded during the COVID-19 pandemic from 2020, when remote work and study made online events a default format for education, sales demonstrations and professional development.

Uses

Businesses use webinars for product launches, lead generation and customer training; universities and professional bodies use them for lectures and continuing education; public agencies use them for briefings and consultation. On-demand recordings extend a webinar's audience well beyond its live slot.
