The DFB-Pokal (German Football Association Cup) is a German knockout football cup competition held annually by the German Football Association (DFB). Sixty-four teams participate, including all clubs from the Bundesliga and the 2. Bundesliga, the top four of the 3. Liga and winners of the regional association cups. It is the second most important club title in German football after the Bundesliga championship.

History

The competition began in 1935 as the Tschammerpokal, named after Reichssportführer Hans von Tschammer und Osten; 1. FC Nürnberg won the first edition. Revived in West Germany in 1952–53, it ran in parallel with East Germany's FDGB-Pokal until reunification in 1990. Since 1985 the final has been staged at Berlin's Olympiastadion, becoming a fixed festival of German football under the slogan "Berlin, Berlin, wir fahren nach Berlin!"

Format

The cup is a single-elimination tournament played over six rounds. Lower-division clubs host ties against Bundesliga opponents in the early rounds, a feature famous for producing upsets ("Pokal" giant-killings, summed up in the saying that the cup "has its own laws"). Matches drawn after 90 minutes go to extra time and then penalties. The winner qualifies for the UEFA Europa League and the DFL-Supercup.

Records

Bayern Munich are the dominant club with a record 20 titles; Werder Bremen are second with six. Bayern also supplied the competition's most frequent finalist appearances, while notable fairy-tale runs include Hannover 96's 1992 victory as a second-division side.
