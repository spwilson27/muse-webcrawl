Cable Television Laboratories, Inc., commonly known as CableLabs, is a non-profit innovation and research and development lab founded in 1988 by American cable operators. Its members include major cable television companies from around the world, and its work focuses on developing interoperable technology specifications for the cable industry.

History

CableLabs was established in 1988 and is based in Louisville, Colorado, with additional offices elsewhere. It was modelled in part on other industry research consortia, pooling the research efforts of cable operators so that equipment from different vendors would work together on their networks.

DOCSIS

CableLabs' best-known project is DOCSIS (Data Over Cable Service Interface Specification), the international standard that defines how data is transmitted over cable television systems. First released in 1997, DOCSIS enabled the widespread deployment of cable modems and high-speed internet access. Successive versions — DOCSIS 2.0, 3.0, 3.1 and 4.0 — have raised speeds through channel bonding, wider channels and full-duplex operation.

Other projects

Beyond DOCSIS, CableLabs has developed specifications for digital video, packet-based voice telephony (PacketCable), interactive television, network security and wireless convergence. It also runs certification and qualification programs that test whether vendor equipment complies with its specifications, and participates in wider standards work through related bodies.

Role in the industry

As cable operators have shifted from television distribution toward broadband connectivity, CableLabs has increasingly focused on network architecture, low-latency services, coherent optics for access networks, and convergence between fixed and mobile networks.
