Adi Shamir

Born: July 6, 1952, Tel Aviv, Israel
Fields: Computer science, cryptography
Institutions: Weizmann Institute of Science
Known for: RSA, Feige–Fiat–Shamir identification scheme, secret sharing, differential cryptanalysis
Awards: Turing Award (2002)

Adi Shamir (Hebrew: עדי שמיר; born July 6, 1952) is an Israeli cryptographer. He is a co-inventor of the Rivest–Shamir–Adleman (RSA) algorithm, a co-inventor of the Feige–Fiat–Shamir identification scheme, one of the inventors of differential cryptanalysis, and has made numerous contributions to cryptography and computer science.

Career

Shamir received his PhD from the Weizmann Institute in 1977 and, after postdoctoral work at the University of Warwick and MIT, joined the Weizmann Institute faculty. With Ron Rivest and Leonard Adleman at MIT, he developed RSA in 1977, the first practical public-key cryptosystem. His other work includes Shamir's secret sharing scheme, breaking the Merkle–Hellman knapsack cryptosystem, and the TWIRL and TWINKLE factoring devices.

Honors

Shamir shared the 2002 ACM Turing Award with Rivest and Adleman. He is a member of the Israel Academy of Sciences and Humanities and a foreign member of the U.S. National Academy of Sciences.
