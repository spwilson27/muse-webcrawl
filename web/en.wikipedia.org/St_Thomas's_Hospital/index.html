St Thomas's Hospital is a large NHS teaching hospital in Central London, England. Administratively part of the Guy's and St Thomas' NHS Foundation Trust, it provides the ground for King's College London's GKT School of Medical Education. It is one of London's most famous hospitals, associated with people such as Sir Astley Cooper, William Cheselden, Florence Nightingale, Linda Richards, and others.

History

The hospital dates its origins to an infirmary of the priory of St Mary Overie in Southwark, named for Saint Thomas Becket, probably by around 1215. After the Dissolution of the Monasteries it was refounded by the City of London under Edward VI in 1551–53. St Thomas's remained in Southwark until the 1860s, when the land was compulsorily purchased for a railway viaduct; it moved in 1871 to its current site in Lambeth, in a pavilion-plan complex designed by Henry Currey, directly across the Thames from the Palace of Westminster. Florence Nightingale established her nursing school at St Thomas's in 1860, funded by the Nightingale Fund raised during the Crimean War; the school shaped modern professional nursing worldwide.

Services

The hospital provides a full range of acute services, including emergency care, cardiology, cancer, children's services (the Evelina London Children's Hospital on the same site), maternity, and specialist intensive care. During the COVID-19 pandemic, St Thomas's treated the then Prime Minister Boris Johnson in intensive care in April 2020. The Florence Nightingale Museum is located within the hospital grounds.
