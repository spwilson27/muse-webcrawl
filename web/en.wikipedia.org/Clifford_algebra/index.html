Clifford algebra

Geometric interpretation of multivectors in a Clifford algebra

In mathematics, a Clifford algebra is an algebra generated by a vector space with a quadratic form, and is a unital associative algebra. As K-algebras, they generalize the real numbers, complex numbers, quaternions and several other hypercomplex number systems.

The theory of Clifford algebras is intimately connected with the theory of quadratic forms and orthogonal transformations. Clifford algebras have important applications in a variety of fields including geometry, theoretical physics and digital image processing. They are named after the English mathematician William Kingdon Clifford (1845–1879).

Definition

Let V be a vector space over a field K, and let Q : V → K be a quadratic form on V. In most cases of interest, the field K is the field of real numbers R, the complex numbers C, or a finite field. A Clifford algebra Cℓ(V, Q) is a pair (A, i) where A is a unital associative algebra over K and i : V → A is a linear map satisfying

i(v)2 = Q(v) · 1 for all v ∈ V,

together with a universal property: any other such pair factors uniquely through it. The identity i(v)2 = Q(v) extends by bilinearity to the relation i(u)i(v) + i(v)i(u) = 2B(u, v), where B is the symmetric bilinear form associated with Q.

Examples

The simplest Clifford algebras are low-dimensional. Over the reals, Cℓ0,0 is just R itself; Cℓ0,1 is the complex numbers; and Cℓ0,2 is the quaternions. In three dimensions, the Clifford algebra of Euclidean space underlies the algebra of Pauli matrices used in quantum mechanics.

Applications

Clifford algebras classify spin groups and pin groups, which appear in the theory of spinors in physics. The Dirac equation of relativistic quantum mechanics is written naturally in terms of gamma matrices generating a Clifford algebra of spacetime. In computer science and engineering, the related formalism of geometric algebra is used in robotics, computer graphics and signal processing. The Clay Mathematics Institute has supported research conferences in Clifford analysis.
