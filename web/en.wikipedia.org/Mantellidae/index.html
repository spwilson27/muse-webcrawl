The Mantellidae are a family of frogs found almost exclusively on Madagascar and the nearby island of Mayotte. With more than 300 described species — and many more still being discovered — they constitute the largest radiation of frogs on Madagascar and one of the most diverse frog families in the world. The family includes the brightly coloured mantella frogs, the large stream-dwelling Mantidactylus, climbing frogs, and burrowing forms.

Taxonomy

The family is usually divided into three subfamilies:

  • Mantellinae — mostly terrestrial frogs, including Mantella, Mantidactylus, Blommersia, Gephyromantis, and Guibemantis.
  • Laliostominae — the genera Laliostoma and Aglyptodactylus.
  • Boophinae — the tree frogs of the genus Boophis.

Mantellids belong to the ranoid (true frog) lineage; their nearest relatives are the Asian family Rhacophoridae, suggesting that their ancestors reached Madagascar by oceanic dispersal from Asia, probably in the Cretaceous or early Cenozoic, and then diversified spectacularly in isolation.

Biology

Mantellids show extraordinary ecological variety. Many are small terrestrial frogs of the forest floor; others are powerful stream specialists, torrent frogs with robust bodies, or arboreal tree frogs with enlarged toe pads. Reproductive modes are correspondingly diverse: some lay eggs in water, others deposit eggs on leaves above streams or on land, with tadpoles washed or dropping into water; some species have endotrophic (non-feeding) tadpoles.

The genus Mantella is famous for its aposematic (warning) coloration — brilliant oranges, golds, greens, and blacks — and for alkaloid toxins in the skin derived from the ants and mites they eat, a striking case of convergent evolution with the poison dart frogs of South America. Male mantellids possess femoral glands on the thighs, used in chemical communication, a characteristic feature of the family.

Conservation

As part of Madagascar's exceptional and highly endemic biota, mantellids are severely threatened by deforestation, habitat fragmentation, and the spread of the chytrid fungus Batrachochytrium dendrobatidis. Several Mantella species are listed on CITES because of collection for the international pet trade, and many range-restricted species are classified as Endangered or Critically Endangered by the IUCN.
