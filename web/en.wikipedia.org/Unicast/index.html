Unicast

Unicast: a single sender transmits to a single receiver.

Unicast is a one-to-one transmission from one point in a computer network to another point; that is, one sender and one receiver, each identified by a network address. It is contrasted with multicast (one-to-many), broadcast (one-to-all), and anycast (one-to-nearest).

Overview

Unicast is the dominant form of traffic on the Internet. Protocols such as TCP establish connections between exactly two endpoints, and even nominally one-to-many services, such as most video streaming, are delivered as many parallel unicast streams. Because each unicast stream consumes its own bandwidth, unicast can scale poorly for simultaneous delivery of identical content to large audiences, a problem multicast addresses in managed networks.

Tunneling

A unicast tunnel carries packets addressed to a single endpoint, possibly encapsulating other traffic. Unicast is also the default addressing scheme of both IPv4 and IPv6; multicast and anycast addresses are special ranges within the address space.
