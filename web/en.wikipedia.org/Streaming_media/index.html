Streaming media

Streaming media refers to multimedia delivered through a network for playback using a media player. Media is transferred in a stream of packets from a server to a client and is rendered in real-time or near real-time; this contrasts with file downloading, a process in which the end-user obtains an entire media file before consuming the content. Streaming is most prevalent in video-on-demand, streaming television and music streaming services, as well as in live streaming.

Clients can start playing the media while the rest of the data is still being sent; a small buffer protects playback against short interruptions. Modern streaming relies on adaptive bitrate protocols such as MPEG-DASH and Apple HLS, which switch between quality levels according to available bandwidth.

History

Experimental streaming dates to the early 1990s: the band Severe Tire Damage gave the first live streamed performance in 1993, and Xerox PARC had streamed audio and video over the MBone earlier. RealNetworks launched RealAudio in 1995, bringing streaming to mass audiences over dial-up connections. Microsoft's Windows Media and Apple's QuickTime followed. Broadband adoption in the 2000s enabled YouTube (founded 2005) and Netflix's streaming service (2007), which together displaced physical rental and much broadcast scheduling; Spotify (2008) did the same for music distribution. By the 2010s, streaming had become the dominant mode of media consumption in many markets, and "streaming wars" among Disney+, HBO Max/Max, Amazon Prime Video and others reshaped the entertainment industry.

Technology

Streaming servers segment media into short chunks indexed by a manifest file. Adaptive bitrate streaming measures the client's throughput and buffer level and requests chunks at the highest sustainable quality. Content delivery networks (CDNs) cache segments close to users to reduce latency and backbone traffic. Live streaming adds encoding and distribution latency concerns, addressed by low-latency variants of HLS and DASH and by WebRTC for sub-second interactivity.
