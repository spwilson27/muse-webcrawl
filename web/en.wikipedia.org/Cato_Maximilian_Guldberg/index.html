Cato Maximilian Guldberg (11 August 1836 – 14 January 1902) was a Norwegian chemist and mathematician. Together with his brother-in-law Peter Waage, he formulated the law of mass action, one of the foundational principles of chemical equilibrium, in work published between 1864 and 1879.

Life and career

Guldberg studied mathematics and physics at the University of Christiania, graduating in 1859, and became professor of applied mathematics there in 1869. He was also an influential science administrator, directing Norway's technical education and serving on bodies concerned with weights and measures. His brother-in-law Peter Waage held the chemistry chair at the same university, and the two collaborated for decades.

Law of mass action

In 1864 Guldberg and Waage published (in Norwegian) the first statement that the rate and equilibrium of a chemical reaction depend on the "active masses" — effectively the concentrations — of the reacting substances, raised to powers. Their fuller 1867 French version and the definitive 1879 paper (in German) presented experimental verification with dozens of reactions. The law was initially little noticed, partly because of its publication venues and Guldberg's mathematical formulation in terms of forces, but by the late 1870s it was taken up by van 't Hoff, Ostwald, and others and became central to physical chemistry.

Other work

Guldberg also worked on thermodynamics of heterogeneous systems, meteorology, and ballistics, and in 1890 proposed Guldberg's rule, the empirical relation that the normal boiling point of a liquid is roughly two-thirds of its critical temperature on the absolute scale.
