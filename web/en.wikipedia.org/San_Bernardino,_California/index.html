San Bernardino is a city in and the county seat of San Bernardino County, California, United States. Located in the Inland Empire region about 60 miles east of Los Angeles, it had a population of 222,101 at the 2020 census. It is one of the oldest communities in the state and a major transportation and logistics hub.

History

The area was named by Spanish missionaries in 1810 after Saint Bernardino of Siena. In 1851 Mormon settlers led by Amasa Lyman and Charles C. Rich purchased the rancho and founded a lasting settlement. The city became a centre for railroads and Route 66. The first McDonald's restaurant was opened here by Richard and Maurice McDonald in 1940. The city filed for Chapter 9 bankruptcy in 2012 and emerged in 2017. In December 2015 the city was the site of a mass shooting at the Inland Regional Center.

Geography

San Bernardino lies at the foot of the San Bernardino Mountains, near the Cajon Pass. California State University, San Bernardino, is located in the north of the city.

Economy

Government, logistics and warehousing are major employers; the former Norton Air Force Base was redeveloped as San Bernardino International Airport.
