Presolar grains

Presolar grains are interstellar solid material in the form of tiny mineral grains that originated around other stars before the formation of the Solar System. These stardust grains survive today inside primitive meteorites, where they are identified by their highly anomalous isotopic compositions, which cannot be explained by any process within the Solar System and instead match the nucleosynthesis products of individual stars.

Types and origins

The most studied presolar minerals include silicon carbide (SiC), graphite, nanodiamonds, corundum, spinel, and various silicates. Their isotopic signatures point to origins in asymptotic giant branch (AGB) stars, red giants, supernovae, and novae. Silicon carbide grains, for example, mostly condensed in the outflows of carbon-rich AGB stars, while a smaller fraction carry the explosive nucleosynthesis signatures of Type II supernovae.

Scientific significance

Because presolar grains can be analysed grain by grain in the laboratory, they provide direct samples of stellar interiors and of the interstellar medium from which the Solar System formed. Some grains extracted from the Murchison meteorite have model ages of up to about 7 billion years, making them the oldest solid material ever dated—older than the Sun itself. Their study bridges astronomy, nuclear astrophysics, and cosmochemistry.
