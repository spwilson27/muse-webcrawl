The Russian Far East (Russian: Dalny Vostok) is the easternmost part of Russia, roughly corresponding to the Far Eastern Federal District between Lake Baikal and the Pacific Ocean. It is one of the largest and least densely populated regions of the country, covering over a third of Russia's territory with only a small fraction of its population.

Geography

The region stretches from the Lena River to the Pacific coast and includes the Kamchatka Peninsula, Sakhalin Island, the Kuril Islands, the Amur River basin, and Arctic coastline along the Chukotka. Its landscapes range from Arctic tundra and vast taiga to the volcanic mountains of Kamchatka and the monsoon-influenced forests of Primorsky Krai. The climate is extreme, with some of the coldest inhabited places on Earth around Yakutsk and Verkhoyansk.

History

Russian explorers and Cossacks reached the Pacific in the 17th century, but the modern boundaries were fixed only in the 19th, when the Treaties of Aigun (1858) and Peking (1860) transferred the Amur's left bank and the Ussuri region from Qing China. Vladivostok was founded in 1860, and the Trans-Siberian Railway, completed in the early 20th century, tied the region to European Russia. In the Soviet period the Far East saw Gulag camps (notably Kolyma), military build-up, and state-driven settlement; after 1991 its population declined sharply.

Economy

The economy rests on natural resources — diamonds in Yakutia, gold, timber, fish, and oil and gas around Sakhalin — and on Pacific trade and the Northern Sea Route. Since the 2010s the Russian government has promoted development through free-port regimes and the Eastern Economic Forum in Vladivostok, oriented increasingly toward Asian markets.
