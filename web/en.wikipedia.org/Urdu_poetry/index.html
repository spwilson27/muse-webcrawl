Urdu poetry

Urdu poetry (Urdu: اردو شاعری) is a rich tradition of poetry in the Urdu language with many different forms. It originated in the Mughal courts of the Indian subcontinent, drawing heavily on Persian poetic forms and imagery while expressing itself in the mixed vernacular of Delhi and the Deccan. Today it is an important part of the cultures of India and Pakistan.

Forms

The best known form is the ghazal, a series of couplets (sher) sharing a rhyme and refrain, traditionally on themes of love, separation, and mysticism. Other classical forms include the nazm (a unified poem on a single theme), the marsiya (an elegy for the martyrs of Karbala), the masnavi (a long narrative poem), the qasida (a panegyric ode), and the rubai (a quatrain). In the twentieth century the free verse azad nazm also gained prominence.

Major figures

Mir Taqi Mir (1723–1810) is honoured as Khuda-e-Sukhan, "God of Poetry". Mirza Ghalib (1797–1869), writing in both Urdu and Persian, is regarded as the most influential Urdu poet, celebrated for the philosophical depth and wit of his divan. Muhammad Iqbal (1877–1938) recast Urdu poetry as a vehicle of philosophical and political awakening, while Faiz Ahmed Faiz (1911–1984) fused the classical ghazal with progressive politics. Poets traditionally gather at a mushaira, a symposium where verses are recited and applauded by connoisseurs — a living institution in South Asian cultural life, where homage is paid through a system of mentorship called ustadi–shagirdi.
