The Supreme Muslim Council (Arabic: المجلس الإسلامي الأعلى, al-Majlis al-Islāmī al-Aʿlā) was the highest body in charge of Muslim community affairs in Mandatory Palestine between 1921 and 1951. It was established by the British Mandate authorities and controlled the funds of the waqf (religious endowments), the Sharia courts, and the appointment of religious officials throughout Palestine.

Establishment

The council was established in December 1921 by the British High Commissioner, Herbert Samuel. It consisted of a president (the Ra'is al-'Ulama') and four members, two representing the Jerusalem district and one each representing the northern and southern districts. Upon its establishment, the Grand Mufti of Jerusalem, Amin al-Husseini, was elected president, a position he combined with that of mufti. The council was funded by government grants and by the revenues from the awqaf properties it administered, which were substantial.

Functions

The Supreme Muslim Council's responsibilities included:

  • Administering the awqaf (Muslim charitable trust funds) and the orphan fund;
  • Appointing judges (qadis) of the Sharia courts, mosque officials, preachers, and muezzins;
  • Supervising mosques, shrines, and Muslim religious schools; and
  • Managing the al-Aqsa Mosque compound in Jerusalem and the Haram al-Sharif.

Under al-Husseini, the council organised extensive fundraising campaigns throughout the Muslim world for the restoration of the al-Aqsa Mosque and the Dome of the Rock, re-gilding the dome and consolidating its political position as a pan-Islamic institution.

Dissolution

During the Arab revolt of 1936–1939, the British dismissed al-Husseini from the presidency in 1937 and removed several members, reducing the council's powers; al-Husseini fled the country and never returned to the position. After the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, the council continued to function in the West Bank, which had come under Jordanian control. In 1951, King Abdullah I of Jordan dissolved the council and transferred its functions to a Jordanian government department, the Department of Awqaf and Islamic Affairs, although a Jerusalem-based waqf administration continued to manage the Haram al-Sharif.

Legacy

The Supreme Muslim Council was the most important Palestinian Arab institution of the Mandate period and gave the Muslim community a degree of self-government in religious affairs. Its control of religious appointments and endowments made it a central arena of Palestinian politics, and its administration of the Jerusalem holy places left a lasting legacy in the Jordanian-administered Islamic Waqf of Jerusalem, which continues to administer the al-Aqsa Mosque compound today.
