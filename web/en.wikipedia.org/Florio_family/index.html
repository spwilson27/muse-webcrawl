Florio family

The Florio family is a prominent Sicilian family, originally from Calabria, who rose to become one of the wealthiest entrepreneurial families in 19th- and early 20th-century Italy. Starting from a spice and wine shop in Palermo, the Florio built an empire spanning Marsala wine, tuna fisheries, shipping, sulphur, banking, and metallurgy, and for a time embodied the Belle Époque splendour of Palermo.

Rise

Paolo Florio moved from Bagnara in Calabria to Palermo in 1783 and opened a shop trading spices and colonial goods. His son Vincenzo Florio (1799–1868) vastly expanded the business: he entered Marsala wine production, founded the Anglo-Sicilian sulphur company, pioneered steam navigation in the Mediterranean, and established the Fonderia Oretea ironworks. In 1832 he founded the Florio winery at Marsala, still a major producer.

Heyday and decline

Under Ignazio Florio Sr. and his sons Ignazio Jr. and Vincenzo Jr., the family's Navigazione Generale Italiana became Italy's largest shipping company, and the Targa Florio road race, founded by Vincenzo Jr. in 1906, made the family name famous in motor sport. Franca Florio, wife of Ignazio Jr., was a celebrated figure of Palermo society. Overextended investments and changing economic conditions led to the empire's collapse in the early decades of the 20th century.

Legacy

Surviving landmarks include the Art Nouveau Villino Florio and Casa Florio in Palermo, the tonnara of Favignana, and the Florio winery in Marsala. The family's story has been retold in Stefania Auci's bestselling novel The Florios of Sicily (2019).
