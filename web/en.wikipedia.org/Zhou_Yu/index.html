Zhou Yu (175–210), courtesy name Gongjin, was a Chinese military general and strategist who served under the warlord Sun Ce in the late Eastern Han dynasty of China. After Sun Ce's death in 200, he continued serving under Sun Ce's brother and successor, Sun Quan. Zhou Yu is best known for his leading role in defeating the numerically superior forces of the northern warlord Cao Cao at the Battle of Red Cliffs in late 208, a victory that secured Sun Quan's control of the territory south of the Yangtze.

Early life and service under Sun Ce

Zhou Yu was born in Shu County, Lujiang Commandery, in present-day Anhui. He came from a distinguished family: his great-granduncle Zhou Jing and Zhou Jing's son Zhou Zhong both served as Grand Commandant in the Han government. As a youth, Zhou Yu befriended Sun Ce, a son of the general Sun Jian, and the two became close friends. When Sun Ce set out to conquer the Jiangdong region in 195, Zhou Yu joined him with troops and supplies raised from his own family, and he played a key role in Sun Ce's campaigns. The two friends later married two sisters, the Qiao sisters: Sun Ce took Elder Qiao and Zhou Yu took Younger Qiao.

Battle of Red Cliffs

In 208, Cao Cao, having unified northern China, led a massive army south to conquer the Yangtze region. Zhou Yu, serving as a frontline commander, urged Sun Quan not to surrender. At the Battle of Red Cliffs, Zhou Yu's forces, allied with those of Liu Bei, exploited a fire attack devised with the help of the veteran general Huang Gai, whose feigned surrender allowed fire ships to be sailed into Cao Cao's fleet. Cao Cao's army, weakened by disease and unfamiliar with naval warfare, was routed and forced to retreat north. The victory preserved the independence of the south and helped set up the tripartite division of China that became the Three Kingdoms.

Later campaigns and death

After Red Cliffs, Zhou Yu led Wu forces in the campaign for Jiangling against Cao Cao's general Cao Ren, eventually capturing the city in 209 after a year-long siege, during which Zhou Yu was wounded by an arrow. He subsequently proposed an ambitious plan to invade Yi Province (present-day Sichuan and Chongqing) and then challenge Cao Cao in the north. While preparing for this campaign, Zhou Yu fell ill and died at Baqiu in 210, at the age of 36 (by East Asian reckoning). Sun Quan deeply mourned him and personally led his funeral procession.

Legacy

Zhou Yu was remembered as a man of great talent, generosity, and refinement, skilled in music as well as strategy. A popular saying went that "if the tune has a mistake, Lord Zhou will look up" (曲有誤，周郎顧), reflecting his ear for music. In the 14th-century historical novel Romance of the Three Kingdoms, Zhou Yu is depicted as a brilliant but jealous rival of the Shu strategist Zhuge Liang, a portrayal at odds with the historical records, which describe him as magnanimous and widely respected.
