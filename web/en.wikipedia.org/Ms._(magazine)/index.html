Ms. is an American feminist magazine co-founded in 1971 by journalists and activists Gloria Steinem and Dorothy Pitman Hughes. The first issue of Ms. as an independent publication appeared in July 1972, following a preview issue inserted into New York magazine in December 1971 that sold out in eight days. The magazine was the first national publication to present the women's liberation movement to a mass audience, covering issues such as domestic violence, sexual harassment, reproductive rights, women in politics, and the gender pay gap.

The magazine's name was chosen because the honorific "Ms." — a form of address for women regardless of marital status — embodied the magazine's refusal to define women by their relationship to men. The magazine helped popularize the term, which entered mainstream usage over the following decade.

History

During its heyday in the 1970s, Ms. combined movement journalism with practical content, publishing writers such as Alice Walker and Susan Brownmiller, the children's features "Stories for Free Children", and the long-running "No Comment" section of reader-submitted examples of sexist media. In 1972 it published a famous cover story, "We Have Had Abortions", in which prominent American women acknowledged having abortions when the procedure was still widely illegal.

Faced with pressure from advertisers, Ms. relaunched as an advertising-free, reader-supported magazine in 1990. In 2001 the Feminist Majority Foundation acquired the magazine and has published it since, from Los Angeles. Ms. now appears quarterly in print and continuously online, and its investigative reporting on abortion access, campus sexual assault, and women's political representation has won multiple journalism awards.
