The grey parrot (Psittacus erithacus), also known as the Congo grey parrot or African grey parrot, is an Old World parrot native to equatorial Africa. It is renowned for its intelligence and ability to mimic human speech, which has made it one of the most popular avian pets — and one of the most heavily trafficked, leading to its listing as Endangered.

Description

The grey parrot is a medium-sized, mostly grey bird with a black bill and striking scarlet tail feathers. Adults measure about 33 cm in length and weigh around 400 grams. Males and females look alike. The timneh parrot, once considered a subspecies, is darker with a maroon tail and is now usually treated as a separate species.

Intelligence

Grey parrots are considered among the most intelligent birds. Researcher Irene Pepperberg's work with the parrot Alex demonstrated abilities in counting, categorising objects by colour and shape, and vocal labelling, suggesting cognitive skills comparable to those of a young child in some tasks.

Distribution and habitat

The species inhabits lowland rainforest, forest edges and clearings across central and west Africa, from Ivory Coast to western Kenya. Wild populations have declined sharply due to trapping for the pet trade and habitat loss, and in 2016 the species was moved to Appendix I of CITES, banning most international commercial trade.

In captivity

Grey parrots are demanding pets requiring extensive mental stimulation and social interaction; neglected birds may develop feather-plucking and other behavioural problems. They can live for 40 to 60 years.
