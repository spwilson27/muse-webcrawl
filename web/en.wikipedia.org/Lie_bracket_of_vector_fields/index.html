Lie bracket of vector fields

In differential geometry, the Lie bracket of vector fields, also known as the Jacobi–Lie bracket or the commutator of vector fields, is an operator that assigns to any two vector fields X and Y on a smooth manifold M a third vector field denoted [X, Y].

Definition

Treating vector fields as derivations on the ring of smooth functions, the Lie bracket is defined by the commutator: [X, Y]f = X(Yf) − Y(Xf) for every smooth function f. In local coordinates, if X = Σ ai ∂i and Y = Σ bi ∂i, then [X, Y] has components Σ (aj ∂j bi − bj ∂j ai) ∂i.

Properties

The Lie bracket is bilinear over the reals, skew-symmetric ([X, Y] = −[Y, X]), and satisfies the Jacobi identity, making the space of vector fields into an infinite-dimensional Lie algebra. It measures the failure of the flows of X and Y to commute: if [X, Y] = 0, the flows commute locally. By Frobenius's theorem, a distribution is involutive — tangent to a foliation — exactly when it is closed under the Lie bracket.

Related concepts

The bracket is the infinitesimal version of the commutator of diffeomorphisms and is related to the Lie derivative: the Lie derivative of Y along X equals [X, Y]. The concept is named after Sophus Lie, who developed it in his theory of transformation groups.
