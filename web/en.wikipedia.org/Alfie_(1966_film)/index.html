Alfie (1966 film)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Directed by: Lewis Gilbert

Screenplay by: Bill Naughton, based on his play

Starring: Michael Caine, Shelley Winters, Millicent Martin, Vivien Merchant

Music by: Sonny Rollins

Release date: 1966

Country: United Kingdom

Alfie is a 1966 British comedy-drama film directed by Lewis Gilbert and starring Michael Caine in the title role. An adaptation of Bill Naughton's 1963 play of the same name, it was a landmark of the British New Wave and made Caine an international star.

Plot

Alfie Elkins is a cheerful, self-centred young London chauffeur who drifts through a series of affairs, treating women as disposable pleasures and addressing the audience directly in asides to explain his philosophy of life. His carefree existence gradually unravels: he fathers a child he rarely sees, causes a married woman to undergo a traumatic abortion, loses his health, and ends the film confronting the emptiness of his choices, asking "What's it all about?"

Production and reception

The film's jazz score was composed by saxophonist Sonny Rollins, and its title song, "Alfie", written by Burt Bacharach and Hal David, became a standard — performed by Cilla Black over the UK credits and by Cher for the American release. The film was nominated for five Academy Awards, including Best Picture, Best Actor for Caine and Best Supporting Actress for Vivien Merchant. It is noted for its direct-address technique, its frank treatment of sexuality and abortion for its era, and Caine's charismatic, cockney performance.

Legacy

A sequel, Alfie Darling, appeared in 1975, and a Hollywood remake starring Jude Law was released in 2004. The original remains one of the defining films of 1960s "Swinging London".
