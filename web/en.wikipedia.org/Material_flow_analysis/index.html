Material flow analysis

Material flow analysis (MFA)
Field: Industrial ecology
Principle: Conservation of mass
Scale: Substances, products, firms, regions, national economies

Material flow analysis (MFA), also known as material flow accounting, is a method of quantifying flows and stocks of materials or substances within a system defined in space and time. It is a core tool of industrial ecology, used to describe the metabolism of economies and ecosystems, to identify losses and inefficiencies, and to support environmental policy, waste management and resource strategy.

Method

An MFA begins by defining the system boundary (a factory, a city, a country, or the global cycle of an element) and the balance period. All inputs — raw materials, imports, extraction — are tracked through processes and stocks to outputs: products, emissions, waste and exports. The method rests on the law of conservation of mass: inputs must equal outputs plus net changes in stock, which provides a powerful check on data quality. Flows are often visualised with Sankey diagrams, in which arrow widths are proportional to quantities.

Variants and applications

Substance flow analysis tracks a single chemical element or compound, such as nitrogen, phosphorus, cadmium or carbon. Economy-wide MFA, standardized by Eurostat and used by the OECD and the UN International Resource Panel, compiles national accounts of domestic extraction, imports and exports in tonnes, yielding indicators such as domestic material consumption (DMC) and material footprint. MFA underpins studies of resource productivity, circular-economy strategies, recycling potential, critical raw materials and the environmental burdens embodied in trade.
