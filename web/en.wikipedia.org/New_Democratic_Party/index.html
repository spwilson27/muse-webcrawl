New Democratic Party

The New Democratic Party (NDP; French: Nouveau Parti démocratique) is a federal political party in Canada, generally described as social democratic. Founded in 1961 through the merger of the Co-operative Commonwealth Federation (CCF) and the Canadian Labour Congress, the NDP is the successor to a tradition of prairie populism and democratic socialism; the Saskatchewan CCF government of Tommy Douglas pioneered publicly funded universal health care in Canada, later adopted nationally.

The party's signature colour is orange. Federally, the NDP has never formed government, but it has held the balance of power in several minority parliaments and formed the Official Opposition for the first time after the 2011 election under Jack Layton — the "Orange Wave", driven by a breakthrough in Quebec. From 2022 to 2024 the NDP, under Jagmeet Singh, sustained the Liberal minority government through a confidence-and-supply agreement in exchange for policy measures including a national dental-care programme and movement toward pharmacare. Provincial NDP parties have formed governments in provinces including British Columbia, Alberta, Manitoba, Saskatchewan, Ontario and Nova Scotia.

Ideology and policies

The NDP advocates universal public health care including dental and pharmaceutical coverage, action on climate change, affordable housing, workers' rights and union support, wealth taxes, reconciliation with Indigenous peoples, and electoral reform. Its founding Regina Manifesto-era democratic socialism has moderated over time toward social democracy comparable to European centre-left parties, though the party remains formally affiliated with organised labour and is a member of the Progressive Alliance.
