Ban Zhao

Ban Zhao (c. 45 – c. 116 CE), courtesy name Huiban, was a Chinese historian, philosopher and author of the Eastern Han dynasty. She is regarded as the first known female historian of China.

Life and works

Ban Zhao was the daughter of the historian Ban Biao and younger sister of the general Ban Chao and the historian Ban Gu. After Ban Gu's death, she completed his great dynastic history, the Book of Han, including its astronomical treatise and chronological tables. She served as an instructor and adviser at the imperial court, teaching the empress and court ladies from the imperial library.

Her best-known work, Lessons for Women (Nüjie), set out Confucian expectations for women's conduct — humility, obedience and diligence — and remained influential, and contested, for nearly two thousand years. She also wrote poems and memorials, only fragments of which survive.
