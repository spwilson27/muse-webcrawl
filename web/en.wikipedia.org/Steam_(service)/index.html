Steam (service)

DeveloperValve Corporation
Launched12 September 2003
PlatformsWindows, macOS, Linux, mobile
TypeDigital distribution, DRM, social networking

Steam is a digital distribution service and storefront developed by Valve Corporation. It was launched as a software client in September 2003 to provide game updates automatically for Valve's games, and expanded to distributing third-party titles in late 2005. Steam offers digital rights management (DRM), game server matchmaking, anti-cheat measures, social networking, and game streaming services.

History

Valve created Steam to simplify the patching of its online games, particularly Counter-Strike, and to combat piracy and cheating. The service gained wide exposure with the 2004 release of Half-Life 2, which required Steam activation even for retail copies — a controversial requirement at the time. From 2005, third-party publishers began distributing games through the store, and the catalog grew to tens of thousands of titles.

Features

Steam provides cloud saves, in-game voice and text chat, achievements, user reviews, community forums, the Steam Workshop for user-created content, and SteamVR for virtual reality games. Its seasonal sales (such as the Summer and Winter Sales) are major events in PC gaming. The Steam Deck, a handheld gaming computer released in 2022, runs the SteamOS operating system and brought the library to a portable form factor.

Market position

Steam is the dominant digital storefront for PC gaming, with over 130 million monthly active users reported in the early 2020s and peak concurrent users exceeding 38 million in 2024. Valve takes a standard 30 percent revenue share on most sales, a model that has drawn criticism and competition from rivals such as the Epic Games Store, as well as legal scrutiny in several jurisdictions.
