The Conservative Party of Canada (French: Parti conservateur du Canada) is a federal political party in Canada, formed in 2003 by the merger of the Canadian Alliance and the Progressive Conservative Party of Canada. It is one of the two major parties that have governed Canada, the other being the Liberal Party.


The modern party unites the western Canadian Alliance and the Progressive Conservatives.

History

On forming government under Stephen Harper in 2006, the party ended 12 years of Liberal rule, governing first as a minority, then winning a majority in 2011. The Harper government lowered the GST and corporate taxes, withdrew Canada from the Kyoto Protocol, and took a more assertive foreign policy stance. The party lost the 2015 election to Justin Trudeau's Liberals and moved into opposition. Andrew Scheer became leader in 2017 and increased the party's seat count in 2019, winning the popular vote but remaining in opposition; Erin O'Toole succeeded him in 2020, and Pierre Poilievre won the leadership in 2022 on a platform of affordability, lower taxes, and "common sense" politics.

Predecessor parties

The party claims descent from the Conservative Party that, under John A. Macdonald, led the first government of the Dominion of Canada in 1867 and built the Canadian Pacific Railway and the National Policy tariffs. Renamed Progressive Conservative in 1942, it governed under John Diefenbaker, Brian Mulroney, and briefly Kim Campbell, before collapsing to two seats in 1993 as the Reform Party (later the Canadian Alliance) captured the western conservative vote. The 2003 merger reunited the federal conservative movement.

Ideology and base

The party combines economic liberalism — low taxes, balanced budgets, free markets — with strands of social conservatism and western populism, and generally favours smaller government, tougher criminal sentencing, and a strong military. Its electoral base is strongest in the Prairie provinces and rural Ontario, and it contests elections in all 338 federal ridings. Provincial conservative parties are organisationally separate, though memberships and personnel overlap.
