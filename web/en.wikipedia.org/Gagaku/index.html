Gagaku

Gagaku (雅楽, literally "elegant music") is a type of Japanese classical music that was historically used for imperial court music and dances. It developed as part of the court rituals of the Imperial Household and has been performed for well over a thousand years, making it one of the oldest continuously performed orchestral traditions in the world. In 2009, UNESCO inscribed gagaku on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

History

Gagaku arrived in Japan from China and Korea during the Asuka and Nara periods (5th–8th centuries), drawing on Chinese yayue, Korean court music, and music from Central and South Asia transmitted along the Silk Road. It was codified under the Taihō Code of 701, which established a court music bureau (Gagakuryō). During the Heian period the repertory was reorganized into the categories still recognized today.

Forms and instruments

Gagaku comprises several genres: kangen (instrumental ensembles), bugaku (dances accompanied by music), ritual songs, and popular-style songs and dances (saibara and rōei). Music from continental Asia is classed as tōgaku (Tang music) and Korean-derived music as komagaku. Characteristic instruments include the shō (mouth organ), hichiriki (double-reed pipe), ryūteki (transverse flute), the biwa lute, the gakuso zither, and percussion such as the kakko, shōko, and great drum (dadaiko). Gagaku melody is notably slow and stately, built on modal scales.

Modern performance

Today gagaku is performed by the musicians of the Imperial Household Agency's Music Department at the Tokyo Imperial Palace, at court ceremonies, shrines, and temples, and by independent ensembles in Japan and abroad.
