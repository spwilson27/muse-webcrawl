San Francisco de Campeche (Yucatec Maya: Ahk'ìin Pech, pronounced [aχkʼiːn˥ peʧ]), commonly known simply as Campeche, is a city and seaport in the Mexican state of Campeche, on the shore of the Bay of Campeche in the Gulf of Mexico. It serves as the municipal seat of the Municipality of Campeche and as the capital city of the state, which bears the same name. As of the 2020 census, the city had a population of 249,623.

The city was founded on 4 October 1540 by Spanish conquistador Francisco Hernández de Córdoba and later established as a port by Francisco de Montejo "el Mozo". Because Campeche was repeatedly attacked by English, French and Dutch pirates during the 17th century, the Spanish crown ordered the construction of extensive fortifications; the walled historic centre, with its bastions, gates and colonial grid, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1999.

History

The site was originally the Maya settlement of Ah Kin Pech. Spanish expeditions reached the bay in 1517, and after two decades of conflict the younger Montejo founded the villa in 1540. Campeche quickly became the principal port of the Yucatán Peninsula, exporting logwood (palo de tinte), salt and other goods to Spain.

Pirate attacks culminated in the devastating raid of 1663, after which construction of the city walls began in 1686. The hexagonal wall, some 2.5 kilometres long with eight bastions, was substantially complete by 1704. The forts of San José el Alto and San Miguel guarded the approaches. After independence, Campeche became capital of the new state of Campeche in 1863, when the territory was separated from Yucatán.

Geography and climate

Campeche lies on the western coast of the Yucatán Peninsula, along a shallow limestone shelf of the Gulf of Mexico. It has a tropical savanna climate (Köppen Aw), with a wet season from June to October and high temperatures throughout the year, averaging around 27 °C.

Landmarks

The historic centre preserves pastel-coloured colonial houses, the Cathedral of Our Lady of the Immaculate Conception, the Puerta de Tierra and Puerta de Mar gates, and bastions such as San Carlos, which houses the city museum. The malecón stretches along the waterfront, and the archaeological site of Edzná lies about 50 kilometres to the southeast.

Economy

Fishing, shrimping, offshore petroleum services associated with the Cantarell field, government administration and heritage tourism form the basis of the local economy. The port handles general cargo and serves the regional oil industry.
