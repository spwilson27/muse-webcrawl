Jambalaya

Jambalaya is a savoury rice dish of Louisiana Creole and Cajun cuisine, influenced by Spanish, French, African and Native American cooking. It typically consists of meat and seafood cooked with rice, the 'holy trinity' of onion, celery and bell pepper, and seasonings.

Varieties

Creole jambalaya, from New Orleans, includes tomatoes and is reddish; Cajun jambalaya, from rural Acadiana, is browned without tomatoes. Common proteins include andouille sausage, chicken, shrimp and ham.

Origins

Its roots are often linked to Spanish paella adapted to Louisiana ingredients, combined with French and West African rice traditions.

Culture

Jambalaya is celebrated in festivals in Louisiana and in Hank Williams' 1952 song 'Jambalaya (On the Bayou)'.
