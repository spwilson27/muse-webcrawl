TNT

Trinitrotoluene
Formula: C₇H₅N₃O₆
Molar mass: 227.13 g/mol
Appearance: pale yellow solid
Detonation velocity: about 6,900 m/s

TNT (trinitrotoluene, more specifically 2,4,6-trinitrotoluene) is a chemical compound with the formula C₇H₅N₃O₆. It is a yellow solid best known as an explosive material with convenient handling properties: it is insensitive to shock and friction, can be melted and cast into shells, and requires a detonator to explode. It is the standard reference for measuring explosive yield — a "kiloton of TNT" is a unit of energy equal to about 4.184 terajoules.

History

TNT was first prepared by the German chemist Julius Wilbrand in 1863 and was used initially as a yellow dye. Its explosive potential was recognised decades later; the German military adopted it as an artillery shell filling in 1902, and it became the principal military explosive of both world wars.

Production and properties

It is prepared by the stepwise nitration of toluene with mixed nitric and sulphuric acids. TNT is toxic and a skin irritant; munitions workers exposed to it historically suffered yellowed skin and liver damage, and contaminated sites remain an environmental problem.
