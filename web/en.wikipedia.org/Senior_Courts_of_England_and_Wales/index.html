Senior Courts of England and Wales

The Senior Courts of England and Wales were originally created by the Judicature Acts as the "Supreme Court of Judicature". It consists of the Court of Appeal, High Court of Justice, and the Crown Court.

The name was changed by the Constitutional Reform Act 2005, which renamed the Supreme Court of Judicature to the Senior Courts of England and Wales, effective from 1 October 2009, when the new Supreme Court of the United Kingdom came into existence as the highest appellate court.

History

The Judicature Acts of 1873 and 1875 fused the administration of the previously separate courts of common law and equity into a single Supreme Court of Judicature, comprising the High Court of Justice and the Court of Appeal. The Courts Act 1971 added the Crown Court, which replaced the courts of assize and quarter sessions as the senior criminal court.

Composition

The Court of Appeal is divided into a Civil Division and a Criminal Division. The High Court consists of three divisions: the King's Bench Division, the Chancery Division, and the Family Division. The Crown Court deals with serious criminal cases tried on indictment.
