Paraconsistent logic

Paraconsistent logic is a type of non-classical logic that attempts to deal with contradictions in a discriminating way. Unlike classical logic, in which a contradiction entails everything (the principle of explosion, ex contradictione quodlibet), paraconsistent systems allow inconsistent premises to be used without trivialising the theory.

Motivation

Paraconsistent logics have been motivated by applications including inconsistent databases and legal or scientific theories, semantic paradoxes such as the liar paradox, and philosophical positions such as dialetheism — the view, defended by Graham Priest and others, that some contradictions may be true. Systems of paraconsistent logic include relevance logics, the Logic of Paradox (LP), and the da Costa hierarchy.

Definition

A logic is paraconsistent if its consequence relation is not explosive: that is, there exist formulas A and B such that from A and not-A, B does not follow. Paraconsistent logics typically achieve this by weakening classical proof rules or by allowing sentences to be both true and false in a model, as in three- or four-valued semantics.
