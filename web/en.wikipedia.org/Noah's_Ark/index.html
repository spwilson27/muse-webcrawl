Noah's Ark (Hebrew: תיבת נח, Tevat Noaḥ) is the vessel in the Genesis flood narrative (chapters 6–9 of the Book of Genesis) through which God spares Noah, his family, and specimens of all animal species from a global deluge sent to destroy humankind. After the waters subside, the ark comes to rest on the mountains of Ararat, and God establishes a covenant with Noah symbolized by the rainbow. The story is shared, with variations, in Judaism, Christianity, and Islam, where Noah (Nuh) is a prophet and the ark a sign of divine mercy.

The biblical narrative

Seeing the wickedness of humanity, God instructs Noah to build an ark of gopher wood, 300 cubits long, 50 cubits wide, and 30 cubits high, with three decks, and to bring aboard his wife, his sons Shem, Ham, and Japheth with their wives, and pairs of all animals (seven pairs of clean animals). Rain falls for forty days and nights and the waters cover the earth for 150 days. Noah sends out a raven and then a dove to test the waters; when the dove returns with an olive leaf, he knows the flood is receding. The occupants disembark, Noah offers sacrifices, and God promises never again to destroy the earth by water.

Origins and parallels

Scholars note strong parallels with earlier Mesopotamian flood stories, including the Epic of Gilgamesh's account of Utnapishtim and the Atrahasis epic, in which a hero builds a boat to survive a divinely sent flood. The Genesis narrative is generally understood as a composite of the Yahwist and Priestly sources, whose interwoven details account for variations in the numbers of animals and the flood's duration.

Searches for the ark

From antiquity to the present, expeditions have searched for remains of the ark, particularly around Mount Ararat in eastern Turkey (the greater of two peaks traditionally associated with the landing). Reports of ark sightings and wooden structures have periodically attracted attention, but no verified archaeological evidence of the vessel has been found, and geological and historical scholarship treats the flood narrative as mythic in genre.

Cultural legacy

The ark is a ubiquitous motif in children's literature, art, and popular culture, and full-scale replica arks have been built, including the Ark Encounter theme park in Kentucky, opened in 2016.
