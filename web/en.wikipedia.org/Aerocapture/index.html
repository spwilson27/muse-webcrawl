Aerocapture


Artist's concept of aerocapture at Mars

Aerocapture is an orbital transfer maneuver in which a spacecraft uses aerodynamic drag from a single pass through a planetary atmosphere to decelerate from a hyperbolic approach trajectory into a bound, elliptical orbit. By contrast, aerobraking uses many passes through the upper atmosphere over weeks or months to gradually reduce orbital energy, and requires the spacecraft to already be in orbit.

Description

During an aerocapture pass, the spacecraft dips into the atmosphere at high speed, and drag removes enough kinetic energy that the vehicle exits the atmosphere on an elliptical orbit rather than escaping the planet. A small propulsive burn at apoapsis then raises the periapsis out of the atmosphere to prevent re-entry. Because a conventional propulsive orbit insertion can consume more than half of a spacecraft's arrival mass in propellant, aerocapture could enable heavier payloads to reach orbit around planets such as Mars, Venus, Titan, Uranus and Neptune.

Challenges

The technique demands precise guidance and navigation, since the corridor between capturing into orbit and either burning up or skipping back out into space is narrow. The vehicle must also survive severe aerodynamic heating during the pass. As of the mid-2020s, aerocapture has been studied extensively but has not yet been flown on an operational mission.
