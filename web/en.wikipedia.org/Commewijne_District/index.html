Commewijne District
Country: Suriname
Capital: Nieuw Amsterdam
Area: 2,353 km²
Population: approx. 31,000

Commewijne is a district of Suriname, located on the right bank of the Suriname River opposite the capital, Paramaribo. Its capital is Nieuw Amsterdam. The district is known for its historic plantation belt and for the ferry and bridge connections to Paramaribo.

Geography

Commewijne is bordered by the Atlantic Ocean to the north, the district of Marowijne to the east, Para to the south, and the Suriname River to the west. Much of the district consists of low-lying coastal plain reclaimed for plantation agriculture.

History

From the late 17th century onward, Dutch colonists established sugar, coffee and cacao plantations along the Commewijne River. Fort Nieuw Amsterdam, built at the confluence of the Suriname and Commewijne rivers, guarded the approach to Paramaribo. After abolition of slavery in 1863, indentured labourers from India and Java settled in the district, shaping its present ethnic mix.

Economy and transport

Agriculture, fishing and small-scale trade dominate the economy. The Jules Wijdenbosch Bridge, opened in 2000, links Commewijne directly to Paramaribo and spurred suburban growth in resorts such as Meerzorg.
