Flavivirus

FamilyFlaviviridae
GenomePositive-sense single-stranded RNA, ~11 kb
TransmissionMosquitoes and ticks

Flavivirus is a genus of positive-strand RNA viruses in the family Flaviviridae. The name derives from the Latin flavus ("yellow"), referring to yellow fever virus, the type species, which causes jaundice. The genus includes many important human pathogens.

Members and diseases

Notable flaviviruses include yellow fever virus, dengue virus, Zika virus, West Nile virus, Japanese encephalitis virus, and tick-borne encephalitis virus. Most are arboviruses transmitted between vertebrate hosts by mosquitoes or ticks.

Structure and genome

Flavivirus particles are roughly 50 nm in diameter, enveloped, with a single open reading frame translated into a polyprotein that is cleaved into three structural proteins (C, prM/M, E) and seven non-structural proteins. Vaccines exist for yellow fever, Japanese encephalitis, and tick-borne encephalitis; dengue control relies largely on vector management.
