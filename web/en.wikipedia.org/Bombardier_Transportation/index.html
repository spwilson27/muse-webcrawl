Bombardier Transportation

Industry: Rail transportation

Founded: 1974

Defunct: 2021 (acquired by Alstom)

Headquarters: Berlin, Germany

Parent: Bombardier Inc. (until 2021)

Bombardier Transportation was the rail equipment division of the Canadian conglomerate Bombardier Inc. and for many years the world's largest manufacturer of railway rolling stock. Headquartered in Berlin, it built locomotives, multiple units, trams, metro trains, signalling systems, and bogies at sites around the world. In January 2021 the division was sold to the French manufacturer Alstom.

History

Bombardier entered the rail business in 1974, when it won a contract to build cars for the Montreal Metro. It expanded rapidly by acquisition: it bought MLW-Worthington's locomotive works, purchased stakes in Mexican and American builders, and in 1990 acquired BN Constructions Ferroviaires et Métalliques of Belgium. The purchase of Germany's Adtranz from DaimlerChrysler in 2001 made Bombardier the industry leader and moved the division's headquarters to Berlin.

Products

Major product families included the TRAXX line of electric and diesel locomotives, Talent and Talent 2 regional trains, the Twindexx double-deck multiple units, Flexity trams and light rail vehicles, Movia metro trains, and the Zefiro family of high-speed trains, developed with partners in China and Italy. The company also supplied propulsion equipment, bogies, and the CITYFLO and EBI rail control systems.

Sale to Alstom

In February 2020 Bombardier, burdened by debt, agreed to sell the division to Alstom. The transaction closed on 29 January 2021 for approximately €5.5 billion, making the combined Alstom the world's second-largest rail manufacturer. Some assets were later divested to satisfy European competition regulators.
