United Nations Security Council Resolution 242 was adopted unanimously on 22 November 1967, in the aftermath of the Six-Day War. Drafted principally by the British ambassador Lord Caradon, it laid down the "land for peace" formula that became the cornerstone of subsequent Arab–Israeli diplomacy, including the Egypt–Israel peace treaty, the Oslo Accords and the Israel–Jordan peace treaty.

Content

The resolution emphasizes "the inadmissibility of the acquisition of territory by war" and affirms that a just and lasting peace requires:

(i) Withdrawal of Israeli armed forces from territories occupied in the recent conflict;
(ii) Termination of all claims or states of belligerency and respect for and acknowledgment of the sovereignty, territorial integrity and political independence of every State in the area and their right to live in peace within secure and recognized boundaries free from threats or acts of force.

It further affirms the necessity of freedom of navigation through international waterways, of a just settlement of the refugee problem, and of guaranteeing territorial inviolability, and it requested the Secretary-General to appoint a Special Representative — a role filled by Gunnar Jarring — to promote agreement.

The missing definite article

Few omissions in diplomatic history have been debated as intensely as the absence of "the" or "all" before "territories" in the English text: the resolution calls for withdrawal "from territories occupied in the recent conflict," not "from the territories." Israel and its supporters have argued this permits retention of some territory pending negotiated secure boundaries; Arab states and most other governments point to the preamble and to the French text ("des territoires occupés") as requiring complete withdrawal. Lord Caradon later confirmed the omission was deliberate.

Acceptance and legacy

Egypt and Jordan accepted the resolution, Israel accepted it while disputing its interpretation, and Syria rejected it until 1973. Resolution 338 (1973) called for its implementation after the Yom Kippur War. Resolution 242 remains the agreed framework reference, reaffirmed in the Madrid Conference, the Oslo process and the Arab Peace Initiative.
