Lechon

Lechon (Spanish: lechón) refers to a roasted suckling pig dish popular in several Spanish-speaking and former Spanish colonial regions, most famously in the Philippines, where it is considered a centerpiece of celebrations and fiestas. The whole pig is seasoned, stuffed in some regional variants, skewered on a spit, and roasted slowly over charcoal until the skin is crisp and golden-brown.


A Filipino lechon ready for serving

Philippine lechon

In the Philippines, lechon is closely associated with Cebu, whose version is seasoned with lemongrass, scallions, garlic, and other aromatics stuffed in the cavity; it is so flavourful that it is often eaten without sauce. In Luzon, lechon is typically served with a sweet liver-based sauce. The dish is a fixture of birthdays, weddings, town fiestas, and Christmas (Noche Buena).

Other variants

In Spain and Latin America — including Cuba and Puerto Rico, where it is called lechón asado — roast suckling pig is prepared for holidays and festivals. Related dishes include Indonesia's babi guling and the roast pigs of Pacific Island feasts.
