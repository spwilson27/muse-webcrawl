Long-distance trail

Hikers on a long-distance mountain trail

A long-distance trail (or long-distance footpath, track, way or greenway) is a recreational route, typically unpaved and intended mainly for walking, hiking, backpacking, cycling or horse riding, that extends over a considerable distance — generally at least several days' travel, and often hundreds or thousands of kilometres. Walking an entire trail in one continuous journey is known as thru-hiking.

Notable trails

  • Appalachian Trail (United States) — about 3,540 km (2,200 mi) from Georgia to Maine, completed in 1937; part of the "Triple Crown of Hiking."
  • Pacific Crest Trail (United States) — about 4,270 km (2,650 mi) from Mexico to Canada through California, Oregon and Washington.
  • Continental Divide Trail (United States) — about 5,000 km (3,100 mi) along the Rocky Mountains.
  • Camino de Santiago (Spain/France) — historic pilgrimage routes, notably the Camino Francés of about 780 km, travelled since the Middle Ages.
  • GR routes (Europe) — the French sentiers de grande randonnée and sister networks, including the GR5 and the trans-European E-paths.
  • National Trails (United Kingdom) — 16 designated routes such as the Pennine Way, South West Coast Path (1,014 km) and Offa's Dyke Path.
  • Te Araroa (New Zealand) — about 3,000 km the length of the country; Great Wall trekking routes in China; and the Trans Canada Trail, a multi-use network of over 28,000 km.

Management and culture

Long-distance trails are typically waymarked, maintained by public agencies, clubs or volunteers, and supported by guidebooks, shelters, hostels and "trail angel" communities. The modern long-distance trail movement is often traced to the founding of the Appalachian Trail (conceived by Benton MacKaye in 1921) and to Britain's National Parks and Access to the Countryside Act 1949. Long trails serve recreation, conservation, public health and rural tourism, and have inspired a substantial literature from Bill Bryson's A Walk in the Woods to Cheryl Strayed's Wild.
