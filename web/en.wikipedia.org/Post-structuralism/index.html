Post-structuralism

Post-structuralism is a philosophical and literary movement that emerged in France in the late 1960s, growing out of — and reacting against — structuralism. Where structuralism, following Ferdinand de Saussure and Claude Lévi-Strauss, sought stable systems of signs and structures underlying language and culture, post-structuralists argued that such structures are themselves unstable: meaning is deferred, texts contain internal contradictions, and the subject is an effect of language and power rather than a self-present origin of meaning.

Origins

A conventional starting point is Jacques Derrida's 1966 lecture "Structure, Sign, and Play in the Discourse of the Human Sciences", delivered at Johns Hopkins University, which criticised structuralism's reliance on a fixed "centre" of structure. In 1967 Derrida published three foundational works, including Of Grammatology, introducing deconstruction and the notion of différance — meaning produced through difference and deferral. In the same period Roland Barthes announced "The Death of the Author" (1967), arguing that a text's meaning is completed by its reader rather than controlled by its author's intentions.

Major figures and themes

Michel Foucault analysed how knowledge is bound up with power ("power/knowledge"), tracing in works such as Discipline and Punish (1975) the historical formation of institutions and subjects; his histories deliberately undermined narratives of continuous progress. Gilles Deleuze, often with Félix Guattari (Anti-Oedipus, 1972), attacked psychoanalytic and structural accounts of desire. Jean-François Lyotard's The Postmodern Condition (1979) defined the postmodern as "incredulity toward metanarratives". Julia Kristeva developed notions of intertextuality and the semiotic; Jean Baudrillard theorised simulation and hyperreality. Common themes include suspicion of binary oppositions (speech/writing, nature/culture, male/female) and their hidden hierarchies, the instability of the sign, and the decentred subject.

Influence and criticism

Post-structuralism profoundly influenced literary theory, comparative literature, gender studies, postcolonial theory and cultural studies, especially in the Anglophone academy from the late 1970s. It has been criticised by analytic philosophers and others for obscurity, relativism and hostility to science — charges aired prominently in the 1996 Sokal affair — and by some Marxists for abandoning material explanation. Many of the thinkers labelled post-structuralist, including Foucault and Derrida, rejected or ignored the label; it functions largely as a retrospective umbrella term.
