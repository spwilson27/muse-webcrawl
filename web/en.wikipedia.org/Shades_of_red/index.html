Shades of red

Varieties of the color red may differ in hue, chroma (also called saturation, intensity, or colorfulness), or lightness (or value, tone, or brightness), or in two or three of these qualities. Variations in value are also called tints and shades, a tint being a red mixed with white, a shade being a red mixed with black.

Notable shades

  • Scarlet — a brilliant red tinged slightly toward orange.
  • Crimson — a deep red slightly inclining toward purple, originally the color of kermes dye.
  • Vermilion — a vivid red-orange historically made from powdered cinnabar (mercuric sulfide).
  • Maroon — a dark brownish red, named after the French word for chestnut.
  • Burgundy — a deep red associated with Burgundy wine.
  • Ruby — a deep pinkish red named after the gemstone.
  • Cardinal red, fire engine red, Indian red, madder, rose, rust, and venetian red are among many other named varieties.

In culture

Red is associated with blood, fire, danger, love, and revolution; particular shades carry specific meanings, from the scarlet of academic and ecclesiastical dress to the crimson of particular universities and sports teams.
