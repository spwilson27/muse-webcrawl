The Succession to the Crown Act 2013 is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that altered the laws of succession to the British throne in accordance with the Perth Agreement of 2011. It replaced male-preference primogeniture with absolute primogeniture for those born after 28 October 2011, ended the disqualification of a person who marries a Roman Catholic, and repealed the Royal Marriages Act 1772.

Background

At the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting in Perth, Australia, in October 2011, the prime ministers of the 16 Commonwealth realms agreed to reform the rules of succession. The changes required legislation in each realm; in the United Kingdom the necessary changes were enacted by this Act.

Provisions

The Act provides that the gender of a person born after 28 October 2011 does not affect their place in the line of succession. A person who marries a Roman Catholic is no longer disqualified from succeeding, although a Roman Catholic may not themselves succeed. The first six persons in line must obtain the sovereign's consent to marry, failing which they and their descendants from that marriage are disqualified.

Commencement

The Act received royal assent on 25 April 2013 and came into force on 26 March 2015, once parallel legislation had been completed across the other realms.
