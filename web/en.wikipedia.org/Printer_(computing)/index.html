Printer (computing)

In computing, a printer is a peripheral machine which makes a durable representation of graphics or text, usually on paper. While most output is human-readable, bar code printers are an example of an expanded use for printers. Different types of printers include 3D printers, inkjet printers, laser printers, and thermal printers.

History

The first computer printer designed was the mechanically driven apparatus of Charles Babbage's difference engine in the 19th century; however, his mechanical printer design was not built until 2000. The first patented printing mechanism for applying a marking medium to a recording medium or more particularly an electrostatic inking apparatus and a method for electrostatic inking was in 1962 by C. R. Winston. Early line printers and daisy wheel printers gave way to dot matrix printers in the 1970s, inkjet printers in the 1980s, and laser printers thereafter.

Types

Inkjet printers propel droplets of liquid ink onto paper; they are popular for home use. Laser printers use a laser beam and toner, producing fast, high-quality text and dominating offices. Thermal printers are used in receipts and labels. 3D printers build objects layer by layer from digital models.

Printing speed and resolution

Speed is measured in pages per minute (ppm); resolution in dots per inch (dpi). Modern printers connect via USB, Ethernet, or wireless protocols such as Wi-Fi, and support standards including AirPrint and IPP.
