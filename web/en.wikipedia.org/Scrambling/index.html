Scrambling

Scrambling is a mountaineering and hill-walking activity that lies between hiking and rock climbing: an ascent of steep terrain requiring the use of the hands for balance and progress, but ordinarily without ropes or technical climbing equipment. Scrambles are typically graded by difficulty, and popular routes include Striding Edge and Sharp Edge in England's Lake District and the Aonach Eagach ridge in Scotland.

Grading

In Britain, scrambles are commonly graded 1 to 3. Grade 1 routes are essentially steep walks where hands are used occasionally; Grade 2 routes involve more sustained exposure and some route-finding; Grade 3 routes approach easy rock climbs (comparable to climbing grades of Moderate or Difficult) and may warrant a rope for security. In North America, similar terrain is often described using the Yosemite Decimal System classes 3 and 4.

Risks and skills

Although scrambling is not technical climbing, falls from scrambles are a significant cause of mountain accidents, since terrain is steep, protection is absent, and loose rock and exposure are common. Scramblers are advised to build skills in route-finding, movement on rock, and judging weather and conditions. Related disciplines include canyoning, via ferrata, and alpine scrambling in larger mountain ranges such as the Alps and the Cascades.
