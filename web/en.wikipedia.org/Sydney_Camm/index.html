Sydney Camm

Image placeholder: Sydney Camm with his most famous design, the Hurricane.

Sir Sydney Camm, CBE, FRAeS (5 August 1893 – 12 March 1966) was an English aeronautical engineer who contributed to many Hawker aircraft designs, from the biplanes of the 1920s to the jet fighters of the 1960s. He is best remembered as the designer of the Hawker Hurricane, the RAF fighter that, alongside the Spitfire, won the Battle of Britain.

Career

Camm joined the Hawker company in 1925 as chief designer. His interwar designs included the Hart light bomber and its many derivatives, the Fury biplane fighter and then the Hurricane (first flight 1935), a rugged monoplane derived from the Fury. During and after the Second World War he produced the Typhoon, the Tempest and the Sea Fury, and then the swept-wing Hunter jet fighter, one of Britain's most successful postwar aircraft.

Later work and honours

In his later years Camm led design work on the P.1127, the experimental aircraft that evolved into the Harrier jump jet — a project he did not live to see fly operationally. He was knighted in 1953. Camm's pragmatic, evolutionary approach to design is credited with giving Britain dependable front-line aircraft for four decades.
