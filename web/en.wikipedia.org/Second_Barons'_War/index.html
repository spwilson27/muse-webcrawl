Second Barons' War

The Second Barons' War (1264–1267) was a civil war in England between the forces of a group of barons led by Simon de Montfort and the royalist forces of King Henry III, led by the king and his son, the future Edward I. It followed the First Barons' War of 1215–17 and the reforming Provisions of Oxford (1258), by which the barons had sought to limit royal power.

Course of the war

After Henry repudiated the Provisions with papal backing, war broke out in 1264. At the Battle of Lewes (14 May 1264) de Montfort defeated and captured the king and Prince Edward, becoming the effective ruler of England. His government summoned the parliament of 1265, notable for including representatives of the boroughs as well as knights of the shires — often cited as a landmark in the development of Parliament. Edward escaped captivity, rallied the royalists and destroyed de Montfort's army at the Battle of Evesham (4 August 1265), where de Montfort was killed and his body mutilated.

Settlement

Remaining rebels held out at Kenilworth Castle and on the Isle of Ely until 1267. The Dictum of Kenilworth (1266) allowed rebels to recover their lands on payment of fines, and the Statute of Marlborough (1267) confirmed parts of Magna Carta and the reform programme in law, restoring peace.
