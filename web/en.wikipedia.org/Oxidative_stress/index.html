Oxidative stress

Oxidative stress
Free radicals can damage DNA, proteins, and lipids
CauseImbalance of reactive oxygen species and antioxidants
Key speciesSuperoxide, hydrogen peroxide, hydroxyl radical
DefencesSuperoxide dismutase, catalase, glutathione peroxidase
Associated withAgeing, cancer, cardiovascular and neurodegenerative diseases

Oxidative stress reflects an imbalance between the systemic production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) — including free radicals such as superoxide and the hydroxyl radical — and a biological system's ability to detoxify those reactive products or repair the resulting damage. Disturbances in this normal redox state can cause toxic effects through damage to proteins, lipids, and DNA.

Sources and defences

ROS are generated as by-products of mitochondrial respiration, by enzymes such as NADPH oxidase in immune cells, and by external factors including ultraviolet light, ionizing radiation, pollution, and cigarette smoke. Cells defend themselves with antioxidant enzymes (superoxide dismutase, catalase, glutathione peroxidase), small-molecule antioxidants (glutathione, vitamin C, vitamin E), and repair systems. At low levels ROS also serve useful signalling functions — a beneficial, adaptive response sometimes termed oxidative eustress or mitohormesis.

Role in disease

Chronic oxidative stress has been implicated in the pathogenesis of atherosclerosis, diabetes complications, cancer, chronic inflammation, and neurodegenerative diseases such as Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease, as well as in the ageing process itself — an idea formalized in the free radical theory of ageing (Denham Harman, 1956). Despite this, large clinical trials of antioxidant supplements have generally failed to show benefit and in some cases showed harm, suggesting the biology is more subtle than simple radical scavenging.

Measurement

Oxidative stress is assessed indirectly through biomarkers of damage, including malondialdehyde and 4-hydroxynonenal (lipid peroxidation), 8-hydroxy-2'-deoxyguanosine (DNA oxidation), and protein carbonyls.
