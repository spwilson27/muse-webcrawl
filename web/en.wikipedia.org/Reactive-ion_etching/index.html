Reactive-ion etching

Reactive-ion etching (RIE) is a dry plasma etching technique used in microfabrication to remove material from a wafer in a patterned, highly directional way. A radio-frequency electromagnetic field ionises a low-pressure process gas into a plasma; ions are accelerated toward the wafer by a self-bias voltage and both chemically react with the surface and physically sputter it away.

Process

The synergy of chemical reaction and ion bombardment makes RIE far more anisotropic than wet etching, producing near-vertical sidewalls essential for integrated-circuit features. Gas chemistry is matched to the material: fluorine gases such as CF4 and SF6 etch silicon and silicon dioxide, while chlorine chemistries etch aluminium and III–V semiconductors. A variant, deep reactive-ion etching (DRIE) using the Bosch process, alternates etch and passivation cycles to carve very deep, high-aspect-ratio structures for MEMS.
