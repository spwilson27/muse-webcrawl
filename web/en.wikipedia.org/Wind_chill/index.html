Wind chill

ConceptPerceived lowering of air temperature due to wind
Developed byPaul Siple and Charles Passel (Antarctica, 1940s)
Modern indexAdopted in North America, 2001

Wind chill (also called the wind chill factor) is the lowering of body temperature due to the passing flow of lower-temperature air. Wind chill numbers express the rate of heat loss from exposed skin caused by wind and cold, and are always lower than the actual air temperature.

Explanation

Wind removes the thin insulating layer of warmed air next to the skin, accelerating heat loss and making cold conditions feel colder and increasing the risk of frostbite. The original index was developed by Paul Siple and Charles Passel from experiments on water freezing in Antarctica during the 1940s. A revised formula based on human trials was adopted by the U.S. National Weather Service and Environment Canada in 2001.
