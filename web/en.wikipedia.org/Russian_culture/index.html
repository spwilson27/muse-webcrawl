Russian culture

Saint Basil's Cathedral in Moscow, an emblem of Russian culture.

Russian culture has a long and rich history, rooted in the culture of the East Slavs and shaped by Russia's vast geography, its Orthodox Christian heritage, and centuries of interaction with Europe and Asia. Russians regard their literary, artistic, and musical traditions as central to national identity, and Russian works hold a prominent place in world culture.

Literature

Russian literature is considered among the world's greatest. The nineteenth century produced Alexander Pushkin, regarded as the founder of modern Russian literature, along with novelists Leo Tolstoy, Fyodor Dostoevsky, Ivan Turgenev, and playwright and story writer Anton Chekhov. Their explorations of faith, morality, and society influenced writers worldwide.

Music, ballet, and art

Composers such as Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky, Sergei Rachmaninoff, and Igor Stravinsky shaped classical music, while the Russian ballet companies — above all the Bolshoi and Mariinsky — set global standards. Russia is also noted for medieval icon painting, the nineteenth-century Peredvizhniki realists, and avant-garde pioneers such as Wassily Kandinsky and Kazimir Malevich.

Language and traditions

The Russian language, written in the Cyrillic alphabet, unites a multi-ethnic federation. Cultural life reflects Orthodox festivals, folk traditions such as Maslenitsa, distinctive cuisine, and symbols like the matryoshka doll and the samovar. Under the Soviet Union, culture was heavily state-directed but produced major achievements in cinema, science, and space exploration.
