Photography

Photography

Photography is the art, application, and practice of creating images by recording light, either electronically with an image sensor or chemically on light-sensitive material such as photographic film. The resulting image is a photograph. Photography serves science, manufacture, business, mass communication, surveillance, and personal expression, and is widely practised as a fine art.

History

The camera obscura had been used for centuries to project images, but fixing them required light-sensitive chemistry. In 1826 or 1827 Nicéphore Niépce made the earliest surviving camera photograph, View from the Window at Le Gras, using bitumen of Judea on a pewter plate. His partner Louis Daguerre developed the daguerreotype, publicly announced in 1839, which produced sharp single images on silvered copper. In Britain, William Henry Fox Talbot's calotype paper-negative process allowed multiple prints from one negative, establishing the negative–positive principle that dominated until the digital era.

Successive innovations — wet collodion plates (1851), dry gelatin plates (1870s), flexible roll film (George Eastman's Kodak, 1888), 35 mm rangefinder cameras (Leica, 1925), and practical colour processes (Kodachrome, 1935) — made photography progressively faster, cheaper, and more portable. Photojournalism, documentary work, and amateur snapshots reshaped 20th-century visual culture.

Digital photography

Charge-coupled device (CCD) sensors, invented in 1969, enabled digital cameras, which reached the mass market in the 1990s and overtook film sales in the early 2000s. The integration of capable cameras into smartphones after 2007 made photography ubiquitous: trillions of photographs are now taken each year. Digital workflows replaced the darkroom with software editing, and images are shared instantly over networks.

Technique

Photographers control exposure through aperture, shutter speed, and sensitivity (ISO), focus and depth of field through lens choice, and composition through framing and viewpoint. Genres include portrait, landscape, documentary, fashion, sports, wildlife, macro, and astrophotography. Related techniques such as photomontage manipulate and combine images after capture.

Art and society

Long debated as a mechanical craft, photography gained recognition as a fine art through figures such as Alfred Stieglitz, Ansel Adams, Dorothea Lange, and Henri Cartier-Bresson. Photographs have documented wars, driven social reform, and shaped public memory, while also raising enduring questions about truth, manipulation, and privacy.
