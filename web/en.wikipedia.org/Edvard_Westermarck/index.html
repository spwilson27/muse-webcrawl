Edvard Alexander Westermarck (20 November 1862 – 3 September 1939) was a Finnish philosopher, sociologist and anthropologist. He was among the first to study marriage and morality from a comparative, Darwinian perspective.

Work

Westermarck's The History of Human Marriage (1891) challenged the prevailing theory that early humans lived in sexual promiscuity, arguing instead for the antiquity of monogamy. In it he proposed what became known as the Westermarck effect: that children raised together in early childhood develop a sexual aversion toward one another — a proposed mechanism for the incest taboo. His The Origin and Development of the Moral Ideas (1906–1908) and Ethical Relativity (1932) defended a form of moral relativism grounded in emotions of approval and disapproval.

Career

Westermarck taught at the London School of Economics, where he was the first professor of sociology in the United Kingdom, and at Åbo Akademi University in Finland. He conducted extensive fieldwork in Morocco, published as Ritual and Belief in Morocco (1926) and Wit and Wisdom in Morocco (1930).
