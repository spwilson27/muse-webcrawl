Laplace's equation

Laplace's equation is a second-order partial differential equation named after Pierre-Simon Laplace, written as ∇²f = 0, where ∇² is the Laplacian. Solutions, called harmonic functions, arise in electrostatics, gravitation, fluid dynamics and heat conduction.

Properties

Harmonic functions are infinitely differentiable, satisfy the mean value property, and obey the maximum principle: they attain their extrema only on the boundary of a domain.

Boundary value problems

Solving Laplace's equation typically requires Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions. It is the steady-state case of the heat equation and a special case of Poisson's equation with zero source term.
