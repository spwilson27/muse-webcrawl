Degree of a continuous mapping

In topology, the degree of a continuous mapping between two compact oriented manifolds of the same dimension is a number that represents, roughly, the number of times the domain manifold wraps around the range manifold under the mapping. The degree is always an integer and may be positive, negative or zero; it is a homotopy invariant.

Definition

Let f : M → N be a continuous map between compact oriented n-manifolds. Choose a regular value y of f (by Sard's theorem such values are dense). The preimage of y is a finite set of points, and the degree is the sum over these points of +1 or −1 according to whether f locally preserves or reverses orientation at each point. The result is independent of the regular value chosen.

Examples

For a map from the circle to itself given by z ↦ zk on the unit circle in the complex plane, the degree is k, reflecting the winding of the circle around itself k times. A constant map has degree 0. The antipodal map of the n-sphere has degree (−1)n+1.

Properties

The degree is invariant under homotopy: homotopic maps have the same degree. Consequently, a map of degree different from ±1 cannot be a homotopy equivalence, and the identity map has degree 1. The degree plays a central role in the Brouwer fixed-point theorem and in the theory of characteristic classes.
