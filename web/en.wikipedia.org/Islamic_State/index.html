The Islamic State (IS), also known as the Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant (ISIL), the Islamic State of Iraq and Syria (ISIS), and by its Arabic acronym Daesh, is a militant Islamist group and former unrecognised quasi-state. Founded in 1999 by the Jordanian militant Abu Musab al-Zarqawi, it grew out of al-Qaeda in Iraq during the Iraqi insurgency that followed the 2003 US-led invasion. In June 2014, after capturing Mosul and large swathes of Iraq and Syria amid the Syrian civil war, the group proclaimed a worldwide caliphate under Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi.

History

Origins

The group's roots lie in Jama'at al-Tawhid wal-Jihad, founded by al-Zarqawi in 1999. In 2004 it pledged allegiance to al-Qaeda and became known as al-Qaeda in Iraq. After al-Zarqawi was killed in a US airstrike in 2006, the group rebranded as the Islamic State of Iraq. Weakened by the US troop surge and the Sunni Awakening, it was revived under Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi from 2010 and exploited the chaos of the Syrian civil war from 2011, operating there as Jabhat al-Nusra's parent organisation before an acrimonious split with both al-Nusra and al-Qaeda's central leadership in 2013–2014.

Territorial expansion and caliphate

In a lightning offensive in June 2014, the group seized Mosul, Iraq's second city, and advanced across northern Iraq. On 29 June 2014 it proclaimed itself a caliphate and renamed itself simply the Islamic State. At its height in late 2014 it controlled territory of roughly 100,000 square kilometres containing several million people, administered from its de facto Syrian capital Raqqa, and drew tens of thousands of foreign fighters from around the world.

Atrocities and persecution

The group committed mass killings, beheadings, and widespread sexual violence. Its campaign against the Yazidi people of Sinjar in August 2014 — involving mass murder and the enslavement of thousands of women and girls — has been recognised by the United Nations and multiple governments as genocide. It also destroyed cultural heritage, including parts of Palmyra and the Mosul Museum's artefacts, and claimed or inspired terrorist attacks worldwide, including the November 2015 Paris attacks.

Territorial defeat

A US-led international coalition, together with Iraqi forces, Kurdish-led Syrian Democratic Forces, and other actors, gradually retook the group's territory: Mosul was liberated in July 2017 and Raqqa in October 2017. The last territorial remnant at Baghouz in Syria fell in March 2019. Al-Baghdadi was killed in a US raid in Syria in October 2019; several of his successors as "caliph" were subsequently killed.

Current status

Since 2019 the group has persisted as a decentralised insurgency in Iraq and Syria and through regional affiliates ("provinces"), notably in Afghanistan (IS-Khorasan), West Africa, the Sahel, Mozambique, and the Philippines. It continues to conduct and inspire attacks internationally despite the loss of its territorial caliphate.

Ideology

The Islamic State adheres to an extreme interpretation of Salafi jihadism, seeking to purify Islam by returning to what it regards as the practices of the earliest generations of Muslims, and violently rejecting other Muslims it deems apostates, particularly Shia Muslims. Its propaganda combined apocalyptic theology with sophisticated social media recruitment.
