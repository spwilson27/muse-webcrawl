Synonym

A synonym is a word, morpheme, or phrase that means exactly or nearly the same as another word, morpheme, or phrase in a given language. For example, in English, begin, start, commence, and initiate are all synonyms of one another. Words that are synonyms occupy the same semantic niche in a thesaurus, and the standard test for synonymy is substitution: two words are synonymous if they can replace one another in a sentence without changing its truth conditions or acceptability—though perfect interchangeability in every context is rare.

Near-synonymy

Linguists distinguish cognitive synonyms, which share denotational meaning, from near-synonyms, which differ subtly in connotation, register, or collocation. Commence is more formal than start; slim and skinny denote similar body shapes but carry opposite evaluations. Complete synonymy is widely considered unstable and uncommon, since languages tend to differentiate words over time.

Related lexical relations

Synonymy is one of several sense relations studied in lexical semantics, alongside antonymy (oppositeness: hot/cold), hyponymy (inclusion: dog/animal), meronymy (part–whole: wheel/car), and homonymy (same form, unrelated meanings). The study of synonyms is central to lexicography, translation, and natural language processing, including the construction of resources such as WordNet, whose entries are organised into synonym sets ("synsets").

In taxonomy

In biological nomenclature, a synonym is a scientific name that has also been applied to a taxon when that taxon already had, or later received, another accepted name; the codes of nomenclature govern which name has priority. This usage differs from the linguistic one in being formal and rule-bound.

Etymology

The word synonym derives from the Greek synōnymos, from syn ("together") and onoma ("name"). The corresponding quality or relation between words is called synonymy.
