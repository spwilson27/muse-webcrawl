Lowell Observatory

Lowell Observatory is an astronomical observatory in Flagstaff, Arizona, founded in 1894 by astronomer Percival Lowell. It is among the oldest observatories in the United States and was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1965.

Discoveries

Lowell used the observatory to promote his (later discredited) theory of canals on Mars. Its most famous achievement was Clyde Tombaugh's discovery of Pluto in 1930, found during a search for Lowell's predicted "Planet X". Vesto Slipher's measurements of galaxy redshifts at Lowell in the 1910s later underpinned evidence for the expansion of the universe. The observatory today operates several telescopes, including the 4.3-metre Lowell Discovery Telescope, and runs extensive public programmes; Flagstaff became the first International Dark Sky City in 2001.
