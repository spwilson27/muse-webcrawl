Monte Carlo Rally

The Monte Carlo Rally or Rallye Automobile de Monte-Carlo (officially Rallye Automobile Monte Carlo) is a rallying event organised each year by the Automobile Club de Monaco. The rally takes place along the French Riviera, in Monaco and southeast France, traditionally in January, and is famous for its changeable conditions — a single stage can feature dry asphalt, wet asphalt, snow and ice, making tyre choice a gamble.

History

The rally was first run in 1911, conceived by Prince Albert I as a way to attract tourists to Monaco in the winter months; competitors started from various European cities and converged on Monte Carlo. It became one of the most prestigious events in rallying and was a founding round of the World Rally Championship in 1973. In 1966 the event was marred by controversy when the leading Minis and other British cars were disqualified over headlight regulations.

Characteristics

Unlike rallies starting from a single point, the modern event is based in Gap or Monaco with special stages in the Maritime Alps. The variable mountain weather means drivers may face ice on shaded corners minutes after dry roads, and "ice note" crews drive the stages before the rally to report conditions.

A competitor on a snowy Col de Turini stage, the rally's most famous test.

Famous stages

The Col de Turini stage, run over a mountain pass at 1,607 m with dozens of hairpins, is the rally's signature test; its "Night of the Long Knives" era, when the stage was run at night before huge crowds, is legendary. Other well-known stages include Sisteron and the Col de Bleine.

Winners

Sébastien Ogier holds the record with nine victories. Sébastien Loeb won eight times, and Tommi Mäkinen four consecutively (1999–2002). Finnish driver Rauno Aaltonen and the Mini Cooper S won famous victories in the 1960s (1964, 1965 and 1967 for the Mini marque, via Paddy Hopkirk, Timo Mäkinen and Aaltonen).
