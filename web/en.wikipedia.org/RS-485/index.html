RS-485


Placeholder image: RS-485

RS-485, officially TIA/EIA-485, is a standard defining the electrical characteristics of drivers and receivers for serial communications systems. Electrical signaling is balanced (differential), and the system may be multipoint, allowing multiple transmitters and receivers on one bus.

Characteristics

RS-485 uses differential voltage levels over a twisted pair, giving good noise immunity over long distances — up to about 1,200 metres at low data rates, and up to 10 Mbit/s over short runs. A network may include up to 32 unit loads (more with modern fractional-load transceivers).

Applications

The standard specifies only the physical layer; protocols such as Modbus RTU, Profibus and DMX512 run over it. It is widely used in industrial automation, building control, and stage lighting. Full-duplex operation uses two pairs; half-duplex uses one.
