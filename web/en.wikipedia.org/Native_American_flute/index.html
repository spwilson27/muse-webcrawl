Native American flute

The Native American flute is a flute that is held in front of the player, has open holes for the fingers, and has two chambers: one for collecting the breath of the player and a second chamber which creates sound. The player breathes into one end of the flute without the need for an embouchure. A block on the outside of the instrument, often carved in the shape of a bird or animal, directs the air across an opening, creating a whistle-like tone.

History and culture

Flutes have a long history among Indigenous peoples of North America; some of the oldest known examples, such as the Belson flutes from Utah, date to over a thousand years ago. Flutes were used in courtship, healing, ceremony, and personal enjoyment. The modern revival of the instrument began in the late 1960s and 1970s, and contemporary makers produce flutes in a wide range of keys, often tuned to a pentatonic minor scale that makes improvisation accessible to beginners.

Construction

Traditional materials include river cane, bamboo, and softwoods such as cedar and juniper. The instrument's distinctive sound hole, flue, and block (fetish) arrangement distinguish it from other folk flutes. Two main styles are recognized: the Plains style and the Eastern Woodlands style, differing in bore and mouthpiece design.
