Armory Show

Armory Show
Official name: International Exhibition of Modern Art
Dates: 17 February – 15 March 1913
Venue: 69th Regiment Armory, New York City
Organised by the Association of American Painters and Sculptors

The Armory Show, formally the International Exhibition of Modern Art, was an exhibition held in New York City in 1913 that introduced American audiences to European avant-garde art on a large scale. It is widely regarded as a turning point in American art history.

Contents

The show included roughly 1,300 works by more than 300 artists, including Henri Matisse, Pablo Picasso, Wassily Kandinsky, Constantin Brâncuși, and Marcel Duchamp, whose Nude Descending a Staircase, No. 2 became the exhibition's most notorious work, mocked in the press as "an explosion in a shingle factory". American artists such as Edward Hopper and Marsden Hartley also exhibited.

Reception and impact

Attendance in New York reached about 87,000 before the show travelled to Chicago and Boston. Though ridiculed by conservative critics (former president Theodore Roosevelt wrote a review titled "A Layman's Views of an Art Exhibition"), the show transformed American taste, collecting, and art-making, spurring the growth of the modern art market in the United States.
