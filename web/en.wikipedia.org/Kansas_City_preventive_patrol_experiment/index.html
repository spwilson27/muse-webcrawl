Kansas City preventive patrol experiment

Kansas City preventive patrol experiment
Conducted: 1972–1973
Sponsor: Police Foundation
Location: Kansas City, Missouri
Finding: routine preventive patrol had no significant effect on crime

The Kansas City preventive patrol experiment was a landmark study of policing conducted in Kansas City, Missouri, in 1972–1973 by the Police Foundation. It tested the assumption that routine motorised preventive patrol deters crime.

Design

Fifteen police beats were divided into three groups: reactive beats, where preventive patrol was withdrawn and officers responded only to calls; control beats, patrolled as usual; and proactive beats, where patrol levels were increased two to three times.

Results

The study found no significant differences between the groups in reported crime, victimisation, citizen fear or satisfaction. The results challenged a core doctrine of modern policing and spurred research into alternative strategies such as directed and problem-oriented patrol. See also Broken windows theory.
