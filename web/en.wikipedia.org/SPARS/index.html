SPARS was the nickname of the United States Coast Guard Women's Reserve, the women's branch of the United States Coast Guard Reserve during the Second World War. The name is a contraction of the Coast Guard motto Semper Paratus and its English translation, "Always Ready".

History

The Women's Reserve was authorised in November 1942, following the model of the Navy's WAVES. Its first director was Lieutenant Commander (later Captain) Dorothy Stratton, who coined the name SPARS. More than 10,000 women enlisted, serving in shore-based jobs — as clerks, radio operators, parachute riggers, control tower operators and in the top-secret LORAN navigation programme — thereby releasing men for sea duty. They served only within the continental United States.

Legacy

The wartime SPARS were demobilised in 1946, but a peacetime women's reserve was later authorised, and women were integrated into the regular Coast Guard in the 1970s. The Coast Guard cutter USCGC Spar and various memorials commemorate the service; the SPARS were the last of the major American women's reserve components of the war to be established.
