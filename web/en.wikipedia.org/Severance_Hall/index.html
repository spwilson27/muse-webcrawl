Severance Hall

Severance Hall facade

Severance Hall, now formally the Jack, Joseph and Morton Mandel Concert Hall at Severance, is a concert hall in the University Circle district of Cleveland, Ohio, and the home of the Cleveland Orchestra. Opened in 1931, it is widely regarded as one of the most beautiful concert halls in the United States, celebrated for both its architecture and its acoustics.

History

The hall was built principally through the philanthropy of John Long Severance, president of the Musical Arts Association, and his wife Elisabeth. Elisabeth Severance died unexpectedly in 1929 as construction began, and John Severance increased his gift and dedicated the building as a memorial to her; he died a few months before it opened. The exterior blends Neoclassical and Art Deco elements, while the interior features Egyptian Revival, Art Nouveau, and classical motifs, with an aluminium-leafed ceiling coffered in lotus patterns. The opening concert took place on 5 February 1931 under music director Nikolai Sokoloff.

Acoustics and renovation

Under George Szell, the hall's acoustics were modified in 1958 with the installation of the "Szell shell," a set of adjustable acoustic reflectors; complaints about the resulting dryness led to a major renovation completed in 2000, which restored much of the original resonance, expanded the stage, and added audience amenities while preserving the historic interior. The hall houses a Skinner/Norton Memorial Organ (E. M. Skinner Opus 816), restored and reinstalled as part of the renovation. Severance Hall was named a National Historic Landmark in 2021.
