Osculating circle

In differential geometry of curves, the osculating circle of a sufficiently smooth plane curve at a given point p on the curve is the circle that best approximates the curve near that point. It is defined as the circle that passes through p and a pair of additional points on the curve infinitesimally close to p; equivalently, it is the limiting circle obtained as two nearby points coalesce with p.

Properties

The centre of the osculating circle is called the centre of curvature and lies on the normal line to the curve at p. Its radius is the radius of curvature, equal to the reciprocal of the curvature κ at that point: R = 1/|κ|. The circle and the curve share the same tangent and the same curvature at p, so the curve and its osculating circle have second-order contact there.

Terminology

The word "osculate" comes from the Latin osculari, "to kiss", because the circle presses against the curve as closely as possible at the point of contact. Leibniz originally used the term circulus osculans. The locus of all centres of curvature of a curve is its evolute.

In three dimensions

For a space curve, the osculating circle lies in the osculating plane spanned by the tangent and normal vectors at the point. Its radius and centre are defined by the curve's curvature in the same way.
