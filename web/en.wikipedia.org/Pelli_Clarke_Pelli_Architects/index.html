Pelli Clarke Pelli Architects

Pelli Clarke Pelli Architects (formerly Cesar Pelli & Associates) is an American architecture firm headquartered in New Haven, Connecticut. Founded in 1977 by Argentine-born architect César Pelli after he left Gruen Associates, the firm is internationally known for designing some of the world's tallest buildings, including the Petronas Towers in Kuala Lumpur, once the tallest buildings in the world.

History

César Pelli founded the firm in 1977, shortly after completing the Pacific Design Center in Los Angeles. The practice grew rapidly with commissions such as the expansion of the Museum of Modern Art in New York and the World Financial Center (now Brookfield Place) in Manhattan. Fred W. Clarke was a co-founder and long-time senior partner; Rafael Pelli, César's son, is also a partner.

Selected projects

  • Petronas Towers, Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia (1998)
  • One Canada Square, Canary Wharf, London (1991)
  • International Finance Centre, Hong Kong (2003)
  • Costanera Center, Santiago, Chile
  • Salesforce Tower, San Francisco (2018)
  • National Museum of Art, Osaka, Japan

Design approach

The firm is known for expressive high-rise design, often using stainless steel, glass and stone cladding and forms responsive to local context and culture—for example, the Islamic-inspired geometry of the Petronas Towers.
