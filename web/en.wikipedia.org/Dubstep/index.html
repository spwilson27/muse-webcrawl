Dubstep

Dubstep is a genre of electronic dance music that originated in South London in the late 1990s. It is generally characterised by sparse, syncopated rhythmic patterns with prominent prominent sub-bass frequencies. The style emerged as a development of UK garage, dub, two-step and jungle, with early releases by producers on labels such as Big Apple Records and Tempa.

Characteristics

Dubstep tracks typically run at a tempo of around 140 beats per minute, with drums in half-time: the snare usually falls on the third beat of each bar, giving a slow, lurching feel despite the fast tempo. Central features are deep sub-bass lines, often modulated with an LFO to produce the oscillating "wobble" bass, sparse minor-key melodies, sampled percussion and large amounts of space and reverb drawn from dub production.

History

The genre coalesced around the Croydon record shop Big Apple Records and the club night FWD>> at Plastic People in London. Producers including Skream, Benga, Digital Mystikz, Kode9 and Burial shaped its first wave. By the late 2000s dubstep split into the atmospheric "post-dubstep" of Mount Kimbie and James Blake and an aggressive, midrange-heavy American variant dubbed "brostep", popularised by Skrillex, which brought the sound to festival main stages. Dubstep's rhythmic and bass techniques have since permeated pop, hip hop and EDM.
