The 11th arrondissement of Paris (XIe arrondissement) is one of the twenty arrondissements of the French capital, also known as the arrondissement de Popincourt. Situated on the right bank of the River Seine, it stretches from the Place de la Bastille and Place de la République in the west to the Place de la Nation in the east, and includes the Oberkampf and Belleville quarters.

History

The area developed during the industrial expansion of the 19th century as a working-class district of workshops, particularly in furniture-making around the Rue du Faubourg Saint-Antoine. It was a centre of revolutionary activity: the Bastille fortress, stormed on 14 July 1789, stood at its edge, and the district's barricades figured in the uprisings of 1830, 1848 and the Paris Commune of 1871. The boundaries of the current arrondissement were fixed in 1860 under Baron Haussmann's reorganisation of Paris.

Demographics

The 11th is one of the most densely populated urban districts in Europe. Its population peaked in the early 20th century and, after a mid-century decline, has stabilised at roughly 140,000–155,000 inhabitants. The area around Oberkampf and Parmentier has become known for its nightlife, bars and music venues, while retaining a mixed, cosmopolitan residential character.

Places of interest

Landmarks include the Place de la Bastille with the July Column and the Opéra Bastille; the Place de la République; the Cirque d'Hiver; the Bataclan theatre, site of the November 2015 terrorist attack in which 90 concertgoers were killed; and part of Père Lachaise Cemetery near its eastern boundary. The Saint-Ambroise and Sainte-Marguerite churches serve its historic parishes.
