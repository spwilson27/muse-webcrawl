The Patent Cooperation Treaty (PCT) is an international patent law treaty concluded in 1970 and administered by the World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO). It provides a unified procedure for filing patent applications in many countries at once: by filing a single "international" application, an applicant seeks patent protection simultaneously in all PCT contracting states. The PCT does not itself grant an "international patent"; the decision to grant remains with each national or regional patent office.

Procedure

An international application is filed with a receiving office, claiming priority from an earlier national application (usually within the 12-month Paris Convention period). An International Searching Authority — one of the major patent offices — carries out a prior-art search and issues an international search report and a written opinion on patentability. The application is published 18 months after the priority date. The applicant may also request an optional international preliminary examination. The procedure culminates in the "national phase" (or regional phase, e.g. before the European Patent Office), generally due 30 or 31 months from the priority date, in which each designated office examines the application under its own law.

Advantages

The PCT delays the substantial costs of foreign filings and translations by up to 18 months beyond the Paris Convention deadline, gives applicants an early assessment of patentability through the search report and opinion, and keeps options open across a very large number of countries with a single filing date. Annual PCT filings number in the hundreds of thousands, making it the principal route for seeking multinational patent protection.
