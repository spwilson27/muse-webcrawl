Seven Years War

The Seven Years War (1756–1763), often written Seven Years' War, was a worldwide conflict fought between the major European powers and their colonies. This article summarises the war with emphasis on its naming in different countries and its principal theatres; for the main treatment, see the article titled Seven Years' War.

Names in different theatres

  • French and Indian War — the North American theatre (1754–1763).
  • Third Silesian War — the Prussian–Austrian struggle over Silesia.
  • Third Carnatic War — the Anglo-French conflict in India.
  • Pomeranian War — the Swedish–Prussian theatre.
  • Fantastic War — the Spanish–Portuguese fighting in South America and the Iberian Peninsula (1762).

Summary of events

Formal hostilities opened in 1756 after two years of fighting in North America. Britain and Prussia faced France, Austria, Russia, Sweden, and (from 1762) Spain. Frederick the Great's Prussia survived largely because Russia withdrew in 1762, while British naval and colonial campaigns under the strategic direction of Pitt the Elder secured Canada, the Caribbean sugar islands, Senegal, and dominance in India after Plassey (1757) and Wandiwash (1760).

Settlement

The treaties of Paris and Hubertusburg (February 1763) ended the war. France lost Canada and its position in India; Spain ceded Florida to Britain but received Louisiana; Prussia retained Silesia. Britain emerged as the foremost maritime and colonial power, though the war debt prompted fiscal reforms that strained relations with its American colonies.
