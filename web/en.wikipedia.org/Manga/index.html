Manga

Manga (Japanese: 漫画) are comics or graphic novels originating from Japan. Most manga conform to a style developed in Japan in the late 19th century, and the form has a long and complex pre-history in earlier Japanese art. In Japan people of all ages and walks of life read manga; the medium includes works in a broad range of genres — action, adventure, romance, sports, fantasy, science fiction, horror, comedy and more.

Production and publication

Manga are typically first serialised in large weekly or monthly anthology magazines (such as Weekly Shōnen Jump), usually printed in black and white, and successful series are then collected into bound volumes called tankōbon. Artists (mangaka) such as Osamu Tezuka — whose post-war work, including Astro Boy, shaped the modern form — often work with assistants under tight deadlines. Stories are read in the traditional Japanese order, right to left.

Markets and influence

Categories are defined by target readership: shōnen (boys), shōjo (girls), seinen (adult men) and josei (adult women). Manga constitutes a multi-billion-dollar industry in Japan and has grown rapidly abroad since the late 20th century, with licensed translations, digital platforms and local imitations such as Korean manhwa and Chinese manhua. Manga has strongly influenced anime, which frequently adapts popular series, and global popular culture more broadly.
