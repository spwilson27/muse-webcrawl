David Attenborough

Sir David Frederick Attenborough (born 8 May 1926) is an English broadcaster, biologist, natural historian, and writer. He is best known for writing and presenting, in conjunction with the BBC Studios Natural History Unit, the nine nature documentary series forming the Life collection, a comprehensive survey of animal and plant life on Earth.

Attenborough joined the BBC in 1952 and rose to become controller of BBC Two, where he helped introduce colour television to Britain, and later director of programming. He resigned in 1973 to work full-time on natural history programmes. His breakthrough series, Life on Earth (1979), was watched by an estimated 500 million people worldwide.

Major series

  • Zoo Quest (1954–1963)
  • Life on Earth (1979)
  • The Living Planet (1984)
  • The Life of Birds (1998)
  • The Blue Planet (2001)
  • Planet Earth (2006)
  • Planet Earth II (2016)
  • Our Planet (2019)
  • A Life on Our Planet (2020)

Recognition and advocacy

Widely considered a national treasure in Britain, Attenborough has received numerous honours, including a knighthood, the Order of Merit, and many species named after him. In his later years his work has increasingly addressed biodiversity loss and climate change, urging action to preserve the natural world.
