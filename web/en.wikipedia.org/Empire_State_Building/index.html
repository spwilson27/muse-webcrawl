The Empire State Building is a 102-storey Art Deco skyscraper in the Midtown South neighbourhood of Manhattan in New York City. Built from 1930 to 1931, it was the tallest building in the world for nearly forty years, longer than any other building has held the record. Its name derives from "Empire State", the nickname of New York state.

Construction

The building was designed by Shreve, Lamb & Harmon and built at extraordinary speed — one storey a day — with a workforce of some 3,400 men, largely Irish and Italian immigrants and Mohawk steelworkers. It was officially opened on 1 May 1931 by President Herbert Hoover, who switched on the lights from Washington. The limestone facade encloses a steel frame rising 381 metres to the roof; with the antenna the height reaches 443 metres.

Cultural significance

The Empire State Building is one of the most recognisable landmarks in the world. It was immortalised in the 1933 film King Kong, has observatories on the 86th and 102nd floors drawing millions of visitors annually, and its floodlights mark holidays and events. It was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1986 and remains the premier symbol of New York City's skyline.
