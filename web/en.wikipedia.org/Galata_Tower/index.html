Galata Tower

Galata Tower (Turkish: Galata Kulesi)
Location: Beyoğlu, Istanbul, Turkey
Built: 1348 (current tower, Genoese)
Height: 66.9 m (219 ft)
Style: Romanesque

The Galata Tower (Turkish: Galata Kulesi), officially the Galata Tower Museum (Turkish: Galata Kulesi Müzesi), is a medieval Genoese tower in the Beyoğlu district of Istanbul, Turkey. Built as the Christea Turris ("Tower of Christ") in 1348, it was the tallest building in the city at the time and remains one of Istanbul's most recognizable landmarks.

History

The tower crowned the fortifications of the Genoese colony of Galata, across the Golden Horn from Constantinople. An earlier Byzantine tower on the site, the Megalos Pyrgos (Great Tower), which controlled the northern end of the great harbour chain, was destroyed during the Fourth Crusade in 1204. The Genoese rebuilt the tower in 1348 as part of their defensive walls.

After the Ottoman conquest of 1453, the tower served variously as a prison, a fire-watch tower, and an observatory. According to the traveller Evliya Çelebi, in the 1630s the legendary Hezarfen Ahmed Çelebi flew from its top across the Bosphorus on artificial wings. The upper section was repeatedly rebuilt after storm and fire damage, acquiring its conical cap; it was restored in the 1960s and again in 2020, when it reopened as a museum.

Description

The Romanesque stone tower is 66.9 metres (219 ft) high and about 16.5 metres in diameter at the base, with walls up to 3.75 metres thick. An observation deck near the top offers panoramic views over the Golden Horn, the Bosphorus, and the historic peninsula. The tower gives its name to the surrounding Galata neighbourhood and to the nearby Galata Bridge.
