Dominated convergence theorem

Dominated convergence theorem
Field: Measure theory, real analysis
Named for: Henri Lebesgue

In measure theory, Lebesgue's dominated convergence theorem gives sufficient conditions under which almost everywhere convergence of a sequence of functions implies convergence of their integrals. It is one of the central limit theorems of Lebesgue integration, alongside the monotone convergence theorem and Fatou's lemma.

Statement

Let (fn) be a sequence of measurable functions converging almost everywhere to a function f. If there exists an integrable function g such that |fn(x)| ≤ g(x) for all n and almost all x, then f is integrable and the integrals of fn converge to the integral of f; moreover, the integrals of |fn − f| tend to zero.

Applications

The theorem justifies exchanging limits and integrals in many arguments, including differentiating under the integral sign and computing limits of expectations in probability theory, where a random-variable version holds for sequences dominated by an integrable random variable.
