Billfish

Billfish are a group of predatory fish characterised by prominent bills, or rostra. The group includes marlins, sailfish, spearfish, and swordfish. Billfish are large, fast swimmers found in warm and temperate oceans worldwide, and they are highly prized as game fish.

Characteristics

The bill is an extension of the upper jaw, used to slash at schools of smaller fish and squid. Billfish have streamlined bodies and stiff, crescent-shaped tails that make them among the fastest fish in the ocean; sailfish have been clocked at over 100 km/h in short bursts.

Species

The family Istiophoridae includes the marlins, sailfish, and spearfish, while the swordfish is the sole member of Xiphiidae. Recognised species include the Atlantic blue marlin, black marlin, Indo-Pacific sailfish, and striped marlin.

Fishing and conservation

Billfish support major recreational fisheries and are also taken commercially, often as bycatch in longline fisheries. Several species are considered threatened or vulnerable, and catch-and-release practices and circle hooks are promoted to reduce mortality.
