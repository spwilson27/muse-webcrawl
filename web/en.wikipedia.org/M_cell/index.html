M cell

Microfold cells (or M cells) are specialized epithelial cells of the mucosa-associated lymphoid tissues of the gut, found in the follicle-associated epithelium overlying Peyer's patches in the small intestine and in similar lymphoid structures elsewhere in the gastrointestinal and respiratory tracts.

Function

Unlike neighboring enterocytes, M cells lack a dense brush border of microvilli and instead have short microfolds. Their role is immune surveillance: they endocytose antigens, particles, and microorganisms from the gut lumen and transport them across the epithelium (transcytosis) to dendritic cells, macrophages, and lymphocytes in a pocket beneath the cell, thereby initiating mucosal immune responses such as IgA production.

Clinical significance

Because they readily take up particles, M cells are exploited as entry portals by several pathogens, including Salmonella Typhi, Shigella, poliovirus, and prions. They are also studied as targets for oral vaccine and drug delivery.
