The Wind River Reservation is a Native American reservation in west-central Wyoming, shared by the Eastern Shoshone Tribe and the Northern Arapaho Tribe. It is the only reservation in Wyoming and one of the largest in the United States.

History

The reservation was established for the Shoshone by the 1863 Treaty of Fort Bridger and reduced by an 1868 treaty under Chief Washakie. In 1878 the Northern Arapaho, traditional allies of the US Army but landless, were moved onto the reservation, an arrangement made permanent despite Shoshone objections. In 1906 the tribes ceded a portion north of the Wind River, including the town of Riverton, under pressure to allot lands. Oil and gas development in the 20th century brought both revenue and disputes.

Geography and people

The reservation spans the Wind River Basin between the Wind River Range and the Owl Creek Mountains. Principal communities include Fort Washakie, the seat of tribal government, and Ethete and Arapahoe. The Shoshone cultural figure Sacagawea is traditionally said to be buried at Fort Washakie. The reservation's water rights were the subject of major litigation culminating in the US Supreme Court case Wyoming v. United States (1989).
