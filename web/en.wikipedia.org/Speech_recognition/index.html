Speech recognition

Speech recognition is an interdisciplinary subfield of computer science and computational linguistics that develops methodologies and technologies that enable the recognition and translation of spoken language into text by computers. It is also known as automatic speech recognition (ASR), computer speech recognition or speech to text (STT). It incorporates knowledge and research in the computer science, linguistics and computer engineering fields.

History

Early systems of the 1950s, such as Bell Labs' "Audrey", recognised single digits spoken by one voice. The 1970s saw DARPA-funded research and the introduction of hidden Markov models in the 1980s, which dominated the field for decades. From around 2010, deep neural networks — and later end-to-end and transformer-based models such as Whisper — dramatically reduced word error rates.

Methods

Modern systems typically extract acoustic features (such as mel-frequency cepstral coefficients or learned representations), then apply an acoustic model and a language model to decode the most likely word sequence. Performance is usually measured by word error rate (WER).

Applications

Applications include virtual assistants (Siri, Alexa, Google Assistant), dictation software, automatic captioning, voice dialling, and transcription services.
