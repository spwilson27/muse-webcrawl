Bushveld

Bushveld
Ecoregion in southern Africa
Location: Limpopo and Mpumalanga, South Africa
Vegetation: Subtropical woodland and savanna

The bushveld is a subtropical woodland ecoregion of southern Africa, centred on South Africa's Limpopo province with extensions into Mpumalanga, North West and neighbouring countries. The name combines "bush" with "veld", the Afrikaans word for field.

Ecology

The bushveld consists of savanna and mixed woodland dominated by acacias (now Vachellia and Senegalia), marula, leadwood and bushwillow trees, interspersed with grassland. The elevation ranges roughly from 750 to 1,400 metres, and rainfall is largely confined to summer thunderstorms. The region supports a rich large-mammal fauna, including the big five game animals, and includes large parts of Kruger National Park and numerous private game reserves.

Geology and mining

Underlying much of the area is the Bushveld Igneous Complex, the largest layered igneous intrusion on Earth, which contains the world's richest reserves of platinum-group metals, chromium and vanadium. Mining is a major economic activity.

Human use

Besides mining and game farming, the bushveld supports cattle ranching, citrus and subtropical fruit farming, and a large ecotourism industry.
