Maria Alyokhina

Alyokhina, artist, activist and co-founder of Mediazona.

Maria Vladimirovna Alyokhina (Russian: Мария Владимировна Алёхина; born 6 June 1988) is a Russian political activist, artist and writer. She is a member of the anti-Putin protest collective Pussy Riot, and was imprisoned for two years after the group's 2012 "Punk Prayer" performance at Moscow's Cathedral of Christ the Saviour. After her release she co-founded the prisoners' rights organisation Zona Prava and the independent news outlet Mediazona, and has remained an outspoken critic of the Russian government, fleeing the country in 2022.

Pussy Riot and imprisonment

On 21 February 2012, Alyokhina and four other Pussy Riot members performed "Punk Prayer – Mother of God, Chase Putin Away!" at the Cathedral of Christ the Saviour in Moscow, protesting the Russian Orthodox Church leadership's support for Vladimir Putin. Alyokhina was arrested in March and, with Nadezhda Tolokonnikova and Yekaterina Samutsevich, convicted of "hooliganism motivated by religious hatred" in August 2012 and sentenced to two years in a penal colony. Amnesty International designated the women prisoners of conscience, and the trial drew worldwide criticism. Alyokhina served her sentence at a colony in Berezniki in the Perm region; she undertook hunger strikes to protest conditions and was released under an amnesty in December 2013, shortly before the term's end.

Later activism

After prison, Alyokhina and Tolokonnikova founded Zona Prava (Zone of Rights) to defend prisoners' rights, and in 2014 launched Mediazona, an independent media outlet covering courts, police and prisons in Russia. Her memoir Riot Days (2017) recounts the protest, trial and imprisonment, and was adapted into a touring theatrical performance. Alyokhina was repeatedly detained for protests, including demonstrations in support of opposition leader Alexei Navalny. In 2021 she was sentenced to one year of restricted freedom in the so-called "sanitary case". In April 2022, facing further imprisonment amid the crackdown following Russia's invasion of Ukraine, she escaped house arrest — reportedly disguised as a food delivery courier — and fled Russia via Belarus and Lithuania. She has since lived in exile, continuing to perform and campaign.
