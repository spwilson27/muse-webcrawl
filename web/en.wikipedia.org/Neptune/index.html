Neptune is the eighth and farthest known planet from the Sun. It is the fourth-largest planet in the Solar System by diameter, the third-most massive, and the densest giant planet. Composed primarily of gases and liquids ("ices"), it is classified as an ice giant alongside Uranus. Neptune is named after the Roman god of the sea and has the astronomical symbol ♆.

Discovery

Neptune was the first planet located by mathematical prediction rather than observation alone. Irregularities in the orbit of Uranus led Urbain Le Verrier (and independently John Couch Adams) to calculate the position of an unseen planet; Johann Galle found Neptune on 23 September 1846 within one degree of Le Verrier's prediction. Galileo had in fact recorded it as a fixed star in 1612–13 without recognising it.

Physical characteristics

Neptune's vivid blue colour comes from traces of methane in its hydrogen–helium atmosphere. It radiates more than twice the heat it receives from the Sun, an internal warmth that drives the fastest winds in the Solar System, recorded at up to 2,100 km/h, and giant storm systems such as the Great Dark Spot imaged by Voyager 2 in 1989 — still the only spacecraft to visit the planet.

Moons and rings

Neptune has a system of faint rings and 16 known moons. The largest, Triton, orbits backwards (retrograde) and is thought to be a captured Kuiper belt object; it is geologically active, with nitrogen geysers, and is slowly spiralling inward.
