Excalibur is the legendary sword of King Arthur, sometimes attributed with magical powers or associated with the rightful sovereignty of Britain. In many versions, Excalibur is the sword given to Arthur by the Lady of the Lake, distinct from the sword in the stone whose drawing proved Arthur's kingship, though in other versions the two are the same weapon.

Origins and etymology

Arthur's sword appears in Welsh tradition as Caledfwlch. Geoffrey of Monmouth, in his Historia Regum Britanniae (c. 1136), latinised the name as Caliburnus, a sword forged on the isle of Avalon; through Old French forms this became Excalibur.

In the legends

In the French Vulgate Cycle and in Thomas Malory's Le Morte d'Arthur, the Lady of the Lake presents Excalibur and its scabbard to Arthur; the scabbard is said to protect the bearer from wounds. After Arthur is mortally wounded at the Battle of Camlann, the sword is returned to the lake by Sir Bedivere, where a hand rises from the water to catch it.
