Seismic wave

A seismic wave is a mechanical wave of acoustic energy that travels through the Earth or another planetary body. It can result from an earthquake (or similar event), a volcanic eruption, magma movement, a large landslide, or a man-made explosion.

Types

Body waves travel through the interior of the Earth. Primary (P) waves are compressional and fastest, moving through solids and liquids alike; secondary (S) waves are shear waves that cannot pass through liquids — their absence beyond Earth's core provided key evidence that the outer core is liquid. Surface waves travel along the surface: Rayleigh waves roll the ground in an elliptical motion, while Love waves shake it horizontally; surface waves usually cause the most damage in earthquakes.

Study

Seismologists record seismic waves with seismometers and use their travel times and reflections to image Earth's internal structure — seismic tomography — and to locate earthquakes. Controlled-source seismology applies the same principles to explore for oil, gas, and minerals.
