Black Power

The raised fist became an emblem of Black Power.

Black Power is a political slogan and a name for various associated ideologies associated with racial pride, economic empowerment, and the creation of political and cultural institutions for Black people in the United States and elsewhere. The movement emphasized self-determination, self-defense, and pride in African heritage rather than integration into white institutions.

Origins

The phrase was popularized by activist Stokely Carmichael (later Kwame Ture) of the Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee (SNCC) during the Meredith March Against Fear in Mississippi in June 1966, when he declared, "We want Black Power!" The slogan marked a shift within the civil rights movement toward more assertive demands and, for some adherents, black separatism and nationalism.

Expressions

Black Power found expression in organizations such as the Black Panther Party, in cultural movements celebrating African aesthetics and the slogan "Black is beautiful," in community control movements in education, and in the growth of Black studies programs at American universities. At the 1968 Mexico City Olympics, sprinters Tommie Smith and John Carlos raised gloved fists during the medal ceremony, an enduring image of the era. The movement also influenced liberation struggles internationally, including South Africa's Black Consciousness movement.

Legacy

Historians note that Black Power reshaped American culture and politics: it spurred ethnic pride movements among other groups, expanded Black political representation, and left a lasting imprint on art, music, and scholarship, even as it drew intense government surveillance and repression.
