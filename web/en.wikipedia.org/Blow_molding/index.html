Blow molding

Blow molding (or blow moulding) is a manufacturing process for forming hollow plastic parts. It is also used for forming glass bottles or other hollow shapes. In general, there are three main types of blow molding: extrusion blow molding, injection blow molding, and injection stretch blow molding.

Process

The process begins with softening plastic by heating and forming it into a parison, a tube-like piece with a hole in one end through which compressed air can pass. In extrusion blow molding, the parison is extruded, clamped into a cooled metal mold, and inflated with air so the plastic takes the shape of the mold cavity. After cooling, the mold opens and the part is ejected and trimmed.

Types

Injection blow molding injects the parison onto a core rod, giving precise neck finishes and no scrap, and suits small bottles. Injection stretch blow molding stretches the preform axially and radially, orienting the polymer chains for strength and clarity; it is the standard process for PET beverage bottles. Extrusion blow molding handles large and irregular parts such as fuel tanks, drums, and toys.

History

The process derives from glassblowing. The first attempts to blow-mold polymers date to the late 19th century, with the first mass-produced plastic bottles appearing in the 1930s–40s; the development of high-density polyethylene in the 1950s made blow molding a major industry.
