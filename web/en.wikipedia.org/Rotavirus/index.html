Rotaviruses are a genus of double-stranded RNA viruses in the family Reoviridae. They are the most common cause of severe diarrhoeal disease in infants and young children worldwide. Nearly every child has been infected with a rotavirus by the age of five. The viruses were first observed by Ruth Bishop and colleagues in Melbourne, Australia, in 1973, and are named for their characteristic wheel-like appearance (Latin rota, "wheel") under the electron microscope.

Virology

The rotavirus particle is a non-enveloped, triple-layered capsid about 76.5 nm in diameter containing 11 segments of double-stranded RNA that encode six structural proteins (VP1–VP4, VP6, VP7) and six non-structural proteins. The outer capsid proteins VP7 (G-type) and VP4 (P-type) define the serotypes and are the targets of neutralizing antibodies. Rotaviruses are classified into species (groups) A through J; group A causes the great majority of human disease.

Disease

Rotavirus is transmitted by the faecal–oral route and infects enterocytes at the tips of the small-intestinal villi, causing vomiting, watery diarrhoea, and fever that can lead to life-threatening dehydration. Before vaccine introduction, rotavirus was estimated to cause more than 215,000 deaths of children under five each year, overwhelmingly in low-income countries, along with millions of hospitalizations. Adults are usually infected asymptomatically or mildly because of immunity from childhood infections.

Prevention and treatment

Live oral rotavirus vaccines — Rotarix (monovalent) and RotaTeq (pentavalent), introduced from 2006 — are recommended by the World Health Organization for all national immunization programmes and have dramatically reduced severe rotavirus disease where used. An earlier vaccine, RotaShield, was withdrawn in 1999 after an association with intussusception, a rare bowel obstruction; current vaccines carry a much smaller residual risk. There is no specific antiviral therapy; treatment is supportive, centred on oral rehydration therapy with glucose-electrolyte solutions, which has saved millions of lives.
