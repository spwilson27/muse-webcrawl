Commonwealth realm

A Commonwealth realm is a sovereign state within the Commonwealth of Nations whose monarch is shared with the United Kingdom and the other realms. As of 2026 there are 15 Commonwealth realms: Antigua and Barbuda, Australia, The Bahamas, Belize, Canada, Grenada, Jamaica, New Zealand, Papua New Guinea, Saint Kitts and Nevis, Saint Lucia, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, Solomon Islands, Tuvalu, and the United Kingdom. Charles III is the current monarch of each.

Constitutional arrangement

Each realm is independent and the Crown is legally distinct in each — the King of Canada, for example, is a separate legal office from the King of the United Kingdom, though held by the same person. The monarch is represented in each realm (other than the UK itself) by a governor-general, appointed on the advice of that realm's prime minister. Governments operate as constitutional monarchies on the Westminster model.

History

The system evolved from the British Empire: the Balfour Declaration of 1926 declared the dominions equal in status, and the Statute of Westminster 1931 gave legal effect to their legislative independence. When India became a republic in 1950 while remaining in the Commonwealth, the distinction arose between realms sharing the monarch and republics with their own heads of state. Several former realms have become republics, most recently Barbados in 2021. Succession is governed by shared rules, amended in the Perth Agreement of 2011 to introduce absolute primogeniture.

Debates

Republican movements exist in several realms, notably Australia (where a 1999 referendum retained the monarchy) and Jamaica. Any realm may become a republic through its own constitutional processes without affecting its Commonwealth membership.
