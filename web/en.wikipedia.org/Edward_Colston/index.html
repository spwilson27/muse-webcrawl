Edward Colston (2 November 1636 – 11 October 1721) was an English merchant, slave trader, Tory MP and philanthropist. Much of his fortune was made through the trade in enslaved Africans as a senior official of the Royal African Company, which held a monopoly on the English trade in enslaved people from West Africa.

Career

Born into a wealthy Bristol merchant family, Colston traded in textiles, oil, wine and enslaved people. He became a member of the Royal Adventurers in 1680 and deputy governor of the Royal African Company from 1689 to 1690; during his involvement the company transported tens of thousands of enslaved Africans to the Americas. He was Member of Parliament for Bristol from 1710 to 1713.

Philanthropy and legacy

Colston endowed almshouses, schools and churches in Bristol and London, including Colston's School. A statue of him erected in Bristol in 1895 became increasingly controversial and was toppled by protesters on 7 June 2020 during the George Floyd protests and thrown into Bristol Harbour. It was later displayed in the M Shed museum lying on its side with the graffiti preserved. Institutions across Bristol, including Colston Hall (renamed Bristol Beacon), subsequently changed names associated with him.
