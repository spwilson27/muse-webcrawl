Semi-empirical mass formula

In nuclear physics, the semi-empirical mass formula (SEMF), also known as the Weizsäcker formula, Bethe–Weizsäcker formula, or Bethe–Weizsäcker mass formula, gives an approximation to the mass and binding energy of an atomic nucleus in terms of its numbers of protons and neutrons.

The formula

The formula is based on the liquid drop model of the nucleus and combines five terms: a volume term, a surface term, a Coulomb term accounting for repulsion between protons, an asymmetry term favouring equal numbers of neutrons and protons, and a pairing term reflecting the greater stability of even–even nuclei. Fitted coefficients reproduce measured binding energies across the nuclear chart to within a few MeV.

Significance

The formula explains the shape of the binding-energy curve, whose peak near iron and nickel underlies energy release in both fission of heavy nuclei and fusion of light ones. It was developed by Carl Friedrich von Weizsäcker in 1935 and elaborated by Hans Bethe and Robert Bacher.
