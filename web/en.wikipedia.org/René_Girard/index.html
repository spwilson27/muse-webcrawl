René Girard
Born: 25 December 1923, Avignon, France
Died: 4 November 2015, Stanford, California
Known for: Mimetic theory; scapegoat mechanism
Institutions: Johns Hopkins University; Stanford University

René Noël Théophile Girard (25 December 1923 – 4 November 2015) was a French historian, literary critic, and philosopher of social science. His work belongs to the tradition of philosophical anthropology and is unified by the theory of mimetic desire and the scapegoat mechanism.

Mimetic theory

In Deceit, Desire and the Novel (1961), Girard argued that human desire is not spontaneous but imitated from models, leading to rivalry over the same objects. In Violence and the Sacred (1972) he extended the theory to religion and culture, proposing that communities resolve mimetic crises by unanimous violence against a scapegoat, a mechanism he saw as foundational to ritual and myth.

Later work

In Things Hidden Since the Foundation of the World (1978) and later books, Girard argued that the Bible and the Gospels uniquely reveal and undermine the scapegoat mechanism by siding with the victim. He was elected to the Académie française in 2005.
