Telematics

Telematics is an interdisciplinary field encompassing telecommunications, vehicular technologies (road transport and safety), electrical engineering (sensors, instrumentation, wireless communications), and computer science (multimedia, the Internet). The word is a portmanteau of telecommunications and informatics (French: télématique), coined in a 1978 report to the French government by Simon Nora and Alain Minc. In its most common contemporary use, "telematics" refers to the integrated use of communications and information technology to transmit, store, and receive data from remote objects — above all, vehicles — over a network.

Vehicle telematics

A typical system combines a GPS receiver and a cellular modem in an onboard unit connected to the vehicle's diagnostic bus. It reports position, speed, engine status, fault codes, and driving behaviour (harsh braking, acceleration, cornering) to a central platform. Applications include:

  • Fleet management — tracking, route optimisation, fuel and maintenance management for commercial fleets.
  • Usage-based insurance — premiums priced from actual driving data ("pay how you drive").
  • Emergency services — automatic crash notification systems such as eCall (mandatory in new EU cars since 2018) and GM's OnStar, which combine GPS with voice and sensor data.
  • Stolen-vehicle recovery, remote diagnostics, and tolling.

Other uses

Beyond vehicles, the term covers health telematics (telemedicine and remote patient monitoring), remote asset and infrastructure monitoring, and, in its original broad French sense, the whole field of networked information services — the télématique that produced France's Minitel system. Telematics data raise significant privacy questions, and their collection and use are governed in many jurisdictions by data-protection law.
