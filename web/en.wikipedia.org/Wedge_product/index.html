The wedge product of two vectors represents the oriented parallelogram they span.

The wedge product is the multiplication operation in exterior algebra that is universally constructed to be antisymmetric, associative, and bilinear. It takes two vectors (or, more generally, multivectors) and produces a multivector of higher grade. For vectors u and v, their wedge product u ∧ v is a bivector representing the oriented plane segment spanned by the two vectors.

Properties

The wedge product is characterised by the following properties: it is bilinear, associative, and alternating — u ∧ u = 0 for every vector u, which implies anticommutativity for vectors, u ∧ v = −v ∧ u. More generally, swapping a k-vector and an l-vector introduces a sign of (−1)kl. A wedge product of linearly independent vectors is nonzero, so it detects linear dependence.

Geometric interpretation

For two vectors in the plane, the magnitude of u ∧ v equals the area of the parallelogram spanned by them. In three dimensions, the wedge product is related to the cross product through Hodge duality. Wedge products of k vectors describe oriented k-dimensional volumes.

Applications

The wedge product underlies the algebra of differential forms, where it gives the familiar calculus of integration on manifolds, and appears in geometric algebra, determinants, and the theory of Grassmannians. It was introduced by Hermann Grassmann in his 1844 work Die lineale Ausdehnungslehre.
