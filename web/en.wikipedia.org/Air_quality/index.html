Air quality

Air quality is the condition of the air within and around buildings and in the outdoor environment, especially as it relates to the health and comfort of occupants and ecosystems. Poor air quality results from air pollution — the presence of harmful substances such as particulate matter (PM2.5 and PM10), ground-level ozone, nitrogen dioxide, sulfur dioxide, and carbon monoxide — emitted by vehicles, industry, power generation, agriculture, and wildfires.

Measurement

Governments communicate air quality to the public through an Air Quality Index (AQI), which converts pollutant concentrations into a colour-coded scale. The World Health Organization publishes guideline values for key pollutants; its 2021 update tightened the recommended annual PM2.5 limit to 5 µg/m³, a level exceeded in most of the world's cities. Monitoring combines ground stations, satellite observations, and increasingly dense networks of low-cost sensors.

Health effects

Air pollution is among the leading environmental risk factors for premature death worldwide, with estimates of several million deaths annually attributed to ambient and household air pollution. Fine particles penetrate deep into the lungs and bloodstream, contributing to asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, heart disease, stroke, and lung cancer. Children, the elderly, and people with pre-existing conditions are especially vulnerable.

Control

Air quality management includes emission standards for vehicles and industry, clean-air legislation such as the United States Clean Air Act and European Union ambient air quality directives, low-emission zones in cities, and the transition to cleaner energy. Indoor air quality is addressed through ventilation standards and control of sources such as cooking stoves and tobacco smoke.
