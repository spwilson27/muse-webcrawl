Global Burden of Disease Study

The Global Burden of Disease Study (GBD) is a comprehensive regional and global research programme that assesses mortality and disability from major diseases, injuries, and risk factors. Begun in 1990 under the World Bank and the World Health Organization, with key contributions from Christopher Murray and Alan Lopez, it is now coordinated by the Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation (IHME) at the University of Washington and involves a worldwide network of collaborators.

Methods

The GBD quantifies health loss using the disability-adjusted life year (DALY), which combines years of life lost to premature death with years lived with disability. Successive iterations — GBD 2010, 2013, 2015, 2016, 2017, 2019, and later — have expanded coverage to hundreds of diseases and injuries and dozens of risk factors across more than 200 countries and territories, with results published principally in The Lancet and made freely available through IHME's visualisation tools.

Findings and impact

The studies document the global epidemiological transition from communicable to non-communicable diseases, the toll of risk factors such as high blood pressure, smoking, air pollution, and obesity, and wide inequalities between countries. GBD estimates are widely used by governments and agencies to set health priorities, though the project's modelling assumptions and disability weights have attracted scholarly debate.
