Alisher Navoiy

Alisher Navoiy (9 February 1441 – 3 January 1501), also known as Nizām-al-Din ʿAlī-Shīr Herawī, was a Timurid poet, writer, statesman, linguist, and painter who is regarded as the greatest representative of Chagatai literature and the founder of early Turkic literature. He is a national hero of Uzbekistan, where his name is rendered Alisher Navoiy.

Life

Navoiy was born in Herat, in present-day Afghanistan, into a family connected to the Timurid court. He studied in Herat, Mashhad, and Samarkand, and became a close friend and adviser of Sultan Husayn Bayqara, under whom Herat flourished as a centre of art and learning. As a high official and generous patron, Navoiy financed the construction of mosques, madrasas, hospitals, bridges, and caravanserais across Khorasan, and supported scholars, calligraphers, and miniaturists including the painter Kamāl ud-Dīn Behzād.

Works

Writing chiefly in Chagatai Turkic under the pen name Navoiy ("the melodious") and in Persian as Fāni, he produced some thirty major works. His masterpiece is the Khamsa (Quintet), five epic poems composed in 1483–85 in imitation of Nizami and Amir Khusrau, including Farhad and Shirin and Layla and Majnun. His treatise Muhakamat al-Lughatayn ("Judgment Between the Two Languages," 1499) argued that Turkic was in no way inferior to Persian as a literary language. His four divans collect tens of thousands of verses of lyric poetry, including ghazals, rubaiyat, and tuyuks.

Legacy

Navoiy is celebrated throughout Central Asia: the city and region of Navoiy in Uzbekistan bear his name, as do the National Library of Uzbekistan and the Alisher Navoiy Theatre in Tashkent. His works profoundly shaped the development of Uzbek and other Turkic literatures.
