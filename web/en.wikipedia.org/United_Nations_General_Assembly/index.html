United Nations General Assembly

The United Nations General Assembly (UNGA) is one of the six principal organs of the United Nations and its main deliberative, policymaking and representative body. All 193 UN member states have equal representation — one state, one vote — along with observer states and organisations. The Assembly meets in regular annual sessions at the United Nations Headquarters in New York City, beginning each September, and may also convene special and emergency special sessions.

Powers and functions

Under the UN Charter, the General Assembly may discuss and make recommendations on any matter within the Charter's scope. It approves the UN budget, elects the non-permanent members of the Security Council and members of other organs, and appoints the Secretary-General upon the Security Council's recommendation. Except in budgetary and certain internal matters, its resolutions are recommendations and are not legally binding on member states — in contrast to Security Council resolutions under Chapter VII. Decisions on "important questions," such as peace and security recommendations and elections, require a two-thirds majority of members present and voting.

Structure

Much of the Assembly's work is conducted in six Main Committees: First (Disarmament and International Security), Second (Economic and Financial), Third (Social, Humanitarian and Cultural), Fourth (Special Political and Decolonisation), Fifth (Administrative and Budgetary) and Sixth (Legal). Each September the Assembly opens with a "general debate" in which heads of state and government address the hall — a fixture of the diplomatic calendar.

Notable resolutions

Famous Assembly actions include the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (Resolution 217, 1948), the Partition Plan for Palestine (Resolution 181, 1947), the "Uniting for Peace" resolution (377, 1950), which allows the Assembly to act when the Security Council is deadlocked by veto, the Declaration on the Granting of Independence to Colonial Countries (1514, 1960), and the 1975 Resolution 3379 ("Zionism is a form of racism"), revoked in 1991. Under "Uniting for Peace," the Assembly has held eleven emergency special sessions, including on Ukraine in 2022.
