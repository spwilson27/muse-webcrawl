Eocene

The Eocene is a geological epoch that lasted from about 56 to 33.9 million years ago. It is the second epoch of the Paleogene Period in the modern Cenozoic Era. The name Eocene comes from the Ancient Greek words for "dawn" and "new", referring to the dawn of modern ('new') fauna that appeared during the epoch.

An Eocene landscape; many modern mammal groups first appear in this epoch

Climate

The Eocene began with the Paleocene–Eocene Thermal Maximum, a sharp warming event, and its early part was the warmest interval of the Cenozoic: palm trees grew at high latitudes and alligators lived in the Arctic. Climates gradually cooled through the epoch, and at the Eocene–Oligocene boundary about 33.9 million years ago a major cooling event accompanied the growth of the Antarctic ice sheet, marking the transition to the "icehouse" world that followed.

Life

Many modern orders of mammals appeared or diversified in the Eocene, including early primates, bats, even- and odd-toed ungulates, and the first whales, which evolved from land-dwelling ancestors early in the epoch. Famous fossil localities include the Messel Pit in Germany and the Green River Formation in the western United States.

Subdivisions

The Eocene is divided into four stages, from oldest to youngest: the Ypresian, Lutetian, Bartonian, and Priabonian.
