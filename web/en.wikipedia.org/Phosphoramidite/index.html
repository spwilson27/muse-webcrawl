Class: Organophosphorus compound
Use: Chemical synthesis of DNA and RNA

Phosphoramidite

A phosphoramidite is a monoester of a phosphoramidous acid, formally a derivative of trivalent phosphorus bearing an amino group and alkoxy substituents. As applied to nucleic acid synthesis, phosphoramidites are the modified nucleoside monomers used as building blocks in the automated chemical synthesis of oligonucleotides.

Oligonucleotide synthesis

In the phosphoramidite method, developed in the early 1980s following work by Marvin Caruthers, a solid-supported growing chain is extended one base at a time through a four-step cycle: detritylation, coupling of the next phosphoramidite activated by an acid catalyst such as tetrazole, capping of unreacted sites, and oxidation of the phosphite triester to a stable phosphate. Protecting groups shield the nucleobases and the 2′-hydroxyl in RNA synthesis.

Properties

Nucleoside phosphoramidites are relatively stable solids that can be stored dry but hydrolyse readily in the presence of water and are sensitive to air oxidation, so they are handled under inert conditions on DNA synthesisers.
