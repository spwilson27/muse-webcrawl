Broadway Limited

Broadway Limited
Operator: Pennsylvania Railroad (1902–1968); Penn Central; Amtrak (1971–1995)
Route: New York – Chicago via Pittsburgh and Philadelphia
First run: 1902 (as Pennsylvania Special)
Last run: 1995

The Broadway Limited was a named passenger train operated by the Pennsylvania Railroad (PRR) between New York City and Chicago, and later by Amtrak. Introduced in 1902 as the Pennsylvania Special and renamed in 1912, it was the PRR's flagship train and the chief rival of the New York Central's 20th Century Limited on the New York–Chicago route. The name referred not to Manhattan's Broadway but to the PRR's "broad way" — its four-track main line.

History

The train ran via Philadelphia, Harrisburg, Pittsburgh and Fort Wayne on the Pennsylvania's main line, covering about 908 miles overnight. In 1937–38 the PRR streamlined it as part of the "Fleet of Modernism": new lightweight cars and livery designed by Raymond Loewy, hauled by streamlined GG1 electrics east of Harrisburg and steam (later diesel) west of it. The all-Pullman train offered drawing rooms, a barber, a secretary/stewardess service, and observation cars; the 1949 re-equipping kept it among America's finest trains through the 1950s.

Declining passenger traffic led to consolidation with the General in 1967 and the train's passage to Penn Central in 1968. Amtrak took it over on 1 May 1971 and operated it — in later years over an altered route — until discontinuing it in 1995, when its functions were largely assumed by the Lake Shore Limited and the Washington–Chicago Capitol Limited (with the Three Rivers briefly retaining a New York–Chicago service via Pittsburgh).
