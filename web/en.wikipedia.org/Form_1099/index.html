Form 1099

Form 1099 is a family of information return forms used in the United States tax system, administered by the Internal Revenue Service (IRS), to report various types of income other than wages, salaries, and tips (which are reported on Form W-2). A person or business that makes certain payments during the year must file the appropriate Form 1099 variant with the IRS and furnish a copy to the payee, who uses it when preparing their own tax return.

Variants

There are numerous variants of Form 1099, each identified by a suffix, including:

  • Form 1099-MISC — miscellaneous information, including rents, prizes and awards, and other income payments.
  • Form 1099-NEC — nonemployee compensation, reintroduced in 2020 for payments to independent contractors and freelancers; payments of $600 or more generally must be reported.
  • Form 1099-INT — interest income paid by banks and other financial institutions.
  • Form 1099-DIV — dividends and distributions from stocks and mutual funds.
  • Form 1099-B — proceeds from broker and barter exchange transactions.
  • Form 1099-R — distributions from pensions, annuities, retirement or profit-sharing plans, and IRAs.
  • Form 1099-G — certain government payments, such as unemployment compensation and state tax refunds.
  • Form 1099-K — payment card and third-party network transactions.
  • Form 1099-S — proceeds from real estate transactions.
  • Form 1099-SA — distributions from health savings accounts and similar arrangements.

Filing and deadlines

Filers generally must furnish copies to recipients by January 31 following the payment year, and file with the IRS by the end of February (paper) or March (electronic). Businesses filing many returns are required to file electronically. Because the IRS matches the information on these forms against taxpayers' returns, discrepancies can trigger notices or audits. The payer requests the payee's taxpayer identification number using Form W-9; failure to provide one can subject payments to backup withholding.

History

Information reporting in the United States dates to the early 20th century; the modern Form 1099 series developed as third-party reporting expanded across interest, dividend, and service payments. The 1099-NEC was revived for tax year 2020 to separate nonemployee compensation from other Form 1099-MISC reporting, reversing a consolidation made in 1982.
