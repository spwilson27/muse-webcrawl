In algebraic topology, the Betti numbers are topological invariants of a space used to distinguish topological spaces by counting the number of "holes" of each dimension. The k-th Betti number bk is the rank of the k-th homology group; informally it counts the number of k-dimensional holes. The sequence of Betti numbers is among the most accessible numerical invariants in topology and is named after Enrico Betti, whose work on the connectivity of surfaces and spaces Henri Poincaré later generalised and formalised.

Intuition and examples

b0 counts connected components; b1 counts independent loops (one-dimensional holes); b2 counts enclosed voids. A circle has Betti numbers (1, 1, 0, …); a sphere has (1, 0, 1, 0, …); a torus has (1, 2, 1, 0, …) — two independent cycles, through the hole and around the tube, and one enclosed cavity. A figure-eight has b1 = 2.

Properties

Betti numbers are invariant under homeomorphism (indeed under homotopy equivalence, except that b0 counts path components). Poincaré duality relates the Betti numbers of a closed orientable n-manifold symmetrically: bk = bn−k. The alternating sum of Betti numbers gives the Euler characteristic. In modern applied topology and data analysis, Betti numbers are computed across scales in persistent homology, where "barcodes" of Betti numbers summarise the shape of data.
