R-process

R-process
Type: Stellar nucleosynthesis
Requirement: Very high neutron flux
Sites: Neutron star mergers, some supernovae
Products: Gold, platinum, uranium, europium

The r-process, or rapid neutron-capture process, is a set of nuclear reactions in astrophysics responsible for the creation of about half of the atomic nuclei heavier than iron. In an environment with an extremely high density of free neutrons, atomic nuclei capture neutrons far faster than they can undergo beta decay, building up very heavy, neutron-rich nuclei that later decay toward stability.

Mechanism

Starting from seed nuclei such as iron, repeated neutron captures drive nuclei far from the valley of stability within seconds. When the neutron supply is exhausted, the accumulated nuclei beta-decay toward stable isotopes, producing characteristic abundance peaks at certain mass numbers where neutron shell closures create bottlenecks — notably around mass 80, 130 and 195 (platinum). The process requires extreme conditions, with neutron densities exceeding 1020 per cubic centimetre.

Astrophysical sites

For decades the site of the r-process was debated. Core-collapse supernovae were long favoured, but the 2017 observation of the kilonova following the neutron-star merger GW170817 — whose spectrum showed freshly synthesised heavy elements — confirmed that neutron star mergers are a major source. Elements such as gold, platinum and uranium on Earth were forged in such events.

Related processes

The r-process complements the slower s-process, which operates in asymptotic giant branch stars, and the p-process, which produces certain proton-rich isotopes.
