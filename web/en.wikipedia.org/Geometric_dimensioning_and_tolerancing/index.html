Geometric dimensioning and tolerancing

Geometric dimensioning and tolerancing (GD&T) is a system for defining and communicating engineering tolerances via a symbolic language on engineering drawings and computer-generated 3D models. It explicitly describes nominal geometry and its allowable variation, including the size, form, orientation, and location of features.

Overview

GD&T is principally governed by the ASME Y14.5 standard in the United States (first published in its modern form in 1982, with major revisions in 1994, 2009, and 2018) and by the ISO GPS (Geometrical Product Specifications) system internationally. The language uses feature control frames — rectangular boxes divided into compartments containing a geometric characteristic symbol, a tolerance value, and references to datums.

Datums and modifiers

A datum is a theoretically exact point, axis, line, plane, or combination thereof derived from a physical datum feature, used as an origin for measurements. Material condition modifiers — maximum material condition (Ⓜ), least material condition (Ⓛ), and regardless of feature size — control how tolerance zones apply as features depart from their limiting sizes, often allowing "bonus tolerance".

Purpose

Compared with traditional plus/minus tolerancing, GD&T communicates design intent more precisely, often permits larger (cheaper) manufacturing tolerances for the same functional requirements, and enables statistical process control and automated inspection with coordinate measuring machines.
