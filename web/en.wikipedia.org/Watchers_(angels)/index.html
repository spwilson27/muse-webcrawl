Watchers (angels)

The Watchers descending to Mount Hermon

In the Book of Enoch, the Watchers (Aramaic: עִירִין, ʿiyrin; Greek: ἐγρήγοροι, egrḗgoroi) are angels sent to earth to watch over humans. They soon begin lusting for human women, and at the prodding of their leader Samyaza, they defect en masse to illicitly instruct humanity and procreate among them, arriving on Mount Hermon. The offspring of these unions are the Nephilim, savage giants who pillage the earth and endanger humanity.

In the Book of Enoch

The "Book of the Watchers" (chapters 1–36 of 1 Enoch) describes two hundred angels who descend, take wives, and teach forbidden arts including metalworking, cosmetics, and the signs of the sun and moon (attributed to the fallen angel Azazel and others). God sends the archangels to bind the Watchers beneath the earth until the final judgement, and the Flood is sent in part to cleanse the earth of their corruption. Samyaza and Azazel are the most prominent of the fallen Watchers.

Scriptural references

The term appears in the Book of Daniel, where "watchers" are holy beings who deliver decrees. Genesis 6:1–4's brief account of the "sons of God" and the Nephilim underlies the Enochic expansion. The story is also alluded to in the New Testament books of Jude and 2 Peter.

Influence

The Watchers tradition influenced early Jewish and Christian angelology and demonology, as well as later literature, and remains influential in popular culture. The books of Enoch are canonical only in the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, but were widely read in the Second Temple period.
