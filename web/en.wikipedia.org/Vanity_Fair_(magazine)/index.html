Vanity Fair is an American monthly magazine of popular culture, fashion, and current affairs published by Condé Nast. The magazine's name derives from the "Vanity Fair" of John Bunyan's Pilgrim's Progress and William Makepeace Thackeray's novel Vanity Fair.

History

Condé Nast first published a magazine called Dress and Vanity Fair in 1913; it was soon shortened to Vanity Fair and edited from 1914 by Frank Crowninshield, under whom it published work by leading modernist writers and photographers such as Dorothy Parker, Robert Benchley, Man Ray and Edward Steichen. The magazine folded during the Great Depression in 1936. Condé Nast revived the title in 1983, and under editors Richard Locke, Leo Lerman and especially Tina Brown (1984–1992) and Graydon Carter (1992–2017) it became known for long-form journalism, celebrity profiles, and photography by Annie Leibovitz and Mario Testino. Radhika Jones became editor in 2017.

Content

The magazine is noted for its in-depth profiles of public figures, investigative reporting, coverage of Hollywood and politics, and its annual Oscar party, one of the most prominent social events of the awards season. Its "Proust Questionnaire" back page is a long-running feature.
