Trade

Trade involves the transfer of goods and services from one person or entity to another, often in exchange for money. Economists refer to a system or network that allows trade as a market. Trade is a basic economic concept involving the buying and selling of goods and services, with compensation paid by a buyer to a seller, or the exchange of goods or services between parties.

History

Trade originated in prehistoric times with the exchange of goods such as obsidian, flint, and shells over long distances. Long-distance trade routes such as the Silk Road and the trans-Saharan routes moved silk, spices, gold, and glass between continents. The rise of money, banking, and joint-stock companies in the early modern period greatly expanded commerce, and industrialisation and container shipping made trade global in scale.

International trade

Trade between countries is governed by comparative advantage, tariffs, quotas, and trade agreements, and is overseen internationally by bodies such as the World Trade Organization. International trade allows countries to specialise in goods they produce relatively efficiently and to consume a wider variety of products than they could produce alone.

Types

Trade may be retail or wholesale, domestic or international, and may involve commodities, manufactured goods, services, or financial instruments.
