Battle of Anzio

Battle of Anzio
Date: 22 January – 5 June 1944
Location: Anzio and Nettuno, Italy
Combatants: Allies (US VI Corps, British forces) vs Germany
Result: Allied victory; Rome liberated

The Battle of Anzio was a battle of the Italian campaign of the Second World War that took place from 22 January 1944 to 5 June 1944. In Operation Shingle, an Allied amphibious landing by US VI Corps under Major General John P. Lucas, reinforced by British divisions, came ashore at Anzio and Nettuno, some 50 kilometres south of Rome, intending to outflank the German Gustav Line and seize the Italian capital.

The Anzio beachhead, early 1944.

Course of the battle

The landing achieved complete surprise, but Lucas consolidated the beachhead instead of advancing inland, allowing Field Marshal Kesselring to surround it with the German Fourteenth Army under General von Mackensen. Savage fighting followed: Allied attempts to break out toward Cisterna in late January were repulsed with heavy losses (including the near-destruction of two US Ranger battalions), and a major German counteroffensive in February (Operation Fischfang) nearly drove the beachhead into the sea. For four months the beachhead was a static, shelled enclave sustained by sea. In late May 1944, in Operation Diadem and the breakout Operation Buffalo, VI Corps under General Lucian Truscott broke out; Mark Clark controversially directed the advance toward Rome rather than cutting off the retreating German Tenth Army. Rome fell on 4 June 1944, two days before D-Day in Normandy. Total casualties on both sides were on the order of 40,000 or more each.
