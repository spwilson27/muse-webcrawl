Chief Leschi

Born1808, near present-day Eatonville, Washington
DiedFebruary 19, 1858, Lake Steilacoom, Washington Territory
Known forLeader in the Puget Sound War; posthumously exonerated in 2004

Chief Leschi (1808 – February 19, 1858) was a chief of the Nisqually Native American tribe. He was executed by hanging in 1858 following the Puget Sound War, after being convicted of the murder of a territorial militiaman — a killing he maintained occurred in battle.

Puget Sound War

Leschi opposed the Medicine Creek Treaty of 1854, which confined his people to an inadequate reservation, and became a central figure in the ensuing conflict of 1855–56. He was arrested, tried twice for the death of Abram Benton Moses, and hanged on February 19, 1858, after the territorial legislature specially authorized the execution site.

Exoneration

In 2004, a historical court of inquiry convened in Washington State concluded that Leschi should not have been tried in a civilian court because he was a lawful combatant, effectively exonerating him. Chief Leschi Schools in Puyallup and features in the Pacific Northwest bear his name.
