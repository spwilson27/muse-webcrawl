Mars Direct

Mars Direct is a proposal for a human mission to Mars developed by Robert Zubrin and David Baker in 1990. It aims to be economical by using existing technology and manufacturing return propellant on Mars from its atmosphere (in-situ resource utilisation).

The plan sends an uncrewed Earth Return Vehicle first to produce methane and oxygen fuel on Mars, followed by a crew in a habitat vehicle that would stay on the surface for about 500 days.

Zubrin popularised the plan in his 1996 book The Case for Mars and through the Mars Society.
