States of Mexico

The states of Mexico are the first-level administrative territorial entities of the United Mexican States. There are 31 states plus Mexico City, a federal entity that is the capital city and a federative entity in its own right since the 2016 constitutional reform.

History

The federation was established by the Constitution of 1824 with 19 states and several territories. Territories were progressively admitted as states; the most recent are Baja California Sur and Quintana Roo, both admitted on 8 October 1974. Mexico City ceased to be a federal district in 2016 and gained its own constitution in 2017.

Government

Each state has its own constitution, an elected governor serving a single six-year term, and a unicameral state congress. Under the federal Constitution of 1917, powers not expressly granted to the federation are reserved to the states, though fiscal centralisation gives the federal government dominant revenue-raising power.

Population and area

The most populous state is the State of Mexico (about 17 million), while the largest by area is Chihuahua (about 247,000 km²). The smallest state is Tlaxcala.
