Nagara architecture

Nagara is one of the three principal styles of Hindu temple architecture in India, associated with the northern regions of the subcontinent, in contrast to the Dravida style of the south and the Vesara style of the Deccan. The style developed from the Gupta period onward and reached maturity between roughly the 9th and 12th centuries CE.

Characteristics

The defining feature of the Nagara temple is the shikhara, a curvilinear tower rising over the sanctum (garbhagriha), crowned by a ribbed stone disc called the amalaka and a finial (kalasha). In later and more developed temples there are several subsidiary shikharas of increasing height, giving the impression of a mountain range — an allusion to Mount Meru and Mount Kailash, dwelling places of the gods. Unlike southern temples, Nagara temples typically lack massive enclosing walls and towering gateway gopurams; the temple stands on a raised platform (jagati), and the plan is often cruciform.

Subtypes

Regional schools include the Odisha (Kalinga) style, with its deula towers; the central Indian Chandela temples at Khajuraho; the Solanki (Maru-Gurjara) temples of Gujarat and Rajasthan, such as the Sun Temple at Modhera; and the hill temples of Himachal Pradesh, some built of wood and stone.

Examples

Notable examples include the Lingaraja Temple in Bhubaneswar, the Kandariya Mahadeva Temple at Khajuraho, the Sun Temple at Konark, and the Dashavatara Temple at Deogarh, one of the earliest surviving examples.
