21 demands of MKS

The 21 demands (Polish: 21 postulatów) were a list of demands issued on 17 August 1980 by the Interfactory Strike Committee (Międzyzakładowy Komitet Strajkowy, MKS) at the Lenin Shipyard in Gdańsk, Poland, during the strikes of August 1980. Negotiated with the communist government of Edward Gierek, the demands led to the Gdańsk Agreement of 31 August 1980 and the legalisation of Solidarity, the first independent trade union in the Soviet bloc. The wooden boards on which the demands were written are inscribed in UNESCO's Memory of the World Register.

The 21 demands displayed on boards at the Gdańsk Shipyard gate.

Background

Strikes broke out across Poland in the summer of 1980 in response to increases in food prices. On 14 August, workers at the Gdańsk Lenin Shipyard struck in defence of dismissed crane operator and activist Anna Walentynowicz. When the shipyard management conceded the strikers' local demands, Lech Wałęsa — persuaded by colleagues including Walentynowicz and Alina Pienkowska — called for a solidarity strike to continue on behalf of other workplaces. Delegates formed the Interfactory Strike Committee, representing hundreds of enterprises, and a commission headed by historian Tadeusz Kowalik helped draft the 21 demands, which were chalked and then painted on boards hung at the shipyard's Gate No. 2.

The demands

The first and most radical demand called for the acceptance of free trade unions independent of the Communist Party and of employers, in accordance with ILO Convention No. 87. Others included:

  • A guarantee of the right to strike and the safety of strikers and their supporters.
  • Freedom of speech, press, and publication, including access to the mass media for all denominations and publication of the agreement itself.
  • Release of political prisoners and an end to repression for one's convictions.
  • Reinstatement of dismissed workers and expelled students, including Walentynowicz and Wałęsa.
  • Automatic wage increases linked to inflation, reform of the economy, and full public information about the socio-economic situation.
  • Social measures: lowering the retirement age, improving health services, paid maternity leave, more nursery places, and a five-day working week.
  • An end to party privilege, including special shops and the sale of scarce goods for hard currency.

Outcome

Government negotiators led by Deputy Premier Mieczysław Jagielski signed the Gdańsk Agreement on 31 August 1980, accepting nearly all the points. Parallel agreements were signed at Szczecin and Jastrzębie. Solidarity was formally registered on 10 November 1980 and grew to about ten million members within a year, until martial law was imposed on 13 December 1981. The demands remain among the foundational documents of the Polish democratic transition of 1989.
