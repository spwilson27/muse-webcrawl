Angle

DefinitionFigure formed by two rays sharing a common endpoint (vertex)
UnitsRadian; degree; gradian; turn
TypesAcute, right, obtuse, straight, reflex

In Euclidean geometry, an angle is the figure formed by two rays, called the sides of the angle, sharing a common endpoint, called the vertex. Angles measure the amount of rotation between the two rays and are fundamental to geometry, trigonometry and physics.

Measurement

Angles are commonly measured in degrees, where a full rotation is 360°, and in radians, where a full rotation is 2π radians and the angle is the ratio of arc length to radius. A right angle is 90° (π/2 rad); angles smaller than a right angle are acute, those larger are obtuse, and angles greater than 180° are reflex.

Types and relationships

Adjacent angles that sum to 90° are complementary, and those summing to 180° are supplementary. Angles formed by intersecting lines produce equal vertical (opposite) angles. Angles also arise between planes (dihedral angles) and in solid geometry (solid angles, measured in steradians).

Applications

Angles are used throughout mathematics and engineering — in navigation (bearings), astronomy, construction, computer graphics and the description of periodic motion through trigonometric functions.
