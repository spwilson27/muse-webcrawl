Chobani is an American food company specializing in strained yogurt (Greek yogurt). The company was founded in 2005 by Hamdi Ulukaya, a Turkish immigrant of Kurdish descent, who bought a plant in the town of South Edmeston, New York, that had been closed by Kraft Foods. Ulukaya hired several of the former Kraft employees as well as a "yogurt master" and launched the brand in 2007. Chobani became the best-selling Greek yogurt brand in the United States and is credited with popularizing strained yogurt in the American market, transforming the stagnant yogurt category.

History

Ulukaya developed his recipe with yogurt master Mustafa Dogan, straining the yogurt to be thicker, higher in protein, and less sweet than typical American yogurts, sold at first in plain and then fruit-on-the-bottom cups. With little advertising, growth came through word of mouth and social media. Chobani expanded rapidly, opening one of the world's largest yogurt plants in Twin Falls, Idaho, in 2012, and an Australian operation in Dandenong, Victoria, in 2011. The company resisted acquisition offers, and in 2021 explored but ultimately withdrew an IPO.

Products and expansion

Beyond cups and tubes of Greek yogurt, Chobani markets Flip snack combinations, drinkable yogurts, non-dairy and oat-based products (oat milk from 2019), dairy and oat coffee creamers, ready-to-drink cold-brew coffee (following the 2020 acquisition of La Colombe, completed 2023), and probiotic beverages.

Social mission

Ulukaya became known for progressive business practices: in 2016 he gave employees shares representing up to ten percent of the company, and Chobani has hired many refugees and immigrants, particularly at its New York and Idaho plants. Ulukaya founded the Tent Partnership for Refugees network to encourage corporate hiring of refugees.

Controversies

The company faced lawsuits over the "Greek" designation of its yogurt (challenged in the UK and US), disputes over acid whey disposal at its plants, and a 2013 recall of mould-affected yogurt cups.
