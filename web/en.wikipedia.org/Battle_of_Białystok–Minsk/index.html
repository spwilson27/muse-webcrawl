Battle of Białystok–Minsk

The Battle of Białystok–Minsk was a major encirclement battle fought in the opening days of Operation Barbarossa, the German invasion of the Soviet Union, from 22 June to 9 July 1941. German panzer groups of Army Group Centre surrounded and destroyed much of the Soviet Western Front in a double pocket around Białystok and Minsk.

Background

The Soviet Western Front, commanded by General Dmitry Pavlov, defended a salient bulging westward around Białystok. When Germany attacked on 22 June 1941, Heinz Guderian's Panzer Group 2 and Hermann Hoth's Panzer Group 3 swept around the salient from south and north, meeting near Minsk on 27 June and trapping the Soviet 3rd, 4th and 10th Armies.

The pocket

German infantry armies closed in behind the panzers and compressed the pockets while Soviet forces attempted desperate breakouts. The Luftwaffe destroyed much of the Western Front's air power on the ground in the first days. By the time the pockets were eliminated in early July, the Western Front had effectively ceased to exist as an organised force.

Losses and consequences

Soviet losses were catastrophic: some 290,000–340,000 men were taken prisoner, and thousands of tanks and guns were destroyed or captured. Pavlov and his senior staff were arrested and executed for the disaster. The victory opened the road to Smolensk and Moscow, though the delay in reducing the pockets contributed to the lengthening German timetable.
