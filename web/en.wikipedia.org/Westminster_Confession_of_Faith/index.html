Westminster Confession of Faith

Westminster Confession of Faith
Drafted: 1643–1647, Westminster Assembly
Adopted by the Church of Scotland: 1647
Chapters: 33
Tradition: Reformed (Calvinist) / Presbyterian

The Westminster Confession of Faith is a Reformed confession of faith drawn up in 1646 by the Westminster Assembly, a council of English and Scottish theologians and parliamentarians summoned by the Long Parliament during the English Civil War. Together with the Westminster Larger and Shorter Catechisms, it remains the subordinate standard of the Church of Scotland and of Presbyterian churches worldwide.

Contents

The Confession consists of 33 chapters setting out Calvinist doctrine: the authority of Scripture, the Trinity, God's eternal decree and predestination, creation, the fall, the covenant of grace, the person and work of Christ, justification by faith alone, the sacraments of baptism and the Lord's Supper, church government and the last judgement. Its first chapter, "Of the Holy Scripture", and its teaching on the Sabbath and on the relationship of church and state were particularly influential.

History and influence

The General Assembly of the Church of Scotland adopted the Confession in 1647, and the Scottish Parliament ratified it in 1649; after the Restoration it was displaced in England but was confirmed again in Scotland in 1690. Emigrant Presbyterians carried it to Ireland, North America and the wider world. In the United States, the Presbyterian Church adopted it with amendments in 1788 that softened its teaching on the civil magistrate, and later revisions (1903, and the American Declaratory Act tradition) modified its statements on predestination. Baptists and Congregationalists produced adapted versions, including the 1689 Baptist Confession of Faith and the Savoy Declaration.
