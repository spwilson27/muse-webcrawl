Long March

The Long March (October 1934 – October 1936) was a strategic retreat by the Chinese Red Army to evade the Nationalist Kuomintang armies during the Chinese Civil War. It became a founding legend of the Chinese Communist Party.

Course

Driven from the Jiangxi Soviet, some 86,000 troops of the First Front Army broke out westward, crossing snow-capped mountains, marshlands and defended rivers such as the Jinsha and Dadu (including the celebrated Luding Bridge crossing). At the Zunyi Conference in January 1935, Mao Zedong rose to effective leadership.

Arrival in Shaanxi

The main force reached northern Shaanxi in October 1935 greatly reduced — only a fraction of those who set out completed the march, which the party later measured at 25,000 li (about 12,500 km, a figure historians debate).

Legacy

The march preserved the party's core, elevated Mao, and was later celebrated in Chinese education and propaganda as a triumph of endurance; Edgar Snow's Red Star Over China introduced it to the world.
