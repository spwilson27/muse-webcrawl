Moons of Pluto

The dwarf planet Pluto has five known natural satellites, or moons. In order of distance from Pluto they are Charon, Styx, Nix, Kerberos, and Hydra. Charon, the largest, is mutually tidally locked with Pluto, and is massive enough that Pluto–Charon is sometimes considered a double dwarf planet.

Charon

Charon was discovered in 1978 by James Christy at the United States Naval Observatory. With a diameter just over half that of Pluto, it is exceptionally large relative to its primary. The two bodies orbit a point in space between them, and each keeps the same face toward the other. The New Horizons spacecraft imaged Charon in detail during its July 2015 flyby, revealing a huge canyon system and a dark reddish polar cap, informally named Mordor Macula.

The small moons

Nix and Hydra were discovered in 2005 by the Hubble Space Telescope Pluto Companion Search Team, and Kerberos and Styx in 2011 and 2012 respectively, also by Hubble. The small moons orbit in nearly the same plane as Charon, in near-resonances with it, and tumble chaotically because of the shifting gravity of the Pluto–Charon pair. They are thought to have formed from debris of the giant impact that created Charon.
