Magic (supernatural)

Magic, sometimes spelled magick, is the application of beliefs, rituals, or actions employed in the belief that they can manipulate natural or supernatural beings and forces. It has been practised in many cultures throughout history and remains part of religious and folk traditions worldwide.

Definitions and study

Anthropologists such as James George Frazer, in The Golden Bough, described magic as based on sympathetic principles of similarity and contact, preceding religion in an evolutionary scheme later scholars rejected. E. E. Evans-Pritchard and others studied magic in social context, notably the Azande. Modern scholarship treats magic as a contested category overlapping religion and science.

Traditions

Practices include divination, astrology, alchemy, shamanism, ceremonial magic of the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn, and modern movements such as Wicca and chaos magic. Aleister Crowley spelled it "magick" to distinguish occult practice from stage magic.

Magic and religion

Many religions incorporate or condemn magical practices; medieval and early modern Europe saw witch trials against alleged harmful magic. Folk magic — healing charms, amulets, curses — persists alongside official religion in many societies.
