The EFL Championship, often known simply as the Championship, is the second tier of the English football league system and the highest division of the English Football League (EFL). It is contested by 24 clubs over a 46-match season, and is widely regarded as one of the most competitive and best-attended second-tier leagues in world football.

History

The division's lineage runs from the Football League Second Division, founded in 1892, through the First Division of 1992–2004 (after the Premier League breakaway demoted the old First Division to second-tier status). It was rebranded as the Football League Championship in 2004 and took its present EFL name in 2016. Sponsorship by Sky Bet has given it the official styled name "Sky Bet Championship".

Competition format

Each club plays every other twice, home and away. The top two clubs are automatically promoted to the Premier League; the clubs finishing third to sixth compete in play-offs culminating in a final at Wembley Stadium, the winner taking the third promotion place. The play-off final is frequently described as the richest single match in football because of the broadcasting revenue awaiting the winner. The bottom three clubs are relegated to EFL League One. Clubs relegated from the Premier League receive "parachute payments" over up to three seasons to soften the financial impact.

Notable features

The Championship has consistently ranked among Europe's top divisions by attendance, ahead of many top flights, and its long 46-game schedule plus play-offs is noted for its unpredictability: relegated Premier League sides frequently struggle, and surprise promotion campaigns are common.
