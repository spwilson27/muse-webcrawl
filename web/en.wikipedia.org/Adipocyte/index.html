Adipocyte

An adipocyte, also known as a lipocyte or fat cell, is a cell specialized for the storage of energy as fat. Adipocytes are derived from mesenchymal stem cells through adipogenesis and are the main constituent of adipose tissue.

Types

White adipocytes contain a single large lipid droplet that fills most of the cell, pushing the nucleus to the periphery; they store energy and act as an endocrine organ, secreting hormones such as leptin and adiponectin. Brown adipocytes contain many small droplets and abundant mitochondria rich in uncoupling protein 1 (UCP1), which generates heat by non-shivering thermogenesis. Intermediate "beige" adipocytes can arise within white fat.

Function

Beyond energy storage, adipocytes insulate and cushion the body, buffer circulating fatty acids, and participate in immune and metabolic signaling. Excess expansion of adipose tissue is central to obesity and its complications, including insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes.
