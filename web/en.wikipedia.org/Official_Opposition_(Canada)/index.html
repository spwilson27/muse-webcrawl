Official Opposition (Canada)

The Official Opposition (formally His Majesty's Loyal Opposition, French: Opposition officielle) in Canada is the political party with the second-largest number of seats in the House of Commons that is not part of the governing party or coalition. Its leader is known as the Leader of the Opposition. The Official Opposition is viewed as the alternative government, ready to take power should the governing party lose the confidence of the House.

Role

The Official Opposition's primary role is to scrutinise the government, challenge its policies during Question Period, and present itself as a credible alternative. It appoints a shadow cabinet of critics who mirror government portfolios. Under the Parliament of Canada Act, the Leader of the Opposition receives a salary and benefits comparable to a cabinet minister, as well as the official residence Stornoway in Ottawa.

Provincial and territorial level

Each province and territory with a party system also recognises an Official Opposition in its legislature, generally the largest non-governing party. In some jurisdictions, a party must hold a minimum number of seats or official party status to be recognised.

History

The concept derives from the Westminster system of the United Kingdom. In Canada, the Official Opposition has most often been held by the Liberal Party or the Conservative Party (and its predecessors). On rare occasions, such as in 1925 and 1993, unusual election results reshaped which party formed the opposition; in 1993 the Bloc Québécois, a sovereigntist party, became the Official Opposition.
