Marquess of Queensberry Rules

The Marquess of Queensberry Rules, also known as the Queensberry Rules, are a code of generally accepted rules in the sport of boxing. Drafted in London in 1865 and published in 1867, they were named so because the 9th Marquess of Queensberry publicly endorsed them.

The twelve rules required gloves, three-minute rounds with one minute's rest, a ten-second count for a knocked-down boxer, and banned wrestling and grappling — replacing the earlier bare-knuckle London Prize Rules.

They form the basis of modern boxing.
