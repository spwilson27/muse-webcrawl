Prajapati

Prajapati (Sanskrit: प्रजापति, romanized: Prajāpati, lit. "lord of creation and protector of all beings") is a Vedic deity of Hinduism. He is a creator god, also known in various texts as the "son of Brahma" or in later tradition identified with Brahma himself, as well as with other figures including Vishvakarma, Agni, Indra, Daksha, and various rishis. The term originally denoted a supreme creative principle, and over time it came to be applied to a group of progenitor deities.

Vedic literature

In the Rigveda, Prajapati appears as a distinct deity in the later books, notably in the Hiranyagarbha Sukta (RV 10.121), which asks "which god shall we honour with our offering?" and answers with Prajapati, the golden embryo (Hiranyagarbha) from whom creation arose. In the Brahmanas, especially the Shatapatha Brahmana, Prajapati becomes central: he creates the world through sacrifice and austerity (tapas), disintegrates in the act of creation, and is reassembled by the gods through the fire ritual—a myth that grounds the sacrificial cult, in which the fire altar itself is identified with Prajapati and with the year.

Later tradition

In the Upanishads and later texts, Prajapati is increasingly identified with Brahma, the creator in the Trimurti. The plural Prajapatis—including Daksha, Marichi, Atri, and others—are described in the Puranas and the Mahabharata as the mind-born sons (manasaputra) of Brahma, progenitors who populate the world. In some narratives, Prajapati's sacrifice gives rise to the castes, the seasons, and the gods themselves.
