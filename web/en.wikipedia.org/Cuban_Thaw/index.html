The Cuban Thaw (Spanish: deshielo cubano) was the normalisation of relations between the United States and Cuba announced on 17 December 2014, ending more than five decades of hostility dating from the Cuban Revolution. After eighteen months of secret negotiations facilitated by Canada and Pope Francis, U.S. President Barack Obama and Cuban President Raúl Castro simultaneously announced the restoration of diplomatic relations, prisoner exchanges and an easing of travel and trade restrictions.

Main developments

Cuba released the American aid contractor Alan Gross, and the remaining members of the Cuban Five spy ring were freed in return. In 2015 the United States removed Cuba from its list of State Sponsors of Terrorism, and on 20 July 2015 the two countries reopened embassies in each other's capitals. Obama visited Havana in March 2016, the first sitting U.S. president to do so since 1928. Commercial flights and cruise ship visits from the U.S. resumed, and rules on remittances, trade and telecommunications were loosened, though the statutory U.S. embargo remained in place.

Reversal

From 2017, the administration of Donald Trump rolled back much of the opening, tightening travel and business rules and later restoring Cuba to the terrorism sponsors list. The thaw nonetheless remains a landmark episode in inter-American diplomacy.
