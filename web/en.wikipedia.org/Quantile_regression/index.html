Quantile regression

Quantile regression is a type of regression analysis used in statistics and econometrics. Whereas ordinary least squares estimates the conditional mean of a response variable, quantile regression estimates conditional quantiles — for example the conditional median — given values of predictor variables.

Method

Introduced by Roger Koenker and Gilbert Bassett in 1978, quantile regression estimates the τ-th conditional quantile by minimising a sum of asymmetrically weighted absolute residuals, using the "check function" ρτ(u) = u(τ − 1{u < 0}). For τ = 0.5 this reduces to least absolute deviations (median) regression. The estimation problem is a linear programme and can be solved with simplex or interior-point methods.

Advantages

  • It characterises the entire conditional distribution of the response, not just its mean, so effects that differ across the distribution — e.g. a variable that matters more for low-income than high-income households — become visible.
  • It is robust to outliers in the response, because quantiles depend on ranks rather than squared distances.
  • It makes no assumption of a parametric error distribution or constant variance.

Applications

Quantile regression is widely used in labour economics (wage equations across the earnings distribution), growth-chart construction in medicine, value-at-risk modelling in finance, and environmental studies. Roger Koenker's quantreg package for R is a standard implementation.
