Water governance refers to the political, social, economic, and administrative systems in place that influence the use and management of water. It encompasses the range of processes by which decisions about water development, allocation, and management are made and implemented — the laws and institutions, formal and informal, through which water users and stakeholders articulate their interests. The concept is broader than "water management" or "water government": it includes the roles of private sector and civil society actors as well as the state.

Key concepts

A widely used framework is Integrated Water Resources Management (IWRM), promoted by the Global Water Partnership since the 1990s, which advocates coordinated management of water, land, and related resources at the level of the river basin, balancing economic efficiency, social equity, and environmental sustainability (the "3Es"). In 2015 the OECD adopted its Principles on Water Governance — twelve principles covering effectiveness, efficiency, and trust and engagement — now used as a benchmark for assessing national and local governance systems.

Issues

Major governance debates include: the tension between water as a human right (recognized by the UN General Assembly in 2010) and water as an economic good subject to pricing; the merits and controversies of private-sector participation and privatization of water supply; allocation conflicts between agriculture, cities, industry, and ecosystems; management of transboundary rivers and aquifers shared between states; groundwater governance, historically neglected because aquifers are invisible; and adaptation to climate change. Corruption, fragmented institutions ("too many" organisations with overlapping mandates), and the exclusion of women and poor users are recurring governance failures.

Reform trends

Reforms commonly involve creating river basin organisations, decentralizing responsibilities to user associations, introducing permits and water markets, and mandating public participation, following the model of the EU Water Framework Directive (2000). Sustainable Development Goal 6 includes targets both on IWRM implementation and on participation of local communities in water governance.
