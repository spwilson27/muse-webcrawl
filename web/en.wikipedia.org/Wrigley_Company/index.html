The Wm. Wrigley Jr. Company, known as the Wrigley Company, is an American multinational chewing gum and confectionery company founded in Chicago in 1891. It became the world's largest manufacturer of chewing gum, with brands including Juicy Fruit, Wrigley's Spearmint, Doublemint, Extra and Orbit. In 2008 it was acquired by Mars, Incorporated in a deal valued at about $23 billion, backed in part by Berkshire Hathaway.

History

William Wrigley Jr. began selling soap and baking powder, offering chewing gum as a premium to merchants. The gum proved more popular than the products it promoted, and by 1893 he was marketing his own brands, Juicy Fruit and Wrigley's Spearmint. Wrigley's pioneered mass advertising of consumer goods, spending heavily on billboards, newspapers and free samples. The family also bought the Chicago Cubs baseball team and Catalina Island off California; Chicago's Wrigley Field bears the family name.

Products and Mars era

During the twentieth century Wrigley's expanded worldwide, and during the Second World War it supplied gum to US troops while marketing the remainder under rationing. Later acquisitions included the Altoids and Life Savers brands (2005). After the Mars acquisition, Wrigley was combined with Mars's chocolate and petcare businesses; in 2016 the Wrigley subsidiary was merged into Mars Wrigley Confectionery, and the historic Wrigley Building on Michigan Avenue remains a Chicago landmark.
