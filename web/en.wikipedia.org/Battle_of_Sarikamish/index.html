The Battle of Sarikamish was an engagement between the Russian Empire and the Ottoman Empire during the First World War, fought from 22 December 1914 to 17 January 1915 as part of the Caucasus Campaign. The Ottoman offensive, personally directed by War Minister Enver Pasha, ended in catastrophe: the Ottoman Third Army was nearly annihilated, largely by the bitter winter weather of the high Caucasus.

Background

Enver Pasha planned an ambitious envelopment: while the Ottoman X Corps and XI Corps fixed the Russians frontally, the IX Corps would march over the Allahuekber Mountains to cut off the Russian garrison at Sarikamish, a key railhead on the line to Kars. The operation was launched in midwinter at altitudes above 2,500 metres, with many troops lacking winter clothing.

The battle

The flanking columns lost cohesion in blizzards and extreme cold; thousands of Ottoman soldiers froze to death on the march before contact. Units arrived piecemeal before Sarikamish and attacked a defence ably organised by General Nikolai Yudenich. Exhausted and outnumbered at the point of contact, the Ottoman attacks failed, and Russian counterattacks from 3 January encircled and destroyed the remnants of IX Corps; X Corps escaped with heavy loss. Hafiz Hakki Pasha, commanding X Corps, died of typhus shortly afterwards.

Aftermath

The Third Army lost the great majority of its strength—estimates run to 60,000–90,000 men—and the Caucasus front thereafter favoured Russia, whose armies advanced into eastern Anatolia in 1916. Enver Pasha publicly blamed the defeat on Armenian volunteers in Russian service, a charge used to help justify the subsequent Ottoman deportations and massacres of Armenians, which began in April 1915.
