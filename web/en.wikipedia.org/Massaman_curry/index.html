Massaman curry

Massaman curry (Thai: แกงมัสมั่น, kaeng matsaman) is a rich, mildly spicy Thai curry notable for its use of warm spices — cardamom, cinnamon, cloves, star anise, cumin, bay leaves and nutmeg — that are not typical of other Thai curries. It is usually made with beef, but also with duck, chicken, goat or, rarely, pork, and includes coconut milk, potatoes, onions, roasted peanuts, tamarind and palm sugar.

History

The name is generally thought to derive from an archaic Thai word for "Muslim", reflecting the dish's origins among Muslim traders and communities in southern Thailand and the influence of Persian and Indian cooking on the royal Thai court. A poem attributed to the era of King Rama II praises massaman, and the dish became established in central Thai cuisine by the 19th century.

Preparation

The curry paste combines dried chillies, shallots, garlic, galangal, lemongrass, coriander root and seeds, cumin, and the warm "dry" spices. The paste is fried in coconut cream until the oil separates, meat is braised in coconut milk until tender, and potatoes, onions and peanuts are added, with tamarind and sugar balancing sourness and sweetness. The result is a thick, fragrant, relatively mild curry.

Recognition

In 2011 CNN's travel site ranked massaman curry first in its "World's 50 most delicious foods" readers' list, and it has repeatedly featured in international polls of favourite dishes. It is served with jasmine rice and often with achat, a cucumber relish.
