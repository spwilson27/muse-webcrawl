Squash (plant)

Squash is the common name for several plant species in the genus Cucurbita, including Cucurbita pepo, C. maxima, C. moschata and C. argyrosperma, which are grown for their edible fruit and seeds. Squashes are herbaceous vines native to the Americas, domesticated thousands of years ago.

Types

"Summer squashes", such as zucchini (courgette) and pattypan squash, are harvested immature while the rind is tender. "Winter squashes", such as butternut squash, acorn squash and pumpkins, are harvested mature with a hard rind that permits long storage.

Cultivation and cuisine

Squashes were one of the "Three Sisters" crops — with maize and beans — of Indigenous agriculture in North America. The fruit, flowers, and seeds are eaten in cuisines worldwide; pumpkin seeds are a popular snack, and squash blossoms are used in Mexican and Italian cooking.
