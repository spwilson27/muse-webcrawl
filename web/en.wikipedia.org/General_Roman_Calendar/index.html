General Roman Calendar

The General Roman Calendar is the liturgical calendar of the Latin Church of the Catholic Church that indicates the dates of celebrations of saints and mysteries of the Lord in the Roman Rite, wherever this liturgical rite is in use. It is the universal framework onto which particular (diocesan and national) calendars and the calendars of religious orders add their own feasts.

Ranking of celebrations

In the calendar as revised after the Second Vatican Council, celebrations are ranked as solemnities, feasts, memorials (obligatory or optional), and weekdays. Solemnities such as Christmas, Easter, and Pentecost take precedence over all else; the Sundays of Advent, Lent, and Easter, Ash Wednesday, and Holy Week have special precedence too. Saints' days are normally memorials, meaning the saint is commemorated within the celebration of the day rather than dominating it.

History

The calendar descends from the sacramentaries and martyrologies of the early Church of Rome. It was standardised after the Council of Trent in the Roman Missal of 1570, and by the early twentieth century had accumulated so many feasts that reform became pressing. Pope Pius X and Pope Pius XII simplified it in the first half of the twentieth century, and Pope Paul VI promulgated a thorough revision in 1969 (the motu proprio Mysterii Paschalis), removing many saints of doubtful historicity and restoring the primacy of Sundays and the great seasons. Popes since have added newly canonised saints and adjusted individual entries, and in 2022 Pope Francis added several new optional memorials. Dioceses and religious orders maintain proper calendars that supplement the general one.
