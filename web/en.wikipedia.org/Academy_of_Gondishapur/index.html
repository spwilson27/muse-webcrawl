Academy of Gondishapur

LocationGondishapur (Jundishapur), Sasanian Persia, near modern Dezful, Iran
Flourished3rd–9th centuries AD
FieldsMedicine, philosophy, astronomy, mathematics, theology

The Academy of Gondishapur (also Gundeshapur or Jundishapur) was the famous intellectual centre of the Sasanian Empire, located in the city of Gundeshapur in present-day Khuzestan, southwestern Iran. Founded by Shapur I in the third century AD and greatly developed under Shapur II and especially Khosrow I (Anushirvan) in the sixth century, it became renowned above all for its medical school and hospital, and for its role in transmitting Greek, Indian and Persian learning into the Islamic world.

History

Gundeshapur was founded by Shapur I and populated partly with Roman prisoners of war, including scholars. The academy grew as a meeting point of traditions: Nestorian Christian scholars expelled from Edessa in 489 brought Greek learning; under Khosrow I, Greek philosophers fleeing the closing of the Athenian Academy in 529 were received there, and Indian physicians and texts contributed Sanskrit medical and astronomical knowledge. Its teaching hospital (bimaristan) is often regarded as one of the earliest public hospitals and a model for later Islamic hospitals. Families such as the Bukhtishu supplied court physicians for generations.

Legacy

After the Muslim conquest of Persia, the academy continued to flourish for two centuries; its scholars and its translation activity helped shape the Abbasid translation movement in Baghdad, and Gondishapur's graduates staffed the caliphs' hospitals and observatories. The institution gradually declined as patronage shifted to Baghdad after the ninth century. Some modern accounts exaggerate its resemblance to a modern university, but its medical school and hospital were genuinely influential in the history of medicine.
