Hermosillo is the capital and largest city of the Mexican state of Sonora. Located in the northwestern part of the country, it is one of Mexico's fastest-growing industrial cities and is noted for its extremely hot desert climate.

History

The settlement was founded in 1700 as Santísima Trinidad del Pitiquín, a mission and presidio intended to protect Spanish settlers from the indigenous Seri and Pima peoples. In 1828 the town was renamed Hermosillo in honour of General José María González Hermosillo, a hero of the Mexican War of Independence. It became the state capital in 1879 (after periods in which Arizpe and Ures held that role).

Geography and climate

Hermosillo lies in a desert valley near the confluence of the Sonora and San Miguel rivers, dominated by the Cerro de la Campana ("Bell Hill"), whose rocks ring when struck. The city has a hot desert climate: summer temperatures frequently exceed 40 °C (104 °F), and Hermosillo is among the hottest cities in Mexico, with a record high of 49.5 °C (121 °F).

Economy

The economy is driven by manufacturing, notably a large Ford Motor Company assembly plant opened in 1986, as well as agriculture and cattle ranching in the surrounding irrigated valleys of the Costa de Hermosillo. The city is a commercial hub for northwestern Mexico and is served by Hermosillo International Airport.

Culture

Hermosillo is the home of the Naranjeros de Hermosillo baseball team, one of the most successful clubs in the Mexican Pacific League. Regional cuisine is famed for carne asada and flour tortillas. The Fiestas del Pitic festival each May celebrates the city's founding.
