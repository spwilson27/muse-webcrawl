Samskara

Samskara (Sanskrit: संस्कार, saṃskāra) is a term in Indian philosophy and religion with several interconnected meanings. Most prominently, the samskaras are the traditional Hindu rites of passage that mark and sanctify the major stages of a person's life, from conception to cremation. In the philosophical schools, the word also denotes the mental impressions and latent tendencies formed by past actions and experiences, which condition future behaviour and rebirth.

Rites of passage

The Grihya Sutras and later texts enumerate the samskaras, with the classical list numbering sixteen (the shodasha samskara). They include rites before and at birth (garbhadhana, pumsavana, simantonnayana, jatakarman), naming (namakarana), first outing and first feeding (nishkramana, annaprashana), first haircut (chudakarana), ear-piercing (karnavedha), the beginning of education (vidyarambha), the sacred-thread initiation (upanayana), successive stages of Vedic study, the ceremonial return home (samavartana), marriage (vivaha) and funeral rites (antyeshti). In contemporary practice the most widely observed are the birth rites, upanayana for some communities, marriage and funeral ceremonies.

Mental impressions

In Yoga, Samkhya and Buddhist philosophy (where the Pali form is sankhara), samskaras are the subliminal imprints left by volitional actions, thoughts and perceptions. Stored in the subconscious, they ripen into habits, dispositions and karmic consequences, perpetuating the cycle of samsara. Meditative practice aims to expose and exhaust these impressions: in Patanjali's Yoga Sutras, liberation follows when the seeds of samskara are "burned" and no longer germinate.

Other uses

The word's root sense — "putting together", "refining", "preparing" — also appears in grammar and ritual theory: in Mimamsa, samskara can denote the consecration of implements and offerings. In modern Indian languages, sanskar commonly signifies culture, upbringing and good values.
