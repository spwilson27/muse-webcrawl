Principal bundle

In mathematics, a principal bundle is a mathematical object that formalises some of the essential features of a product space together with a free group action. A principal G-bundle consists of a total space P, a base space M, a projection map π: P → M, and a free, transitive (right) action of a Lie group G on P preserving the fibres of π. Each fibre is then a copy of G itself — a G-torsor — without a preferred identity element. Locally the bundle looks like a product U × G, but globally it may be twisted.

A principal bundle: each fibre is a torsor for the structure group G.

Examples

The prototype is the frame bundle of a manifold, whose structure group is the general linear group GL(n); a point of the frame bundle is a basis of the tangent space. The Hopf fibration is a principal U(1)-bundle over the 2-sphere with total space the 3-sphere. In physics, principal bundles provide the geometric foundation of gauge theory: the gauge group acts on the bundle, and a connection on the bundle corresponds to a gauge field such as the electromagnetic potential.

Associated bundles and connections

From a principal bundle and a representation of G one constructs associated fibre bundles, such as vector bundles; conversely, the frame bundle of a vector bundle recovers a principal bundle. A connection on a principal bundle is a G-invariant choice of horizontal subspaces, equivalently a Lie-algebra-valued 1-form, whose curvature measures the field strength. Sections of principal bundles correspond to gauge choices, and a global section exists only if the bundle is trivial.
