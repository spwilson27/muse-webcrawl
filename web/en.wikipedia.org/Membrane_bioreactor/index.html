Membrane bioreactor

Membrane bioreactors (MBR) are combinations of membrane processes like microfiltration or ultrafiltration with a biological wastewater treatment process, the activated sludge process. It is widely used for municipal and industrial wastewater treatment.

Overview

In an MBR, membranes submerged in the aeration tank (or in a separate membrane tank) retain suspended solids and microorganisms, replacing the secondary clarifier and often tertiary filtration of a conventional plant. Two main configurations exist: submerged (immersed) membranes, where the membranes sit in the mixed liquor and are cleaned by coarse-bubble air scouring; and sidestream membranes, where mixed liquor is pumped through external membrane modules.

Advantages and disadvantages

MBRs produce a very high quality, disinfected effluent suitable for reuse, operate at higher biomass concentrations, and have a smaller footprint. Their drawbacks are higher capital and energy costs, and membrane fouling, which must be managed by cleaning regimes and operational control.

History

The concept dates to the late 1960s, but commercial submerged membranes introduced in the early 1990s greatly expanded adoption; MBRs are now used in thousands of plants worldwide, from small packaged units to large municipal works.
