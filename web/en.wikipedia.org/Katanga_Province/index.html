Katanga Province

Katanga was one of the eleven provinces of the Democratic Republic of the Congo between 1966 and 2015, when it was split into the Tanganyika, Haut-Lomami, Lualaba, and Haut-Katanga provinces. Its capital was Lubumbashi (formerly Elisabethville). The region is exceptionally rich in copper, cobalt, uranium, and other minerals.

History

Katanga formed part of the Congo Free State and Belgian Congo, where the Union Minière du Haut-Katanga exploited its copper belt from the early 20th century. Days after Congolese independence in 1960, Moise Tshombe declared Katanga's secession, sparking the Congo Crisis; the breakaway State of Katanga, backed by Belgian interests, held out until 1963 when UN forces ended the secession. Under Mobutu the province was renamed Shaba (1971–1997), and it saw the Shaba I and II invasions in 1977–78.

Economy

Mining dominates the economy: the Central African Copperbelt yields a large share of the world's cobalt and significant copper, along with zinc, uranium (historically from Shinkolobwe, source of uranium for the Manhattan Project), and other minerals. The state company Gécamines long managed production, which declined in the 1990s before reviving with foreign investment.

Geography

Katanga occupies the southeastern DRC, bordering Angola, Zambia, and Tanzania. It is a high plateau crossed by the Lufira, Lualaba (upper Congo), and Luapula river systems, with savanna vegetation and several national parks, including Upemba and Kundelungu.
