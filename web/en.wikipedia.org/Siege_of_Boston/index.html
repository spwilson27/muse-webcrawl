Siege of Boston

The siege of Boston (April 19, 1775 – March 17, 1776) was the opening phase of the American Revolutionary War. American militiamen, later joined by the Continental Army under George Washington, surrounded the town of Boston, where British forces under General Thomas Gage and later William Howe were garrisoned. The siege ended with the British evacuation of Boston on March 17, 1776.

Background

Following the Battles of Lexington and Concord on April 19, 1775, colonial militia converged on Boston, bottling up the British garrison on the peninsula, which was connected to the mainland only by the narrow Boston Neck. The Americans built fortifications around the town under commanders including Artemas Ward and Israel Putnam.

Battle of Bunker Hill

On June 17, 1775, British troops assaulted American positions on Breed's Hill on the Charlestown peninsula in the Battle of Bunker Hill. The British captured the position but suffered over 1,000 casualties, a Pyrrhic victory that made them cautious for the remainder of the siege. George Washington arrived in July 1775 to take command of the newly created Continental Army.

Fortification of Dorchester Heights

In the winter of 1775–76, artillery captured at Fort Ticonderoga was transported to Boston by Henry Knox in the "noble train of artillery." On the night of March 4, 1776, American troops fortified Dorchester Heights overlooking Boston and its harbour. Unable to dislodge them and facing the threat to his fleet, Howe evacuated Boston on March 17 — now celebrated as Evacuation Day in Massachusetts — sailing to Halifax, Nova Scotia, with about 11,000 troops and many Loyalists.

Aftermath

The American victory raised morale and removed British power from New England for the rest of the war. Washington moved his army to New York to prepare for the next British campaign.
