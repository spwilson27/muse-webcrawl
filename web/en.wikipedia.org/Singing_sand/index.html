Singing sand

Large desert dunes can emit a loud booming sound when sand avalanches down their slip faces.

Singing sand, also called whistling sand, barking sand or booming sand, is sand that produces sound. The sound emission may be caused by wind passing over dunes or by walking on the sand. Certain conditions must come together to create singing sand: the grains must be round and between 0.1 and 0.5 mm in diameter, the sand must contain silica, and it must be kept at a certain humidity.

Mechanism

The most common theory is that the sound is produced by the synchronised shear of layers of grains sliding over one another during an avalanche, generating a periodic vibration that radiates as a low-frequency boom, typically between about 60 and 105 hertz. Beach sands can produce a higher-pitched squeak or whistle underfoot — "barking sand" — by a related shearing mechanism over a much smaller scale.

Occurrences

Booming dunes are known from some 35 desert locations worldwide, including the Dumont Dunes and Kelso Dunes in California, Sand Mountain in Nevada, the Badain Jaran Desert in China — where the boom has been described as the "singing sands" noted by Marco Polo — and sites in the Namib and the Sahara. The phenomenon features in the folklore of several desert cultures, which attributed the sounds to spirits or monsters.
