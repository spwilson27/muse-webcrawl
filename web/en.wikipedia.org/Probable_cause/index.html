Probable cause

In United States criminal law, probable cause is the standard by which police authorities have reason to obtain a warrant for the arrest of a suspected criminal or the issuing of a search warrant. It is also the standard a grand jury uses to decide whether to issue an indictment.

Definition

The Fourth Amendment to the United States Constitution requires that searches, seizures, and warrants be supported by probable cause. The Supreme Court has described probable cause as a "fair probability" that contraband or evidence of a crime will be found in a particular place (Illinois v. Gates, 1983), assessed under the totality of the circumstances. It requires more than bare suspicion but considerably less evidence than is needed to convict.

Related standards

Probable cause sits within a hierarchy of legal standards: reasonable suspicion (a lower standard justifying brief investigative stops under Terry v. Ohio), probable cause (for arrests and search warrants), and proof beyond a reasonable doubt (required for criminal conviction). Evidence obtained without probable cause may be excluded under the exclusionary rule.
