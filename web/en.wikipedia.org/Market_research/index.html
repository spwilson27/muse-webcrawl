Market research

Market research is an organized effort to gather information about target markets and customers: know about them, starting with who they are. It is an important component of business strategy and a major factor in maintaining competitiveness. Market research helps to identify and analyze the needs of the market, the market size and the competition. Its techniques encompass both qualitative research, such as focus groups and in-depth interviews, and quantitative techniques such as customer surveys and analysis of secondary data.

Types

Research is conventionally divided into primary research, which collects new data directly from respondents, and secondary research, which uses data already gathered by others. Primary methods include surveys, questionnaires, interviews, observation and experiments such as A/B tests and product clinics. Secondary sources include trade publications, census statistics, sales records and syndicated retail audits such as those produced by firms like Nielsen and Kantar.

Process

A typical study proceeds through defining the problem and research objectives, developing a research plan, collecting data, analyzing it, and presenting findings to decision-makers. Common outputs include estimates of market size and share, segmentation of customers by demographics or behaviour, measures of brand awareness and satisfaction, and concept and price tests — for example conjoint analysis, which infers the value consumers place on individual product features.

Industry

Market research is a large global industry, with worldwide research revenues in the tens of billions of dollars annually. Major firms include Kantar, Ipsos, GfK and NielsenIQ, and the profession is governed by codes of practice such as those of ESOMAR and national associations, which set standards for respondent privacy and data quality. Digital data collection — online panels, mobile surveys, social listening and web analytics — has largely displaced telephone and mail surveys.
