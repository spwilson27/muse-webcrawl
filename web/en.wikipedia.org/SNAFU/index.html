SNAFU

SNAFU is an acronym that is widely used as a slang term in the United States, originating in the United States Armed Forces. It is commonly expanded as "Situation Normal: All Fucked Up" — sometimes bowdlerized to "All Fouled Up" — and expresses the sardonic observation that a chaotic or botched state of affairs is entirely typical. The word is used both as a noun, describing a confused mess, and as a verb meaning to throw into disorder.

Private Snafu, the animated character of wartime US Army instructional films.

Origin

The acronym dates to the Second World War, with the earliest recorded attestations appearing in 1941–42 among American servicemen. It belongs to a family of cynical military coinages of the period, including FUBAR ("Fucked Up Beyond All Recognition"), SUSFU, and JANFU. The term also gave its name to Private Snafu, the protagonist of a series of risqué animated shorts produced by the US Army Signal Corps between 1943 and 1945 — written in part by Theodor Geisel (Dr. Seuss) and animated by Warner Bros. artists — intended to teach soldiers about security, hygiene and morale while entertaining them.

Usage

After the war the word passed into general English, where it retains a military flavour and is frequently used in computing and engineering contexts to describe a routine malfunction or an operation gone wrong in an entirely predictable fashion.
