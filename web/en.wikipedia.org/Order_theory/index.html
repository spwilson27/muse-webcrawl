Order theory

Order theory
Branch of: Mathematics
Studies: Binary relations capturing an intuitive notion of ordering

Order theory is a branch of mathematics that investigates the intuitive notion of order using binary relations. It provides a formal framework in which to state and prove results about ordering across a wide variety of mathematical structures. Familiar examples of orders are the arrangement of numbers by size, the divisibility relation on integers, and the subset relation on sets.

Basic definitions

A partial order is a binary relation that is reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive; a set equipped with such a relation is a partially ordered set, or poset. If every two elements are comparable, the order is a total order (or linear order), as with the real numbers under ≤. Weaker structures such as preorders, and stronger ones such as well-orders, are also studied. Hasse diagrams give a graphical representation of finite posets.

Topics

Order theory encompasses the study of lattices (posets in which every pair of elements has a least upper bound and greatest lower bound), complete lattices, Galois connections, order-preserving functions, fixed-point theorems such as the Knaster–Tarski theorem, and duality theory. Orders arise centrally in algebra, logic, domain theory in computer science, and the foundations of mathematics.
