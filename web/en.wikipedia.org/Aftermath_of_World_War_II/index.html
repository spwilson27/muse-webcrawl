Aftermath of World War II
Period1945 onward
Key eventsDivision of Germany, founding of the UN, start of the Cold War, decolonization

The aftermath of World War II reshaped the political and social order of the world. The war ended in 1945 with the defeat of the Axis powers, leaving tens of millions dead, much of Europe and Asia in ruins, and millions of displaced persons. The United States and the Soviet Union emerged as the two global superpowers, setting the stage for the Cold War.

Occupation and division

Germany and Austria were divided into occupation zones; Germany eventually split into the Federal Republic of Germany (West) and the German Democratic Republic (East) in 1949. Japan was occupied by Allied forces under General Douglas MacArthur, adopted a new constitution, and retained Emperor Hirohito. War crimes tribunals were held at Nuremberg and Tokyo.

New international order

The United Nations was founded in 1945, and the Bretton Woods system established the International Monetary Fund and the World Bank. The United States funded European recovery through the Marshall Plan (1948). The Universal Declaration of Human Rights was adopted in 1948.

Decolonization

The war weakened the European colonial empires, accelerating independence movements across Asia and Africa, including the independence and partition of India in 1947 and the creation of the State of Israel in 1948. The ideological rivalry between the superpowers soon hardened into the Cold War, with NATO (1949) and later the Warsaw Pact (1955) dividing Europe.
