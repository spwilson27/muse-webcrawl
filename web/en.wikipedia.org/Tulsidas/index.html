Tulsidas

Bornc. 1532, Rajapur (traditionally)
Died1623, Varanasi
OccupationPoet, saint, philosopher
Major workRamcharitmanas
TraditionRamanandi Sampradaya; Vaishnavism

Tulsidas (c. 1532–1623), also known as Goswami Tulsidas, was a Hindu saint and poet renowned for his devotion to the deity Rama. He is best known as the author of the Ramcharitmanas, a retelling of the Sanskrit Ramayana in the Awadhi language, which made the story of Rama accessible to ordinary people across northern India.

Life

Tradition holds that Tulsidas was born in Rajapur in present-day Uttar Pradesh. After the early death of his parents he was raised by the saint Narharidas, from whom he received initiation into the Ramanandi order. He married Ratnavali, and legend says that her rebuke of his attachment awakened him to devote his life wholly to God. He spent much of his later life in Varanasi, where he founded the Sankat Mochan Hanuman Temple.

Works

Twelve works are generally attributed to Tulsidas, including the Ramcharitmanas, Vinaya Patrika, Dohavali, Kavitavali, Gitavali and the widely recited Hanuman Chalisa. His writings blend devotion (bhakti), philosophy and ethics, and remain central to Hindu religious life.

Legacy

The Ramcharitmanas is often called the "Bible of northern India"; its verses are recited daily in millions of homes and form the basis of the Ramlila folk theatre tradition, recognised by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage.
