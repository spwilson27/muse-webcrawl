Dose–response relationship

The dose–response relationship, or exposure–response relationship, describes the magnitude of the response of an organism, as a function of exposure (or doses) to a stimulus or stressor (usually a chemical) after a certain exposure time. Dose–response relationships are fundamental to pharmacology and toxicology.

The dose–response curve

Plotting response against the logarithm of dose usually produces a sigmoid (S-shaped) curve, rising from a threshold to a maximal effect. Key parameters include the EC50 (the concentration giving half-maximal response), the slope (Hill coefficient), and the maximal efficacy. In toxicology, analogous measures are the LD50 (median lethal dose) and benchmark doses.

Quantal and graded responses

Graded responses are measured on a continuous scale in a single subject, whereas quantal responses are all-or-none outcomes (such as death or cure) counted across a population, yielding a cumulative frequency curve whose median is the median effective or lethal dose.

Modifiers

Potency, efficacy, therapeutic index, tolerance, and interactions with other substances (synergy and antagonism) all shape the observed relationship, as do non-monotonic patterns such as hormesis, in which low doses stimulate while high doses inhibit.
