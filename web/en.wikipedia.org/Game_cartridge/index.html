Game cartridge

A game cartridge, also known as a cartridge, video game cartridge, or Game Pak, is a removable memory card containing one or more ROM chips holding video game software. Cartridges were the dominant distribution medium for home video game consoles from the late 1970s through the mid-1990s, and remain in use in modified form for systems such as the Nintendo Switch.

History

The Fairchild Channel F (1976) was the first console to use interchangeable ROM cartridges. The Atari 2600 (1977) popularized the format. Nintendo's NES, Game Boy, and Nintendo 64, and Sega's Genesis and Master System, all used cartridges. Sony's PlayStation shifted the industry toward optical discs, which were cheaper to manufacture and held far more data.

Advantages and disadvantages

Cartridges offer near-instant loading, durability, and the possibility of including enhancement chips (such as Nintendo's Super FX). Their drawbacks include higher manufacturing cost and limited storage compared with optical media. Modern flash-based cartridges, such as Nintendo Switch game cards, combine solid-state convenience with large capacities.
