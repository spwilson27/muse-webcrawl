Ergocalciferol

Ergocalciferol
Other nameVitamin D2
FormulaC28H44O
SourceErgosterol in fungi and plants, exposed to ultraviolet light
UsePrevention and treatment of vitamin D deficiency

Ergocalciferol, also known as vitamin D2, is a form of vitamin D produced by the ultraviolet irradiation of ergosterol, a sterol found in fungi and some plants. It is used to prevent and treat vitamin D deficiency, including rickets, and is widely used to fortify foods and as a dietary supplement.

Biochemistry

Like cholecalciferol (vitamin D3), ergocalciferol is a prohormone: it is converted in the liver to 25-hydroxyvitamin D (calcifediol) and then in the kidneys to the active hormone calcitriol, which regulates calcium and phosphate absorption and bone health. The D2 metabolites bind vitamin D-binding protein less strongly than D3 forms and are cleared somewhat faster, leading to debate about relative potency of the two forms at equivalent doses.

Sources and uses

Ergocalciferol is obtained commercially by UV irradiation of ergosterol from yeast. Mushrooms exposed to sunlight or UV light are a natural dietary source. It appears on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines and is commonly supplied in high-dose capsules (e.g. 50,000 IU) for treatment of deficiency in some countries, as well as in lower-dose supplements and fortified foods.

History

The compound was isolated in the early 1930s following work by Adolf Windaus and others on irradiated ergosterol; it was marketed under names such as Viosterol and was instrumental in the eradication of rickets in industrialised countries through milk fortification.
