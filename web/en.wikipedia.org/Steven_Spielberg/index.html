Steven Allan Spielberg (born 1946) is an American filmmaker, among the most commercially successful and critically acclaimed directors in history. A leading figure of the New Hollywood era, he co-founded Amblin Entertainment and DreamWorks.

Career

Spielberg's Jaws (1975) effectively invented the summer blockbuster. He followed with Close Encounters of the Third Kind (1977), the Indiana Jones series, E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial (1982) and Jurassic Park (1993), for years the highest-grossing films ever made. He won Academy Awards for Best Director for Schindler's List (1993), which also won Best Picture, and Saving Private Ryan (1998). Later works range from Munich and Lincoln to the semi-autobiographical The Fabelmans (2022).

Legacy

Spielberg's films have grossed over 0 billion worldwide. He founded the Shoah Foundation to record Holocaust survivors' testimonies and has received the Presidential Medal of Freedom and an honorary knighthood.
