Echelon

An echelon is a formation in military and aviation contexts in which units, vehicles, or aircraft are arranged diagonally, each offset behind and to the side of the one in front, like steps. The word comes from the French échelle, meaning a ladder.

Military use

In land warfare, forces may attack or move in echelon to bring successive units into action, protect a flank, or refuse (hold back) one wing. At higher levels of organisation, "echelon" also denotes a level of command or a grouping of forces — a usage preserved in terms such as "rear echelon" and in the names of headquarters elements. Cold War Soviet doctrine made extensive use of first and second echelons in offensive operations.

Aviation and other uses

In aviation, an echelon formation positions aircraft stepped up or down and to one side of the leader, used in displays and by migrating birds in related V formations to save energy. The word has also entered general usage to mean a level or rank in any hierarchy, as in "the upper echelons of management." ECHELON is additionally the name commonly given to a signals intelligence network operated by the Five Eyes countries.
