Least developed countries

The least developed countries (LDCs) are developing countries listed by the United Nations as exhibiting the lowest indicators of socioeconomic development, with the lowest Human Development Index ratings of all countries in the world. The category was established in 1971 by the UN Economic and Social Council, and countries on the list receive special support measures, including concessional finance, trade preferences and technical assistance. Since 2023 there have been 44 LDCs, most of them in Africa.

Least developed countries are concentrated in sub-Saharan Africa, with others in Asia and the Pacific.

Criteria

The UN Committee for Development Policy reviews the list every three years using three criteria: gross national income per capita, a Human Assets Index (health and education indicators) and an Economic and Environmental Vulnerability Index. A country is added if it meets all three thresholds; it "graduates" if it exceeds two of the three in two consecutive reviews.

Graduation

Countries that have graduated include Botswana (1994), Cabo Verde (2007), Maldives (2011), Samoa (2014), Equatorial Guinea (2017), Vanuatu (2020), Bhutan (2023) and São Tomé and Príncipe (2024). Bangladesh, Laos and Nepal are among those scheduled to graduate in 2026.

International support

Successive UN programmes of action — most recently the Doha Programme of Action (2022–2031) — set targets for aid, duty-free and quota-free market access, and technology transfer, including through the UN Technology Bank for LDCs. LDCs also negotiate as a bloc in climate talks, where they press for loss-and-damage funding.
