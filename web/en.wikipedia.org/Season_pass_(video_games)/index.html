Season pass (video games)

Season passes bundle a game's future downloadable content at a discount.

A season pass is a term used by video game publishers for purchasable downloadable content (DLC) packages encompassing most or all of a game's planned expansions. Typically sold before or alongside the game's release, a season pass grants the buyer access to each piece of content as it is released, generally at a lower total price than buying the items separately.

History

The model was pioneered around 2011, when publishers such as Rockstar Games offered the "L.A. Noire Rockstar Pass" and Warner Bros. bundled future content for Mortal Kombat. It spread rapidly among large publishers during the PlayStation 3 and Xbox 360 era as a way to extend a game's revenue life and discourage trade-ins of used copies.

Approach

Season passes are usually marketed with promises of expansions, story episodes, maps, characters or cosmetic items to be delivered over a defined "season" following launch. Some games have offered multiple successive passes, annual passes or renamed variants such as expansion passes and ultimate editions bundling the pass with the base game.

Controversy

Season passes have been criticised for asking players to pay in advance for unspecified content, sometimes announced before the base game itself is released, and for fragmenting content that critics argue should have been included at launch. The model has partially been displaced by free-to-play "battle passes", popularised by Fortnite, which reward play over a limited season with tiered cosmetic unlocks.
