Thomas Reid (26 April 1710 – 7 October 1796) was a Scottish philosopher, founder of the Scottish School of Common Sense and a contemporary critic of David Hume. His direct realism about perception and his defence of innate principles of common sense were highly influential in Britain, France, and the early United States.

Philosophy

In An Inquiry into the Human Mind on the Principles of Common Sense (1764), Reid attacked the "theory of ideas" running from Descartes through Locke and Berkeley to Hume, arguing that perception gives direct awareness of external objects rather than of mental images. He held that certain first principles — belief in an external world, in other minds, and in the reliability of our faculties — are irresistible and presupposed by all reasoning.

Influence

Reid taught at King's College, Aberdeen, and succeeded Adam Smith in the chair of moral philosophy at Glasgow. His philosophy dominated Scottish universities for decades, shaped the teaching of philosophy in America, and has been revived by contemporary philosophers of perception and epistemology.
