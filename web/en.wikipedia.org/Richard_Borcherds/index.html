Richard Borcherds


Richard Borcherds, British mathematician

Richard Ewen Borcherds (born 29 November 1959) is a British mathematician known for his work in lattice theory, group theory and infinite-dimensional algebras. He received the Fields Medal in 1998 for, among other achievements, his proof of the Conway–Norton "monstrous moonshine" conjectures.

Education and career

Borcherds was born in Cape Town, South Africa, and educated at King Edward's School, Birmingham, and Trinity College, Cambridge, where he completed his PhD in 1985 under John Horton Conway. After positions at Cambridge and elsewhere, he became a professor at the University of California, Berkeley, and later returned to Cambridge as a professor of mathematics. He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1994 and a member of the U.S. National Academy of Sciences.

Work

In his thesis and subsequent work Borcherds introduced vertex algebras (with Igor Frenkel and others developing the theory in parallel) and the monster vertex algebra, using them to prove the monstrous moonshine conjectures, which connect the largest sporadic finite simple group — the Monster — with modular functions. He invented Borcherds algebras (generalised Kac–Moody algebras), and introduced the notion of the Borcherds product, an infinite product expansion for automorphic forms on orthogonal groups, with applications to moduli spaces and string theory. His work has deep connections to the Leech lattice and the fake monster Lie algebra.

Honours

Borcherds won the Whitehead Prize (1992), the EMS Prize, and the Fields Medal at the 1998 International Congress of Mathematicians in Berlin.
