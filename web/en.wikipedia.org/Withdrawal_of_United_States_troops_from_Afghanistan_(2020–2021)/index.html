Withdrawal of United States troops from Afghanistan (2020–2021)

Date29 February 2020 – 30 August 2021
BasisDoha Agreement (2020)
ResultEnd of the War in Afghanistan; Taliban return to power

The withdrawal of United States troops from Afghanistan began under the February 2020 Doha Agreement between the United States and the Taliban and was completed on 30 August 2021 under President Joe Biden, ending America's longest war. The final phase coincided with a rapid Taliban offensive that captured Kabul on 15 August 2021 and a massive multinational airlift of evacuees from Hamid Karzai International Airport.

Background

United States forces had been in Afghanistan since October 2001, when they invaded following the September 11 attacks. By 2020 about 13,000 US troops remained. Under the Doha Agreement, signed on 29 February 2020, the US committed to withdraw all forces within 14 months in exchange for Taliban counter-terrorism assurances. Drawdowns during 2020 reduced the force to about 2,500 by January 2021.

Final withdrawal and evacuation

In April 2021 President Biden announced that all remaining troops would leave by 11 September 2021, later moved to 31 August. Bagram Airfield was handed over in early July. As provincial capitals fell in days, the Afghan government collapsed and President Ashraf Ghani fled on 15 August. More than 120,000 people were airlifted from Kabul in the following two weeks; on 26 August a suicide bombing at the airport's Abbey Gate killed 13 US service members and around 170 Afghans. The last US aircraft departed on 30 August 2021.

Aftermath and debate

The withdrawal ended the War in Afghanistan but left the Taliban in control; thousands of Afghan partners remained behind, and the speed of the collapse prompted congressional inquiries and lasting political debate in the United States over the planning and execution of the exit.
