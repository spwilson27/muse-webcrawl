The K'iche' (also spelled Quiché) are an Indigenous people of the Maya peoples, native to the highlands of Guatemala. They are among the most numerous Indigenous groups in the Americas, with over a million speakers of the K'iche' language, the most widely spoken Mayan language. Their historical capital was Q'umarkaj (Utatlán), near present-day Santa Cruz del Quiché.

History

In the Late Postclassic period (c. 1200–1524) the K'iche' kingdom was the most powerful state in the Guatemalan highlands, expanding under rulers such as K'iq'ab. In 1524 the kingdom was conquered by Spanish forces under Pedro de Alvarado, aided by Kaqchikel allies; the last kings were executed and Q'umarkaj was destroyed. The famous Popol Vuh, the K'iche' creation epic and dynastic history, was written in the K'iche' language in Latin script in the 1550s.

Culture

K'iche' communities are known for vibrant backstrap-loom weaving with municipality-specific patterns, markets such as Chichicastenango, and syncretic religious practice combining Catholicism with Maya ritual, maintained by daykeepers (ajq'ijab) who use the 260-day calendar. The Guatemalan civil war (1960–1996) devastated many K'iche' communities. The Nobel Peace Prize laureate Rigoberta Menchú is a K'iche' woman.
