Strategos

Strategos (Greek: στρατηγός, plural strategoi) was a title used in ancient Greece for a military general. It is the origin of the English word "strategy". In classical Athens, the strategoi were among the most important elected officials of the state.

Athens

After the reforms of Cleisthenes (508/507 BC), Athens elected a board of ten strategoi, one from each tribe, initially as commanders of the tribal regiments. Unlike most Athenian magistrates, who were chosen by lot, strategoi were elected and could be re-elected indefinitely, allowing capable commanders such as Pericles, who was re-elected many times, to wield great political influence. Strategoi commanded armies and fleets, conducted diplomacy, and could be prosecuted or fined for failure; several were executed after the Battle of Arginusae in 406 BC.

Hellenistic and Byzantine use

In Hellenistic kingdoms the title designated provincial military governors. In the Byzantine Empire, the strategos was the military governor of a theme, combining military and (initially) civil authority over the district, before the office declined in importance after the 11th century.
