Policy analysis

Policy analysis is a technique used in public administration and policy studies to enable civil servants, activists, and others to examine and evaluate the available options to implement the goals of laws and elected officials. Policy analysis is frequently deployed in the public sector but is similarly applicable to other kinds of organisations, such as nonprofit and non-governmental organisations. It draws on economics, political science, sociology, statistics and operations research.

Approaches

Analysts commonly distinguish between two broad types of policy analysis. Analysis of policy is the academic study of policies, their origins and consequences; analysis for policy is applied work intended to inform a decision, comparing alternative courses of action against criteria such as effectiveness, efficiency, equity, feasibility and acceptability. Classic models of the analytic process involve defining the problem, assembling evidence, constructing alternatives, selecting criteria, projecting outcomes, and confronting trade-offs.

Methods

Techniques used in policy analysis include cost–benefit analysis, cost-effectiveness analysis, risk assessment, forecasting, econometric modelling, surveys and case studies. Modern policy analysis increasingly emphasises programme evaluation and the use of experimental and quasi-experimental evidence to determine "what works".

Professions and institutions

Policy analysts work in government departments, legislative research services, think tanks, consultancies and international organisations. Many universities offer professional master's degrees in public policy, such as the Master of Public Policy (MPP), which train students in policy analysis.
