Fort Henry, Ontario

Fort Henry
LocationKingston, Ontario, Canada
Built1832–1837 (present fort)
StatusNational Historic Site; museum

Fort Henry is a military fortification and living history museum in Kingston, Ontario, Canada. It is located on Point Henry, a strategic headland overlooking the entrance to the Cataraqui River where it flows into Lake Ontario near the St. Lawrence River. It is a National Historic Site of Canada and part of the Kingston Fortifications UNESCO World Heritage Site designation, associated with the Rideau Canal.

History

The first fort on the site was built during the War of 1812. The present, much larger fort was constructed between 1832 and 1837 as part of the British defence system for the Rideau Canal, guarding the naval dockyard at Point Frederick. British garrisons occupied the fort until 1870, and Canadian troops used it into the twentieth century; during both World Wars it served in part as an internment camp.

Museum

Restored in the 1930s, Fort Henry opened as a museum in 1938. It is famous for the Fort Henry Guard, a ceremonial unit of student interpreters in 1867 British infantry uniforms who perform drill, artillery firings, and the Sunset Ceremony during the tourist season.
