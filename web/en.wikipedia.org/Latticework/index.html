Latticework

Decorative latticework in a garden trellis

Latticework is an openwork framework consisting of a criss-crossed pattern of strips of building material, typically wood or metal. The design is created by crossing the strips to form a grid or weave.

Uses

Latticework is used decoratively in fences, trellises for climbing plants, gazebo and porch panels, and window screens. In Islamic architecture, carved lattice screens known as mashrabiya and jali provide shade, ventilation, and privacy. In engineering, lattice girders and lattice trusses use the same crossing principle structurally.

Materials and construction

Traditional lattice is made from thin laths of cedar, redwood, or other weather-resistant woods, fastened at the intersections with nails, staples, or weaving. Modern versions are also produced in vinyl, aluminium, and composite materials, and are commonly sold in standard panel sizes for garden use.

Cultural history

Lattice screens appear in Egyptian, Roman, Chinese, Japanese, and Indian architecture. The ornate stone jali of Mughal buildings, such as those at Fatehpur Sikri and the Taj Mahal complex, are celebrated examples of the craft.
