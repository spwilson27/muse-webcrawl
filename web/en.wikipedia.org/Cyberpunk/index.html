Cyberpunk

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cyberpunk is a subgenre of science fiction in a dystopian futuristic setting that tends to focus on a "combination of low-life and high tech", featuring futuristic technological and scientific achievements, such as artificial intelligence and cybernetics, juxtaposed with societal collapse, dystopia or decay. Much of cyberpunk is rooted in New Wave science fiction of the 1960s and 1970s.

Origins and literature

The term was coined by Bruce Bethke in his 1980 short story "Cyberpunk" and popularised by editor Gardner Dozois. The genre crystallised with William Gibson's Neuromancer (1984), whose vision of cyberspace, console cowboys and megacorporations set the template, alongside works by Bruce Sterling, Pat Cadigan, Rudy Rucker, John Shirley and Neal Stephenson. The Mirrorshades anthology (1986) served as an informal manifesto.

Themes and style

Cyberpunk settings feature dominant megacorporations, invasive body modification, virtual realities, film-noir detection and street-level protagonists — hackers, mercenaries and outcasts — operating at the margins of a decaying urban order. Its aesthetic of neon, rain, dense cityscapes and Japanese-influenced design spread worldwide through Ridley Scott's film Blade Runner (1982) and anime such as Akira (1988) and Ghost in the Shell (1995).

Influence

Cyberpunk shaped later media from The Matrix (1999) to the video games Deus Ex and Cyberpunk 2077, and spawned derivative "-punk" genres including steampunk, biopunk and solarpunk. Its predictions — networked computing, virtual reality, corporate power and surveillance — have been widely discussed as the real world has converged with its fictions.
