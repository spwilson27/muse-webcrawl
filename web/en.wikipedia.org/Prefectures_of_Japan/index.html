Prefectures of Japan

Japan is divided into 47 prefectures (都道府県, todōfuken), which form the country's first level of jurisdiction and administrative division. They consist of 43 prefectures proper (県, ken), two urban prefectures (府, fu: Osaka and Kyoto), one "circuit" (道, dō: Hokkaido) and one metropolis (都, to: Tokyo).

History

The prefectural system was created in the Meiji era with the abolition of the han system in 1871, replacing the feudal domains of the Tokugawa period. The number of prefectures, initially over 300, was consolidated to 47 by 1888. The modern framework of local autonomy was established by the Local Autonomy Law of 1947, under which each prefecture elects a governor and a unicameral assembly.

Government

Prefectures are larger than the cities, towns and villages into which they are subdivided, and they administer police, public high schools, hospitals, roads and parts of social welfare. The most populous prefecture is Tokyo with over 14 million residents, while Tottori is the least populous with under 550,000. Prefectures are commonly grouped into eight regions, from Hokkaido in the north to Kyushu and Okinawa in the south.
