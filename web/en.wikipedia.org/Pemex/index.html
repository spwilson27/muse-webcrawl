Pemex

Pemex (Petróleos Mexicanos) is the Mexican state-owned petroleum company. Created in 1938 following President Lázaro Cárdenas's expropriation of foreign oil companies, it held a constitutional monopoly over Mexico's oil and gas industry for most of the twentieth century.

History

The 1938 expropriation, commemorated annually on 18 March, made Pemex the sole operator of exploration, production, refining, and retail fuel sales in Mexico. Production peaked in 2004 at about 3.4 million barrels of crude per day, anchored by the giant Cantarell field in the Bay of Campeche, discovered in 1976 and once the second-largest producing field in the world. Cantarell's steep decline thereafter drove overall output down to under two million barrels per day.

Reform and finances

The energy reform of 2013–14 under President Enrique Peña Nieto ended Pemex's monopoly, opening the sector to private and foreign investment; subsequent administrations under Andrés Manuel López Obrador sought to re-strengthen the state company, including building the Olmeca (Dos Bocas) refinery. Pemex is among the most indebted oil companies in the world, with financial debt around US$100 billion, and has faced recurring accidents, including the 2021 pipeline fire in the Gulf and the 2013 explosion at its Mexico City headquarters.
