Bad Muskau

Bad Muskau (Upper Sorbian: Mužakow) is a spa town in the Görlitz district of Saxony, Germany, on the Lusatian Neisse river at the border with Poland. It is best known for Muskau Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

Muskau was first mentioned in the thirteenth century and granted town rights in 1452. Between 1815 and 1845 Prince Hermann von Pückler-Muskau created the great English-style landscape park around his castle, one of the finest examples of landscape gardening in Europe. After 1945 the Neisse became the German–Polish border, dividing the park between the two countries.

Muskau Park

The park covers some 830 hectares in total, with the larger part lying in Poland. It was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list in 2004 as a transboundary property. The Old and New Castles, heavily damaged in 1945, have been progressively restored.

The town today

Bad Muskau is a recognised spa (Bad) with brine and moor treatments, drawing visitors to the park and its health facilities. See also Lusatia and Hermann von Pückler-Muskau.
