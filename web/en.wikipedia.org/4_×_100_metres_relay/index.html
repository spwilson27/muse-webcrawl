4 × 100 metres relay

The 4 × 100 metres relay (or sprint relay) is an athletics track event in which teams of four runners each complete 100 metres of a 400-metre track, passing a baton to the next runner within a designated changeover zone. It is one of the fastest events in sport, and is contested at the Olympic Games and the World Athletics Championships for both men and women.

Rules and technique

Each leg is run in lanes over the full lap of the track. Baton exchanges must occur within a 30-metre takeover zone; dropping the baton or passing outside the zone leads to disqualification. Because incoming runners approach at near-maximum speed, teams practise blind passes, in which the outgoing runner accelerates on a check mark and receives the baton without looking back. Smooth exchanges are as important as raw speed: the fastest individual sprinters do not always produce the fastest relay teams.

History and records

The men's event has been held at every Olympics since 1912 and the women's since 1928. Jamaica dominated the event in the era of Usain Bolt, whose teams set the men's world record of 36.84 seconds at the London 2012 Olympics. The women's world record of 40.82 seconds was set by the United States at the same Games. The United States remains the most successful nation overall, although its teams have repeatedly been undone by botched exchanges and disqualifications.
