The Heartland Championship is an amateur and semi-professional rugby union competition in New Zealand. It is contested annually by twelve provincial unions and sits below the professional National Provincial Championship (NPC, now branded the Bunnings NPC) in the structure of New Zealand provincial rugby.

History

The competition was created in 2006 when the old National Provincial Championship was restructured into a professional top division and the Heartland Championship for the smaller, largely amateur unions. The twelve founding unions came mostly from the former second and third divisions.

Format

Teams play a round-robin regular season, after which the top four compete for the Meads Cup, named after legendary All Black Colin Meads, and the next four compete for the Lochore Cup, named after Brian Lochore. The unions taking part include Buller, East Coast, Horowhenua Kapiti, King Country, Mid Canterbury, North Otago, Poverty Bay, South Canterbury, Thames Valley, Wairarapa Bush, West Coast and Whanganui.
