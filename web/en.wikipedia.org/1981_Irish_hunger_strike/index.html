1981 Irish hunger strike

Date1 March – 3 October 1981
LocationHM Prison Maze, Northern Ireland
Deaths10 prisoners

The 1981 Irish hunger strike was a protest by Irish republican prisoners in Northern Ireland demanding the restoration of political (Special Category) status, which the British government had withdrawn in 1976.

The strike

Bobby Sands, the Provisional IRA's commanding officer in the Maze Prison, began the strike on 1 March 1981; other prisoners joined at intervals so that deaths would be staggered and publicity sustained. Sands was elected to the Westminster Parliament for Fermanagh and South Tyrone while on hunger strike, but died on 5 May after 66 days without food.

Aftermath

Ten men died before the strike was called off on 3 October 1981, after which the government quietly conceded most of the prisoners' demands in substance. Sands's election victory demonstrated the electoral potential of Sinn Féin and is widely seen as a turning point that led the republican movement toward electoral politics.
