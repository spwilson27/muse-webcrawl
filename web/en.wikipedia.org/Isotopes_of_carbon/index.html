Isotopes of carbon

Carbon has 15 known isotopes, from carbon-8 to carbon-22. Two are stable: carbon-12 (about 98.9% of natural carbon) and carbon-13 (about 1.1%). Carbon-12 defines the atomic mass unit.

Carbon-14

Carbon-14 is a radioactive isotope with a half-life of about 5,730 years, produced in the atmosphere by cosmic rays. Its decay underlies radiocarbon dating of organic material up to roughly 60,000 years old, developed by Willard Libby.

Carbon-13

Carbon-13 is used in NMR spectroscopy and in isotope-ratio studies of diet, climate and metabolism.

Other isotopes

Other isotopes are short-lived; carbon-11 is used as a tracer in positron emission tomography.
