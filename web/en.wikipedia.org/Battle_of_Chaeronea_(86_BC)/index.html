Battle of Chaeronea (86 BC)

Date86 BC
LocationChaeronea, Boeotia, Greece
ResultRoman victory
BelligerentsRoman Republic vs. Kingdom of Pontus
CommandersLucius Cornelius Sulla; Archelaus (Pontus)

The Battle of Chaeronea of 86 BC was fought near Chaeronea in Boeotia, on nearly the same ground as the famous battle of 338 BC, during the First Mithridatic War. The Roman army of Lucius Cornelius Sulla defeated the much larger Pontic army of Archelaus, general of Mithridates VI of Pontus, who had overrun Roman Asia and Greece and massacred tens of thousands of Romans and Italians in the Asiatic Vespers of 88 BC.

The battle

Sulla had about 30,000–40,000 men, including allied Greek contingents, while ancient sources credit Archelaus with up to 120,000, a figure modern historians reduce considerably. The Pontic army deployed a Macedonian-style phalanx, masses of cavalry, and scythed chariots. The chariot attack failed against the disciplined legions, whose men opened lanes or met the horses with stakes and javelins. Sulla's cavalry and infantry then struck the Pontic flanks near the town of Thurium, where Roman detachments seized the heights, and the phalanx was driven back onto its own camp with heavy slaughter. Plutarch reports that Sulla erected two trophies, one on the plain and one where the enemy was dislodged from the heights.

Aftermath

Archelaus escaped to Chalcis with the remnants of his army. Reinforced from Asia, he met Sulla again at Orchomenus later in 86 BC and was again decisively defeated, effectively ending Mithridates' hold on Greece. Sulla then turned his victorious legions toward Italy and civil war against the Marian regime in Rome.
