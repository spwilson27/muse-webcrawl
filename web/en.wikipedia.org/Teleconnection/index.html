Teleconnection

Teleconnection in atmospheric science refers to climate anomalies that are related to one another at large distances — typically thousands of kilometres. Such patterns are persistent, large-scale anomalies of pressure and circulation that recur over a season or longer, linking weather and climate conditions in geographically remote regions. The best-known teleconnection arises from the El Niño–Southern Oscillation (ENSO), whose warm (El Niño) and cold (La Niña) phases shift rainfall, temperature, and storm tracks across much of the globe.

History

In the 1920s Sir Gilbert Walker, seeking predictors of the Indian monsoon, documented the Southern Oscillation — a seesaw in surface pressure between the eastern and western tropical Pacific — and related oscillations in the North Atlantic and North Pacific. Anders Ångström coined the term "teleconnection" in 1928. Jacob Bjerknes in the 1960s connected the Southern Oscillation to El Niño ocean warming and proposed the atmospheric bridge (the Walker circulation) that physically links distant regions; stationary Rossby waves radiating from tropical heating anomalies are now understood to transmit many teleconnection signals into the extratropics.

Major patterns

  • ENSO — affects rainfall in Australia, Indonesia, India, southern Africa, and the Americas, and modulates hurricane activity and jet streams.
  • North Atlantic Oscillation — the pressure seesaw between the Azores high and Icelandic low; its positive phase brings mild, wet winters to northern Europe and cold to the eastern Mediterranean.
  • Pacific/North American pattern — a wave train across the North Pacific and North America strongly tied to ENSO phase.
  • Arctic and Antarctic Oscillations — hemispheric annular modes governing the strength of the polar vortices and mid-latitude storm tracks.
  • Indian Ocean Dipole — an east–west sea-surface temperature gradient influencing East African and Australian rainfall.

Applications

Because teleconnection indices vary slowly compared to daily weather, they underpin seasonal forecasting: statistical and dynamical predictions of winter severity, monsoon strength, drought risk, and energy and agricultural planning routinely condition on ENSO, NAO, and related indices. Teleconnections also help attribute extreme events and are a key test of climate models' fidelity.
