Agrippina the Elder
Born: c. 14 BC, Athens
Died: 18 October AD 33, Pandateria
Spouse: Germanicus
Issue: Caligula; Agrippina the Younger; Nero Caesar; Drusus Caesar; Julia Livilla; Julia Drusilla

Agrippina the Elder (c. 14 BC – AD 33), also called Agrippina Major or Agrippina I, was a prominent, powerful member of the Julio-Claudian dynasty of the early Roman Empire. She was the daughter of Marcus Vipsanius Agrippa and Julia the Elder, granddaughter of Augustus, wife of the popular general Germanicus, and mother of the emperor Caligula and of Agrippina the Younger.

Life

Agrippina accompanied Germanicus on his campaigns in Germania and the East, winning fame for her courage — notably preventing the destruction of a bridge over the Rhine during a retreat. After Germanicus died in AD 19, she publicly accused Gnaeus Calpurnius Piso of poisoning him, making her a focus of popular sympathy and of the enmity of the emperor Tiberius and his prefect Sejanus.

Fall and death

Agrippina's outspoken opposition to Tiberius led to her arrest and exile to the island of Pandateria in AD 29, after her sons Nero Caesar and Drusus Caesar were destroyed by Sejanus's intrigues. She died there in AD 33, reportedly of starvation. Her surviving son Caligula, on becoming emperor in AD 37, honoured her memory and returned her ashes to Rome.
