Bikeway

A bikeway is any road, street, path, or other route designed for or used by bicycle traffic. The term covers a spectrum of infrastructure, from cycle tracks physically separated from motor traffic, through painted bicycle lanes and shared bus lanes, to off-road greenways and rail trails closed to motor vehicles.

Types

Facility types are commonly classified by their degree of separation. Segregated cycle facilities include cycle tracks and bicycle paths; on-road facilities include marked bike lanes, contraflow lanes, and bicycle boulevards on low-traffic streets. Guidelines such as the Dutch CROW Design Manual for Bicycle Traffic and the American NACTO Urban Bikeway Design Guide set standards for width, intersection treatment, and signage.

Networks

Cities increasingly plan bikeways as connected networks rather than isolated segments, including national and international routes such as the EuroVelo network in Europe and the United States Bicycle Route System. Long-distance routes frequently reuse disused railway corridors and canal towpaths.

Safety and usage

Research generally associates separated infrastructure with higher cycling levels and lower injury risk, and planners emphasise protected intersections and visibility at junctions, where most serious collisions occur. The provision of high-quality bikeways is a central element of policies to increase cycling's mode share for transport and recreation.
