Michael John Muuss (16 October 1958 – 20 November 2000) was an American computer scientist at the U.S. Army's Ballistic Research Laboratory (BRL), best known as the author of the ubiquitous network utility ping. He was also the principal architect of BRL-CAD, an open-source constructive solid geometry modeling system used for vulnerability analysis of military vehicles.

Ping

Muuss wrote ping in December 1983, reportedly in a single evening, to debug network problems at BRL. Named after the sound of sonar — and later given the backronym "Packet InterNet Groper" — the program sends ICMP echo requests to measure reachability and round-trip time. It became a standard tool on virtually every networked operating system.

Other work

At BRL, Muuss led work on high-performance computing and networking, contributing to early Internet infrastructure and to the laboratory's supercomputing facilities. He was a vocal advocate of open standards and software sharing within the Unix community.

Death and legacy

Muuss died in an automobile accident on Interstate 95 in Maryland in November 2000. His ping program remains one of the most widely used network diagnostics in history, and his story of its creation is a frequently cited piece of Internet folklore.
