January Events

The January Events (Lithuanian: Sausio įvykiai) were a series of violent confrontations in Lithuania in January 1991, when Soviet military forces attempted to crush the republic's independence movement, less than a year after Lithuania declared the restoration of its independence on 11 March 1990 — the first Soviet republic to do so.

The crackdown

After economic blockade and political pressure failed, Soviet forces moved on strategic sites: on 11 January they seized the Press House in Vilnius, and on the night of 12–13 January paratroopers and KGB Alpha Group units attacked the Vilnius TV tower and the radio and television committee building. Civilians maintained a non-violent defence, singing and standing before tanks; 14 people were killed and more than 700 injured. Loreta Asanavičiūtė, crushed by a tank at the TV tower, became the best-known victim.

Outcome

The parliament building (Seimas), barricaded and defended by volunteers, was not stormed. International condemnation, a huge protest response in Russia itself, and a February referendum endorsing independence (over 90% in favour) undermined the crackdown. Iceland recognised Lithuania in February 1991; after the August coup in Moscow, recognition became general, and the USSR recognised Lithuanian independence in September 1991. 13 January is commemorated in Lithuania as the Day of the Defenders of Freedom.
