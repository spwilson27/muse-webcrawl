Public university

A public university or public college is a university or college that is primarily supported by public funds, usually through a national or subnational government, as opposed to private universities. Whether a national university is considered public varies from country to country.

By country

In the United States, public universities are primarily state universities founded and operated by state government entities; flagship campuses such as the University of California and University of Michigan systems are among the largest and most research-intensive. In many European countries, most universities are public and charge low or no tuition. In the United Kingdom, nearly all universities are publicly funded though legally independent charities.

Funding

Public universities receive funding from government appropriations, tuition fees, research grants and philanthropy. Declines in state funding in the US have been associated with rising tuition and greater reliance on out-of-state and international students.
