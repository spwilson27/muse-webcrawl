Hair transplantation

Hair transplantation is a surgical technique that removes hair follicles from one part of the body and transplants them to a balding area.

Hair transplantation is a surgical technique that removes hair follicles from one part of the body, called the "donor site", to a bald or balding part of the body known as the "recipient site". The technique is primarily used to treat male pattern baldness. In this minimally invasive procedure, grafts containing hair follicles that are genetically resistant to balding (like those on the back of the head) are transplanted to the bald scalp. Hair transplantation can also be used to restore eyelashes, eyebrows, beard hair, chest hair, pubic hair and to fill in scars caused by accidents or surgery such as face-lifts and previous hair transplants.

History

The use of both scalp flaps, as well as free grafts, dates back to the 19th century. Modern transplant techniques began in Japan in the 1930s, where surgeons used small grafts to restore eyebrows and lashes and to address hair loss in burn victims. In the 1950s, New York dermatologist Norman Orentreich established that hair taken from the back and sides of the scalp retained its resistance to balding when transplanted — the principle of "donor dominance". Early Western techniques used large round "plugs" that produced an unnatural, doll-like appearance. The adoption of mini- and micrografts in the 1980s, and of follicular unit transplantation in the 1990s, greatly improved cosmetic results.

Techniques

Follicular unit transplantation (FUT), or strip surgery, removes a strip of scalp from the donor area; the strip is dissected under a microscope into individual follicular units of one to four hairs, which are implanted into tiny incisions in the recipient area. It leaves a linear scar but allows large numbers of grafts in one session.

Follicular unit extraction (FUE) removes individual follicular units directly from the scalp using small punches, avoiding a linear scar at the cost of longer operating time. Robotic systems can assist with extraction. Transplanted hair typically sheds within weeks and regrows over the following months, with final results visible after about a year.

Considerations

Candidates need adequate donor hair, and progressive hair loss may require further procedures. Risks include infection, scarring, unnatural appearance, and shock loss of existing hair. Hair transplantation is also a significant medical-tourism industry, with lower-cost clinics in countries such as Turkey attracting large numbers of patients, alongside concerns about unlicensed practice.
