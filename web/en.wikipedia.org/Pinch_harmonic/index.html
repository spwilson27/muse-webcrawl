Pinch harmonic

A pinch harmonic is a guitar technique in which the player produces an artificial harmonic by lightly touching the string with the edge of the thumb (or another part of the picking hand) immediately after picking it. The thumb's contact damps the string's fundamental and lower partials at a nodal point, leaving a high, ringing overtone that "squeals"—hence the technique's other names, squealies, squealers and pick harmonics.

Technique

The player picks the string while allowing the side of the thumb holding the pick to graze the string at the same instant. The exact pitch of the harmonic depends on where along the string the thumb makes contact, corresponding to the string's natural harmonic nodes; moving the picking hand toward the bridge or neck produces different harmonics from the same fretted note. Pinch harmonics are most prominent with high-gain distortion, which amplifies the weak overtone into the technique's characteristic piercing cry.

Use

Pinch harmonics are a staple of rock and heavy metal guitar. Roy Buchanan made early expressive use of them, Billy Gibbons of ZZ Top built his signature sound around them, and players such as Zakk Wylde and Dimebag Darrell made the screaming pinch harmonic a hallmark of modern metal lead playing. The effect can be combined with vibrato and whammy-bar bends for dramatic, vocal-like effects.
