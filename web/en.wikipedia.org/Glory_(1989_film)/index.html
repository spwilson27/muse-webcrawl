Glory (1989 film)

Glory is a 1989 American epic war drama film directed by Edward Zwick and written by Kevin Jarre, based on the personal letters of Colonel Robert Gould Shaw, the white commanding officer of the 54th Massachusetts Infantry Regiment — one of the first African-American units of the Union Army during the American Civil War. The film stars Matthew Broderick as Shaw, alongside Denzel Washington, Cary Elwes and Morgan Freeman.

Plot

After surviving the Battle of Antietam, Captain Robert Gould Shaw is promoted to colonel and given command of the newly formed 54th Massachusetts, composed of Black enlisted men led by white officers. Shaw must overcome prejudice from within the Union Army — including unequal pay for his soldiers — and forge the regiment into an effective fighting force. The film culminates in the regiment's heroic but doomed assault on Fort Wagner, South Carolina, in July 1863, where Shaw and many of his men are killed.

Reception and awards

Glory was praised for its battle scenes, James Horner's score and its performances. Denzel Washington won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor for his role as Private Silas Trip. The film received five Academy Award nominations in total, also winning for Best Cinematography and Best Sound. It is frequently cited as one of the finest films about the American Civil War and is credited with bringing wider public attention to the story of the 54th Massachusetts.
