Alcohol (drug)

Alcohol (ethanol)
TypeDepressant
Legal statusLegal in most countries (age-restricted)

Alcohol, in the context of drug use, refers to ethanol (ethyl alcohol), the psychoactive substance found in beer, wine, and spirits. It is one of the most widely used recreational drugs in the world and acts as a central nervous system depressant.

Effects

At low doses alcohol produces relaxation, euphoria, and reduced inhibition; higher doses impair coordination, judgment, and memory, and heavy intoxication can cause alcohol poisoning, which may be fatal. Ethanol acts mainly by enhancing GABAA receptor activity and inhibiting NMDA glutamate receptors.

Health consequences

Chronic heavy use can cause liver disease (including cirrhosis), cardiovascular disease, several cancers, and alcohol use disorder, characterized by tolerance and withdrawal. Alcohol contributes to a substantial global burden of disease and injury, including traffic deaths.

History and society

Alcoholic beverages have been produced since the Neolithic period. Because of its risks, sale is regulated in most countries through licensing, taxes, and minimum drinking ages; it is prohibited in some jurisdictions for religious reasons.
