Work hardening

Work hardening, also known as strain hardening, is the strengthening of a metal or polymer by plastic deformation. Cold working below the recrystallisation temperature increases the material's yield strength and hardness while reducing its ductility.

Mechanism

Plastic deformation multiplies dislocations within the crystal structure. As dislocation density rises, dislocations impede each other's motion, so progressively greater stress is needed to continue deforming the material. The effect can be reversed by annealing, which allows recrystallisation and restores ductility.

Applications

Work hardening is exploited in processes such as cold rolling, drawing of wire, and shot peening, and contributes to the strength of many copper, aluminium, and austenitic stainless steel products. Uncontrolled, it can lead to cracking during forming operations.
