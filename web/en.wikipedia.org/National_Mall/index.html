National Mall

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

National Mall
Location: Washington, D.C., United States
Administered by: National Park Service
Area: approx. 309 acres (incl. West Potomac Park)
Visitors: approx. 25 million annually

The National Mall is a landscaped park near the downtown area of Washington, D.C., the capital of the United States. It contains numerous museums and monuments and stretches from the grounds of the United States Capitol in the east to the Potomac River in the west, with the Washington Monument near its centre and the Lincoln Memorial at its western end.

Layout

The Mall's design originates in Pierre Charles L'Enfant's 1791 plan for the capital, which envisioned a grand avenue lined with gardens. The McMillan Plan of 1901–02 realised the vision, clearing railroads and shantytowns and creating the open, elm-lined green framed today by Smithsonian museums. Landmarks along it include the National Gallery of Art, the National Museum of American History, the National Museum of Natural History, and the memorials to Vietnam and Korean War veterans and to World War II.

Public use

Often called "America's front yard", the Mall is the country's pre-eminent stage for civic life: presidential inaugurations, festivals such as the Smithsonian Folklife Festival and the National Cherry Blossom Festival, and great demonstrations including the 1963 March on Washington, where Martin Luther King Jr. delivered his "I Have a Dream" speech from the Lincoln Memorial steps.

Administration

The National Park Service administers the Mall as part of National Mall and Memorial Parks. Heavy visitation — around 25 million people a year — has prompted long-term restoration projects for the turf panels and the Tidal Basin.
