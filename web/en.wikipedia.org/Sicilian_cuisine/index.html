Sicilian cuisine

Sicilian cuisine is the cooking style of the island of Sicily in Italy. It shows traces of all the cultures that have existed on the island over the last two millennia. Although its food is broadly Italian, Sicilian cuisine also has Spanish, Greek, and Arab influences.

History

The ancient Greeks introduced wine, olives, and cheese-making; the Romans brought fava beans and wheat cultivation on a large scale. During Arab rule (9th–11th centuries), Sicily gained citrus fruits, rice, sugar, eggplant, and techniques such as distillation and the making of sorbet-like desserts. The Normans and Spanish added further elements, and New World crops such as tomatoes and peppers arrived under Spanish rule.

Dishes

Well-known Sicilian dishes include arancini (fried stuffed rice balls), caponata (sweet-and-sour eggplant relish), pasta alla Norma (with tomato, eggplant, and ricotta salata), pasta con le sarde (with sardines and wild fennel), and panelle (chickpea fritters). Street food is a major feature of Sicilian food culture, particularly in Palermo.

Desserts

Sicily is especially famous for its sweets: cannoli filled with sweetened ricotta, cassata (sponge cake with ricotta and candied fruit), granita, and frutta martorana (marzipan fruits). Many desserts are linked to religious festivals and to the island's convent baking traditions.

Wines

Sicily is one of Italy's largest wine-producing regions, known for wines such as Nero d'Avola, Etna wines grown on volcanic soil, and the fortified Marsala wine.
