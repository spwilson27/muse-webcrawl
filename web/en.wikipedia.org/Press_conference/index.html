Press conference

A press conference, or news conference, is a media event in which an individual or organization invites journalists to hear an announcement and, usually, to ask questions. Press conferences are commonly held by politicians, government agencies, corporations, sports teams, celebrities, and law enforcement to communicate news directly to the press corps in a controlled setting.

Format

A typical press conference consists of a prepared statement by one or more speakers, followed by a question-and-answer session moderated so that journalists may be recognized in turn. Organizers usually distribute supporting material, such as a press release, fact sheets, or visual displays, and events are frequently broadcast live or streamed. In some settings, such as White House briefings, press conferences are regular institutional fixtures; in others, they are convened in response to breaking news, crises, product launches, or legal proceedings.

Purposes and criticism

Press conferences allow newsmakers to shape coverage by delivering a message simultaneously to many outlets and demonstrating transparency by facing unscripted questions. Critics note that staged events can limit genuine scrutiny, since organizers control access, timing, and sometimes the selection of questioners. The related informal formats include the press gaggle, the media scrum, and the photo opportunity.
