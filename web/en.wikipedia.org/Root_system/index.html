Root system

In mathematics, a root system is a configuration of vectors in a Euclidean space satisfying certain geometrical properties, introduced in the theory of Lie groups and Lie algebras. Root systems classify semisimple Lie algebras and underlie the theory of Weyl groups and Dynkin diagrams.

Definition

A root system is a finite set of non-zero vectors (roots) spanning the space, closed under reflection through the hyperplane perpendicular to each root, with all scalar multiples of a root limited to the root itself and its negative, and satisfying an integrality condition on the angles between roots.

Classification

Irreducible root systems fall into four infinite families — An, Bn, Cn, Dn — and five exceptional types: G2, F4, E6, E7 and E8. Each corresponds to a family of simple Lie algebras. Roots also describe the weights of representations and appear in the geometry of Coxeter groups. A separate botanical sense of "root system" denotes the underground parts of a plant.
