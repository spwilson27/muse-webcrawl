Federal Electoral Institute

The Federal Electoral Institute (Spanish: Instituto Federal Electoral, IFE) was an autonomous public body responsible for organising federal elections in Mexico from 1990 until 2014. It organised elections for President of the Republic and for members of the Chamber of Deputies and the Senate, maintained the national electoral register, and issued Mexico's voting credential, which became the country's de facto national identity document. In 2014 a constitutional reform replaced the IFE with the National Electoral Institute (INE), which also assumed responsibilities for state and local elections.

History

The IFE was created in 1990 as part of a series of electoral reforms following the disputed presidential election of 1988, in which a notorious "system failure" during the vote count undermined public confidence. Initially linked to the Interior Ministry, the institute was made fully independent of the executive by a 1996 constitutional reform that placed citizen councillors — chosen by a two-thirds vote of the Chamber of Deputies — in charge of its General Council, removing the Secretary of the Interior from its presidency.

Under its strengthened mandate, the IFE organised the 2000 presidential election, in which Vicente Fox of the National Action Party defeated the Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI) candidate, ending 71 years of uninterrupted PRI rule. The institute also administered the extremely close and contested 2006 presidential election, whose result was upheld by the Federal Electoral Tribunal after a partial recount.

Functions

Beyond election administration, the IFE registered political parties, enforced campaign finance rules, allocated official broadcast time to parties, and ran civic education programmes. Its voter identification card, the credencial para votar with photograph, was held by virtually every adult Mexican citizen.
