Zambia, officially the Republic of Zambia, is a landlocked country at the crossroads of Central, Southern and East Africa. It is bordered by the Democratic Republic of the Congo to the north, Tanzania to the northeast, Malawi to the east, Mozambique to the southeast, Zimbabwe and Botswana to the south, Namibia to the southwest, and Angola to the west. The capital is Lusaka.

History

The territory was administered by the British South Africa Company and then as the British protectorate of Northern Rhodesia. Zambia gained independence on 24 October 1964 under Kenneth Kaunda, who led the country until 1991. After the introduction of multiparty politics, Zambia became known for peaceful transfers of power; Hakainde Hichilema won the presidency in 2021.

Geography and economy

Zambia is a high plateau drained by the Zambezi, Kafue and Luangwa rivers; Victoria Falls lies on its southern border. Copper mining in the Copperbelt dominates exports and the wider economy, alongside agriculture and growing tourism centred on national parks such as South Luangwa and Lower Zambezi. The Kariba Dam on the Zambezi supplies much of the country's electricity.
