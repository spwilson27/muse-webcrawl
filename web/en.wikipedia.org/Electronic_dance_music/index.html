Electronic dance music

Electronic dance music (EDM), also known as dance music, club music, or simply dance, is a broad range of percussive electronic music genres made largely for nightclubs, raves, and festivals. It is generally produced for playback by DJs who create seamless selections of tracks, called mixes, by segueing from one recording to another.

Massive festivals such as Tomorrowland and Ultra Music Festival helped make EDM a dominant popular genre in the 2010s.

History

EDM grew from disco and the electronic experiments of the 1970s (Kraftwerk, Giorgio Moroder). In the 1980s, Chicago house, Detroit techno, and New York garage emerged from club scenes, followed by acid house and the UK rave scene of the late 1980s. The 1990s produced trance, drum and bass, big beat, and UK garage. In the 2000s and 2010s, dubstep, electro house, and trap brought EDM into the pop mainstream, particularly in the United States, where the term "EDM" became a marketing label for the phenomenon.

Genres

Major genres include house, techno, trance, drum and bass, dubstep, electro, hardstyle, and ambient/downtempo styles. Most dance tracks use a four-on-the-floor kick drum pattern and tempos roughly between 110 and 150 beats per minute.

Culture

Dance music culture centres on clubs, raves, and festivals, with the DJ as performer. Large commercial festivals draw hundreds of thousands of attendees, while underground scenes continue to develop new styles.
