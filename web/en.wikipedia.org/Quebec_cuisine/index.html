Quebec cuisine

Quebec cuisine is the traditional food culture of the Canadian province of Quebec. Rooted in French culinary traditions adapted to the climate and ingredients of the St. Lawrence valley, it is known for hearty, rustic dishes, an extensive use of pork, root vegetables, beans, and maple products, and a strong tradition of communal winter meals.

History

French settlers in the 17th and 18th centuries brought techniques such as slow braising, pâtés, and meat pies, adapting them to local game, fish, and produce. Indigenous peoples contributed corn, beans, squash, and maple syrup production. In the 19th and 20th centuries, British, Irish, Jewish, Italian, and later global immigrant influences added dishes and ingredients, while industrialization popularized snack-bar fare.

Signature dishes

Well-known dishes include poutine (French fries with cheese curds and gravy), tourtière (spiced meat pie), pea soup, fèves au lard (baked beans with salt pork), cretons (pork spread), cipaille (layered meat and pastry pie), and pâté chinois (a shepherd's-pie-like casserole). Desserts include pouding chômeur, sugar pie (tarte au sucre), and maple taffy on snow (tire d'érable).

Maple and the sugar shack

Quebec produces the majority of the world's maple syrup. The cabane à sucre (sugar shack) is a seasonal institution where sap is boiled and traditional meals are served during the spring thaw.

Drinks and contemporary cuisine

Quebec has a notable craft beer scene, ice cider producers, and a fine-dining sector, centred in Montreal and Quebec City, that reinterprets traditional ingredients with modern techniques.
