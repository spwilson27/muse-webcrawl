Spleen

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The human spleen, located beneath the diaphragm on the left side

The spleen is an organ found in almost all vertebrates. Situated in the upper left quadrant of the abdomen, behind the stomach and below the diaphragm, it is the largest organ of the lymphatic system. In healthy adults it is roughly the size of a fist, weighing about 150 grams.

Structure and function

The spleen consists of red pulp, which filters the blood and removes old or damaged red blood cells, recycling their iron; and white pulp, lymphoid tissue that mounts immune responses against blood-borne pathogens. It also stores platelets and monocytes and acts as a reservoir of blood. The spleen is especially important for defence against encapsulated bacteria such as Streptococcus pneumoniae.

Clinical significance

Enlargement of the spleen (splenomegaly) occurs in infections such as mononucleosis, in haemolytic anaemias, portal hypertension and some cancers. The spleen can rupture after abdominal trauma, causing life-threatening bleeding. Surgical removal (splenectomy) is survivable because other organs compensate, but asplenic patients have a lifelong increased risk of overwhelming infection and are advised to receive specific vaccinations.
