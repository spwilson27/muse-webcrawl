Leh

Leh is a city in the Indian union territory of Ladakh, set in the Indus valley of the western Himalayas at about 3,500 metres above sea level. It is the largest town of Ladakh and the joint capital of the territory, long a gateway for trade between India, Tibet and Central Asia.

History

Leh grew around the royal palace built in the early 17th century by the Namgyal dynasty, modelled in part on the Potala in Lhasa. Caravans carrying pashmina wool, salt and silk passed through its bazaar until trade routes closed in the mid-20th century.

Geography and climate

The city sits in a cold desert: winters are long and severe, summers mild and dry. The short-stay visitor must acclimatise to the altitude. The Indus river flows below the town, irrigating poplar and barley fields.

Tourism

Leh is a base for visiting Buddhist monasteries such as Thiksey and Hemis, the confluence of the Indus and Zanskar, and high passes on the road to Manali and Srinagar.
