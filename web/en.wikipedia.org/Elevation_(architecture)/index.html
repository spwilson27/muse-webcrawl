Elevation (architecture)


An orthographic elevation drawing of a building's front facade

In architecture, an elevation is a two-dimensional drawing of a building's exterior (or interior) as seen from one side — a flat representation of one facade. It is one of the standard orthographic projections used in architectural drawing, alongside the plan (viewed from above) and the section (a cut-through view).

Purpose

Elevations communicate the external appearance of a building: its proportions, materials, openings such as windows and doors, roof lines and ornament. Because an elevation is drawn to scale without perspective distortion, dimensions can be taken directly from it. Building-permit and construction documents normally include elevations of all principal sides, conventionally named by compass direction (north elevation, south elevation) or by what they face (front, rear, side; street elevation).

Conventions

Elevations are typically drawn at scales such as 1:50 or 1:100. They are generated by projecting the building onto a vertical plane parallel to the facade. Line weights distinguish elements close to the viewer from recessed elements; materials may be indicated by hatching or annotation, and level markers record floor and ridge heights relative to a datum. Interior elevations are used for walls with built-in joinery, kitchens and bathrooms.

Related drawings

The term is distinct from elevation in surveying, meaning height above sea level or datum. In modern practice, elevations are produced from CAD or BIM models, which can generate consistent plans, sections and elevations from a single three-dimensional model, though hand-drawn presentation elevations remain common in design work.
