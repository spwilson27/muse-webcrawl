The Great Divide Mountain Bike Route (GDMBR) is a long-distance cycling route in North America, generally considered the longest off-pavement bicycle route in the world. Developed and mapped by the Adventure Cycling Association, it runs from Banff, Alberta, Canada, to Antelope Wells, New Mexico, at the Mexican border, roughly following the Continental Divide of the Americas through the Rocky Mountains.

Route

The route is approximately 4,340 km (2,700 mi) long, about 90% of it on unpaved roads and trails, crossing the Divide dozens of times and passing through Alberta, British Columbia, Montana, Idaho, Wyoming, Colorado and New Mexico. Riders climb on the order of 200,000 feet (60,000 m) in total and traverse remote country where resupply points may be days apart.

Tour Divide race

The route is famous as the course of the Tour Divide, an annual self-supported ultra-endurance race typically starting in June. Racers ride the entire route unsupported; the fastest completions take a little over two weeks, while touring cyclists commonly take six to ten weeks.
