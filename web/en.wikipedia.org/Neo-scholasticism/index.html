Neo-scholasticism

Neo-scholasticism (also called neo-Thomism) is a revival and development of medieval scholastic philosophy and theology, centred on the thought of Thomas Aquinas, that began in the mid-19th century within the Roman Catholic Church.

History

The movement gathered force in Italy and Germany and received decisive papal endorsement in Leo XIII's 1879 encyclical Aeterni Patris, which recommended Thomistic philosophy as the Church's intellectual framework. Centres such as the University of Louvain (where Cardinal Mercier founded a higher institute of philosophy) systematized the approach. In the 20th century its leading figures included Étienne Gilson, Jacques Maritain, and Reginald Garrigou-Lagrange, though they disagreed among themselves on method and on engagement with modern philosophy.

Doctrines

Neo-scholasticism typically defends metaphysical realism, the act–potency distinction, hylomorphism, natural-law ethics, and the harmony of faith and reason. It declined in Catholic seminaries after the Second Vatican Council as other approaches gained ground, though Thomistic scholarship continues.
