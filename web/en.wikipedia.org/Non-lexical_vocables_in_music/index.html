Non-lexical vocables in music

Non-lexical vocables are sounds and syllables sung without literal meaning—such as "la la la", "doo wop", or scat syllables—used in music for rhythm, melody, timbre, or emotional effect rather than for conveying words.

Forms and traditions

Well-known forms include scat singing in jazz, associated with Louis Armstrong and Ella Fitzgerald; the "doo-wop" syllables of 1950s vocal groups; vocables in Native American and other Indigenous singing traditions; Irish lilting and Scottish puirt à beul; konnakol rhythmic syllables in Carnatic music; and nonsense refrains in folk songs, such as "fa la la". Vocables can carry a melody where lyrics are absent, forgotten, or deliberately avoided, and can imitate instruments.

Functions

Beyond decoration, vocables serve structural roles: marking refrains, facilitating group participation, and in some ceremonial traditions carrying meanings understood by the community even though the syllables are not words of the spoken language.
