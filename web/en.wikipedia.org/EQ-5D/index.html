EQ-5D

The EQ-5D is a standardised, generic instrument for measuring health-related quality of life, developed by the EuroQol Group beginning in 1987. It is widely used in clinical trials, population health surveys and health economic evaluations, in particular for calculating quality-adjusted life years (QALYs) in cost-utility analyses.

Structure

The instrument consists of two parts. The descriptive system asks respondents to rate their health on five dimensions — mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression. In the original EQ-5D-3L each dimension has three levels of severity (no problems, some problems, extreme problems), defining 243 possible health states; the newer EQ-5D-5L uses five levels, defining 3,125 states and reducing ceiling effects. The second part is a visual analogue scale (EQ VAS), on which respondents rate their overall health from 0 ("worst imaginable") to 100 ("best imaginable").

Scoring and use

Responses to the descriptive system can be converted into a single index value using a country-specific value set derived from population preference studies (such as time trade-off valuations), with 1 representing full health and 0 equivalent to being dead; some states are valued below 0. Health technology assessment bodies, including NICE in England, have recommended or required EQ-5D data in submissions. A youth version, EQ-5D-Y, exists for children and adolescents. The instrument is available in many languages and is administered under licence by the EuroQol Research Foundation.
