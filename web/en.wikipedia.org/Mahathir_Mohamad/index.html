Mahathir bin Mohamad (born 10 July 1925) is a Malaysian politician, author and physician who served as the fourth and seventh prime minister of Malaysia. He held the office from July 1981 to October 2003 and again from May 2018 to March 2020, giving him a combined tenure of about 24 years, the longest of any Malaysian prime minister. His first premiership oversaw rapid economic growth and modernisation, including major infrastructure projects such as the North–South Expressway, the Petronas Towers and the new administrative capital of Putrajaya.

Returning to politics after the 2018 general election, Mahathir led the Pakatan Harapan coalition to victory over Barisan Nasional, ending six decades of rule by the latter. At 92, he became the world's oldest serving head of government. He resigned in February 2020 amid a political crisis.

Early life and career

Mahathir was born in Alor Setar, Kedah, the youngest of nine children. He studied medicine at the King Edward VII College of Medicine in Singapore, graduating in 1953, and worked as a government medical officer before entering private practice. He joined the United Malays National Organisation (UMNO) and was elected to parliament in 1964.

First premiership (1981–2003)

Mahathir's "Look East Policy" encouraged emulation of Japanese and South Korean work practices. His government pursued privatisation, heavy industrialisation through the national car project Proton, and large-scale development. He clashed with the judiciary in the 1988 constitutional crisis and weathered the 1997 Asian financial crisis by imposing capital controls, contrary to IMF advice, a stance later credited with softening the downturn.

Second premiership (2018–2020)

After falling out with Prime Minister Najib Razak over the 1MDB scandal, Mahathir co-founded the Malaysian United Indigenous Party (Bersatu) and led the opposition to a historic victory in May 2018. His government repealed the goods and services tax and opened investigations into 1MDB. He resigned on 24 February 2020.

Legacy

Mahathir is regarded as the architect of modern Malaysia's economic transformation, though critics cite his authoritarian tendencies and the detention of opponents under the Internal Security Act during events such as Operation Lalang in 1987.
