Slider (sandwich)

CourseAppetizer, snack, main course
Place of originUnited States
Main ingredientsGround beef, bun, onion

A slider is an American term for a steam-grilled sandwich, typically made with a small amount of ground beef cooked on a griddle with onions and pickles, served on a small bun. The term originated with the White Castle restaurant chain, which began selling small, square hamburgers in the 1920s and trademarked the name "Slyder".

History

White Castle founder Billy Ingram is credited with popularizing the format: five-cent hamburgers cooked quickly on a bed of onions so the steam flavoured the patties and buns. The name is sometimes said to derive from the sandwiches being so small and greasy that they "slide" down easily.

Modern usage

Since the 2000s, "slider" has been used generically for any miniature sandwich served on a small roll, including pulled pork, chicken, meatball and vegetarian variants, and has become a staple of pub and gastropub menus, often sold in groups of two or three.
