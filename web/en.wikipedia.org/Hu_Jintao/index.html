Hu Jintao

Hu Jintao (born 21 December 1942) is a Chinese retired politician who served as the paramount leader of China from 2002 to 2012. He was General Secretary of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) from 2002 to 2012, Chairman of the Central Military Commission from 2004 to 2012, and President of the People's Republic of China from 2003 to 2013.

Early life and rise

Hu was born in Jiangyan, Jiangsu province, and studied hydraulic engineering at Tsinghua University, where he also did political work with the Communist Youth League. After graduation he worked on hydroelectric projects in Gansu province, then advanced through party posts in Gansu, Guizhou, and the Tibet Autonomous Region, becoming a member of the Politburo Standing Committee in 1992 at the unusually young age of 49. He served as Vice President from 1998 and was the first leader of the party's fourth generation of leadership.

Leadership

Hu's era was characterised by rapid economic growth, China's rise in global influence, and events such as the 2008 Beijing Olympics and the 2010 Shanghai World Expo. Domestically he promoted the "Scientific Outlook on Development" and the building of a "Harmonious Society", emphasising balanced growth, social welfare, and reduced inequality, while maintaining strict political control. His tenure also saw the handling of the SARS epidemic, the 2008 Sichuan earthquake, unrest in Tibet (2008) and Xinjiang (2009), and a major anti-corruption and military modernisation drive.

Hu retired from all posts by March 2013 in an orderly succession to Xi Jinping. In October 2022, during the closing ceremony of the 20th CCP National Congress, Hu was escorted from his seat next to Xi in an incident that drew international attention; Chinese state media attributed it to ill health.
