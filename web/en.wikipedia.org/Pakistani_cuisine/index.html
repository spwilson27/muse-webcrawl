Pakistani cuisine

Pakistani cuisine (Urdu: پاکستانی پکوان, romanized: Pākistānī pakwān) is part of the wider cuisine of South Asia and Central Asia and can be characterised as a blend of regional cooking traditions of the Indian subcontinent, influenced by Persian, Mughlai, and Afghan traditions. The cuisine varies greatly across Pakistan's ethnic and cultural diversity — from the meat-heavy dishes of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Balochistan to the seafood of coastal Sindh and the vegetarian traditions of parts of Punjab.

Characteristics

Pakistani food is known for its rich, aromatic use of spices such as cumin, coriander, turmeric, garam masala, and chilli, and for slow-cooked meat dishes inherited from Mughlai cuisine. Meals are typically accompanied by flatbreads (naan, roti, paratha) or rice. Because of the predominantly Muslim population, food is halal and pork is absent; alcohol is generally avoided.

Notable dishes

Signature dishes include biryani (layered spiced rice, especially Sindhi and Karachi styles), nihari (slow-cooked beef shank stew), haleem, chicken karahi, seekh kebabs, chapli kebabs, sajji (whole roasted lamb or chicken from Balochistan), pulao, and daal. Popular desserts include gulab jamun, falooda, kheer, and jalebi. Breakfast often features halwa puri, while chai (spiced milk tea) is consumed throughout the day.

Regional varieties

Lahore is famed for its food streets and rich Mughlai-influenced fare; Karachi offers diverse urban cuisine including its distinctive biryani with potatoes; Peshawar is known for chapli kebab and namkeen (salted) roasts; and Balochistan for sajji and kaak bread. Kashmiri cuisine contributes dishes such as rogan josh and pink salt tea (noon chai).
