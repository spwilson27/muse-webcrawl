British honours system

The British honours system is the system of orders, decorations and medals by which the United Kingdom recognises personal bravery, achievement and service. The sovereign, as fount of honour, confers honours on the advice of the government, with lists published at New Year and on the sovereign's official birthday.

Orders and appointments

The principal order is the Order of the British Empire, with grades from Knight or Dame Grand Cross (GBE) through Knight or Dame Commander (KBE/DBE), Commander (CBE), Officer (OBE) and Member (MBE). Older orders include the Order of the Garter, the Order of the Thistle and the Order of the Bath. Knights and dames use the titles Sir and Dame; other recipients use post-nominal letters.

Criticism

The system has periodically faced criticism over political patronage and the word "Empire", prompting some recipients to decline honours.
