The United States Code (U.S.C.) is the official compilation and codification of the general and permanent federal statutes of the United States. It contains 54 titles, organized by subject matter, ranging from Title 1 (General Provisions) to Title 54 (National Park Service and Related Programs). The Code is prepared by the Office of the Law Revision Counsel of the U.S. House of Representatives and published by the Government Publishing Office.

History

Before codification, federal statutes were published in the United States Statutes at Large in chronological order. The first official codification, the Revised Statutes of 1873–74, was enacted into law in 1874 and updated in 1878. In 1925, Congress authorized a new code; the first edition of the United States Code was published in 1926, though it was not itself enacted — it was prima facie evidence of the law, with the Statutes at Large remaining authoritative in case of discrepancy.

Since 1926, Congress has progressively enacted individual titles of the Code as positive law, beginning with Title 1 in 1947. About half of the titles have been enacted as positive law; for those titles, the Code text is legal evidence of the law itself.

Publication cycle

A new edition of the Code is published every six years, following the federal election cycle. Between editions, annual cumulative supplements (Supplements I–V) incorporate amendments. In recent years the online version, maintained by the Office of the Law Revision Counsel, has become the most widely used form, updated continuously as new public laws are enacted.

Codification process

When Congress passes a new statute, the Office of the Law Revision Counsel classifies its provisions to the appropriate title and section of the Code, amending, renumbering, or repealing existing text as the statute directs. Provisions that are temporary, appropriations-related, or otherwise not general and permanent are generally not codified; they may instead appear as statutory notes following the relevant section.
