Derrick May

Derrick May (born April 6, 1963) is an American electronic musician from Detroit, Michigan, widely credited — together with Juan Atkins and Kevin Saunderson, the so-called "Belleville Three" — as one of the originators of techno music. Recording largely under the name Rhythim Is Rhythim, May produced a series of records in the late 1980s, including "Nude Photo" (1987) and "Strings of Life" (1987), that defined the melodic, high-energy Detroit techno sound and became anthems of the British acid house and rave scenes.

Early life and the Belleville Three

May grew up in Detroit and attended Belleville High School, where he met Juan Atkins and Kevin Saunderson. The three shared an interest in the futuristic funk of Parliament-Funkadelic, Kraftwerk, and the eclectic radio shows of Detroit DJ Charles "The Electrifying Mojo" Johnson. After Atkins began releasing records as Cybotron and Model 500, May started producing his own tracks and founded the Transmat label in 1986.

Career

May's 1987 Rhythim Is Rhythim singles, especially "Strings of Life" — built around Michael James's piano riff — crossed over to the United Kingdom during the Second Summer of Love and helped ignite the European rave movement. He famously described techno as "George Clinton and Kraftwerk stuck in an elevator". In the 1990s May released fewer records, concentrating on DJing worldwide, remixing, and running Transmat, which issued music by artists including Carl Craig and Aril Brikha. His rise to prominence was accompanied by decades of international touring as one of the most in-demand DJs of the Detroit school.

Legacy

May's productions of 1987–1989 are widely regarded as foundational documents of techno, and his work is routinely cited in histories of electronic dance music as the bridge between Chicago house, European synth-pop and the harder techno that followed.
