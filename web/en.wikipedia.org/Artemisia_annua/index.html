Artemisia annua

Family: Asteraceae

Common names: Sweet wormwood, sweet annie, qinghao

Artemisia annua, commonly known as sweet wormwood or sweet annie, is an annual flowering plant in the aster family, native to temperate Asia and now naturalised worldwide. It is the source of the antimalarial compound artemisinin.

Uses

Known in Chinese medicine as qinghao, the plant was used for centuries to treat fevers. In the 1970s, Tu Youyou and her team isolated artemisinin from the plant, work that led to artemisinin-based combination therapies, now the standard treatment for malaria, and to Tu's share of the 2015 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.

Cultivation

The plant is grown commercially in China, Vietnam and East Africa. It is a short-day plant that grows up to about 2 metres tall, with fern-like leaves and small yellow flowers.
