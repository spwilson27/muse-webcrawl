Charro

A charro is a traditional horseman of Mexico, originating in the state of Jalisco, whose distinctive embroidered clothing, horsemanship, and roping skills are central to the sport of charreada. The charro is an enduring symbol of Mexican national identity, and female riders are known as escaramuzas.

Origins

Charros descend from the mounted herdsmen of colonial New Spain, who worked the great haciendas of the central highlands. The word itself may be a Spanish term for rustic or unpolished, later applied to the horsemen and their elaborate dress. Ranching techniques developed by charros travelled north and influenced the American cowboy tradition.

Clothing

The charro outfit is formal and richly decorated: tight trousers or calzoneras, a short jacket embroidered with silver thread (suede and botonadura buttons), a silk sash, bow tie, and the wide-brimmed sombrero charro. Horses are similarly adorned, and saddles are elaborately tooled.

Charreada

The charreada, Mexico's national sport, consists of nine scored events (suertes) including cala (reining demonstration), coleadero (bull tailing), manganas (roping), and the paso de la muerte. In 2016 UNESCO inscribed charrería on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
