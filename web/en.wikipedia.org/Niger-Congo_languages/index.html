Niger–Congo languages

The Niger–Congo languages form one of the world's major language families and Africa's largest in geographical area, number of speakers and number of distinct languages — roughly 1,500 languages. They are spoken across most of sub-Saharan Africa.

Characteristics and branches

Many Niger–Congo languages use noun class systems (famously elaborate in the Bantu languages), are tonal, and tend toward open syllables. Major branches include Atlantic, Mande (whose inclusion is debated), Gur, Kru, Kwa, Benue–Congo (which includes the large Bantu group), Adamawa–Ubangi and Ijoid. The Bantu expansion spread one subgroup from the Nigeria–Cameroon borderlands across central, eastern and southern Africa.

Major languages

Widely spoken Niger–Congo languages include Swahili, Yoruba, Igbo, Fula, Hausa is Afroasiatic and not included, Shona, Zulu and Lingala.
