Osei Tutu

Bornc. 1660
Died1717
Reignc. 1701 – 1717
TitleAsantehene (King of Ashanti)
SuccessorOpoku Ware I

Osei Tutu (c. 1660 – 1717) was the founder of the Ashanti Empire (Asante) in present-day Ghana and its first Asantehene (king). Together with his chief priest and adviser Okomfo Anokye, he united the Akan city-states of the region into a powerful confederacy centred on the city of Kumasi, which he founded around 1680 and made his capital.

Early life

Osei Tutu was born into the Oyoko clan, the royal lineage of the state of Kwaaman, from which Kumasi grew. According to tradition, he spent part of his youth at the court of the Denkyirahene, ruler of the dominant Denkyira state, where he may have received military training and observed Denkyira's centralised administration — experience he later turned against Denkyira itself. After returning home he succeeded his uncle, Obiri Yeboa, as chief of Kwaaman.

Founding of the Ashanti Union

With the spiritual and political support of Okomfo Anokye, Osei Tutu persuaded the chiefs of the surrounding Akan states to unite under his leadership, symbolised by the sacred Golden Stool (Sika Dwa), which tradition says Anokye summoned down from the sky in 1701. The Golden Stool was believed to embody the soul of the Ashanti nation, and it remains the supreme symbol of Ashanti unity and kingship.

Wars and expansion

In the Ashanti–Denkyira War of 1699–1701, Osei Tutu's forces defeated Denkyira at the Battle of Feyiase (1701), freeing the Ashanti states from tribute obligations and opening trade routes to the coast. He then expanded the empire through further campaigns, building a centralised state with a professional army organised around firearms obtained from European traders on the Gold Coast.

Death and legacy

Osei Tutu was killed in 1717 during a campaign across the Volta River against the Akyem. He was succeeded by Opoku Ware I. Revered as the father of the Ashanti nation, his name remains central to Ashanti identity, and the Golden Stool he and Anokye established is still guarded in Kumasi as the soul of the Asante people.
