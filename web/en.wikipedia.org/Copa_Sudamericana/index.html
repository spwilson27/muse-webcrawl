Copa Sudamericana

The Copa Sudamericana is an annual international club football competition organised by CONMEBOL since 2002. It is the second-most prestigious club tournament in South American football, behind the Copa Libertadores, and is broadly equivalent in status to the UEFA Europa League in Europe. Its official name has carried sponsorship prefixes, including Copa Conmebol Sudamericana.

History

The competition was created in 2002 to replace the Copa Merconorte and Copa Mercosur, which had split South American clubs into northern and southern tournaments. The first edition was won by San Lorenzo of Argentina. For many years Brazilian clubs were absent or joined part-way because of scheduling clashes, and Mexican clubs participated as guests between 2005 and 2008. From 2017 the tournament was rescheduled to run across most of the calendar year, in parallel with the Libertadores.

Format

Clubs qualify through their domestic league and cup performances. The modern format begins with a preliminary round of two-legged or single ties, followed by a group stage and then knockout play-offs and rounds played over two legs, with the final staged as a single match at a pre-chosen neutral venue since 2019. The champion earns a place in the following season's Copa Libertadores group stage and contests the Recopa Sudamericana against the Libertadores champion.

Records

Argentine clubs have dominated the winners' list: Boca Juniors and Independiente are among the most successful, and Athletico Paranaense became the first Brazilian club to win it twice. Ecuador's Independiente del Valle and LDU Quito have also won the tournament, LDU having earlier won the Libertadores.
