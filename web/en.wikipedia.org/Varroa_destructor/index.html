Varroa destructor

Varroa destructor
FamilyVarroidae
HostsApis cerana, Apis mellifera
DistributionWorldwide (except Australia until 2022 detection)

Varroa destructor, the Varroa mite, is an external parasitic mite that attacks and feeds on honey bees and is one of the most damaging pests of the western honey bee (Apis mellifera). It originally parasitized the Asian honey bee (Apis cerana) and jumped hosts in the 20th century, spreading worldwide through the trade in bees and queens.

Life cycle and effects

Female mites enter brood cells shortly before they are capped and reproduce on the developing pupa, feeding on the bee's fat body tissue. Emerging bees may be weakened, deformed, or short-lived. The mite also vectors viruses, notably deformed wing virus, and heavy infestations cause colony collapse — a syndrome central to modern honey bee losses.

Control

Beekeepers manage Varroa with chemical miticides (such as amitraz and fluvalinate), organic acids (formic and oxalic acid), thymol, brood interruption, and screened bottom boards. Breeding programs aim for hygienic bees that detect and remove infested brood (Varroa-sensitive hygiene).
