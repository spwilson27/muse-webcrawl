Alpine (automobile)

Automobiles Alpine, commonly known as Alpine, is a French manufacturer of racing and sports cars founded in 1955 by Jean Rédélé in Dieppe, Normandy. A subsidiary of Renault since 1973, the marque is famous for the A110 Berlinette, which won the inaugural World Rally Championship for manufacturers in 1973.

Alpine
Founded: 1955, Dieppe, France
Founder: Jean Rédélé
Parent: Renault Group

History

Rédélé, a Renault dealer and rally driver, developed lightweight fibreglass-bodied coupes using Renault mechanicals: the A106, A108, and from 1963 the A110. Alpine won the Monte Carlo Rally in 1971 and 1973 and Le Mans in 1978 with the A442. Production of the later A310 and GTA/A610 continued until 1995, when the Dieppe plant turned to Renault Sport models.

Revival

Renault revived Alpine with a new mid-engined A110 unveiled in 2017, praised for its light weight (~1,100 kg) and agility. The brand became Renault Group's premium sports arm, entered Formula One as Alpine F1 Team from 2021 (the former Renault team), and competes in endurance racing while developing electric successors including the A290 and A390.
