Nuclear warfare

Nuclear warfare is a military conflict or political strategy involving the use of nuclear weapons. Nuclear weapons have been used in warfare only twice, by the United States against the Japanese cities of Hiroshima and Nagasaki in August 1945, but their existence has shaped international politics throughout the Cold War and beyond.

History

The Manhattan Project produced the first nuclear weapons during the Second World War. The Soviet Union tested its first device in 1949, beginning an arms race that produced tens of thousands of warheads, intercontinental ballistic missiles, submarine-launched missiles and strategic bombers — the "nuclear triad". Crises such as the Cuban Missile Crisis of 1962 brought the superpowers close to war.

Strategy and deterrence

Doctrine centred on deterrence, second-strike capability and mutually assured destruction (MAD): the idea that a nuclear attack would invite devastating retaliation, making such a war unwinnable. Arms control agreements, including the Partial Test Ban Treaty, the Non-Proliferation Treaty, SALT and START, sought to limit arsenals.

Effects

Nuclear explosions cause destruction through blast, heat and radiation, and large-scale use could produce climatic effects known as nuclear winter. Nine states are believed to possess nuclear weapons today.
