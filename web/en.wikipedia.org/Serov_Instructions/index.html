Serov Instructions

The Serov Instructions, formally "On the Procedure for carrying out the Deportation of Anti-Soviet Elements from Lithuania, Latvia, and Estonia", were a top-secret document issued by the Soviet NKVD in early 1941 and signed by Ivan Serov, Deputy People's Commissar of State Security of the USSR. They prescribed in detail how the mass deportations from the Soviet-occupied Baltic states were to be conducted, including the separation of families, the packing of possessions, and the transport of deportees to the Soviet interior.

Contents

The instructions ordered that arrests and deportations be carried out simultaneously at night, that heads of families be separated from their families (ostensibly for "processing"), that deportees be allowed a limited weight of belongings and food, and that trains of freight cars be used to transport them to assigned places of exile. They stressed secrecy and speed to prevent escape or resistance, and assigned roles to operational groups of security officers and local party activists.

Implementation

The instructions formed the procedural basis for the June deportation of 14 June 1941, in which more than 40,000 people from Lithuania, Latvia, and Estonia — including officials, military officers, landowners, and their families, among them thousands of children — were deported to Siberia and other remote regions. Many deportees died from hunger, cold, and disease. Similar instructions underlay later waves of deportation, including Operation Priboi in 1949.

Discovery and significance

Copies of the instructions came to light after the German invasion of the USSR in June 1941 and were published in the West, becoming key documentary evidence of the planned and systematic character of the Soviet deportations from the Baltic states, which the Baltic nations regard as crimes against humanity.
