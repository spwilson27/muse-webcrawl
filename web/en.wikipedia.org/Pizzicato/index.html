Pizzicato

Pizzicato (Italian for "pinched") is a playing technique used on bowed string instruments in which the player plucks the strings with the fingers instead of drawing the bow across them. It produces a short, percussive, guitar-like sound that contrasts sharply with the sustained tone of bowing (arco). In scores it is indicated by the abbreviation pizz., with arco marking the return to the bow.

A player plucking a string to produce pizzicato.

Technique

Violinists and violists normally pluck with the index finger of the right hand while holding the bow, or set the bow down for extended passages. Cellists and double bassists pluck with one or two fingers; in jazz, walking bass lines are played almost entirely pizzicato. Variants include the Bartók pizzicato, in which the string is snapped against the fingerboard, and left-hand pizzicato, used in virtuoso works by Paganini.

Famous examples

Well-known pizzicato passages include the "Pizzicato Polka" by Johann Strauss II and Josef Strauss, the pizzicato scherzo of Tchaikovsky's Fourth Symphony, Delibes' "Pizzicati" from the ballet Sylvia, and the plucked-string movements of Britten's Simple Symphony.
