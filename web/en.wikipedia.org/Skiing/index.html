Skiing

Skiing is the use of skis to glide on snow. Variations can be combined with powered transportation such as ski lifts, which promote access to higher slopes. It is practiced as a means of transport, a recreational activity and a competitive winter sport in which participants attach skis to boots or shoes on their feet and travel on top of snow. Internationally, the sport is contested at the Winter Olympic Games and in world championships.

History

Skiing has its roots in prehistoric Scandinavia and Siberia, where wooden skis were used for hunting and travel across snow-covered ground thousands of years ago. Fragmentary ski finds include the Rødøy ski of Norway, dated to around 3200 BCE, and ancient rock carvings depicting skiers. By the nineteenth century, the Norwegian military officer and explorer Fridtjof Nansen's crossing of Greenland on skis in 1888 popularized the activity, while Sondre Norheim of Telemark is credited with innovations — including the Telemark turn and modern bindings — that shaped recreational skiing. Alpine, or downhill, skiing developed in the Alps in the early twentieth century, and the first Winter Olympics in 1924 included Nordic events.

Types

Alpine skiing

Alpine skiing, also called downhill skiing, is practiced at ski resorts where skiers ride mechanical lifts to the top of slopes and descend prepared runs, or "pistes," graded by difficulty. Its racing disciplines — downhill, super-G, giant slalom, slalom and combined — are governed by the International Ski and Snowboard Federation (FIS).

Nordic skiing

Nordic skiing includes cross-country skiing, ski jumping, Nordic combined and Telemark skiing. Cross-country skiers propel themselves across flatter terrain using either the classical stride or the skating technique, and the sport is widely practiced for fitness as well as racing.

Freestyle and other forms

Freestyle skiing encompasses moguls, aerials, half-pipe, slopestyle and ski cross. Backcountry and ski touring variants involve climbing slopes under one's own power using climbing skins, away from patrolled resort terrain, while ski mountaineering combines touring with alpine climbing skills.

Equipment

Modern skiing equipment includes shaped skis with steel edges, rigid plastic boots, release bindings designed to reduce leg injuries, and poles. Clothing is layered and insulated, and helmets, goggles and avalanche safety equipment (transceiver, probe and shovel) are used depending on the terrain and conditions.
