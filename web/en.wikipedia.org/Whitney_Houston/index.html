Whitney Elizabeth Houston (9 August 1963 – 11 February 2012) was an American singer and actress. Known as "the Voice" for her powerful, soulful singing, she is one of the best-selling music artists of all time, with over 220 million records sold worldwide. Her singing style influenced generations of vocalists, and she was the first artist to achieve seven consecutive number-one singles on the Billboard Hot 100.

Career

Born into a musical family — her mother was gospel singer Cissy Houston and her cousin was Dionne Warwick — Houston sang in church and worked as a model and backing singer before signing to Arista Records. Her debut album, Whitney Houston (1985), became the best-selling debut album by a solo artist at the time, producing hits including "Greatest Love of All" and "How Will I Know." The follow-up, Whitney (1987), debuted at number one.

Houston starred in the film The Bodyguard (1992); its soundtrack, featuring her version of "I Will Always Love You," became the best-selling soundtrack album of all time and won the Grammy for Album of the Year. She also acted in Waiting to Exhale (1995) and The Preacher's Wife (1996).

Later life and death

Houston's later career was marked by personal struggles and a tumultuous marriage to singer Bobby Brown. She died on 11 February 2012, aged 48, from accidental drowning in a hotel bathtub, with heart disease and cocaine use as contributing factors. She was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2020.
