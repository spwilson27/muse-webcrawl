Sampling distribution

Field: Statistics

Definition: Probability distribution of a statistic over repeated samples

Key quantity: Standard error

Key theorem: Central limit theorem

In statistics, a sampling distribution is the probability distribution of a given statistic based on a random sample. If one were to draw every possible sample of a fixed size n from a population, compute the statistic (such as the mean) for each sample, and tabulate the results, the distribution of those values is the sampling distribution of that statistic. Because a statistic is a random variable that varies from sample to sample, its sampling distribution describes how much it varies — the foundation of confidence intervals and hypothesis tests.

Standard error

The standard deviation of a sampling distribution is called the standard error of the statistic. For the sample mean drawn from a population with standard deviation σ, the standard error is σ/√n (with a finite population correction when the sample is a substantial fraction of the population). Standard error therefore shrinks as sample size increases: estimates from larger samples are more precise.

Central limit theorem

The central limit theorem states that, under broad conditions, the sampling distribution of the (standardised) sample mean tends toward a normal distribution as the sample size grows, regardless of the shape of the population distribution. This result justifies the widespread use of normal-based inference even for non-normal data, given moderately large samples.

Examples

  • Sample mean: exactly normal if the population is normal, with mean μ and standard error σ/√n; approximately normal otherwise by the central limit theorem.
  • Sample proportion: approximately normal with mean p and standard error √(p(1−p)/n) for large samples.
  • Student’s t-distribution: governs the standardised sample mean when σ is estimated from the sample.
  • Chi-squared and F distributions: sampling distributions of variance ratios and related quantities.
