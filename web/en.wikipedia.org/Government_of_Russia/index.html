The Government of the Russian Federation (Russian: Правительство Российской Федерации) is the highest federal body exercising executive power in Russia. It is headed by the Chairman of the Government, commonly called the Prime Minister, and consists of deputy prime ministers and federal ministers. Its powers and procedures are set out in Chapter 6 of the Constitution of Russia, adopted in 1993.

Composition and appointment

The Chairman of the Government is appointed by the President of Russia with the consent of the State Duma, the lower house of the Federal Assembly. If the Duma rejects the president's nominee three times, the president may dissolve the Duma and call new elections. The Chairman then proposes the structure of the federal executive bodies and candidates for deputy chairs and federal ministers (other than the "power" ministries of defence, foreign affairs, interior and emergencies, whose heads are appointed by the president after consultation with the Federation Council).

Powers

The Government drafts and executes the federal budget, conducts unified financial, credit and monetary policy, manages federal property, implements policy in science, culture, education, health and social security, and ensures defence, state security and the rule of law. It issues decrees and orders binding throughout Russia and can resign or be dismissed by the president; the State Duma may also pass a motion of no confidence.
