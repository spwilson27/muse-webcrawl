Pollen

Produced byAnthers of seed plants
ContainsMicrogametophytes (male gametophytes)
Typical size6–100 micrometres
Wall materialSporopollenin

Pollen is a powdery substance produced by most types of flowers of seed plants for the purpose of sexual reproduction. It consists of pollen grains (highly reduced microgametophytes), which produce male gametes (sperm cells).

Structure

Each pollen grain is enclosed in a tough double wall: an inner intine of cellulose and an outer exine made of sporopollenin, one of the most chemically inert biological polymers, which protects the grain and allows it to fossilize. The exine's sculptured patterns are species-specific and are used to identify plants in forensic and geological samples.

Pollination

During pollination, pollen is transferred from the anther to the stigma of a flower — by wind (anemophily), insects (entomophily), birds, bats, or water. On the stigma the grain germinates, growing a pollen tube that delivers two sperm cells to the ovule, enabling double fertilization in flowering plants.

Human relevance

Wind-borne pollen from trees, grasses, and weeds such as ragweed triggers allergic rhinitis (hay fever) in sensitized people; pollen counts are routinely forecast. Bees collect pollen as a protein source, and palynology — the study of pollen — reconstructs past vegetation and climates from sediment cores.
