Sopot

Sopot (German: Zoppot) is a seaside resort city in Pomeranian Voivodeship, northern Poland, on the southern coast of the Baltic Sea. Together with Gdańsk and Gdynia it forms the Tricity metropolitan area. Sopot is known for its long wooden pier, sandy beaches, and spa heritage.

History

A Slavic stronghold existed at the site from the early Middle Ages. Sopot developed as a fishing village under Gdańsk, then became a fashionable spa resort in the 19th century after the physician Jean Georg Haffner established bathing facilities in 1823. Between the world wars it lay in the Free City of Danzig, famous for its casino and resort life. After 1945 it became part of Poland.

Landmarks

The Sopot Pier (Molo), at about 511 metres, is the longest wooden pier in Europe. Other attractions include Monte Cassino Street (ulica Bohaterów Monte Cassino), a lively pedestrian thoroughfare; the crooked little house (Krzywy Domek); the Grand Hotel; the Forest Opera (Opera Leśna), an open-air amphitheatre that has hosted the Sopot International Song Festival; and the historic lighthouse.
