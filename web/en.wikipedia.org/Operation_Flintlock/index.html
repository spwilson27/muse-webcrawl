Operation Flintlock

Operation Flintlock
Date31 January – 24 February 1944
LocationMarshall Islands, central Pacific
ObjectivesKwajalein, Roi-Namur, Eniwetok atolls
CommandersAdm. Raymond A. Spruance; Adm. Chester W. Nimitz (theatre)
ResultAmerican victory; Marshall Islands secured as a base for the Marianas campaign

Operation Flintlock was the code name for the capture of the Marshall Islands by United States forces in January and February 1944, a central part of Admiral Chester W. Nimitz's island-hopping drive across the Central Pacific during the Second World War. The operation seized Kwajalein Atoll — the largest coral atoll in the world — and was followed by the capture of Eniwetok (Operation Catchpole) and the devastating carrier air raid on the Japanese anchorage at Truk (Operation Hailstone).

Background

After the costly victory at Tarawa in the Gilbert Islands (November 1943), the United States adopted refined amphibious techniques — heavier preliminary bombardment, underwater demolition teams, better ship-to-shore logistics — and leapfrogged the heavily defended Marshalls outposts of Jaluit, Wotje, Maloelap and Mili, striking instead at Kwajalein in the centre of the archipelago, where Japan expected no attack so soon. Vice Admiral Raymond A. Spruance's Fifth Fleet, including Rear Admiral Marc Mitscher's fast carrier task force (TF 58), provided overwhelming sea and air power.

Battle of Kwajalein

Landings began on 31 January 1944. The 4th Marine Division assaulted the twin islands of Roi and Namur at the atoll's northern end, securing them by 2 February, while the Army's 7th Infantry Division landed on Kwajalein Island in the south, which fell by 4 February after fighting through dense Japanese bunkers. Total American dead numbered about 370, against some 8,000 Japanese defenders killed — a markedly better exchange than at Tarawa, validating the new techniques.

Eniwetok and Truk

The success at Kwajalein allowed the timetable to be advanced: Marine and Army units captured Eniwetok Atoll from 17 to 23 February 1944. Simultaneously, TF 58's Operation Hailstone (17–18 February) destroyed hundreds of Japanese aircraft and dozens of ships at Truk, Japan's principal central Pacific base, which was thereafter bypassed and neutralised, as were the remaining Japanese garrisons in the Marshalls.

Consequences

The Marshalls gave the U.S. Navy fleet anchorages and air bases within range of the Marianas, captured in the summer of 1944, and demonstrated the maturing American model of carrier-supported amphibious assault that carried the Central Pacific offensive to Iwo Jima and Okinawa.
