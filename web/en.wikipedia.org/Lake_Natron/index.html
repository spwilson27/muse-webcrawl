Lake Natron

Lake Natron is a salt and soda lake in Arusha Region in northern Tanzania. It is in the Gregory Rift, the eastern branch of the East African Rift. Its shallow, alkaline waters are fed by hot springs, rivers including the Southern Ewaso Ng'iro, and rainfall.

Characteristics

The lake is less than three metres deep and varies in width with water level. High evaporation leaves concentrations of natron — sodium carbonate decahydrate — and trona. The alkalinity can reach pH 10.5 to 12, and water temperatures can exceed 40 °C, sometimes reaching 60 °C. Red accessory pigments of salt-loving microorganisms colour parts of the lake red and orange.

Ecology

The lake is the most important breeding site in East Africa for lesser flamingos, with up to 2.5 million birds; flamingo breeding depends on the availability of the cyanobacterium Spirulina, which grows in alkaline water. The lake also supports endemic alkaline tilapia species, including Alcolapia alcalica, Alcolapia grahami, Alcolapia latilabris and Alcolapia ndalalani.

Threats and conservation

Proposed soda-ash extraction, hydroelectric development on the Ewaso Ng'iro, and increased siltation have been identified as threats to the flamingo breeding habitat. The lake is a Ramsar site designated in 2001.

Ol Doinyo Lengai

Nearby Ol Doinyo Lengai is an active volcano whose natrocarbonatite lavas contribute minerals to the lake basin.
