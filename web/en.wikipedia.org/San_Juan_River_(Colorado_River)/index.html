San Juan River

SourceSan Juan Mountains, Colorado
MouthColorado River at Lake Powell, Utah
Length383 mi (616 km)
Basin size23,000 sq mi (60,000 km²)
Dischargeavg. 1,500 cu ft/s (42 m³/s)

The San Juan River is a major tributary of the Colorado River in the southwestern United States, flowing 383 miles (616 km) from the San Juan Mountains of southern Colorado through northern New Mexico and the Four Corners region into Utah, where it joins the Colorado at Lake Powell. Its drainage basin covers about 23,000 square miles across Colorado, New Mexico, Arizona and Utah.

Course

The river rises near Wolf Creek Pass and flows south through Pagosa Springs, then west into New Mexico, where it is impounded by Navajo Dam (completed 1962). Below Farmington it receives the Animas River, crosses the northern Navajo Nation, and enters Utah near Mexican Hat. West of Bluff it cuts the spectacular entrenched meanders known as the Goosenecks, preserved in Goosenecks State Park, before emptying into Lake Powell near the Glen Canyon National Recreation Area.

History and use

The basin was home to Ancestral Puebloan communities whose ruins survive at Aztec and Salmon. The Domínguez–Escalante expedition crossed the river in 1776. Today the San Juan supplies irrigation, municipal water for Farmington, and the Navajo Agricultural Products Industry near Shiprock. The tailwater below Navajo Dam is a renowned trout fishery, while the lower river is popular for rafting past sandstone canyons and archaeological sites.

Geology

The river drains the northwestern San Juan Basin, a structural depression rich in oil, natural gas and coal, and incises the uplifted Colorado Plateau, exposing Permian and Pennsylvanian strata in its gorges.
