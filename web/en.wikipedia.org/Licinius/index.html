Licinius, Roman emperor in the East, rival of Constantine the Great.

Licinius (Gaius Valerius Licinianus Licinius; c. 265 – spring 325) was a Roman emperor from 308 to 324. Of humble Dacian peasant origins, he rose through the army as a close comrade of the emperor Galerius, who appointed him Augustus of the West in 308; in practice he ruled the Balkans and later, after defeating Maximinus Daia at the Battle of Tzirallum in 313, the whole eastern half of the empire.

Edict of Milan

In February 313 Licinius met Constantine I at Milan, married Constantine's half-sister Constantia, and agreed the policy known as the Edict of Milan, which granted religious toleration throughout the empire and restored confiscated Christian property. Licinius promulgated it in the East in June 313.

Wars with Constantine

The alliance between the two Augusti repeatedly collapsed into civil war. Constantine defeated Licinius at Cibalae (314) and Mardia, taking the Balkan provinces; a tense peace followed, but in 324 Constantine attacked again, winning at Adrianople, the Hellespont and Chrysopolis. Licinius surrendered on terms at Nicomedia and abdicated on 19 September 324. Despite Constantine's promise and Constantia's intercession, Licinius was executed in 325 on a charge of treason, leaving Constantine sole emperor.

Religious policy

Later Christian sources accused Licinius of turning against Christians in the 320s, purging them from his court and army; the extent of this "persecution" is debated, but it served to justify Constantine's final war against him.
