Balsamic vinegar

Balsamic vinegar
Type: Condiment
Origin: Modena and Reggio Emilia, Italy
Main ingredient: Cooked grape must

Balsamic vinegar (Italian: aceto balsamico) is a dark, concentrated, intensely flavoured vinegar originating in Italy. Unlike wine vinegar, it is made from grape must — freshly crushed grape juice including skins and seeds — that is cooked down and aged.

Traditional balsamic vinegar

Aceto Balsamico Tradizionale is produced only in Modena and Reggio Emilia under protected designation of origin (DOP) rules. The cooked must ferments and ages for a minimum of 12 years, passing through a series of barrels of different woods (oak, chestnut, cherry, juniper and others), becoming thick, glossy and complex. Some is aged 25 years or more and is used sparingly, even sipped as a digestif.

Balsamic Vinegar of Modena IGP

The much more common commercial product is made from grape must blended with wine vinegar and sometimes caramel colouring, aged for a minimum of two months. It is widely used in salad dressings, marinades and glazes.

Uses

Balsamic vinegar pairs classically with strawberries, Parmesan cheese, tomatoes and good olive oil, and is reduced to a glaze for meats and vegetables.
