The 14th Dalai Lama (born Lhamo Thondup, 6 July 1935), known as Tenzin Gyatso, is the current Dalai Lama, the spiritual leader of Tibetan Buddhism and, until 2011, the political leader of the Tibetan government-in-exile. He is regarded by followers of the Gelug school as the reincarnation of his predecessor, the 13th Dalai Lama, and as an emanation of Avalokiteśvara, the bodhisattva of compassion.

Early life and recognition

Lhamo Thondup was born in the village of Taktser in the Amdo region of northeastern Tibet (now Qinghai province, China) to a farming family of Tibetan ethnicity. Following the death of the 13th Dalai Lama in 1933, a search party sent by the Tibetan government identified the two-year-old boy as the new incarnation after he reportedly recognised possessions belonging to his predecessor. He was renamed Tenzin Gyatso and enthroned in Lhasa in 1940.

Assumption of political power and exile

In November 1950, at the age of fifteen, he assumed full political power as head of state of Tibet following the People's Republic of China's incorporation of Tibet. After the failed Tibetan uprising of 1959, during which Chinese forces suppressed protests in Lhasa, the Dalai Lama fled to India, crossing the Himalayas on foot. He settled in Dharamshala, Himachal Pradesh, where he established the Central Tibetan Administration, often described as the Tibetan government-in-exile.

International advocacy

From exile, the Dalai Lama pursued international support for Tibetan autonomy, proposing a "Middle Way Approach" that seeks genuine autonomy for Tibet within the framework of the People's Republic of China rather than full independence. In 1989 he was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize for his non-violent struggle for the liberation of Tibet. He has authored numerous books on Buddhism, ethics, and the intersection of science and spirituality, and has engaged in dialogues with scientists through the Mind and Life Institute.

Retirement from politics

In 2011 he formally devolved his political authority to an elected leadership, ending the centuries-old tradition of the Dalai Lamas serving as both spiritual and political heads of Tibet. He has stated that the institution of the Dalai Lama may or may not continue after him, and that any successor would be born in the "free world" if the institution persists.
