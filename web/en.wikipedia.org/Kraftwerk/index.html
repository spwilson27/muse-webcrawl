Kraftwerk (German for "power plant") are a German band formed in Düsseldorf in 1970 by Ralf Hütter and Florian Schneider. Widely considered the pioneers of electronic music, they developed a minimalist, machine-like style — performed on synthesizers, drum machines and custom-built instruments — that profoundly influenced synth-pop, hip hop, techno and modern dance music.

Early years

Hütter and Schneider met at the Düsseldorf Conservatory and recorded with the group Organisation before founding Kraftwerk. Their first albums mixed experimental rock with electronic textures. The breakthrough came with Autobahn (1974), whose 22-minute title track simulated a motorway journey and reached the top five in the United States.

Classic period

Working from their Kling Klang Studio, Kraftwerk released a sequence of concept albums: Radio-Activity (1975), Trans-Europe Express (1977), The Man-Machine (1978, including "The Robots" and "The Model", a UK number-one single in 1982) and Computer World (1981). Their robotic stage personas and multilingual lyrics explored themes of transport, communication and technology. Afrika Bambaataa's sampling of "Trans-Europe Express" on "Planet Rock" (1982) carried their sound into hip hop.

Later career and legacy

Later releases included Electric Café (1986) and Tour de France Soundtracks (2003). Florian Schneider left in 2008 and died in 2020. Under Hütter, Kraftwerk continue to tour with elaborate 3-D concert productions of their catalogue. Their influence spans artists from David Bowie and Depeche Mode to Daft Punk and the Detroit techno pioneers.
