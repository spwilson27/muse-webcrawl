Kingston is the capital and largest city of Jamaica, located on the south-eastern coast of the island. It faces a natural harbour protected by the Palisadoes, a long sand spit that connects the town of Port Royal and the Norman Manley International Airport to the rest of the island.

History

Kingston was founded in July 1692 as a refuge for survivors of the earthquake that destroyed nearby Port Royal. It grew as a centre for trade in agricultural produce and became the capital in 1872, replacing Spanish Town. The city was badly damaged by an earthquake in 1907 and rebuilt with wide streets and concrete buildings.

Geography and districts

The city stretches between the Blue Mountains to the north and one of the world's largest natural harbours to the south. Downtown Kingston holds the historic commercial core, while New Kingston, developed from the 1960s, is the main financial district. Neighbourhoods such as Trench Town are famous in music history.

Culture

Kingston is the birthplace of reggae, ska, rocksteady and dancehall. The Bob Marley Museum, the National Gallery and the Institute of Jamaica are major cultural institutions, and the city's reggae culture is recognised by UNESCO.

Economy and transport

The Port of Kingston is a major Caribbean transshipment hub. The city is served by Norman Manley International Airport and Tinson Pen Aerodrome, and by a network of buses and route taxis.
