Beck Depression Inventory

The Beck Depression Inventory (BDI) is a 21-item, self-report rating inventory that measures the severity of depression in adolescents and adults. It was created by American psychiatrist Aaron T. Beck, first published in 1961, and revised as the BDI-IA (1978) and the BDI-II (1996). It is one of the most widely used psychometric instruments for measuring depression severity in both clinical practice and research.

Structure and scoring

Each of the 21 items covers a symptom or attitude associated with depression — such as sadness, pessimism, loss of pleasure, self-criticism, sleep and appetite changes, and suicidal thoughts — and is rated on a four-point scale from 0 to 3. Total scores range from 0 to 63. For the BDI-II, commonly used cut-offs classify scores of 0–13 as minimal, 14–19 as mild, 20–28 as moderate and 29–63 as severe depression. The questionnaire usually takes about ten minutes to complete.

Development and versions

Beck developed the inventory from clinical observations of depressed patients' attitudes and symptoms, an approach that reflected his emerging cognitive theory of depression. The BDI-II was revised to align more closely with the diagnostic criteria of the DSM-IV, replacing some items and extending the time frame to two weeks. A short form, the BDI Fast Screen, contains seven items for use in medical settings.

Use and limitations

The BDI is used to screen for depression, monitor treatment response and conduct research. It is a measure of severity rather than a diagnostic instrument by itself, and like other self-report scales it can be influenced by physical illness and reporting style. It is published by Pearson and is a copyrighted, commercial instrument.
