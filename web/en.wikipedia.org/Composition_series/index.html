Composition series

A composition series refines a group into simple factors

In abstract algebra, a composition series of a group (or, more generally, a module) is a maximal chain of subgroups (submodules) in which each step is a normal subgroup (submodule) of the next and each quotient — called a composition factor — is simple.

Definition

For a group G, a composition series is a finite sequence of subgroups 1 = G0 ◀ G1 ◀ ... ◀ Gn = G such that each Gi is a normal subgroup of Gi+1 and the quotient group Gi+1 / Gi is a simple group. A group may have no composition series if it is infinite in certain ways, but every finite group has one.

Jordan–Hölder theorem

The Jordan–Hölder theorem states that any two composition series of the same group have the same length and the same composition factors, up to permutation and isomorphism. The analogous result holds for modules of finite length. This theorem, due to Camille Jordan and Otto Hölder, makes composition factors a fundamental invariant, analogous to prime factorization of integers.

Examples

The cyclic group of order 6 has composition factors of orders 2 and 3 (cyclic groups of prime order). A finite abelian group has all composition factors cyclic of prime order, with each prime appearing according to its multiplicity in the group order. In Galois theory, a polynomial is solvable by radicals exactly when its Galois group has a composition series with abelian factors (a solvable group).
