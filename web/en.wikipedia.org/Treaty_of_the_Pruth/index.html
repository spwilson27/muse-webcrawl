Treaty of the Pruth

Signed23 July 1711
LocationNear the Pruth River, Moldavia
PartiesOttoman Empire; Tsardom of Russia

The Treaty of the Pruth was signed on 23 July 1711, ending the Russo-Turkish War of 1710–1711, a theatre of the Great Northern War. It was concluded after the Ottoman army under Grand Vizier Baltacı Mehmed Pasha surrounded Peter the Great's outnumbered Russian army on the Pruth River during its Moldavian campaign.

Background

After his defeat at Poltava in 1709, Charles XII of Sweden had taken refuge in Ottoman territory and lobbied the sultan to declare war on Russia. Peter invaded Moldavia in 1711 expecting support from the Balkan Christians and supplies from the Moldavian ruler Dimitrie Cantemir, but the Russian force was trapped and short of food.

Terms

Russia returned Azov to the Ottomans, demolished the fortress of Taganrog and other strongholds, withdrew from Polish affairs, and granted Charles XII safe passage back to Sweden. The surprisingly lenient terms — often attributed to the vizier's caution, and in legend to bribery or the intervention of Catherine, Peter's wife — allowed the Russian army to escape destruction and were controversial at the Ottoman court.
