Stationary process

In mathematics and statistics, a stationary process (or a strict(ly) stationary process) is a stochastic process whose unconditional joint probability distribution does not change when shifted in time. Consequently, parameters such as mean and variance also do not change over time. A stationary process contrasts with a non-stationary process, for example a process with a trend or seasonal component.

Weak stationarity

A weaker property widely used in applications is wide-sense stationarity (WSS), which requires only that the mean function and the autocovariance function do not vary with time. Any strictly stationary process with finite second moments is also wide-sense stationary, but not conversely. White noise is the simplest example of a stationary process; a random walk, by contrast, is non-stationary because its variance grows with time.

Applications

Stationarity is a central assumption in time series analysis and signal processing: stationary processes are amenable to spectral analysis, and the Wiener–Khinchin theorem relates their autocorrelation function to their power spectral density. In ergodic theory, stationary sequences are studied via the Birkhoff ergodic theorem, which underlies many statistical estimation procedures.
