Rockwell scale

Rockwell hardness test
InventorsHugh M. Rockwell and Stanley P. Rockwell
Patented1919
Common scalesHRA, HRB, HRC

The Rockwell scale is a hardness scale based on indentation hardness of a material. The Rockwell test measures the depth of penetration of an indenter under a large load (major load) compared to the penetration made by a preload (minor load).

History

The differential-depth method was patented in 1919 by Hugh M. Rockwell and Stanley P. Rockwell of Connecticut. It quickly became the most common hardness test in industry because it is fast, requires no optical measurement, and leaves only a small indentation.

Scales

Different scales use different indenters and loads. The most common are HRC (diamond cone, 150 kgf, for hard steels), HRB (1/16-inch steel ball, 100 kgf, for softer metals such as aluminium and brass), and HRA (diamond cone, 60 kgf, for very hard materials). Superficial Rockwell scales use lighter loads for thin materials.

Procedure

A minor load seats the indenter, the major load is applied and removed, and the permanent increase in depth is read directly from a dial or digital display as a hardness number. Higher numbers indicate harder material.

Comparison

Rockwell is less precise for very thin or brittle samples than microhardness methods such as the Knoop hardness test or the Vickers hardness test, but its speed makes it dominant in production quality control.
