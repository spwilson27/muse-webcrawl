Knoop hardness test

Knoop hardness test
Developed1939, by Frederick Knoop and colleagues at the U.S. National Bureau of Standards
IndenterElongated pyramidal diamond
SymbolHK

The Knoop hardness test is a microhardness test used particularly for very brittle materials, thin sheets, and small or shallow regions of a part. It was developed in 1939 by Frederick Knoop, R.G. Peters, and Emerson at the National Bureau of Standards (now NIST) in the United States.

Method

An elongated pyramidal diamond indenter with opposite angles of 172.5° and 130° is pressed into a polished surface under a known load, typically from a few grams to a few kilograms. The resulting indentation is long and shallow — about seven times longer than it is wide and about thirty times longer than it is deep. Because the indentation is shallow, the test is suited to thin layers and coatings.

The length of the long diagonal is measured under a microscope and the Knoop hardness number (HK) is calculated from the load divided by the projected area of the indentation.

Comparison with Vickers

Like the Vickers hardness test, Knoop uses a diamond indenter, but its elongated shape allows hardness gradients and thin sections to be measured with less damage and allows indentations to be spaced closer together. It is often compared with the Rockwell scale for metals, though Knoop excels at micro-scale work such as glass, ceramics, enamels, and tooth enamel.

Applications

  • Ceramics and glass
  • Thin films and surface treatments
  • Dental materials
  • Geological and metallurgical specimens
