Brinell scale

The Brinell scale measures indentation hardness of materials by pressing a hardened steel or tungsten carbide ball into the surface under a standard load and measuring the diameter of the resulting impression. It was proposed by Swedish engineer Johan August Brinell in 1900.

Brinell hardness
Proposed: 1900, J. A. Brinell
Indenter: 10 mm ball (typical)
Symbol: HBW / HBS
Standards: ISO 6506, ASTM E10

Method

The Brinell hardness number (BHN) is the load divided by the surface area of the indentation. Typical loads range from 500 to 3,000 kgf. The test suits materials with coarse or inhomogeneous grain, such as cast iron and forgings, because the large indentation averages local variations, but it is less suitable for very hard materials or thin sheets where Vickers is preferred.

Values

Soft woods are below 10 HB, pure aluminium around 15, copper about 35, mild steel about 120, hardened tool steel 600 or more. Conversions to Rockwell and Vickers and to tensile strength are approximate. See Hardness.
