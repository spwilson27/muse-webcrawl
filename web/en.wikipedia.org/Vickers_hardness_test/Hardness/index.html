Hardness

In materials science, hardness is a measure of resistance to localized plastic deformation, such as indentation or scratching. It is not a fundamental physical property but a composite response related to strength, ductility and wear resistance.

Hardness
Tests: Vickers, Brinell, Rockwell, Mohs
Unit examples: HV, HB, HRC
Related: Strength, toughness

Measurement

Indentation hardness tests press a hard indenter into a material: Brinell uses a steel or carbide ball, Vickers a diamond pyramid, Rockwell measures depth under load. Scratch hardness is exemplified by the Mohs scale for minerals, from talc (1) to diamond (10). Rebound hardness measures the bounce of a dropped object.

Concepts

Hardness correlates with tensile strength in many metals and with wear resistance, but hard materials can be brittle. Work hardening, heat treatment, alloying and grain size all affect hardness. See Brinell scale.
