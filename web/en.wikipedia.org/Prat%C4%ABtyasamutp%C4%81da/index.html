Pratītyasamutpāda

Pratītyasamutpāda (Sanskrit: प्रतीत्यसमुत्पाद; Pali: paṭiccasamuppāda), commonly translated as dependent origination or dependent arising, is a key doctrine in Buddhism shared by all schools. It states that all dharmas (phenomena) arise in dependence on other dharmas: "if this exists, that exists; if this ceases to exist, that also ceases to exist."

The twelve nidānas

The doctrine is commonly presented as a chain of twelve links (nidānas): ignorance, volitional formations, consciousness, name-and-form, the six sense bases, contact, feeling, craving, clinging, becoming, birth, and old age and death. The chain explains how suffering arises through conditioned processes and how it can cease when ignorance is dispelled.

Interpretations

The principle of dependent origination is closely associated with the doctrines of emptiness (śūnyatā) and no-self (anātman). In Mahayana Buddhism, especially in the works of Nagarjuna, pratītyasamutpāda is identified with emptiness itself: because things arise dependently, they lack intrinsic, independent existence.
