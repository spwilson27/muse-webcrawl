SGP4 (Simplified General Perturbations 4) is a mathematical model used to calculate the position and velocity of Earth-orbiting satellites from orbital element data. It is the standard propagator used with the two-line element sets (TLEs) published for catalogued objects in Earth orbit.

Description

The model accounts for the major perturbations on a satellite's orbit, including the effects of Earth's oblateness (zonal harmonics), atmospheric drag through a simple drag parameter related to the TLE's B* term, and third-body influences in its deep-space companion version, SDP4, which applies to objects with orbital periods of 225 minutes or more. Given a TLE epoch and a target time, SGP4 produces the satellite's state vector, typically in the TEME coordinate frame.

History

The SGP family of models was developed by the United States Air Force's space surveillance community from the 1960s onward; SGP4 was described in the widely used 1980 report by Hoots and Roehrich, "Models for Propagation of NORAD Element Sets". The model and its reference implementation are distributed with the public satellite catalogue, and reimplementations exist in many programming languages. Accuracy is typically on the order of a kilometre at the TLE epoch, degrading over days to weeks.
