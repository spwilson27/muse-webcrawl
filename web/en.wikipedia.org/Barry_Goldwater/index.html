Barry Morris Goldwater (January 2, 1909 – May 29, 1998) was an American politician and United States Air Force officer who was a five-term U.S. senator from Arizona (1953–1965, 1969–1987) and the Republican Party nominee for president of the United States in 1964.

Goldwater is widely credited with having sparked the resurgence of the American conservative political movement in the 1960s; he is often called "Mr. Conservative." His 1960 book The Conscience of a Conservative became a foundational text of modern American conservatism.

Early life and military service

Goldwater was born in Phoenix, in what was then the Arizona Territory, into a family that ran Goldwater's Department Store. He attended the University of Arizona for a year before leaving to work in the family business after his father's death. During World War II he served in the U.S. Army Air Forces, flying supply missions over the Himalayas ("the Hump") to China; he remained in the Air Force Reserve after the war and retired as a major general.

Senate career

After serving on the Phoenix City Council, Goldwater defeated incumbent Ernest McFarland in 1952 to enter the Senate. A staunch anti-communist and critic of organized labor and the welfare state, he became the standard-bearer of the Republican right.

1964 presidential campaign

Goldwater won the Republican nomination in 1964 after a hard-fought convention, declaring in his acceptance speech that "extremism in the defense of liberty is no vice" and "moderation in the pursuit of justice is no virtue." Facing President Lyndon B. Johnson in the aftermath of the Kennedy assassination, he lost in a landslide, carrying only his home state of Arizona and five states in the Deep South. His campaign slogan was "In your heart, you know he's right." The Johnson campaign's famous "Daisy" advertisement implied Goldwater might risk nuclear war.

Later years and legacy

Goldwater returned to the Senate in 1969 and served until 1987, chairing the Armed Services Committee and the Intelligence Committee. In 1974 he played a key role in informing President Richard Nixon that Republican support had collapsed during Watergate. In his later years he became known for libertarian-leaning social views, supporting abortion rights and gay rights. He died in 1998 of complications from Alzheimer's disease. Goldwater's 1964 campaign is credited with laying the groundwork for Ronald Reagan's victory in 1980.
