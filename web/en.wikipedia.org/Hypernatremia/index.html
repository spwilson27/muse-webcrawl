DefinitionSerum sodium > 145 mmol/L

Hypernatremia is a high concentration of sodium in the blood, defined as a serum sodium level above 145 mmol/L. It almost always reflects a deficit of total body water relative to sodium rather than a primary excess of sodium. Early symptoms may include a strong feeling of thirst, weakness, nausea, and loss of appetite; severe cases can produce confusion, muscle twitching, seizures, coma, and intracranial bleeding.

Causes

Hypernatremia develops when water intake is inadequate — for example in infants, older adults, or people with impaired consciousness or thirst — or when water losses are excessive: through insensible losses and fever, osmotic diuresis in poorly controlled diabetes mellitus, profuse diarrhoea, or urinary concentrating defects such as central or nephrogenic diabetes insipidus. Less commonly, it results from large sodium loads, such as ingestion of concentrated salt solutions or hypertonic intravenous fluids.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis combines the history with serum and urine osmolality and urine sodium measurements, which distinguish hypovolemic, euvolemic, and hypervolemic forms. Treatment centres on replacing the free-water deficit, usually with oral water or intravenous dextrose solutions, while correcting the underlying cause. Chronic hypernatremia must be corrected slowly — generally no faster than about 0.5 mmol/L per hour — because rapid lowering of serum sodium can cause cerebral oedema as brain cells that have adapted to the hypertonic state take up water.
