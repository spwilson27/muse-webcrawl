Peace

Peace is a state of harmony and the absence of hostility, violence and war. In a societal sense, peace means a lack of conflict and freedom from fear of violence between individuals and groups. Peace researcher Johan Galtung distinguished negative peace — the mere absence of violence — from positive peace, which includes the presence of justice, equality and social structures that resolve conflicts constructively.

History

Throughout history, leaders have used peace-making and diplomacy to establish truces, alliances and treaties, from the ancient Egyptian–Hittite peace treaty of c. 1259 BC to the Peace of Westphalia (1648), the Congress of Vienna (1815) and the treaties following the two world wars. The 20th century saw the creation of the League of Nations and then the United Nations to prevent war through collective security and international law.

Theories of peace

Theories about how peace is achieved include the democratic peace theory, which holds that democracies rarely fight one another; liberal theories emphasising trade and international institutions; balance-of-power theories; and disarmament and peace-movement activism. Philosophers from Kant, in his essay Perpetual Peace (1795), to modern peace and conflict studies have analysed the conditions under which lasting peace emerges.

Peace movements and symbols

Organised peace movements have campaigned against particular wars or weapons, from 19th-century peace societies through anti-nuclear campaigns to protests against the Iraq War. Common symbols of peace include the dove with an olive branch, the peace sign (originally designed for the British nuclear disarmament movement in 1958), and the white poppy. The Nobel Peace Prize is awarded annually to persons or organisations advancing peace.

Inner peace

In religious and psychological contexts, peace also denotes an inner state of calm and serenity. Many traditions — including Buddhism, Christianity, Hinduism and Islam — cultivate inner peace through prayer, meditation and ethical living, regarding it as a foundation for peace in the wider world.
