Waterloo is a city in the Canadian province of Ontario, one of three cities in the Regional Municipality of Waterloo (formerly Waterloo County). It is adjacent to the larger city of Kitchener, with which it forms the twin cities of "Kitchener–Waterloo" or "KW". Waterloo is known for its two universities and its concentration of technology and insurance companies, forming part of the Toronto–Waterloo Corridor technology cluster.

History

The land, part of the Haldimand Tract along the Grand River, was settled in the early 1800s largely by Mennonite farmers from Pennsylvania, followed by German settlers; the area's German heritage is still marked by the nearby Kitchener–Waterloo Oktoberfest, the largest outside Germany. Waterloo was incorporated as a village in 1857, a town in 1876 and a city in 1948. Its early economy featured brewing, distilling (Seagram's distillery operated here for over a century) and insurance — the city remains a major insurance centre, home to the head office of Manulife's Canadian division and Sun Life operations.

Education and technology

The University of Waterloo (founded 1957) is internationally known for mathematics, computer science and engineering and for the world's largest co-operative education programme; Wilfrid Laurier University is also located in the city. The Perimeter Institute for Theoretical Physics, founded by BlackBerry co-founder Mike Lazaridis, is headquartered in Waterloo. Research In Motion (BlackBerry) was founded in Waterloo in 1984 and grew into a global smartphone maker; although it later contracted, the region's start-up ecosystem — centred on hubs in the former industrial buildings of the twin cities — continues to attract technology firms, and Google maintains a major Canadian engineering office in neighbouring Kitchener.
