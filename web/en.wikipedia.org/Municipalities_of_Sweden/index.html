Municipalities of Sweden

The municipalities of Sweden (Swedish: Sveriges kommuner) are the country's lower-level local government entities. There are 290 municipalities, responsible for a large proportion of public services such as schools, social services, elder care, water supply and waste management.

Organization

Each municipality is governed by an elected municipal assembly (kommunfullmäktige) and an executive board (kommunstyrelse). Municipalities levy income taxes and receive state grants. They are grouped into 21 counties (län) and regions, which handle matters such as healthcare.

History

The modern system grew out of the local government reforms of 1862, which created civil municipalities separate from church parishes. Amalgamation reforms in 1952 and between 1962 and 1974 drastically reduced the number of municipalities from over 2,500 to fewer than 300. Stockholm is the largest, with around a million inhabitants, while some rural municipalities in the north have only a few thousand.
