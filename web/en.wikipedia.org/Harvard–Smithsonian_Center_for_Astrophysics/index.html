The Center for Astrophysics | Harvard & Smithsonian (CfA; previously the Harvard–Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics) is an astrophysics research institute jointly operated by Harvard University and the Smithsonian Institution. Founded in 1973 through the combination of the Harvard College Observatory (founded 1839) and the Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory (founded 1890), it is headquartered at 60 Garden Street in Cambridge, Massachusetts, and employs several hundred scientists, making it one of the largest astrophysics research organizations in the world.

Research

The CfA is organized into research divisions covering atomic and molecular physics, high-energy astrophysics, optical and infrared astronomy, radio and geoastronomy, solar and stellar physics, and theoretical astrophysics. It operates or partners in major facilities including the Fred Lawrence Whipple Observatory in Arizona (home to the MMT Observatory), the Submillimeter Array in Hawaii, the Chandra X-ray Observatory's science center, and observing programs such as Pan-STARRS and the Event Horizon Telescope, whose collaboration produced the first image of a black hole in 2019. The institute hosts the Minor Planet Center of the International Astronomical Union, the clearinghouse for observations of minor planets, comets and outer irregular natural satellites, as well as the Central Bureau for Astronomical Telegrams.

History

The Harvard College Observatory was among the first American observatories; under director Edward Charles Pickering it employed the "Harvard Computers," including Williamina Fleming, Annie Jump Cannon and Henrietta Swan Leavitt, whose work on stellar classification and the period–luminosity relation transformed astronomy. The Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory, founded by Samuel Pierpont Langley, moved from Washington, D.C. to Cambridge in 1955 under Fred Lawrence Whipple; the two institutions merged their operations in 1973 under a single director, George B. Field. In 2018 the organization rebranded publicly as the Center for Astrophysics | Harvard & Smithsonian. Directors have included Irwin Shapiro, Charles Alcock, and Lisa Kewley (from 2022).
