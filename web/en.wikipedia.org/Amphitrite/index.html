In Greek mythology, Amphitrite (Greek: Ἀμφιτρίτη) is a sea goddess and the wife of Poseidon, god of the sea. She is usually counted among the Nereids, the fifty daughters of the sea god Nereus and the Oceanid Doris, though some sources make her an Oceanid herself. As queen of the sea she was often depicted beside Poseidon in processions of marine deities.

Mythology

According to a well-known tale, Amphitrite fled Poseidon's courtship and hid beyond the Pillars of Heracles, but the dolphin Delphinus found her and persuaded her to return and marry the god; in gratitude Poseidon placed the dolphin among the stars. Amphitrite bore Poseidon the merman Triton and the nymph Rhode. She appears in the retinues of the Olympians at weddings and divine assemblies, and was honoured in cult particularly in parts of the Greek world with maritime traditions.

Iconography and legacy

In art Amphitrite is shown riding in a chariot drawn by sea creatures, carrying a trident or a small fish, and accompanied by Nereids and Tritons. Her Roman counterpart was Salacia. The name Amphitrite has been given to ships of several navies, and the main-belt asteroid 29 Amphitrite, discovered in 1854, is named for her.
