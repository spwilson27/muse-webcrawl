Federal Emergency Management Agency

Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA)
Formed: 1 April 1979
Headquarters: Washington, D.C.
Parent department: U.S. Department of Homeland Security
Employees: over 20,000
FEMA personnel coordinate federal assistance to states and communities after disasters.

The Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) is an agency of the United States Department of Homeland Security whose purpose is to coordinate the federal government's response to disasters that overwhelm the resources of local and state authorities. The governor of an affected state must declare a state of emergency and formally request that the President declare a federal disaster before FEMA can respond. Through its ten regional offices, the agency delivers financial assistance to individuals and governments, coordinates search and rescue and temporary housing, and administers the National Flood Insurance Program.

History

Federal disaster assistance in the United States stretches back to an 1803 congressional act aiding a New Hampshire town after a fire, but a single dedicated agency came only in 1979, when President Jimmy Carter created FEMA by executive order, consolidating civil defense and disaster relief functions that had been scattered across several departments. In 2003, following the September 11 attacks, FEMA was absorbed into the new Department of Homeland Security.

Hurricane Katrina and reform

FEMA's response to Hurricane Katrina in 2005 was widely condemned as slow and ineffective; its director, Michael D. Brown, resigned in the aftermath. Congress responded with the Post-Katrina Emergency Management Reform Act of 2006, which restored much of the agency's autonomy within the department, strengthened its preparedness mission, and required its administrator to have demonstrated emergency management experience.

Programmes

FEMA administers disaster relief under the Robert T. Stafford Disaster Relief and Emergency Assistance Act, runs the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP), which provides flood insurance in participating communities, operates the Center for Domestic Preparedness and the Emergency Management Institute for training, and maintains the nation's Integrated Public Alert and Warning System for emergency alerts.
