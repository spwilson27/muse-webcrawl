Buttermilk

Buttermilk is a fermented dairy drink. Traditionally it was the liquid left behind after churning cream into butter; most modern commercial buttermilk is cultured, made by adding lactic acid bacteria to milk.

Types

Traditional buttermilk is thin, tangy and slightly effervescent from churning. Cultured buttermilk is thicker and tart, and powdered buttermilk is sold for baking. In South Asia, drinks such as chaas are spiced, diluted forms of buttermilk made from curd.

Culinary uses

Buttermilk's acidity tenderises meat in marinades, reacts with baking soda to leaven pancakes, biscuits and soda bread, and forms the base of ranch dressing and the batter for Southern fried chicken. It is also drunk plain, especially in summer.
