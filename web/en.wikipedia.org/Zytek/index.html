Zytek is a British motorsport and automotive engineering group founded in 1981, based at Repton in Derbyshire. It grew out of engine-management electronics work and became a full powertrain engineering company, supplying racing engines, control systems and complete chassis programmes, as well as hybrid and electric vehicle technology for road cars.

Motorsport

Zytek engines powered cars in Formula 3000 — where its V8 became the spec engine of the international championship in the 1990s and 2000s — and in sports-prototype racing. In partnership with Japanese manufacturer work, Zytek built and ran its own LMP prototypes at the 24 Hours of Le Mans and in the Le Mans Series, and supplied engines to numerous customer teams. Its hybrid-petrol LMP1 programme was among the early hybrid entries in endurance racing. After a corporate restructuring, the motorsport business continued as Gibson Technology, whose V8 engine became the standard powerplant for the LMP2 class worldwide.

Automotive work

Zytek Automotive developed electric drivetrains and vehicle control systems, including work on electric conversions and hybrid programmes for manufacturers, and an early electric sports-car collaboration. In 2014 Continental AG acquired full ownership of the automotive division, integrating it into its powertrain business, while the racing operations were separated and rebranded.
