Laplace operator

In mathematics, the Laplace operator or Laplacian is a differential operator given by the divergence of the gradient of a scalar function on Euclidean space. It is usually denoted by the symbols ∇·∇, ∇², or Δ. In Cartesian coordinates, the Laplacian of a function f is the sum of the unmixed second partial derivatives of f.

Definition

For a twice-differentiable function f(x1, ..., xn), the Laplacian is Δf = Σ ∂²f/∂xi². Equivalently, Δf = ∇·(∇f): the divergence of the gradient. Functions with vanishing Laplacian (Δf = 0) are called harmonic functions and satisfy Laplace's equation.

Applications

The Laplacian is central to physics and mathematics. It appears in the wave equation, the heat equation, the Schrödinger equation, Poisson's equation for electrostatics and gravitation, and the Helmholtz equation. Physically, Δf at a point measures how much the average value of f over small spheres around the point deviates from f at the point itself—a measure of local curvature or diffusion.

Generalizations

The Laplace–Beltrami operator generalizes the Laplacian to functions on Riemannian manifolds, and the graph Laplacian (defined via the graph's adjacency and degree matrices) plays an analogous role in discrete mathematics, spectral graph theory, and machine learning. The operator is named after Pierre-Simon Laplace, who applied it in celestial mechanics in the 1780s.
