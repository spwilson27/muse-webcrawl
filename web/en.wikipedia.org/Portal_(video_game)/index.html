Portal is a 2007 puzzle-platform game developed and published by Valve. It was released in a bundle, The Orange Box, for Windows, Xbox 360, and PlayStation 3, and has since been ported to other platforms including macOS, Linux, Android, and Nintendo Switch. The game is set in the Aperture Science Enrichment Center, where the player character Chell completes a series of test chambers using the portal gun, supervised by the computer system GLaDOS.

Gameplay and plot

The portal gun fires two linked portals; walking, falling, or throwing objects through one brings them out of the other with momentum preserved — summarised by the developers as "flinging": falling into a floor portal converts vertical speed into horizontal launch. Across nineteen test chambers the game teaches its mechanics incrementally, introducing weighted cubes, energy pellets, and turrets. The story, written by Erik Wolpaw, Marc Laidlaw, and Chet Faliszek, unfolds through GLaDOS's increasingly sinister commentary: her promised reward of cake proves a lure, and Chell escapes behind the walls of the facility before confronting and destroying GLaDOS's personality cores.

Development and reception

Portal grew out of Narbacular Drop, a 2005 student game from DigiPen whose team Valve hired. With a development team of about ten people and a play time of only a few hours, the game was included in The Orange Box partly as an experiment — and became its breakout hit. Critics praised its innovative mechanics, minimalist storytelling, and humour; the Weighted Companion Cube and the end-credits song "Still Alive" became cultural touchstones. It won numerous awards and is routinely cited among the greatest video games of all time. A sequel, Portal 2, followed in 2011.
