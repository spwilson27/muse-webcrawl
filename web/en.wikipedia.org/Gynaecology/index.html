Gynaecology

Gynaecology (or gynecology in American English) is the area of medicine concerned with the health of the female reproductive system. The word derives from the Greek gynē (woman) and -logia (study). Almost all modern gynaecologists are also obstetricians, and the combined specialty is commonly known as obstetrics and gynaecology (OB-GYN).

Scope

Gynaecology deals with diseases and conditions of the uterus, ovaries, fallopian tubes, cervix, vagina and vulva, as well as related aspects of urinary and rectal function. Common areas include menstrual disorders, endometriosis, fibroids, ovarian cysts, pelvic inflammatory disease, cervical screening and the management of menopause. Subspecialties include gynaecological oncology, reproductive medicine and infertility, urogynaecology and maternal–fetal medicine (within obstetrics).

Examination and procedures

The gynaecological examination typically includes a speculum examination and bimanual palpation. Diagnostic procedures include colposcopy, hysteroscopy, laparoscopy, ultrasound imaging and cervical cytology (the Pap test). Surgical procedures range from dilation and curettage to hysterectomy and laparoscopic surgery.

History

The Kahun Gynaecological Papyrus, dating to about 1800 BCE, is among the oldest medical texts dealing with women's health. In the 19th century, J. Marion Sims in the United States and others pioneered surgical techniques, though Sims's work — conducted on enslaved women without anaesthesia — remains deeply controversial. The 20th century brought major advances, including hormonal contraception, in-vitro fertilisation and effective screening for cervical cancer.
