Golden Gate Park

Golden Gate Park is a large urban park in San Francisco, California, United States. Covering 1,017 acres (412 hectares), it stretches roughly three miles (5 km) east to west from Stanyan Street to the Pacific Ocean, making it about 20 percent larger than New York's Central Park. It receives roughly 24 million visitors annually.

History

The park was created in the 1870s on a site of windswept sand dunes known as the "Outside Lands." Engineer William Hammond Hall designed the park, and Scottish gardener John McLaren served as its superintendent from 1890 until his death in 1943, stabilising the dunes with grasses and trees and shaping the park's landscape. The park hosted the California Midwinter International Exposition of 1894 and sheltered thousands of refugees in tent camps after the 1906 earthquake.

Attractions

Major attractions include the Conservatory of Flowers (1879), the de Young Museum, the California Academy of Sciences, the Japanese Tea Garden (the oldest public Japanese garden in the United States), the San Francisco Botanical Garden, Stow Lake, the Dutch Windmill, and a resident herd of American bison. The park hosts events such as the Outside Lands music festival and Hardly Strictly Bluegrass.
