Contract bridge

Contract bridge, or simply bridge, is a trick-taking card game using a standard 52-card deck. In its basic format, it is played by four players in two competing partnerships, with partners sitting opposite each other around a table. Millions of people play bridge worldwide in clubs, tournaments, online and with friends at home, making it one of the world's most popular card games, particularly among seniors. The World Bridge Federation (WBF) is the governing body for international competitive bridge, with numerous other bodies governing it at the regional level.

Overview

Bridge is a member of the family of trick-taking games and is a development of whist, which had become the dominant such game and enjoyed a loyal following for centuries. The game consists of a number of deals, each progressing through four phases. The cards are dealt to the players; then the players call (or bid) in an auction seeking to take the contract, specifying how many tricks the partnership receiving the contract (the declaring side) needs to take to receive points for the deal; the winning bidder's side then declares trump and plays out the hand, with the declarer playing both sides' hands in the dummy variant of the game; scoring follows at the end of the deal.

History

The modern game of bridge is usually attributed to Harold Stirling Vanderbilt and others, who around 1925 introduced the scoring system that made contract bridge decisively more attractive than its predecessor, auction bridge. Bridge subsequently supplanted whist as the internationally favoured card game among many card players, and the game rapidly spread around the world. Ely Culbertson did much to popularise the game in the 1930s, and figures such as Charles Goren became household names through syndicated newspaper columns and books.

Gameplay

Each deal consists of thirteen tricks, and the side that wins the auction must take at least the number of tricks it contracted for, plus the basic six tricks (the "book"). The declarer's partner, the dummy, lays their cards face up on the table after the opening lead, and the declarer plays both hands. Points are awarded for making the contract, with bonuses for bidding and making game and slam contracts, and penalties for failing to make the contract ("going down").
