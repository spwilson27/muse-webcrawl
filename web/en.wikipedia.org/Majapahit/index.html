Majapahit was a Javanese Hindu-Buddhist thalassocratic empire in Southeast Asia, founded in 1293 and centred in East Java. At its height under the prime minister Gajah Mada it was one of the largest empires in Southeast Asian history, exercising influence across much of the Malay Archipelago.

Founding

The empire was founded by Raden Wijaya, who allied with invading Mongol forces from Yuan China to defeat his rival Jayakatwang of Kediri, then turned on the Mongols and drove them from Java. He was crowned in 1293 as Kertarajasa Jayawardhana, with his capital near the Brantas River in what is now the Trowulan area of Mojokerto, East Java.

Golden age

Majapahit reached its apex during the reign of Queen Tribhuwana (1328–1350) and especially her son Hayam Wuruk (1350–1389), whose powerful prime minister Gajah Mada swore the famous Palapa oath not to rest until the whole archipelago was united under Majapahit. The court poem Nagarakretagama (1365) lists dozens of tributary states from Sumatra to New Guinea. The empire grew rich on the spice trade and rice exports, and its ports linked the trade routes of India and China.

Decline and legacy

After Hayam Wuruk's death, a succession war (the Paregreg war, 1404–1406) weakened the empire. The rise of the Muslim trading state of Malacca and the conversion of coastal ports to Islam eroded Majapahit's control; the capital fell to the Demak Sultanate around 1527, and surviving Hindu courtiers retreated to Bali. Today Majapahit is remembered in Indonesia as a symbol of national unity — its red-and-white banner is echoed in the Indonesian flag, and the national motto Bhinneka Tunggal Ika ("Unity in Diversity") comes from the Majapahit-era poem Sutasoma.
