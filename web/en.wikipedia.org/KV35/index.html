KV35

KV35 is the tomb of the Eighteenth Dynasty pharaoh Amenhotep II in the Valley of the Kings in Egypt. Discovered by Victor Loret in 1898, the tomb is famous for the cache of royal mummies hidden in a side chamber by priests during the Twenty-first Dynasty, which preserved the bodies of many of Egypt's most famous kings.

Layout and discovery

The tomb follows the bent-axis plan typical of the early Eighteenth Dynasty, descending through corridors and a well shaft to a pillared burial chamber, where Amenhotep II's quartzite sarcophagus still stands and where the king's mummy was found undisturbed in 1898 — one of the few pharaohs discovered in his own tomb.

The royal cache

In a side chamber Loret found a hidden cache of mummies rewrapped and moved by priests of Amun to protect them from tomb robbers. They included Thutmose IV, Amenhotep III, Merneptah, Seti II, Ramesses IV, Ramesses V and Ramesses VI, together with an as-yet-debated "Elder Lady", proposed by some DNA studies to be Queen Tiye, grandmother of Tutankhamun. The mummies were transported to Cairo, a journey photographed as the great fleet of royal barges on the Nile.
