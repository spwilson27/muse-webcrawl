Mount Kinabalu (Malay: Gunung Kinabalu) is the highest mountain in Borneo and Malaysia, rising to 4,095 metres (13,435 ft) at its summit, Low's Peak. It stands in Kinabalu Park in the state of Sabah on the island of Borneo, and is one of the most prominent peaks in the world by topographic prominence. The mountain and its surroundings are among the world's most important biological sites, with between 5,000 and 6,000 species of plants.

Name and significance

The name is often derived from the Kadazan-Dusun phrase Aki Nabalu, "the revered place of the dead," and the mountain is sacred to the local Kadazan-Dusun people, who traditionally believed the spirits of their ancestors resided on the summit. The first recorded ascent of the summit plateau was made in 1851 by Hugh Low, a British colonial administrator and naturalist, for whom Low's Peak and the precipitous Low's Gully are named; the highest point was reached later in the 19th century.

Biodiversity

Kinabalu Park, designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2000, spans elevations from lowland rainforest to alpine meadow. It harbours an extraordinary concentration of species: thousands of orchids, hundreds of bird species, giant pitcher plants such as Nepenthes rajah, and the world's largest flower, Rafflesia, in nearby areas. Botanists regard it as one of the richest floras on Earth, comparable to the Amazon and Himalaya regions in diversity.

Climbing

Climbing Kinabalu requires a permit and a licensed guide; most climbers take two days, overnighting at Laban Rata before a pre-dawn push to the summit. In June 2015 a magnitude-6.0 earthquake struck the mountain, killing 18 climbers and guides and breaking off one of the iconic Donkey's Ears peaks.
