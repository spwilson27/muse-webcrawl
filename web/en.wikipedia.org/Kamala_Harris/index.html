Kamala Devi Harris (born 20 October 1964) is an American politician and attorney who served as the 49th vice president of the United States from 2021 to 2025 under President Joe Biden. She is the first woman, the first African American, and the first Asian American to hold the office. In 2024 she became the Democratic nominee for president after Biden withdrew from the race, but lost the general election to Donald Trump.

Early life and career

Harris was born in Oakland, California, to Shyamala Gopalan, a Tamil Indian cancer researcher, and Donald Harris, a Jamaican-born economist. Raised in Berkeley and Montreal, she studied political science and economics at Howard University and earned her law degree from UC Hastings. She began her career in the Alameda County District Attorney's office and in 2003 was elected district attorney of San Francisco.

Attorney General and Senator

Elected attorney general of California in 2010 and re-elected in 2014, Harris secured a $25 billion settlement with banks over foreclosure abuses, defended the state's climate laws, and declined to defend Proposition 8. In 2016 she was elected to the U.S. Senate, where she served on the Judiciary and Intelligence committees and gained a national profile for her questioning of Trump administration officials. Her 2019 campaign for the Democratic presidential nomination ended before the primaries, and in August 2020 Biden selected her as his running mate.

Vice presidency

As vice president, Harris frequently cast tie-breaking votes in the evenly divided Senate — eventually setting the record for such votes — and was assigned portfolios including migration from Central America, voting rights, and abortion access following the Dobbs decision. She chaired the National Space Council and represented the administration abroad, including at the Munich Security Conference.

2024 presidential campaign

After Biden ended his re-election bid on 21 July 2024, Harris secured the Democratic nomination within weeks, selected Minnesota governor Tim Walz as her running mate, and raised record sums. She lost the November election to Donald Trump, who won the Electoral College and the popular vote, and as vice president she presided over the certification of the result in January 2025.
