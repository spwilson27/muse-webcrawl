Cambridge, Massachusetts

Cambridge is a city in Middlesex County, Massachusetts, United States, situated directly across the Charles River from Boston. As of the 2020 census its population was about 118,000. Founded in 1630 as "Newe Towne," it was renamed in 1638 after the University of Cambridge in England. Cambridge is world-renowned as a center of education and research, home to Harvard University (founded 1636, the oldest university in the United States) and the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT).

History

Cambridge was founded by Puritan colonists in 1630 and chosen in 1636 as the site of Harvard College. During the American Revolution, George Washington took command of the Continental Army on Cambridge Common in July 1775. The city grew with the arrival of streetcar lines and industry in the 19th century, and annexed surrounding districts. In the 20th century it evolved from a manufacturing center into a hub of academia, publishing, and later biotechnology.

Geography

Cambridge is bordered by Boston to the south across the Charles River, Somerville to the north, and Belmont, Arlington, and Watertown to the west. Its best-known squares include Harvard Square, Central Square, Kendall Square, Porter Square, and Inman Square. Fresh Pond is a notable municipal reservoir and park.

Education

Harvard University and MIT anchor the city's economy and identity. Other institutions include Lesley University, the Longy School of Music, and Cambridge College. Harvard Square serves as a commercial and cultural center around the university.

Economy

Kendall Square has become one of the most concentrated centers of biotechnology and technology companies in the world, hosting firms in life sciences, pharmaceuticals, and software, along with research institutes such as the Broad Institute and the Whitehead Institute.
