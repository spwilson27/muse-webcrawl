Mount Elbert is the highest summit of the Rocky Mountains of North America and the highest point in the U.S. state of Colorado, with an elevation of 14,440 feet (4,401 m). It is the second-highest summit in the contiguous United States after Mount Whitney in California, and the highest of Colorado's 53 "fourteeners" — peaks above 14,000 feet. The mountain lies in the Sawatch Range, about ten miles southwest of Leadville in the San Isabel National Forest.

Name

The peak was named in honour of Samuel Hitt Elbert, governor of the Territory of Colorado in 1873–74, by members of the Hayden Geological Survey, whose party made the first recorded ascent in 1874.

Climbing

Despite its height, Mount Elbert's broad slopes make it one of the more accessible fourteeners: the standard routes, the North (Main) Elbert Trail and the South Elbert Trail, are non-technical hikes in summer, though altitude and fast-changing weather make the climb demanding. Tens of thousands of climbers attempt the summit each year. Nearby rises Mount Massive, Colorado's second-highest peak, whose supporters once piled rocks on its summit in a light-hearted attempt to make it taller than Elbert.

Geology

Mount Elbert is composed largely of Precambrian metamorphic and igneous rocks uplifted during the Laramide orogeny some 70 million years ago, and its present form was sculpted by Pleistocene glaciation.
