Torrens title is a system of land registration in which the state maintains a register of land holdings that serves as conclusive evidence of ownership. It is named after Sir Robert Richard Torrens, premier of South Australia, who modelled it on the registration of merchant ships and secured its enactment in the Real Property Act 1858 (SA). Under Torrens, a transfer of land is effected not by deeds but by registration, and the registered proprietor's title is generally indefeasible — immune to most challenges based on defects in earlier transactions.

Principles

The system is often summarised by three principles: the mirror principle (the register reflects all material facts about the title), the curtain principle (a purchaser need not look behind the register to investigate the history of the title), and the insurance principle (the state guarantees the register and compensates anyone who suffers loss through its errors, funded by an assurance fund). Interests not on the register — notably the rights of persons in actual occupation in some jurisdictions, and certain statutory charges — may "override" the register, a recognised exception to indefeasibility, as is fraud by the registered proprietor.

Adoption

From South Australia the system spread to the other Australian colonies (now states and territories), New Zealand, Singapore, Malaysia (in modified form), Fiji, and Canadian provinces including British Columbia, Alberta, Saskatchewan and Manitoba, as well as limited use in some US states and in Ireland's and England's registered-title systems, which it influenced. Much older "old system" (deeds) land has progressively been converted to Torrens registration.

Comparison with deeds registration

Under a deeds (recording) system, the deed itself is the operative document and the register merely records it, so a buyer must trace a chain of title and bears the risk of defects; title insurance developed in the United States to manage this risk. Torrens registration replaces that inquiry with reliance on the state's register, making conveyancing faster and cheaper.
