Finland, officially the Republic of Finland, is a Nordic country in Northern Europe bordered by Sweden to the west, Norway to the northwest, and Russia to the east, with the Gulf of Bothnia to the west and the Gulf of Finland to the south, opposite Estonia. Finland covers an area of 338,455 square kilometres with a population of about 5.6 million. Helsinki is the capital and largest city.

Finland has a population of primarily ethnic Finns, and its official languages are Finnish and Swedish. The climate varies considerably by latitude; southern Finland has a humid continental climate, while northern Finland, above the Arctic Circle in Lapland, has a subarctic climate.

History

Finland was first settled around 9000 BC after the Last Ice Age. The area that is now Finland came under Swedish rule in the Middle Ages and remained part of Sweden until 1809, when it was ceded to the Russian Empire and became the autonomous Grand Duchy of Finland. Finland declared independence in 1917 during the upheaval of the Russian Revolution, and survived a civil war in 1918, the Winter War and Continuation War against the Soviet Union, and decades of careful neutrality during the Cold War. Finland joined the European Union in 1995 and adopted the euro in 1999; it joined NATO in 2023 following Russia's invasion of Ukraine.

Geography

Finland is known as the "land of a thousand lakes" — it has approximately 168,000 lakes and 179,000 islands. About 75 percent of the country is covered by forest, the highest proportion in Europe. The landscape is mostly flat with low hills, rising to Halti (1,324 m) in the far northwest.

Economy and society

Finland has a highly industrialised, largely free-market economy, with major sectors including electronics (historically led by Nokia), machinery, forestry and chemicals. It consistently ranks near the top of international comparisons of education (PISA), press freedom, and quality of life; since 2018 it has repeatedly been ranked the world's happiest country in the World Happiness Report. Finnish social institutions, the sauna, and the concept of sisu — grit and perseverance — are central to national identity.
