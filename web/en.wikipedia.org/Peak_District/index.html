Peak District

The Peak District is an upland area in central England, at the southern end of the Pennines. It lies mostly in Derbyshire, with parts in Cheshire, Greater Manchester, Staffordshire, West Yorkshire and South Yorkshire. In 1951 it became the first area to be designated a national park in the United Kingdom. An estimated 13 million people visit the park each year.

Geography

The district is traditionally divided into the Dark Peak, the gritstone moorlands of the north, and the White Peak, the limestone plateau of the centre and south with its dales and gorges. The highest point is Kinder Scout, site of the famous Mass Trespass of 1932, a landmark event in the campaign for public access to open country.

History

The area has been settled since the Mesolithic period. It was mined for lead from Roman times onward, and its fast-flowing streams powered early cotton mills of the Industrial Revolution, notably at Cromford and along the Derwent Valley. Towns such as Buxton became fashionable spa resorts in the Georgian and Victorian eras.

Tourism and recreation

The Peak District is popular for walking, climbing, caving and cycling. The Pennine Way long-distance footpath begins at Edale. Attractions include Chatsworth House, Haddon Hall, the show caves of Castleton, and the plague village of Eyam.
