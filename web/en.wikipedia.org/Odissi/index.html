Odissi

Odissi is an ancient Indian classical dance that originated in the temples of Odisha, on the eastern coast of India. It is recognised as one of the eight major forms of Indian classical dance and is distinguished by its fluid upper-body movements, sculptural poses inspired by temple carvings, and the tribhangi posture, in which the body is bent in three places.

An Odissi dancer in the tribhangi posture.

History

Archaeological evidence, including the dance reliefs of the Udayagiri caves, suggests a tradition over two thousand years old. Odissi was performed by maharis (temple dancers) in the Jagannath Temple at Puri and later also by gotipua boy dancers. After a period of decline, the form was reconstructed and codified in the mid-twentieth century by gurus such as Kelucharan Mohapatra.

Repertoire and technique

A traditional recital progresses from the mangalacharan invocation through batu, pallavi, and abhinaya interpretive pieces — often setting verses from Jayadeva's Gita Govinda — to a concluding moksha. Dancers wear silk sarees, silver filigree jewellery, and ankle bells, accompanied by the pakhawaj drum, flute, violin, and manjira cymbals.
