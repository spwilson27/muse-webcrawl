Salute

A military hand salute.

A salute is a gesture or other action used to display respect. Especially associated with the military, the salute most commonly takes the form of raising the right hand to the forehead or cap brim, though forms vary widely between countries and services.

Origins

The hand salute is often traced to customs in which a subordinate demonstrated an empty, weaponless right hand, to knights raising their visors to identify themselves, or to the practice of removing or touching one's hat in deference. By the 18th and 19th centuries the gesture was standardised in European armies.

Forms around the world

British Commonwealth forces traditionally salute with the palm facing forward; United States forces with the palm facing down, a style sometimes attributed to naval custom, where sailors' hands were dirty with tar. The Polish two-finger salute reflects national traditions, while many navies and armies salute only when wearing headgear and render other courtesies when uncovered.

Other salutes

The term also covers gun salutes (a 21-gun salute being the highest national honour), salutes by cannon or colours, and civilian gestures of respect. Distinct and historically loaded gestures, such as the Roman salute and its later fascist adaptations, are treated separately because of their political associations.
