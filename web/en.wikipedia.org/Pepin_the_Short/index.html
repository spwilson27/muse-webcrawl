Pepin the Short

Pepin the Short (French: Pépin le Bref; c. 714 – 24 September 768) was King of the Franks from 751 until his death in 768. He was the first Carolingian monarch and the father of Charlemagne. The son of Charles Martel, Pepin served as Mayor of the Palace before deposing the last Merovingian king, Childeric III, with papal approval.

Reign

Crowned at Soissons in 751 and anointed again by Pope Stephen II in 754, Pepin campaigned against the Lombards in Italy and, by the Donation of Pepin (756), granted the pope territories that formed the core of the Papal States. He also fought in Aquitaine and Septimania, consolidating Frankish power.

Legacy

Pepin strengthened royal authority, reformed the Frankish church and coinage, and laid the foundations for the Carolingian Empire his son Charlemagne would build. His nickname "the Short" refers to his stature in later tradition.
