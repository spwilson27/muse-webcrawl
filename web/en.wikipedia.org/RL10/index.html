RL10

An RL10 engine

The RL10 is a liquid-fuel cryogenic rocket engine built by Aerojet Rocketdyne (formerly Pratt & Whitney Rocketdyne) in the United States, burning liquid hydrogen and liquid oxygen. It was the first hydrogen-fuelled rocket engine to be built in the U.S., and versions of it have powered upper stages for more than half a century.

Design

The RL10 uses an expander cycle: cryogenic hydrogen fuel circulates through tubes in the thrust-chamber wall, absorbing heat and expanding to drive the turbopumps before being injected and burned. The engine is restartable in flight, produces roughly 110 kN of thrust in its current RL10B-2 and RL10C forms, and achieves a specific impulse of about 460 seconds in vacuum, among the highest of operational chemical engines.

Service history

The RL10 first flew in 1963 on the Centaur upper stage. It powered the S-IV stage of the Saturn I, and variants have flown on Atlas, Titan, and Delta rockets. Current versions power the Centaur stage of Atlas V and Vulcan Centaur, the Delta Cryogenic Second Stage, and the Interim Cryogenic Propulsion Stage of the Space Launch System.
