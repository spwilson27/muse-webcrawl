Monte Perdido (in Catalan: Mont Perdut; in French: Mont Perdu; in Aragonese: As Tres Serols) is the third highest mountain in the Pyrenees. The summit of Monte Perdido (3355 m), located in Spain, lies inside the Spanish Ordesa y Monte Perdido National Park, in the province of Huesca, autonomous community of Aragon. The mountain forms part of the Monte Perdido massif, one of the largest and most classic limestone massifs in Europe.

The mountain's name — "lost mountain" — is sometimes explained by its being hidden from view from France by intervening peaks, so that early French explorers could not see it from their side of the range.

Geology and geography

The massif is built of limestone and is famed for its spectacular glacial valleys, including the Ordesa Valley, Añisclo Canyon, Escuaín Gorge, and Pineta Valley, which radiate from the summit area. The area contains some of the deepest canyons in Europe and notable karst features. The north face holds one of the few remaining Pyrenean glaciers, which has been retreating steadily with climate warming.

Protected status

The Spanish side was declared a national park in 1918 (Ordesa Valley) and expanded in 1982 to create the Ordesa y Monte Perdido National Park. Together with the adjacent French Pyrénées National Park, the area was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1997 as the transboundary site "Pyrénées – Mont Perdu," recognized for both its natural and cultural landscapes.

Climbing

The normal route ascends from the Góriz refuge via the Escupidera ("spittoon") couloir, a slope notorious for accidents in icy conditions. The first recorded ascent was made in 1802 by French soldiers Rondo and Laurens, guided by a Spanish shepherd, as part of the exploration promoted by Louis Ramond de Carbonnières, who had promoted the mountain's fame from the French side.
