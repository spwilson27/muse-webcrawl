Callimachus (Polemarch)

Callimachus (died 490 BC) was the polemarch (chief military magistrate) of Athens in 490 BC, best known for his decisive role and death at the Battle of Marathon during the first Persian invasion of Greece.

Marathon

Herodotus relates that the ten Athenian strategoi were divided on whether to fight the landed Persian force, and Callimachus, as polemarch, held the casting vote. Miltiades persuaded him with a speech on freedom versus slavery, and Callimachus voted for battle. By custom the polemarch commanded the right wing, the position of honour.

Death

Callimachus fought bravely and was killed during the pursuit at the Persian ships, along with the general Stesilaus and Aeschylus's brother Cynegeirus.

Legacy

The Stoa Poikile in Athens displayed a famous painting of Marathon in which Callimachus was depicted prominently. A statue base dedicated by him on the Acropolis, and the Nike of Callimachus monument, preserve his name.
