Kermit the Frog is a Muppet character created and performed by Jim Henson. Introduced in 1955, Kermit is the central character and de facto leader of the Muppets, serving as the showrunner and host of The Muppet Show and appearing in numerous films, television series and specials.

History

Kermit first appeared on Henson's early television show Sam and Friends in 1955; the original puppet was made from Henson's mother's green coat and two ping pong balls for eyes. He gained wider fame as a reporter on Sesame Street and as the perpetually harried host of The Muppet Show (1976–1981), whose catchphrase opening cry of "Hi-ho!" and gentle everyman persona made him one of the world's most recognisable puppets.

Character

Kermit is known for his banjo-playing rendition of "Rainbow Connection" from The Muppet Movie (1979), a hit song later inducted into the Library of Congress's National Recording Registry, and for "Bein' Green". His long-running romantic entanglement with Miss Piggy is a running theme of the franchise. After Henson's death in 1990, Steve Whitmire performed Kermit until 2016, when Matt Vogel took over the role.
