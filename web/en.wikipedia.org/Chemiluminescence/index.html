Chemiluminescence

Chemiluminescence is the emission of light as a result of a chemical reaction, in which a reaction product is formed in an electronically excited state and returns to the ground state by emitting a photon, with little or no heat produced.

Examples

The oxidation of luminol, catalysed by iron in blood haemoglobin, produces a blue glow used in forensic science to detect trace blood. Glow sticks use the peroxyoxalate reaction, in which hydrogen peroxide oxidises an oxalate ester and excites a fluorescent dye.

Applications

Chemiluminescent immunoassays are widely used in clinical diagnostics and research because of their sensitivity. Gas-phase chemiluminescence detects nitric oxide and ozone in air monitoring. When the reaction occurs in living organisms it is called bioluminescence.
