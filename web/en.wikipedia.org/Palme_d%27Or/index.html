The Palme d'Or (French for "Golden Palm") is the highest prize awarded at the Cannes Film Festival. It is presented to the director of the best film in the festival's official competition and is widely considered one of the most prestigious awards in the film industry.

History

The festival's top prize was introduced as the Grand Prix in 1946 (a prize shared in the first year among several films). In 1955 the organising committee introduced the Palme d'Or, designed by jeweller Lucienne Lazon and modelled on the palm fronds of Cannes' coat of arms and the Croisette's palms. Between 1964 and 1974 the prize was again called the Grand Prix, after which the Palme d'Or was reinstated.

Notable winners

Winning directors include Federico Fellini (La Dolce Vita), Martin Scorsese (Taxi Driver), Francis Ford Coppola (twice, for The Conversation and Apocalypse Now), Quentin Tarantino (Pulp Fiction), and Bong Joon-ho (Parasite). A handful of directors — among them Alf Sjöberg, the Dardenne brothers, Ken Loach, and Michael Haneke — have won the award twice.
