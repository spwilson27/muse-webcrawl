Dersu Uzala

Dersu Uzala (c. 1849–1908) was a Nanai trapper and hunter from the Russian Far East, best known through the memoir of the explorer Vladimir Arsenyev, who travelled with him as a guide during expeditions in the Sikhote-Alin mountains between 1902 and 1907. Arsenyev's 1923 book Dersu Uzala describes their journeys and the hunter's extraordinary knowledge of the taiga.

Life

Dersu was a member of the Goldi (Nanai) people, an indigenous Tungusic group of the Amur and Ussuri region. After his family died in a smallpox epidemic, he lived a solitary life in the wilderness. He became famed for his tracking skills, marksmanship and animist respect for nature, addressing tigers and other beings as "people". In 1907 Arsenyev invited the ageing, partially blinded Dersu to live with him in Khabarovsk, but he could not adapt to city life; he returned to the forest and was murdered in 1908, reportedly for his rifle.

Legacy

The story was filmed twice: in 1961 by Agasi Babayan and, most famously, in 1975 by Akira Kurosawa, whose Soviet-Japanese production won the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film.
