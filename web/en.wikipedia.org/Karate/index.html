Karate (空手, "empty hand") is a martial art developed in the Ryukyu Islands, in what is now Okinawa, Japan. It arose from the indigenous fighting methods of the Ryukyuan people, influenced by Chinese martial arts brought through trade and cultural exchange. Karate emphasises striking techniques — punching, kicking, knee and elbow strikes, and open-hand techniques such as the knife-hand — along with blocking and, in some styles, throws and joint locks.

History

Under weapons bans imposed first by King Shō Shin and later by the Satsuma domain, unarmed fighting arts developed in the Ryukyu Kingdom, centred on the towns of Shuri, Naha and Tomari, whose names survive in the styles Shuri-te, Naha-te and Tomari-te. In the early 20th century, masters such as Anko Itosu introduced karate into Okinawan schools, and Gichin Funakoshi brought it to mainland Japan in 1922. After the Second World War it spread worldwide through returning servicemen, films and international competition.

Practice

Training is traditionally divided into three parts: kihon (basics), kata (pre-arranged forms) and kumite (sparring). Practitioners wear a white uniform (karategi) with a coloured belt indicating rank, and train in a dojo. Major styles (ryū) include Shotokan, Goju-ryu, Shito-ryu and Wado-ryu, whose founders standardised the art in the 1930s. The governing ethos is summarised in principles such as Funakoshi's twenty precepts (Niju kun) and the dojo kun.

Sport karate

Competitive karate includes kata performance and point-based kumite, organised internationally by the World Karate Federation. Karate made its Olympic debut at the Tokyo 2020 Games, though it was not retained for Paris 2024. Full-contact offshoots such as Kyokushin, founded by Mas Oyama, conduct knockdown competition.
