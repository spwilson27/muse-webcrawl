Smoky quartz

Smoky quartz is a grey, translucent variety of quartz whose colour ranges from almost completely transparent to an almost-opaque brownish-grey or black. The smoky colour results from free silicon formed by natural irradiation acting on trace amounts of aluminium in the crystal structure.

Varieties and occurrence

Very dark brown to black opaque specimens are called morion, while the reddish-brown to golden-brown Scottish variety from the Cairngorm Mountains is known as cairngorm. Smoky quartz is common worldwide, occurring in pegmatites and alpine veins in Brazil, Switzerland, Scotland, Ukraine, Madagascar, and the Pikes Peak region of Colorado.

Uses

Smoky quartz has long been used in jewellery and ornament; it is the national gem of Scotland, where it was set in brooches, kilt pins, and the handles of sgian-dubhs. Large faceted stones, beads, and carved objects are common. Because irradiated clear quartz can be artificially darkened, much commercial material is treated, though the product is chemically identical to natural smoky quartz.
