Halley Research Station

Halley Research Station is a British Antarctic research facility run by the British Antarctic Survey (BAS), situated on the floating Brunt Ice Shelf in Coats Land, Antarctica, overlooking the Weddell Sea. The station, named after the astronomer Edmond Halley, was established in 1956 for the International Geophysical Year. Six successive stations have borne the name; the current one, Halley VI, opened in 2013.

Research

Halley's long series of meteorological and upper-atmosphere observations are among the longest continuous records in Antarctica. Scientists at Halley discovered the Antarctic ozone hole: measurements published in 1985 by Joe Farman, Brian Gardiner and Jonathan Shanklin showed dramatic springtime losses of stratospheric ozone, prompting the Montreal Protocol. The station also studies space weather, glaciology and the behaviour of the Brunt Ice Shelf.

Halley VI

Because the ice shelf flows toward the sea at several hundred metres per year and snow buries buildings, earlier Halley stations were buried and crushed within about a decade. Halley VI, designed by Hugh Broughton Architects, consists of eight linked modules on hydraulic legs fitted with skis; the legs are raised periodically to keep the modules above the accumulating snow, and the whole station can be towed to a new position. In 2016–17 the station was relocated 23 km inland because of growing cracks (chasms) in the ice shelf, and from 2017 the station was for several winters operated remotely without a wintering crew owing to the risk of calving.

Environment

Summer temperatures rarely exceed −10 °C and winter temperatures fall below −50 °C; the sun does not rise for about 105 days each year. A colony of emperor penguins breeds nearby on the sea ice at Windy Creek.
