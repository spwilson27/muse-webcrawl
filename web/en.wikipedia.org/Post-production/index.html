Post-production

Post-production is part of the process of filmmaking, video production, audio production, and photography, comprising all the work that occurs after principal photography or recording has wrapped. It follows pre-production and production, and traditionally includes editing, visual effects, sound design, and colour finishing.

Film and video processes

Picture editing assembles the footage into a coherent sequence, progressing from rough cut to final cut. Sound post-production includes dialogue editing, ADR (automated dialogue replacement), Foley, sound effects, and the final mix, along with composition and recording of the score. Visual effects (VFX) — CGI, compositing, matte painting — are integrated during post. Colour correction and colour grading set the image's look, after which the finished work is mastered and delivered in formats for cinema, broadcast, or streaming.

Audio and photography

In music and radio, post-production includes editing takes, mixing, and mastering. In photography, it encompasses developing film or processing RAW files, retouching, and preparation for print or web.

Digital workflows

The shift from photochemical to digital workflows — non-linear editing systems, digital intermediates, and file-based delivery — has blurred the boundary between production and post-production; editing often now begins during the shoot, and "fix it in post" has become both a genuine capability and a production joke.
