Vending machine

A vending machine is an automated machine that dispenses items such as snacks, beverages, cigarettes, and tickets to consumers after they insert currency or a payment card. It is an example of unattended retail automation.

History

The earliest known reference is a coin-operated holy-water dispenser described by Hero of Alexandria in the 1st century AD. Modern machines date from 19th-century London (postcards, 1880s) and New York (gum machines on train platforms, 1888). In the 20th century they expanded to soft drinks, coffee, and full meals (automats). Japan has among the highest densities of vending machines in the world — several million machines selling everything from drinks to hot food.

Operation and types

Machines accept coins, banknotes, cards, or mobile payments, verify the payment, and release the selected item via spirals, belts, or robotic mechanisms; refrigerated units sell cold or hot goods. Operators restock machines and collect revenue; telemetry now allows remote monitoring of stock and faults. Specialized machines dispense tickets, stamps, hygiene products, and electronics.
