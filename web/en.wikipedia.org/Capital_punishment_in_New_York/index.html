Capital punishment is not in effect in the state of New York. New York was the first state to use the electric chair, carrying out the execution of William Kemmler in 1890. The state executed 695 people between 1608 and 1963, the last being Eddie Lee Mays in 1963.

Modern history

After the U.S. Supreme Court's decisions in the 1970s, New York did not restore a valid death penalty statute until 1995, under Governor George Pataki. Seven people were sentenced to death under the new law, but none were executed. In 2004, the New York Court of Appeals ruled in People v. LaValle that the statute's deadlock instruction violated the state constitution. The legislature declined to fix the law, and in 2007 the last death sentence was reduced; the state's death row facility was closed in 2008.
