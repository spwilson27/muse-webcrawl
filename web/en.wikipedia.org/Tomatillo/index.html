Tomatillo

The tomatillo (Physalis philadelphica and Physalis ixocarpa), also known as the Mexican husk tomato, is a plant of the nightshade family bearing small, spherical, green or green-purple fruit of the same name, enclosed in a papery husk formed from the calyx.

Cultivation and description

Tomatillos originated in Mexico and were cultivated in the pre-Columbian era; they are a staple of Mexican cuisine and were domesticated earlier than the tomato there. The plant is an annual growing about 1–1.5 metres high. The fruit is harvested while green and firm, and the inedible husk is removed and the sticky coating rinsed off before use. The plants are not self-fertile, so several must be grown together for pollination.

Culinary use

The tomatillo's tart, citrusy flavour is the key ingredient in Latin American green sauces such as salsa verde and chilaquiles sauce, in which the fruits are boiled, roasted or blended raw with chillies, onion, garlic and coriander. Yellow or purple ripe fruits are sweeter and can be used in jams and desserts.
