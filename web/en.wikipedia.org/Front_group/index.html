Front group

A front group is an organisation that appears to be independent — often a grassroots citizens' movement, charity, or research institute — but is in fact created, funded, or controlled by another organisation, such as a corporation, industry association, political party, or government, whose interests it serves while concealing that sponsorship.

Purpose and methods

Front groups are used to give sponsor-friendly messages the credibility of apparent independence: a health warning sounds more persuasive from a "consumers' alliance" than from a tobacco company. Industry-funded front groups have been documented in controversies over tobacco, fossil fuels and climate policy, pharmaceuticals, and food additives. Related techniques include astroturfing — fabricating the appearance of grassroots support — and the use of front companies in commerce and espionage.

History

The term gained currency in the Cold War, when both Western and communist states operated front organisations to influence foreign opinion; the Comintern's fronts of the 1920s and 1930s are classic examples. In the modern era, disclosure laws and investigative reporting are the principal countermeasures, and regulators in several countries require transparency about lobbying and funding.
