Personality disorder

A personality disorder is a class of mental disorders characterised by enduring maladaptive patterns of behaviour, cognition and inner experience, evident across many contexts and deviating markedly from cultural expectations. Patterns begin in adolescence or early adulthood and cause distress or impairment.

Personality disorders (DSM-5)
Cluster A: paranoid, schizoid, schizotypal
Cluster B: antisocial, borderline, histrionic, narcissistic
Cluster C: avoidant, dependent, obsessive-compulsive

Classification

The DSM-5 groups ten personality disorders into three clusters by descriptive similarity. The ICD-11 replaced categorical types with a severity-based model qualified by trait domains. Diagnosis is debated because of overlap, stigma and questions about cultural norms.

Causes and treatment

Development involves genetic temperament and environmental factors including childhood adversity. The main treatment is psychotherapy — for example dialectical behaviour therapy for borderline personality disorder — with medication used for co-occurring conditions or specific symptoms.
