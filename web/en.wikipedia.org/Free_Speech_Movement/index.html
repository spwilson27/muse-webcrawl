Free Speech Movement

The Free Speech Movement (FSM) was a student protest movement at the University of California, Berkeley, during the academic year 1964–65. Sparked by the university's ban on political advocacy on campus, the movement won the right for students to engage in political speech and organising at the university, and it became a formative moment for the broader student protest culture of the 1960s.

Course of the movement

In the autumn of 1964, administrators enforced regulations prohibiting students from soliciting members and funds for off-campus political causes at the campus entrance. After former student Jack Weinberg was arrested on October 1 while staffing a civil-rights table, thousands of students surrounded the police car holding him for over 30 hours. Mario Savio emerged as the movement's most famous speaker, delivering the celebrated "bodies upon the gears" address on December 2, 1964, before a mass sit-in at Sproul Hall that ended with nearly 800 arrests — then the largest mass arrest in California history.

Outcome and legacy

Within weeks, the Berkeley Academic Senate voted overwhelmingly for a resolution supporting free speech, and the regents adopted new rules permitting political advocacy, a substantial victory for the students. The FSM pioneered tactics and a style of campus activism that fed into anti-war and other movements later in the decade, and Berkeley's campus remains marked by commemorations including the Mario Savio Memorial Lecture.
