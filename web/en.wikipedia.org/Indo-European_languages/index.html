Indo-European languages

Indo-European languages
Branches: Indo-Iranian, Hellenic, Italic, Germanic, Balto-Slavic, Celtic, Armenian, Albanian, Anatolian (extinct), Tocharian (extinct)
Speakers: ~3.5 billion native
Ancestor: Proto-Indo-European

The Indo-European languages are a language family native to the overwhelming majority of Europe, the Iranian plateau and the northern Indian subcontinent, now spoken worldwide.

Origins

Most scholars place Proto-Indo-European on the Pontic-Caspian steppe about 4500–2500 BCE; expansions carried branches from Ireland to India. Sir William Jones's 1786 observation of similarities between Sanskrit, Greek and Latin launched comparative linguistics.

Branches

Major living branches include Indo-Aryan and Iranian (together Indo-Iranian), Romance, Germanic (English), and Balto-Slavic. Earliest texts include Vedic Sanskrit and Hittite, written in scripts ultimately related to Brahmic scripts for the Indian branch.
