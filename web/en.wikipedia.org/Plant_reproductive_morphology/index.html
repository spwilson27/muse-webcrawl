Plant reproductive morphology

Plant reproductive morphology is the study of the physical form and structure of the parts of plants concerned with sexual reproduction. In flowering plants these structures are borne in the flower: the male androecium of stamens producing pollen, and the female gynoecium of carpels containing ovules.

Variation

Flowers may be bisexual or unisexual; plants may be monoecious, with separate male and female flowers on one individual, or dioecious, with them on different individuals. Non-flowering plants reproduce via cones, as in conifers, or spores, as in ferns and mosses, and all land plants show an alternation of generations between sporophyte and gametophyte.

Terminology

The field supplies much of the descriptive vocabulary of botany — inflorescence types, ovary position, placentation and fruit types — used in identifying and classifying plants.
