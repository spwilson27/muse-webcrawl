Brown County is a county in the U.S. state of Indiana. Its county seat (and only incorporated town) is Nashville. The county was formed in 1836 from parts of Monroe, Bartholomew, and Jackson counties and named for Jacob Brown, a general who served in the War of 1812. It is known for its rugged, forested hills, which are a remnant of glacial boundaries, and for Brown County State Park, the largest state park in Indiana.

Geography

Brown County lies in the south-central part of Indiana. Unlike most of the state, its terrain is hilly and heavily wooded, formed by the Norman Upland. Much of the county is covered by Brown County State Park, the Hoosier National Forest, and the Yellowwood State Forest, making it one of the least densely populated counties in Indiana.

Arts colony

In the early 20th century, Brown County became home to a noted artists' colony. Painters such as T. C. Steele, Adolph Shulz, and Will Vawter were drawn by the scenic hills and distinctive light. The village of Nashville developed into a centre for arts, crafts, and tourism, and it remains a popular destination, especially in autumn when the forest foliage changes colour.

Tourism

Brown County State Park, established in 1929, covers nearly 16,000 acres and is sometimes called the "Little Smokies" for its misty ridges. Tourism centred on Nashville's shops and galleries is the county's main industry.
