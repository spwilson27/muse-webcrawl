Game Boy

Game Boy
Manufacturer: Nintendo
Released: April 21, 1989 (Japan)
Units sold: 118.69 million (incl. Game Boy Color)

The Game Boy is a handheld game console developed and manufactured by Nintendo. It was first released in Japan on April 21, 1989, in North America later the same year, and in Europe in 1990. It was the first handheld console in the Game Boy line, created and designed by Gunpei Yokoi and Nintendo Research & Development 1, the team behind the Game & Watch handhelds.

Hardware

The Game Boy has a dot-matrix screen with a 160 × 144 pixel resolution displaying four shades of green-grey, a custom 8-bit Sharp LR35902 processor running at about 4.19 MHz, and 8 KB of internal RAM. It runs on four AA batteries, giving roughly 15–30 hours of play, and games are distributed on ROM cartridges. A link cable allowed two-player gaming between consoles.

Games

The system launched with titles including Super Mario Land and was famously bundled in North America with Tetris, which became one of the best-selling video games of all time. The library later included Pokémon Red and Blue, which sparked a global phenomenon in the late 1990s, as well as The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening and the Kirby series.

Legacy

Despite technically inferior hardware compared with colour-screen rivals such as the Sega Game Gear and Atari Lynx, the Game Boy's low price, durability and long battery life made it a mass-market success. Combined with the Game Boy Color, it sold about 118.69 million units worldwide, and it was succeeded by the Game Boy Advance in 2001.
