HITECH Act

The Health Information Technology for Economic and Clinical Health Act, abbreviated as the HITECH Act, is United States legislation enacted as part of the American Recovery and Reinvestment Act of 2009 and signed into law by President Barack Obama on 17 February 2009. It is widely regarded as the most significant expansion of federal health information technology policy in the United States.

Provisions

The Act allocated roughly $27–30 billion in incentives to promote the adoption and "meaningful use" of electronic health records (EHRs) by hospitals and physicians through the Medicare and Medicaid programmes. Eligible providers who demonstrated meaningful use received incentive payments, while from 2015 non-adopters faced payment penalties. HITECH also substantially strengthened the privacy and security provisions of HIPAA, extending them directly to business associates, increasing penalties for breaches, and introducing the first federal data breach notification requirement for health information.

The Act expanded the role of the Office of the National Coordinator for Health Information Technology (ONC), which established certification criteria for EHR systems and funded regional extension centres, health information exchanges and workforce training.

Impact

Hospital adoption of basic EHR systems rose from under 10% in 2008 to over 95% within a decade, a transformation widely attributed to the Act's incentives. The "meaningful use" programme later evolved into the Promoting Interoperability programmes under MACRA.
