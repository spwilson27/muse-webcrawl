Warhead

A warhead is the forward section of a device, usually a missile, rocket, torpedo, or guided bomb, that contains the explosive agent or toxic (biological, chemical, or nuclear) material that is delivered to the target. The warhead is distinct from the delivery system's propulsion and guidance sections; it is initiated by a fuze, which may act on contact, by proximity, by altitude, or after a set time or distance.

Types

Conventional warheads include high-explosive, fragmentation, shaped-charge (used against armour), blast/fragmentation combinations, incendiary, and specialized types such as runway-penetrating and thermobaric designs. Chemical, biological, and nuclear warheads deliver weapons of mass destruction; nuclear warheads on ballistic missiles are often carried in multiple independently targetable reentry vehicles (MIRVs). Torpedo warheads are designed to detonate beneath or against a ship's hull, and naval mines are sometimes described as stationary warheads.

Design considerations

Warhead design balances yield against weight, volume, safety, and the target's vulnerabilities. Modern designs incorporate insensitive explosives, multi-point initiation, and fuzing logic intended to prevent accidental or unauthorized detonation, alongside safe-and-arm devices that enable the warhead only after launch conditions are met.
