Circassian genocide

The Circassian genocide was the Russian Empire's systematic mass killing, ethnic cleansing, and expulsion of the Circassian population of the northwestern Caucasus in the 1860s, culminating in 1864 at the end of the Russo-Circassian War. The great majority of Circassians—estimates commonly range from several hundred thousand to around 1.5 million people killed or deported—were expelled to the Ottoman Empire, and many died of disease, starvation, and shipwreck during the deportations.

Background

The Russo-Circassian War, lasting roughly a century, ended with Russian victory in 1864. Russian commanders pursued a policy of clearing the Circassian lands for Russian and Cossack settlement; Circassian villages were destroyed and their inhabitants driven to Black Sea embarkation points such as Sochi.

Recognition

Georgia became the first country to formally recognize the events as genocide, in 2011. Circassian diaspora communities, today concentrated in Turkey, Jordan, Syria, and elsewhere, commemorate 21 May as a day of mourning. Russia has not recognized the events as genocide.
