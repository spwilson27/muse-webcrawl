National Electoral Institute

Country: Mexico
Founded: 2014 (as INE); predecessor IFE founded 1990
Function: Organising federal elections

The National Electoral Institute (Spanish: Instituto Nacional Electoral, INE) is an autonomous public body responsible for organising federal elections in Mexico, including elections for the president, senators, and federal deputies, and for regulating political parties and campaign finance jointly with state electoral bodies.

History

The INE was created by constitutional reform in 2014, succeeding the Federal Electoral Institute (IFE), founded in 1990 as Mexico moved away from decades of one-party rule and government-run elections. The IFE gained full autonomy in 1996 and administered the landmark 2000 election in which the opposition won the presidency for the first time in 71 years. The 2014 reform renamed the body and extended its role over local elections nationwide.

Functions

The institute maintains the electoral roll and issues the free voter ID card (credencial para votar), which serves as Mexico's principal form of identification. It trains poll workers, organises counting and quick counts, allocates public funding and broadcast time to parties, and oversees sanctions for electoral offences.

Governance

The INE is directed by a General Council of eleven electoral councillors, including a president councillor, elected by a two-thirds vote of the Chamber of Deputies for nine-year terms.
