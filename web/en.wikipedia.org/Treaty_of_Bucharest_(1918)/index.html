Treaty of Bucharest (1918)

Signed7 May 1918
LocationBucharest, Romania
PartiesRomania; Central Powers

The Treaty of Bucharest of 1918 was a peace treaty between Romania and the Central Powers — Germany, Austria-Hungary, Bulgaria, and the Ottoman Empire — signed on 7 May 1918 during the First World War. It followed the Treaty of Buftea, a preliminary peace agreement, after Romania was left isolated by Russia's withdrawal from the war under the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk.

Terms

Romania ceded Dobruja to Bulgaria and the Central Powers, gave Austria-Hungary control of the Carpathian passes, and granted Germany extensive economic concessions, including a 90-year lease on Romania's oil fields. Romania was permitted to retain Bessarabia, which had united with it after the Russian Revolution. King Ferdinand I refused to promulgate the treaty, and it was never fully ratified.

Aftermath

Romania re-entered the war on the side of the Allies on 10 November 1918, one day before the armistice in the west. The Treaty of Bucharest was annulled by the subsequent peace settlement, and Romania emerged from the war greatly enlarged.
