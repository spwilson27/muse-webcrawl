The Shanghai Metro (Chinese: 上海地铁) is the rapid transit system of Shanghai, China, operated by the Shanghai Shentong Metro Group. Opened on 28 May 1993 with the first section of Line 1, it has grown into one of the largest metro systems in the world by route length, with more than 800 kilometres of lines and over 500 stations. Its annual ridership, on the order of 3.6–3.8 billion trips in peak pre-2020 years, ranks among the highest of any metro system worldwide.

History

Planning for a Shanghai subway began in the 1950s and 1960s, when tunnel test sections were dug beneath the city, partly with civil-defence purposes. Serious construction of Line 1 began in the late 1980s with foreign loans and technology from Germany and France; the first short section, between Jinjiang Park and Xujiahui, opened in May 1993, and the full first line from Shanghai Railway Station to Xinzhuang opened in 1995 and was later extended north to Fujin Road.

Expansion accelerated dramatically in the 2000s. Line 2, crossing the Huangpu River to Pudong, opened in 1999–2000; the Pearl Line (Line 3) and Line 4 ring followed; and a massive building programme ahead of Expo 2010 brought the network to eleven lines and 420 km by the time of the fair, making it briefly the world's longest metro. Lines continued to reach the suburban districts of Jiading, Songjiang, Fengxian, and Chongming (via bridge-and-tunnel links), and to both of the city's airports.

Network

The network comprises numbered lines 1–18 (with some numbers, such as 5 and the Pujiang line, using lighter-capacity rolling stock), the Shanghai maglev train to Pudong International Airport, and suburban extensions. Major interchange stations include People's Square (lines 1, 2, 8), Century Avenue (2, 4, 6, 9), Xujiahui, and Shanghai Railway Station. Line 4 is a ring line sharing a section with Line 3's elevated alignment.

Operations and fares

Trains run roughly from 05:30 to 23:00, with headways as short as two minutes on the busiest central sections. Fares are distance-based, starting at 3 yuan for the first 6 km (4 yuan on Line 16's longer-distance tariff structure in some periods), and are paid with the Shanghai Public Transportation Card, single-journey tickets, or mobile QR-code apps. The system is notable for platform screen doors on nearly all underground stations, bilingual Chinese–English announcements, and extensive security screening at station entrances.

Ridership records

Daily ridership regularly exceeds 10 million trips; the record daily ridership surpassed 13 million rides in March 2019. The network's growth has been credited with transforming commuting in the Shanghai metropolitan area and underpinning the development of new towns across the municipality.

Future expansion

Approved plans extend the system further, including extensions of Line 2 westward, the Chongming line, Line 19, Line 20, and additional suburban (city railway) lines operated under the metropolitan rail programme, with total municipal rail length projected to pass 1,000 km in the late 2020s.
