Calamus (palm)

KingdomPlantae
FamilyArecaceae
GenusCalamus L.

Calamus is a genus of flowering plants in the palm family Arecaceae that are among those commonly called rattan palms. With about 400 species, it is one of the largest palm genera. They are distributed in tropical and subtropical regions of Africa, Asia, and Australia, with the greatest diversity in Southeast Asian forests.

Description

Most Calamus species are long-stemmed climbers (lianas) that scramble through forest canopies using grapnel-like spines and whip-like extensions of the leaf rachis called cirri, or spiny flagella arising from leaf sheaths. Some species are instead stemless or grow as erect shrubs or trees. Their slender, flexible stems are among the longest of any plant — stems of Calamus manan can exceed 100 metres.

Uses

The stems of several species, particularly Calamus rotang and relatives, are harvested as rattan cane, a major commercial material for furniture, baskets, and handicrafts, and an important source of income for forest communities. Some species produce the red resin known as dragon's blood, and the fruits and shoots of some are eaten locally.
