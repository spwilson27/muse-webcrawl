Decimal representation

Decimal (base-10) representation of numbers

A decimal representation of a non-negative real number r is an expression in the form of a series, traditionally written as a sum r = Σ bi 10−i for i from some starting integer to infinity, where the coefficients bi are digits 0–9.

Terminating and repeating decimals

A rational number has a terminating decimal representation exactly when its denominator, in lowest terms, has no prime factors other than 2 and 5. Other rationals have eventually repeating decimals, such as 1/3 = 0.333…. Irrational numbers have non-terminating, non-repeating decimal representations.

Non-uniqueness

Some numbers have two decimal representations: for example, 0.999… = 1. These dual representations occur exactly for numbers with a terminating expansion.
