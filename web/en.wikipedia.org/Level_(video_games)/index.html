Level (video games)

In video games, a level (also called a map, stage, course, act or round, depending on genre) is a distinct area or challenge within the game world that a player must navigate or complete. Levels structure gameplay into discrete, escalating units.

Design and structure

Levels are created by level designers using level editors, often within the game's engine. A level typically introduces its mechanics in a safe setting, develops them, and tests mastery in a climax, sometimes ending in a boss fight. Progress may be linear, hub-based, or — in open-world games — replaced by a continuous world in which "levels" persist mainly as missions or dungeons.

Terminology by genre

Platform games use "levels" or "stages" (e.g. World 1-1 of Super Mario Bros.); shooters use "maps"; racing games use "tracks"; fighting games use "rounds" and "stages"; role-playing games feature dungeons and quests. The word can also denote character level, a measure of a player's experience and power, treated in a separate article.

Custom levels

Many games ship with editors or support mods, letting players create and share their own levels — from Doom WADs to Super Mario Maker — extending a game's life and community.
