Korma

Korma (from Urdu qormah, ultimately from Turkish kavurma) is a dish originating in the Indian subcontinent, consisting of meat or vegetables braised in a spiced sauce made with yoghurt, cream, or nut and seed pastes, producing a mild, rich gravy.

History and varieties

Korma developed in the Mughlai cuisine of the Mughal Empire, with dishes such as shahi korma served at court. Regional forms include the white, yoghurt-based kormas of Lucknow and Hyderabad, Kashmiri versions coloured with dried cockscomb or chilli, and South Indian kurma made with coconut. In Britain, chicken korma became a staple of curry houses, typically among the mildest dishes on the menu.

Preparation

The base is usually onions browned in ghee or oil, ginger, garlic and whole and ground spices such as cardamom, cloves, cinnamon, coriander and cumin, finished with yoghurt or cream added carefully to avoid splitting.
