Sakamoto Ryōma

Born3 January 1836, Kōchi, Tosa Domain
Died10 December 1867, Kyoto (assassinated)
OccupationSamurai, political activist, businessman
Known forSatsuma–Chōshū Alliance; Kaientai company

Sakamoto Ryōma (1836–1867) was a Japanese samurai of the Tosa Domain and a leading figure in the movement to overthrow the Tokugawa shogunate during the Bakumatsu period.

Activities

A masterless samurai after leaving Tosa, Ryōma studied under Katsu Kaishū and founded the Kaientai, a private naval and shipping enterprise. He brokered the Satsuma–Chōshū Alliance of 1866, which united the shogunate's two most powerful opponents, and proposed an eight-point programme for a new national government that influenced the Meiji Restoration.

Death and legacy

Ryōma was assassinated with his associate Nakaoka Shintarō at an inn in Kyoto in December 1867, weeks before the Restoration he had helped prepare. He remains one of Japan's most popular historical heroes, the subject of many novels, films and television dramas.
