Kashubians

The Kashubians (Kashubian: Kaszëbi; Polish: Kaszubi) are a West Slavic ethnic group native to the historical region of Pomerania in north-central Poland, especially the area around Gdańsk known as Kashubia. They speak the Kashubian language, the only surviving descendant of the Pomeranian languages.

History

The Kashubians descend from the Slavic Pomeranian tribes who settled the Baltic coast in the early Middle Ages. Over the centuries their lands passed between Polish, Teutonic, Prussian and German rule, and many Kashubians were Germanised or Polonised. After the Second World War Kashubia remained within Poland, and Kashubians were recognised as an ethnic minority.

Language and culture

Kashubian is recognised in Poland as a regional language — the only language with that status in the country. In the 2011 census, roughly 228,000 people declared Kashubian identity and over 100,000 reported speaking the language at home. Kashubian is taught in schools, used in local media and on bilingual road signs in many communes. The Kashubian capital is often considered to be Kartuzy. Distinctive cultural features include Kashubian embroidery with its seven-colour pattern, snuff-taking traditions, and a rich folk literature; the first Kashubian-language writings date to the 16th century.

Identity

Kashubian identity combines regional Slavic heritage with strong ties to Poland; the movement's motto is "Zemia Rodnô" and its flag is black over yellow. Donald Tusk, former prime minister of Poland and president of the European Council, is of Kashubian descent.
