Joint probability distribution

Given two random variables defined on the same probability space, the joint probability distribution is the probability distribution that gives the probability that each variable falls into a particular range or discrete set of values. The concept generalizes to any number of random variables (the multivariate distribution) and is fundamental to the study of probability and statistics.

Discrete and continuous cases

For two discrete random variables X and Y, the joint distribution is given by the joint probability mass function pX,Y(x, y) = P(X = x and Y = y). For continuous variables, it is described by a joint probability density function fX,Y(x, y), such that the probability of (X, Y) lying in a region is the integral of the density over that region.

Marginal and conditional distributions

The distribution of a single variable obtained from the joint distribution — by summing (discrete case) or integrating (continuous case) over the other variable — is called its marginal distribution. The conditional distribution of one variable given the value of another can be derived by dividing the joint density or mass by the marginal of the conditioning variable. Two variables are independent if and only if their joint distribution factors into the product of their marginals. Quantities such as covariance and correlation are computed from the joint distribution.
