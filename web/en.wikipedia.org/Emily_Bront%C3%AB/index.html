Emily Brontë

Emily Jane Brontë (30 July 1818 – 19 December 1848) was an English novelist and poet best known for her only novel, Wuthering Heights, now considered a classic of English literature. She also published a book of poetry with her sisters Charlotte and Anne, titled Poems by Currer, Ellis and Acton Bell, with her poems occupying a significant presence. Emily used the pen name Ellis Bell.

Born:30 July 1818, Thornton, Yorkshire, England
Died:19 December 1848 (aged 30), Haworth, Yorkshire
Pen name:Ellis Bell
Notable work:Wuthering Heights (1847)

Life

Emily was the fifth of six children of Patrick Brontë, an Irish Anglican clergyman, and Maria Branwell. The family moved to the parsonage at Haworth on the Yorkshire moors, whose wild landscape deeply influenced her writing. With her siblings she created elaborate imaginary worlds, including the kingdom of Gondal, the setting of many of her poems. She worked briefly as a teacher and studied in Brussels with Charlotte in 1842.

Wuthering Heights

Published in 1847, a year before her death from tuberculosis at the age of 30, Wuthering Heights initially baffled critics with its savage passions and unconventional nested-narrative structure. It tells the story of the intense, almost demonic love between Heathcliff and Catherine Earnshaw and its destructive consequences across two generations. The novel's reputation grew steadily after her death and it is now regarded as one of the greatest novels in the English language.

Poetry

Emily's poetry — including "No coward soul is mine" — is noted for its intensity, mysticism, and love of the moors, and many critics regard her as the finest poet of the Brontë sisters.
