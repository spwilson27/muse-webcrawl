John Wilkes Booth (May 10, 1838 – April 26, 1865) was an American stage actor who assassinated United States President Abraham Lincoln at Ford's Theatre in Washington, D.C., on April 14, 1865.

Early life and acting career

Booth was born into the prominent Booth theatrical family, the son of the actor Junius Brutus Booth and younger brother of Edwin Booth. Handsome and athletic, he became one of the most popular actors in America, famous for Shakespearean roles and earning a fortune on the stage.

Assassination plot

A fervent supporter of the Confederacy and of slavery, Booth conspired with others first to kidnap Lincoln and later to kill him, along with planned attacks on Vice President Andrew Johnson and Secretary of State William H. Seward. On Good Friday, 14 April 1865, Booth shot Lincoln in the back of the head during a performance of Our American Cousin, leapt to the stage shouting "Sic semper tyrannis", and escaped on horseback despite breaking his leg.

Death

After a twelve-day manhunt, Union cavalry cornered Booth and an accomplice in a tobacco barn near Port Royal, Virginia. The barn was set on fire; Booth was shot (by Sergeant Boston Corbett, though the circumstances are disputed) and died on 26 April 1865. Several of his co-conspirators were later hanged.
