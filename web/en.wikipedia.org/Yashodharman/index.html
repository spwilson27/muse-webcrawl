Yashodharman

Yashodharman
King of Malwa (Aulikara dynasty)
Reign: c. 515 – 545 CE
Capital: Mandsaur (Dashapura)
Famous for: Defeating the Huna king Mihirakula

Yashodharman (c. early 6th century CE) was a ruler of Malwa in central India, of the Aulikara dynasty, celebrated for defeating the Alchon Huna invader Mihirakula around 528 CE and checking Huna expansion into the Indian heartland.

Sources

Yashodharman is known principally from his inscriptions at Mandsaur in modern Madhya Pradesh, including two identical victory pillars (the Mandsaur stone pillar inscriptions, dated to about 532 CE) and a record of his feudatory-era predecessors. The pillars boast that his dominion extended from the Brahmaputra to the western ocean and from the Himalayas to the Mahendra mountains — a conventional panegyric that nevertheless reflects a real, if briefly held, supremacy across northern India following the decline of the Gupta Empire.

Defeat of Mihirakula

The inscriptions claim that Mihirakula, the feared Huna king who had ravaged Gandhara and Kashmir, "bowed his head" before Yashodharman — taken by historians to record a Huna defeat in Malwa around 528 CE. Together with the Gupta emperor Narasimhagupta Baladitya's resistance, this victory ended the Huna threat east of the Indus plains. The poet Vasula composed the Mandsaur inscriptions, and a later inscription of 589 CE still honoured Yashodharman's memory at Mandasor.

Legacy

Yashodharman's empire dissolved soon after his death, and Malwa passed between regional powers. In Indian historical memory he stands, alongside Skandagupta, as a defender of India against the Huna invasions.
