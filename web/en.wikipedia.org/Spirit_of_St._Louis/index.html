Spirit of St. Louis

Spirit of St. Louis
Type: Ryan NYP monoplane
First flight: 28 April 1927
Crew: 1 (Charles Lindbergh)

The Spirit of St. Louis is the custom-built, single-engine, single-seat monoplane flown by Charles Lindbergh on the first solo nonstop transatlantic flight, from New York to Paris on 20–21 May 1927. The flight covered about 3,600 miles (5,800 km) in 33½ hours and won the Orteig Prize.

Design

Built by Ryan Airlines in San Diego and formally designated the Ryan NYP (for "New York to Paris"), the aircraft was based on the Ryan M-2 mail plane, with an enlarged fuel capacity of 450 gallons and the cockpit moved behind the fuel tanks; Lindbergh navigated with the aid of a periscope. It was named in honour of Lindbergh's supporters in St. Louis, Missouri.

Preservation

After a final flight in 1928, Lindbergh donated the aircraft to the Smithsonian Institution, where it is displayed in the National Air and Space Museum in Washington, D.C.
