Ei-ichi Negishi (1935–2021)

Ei-ichi Negishi (July 14, 1935 – June 6, 2021) was a Japanese chemist who is best known for his discovery of the Negishi coupling. He spent most of his career at Purdue University in the United States, where he was the Herbert C. Brown Distinguished Professor of Chemistry.

Life and career

Negishi was born in Changchun, then in Japanese-occupied Manchuria, and raised in Japan. He graduated from the University of Tokyo in 1958 and worked at Teijin before moving to the United States as a Fulbright scholar. He earned his PhD from the University of Pennsylvania in 1963, working under Allan R. Day. He joined Purdue University in 1966 as a postdoctoral associate with Herbert C. Brown, later held positions at Syracuse University, and returned to Purdue as a full professor in 1979.

Nobel Prize

In 2010, Negishi shared the Nobel Prize in Chemistry with Richard F. Heck and Akira Suzuki "for palladium-catalyzed cross couplings in organic synthesis." The Negishi coupling, reported in 1977, uses organozinc reagents and palladium or nickel catalysts to form carbon–carbon bonds, and is widely used in the synthesis of pharmaceuticals and advanced materials.
