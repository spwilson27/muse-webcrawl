Anti-Americanism

Anti-Americanism is a negative predisposition, hostility or opposition directed towards the United States of America — its government, foreign policy, culture, society or people. The term covers a wide range of attitudes, from criticism of particular policies to wholesale rejection of American values and influence, and its meaning and extent are much debated by scholars and commentators.

History

European intellectuals expressed contempt for the New World from the eighteenth century — the naturalist Buffon argued that American nature was degenerate — long before the United States became a great power. In the twentieth century, anti-American sentiment grew with American global influence: it was a theme of both fascist and communist propaganda during the Second World War and Cold War, a rallying point in decolonisation movements, and a reaction to interventions such as the Vietnam War and the 2003 invasion of Iraq, after which favourable views of the United States fell sharply in many countries in opinion surveys.

Debates

Analysts distinguish between opposition to specific policies, which may be shared by many Americans themselves, and a deeper ideological anti-Americanism that treats the United States as inherently malign. Others argue the term is sometimes used to deflect legitimate criticism. Attitudes vary widely by country and period and tend to fluctuate with American administrations and foreign policy crises.
