Pentatonic scale

A pentatonic scale is a musical scale with five notes per octave, in contrast to the heptatonic scale, which has seven notes per octave (such as the major and minor scales). Pentatonic scales were developed independently by many ancient civilisations and are still used in a wide variety of musical styles.

Types

The two basic types are the major pentatonic scale (scale degrees 1–2–3–5–6, e.g. C–D–E–G–A) and the minor pentatonic scale (1–♭3–4–5–♭7, e.g. A–C–D–E–G). Scales lacking semitone steps are called anhemitonic; nearly all folk pentatonic systems belong to this family. The black keys of the piano form an anhemitonic pentatonic scale.

Around the world

Pentatonic scales underpin much East and Southeast Asian music (Chinese, Japanese, Indonesian gamelan sléndro), West African music, Celtic folk music, Hungarian folk song, and many Indigenous American traditions. The minor pentatonic is foundational to blues, rock, and heavy metal guitar improvisation; adding the flat fifth yields the blues scale.

Properties

Because the anhemitonic pentatonic scale contains no semitones or tritones, any combination of its notes is relatively consonant, which makes it a favourite tool in music education (notably the Orff and Kodály methods) and improvisation pedagogy.
