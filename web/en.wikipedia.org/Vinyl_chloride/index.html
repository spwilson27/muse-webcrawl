Vinyl chloride is an organochloride with the formula H2C=CHCl. Also called vinyl chloride monomer (VCM) or chloroethene, this colourless gas is one of the world's most important commodity chemicals, produced on a scale of tens of millions of tonnes per year almost entirely for conversion into the plastic polyvinyl chloride (PVC).

Production

Most vinyl chloride is made by the "balanced process": ethylene is chlorinated to 1,2-dichloroethane, which is thermally cracked to vinyl chloride and hydrogen chloride; the hydrogen chloride is reacted with more ethylene and oxygen (oxychlorination) to make additional dichloroethane. Acetylene-based routes remain in use in China, where carbide-derived acetylene reacts with hydrogen chloride.

Health effects

Vinyl chloride is a potent carcinogen. In the 1970s, workers cleaning polymerisation autoclaves were found to develop angiosarcoma of the liver, a rare cancer, leading to strict occupational exposure limits (1 ppm in the United States) and closed production systems. It is also associated with acroosteolysis and Raynaud's phenomenon in exposed workers. Vinyl chloride featured in the aftermath of the 2023 East Palestine, Ohio train derailment, where tank cars carrying it were vented and burned.

Uses

About 95 per cent of production goes to PVC manufacture; small quantities are used for other chlorinated solvents and copolymers. Because of its volatility and toxicity it is rarely encountered by consumers in free form.
