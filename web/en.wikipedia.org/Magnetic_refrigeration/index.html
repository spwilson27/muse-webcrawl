Magnetic refrigeration


Illustration: Magnetic refrigeration

Magnetic refrigeration is a cooling technology based on the magnetocaloric effect: certain materials heat up when magnetised and cool down when demagnetised. Adiabatic demagnetisation of paramagnetic salts was one of the first methods used to reach temperatures below 1 kelvin.

Principle

Applying a magnetic field aligns magnetic moments and reduces magnetic entropy, warming the material; removing the field under adiabatic conditions lets the entropy increase again, absorbing heat and dropping the temperature. Gadolinium and its alloys show a giant magnetocaloric effect near room temperature, raising hopes for vapour-compression-free refrigerators.

History

The effect was noted by Emil Warburg in 1881, and adiabatic demagnetisation was proposed independently by Peter Debye (1926) and William Giauque (1927); Giauque used it to reach 0.25 K in 1933. It complements optical methods such as laser cooling at the coldest temperatures.
