Satyendra Nath Bose (1 January 1894 – 4 February 1974) was an Indian theoretical physicist and polymath. He is best known for his work on quantum mechanics in the 1920s, in particular the derivation of Planck's radiation law that led, with Albert Einstein, to Bose–Einstein statistics and the prediction of the Bose–Einstein condensate. Paul Dirac named the class of particles obeying these statistics bosons after him.

Early life and career

Bose was born in Calcutta and studied at Hindu School and Presidency College, where he was taught by Jagadish Chandra Bose and Prafulla Chandra Ray. He joined the new University College of Science in Calcutta in 1916 as a lecturer, and from 1921 worked at the University of Dhaka, where he wrote his famous 1924 paper.

The 1924 derivation

While teaching, Bose derived Planck's law of black-body radiation by treating light as a gas of indistinguishable photons and counting their states in a new way. After an Indian journal rejected the paper, he sent it to Einstein, who recognised its importance, translated it into German and arranged its publication in the Zeitschrift für Physik. Einstein extended Bose's counting method to atoms, predicting that at very low temperatures a gas of such particles would collapse into a single quantum state — the Bose–Einstein condensate, first produced in the laboratory in 1995.

Later work

Bose spent 1924–1926 in Europe, working with figures including Marie Curie and Einstein. Back in Dhaka he became head of the physics department and later dean; after Partition he returned to Calcutta, serving as vice-chancellor of Visva-Bharati (1956–1958). He worked on unified field theory, crystallography, chemistry and even Bengali literature and the promotion of science education in Bengali. He received the Padma Vibhushan in 1954 and was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society. India's S. N. Bose National Centre for Basic Sciences in Kolkata is named for him.
