Chromium is a chemical element; it has symbol Cr and atomic number 24. It is the first element in group 6 of the periodic table. It is a steely-grey, lustrous, hard, and brittle transition metal. Chromium is valued for its high corrosion resistance and hardness. A major development in steel production was the discovery that steel could be made highly resistant to corrosion and discolouration by adding metallic chromium to form stainless steel. Stainless steel and chrome plating (electroplating with chromium) together account for about 85% of commercial use.

Chromium is also greatly valued as a metal that can be highly polished while resisting tarnishing. Polished chromium reflects almost 70% of the visible spectrum. The name derives from the Greek word χρῶμα (chrōma), meaning "colour", because many chromium compounds are intensely coloured.

History

Chromium minerals first came to the attention of the Western world in the 18th century, when the Siberian red lead ore crocoite (lead chromate) was identified. In 1794, Louis Nicolas Vauquelin obtained samples of crocoite ore and produced chromium trioxide; in 1797 he isolated the metal by heating the oxide with charcoal. Chromium was later also detected in emerald and ruby gemstones, whose colour is due to traces of chromium compounds.

Production

Chromium is mined as chromite ore (FeCr2O4). Roughly half of the world's chromite ore comes from South Africa, with Kazakhstan, India, Russia, and Turkey also significant producers. About 28.8 million tonnes of marketable chromite ore were produced in 2022. The ore is roasted and leached to produce sodium dichromate, from which ferrochromium and chromium metal are made.

Applications

In metallurgy, chromium gives corrosion resistance and a shiny finish to alloys, notably stainless steel. It is used in chrome plating, in pigments such as chrome yellow, in leather tanning (chrome alum), as a catalyst, and in refractory materials. Trivalent chromium (Cr(III)) is an essential trace nutrient involved in insulin, sugar, and lipid metabolism, whereas hexavalent chromium (Cr(VI)) compounds are toxic and carcinogenic.
