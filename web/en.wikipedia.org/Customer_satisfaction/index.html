Customer satisfaction

Customer satisfaction (often abbreviated as CSAT) is a term frequently used in marketing to measure how products and services supplied by a company meet or surpass customer expectation. It is defined as "the number of customers, or percentage of total customers, whose reported experience with a firm, its products, or its services exceeds specified satisfaction goals."

Measurement

Satisfaction is commonly measured with survey questionnaires using Likert scales, through composite indices such as the American Customer Satisfaction Index (ACSI), and through behavioural metrics including the Net Promoter Score (NPS), which classifies customers as promoters, passives, or detractors based on their likelihood to recommend a company.

Theoretical models

The expectancy–disconfirmation paradigm holds that satisfaction results from comparing perceived performance with prior expectations: performance above expectations produces satisfaction, while performance below expectations produces dissatisfaction. The SERVQUAL model measures service quality across dimensions such as reliability, assurance, tangibles, empathy, and responsiveness.

Importance

Satisfied customers are more likely to make repeat purchases, remain loyal, and generate positive word of mouth, while dissatisfied customers may switch to competitors and discourage others. Organisations therefore track satisfaction as a leading indicator of business performance alongside financial measures.
