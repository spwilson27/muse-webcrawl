American Railway Union
Founded: 1893
Founder: Eugene V. Debs
Defunct: 1897
Type: Industrial union

The American Railway Union (ARU) was, briefly, the largest labor union in the United States. Founded in Chicago in 1893 by Eugene V. Debs, it organized railway workers into a single industrial union regardless of craft, in contrast to the craft-based railway brotherhoods.

Pullman Strike

In 1894 the ARU launched a nationwide boycott in support of striking workers at the Pullman Palace Car Company. The boycott disrupted rail traffic across the country, and the federal government obtained a sweeping injunction and sent troops to Chicago. Debs was jailed for contempt of court, and the strike was broken.

Legacy

The union dissolved in 1897, but its suppression radicalized Debs, who became the Socialist Party's perennial presidential candidate, and the episode became a landmark in American labor history, leading to the creation of Labor Day as a federal holiday.
