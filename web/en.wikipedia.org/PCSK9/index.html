PCSK9

Proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 (PCSK9) is an enzyme encoded by the PCSK9 gene in humans on chromosome 1. It plays a central role in regulating cholesterol metabolism by binding to the low-density lipoprotein receptor (LDLR) and targeting it for lysosomal degradation, thereby reducing the liver's ability to clear LDL cholesterol from the blood.

Discovery

PCSK9 was identified in 2003, when gain-of-function mutations in the gene were found in families with autosomal dominant hypercholesterolemia. Soon afterward, loss-of-function mutations were discovered in individuals with unusually low LDL cholesterol and a markedly reduced risk of coronary heart disease, making PCSK9 an attractive drug target.

Mechanism

PCSK9 is secreted mainly by hepatocytes. It binds the extracellular domain of the LDL receptor. Normally, after delivering LDL particles into the cell, the receptor recycles to the cell surface. When PCSK9 is bound, the receptor is instead routed to the lysosome and destroyed, so fewer receptors return to the surface and more LDL remains in circulation.

Clinical significance

Monoclonal antibodies that block PCSK9 — alirocumab and evolocumab — lower LDL cholesterol by roughly 50–60% and reduce cardiovascular events. Inclisiran, a small interfering RNA therapy approved in the early 2020s, suppresses PCSK9 synthesis in the liver and is given twice yearly. See also cholesterol and statin.
