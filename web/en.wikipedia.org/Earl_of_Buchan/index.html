The Earl of Buchan is a title in the Peerage of Scotland, originating in the medieval mormaerdom of Buchan, one of the ancient provinces of north-east Scotland centred on the district of Buchan in modern Aberdeenshire.

Medieval earls

The earldom was held in the 12th and 13th centuries by the Comyn family, who became the most powerful magnate dynasty in Scotland. After the Comyns backed the losing side in the Wars of Scottish Independence, Robert the Bruce destroyed their power in the region in the "Harrowing of Buchan" of 1308. The title later passed by marriage through several families, including the Stewarts: Alexander Stewart, the notorious "Wolf of Badenoch", held Buchan in the late 14th century.

Later creations

In 1469 James III granted the earldom to his half-uncle James Stewart. Through a 17th-century marriage the title passed to the Erskine family, who have held it since: the Erskine earls include Thomas Erskine, a prominent 18th–19th century figure associated with the title, and in the modern era the earldom has descended in the Erskine line alongside subsidiary titles such as Lord Cardross. The family seat was historically associated with properties in Scotland and, later, England.
