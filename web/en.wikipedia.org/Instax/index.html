Instax (stylised as instax) is a brand of instant still cameras and instant films marketed by Fujifilm. First introduced in 1998, the system produces credit-card-sized or larger prints that develop automatically after exposure. Instax became the best-selling instant camera system in the world as instant photography revived in the 2010s.

History

Fujifilm had produced instant films since the 1980s, compatible in part with Polaroid systems under licensing arrangements. The Instax system launched in 1998 with the Instax 100 camera and Instax Wide film; the smaller Instax Mini format followed in 1999 and became the most popular variant. After Polaroid ceased instant film production in 2008, Instax dominated the market.

Formats and cameras

Three formats are offered: Mini (image size 46 × 62 mm), Wide (99 × 62 mm), and Square (62 × 62 mm). Cameras range from simple point-and-shoot models such as the Instax Mini series to hybrid models with digital sensors and built-in printers, such as the Instax Mini Evo. Fujifilm also sells Instax printers that produce prints from smartphone photos.

Popularity

Instax cameras found a large youth market, particularly in East Asia, driven by their colourful designs and the appeal of physical prints. Annual sales of Instax products reached tens of millions of units, and the line has been credited with sustaining Fujifilm's photographic film business.
