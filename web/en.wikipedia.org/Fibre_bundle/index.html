Fibre bundle

A fibre bundle: total space, base space and fibres

In mathematics, and particularly topology, a fibre bundle (or fiber bundle) is a space that is locally a product space, but globally may have a different topological structure. Specifically, a fibre bundle consists of a total space E, a base space B, a fibre F, and a projection map π: E → B such that every point of B has a neighbourhood over which the bundle looks like the product U × F.

Examples

The Möbius strip is a nontrivial fibre bundle over the circle with fibre an interval; by contrast, the cylinder is the trivial bundle. The tangent bundle of a smooth manifold, whose fibre at each point is the tangent space there, is a central example, as is the Hopf fibration of the 3-sphere by circles over the 2-sphere.

Importance

Fibre bundles are fundamental in differential geometry and mathematical physics, where they provide the language for gauge theories, connections and characteristic classes. Important special cases include vector bundles, principal bundles and covering spaces.
