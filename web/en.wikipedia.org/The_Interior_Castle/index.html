The Interior Castle

The Interior Castle (Spanish: El Castillo Interior or Las Moradas) is a 1577 work by Spanish Carmelite nun and mystic Saint Teresa of Ávila. It is a classic of Christian mystical theology and Spanish Renaissance literature.

Structure

Teresa imagines the soul as a castle made of a single diamond containing seven sets of mansions (dwelling places). Through prayer and meditation the soul progresses inward: the first three mansions are reached by human effort with God's grace, the fourth marks the transition to infused contemplation, and the seventh is spiritual marriage — union with God. Her famous image of the silkworm transforming into a butterfly illustrates the fifth mansion.

Influence

Written under obedience to her confessor while the Spanish Inquisition scrutinized mystics, the work remains central to Carmelite spirituality. See also The Guide for the Perplexed.
