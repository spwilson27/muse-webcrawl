In inorganic chemistry, bicarbonate (IUPAC-recommended nomenclature: hydrogencarbonate) is an intermediate form in the deprotonation of carbonic acid. It is a polyatomic anion with the chemical formula HCO3-.

Role in physiology

Bicarbonate serves a crucial biochemical role as the principal buffer of blood pH. In the bicarbonate buffer system, carbon dioxide dissolves in blood and interconverts with carbonic acid and bicarbonate, a reaction catalysed by carbonic anhydrase in red blood cells and the lungs. The kidneys regulate bicarbonate reabsorption and generation, while the lungs regulate carbon dioxide excretion, together keeping arterial pH near 7.4.

Compounds

The most common bicarbonate salt is sodium bicarbonate (baking soda), used in cooking, cleaning, and as an antacid. Other salts include potassium bicarbonate, ammonium bicarbonate (a leavening agent), and calcium bicarbonate, which exists only in aqueous solution and is central to the formation of stalactites and stalagmites and to temporary water hardness.

Ocean chemistry

Dissolved inorganic carbon in seawater is mostly bicarbonate, and the carbonate system buffers ocean pH. Increasing atmospheric carbon dioxide shifts the equilibrium toward carbonic acid, causing ocean acidification and reducing the availability of carbonate ions for calcifying organisms.
