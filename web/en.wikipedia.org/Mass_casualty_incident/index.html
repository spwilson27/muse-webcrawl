Mass casualty incident

A mass casualty incident (often abbreviated MCI or mass-cas) is any incident in which the number of victims overwhelms the available emergency medical and public-safety resources, so that normal standards of care cannot immediately be provided to every patient. Such incidents include major transport crashes, building collapses, natural disasters, industrial accidents, pandemics, and acts of violence such as bombings and mass shootings.

Definition and scale

Whether an event is an MCI depends on the resources at hand: a bus crash may be a mass casualty incident for a rural service but routine for a large city trauma system. Many agencies use a graded classification, from a manageable incident requiring mutual aid up to disasters requiring regional, national, or international response. The defining feature is the mismatch between need and resources, which forces a shift in medical philosophy from treating the most seriously injured first to doing the greatest good for the greatest number of patients.

Response

Responses are typically organised under an incident command system, which establishes a single command structure, a casualty collection point, and staging areas for ambulances and supplies. On-scene care focuses on scene safety, rapid triage, life-saving interventions (such as controlling catastrophic bleeding and opening airways), and organised transport to hospitals, which in turn activate surge and disaster plans.

Triage

Triage categorises patients by the urgency of their condition and their chance of survival. The widely used START system (Simple Triage and Rapid Treatment) sorts adults in under a minute each using respiration, perfusion, and mental status, tagging patients as immediate (red), delayed (yellow), minimal (green), or expectant/deceased (black). JumpSTART adapts the system for children.

Preparedness

Because MCIs are rare for any single agency, preparedness relies on pre-written plans, mutual-aid agreements, stockpiled equipment, and regular exercises and drills involving hospitals, EMS, fire, police, and voluntary organisations.
