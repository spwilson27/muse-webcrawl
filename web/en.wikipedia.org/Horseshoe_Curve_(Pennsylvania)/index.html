Horseshoe Curve (Pennsylvania)

The Horseshoe Curve is a three-track railroad curve on the Norfolk Southern Railway's Pittsburgh Line in Blair County, Pennsylvania, near Altoona. Completed in 1854 by the Pennsylvania Railroad under chief engineer J. Edgar Thomson, the curve was built to lessen the grade for trains crossing the Allegheny Mountains, looping around a dam and lake and filling in two ravines.

Engineering

The curve is about 2,375 feet (724 m) long and around 1,300 feet (400 m) in diameter, with a grade of about 1.8 percent (91 feet per mile). It allowed trains to ascend the Alleghenies without the extremely steep inclines that would otherwise have been required, and it remains a vital part of the main line between the East Coast and the Midwest, carrying dozens of freight trains daily; Amtrak's Pennsylvanian passenger train also passes around it.

History

During World War II the curve was guarded because of its strategic importance, and in 1942 it was a target of the Nazi sabotage mission Operation Pastorius, whose agents were captured before they could act. The curve was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1966. A visitor centre and viewing area, reached by a funicular or stairs, overlook the curve, and it is one of the most famous railroad landmarks in the United States and a popular destination for railfans.
