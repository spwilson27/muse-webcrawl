Umm Kulthum

Umm Kulthum
Born: c. 1902/1904, Tamay ez-Zahayra, Egypt
Died: 3 February 1975, Cairo
Occupations: Singer, actress

Umm Kulthum (Arabic: أم كلثوم; born Fatima Ibrahim es-Sayyid el-Beltagi, فاطمة إبراهيم السيد البلتاجي, c. 1904 – 3 February 1975) was an Egyptian singer, songwriter, and film actress active from the 1920s to the 1970s. She was given the honorific title Kawkab el-Sharq ("Star of the East") and is widely regarded as the greatest Arab singer of the twentieth century.

Early life

Born in the village of Tamay ez-Zahayra in the Dakahlia Governorate of the Nile Delta, she learned to sing from her father, an imam, and performed religious songs dressed as a boy in a travelling family troupe before moving to Cairo in the 1920s.

Career

In Cairo she worked with composers such as Zakaria Ahmed, Riyad al-Sunbati, and Mohammed Abdel Wahab, and poets including Ahmed Rami. Her monthly Thursday-night radio concerts, begun in 1934, became a national institution across the Arab world. Famous songs include "Enta Omri" (1964), "Al Atlal" (1966), and "Rubaiyat al-Khayyam". She also starred in several films in the 1930s and 1940s.

Death and legacy

Umm Kulthum died of kidney failure in Cairo on 3 February 1975. Her funeral procession drew millions of mourners and ranks among the largest gatherings in Egyptian history. Her recordings remain bestsellers decades after her death.
