Shaolin Monastery

The Shaolin Monastery (Chinese: 少林寺, Shàolín Sì), also known as the Shaolin Temple, is a renowned Chan Buddhist temple in Dengfeng, Henan Province, China, situated at the foot of Mount Song. Founded in 495 AD under the Northern Wei dynasty, it is famous above all for its association with Chinese martial arts — Shaolin kung fu — and as the legendary birthplace of Chan (Zen) Buddhism, whose patriarch Bodhidharma is said to have meditated there facing a wall for nine years.

History

The monastery was founded in 495 by Emperor Xiaowen of Northern Wei for the Indian monk Batuo (Buddhabhadra). Tradition credits Bodhidharma, who arrived around 527, with founding Chan Buddhism and — in later legend — with teaching the monks the exercises that became Shaolin kung fu, after finding them too weak for meditation. In 621, thirteen Shaolin monks aided the Tang prince Li Shimin in battle, earning the temple imperial patronage and the right to maintain fighting monks.

The monastery was destroyed and rebuilt repeatedly — notably burned by the warlord Shi Yousan in 1928, when much of its library and halls were lost. During the Cultural Revolution the temple was largely abandoned; its fame revived after the 1982 film The Shaolin Temple starring Jet Li, and it has since become a major tourist site and centre of martial-arts schools. In 2010 it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as part of the "Historic Monuments of Dengfeng in the Centre of Heaven and Earth".

Architecture and the Pagoda Forest

The temple complex, aligned on a central axis, includes the Shanmen (Mountain Gate), the Hall of Heavenly Kings, the Mahavira Hall and the Thousand-Buddha Hall, whose floor bears pits worn by generations of monks practising stances. West of the temple stands the Pagoda Forest (塔林), a cemetery of some 246 brick and stone pagodas — the largest such group in China — marking the graves of eminent monks from the Tang dynasty onward.

Shaolin kung fu

Shaolin kung fu encompasses hundreds of documented forms — empty-hand, weapons (staff, spear, sword), and qigong systems such as the Yijin Jing ("Muscle/Tendon Change Classic") attributed to Bodhidharma. It has profoundly influenced Chinese martial culture and global popular imagery of the fighting monk. The temple's modern warrior-monk troupes tour internationally, and tens of thousands of students train at academies in Dengfeng.
