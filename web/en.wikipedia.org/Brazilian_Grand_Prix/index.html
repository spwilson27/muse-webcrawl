Brazilian Grand Prix

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Interlagos circuit, São Paulo

The Brazilian Grand Prix (Portuguese: Grande Prêmio do Brasil) is a Formula One championship race held since 1972. It was first run at the Interlagos circuit in São Paulo, moved to Jacarepaguá in Rio de Janeiro in 1978, and returned permanently to a shortened Interlagos in 1990. From 2021 the São Paulo race has been officially titled the São Paulo Grand Prix, while the Brazilian Grand Prix name has been retained for the event's continuing history.

History

Brazil's first world championship Grand Prix was held in 1973 at Interlagos and won by local hero Emerson Fittipaldi. The race became famous for dramatic championship deciders, including the 2007, 2008 and 2012 finales. The 2008 race, in which Lewis Hamilton secured the title at the final corner of the final lap after rain arrived late in the race, is widely considered one of the most dramatic finishes in the sport's history.

Winners

The most successful driver at the Brazilian Grand Prix is Alain Prost with six victories. Michael Schumacher won four times, while Ayrton Senna, a national hero in Brazil, won his home race twice, in 1991 and 1993. Among constructors, McLaren is the most successful team at the event. The circuit, officially named the Autódromo José Carlos Pace, is 4.309 km long and runs anti-clockwise, unusual among Formula One venues.

The circuit

Interlagos sits between two artificial lakes in the southern zone of São Paulo. Its layout features the Senna 'S' curves at the start of the lap, a long uphill run to the finish line, and frequent changeable weather that has produced many unpredictable races.
