Kellermann, victor of Valmy

François-Étienne-Christophe Kellermann or de Kellermann, 1st Duke of Valmy (François-Christophe Kellermann; 28 May 1735 – 23 September 1820) was a French military commander, later the Duke of Valmy and a Marshal of the Empire. He is best remembered for commanding the French army, together with Charles François Dumouriez, at the Battle of Valmy on 20 September 1792, the first major victory of the French Revolutionary Wars, which is said to have saved the young French Republic.

Early career

Born in Strasbourg into a family of Saxon origin, Kellermann entered the French army as a cadet in 1752 and served with distinction in the Seven Years' War and in campaigns in Poland. A professional soldier of the old royal army, he rose steadily, reaching the rank of maréchal de camp (major general) by 1788, and welcomed the early stages of the Revolution.

Valmy

In 1792 Kellermann commanded the Army of the Centre. On 20 September 1792 his troops held the heights around the village of Valmy against the advancing Prussian army of the Duke of Brunswick. In the famous "cannonade of Valmy" — more an artillery duel in fog and rain than a pitched battle — the French line stood firm, Kellermann famously raising his hat on the point of his sword and shouting "Vive la Nation!", and the Prussians withdrew. Goethe, present with the Prussian army, wrote that a new epoch of world history had begun. The victory secured the survival of the Revolution and was followed days later by the proclamation of the French Republic.

Later life

Despite his fame, Kellermann was arrested during the Terror in 1793 and spent thirteen months in prison before being acquitted after Robespierre's fall. He held administrative and reserve commands under the Directory and Consulate. Napoleon made him a Marshal of the Empire in 1804 (an honorary appointment in recognition of Valmy), a senator, and Duke of Valmy in 1808; he voted, however, for Napoleon's deposition in 1814 and served the restored Bourbons as a peer of France. Kellermann died in Paris in 1820, the last surviving of the original marshals of 1804 at that date and the longest-lived of them all. His heart is buried at Valmy, beneath the battlefield monument.
