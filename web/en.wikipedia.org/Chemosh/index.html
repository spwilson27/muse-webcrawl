Chemosh (Moabite: 𐤊𐤌𐤔 Kamāš) was the national god of the Moabites, an Iron Age people living east of the Dead Sea in what is now Jordan. He is known principally from the Mesha Stele, a 9th-century BCE inscription in which King Mesha of Moab attributes his victories over Israel to Chemosh's favour, and from references in the Hebrew Bible.

In the Mesha Stele

The Mesha Stele records that Chemosh was angry with his land and allowed Israel to oppress Moab, but later restored Mesha's fortunes. Mesha describes devoting captured Israelite towns and their populations to Ashtar-Chemosh and dragging vessels of Yahweh before Chemosh, presenting Chemosh as a national deity closely parallel to Israel's god in the rhetoric of the inscription.

Biblical references

The Hebrew Bible calls Chemosh the "abomination of Moab" and associates his worship with Solomon's foreign shrines on the Mount of Olives, later destroyed by Josiah. Jeremiah's oracles prophesy that Chemosh will go into exile with his priests and officials, and Numbers links him to the Moabite cult that Israelites encountered at Peor.

Character and cult

Scholars debate whether Chemosh was a war god, a form of the West Semitic god Athtar, or a deity with astral traits. The compound name Ashtar-Chemosh in the Mesha Stele suggests association or identification with Athtar. Little is known of his temples or priesthood beyond the stele's evidence and later classical references.
