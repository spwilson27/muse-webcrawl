Lobola

Lobola or lobolo is a Southern African custom by which a man pays the family of his fiancée for her hand in marriage. The tradition is a longstanding one in Southern African customs, and in contemporary times it often takes the form of a cash payment or a gift in kind, historically cattle, made by the groom's family to the bride's family.

Practice

Negotiations over lobola are typically conducted between representatives of the two families and can be an elaborate, ceremonial process. Among the Zulu people the custom is known as ilobolo; among the Shona of Zimbabwe it is roora. The amount agreed reflects considerations such as the bride's education, her family's standing, and the relationship between the families.

Legal status

In South Africa, lobola is recognised under customary law, and payment of lobola is one of the requirements for a valid customary marriage under the Recognition of Customary Marriages Act. The practice has been debated: supporters view it as an important cultural rite that bonds families, while critics argue that it can be commercialised, delay marriages, or burden young couples financially.
