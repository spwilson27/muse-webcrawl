Color blindness

Color blindness or color vision deficiency (CVD) is the decreased ability to see color or differences in color. It most commonly affects the distinction between red and green hues, and is usually inherited in an X-linked recessive pattern, affecting about 8% of men and 0.5% of women of Northern European descent.

Causes

The retina contains cone cells sensitive to short (blue), medium (green), and long (red) wavelengths. Mutations in the opsin genes OPN1LW and OPN1MW on the X chromosome cause protan and deutan defects. Blue-yellow defects (tritan) are rarer and autosomal. Acquired deficiency can result from eye disease, ageing, or medication.

Types

Classification includes anomalous trichromacy (protanomaly, deuteranomaly, tritanomaly) and dichromacy (protanopia, deuteranopia, tritanopia), and the very rare total color blindness, achromatopsia.

Diagnosis and impact

The Ishihara test is the standard screening tool. Color blindness can affect occupations such as aviation and electrical work, and is considered in accessible design, where information should not be conveyed by color alone.
