Fox River (Wisconsin)

The Fox River is a river in eastern and central Wisconsin in the United States. About 200 miles (320 km) long, it is the principal tributary of Green Bay, an arm of Lake Michigan, and has one of the highest concentrations of paper mills of any river in the world along its lower course.

Course

The river is usually divided into the Upper Fox River, which flows southwest from its headwaters near Pardeeville through Portage and into Lake Winnebago, and the Lower Fox River, which flows northeast from Lake Winnebago through the Fox Cities — Neenah, Menasha, Appleton, and Kaukauna — to Green Bay. At Portage, the Fox lies only about two miles from the Wisconsin River, a proximity that historically created an important portage between the Great Lakes and Mississippi River watersheds.

History

French explorers Jean Nicolet and later Jacques Marquette and Louis Jolliet used the Fox–Wisconsin portage route in the seventeenth century, making the river a key fur-trade corridor. In the nineteenth and twentieth centuries the Lower Fox was developed with locks and dams for navigation and hydroelectric power, and its water power attracted the paper industry that still defines the Fox Cities. Severe pollution from paper-mill waste, including polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs), led to one of the largest sediment cleanup projects in United States history, largely completed in the 2010s.
