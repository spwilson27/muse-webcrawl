The Kimmel Center for the Performing Arts is a large performing arts venue at 300 South Broad Street on the Avenue of the Arts in Center City, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. Opened in December 2001, it is operated by the non-profit Kimmel Cultural Center (formerly Kimmel Center, Inc.) and is the home of the Philadelphia Orchestra.

Building

Designed by architect Rafael Viñoly, the centre is enclosed in a dramatic barrel-vaulted glass roof — often compared to a greenhouse — rising about 150 feet over the entire block. Beneath the vault stand two free-standing performance "buildings": Verizon Hall, a roughly 2,500-seat concert hall wrapped in mahogany with a cello-like curved form, designed acoustically with Artec Consultants, and the smaller Perelman Theater, a recital hall of about 650 seats with a rotating stage. The building also contains the Innovation Studio black-box theatre, rehearsal spaces, restaurants, and a rooftop terrace.

History

The project grew out of 1980s–90s plans for a regional performing arts centre championed by Mayor Edward Rendell and the Greater Philadelphia Cultural Alliance. Philanthropist Sidney Kimmel gave a major naming gift. The centre opened on 16 December 2001 with a gala concert; Verizon Hall's Fred J. Cooper Memorial Organ, among the largest mechanical-action concert hall organs in the United States, was inaugurated in 2006.

Resident companies

Besides the Philadelphia Orchestra, resident and presenting partners have included the Philly Pops, the Chamber Orchestra of Philadelphia, and — after the Kimmel organisation's 2021 merger with the Philadelphia Orchestra — a combined presenting body; the nearby Academy of Music also falls under the same organisational umbrella for many productions, hosting Opera Philadelphia and Philadelphia Ballet performances.
