Midas

Midas (Greek: Μίδας) is the name of one of several kings of Phrygia in Greek mythology, most famous for the story of his golden touch, in which everything he touched turned to gold. Historical records attest to a King Midas of Phrygia in the 8th century BC, son of Gordias.

The golden touch

According to the myth, Midas showed hospitality to Silenus, a companion of Dionysus, and was granted a wish. He wished that everything he touched would turn to gold. The gift became a curse when his food and drink — and in later versions, his daughter — turned to gold as well. Dionysus allowed him to wash the power away in the river Pactolus, which thereafter was said to carry gold dust.

The ass's ears

In another myth, Midas judged a musical contest between Apollo and Pan in Pan's favor; Apollo punished him by giving him the ears of an ass, which he hid under a cap until his barber discovered the secret and whispered it into a hole in the ground, from which reeds grew that broadcast it.

Historical Midas

Assyrian records mention a Mita of Mushki, identified with Midas, who ruled Phrygia in the late 8th century BC. He is associated with the great tumulus at Gordion and is said to have died during a Cimmerian invasion around 695 BC.
