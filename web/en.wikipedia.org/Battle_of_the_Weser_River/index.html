The Battle of the Weser River, also known as the battle at Idistaviso, was fought in AD 16 between Roman legions commanded by Germanicus and an alliance of Germanic tribes led by Arminius, on the plain of Idistaviso beside the Weser River in northern Germany.

Background

Arminius, a Cheruscan prince, had annihilated three Roman legions at the Battle of the Teutoburg Forest in AD 9. Germanicus, nephew and adopted son of the emperor Tiberius, led a series of punitive campaigns across the Rhine to restore Roman prestige and recover the lost legionary standards. In AD 16 he transported his army by sea and river to the Weser.

The battle

Arminius chose a position on a plain hemmed in by the river and wooded hills, hoping to trap the Romans as before. According to Tacitus, the Romans attacked in good order; fierce fighting followed in which the Germanic forces on the open ground were routed, though Arminius himself escaped. A second engagement followed at the Angrivarian Wall, where the Romans again prevailed.

Aftermath

Despite the victories, Tiberius recalled Germanicus and ended the attempt to conquer Germania east of the Rhine. The recovered standards and the triumph celebrated in Rome closed the campaign; the Rhine remained the empire's frontier.
