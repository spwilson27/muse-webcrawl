Norwegian Current

OceanNorth Atlantic / Norwegian Sea
TypeWarm ocean current
SourceNorth Atlantic Current
DirectionNortheastward along the Norwegian coast

The Norwegian Current (also called the Norway Current) is a warm ocean current that flows northeastward along the coast of Norway from the North Sea (around 58°N) into the Barents Sea. It is a continuation of the North Atlantic Current and, ultimately, of the Gulf Stream system, carrying relatively warm, saline Atlantic water into high northern latitudes.

Characteristics

The current transports on the order of several Sverdrups of Atlantic water, with the inflow concentrated in the eastern part of the Norwegian Sea along the continental slope. Surface temperatures range from about 2 to 5 °C in winter and 8 to 12 °C in summer, far warmer than other seas at the same latitude. It keeps Norway's coast largely ice-free in winter and moderates the regional climate. At the North Cape, part of the flow continues eastward into the Barents Sea as the North Cape Current, while another portion turns north along the west coast of Spitsbergen as the West Spitsbergen Current.

Significance

The influx of warm Atlantic water shapes the rich ecosystems of the Norwegian and Barents Seas, supporting major cod and herring fisheries, and influences weather patterns over northern Europe. Variations in the current's temperature and volume are closely monitored as an indicator of changes in Atlantic circulation.
