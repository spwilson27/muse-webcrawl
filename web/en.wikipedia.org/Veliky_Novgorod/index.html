Veliky Novgorod

Veliky Novgorod
Country: Russia
Oblast: Novgorod Oblast
Population: approx. 220,000
Founded: 9th–10th century

Veliky Novgorod (Russian: Великий Новгород, "Great Novgorod") is one of the oldest and historically most important cities in Russia, located in the northwest of the country near the southern shore of Lake Ilmen, about 190 kilometres southeast of Saint Petersburg. It is the administrative centre of Novgorod Oblast and is famous for its exceptionally well-preserved medieval architecture and its role as the seat of the Novgorod Republic.

History

According to chronicle tradition, Novgorod was associated with the Varangian prince Rurik, invited in 862 to rule over the Slavic and Finnic tribes, an event conventionally taken as the foundation of the Rus' state. The city grew wealthy through trade along the route from the Varangians to the Greeks and through commerce with the Hanseatic League, exporting furs, wax, and honey. From the 12th century Novgorod functioned as a republic governed by a popular assembly, the veche, which invited and dismissed princes.

Novgorod escaped destruction during the Mongol invasions of the 13th century, paying tribute while preserving its institutions, and under Prince Alexander Nevsky it repelled Swedish and Teutonic attacks. The republic's independence ended in 1478 when Ivan III of Moscow annexed it; in 1570 Ivan the Terrible's oprichniki massacred much of the population. During the Second World War the city was occupied and heavily damaged, but its monuments were painstakingly restored.

Landmarks

The city's historic monuments, including the Kremlin (Detinets) and the Cathedral of Saint Sophia (built 1045–1050, among the oldest church buildings in Russia), are inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Novgorod is also renowned for the birch bark manuscripts unearthed since 1951, which illuminate everyday medieval literacy.
