Royal Prussia

Image: Royal Prussia within the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, 1569

Royal Prussia (Polish: Prusy Królewskie; German: Königlich-Preußen) was a province of the Crown of the Kingdom of Poland, created in 1466 by the Second Peace of Thorn after the Thirteen Years' War between Poland and the Teutonic Order.

Territory and governance

Royal Prussia comprised the lands west of the lower Vistula ceded by the Teutonic Knights — including Gdańsk Pomerania (Pomerelia), the Chełmno Land, and the cities of Gdańsk (Danzig), Elbląg (Elbing), and Toruń (Thorn) — together with the Prince-Bishopric of Warmia. It enjoyed considerable autonomy within Poland, with its own estates and treasury, and its great cities were part of the Hanseatic League. The German-speaking cities of Royal Prussia maintained close cultural ties with the Holy Roman Empire.

Later history

Following the Union of Lublin (1569), Royal Prussia became more closely integrated into the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth. In the First Partition of Poland in 1772, most of Royal Prussia was annexed by Frederick the Great's Kingdom of Prussia (becoming the province of West Prussia), with Gdańsk and Toruń following in the Second Partition of 1793.
