The Principles of Mathematics

AuthorBertrand Russell
Published1903
SubjectPhilosophy of mathematics, logicism

The Principles of Mathematics is a 1903 book by Bertrand Russell, in which the author presents his famous paradox of set theory and argues his thesis that mathematics and logic are identical — the doctrine known as logicism.

Contents

The book treats the indefinables of mathematics, number, quantity, order, infinity and continuity, space, time, and motion. In its appendix, Russell discusses Frege's work and presents the contradiction — Russell's paradox — arising from the set of all sets that are not members of themselves, which he had communicated to Frege in 1902.

Legacy

The Principles introduced many readers to modern mathematical logic and laid the groundwork for Russell and Alfred North Whitehead's later three-volume Principia Mathematica (1910–1913), which attempted a full formal derivation of mathematics from logic.
