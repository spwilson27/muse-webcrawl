POVM

In quantum mechanics, a positive operator-valued measure (POVM) is a generalisation of the projection-valued measure (PVM) used to describe the statistics of quantum measurements. A POVM is a set of positive semi-definite operators that sum to the identity operator on the Hilbert space of the system.

Definition

Given a Hilbert space, a POVM is a collection {Em} of Hermitian positive semi-definite operators such that Σ Em = I. The probability of measurement outcome m for a system in state ρ is given by the generalised Born rule, p(m) = Tr(ρ Em). Unlike projective measurements, the elements of a POVM need not be orthogonal, and their number can exceed the dimension of the Hilbert space.

Applications

POVMs provide the most general description of measurement in quantum theory, encompassing projective measurements and detector inefficiencies. By Neumark's dilation theorem, any POVM can be realised as a projective measurement on a larger system. POVMs are central to quantum state tomography, unambiguous state discrimination, and quantum key distribution, where optimal measurements are often genuinely non-projective.
