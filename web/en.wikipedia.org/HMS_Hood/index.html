HMS Hood

HMS Hood
Class: Admiral-class battlecruiser
Launched: 22 August 1918
Commissioned: 15 May 1920
Fate: Sunk 24 May 1941 by the German battleship Bismarck

HMS Hood (pennant number 51) was a battlecruiser of the Royal Navy, the lead ship of the Admiral class. Commissioned in 1920, she was for two decades the largest warship in the world and a celebrated symbol of British sea power, widely known as "the Mighty Hood".

Interwar service

Hood spent much of the interwar period as flagship of the Battle Cruiser Squadron based at Scapa Flow, undertaking high-profile world cruises, including the Empire Cruise of 1923–24 with the Special Service Squadron. Although a major modernisation was planned, it was repeatedly deferred and cancelled by the outbreak of war.

Loss

On 24 May 1941, during the Battle of the Denmark Strait, Hood engaged the German battleship Bismarck and heavy cruiser Prinz Eugen. Minutes into the action, a shell from Bismarck penetrated Hood's after magazines; the ship exploded and sank within minutes, with the loss of 1,415 men. Only three survived. Her sinking shocked Britain and prompted the massive hunt that destroyed Bismarck three days later. The wreck was located in 2001 at a depth of about 2,800 metres.
