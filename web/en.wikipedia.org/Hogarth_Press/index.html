Hogarth Press
Founded: 1917
Founders: Leonard Woolf and Virginia Woolf
Country: United Kingdom

The Hogarth Press was a British publishing house founded in 1917 by Leonard Woolf and Virginia Woolf. Named after Hogarth House, their home in Richmond, London, it began as a hand-press hobby and grew into an influential publisher of modernist literature and psychoanalysis.

History

The Woolfs printed early works themselves, including Virginia Woolf's Kew Gardens and T. S. Eliot's poems. The press published works by members of the Bloomsbury Group and became the English publisher of Sigmund Freud and the International Psychoanalytical Library.

Legacy

The Hogarth Press issued major works of modernism and translations, later becoming an imprint associated with Chatto & Windus and, in the United States, Crown Publishing.
