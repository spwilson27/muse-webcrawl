First Lord of the Treasury

The First Lord of the Treasury is the head of the commission exercising the ancient office of Lord High Treasurer in the United Kingdom. By modern convention, the office is held by the Prime Minister, and the First Lord's official residence is 10 Downing Street. The Chancellor of the Exchequer serves as Second Lord of the Treasury, with 11 Downing Street as residence.

History

The Treasury was increasingly put "in commission" from the 17th century, with a board of Lords Commissioners of the Treasury replacing a single Lord High Treasurer; the last Lord High Treasurer in Great Britain left office in 1714. Through the 18th and 19th centuries the First Lordship became associated with the leadership of the government, and by the early 20th century the linkage with the premiership was complete — although the two offices remain legally distinct, and the Prime Minister is formally appointed First Lord as part of taking office.

Role today

The First Lord has little direct involvement in the day-to-day work of HM Treasury, which is directed by the Chancellor. The office survives chiefly as the legal basis for the Prime Minister's Downing Street residence, and the title appears on the letterbox of No. 10.
