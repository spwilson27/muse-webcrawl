California Pacific International Exposition

Dates29 May 1935 – 11 September 1936
LocationBalboa Park, San Diego, California
Attendance~7.2 million

The California Pacific International Exposition was an exposition held in San Diego, California's Balboa Park from May 29, 1935, to November 11, 1935, and again from February 12, 1936, to September 9, 1936. The fair was intended to boost San Diego's economy during the Great Depression and to promote the city and the American Southwest.

History

The exposition reused and expanded the grounds and ornate Spanish Colonial Revival buildings of the 1915 Panama–California Exposition. New structures were added, including the Old Globe Theatre, a replica of Shakespeare's theatre; the Spanish Village art colony; and the now-iconic Botanical Building area displays. Frank G. Belcher was a leading promoter of the event, and Richard Requa served as chief architect.

Attractions

Attractions included the Palace of Better Housing, a nudist colony in the Zoro Garden (one of the fair's most talked-about features), performances by a young fan dancer Sally Rand, "Midget Village", submarines and naval displays, and extensive gardens. The exposition drew about 7.2 million visitors over its two seasons.

Legacy

Many exposition buildings still anchor Balboa Park today, housing museums and the Old Globe, which continues as a leading regional theatre. The 1935–36 fair cemented Balboa Park's architectural identity and is commemorated in the park's museums.
