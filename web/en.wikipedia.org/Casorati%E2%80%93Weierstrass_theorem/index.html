Casorati–Weierstrass theorem

In complex analysis, the Casorati–Weierstrass theorem describes the behaviour of holomorphic functions near their essential singularities. It states that the image of any punctured neighbourhood of an essential singularity under the function is dense in the complex plane.

Statement

Let f be holomorphic in a punctured neighbourhood of a point z0, and suppose z0 is an essential singularity of f. Then for every neighbourhood U of z0, the image f(U \ {z0}) is dense in the complex plane C.

History and refinements

The theorem is named for Felice Casorati and Karl Weierstrass, who developed the theory of essential singularities in the 1860s and 1870s; a version also appeared in the work of Yulian Sokhotski. The much stronger Picard great theorem later showed that near an essential singularity a function takes every complex value infinitely often, with the possible exception of one value.

Example

The function exp(1/z) has an essential singularity at the origin, and its values on any punctured neighbourhood of 0 come arbitrarily close to every complex number, illustrating the theorem.
