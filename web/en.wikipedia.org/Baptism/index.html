Baptism

Baptism (from Greek βάπτισμα, baptisma, "immersion, dipping in water") is a Christian sacrament of initiation and admission to the Christian church. The canonical Gospels report that Jesus was baptised by John the Baptist in the River Jordan, and Jesus' commission to his disciples in Matthew 28 commands baptism "in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit". Almost all Christian denominations practise baptism, though they differ over its mode and theological significance.

Modes of baptism

Baptism may be performed by immersion (submersion in water), affusion (pouring water on the head) or aspersion (sprinkling). Churches including Baptists, many Pentecostals and restorationist movements practise believer's baptism by immersion only, baptising those old enough to profess faith. Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, Lutheran, Anglican, Methodist and Presbyterian churches also baptise infants, a practice called paedobaptism, usually by pouring or sprinkling, though the Orthodox immerse infants.

Theology

Most traditions understand baptism as a means of grace associated with the forgiveness of sins, regeneration and incorporation into the church; some, notably many Baptists and the Salvation Army's position on ordinances, treat it primarily as a symbolic act of obedience and public testimony. The Roman Catholic Church teaches that baptism removes original sin and is necessary for salvation, while recognising baptism by desire and by blood (martyrdom). Mutual recognition of baptism is a major theme of modern ecumenism; the 1982 Lima text Baptism, Eucharist and Ministry helped many churches recognise one another's baptisms.

History

Baptism developed within the context of Jewish ritual immersion (mikveh) and John's baptism of repentance. The early church baptised converts, typically at Easter; the catechumenate prepared candidates. Infant baptism became widespread by late antiquity and was defended by Augustine. The sixteenth-century Anabaptists revived believer's baptism, influencing later Baptist and free-church traditions.

Other religions

Ritual washing similar to baptism appears in Mandaeism (masbuta, repeated immersion), Sikhism (Amrit Sanskar initiation) and, as ritual ablution rather than initiation, in Islam and Judaism.
