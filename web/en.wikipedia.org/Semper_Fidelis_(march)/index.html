Semper Fidelis (march)

"Semper Fidelis" is a march composed in 1888 by John Philip Sousa. Written at the request of the Commandant of the United States Marine Corps, it is regarded as the official march of the Marine Corps, whose motto is Semper Fidelis—Latin for "Always Faithful".

History

Before the Marine Corps adopted a motto and emblem in 1883, its unofficial mottos included "Fortitudine" and "By Sea and by Land" (a translation of the British Royal Marines' Per Mare, Per Terram). Colonel Charles McCawley, the Commandant, selected "Semper Fidelis" as the official motto in 1883. According to Sousa's account, he was asked for a march worthy of the Corps; he composed "Semper Fidelis" in 1888 and later recounted the story of writing it in one night. There is no known original manuscript; the earliest known contemporary source is the piano version published by Carl Fischer.

Music

The march is written in the keys of E-flat and A-flat major and follows Sousa's characteristic march form, with an introduction, contrasting strains, and a trio. In a 1920 interview, Sousa rated it among his best marches. The Marine Band performs it for the President of the United States at ceremonial occasions, and it is a staple of military band repertoire.

Lyrics

Lyrics to the trio section, beginning "Here's health to you and to our Corps," were later written for the march and are sung by Marines, though there is disagreement as to their authorship.
