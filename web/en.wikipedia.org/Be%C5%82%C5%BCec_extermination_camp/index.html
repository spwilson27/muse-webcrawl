Bełżec extermination camp

LocationNear Bełżec, German-occupied Poland
OperatedMarch – December 1942
FunctionExtermination camp, Operation Reinhard
VictimsEstimated 430,000–500,000 people murdered

Bełżec was a Nazi German extermination camp built during the Second World War for the sole purpose of mass murder. Located near the village of Bełżec in occupied Poland, it was the first of the Operation Reinhard killing centres to begin operations, in March 1942.

Operations

Deportees, overwhelmingly Jews from the General Government and other parts of Europe, were murdered in gas chambers using carbon monoxide from engine exhaust. Estimates of the number killed range from roughly 430,000 to 500,000; only a handful of prisoners survived the war. Roma and Poles were also among the victims.

Closure and memory

The camp was dismantled in 1943 and the site disguised as a farm to conceal the crimes. A memorial and museum now stand on the grounds, dedicated in 2004.
