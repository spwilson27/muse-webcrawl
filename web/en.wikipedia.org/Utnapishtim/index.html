Utnapishtim

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A cuneiform tablet bearing the Epic of Gilgamesh flood narrative — placeholder]

Utnapishtim, also called Uta-napishti ("he found life") or Atrahasis ("exceedingly wise"), is the hero of the flood myth in the Epic of Gilgamesh and one of the central figures of ancient Mesopotamian literature. He is the only mortal granted eternal life by the gods, and Gilgamesh seeks him out in the epic's eleventh tablet to learn the secret of immortality.

The flood narrative

According to the epic, the gods decided to destroy humanity with a flood, but the god Ea warned Utnapishtim, king of Shuruppak, in secret — speaking the warning to the wall of his reed hut so that he could "overhear" it without violating the divine council's oath. Utnapishtim was instructed to tear down his house and build a boat of specific dimensions, loading it with his family, craftsmen, and "the seed of all living creatures."

After six days and nights of storm, the boat came to rest on Mount Nimush. Utnapishtim released a dove, a swallow, and a raven; the raven did not return, signalling dry land. The gods, chastened, granted Utnapishtim and his wife immortality and set them to dwell "at the mouth of the rivers."

Significance

The tale is the oldest known flood narrative and closely parallels later accounts, including the biblical story of Noah. Scholars debate the relationship between the Mesopotamian and biblical traditions, but the structural similarities — divine decision, warned hero, ark, birds, sacrifice — are striking. The Atrahasis epic gives an older, fuller version of the story.
