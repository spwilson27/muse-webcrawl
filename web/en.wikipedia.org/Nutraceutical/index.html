Nutraceutical

[Image placeholder: Nutraceutical]

Nutraceutical is a portmanteau of "nutrition" and "pharmaceutical", coined in 1989 by Stephen DeFelice of the Foundation for Innovation in Medicine. It generally refers to a food or food product that provides health and medical benefits, including the prevention and treatment of disease.

Scope and regulation

The term is applied to dietary supplements, herbal products, fortified and functional foods, and isolated nutrients such as omega-3 fatty acids and probiotics. Unlike pharmaceuticals, nutraceuticals are not subject to pre-market proof of efficacy in most jurisdictions: in the United States they are regulated mainly as dietary supplements under the Dietary Supplement Health and Education Act of 1994, and the category has no separate statutory definition.

Criticism

Health professionals and regulators have criticised the term as a marketing label that can imply drug-like benefits without drug-level evidence, and product quality and claims vary widely.
