Trinidad and Tobago Carnival

TypeCarnival
LocationTrinidad and Tobago
DatesMonday and Tuesday before Ash Wednesday

The Trinidad and Tobago Carnival is an annual festival held on the Monday and Tuesday before Ash Wednesday in Trinidad and Tobago. Dubbed "the greatest show on earth", it is the country's most significant cultural celebration and a major tourist attraction. The event is well known for participants' colourful costumes and exuberant celebrations. There are numerous cultural events such as "band launch" fetes running into the lead-up to the street parade on Carnival Monday and Tuesday, known locally as the season of mas (masquerade).

History

Carnival was introduced to Trinidad by French settlers in the late 18th century as masked balls and festivities before Lent. After the emancipation of enslaved Africans in the 1830s, they took over the street celebrations, blending them with African traditions of drumming, stick-fighting (kalenda), and Canboulay processions — producing a festival that colonial authorities repeatedly tried to suppress, notably leading to the Canboulay riots of 1881.

Music and competitions

The festival is inseparable from calypso and its descendant soca music, and from the steelpan, the only acoustic musical instrument invented in the 20th century. Major competitions include Panorama (for steelbands), the Calypso Monarch, the Soca Monarch, and the King and Queen of the Bands costume contests. Festivities begin with J'ouvert, a pre-dawn street party on Carnival Monday, followed by two days of masquerade bands crossing stages such as the Queen's Park Savannah.
