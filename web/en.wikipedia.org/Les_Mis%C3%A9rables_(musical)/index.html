Les Misérables (musical)

Les Misérables is a sung-through musical adaptation of Victor Hugo's 1862 novel of the same name, with music by Claude-Michel Schönberg, lyrics by Alain Boublil and Jean-Marc Natel (in French) and Herbert Kretzmer (in English). It is one of the longest-running and most popular musicals in the world.

Synopsis

Set in 19th-century France, the story follows Jean Valjean, a former convict hounded by the policeman Javert, as he seeks redemption while caring for the orphan Cosette amid poverty, revolution and the June Rebellion of 1832. Well-known songs include "I Dreamed a Dream", "On My Own", "One Day More" and "Bring Him Home".

Productions

After a 1980 Paris concept album and stage version, Cameron Mackintosh produced the English-language production, which opened in London in 1985 and on Broadway in 1987, winning eight Tony Awards. The show has been staged in dozens of countries and languages, and a film adaptation was released in 2012.
