Lodestone

Lodestone attracting iron nails

Lodestones are naturally magnetised pieces of the mineral magnetite. They are naturally occurring magnets, which can attract iron. The property of magnetism was first discovered in antiquity through lodestones. Pieces of lodestone, suspended so they could turn, were the first magnetic compasses, and their importance to early navigation is indicated by the name lodestone, which in Middle English means "course stone" or "leading stone": literally "the stone that leads".

Origin

Only a small fraction of magnetite deposits consist of lodestones. The leading explanation is that lodestones are magnetised by the strong magnetic fields surrounding lightning strikes, which can impart permanent magnetisation as they pass near magnetite close to the surface. Lodestones are typically found in surface deposits and outcrops.

History

One of the earliest references to the magnetic properties of lodestone is by the Greek philosopher Thales of Miletus in the 6th century BC, who said that lodestone attracts iron because it has a soul. In China, the earliest literary reference to magnetism appears in the 4th century BC, and a spoon-shaped lodestone device (the south-pointing spoon) was used for geomancy and later navigation. The scientific study of magnetism began with William Gilbert's De Magnete (1600), which distinguished the attraction of lodestone from the static electricity of amber.
