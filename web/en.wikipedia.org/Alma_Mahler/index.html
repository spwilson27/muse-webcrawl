Alma Mahler-Werfel (born Alma Margaretha Maria Schindler; 31 August 1879 – 11 December 1964) was an Austrian composer, author and socialite who became a central figure in the cultural life of Vienna, and later of Los Angeles and New York, through her marriages to the composer Gustav Mahler, the architect Walter Gropius and the novelist Franz Werfel, and her relationships with artists including Gustav Klimt and Oskar Kokoschka.

Early life

Born into an artistic family — her father was the landscape painter Emil Jakob Schindler — Alma studied composition with Alexander von Zemlinsky and wrote songs in her youth. She met Gustav Mahler in 1901 and married him in 1902; at his insistence she gave up composing, a condition that strained the marriage, though Mahler later relented and helped publish some of her songs.

Later life

After Mahler's death in 1911 she had a passionate affair with the painter Oskar Kokoschka, then married Walter Gropius in 1915. Following their divorce she married the writer Franz Werfel in 1929. Fleeing the Nazis in 1938, the couple eventually settled in California. Her memoirs, Mein Leben, appeared in 1960.

Music and legacy

Only a small number of her songs survive — around seventeen published lieder — but they are now regularly performed and recorded. Her diaries and letters are an important, if self-edited, source for the artistic world of fin-de-siècle Vienna, and her portrayal in later scholarship has been much debated.
