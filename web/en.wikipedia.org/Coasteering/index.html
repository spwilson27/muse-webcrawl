Coasteering

Coasteering is an adventure sport involving movement along the intertidal zone of a rocky coastline on foot or by swimming, without the aid of boats, surf boards, or other craft. It combines scrambling, cliff jumping, swimming, and traversing sea caves, arches, and rock pools.

History

The activity developed in the United Kingdom, particularly on the Pembrokeshire coast of Wales and in Cornwall, where commercial guided coasteering became established in the 1980s and 1990s. It is also popular in Ireland, other parts of the British coast, and increasingly in other countries with suitable rocky shores.

Activity and safety

Participants typically wear wetsuits, buoyancy aids, helmets, and sturdy footwear. Guided sessions are led by trained instructors who assess tides, swell, and weather. Typical features include swimming through gullies and caves, scrambling over barnacle-covered rocks, and jumping from ledges into deep water. Because conditions can change quickly, coasteering carries risks from cold water, waves, and falls, and is generally undertaken with experienced guides who know the local coastline.

Related activities

Coasteering is related to canyoning (which takes place in inland gorges), sea-level traversing in climbing, and tombstoning, an informal and often discouraged form of cliff jumping.
