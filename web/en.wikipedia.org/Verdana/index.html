Verdana

Verdana is a humanist sans-serif typeface designed by Matthew Carter and released in 1996 by Microsoft. It was designed specifically to be legible at small sizes on computer screens, with a large x-height, wide letterforms, and generous spacing. Hand-hinted by Thomas Rickner, Verdana was one of the core fonts for the Web distributed by Microsoft and became ubiquitous in early web design.

The name is a portmanteau of verdant (green, referencing the greenery of the Seattle area) and Ana, the name of Carter's daughter. A companion serif design, Georgia, was released at the same time.

Characteristics and usage

Verdana's distinguishing features include a tall x-height, open apertures, and clearly differentiated similar characters such as the capital I, lowercase l, and numeral 1. Because its letterforms are large for a given point size, Verdana appears bigger than many other fonts at the same nominal size. IKEA controversially switched its catalog typography to Verdana in 2009, and the font has been widely used in user interfaces and websites.

An updated version, Verdana Pro, was released by Monotype in 2011 with additional weights and extended language support.
