Molecular chemistry

Molecular chemistry is the branch of chemistry concerned with molecules — their structure, composition, properties, synthesis, and the covalent bonds that hold their atoms together — as distinct from the chemistry of extended networks, ions, and metals. It deals with the laws governing the combination of atoms into discrete molecular species and the reactions by which molecules are transformed.

Scope

Molecular chemistry encompasses most of organic chemistry (carbon-based molecules), much of inorganic chemistry in its molecular compounds (coordination complexes, organometallics), and the molecular side of biochemistry, including proteins, nucleic acids, and metabolites. It contrasts with solid-state and materials chemistry where structures are extended lattices rather than discrete molecules.

Concepts

Key concepts include molecular structure and geometry (explained by valence bond theory, molecular orbital theory, and VSEPR theory), chemical bonding (covalent, polar covalent, and coordinate bonds), functional groups, stereochemistry and isomerism, and reaction mechanisms. Spectroscopic methods — NMR, infrared, mass spectrometry, and X-ray crystallography — are the principal tools for determining molecular structure.

Supramolecular chemistry

An important extension is supramolecular chemistry, described by Jean-Marie Lehn as "chemistry beyond the molecule", which studies assemblies held together by non-covalent interactions such as hydrogen bonding, host–guest recognition, and self-assembly. Lehn shared the 1987 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for work in this field.
