Solubility

Solubility is the ability of a substance, the solute, to form a solution with another substance, the solvent. Insolubility is the opposite property, the inability of the solute to form such a solution. The extent of the solubility of a substance in a specific solvent is generally measured as the concentration of the solute in a saturated solution, one in which no more solute can be dissolved.

Solubility is commonly expressed in units of mass of solute per volume of solution (such as grams per litre) or as a mole fraction. At equilibrium, the solubility product constant (Ksp) governs the dissolution of sparingly soluble ionic compounds.

Factors affecting solubility

Temperature usually increases the solubility of solid solutes in liquids but decreases that of gases. Pressure has a strong effect on gas solubility, described by Henry's law, and little effect on liquids and solids. The chemical nature of solute and solvent matters — the rule of thumb "like dissolves like" reflects the importance of intermolecular forces, polarity, and hydrogen bonding. For ionic compounds, the common-ion effect and pH can sharply change solubility.

Applications

Solubility underlies recrystallization and purification in chemistry, drug formulation and bioavailability in pharmacy, mineral scaling and water treatment in engineering, and the transport of nutrients and pollutants in the environment.
