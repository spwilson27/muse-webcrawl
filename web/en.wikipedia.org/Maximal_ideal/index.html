Maximal ideal

In mathematics, more specifically in ring theory, a maximal ideal is an ideal that is maximal (with respect to set inclusion) amongst all proper ideals. In other words, I is a maximal ideal of a ring R if there are no other proper ideals contained between I and R.

Maximal ideals are important because the quotient ring of a ring by a maximal ideal is a field, and special cases of maximal ideals are prime ideals, which play an important role in algebraic geometry through the spectrum of a ring.

Definition

A proper ideal I of a commutative ring R is maximal if there is no proper ideal J with I ⊊ J ⊊ R. More precisely, I is maximal if and only if the quotient ring R/I is a field.

Existence

Krull's theorem states that every unital ring has at least one maximal ideal; its proof uses Zorn's lemma, and hence the axiom of choice. The set of all maximal ideals of a commutative ring R is called the maximal spectrum of R.

Examples

  • In the ring of integers Z, the maximal ideals are the principal ideals (p) generated by a prime number p.
  • In a polynomial ring k[x] over a field, maximal ideals are generated by irreducible polynomials.
  • Every field has exactly one maximal ideal: the zero ideal.
