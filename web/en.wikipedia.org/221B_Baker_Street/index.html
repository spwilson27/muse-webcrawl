221B Baker Street is the London address of the fictional detective Sherlock Holmes, created by author Sir Arthur Conan Doyle. In the United Kingdom, postal addresses with a number followed by a letter indicate a separate address within a larger, often residential building. Baker Street is in the Marylebone district of the City of Westminster.

In the stories

Holmes and his friend and chronicler Dr. John Watson took rooms at 221B from 1881, renting from their landlady Mrs Hudson. The address first appears in A Study in Scarlet (1887). At the time the stories were published, addresses on Baker Street did not run as high as 221; the street was later extended, and the location became a point of pilgrimage for readers who wrote letters to Holmes.

The real address

The Sherlock Holmes Museum, situated at 239 Baker Street since 1990, was granted permission by the City of Westminster to bear the number 221B. A blue plaque commemorating Holmes stands at the museum. Letters addressed to Sherlock Holmes have been delivered there for decades, once famously handled by a dedicated secretary at the Abbey National building society, which formerly occupied the relevant stretch of the street.
