Operation Weserübung (German: Unternehmen Weserübung, "Operation Weser Exercise") was the German invasion of Denmark and Norway during the Second World War, launched on 9 April 1940. It was the first joint air, sea and land operation of the war, mounted to secure Germany's supply of Swedish iron ore shipped through the Norwegian port of Narvik and to pre-empt Allied intervention in Scandinavia.

German troops landing in Norway, April 1940.

The invasions

Denmark, attacked simultaneously by land, sea and air, capitulated within about six hours — the Danish government ordering a ceasefire to avoid the destruction of Copenhagen — and remained under a comparatively lenient occupation until 1943. In Norway, German naval groups seized Oslo, Bergen, Trondheim, Kristiansand and Narvik; the heavy cruiser Blücher was sunk by the Oscarsborg Fortress in the Oslofjord, delaying the occupation of the capital and allowing the king and government to escape.

The Norwegian campaign

Norwegian forces, joined by British, French and Polish expeditionary forces, resisted for two months. Allied troops landed near Narvik and recaptured the town on 28 May, the first German land defeat of the war, but the collapse of France forced an Allied evacuation (Operation Alphabet) in early June. King Haakon VII and his government went into exile in London; Norway was governed by a Reichskommissariat under Josef Terboven, with Vidkun Quisling's collaborationist regime.

Consequences

The campaign cost the German Kriegsmarine heavily — three cruisers and ten destroyers lost — crippling its surface fleet, but Germany secured the iron ore route, gained naval and air bases facing the North Atlantic, and occupied both countries until May 1945.
