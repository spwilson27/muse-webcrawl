Pilot ladder

A pilot ladder is a highly specialised form of rope ladder used on merchant ships to allow maritime pilots and other authorised persons to embark and disembark safely. Because pilot boats come alongside moving ships at sea, the ladder must meet strict international standards, and its rigging and use are regulated under the SOLAS Convention.

A pilot ladder rigged on a ship's side for a pilot to board from a pilot boat.

Construction

A pilot ladder consists of two side ropes of manila or equivalent material with hardwood or equivalent steps securely attached at regular intervals of about 310–350 mm. Every ninth step (approximately) is an elongated "spreader" step, at least 1.8 metres long, which prevents the ladder from twisting against the ship's side. Steps must be horizontal, non-slip, and free of defects; metal fastenings are positioned so as not to endanger the climber.

Use and safety

The ladder is rigged on the lee side where possible, clear of discharges and obstructions, with the bottom step a prescribed distance above the sea. When the freeboard exceeds nine metres, the ladder must be used in combination with an accommodation ladder. Pilots climb wearing lifejackets, often with a crew member standing by and a lifebuoy and heaving line ready. Despite the rules, falls from pilot ladders remain a recognised hazard, and pilotage organisations campaign for strict compliance with rigging standards.

Regulation

Requirements for pilot transfer arrangements are set out in SOLAS Chapter V, Regulation 23, supplemented by IMO resolutions and ISO standards specifying construction, marking, inspection, and maintenance of pilot ladders.
