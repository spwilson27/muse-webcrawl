Peter–Weyl theorem

In mathematics, the Peter–Weyl theorem is a basic result in the theory of harmonic analysis, applying to topological groups that are compact, but not necessarily abelian. It was initially proved by Hermann Weyl and his student Fritz Peter in the context of the group of invertible linear transformations of a finite-dimensional vector space (1927), and is the key structural theorem for compact groups.

Statement

The theorem has three main parts. Let G be a compact group with normalised Haar measure.

  1. Completeness of matrix coefficients. For every irreducible unitary representation (π, V) of G, the matrix coefficients g ↦ ⟨π(g)v, w⟩ span a subspace of the space of continuous functions on G; the union over all irreducible representations is dense in both the continuous functions (in the uniform norm) and in L²(G).
  2. Decomposition of the regular representation. The Hilbert space L²(G) decomposes as a completed direct sum over all isomorphism classes of irreducible unitary representations, each appearing with multiplicity equal to its dimension: L²(G) ≅ ⊕π Vπ ⊗ Vπ*.
  3. Approximation of the identity and of functions. Every continuous function on G can be uniformly approximated by "trigonometric polynomials" — finite linear combinations of matrix coefficients.

Significance

The Peter–Weyl theorem generalises Fourier series: for the circle group, the irreducible representations are one-dimensional and the theorem reduces to the completeness of the exponentials einx. For general compact groups it shows that there are "enough" finite-dimensional representations to separate points of the group, underpins the classification of representations of compact Lie groups, and yields the existence of a faithful finite-dimensional representation for compact linear groups. It also implies that every compact group is an inverse limit of Lie groups (hence, in a suitable sense, approximable by matrix groups).

History

Peter and Weyl published the result in 1927 in Mathematische Annalen, in the paper "Die Vollständigkeit der primitiven Darstellungen einer geschlossenen kontinuierlichen Gruppe". The proof made novel use of the spectral theory of compact self-adjoint operators on Hilbert space, applied to convolution operators on the group.
