Saṃsāra

Saṃsāra (Sanskrit: संसार; Pali: saṃsāra) is the cycle of birth, death and rebirth — the "wandering" of beings through successive lives — that is a central concept of Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism. In these traditions the phenomenal world, driven by karma and by ignorance, craving and aversion, is characterised by suffering (dukkha), and liberation from the cycle — moksha in Hinduism and Jainism, nirvana in Buddhism — is the ultimate spiritual goal.

In Hinduism

In Hindu thought the individual self (atman) transmigrates from body to body according to its karma, passing through lives as gods, humans, animals or other beings until it realises its identity with the ultimate reality (Brahman) and attains release. The doctrine was first articulated in the early Upanishads and is presupposed in the Bhagavad Gita, which describes the self discarding worn-out bodies as a person discards worn-out clothes.

In Buddhism

Buddhism teaches samsara without a permanent soul: rebirth is the continuation of a causal process conditioned by karma and the twelve links of dependent origination (pratitya-samutpada), depicted in the bhavacakra or wheel of life. Beings wander among six realms — gods, demigods (asuras), humans, animals, hungry ghosts (pretas) and hells — with human rebirth considered especially precious because it offers the best opportunity to practise the Dharma and achieve awakening. Samsara and nirvana are distinguished by the presence or absence of ignorance and craving; Mahayana philosophy, notably Nagarjuna, argued that they are not ultimately different realities.

In Jainism

Jainism holds that the soul (jiva) is bound to samsara by karmic matter accumulated through action; through the three jewels of right faith, right knowledge and right conduct — above all through ahimsa (non-violence) — the soul sheds its karma, rises to the top of the universe and is liberated forever.
