Military junta
From Spanish junta ("committee, council")
Type: Military government ruled by a committee of officers
Typical origin: Coup d'état

A military junta is a form of government in which the state is ruled by a committee of military leaders, usually after seizing power in a coup d'état. The word comes from the Spanish and Portuguese junta, meaning a committee or council.

Characteristics

Juntas typically suspend constitutions and parliaments, ban or restrict political parties, censor the press and rule by decree, justifying themselves as temporary guardians restoring order or defending the nation against corruption, communism or separatism. Some juntas are collegiate bodies of service chiefs; others are dominated by a single strongman. Their stated intention to return power to civilians is sometimes honoured after a transition, and sometimes indefinitely deferred.

Examples

Notable juntas include the Greek junta of 1967–74, Chile's Government Junta under Augusto Pinochet (1973–90), Argentina's National Reorganization Process (1976–83), the successive military councils of Myanmar, and Thailand's National Council for Peace and Order (2014–19). Military governments have been especially common in Latin America, Africa, the Middle East and Southeast Asia at various points in the twentieth and twenty-first centuries.
