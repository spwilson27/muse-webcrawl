Shakespeare's plays

The plays of William Shakespeare comprise some 38 works written between about 1585 and 1613, traditionally divided into comedies, histories, and tragedies following the classification of the First Folio (1623), the collection published by his colleagues John Heminges and Henry Condell. Modern scholarship often adds a fourth group, the late romances (or tragicomedies), for plays such as The Winter's Tale and The Tempest.

Major works

Famous tragedies include Hamlet, Othello, King Lear, Macbeth, Romeo and Juliet, Julius Caesar, and Antony and Cleopatra. Celebrated comedies include A Midsummer Night's Dream, Much Ado About Nothing, As You Like It, Twelfth Night, and The Merchant of Venice. The English histories dramatize the reigns from King John to Henry VIII, including the Henriad cycle and Richard III.

Texts and attribution

Plays circulated in quarto editions of varying quality during Shakespeare's lifetime; the First Folio preserved 18 plays that might otherwise have been lost. A few works are now thought to be collaborations, including The Two Noble Kinsmen and Pericles, Prince of Tyre (with George Wilkins) and parts of Henry VI. Shakespeare's plays have been translated into every major language and are performed more often than those of any other playwright.
