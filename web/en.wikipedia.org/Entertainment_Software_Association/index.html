The Entertainment Software Association (ESA) is the trade association of the video game industry in the United States. It was formed in 1994 as the Interactive Digital Software Association (IDSA) and renamed in 2003. Many of the largest companies in the video game industry are members, including publishers and console manufacturers.

Activities

The ESA conducts lobbying and government-relations work on issues such as intellectual property, censorship and trade; commissions annual research on game sales and demographics (the "Essential Facts" reports, produced with Circana); runs anti-piracy programmes; and funds the ESA Foundation, which awards scholarships. In litigation, the association was the lead industry party in Brown v. Entertainment Merchants Association (2011), in which the US Supreme Court held that video games are protected speech under the First Amendment.

ESRB

In 1994, amid congressional concern over violent games such as Mortal Kombat and Night Trap, the association founded the Entertainment Software Rating Board (ESRB), a self-regulatory body that assigns age and content ratings (E, E10+, T, M, AO) to games sold in North America.

E3

From 1995 the ESA organised the Electronic Entertainment Expo (E3), long the industry's flagship trade show held in Los Angeles. As major publishers withdrew in favour of their own digital showcases, E3 shrank, went online-only, and was permanently cancelled in December 2023.
