Peak expiratory flow

The peak expiratory flow (PEF), also called peak expiratory flow rate (PEFR), is a person's maximum speed of expiration, as measured with a peak flow meter, a small hand-held device used to monitor a person's ability to breathe out air. It measures the airflow through the bronchi and thus the degree of obstruction in the airways.

Peak expiratory flow
Measured with: Peak flow meter
Units: Litres per minute (L/min)
Used in: Asthma monitoring

Measurement

The patient blows as hard and fast as possible into the meter after a full inhalation; the best of three attempts is recorded. Values are compared with predicted values based on age, sex and height, and with the individual's personal best.

Clinical use

Peak flow monitoring is a standard part of asthma self-management. Diurnal variation and falls in PEF can warn of worsening airway obstruction before symptoms become severe, and action plans are often written around percentage zones (green, yellow and red) of personal best.

Limitations

PEF is effort-dependent and reflects mainly large-airway function; it complements rather than replaces spirometry measures such as FEV1.
