Casino Royale

First edition cover of Ian Fleming's Casino Royale (1953)

Casino Royale is the title of the first novel by the British author Ian Fleming, published in 1953, which introduced the character of James Bond. The name may also refer to several adaptations and other works.

The novel

In Fleming's novel, British secret agent James Bond is sent to bankrupt Le Chiffre, the treasurer of a French union and a member of the Soviet counter-intelligence organisation SMERSH, by out-gambling him at baccarat at the casino of the fictional French resort of Royale-les-Eaux. Assisted by Vesper Lynd, René Mathis, and CIA agent Felix Leiter, Bond wins the game, but is betrayed by Vesper — revealed to be a double agent — and the novel ends with her death and Bond's bitter concluding line, "The bitch is dead now." The spare, journalistic style of the novel founded the Bond formula and launched a series of twelve novels and two short-story collections by Fleming.

Adaptations

  • 1954 television adaptation — an episode of the American anthology series Climax!, starring Barry Nelson as an Americanised "Jimmy Bond."
  • Casino Royale (1967 film) — a satirical spy comedy produced by Charles K. Feldman, starring David Niven as Sir James Bond, with an ensemble cast including Peter Sellers, Ursula Andress, Orson Welles, and Woody Allen.
  • Casino Royale (2006 film) — the twenty-first film in the Eon Productions James Bond series and the debut of Daniel Craig as Bond, rebooting the franchise and adapting Fleming's novel with a high-stakes poker game at Casino Royale in Montenegro. It was the highest-grossing Bond film of its time and is widely credited with revitalising the series.

Other uses

"Casino Royale" is also the name of real-world casino resorts, including the Casino Royale Hotel & Casino on the Las Vegas Strip.
