Narvik

Narvik is a town and municipality in Nordland county, Norway, located on the shores of the Ofotfjorden, north of the Arctic Circle. It is best known as an ice-free port for the export of Swedish iron ore and for the Battles of Narvik in 1940.

History

The port was developed in the 1880s and 1890s when the Ofoten Line railway was built to carry iron ore from the mines at Kiruna in Sweden to an Atlantic harbour that, warmed by the Gulf Stream, remains free of ice all year. Narvik was founded as a town in 1902. In April and May 1940, during the Second World War, German forces seized the town and were opposed by Allied and Norwegian forces in a major naval and land campaign; the town was heavily damaged before being retaken and then abandoned as the Allies withdrew.

Geography and economy

Surrounded by mountains and fjords, Narvik is a centre for alpine skiing, notably at Narvikfjellet, and for transport: the E6 highway and the Ofoten Line (with its famous Rallarveien hiking route) pass through. Iron ore shipping through the port of Narvik remains central to the local economy.
