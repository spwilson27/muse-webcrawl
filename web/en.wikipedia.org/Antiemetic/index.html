Antiemetic

An antiemetic is a drug that is effective against vomiting and nausea. Antiemetics are typically used to treat motion sickness and the side effects of opioid analgesics, general anaesthetics, and chemotherapy directed against cancer. They may also be used for severe cases of gastroenteritis, especially if the patient is dehydrated.

Types

  • 5-HT3 receptor antagonists — such as ondansetron, block serotonin receptors in the gut and chemoreceptor trigger zone; widely used for chemotherapy-induced and postoperative nausea.
  • Dopamine antagonists — such as metoclopramide and domperidone, act on the chemoreceptor trigger zone.
  • NK1 receptor antagonists — such as aprepitant, used in chemotherapy regimens.
  • Antihistamines — such as dimenhydrinate and meclizine, used for motion sickness.
  • Anticholinergics — such as scopolamine (hyoscine), used for motion sickness.
  • Cannabinoids — such as dronabinol, used in some chemotherapy patients.
