Experian

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Experian plc is a multinational data analytics and consumer credit reporting company headquartered in Dublin, Ireland, and listed on the London Stock Exchange as a constituent of the FTSE 100 Index. Together with Equifax and TransUnion, it is one of the "Big Three" credit-reporting agencies. Experian collects and aggregates information on more than a billion people and businesses worldwide, and sells credit reports, credit scores, and data and analytics services to lenders, insurers, employers, and consumers.

History

The company's origins lie in credit reporting businesses in the United Kingdom and the United States, including the Manchester Guardian Society (1826) in Britain and TRW Information Systems in the United States, which compiled credit files on American consumers. These businesses came together under the British conglomerate GUS plc, and Experian was demerged as a separate listed company in 2006. Major operational centres are in Costa Mesa, California; Nottingham, England; and São Paulo, Brazil.

Operations and controversies

Experian's services include consumer credit monitoring, identity theft protection, fraud detection, and marketing data services. The company has faced criticism and regulatory action over the accuracy of credit files and the sale of consumer data. In 2015 it disclosed a breach of a server holding data on behalf of its client T-Mobile US, exposing personal information of about 15 million people; other incidents involving its subsidiaries' data have also been reported. Under laws such as the U.S. Fair Credit Reporting Act and the U.K. data protection regime, consumers have rights to obtain and dispute their files.
