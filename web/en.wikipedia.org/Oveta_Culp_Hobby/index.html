Oveta Culp Hobby

Born19 January 1905, Killeen, Texas
Died16 August 1995, Houston, Texas
Offices1st U.S. Secretary of Health, Education, and Welfare (1953–1955); Director of the Women's Army Corps

Oveta Culp Hobby (19 January 1905 – 16 August 1995) was an American government official and newspaper publisher. She was the first director of the Women's Army Auxiliary Corps (later the Women's Army Corps) during the Second World War and, in 1953, became the first Secretary of the U.S. Department of Health, Education, and Welfare and the second woman to serve in a U.S. presidential cabinet.

Early life and publishing

Born in Killeen, Texas, Hobby worked as a parliamentarian of the Texas House of Representatives and in journalism. She married William P. Hobby, a former governor of Texas and publisher of the Houston Post, where she rose to executive vice president and later publisher.

Public service

Recruited to the War Department in 1941, Hobby organised and led the women's corps, attaining the rank of colonel and receiving the Distinguished Service Medal. As HEW Secretary under President Dwight D. Eisenhower, she oversaw the distribution approval of the Salk polio vaccine; criticism of the vaccine rollout contributed to her resignation in 1955.
