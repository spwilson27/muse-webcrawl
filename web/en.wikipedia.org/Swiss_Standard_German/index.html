Swiss Standard German

Swiss Standard German (German: Schweizer Hochdeutsch or Schweizer Schriftdeutsch) is the variety of Standard German used in Switzerland as a written language and in formal spoken contexts. It differs from the standard German of Germany and Austria in vocabulary, spelling, pronunciation and some grammatical features, and it contrasts with the spoken Swiss German dialects used in everyday life, a situation known as diglossia.

Differences from Standard German

Swiss Standard German uses Helvetisms — words and expressions particular to Switzerland, such as Velo for bicycle, Billett for ticket, and parkieren for parking a car — many borrowed from French and Italian. In orthography, the letter ß is not used and is replaced by ss. Pronunciation features include the absence of glottal stops before initial vowels, different vowel qualities, and a rolled or uvular /r/ depending on region. Swiss Standard German is normally learned at school and used in education, news media, official documents and formal speech, while dialect dominates conversation.

Status

German is an official language of the Swiss Confederation (together with French, Italian and Romansh), and Swiss Standard German is the standard form codified for federal administration, differing in many usages from the standards used in Berlin or Vienna.
