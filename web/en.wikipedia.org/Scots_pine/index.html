Scots pine

Scots pine

Scientific name: Pinus sylvestris

Family: Pinaceae

Range: Europe and Asia

The Scots pine (Pinus sylvestris), also called the Scotch pine or Baltic pine, is a species of pine native to Europe and Asia, ranging from Scotland and Spain in the west to eastern Siberia in the east. It is the most widely distributed pine in the world and the national tree of Scotland.

Description

Scots pine is an evergreen conifer growing to 25–35 metres tall, with a distinctive orange-red, flaky bark on the upper trunk. Its blue-green needles grow in pairs and are 3–7 cm long. The cones are conical, 3–7.5 cm long, and mature about 20 months after pollination. Mature trees often have a long bare trunk topped by a rounded or flat-topped crown.

Ecology

The species grows on a wide range of soils and is an important component of boreal forests and the Caledonian Forest of the Scottish Highlands. It supports a variety of wildlife, including red squirrels, crossbills and capercaillie.

Uses

Scots pine is an important timber tree, yielding the wood traded as red deal or Baltic redwood, used in construction, furniture and paper pulp. It is also planted as an ornamental and as a Christmas tree in some regions.
