Lydia

Lydia
Region: western Anatolia (modern Turkey)
Capital: Sardis
Notable kings: Gyges, Alyattes, Croesus
Conquered by Persia: 546 BC

Lydia was an Iron Age kingdom in western Asia Minor, roughly modern Turkish provinces of Manisa and İzmir, famed for its wealth and for producing the world's first true coinage.

History

The Lydian kingdom rose after the fall of the Phrygian kingdom. Under the Mermnad dynasty — Gyges, Ardys, Sadyattes, Alyattes and Croesus — it subdued the Greek cities of the Anatolian coast and grew rich on trade and on the gold of the Pactolus river.

Coinage

The Lydians were the first to mint coins of electrum, later of pure gold and silver under Croesus, whose name became a byword for wealth ("rich as Croesus").

Fall and later history

In 546 BC Cyrus the Great of Persia defeated Croesus and captured Sardis. Lydia thereafter became a Persian satrapy, then passed to Hellenistic, Roman and Byzantine rule, remaining a distinct region name into the Roman province of Lydia.
