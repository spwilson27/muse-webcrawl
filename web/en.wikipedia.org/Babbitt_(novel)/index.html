Babbitt (novel)

Babbitt (1922), by Sinclair Lewis, is a satirical novel about American culture and society that critiques the vacuity of middle-class life and the social pressure toward conformity. Its protagonist is George F. Babbitt, a middle-aged real-estate broker in the fictional Midwestern city of Zenith.

Plot

Babbitt outwardly prospers and espouses boosterism and business values, but grows restless and briefly rebels — flirting with liberal politics and an extramarital affair — before social pressure returns him to conformity, though he encourages his son to resist it.

Legacy

The novel made "Babbitt" a common word for a smug, conventional bourgeois materialist and contributed to Lewis becoming the first American to win the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1930.
