Wood

Wood
CompositionCellulose, hemicellulose, lignin
SourceSecondary xylem of trees and shrubs
TypesHardwood, softwood
UsesConstruction, fuel, furniture, paper

Wood is a structural tissue found in the stems and roots of trees and other woody plants. It is an organic material — a natural composite of cellulose fibres held in a matrix of lignin — that has been used for thousands of years as fuel, a construction material, and a raw material for tools, furniture and paper.

Formation and structure

Wood is secondary xylem produced by the vascular cambium, a layer of dividing cells beneath the bark. Seasonal growth produces annual rings, which can be counted to date the tree (dendrochronology). The inner, darker heartwood is dead tissue that provides strength, while the outer sapwood conducts water and nutrients.

Hardwood and softwood

"Hardwood" comes from angiosperm (flowering) trees such as oak, maple and teak, while "softwood" comes from gymnosperms such as pine, spruce and cedar. The terms describe the botanical origin, not necessarily the actual hardness — balsa, a hardwood, is softer than most softwoods.

Uses

Wood is used in building construction (timber framing, plywood, engineered wood), furniture, flooring, shipbuilding, musical instruments, and as the raw material for pulp and paper. It remains a major source of fuel worldwide, burned directly or converted to charcoal. Its properties — strength, workability, insulation and renewability — keep it one of humanity's most important materials.
