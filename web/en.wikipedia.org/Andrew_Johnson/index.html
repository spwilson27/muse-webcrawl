Andrew Johnson (29 December 1808 – 31 July 1875) was the 17th president of the United States, serving from 1865 to 1869. He assumed the presidency following the assassination of Abraham Lincoln, as he was vice president at that time.

Early life and political rise

Born into poverty in Raleigh, North Carolina, Johnson was apprenticed as a tailor and never attended school, teaching himself to read. He settled in Greeneville, Tennessee, and rose through local and state politics to the US House of Representatives, the governorship of Tennessee, and the US Senate. A Southern Democrat who owned enslaved people, he nonetheless remained loyal to the Union during the Civil War and served as military governor of Tennessee. In 1864 Lincoln chose him as a running mate on the National Union ticket to broaden its appeal.

Presidency and impeachment

As president, Johnson clashed with the Republican-controlled Congress over Reconstruction. His lenient terms for the former Confederate states and his vetoes of civil rights legislation for freedpeople led Congress to override him and pass the Reconstruction Acts. In 1868 the House of Representatives impeached him, largely over his removal of Secretary of War Edwin Stanton; the Senate acquitted him by a single vote.

Later life

Johnson returned to the US Senate from Tennessee in 1875, the only former president to serve there, but died of a stroke a few months later. Historians have generally ranked his presidency among the weakest, chiefly for his opposition to the rights of freed slaves.
