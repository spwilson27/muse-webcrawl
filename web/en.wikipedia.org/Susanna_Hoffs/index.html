Susanna Lee Hoffs (born January 17, 1959) is an American singer, guitarist, songwriter, actress, and author. She is best known as a co-founder of the pop-rock band the Bangles, with whom she sang lead vocals on hits including "Manic Monday", "Walk Like an Egyptian", "Eternal Flame", and "In Your Room".

Early life

Hoffs was born in Los Angeles, California, the daughter of psychoanalyst Joshua Hoffs and writer-director Tamar Simon Hoffs. She studied dance and art, graduating from the University of California, Berkeley, in 1980 with a degree in art, before turning to music.

The Bangles

In 1981 Hoffs co-founded the Bangles (initially the Bangs) with sisters Vicki and Debbi Peterson. Part of Los Angeles' Paisley Underground scene, the band signed to Columbia Records and achieved international success in the mid-to-late 1980s. Hoffs sang the ballad "Eternal Flame" (1989), co-written with Billy Steinberg and Tom Kelly, which became one of the band's biggest hits. The group disbanded in 1989 and re-formed in 1998–99.

Solo career and other work

Hoffs released solo albums including When You're a Boy (1991) and Susanna Hoffs (1996), and recorded three volumes of Under the Covers (2006–2016) with Matthew Sweet, performing songs of the 1960s, 1970s, and 1980s. She contributed "Now and Then" to the final Beatles-era releases discourse via her 2023 recording, and published her debut novel, This Bird Has Flown, in 2023. She also appeared in the Austin Powers films (as part of Ming Tea, with her husband, director Jay Roach, involved in the series).
