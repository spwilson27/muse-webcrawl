The Yoga Sutras of Patanjali (Sanskrit: योगसूत्र, Yogasūtra) is a collection of 195 Sanskrit sutras — terse aphorisms — on the theory and practice of yoga, attributed to the sage Patanjali. It is the foundational text of the Yoga school (darśana) of Indian philosophy and defines what is often called Raja Yoga, the "royal" path of meditation. Yoga is famously defined in its second sutra as yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ — "yoga is the stilling of the fluctuations of the mind."

Structure

The text is divided into four books (padas): Samadhi Pada (on contemplation), Sadhana Pada (on practice), Vibhuti Pada (on powers), and Kaivalya Pada (on liberation). In the second book Patanjali sets out the celebrated eight limbs (aṣṭāṅga) of yoga: yama (ethical restraints), niyama (observances), asana (posture), pranayama (breath control), pratyahara (withdrawal of the senses), dharana (concentration), dhyana (meditation), and samadhi (absorption).

Philosophy

The text's metaphysics is dualist, drawing on Samkhya: pure consciousness (purusha) is entangled with material nature (prakriti), and liberation (kaivalya) consists in discriminative discernment between them. A famous commentary, the Yogabhashya attributed to Vyasa, was composed alongside the sutras, and dozens of further commentaries followed.

Influence

Though medieval yoga traditions developed largely around other texts, the Yoga Sutras were rediscovered in the 19th century and promoted by figures such as Swami Vivekananda, becoming the most influential classical source for modern yoga worldwide. The text's dating remains debated, with proposals spanning from the 5th century BCE to the 4th century CE.
