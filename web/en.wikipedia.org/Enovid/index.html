Enovid

Enovid
Manufacturer: G. D. Searle
Approved: 1960 (contraception, US)
Components: Mestranol and norethynodrel

Enovid was the first combined oral contraceptive pill approved for contraceptive use. Developed by G. D. Searle & Company, it contained mestranol and norethynodrel.

Development

The pill grew from research by Gregory Pincus, Min Chueh Chang and John Rock, with funding from Margaret Sanger and Katharine McCormick. Clinical trials were conducted in Puerto Rico in the 1950s.

Approval and impact

The US Food and Drug Administration first approved Enovid for menstrual disorders in 1957 and for contraception in 1960. Its availability transformed reproductive medicine and had wide social effects, though early formulations used much higher hormone doses than modern pills and carried greater side-effect risks.
