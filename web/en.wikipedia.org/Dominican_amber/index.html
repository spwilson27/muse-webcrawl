Dominican amber

Dominican amber is amber from the Dominican Republic derived from the resin of the extinct tree Hymenaea protera. It is mined in the Cordillera Septentrional and other mountain ranges of the country, and dates broadly to the Miocene, roughly 15 to 20 million years ago.

Characteristics

Dominican amber is notable for its clarity and for the abundance and variety of its fossil inclusions — insects, arachnids, plant material, and occasionally small vertebrates — which make it an important source of information about ancient tropical forest ecosystems. Natural colors range from yellow and honey to rare blue and green varieties; Dominican blue amber fluoresces a striking blue under ultraviolet light.

Mining

Amber is extracted from hand-dug pits and tunnels in the mountains, often under hazardous conditions. The Dominican Republic is, along with the Baltic region, one of the world's major sources of amber.
