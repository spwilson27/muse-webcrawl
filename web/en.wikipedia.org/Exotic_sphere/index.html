Exotic sphere

An exotic sphere is topologically a sphere but carries a non-standard smooth structure.

In differential topology, an exotic sphere is a differentiable manifold that is homeomorphic but not diffeomorphic to the standard Euclidean n-sphere. In other words, it is a sphere from the point of view of all its topological properties, but carries a smooth structure that is not the familiar one.

History

The first exotic spheres were constructed by John Milnor in 1956, who exhibited seven distinct smooth structures on the 7-sphere — a result that surprised the mathematical community and earned him the Fields Medal in 1962. Michel Kervaire and Milnor then showed that the oriented 7-sphere has exactly 28 differentiable structures. Exotic spheres exist in many dimensions but not in dimensions 1, 2, 3, 5 or 6; whether an exotic 4-sphere exists remains one of the major open problems related to the smooth Poincaré conjecture in dimension four.

Construction

Standard constructions glue two balls together along their boundary by a diffeomorphism that cannot be extended smoothly to the interior, or use Brieskorn spheres — intersections of small spheres with complex hypersurfaces having isolated singularities — which realise many exotic structures.
