Submersible

A submersible is a small underwater vehicle that, unlike a submarine, is typically supported by a surface vessel or platform and has limited endurance and mobility. Submersibles are used for oceanographic research, deep-sea exploration, salvage, and tourism.

Submersible
Type: Underwater vehicle
Support: Surface vessel required
Examples: Alvin, Trieste, Limiting Factor

Design

Crewed submersibles protect occupants in a strong pressure hull, often a titanium sphere, surrounded by buoyancy material such as syntactic foam. They manoeuvre with electric thrusters and carry lights, cameras, sonar, and manipulator arms. Uncrewed remotely operated vehicles (ROVs) and autonomous underwater vehicles serve similar roles without risking a crew.

Famous dives

The bathyscaphe Trieste reached the bottom of the Mariana Trench in 1960. The research submersible Alvin, operated by Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution, has explored hydrothermal vents and the wreck of the Titanic. In 2019 the Limiting Factor completed dives to the deepest points of all five oceans.

Safety

Deep submersibles face enormous pressure, and the 2023 implosion of the tourist submersible Titan renewed scrutiny of certification and safety standards in the industry.
