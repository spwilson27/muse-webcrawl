Schedule 13D

Schedule 13D is a form that United States Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) rules require anyone who acquires beneficial ownership of more than 5% of a class of publicly traded securities to file, disclosing the size of their stake and their intentions. It is commonly known as a beneficial ownership report. The requirement was created by the Williams Act of 1968, which amended the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 to police the then-unregulated practice of accumulating large blocks of stock as a prelude to a takeover.

Requirements

A filer must disclose, among other things: their identity and background; the source and amount of funds used; the purpose of the acquisition, including any plans to merge, liquidate, change the board or management, or alter the company's capitalization; their full holdings and recent transactions; and any contracts or understandings with other persons regarding the securities. Amendments (Schedule 13D/A) must be filed promptly upon any material change—acquiring or disposing of 1% or more of the class is deemed material. Under rules adopted in 2023 and effective in October of that year, the initial filing deadline was shortened from 10 days to five business days after crossing the 5% threshold, and amendment deadlines were likewise tightened.

Schedule 13G

Certain investors may instead file the short-form Schedule 13G: qualified institutional investors, and passive investors who hold less than 20% and acquired the securities without any purpose or effect of changing or influencing control of the issuer. An investor who develops control intentions must switch to Schedule 13D. Because filing a 13D signals possible activism, Schedule 13D filings are watched closely by arbitrageurs, journalists, and target-company boards; activist investors such as hedge funds frequently use the filing—and the "Item 4" statement of purpose—as the opening move in a campaign for board seats or strategic change.
