Lady Day

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Lady Day is the nickname of the American jazz singer Billie Holiday (born Eleanora Fagan; 7 April 1915 – 17 July 1959). The nickname was given to her by her friend and musical partner, tenor saxophonist Lester Young, who was himself nicknamed "Prez" by Holiday. "Lady Day" has since become her enduring sobriquet in jazz history.

Usage

The name appears widely in works about Holiday: her 1956 autobiography was titled Lady Sings the Blues, and the 1972 biographical film of the same name starred Diana Ross. The play Lady Day at Emerson's Bar and Grill (1986), by Lanie Robertson, dramatizes one of Holiday's final performances in a Philadelphia bar in 1959. In Britain, "Lady Day" also traditionally denotes 25 March, the Feast of the Annunciation of the Virgin Mary, historically the start of the legal and financial year in England.
