The Mongol invasion of Java was a punitive expedition launched in 1292–1293 by Kublai Khan of the Yuan dynasty against the island of Java, in present-day Indonesia. It was prompted by the insult offered to Kublai's envoy Meng Qi, who was tattooed on the face — the treatment of a criminal — by Kertanagara, king of Singhasari, when demanding Java's submission. The campaign is famous for its ironic outcome: the invaders destroyed the usurping kingdom of Kediri but were then outmanoeuvred and expelled by their local ally, Raden Wijaya, who used the victory to found the Majapahit empire.

Background

By the time the Yuan fleet of some 20,000–30,000 men sailed from Quanzhou, the political situation in Java had changed: Kertanagara had been killed in 1292 by Jayakatwang, ruler of Kediri, who seized the Singhasari throne. Kertanagara's son-in-law, Raden Wijaya, fled to Madura and then offered his services to the arriving Mongols, promising submission to the Yuan in exchange for help against Jayakatwang.

Campaign

The combined Yuan and Javanese forces advanced inland in 1293, defeated Jayakatwang's armies and stormed his capital at Daha; Jayakatwang surrendered and was later executed. Having fulfilled his purpose, Wijaya asked leave to return to his territory of Majapahit to prepare the formal submission — then ambushed the Mongol escort sent with him and turned on his allies. Harried by attacks, tropical disease and the approaching end of the sailing season, the Yuan commanders Shi Bi and Gao Xing withdrew to their fleet and sailed for China with their prisoners and plunder.

Aftermath

Kublai, dissatisfied with the expedition's ambiguous result, punished its commanders, though they were later pardoned. Wijaya crowned himself ruler of Majapahit in late 1293, founding the empire that would dominate the Indonesian archipelago for two centuries. The invasion was the Yuan dynasty's last major overseas expedition, closing the era of Mongol expansion into Southeast Asia begun by the Mongol Empire's conquests.
