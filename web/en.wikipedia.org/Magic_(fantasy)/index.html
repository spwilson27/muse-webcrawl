Magic (fantasy)

Magic, in the context of fantasy fiction, is the fictional use of supernatural powers within an imagined world. It is one of the defining elements of the fantasy genre, appearing in literature, film, tabletop games, and video games, where it is typically governed by internal rules that structure how characters can wield it.

Magic systems

Authors often distinguish between loosely defined "soft" magic, whose limits remain mysterious to characters and readers alike, and rule-based "hard" magic, whose costs and capabilities are spelled out. The writer Brandon Sanderson popularised this distinction with his informal "laws" of magic systems, arguing that a reader's satisfaction depends on how well the author foreshadows a magic's capabilities before using it to resolve conflict.

Common forms

Fantasy magic commonly includes spellcasting with spoken incantations or gestures, enchanted objects such as wands and rings, potions and alchemy, divination, summoning, shapeshifting, and elemental manipulation of fire, water, earth, and air. Practitioners are variously called wizards, witches, sorcerers, mages, or warlocks, and their power may derive from innate talent, study, divine favour, or pacts with supernatural beings.

Origins and influences

Fictional magic draws heavily on real-world magical traditions, folklore, and mythology, as well as on literary sources such as the Arthurian legends and the works of J. R. R. Tolkien, whose wizards in The Lord of the Rings set a template for the genre. In role-playing games, beginning with Dungeons & Dragons, magic was systematised into spell lists and levels, an approach widely imitated in video games through mechanics such as mana points.
