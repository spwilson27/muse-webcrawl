Astor Place


The Alamo (Astor Place Cube) is a landmark of the plaza.

Astor Place is a short street and plaza in the East Village and NoHo neighbourhoods of Manhattan, New York City, running between Broadway and Lafayette Street near Cooper Square. It is named for John Jacob Astor, once the richest man in the United States, whose estate occupied the area.

History

The area was developed in the 1820s–40s as the fashionable Lafayette Place district. In 1849 the Astor Place Riot erupted at the Astor Opera House, when rivalry between fans of the actors Edwin Forrest and William Macready turned into a deadly clash in which more than twenty people were killed. The Astor Library, founded with John Jacob Astor's bequest, later became part of the New York Public Library; its building now houses the Public Theater. The neighbourhood is closely associated with Cooper Union, founded nearby in 1859.

Landmarks

The plaza is dominated by Tony Rosenthal's rotating cube sculpture Alamo (1967). The Astor Place subway station on the Lexington Avenue line retains its original 1904 faience plaques depicting beavers, a nod to Astor's fur-trade fortune. A major redesign completed in 2016 pedestrianised much of the area.
