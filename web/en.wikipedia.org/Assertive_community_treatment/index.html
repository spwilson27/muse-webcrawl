Assertive community treatment

FieldPsychiatry / community mental health
Developed1970s, Madison, Wisconsin, USA
Population servedPeople with severe mental illness

Assertive community treatment (ACT) is a model of community-based mental health care for people with severe mental illnesses such as schizophrenia. A multidisciplinary team provides comprehensive, integrated services directly in the community — in clients' homes and neighbourhoods rather than in a clinic — with low staff-to-client ratios and round-the-clock availability.

Model

ACT teams typically include psychiatrists, nurses, social workers, substance-abuse specialists, and vocational specialists who share a caseload of roughly 100 clients, with each client served by the team as a whole. Services include medication management, assistance with housing and daily living, crisis intervention, and support for employment. Outreach is "assertive": the team actively seeks out clients who disengage rather than waiting for them to attend appointments.

Evidence and history

The model was developed in the 1970s in Madison, Wisconsin, by providers adapting hospital-based treatment for community settings during deinstitutionalisation. Numerous studies have found that ACT reduces hospital admissions and homelessness and improves housing stability for people with the most severe needs, and it has been adopted widely in the United States, the United Kingdom, and elsewhere.
