Adsorption

Adsorption is the adhesion of atoms, ions, or molecules from a gas, liquid, or dissolved solid to a surface, forming a film of the adsorbate on the adsorbent. It differs from absorption, in which a substance diffuses into the bulk of another material. Adsorption is a surface phenomenon driven by surface energy, and is generally reversible.

Mechanisms

In physisorption, weak van der Waals forces hold the adsorbate, and multiple layers may form; it is described by isotherms such as the Langmuir and BET models. In chemisorption, chemical bonds form between adsorbate and surface, usually limiting uptake to a monolayer. Adsorption increases with adsorbent surface area, so highly porous materials such as activated carbon, silica gel, and zeolites are effective adsorbents.

Applications

Adsorption is used in gas masks and air purification, water treatment, chromatography, refrigeration cycles, heterogeneous catalysis (where adsorption of reactants is an essential step), and gas storage and separation, including pressure swing adsorption for producing oxygen and nitrogen.
