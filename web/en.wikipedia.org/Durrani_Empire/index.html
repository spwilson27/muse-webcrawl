The Durrani Empire (Pashto: د درانيانو واکمني) was an Afghan empire founded in 1747 by Ahmad Shah Durrani. At its greatest extent it ruled over modern Afghanistan, Pakistan, northeastern Iran, and parts of Central Asia and India including Kashmir and Punjab, and it is regarded as the founding state of modern Afghanistan.

Foundation

After the assassination of Nader Shah of Persia in 1747, Ahmad Shah, a young commander of the Abdali Pashtuns, was chosen as king at a loya jirga in Kandahar. Crowned with a sheaf of wheat, he took the title Durr-i-Durran ("Pearl of Pearls"), and the Abdali became known as the Durrani. His treasury was famously boosted by capturing a caravan of treasure looted from Delhi.

Expansion

Ahmad Shah campaigned repeatedly in India, sacking Delhi and Mathura in 1757 and defeating the Maratha Empire at the Third Battle of Panipat in 1761, one of the largest battles of the eighteenth century. He also took Mashhad, Herat, Kabul, Peshawar, Lahore, Kashmir and Sindh. Under his son Timur Shah the capital moved to Kabul. After Timur's death in 1793, succession struggles among his many sons weakened the empire; it lost Punjab to the Sikhs under Ranjit Singh, and the empire fragmented, ending with the Barakzai takeover in 1826.

Legacy

Ahmad Shah is honoured in Afghanistan as Ahmad Shah Baba, father of the nation. The Durrani Empire established the territorial and political foundations of the modern Afghan state.
