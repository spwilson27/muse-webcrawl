Singapore, officially the Republic of Singapore, is an island country and city-state in Southeast Asia, situated at the southern tip of the Malay Peninsula, about 137 km north of the equator. It comprises one main island and 63 satellite islands. Singapore is among the world's most densely populated countries and one of its busiest ports and leading financial centres.

History

The island, known to early mariners as Temasek, was part of the Srivijaya and later Majapahit spheres, and by legend was named Singapura ("Lion City") by the prince Sang Nila Utama. In 1819 Stamford Raffles founded a British trading post, and Singapore became a Straits Settlement and a great free port, drawing Chinese, Indian, Malay and Arab migrants. It fell to Japan in February 1942 in one of Britain's worst military defeats, and was reoccupied in 1945.

After self-government in 1959 under Lee Kuan Yew's People's Action Party, Singapore merged with Malaysia in 1963 but was expelled on 9 August 1965, becoming a sovereign republic. Under Lee and his successors it transformed from a developing port into a high-income economy built on trade, manufacturing, finance and shipping, with extensive public housing and a reputation for low corruption and strict law enforcement.

Geography and government

Singapore has a tropical rainforest climate with no distinct seasons. Land reclamation has expanded its area by roughly a quarter since independence. It is a unitary parliamentary republic; the president is head of state and the prime minister heads the government. The People's Action Party has governed continuously since 1959.

Economy and society

With one of the world's highest GDPs per capita, Singapore's economy rests on entrepôt trade, electronics and chemicals manufacturing, financial services, biotechnology and tourism. Its four official languages reflect its multicultural population of Chinese, Malay, Indian and other communities, with English as the lingua franca.
