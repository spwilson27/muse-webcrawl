Methuselah

Methuselah (Hebrew: מְתוּשֶׁלַח, Məṯûšélaḥ) is a biblical patriarch mentioned in the Book of Genesis. He is famous for having lived longer than any other figure in the Bible: according to Genesis 5:27, he died at the age of 969 years.

Biblical account

Methuselah was the son of Enoch, the father of Lamech, and the grandfather of Noah. Genesis places him in the genealogy from Adam to Noah; on the Masoretic chronology, he died in the year of the Flood. He also appears in the genealogy of Jesus in the Gospel of Luke and is mentioned in the apocryphal Book of Enoch.

Interpretations

Scholars have offered various explanations for the extreme lifespans of the antediluvian patriarchs, including translation differences between the Masoretic Text, Septuagint and Samaritan Pentateuch (which give Methuselah ages of 969, 969 and 720 years respectively), symbolic or calendrical readings of the numbers, and the possibility that "years" originally denoted shorter periods.

Cultural legacy

Methuselah's name has become a byword for extreme old age ("as old as Methuselah"). It is also given to a ~4,850-year-old bristlecone pine in California, the 6-litre wine bottle format, and the Methuselah star (HD 140283), once thought to be older than the universe.
