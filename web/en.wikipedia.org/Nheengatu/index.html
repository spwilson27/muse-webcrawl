Nheengatu

Nheengatu (also called Modern Tupi, Amazonian Lingua Geral, or língua geral amazônica) is a Tupian language of Brazil, developed from the Tupinambá language and used historically as a lingua franca throughout colonial Amazonia.

History

Jesuit missionaries codified Tupinambá in the 16th–17th centuries into the língua geral ("general language"), which spread along the Amazon and Rio Negro as the main language of contact between Indigenous peoples, missionaries and settlers. Although Portuguese administration suppressed it from the 18th century, Nheengatu survived in the upper Rio Negro region. Today it is spoken by roughly ten to twenty thousand people, is co-official in the municipality of São Gabriel da Cachoeira (since 2002), and is taught in local schools.
