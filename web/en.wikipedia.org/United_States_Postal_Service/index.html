United States Postal Service

The United States Postal Service (USPS) is an independent agency of the executive branch of the U.S. federal government responsible for providing postal service throughout the United States. It operates one of the world's largest civilian vehicle fleets and delivers to more than 160 million addresses.

History

American postal service dates to 1775, when the Second Continental Congress appointed Benjamin Franklin the first Postmaster General. The Post Office Department was a cabinet department until the Postal Reorganization Act of 1970 transformed it into the modern, self-financing USPS in 1971.

Operations

USPS is legally obligated to serve all Americans at uniform rates, a universal service obligation that sustains delivery to remote rural addresses. It processes and delivers a very large share of the world's mail and a growing volume of e-commerce parcels, competing with private carriers while retaining a statutory monopoly on letter delivery and mailbox access.

Finances

The agency is intended to be self-supporting through postage and product revenue. Long-running debates over its finances, service standards, and network modernization plans have kept postal reform a recurring subject in Congress.
