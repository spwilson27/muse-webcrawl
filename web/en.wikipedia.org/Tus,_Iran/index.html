Tus, Iran

The mausoleum of Ferdowsi at Tus

Tus (Persian: توس or طوس, Ṭūs) is an ancient city in Razavi Khorasan Province, Iran, near modern Mashhad. It was a major city of Khorasan in the Islamic Golden Age and is best known as the home and burial place of the poet Ferdowsi, author of the Shahnameh.

History

Known in Greek sources as Susia, Tus was captured by Alexander the Great in 330 BCE. It became an important city under the Parthian and Sasanian empires and was conquered by Muslim Arab armies in the 7th century CE. The Abbasid caliph Harun al-Rashid died at Tus in 809 and was buried there. The city flourished in the medieval period as a centre of learning and trade; the theologian al-Ghazali was born (in the district of Tabaran) and buried there.

Tus suffered devastation in the Mongol invasions of the 13th century and further destruction by the Timurid prince Miran Shah in 1389, after which it declined, overshadowed by nearby Mashhad, which grew around the shrine of Imam Reza. Today Tus is largely an archaeological site.

Notable figures and sites

  • Ferdowsi (c. 940–1020) — poet of the Shahnameh, buried at Tus, where a grand mausoleum was built in the 20th century.
  • Al-Ghazali — theologian and philosopher, born in Tus in 1058.
  • Nasir al-Din al-Tusi — polymath whose family name derives from Tus.
  • Haruniyeh — a domed mausoleum traditionally associated with Harun al-Rashid.
