Fifth Generation Computer Systems

CountryJapan
Launched1982
Lead institutionICOT (MITI)

The Fifth Generation Computer Systems (FGCS) was a ten-year initiative begun by Japan's Ministry of International Trade and Industry (MITI) in 1982 to create computers using massively parallel computing and logic programming. It aimed to leapfrog Western computing by building machines that could reason, process natural language, and learn.

Background

The "generations" of computers were counted from vacuum tubes (first), transistors (second), integrated circuits (third), and microprocessors (fourth). Japan, then a rising economic power, sought to dominate the anticipated fifth generation with parallel inference machines running the language KL1, a concurrent logic programming language derived from Prolog.

Outcome

The project spent over ¥57 billion and produced specialised parallel hardware, but commercial workstations soon matched its performance, and the project concluded in 1992 without achieving its commercial goals. It nevertheless influenced research in parallel computing and alarmed Western governments into launching rival programmes.
