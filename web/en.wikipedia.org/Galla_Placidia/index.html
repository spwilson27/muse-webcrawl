Aelia Galla Placidia (388–389 – 27 November 450) was the daughter of the Roman emperor Theodosius I and his second wife Galla. She was regent for her son, the emperor Valentinian III, from 425 until 437, and a major force in Roman politics for most of her life.

Early life and captivity

Born in Constantinople, Placidia was raised largely in the household of the general Stilicho. She was in Rome during Alaric's siege and was captured when the Visigoths sacked the city in 410. She married Alaric's successor Ataulf in 414 in Narbonne; after his assassination she was returned to the Romans in exchange for grain.

Empress and regent

In 417 she married the general Constantius, who briefly reigned as Constantius III in 421. Widowed again, she clashed with her half-brother Honorius and fled to Constantinople. When Honorius died, she returned with an eastern army, defeated the usurper Joannes, and had her young son proclaimed Valentinian III in 425. As regent she contended with the rival generals Aëtius, Bonifatius, and Felix, whose feuds destabilised the western empire.

Patronage and legacy

Placidia was a devoted Christian and a notable church builder. The so-called Mausoleum of Galla Placidia in Ravenna, with its luminous blue and gold mosaics, is among the best-preserved monuments of early Christian art.
