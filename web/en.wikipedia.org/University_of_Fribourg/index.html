The University of Fribourg (French: Université de Fribourg; German: Universität Freiburg) is a public university in the city of Fribourg, Switzerland. Founded in 1889, it is the only bilingual university in Switzerland, offering courses and degrees in both French and German, and is known for its strong faculties of law, theology, humanities, economics and science.

The Miséricorde campus building, designed by Denis Honegger.

History

The university was founded in 1889 by the cantonal government of Fribourg, building on a tradition of higher learning that included a Jesuit college founded by Saint Peter Canisius in 1582. The Faculty of Theology, entrusted to the Dominican Order, became a centre of Catholic scholarship, and the university developed a distinctively international and Catholic character while remaining a public institution of the canton. In 1941 the landmark Miséricorde building, designed by architect Denis Honegger, a student of Auguste Perret, was opened.

Bilingualism

Fribourg lies on the language border between French-speaking and German-speaking Switzerland. Students may study entirely in French or German, or follow bilingual programmes and obtain a bilingual degree notation. The university has promoted this model as an embodiment of Swiss multilingualism, and also offers an increasing number of courses in English, particularly at master's level.

Faculties

The university comprises faculties of Law; Theology; Humanities; Science and Medicine; and Economics and Social Sciences. Research strengths include human rights, multilingualism, nanoscience and materials research at the Adolphe Merkle Institute, and interdisciplinary work on Europe at the Institute of Federalism. The university hospital and medical programme were expanded in the twenty-first century with the introduction of a full master's degree in medicine.

Notable people

Associated figures include the philosopher Józef Maria Bocheński, who served as rector, the theologian Hans Küng (briefly), and several Swiss federal councillors and judges. The university's alumni include Nobel laureate poet and writer in the broader Fribourg scholarly milieu, and numerous European jurists trained in its bilingual law programme.
