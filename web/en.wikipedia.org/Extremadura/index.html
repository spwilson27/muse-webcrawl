Extremadura

Extremadura is a landlocked autonomous community of Spain, in the west of the country, bordering Portugal. Its capital is Mérida, and its largest city is Badajoz. The region comprises the provinces of Cáceres and Badajoz and covers about 41,634 km², making it one of Spain's largest but most sparsely populated regions.

Geography

The Tagus and Guadiana rivers cross the region, which is known for its dehesa landscapes of holm and cork oak, and for Monfragüe National Park, a noted birdwatching area.

History

Extremadura was the homeland of many conquistadors, including Hernán Cortés and Francisco Pizarro. The Augustan colony of Emerita Augusta (Mérida) preserves major Roman remains, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
