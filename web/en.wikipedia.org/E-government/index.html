E-government

Electronic government (or e-government) is the application of information and communication technologies (ICTs) for delivering government information and services to citizens, businesses, government employees and other governments. It uses digital interactions—through websites, mobile applications, email, and self-service kiosks—to improve efficiency, transparency and citizen participation. The term encompasses government-to-citizen (G2C), government-to-business (G2B), government-to-government (G2G) and government-to-employee (G2E) interactions.

Development

E-government grew with the commercial internet in the 1990s, progressing from simple publication of information online, through transactional services such as online tax filing, licence renewal and benefit claims, to integrated portals and, in leading countries, proactive and personalised "digital government" services. Estonia is frequently cited as a pioneer, with its X-Road data exchange layer, national e-ID and services such as i-Voting. The United Nations E-Government Development Index (EGDI) ranks member states; Denmark, Finland, South Korea, New Zealand and Estonia have consistently ranked near the top.

Benefits and challenges

Advocates cite reduced administrative cost, faster service delivery, reduced corruption through transparency, and greater citizen engagement. Challenges include the digital divide—excluding citizens without internet access or digital literacy—privacy and cybersecurity risks, interoperability between agencies, and the cost and failure risk of large IT programmes. E-government is closely related to concepts of open government, open data and e-democracy.

Forms

Forms of e-government include online public services, mobile government (m-government), electronic voting, e-procurement systems such as public tender portals, and participatory platforms for consultation and petitioning.
