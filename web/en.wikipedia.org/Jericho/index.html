Jericho

Arabic: أريحا (Arīḥā)

Governorate: Jericho Governorate, Palestine

Elevation: −258 m (lowest city on Earth)

Population: about 20,000

Jericho is a city in the West Bank, Palestine, in the Jordan Valley, with the Jordan River to the east and Jerusalem to the west. Lying 258 metres below sea level, it is the lowest permanently inhabited site on Earth and is often described as the oldest continuously inhabited city in the world, with evidence of settlement dating back some 11,000 years.

History

The mound of Tell es-Sultan preserves more than twenty successive settlements. The Neolithic tower of Jericho, built around 8000 BC, is among the earliest stone monuments of humanity, and the site's massive fortifications gave rise to the biblical account of the Battle of Jericho in the Book of Joshua, in which the city's walls are said to have fallen at the sound of trumpets. Excavations by Kathleen Kenyon in the 1950s transformed understanding of the early Neolithic Levant.

Modern city

Today Jericho is an agricultural centre known for its dates, bananas and citrus, and a modest tourist destination: visitors ride a cable car to the Mount of Temptation monastery, and sites include Hisham's Palace and the Zacchaeus sycamore tree. Under the Oslo Accords it was the first city transferred to Palestinian self-rule in 1994.
