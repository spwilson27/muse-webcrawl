Montgolfier brothers

Joseph-Michel Montgolfier (26 August 1740 – 26 June 1810) and Jacques-Étienne Montgolfier (6 January 1745 – 2 August 1799) were paper manufacturers from Annonay, in the Ardèche region of France, best known as inventors of the Montgolfière-style hot air balloon, globe aérostatique. On 21 November 1783, their balloon carried out the first confirmed piloted ascent by man, carrying Jean-François Pilâtre de Rozier and François Laurent d'Arlandes on a flight over Paris.

Background

The brothers were born into a family of paper manufacturers who had founded their business in 1534. Joseph, the twelfth of sixteen children, was an inventive, impractical thinker; Étienne had a more practical, businesslike disposition and ran the family firm, introducing innovations such as Dutch-style paper production techniques to France.

The invention

According to family tradition, Joseph conceived the idea of the balloon while watching laundry drying over a fire and observing pieces billowing upward, or while contemplating the French military problem of assaulting the fortress of Gibraltar. In November 1782, experimenting at Avignon, he made a small box-like chamber of thin taffeta and lifted it to the ceiling by lighting paper beneath it.

The brothers progressed to larger unmanned balloons of linen and paper, first demonstrated publicly on 4 June 1783 at Annonay, where a balloon of about 35 feet in diameter rose an estimated 1,000–2,000 metres. News reached Paris, and the brothers were summoned to demonstrate before the king. On 19 September 1783 at Versailles, a balloon carried aloft a sheep, a duck, and a rooster — the first living passengers — in the presence of Louis XVI and Marie Antoinette.

The first human flight

On 21 November 1783, in Paris, Pilâtre de Rozier and the Marquis d'Arlandes made the first free (untethered) human flight, travelling about 9 kilometres in roughly 25 minutes in a Montgolfier balloon decorated in blue and gold with signs of the zodiac and the royal cipher. Benjamin Franklin, then American ambassador, was among the spectators.

Later life and legacy

Rivalry with the hydrogen balloons of Jacques Charles soon eclipsed the hot-air design in early ballooning, but the brothers continued their paper business and inventions; Joseph later invented the hydraulic ram and the parachute was tested from one of his balloons. Étienne died in 1799 and Joseph in 1810. Their name endures in the French word montgolfière for a hot air balloon, and hot-air ballooning remains popular worldwide.
