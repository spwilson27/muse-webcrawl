The Free Aceh Movement (Indonesian: Gerakan Aceh Merdeka, GAM) was a separatist movement seeking independence for the Aceh region of Sumatra, Indonesia, from 1976 until the peace settlement of 2005. Its armed wing fought the Indonesian military in a conflict that claimed an estimated 15,000 lives.

History

GAM was founded by Hasan di Tiro, a descendant of Acehnese sultans, who declared Acehnese independence on 4 December 1976. Grievances included Jakarta's control of Aceh's large natural-gas revenues, Javanese transmigration, and human rights abuses, especially during the military operations zone (DOM) period of 1989–98. An initial rebellion was crushed, with di Tiro fleeing to Sweden, but the movement revived strongly after Suharto's fall in 1998.

Peace settlement

After a failed cessation-of-hostilities agreement (2002–03) and a major Indonesian offensive, the devastating December 2004 tsunami helped push both sides to talks mediated by former Finnish president Martti Ahtisaari. The Helsinki Memorandum of Understanding of 15 August 2005 granted Aceh extensive self-government; GAM decommissioned its weapons and its members entered politics, with former rebels winning provincial elections.
