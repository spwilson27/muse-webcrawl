Frequency is the number of occurrences of a repeating event per unit of time. It is also occasionally referred to as temporal frequency to emphasise the contrast to spatial frequency, and ordinary frequency to emphasise the contrast to angular frequency. Frequency is measured in hertz (Hz), which is equal to one event per second.

Definitions and units

The period T is the duration of one cycle of a repeating event, so the period is the reciprocal of the frequency:

f = 1 / T

For periodic motion such as oscillations and waves, the frequency can be computed by counting the number of cycles in a known time interval and dividing. The hertz is named after the German physicist Heinrich Hertz, who made important contributions to electromagnetism.

Related quantities

Angular frequency ω, measured in radians per second, is related to ordinary frequency by ω = 2πf. For electromagnetic waves in vacuum, frequency relates to wavelength λ through the speed of light: f = c/λ. Spatial frequency measures how often a structure repeats per unit distance.

Examples

Human hearing covers roughly 20 Hz to 20 kHz. The mains electricity supply oscillates at 50 or 60 Hz depending on the country. Radio waves span from a few hertz to hundreds of gigahertz, while visible light has frequencies around 400–790 terahertz. The A above middle C used for tuning orchestras is conventionally 440 Hz.

Measurement

Frequency can be measured with frequency counters, stroboscopes, or by analysing a sampled signal with Fourier methods. The definition of the second itself rests on counting the cycles of radiation from the caesium-133 atom at 9,192,631,770 Hz.
