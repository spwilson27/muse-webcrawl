Area postrema

The area postrema is a paired structure in the medulla oblongata of the brainstem, located at the caudal end of the fourth ventricle. It is one of the circumventricular organs, whose capillaries lack a normal blood–brain barrier, allowing it to sample substances circulating in the blood.

Function

The area postrema acts as a chemoreceptor trigger zone: it detects toxins and other emetic agents in the blood and cerebrospinal fluid and helps initiate vomiting as a protective response. It is also involved in cardiovascular regulation and in sensing hormones that regulate food intake and fluid balance.

Clinical significance

Lesions or inflammation of the area postrema, as in neuromyelitis optica, can cause intractable nausea, vomiting, and hiccups ("area postrema syndrome").
