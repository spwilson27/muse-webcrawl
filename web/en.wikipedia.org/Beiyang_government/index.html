Beiyang government

The Beiyang government was the internationally recognised government of the Republic of China between 1913 and 1928, based in Beijing. It is named after the Beiyang Army, which dominated its politics with the rise of Yuan Shikai, a general of the Qing government, as president.

History

After Yuan Shikai's death in 1916, China fragmented into warring cliques of the Beiyang Army — principally the Zhili, Anhui and Fengtian cliques — and the Beijing government changed hands repeatedly during the Warlord Era. Despite the instability, the period saw advances in education, industry and diplomacy, including China's participation in the First World War on the Allied side. The government was finally displaced in 1928 by Chiang Kai-shek's Northern Expedition, after which the Nationalist government in Nanjing was recognised internationally.
