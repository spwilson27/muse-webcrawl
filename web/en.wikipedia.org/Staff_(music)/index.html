Staff (music)

The five-line staff with treble and bass clefs.

In Western musical notation, the staff (also called a stave) is a set of five horizontal lines and four spaces that each represent different musical pitches. Notes are placed on the lines and spaces according to pitch, with their vertical position determined by the clef at the beginning of the staff. The modern staff is the foundation of the system of musical notation used worldwide.

History

The staff developed from the neumatic notation of Gregorian chant. The Italian Benedictine monk Guido d'Arezzo (c. 991–c. 1033) is credited with introducing staff lines—first one, then four—and the use of letters and colours to identify pitches, a revolutionary improvement that allowed melodies to be learned without hearing them first. By the 13th century the five-line staff had become standard.

Elements

Each staff begins with a clef (treble, bass, alto or tenor), followed by a key signature and time signature. Short additional lines called ledger lines extend the staff above and below for higher and lower notes. Bar lines divide the staff into measures. Multiple staves joined together form a system; the grand staff, combining treble and bass staves joined by a brace, is used for piano music. A musical score may contain many systems.

Usage

Different instruments read different clefs according to their ranges. Transposing instruments read parts written at a different pitch from their sounding pitch. Although alternatives such as tablature exist for some instruments, staff notation remains the universal standard of Western art and popular music.
