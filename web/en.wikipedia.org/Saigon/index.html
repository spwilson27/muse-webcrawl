Ho Chi Minh City (Saigon)

Ho Chi Minh City, formerly (and still informally) known as Saigon, is the largest city in Vietnam, with a population of around nine million in the city proper and over twenty million in its metropolitan area. Located in southeastern Vietnam on the Saigon River, it is the country's economic and financial centre.

History

Originally a Khmer settlement called Prey Nokor, the area became Vietnamese in the 17th–18th centuries and was formally named Gia Định. The French conquered Saigon in 1859 and made it the capital of Cochinchina, reshaping it with colonial architecture including the Saigon Central Post Office and Notre-Dame Basilica. From 1955 to 1975 Saigon was the capital of the Republic of Vietnam (South Vietnam). After the Fall of Saigon on 30 April 1975, the city was renamed in 1976 for Hồ Chí Minh, though "Saigon" remains in everyday use and denotes the central districts.

Economy and geography

The city is Vietnam's principal commercial hub, with major industry, trade and services, and Tan Son Nhat International Airport is the country's busiest airport. Its low-lying riverine geography faces seasonal flooding and land subsidence challenges.
