Yakov Grigorevich Sinai (born September 21, 1935) is a Russian-American mathematician known for his foundational work on dynamical systems, ergodic theory, mathematical physics and probability theory. He received the Abel Prize in 2014 "for his fundamental contributions to dynamical systems, ergodic theory, and mathematical physics".

Life and career

Sinai was born in Moscow into a family of scientists; his grandfather was the mathematician Veniamin Kagan. He studied at Moscow State University, earning his doctorate in 1960 under Andrey Kolmogorov. He worked at Moscow State University and the Landau Institute for Theoretical Physics, and after emigrating to the United States in 1993 became a professor at Princeton University.

Work

With Kolmogorov, he introduced the notion of Kolmogorov–Sinai entropy, a fundamental invariant measuring the unpredictability of a dynamical system. His study of Sinai billiards — a particle bouncing inside a square with a circular obstacle — provided the first rigorous example of a deterministic chaotic system and a model connecting mechanics with statistical physics. He also made major contributions to phase transitions, the theory of random walks, and the mathematical foundations of statistical mechanics.

Awards

Besides the Abel Prize and the Wolf Prize in Mathematics (1997), Sinai received the Boltzmann Medal, the Dirac Prize, the Poincaré Prize and the Nemmers Prize, and is a member of numerous national academies.
