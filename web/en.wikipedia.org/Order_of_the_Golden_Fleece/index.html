Order of the Golden Fleece

The Order of the Golden Fleece (Spanish: Orden del Toisón de Oro; German: Orden vom Goldenen Vlies) is a Catholic order of chivalry founded in Bruges in 1430 by Philip the Good, Duke of Burgundy, to celebrate his marriage to Isabella of Portugal. It became one of the most prestigious orders in Europe, associated successively with the Burgundian, Spanish and Austrian Habsburgs.

History

Named for the Golden Fleece of Greek myth sought by Jason and the Argonauts, the order was limited to a small number of knights of impeccable lineage and Catholic faith. With the Burgundian inheritance it passed to the Habsburgs, and after the War of the Spanish Succession two branches emerged: a Spanish order, conferred by the Spanish monarch, and an Austrian order, headed by the head of the House of Habsburg.

Insignia

The badge is a golden fleece hanging from a flintstone emitting flames, worn on a collar of interlocked flints and sparks or, in less formal dress, on a red ribbon. The order's mottoes include Pretium non vile laborum ("No mean reward for labours").

Today

The Spanish branch remains a dynastic order of the Spanish crown, awarded to Spanish and foreign royalty and heads of state. The Austrian branch continues under the head of the House of Habsburg-Lorraine. Both count among the most senior chivalric orders in the world.
