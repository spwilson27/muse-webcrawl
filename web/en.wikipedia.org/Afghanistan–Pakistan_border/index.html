Afghanistan–Pakistan border

Length2,670 km (1,660 mi)
Established1893 (Durand Line)
Major crossingsTorkham, Chaman–Spin Boldak

The Afghanistan–Pakistan border is 2,670 kilometres (1,659 mi) long and runs from the tripoint with Iran to the tripoint with China. It follows the Durand Line, established in 1893 by an agreement between British India and the Emirate of Afghanistan. The border divides the Pashtun and Baloch ethnic regions and has been a persistent source of tension between the two countries.

History

The line was drawn after the Second Anglo-Afghan War and confirmed in later Anglo-Afghan treaties, including the Treaty of Rawalpindi (1919). Successive Afghan governments have disputed the line's permanence, while Pakistan regards it as the settled international border.

Crossings and fencing

Major official crossings include Torkham in the Khyber Pass and Chaman–Spin Boldak. Beginning in 2017, Pakistan constructed a border fence along most of the line, citing militant infiltration, which drew protests from Kabul. Trade and migration across the border remain vital to communities on both sides.
