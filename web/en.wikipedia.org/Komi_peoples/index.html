Komi peoples

Regions: Komi Republic, Perm Krai, Russia
Language: Komi (Zyryan, Permyak), Russian
Religion: Russian Orthodox Christianity

The Komi are a Permic ethnic group indigenous to the northeastern European part of Russia, primarily the Komi Republic and the Komi-Permyak Okrug of Perm Krai. They speak the Komi language, a member of the Permic branch of the Finno-Ugric languages.

Subgroups and language

The Komi comprise two principal subgroups: the Komi-Zyryans of the north and the Komi-Permyaks of the south, whose literary languages, Komi-Zyryan and Komi-Permyak, differ mainly in dialect. Komi is written in Cyrillic; an older script devised by Saint Stephen of Perm in the 14th century, known as Abur, was among the earliest alphabets created for a Uralic language.

History

The Komi lands were gradually incorporated into the Novgorod Republic and then Muscovy. Christianity spread from the mission of Stephen of Perm in the late 14th century. In the Soviet era the Komi Autonomous Oblast (1921) became an autonomous republic. Censuses in recent decades record a declining population, numbering roughly 300,000–350,000 people who identify as Komi.
