Urban area

An urban area is characterized by continuous built-up development and high population density.

An urban area, built-up area, or urban agglomeration is a human settlement with a high population density and developed infrastructure — buildings, roads, and services — as opposed to rural surroundings. Definitions vary by country, but urban areas generally comprise a city or town and its contiguous suburbs, irrespective of administrative boundaries.

Definitions

National statistical offices define urban areas differently: the United States Census Bureau delineates urban areas as continuously built-up territory with at least 2,000 housing units or 5,000 residents (under its 2020 criteria); the United Kingdom's Office for National Statistics uses built-up land with a population of at least 10,000; and countries such as Sweden classify any settlement of 200 or more inhabitants as urban. Urban areas differ from metropolitan areas, which are defined by commuting flows and may include rural land.

Global urbanization

According to the United Nations, about 57% of the world's population lived in urban areas in the mid-2020s, a share projected to reach roughly 68% by 2050. The largest urban areas by population include Tokyo, Delhi, Shanghai, Dhaka, and São Paulo, each exceeding 20 million inhabitants. The fastest urban growth is in Africa and Asia, where many cities expand informally.

Characteristics

Urban areas concentrate employment, education, health care, and cultural amenities, and typically have higher incomes than rural areas, but also face congestion, air pollution, heat-island effects, and housing affordability problems. Urban planning, public transport, and zoning govern their development.
