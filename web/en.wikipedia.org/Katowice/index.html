Katowice is a city in Upper Silesia in southern Poland, capital of the Silesian Voivodeship and the centre of a large industrial and metropolitan region. It grew in the nineteenth century as a German city (Kattowitz) founded on coal mining and steel, and passed to Poland after the Silesian plebiscite and uprisings following the First World War.

History and economy

Katowice received city rights in 1865 and developed into a hub of heavy industry in the Prussian and then Polish state. During the communist era it was briefly renamed Stalinogród (1953–1956). After 1989 the region's coal and steel industries contracted sharply, and Katowice has reinvented itself as a centre of services, technology, conferences, and culture.

Culture

The city's best-known landmark is the Spodek ("Saucer") arena, opened in 1971, a landmark of Polish modernism that hosts major sporting and esports events, including the Intel Extreme Masters. Other institutions include the Polish National Radio Symphony Orchestra's acclaimed concert hall (opened 2014), the Silesian Museum built on a former mine site, the Nikiszowiec workers' housing district, and the annual OFF and Tauron Nowa Muzyka festivals. Katowice hosted the UN Climate Conference (COP24) in 2018.
