Lunar maria
Singular: mare (Latin, "sea")
Composition: Basaltic lava plains
Coverage: about 16% of the lunar surface
Notable: Mare Imbrium, Mare Tranquillitatis, Oceanus Procellarum

The lunar maria (singular mare) are the large, dark, basaltic plains on Earth's Moon, formed by ancient asteroid impacts that triggered volcanic eruptions. Early astronomers mistook them for actual seas, hence their Latin name.

Formation

The maria formed roughly 3 to 3.5 billion years ago when lava flooded giant impact basins, chiefly on the Moon's near side, where the crust is thinner. Their basalt is richer in iron and magnesium than the lighter anorthositic highlands, which makes them appear dark.

Distribution

Maria cover about 31% of the near side but only about 2% of the far side. The largest, Oceanus Procellarum, spans more than 2,500 km. Mare Tranquillitatis was the landing site of Apollo 11 in 1969.
