Treaty of Wuchale

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Treaty of Wuchale (also spelled Uccialli) was a treaty signed between Ethiopia, under Emperor Menelik II, and the Kingdom of Italy on 2 May 1889 in the town of Wuchale (Uccialli) in the Ethiopian Empire. The treaty granted Italy territory in the northern highlands, which became the colony of Eritrea, in exchange for recognition of Menelik's rule and a loan.

Article 17 controversy

The treaty became notorious for a discrepancy between its Amharic and Italian texts. Article 17 in Amharic stated that the Emperor could conduct foreign affairs through Italy if he so wished; the Italian text stated that he must do so, which Italy interpreted as establishing a protectorate over Ethiopia. Italy notified the other European powers of its supposed protectorate.

Consequences

When the deception became clear, Menelik denounced the treaty in 1893. Diplomatic rupture led to the First Italo-Ethiopian War, decided by Ethiopia's victory at the Battle of Adwa on 1 March 1896. By the Treaty of Addis Ababa (1896) Italy recognised Ethiopia's absolute independence, while retaining Eritrea.
