Chang'e 4
OperatorChina National Space Administration (CNSA)
Launch7 December 2018
Landing3 January 2019, Von Kármán crater
ComponentsLander and Yutu-2 rover; Queqiao relay satellite

Chang'e 4 is a Chinese lunar exploration mission that achieved humanity's first soft landing on the far side of the Moon. Its lander and the Yutu-2 rover touched down in Von Kármán crater, within the South Pole–Aitken Basin, on 3 January 2019. Because the Moon's far side never faces Earth, communications were relayed by the Queqiao satellite stationed at the Earth–Moon L2 point.

Mission

The lander carried instruments to measure lunar radio emissions free of Earth's interference, analyse surface composition, and observe radiation. A small biosphere experiment germinated a cotton seed — the first plant to sprout on the Moon. Yutu-2 went on to become the longest-lived lunar rover, exploring for years beyond its design life and studying subsurface layers with ground-penetrating radar, including material possibly excavated from the lunar mantle.

Names

The mission is named after Chang'e, the Chinese goddess of the Moon; the rover name Yutu-2 ("Jade Rabbit 2") follows its predecessor on Chang'e 3, and Queqiao means "Magpie Bridge".
