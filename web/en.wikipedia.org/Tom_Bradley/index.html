Thomas Bradley (29 December 1917 – 29 September 1998) was an American politician and police officer who served as the 38th mayor of Los Angeles from 1973 to 1993. He was the city's first and, for decades, only African American mayor, and its longest-serving.

Early life and police career

Born in Texas to sharecropper parents, Bradley grew up in Los Angeles, attended UCLA, and joined the Los Angeles Police Department in 1940, rising to lieutenant despite the department's racial barriers. While still an officer he earned a law degree; he left the force in 1961, practised law, and was elected to the city council in 1963.

Mayoralty

Bradley lost the 1969 mayoral race to Sam Yorty but defeated him in 1973 with a multiracial coalition, and was re-elected four times. His tenure brought the 1984 Summer Olympics to Los Angeles, downtown redevelopment and skyscraper growth, expansion of the airport and port, and the beginnings of a modern transit system. He twice ran unsuccessfully for governor of California (1982 and 1986); the narrow 1982 loss gave rise to the term "Bradley effect". His final term was scarred by the 1992 Los Angeles riots following the Rodney King verdicts. The international terminal at Los Angeles International Airport is named for him.
