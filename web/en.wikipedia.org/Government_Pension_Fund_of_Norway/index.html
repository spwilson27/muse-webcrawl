Government Pension Fund of Norway


Norges Bank, Oslo, manages the fund's global investments.

The Government Pension Fund of Norway (Norwegian: Statens pensjonsfond) comprises two sovereign wealth funds owned by the government of Norway: the Government Pension Fund Global (GPFG), commonly called the Oil Fund (Oljefondet), and the smaller, domestically invested Government Pension Fund Norway (GPFN).

Government Pension Fund Global

The GPFG was established in 1990 as the Petroleum Fund to deposit surplus revenues from Norway's petroleum sector and to shield the domestic economy from oil-price volatility. The first capital was transferred in 1996. Invested entirely abroad in equities, bonds and real estate, it grew into the world's largest sovereign wealth fund, exceeding NOK 18 trillion (about USD 1.7 trillion) in 2024 and holding on average around 1.5% of all listed companies worldwide. It is managed by Norges Bank Investment Management (NBIM) under a mandate from the Ministry of Finance. A fiscal rule (handlingsregelen) adopted in 2001 limits annual government spending of oil revenues to the fund's expected real return, estimated at 3%.

Ethics

An independent Council on Ethics recommends exclusions of companies involved in, among other things, severe environmental damage, gross corruption, production of tobacco, and certain weapons. The fund's exclusion list — covering more than a hundred companies — is published, and its holdings, votes and returns are disclosed in exceptional detail, making it a benchmark for sovereign fund transparency.
