World government

World government would place global affairs under a single political authority.

World government is the concept of a single political authority with jurisdiction over all of Earth and humanity. It is conceived variously as a federation of states, a world republic, or a strengthened system of international institutions capable of making and enforcing law on global issues such as war, climate change, pandemics, and weapons of mass destruction. No such government exists; the United Nations is an intergovernmental organization whose authority rests on treaties among sovereign states.

History of the idea

Visions of universal rule go back to antiquity — the empires of Alexander the Great and Rome, Dante's De Monarchia, and the Chinese concept of Tianxia. Immanuel Kant's essay Perpetual Peace (1795) proposed instead a federation of free republics bound by cosmopolitan law. The trauma of two world wars produced the League of Nations and then the United Nations, alongside an organized world federalist movement; figures such as Albert Einstein argued that only a supranational authority could control atomic weapons.

Arguments for and against

Supporters argue that global problems exceed the capacity of nation-states acting alone, and that a democratic world federation could prevent war and enforce human rights. Critics warn that a single global authority would risk tyranny with no possibility of exit, would struggle to represent eight billion people democratically, and that cultural and political diversity makes one-size-fits-all rule dangerous. Intermediate proposals include reforming the UN Security Council, creating a United Nations Parliamentary Assembly, and expanding the jurisdiction of international courts.

In fiction

World government is a staple of science fiction, appearing in works from H. G. Wells's The World Set Free to the United Earth of Star Trek and the dystopian regimes of Aldous Huxley's Brave New World.
