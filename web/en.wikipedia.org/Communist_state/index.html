Communist state

A communist state is a state governed by a single communist party, typically organised on the principles of Marxism–Leninism and democratic centralism. Although such states generally describe themselves as socialist states building toward communism, the term "communist state" is standard in Western political science for states in which a communist party holds a monopoly of political power.

Characteristics

Communist states characteristically combine the leading role of the ruling party, state or collective ownership of the means of production, central economic planning (in the classical model), and a party-controlled apparatus of mass organisations covering trade unions, youth, and cultural life. Constitutions typically enshrine the party's leading role.

History

The first communist state was the Russian Soviet Federative Socialist Republic, established after the October Revolution of 1917 and incorporated into the Soviet Union in 1922. At their greatest extent in the 1980s, communist states covered roughly one-third of the world's population, including the Soviet Union, China, the Eastern European people's democracies, Cuba, Vietnam, and others. Most European communist states collapsed in 1989–1991.

Current communist states

Five states are generally classed as communist states today: China, Cuba, Laos, North Korea, and Vietnam. Of these, China and Vietnam have introduced extensive market reforms under continued one-party rule ("socialist market economy" and Đổi Mới respectively), while Cuba and Laos have undertaken more limited openings.
