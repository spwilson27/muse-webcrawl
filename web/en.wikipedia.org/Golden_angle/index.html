Golden angle

Golden angle
Value≈ 137.5077640° (2.39996323 rad)
Related toGolden ratio, phyllotaxis

In geometry, the golden angle is the smaller of the two angles created by dividing the circumference of a circle by the golden ratio; that is, into two arcs such that the ratio of the length of the larger arc to the length of the smaller arc is the same as the ratio of the full circumference to the length of the larger arc.

Derivation

Let the circumference be divided into a longer arc of length a and a shorter arc of length b, with (a + b)/a = a/b = φ, the golden ratio. The golden angle, subtended by the shorter arc, is then 360°/(1 + φ) = 360°/φ² ≈ 137.508°. In radians it equals 2π/φ².

Role in nature

The golden angle plays a central role in phyllotaxis, the arrangement of leaves, seeds, and florets in plants. Successive primordia on a growing stem emerge separated by approximately the golden angle, which packs them with remarkable efficiency and avoids periodic alignment, because φ is the irrational number "hardest" to approximate by rationals. This produces the familiar spiral patterns in sunflower heads and pinecones.
