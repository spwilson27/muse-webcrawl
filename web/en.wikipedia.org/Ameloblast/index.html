Ameloblast

Ameloblasts are cells present only during tooth development that deposit tooth enamel, which is the hardest substance in the human body and forms the outer protective layer of the tooth crown. Ameloblasts are derived from oral epithelium tissue of ectodermal origin.

Ameloblast
Location: Developing tooth
Function: Enamel formation
Stage: Bell stage of tooth development

Development

Ameloblasts differentiate from the inner enamel epithelium of the enamel organ during the bell stage of tooth development. Before they can secrete enamel matrix, the cells become polarized, elongate, and develop a specialized secretory extension known as Tomes' process. Their differentiation depends on reciprocal signaling with the adjacent odontoblasts, which produce the first layer of dentin.

Structure

Each ameloblast is a tall columnar cell roughly 4 micrometers in diameter and 40 micrometers in length. The secretory end of the cell forms a six-sided pyramidal structure called Tomes' process, which controls the orientation of the enamel rods (prisms) that give enamel its strength. As enamel matures, ameloblasts lose their secretory process and transition through ruffle-ended and smooth-ended phases that modulate pH and transport calcium into the maturing enamel.

Function

During the secretory stage, ameloblasts secrete enamel matrix proteins including amelogenin, ameloblastin, and enamelin. During the maturation stage they help remove water and organic material while increasing the mineral content of the enamel. Once tooth eruption is complete, the ameloblast layer is lost, which is why enamel cannot be regenerated naturally after damage.

Clinical significance

Defects in ameloblast function or in the genes encoding enamel proteins can cause amelogenesis imperfecta, a group of inherited conditions affecting the quantity and quality of enamel. Residual ameloblast-like cells are also associated with certain odontogenic tumors, such as ameloblastoma, although that tumor arises from odontogenic epithelium rather than mature ameloblasts.
