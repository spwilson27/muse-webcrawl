Diego Rodríguez de Silva y Velázquez (baptised 6 June 1599 – 6 August 1660) was a Spanish painter, the leading artist in the court of King Philip IV of Spain and the most important artist of the Spanish Golden Age. He was an individualistic artist of the Baroque period, famous for his virtuoso brushwork and profound portraits.

Career

Trained in Seville under Francisco Pacheco, Velázquez painted bodegones (kitchen scenes) in his youth before moving to Madrid in 1623, where he became Philip IV's court painter — a position he held for the rest of his life, rising through court offices to become chamberlain of the palace. Two journeys to Italy (1629–31 and 1649–51) deepened his art; in Rome he painted his penetrating Portrait of Innocent X.

Las Meninas

His masterpiece, Las Meninas (1656), is one of the most analysed paintings in Western art: it depicts the Infanta Margarita Teresa surrounded by her entourage in the artist's studio, with Velázquez himself at work on a large canvas and the king and queen reflected in a mirror.

Legacy

Velázquez's loose, rapid facture and optical realism profoundly influenced later painters, especially the French Impressionists; Édouard Manet called him "the painter of painters". His surviving oeuvre comprises roughly 110–120 known works.
