Alvin York

Alvin Cullum York (December 13, 1887 – September 2, 1964), better known as Sergeant York, was one of the most decorated United States Army soldiers of World War I. He received the Medal of Honor for leading an attack on a German machine gun nest during the Meuse–Argonne offensive on October 8, 1918, in which his small group killed at least 25 enemy soldiers and captured 132.

Early life

York was born in a two-room log cabin in Pall Mall, Tennessee, in the Wolf River valley, the third of eleven children. A skilled marksman from hunting, he was a hard-drinking young man until a religious conversion in 1914 made him a devout member of a pacifist-leaning church. When drafted in 1917 he wrestled with conscientious objection before concluding that military service was compatible with his faith.

The action of October 8, 1918

Serving with the 82nd Division, Corporal York was part of a detachment sent to silence machine guns raking the American advance near Châtel-Chéhéry, France. When the patrol was pinned down and several men killed, York took command and, shooting with rifle and pistol, forced a large body of German soldiers to surrender; the captured group totalled 132 prisoners marched back to American lines. He was promoted to sergeant and awarded the Medal of Honor.

Later life

York returned to national fame, declined many commercial offers, and devoted himself to improving education in his home region, founding the York Institute in Jamestown, Tennessee. His story was popularized by the 1941 film Sergeant York, starring Gary Cooper, which York authorized partly to aid his charitable work. He died in 1964 and is buried in Pall Mall.
