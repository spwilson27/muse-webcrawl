Houston is the most populous city in the U.S. state of Texas and the fourth-most populous city in the United States, with about 2.3 million residents. Founded in 1836 by the Allen brothers on Buffalo Bayou and named for General Sam Houston, it briefly served as capital of the Republic of Texas.

Economy

Houston is a global centre of the energy industry, particularly oil and natural gas, and home to the world's largest medical complex, the Texas Medical Center. The Port of Houston is among the busiest in the United States, and NASA's Johnson Space Center — mission control for human spaceflight — gave the city its "Space City" nickname; "Houston" was the first word spoken from the Moon in 1969.

Geography and culture

Located in Southeast Texas near the Gulf of Mexico, Houston has a humid subtropical climate and is prone to flooding, most catastrophically during Hurricane Harvey in 2017. It is one of the most ethnically diverse large cities in the United States, notable for having no formal zoning code, a major museum and theatre district, and professional teams including the Astros (MLB), Texans (NFL) and Rockets (NBA).
