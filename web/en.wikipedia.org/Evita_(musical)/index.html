Evita is a musical with music by Andrew Lloyd Webber and lyrics by Tim Rice. It concentrates on the life of Argentine political leader Eva Perón, wife of President Juan Perón, following her rise from poverty to power, her charity work, and her early death.

History

The musical began as a rock opera concept album released in 1976, starring Julie Covington as Eva, before opening in London's West End in 1978, directed by Harold Prince and starring Elaine Paige. The Broadway production opened in 1979 with Patti LuPone in the title role and won seven Tony Awards, including Best Musical.

Synopsis

Narrated by Che, a critical observer modelled partly on Che Guevara, the sung-through musical traces Eva's journey to Buenos Aires, her marriage to Perón, her popularity with the working class, and her death at 33. Its best-known song is "Don't Cry for Me Argentina".

Film adaptation

A film adaptation was released in 1996, directed by Alan Parker and starring Madonna as Eva, Antonio Banderas as Che and Jonathan Pryce as Perón. The song "You Must Love Me", written for the film, won the Academy Award for Best Original Song.
