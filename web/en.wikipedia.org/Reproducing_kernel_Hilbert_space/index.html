Reproducing kernel Hilbert space

In functional analysis, a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) is a Hilbert space of functions in which point evaluation is a continuous linear functional: for each point x there is a constant such that |f(x)| ≤ Cx‖f‖. Spaces of this kind arise wherever one studies functions through their inner products — integral equations, approximation theory, probability, and machine learning.

The reproducing kernel

By the Riesz representation theorem, continuity of evaluation guarantees a function K(x, ·) in the space such that f(x) = ⟨f, K(x, ·)⟩ for every f — the reproducing property. The two-variable function K(x, y), the reproducing kernel, is symmetric (Hermitian) and positive semi-definite. Conversely, the Moore–Aronszajn theorem states that every positive semi-definite kernel determines a unique RKHS of which it is the reproducing kernel.

Examples

The Hardy space H² on the unit disc has the Szegő kernel 1/(1 − xȳ); the Bergman space has the Bergman kernel. The Sobolev space H¹(ℝ) is an RKHS with a kernel built from a decaying exponential. Finite-dimensional examples include ℝn with its standard inner product.

Machine learning

RKHS theory underlies kernel methods. The representer theorem shows that minimising a regularised empirical risk over an RKHS yields a solution that is a finite linear combination of kernel functions centred on the training points — the principle behind support vector machines and kernel ridge regression, and the basis of the "kernel trick".
