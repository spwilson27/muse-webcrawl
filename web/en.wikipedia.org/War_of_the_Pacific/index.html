War of the Pacific

The naval campaign decided control of the nitrate coast.

War of the Pacific
Date5 April 1879 – 20 October 1883 (treaty with Peru)
LocationPacific coast of South America (Peru, Bolivia, northern Chile)
BelligerentsChile vs Peru and Bolivia
ResultChilean victory; Chile annexes Tarapacá and Antofagasta; Bolivia loses its coastline

The War of the Pacific (1879–1884), also known in Spanish as the Guerra del Pacífico, was fought between Chile and a Bolivian–Peruvian alliance over control of the nitrate-rich Atacama Desert coast. Chile's victory redrew the map of western South America: it gained the Peruvian province of Tarapacá and the Bolivian province of Antofagasta, leaving Bolivia landlocked — a grievance that endures to this day.

Origins

The dispute arose from Bolivian taxation of Chilean nitrate companies operating in Antofagasta, contrary to an 1874 treaty. When Bolivia seized the assets of the Chilean-owned Antofagasta Nitrate and Railway Company in February 1879, Chile occupied the port of Antofagasta. Bolivia declared war, invoking its secret 1873 defensive alliance with Peru, and Chile declared war on both countries on 5 April 1879.

Course of the war

Chile first had to win command of the sea. The naval campaign turned on the capture of the Peruvian ironclad Huáscar at the Battle of Angamos (8 October 1879), after her commander Miguel Grau had raided the Chilean coast for months. Chilean forces then invaded Tarapacá, won the battles of Dolores and Tacna (1880), destroyed the Peruvian fleet's remnants, and in January 1881 stormed and occupied Lima after the battles of Chorrillos and Miraflores. A gruelling guerrilla resistance continued in the Peruvian sierra until the Treaty of Ancón (1883) ended the war with Peru; Bolivia signed a truce in 1884 and a definitive treaty in 1904.

Consequences

Chile controlled the world's richest nitrate fields, fuelling a half-century export boom. Peru descended into political turmoil and civil conflict; Bolivia's loss of the sea became a permanent national cause, commemorated annually on the Día del Mar and pursued diplomatically — including a case at the International Court of Justice, rejected in 2018.
