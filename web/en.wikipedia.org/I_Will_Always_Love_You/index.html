I Will Always Love You

"I Will Always Love You" is a song written and originally recorded in 1973 by American singer-songwriter Dolly Parton. Written as a farewell to her business partner and mentor Porter Wagoner, it was released in 1974 as a single from Parton's album Jolene and topped the US country singles chart. Whitney Houston recorded a soul-ballad arrangement of the song for the 1992 film The Bodyguard; her version spent a then-record 14 weeks at number one on the Billboard Hot 100 and became one of the best-selling singles of all time.

Houston's 1992 recording is one of the best-selling singles by a female artist.

Background and Dolly Parton versions

Parton wrote the song on the same day she wrote "Jolene", as a parting gesture when she left The Porter Wagoner Show and their partnership to pursue a solo career. Her recording reached number one on the Billboard Hot Country Songs chart in 1974 and again in 1982, when she re-recorded it for the film The Best Little Whorehouse in Texas, making her the first artist to reach number one with the same song in two different recordings. Elvis Presley later wanted to record it, but Parton refused the standard requirement of surrendering half the publishing rights. The song returned to prominence again in 1995 as a duet with Vince Gill.

Whitney Houston version

Houston's recording, produced by David Foster, opens with an a cappella introduction and builds to a key-changing climax with a saxophone solo by Kirk Whalum. It was the lead single from The Bodyguard soundtrack, which became the best-selling soundtrack album of all time. The single topped charts worldwide, won the Grammy Awards for Record of the Year and Best Female Pop Vocal Performance, and has sold over 20 million copies worldwide by some estimates, making it the best-selling single by a female artist. After Houston's death in February 2012, the song re-entered the Billboard Hot 100 at number seven. It was a perennial top seller on digital stores such as iTunes.

Other versions

The song has been covered by many artists, including Linda Ronstadt (1975), John Doe, and numerous performers in tribute to Houston. It is frequently cited among the greatest love songs of all time, despite its actual subject being a fond professional farewell.
