Skookum Jim Mason

Keish (c. 1855–1916), known as Skookum Jim Mason

Skookum Jim Mason, born Keish (c. 1855 – July 11, 1916), was a member of the Tagish First Nation in what is now Yukon, Canada, who is credited with making the gold discovery at Rabbit Creek (renamed Bonanza Creek) that triggered the Klondike Gold Rush in 1896.

Life

Keish was born near Bennett Lake into the Dakl'aweidí clan of the Tagish people. His nickname "Skookum" — a Chinook Jargon word meaning strong or reliable — was earned through his reputation carrying heavy packs over the Chilkoot Trail. In August 1896, prospecting with his nephew Dawson Charlie, his sister Kate (Shaaw Tláa) and her husband George Carmack, the party found gold on a tributary of the Klondike River. The discovery claim was registered in Carmack's name, a fact that has long raised questions about the recognition given to the Indigenous members of the party.

Later years and legacy

Skookum Jim became wealthy from his claims, though much of his fortune was spent or lost. He died in Whitehorse in 1916. In accordance with his will, his estate established the Skookum Jim Friendship Centre in Whitehorse, which continues to serve Indigenous people in Yukon. He was inducted into the Canadian Mining Hall of Fame and is increasingly recognized as a co-discoverer of the Klondike goldfields.
