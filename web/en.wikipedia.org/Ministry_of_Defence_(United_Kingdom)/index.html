Ministry of Defence (United Kingdom)

Formed: 1 April 1964

Headquarters: Main Building, Whitehall, London

Minister responsible: Secretary of State for Defence

Jurisdiction: United Kingdom

The Ministry of Defence (MoD) is the British government department responsible for implementing the defence policy set by His Majesty's Government, and is the headquarters of the British Armed Forces. It employs a combined force of military and civilian personnel across the Royal Navy, British Army, and Royal Air Force.

History

The modern MoD dates from 1964, when the separate service ministries — the Admiralty, War Office, and Air Ministry — were merged under a single Secretary of State for Defence, following the creation of a unified ministry under Clement Attlee in 1947 and an intermediate centralised structure in 1964. The ministry's head office is the Main Building on Whitehall.

Organisation

The department is led by the Secretary of State for Defence, supported by the Permanent Under-Secretary and the Chief of the Defence Staff, the professional head of the armed forces. Its responsibilities include defence procurement (through Defence Equipment and Support), the nuclear deterrent, intelligence assessment, and the defence estate. The United Kingdom's defence budget is among the largest in the world.
