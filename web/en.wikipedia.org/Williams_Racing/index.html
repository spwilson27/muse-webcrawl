Williams Racing, legally Williams Grand Prix Engineering Limited, is a British Formula One racing team and constructor based at Grove, Oxfordshire. Founded in 1977 by Sir Frank Williams and Patrick Head, it is one of the most successful teams in the sport's history, with nine Constructors' Championships and seven Drivers' Championships.

History

After earlier ventures under his own name, Frank Williams founded the present team in 1977. Alan Jones won its first Drivers' Championship in 1980, and the team dominated periods of the 1980s and 1990s with cars designed by Patrick Head and, later, Adrian Newey, and drivers including Keke Rosberg, Nelson Piquet, Nigel Mansell, Alain Prost, Damon Hill and Jacques Villeneuve. The team's triumphs were shadowed by the death of Ayrton Senna in a Williams at Imola in 1994. Williams won its last titles in 1997 and its last race for a long period in 2012, as works-team budgets outgrew the independent outfit.

Recent years

The Williams family sold the team to the American investment firm Dorilton Capital in 2020, ending 43 years of family ownership; Frank Williams died in 2021. Claire Williams, the last family member in management, stepped down in 2020. Under team principal James Vowles, appointed in 2023, the team has pursued a rebuilding programme in facilities and personnel.
