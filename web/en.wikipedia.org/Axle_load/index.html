Axle load

The axle load of a wheeled vehicle is the total weight of the vehicle bearing on the roadway or track for all wheels connected to a given axle. It is an important measure in road and railway engineering, because the damage a vehicle does to a surface, and the capacity a surface must bear, depend on the load carried by each axle.

Roads

For road vehicles, axle load limits are set by regulation and enforced with weighbridges and weigh-in-motion systems. Pavement damage grows roughly with the fourth power of axle load — a relationship known as the fourth power law — so a modest increase in axle load causes a disproportionately large increase in road wear. Engineers use the equivalent single axle load (ESAL) to design pavements for mixed traffic.

Railways

On railways, axle load is a key parameter determining which lines a locomotive or wagon may use. Track, bridges, and earthworks are classified by the maximum axle load they can carry. Heavy-haul freight lines may permit axle loads of 30 tonnes or more, while light rail and older branch lines allow much less. Typical maximum values range from about 17 tonnes on light lines to around 22.5–25 tonnes on many main lines, and higher on dedicated heavy-haul routes.
