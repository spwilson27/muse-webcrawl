The Dnieper (also spelled Dnipro; Ukrainian: Дніпро; Russian: Днепр; Belarusian: Дняпро) is one of the major rivers of Europe, the fourth-longest in Europe after the Volga, Danube and Ural. Rising in the Valdai Hills of western Russia, it flows south through Belarus and Ukraine to the Black Sea, with a total length of about 2,285 kilometres.

Course

The Dnieper rises near Smolensk in Russia, crosses Belarus, where it is joined by the Berezina and Sozh, and enters Ukraine at the north, flowing through Kyiv, Cherkasy, Kremenchuk, Dnipro, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson before emptying into the Dnieper–Bug estuary on the Black Sea. Its main tributaries include the Pripyat, Desna, Berezina and Sozh. The river is navigable along much of its length, and a series of large reservoirs and hydroelectric stations—notably the Kyiv, Kaniv, Kremenchuk and Kakhovka dams—were built during the Soviet period.

History

The Dnieper has been a major trade route since antiquity. In the Viking Age it formed the central artery of the trade route from the Varangians to the Greeks, linking the Baltic to Constantinople, and it was the heartland of Kievan Rus', whose capital, Kyiv, stands on its banks. The river's famous rapids in the lower reaches, the porohy, obstructed navigation and were the site of the Zaporozhian Cossack settlements; they were submerged by the Kakhovka reservoir in the twentieth century. During the Second World War the Dnieper was the site of major battles, including the Battle of the Dnieper in 1943, one of the largest operations of the war.

Economy and environment

The Dnieper supplies drinking water to much of Ukraine, irrigates farmland, and generates hydroelectric power. Its reservoirs support fisheries, and its banks are home to several large cities. Pollution from industry, agriculture and urban runoff has been a persistent environmental problem, and the destruction of the Kakhovka Dam in June 2023, during the Russian invasion of Ukraine, drained the Kakhovka Reservoir and caused extensive flooding and ecological damage downstream.
