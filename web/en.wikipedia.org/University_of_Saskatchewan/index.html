The University of Saskatchewan (U of S, or USask) is a Canadian public research university, founded on 3 April 1907, and located on the east side of the South Saskatchewan River in Saskatoon, Saskatchewan, Canada. It is the largest education institution in the province of Saskatchewan.

History

The university was established by an act of the provincial legislature in 1907, with the support of Premier Walter Scott, who championed a single provincial university with an agricultural college to serve the farming frontier. Classes began in 1909 in downtown Saskatoon; the first permanent buildings on the riverbank campus — designed in the Collegiate Gothic style and faced with local greystone — rose over the following decades.

Research

USask is a member of Canada's U15 group of research-intensive universities. It hosts unique national facilities, including the Canadian Light Source synchrotron (one of the largest science projects in Canadian history), the Vaccine and Infectious Disease Organization (VIDO), and the Global Institute for Food Security. The university has longstanding strengths in agriculture, veterinary medicine, and water research.

Campus and athletics

The main campus covers 1,865 acres and is often described as one of Canada's most beautiful, centred on "the Bowl" green space. The Huskies athletic teams compete in U Sports; the football team has won multiple Vanier Cup championships. Notable alumni include former prime minister John Diefenbaker, who is buried on campus alongside his wife Olive.
