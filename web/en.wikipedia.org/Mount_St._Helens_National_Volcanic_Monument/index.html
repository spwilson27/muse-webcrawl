Mount St. Helens National Volcanic Monument

Established27 August 1982
LocationSouthwestern Washington, U.S.

The Mount St. Helens National Volcanic Monument is a U.S. national monument in southwestern Washington state, established in 1982 to preserve the landscape around Mount St. Helens for research, recreation and education following the volcano's catastrophic eruption of 18 May 1980. It covers about 445 km² (110,000 acres) within the Gifford Pinchot National Forest and is administered by the U.S. Forest Service.

The 1980 eruption

The eruption began with a magnitude-5.1 earthquake that triggered the largest debris avalanche in recorded history, removing the top 400 m (1,300 ft) of the mountain and unleashing a lateral blast that devastated 600 km² (230 sq mi) of forest. Fifty-seven people died. Within the monument, the blast zone has been left to recover naturally, making it a unique long-term ecological study site where scientists have tracked the return of plants and animals — including the role of pocket gophers and lupine in rebuilding soil.

Visitor facilities

The Johnston Ridge Observatory, named for volcanologist David Johnston, who died in the eruption at that site, overlooks the crater and its growing lava dome. Other facilities include the Forest Learning Center, Coldwater Ridge, and trails into the crater's blast zone and to Spirit Lake. The volcano erupted again from 2004 to 2008, extruding a new dome, and remains closely monitored.
