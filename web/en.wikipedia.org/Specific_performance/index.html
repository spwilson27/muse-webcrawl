Specific performance

Specific performance is an equitable remedy in the law of contract, whereby a court issues an order requiring a party to perform a specific act, such as to complete performance of the contract. It is typically available in the sale of land law, but otherwise is not generally available if damages are an adequate remedy. Specific performance is almost never available for contracts of personal service, and it is often refused where the court would have to supervise performance over a long period.

Requirements

Specific performance is granted only where the claimant can show that monetary damages would not adequately compensate for the breach — classically where the subject matter is unique, as with land or a rare chattel. The claimant must also have clean hands and not be guilty of delay (laches).

Common law vs civil law

In civil law jurisdictions, specific performance is generally the primary remedy for breach of contract, whereas in common law systems damages are primary and specific performance exceptional. In English law, the remedy developed in the Court of Chancery.

Examples

A purchaser of land who is refused completion by the vendor can obtain specific performance because every parcel of land is treated as unique. By contrast, contracts to build or to render personal services are normally enforced, if at all, only through damages or negative injunctions.
