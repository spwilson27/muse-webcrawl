Hormone replacement therapy
SpecialtyEndocrinology, gynaecology
Main usesMenopause symptoms, hypogonadism, gender-affirming care
AgentsEstrogens, progestogens, testosterone

Hormone replacement therapy (HRT) is a form of hormone therapy used to treat symptoms associated with female menopause, male hypogonadism, and as part of gender-affirming care. Menopausal HRT typically combines an estrogen with a progestogen in women with a uterus, to protect the endometrium.

Menopausal therapy

HRT is the most effective treatment for vasomotor symptoms such as hot flushes and night sweats, and helps prevent postmenopausal bone loss and fractures. It may be given orally, transdermally (patches, gels) or vaginally; local vaginal estrogen treats genitourinary syndrome of menopause with minimal systemic absorption.

Risks and the Women's Health Initiative

The 2002 Women's Health Initiative trial reported that combined conjugated estrogen plus medroxyprogesterone increased risks of breast cancer, stroke and venous thromboembolism, leading to a sharp fall in prescribing. Later analyses emphasised that risks and benefits depend strongly on age, time since menopause, dose and route; starting before age 60 generally has a more favourable profile.

Other uses

Testosterone replacement treats confirmed male hypogonadism. Feminising and masculinising hormone therapy are core components of transgender healthcare. HRT is also used in premature ovarian insufficiency and after bilateral oophorectomy.
