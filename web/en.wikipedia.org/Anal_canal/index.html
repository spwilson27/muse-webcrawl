Anal canal

Anal canal
Part ofGastrointestinal tract
PrecursorHindgut and proctodeum
Length2.5–4 cm (adult)

The anal canal is the terminal segment of the large intestine, between the rectum and the anus, below the level of the pelvic diaphragm. In adult humans it is about 2.5 to 4 cm long and is surrounded by the internal and external anal sphincters, which maintain continence.

Structure

The pectinate (dentate) line divides the canal into an upper two-thirds, derived from the hindgut and lined by columnar epithelium with autonomic innervation, and a lower third, derived from the proctodeum and lined by stratified squamous epithelium with somatic (pudendal) innervation. The anal columns of Morgagni and the anal valves form characteristic folds in the upper canal. The intersphincteric plane houses the anal glands.

Function

The anal canal samples rectal contents via the rich sensory innervation below and above the dentate line and, together with the sphincter complex, controls defecation.

Clinical significance

Conditions of the anal canal include hemorrhoids, anal fissure, fistula-in-ano, and anal carcinoma; the lymphatic drainage differs above and below the pectinate line, which affects how cancers spread.
