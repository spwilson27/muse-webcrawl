A radiator is a type of heat exchanger used to transfer thermal energy from one medium to another for the purpose of heating a space. Despite the name, most radiators transfer the bulk of their heat by convection rather than thermal radiation. They are a key component of hydronic central heating systems, in which a boiler heats water that is pumped through a network of pipes and radiators.

Types

Common forms include column radiators of cast iron, pressed-steel panel radiators (often with convector fins), aluminium sectional radiators, and low-profile skirting and trench convectors. Towel rails in bathrooms are small radiators designed to warm towels as well as the room. Steam radiators, common in older buildings in North America, operate at higher temperatures and require air vents.

Operation and control

Heat output depends on the temperature difference between the water and the room and on the radiator's surface area. Thermostatic radiator valves (TRVs) regulate flow to individual radiators, and bleeding valves release trapped air that reduces performance. Modern low-temperature systems with condensing boilers or heat pumps use larger radiators or underfloor heating to compensate for lower flow temperatures.

History

The cast-iron radiator is credited to Russian businessman Franz San Galli, who patented a design in 1855–57; American manufacturers such as the American Radiator Company popularised sectional cast-iron radiators in the late 19th century.
