Bernoulli number

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, the Bernoulli numbers Bn are a sequence of rational numbers which occur frequently in analysis. The Bernoulli numbers appear in (and can be defined by) the Taylor series expansions of the tangent and hyperbolic tangent functions, in Faulhaber's formula for the sum of powers of integers, in the Euler–Maclaurin formula, and in expressions for certain values of the Riemann zeta function.

Values

The first few Bernoulli numbers are B0 = 1, B1 = −1/2 (in the common convention), B2 = 1/6, B4 = −1/30, B6 = 1/42, B8 = −1/30 and B10 = 5/66. All odd Bernoulli numbers except B1 are zero. They are named after Jacob Bernoulli, who studied them in connection with the problem of summing powers of consecutive integers; they were discovered independently at about the same time by Seki Takakazu in Japan.

Applications

Bernoulli numbers are deeply connected to number theory: they occur in the denominators relevant to Kummer's criterion for Fermat's Last Theorem and the theory of "irregular primes", and through the Riemann zeta function at even integers, ζ(2n) is a rational multiple of π2n involving B2n. Ada Lovelace's celebrated 1843 notes on the Analytical Engine described an algorithm for computing Bernoulli numbers, often cited as the first computer program. In topology they appear in formulas for exotic spheres, and they can be generalized to Bernoulli polynomials.
