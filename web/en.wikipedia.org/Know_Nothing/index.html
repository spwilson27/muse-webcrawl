Know Nothing

Founded1844 (as Native American Party); c. 1854 as American Party
Dissolvedc. 1860
IdeologyNativism, anti-Catholicism
CountryUnited States

The Know Nothings were a nativist political movement in the United States in the 1840s and 1850s, formally organised as the American Party. Arising in reaction to large-scale immigration — especially of Irish Catholics and Germans — the movement sought to extend the naturalisation waiting period, restrict office-holding to the native-born, and curb the influence of the Roman Catholic Church.

Origins and name

The movement grew out of secret fraternal lodges such as the Order of the Star Spangled Banner, founded in New York in 1849. Members instructed to say "I know nothing" when asked about the organisation gave the movement its famous nickname. By 1854–1855 the American Party had become a national force, electing governors, congressmen, and hundreds of state legislators, and briefly rivalling the collapsing Whig Party.

Platform and violence

Know Nothing proposals included a 21-year residency requirement for naturalisation, literacy tests for voting in some states, and restrictions on immigrant and Catholic participation in public life. Nativist agitation sometimes turned violent, as in the Bloody Monday riots in Louisville, Kentucky, in August 1855, in which mobs attacked German and Irish neighbourhoods.

Decline

The party's 1856 presidential candidate, former president Millard Fillmore, won over 21 per cent of the popular vote but carried only Maryland. The movement soon split over slavery — the issue that destroyed it as a national party — with many northern members joining the new Republican Party and southern members drifting to the Constitutional Union Party by 1860.
