Ballistic reentry

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ballistic reentry is a mode of atmospheric entry in which a vehicle follows a purely ballistic trajectory — generating no (or negligible) aerodynamic lift — as it descends through the atmosphere to the surface. The vehicle's path is governed by gravity and drag alone, in contrast to a lifting reentry, in which the vehicle (like the Space Shuttle) uses lift to extend its range, maneuver cross-range, and moderate its deceleration.

Characteristics

A ballistic entry is steep and fast. Because drag is the only force slowing the vehicle, peak deceleration is high: early crewed capsules flying purely ballistic profiles from orbit pulled on the order of 8–9 g, and ballistic missile warheads with high ballistic coefficients can experience far more. The governing parameter is the ballistic coefficient — mass divided by drag coefficient and reference area. A high-ballistic-coefficient object penetrates deep into the atmosphere before slowing, producing a short, intense heating pulse; a low-coefficient object (such as a blunt capsule) decelerates high in the atmosphere, spreading the heating load and reducing peak heat flux.

Applications

Purely ballistic reentry is used by intercontinental ballistic missile reentry vehicles, sounding rockets, and simple sample-return capsules. Crewed capsules such as Vostok, Mercury, and Soyuz in its emergency/backup mode use (nearly) ballistic entries; Soyuz normally flies a guided, slightly lifting entry at about 4–5 g, but reverts to a true ballistic profile — at roughly double the g-load — if its guidance fails, as occurred on Soyuz TMA-10 (2007) and TMA-11 (2008). Deliberately steep ballistic entries are also used for high-speed sample returns such as Stardust and Hayabusa2 because they are simple and robust.

Comparison with lifting entry

Lifting entries trade complexity for gentleness and precision: they achieve lower peak g, larger landing footprints adjusted in flight, and better landing accuracy near recovery forces. Ballistic entries have larger dispersion (unguided trajectories drift with winds and atmospheric variation) but require no functioning guidance or control surfaces during the hypersonic phase, making them extremely reliable.
