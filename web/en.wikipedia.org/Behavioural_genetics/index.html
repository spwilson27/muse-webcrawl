Behavioural genetics

Behavioural genetics, also referred to as behaviour genetics, is a field of scientific research that uses genetic methods to investigate the nature and origins of individual differences in behaviour. Whereas the name "behavioural genetics" connotes the focus on genetic influences, the field broadly investigates the extent to which both genetic and environmental factors influence individual differences, and the development of research designs that can remove the confounding of genes and environment.

History

Behavioural genetics was founded as a scientific discipline by Francis Galton in the late 19th century, only to be discredited through association with eugenics movements before and during World War II. In the latter half of the 20th century, the field saw renewed prominence with research on the inheritance of behaviour and mental illness in humans (typically by studying twins and families), and of well-characterised traits in select model organisms.

Methods

The primary methods used are twin studies, adoption studies and family studies, which allow researchers to estimate heritability — the proportion of variance in a trait attributable to genetic variation. More recent methods include genome-wide association studies (GWAS) and the computation of polygenic scores, which aggregate the small effects of many genetic variants.

Findings

Behaviour geneticists have found that most reliably measured psychological traits — including intelligence, personality dimensions and liability to common mental disorders — show moderate to substantial genetic influence, a pattern sometimes summarised as the "first law of behaviour genetics": all human behavioural traits are heritable.
