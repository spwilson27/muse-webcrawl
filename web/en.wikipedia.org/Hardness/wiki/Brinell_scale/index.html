Brinell scale

Brinell hardness
Proposed: 1900, Johan August Brinell
Indenter: Hardened steel / tungsten carbide ball
Symbol: HBW / BHN

The Brinell scale characterises the indentation hardness of materials through the penetration of an indenter. Proposed by Swedish engineer Johan August Brinell in 1900, it was the first widely used and standardised hardness test in engineering and metallurgy.

Method

A ball — typically 10 mm diameter tungsten carbide — is pressed into the material under loads of 500–3000 kgf for 10–30 seconds. The diameter of the indentation is measured and the Brinell hardness number (BHN) computed from load divided by the curved surface area of the dent.

Uses and limits

Well suited to castings and forgings with coarse grain, but less accurate for very hard materials and leaves a relatively large impression.
