Toughness

Toughness
Field: Materials science
Unit: Joules per cubic metre (energy per volume)
Tests: Charpy, Izod impact tests

In materials science and metallurgy, toughness is the ability of a material to absorb energy and plastically deform without fracturing. Toughness is the strength with which the material opposes rupture; it is the energy per unit volume a material can absorb before rupturing, represented by the area under the stress–strain curve.

Hardness vs toughness

Hardness (resistance to surface indentation, see Mohs scale) differs from toughness: a diamond is very hard but can shatter, while many steels are less hard but much tougher.

Measurement

Impact toughness is measured by Charpy and Izod pendulum tests on notched specimens. Fracture toughness (KIc) measures resistance to crack propagation.

Factors

Temperature (ductile-to-brittle transition in steels), grain size, alloying and strain rate all affect toughness.
