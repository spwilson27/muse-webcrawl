Mohs scale

Mohs scale of mineral hardness
Created: 1812, Friedrich Mohs
Range: 1 (talc) to 10 (diamond)
Type: Ordinal, scratch test

The Mohs scale of mineral hardness is a qualitative ordinal scale, from 1 to 10, characterising scratch resistance of minerals through the ability of harder material to scratch softer material. Created in 1812 by German geologist Friedrich Mohs, it remains widely used in geology and gemology.

Reference minerals

  1. Talc
  2. Gypsum
  3. Calcite
  4. Fluorite
  5. Apatite
  6. Orthoclase feldspar
  7. Quartz
  8. Topaz
  9. Corundum
  10. Diamond

Everyday references: fingernail ~2.5, copper penny ~3, steel nail ~5.5, glass ~5.5–6. The scale is non-linear: diamond is far harder in absolute terms than corundum.
