Cost–benefit analysis

Cost–benefit analysis (CBA), sometimes also called benefit–cost analysis, is a systematic process for calculating and comparing the benefits and costs of a decision, project or policy in monetary terms.

Overview

The method originated in 19th-century French engineering economics (Jules Dupuit) and US water-project appraisal, and was generalised after the Second World War. It underpins regulatory impact analysis in many governments.

Technique

Costs and benefits are expressed in present values using a social discount rate; options are ranked by net present value. Where effects cannot be monetised, cost-effectiveness analysis is an alternative. Common criticisms involve the value of a statistical life, equity weighting and long-term environmental harms.
