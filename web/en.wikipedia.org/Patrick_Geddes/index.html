Patrick Geddes

Sir Patrick Geddes FRSE (2 October 1854 – 17 April 1932) was a Scottish biologist, sociologist, and pioneering town planner. He is known for concepts such as the "valley section", the regional survey, and the dictum "survey before plan".

Life and work

Born in Ballater, Aberdeenshire, Geddes studied biology under T. H. Huxley in London, though ill health and poor eyesight turned him toward social science. In Edinburgh he established the Outlook Tower on the Royal Mile (1892) as an observatory of the city and region, and worked with his son-in-law Frank Mears on urban renewal in the Old Town.

Planning career

Geddes prepared plans for dozens of cities in India, including Lucknow and Indore, and for Dunfermline in Scotland. In 1919 he took the chair of sociology at the University of Bombay. Appointed to plan the new Hebrew University in Jerusalem and consulted on Tel Aviv, he advocated "conservative surgery" — improving existing fabric rather than wholesale demolition. His 1915 book Cities in Evolution popularised the term "conurbation".

Legacy

Geddes influenced Lewis Mumford and modern regional and ecological planning. His triad "Place–Work–Folk" (adapted from Le Play) framed planning as the study of people in their environment.
