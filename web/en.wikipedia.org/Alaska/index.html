Alaska is a state of the United States located at the northwestern extremity of North America. Separated from the contiguous United States by Canada, it borders the Arctic and Pacific Oceans and shares a maritime boundary with Russia across the Bering Strait. Alaska is by far the largest U.S. state by area — more than twice the size of Texas — yet one of the least populated, with about 733,000 residents.

History

Alaska was inhabited for millennia by diverse Indigenous peoples, including the Iñupiat, Yupik, Aleut, Tlingit and Athabascan peoples. Russian colonisation began in the eighteenth century after Vitus Bering's expeditions. In 1867 the United States purchased Alaska from the Russian Empire for $7.2 million — a deal derided at the time as "Seward's Folly" but vindicated by gold rushes (notably Nome and Fairbanks) and later oil discoveries. Alaska became a territory in 1912, was strategically vital in the Second World War, and achieved statehood in 1959. The discovery of oil at Prudhoe Bay in 1968 led to the Trans-Alaska Pipeline (completed 1977) and the Alaska Permanent Fund, which pays an annual dividend to residents.

Geography and economy

The state spans a subarctic to Arctic climate, contains North America's highest peak, Denali, and holds vast protected lands, including eight national parks. Its economy rests on oil and gas, commercial fishing (especially salmon), tourism, mining and government. Anchorage is the largest city; the capital, Juneau, is inaccessible by road.
