The Ulster Defence Association (UDA) is an Ulster loyalist paramilitary group in Northern Ireland. It was formed in September 1971 as an umbrella group for various loyalist groups, in response to the growth of republican paramilitarism during the Troubles. At its peak of strength in the early 1970s, it claimed a membership of up to 40,000 and was the largest loyalist paramilitary organisation.

History

The UDA presented itself as a defensive organisation protecting Protestant and unionist neighbourhoods. Its attacks were usually claimed under the cover name Ulster Freedom Fighters (UFF). The group was responsible for more than 400 deaths during the Troubles, the great majority of them Irish Catholic civilians, often killed in sectarian attacks. Notable atrocities included the Milltown Cemetery attack of 1988 by UDA member Michael Stone and the Castlerock killings of 1993. The UDA was also implicated in collusion with members of the British security forces, most controversially in the 1989 murder of solicitor Pat Finucane, which prompted the Stevens Inquiries.

The UDA remained a legal organisation until 10 August 1992, when it was proscribed by the British government. It declared a ceasefire in October 1994 as part of the Northern Ireland peace process. In November 2007 its leadership announced that the UFF would be stood down and weapons put beyond use; decommissioning was confirmed as complete in January 2010, although splinter activity by individual brigades and involvement in organised crime have continued.
