Astrocyte

Astrocytes (from Ancient Greek ástron, "star" + kútos, "cell"), also known collectively as astroglia, are characteristic star-shaped glial cells in the brain and spinal cord. They perform many functions, including biochemical control of endothelial cells that form the blood–brain barrier, provision of nutrients to nervous tissue, maintenance of extracellular ion balance, and repair following injury.

Structure

Astrocytes are a sub-type of glial cells in the central nervous system. They are known for their distinctive star shape and express the intermediate filament protein GFAP (glial fibrillary acidic protein), a common marker. Two main forms are recognized: fibrous astrocytes in white matter and protoplasmic astrocytes in grey matter.

Functions

Astrocytes help form the blood–brain barrier, regulate blood flow, recycle neurotransmitters such as glutamate, buffer potassium ions, store glycogen, and modulate synaptic transmission as part of the "tripartite synapse." They also respond to injury by reactive astrogliosis, forming glial scars.

Clinical significance

Astrocyte dysfunction is implicated in epilepsy, Alzheimer's disease, and other neurodegenerative conditions. Astrocytomas are brain tumors arising from astrocytes or their precursors.
