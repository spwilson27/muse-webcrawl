The CAF Confederation Cup, known as the TotalEnergies CAF Confederation Cup for sponsorship reasons, is an annual association football club competition run by the Confederation of African Football (CAF). It is the second-tier continental club tournament in Africa, below the CAF Champions League.

History

The competition was created in 2004 by merging the CAF Cup and the African Cup Winners' Cup. It is contested by the cup winners and high-placed league finishers of CAF member associations, together with clubs eliminated from the Champions League at certain stages.

Format

Teams enter through preliminary and play-off rounds before a group stage of four groups of four, followed by knockout quarter-finals, semi-finals and a two-legged final. The winner qualifies for the CAF Super Cup against the Champions League winner. Clubs from North Africa, notably Morocco, Egypt and Tunisia, have dominated the honours list, with teams such as RS Berkane, USM Alger and Zamalek among the champions.
