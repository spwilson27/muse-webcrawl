Joseph Cinqué (c. 1814 – c. 1879), whose name in his native Mende was Sengbe Pieh, was a West African man who led a successful revolt aboard the Spanish slave ship La Amistad in 1839. The ensuing American court case, United States v. The Amistad, became a landmark in the abolitionist movement.

Capture and revolt

Cinqué was kidnapped in Sierra Leone in 1839 and sold in Havana, Cuba, in violation of treaties banning the transatlantic slave trade. Aboard La Amistad, bound for a Cuban sugar plantation, he freed himself and led the captives in seizing the ship, killing the captain and the cook. Ordering the surviving Spaniards to sail for Africa by day while they steered for America by night, the ship zigzagged up the American coast before being seized by a U.S. naval brig off Long Island.

Trial and return

Supported by abolitionists and defended in the Supreme Court by former president John Quincy Adams, the Africans were ruled in 1841 to be free people who had been illegally enslaved and had a right to resist. Cinqué and the other survivors returned to Sierra Leone in 1842 with American missionaries. Little is reliably known of his later life; he is thought to have died around 1879. His story was dramatised in Steven Spielberg's film Amistad (1997), in which he was played by Djimon Hounsou.
