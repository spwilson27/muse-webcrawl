Battle of Rorke's Drift

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Date22–23 January 1879
LocationRorke's Drift, Natal (now KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa)
CombatantsBritish garrison (c. 150 men) vs Zulu army (c. 3,000–4,000)
ResultBritish defensive victory
Awards11 Victoria Crosses — the most for a single action

The Battle of Rorke's Drift, fought on 22–23 January 1879 during the Anglo-Zulu War, was the successful defence of a mission station in Natal by a small British and colonial garrison against repeated attacks by several thousand Zulu warriors. It took place hours after the disastrous British defeat at Isandlwana, where many of the Zulu attackers had just fought.

The battle

The garrison of about 150 men, mostly of B Company, 2nd Battalion, 24th Regiment of Foot, was commanded by Lieutenant John Chard of the Royal Engineers, with Lieutenant Gonville Bromhead leading the infantry. Warned of the approaching Zulu force under Prince Dabulamanzi, the defenders barricaded the station with mealie bags and biscuit boxes, loopholed the buildings, and held the perimeter through a night of close assaults, including the burning of the hospital, from whose patients several famous rescues were made. The Zulu withdrew at dawn.

Aftermath

Seventeen defenders were killed and about fifteen wounded; Zulu dead numbered in the hundreds. Eleven Victoria Crosses were awarded for the action — the largest number ever given for a single engagement. In Britain the stand at Rorke's Drift partly redeemed the news of Isandlwana, and it was later immortalised in the 1964 film Zulu.
