Wang Shouren (Chinese: 王守仁; 1472–1529), courtesy name Bo'an, commonly known by his honorific Wang Yangming, was a Chinese statesman, general and Neo-Confucian philosopher of the Ming dynasty. He founded the School of Mind (Xinxue), one of the two great traditions of later Neo-Confucianism, and is considered, with Confucius, Mencius and Zhu Xi, among the most influential Confucian thinkers. He lived more than a millennium after the Wei-dynasty commentator Wang Bi, to whom he is unrelated.

Career

Wang passed the imperial examinations in 1499 and served in several ministries. In 1506 he was beaten and exiled to remote Guizhou for protesting the power of the eunuch Liu Jin; there, in 1508, he experienced his famous enlightenment that principle (li) is found within the mind, not in external things. Recalled to service, he became a successful military commander, suppressing rebellions in southern China and, in 1519, swiftly defeating the revolt of Zhu Chenhao, Prince of Ning.

Philosophy

Wang taught that knowledge and action are one (zhixing heyi): to truly know the good is already to act on it, and failure to act shows knowledge is incomplete. He developed the idea of liangzhi, an innate moral knowing present in everyone, which is extended through sincere effort. His recorded conversations are collected in the Chuanxilu ("Instructions for Practical Living").

Influence

Yangming learning spread widely in China and to Japan, where it influenced figures of the Meiji era, and Korea. Within China his school later fragmented into competing branches, and his doctrines were both celebrated and attacked as excessively subjective by later Confucians.
