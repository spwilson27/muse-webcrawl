F. O. Matthiessen

Francis Otto Matthiessen (February 19, 1902 – April 1, 1950) was an American educator, scholar, and literary critic associated with Harvard University. He is best known for American Renaissance: Art and Expression in the Age of Emerson and Whitman (1941), a landmark work of scholarship that canonised five nineteenth-century American writers and helped establish American literature as a field of academic study.

F. O. Matthiessen, critic and Harvard professor

Career

Matthiessen was educated at Yale and Oxford, where he was a Rhodes Scholar, and earned his doctorate at Harvard, joining its faculty in 1929. His first book, Sarah Orne Jewett (1929), was followed by studies of T. S. Eliot and Henry James, including The James Family (1947). American Renaissance examined Ralph Waldo Emerson, Henry David Thoreau, Nathaniel Hawthorne, Herman Melville, and Walt Whitman, arguing that their writing embodied a distinctly American democratic culture. The period designation "American Renaissance" derives from his book.

Politics and death

Matthiessen was a Christian socialist and an active figure on the academic left, drawing scrutiny in the early Cold War. Suffering from depression, he died by suicide in Boston in 1950. A chair at Harvard, the F. O. Matthiessen Visiting Professorship of Gender and Sexuality, was later established in his name — the first endowed chair in LGBTQ studies at an American university.
