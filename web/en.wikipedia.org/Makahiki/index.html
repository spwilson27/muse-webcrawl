Makahiki

Makahiki was an ancient Hawaiian New Year festival, held in honour of the god Lono, associated with fertility, rainfall, agriculture and peace. The festival lasted roughly four lunar months, approximately from October or November through February or March, coinciding with the rainy season.

Observances

During Makahiki, work and warfare were forbidden (kapu), and Hawaiians celebrated with feasting, games, sports competitions such as boxing and wrestling, hula, and offerings of produce and goods to chiefs and to the image of Lono, which was carried clockwise around each island.

Historical significance

Captain James Cook arrived in Hawai'i during the Makahiki season in 1778–1779, and some scholars have debated the extent to which his reception related to the festival's rituals honouring Lono. The festival declined after the abolition of the kapu system in 1819 but has been revived in modern times as part of Native Hawaiian cultural renewal.
