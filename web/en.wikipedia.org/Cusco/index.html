Cusco

Cusco (Quechua: Qusqu) is a city in southeastern Peru, near the Urubamba Valley of the Andes, at an elevation of about 3,400 m (11,200 ft). It was the historic capital of the Inca Empire from the 13th century until the Spanish conquest in the 16th century, and is today a major tourist destination and a UNESCO World Heritage Site (designated 1983).

History

According to Inca legend, the city was founded by Manco Cápac and Mama Ocllo, sent by the sun god Inti. Under Pachacuti (reigned 1438–1471) Cusco was rebuilt in stone as the centre of the expanding empire, said to be laid out in the shape of a puma. The Spanish under Francisco Pizarro took the city in 1533–34; colonial churches and palaces were built atop Inca foundations, which famously survived earthquakes in 1650 and 1950 that damaged Spanish structures.

Landmarks

Sights include the Plaza de Armas with its cathedral and the church of La Compañía, the Coricancha sun temple (now the Santo Domingo convent), and the massive fortress of Sacsayhuamán above the city, built of enormous fitted stones. The San Blas quarter is known for artisans. Cusco is the main gateway for travel to Machu Picchu and the Sacred Valley.
