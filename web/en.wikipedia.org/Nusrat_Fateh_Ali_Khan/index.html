Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Nusrat performing with his ensemble

Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan (13 October 1948 – 16 August 1997) was a Pakistani musician, composer, and music director, primarily a singer of qawwali, the devotional music of the Sufis. Widely regarded as one of the greatest voices ever recorded, he was known in Pakistan as Shahenshah-e-Qawwali, "the King of Kings of Qawwali".

Life and career

Born in Faisalabad into a family of qawwali singers stretching back some 600 years, Nusrat first performed at his father's funeral in 1964 and later led the family party, Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan, Mujahid Mubarak Ali Khan & Party. His powerful voice, capable of singing at high intensity for hours, and his improvisational mastery of sargam brought qawwali to international audiences.

International recognition

Signed to Peter Gabriel's Real World label, he released albums including Mustt Mustt (1990) and Night Song (1996), the latter a collaboration with Michael Brook. He contributed to film soundtracks including Natural Born Killers and Dead Man Walking, and collaborated with Eddie Vedder. He held the Guinness World Record for the largest recorded output by a qawwali artist, with over 125 albums.

Death and legacy

Khan died in London on 16 August 1997 of sudden cardiac arrest, aged 48. His nephew Rahat Fateh Ali Khan has continued the family tradition. UNESCO recognised his contribution to music, and his recordings remain central to world music.
