Knight Bachelor


The badge of a Knight Bachelor, instituted in 1926.

The title of Knight Bachelor is the basic rank granted to a man who has been knighted by the monarch of the United Kingdom but not inducted as a member of one of the organised orders of chivalry. Knights Bachelor are styled "Sir" before their forename. It is the oldest type of knighthood, originating in the medieval period, and is granted for public service or personal achievement.

Conferral and insignia

Knighthoods are typically announced in the New Year Honours and the Sovereign's Birthday Honours lists. Recipients are knighted at an investiture ceremony, kneeling before the sovereign (or a representative) who touches each shoulder with a sword. Unlike members of the orders of chivalry, Knights Bachelor historically had no insignia; a badge was designed in 1926 and may be worn on appropriate occasions. Knights Bachelor do not use post-nominal letters.

Women

Women receiving the equivalent honour are appointed Dame Commander of the Order of the British Empire (DBE), since there is no female equivalent of Knight Bachelor within the honours system; a woman so honoured is styled "Dame".
