Demotic (Egyptian)

Demotic
RegionAncient Egypt
Erac. 650 BCE – 5th century CE
Language familyAfro-Asiatic → Egyptian → Late Egyptian stage
Writing systemDemotic script (derived from hieratic)
Succeeded byCoptic (language stage written in a Greek-based alphabet)

Demotic (from Greek δημοτικά, "popular") refers both to a stage of the ancient Egyptian language written from about 650 BCE onward, and to the highly cursive script in which it was written. The term was used by Herodotus for the everyday writing of Egypt, as opposed to the priestly hieratic and hieroglyphic scripts. Demotic developed in the Late Period, spread under the Saite Twenty-sixth Dynasty, and remained in use for over a thousand years, into the Roman period.

Script and language

The Demotic script evolved from the northern forms of hieratic, compressing sign groups into ligatures that make it notoriously hard to read: individual signs merged, and the same word could be written with historical spellings far removed from its pronunciation. The language itself continues Late Egyptian, with analytic grammar, a definite article, and later a substantial admixture of Greek loanwords under the Ptolemies and Romans. Egyptologists conventionally distinguish Early, Middle (Ptolemaic), and Late (Roman) Demotic.

Texts

Demotic was the script of administration, law, business, letters, and much literature: contracts, tax receipts, and legal codes (such as the Hermopolis legal code), wisdom instructions (the Teachings of Ankhsheshonq), narrative tales (the Setne Khaemwaset cycle), magical and medical papyri, and priestly decrees. The most famous Demotic text is the middle register of the Rosetta Stone (196 BCE), whose parallel Greek version was a key to the decipherment of Egyptian scripts in the 19th century.

Decline

Under Roman rule Demotic retreated gradually before Greek in administration, surviving longest in temple and funerary contexts; some of the last dated Demotic graffiti, at the temple of Isis at Philae, belong to the mid-5th century CE. The Egyptian language itself continued as Coptic, written mainly in Greek letters. Modern study of Demotic rests on resources such as the Chicago Demotic Dictionary project.
