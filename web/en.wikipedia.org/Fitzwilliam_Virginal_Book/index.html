Fitzwilliam Virginal Book

The Fitzwilliam Virginal Book is a primary source of late Renaissance English keyboard music. It is one of the few surviving collections of the genre from the Elizabethan and Jacobean eras, and the most voluminous. The manuscript, a large folio volume containing 297 pieces (about 700 pages), is housed at the Fitzwilliam Museum in Cambridge, from which it takes its name. It was formerly known as Queen Elizabeth's Virginal Book, a title that proved to be unfounded.

History

The collection was copied in the early 17th century, probably between about 1609 and 1619. The scribe is traditionally identified as Francis Tregian the Younger, a Catholic recusant who was imprisoned in the Tower of London and later the Fleet Prison; the long-standing theory holds that he compiled the book during his imprisonment. The manuscript passed through the hands of collectors including Robert Hatton and was bequeathed to the Fitzwilliam Museum in 1816 as part of the founder's collection.

Contents

The book contains music by the leading English virginalists — including John Bull (62 pieces), William Byrd (72 pieces) and Orlando Gibbons — alongside works by Thomas Morley, Giles Farnaby, Martin Peerson, Peter Philips and William Tisdale, and by continental composers such as Jan Pieterszoon Sweelinck. The pieces include dances (pavans, galliards, almains, corantos), variations on popular tunes, fantasias, preludes and settings of folk melodies. Famous items include Byrd's "The Bells", Bull's "Walsingham" variations and Farnaby's "His Dream" and "His Humour".

Editions

The collection was first published in a complete modern edition by J. A. Fuller Maitland and W. Barclay Squire (1894–99), which remains widely used. The manuscript is regarded as one of the most important sources for understanding English keyboard music of the period and the virtuoso "virginalist" style.
