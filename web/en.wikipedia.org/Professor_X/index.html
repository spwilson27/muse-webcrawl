Professor X

Professor X (Charles Francis Xavier) is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by writer Stan Lee and artist Jack Kirby, he first appeared in The X-Men #1 (September 1963). Xavier is a powerful mutant telepath, the founder of the X-Men and of Xavier's School for Gifted Youngsters, where young mutants are trained to use their powers while being sheltered from a hostile world.

Fictional biography

Charles Xavier was born into wealth in New York, the son of nuclear researcher Brian Xavier. His telepathic powers manifested in childhood. After earning doctorates and travelling the world — including a fateful friendship and falling-out with Erik Lehnsherr, who would become Magneto — Xavier dedicated his life to peaceful coexistence between mutants and humans. A confrontation with the alien Lucifer cost him the use of his legs, and he is usually depicted using a wheelchair (in some eras a hoverchair). He founded the X-Men, a team of mutant heroes, and uses the device Cerebro to locate mutants worldwide.

Powers and character

Xavier is among the most powerful telepaths on Earth: he can read and control minds, project illusions, alter memories, and project his astral form. His philosophy of peaceful integration is often contrasted with Magneto's militancy; the two have been compared to Martin Luther King Jr. and Malcolm X, though creators have resisted a simple mapping. Later storylines have complicated Xavier's morality, revealing manipulative decisions made in the name of the greater good, notably in the Krakoan era beginning with House of X (2019), in which Xavier helped found a mutant nation-state.

In other media

Patrick Stewart portrayed Xavier in the 20th Century Fox X-Men films (2000–2017) and in Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness (2022); James McAvoy played a younger Xavier in the prequel films beginning with X-Men: First Class (2011). The character also appears in the animated series X-Men (1992–1997) and X-Men '97 (2024).
