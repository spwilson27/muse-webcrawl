Dunhuang manuscripts

The Dunhuang manuscripts are a cache of some 60,000 documents discovered in 1900 in the sealed "Library Cave" (Cave 17) of the Mogao Caves at Dunhuang, in Gansu, China. Dating mainly from the 5th to the early 11th centuries, they were sealed up around 1006 and preserved by the dry desert climate.

Contents and dispersal

The collection includes Buddhist sutras, Taoist, Manichaean, and Nestorian Christian texts, contracts, letters, calendars, medical and astronomical works, and the world's earliest dated printed book, the Diamond Sutra of 868. The Daoist monk Wang Yuanlu, who found the cave, sold large numbers of manuscripts to Aurel Stein (1907) and Paul Pelliot (1908); today they are held chiefly by the British Library, the National Library of China, the Bibliothèque nationale de France, and institutions in Russia and Japan. The International Dunhuang Programme digitises and reunites them online.
