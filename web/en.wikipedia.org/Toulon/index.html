Toulon

Country: France
Region: Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur
Department: Var (prefecture)
Population: approx. 180,000

Toulon is a city on the Mediterranean coast of southeastern France, in the department of Var, of which it is the prefecture. It is one of France's principal naval bases and the home port of the French Navy's Mediterranean fleet, including the aircraft carrier Charles de Gaulle. The city lies about 65 kilometres southeast of Marseille.

History

The site was settled in antiquity and known to the Romans as Telo. Toulon's great natural harbour made it strategically important, and under Louis XIV and his minister Colbert the port was greatly expanded and fortified by Vauban, becoming France's main Mediterranean naval arsenal. In 1793, during the French Revolutionary Wars, royalists handed the port to an Anglo-Spanish fleet; the young artillery officer Napoleon Bonaparte distinguished himself in the siege that retook the city, launching his military career. In November 1942, the French fleet scuttled itself at Toulon to prevent its capture by German forces following the occupation of Vichy France.

Geography

Toulon occupies a sheltered bay, the Rade de Toulon, protected by the Saint-Mandrier peninsula. Limestone hills, including Mont Faron (reached by cable car), rise behind the city. The old town, with its narrow streets, cathedral, and fountains, lies between the port and the newer boulevards to the north.

Naval base and economy

The military port remains the economic heart of Toulon, employing thousands in shipbuilding, repair, and naval administration. Tourism, the university, and regional administration also contribute. Toulon is the home of the rugby union club RC Toulon (RCT), one of France's most successful teams.
