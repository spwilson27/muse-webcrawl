Operation Grapple was a set of four series of British nuclear weapons tests of early atomic bombs and hydrogen bombs carried out in 1957 and 1958 at Malden Island and Christmas Island in the Pacific Ocean as part of the British hydrogen bomb programme. Nine nuclear explosions were initiated, culminating in the United Kingdom becoming the third recognised possessor of thermonuclear weapons.

The first test, Grapple 1 (Short Granite), on 15 May 1957, underperformed, but the Grapple X test on 8 November 1957 achieved a yield of 1.8 megatons, exceeding expectations. Grapple Y on 28 April 1958, at 3 megatons, remains the largest British nuclear test.

Background

Britain had tested its first atomic bomb in Operation Hurricane (1952) and sought a megaton-range hydrogen bomb to maintain an independent deterrent and influence with the United States. The tests were led scientifically by William Penney and the Aldermaston establishment (AWRE), with devices air-dropped by Vickers Valiant bombers of the Royal Air Force.

Test series

  • Grapple 1–3 (May–June 1957, Malden Island): three air-dropped tests with mixed success.
  • Grapple X (November 1957, Christmas Island): 1.8 Mt, the first fully successful British thermonuclear test.
  • Grapple Y (April 1958): 3 Mt, Britain's largest test.
  • Grapple Z (August–September 1958): four tests, the last British atmospheric tests before the 1958–1961 moratorium.

Legacy

The Grapple tests helped secure the 1958 US–UK Mutual Defence Agreement, restoring nuclear collaboration with the United States. Servicemen and islanders exposed to the tests later pursued compensation claims; the tests remain the subject of historical and legal debate in the UK and Kiribati.
