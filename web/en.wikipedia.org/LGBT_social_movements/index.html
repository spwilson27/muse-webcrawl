LGBT social movements

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

LGBT social movements are social movements that advocate for LGBT people in society. They seek legal equality, an end to discrimination and violence, and social acceptance of lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender and queer people.

History

Early activism began in late 19th-century Germany, where Magnus Hirschfeld founded the Scientific-Humanitarian Committee in 1897 to campaign against Paragraph 175. After the Second World War, "homophile" organisations such as the Mattachine Society (1950) and the Daughters of Bilitis (1955) formed in the United States. The Stonewall riots of June 1969 in New York City — a spontaneous resistance to a police raid on a gay bar — are widely seen as the start of the modern gay liberation movement.

AIDS era to marriage equality

The AIDS crisis of the 1980s spurred new forms of activism, such as ACT UP, demanding faster drug research and an end to official indifference. From the 1990s the movements pursued anti-discrimination laws, hate-crime legislation, military service and ultimately marriage equality, achieved nationwide in the United States by the Supreme Court's 2015 decision in Obergefell v. Hodges, and in many other countries by legislation. Debates continue over transgender rights and the movement's priorities.
