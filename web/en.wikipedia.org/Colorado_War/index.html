The Colorado War was an Indian War fought in 1864 and 1865 between the Southern Cheyenne, the Arapaho, and allied Brulé and Oglala Lakota peoples against the United States Army and Colorado Territory militia. It erupted as white settlement and gold-rush traffic spread across the Central Plains, competing for the hunting grounds of the Platte and Arkansas river valleys guaranteed to the tribes by the Treaty of Fort Laramie (1851) and the Treaty of Fort Wise (1861).

Background

The 1859 Colorado gold rush brought tens of thousands of settlers into territory claimed by the Cheyenne and Arapaho. Under the contested Treaty of Fort Wise, a minority of chiefs ceded most tribal land, but many bands rejected the agreement. By 1864 tension, raids, and settler demands for military action had escalated; territorial governor John Evans issued proclamations ordering peaceful Indians to report to military posts while authorising citizens to kill hostile ones.

Sand Creek massacre

On 29 November 1864, about 700 Colorado volunteers under Colonel John Chivington attacked a village of Cheyenne and Arapaho at Sand Creek, led by chiefs including Black Kettle, who had camped under the protection of the army and flew a United States flag. Between roughly 70 and 160 Native people, mostly women, children, and the elderly, were killed. Congressional investigations condemned the attack as a massacre, and it became one of the most notorious atrocities of the Indian Wars.

Retaliation and aftermath

In response, Cheyenne, Arapaho, and Lakota warriors retaliated across the plains in early 1865, attacking ranches, stage stations, and the town of Julesburg, and effectively cutting the Overland Trail. The war widened into a general plains conflict. The Treaty of the Little Arkansas (1865) attempted a settlement but failed to hold; conflict continued further north and west in the Powder River country, and Black Kettle himself was killed in the Battle of Washita River in 1868.
