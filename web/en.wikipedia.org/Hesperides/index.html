Hesperides

In Greek mythology, the Hesperides are the nymphs of evening and the golden light of sunset, daughters of the Titan Atlas in most accounts (or of Night, or of Hesperus). They tended a blissful garden at the western edge of the world, beyond the Atlas Mountains, where a tree bearing golden apples grew.

The garden and the golden apples

The golden apples were a wedding gift from Gaia to Hera, guarded by the Hesperides and by the hundred-headed dragon Ladon, who never slept. Stealing the apples was the eleventh of the twelve labours of Heracles, who in some versions tricked Atlas into fetching them while he held up the sky in the Titan's place.

Names and legacy

The individual nymphs are most often named Aegle, Erytheia, and Hesperia (or Hesperethusa). The garden's location at the far west associated the Hesperides with the setting sun; later geographers identified their home with sites in Libya or with the Fortunate Isles.
