The World Wide Web Consortium (W3C) is the main international standards organization for the World Wide Web. Founded in 1994 by Tim Berners-Lee, the inventor of the Web, W3C develops open standards — including HTML, CSS, XML, and the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines — to ensure the long-term growth and interoperability of the Web. Its motto is "Leading the Web to its full potential."

History

Berners-Lee founded the consortium at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in October 1994, after leaving CERN. Host institutions have included MIT, INRIA (later ERCIM) in Europe, Keio University in Japan, and Beihang University in China. In 2023 W3C was re-formed as a public-interest non-profit organization, W3C Inc.

Standards process

W3C standards, called Recommendations, are developed by working groups of member organizations and invited experts through a staged process: Working Draft, Candidate Recommendation, Proposed Recommendation, and finally W3C Recommendation. W3C operates under a royalty-free patent policy so that its standards can be implemented by anyone without licensing fees.

Key technologies

Beyond HTML and CSS, W3C has standardized the Document Object Model (DOM), Scalable Vector Graphics (SVG), XML and related technologies (XSLT, XPath), the Semantic Web stack (RDF, OWL, SPARQL), WebAssembly core specifications in cooperation with other bodies, WebRTC, WebAuthn, and the WCAG accessibility guidelines.
