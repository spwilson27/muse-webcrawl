Frumentius

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Saint Frumentius (Ge'ez: Fremnatos; died c. 383 AD) was a Phoenician-born missionary who brought Christianity to the Kingdom of Aksum in present-day Ethiopia and Eritrea. He became the first bishop of Aksum and is venerated as the "Apostle of Ethiopia". In the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church he is known as Abba Salama ("Father of Peace").

Life

The principal account comes from the church historian Rufinus of Aquileia. As boys, Frumentius and his brother Aedesius accompanied their uncle Meropius of Tyre on a voyage to India. On the return journey their ship was attacked at an Eritrean port and the crew killed; the brothers were taken to the court of the king at Aksum, where they rose to positions of trust. Frumentius became tutor to the young crown prince, Ezana, and used his influence to protect Christian merchants and encourage the faith at court.

When Ezana came of age, Frumentius travelled to Alexandria and asked Patriarch Athanasius to appoint a bishop for Aksum. Athanasius consecrated Frumentius himself, remarking that no one was more worthy. Returning to Ethiopia, Frumentius baptised converts, built churches, and is credited with helping convert King Ezana, whose coinage subsequently bore the cross — among the earliest coinage anywhere to do so.

Legacy

Frumentius's mission established the Ethiopian Church's historic link with the Coptic Patriarchate of Alexandria, which supplied Ethiopia's abuna (archbishop) until the twentieth century. The Ge'ez alphabet is also traditionally associated with the translation work of his circle, and the first Ge'ez translations of the Scriptures are dated to this period.
