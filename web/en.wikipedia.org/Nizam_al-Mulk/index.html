Nizam al-Mulk (Persian: نظام‌الملک, "Order of the Realm"; 1018–1092), born Abu Ali Hasan ibn Ali Tusi, was a Persian scholar and statesman who served as vizier of the Seljuk Empire for nearly three decades under sultans Alp Arslan and Malik-Shah I. Effectively the empire's chief administrator, he is remembered as one of the great statesmen of medieval Islam.

Career and achievements

Appointed vizier in 1064, Nizam al-Mulk reorganized the Seljuk administration along Persian bureaucratic lines, regularized the iqta military land-grant system, and patronized Sunni Islam against Ismaili Shi'a influence. He founded a network of colleges — the Nizamiyya — in Baghdad (1065), Nishapur, Isfahan and other cities; the Baghdad Nizamiyya, where al-Ghazali later taught, became the model for the madrasa as an institution. Near the end of his life he composed the Siyāsatnāma ("Book of Government"), a classic of the "mirror for princes" genre advising rulers on justice, administration and vigilance against heresy and corruption.

Death

Nizam al-Mulk was assassinated in October 1092, stabbed by a man widely believed to have been an Ismaili fidā'ī of the Assassins of Hassan-i Sabbah, though court rivals have also been suspected. Sultan Malik-Shah died only weeks later, and the empire descended into succession wars from which it never fully recovered.
