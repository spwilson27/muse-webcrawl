Eight-thousander

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The eight-thousanders are the 14 mountains on Earth that rise more than 8,000 metres (26,247 ft) above sea level. All are located in the Himalayan and Karakoram mountain ranges in Asia. They are the only mountains whose summits lie in the so-called "death zone", the altitude at which the amount of oxygen in the air is insufficient to sustain human life for an extended period.

The first of the peaks to be climbed was Annapurna I, summited on 3 June 1950 by Maurice Herzog and Louis Lachenal of a French expedition. Everest followed in 1953, climbed by Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay.

Climbing all fourteen

Climbing all 14 eight-thousanders is considered one of the ultimate achievements in mountaineering. The first person to do so was Reinhold Messner, completing the set in 1986, all without supplementary oxygen. The first woman to complete the list under uncontested circumstances was Edurne Pasaban (2010), whose completion followed Oh Eun-sun's disputed Kangchenjunga ascent. In 2019, Nirmal Purja climbed all 14 in just 6 months and 6 days, using supplementary oxygen, a feat documented in the film 14 Peaks: Nothing Is Impossible. Fatality rates vary widely by peak: Annapurna I and K2 have historically been the most dangerous.

Debates continue over the reliability of some recorded ascents, and the "8000ers.com" documentation project has shown that several climbers once credited with all 14 stopped at false summits on peaks such as Manaslu and Dhaulagiri; the list of verified finishers was substantially revised in the 2020s.
