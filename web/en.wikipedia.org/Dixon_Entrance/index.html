Dixon Entrance

The Dixon Entrance (Haida: Seegaay) is a strait in the Pacific Ocean on the Canada–United States border, between the U.S. state of Alaska and the province of British Columbia. It lies between Prince of Wales Island to the north and Haida Gwaii (the former Queen Charlotte Islands) to the south, and forms the northern gateway to the Inside Passage.

Geography

The entrance is about 80 kilometres (50 mi) long and wide, opening westward to the open Pacific and connecting eastward with Hecate Strait and Clarence Strait. It was named by Captain Joseph Banks for Captain George Dixon, who surveyed the area in 1787.

Boundary dispute

The Dixon Entrance is the site of a long-standing maritime boundary dispute between Canada and the United States. The 1903 Alaska boundary tribunal left the seaward boundary ambiguous: Canada claims the "A–B line" from the 1825 Anglo-Russian treaty as the maritime border, which would place the entire entrance in Canadian waters, while the United States claims an equidistant line farther south. The dispute affects fisheries enforcement and potential seabed resources, and both countries manage the area pragmatically without resolving the underlying claim.
