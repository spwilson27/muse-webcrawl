Réal Caouette

Réal Caouette (September 26, 1917 – December 16, 1976) was a Canadian politician from Quebec. He was the leader of the Social Credit Party of Canada from 1962 until his death and a charismatic figure in Quebec politics, known for his fiery oratory and populist appeals.

Early life

Born in Amos, Abitibi, Caouette worked as a car dealer and became a devoted follower of Louis Even's Social Credit movement in Quebec. He was first elected to the House of Commons in a 1946 by-election for the Social Credit Party, lost his seat in 1949, and returned to Parliament in 1958.

Leader of Social Credit

After the 1962 federal election, in which Quebec Social Credit candidates (Caouette's Ralliement des créditistes) won 26 seats and held the balance of power, Caouette became the dominant figure of federal Social Credit, though clashes with the national leader Robert N. Thompson of Alberta split the movement. Caouette's Quebec wing ran separately as the Ralliement créditiste from 1963, winning significant Quebec support through the 1960s and early 1970s, before reuniting with the national party in 1971 under his leadership.

Death and legacy

Caouette was seriously injured in a snowmobile accident in 1976 and died in office that December. His son Gilles Caouette also served as a créditiste MP. Réal Caouette is remembered as one of the most entertaining and effective stump speakers in Canadian political history.
