The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR; Regulation (EU) 2016/679) is a regulation in EU law on data protection and privacy in the European Union and the European Economic Area. It also addresses the transfer of personal data outside the EU and EEA. Its primary aim is to give individuals control over their personal data and to simplify the regulatory environment for international business by unifying regulation within the EU. The GDPR became fully applicable on 25 May 2018 after a two-year transition period, replacing the 1995 Data Protection Directive.

Key provisions

The regulation defines roles for data controllers and processors and sets out principles — lawfulness, fairness and transparency; purpose limitation; data minimisation; accuracy; storage limitation; integrity and confidentiality; and accountability. Processing requires a lawful basis such as consent, contract or legitimate interests. Individuals hold rights including access, rectification, erasure (the "right to be forgotten"), restriction, data portability, objection, and rights concerning automated decision-making and profiling. Controllers must implement data protection by design and by default, maintain records, appoint data protection officers in specified cases, and notify supervisory authorities of personal data breaches within 72 hours.

Enforcement and fines

Each member state has an independent supervisory authority; the European Data Protection Board coordinates cross-border cases. Fines can reach €20 million or 4% of global annual turnover. Major penalties have included €1.2 billion against Meta (2023, data transfers), €746 million against Amazon (2021), and €50 million against Google (2019). The GDPR has extraterritorial reach to organisations outside the EU that offer goods or services to, or monitor, EU residents, and it has served as a model for laws such as Brazil's LGPD and California's CCPA/CPRA.

Reception

Supporters credit the GDPR with raising global privacy standards (the "Brussels effect"); critics cite compliance burdens, cookie-banner fatigue, and uneven enforcement, especially in cross-border complaints.
