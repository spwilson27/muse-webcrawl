Viral video

A viral video is a video that becomes popular through a viral process of Internet sharing, typically through video sharing websites such as YouTube and TikTok, social media and email. Viral videos often contain humorous content and are spread far beyond their creator's original audience, sometimes accumulating hundreds of millions of views. The term derives from the metaphor of a virus spreading from person to person.

History

Before video platforms, clips circulated by email and on websites of the early 2000s — examples include the "Dancing baby" animation of the 1990s and flash cartoons such as Peanut Butter Jelly Time. YouTube's launch in 2005 massively accelerated the phenomenon: early viral hits included "Evolution of Dance" (2006), "Charlie Bit My Finger" (2007) and Susan Boyle's 2009 Britain's Got Talent audition. The most famous viral music video, "Gangnam Style" (2012), was the first YouTube video to reach one billion views.

Characteristics and impact

Common ingredients include humour, surprise, emotion, music and participatory potential (remixes, dances, challenges such as the Ice Bucket Challenge). Virality can launch entertainment careers, drive marketing campaigns, and move news and politics, but can also subject private individuals to unwanted fame or harassment. Marketers deliberately attempt to engineer viral content, with mixed success.
