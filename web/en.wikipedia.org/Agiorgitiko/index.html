Agiorgitiko

OriginNemea, Greece

Agiorgitiko (Greek: Αγιωργίτικο, "St George's") is a red Greek wine grape variety, the most widely planted red variety in Greece. It is associated above all with Nemea in the northeastern Peloponnese, where it produces the wines of the Nemea Protected Designation of Origin. The name derives from Agios Georgios ("Saint George"), the former name of the town of Nemea.

Viticulture

Agiorgitiko is a versatile but demanding variety, grown at altitudes from about 250 to 900 metres around Nemea, where soil and elevation produce markedly different wine styles. It buds and ripens late, is prone to viral diseases and water stress, and can yield generously; careful canopy management and yield control are needed for quality.

Wine styles

The grape produces deeply coloured, aromatic red wines with flavours of red and black fruit, sweet spice, and supple tannins. Depending on altitude and winemaking, styles range from fresh, fruity wines for early drinking to structured, oak-aged wines capable of long cellaring, as well as rosés and even semi-sweet wines. Lowland fruit tends to ripeness and softness; highland fruit gives freshness and ageing potential.

History and legend

Local tradition links the vineyards of Nemea with the myth of Heracles and the Nemean lion, and with the ancient wine of Phlius. Agiorgitiko is sometimes poetically called the "blood of Heracles". Modern Nemea winemaking revived after the 1970s and the appellation is now regarded as Greece's flagship red wine region.
