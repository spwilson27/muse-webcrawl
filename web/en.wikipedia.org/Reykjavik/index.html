Reykjavík is the capital and largest city of Iceland. Located in southwestern Iceland on the shore of Faxaflói bay, it is the world's northernmost capital of a sovereign state. Around two-thirds of Iceland's population lives in Reykjavík and the surrounding Capital Region municipalities.

History

According to the medieval Landnámabók, the Norse settler Ingólfur Arnarson established his farm at Reykjavík around 874 AD, casting his high-seat pillars into the sea and settling where they washed ashore; steam rising from nearby hot springs gave the place its name, "smoky bay". For centuries it remained a small farm and trading site. Urban development began in the 18th century with Danish wool workshops, and Reykjavík received its town charter in 1786. It became the seat of the restored Althing in 1845 and capital of an independent Iceland in 1918 (sovereignty) and 1944 (republic).

Geography and climate

The city sprawls across a low coastal plain backed by Mount Esja across the bay. Its subpolar oceanic climate is moderated by the North Atlantic Current: winters are cold but milder than many places at similar latitudes (January averages near 0 °C), summers cool (July averages around 11–12 °C). Daylight ranges from a few hours in midwinter to nearly 24 hours around the summer solstice. The city is powered almost entirely by renewable geothermal and hydroelectric energy, with geothermal water heating most buildings.

Culture and landmarks

Landmarks include Hallgrímskirkja, the towering Lutheran church visible across the city; the Harpa concert hall (opened 2011) on the old harbour; the Sun Voyager sculpture; and the National Museum and Settlement Exhibition, which displays a Viking longhouse. Reykjavík hosts the Iceland Airwaves music festival, a lively literary culture (Iceland publishes more books per capita than nearly any country), and — as a small, pedestrian-friendly capital — is the base for most visitors touring Iceland's natural attractions. In 1986 it hosted the Reykjavík Summit between Ronald Reagan and Mikhail Gorbachev at Höfði house, a step toward the end of the Cold War.
