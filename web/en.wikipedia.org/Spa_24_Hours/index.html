Spa 24 Hours

The Spa 24 Hours is an endurance racing event held annually at the Circuit de Spa-Francorchamps in Belgium. First held in 1924, it is one of the world's most prestigious endurance races, alongside the 24 Hours of Le Mans, the Nürburgring 24 Hours, and the 24 Hours of Daytona.

History

The race was conceived by Jules de Their and Henri Langlois Van Ophem after the success of the Le Mans 24 Hours. Early editions were for touring cars on the original 15 km circuit. After lapses in the 1930s and during the Second World War, the race was revived and became a fixture of the European Touring Car Championship.

Modern era

Since 2011 the race has been the headline event of the Blancpain Endurance Series (now the GT World Challenge Europe Endurance Cup) and the Intercontinental GT Challenge, run for GT3-specification cars. Fields regularly exceed 60 cars, with factory-backed entries from manufacturers such as Audi, BMW, Ferrari, Lamborghini, Mercedes-AMG, and Porsche.

The race is typically held in late June or early July and is noted for unpredictable Ardennes weather, which can see rain on one part of the 7 km circuit while another remains dry.

Notable winners

Multiple overall winners include Eric van de Poele, who holds the record with five victories, and teams run by BMW, which has the most manufacturer wins.
