Professional writing

Professional writing is writing done for a specific audience and purpose in a workplace or business context, as opposed to academic, creative, or personal writing. It encompasses the documents professionals produce to inform, instruct, record, persuade, or transact: reports, proposals, manuals, memoranda, policies, business letters, press releases, and web content. A person employed to produce such writing is a professional writer; related specialisations include technical writing, medical writing, grant writing, and copywriting.

Characteristics

Professional writing is distinguished by its attention to audience and purpose: the writer analyses who will read the document, what they need, and what action or understanding should result. Qualities prized in professional writing include clarity, concision, accuracy, correct tone, and usability — readers typically skim workplace documents for the information they need rather than reading them in full. Conventions of formatting, headings, and document design aid this task-oriented reading.

Forms

Common forms include business reports and feasibility studies, grant and business proposals, technical documentation and user guides, standard operating procedures, internal memoranda and email, marketing copy, and content written for organisational websites and intranets. Professional writers may also ghostwrite speeches, articles, and books credited to executives or experts.

Practice and ethics

Professional writers frequently work as employees of corporations, government agencies, and nonprofits, or as freelancers and consultants. The field has associated professional bodies, such as the Society for Technical Communication, and academic programs in professional and technical writing. Ethical standards emphasise honesty, avoidance of plagiarism, respect for confidentiality, and accessibility — including plain-language requirements for government communication in several countries.

Distinction from other writing

Whereas academic writing serves scholarly audiences and creative writing serves artistic aims, professional writing is judged by its effectiveness in accomplishing an organisational goal. Its style is correspondingly direct, and its success is measured by outcomes — decisions made, tasks completed, funds secured — rather than by literary merit.
