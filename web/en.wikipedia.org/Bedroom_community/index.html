Bedroom community

A bedroom community, also called a commuter town, dormitory town or dormitory suburb, is a residential community whose inhabitants mostly commute to work in a nearby larger city or employment centre. Such towns have few local jobs relative to their population and are characterised by quiet daytime streets, schools and local retail oriented to residents, and heavy rush-hour traffic flows.

Characteristics

Bedroom communities typically grew around railway stations or highway interchanges, offering cheaper or larger housing than the central city. The term is sometimes mildly pejorative, implying a place that "sleeps" during the working day and lacks its own economic or cultural life. Related concepts include the exurb — a community beyond the suburbs, often with longer commutes — and the "edge city", which by contrast has substantial employment.

Examples and effects

Examples include commuter towns in London's metropolitan area, suburbs of New York City such as those in Westchester County and New Jersey, and towns around many other major cities worldwide. Long commutes from bedroom communities contribute to congestion, transport emissions and demand for rail and park-and-ride infrastructure; conversely, they can offer residents access to urban wages with suburban amenities. Remote working has reduced daily commuting from some such towns since 2020.
