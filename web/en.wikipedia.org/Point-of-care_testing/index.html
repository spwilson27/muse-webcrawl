Point-of-care testing

Point-of-care testing (POCT), also known as bedside testing, is defined as medical diagnostic testing performed at or near the patient's site of care, rather than in a centralized laboratory. It typically involves small, portable devices operated by clinical staff or by patients themselves, providing results rapidly enough to inform immediate treatment decisions.

Examples

  • Blood glucose monitoring with handheld meters
  • Blood gas and electrolyte analysis
  • Rapid coagulation testing (e.g., INR)
  • Urine dipstick and pregnancy tests
  • Rapid antigen and molecular tests for infectious diseases, including influenza, streptococcal pharyngitis, HIV, and SARS-CoV-2
  • Cardiac markers such as troponin
  • Hemoglobin and hematocrit measurement

Advantages and limitations

POCT reduces turnaround time, eliminates sample transport, and can improve outcomes in acute care, remote settings, and chronic disease self-management. Limitations include higher per-test cost, variable analytical quality compared with central laboratory testing, and the need for operator training, quality control, and connectivity to medical records. Regulatory frameworks generally require that POCT be performed under a laboratory quality management system, with devices such as CLIA-waived tests in the United States permitted for use by non-laboratory personnel.

Technology

Common technologies include lateral flow immunoassays, electrochemical biosensors, dry chemistry slides, and compact nucleic acid amplification platforms. Increasingly, smartphone-based readers and connected devices allow results to be documented automatically in electronic health records.
