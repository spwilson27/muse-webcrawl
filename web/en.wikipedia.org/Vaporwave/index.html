Vaporwave

Vaporwave is a microgenre of electronic music and an Internet meme that emerged in the early 2010s. The style is defined by its appropriation of 1980s and 1990s mood music — such as smooth jazz, R&B, and lounge music — and generally features extensive use of samples, slowed-down and chopped-and-screwed techniques, and a visual aesthetic drawing on early Internet imagery, late 1990s web design, glitch art, anime, Greco-Roman statuary, and cyberpunk tropes.

Origins

Vaporwave grew out of the hypnagogic pop and chillwave scenes and online communities on sites such as Tumblr. Key early releases include Eccojams Vol. 1 (2010) by Chuck Person (Daniel Lopatin) and Floral Shoppe (2011) by Macintosh Plus (Ramona Xavier), whose track "リサフランク420 / 現代のコンピュー" became the genre's most recognisable work. The name derives from "vaporware", products announced but never released.

Style and themes

Producers often adopt Japanese text, corporate slogans, and pseudonyms evoking fictional companies. The genre is frequently read as a critique — or parody — of consumer capitalism, Internet culture, and nostalgia for the mass media of the 1980s–90s: advertising jingles, hold music, and Windows 95-era interface sounds are recontextualised into dreamy, uncanny collages.

Legacy and subgenres

Vaporwave's visual language ("vaporwave aesthetic", ＡＥＳＴＨＥＴＩＣＳ in full-width characters) spread widely through Internet culture. It spawned numerous offshoots, including future funk, hardvapour, mallsoft, and the more meditative "signalwave"; in 2019 the style saw a popular resurgence with the "barber beats" scene.
