Disc dog

Disc dog (commonly called Frisbee dog in the United States) is a dog sport in which handlers and dogs compete in events such as distance catching and choreographed freestyle catching. Dogs leap to catch a flying disc thrown by their handler, and routines are judged on athleticism, accuracy, teamwork, and showmanship.

History

The sport gained wide attention on August 4, 1974, when Alex Stein ran onto the field during a nationally televised baseball game at Dodger Stadium and performed catches with his whippet, Ashley. The demonstration drew such interest that it is credited with launching organized canine disc competitions, including the Frisbee Dog World Championship, first held the following year.

Competition formats

Toss and fetch events score points for catches at increasing distances within a time limit. Freestyle routines, set to music, feature tricks such as vaults off the handler's body, multiple-disc sequences, and dog catches in mid-flip. Popular breeds include Border Collies, Australian Shepherds, and whippets. Organizers such as the UFO, USDDN, and Skyhoundz run international circuits, and competitors are advised on safe disc use and gradual conditioning to reduce injury risk.
