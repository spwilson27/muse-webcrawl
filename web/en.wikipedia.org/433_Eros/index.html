433 Eros is a stony asteroid of the Amor group and the first discovered, and second-largest, near-Earth object. Discovered by German astronomer Carl Gustav Witt at the Urania Observatory in Berlin on 13 August 1898, it measures about 16.8 kilometres in mean diameter and has an elongated, peanut-like shape.

NEAR Shoemaker mission

NASA's NEAR Shoemaker spacecraft orbited Eros for a year beginning in February 2000, mapping its surface, composition and internal structure. On 12 February 2001 the spacecraft made a controlled descent and landed on the asteroid's surface — the first landing on an asteroid — and transmitted data for about two weeks afterward. The mission showed Eros to be an undifferentiated, fractured but coherent body covered in regolith, craters and large boulders.

Astronomical importance

Eros played a historic role in measuring the scale of the Solar System: during its close approaches in 1900–01 and 1930–31, worldwide parallax campaigns against Eros produced the most accurate values then available for the astronomical unit. Its orbit brings it within about 0.15 AU of Earth, though it does not cross Earth's orbit.
