San Pedro cactus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

San Pedro cactus

Scientific name: Trichocereus macrogonus var. pachanoi (syn. Echinopsis pachanoi)

Family: Cactaceae

Native range: Andes of Ecuador and Peru, 2,000–3,000 m

Traditional name: Wachuma / Huachuma

Active compound: Mescaline

The San Pedro cactus is a fast-growing columnar cactus native to the Andes of Ecuador and Peru. It is cultivated worldwide as an ornamental and is one of the best-known mescaline-containing cacti, with a documented history of traditional use in Andean healing and divination ceremonies (under the Quechua name wachuma) extending back thousands of years. Its common name derives from the belief that, as Saint Peter holds the keys to heaven, the cactus allows its users to reach heaven while still on earth.

Description

The stems are light to dark green or blue-green columns, typically 3–6 metres tall in cultivation and occasionally much taller, with six to eight rounded ribs and short spines. It branches from the base, grows quickly for a cactus (up to 30–60 cm a year in good conditions), is frost-tolerant for short periods, and propagates readily from cuttings. Large, fragrant white flowers open at night in summer.

Traditional use

Ceramic and stone art of the Chavín, Salasaca and Moche cultures, some dating back more than 3,000 years, depict the cactus in ritual contexts. In contemporary Peruvian traditional medicine, curanderos use San Pedro in night-time healing ceremonies (mesas) to diagnose illness and ward off spiritual harm. Mescaline, its principal psychoactive alkaloid, makes the plant controlled or restricted in many countries, though the cactus itself is widely and legally sold as an ornamental in much of the world.
