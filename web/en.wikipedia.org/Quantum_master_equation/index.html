Quantum master equation

A quantum master equation is a differential equation describing the time evolution of the density matrix of an open quantum system — a quantum system interacting with an environment (or "bath"). Whereas a closed system evolves unitarily under the von Neumann equation, coupling to an environment produces dissipation and decoherence, which master equations model.

Lindblad form

The most widely used Markovian master equation is the Gorini–Kossakowski–Sudarshan–Lindblad (GKSL) equation, derived independently in 1976. It adds dissipator terms built from Lindblad (jump) operators to the Hamiltonian commutator; this form guarantees that the evolution is completely positive and trace-preserving, so the density matrix remains physically valid.

Derivations and applications

Master equations can be derived microscopically under assumptions such as weak coupling (Born approximation) and fast bath correlations (Markov approximation), as in the Redfield equation, which however need not preserve positivity. They are fundamental tools in quantum optics (where they describe laser dynamics, spontaneous emission, and cavity losses), quantum information science, and quantum thermodynamics.
