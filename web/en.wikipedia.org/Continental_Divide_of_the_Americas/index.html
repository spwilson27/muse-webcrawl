The Continental Divide of the Americas, also known as the Great Divide, is the principal hydrological divide of the Americas. It separates the watersheds that drain into the Pacific Ocean from those river systems that drain into the Atlantic and Arctic oceans, including those draining via the Gulf of Mexico, the Caribbean Sea, and Hudson Bay.

Course

The divide runs from Cape Prince of Wales in Alaska, through the Canadian Rockies and the Rocky Mountains of the United States — crossing Montana, Wyoming, Colorado, and New Mexico — then through Mexico's Sierra Madre Occidental, Central America, and the Andes of South America, finally reaching Tierra del Fuego. In the United States its highest point is Grays Peak in Colorado at 14,278 feet (4,352 m). The divide passes through Triple Divide Peak in Montana, where the Atlantic, Pacific, and Arctic watersheds meet.

Features and crossings

Notable crossings include numerous Rocky Mountain passes used by railways and highways, and the Panama Canal, which cuts through the divide at the Culebra Cut. The Continental Divide Trail, one of the United States' National Scenic Trails, follows the divide for some 3,100 miles (5,000 km) from Mexico to Canada. In parts of Wyoming's Great Divide Basin, the divide splits around an endorheic basin from which no water reaches any ocean.
