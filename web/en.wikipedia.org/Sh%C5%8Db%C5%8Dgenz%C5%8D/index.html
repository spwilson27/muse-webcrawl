Shōbōgenzō

The Shōbōgenzō (Japanese: 正法眼蔵, "Treasury of the True Dharma Eye") is the masterwork of the Japanese Zen Buddhist monk Eihei Dōgen (1200–1253), founder of the Sōtō school of Zen in Japan. Written in Japanese rather than classical Chinese, it consists of a series of essays and sermons composed between 1231 and 1253.

Contents

The most widely known version contains 95 fascicles (chapters). Famous fascicles include "Genjōkōan" (Actualizing the Fundamental Point), "Uji" (Being-Time), "Sansuikyō" (Mountains and Waters Sutra), and "Bendōwa" (A Talk about Pursuing the Truth). Dōgen's writing is celebrated for its philosophical depth and poetic use of language, treating ordinary activities and natural imagery as expressions of Buddha-nature.

Other works of the same title

Dōgen also compiled the Shōbōgenzō Sanbyakusoku, a collection of 300 kōans in Chinese, and the title was used by earlier Chinese Chan masters. The Japanese Shōbōgenzō remains one of the most studied texts in Zen Buddhism and in Japanese philosophy.
