Israelites

The Israelites were a group of Semitic-speaking peoples in the ancient Levant, described in the Hebrew Bible as descendants of Jacob, also called Israel, organised into Twelve Tribes. They formed the kingdoms of Israel and Judah in the Iron Age.

Biblical account

The Bible recounts descent from Abraham, enslavement in Egypt, the Exodus under Moses, covenant at Sinai, and settlement in Canaan. Historians debate the historicity and scale of these narratives, with archaeological evidence for Israel appearing by the Merneptah Stele around 1208 BCE.

Kingdoms

A united monarchy under Saul, David and Solomon is traditionally dated to around 1000 BCE, followed by divided kingdoms. The northern Kingdom of Israel fell to Assyria in 722 BCE; Judah fell to Babylon in 586 BCE.

Legacy

The Israelites are ancestors in tradition of the Jewish and Samaritan peoples.
