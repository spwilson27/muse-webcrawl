E8 (mathematics)

In mathematics, E8 is the name given to several closely related structures: an exceptional simple Lie algebra of complex dimension 248, the corresponding Lie groups, and an even unimodular lattice in eight dimensions. The E8 Lie algebra is the largest and most complicated of the five exceptional simple Lie algebras (the others being G2, F4, E6 and E7), and its root system, with 240 roots, is one of the most symmetric objects in mathematics.

Basic features

The E8 Lie algebra has rank 8 and dimension 248. Its Dynkin diagram consists of eight nodes arranged with a branch of lengths 1, 2 and 4 meeting at a central node. The root system contains 240 roots: 112 with integer coordinates and 128 with half-integer coordinates in an eight-dimensional space. The Weyl group of E8 has order 696,729,600 and is the largest of the exceptional Weyl groups; it is the symmetry group of the E8 lattice and of the 421 polytope, whose 240 vertices correspond to the roots.

The E8 lattice

The E8 lattice is the unique (up to isometry) positive-definite even unimodular lattice of rank 8. It gives the densest sphere packing in eight dimensions, a fact conjectured for many years and proved by Maryna Viazovska in 2016. The lattice's theta function is related to the Eisenstein series and to modular forms.

Applications

E8 appears in string theory, where the gauge group E8 × E8 arises in heterotic string theory, and in attempts at unified theories of physics, notably Garrett Lisi's 2007 paper "An Exceptionally Simple Theory of Everything", which attracted wide attention but was widely criticised by physicists. In 2007 a team of mathematicians completed a computation of the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials for the split real form of E8, a landmark calculation in the Atlas of Lie Groups project.
