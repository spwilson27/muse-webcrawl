Data science is an interdisciplinary field that uses scientific methods, processes, algorithms, and systems to extract knowledge and insights from structured and unstructured data. It combines statistics, computer science, and subject-matter expertise, applying techniques from machine learning, data mining, and data visualisation to problems in business, science, and government.

History

The term "data science" was used as early as the 1960s as a synonym for computer science, and emerged in its modern sense in the 1990s, notably through the work of William S. Cleveland, who argued for expanding statistics into a broader discipline of learning from data. The explosive growth of "big data" in the 2000s and 2010s, together with advances in machine learning, made "data scientist" a prominent job title; a widely cited 2012 Harvard Business Review article called it "the sexiest job of the 21st century".

Workflow

Typical data science work involves framing a problem, collecting and cleaning data (often the most time-consuming step), exploratory analysis, building statistical or machine-learning models, evaluating them, and communicating or deploying results. Practitioners commonly use Python and R, SQL databases, and distributed frameworks for large datasets.

Relationship to other fields

Data science overlaps with statistics, artificial intelligence, data engineering, and business analytics. Critics note that the label is sometimes applied loosely, while supporters emphasise its integrative character: joining computation, inference, and domain knowledge in a single practice.
