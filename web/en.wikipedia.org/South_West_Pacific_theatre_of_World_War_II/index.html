South West Pacific theatre of World War II

The South West Pacific theatre, during World War II, was a major theatre of the war between the Allies and the Axis. It included the Philippines, the Dutch East Indies (excluding Sumatra), Borneo, Australia and its Mandate of New Guinea (including the Bismarck Archipelago) and the western part of the Solomon Islands. The theatre took its name from the major Allied operational command in the region, the South West Pacific Area (SWPA), commanded from 1942 by U.S. General Douglas MacArthur.

Japanese forces overran the Philippines, Malaya, and the Dutch East Indies in early 1942. The Allied counteroffensive began in the second half of 1942 with the bitter campaigns on the Kokoda Track and at Milne Bay in New Guinea and on Guadalcanal (under the adjacent South Pacific Area command). MacArthur's forces then advanced along New Guinea's northern coast using a "leapfrogging" strategy — bypassing strongpoints such as Rabaul to seize airfields and cut off Japanese garrisons.

Return to the Philippines

In October 1944 Allied forces landed at Leyte, beginning the liberation of the Philippines, including the Battle of Leyte Gulf, one of the largest naval battles in history. Campaigns continued in Luzon and the southern Philippines, and in 1945 Australian forces fought in Borneo (the Borneo campaign), while isolated Japanese units held out until Japan's surrender in August–September 1945.
