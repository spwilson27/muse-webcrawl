Peroxisomes: defective assembly of these organelles causes the PBD spectrum.

The peroxisome biogenesis disorders (PBDs) are a group of autosomal recessive genetic disorders caused by defects in the assembly of peroxisomes, the cellular organelles that carry out oxidation of very-long-chain fatty acids, synthesis of plasmalogens, and other metabolic functions. They arise from mutations in any of at least 14 PEX genes, which encode proteins called peroxins required for importing enzymes into peroxisomes.

Spectrum

The PBD–Zellweger spectrum ranges in severity. Zellweger syndrome, the most severe form, presents in newborns with profound hypotonia, seizures, liver dysfunction, distinctive craniofacial features, and skeletal stippling, and is usually fatal in the first year of life. Milder forms historically called neonatal adrenoleukodystrophy and infantile Refsum disease present later with developmental delay, vision and hearing loss, leukodystrophy and variable survival into childhood or adulthood.

Diagnosis and management

Diagnosis rests on biochemical testing — characteristically elevated very-long-chain fatty acids and reduced plasmalogens in blood — confirmed by genetic testing. There is no curative treatment; care is supportive, including nutrition, hearing and vision support, seizure management and surveillance of liver and adrenal function.
