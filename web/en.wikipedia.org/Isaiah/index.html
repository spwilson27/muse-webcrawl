Isaiah

Isaiah was an 8th-century BC Israelite prophet who lived in the Kingdom of Judah. He is traditionally regarded as the author of the Book of Isaiah, one of the major prophetic books of the Hebrew Bible and the Old Testament, though modern scholars generally attribute the book to multiple authors across several centuries: chapters 1–39 ("First Isaiah"), 40–55 ("Second Isaiah", from the Babylonian exile) and 56–66 ("Third Isaiah").

Life and message

According to the book's opening, Isaiah prophesied during the reigns of Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah. He witnessed the Assyrian crisis, the fall of the northern kingdom of Israel in 722 BC, and Sennacherib's invasion of Judah. His message combined denunciation of injustice and empty ritual with promises of a remnant and a coming age of peace under a Davidic ruler.

Influence

Isaiah is among the most quoted books in the New Testament, and its "suffering servant" songs have been central to Christian theology. Handel's Messiah sets many Isaianic texts. The complete Great Isaiah Scroll found among the Dead Sea Scrolls is one of the oldest biblical manuscripts known.
