Disintegration is the eighth studio album by the English rock band the Cure, released on 2 May 1989 by Fiction Records. The record marks a return to the introspective gothic rock style the band had established in the early 1980s. As he neared the age of 30, vocalist and guitarist Robert Smith had felt an increased pressure to follow up on the group's pop successes with a more enduring work, and feared the band had not yet produced their masterpiece.

Background and recording

Smith retreated into heavy use of psychedelic drugs to help generate the album's dark, brooding atmosphere. The album was recorded at Hook End Manor in Oxfordshire and mixed at RAK Studios in London. Friction arose with the band's label, which considered the record "wilfully obscure"; the album's closing track and the long, funereal opening song "Plainsong" ran counter to commercial expectations.

Music and reception

Despite the label's fears, Disintegration became the band's commercial peak, charting at number three in the United Kingdom and number 12 in the United States. It produced four singles: "Lullaby", "Fascination Street", "Lovesong" and "Pictures of You". "Lovesong" became the band's most successful single in the US, reaching number two on the Billboard Hot 100. The album has since been widely acclaimed and frequently appears on lists of the greatest albums of all time; it is regarded as the crowning achievement of the Cure's dark trilogy with Pornography (1982).

Track listing

Tracks include "Plainsong", "Pictures of You", "Closedown", "Last Dance", "Lullaby", "Fascination Street", "Prayers for Rain", "The Same Deep Water as You", "Disintegration", "Homesick" and "Untitled", among others on expanded editions.
