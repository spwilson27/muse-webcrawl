Hermann Günther Grassmann

Hermann Günther Grassmann (15 April 1809 – 26 September 1877) was a German polymath known in his own time as a linguist and now recognised as a mathematician of the first rank. He was the inventor of exterior algebra and one of the founders of linear algebra, though his work was largely ignored during his lifetime.

Life

Grassmann was born in Stettin (now Szczecin, Poland), where his father taught mathematics and physics at the Gymnasium. He studied theology and philology at the University of Berlin, returning to Stettin to teach mathematics, physics, German and Latin at secondary schools for the rest of his career. He never held a university chair in mathematics.

Mathematics

In 1844 Grassmann published his masterpiece, Die lineale Ausdehnungslehre ("The Theory of Linear Extension"), which introduced the ideas of an n-dimensional vector space, linear independence, dimension, and the exterior (wedge) product. The work was written in a philosophical style that contemporaries found impenetrable, and it attracted little attention. A revised edition appeared in 1862. Only later, through the work of figures such as Giuseppe Peano and Élie Cartan, was Grassmann's priority recognised. The Grassmannian manifold and the Grassmann algebra (the algebra of differential forms) bear his name, and the Grassmann–Plücker relations appear in modern geometry and physics.

Linguistics and other work

As a Sanskrit scholar, Grassmann formulated Grassmann's law of aspiration dissimilation in Greek and Sanskrit, which still bears his name in Indo-European linguistics. He produced a dictionary of the Rigveda and a translation of it. He also worked on the theory of tides, acoustics, and colour theory, formulating Grassmann's laws of colour mixture.
