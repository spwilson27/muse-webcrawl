The University of Manitoba (U of M) is a public research university in Winnipeg, Manitoba, Canada. Founded in 1877, it was Western Canada's first university. It is a member of the U15 group of research universities and is Manitoba's largest and only research-intensive university.

History and campuses

The university began by granting degrees to students of three denominational colleges — St. Boniface College, St. John's College, and Manitoba College. Its main Fort Garry Campus occupies former river-lot farmland along the Red River in south Winnipeg, while the Bannatyne Campus in the city centre houses the Rady Faculty of Health Sciences, including medicine, dentistry, pharmacy, and rehabilitation sciences.

Academics

Around 31,000 students study in over 100 programs. The university is internationally known for research in infectious diseases — its scientists collaborate with the National Microbiology Laboratory in Winnipeg — Arctic science through the Centre for Earth Observation Science and the Churchill Northern Studies Centre, and agricultural science. Alumni include Marshall McLuhan, who studied at its colleges, and several Rhodes Scholars; contralto and educator figures aside, its medical school has produced leading researchers in HIV/AIDS in Kenya and Canada.

Athletics

The Manitoba Bisons compete in Canada West competition within U Sports, with notable programs in football (Vanier Cup champions, including 2007), volleyball, and hockey.
