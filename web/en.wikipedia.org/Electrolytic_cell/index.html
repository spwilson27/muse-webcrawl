Electrolytic cell

An electrolytic cell is an electrochemical cell that uses electrical energy from an external source to drive a non-spontaneous chemical reaction — a process called electrolysis.

The cell contains two electrodes, an anode and a cathode, immersed in an electrolyte. When a voltage is applied, cations migrate to the cathode, where they are reduced, and anions migrate to the anode, where they are oxidized. In an electrolytic cell the anode is positive and the cathode negative, the reverse of a galvanic (voltaic) cell.

Applications

Electrolytic cells are used to split water into hydrogen and oxygen, to extract reactive metals such as aluminium (Hall–Héroult process) and sodium, to produce chlorine and caustic soda in the chloralkali process, and for electroplating and electrorefining of metals.
