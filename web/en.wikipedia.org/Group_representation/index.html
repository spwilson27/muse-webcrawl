Group representation

In the mathematical field of representation theory, group representations describe abstract groups in terms of bijective linear transformations of a vector space to itself (that is, automorphisms) — in particular, they can be used to represent group elements as invertible matrices so that the group operation can be represented by matrix multiplication. Representations and their character theory are essential tools in group theory, number theory, geometry, and physics, where they are used to study the symmetry of structures through linear algebra.

Definitions

A representation of a group G on a vector space V over a field F is a group homomorphism from G to GL(V), the general linear group of invertible linear transformations of V. For finite V of dimension n, choosing a basis turns this into a homomorphism into the invertible n×n matrices. V is then called the representation space and its dimension the dimension (or degree) of the representation. The trivial representation sends every group element to the identity.

Finite groups

For a finite group over the complex numbers, Maschke's theorem guarantees that every representation decomposes as a direct sum of irreducible representations. The irreducible representations are classified by their characters — the traces of the matrices — which form a character table whose entries satisfy orthogonality relations; the number of irreducible characters equals the number of conjugacy classes, and the sum of the squares of their dimensions equals the order of the group. Frobenius founded the theory in the 1890s, exchanging ideas with Dedekind; Burnside and Schur developed it further, and Schur's lemma is a basic structural result.

Other settings

Important generalisations include permutation representations; representations of infinite groups, where analytic questions arise; unitary representations on Hilbert spaces; representations of Lie groups and Lie algebras, central to the classification of elementary particles (for instance through SU(3)); and modular representation theory, where the field characteristic divides the group order and Maschke's theorem fails. Group representations are closely related to representations of the group algebra, and (for finite groups) to modules over that algebra.
