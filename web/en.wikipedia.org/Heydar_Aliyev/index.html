Heydar Aliyev

Heydar Alirza oghlu Aliyev (10 May 1923 – 12 December 2003) was an Azerbaijani politician who led Azerbaijan twice: as First Secretary of the Azerbaijan Communist Party from 1969 to 1982, and as President of the Republic of Azerbaijan from 1993 until his death in 2003. A former KGB general, he rose to become a full member of the Soviet Politburo and First Deputy Premier of the USSR under Yuri Andropov.

Heydar Aliyev
Born: 10 May 1923, Nakhchivan
Died: 12 December 2003, Cleveland, USA
President of Azerbaijan: 1993–2003

Presidency

Recalled to power amid the chaos of the First Nagorno-Karabakh War in 1993, Aliyev secured a ceasefire in 1994 and signed the "Contract of the Century" with Western oil companies to develop Caspian offshore fields, anchoring Azerbaijan's post-Soviet economy to energy exports. His rule brought stability but was criticized for authoritarianism and a pervasive personality cult.

Legacy

He was succeeded by his son Ilham Aliyev. Baku's international airport, the Heydar Aliyev Center designed by Zaha Hadid, and numerous institutions bear his name. See also Azerbaijan.
