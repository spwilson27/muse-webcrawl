Abraham Lincoln Battalion

The Abraham Lincoln Battalion was a unit of American volunteers who fought for the Spanish Republic during the Spanish Civil War (1936–1939) as part of the XV International Brigade. Named after President Abraham Lincoln, it was the first racially integrated American military unit. Approximately 2,800 Americans served in Spain, most of them in the Lincoln Battalion and associated units; about 750 were killed.

Formation

The first American volunteers reached Spain in late 1936. Organised through the Communist International's recruitment networks, though not all were communists, the battalion was formed in early 1937 at Albacete and trained at Tarazona. Its first commander was Robert Hale Merriman, a University of California economics instructor. The battalion fought under the XV Brigade alongside British, Canadian, and other battalions.

Combat record

The battalion saw heavy action at the Battle of Jarama (February 1937), where it suffered severe casualties attacking entrenched positions at "Suicide Hill"; at Brunete (July 1937); at Quinto and Belchite during the Aragon offensive; at Teruel; and finally in the Ebro offensive of 1938. In September 1938 the Republican government withdrew the International Brigades, and the surviving Americans marched in a farewell parade in Barcelona before returning home.

Legacy

Veterans, organised in the Veterans of the Abraham Lincoln Brigade, faced suspicion during the McCarthy era — premature anti-fascism was treated as evidence of disloyalty. Notable members and associates included writer Ernest Hemingway (as a correspondent), nurse Salaria Kea, and Oliver Law, an African American who briefly commanded the battalion in 1937, the first Black officer to command white American troops in battle. The Abraham Lincoln Brigade Archives preserve their records.
