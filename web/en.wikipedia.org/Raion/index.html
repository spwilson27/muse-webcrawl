Raion

A raion (also spelled rayon) is a type of administrative unit of several post-Soviet states. The term derives from the French rayon, meaning a district or borough. In the Soviet Union, raions were the standard second-level subdivision: the country was divided into oblasts (regions), which were in turn divided into raions, roughly equivalent to counties or districts in other systems. Urban areas could themselves be divided into city raions.

History

The raion system was introduced in the administrative-territorial reforms of the 1920s, replacing the uyezds of the Russian Empire. Raions were intended to be economically coherent units centred on a town or large settlement, containing collective farms, small industry and local services, and governed by a district soviet and party committee.

Current use

After 1991, successor states retained or modified the system. Russia has hundreds of municipal raions (districts) as subdivisions of its federal subjects. In Ukraine, a major reform in 2020 consolidated the country into 136 enlarged raions. Belarus, Kazakhstan, Azerbaijan, Moldova (raioane) and others also use raions. In some Central Asian states the equivalent unit is called a tuman or district, though "raion" remains common in Russian-language use.

City raions

Large cities in several post-Soviet countries are divided into urban raions, each with its own local administration, a system inherited from Soviet urban planning where each district provided its own housing, schools and services.
