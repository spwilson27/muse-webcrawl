Film producer

A film producer is a person who oversees film production. Either employed by a production company or working independently, producers plan and coordinate various aspects of film production, such as selecting the script, coordinating writing, directing and editing, and arranging financing.

Responsibilities

The producer typically initiates a project by acquiring rights to a screenplay or other source material, hires the director and principal creative staff, secures financing and a distribution deal, supervises casting and production, and remains involved through post-production and release. During filming, the producer oversees the budget and schedule together with the line producer and production manager.

Types of producer

Producer credits vary widely. An executive producer is usually a major source of financing or a rights holder. A line producer manages day-to-day logistics and the budget. Co-producers, associate producers and consulting producers hold more limited roles. In television, the showrunner is typically the senior producer with creative control.

Recognition

Producers are the recipients of the Academy Award for Best Picture, since the award goes to the film's producers. The Producers Guild of America represents producers and works to standardise credits, including through its "p.g.a." mark.

Producer vs. director

Whereas the director is generally responsible for the creative realisation of the film on set, the producer holds ultimate responsibility for the production as a business and organisational undertaking, including the power to hire and replace creative personnel.
