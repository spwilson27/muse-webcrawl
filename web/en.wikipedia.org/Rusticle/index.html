Rusticle

TypeBiogeological formation (biocorrosion product)
EnvironmentSubmerged iron/steel in deep water
Famous exampleWreck of RMS Titanic
Key organismsConsortia of bacteria and fungi; Halomonas titanicae

A rusticle is a formation of rust similar to an icicle or stalactite in appearance that occurs on the surface of submerged iron or steel, most famously on the wreck of the RMS Titanic. The word is a portmanteau of "rust" and "icicle". Rusticles are not simple oxidation: they are complex structures created by consortia of microorganisms that colonise the metal and consume its iron, implying that the structures grow, reproduce and metabolise.

Structure and formation

Rusticles were first observed on the Titanic wreck after its discovery in 1985, hanging from the hull in delicate reddish-brown strands up to several metres long. Research led by Roy Cullimore of the University of Regina showed that a rusticle consists of an outer hardened shell of iron oxides (including goethite and hematite) surrounding a porous, channelled interior through which water circulates. Different zones of the structure host different microbial communities — bacteria, archaea and fungi — that cooperate in extracting iron from the steel.

Halomonas titanicae

In 2010, researchers Henrietta Mann and colleagues described a new species of iron-eating bacterium isolated from a rusticle sample retrieved from the Titanic wreck in 1991, naming it Halomonas titanicae. The bacterium thrives in the wreck's conditions of high pressure, cold and darkness and accelerates the corrosion of the ship's steel.

Significance

Rusticles are the main mechanism by which the Titanic wreck is being consumed; expeditions have documented progressive collapse of the structure, and estimates suggest the wreck could be largely dissolved within decades to a few centuries. Similar formations are studied for their relevance to the degradation of offshore structures, pipelines and other submerged wrecks, and as possible analogues for microbial iron cycling in natural environments.
