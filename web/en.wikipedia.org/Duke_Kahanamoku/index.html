Duke Kahanamoku

Duke Kahanamoku, Olympic swimmer and surfing pioneer (placeholder image)

Duke Paoa Kahinu Mokoe Hulikohola Kahanamoku (August 24, 1890 – January 22, 1968) was a Hawaiian competition swimmer, lifeguard, and actor. He won multiple Olympic medals in swimming and is widely credited with popularizing the sport of surfing around the world, earning him the title "father of modern surfing".

Swimming career

Kahanamoku burst into prominence in 1911 in Honolulu Harbor, where his freestyle times far exceeded existing records — so dramatically that mainland officials initially doubted them. At the 1912 Stockholm Olympics he won gold in the 100-metre freestyle and silver with the US 4×200-metre relay team. He repeated as 100-metre champion at the 1920 Antwerp Games and took silver in 1924, finishing behind Johnny Weissmuller.

Surfing ambassador

Between swimming competitions, Kahanamoku gave surfing exhibitions, notably in California and in Australia in 1914–15, where his demonstration at Freshwater Beach on a board he shaped from local sugar pine is regarded as a founding moment of Australian surfing. He helped spread the Hawaiian sport globally and was a founding figure of the Waikiki beachboy culture.

Later life

Kahanamoku served as sheriff of Honolulu for many years, appeared in Hollywood films, and in 1925 rescued eight men from a capsized boat in heavy surf at Newport Beach, California, an event that led US lifeguards to adopt surfboard rescue techniques. He died in 1968. His statue stands at Waikiki Beach, and he remains one of Hawaii's most beloved figures.
