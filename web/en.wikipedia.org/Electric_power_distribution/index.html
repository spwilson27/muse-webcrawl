Electric power distribution

Electric power distribution is the final stage in the delivery of electricity to end users. A distribution network carries electricity from the transmission system — typically via distribution substations where voltage is stepped down — to individual consumers through feeders, distribution transformers, and service drops. Distribution voltages are much lower than transmission voltages, commonly a few kilovolts on primary feeders and a few hundred volts at the point of use.

Structure

Substations step transmission voltages down to primary distribution levels (commonly 4–35 kV). Primary feeders, arranged radially or in loops, carry power to pole-mounted or ground-level distribution transformers, which produce the secondary voltages supplied to homes and businesses — 120/240 V in North America and 230/400 V in much of the rest of the world.

Operation

Distribution systems include protective devices such as fuses, reclosers and circuit breakers, voltage regulators and capacitor banks. Modern 'smart grid' developments add advanced metering infrastructure, automated switching and integration of distributed generation such as rooftop solar, which can reverse traditional one-way power flows.
