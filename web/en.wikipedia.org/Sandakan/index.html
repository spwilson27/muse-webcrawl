Sandakan

Country: Malaysia

State: Sabah

Founded: 1879 (as Elopura)

Former role: Capital of British North Borneo (1884–1946)

Population: roughly 400,000 (district)

Sandakan is a city on the north-eastern coast of Borneo, in the Malaysian state of Sabah, facing Sandakan Bay and the Sulu Sea. It was the capital of British North Borneo from the 1880s until after the Second World War, grew rich as a timber port, and is today the gateway to some of Borneo’s most famous wildlife attractions, including the Sepilok Orangutan Rehabilitation Centre and the Kinabatangan River.

History

The settlement was founded in 1879 by the Scots trader William Pryer as Elopura (“beautiful city”) and became the seat of the British North Borneo Chartered Company administration. Timber, rattan, birds’ nests and marine produce made it a booming multi-ethnic port with a large Chinese community. Under Japanese occupation (1942–45) Sandakan was the site of a prisoner-of-war camp where some 2,400 Australian and British prisoners were held; in 1945 the Japanese forced them on the Sandakan Death Marches to Ranau, of whom only six Australians survived — among the worst atrocities against Australian servicemen. Allied bombing and the occupation destroyed the town almost completely, and after the war the capital of the crown colony moved to Jesselton (now Kota Kinabalu).

Modern Sandakan

Rebuilt on a new waterfront site, modern Sandakan is a centre for palm oil, timber, fishing and tourism. Visitors use it as the base for Sepilok’s orangutan and sun bear sanctuaries, the Rainforest Discovery Centre, the Gomantong Caves, and boat trips to see proboscis monkeys and pygmy elephants along the Kinabatangan. Sights in town include the Agnes Keith House (her memoir Land Below the Wind made Sandakan famous), the Sandakan Memorial Park at the POW camp site, and the hilltop Puu Jih Shih Buddhist temple.
