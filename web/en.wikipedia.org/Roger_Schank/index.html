Roger Schank

Roger Carl Schank
Born: 1946, New York City
Died: 29 January 2023
Fields: Artificial intelligence, cognitive science, learning sciences
Institutions: Yale University, Northwestern University

Roger Carl Schank (1946 – January 29, 2023) was an American artificial intelligence theorist, cognitive psychologist, learning scientist, and educational reformer. He was a pioneer of case-based reasoning and natural language processing, and a professor at Stanford, Yale, and Northwestern universities.

Artificial intelligence research

In the 1970s, Schank introduced conceptual dependency theory, a framework for representing the meaning of natural language sentences in terms of primitive concepts and actions. With his students at Yale, he developed early programs for story understanding, including SAM and PAM, and the theory of scripts and plans, described in Scripts, Plans, Goals, and Understanding (1977), written with Robert Abelson. In the 1980s he developed case-based reasoning, the idea that intelligence and memory work by retrieving and adapting past cases, founding the Institute for the Learning Sciences at Northwestern in 1989.

Education reform

Schank became a vocal critic of conventional schooling, arguing that learning happens by doing and by storytelling rather than by lectures and tests. He advocated "learning by doing" simulations and goal-based scenarios, and founded several education technology companies, including Socratic Arts and Engineated Arts.
