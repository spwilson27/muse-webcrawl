Stope

An open stope underground

A stope is a dugout tunnel in an underground mine from which ore is extracted. Stopes are the void spaces created by the process of stoping, in which ore is broken from the surrounding rock in a series of layers or slices and removed, leaving open cavities or voids supported by pillars, timber, or backfill.

Stoping methods

Methods include overhand and underhand stoping, shrinkage stoping (in which broken ore is temporarily left in the stope as a working platform and support), cut-and-fill stoping, square-set stoping for weak ground, and longhole or sublevel open stoping used in modern mechanised mines. The method chosen depends on the dip, thickness, and strength of the orebody and walls.

Terminology

The hanging wall is the rock above the stope and the footwall the rock below. Levels are connected by raises and winzes, and mined stopes are accessed through drifts. Backfilled stopes are refilled with waste rock, sand, or cemented tailings to support the surrounding ground and allow adjacent pillars to be mined.
