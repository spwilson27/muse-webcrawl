Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution

The Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution (WHOI, pronounced "hooey") is a private, nonprofit research and higher education facility dedicated to the study of the ocean, based in the village of Woods Hole on Cape Cod, Massachusetts. Founded in 1930, it is the largest independent oceanographic research institution in the United States.

Research and facilities

WHOI employs roughly a thousand scientists, engineers and staff across departments of physical oceanography, marine chemistry and geochemistry, geology and geophysics, biology, and applied ocean physics and engineering. It operates research vessels including R/V Atlantis and R/V Neil Armstrong, the deep-submergence vehicle Alvin — famous for exploring the wreck of the Titanic in 1986 — and robotic systems such as the remotely operated vehicle Jason.

History and education

The institution was established with Rockefeller Foundation funding at the recommendation of the National Academy of Sciences, and grew rapidly through naval research in the Second World War. Since 1968 it has run a joint graduate program with the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. WHOI scientists have contributed to discoveries including hydrothermal vents (1977), the wreck of RMS Titanic (1985, with French partners), and major advances in understanding ocean circulation and climate.
