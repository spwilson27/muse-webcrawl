Quirinus

Quirinus
Member of the Archaic Triad
Festival: Quirinalia (17 February)
Temple: Quirinal Hill, Rome

Quirinus is an early god of the Roman state, one of the most ancient deities of Roman religion. Together with Jupiter and Mars he formed the Archaic Triad, served by the three major flamens. In later times Quirinus was identified with the deified Romulus, the legendary founder of Rome.

Origins

The origin of Quirinus and of his name is uncertain and was debated already in antiquity. He may have begun as the divine embodiment of the Roman citizen body — the Quirites — or as a Sabine war god imported with the Sabine population of the Quirinal Hill, where his temple stood. His priest, the Flamen Quirinalis, ranked among the most senior of Rome's flamens.

Identification with Romulus

According to legend, Romulus disappeared during a storm and was seen in a vision by Proculus Julius, announcing that he had become the god Quirinus and would watch over his people. The story is told by Livy and Ovid, and the identification made Quirinus the divine form of Rome's founder. His festival, the Quirinalia, was celebrated on 17 February and was also jokingly called the Feast of Fools, since it served as a last chance for those who had missed their own curia's Fornacalia to bake their spelt.

Worship

Quirinus's very old temple on the Quirinal Hill was vowed in 293 BC. As Roman religion developed, Quirinus faded in prominence relative to Jupiter and Mars, and his original functions became obscure even to Roman antiquarians.
