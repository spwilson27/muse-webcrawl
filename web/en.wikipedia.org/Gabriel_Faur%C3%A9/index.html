Gabriel Fauré

Gabriel Urbain Fauré (pronounced [ɡabʁi.ɛl yʁbɛ̃ foʁe]; 12 May 1845 – 4 November 1924) was a French composer, organist, pianist and teacher. He was one of the foremost French composers of his generation, and his musical style influenced many 20th-century composers. Among his best-known works are his Pavane, Requiem, Sicilienne, nocturnes for piano and the songs "Après un rêve" and "Clair de lune".

Life

Born in Pamiers, Ariège, Fauré showed early musical talent and at nine was sent to the École Niedermeyer in Paris, where he was taught by Camille Saint-Saëns among others. He worked for many years as a church organist — notably at La Madeleine in Paris — and as a teacher, while composing largely in his spare time. From 1905 to 1920 he was director of the Paris Conservatoire, where he introduced reforms and taught a generation of younger composers. Progressive deafness afflicted his later years.

Music

Fauré's music is characterised by refinement, restrained emotion, modal inflections, and fluid melodic lines. His output includes the opera Pénélope, incidental music for Pelléas et Mélisande, chamber works including two piano quartets and two piano quintets, the Dolly suite, thirteen nocturnes and thirteen barcarolles for piano, the song cycles La bonne chanson and L'horizon chimérique, and the beloved Requiem in D minor, Op. 48.

Legacy

Although his works reached only a small audience during the first part of his career, Fauré is now recognised as a master of French art song and chamber music, and a pivotal link between Romanticism and the music of Ravel and later modernists.
