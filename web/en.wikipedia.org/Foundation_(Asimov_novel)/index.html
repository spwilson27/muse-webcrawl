Foundation is a science fiction novel by American author Isaac Asimov, first published in book form in 1951 by Gnome Press. It is the first volume of the Foundation trilogy (later expanded into the Foundation series) and consists of five interrelated stories originally published in Astounding Science Fiction between 1942 and 1944, plus an introductory section written for the book edition.

Plot summary

Mathematician Hari Seldon, founder of the science of psychohistory—which predicts the future behaviour of vast populations—foresees the fall of the 12,000-year-old Galactic Empire and a 30,000-year dark age to follow. Unable to prevent the fall, Seldon wins imperial permission to establish a Foundation of scientists and scholars on the remote planet Terminus at the galaxy's edge, ostensibly to compile an Encyclopedia Galactica, in truth to shorten the interregnum to 1,000 years.

The succeeding stories follow the young Foundation through a sequence of "Seldon crises," in which political and historical pressures force a single viable course of action. Mayor Salvor Hardin outmanoeuvres the neighbouring kingdoms, first using the balance of power and later a science-based religion ("scientism") centred on the Foundation's control of atomic technology. In the final story, trader Hober Mallow defeats the Republic of Korell and the imperial general Bel Riose's era approaches, shifting the Foundation's power base from religion to commerce.

Background and reception

Asimov conceived the series after reading Edward Gibbon's Decline and Fall of the Roman Empire and proposed it to editor John W. Campbell in 1941. The Foundation trilogy won the one-time Hugo Award for "Best All-Time Series" in 1966. Foundation has remained continuously in print and is regarded as a landmark of the genre; a television adaptation premiered on Apple TV+ in 2021.
