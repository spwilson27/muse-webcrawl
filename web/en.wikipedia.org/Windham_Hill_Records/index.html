Windham Hill Records

Windham Hill Records was an American independent record label based in California, founded in 1976 by guitarist William Ackerman and Anne Robinson. It became the defining label of the acoustic instrumental style later marketed as new-age music, known for its pristinely recorded albums and minimalist, art-gallery cover design.

Founded1976, Palo Alto, California
FoundersWilliam Ackerman, Anne Robinson
GenreAcoustic instrumental, new-age, contemporary jazz
StatusAbsorbed into larger labels; catalog now with Sony Music/Legacy

History

Ackerman, a fingerstyle guitarist, began the label to distribute his own recordings, The Search for the Turtle's Navel (1976). Through word of mouth and public-radio airplay, Windham Hill's roster grew to include pianists George Winston — whose albums Autumn (1980) and December (1982) sold in the millions — as well as Michael Hedges, Alex de Grassi, the jazz group Shadowfax, and the sampler series that introduced many listeners to the label.

Sound and aesthetic

The label favored solo piano, steel-string guitar, and small ensembles recorded with audiophile clarity. Its covers used muted photography and uniform typography, deliberately resembling contemporary art rather than pop packaging. Though its artists resisted the "new-age" label, Windham Hill's success helped create that retail category.

Sale and legacy

A&M Records took a distribution and ownership stake in the 1980s; the label passed through BMG and later Sony's Legacy Recordings, which reissued key titles. Windham Hill's influence persists in acoustic instrumental and "contemporary piano" music and in the marketing of contemplative lifestyle music.
