Nassau, Bahamas

Nassau (/ˈnæsɔː/) is the capital and largest city of the Commonwealth of The Bahamas, located on the island of New Providence. With a population of more than 270,000, it contains about 70% of the country's population and is its commercial, political and cultural centre.

History

Founded by the English around 1670 as Charles Town, the settlement was renamed Nassau in 1695 in honour of William III of the House of Orange-Nassau. In the early 18th century it became the capital of the pirate republic, hosting figures such as Blackbeard, Calico Jack and Anne Bonny, until Governor Woodes Rogers restored British rule in 1718. During American Prohibition it prospered as a rum-running centre.

Geography and economy

Nassau has a tropical monsoon climate. Its economy is dominated by tourism — cruise ships dock at Prince George Wharf, and the Atlantis resort lies across the harbour on Paradise Island, connected by two bridges — and by offshore financial services. Landmarks include Bay Street, the Straw Market, Fort Charlotte, Fort Fincastle, the Queen's Staircase and Government House. Lynden Pindling International Airport serves the city.
