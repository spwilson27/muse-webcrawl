Backstroke

Backstroke or back crawl is one of the four swimming styles used in competitive events, and the only one swum on the back. Swimmers use alternating circular arm movements and an up-and-down flutter kick, with the face above the water allowing easy breathing.

Technique

The arms alternate in a windmill motion: each arm recovers above the water with a straight elbow-lift and pulls underwater with a bent arm, while the body rolls slightly from side to side. The head remains still, looking upward.

Competition

Backstroke races are contested over 50 m, 100 m and 200 m, and the stroke also opens the medley relay. Uniquely, backstrokers start in the water, gripping the wall or starting handles, and a backstroke ledge is now commonly used. Swimmers may remain submerged for up to 15 metres after the start and each turn.
