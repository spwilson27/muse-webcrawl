Icing (food)

Icing, or frosting in American English, is a sweet, often creamy glaze made of sugar with a liquid such as water or milk, frequently enriched with butter, egg whites, cream cheese or flavourings. It is used to coat or decorate baked goods such as cakes, pastries and biscuits.

Types

Common varieties include royal icing, a hard white icing of egg white and icing sugar used for piping and wedding cakes; buttercream, soft and spreadable; fondant, which can be rolled into smooth sheets; ganache, a chocolate-and-cream glaze; and simple water icings and glazes flavoured with lemon or coffee.

History and decoration

The term "icing" derives from ice, referring to the frosty white appearance of early sugar coatings; refined sugar coatings on cakes appeared in the 17th century, and the first buttercream-style frostings followed in the 19th. Icing is applied by spreading, piping, pouring or dipping, and is tinted with food colouring for decoration.
