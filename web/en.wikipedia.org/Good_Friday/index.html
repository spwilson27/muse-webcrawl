Good Friday is a Christian holy day observing the crucifixion of Jesus Christ and his death at Calvary. It is observed during Holy Week as part of the Paschal Triduum, on the Friday preceding Easter Sunday. It is also known as Holy Friday, Great Friday, Great and Holy Friday (in the Eastern churches), and Black Friday. Members of many Christian denominations observe the day with fasting and church services, and it is a public holiday in numerous countries.

Etymology

The origin of "Good" in Good Friday is debated. One common explanation derives it from an older sense of "good" meaning "holy" or "pious" (as in "good book" for the Bible); another traces it to a corruption of "God's Friday." Older English usage also called the day "Long Friday" for the long services and fast.

Biblical account

According to the Gospels, Jesus was arrested in the Garden of Gethsemane after the Last Supper, tried before the Sanhedrin and then before the Roman prefect Pontius Pilate, and sentenced to death. He carried his cross to Golgotha ("the place of the skull"), was crucified between two thieves, and died after some six hours, with the words reported variously by the evangelists — "Father, into your hands I commend my spirit" (Luke) and "It is finished" (John). His body was laid in the tomb of Joseph of Arimathea before sundown, ahead of the Sabbath. Christians believe his death atones for sin and that it was followed by the Resurrection on Easter Sunday.

Observance

In the Roman Catholic Church, Good Friday is one of two days of obligatory fasting and abstinence. No Mass is celebrated; instead, a liturgy of the Passion includes readings, the solemn intercessions, veneration of the cross, and distribution of communion consecrated on Maundy Thursday. The Stations of the Cross are prayed in many parishes, and Three Hours' Agony services (noon to 3 p.m., the hours of the crucifixion) are common in Anglican and other traditions. Passion plays and processions are famous in the Philippines, Mexico, Spain and elsewhere; in Bermuda, kites are traditionally flown, said to symbolize the Ascension.

Date

Good Friday falls on the Friday before Easter, the first Sunday after the first full moon on or after the spring equinox (as computed by church tables). Eastern and Western computations often differ, so Orthodox and Western Good Fridays frequently fall on different dates.
