Dominion Lands Act

The Dominion Lands Act was a Canadian law passed in 1872 that aimed to encourage the settlement of the Canadian Prairies and to help prevent the area from being claimed by the United States. Modeled in part on the American Homestead Act of 1862, it offered prospective settlers free homesteads of 160 acres (a quarter section).

Provisions

Any male farmer at least 21 years of age (later extended more broadly) could apply for a quarter-section of land for a registration fee of ten dollars, provided he built a house, cultivated a set amount of land, and resided on the homestead for a portion of each year — conditions known as "proving up." Alternating sections were reserved for railway grants, the Hudson's Bay Company, and school lands.

Impact

The Act governed settlement across what became Manitoba, Saskatchewan, and Alberta, drawing millions of immigrants to the West. Its implementation, however, displaced Indigenous peoples and the Métis, whose land rights were inadequately protected. The Act remained in force until 1930, when control of natural resources was transferred to the prairie provinces.
