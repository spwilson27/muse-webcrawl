Divertimento for String Orchestra

ComposerBéla Bartók
CatalogueSz. 113, BB 118
Composed1939
Premiere11 June 1940, Basel
ScoringString orchestra

The Divertimento for String Orchestra, Sz. 113, BB 118, is a three-movement work composed by Béla Bartók in 1939. It was commissioned by the Swiss conductor and patron Paul Sacher, who had previously commissioned Bartók's Music for Strings, Percussion and Celesta, and was written for Sacher's Basler Kammerorchester. Bartók composed the work in the space of about two weeks in August 1939 while staying at Sacher's chalet in Saanen, Switzerland, in the shadow of the approaching Second World War. The premiere took place in Basel on 11 June 1940, conducted by Sacher.

Background

By the late 1930s Bartók was deeply troubled by the rise of fascism in Europe and was considering emigration to the United States, a decision he finally took in 1940. The Divertimento was among the last works he completed in Europe. Despite the circumstances, the piece is generally light-hearted and accessible in character, in the spirit of the eighteenth-century divertimento and the Baroque concerto grosso, though the central slow movement is darker and more troubled in mood.

Music

The work is scored for a string orchestra divided in concerto grosso fashion into a concertino group of soloists and the full ripieno ensemble. It consists of three movements:

  1. Allegro non troppo — a lively movement in sonata form with folk-dance-like themes.
  2. Molto adagio — a slow, nocturnal movement, its intense and brooding atmosphere often associated with "night music".
  3. Allegro assai — a spirited rondo finale in folk style, full of rhythmic drive and humour.

A performance lasts roughly 22 to 25 minutes. The Divertimento has become one of Bartók's most frequently performed works and a staple of the string orchestra repertoire.
