Skipton Castle

Skipton Castle is a Grade I listed medieval castle in Skipton, North Yorkshire, England. It was built in 1090 by Robert de Romille, a Norman baron, initially as a motte and bailey castle made of earth and wood, later rebuilt in stone.

In 1310 the castle passed to the Clifford family, who held it for centuries. During the English Civil War it was the last Royalist stronghold in the north, holding out under siege for three years before surrendering in December 1645. Lady Anne Clifford restored the castle afterwards and planted the yew tree in the Conduit Court in 1659.

The castle is among the best-preserved and most complete medieval castles in England, and is open to the public.
