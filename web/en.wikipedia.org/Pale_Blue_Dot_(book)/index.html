Pale Blue Dot: A Vision of the Human Future in Space is a 1994 non-fiction book by the astronomer Carl Sagan. It takes its title and opening theme from the Pale Blue Dot photograph, taken in 1990 by the Voyager 1 spacecraft at Sagan's urging from a distance of about 6 billion kilometres, in which Earth appears as a fraction-of-a-pixel point suspended in a band of scattered light.

Contents

The book develops Sagan's reflections on the photograph — most famously the passage beginning "Look again at that dot. That's here. That's home. That's us." — into an argument about humanity's place in the universe. Chapters review the history of geocentrism and anthropocentrism, survey the planets explored by robotic spacecraft, and argue for the continued exploration and eventual human settlement of other worlds as both a scientific enterprise and a long-term safeguard for the species.

Sagan also addresses environmental stewardship of Earth, the hazards of asteroids, and the economics and ethics of spaceflight. A later audiobook edition, released posthumously, includes a revised preface by his widow and collaborator Ann Druyan.

Reception

The book was a bestseller and is regarded as a companion and sequel to Sagan's Cosmos (1980). Its "pale blue dot" meditation has been widely quoted in discussions of planetary perspective and environmental ethics.
