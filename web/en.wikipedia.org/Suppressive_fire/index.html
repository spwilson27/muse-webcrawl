Suppressive fire

Suppressive fire is fire from a weapon or weapons intended to degrade the performance of an enemy force below the level needed to fulfil its mission, rather than to destroy it outright. In modern military tactics, suppressive fire is chiefly used to allow friendly forces to close with and destroy an enemy that is well-armed, well-equipped and well-entrenched, or to protect friendly forces withdrawing or advancing under fire.

Purpose

The primary purpose of suppressive fire is to stop the enemy from being able to observe, fire effectively, or move. A force that is suppressed is likely to remain behind cover rather than expose itself to fire, even if the volume or accuracy of the suppressive fire is not immediately lethal. Suppression is often measured not in casualties inflicted but in the enemy's reduced ability to perform combat tasks.

Employment

Suppressive fire is a core element of fire and movement and of combined arms manoeuvre. Typically, one element of a unit — for example a fire team with a light machine gun — lays down a high volume of fire on a known or suspected enemy position while another element moves across open ground to a position of advantage. Machine guns such as the M249 and M240, automatic rifles, and even designated marksmen contribute; indirect weapons such as mortars and artillery can also deliver suppressive fires.

History

The concept became central during the First World War, when massed rifle and machine-gun fire was used to pin defenders in their trenches during assaults, and was refined in the Second World War with the development of the base-of-fire element in infantry squad doctrine. Modern doctrine in many armies, including that of the United States, treats suppression as a distinct task alongside neutralization and destruction.
