Stamford is a town and civil parish in the South Kesteven district of Lincolnshire, England, on the River Welland. It is famed for its exceptionally well-preserved Georgian stone architecture and has been described by the architectural historian Nikolaus Pevsner as one of the finest stone towns in England; Sir Walter Scott called it "the finest stone town in England".

History

Stamford was one of the Five Boroughs of the Danelaw, fortified by the Danes in the 9th century at a crossing of the Welland on the Roman road of Ermine Street. In the Middle Ages it was a prosperous wool town with a famous fair, and in 1333–34 scholars fleeing Oxford attempted briefly to found a university there. The town was bypassed by the main railway lines in the 19th century, which helped preserve its historic centre — it became England's first conservation area in 1967.

Architecture

The town has more than 600 listed buildings of mellow limestone, five medieval parish churches, and notable buildings including the 15th-century Browne's Hospital almshouses and the George Hotel, a historic coaching inn. Nearby stands Burghley House, one of the grandest Elizabethan houses in England, built for William Cecil, Lord Burghley.

Transport and culture

Stamford railway station provides services towards Leicester, Birmingham and Peterborough. The town hosts the Burghley Horse Trials each autumn, one of the world's leading three-day eventing competitions.
