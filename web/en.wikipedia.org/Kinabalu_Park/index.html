Kinabalu Park

Kinabalu Park (Malay: Taman Kinabalu) is Malaysia's first World Heritage Site, in West Coast Division of Sabah, Malaysian Borneo. Established in 1964 as one of the country's first national parks, it covers 754 square kilometres around Mount Kinabalu, at 4,095 metres the highest mountain in Malaysia. The park is one of the world's most important biological sites, with more than 4,500 species of flora and fauna, including 326 bird species, over 100 mammal species, and an extraordinary concentration of endemic plants.

Geology and geography

The Kinabalu massif is a young pluton of granodiorite thrust upward within the last 15 million years and still rising about 5 mm per year. Glacial striations and the U-shaped summit valleys record Pleistocene glaciation rare in the tropics. Climbers ascend from Timpohon Gate (1,866 m) to overnight huts at Laban Rata (3,273 m) before a pre-dawn summit push to Low's Peak. In June 2015 a magnitude-6.0 earthquake centred near the mountain killed 18 climbers and guides and damaged climbing infrastructure.

Biodiversity

The park spans lowland rainforest, montane oak-chestnut forest, coniferous forest and subalpine scrub, compressing habitats equivalent to a journey from the equator to the Arctic. It is a centre of plant diversity for Southeast Asia, famed for orchids (over 800 species recorded in the wider area), carnivorous pitcher plants (Nepenthes rajah among them) and some 5,000–6,000 plant species overall. Fauna includes orangutans, the Bornean gibbon, clouded leopards at its margins, and endemic birds such as the Kinabalu friendly warbler. The mountain is sacred to the Kadazan-Dusun people as the resting place of ancestral spirits (Aki Nabalu, "the revered place of the dead").

Management

Sabah Parks administers the park; climbing permits are limited and licensed mountain guides are mandatory. Park headquarters near Kundasang, the Poring Hot Springs substation with its canopy walkway, and the Mesilau area are the main visitor nodes.
