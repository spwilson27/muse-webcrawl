History of Bengal

The history of Bengal spans several millennia, encompassing the region in the eastern part of the Indian subcontinent that today comprises the Indian state of West Bengal and the country of Bangladesh. Bengal has been successively part of ancient kingdoms, the Delhi Sultanate, an independent sultanate, the Mughal Empire, and British India.

Ancient and medieval periods

Early Bengal was home to the kingdom of Vanga and was incorporated into the Maurya and Gupta empires. The Pala Empire (8th–12th centuries), founded by Gopala, made Bengal a major centre of Mahayana Buddhism, while the succeeding Sena dynasty promoted Hinduism. From the thirteenth century, Bengal came under Muslim rule, first as a province of Delhi and then as the independent Bengal Sultanate, a wealthy trading power.

Mughal and colonial eras

Under the Mughals, Bengal Subah was among the empire's richest provinces, a global centre of textile production, particularly muslin. European trading companies arrived in the seventeenth century; after the Battle of Plassey (1757) and the Battle of Buxar (1764), the British East India Company gained control. Calcutta became the capital of British India until 1911. The devastating Bengal famine of 1770 and that of 1943 each killed millions.

Partition

Bengal was first partitioned in 1905 by Lord Curzon (a decision annulled in 1911), and again in 1947, when the region was divided between India and Pakistan along religious lines. East Bengal became East Pakistan and, after the Liberation War of 1971, the independent state of Bangladesh.
