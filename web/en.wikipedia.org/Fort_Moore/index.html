Fort Moore was a United States Army post established in Los Angeles, California, during the Mexican–American War. Built in 1847 on a hill overlooking the pueblo of Los Angeles, it was named for Captain Benjamin D. Moore, a U.S. Army officer killed at the Battle of San Pasqual in December 1846. It was among the earliest American military installations in California.

History

After U.S. forces reoccupied Los Angeles in January 1847, Mormon Battalion soldiers and regular troops began constructing an earthen breastwork on the hill above the town. The fort was dedicated on July 4, 1847, with a celebration that included one of the first Independence Day observances in Los Angeles, at which the American flag was raised over the post.

The garrison guarded against unrest during the transition from Mexican to American rule and the early California gold rush. The army used the post intermittently until about 1853, after which the site was abandoned. The hill was later graded and developed as the city grew; the location is now near the Los Angeles Civic Center.

Fort Moore Pioneer Memorial

In 1958, the Fort Moore Pioneer Memorial was dedicated on Hill Street near the site of the old fort. The memorial, featuring a large waterfall and a 78-foot bas-relief wall designed by Kazumi Adachi and Dike Nagano, honours the Mormon Battalion, the New York Volunteers, and other pioneers who contributed to early Los Angeles.
