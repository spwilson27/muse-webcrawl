Narragansett Bay is a bay and estuary on the north side of Rhode Island Sound in New England. Formed as a flooded glacial river valley (a ria), it opens into the Atlantic Ocean and forms the defining geographic feature of the state of Rhode Island, whose name it influenced: the mainland was originally called "Rhode Island" after the island now known as Aquidneck.

Geography

The bay extends about 45 km (28 mi) from the Atlantic to the head of tide at Providence, where the Providence, Seekonk, and Blackstone rivers empty. Its three passages — West, East, and Sakonnet — enclose more than thirty islands, the largest being Aquidneck (site of Newport and Middletown), Conanicut (Jamestown), and Prudence. The bay is spanned by the Claiborne Pell (Newport) Bridge, the Jamestown Verrazzano Bridge, and the Mount Hope Bridge.

History and ecology

Giovanni da Verrazzano explored the bay in 1524, and Roger Williams founded Providence at its head in 1636 alongside the Narragansett people, for whom the bay is named. Newport became a great colonial port and later a naval and yachting center, hosting the America's Cup for decades. The estuary supports important shellfisheries — notably the quahog clam — and commercial shipping at the Port of Providence, though it has long struggled with nutrient pollution and hypoxia.
