The Aston Martin DB5 is a British grand tourer (GT) automobile manufactured by Aston Martin and designed by the Italian coachbuilder Carrozzeria Touring Superleggera. Released in 1963, it was an evolution of the final Series 5 of its predecessor, the DB4. Although not the first in the "DB" series, the DB5 is the best-known Aston Martin model, largely because of its famous role as James Bond's car in the film Goldfinger (1964) and subsequent appearances throughout the Bond franchise.

Design and specifications

The principal differences from the DB4 were the enlargement of the all-aluminium Tadek Marek straight-six engine from 3.7 to 4.0 litres (3,995 cc), producing 282 bhp (210 kW) with triple SU carburettors in standard specification, and the standard fitting of a ZF five-speed manual gearbox and Girling disc brakes. The Vantage version, with triple Weber carburettors, produced 314 bhp (234 kW). Top speed was approximately 145 mph (233 km/h). The body retained Touring's Superleggera construction of aluminium panels over a tubular steel frame, built at Newport Pagnell under licence. Variants included a convertible (123 built) and a small number of shooting-brake estate conversions by coachbuilder Harold Radford.

James Bond

A DB5 prototype (chassis DP/216/1), fitted with gadgets by special-effects supervisor John Stears, starred in Goldfinger — modifications included an ejector seat, revolving number plates, machine guns, an oil-slick sprayer and a passenger-side radar screen. The car became so iconic that Aston Martin returned it to production in spirit several times: a DB5 featured in GoldenEye (1995), Skyfall (2012) and No Time to Die (2021). In 2018 Aston Martin announced 25 "Goldfinger Continuation" cars built to the film specification (with replica gadgets) at £2.75 million each. The original film car DP/216/1 was stolen in 1997 and remains the subject of investigation; the second film car was sold at auction for $4.6 million in 2010.
