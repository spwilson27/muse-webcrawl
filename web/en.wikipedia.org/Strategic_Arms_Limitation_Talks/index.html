Strategic Arms Limitation Talks

The Strategic Arms Limitation Talks (SALT) were two rounds of bilateral conferences and corresponding international treaties involving the United States and the Soviet Union. The Cold War superpowers dealt with arms control for nuclear weapons, primarily the limitation of strategic ballistic missile launchers. The negotiations, held in Helsinki, Vienna, and Geneva, produced the SALT I agreements (1972) and the SALT II treaty (1979).

SALT I

SALT I, signed in Moscow on 26 May 1972 by Richard Nixon and Leonid Brezhnev, comprised the Anti-Ballistic Missile (ABM) Treaty, limiting each side to two ABM deployment areas (later one), and an Interim Agreement freezing the numbers of intercontinental and submarine-launched ballistic missile launchers at existing levels.

SALT II

SALT II, signed in Vienna on 18 June 1979 by Jimmy Carter and Brezhnev, set equal aggregate limits on strategic nuclear delivery vehicles and MIRVed missiles. Following the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan, the treaty was never ratified by the U.S. Senate, though both sides largely observed its terms until superseded by the START negotiations of the 1980s.

Legacy

SALT established verification by "national technical means" (satellites) and a standing consultative commission, laying groundwork for later arms control. See also Cold War, START I, and ABM Treaty.
