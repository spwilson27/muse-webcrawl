PurposeMeasure blood sugar in diabetes care
MethodsFingerstick meter; continuous glucose monitor (CGM)

Blood glucose monitoring is the ongoing measurement of glucose levels in the blood, performed primarily by people with diabetes mellitus to manage insulin dosing, diet, and activity, and to avoid both hyperglycemia and dangerous hypoglycemia.

Fingerstick testing

The traditional method uses a lancet to obtain a drop of capillary blood, applied to a disposable test strip read by a glucose meter. The strip contains enzymes such as glucose oxidase or glucose dehydrogenase that react with glucose to produce an electrical signal proportional to concentration. Meters are required to meet accuracy standards such as ISO 15197, generally within ±15% of laboratory reference values.

Continuous glucose monitoring

Continuous glucose monitors (CGMs) use a small sensor inserted under the skin to measure glucose in interstitial fluid every few minutes, transmitting readings to a receiver or phone. They provide trend arrows, alarms for low glucose, and metrics such as "time in range." Widely adopted systems include the Dexcom and FreeStyle Libre families; CGM data increasingly drive automated insulin pump delivery ("closed loop") systems.
