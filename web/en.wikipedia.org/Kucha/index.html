Kucha (also Kuqa) is a city and former Buddhist kingdom on the northern edge of the Tarim Basin in present-day Xinjiang, China. Situated on the northern branch of the Silk Road, it was an important centre of Buddhism, music, and trade for over a thousand years.

History

Kucha was an Indo-European-speaking kingdom whose language, Kuchean (Tocharian B), is attested in manuscripts from the first millennium CE. The kingdom was a major centre of Hinayana Buddhism; the monk Kumārajīva (344–413), born in Kucha, became one of the most influential translators of Buddhist texts into Chinese. The Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang visited in the 7th century and described its monasteries and stupas.

Culture

Kuchean music and dance were celebrated at the Tang dynasty court, and the nearby Kizil Caves preserve some of the earliest Buddhist cave painting in China. The ruins of the ancient city of Subash lie nearby.
