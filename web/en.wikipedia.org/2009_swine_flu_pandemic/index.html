The 2009 swine flu pandemic was an influenza pandemic that lasted from early 2009 to late 2010. Caused by a novel strain of H1N1 influenza virus with genes derived from human, avian and swine influenza viruses, it was first detected in North America in March–April 2009. The World Health Organization declared it a pandemic on 11 June 2009 — the first influenza pandemic of the 21st century.

Spread and impact

The virus spread to more than 200 countries. Unlike typical seasonal influenza, the new strain disproportionately infected children and younger adults, while many people over 60 had some pre-existing immunity from exposure to earlier H1N1 strains. The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention estimated that between 43 million and 89 million cases occurred in the United States in the first year, and a later modelling study estimated 151,700 to 575,400 respiratory and cardiovascular deaths globally in the first year of circulation — considerably fewer, relative to cases, than the 1918 pandemic virus, to which it was genetically related.

Response

Governments closed schools in some regions, stockpiled antivirals such as oseltamivir, and mounted mass vaccination campaigns once a matched vaccine became available in autumn 2009. The WHO declared the pandemic over on 10 August 2010; the A(H1N1)pdm09 virus continues to circulate as a seasonal influenza strain.
