Muscat

Muscat (Arabic: مسقط, Masqaṭ) is the capital and most populous city of Oman. It is also the seat of government and the country's largest city, with a metropolitan population of well over one million. The city lies on the Gulf of Oman along the northern coast, with the rocky Al Hajar Mountains forming a backdrop to the west and south.

Muscat has been an important trading port since ancient times, mentioned by Ptolemy in the 2nd century AD. It was captured by the Portuguese in 1507, who held it until 1650, when Omani forces under the Ya'rubid dynasty expelled them. Under Sultan Qaboos bin Said, who reigned from 1970 to 2020, the city expanded dramatically from the old walled town and its twin harbour town of Muttrah into a modern, low-rise metropolis.

Landmarks

Notable sites include the Al Jalali and Al Mirani forts guarding the old harbour, the Sultan Qaboos Grand Mosque in Bawshar, the Royal Opera House Muscat, and the Muttrah Corniche and souq. The Al Alam Palace, the ceremonial palace of the Sultan, stands in Old Muscat between the two forts.
