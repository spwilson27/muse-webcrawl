Cubic equation

In algebra, a cubic equation is an equation of the form ax3 + bx2 + cx + d = 0, where a ≠ 0. All cubic equations with real coefficients have at least one real root, and three roots in total when counted with multiplicity over the complex numbers, in accordance with the fundamental theorem of algebra.

History

Cubic equations were known to the ancient Babylonians, Greeks, Chinese, Indians, and Egyptians. The general algebraic solution was found in the 16th century in Italy: Scipione del Ferro discovered a method for one class of cubics, Niccolò Tartaglia independently found the general method, and Gerolamo Cardano published the solution in his Ars Magna (1545). Cardano's student Lodovico Ferrari extended the work to the quartic equation. The priority dispute between Tartaglia and Cardano became famous in the history of mathematics.

Cardano's formula

Any cubic can be reduced to a depressed cubic t3 + pt + q = 0 by a linear substitution. Cardano's formula then gives a root in terms of cube roots of expressions involving p and q. In the case of three distinct real roots (the casus irreducibilis), the formula necessarily passes through complex numbers, which motivated the early development of complex arithmetic by Rafael Bombelli.

Discriminant

The discriminant Δ = 18abcd − 4b3d + b2c2 − 4ac3 − 27a2d2 determines the nature of the roots: Δ > 0 gives three distinct real roots, Δ < 0 gives one real and two non-real complex roots, and Δ = 0 indicates a multiple root.
