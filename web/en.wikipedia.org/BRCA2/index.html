BRCA2

BRCA2 (breast cancer type 2 susceptibility protein) is a human protein encoded by the BRCA2 gene on chromosome 13. Like BRCA1, it is a tumour suppressor involved in the repair of DNA double-strand breaks by homologous recombination.

Function

BRCA2's best-understood job is to load the recombinase RAD51 onto single-stranded DNA at sites of damage, a step essential for accurate repair using the sister chromatid as a template. The large BRCA2 protein contains repeated BRC motifs that bind RAD51, and a DNA-binding domain. Individuals born with two defective copies of BRCA2 develop Fanconi anaemia (complementation group D1), a disorder of bone-marrow failure and cancer predisposition.

Cancer risk

A single inherited pathogenic variant in BRCA2, identified by linkage studies and cloned in 1995, substantially raises the risk of breast cancer (including in men), ovarian cancer, prostate cancer and pancreatic cancer. Carriers may be offered intensified screening, risk-reducing surgery and, if cancer develops, treatments exploiting the repair defect such as PARP inhibitors.
