Electronic oscillator

An electronic oscillator is an electronic circuit that produces a periodic, oscillating electronic signal, often a sine wave or a square wave. Oscillators convert direct current (DC) from a power supply to an alternating current (AC) signal.

Principle of operation

Oscillators use positive feedback: a portion of the output signal is returned to the input in phase, sustaining oscillation at a frequency determined by the circuit's components, a condition described by the Barkhausen stability criterion.

Harmonic oscillators

Linear, or harmonic, oscillators use a resonant circuit — LC or RC networks such as the Colpitts, Hartley, and Wien bridge designs — to produce a sinusoidal output. Crystal oscillators use a piezoelectric quartz resonator for exceptional frequency stability and provide clock signals for computers and radios.

Relaxation oscillators

Nonlinear oscillators, including multivibrators and voltage-controlled oscillators, repeatedly charge and discharge an energy-storage element, producing square, sawtooth, or triangular waveforms used in timers and function generators.

Applications

Oscillators generate carrier waves in transmitters, local oscillator signals in receivers, clocks in digital systems, and tones in musical instruments.
