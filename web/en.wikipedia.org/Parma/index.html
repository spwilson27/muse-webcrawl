Parma is a city in the northern Italian region of Emilia-Romagna, famous for its architecture, music, art, prosciutto (ham) and cheese. It is the home of the University of Parma, one of the oldest universities in the world, founded in the 11th century. The city is divided in two by the Parma stream, a tributary of the Po River.

Parma was already a built-up area in the Bronze Age. It became a Roman colony in 183 BC and lay on the Via Aemilia. In the Middle Ages it passed through Lombard, Frankish and papal hands, and from 1545 to 1860 it was the capital of the Duchy of Parma, ruled successively by the Farnese, the Bourbons and, briefly, Marie Louise of Austria, Napoleon's second wife.

Culture

Parma is celebrated in music as the birthplace of conductor Arturo Toscanini and the city of composer Giuseppe Verdi, born in the nearby village of Le Roncole. The Teatro Regio is one of Italy's most renowned opera houses. The painter Parmigianino and the printmaker Giambattista Bodoni also worked in the city.

Main sights

Parma Cathedral, with its frescoed dome by Correggio, and the octagonal Baptistery in pink Verona marble are masterpieces of Romanesque and early Gothic art. Other landmarks include the Palazzo della Pilotta, the Farnese Theatre (1618), and the church of San Giovanni Evangelista.

Cuisine

The city gives its name to Parmigiano-Reggiano cheese and Parma ham (Prosciutto di Parma), both protected designation of origin products. Local dishes include tortelli d'erbetta, anolini in broth and tagliatelle with porcini mushrooms.

Sport

Parma Calcio 1913 is the city's football club, which won several European trophies in the 1990s, including two UEFA Cups. The city also has notable rugby, volleyball and baseball teams.
