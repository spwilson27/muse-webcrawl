Purdue University

Established: 1869
Type: Public land-grant research university
Location: West Lafayette, Indiana, United States
Nickname: Boilermakers
Motto: "Education, Research, Service"

Purdue University is a public land-grant research university in West Lafayette, Indiana, United States, and the flagship campus of the Purdue University system. It was founded in 1869 after businessman John Purdue donated land and money to establish a college of science, technology, and agriculture under the Morrill Land-Grant Acts; classes began in 1874.

Academics

Purdue enrols more than 50,000 students and is organised into colleges including Engineering, Agriculture, Science, the Krannert School of Management, Liberal Arts, and Health and Human Sciences, as well as the Purdue Polytechnic Institute. The university is especially noted for engineering and agriculture and operates one of the largest aviation programmes in the United States. Its professional flight programme has led to the nickname "Cradle of Astronauts": alumni include Neil Armstrong, the first person to walk on the Moon, and Eugene Cernan, the last.

Campus and student life

The main campus covers roughly 2,600 acres along the Wabash River. Landmarks include the Engineering Fountain, the Bell Tower, and Ross–Ade Stadium. Purdue's athletic teams, the Boilermakers, compete in the Big Ten Conference; the marching band's "World's Largest Drum" is a well-known tradition. The unofficial nickname "Boilermaker" dates to 1890s newspaper references to the burly railway-shop workers on the football team.

Research

Purdue is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities — Very high research activity" and hosts the Discovery Park research complex and the Birck Nanotechnology Center. Notable innovations associated with the university include work in supersonic flight, food science, and the development of Ross–Ade's stadium namesake David E. Ross's engineering enterprises.
