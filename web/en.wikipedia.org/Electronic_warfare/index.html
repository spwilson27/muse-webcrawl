Electronic warfare

Electronic warfare (EW) is any action involving the use of the electromagnetic spectrum or of directed energy to control the spectrum, attack an enemy, or impede enemy assaults. The purpose of electronic warfare is to deny the opponent the advantage of — and ensure friendly unimpeded access to — the electromagnetic spectrum. EW can be applied from air, sea, land, and space by crewed and uncrewed systems, and can target communication, radar, or other military and civilian assets.

Subdivisions

Electronic warfare is commonly divided into three areas. Electronic attack (EA) uses electromagnetic or directed energy to attack personnel, facilities or equipment, and includes radar jamming, communications jamming, radar decoys such as chaff, and anti-radiation missiles that home on enemy transmitters. Electronic protection (EP) comprises measures to defend friendly use of the spectrum against enemy electronic attack, such as frequency agility and emission control. Electronic warfare support (ES) involves intercepting, locating and identifying electromagnetic emissions to support decision-making, overlapping with signals intelligence.

History

Electronic warfare began with the advent of military radio and radar. In the Second World War both sides engaged in the "Battle of the Beams" over navigation aids, dropped chaff (Window) to blind radar, and jammed each other's communications and radar. Cold War developments included dedicated electronic warfare aircraft such as the EA-6B Prowler and EF-111 Raven. Modern conflicts have highlighted counter-drone jamming, GPS spoofing and jamming, and the contest for spectrum dominance in anti-access/area-denial environments.

Modern concepts

Contemporary doctrine treats the electromagnetic spectrum as a warfighting domain alongside land, sea, air, space and cyberspace. Capabilities include communications intelligence, radar warning receivers, directed-energy weapons, and electromagnetic battle management systems coordinating effects across a force.
