Singhasari was a Javanese Hindu-Buddhist kingdom located in east Java between 1222 and 1292. Founded by Ken Arok, it replaced the Kediri Kingdom as the dominant power in Java and, under its last and greatest king Kertanegara, projected power across the Indonesian archipelago before its sudden destruction — an event that immediately preceded the rise of Majapahit.

Founding

The kingdom's story, told in the semi-legendary chronicle Pararaton, begins with Ken Arok, a low-born adventurer who murdered Tunggul Ametung, the ruler of Tumapel, married his widow Ken Dedes, and in 1222 defeated the Kediri king Kertajaya at the Battle of Ganter. As Rajasa, he founded a dynasty whose descendants ruled for seventy years, plagued by a chain of vengeance killings linked to the legendary cursed kris of the smith Mpu Gandring.

Kertanegara

King Kertanegara (r. 1268–1292) was the kingdom's most ambitious ruler. A devout adherent of Tantric Shiva-Buddha syncretism, he launched the Pamalayu expedition (1275) to bring Sumatra's Melayu kingdom into his orbit, conquered Bali (1284), and sought to unite the archipelago against the expanding Mongol Yuan dynasty of China. He famously insulted envoys of Kublai Khan, returning one with his face branded.

Fall

In 1292, while Kertanegara's forces were deployed abroad, Jayakatwang, the vassal duke of Kediri, rebelled and killed the king. Kertanegara's son-in-law Raden Wijaya first allied with the arriving Mongol invasion force to destroy Jayakatwang, then turned on the Mongols and founded the Majapahit Empire in 1293.

Legacy

Singhasari's monuments include the temples of Singhasari, Jago and Kidal near Malang, and the colossal Dvarapala statues that guarded the capital. Its vision of an archipelago-wide realm directly inspired Majapahit and, later, Indonesian nationalist ideals.
