School shooting

Memorials are commonly placed outside schools after a shooting.

A school shooting is an armed attack at an educational institution, such as a primary school, secondary school, high school, or university, involving the use of a firearm. Many school shootings are classified as mass shootings. The phenomenon is most widely reported in the United States, where the availability of firearms has made it a focus of ongoing debate about gun violence, gun control, and school safety, though such incidents have occurred in many other countries.

Characteristics

Research by the United States Secret Service and the FBI has found that most school attackers are current or former students of the school, that attacks are rarely impulsive, and that attackers often communicate their intentions to others beforehand. Motives commonly include grievances, bullying, revenge, and a desire for notoriety.

Notable incidents

Widely reported incidents include the Columbine High School massacre in 1999, the Virginia Tech shooting in 2007, the Sandy Hook Elementary School shooting in 2012, the Marjory Stoneman Douglas High School shooting in 2018, and the Robb Elementary School shooting in Uvalde, Texas in 2022. Outside the United States, major incidents include the Dunblane massacre in Scotland in 1996 and the Erfurt school massacre in Germany in 2002.

Responses

Responses have included changes to gun laws (such as the United Kingdom's ban on handguns after Dunblane), security measures including lockdown drills and armed personnel, threat-assessment programmes, and student activism for gun reform, notably the March for Our Lives movement following the Parkland shooting.
