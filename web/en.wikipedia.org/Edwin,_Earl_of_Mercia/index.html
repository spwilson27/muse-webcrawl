Edwin, Earl of Mercia

Edwin (Old English: Ēadwine; died 1071) was the elder son of Ælfgar, Earl of Mercia, and brother of Morcar, Earl of Northumbria. He became Earl of Mercia in about 1058 on his father's death, ruling the great midland earldom in the last years of Anglo-Saxon England.

The Norman Conquest

In 1066 Edwin and Morcar fought the Norwegian invasion of Harald Hardrada at the Battle of Fulford near York, where they were defeated, escaping before Harold Godwinson's victory at Stamford Bridge. They did not reach Hastings in time to fight the Normans. After William the Conqueror's victory, the brothers initially submitted, attending William's court, but their position steadily deteriorated as Norman castles and confiscations encroached on their lands.

Rebellion and death

In 1068 Edwin and Morcar fled the court and raised rebellion in the north, the first of several revolts; they submitted again and were pardoned. In 1071, after further grievances, Edwin attempted to flee to Scotland but was betrayed by his own retainers and killed. Morcar joined the resistance at Ely under Hereward the Wake, was captured, and spent the rest of his life in confinement. With Edwin's death the old earldom of Mercia effectively ended.

Edwin figures in the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle and in the work of the chronicler Orderic Vitalis, who portrays him as a handsome and popular lord whose destruction exemplified the dispossession of the English aristocracy after the Conquest.
