The Twelve Days of Christmas (song)

The Twelve Days of Christmas

Type: English Christmas carol

First published: c. 1780

Melody standardised: Frederic Austin, 1909

Form: Cumulative song

"The Twelve Days of Christmas" is an English Christmas carol in the form of a cumulative song, first published around 1780, possibly of French origin. It enumerates a series of increasingly numerous gifts given by the singer's "true love" on each of the twelve days of Christmas, from a partridge in a pear tree to twelve drummers drumming.

Lyrics and structure

Each verse adds a new gift and repeats all the previous ones: two turtle doves, three French hens, four calling birds, five gold rings, six geese a-laying, seven swans a-swimming, eight maids a-milking, nine ladies dancing, ten lords a-leaping, eleven pipers piping, and twelve drummers drumming. The familiar melody, including the distinctive treatment of the "five gold rings", was arranged by Frederic Austin in 1909.

Cultural notes

The song began as a memory-and-forfeits game. Since 1984 the American bank PNC has published a humorous "Christmas Price Index" costing the full set of gifts. Claims that the gifts encode secret catechism symbols are a modern urban legend.
