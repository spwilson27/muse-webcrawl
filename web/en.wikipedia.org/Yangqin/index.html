Yangqin

ClassificationHammered dulcimer (struck chordophone)
OriginChina (via the Persian santur)
PlayingThin bamboo hammers

The yangqin is a Chinese hammered dulcimer: a trapezoidal wooden box strung with many courses of steel and brass strings, played by striking the strings with thin bamboo hammers. Its name literally means "foreign zither", reflecting its arrival in China from Persia via the Silk Road and maritime trade.

History

Derived from the Persian santur, the yangqin reached China around the late Ming dynasty and became established in Cantonese music, Jiangnan sizhu, and other regional ensembles, as well as an accompaniment to narrative singing and opera.

Construction and technique

Modern concert yangqins have over a hundred strings arranged over multiple bridges, giving a range of more than four octaves. Players can strike, pluck, damp, and slide on the strings, and use the hammers' flexibility for rapid tremolo effects. The instrument is a standard member of the modern Chinese orchestra.
