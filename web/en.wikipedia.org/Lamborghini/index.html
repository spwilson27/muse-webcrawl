Automobili Lamborghini S.p.A. is an Italian manufacturer of luxury sports cars and SUVs based in Sant'Agata Bolognese. Founded in 1963 by tractor magnate Ferruccio Lamborghini — motivated, legend holds, by dissatisfaction with his Ferrari and a quarrel with Enzo Ferrari — the company set out to build refined grand tourers and soon eclipsed rivals in flamboyance.

History and models

The mid-engined Miura (1966) defined the modern supercar layout. The Countach (1974) became the archetypal wedge-shaped poster car, followed by the Diablo (1990), Murciélago (2001), Gallardo (2003 — long the best-seller), Aventador (2011), and Huracán (2014). After turbulent decades of changing ownership, Audi acquired Lamborghini in 1998, bringing stability and growth. The Urus SUV (2018) transformed sales volumes, and in 2023 the V12 flagship became the hybrid Revuelto, with the Huracán succeeded by the Temerario (2024).

Identity

Lamborghini cars are named mostly after Spanish fighting bulls, carry a charging bull in the badge, and are known for dramatic styling, scissor doors on V12 models, and naturally aspirated engines long retained against turbocharged rivals. The company also builds marine engines and runs the Super Trofeo one-make racing series, with its Squadra Corse division entering top-level endurance racing in 2024.
