Thutmose III (reigned 1479–1425 BC) was the sixth pharaoh of the Eighteenth Dynasty of Egypt and one of its greatest rulers. After two decades in which his stepmother and aunt Hatshepsut ruled as pharaoh, he emerged as a brilliant military commander who expanded Egypt's empire to its greatest extent — sometimes called the "Napoleon of Egypt".

Military campaigns

In his first campaign (c. 1457 BC) Thutmose defeated a Canaanite coalition led by the king of Kadesh at the Battle of Megiddo — the first battle in history recorded in reliable detail — after boldly marching his army through the narrow Aruna pass. Over some 17 campaigns he asserted Egyptian control over Syria-Palestine up to the Euphrates, and campaigned in Nubia to the south, bringing tribute and stability to the empire.

Building and administration

A prolific builder, Thutmose enlarged the temple of Amun at Karnak, adding the Festival Hall and botanical garden reliefs depicting exotic plants and animals from his campaigns, and erected obelisks including one now in Istanbul and others ("Cleopatra's Needles") in London and New York. Late in his reign, Hatshepsut's images and names were systematically defaced, though the motive — political or personal — remains debated.
