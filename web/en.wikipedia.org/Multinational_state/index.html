Multinational state

A multinational state is a sovereign state that comprises two or more nations — communities united by shared identity, culture, language or history that regard themselves as distinct peoples. It contrasts with the idealised nation state, in which state boundaries coincide with the boundaries of a single nation. Multinational states are frequently also multiethnic, though a nation is understood by its members as a political community, not merely an ethnic group.

Examples and forms

Historical examples include the Habsburg monarchy, the Ottoman Empire and the Soviet Union. Contemporary multinational states are often identified in countries such as the United Kingdom (comprising the nations of England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland), Spain (with Catalans, Basques and others), Belgium (Flemings and Walloons), Canada (including the Québécois and Indigenous nations) and India, whose constitution organises many states along linguistic lines.

Governance

Multinational states manage their diversity through arrangements such as federalism, devolution, official multilingualism, autonomy statutes and consociational power-sharing. Where such arrangements fail, multinational states may face secessionist movements, as in the breakup of Yugoslavia and Czechoslovakia.
