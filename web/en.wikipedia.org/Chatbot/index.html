Chatbot

A chatbot (originally chatterbot) is a software application or web interface designed to simulate conversation with human users, especially over the internet. Chatbots range from simple rule-based systems that respond to keywords to generative systems built on large language models that produce free-form, context-sensitive replies. They are used in customer service, information retrieval, education, entertainment and as general-purpose virtual assistants.

History

The field traces back to Alan Turing's 1950 proposal of the "imitation game" (the Turing test). Joseph Weizenbaum's ELIZA (1966) simulated a Rogerian psychotherapist using pattern matching and substitution, and Kenneth Colby's PARRY (1972) modelled paranoid behaviour. Later landmarks include ALICE, which used the AIML markup language, and SmarterChild, popular on instant-messaging platforms in the early 2000s. In 2022 the release of ChatGPT, built on a large language model, brought generative chatbots to mass audiences, followed by competing systems such as Claude, Gemini and others.

Technology

Early chatbots relied on hand-written rules, pattern matching and decision trees. Modern systems typically use machine learning — especially transformer-based language models trained on large text corpora and fine-tuned with human feedback — to generate responses, maintain context, call external tools and perform multi-step tasks. Speech-enabled chatbots add automatic speech recognition and text-to-speech, forming voice assistants such as Siri, Alexa and Google Assistant.

Applications and criticism

Chatbots handle customer enquiries, book services, tutor students and provide companionship. Criticisms include inaccuracy and fabricated information ("hallucination"), bias inherited from training data, privacy concerns, and social effects of anthropomorphic design, in which users attribute understanding or feeling to a program — an effect already observed with ELIZA.
