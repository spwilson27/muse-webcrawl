Merina people

The Merina people (also called Imerina, Antimerina or Hova) are the largest ethnic group in Madagascar, concentrated in the central highlands around Antananarivo. They speak a dialect of Malagasy with Austronesian origins.

History

Merina ancestors arrived from maritime Southeast Asia and settled the highlands. By the late 18th century Andrianampoinimerina united the Merina kingdoms; his son Radama I expanded the Kingdom of Madagascar over much of the island with British support.

Society

Traditional society was stratified into Andriana (nobles), Hova (free commoners) and Andevo (slaves), a hierarchy abolished under French colonial rule after 1896. Rice terraces, zebu cattle and the famadihana reburial ceremony remain culturally central.

Culture

Merina culture emphasizes fihavanana (solidarity), eloquent public speaking (kabary), and respect for ancestors (razana).
