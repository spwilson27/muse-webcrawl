Frederick Albert Cook (June 10, 1865 – August 5, 1940) was an American explorer, physician, and ethnographer who is infamous for his fraudulent claims of reaching the North Pole on April 21, 1908 — nearly a year before Robert Peary's disputed expedition. He also claimed to have made the first ascent of Denali (Mount McKinley) in 1906, a claim now generally discredited.

Early expeditions

Cook served as surgeon on Peary's 1891–92 Greenland expedition and on the Belgian Antarctic Expedition (1897–1899) aboard the Belgica, the first expedition ever to overwinter in Antarctica, where his resourcefulness — including measures against scurvy and the cutting of a channel to free the icebound ship — helped save the crew, among them the young Roald Amundsen, who remained a lifelong friend.

Denali and the North Pole claims

In 1906 Cook announced that he and Edward Barrill had reached the summit of Denali, the highest mountain in North America. Barrill later signed an affidavit saying they had never approached the summit, and the photograph Cook presented as the summit was shown to depict a much lower outcrop, now known as "Fake Peak".

In September 1909 Cook announced that he had reached the North Pole the previous April, travelling with two Inuit companions, Etukishook and Ahwelah. Peary claimed the Pole days later. The University of Copenhagen examined Cook's records and concluded they did not prove he had reached the Pole. Modern analyses of his accounts, including inconsistencies in his descriptions and navigation, have led most historians to regard both the polar claim and the Denali ascent as fabrications, though a small society of supporters continued to defend Cook's reputation.

Later life

Cook later entered the oil business in Texas and was convicted of mail fraud in 1923, serving prison time until paroled in 1930; he received a presidential pardon from Franklin D. Roosevelt shortly before his death in 1940.
