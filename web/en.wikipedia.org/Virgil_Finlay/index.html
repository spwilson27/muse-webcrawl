Virgil Warden Finlay (23 July 1914 – 18 January 1971) was an American pulp fantasy, science fiction and horror illustrator. One of the most celebrated artists of the pulp era, he produced more than 2,600 drawings and paintings over a 35-year career, renowned for their meticulous pen-and-ink stipple work.

Early life and pulp career

Finlay was born in Rochester, New York, and showed early talent for art and poetry. In 1935 he sent unsolicited work to Weird Tales, where editor Farnsworth Wright bought his illustrations for H. P. Lovecraft's "The Haunter of the Dark" and other stories. Finlay soon became a regular for Weird Tales, Amazing Stories, Famous Fantastic Mysteries and many other magazines, illustrating stories by Lovecraft, Robert E. Howard, Clark Ashton Smith and Ray Bradbury.

Style and technique

Finlay's hallmark was an extraordinarily detailed technique combining stippling, cross-hatching and scratchboard effects, often working from photographic reference for figures. His images — ethereal women, otherworldly landscapes, dense cosmic vistas — were among the most reproduced of the pulp period, despite the laboriousness of his method limiting his output and income.

Later years and legacy

After serving in the U.S. Army in the Pacific during the Second World War, Finlay adapted with difficulty to the post-pulp market, working in astrology magazines and book covers. He died of liver cancer in 1971, just before a planned revival of interest in his work. He was elected to the Science Fiction Hall of Fame and remains regarded as one of the great illustrators of the fantastic.
