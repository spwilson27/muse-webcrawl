The National Minimum Wage Act 1998 created a minimum wage across the United Kingdom for the first time in British history. The Act was a flagship policy of the Labour government elected in 1997 and established the Low Pay Commission to advise on the rate.

Provisions

When the minimum wage took effect on 1 April 1999, the main rate for workers aged 22 and over was £3.60 per hour, with a lower development rate of £3.00 for those aged 18 to 21. Rates are reviewed annually, generally each April, on the advice of the independent Low Pay Commission. Separate rates now exist for apprentices and younger workers, and since 2016 a higher "National Living Wage" rate has applied to older workers — set at age 21 and over from April 2024, with the adult rate at £11.44 per hour that year.

Enforcement and impact

HM Revenue and Customs enforces the Act; employers who underpay must repay arrears and may face penalties and public naming. Economic studies have generally found that the minimum wage raised pay for the lowest-paid without the large job losses predicted by some opponents, though it prompted growth in research on wage compression and productivity responses.
