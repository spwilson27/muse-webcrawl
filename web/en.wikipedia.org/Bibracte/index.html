Bibracte

Bibracte
Type: Oppidum
Location: Mont Beuvray, Burgundy, France
Culture: Aedui (Gaulish)

Bibracte, a Gaulish oppidum (fortified settlement), was the capital of the Aedui and one of the most important hillforts in Gaul. It was situated near modern Autun in Burgundy, France. Bibracte is traditionally identified with the site on Mont Beuvray, on the borders of the modern departments of Nièvre and Saône-et-Loire.

History

The fortified settlement on Mont Beuvray covered some 135 hectares and was protected by massive ramparts of the murus gallicus type. In 58 BC, near Bibracte, Julius Caesar defeated the migrating Helvetii in the opening campaign of the Gallic Wars. It was at Bibracte in 52 BC that Vercingetorix was proclaimed leader of the Gaulish coalition against Rome. After the Roman conquest, the population was gradually moved to the new foundation of Augustodunum (Autun), and Bibracte declined.

Archaeology

Excavations begun in the nineteenth century by Jacques-Gabriel Bulliot were continued from 1984 by an international programme based at the European Archaeological Centre at Glux-en-Glenne. Excavations have revealed workshops, public buildings, and a large Roman-style house, showing the blend of Gaulish and Roman culture in the years around the conquest. The Museum of Celtic Civilisation at the site opened in 1996.
