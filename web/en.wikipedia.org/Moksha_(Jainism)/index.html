Moksha (Jainism)

The liberated soul rises to the siddhashila at the top of the Jain universe

Moksha (Sanskrit: मोक्ष), also called mukti, is the ultimate goal of Jainism: the liberation of the soul (jiva) from the cycle of birth, death, and rebirth (saṃsāra) and from all karmic matter. A liberated soul, or siddha, has shed every particle of karma, regained its innate qualities of infinite knowledge, perception, bliss, and energy, and risen to the very top of the universe, the siddhashila, where it remains in eternal, undisturbed omniscience, never to be reborn.

Karma and bondage

Uniquely among Indian religions, Jainism treats karma as a physical substance — fine matter that flows into the soul (āsrava) when it acts with passion, and sticks to it, obscuring its natural purity. Bondage (bandha) is ended by stopping the influx (saṃvara) through the five great vows — non-violence (ahimsa), truth, non-stealing, celibacy, and non-possession — and by shedding accumulated karma (nirjarā) through austerity and equanimity.

The path to liberation

The Jain path is summarised as the three jewels (ratnatraya): right faith, right knowledge, and right conduct. Progress is mapped through fourteen stages of spiritual development (gunasthana). One who destroys the four "destructive" karmas attains omniscience (kevala jnana) while still embodied, becoming an arhat; at death the remaining karmas fall away and the soul attains moksha. The twenty-four tirthankaras, most recently Mahavira (6th century BCE), are revered as teachers who crossed over and showed the way. The final shedding may be hastened by sallekhana, the religious fast unto death undertaken dispassionately.
