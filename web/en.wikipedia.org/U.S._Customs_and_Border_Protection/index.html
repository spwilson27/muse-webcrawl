U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) is the largest federal law enforcement agency of the United States Department of Homeland Security. It is responsible for regulating and facilitating international trade, collecting import duties, and enforcing U.S. regulations on trade, customs and immigration.

Organisation

CBP's components include the Office of Field Operations, which staffs ports of entry; the United States Border Patrol, which guards the borders between ports of entry; and Air and Marine Operations. It was formed in 2003 by merging the U.S. Customs Service with parts of the Immigration and Naturalization Service and other agencies.

Role

CBP officers inspect travellers and cargo at more than 300 ports of entry, work to intercept narcotics, counterfeit goods and agricultural pests, and enforce hundreds of laws for dozens of other federal agencies.
