Wakatsuki Reijirō

Baron Wakatsuki Reijirō (若槻 禮次郎, 21 March 1866 – 20 November 1949) was a Japanese politician who served twice as Prime Minister of Japan, from 1926 to 1927 and again in 1931. A former finance ministry official and finance minister, he led the Kenseikai and later the Rikken Minseitō party during the era of Taishō democracy.

Career

Born in Matsue in the former Izumo Province, Wakatsuki graduated in law from Tokyo Imperial University and rose through the Ministry of Finance, serving as vice minister and as finance minister in the third Katsura and second Ōkuma cabinets. He was a Japanese delegate at the Paris Peace Conference and a supporter of party government and fiscal retrenchment.

Premierships

Wakatsuki first became prime minister in January 1926 on the death of Katō Takaaki. His first cabinet fell in April 1927 amid the Shōwa financial crisis, after the Privy Council rejected an emergency decree to rescue a failing bank. Returning to office in April 1931, he led Japan's delegation policy of naval limitation ratified at the London Naval Conference, and his cabinet struggled with the Great Depression and the Manchurian Incident of September 1931, which his government failed to control; he resigned in December 1931. After the Second World War he testified at the Tokyo Trials and died in 1949.
