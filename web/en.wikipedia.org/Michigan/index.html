Michigan

Michigan is a state in the Great Lakes region of the Upper Midwestern United States. It is the only state formed from two peninsulas — the Lower Peninsula, shaped like a mitten, and the more sparsely populated Upper Peninsula — separated by the Straits of Mackinac and connected since 1957 by the Mackinac Bridge. Michigan borders four of the five Great Lakes plus Lake St. Clair and has the longest freshwater coastline of any U.S. state. Its capital is Lansing, and its largest city is Detroit.

History

The region was inhabited by Native American peoples including the Ojibwe, Odawa and Potawatomi — the Council of Three Fires — before French explorers and missionaries arrived in the seventeenth century; Detroit was founded by Antoine de la Mothe Cadillac in 1701. Control passed to Britain after the French and Indian War and to the United States after the American Revolution, though Michigan was contested again in the War of 1812. Michigan became a territory in 1805 and a state in 1837, after the nearly bloodless "Toledo War" boundary dispute with Ohio, in which it ceded the Toledo Strip in exchange for the western Upper Peninsula.

Economy

Michigan is the historic centre of the American automobile industry: Ford, General Motors and Stellantis (formerly Chrysler) are headquartered in the Detroit metropolitan area, and the state remains a leader in automotive manufacturing and research. Other major sectors include agriculture — Michigan is a leading producer of cherries, blueberries, apples and dry beans — tourism, forestry and mining in the Upper Peninsula, and a growing technology and life-sciences sector centred on university towns such as Ann Arbor, home of the University of Michigan.

Geography and demographics

Michigan consists of about 58,000 square miles of land and some 38,000 square miles of Great Lakes waters. The Lower Peninsula is largely flat to rolling farmland and forest; the Upper Peninsula is rugged and heavily forested, containing the state's highest point, Mount Arvon. With about 10 million residents, most of them in the south-eastern Lower Peninsula, Michigan is the tenth-most-populous state. Government is led by a governor and a bicameral legislature; the state has frequently been a closely contested "swing state" in presidential elections.
