Bakken formation

The Bakken formation is a rock unit of the Late Devonian to Early Mississippian age underlying parts of Montana, North Dakota, Saskatchewan, and Manitoba in the Williston Basin. Consisting of shale, dolomite, and siltstone members, it became one of the largest oil-producing formations in North America through horizontal drilling and hydraulic fracturing.

Geology

The formation comprises an upper and lower organic-rich black shale enclosing a middle dolomitic siltstone. Named for Henry O. Bakken, a North Dakota farmer on whose land it was discovered in 1953.

Oil production

Advances in horizontal drilling in the 2000s triggered a drilling boom that transformed North Dakota into one of the top oil-producing states in the US and drove rapid growth in towns such as Williston and Watford City.
