Chandravamsha


The Chandravamsha (Lunar dynasty), one of the principal lineages of Hindu mythology.

The Chandravamsha (Sanskrit: Candravaṃśa, "Lunar dynasty") is one of the two great dynasties of heroes described in Hindu texts, alongside the Suryavamsha or Solar dynasty. Its legendary progenitor is Chandra, the moon god. Many royal families of India, including several Kshatriya clans, traditionally traced their descent from this line.

Origins

According to the Puranas, the dynasty begins with Chandra's son Budha (the planet Mercury), who married Ila, the daughter of Manu. Their son was Pururava, the first king of the Lunar line, celebrated in the story of his love for the apsara Urvashi. Pururava's descendants included Ayus, Nahusha and Yayati.

Yayati and his sons

King Yayati divided his kingdom among his five sons — Yadu, Turvasu, Druhyu, Anu and Puru — from whom the principal branches of the dynasty descend. The Yadava line of Yadu produced Krishna, while the Paurava line of Puru, through Bharata, Dushyanta and the Kuru kings, produced the heroes of the Mahabharata.

In the epics

The protagonists of the Mahabharata — the Pandavas and the Kauravas — belong to the lunar dynasty's Kuru branch. Its genealogies, preserved in texts such as the Harivamsha and the Bhagavata Purana, link mythic kings to historical dynasties and were used for centuries by royal houses to legitimate their rule.
