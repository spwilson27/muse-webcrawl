James Edward Oglethorpe (22 December 1696 – 30 June 1785) was a British soldier, Member of Parliament and philanthropist who founded the Province of Georgia in British North America. As a social reformer he championed causes including prison reform and, through the Georgia colony, an experiment in settlement by the deserving poor under rules that initially prohibited slavery and large landholdings.

Early life and Parliament

Oglethorpe was born in London, the youngest son of Sir Theophilus Oglethorpe of Westbrook, Surrey. Educated at Eton and Oxford, he served as a young officer in the imperial army of Prince Eugene of Savoy against the Turks before entering Parliament in 1722 as MP for Haslemere, a seat he held for over thirty years. After his friend Robert Castell died in a debtors' prison, Oglethorpe chaired parliamentary committees investigating prison conditions, becoming a leading voice for penal reform.

Founding Georgia

In 1732 Oglethorpe and fellow trustees obtained a royal charter for a new colony between Spanish Florida and South Carolina, conceived both as a buffer against Spain and as a refuge where Britain's poor could start anew. Sailing with the first 114 settlers aboard the Anne, he founded Savannah in February 1733 on land made available through his alliance with the Yamacraw chief Tomochichi. Oglethorpe laid out Savannah's celebrated plan of wards and squares and governed the colony in person for most of its first decade. The trustees' rules limiting land ownership and banning slavery and rum — the "Georgia experiment" — gradually collapsed under settler pressure after his departure.

War and later life

During the War of Jenkins' Ear, Oglethorpe commanded British forces in the south, repelling a Spanish invasion at the Battle of Bloody Marsh on St. Simons Island in 1742. Returning to England, he served against the Jacobite rising of 1745 but was court-martialled (and acquitted) over the pursuit of the retreating Jacobite army. He lived quietly in later years, remained interested in reform — meeting figures such as Samuel Johnson and John Howard — and died at Cranham Hall in Essex in 1785, the last surviving Georgia trustee. Oglethorpe University in Atlanta and numerous places in Georgia bear his name.
