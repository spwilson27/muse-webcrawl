Nucleosome

A nucleosome is the basic structural unit of DNA packaging in eukaryotes. It consists of a segment of DNA wound around eight histone proteins — two copies each of H2A, H2B, H3, and H4 — forming a particle about 11 nanometres in diameter. This structure condenses the roughly two metres of DNA in a human cell so it can fit inside the nucleus.

Structure

About 147 base pairs of DNA wrap around the histone octamer in approximately 1.65 left-handed turns. Adjacent nucleosomes are connected by stretches of "linker DNA", typically 20–60 base pairs long, which may bind the linker histone H1 and promote further compaction into the 30-nanometre chromatin fibre. The atomic structure of the nucleosome core particle was resolved by X-ray crystallography by Karolin Luger and colleagues in 1997.

Function

Nucleosomes package DNA but also regulate access to it: genes wrapped tightly in nucleosomes are generally inaccessible to the transcription machinery. Cells remodel nucleosomes using ATP-dependent complexes and modify histones chemically — acetylation, methylation, phosphorylation, and ubiquitination — creating a "histone code" that influences gene expression. These modifications are a major topic in epigenetics.

Discovery

The repeating "beads on a string" structure was observed by electron microscopy in 1974 by Ada and Donald Olins and by Pierre Oudet, and Roger Kornberg proposed the octamer model the same year.
