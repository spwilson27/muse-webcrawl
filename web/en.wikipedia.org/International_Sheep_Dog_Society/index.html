The International Sheep Dog Society (ISDS) was formed in 1906 with the intention of stimulating interest in the Border Collie and the shepherd and his dog, and in securing the better management of stock by improving the shepherd's dog. The Society keeps the principal stud book for working Border Collies in the United Kingdom and Ireland and supports sheepdog trials at local, national and international level.

Stud book

The ISDS registers Border Collie puppies from registered parents and records dogs "registered on merit" that have proven their working ability. Its stud book, published annually since the early years of the Society, is a key record of working sheepdog bloodlines; famous foundation sires such as Old Hemp appear in the pedigrees of most modern Border Collies. Registration with the ISDS is based on working heritage rather than conformation showing, distinguishing it from kennel club registration.

Trials

The Society organises national trials for England, Scotland, Wales and Ireland, the winners of which qualify for the International Supreme Championship, one of the most prestigious events in the sheepdog world. Handlers and dogs complete a course involving an outrun, lift, fetch, drive and penning of sheep under time limits and judges' scoring. The BBC television programme One Man and His Dog popularised these trials with a wide audience.
