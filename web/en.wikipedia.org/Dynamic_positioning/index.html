Dynamic positioning

Dynamic positioning (DP) is a computer-controlled system that automatically maintains a vessel's position and heading using only its own propellers and thrusters, without anchors or mooring lines.

How it works

The system combines position measurements from satellite navigation (DGPS), wind sensors, gyrocompasses and motion reference units. A control computer models the forces acting on the vessel and commands the thrusters to counteract them, holding station within a few metres even in deep water.

Applications

Dynamic positioning is essential for offshore drilling vessels, pipe-laying ships, cable layers, shuttle tankers and cruise ships docking in sensitive areas. Redundancy classes (DP1, DP2, DP3) specify tolerance to equipment failures, with DP2 and DP3 required for critical operations such as drilling.

The first DP vessel, the drill ship Eureka, entered service in 1961; modern systems were developed largely for the offshore oil and gas industry.
