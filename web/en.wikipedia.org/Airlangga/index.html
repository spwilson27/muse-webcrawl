Airlangga (c. 1002 – 1052), regnal name Rakai Halu Sri Lokeswara Dharmawangsa Airlangga Anantawikramottunggadewa, was the only king of the Kahuripan kingdom in Java, which he founded after the collapse of the Mataram Kingdom. He is remembered as one of the great restorers of Javanese civilisation, rebuilding a unified kingdom in East Java after a catastrophic invasion.

Early life and restoration

Airlangga was the son of King Udayana of Bali and Queen Mahendradatta of Java, and through his mother a nephew of King Dharmawangsa of Mataram. In 1016, during his wedding celebrations in the Mataram capital, the city was attacked and destroyed — an event known as the pralaya — and the young prince escaped into the jungle, living for years as an ascetic. From about 1019 he gradually rebuilt a realm in East Java, winning over local rulers, defeating rival claimants, and by the 1030s re-establishing Javanese authority from his capital at Kahuripan.

Reign and legacy

Airlangga's reign is noted for religious tolerance among Hinduism and Buddhism, irrigation works, the restoration of ports and trade, and the flourishing of Old Javanese literature: the Arjunawiwaha, an early masterpiece of kakawin poetry by Mpu Kanwa, was composed in his honour. Late in life, around 1052, he divided his kingdom between two sons, creating the kingdoms of Janggala and Kediri — a partition whose boundary later tradition associates with the sage Mpu Bharada. After his death he was deified as Vishnu; a celebrated statue shows him mounted on Garuda. Airlangga University in Surabaya, one of Indonesia's leading universities, is named after him.
