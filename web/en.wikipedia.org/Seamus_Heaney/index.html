Seamus Heaney

Seamus Justin Heaney (13 April 1939 – 30 August 2013) was an Irish poet, playwright and translator, widely regarded as one of the greatest poets of the twentieth century. Born into a farming family in County Londonderry, Northern Ireland, he drew on the rural world of his childhood and the landscapes and history of Ireland throughout his work. He received the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1995, "for works of lyrical beauty and ethical depth, which exalt everyday miracles and the living past".

Work

His first major collection, Death of a Naturalist (1966), includes the celebrated poem "Digging". Later collections include North (1975), which addressed the Troubles through images of bog bodies, Station Island (1984), The Haw Lantern (1987) and District and Circle (2006). His translation of the Old English epic Beowulf (1999) was a bestseller and won the Whitbread Book of the Year Award. He taught at Harvard University and was Professor of Poetry at Oxford.
