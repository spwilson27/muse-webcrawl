Altneuland

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Altneuland (German for "Old New Land") is a utopian novel by Theodor Herzl, the founder of modern political Zionism, published in 1902. It imagines a Jewish society established in Palestine twenty years in the future, prosperous, technologically advanced, and tolerant, in which Jewish immigrants and the existing Arab population share in a cooperative commonwealth.

Plot and themes

The story follows two visitors who return to Palestine after a long absence on a remote island and find the once-backward region transformed. The novel develops Herzl's programme from his earlier pamphlet Der Judenstaat (1896) into narrative form, stressing modern technology, cooperative economics, and equal rights for all inhabitants regardless of religion.

Influence

The book's Hebrew title, Tel Aviv — chosen by its translator Nahum Sokolow from the Book of Ezekiel — was later given to the city founded near Jaffa in 1909. Herzl's motto from the novel, "If you will it, it is no dream", became a slogan of the Zionist movement.
