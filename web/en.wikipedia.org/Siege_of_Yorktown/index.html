Siege of Yorktown

The siege of Yorktown, also known as the Battle of Yorktown and the surrender at Yorktown, was the decisive engagement of the American Revolutionary War. From September 28 to October 19, 1781, Franco-American forces besieged the British army of Lieutenant General Lord Cornwallis at Yorktown, Virginia.

Background

Cornwallis had fortified Yorktown on the York River expecting support from the Royal Navy. The French fleet under Admiral de Grasse defeated the British at the Battle of the Chesapeake in September 1781, sealing the peninsula, while Washington and Rochambeau marched their armies south from New York.

The siege

Allied engineers opened formal siege parallels and bombarded the British works. On 14 October, French and American assault parties stormed Redoubts 9 and 10, allowing the second parallel to be completed and the British position to become untenable.

Surrender and aftermath

Cornwallis surrendered some 8,000 troops on 19 October 1781. The defeat destroyed British political will to continue the war and led directly to the negotiations that produced the Treaty of Paris in 1783, recognising American independence.
