LocationAtlantic Ocean, about 300 nmi (560 km) west of Brest, France
ResultBritish victory; Bismarck sunk

The last battle of the German battleship Bismarck took place in the Atlantic Ocean on 26–27 May 1941, near the end of Operation Rheinübung, the Kriegsmarine's attempt to send the battleship and the heavy cruiser Prinz Eugen into the Atlantic to attack Allied convoys. After sinking HMS Hood at the Battle of the Denmark Strait on 24 May, Bismarck was hunted by a large Royal Navy force; crippled by an aerial torpedo that jammed her rudders, she was brought to action and sunk on the morning of 27 May.

Background

Following the Denmark Strait battle, in which Hood blew up with the loss of all but three of her crew, the Admiralty committed nearly every available warship to the pursuit. Bismarck, under Captain Ernst Lindemann and fleet commander Admiral Günther Lütjens, shook off her shadowers on the night of 24–25 May and headed for Brest in occupied France for repairs to damage sustained in the battle, including a flooded bow and leaking fuel tanks. She was relocated on 26 May by a Catalina flying boat of RAF Coastal Command.

The rudder hit

On the evening of 26 May, Fairey Swordfish torpedo bombers from the carrier HMS Ark Royal attacked in rough weather. One torpedo struck Bismarck's stern, jamming both rudders at an angle and flooding the steering machinery spaces. Repair attempts failed; the ship could only steam in circles into the wind and sea, slowly closing with the pursuing British battleships. During the night, British destroyers harassed her with torpedo attacks, exhausting her crew but scoring no further decisive hits.

The final action

At about 08:47 on 27 May, the battleships HMS Rodney and HMS King George V, with the cruisers Norfolk and Dorsetshire, opened fire. Bismarck's gunnery was progressively disabled; her main turrets were knocked out, her bridge destroyed, and fires raged amidships. By about 10:15 she was a silent wreck. The British ships, short of fuel, broke off the action; Dorsetshire fired torpedoes into the hulk, and Bismarck capsized and sank at about 10:40. Whether the sinking was finally caused by British torpedoes or by scuttling charges set by her crew has long been debated; wreck surveys suggest scuttling hastened the end. Only about 110–115 men survived from a crew of over 2,200.
