Five Families

The Five Families is the name given to the five major New York City organised crime families of the Italian-American Mafia. The term was coined after the Castellammarese War ended in 1931, when Salvatore Maranzano reorganised the city's gangs into five families, a structure formalised under the Commission established by Lucky Luciano. The five families are the Bonanno, Colombo, Gambino, Genovese and Lucchese crime families.

History

New York's Italian-American gangs fought the bloody Castellammarese War of 1930–31 between the factions of Joe Masseria and Salvatore Maranzano. After both men were murdered, Lucky Luciano replaced Maranzano's title of capo di tutti capi with a governing board, the Commission, and the five-family structure was recognised: the families that later took the names Bonanno, Colombo (originally Profaci), Gambino (originally Mangano), Genovese (originally Luciano) and Lucchese (originally Gagliano).

Decline

From the 1970s onward, federal prosecutions under the RICO Act, the use of informants such as Sammy Gravano, and the Commission Trial of 1985–86 severely weakened the families. High-profile convictions included Gambino boss John Gotti in 1992. The families remain active but are a shadow of their mid-twentieth-century power.
