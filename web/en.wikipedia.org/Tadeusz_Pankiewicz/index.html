Tadeusz Pankiewicz

Born21 November 1908, Sambor (now Ukraine)
Died5 November 1993, Kraków, Poland
NationalityPolish
OccupationPharmacist
Known forAiding Jews in the Kraków Ghetto

Tadeusz Pankiewicz (21 November 1908 – 5 November 1993) was a Polish pharmacist who ran the Under the Eagle pharmacy in the Kraków Ghetto during the German occupation of Poland in the Second World War. As the only non-Jewish resident permitted to live and work inside the ghetto, he used his pharmacy to supply medicines, food, information, and assistance to its Jewish inhabitants, and he witnessed the ghetto's liquidation in March 1943. He was recognized by Yad Vashem as Righteous Among the Nations.

The Under the Eagle pharmacy (Apteka Pod Orłem) in Kraków's Plac Bohaterów Getta

The pharmacy in the ghetto

Pankiewicz's pharmacy, founded by his father Józef, stood at Plac Zgody (now Plac Bohaterów Getta) in the Podgórze district. When the Germans established the Kraków Ghetto in March 1941, the pharmacy fell within its boundaries. Pankiewicz obtained permission to keep it open and lived on the premises with his staff, becoming a lifeline for the ghetto's residents: the pharmacy distributed free medicines, tranquilizers that helped Jews sedate children during roundups, hair dye used to disguise identities, and news from outside, and it served as a meeting place and conduit for smuggled goods and documents.

Postwar

After the war Pankiewicz published a memoir, The Cracow Ghetto Pharmacy (1947), one of the earliest testimonies about the ghetto's destruction. He continued to run the pharmacy until his retirement. In 1983 he was honored by Yad Vashem. The pharmacy building is now a museum and branch of the Historical Museum of Kraków, and Pankiewicz is buried at Rakowicki Cemetery in Kraków.
