Circumcision of Jesus

The circumcision of Jesus is an event in the life of Jesus of Nazareth, recorded in the Gospel of Luke (2:21), in which he was circumcised eight days after his birth in accordance with Jewish law, and formally given the name Jesus. In Christian tradition it is also regarded as the first time Christ's blood was shed.

Feast

The Feast of the Circumcision of Christ is celebrated on 1 January, eight days after Christmas, in many Christian churches, including the Eastern Orthodox, Roman Catholic (as the Solemnity of Mary, Mother of God, with the circumcision still commemorated), Lutheran, and Anglican traditions.

In art and relics

The circumcision was a frequent subject in medieval and Renaissance religious art. The Holy Prepuce—the foreskin said to have been preserved—was claimed by numerous churches as a relic during the Middle Ages, most famously by Charroux Abbey and the Lateran in Rome.
