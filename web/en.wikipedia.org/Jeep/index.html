Jeep

Jeep is an American automobile brand, now owned by the multinational corporation Stellantis, known for sport utility vehicles and off-road vehicles. The name derives from the World War II military vehicle officially designated the Willys MB and Ford GPW, whose versatility made it an icon of the Allied war effort.

Origins

In 1940 the US Army sought a light, four-wheel-drive reconnaissance vehicle. The American Bantam Car Company produced the first prototype, but production contracts went to Willys-Overland and Ford, whose standardised design — the Willys MB and Ford GPW — became the wartime jeep. More than 600,000 were built. The origin of the name is disputed: it may derive from "GP" (for "General Purpose") slurred in pronunciation, or from Eugene the Jeep, a character in the Popeye cartoons.

Civilian models

Willys launched the civilian CJ (Civilian Jeep) series in 1945 and registered "Jeep" as a trademark. Successive models included the Wagoneer (1963), an early luxury SUV, the Cherokee, and the Wrangler (1987), the direct heir of the original design with its removable doors and fold-down windscreen. The brand passed through Kaiser, American Motors and Chrysler before becoming part of Stellantis in 2021.

Cultural significance

General George C. Marshall called the jeep "America's greatest contribution to modern warfare". The name has passed into generic use for small off-road vehicles in many countries, and Jeep models such as the Grand Cherokee are sold worldwide.
