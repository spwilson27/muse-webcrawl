Newell Convers Wyeth (October 22, 1882 – October 19, 1945), known as N. C. Wyeth, was an American illustrator and painter, one of the foremost illustrators of the Golden Age of American illustration. A pupil of Howard Pyle, he became famous for dramatic, richly detailed illustrations for adventure classics.

Career

From Massachusetts, Wyeth joined Pyle's school in Wilmington, Delaware, in 1902 and within a year sold his first cover to the Saturday Evening Post. A 1903 trip to the American West, where he worked as a cowhand and mail rider, grounded the western subjects of his early career. His celebrated commissions included Robert Louis Stevenson's Treasure Island (1911), whose seventeen paintings of pirates and sea fights became iconic, as well as Kidnapped, The Last of the Mohicans, Robin Hood, The White Company and Robinson Crusoe. He settled at Chadds Ford, Pennsylvania, where he raised a family of artists, most famously the painter Andrew Wyeth, and also produced murals, landscapes and easel paintings — work he valued more than the illustration that made his living.

Death and legacy

Wyeth died in 1945 when his car was struck by a train at a crossing near his home, an accident that also killed his young grandson. His home and studio at Chadds Ford are preserved by the Brandywine Museum of Art, which also holds a major collection of work by three generations of the Wyeth family.
