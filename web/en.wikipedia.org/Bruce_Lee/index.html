Bruce Lee (born Lee Jun-fan; 27 November 1940 – 20 July 1973) was a Hong Kong-American martial artist, actor, and filmmaker. He is widely considered one of the most influential martial artists of all time and a pop culture icon of the 20th century, who bridged the gap between East and West. He is credited with promoting Hong Kong action cinema and helping to change the way Chinese people were presented in American films.

Early life

Lee was born in San Francisco's Chinatown while his father, Cantonese opera star Lee Hoi-chuen, was touring, and was raised in Hong Kong. A child actor in Hong Kong films, he studied Wing Chun kung fu under Ip Man as a teenager. In 1959 he moved to the United States, studying philosophy at the University of Washington and teaching martial arts in Seattle and California.

Career

Lee gained American attention playing Kato in the television series The Green Hornet (1966–67). Frustrated by Hollywood's limited roles, he returned to Hong Kong, where The Big Boss (1971) and Fist of Fury (1972) made him a superstar across Asia. He wrote, directed and starred in Way of the Dragon (1972), featuring his famous Colosseum duel with Chuck Norris. His Hollywood co-production Enter the Dragon premiered weeks after his death and became a global phenomenon.

Jeet Kune Do and philosophy

Lee developed Jeet Kune Do, the "way of the intercepting fist" — a philosophy of martial arts emphasising simplicity, directness and adaptability, summed up in his maxim to "be water". His writings on martial art and life, including the posthumous Tao of Jeet Kune Do, remain influential.

Death and legacy

Lee died suddenly in Hong Kong on 20 July 1973, aged 32, of cerebral oedema; his death has been the subject of enduring speculation. He is buried in Seattle. Statues of Lee stand in Hong Kong and elsewhere, and his impact on martial arts cinema, mixed martial arts and popular culture is immeasurable.
