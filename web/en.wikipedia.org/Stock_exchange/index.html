Stock exchange

A modern securities exchange trading floor

A stock exchange, securities exchange, or bourse is an exchange where stockbrokers and traders can buy and sell securities, such as shares of stock, bonds and other financial instruments. Stock exchanges may also provide facilities for the issue and redemption of such securities and instruments and capital events including the payment of income and dividends.

History

Organized securities trading developed in the Low Countries in the 17th century; the Amsterdam Stock Exchange, founded in 1602 by the Dutch East India Company, is considered the world's first formal stock exchange. Exchanges spread to London, Paris, and New York over the following centuries, and electronic trading progressively replaced open-outcry floor trading in the late 20th century.

Functions

Exchanges provide liquidity for investors, a venue for companies to raise capital through initial public offerings, price discovery for listed securities, and regulated, transparent trading under rules enforced by the exchange and securities regulators.

Major exchanges

The largest exchanges by market capitalization include the New York Stock Exchange, Nasdaq, the Shanghai Stock Exchange, Euronext, and the Japan Exchange Group.

Listing requirements

Companies must meet minimum standards for market value, earnings, disclosure, and corporate governance to list and remain listed on an exchange.
