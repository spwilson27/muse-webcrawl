Çatalhöyük

Çatalhöyük was a very large Neolithic and Chalcolithic proto-city settlement in southern Anatolia, in present-day Turkey, which existed from approximately 7500 BC to 5700 BC. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Settlement

The site consists of two mounds on the Konya Plain. At its height the eastern mound housed perhaps 3,500–8,000 people in mudbrick houses packed together without streets: residents moved across rooftops and entered their homes by ladders through holes in the roof. The dead were buried beneath house floors, sometimes with heads removed and plastered.

Art and society

Çatalhöyük is famed for its wall paintings, reliefs, and figurines — including the "Seated Woman of Çatalhöyük" flanked by felines — and for evidence of a remarkably egalitarian society with little sign of chiefs or social hierarchy. Excavated first by James Mellaart in the 1960s, long-term excavations under Ian Hodder from 1993 reshaped understanding of Neolithic life, ritual, and symbolism.
