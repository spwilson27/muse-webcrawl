Special wards of Tokyo

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Map of the 23 special wards (coloured) within Tokyo Metropolis

The special wards of Tokyo (Japanese: 特別区, tokubetsu-ku) are the 23 municipalities that together form the core and the most populous part of Tokyo Metropolis, Japan. They cover the land area that was the City of Tokyo before it was abolished in 1943. Each ward is governed by an elected mayor and assembly, and together the 23 wards occupy roughly 619 square kilometres with a combined population of over 9.7 million people.

History

When Tokyo City was abolished in 1943, its wards came under the direct administration of the prefectural government. Under the Local Autonomy Law of 1947 the wards were reconstituted as "special wards" with considerable self-government, though some functions — such as water supply, sewerage and fire services — remain with the Tokyo Metropolitan Government. Mayors of the special wards have been directly elected since 1975.

List of wards

The 23 special wards are: Adachi, Arakawa, Bunkyō, Chiyoda, Chūō, Edogawa, Itabashi, Katsushika, Kita, Kōtō, Meguro, Minato, Nakano, Nerima, Ōta, Setagaya, Shibuya, Shinagawa, Shinjuku, Suginami, Sumida, Toshima and Taitō. Setagaya is the most populous, while Chiyoda — home to the Imperial Palace and the national government — has the smallest resident population but an enormous daytime population.

Government and services

Unlike ordinary Japanese cities, the special wards delegate certain city-level services to the metropolis: the Tokyo Fire Department, the Tokyo Metropolitan Police Department and metropolitan waterworks serve the whole special-ward area. In 2000 the wards were formally recognised as basic local public entities, strengthening their fiscal autonomy.
