The Battle of the Trebia was the first major battle of the Second Punic War, fought on 18 December 218 BC between the Carthaginian army of Hannibal and a Roman army under the consul Tiberius Sempronius Longus, near the river Trebbia in northern Italy.

Background

Hannibal had crossed the Alps into Italy in the autumn of 218 BC and won a cavalry skirmish at the Ticinus, where the other consul, Publius Cornelius Scipio, was wounded. Sempronius arrived with reinforcements and, eager for a victory before his term ended, was drawn into battle despite Scipio's advice of caution.

The battle

Hannibal provoked the Romans to cross the icy Trebia at dawn, unfed and numb with cold, onto ground he had prepared. He concealed a picked force under his brother Mago in a watercourse. After the Carthaginian cavalry and elephants pressed the Roman flanks, Mago's ambush struck the Roman rear. Most of the Roman army was trapped and destroyed, though a body of about 10,000 infantry cut its way through the Carthaginian centre to safety at Placentia.

Aftermath

The victory secured Hannibal's position in Cisalpine Gaul and won him Gallic allies and recruits. It set the pattern for his subsequent victories at Lake Trasimene and Cannae.
