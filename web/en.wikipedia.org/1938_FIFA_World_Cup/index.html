1938 FIFA World Cup

The 1938 FIFA World Cup was the third edition of the FIFA World Cup, held in France from 4 to 19 June 1938. Italy defended its title, defeating Hungary 4–2 in the final at the Stade Olympique Yves-du-Manoir in Colombes, Paris, to become the first nation to win consecutive World Cups. It was the last World Cup for twelve years, as the tournaments of 1942 and 1946 were cancelled because of the Second World War.

Background

The decision to stage a second consecutive tournament in Europe angered South American nations; Uruguay and Argentina boycotted, and Brazil was the only major South American entrant. Austria qualified but was annexed by Germany in the Anschluss before the tournament, leaving 15 teams. The Dutch East Indies (now Indonesia) became the first Asian nation to appear at a World Cup.

Tournament

The straight knockout format produced famous matches: Brazil's 6–5 extra-time win over Poland, featuring a hat-trick for Poland's Ernest Wilimowski and three goals for Brazil's Leônidas, and Brazil's semi-final loss to Italy, in which Leônidas was controversially rested. In the final, Italy led through goals from Gino Colaussi and Silvio Piola (two each) to retain the Jules Rimet Trophy under coach Vittorio Pozzo.

Legacy

Captain Giuseppe Meazza and Giovanni Ferrari joined coach Pozzo as the only men to win two World Cups in the pre-war era. Italy's players famously gave the fascist salute before matches, and legend holds that Mussolini sent a telegram reading "Win or die" before the final (likely apocryphal in its literal sense). The trophy itself was hidden in a shoebox under the bed of Italian football official Ottorino Barassi during the war.
