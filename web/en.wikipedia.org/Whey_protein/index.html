Whey protein

Whey protein is the protein fraction of whey, the liquid portion of milk that separates from the curds during cheese production. It is a mixture of globular proteins including beta-lactoglobulin, alpha-lactalbumin, serum albumin, and immunoglobulins, and is rich in essential amino acids, particularly the branched-chain amino acid leucine.

Production and types

Commercial whey protein is produced by drying and processing liquid whey from cheese making. Three main forms are sold: whey protein concentrate (typically 70–80% protein, containing some lactose and fat), whey protein isolate (90% or more protein, with most lactose and fat removed, usually by ion exchange or microfiltration), and whey protein hydrolysate, which is pre-digested by hydrolysis for faster absorption.

Uses

Whey protein is widely marketed as a dietary supplement for athletes and bodybuilders to support muscle protein synthesis and recovery, and is also used in infant formula, medical nutrition products, and as a food ingredient. Research supports its effectiveness for increasing lean body mass when combined with resistance training; it is generally regarded as safe, though it may be unsuitable for people with milk allergy, and those with lactose intolerance usually tolerate isolates better than concentrates.
