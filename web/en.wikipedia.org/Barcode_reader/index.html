Barcode reader

A barcode reader (or barcode scanner) is an optical scanner that can read printed barcodes, decode the data contained in them, and send the data to a computer. Like a flatbed scanner, it consists of a light source, a lens, and a light sensor translating optical impulses into electrical ones. Virtually all barcode readers also contain decoder circuitry to analyze the barcode's image and output the decoded characters.

Types

The earliest scanners used a light pen; later designs used moving mirrors with visible laser diodes and fixed mirrors, and modern readers use CCD and CMOS camera-based imagers that can read two-dimensional codes such as QR codes as well as stacked and linear symbologies. Handheld, fixed-mount, and smartphone-based readers are used in retail point-of-sale systems, warehousing, libraries, and healthcare.

History

Barcode reading was first commercialized in the 1970s with the adoption of the Universal Product Code (UPC) in grocery retail; the first UPC-scanned product was a pack of chewing gum in 1974.
