Plutarco Elías Calles (25 September 1877 – 19 October 1945) was a Mexican general and politician who served as President of Mexico from 1924 to 1928 and remained the dominant figure in Mexican politics — as Jefe Máximo ("Supreme Chief") of the Revolution — during the period known as the Maximato (1928–1934). He founded the National Revolutionary Party (PNR), ancestor of the Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI) which governed Mexico for most of the twentieth century.

Early life and revolution

Born in Guaymas, Sonora, of a family of mixed Mexican and Lebanese descent, Calles worked as a schoolteacher and bartender before joining the Mexican Revolution in 1913 under Venustiano Carranza. As military governor of Sonora he defeated the forces of Pancho Villa at Agua Prieta in 1915. He served as Secretary of Industry and Labour and then of the Interior under Álvaro Obregón, and was elected president in 1924 as Obregón's chosen successor.

Presidency

Calles's administration pursued economic reconstruction — founding the Bank of Mexico (1925), building roads and irrigation works, and renegotiating foreign debt — while enforcing the anticlerical articles of the 1917 Constitution. His strict enforcement, including the "Calles Law" of 1926 restricting Catholic worship and clergy, provoked the Cristero War (1926–29), a violent Catholic uprising in west-central Mexico suppressed with great bloodshed. After Obregón's assassination in 1928 by a Catholic activist, Calles announced the end of the era of caudillos and founded the PNR in 1929 to institutionalise revolutionary power.

The Maximato and exile

From 1928 to 1934 Calles ruled through three subordinate presidents — Emilio Portes Gil, Pascual Ortiz Rubio, and Abelardo Rodríguez — while retaining control of the party and army. His conflict with President Lázaro Cárdenas, who built an independent base among workers and peasants, ended with Calles's arrest and exile to the United States in 1936. He was allowed to return to Mexico in 1941 under Manuel Ávila Camacho and died in Mexico City in 1945.
