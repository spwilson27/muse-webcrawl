Barrie Kosky (born 1967 in Melbourne) is an Australian theatre and opera director. He has directed productions at opera houses and festivals around the world and served as artistic director (Intendant) and chief director of the Komische Oper Berlin from 2012 to 2022, where his flamboyant, musically eclectic programming won the company the title of Opera House of the Year.

Early life and education

Kosky was born in Melbourne to a family of Eastern European Jewish descent. He studied piano and music history at the University of Melbourne. In 1990 he founded the Gilgul Theatre, one of Australia's best-known experimental companies, and directed productions drawing on Jewish history and mythology.

Career in Australia

At 29 Kosky became the youngest artistic director of the Adelaide Festival (1996). He directed extensively for Opera Australia, Sydney Theatre Company and the Melbourne International Festival, earning a reputation for provocative, visually extravagant work.

Work in Europe

From the early 2000s Kosky worked increasingly in Europe, directing at the Vienna Schauspielhaus, the Bayerische Staatsoper, the Royal Opera House in London, and other major venues. His 2017 production of Shostakovich's The Nose and his Bayreuth Festival production of Wagner's Die Meistersinger von Nürnberg (2017) — the first at Bayreuth by a Jewish director — attracted wide attention. At the Komische Oper he staged operettas, musicals and rarely performed works alongside standard repertoire, significantly expanding the house's audience.

Style

Kosky's productions are noted for their theatricality, dark humour, grotesque imagery and mixture of high and popular culture, and for placing music-making and performers' virtuosity at the centre of the staging.
