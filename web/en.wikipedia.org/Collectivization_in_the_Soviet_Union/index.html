Collectivization in the Soviet Union

Collectivization was a policy under Joseph Stalin, pursued between 1928 and 1940, to consolidate individual landholdings and labour into collective farms (kolkhozy) and state farms (sovkhozy).

Implementation

The First Five-Year Plan set targets for collectivising agriculture. Wealthier peasants labelled kulaks were deported, imprisoned or executed in the dekulakization campaign. Peasant resistance included slaughtering livestock on a massive scale; the horse and cattle populations fell by roughly half.

Consequences

Forced grain procurements contributed to the Soviet famine of 1932–1933, including the Holodomor in Ukraine, which killed millions. By 1936 over 90% of Soviet farmland was collectivised, though private plots remained highly productive.
