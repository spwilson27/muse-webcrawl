Master of the Rolls

The Master of the Rolls, in full the Master of the Rolls and Records of the Chancery of England, is a senior judge in England and Wales and the second most senior judge after the Lord Chief Justice. The Master of the Rolls is the Head of Civil Justice and presides over the Civil Division of the Court of Appeal.

History

The office began as the clerk responsible for the "rolls" — the parchment records of the Court of Chancery — housed in the Rolls Chapel. The keeper of the rolls gradually acquired judicial functions, becoming by the 16th century a judge of the Chancery in his own right, and in the 19th century the Master of the Rolls was transferred to the new Court of Appeal.

Roles

As Head of Civil Justice, the Master of the Rolls oversees the civil justice system of England and Wales and chairs bodies concerned with civil procedure rules. The office also carries responsibilities connected with public records: the Master of the Rolls historically chaired the Advisory Council on National Records and Archives and has functions relating to The National Archives and to the admission of solicitors, whose "roll" of names the office originally kept.

Notable holders have included Lord Denning (1962–1982), Lord Bingham, Lord Dyson, and, from 2021, Sir Geoffrey Vos.
