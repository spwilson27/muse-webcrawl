Celtic languages

Celtic languages
Family: Indo-European
Branches: Goidelic, Brittonic, Continental (extinct)
Living: Irish, Welsh, Breton, Scottish Gaelic, Cornish, Manx

The Celtic languages are a branch of the Indo-European family descended from Proto-Celtic, once spoken across much of Europe and Anatolia. Today they survive mainly in Ireland, Scotland, Wales, Brittany, Cornwall and the Isle of Man.

Branches

The Goidelic branch comprises Irish, Scottish Gaelic and Manx. The Brittonic branch comprises Welsh, Breton and Cornish. Continental languages such as Gaulish, Celtiberian and Galatian are extinct and known from inscriptions.

Features and revival

Common features include verb-subject-object order in the Insular languages, initial consonant mutations, and inflected prepositions. Welsh has the largest speaker base; Cornish and Manx were revived after their last native speakers died.
