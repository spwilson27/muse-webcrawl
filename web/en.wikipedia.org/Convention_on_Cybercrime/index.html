Convention on Cybercrime

The Convention on Cybercrime, also known as the Budapest Convention, is the first international treaty seeking to address internet and computer crime by harmonising national laws, improving investigative techniques, and increasing cooperation among nations.

History

Drafted by the Council of Europe with participation from the United States, Canada, Japan, and others, the convention was opened for signature in Budapest on 23 November 2001 and entered into force on 1 July 2004. It has since been ratified by dozens of states, including many outside Europe.

Provisions

The convention requires parties to criminalise offences including illegal access, illegal interception, data and system interference, misuse of devices, computer-related forgery and fraud, child pornography, and copyright offences. It also establishes procedural powers for preserving and obtaining electronic evidence and a 24/7 network for rapid mutual assistance. A Second Additional Protocol, opened for signature in 2022, addresses enhanced cooperation and disclosure of electronic evidence.
