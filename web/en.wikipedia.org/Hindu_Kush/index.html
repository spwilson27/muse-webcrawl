Hindu Kush

Highest peakTirich Mir (7,708 m)
Lengthc. 800 km
CountriesAfghanistan, Pakistan
Parent rangeHindu Kush–Karakoram–Himalaya system

The Hindu Kush is an 800-kilometre-long mountain range in Central and South Asia, running north-east to south-west from the Pamir and Karakoram region of the Wakhan Corridor through north-eastern Afghanistan into northern Pakistan. Its highest peak is Tirich Mir (7,708 m / 25,289 ft) in the Chitral District of Pakistan. The range forms the western arm of the great mountain system sometimes grouped as the Hindu Kush–Himalaya region, a critical water source feeding the Indus, Amu Darya and Helmand river basins.

Name

The origin of the name is debated. In Persian usage, Hindu Kush has often been interpreted as "Hindu-killer", a name popularised in medieval accounts referring to the dangers of the passes — and, in some tellings, to Indian slaves who perished crossing them — though some scholars derive it simply from a term for the mountains of "Hind" (India).

Geography and history

The range is crossed by historic passes — including the Salang, Shibar and the passes of the Wakhan — that have channelled trade and invasion between Central Asia and the Indian subcontinent for millennia. Alexander the Great campaigned here, and the region later formed part of the Greco-Bactrian and Kushan worlds; the Bamiyan valley, on the range's southern flank, was a Buddhist centre on the Silk Road. The Hindu Kush marks a major watershed and an ethnic and linguistic crossroads, home to Pashtun, Tajik, Nuristani, Kalash and other peoples.

Glaciers and climate

The range holds thousands of glaciers that sustain irrigation and hydropower downstream. Like the rest of the "Third Pole", it is experiencing rapid glacier retreat under climate change, with consequences for the roughly two billion people who depend on the rivers of the greater region.
