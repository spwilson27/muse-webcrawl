Smiley

A smiley, sometimes called a smiley face, is a basic ideogram representing a smiling face. Since the 1950s it has become part of popular culture worldwide, used either as a stand-alone image or as a form of communication such as an emoticon. The smiley began as two dots and a line representing eyes and a mouth on a yellow background, and by the 1970s had been developed into a more recognizable design.

The classic smiley face: two dots and an arc on a yellow circle

History

Simple smiling-face drawings appeared in advertising and popular culture in the early twentieth century. The iconic yellow version is widely credited to American commercial artist Harvey Ross Ball, who in 1963 designed the face for the State Mutual Life Assurance Company of Worcester, Massachusetts, as part of an employee morale campaign. Ball was paid $45 for the design and never trademarked it. In the early 1970s, Philadelphia brothers Bernard and Murray Spain added the slogan "Have a happy day", and the image became a ubiquitous fad symbol of the decade.

Emoticons and emoji

In 1982, computer scientist Scott Fahlman proposed the character sequence :-) on a Carnegie Mellon bulletin board as a joke marker — the first documented emoticon. Text-based smileys spread through email and Usenet, and graphical smileys later evolved into emoji, standardized in Unicode from 2010 onward.

In popular culture

The smiley became an emblem of acid house and rave culture in the late 1980s, notably during the 1988 "Second Summer of Love" in Britain, and appeared in the work of bands such as Nirvana, whose crossed-out-eyes variant became famous. Variations of the smiley have been used in film, advertising, and fashion worldwide.
