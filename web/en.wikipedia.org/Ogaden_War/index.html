Ogaden War

The Ogaden War, also known as the Ethio-Somali War, was a conflict fought in 1977–78 between Somalia and Ethiopia over the disputed Ogaden region. Somali forces, supporting the Western Somali Liberation Front (WSLF) and later invading directly, initially overran much of the Ogaden, but a massive Soviet airlift and the deployment of Cuban troops turned the tide in Ethiopia's favour.

Background

The Ogaden, inhabited largely by ethnic Somalis, had been contested since imperial times. Somalia's government under Siad Barre pursued the goal of a "Greater Somalia" uniting Somali-inhabited territories. After the 1974 Ethiopian revolution brought the Marxist Derg to power, relations between Somalia and Ethiopia deteriorated, and the United States and Soviet Union effectively switched clients in the Horn of Africa.

Course of the war

WSLF guerrillas began operations in early 1977; the Somali National Army invaded openly in July. Somali forces captured much of the Ogaden, including the town of Jijiga, and besieged Harar and Dire Dawa. In late 1977 the Soviet Union mounted a huge airlift of arms to Ethiopia, and some 15,000–18,000 Cuban combat troops arrived. In February–March 1978 a combined Ethiopian-Cuban offensive recaptured Jijiga and drove the Somali army out of the Ogaden; Barre announced a withdrawal on 9 March 1978.

Aftermath

The defeat weakened Barre's regime and contributed to the insurgencies that eventually led to the Somali Civil War. Hundreds of thousands of refugees fled into Somalia. The war marked one of the largest Cold War proxy conflicts in Africa.
