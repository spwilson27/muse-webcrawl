The John Birch Society (JBS) is an American right-wing political advocacy group founded in 1958 by businessman Robert W. Welch Jr. It is named for John Birch, an American Baptist missionary and military intelligence officer killed by Chinese Communists in 1945, whom Welch regarded as a first casualty of the Cold War.

Positions and history

The society advocates limited government, constitutional originalism and opposition to communism and to international institutions; it campaigned for United States withdrawal from the United Nations and against the civil rights movement, water fluoridation and the Federal Reserve. In the early 1960s it grew rapidly but became controversial for conspiracy claims, including Welch's attacks on President Dwight D. Eisenhower, and mainstream conservatives distanced themselves from it.

Later years

Membership declined from the 1970s, though the society continues to publish The New American magazine and campaign on constitutional and sovereignty issues.
