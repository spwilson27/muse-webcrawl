Muck (Scottish Gaelic: Eilean nam Muc) is the smallest of the four main islands of the Small Isles, part of the Inner Hebrides of Scotland. It measures roughly 4 km (2.5 mi) east to west and is mostly low-lying and fertile, with the highest point, Beinn Airein, at 137 metres (449 ft). The island has been owned by the MacEwen family since 1896 and is run as a single farm. Most residents — a population of only a few dozen — live in the village of Port Mòr; the other settlement is the farm at Gallanach.

Geography and history

The name comes from the Gaelic muc, "pig" (or possibly "whale"). Unlike the rugged, mountainous neighbouring island of Rùm, Muck is green and pastoral; its geology is dominated by basalt lavas, and its soils are among the most fertile in the Hebrides. There is evidence of settlement since the Bronze Age and possibly earlier, and the island passed through the hands of the Lords of the Isles and various clan landlords before the MacEwens bought it in 1896. The population, once over 200, fell through evictions and emigration in the 19th century and has remained small ever since, though comparatively stable for a Hebridean island.

Life on the island

A Caledonian MacBrayne ferry links Port Mòr with Mallaig on the mainland, calling at Eigg, Rùm, and Canna. There is a small primary school, a craft shop and tearoom, and accommodation for visitors; the island is known for its seals, porpoises, and seabirds. In 2013 Muck came to national attention when the winter storms left it dependent on its own wind and solar generators; the island community operates a renewable energy scheme supplying most of its electricity.
