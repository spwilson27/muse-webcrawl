American Wire Gauge

American Wire Gauge (AWG) is a logarithmic stepped standardized wire gauge system used since 1857, predominantly in North America, for the diameters of round, solid, nonferrous, electrically conducting wire. Dimensions of the wires are given in ASTM standard B 258.

Formula

The diameter of an AWG-sized wire is a geometric progression: each step up in gauge number multiplies the diameter by a constant factor of about 0.8905, so that a wire three gauges smaller has about twice the cross-sectional area, and six gauges smaller about twice the diameter. Gauge numbers run from 36 AWG (0.005 inch) up to 0000 (4/0) AWG (0.46 inch).

Applications

AWG sizes are used throughout electrical wiring, electronics, and jewelry making. Increasing gauge numbers denote smaller diameters, a convention inherited from the number of drawing dies the wire was originally pulled through.
