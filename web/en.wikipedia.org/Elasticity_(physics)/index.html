Elasticity (physics)

In physics and materials science, elasticity is the ability of a body to resist a distorting influence and to return to its original size and shape when that influence or force is removed. Solid objects will deform when adequate loads are applied to them; if the material is elastic, the object will return to its initial shape and size after removal of the load.

A spring demonstrates elastic behaviour within its elastic limit.

Hooke's law

For small deformations, the displacement or size of the deformation is directly proportional to the deforming force or load. This principle, Hooke's law, can be expressed for a spring as F = −kx, where k is the spring constant and x the extension. For a stretched material the corresponding statement relates stress (force per unit area) to strain (relative deformation) through an elastic modulus such as Young's modulus.

Elastic moduli

Different modes of deformation are characterised by different moduli: Young's modulus for tension and compression, the shear modulus for twisting and shearing, and the bulk modulus for uniform compression. In an isotropic material these are related through Poisson's ratio.

Elastic limit and plasticity

Elastic behaviour holds only up to the elastic limit (yield point). Beyond it, deformation becomes permanent—a behaviour called plasticity—and further loading may lead to fracture. The physical basis of elasticity is the stretching of interatomic bonds: atoms are displaced slightly from equilibrium positions but return when the load is removed. In rubber-like materials, elasticity instead arises largely from entropy changes in long polymer chains.
