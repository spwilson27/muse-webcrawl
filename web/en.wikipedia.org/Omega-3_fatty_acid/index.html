Omega-3 fatty acid

Omega-3 fatty acids (ω-3 fatty acids) are polyunsaturated fatty acids characterised by a double bond three atoms away from the terminal methyl group. The three types most important in human nutrition are alpha-linolenic acid (ALA), eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA), and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA).

Sources

EPA and DHA are found mainly in oily fish such as salmon, mackerel, sardines, and anchovies, and in algae. ALA occurs in plant sources including flaxseed, chia seeds, walnuts, and canola oil. Humans convert ALA to EPA and DHA only inefficiently, so EPA and DHA are often consumed directly or as fish oil supplements.

Health effects

Omega-3s are components of cell membranes and precursors to signalling molecules called eicosanoids. Evidence supports benefits for triglyceride reduction; claims regarding cardiovascular disease, cognition, and inflammation have been extensively studied with mixed results. DHA is a major structural component of the retina and cerebral cortex.

History

Interest grew in the 1970s from studies of Greenland Inuit populations, whose fish-rich diet was associated with low rates of heart disease.
