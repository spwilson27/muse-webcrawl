Sporting Club de Bastia (SC Bastia) is a French professional football club based in Bastia on the island of Corsica. Founded in 1905 by the Swiss teacher Hans Ruesch, it is the island's most successful club and a potent symbol of Corsican identity; its supporters are known for their passionate, sometimes controversial, fervour. The club plays at the Stade Armand-Cesari in Furiani and wears blue shirts.

History

Bastia turned professional in 1965 and spent much of the following half-century in France's top division. Its golden era came under coach Jean-Antoine Redin in the 1970s: the team, built around players such as Dragan Džajić and Claude Papi, reached the final of the 1978 UEFA Cup, eliminating Sporting CP, Newcastle United, Torino and Grasshopper before losing to PSV Eindhoven (0–0 at home, 0–3 away) in a campaign still celebrated in Corsica. Bastia won the Coupe de France in 1981, defeating Saint-Étienne, and the Coupe de la Ligue in 1995 was followed by victory in the 1997 UEFA Intertoto Cup. The club's darkest day was 5 May 1992, when a temporary stand at Furiani collapsed before a Coupe de France semi-final against Marseille, killing 18 people and injuring over 2,300; 5 May is now a day on which no matches are played in France.

Recent decades

Relegated from Ligue 1 in 2005, Bastia fell as far as the third tier before back-to-back promotions returned it to Ligue 1 in 2012 under Frédéric Hantz, and it reached the 2015 Coupe de la Ligue final. Financial and administrative sanctions sent the club to the fifth division (National 3) in 2017; it then climbed back through the amateur ranks, returning to professional football in Ligue 2 for the 2021–22 season. The club's fiercest rivalries are with fellow Corsican club AC Ajaccio (the Corsican derby) and with OGC Nice.
