Triassic–Jurassic extinction event

Triassic–Jurassic extinction
Date: about 201.4 million years ago
One of the five major mass extinctions

The Triassic–Jurassic (Tr-J) extinction event, often called the end-Triassic extinction, is a mass extinction that occurred about 201.4 million years ago, marking the boundary between the Triassic and Jurassic periods. It is one of the five largest mass extinctions of the Phanerozoic eon.

Roughly half of marine genera disappeared, including most conodonts and many ammonoids, while on land many large crurotarsan archosaurs, therapsids and large amphibians vanished. The clearing of ecological niches is widely thought to have enabled dinosaurs to become the dominant land vertebrates of the Jurassic.

Causes

The leading explanation is massive volcanism from the Central Atlantic Magmatic Province, associated with the breakup of Pangaea and the opening of the Atlantic Ocean. Carbon dioxide release caused global warming and ocean acidification, while sulphur aerosols may have caused short-term cooling.
