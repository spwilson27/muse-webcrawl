Near side of the Moon

The near side of the Moon is the lunar hemisphere that always faces towards Earth, because the Moon rotates on its axis at the same rate at which it orbits Earth—a state known as synchronous rotation or tidal locking. The opposite hemisphere is the far side of the Moon.

Appearance

The near side is marked by dark basaltic plains, the lunar maria, which formed when ancient lava flooded impact basins. These include Oceanus Procellarum and the maria Tranquillitatis, Serenitatis and Imbrium. The surface is about one-third covered by maria, far more than the far side.

Exploration

All crewed and most robotic lunar landings have taken place on the near side, beginning with Apollo 11 in Mare Tranquillitatis in 1969, because direct communication with Earth is possible there without relay satellites.
