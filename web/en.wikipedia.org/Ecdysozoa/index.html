Ecdysozoa

Ecdysozoa
Rank: Superphylum (clade)
Includes: Arthropoda, Nematoda, Priapulida, Onychophora, Tardigrada and others
Defining trait: Moulting of cuticle (ecdysis)

Ecdysozoa is a major group of protostome animals united by the shared trait of growing by moulting a cuticle, a process called ecdysis controlled by ecdysteroid hormones. It includes the arthropods (insects, spiders, crustaceans and myriapods), nematodes, velvet worms, water bears and several smaller phyla — together the majority of described animal species.

Phylogeny

The group was proposed in 1997 by Anna Marie Aguinaldo and colleagues on the basis of 18S ribosomal RNA sequences, a result initially controversial because it separated arthropods from the annelids with which they had traditionally been grouped. Extensive molecular evidence has since confirmed the clade, which is the sister group of the Spiralia (including molluscs and annelids) within the Protostomia.

Characteristics

Ecdysozoans share a three-layered cuticle periodically shed and replaced, the loss of locomotory body cilia, and (in many lineages) a terminal mouth. The largest phylum, Arthropoda, alone contains well over a million described species.
