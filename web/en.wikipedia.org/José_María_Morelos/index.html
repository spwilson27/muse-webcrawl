José María Morelos

José María Teclo Morelos Pérez y Pavón (30 September 1765 – 22 December 1815) was a Mexican Catholic priest, statesman, and military leader who led the Mexican War of Independence movement after the execution of Miguel Hidalgo y Costilla in 1811. He is considered a national hero of Mexico, and the state of Morelos and the city of Morelia are named in his honour.

Early life

Morelos was born in Valladolid (present-day Morelia, Michoacán) to a family of mixed Spanish, Indigenous, and African ancestry. He worked as a mule driver and farm labourer before studying for the priesthood at the Colegio de San Nicolás, where Hidalgo had been rector. Ordained in 1797, he served as parish priest in Carácuaro and Nocupétaro.

Independence movement

After Hidalgo launched the independence revolt with the Grito de Dolores in September 1810, Morelos met with him and was commissioned to raise the south. Following Hidalgo's capture and execution, Morelos became the insurgency's principal leader. A gifted strategist, he captured much of southern Mexico, including the port of Acapulco, and conducted the celebrated Siege of Cuautla in 1812, breaking out after 72 days.

In 1813 Morelos convened the Congress of Chilpancingo, which issued the Solemn Act of the Declaration of Independence of Northern America. He presented his political programme, the Sentimientos de la Nación ("Feelings of the Nation"), which called for independence, popular sovereignty, the abolition of slavery and the caste system, and equality before the law. In 1814 the Congress promulgated the Constitution of Apatzingán, the first constitution of Mexico, though it never fully took effect.

Capture and death

After a series of defeats, Morelos was captured by royalist forces in November 1815 while escorting the Congress to safety. He was tried by the Inquisition, defrocked, and executed by firing squad at San Cristóbal Ecatepec on 22 December 1815. His lieutenant Vicente Guerrero carried on the struggle, which culminated in independence in 1821.
