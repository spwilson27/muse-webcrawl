In the history of the United Kingdom and the British Empire, the Victorian era was the reign of Queen Victoria, from 20 June 1837 until her death on 22 January 1901. The period followed the Georgian era and preceded the Edwardian era, and its later half overlaps with the first part of the Belle Époque era of continental Europe.

The era saw the industrialisation of Britain accelerate, the railway network and London Underground built, the population of England and Wales roughly double, and the British Empire reach toward its greatest extent, celebrated at the Great Exhibition of 1851 and the Golden and Diamond Jubilees of 1887 and 1897.

Society and reform

Victorian society was structured by a large and growing middle class with a distinctive moral code emphasizing duty, respectability, and domesticity. Successive Reform Acts (1867 and 1884) extended the franchise to most working men, while movements agitated for women's suffrage. Parliament regulated factories and mines, limited child labour, built public health infrastructure after cholera epidemics, and made elementary education compulsory (1880). Campaigns against slavery, public executions (abolished 1868), and cruelty to animals marked the era's reforming energies alongside its inequalities.

Culture

The Victorian age produced the great realist novels of Charles Dickens, the Brontë sisters, George Eliot, Thomas Hardy, and Anthony Trollope, and the poetry of Tennyson and Browning. Charles Darwin's On the Origin of Species (1859) transformed science and provoked debates between science and religion. Architecture revived Gothic styles — seen in the new Palace of Westminster — while painting was dominated by the Pre-Raphaelites and academic art.

Empire and foreign affairs

Britain avoided major European wars (except the Crimean War, 1853–1856) while expanding colonial rule in India (following the rebellion of 1857, the Raj replaced East India Company rule), Africa, and the Pacific, and fighting wars including the Opium Wars with China and the Anglo-Zulu and Boer wars in southern Africa.
