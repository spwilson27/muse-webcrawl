Helix–loop–helix

A helix–loop–helix (HLH) is a structural motif found in proteins, characterised by two alpha-helices connected by a short loop. The best-known form is the basic helix–loop–helix (bHLH) domain, one of the largest families of dimerising transcription factors in eukaryotes. In the bHLH motif, one helix is preceded by a stretch of basic amino acids that binds DNA, while the two helices allow two protein subunits to pair up (dimerise).

Structure and DNA binding

The bHLH domain is typically about 60 amino acids long. The basic region, rich in arginine and lysine, fits into the major groove of DNA and recognises a hexanucleotide sequence called the E-box, most commonly CANNTG, with the consensus CACGTG bound by many family members. The two helices of each monomer pack against the helices of a partner protein, so that the dimer presents two basic regions to adjacent DNA half-sites.

Biological roles

bHLH proteins regulate gene expression in cell differentiation and development across animals, plants and fungi. Well-known examples include MyoD, which drives muscle cell differentiation; the proneural proteins of the achaete-scute family in the nervous system; Myc and Max, involved in cell growth and cancer; and the circadian clock proteins CLOCK and BMAL1. In plants, bHLH factors control responses to light, iron uptake and the development of stomata and root hairs.

Classification

Family members are often grouped by their tissue distribution and dimerisation partners into classes: ubiquitously expressed class I proteins (E-proteins such as E12/E47), tissue-specific class II proteins (such as MyoD and neurogenins) that heterodimerise with class I partners, and other classes including Myc-like proteins, hairy/enhancer-of-split repressors, and PAS-containing proteins such as the hypoxia-inducible factors. Proteins containing the HLH region but lacking the basic DNA-binding region, such as the Id proteins, act as natural dominant-negative regulators by sequestering partners into inactive dimers.

Related motifs

The helix–loop–helix fold also appears independently of DNA binding — for example, in the EF-hand calcium-binding motif of proteins such as calmodulin, where the "helices" are labelled E and F and the loop coordinates a calcium ion. The bHLH motif is distinct from, but frequently compared with, other transcription-factor folds such as the leucine zipper, with which it is combined in the bHLH-ZIP proteins.
