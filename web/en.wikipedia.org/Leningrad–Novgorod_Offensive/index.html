Leningrad–Novgorod Offensive

Leningrad–Novgorod Strategic Offensive
Date14 January – 1 March 1944
LocationLeningrad and Novgorod regions, USSR
ResultSoviet victory; siege of Leningrad fully lifted
BelligerentsSoviet Union vs Nazi Germany
CommandersLeonid Govorov, Kirill Meretskov vs Georg von Küchler, Walter Model

The Leningrad–Novgorod Offensive was a strategic offensive by the Red Army of the Soviet Union against the German Army Group North in January–March 1944, during the Second World War. It fully lifted the siege of Leningrad, which had lasted nearly 900 days, liberated Novgorod, and pushed German forces back toward the Baltic states.

Background

Operation Iskra in January 1943 had opened a narrow land corridor into Leningrad, but German forces of the 18th Army still held positions within artillery range of the city. For the winter of 1944 the Soviet Stavka planned a coordinated assault by the Leningrad Front under Leonid Govorov and the Volkhov Front under Kirill Meretskov, supported by the 2nd Baltic Front.

The offensive

The offensive opened on 14 January 1944 with attacks from the Oranienbaum bridgehead and the Pulkovo heights. Soviet forces broke through the German defences, linked up at Ropsha, and destroyed the German heavy artillery that had shelled Leningrad. On 20 January Soviet troops liberated Novgorod, and on 27 January 1944 Moscow announced the complete lifting of the blockade, marked by a 324-gun salute in Leningrad.

Through February the Red Army advanced 220–280 kilometres, liberating Luga and driving Army Group North back to the Narva river and the Panther Line defences in Estonia. The Volkhov Front was disbanded in mid-February, its armies transferred to the Leningrad Front.

Aftermath

The operation ended the longest and deadliest siege in modern history, in which more than a million Leningrad civilians had died. It set the stage for the summer 1944 offensives against Finland and into the Baltic states, and marked the beginning of the final expulsion of German forces from Soviet territory in the northwest.
