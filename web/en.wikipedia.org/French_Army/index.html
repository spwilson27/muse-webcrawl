The French Army (French: Armée de terre, lit. "Land Army") is the land-based component of the French Armed Forces and the largest army in the European Union. Founded as a standing force under Charles VII in 1445, it is one of the world's oldest and most historically influential armies, having fought in conflicts from the Hundred Years' War to modern operations in Africa and the Middle East.

History

The compagnies d'ordonnance of 1445 made France the first European state with a permanent standing army. Under Louis XIV, France fielded Europe's largest military. The levée en masse of 1793 revolutionized warfare by national conscription, and Napoleon's Grande Armée dominated Europe until 1815. France bore the main weight of the Western Front in the First World War, collapsed in the Battle of France in 1940, and was rebuilt by Free French and colonial forces. After the colonial wars in Indochina and Algeria, conscription was suspended in 1996–2001, making the army all-volunteer.

Organization and equipment

The modern army is organized into divisions, brigades, and specialized commands, including the French Foreign Legion, mountain troops (chasseurs alpins), marine infantry, and the special forces command. It deploys the Leclerc main battle tank, VBCI and Griffon armoured vehicles, CAESAR self-propelled howitzers, and Tiger attack helicopters, produced largely by French industry under the SCORPION modernization programme.

Role

Beyond territorial defence within NATO and the EU, the army maintains a strong expeditionary tradition, with long-running deployments in the Sahel (Operation Barkhane, 2013–2022), the Levant, and overseas territories.
