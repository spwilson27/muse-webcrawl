Good Neighbor policy

The Good Neighbor policy was the foreign policy of the administration of United States President Franklin D. Roosevelt toward Latin America, announced in his first inaugural address in March 1933 and formalised at the Montevideo Convention in December 1933. It marked a retreat from the era of US military intervention in the hemisphere associated with the Roosevelt Corollary and "banana wars."

Principles and actions

The policy pledged non-intervention and non-interference in the domestic affairs of Latin American nations. The United States withdrew Marines from Haiti (1934), abrogated the Platt Amendment governing Cuba (Treaty of Relations, 1934), accepted Mexico's oil expropriation of 1938 without military response, and pursued reciprocal trade agreements, cultural exchange, and the creation of bodies such as the Export–Import Bank and Nelson Rockefeller's Office of Inter-American Affairs, which sponsored Orson Welles's ill-fated film It's All True and Disney's Saludos Amigos.

Legacy

Intended partly to secure hemispheric solidarity as war approached, the policy gained the US considerable goodwill and underpinned cooperation during the Second World War. After 1945 it was eroded by Cold War anti-communist interventions, beginning with the 1954 coup in Guatemala.
