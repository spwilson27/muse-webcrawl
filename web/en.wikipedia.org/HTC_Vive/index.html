HTC Vive

HTC Vive
Developer: HTC, Valve
Type: Virtual reality headset
Release date: 5 April 2016
Display: 2160 × 1200 (1080 × 1200 per eye), 90 Hz
Tracking: Lighthouse (laser base stations)

The HTC Vive is a virtual reality headset developed by HTC and Valve Corporation. Released on 5 April 2016, it was the first commercially available VR system to offer "room-scale" virtual reality, allowing users to physically walk around a tracked space while wearing the headset.

Hardware

The headset contains two OLED displays with a combined resolution of 2160 × 1200 running at 90 Hz. Motion tracking is provided by Valve's Lighthouse system: two infrared base stations sweep the room with lasers, and photodiodes on the headset and its two wand-like controllers determine their positions with sub-millimetre precision. A front-facing camera lets users see the real world without removing the headset, and a "chaperone" grid warns when the user approaches the edge of the play area. The system connects to a PC and runs software through Valve's SteamVR platform.

History and successors

The Vive was unveiled at Mobile World Congress in March 2015. It competed directly with the Oculus Rift, which launched days earlier but initially lacked motion controllers and room-scale tracking. HTC followed it with the Vive Pro (2018), with higher-resolution displays, the standalone Vive Focus line, the Vive Cosmos (2019) using inside-out tracking, and the Vive Pro 2 (2021). Valve later released its own headset, the Valve Index (2019), which remains compatible with the Lighthouse ecosystem.

Reception

Reviewers praised the Vive's tracking accuracy and immersive room-scale experience while criticising its price ($799 at launch), bulky cabling and setup requirements. It was widely adopted in location-based entertainment, arcades, design visualisation and enterprise training.
