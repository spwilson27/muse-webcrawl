Guildhall is a municipal building in the City of London, England, off Gresham and Basinghall streets. It has been the centre of City government since the Middle Ages and the seat of the City of London Corporation. The term "Guildhall" refers both to the whole building complex and to its main room, the Great Hall, a medieval great hall which is the third largest civic hall in England.

History

A guildhall is thought to have existed on the site since at least the 12th century. The present Great Hall was built between 1411 and 1440, funded by the City's wealthy guilds. It was severely damaged in the Great Fire of London in 1666 — its roof and interior were destroyed, though the stone walls survived — and again during the Blitz in December 1940. On both occasions it was restored, and a new hammerbeam-style roof was installed in 1954.

Uses

Guildhall hosts the meetings of the Court of Common Council, state and civic banquets, and the annual Lord Mayor's Banquet, at which the Prime Minister traditionally gives a major foreign-policy speech. Historic trials have been held there, including those of Lady Jane Grey and Anne Askew. The complex also houses the Guildhall Library, a major collection on London history, and the Guildhall Art Gallery, beneath which lie the excavated remains of London's Roman amphitheatre, discovered in 1988.

Gog and Magog

Two giants, Gog and Magog, traditionally associated with the City, stand as statues in the Great Hall. Carved wooden effigies destroyed in the Blitz were replaced in 1953; the figures are carried in the Lord Mayor's Show.
