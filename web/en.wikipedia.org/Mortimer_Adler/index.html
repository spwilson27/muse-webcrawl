Mortimer Jerome Adler (December 28, 1902 – June 28, 2001) was an American philosopher, educator, encyclopedist, and popular author. As a philosopher he worked within the Aristotelian and Thomistic traditions. He taught at Columbia University and the University of Chicago, served as chairman of the board of editors of Encyclopædia Britannica, and founded the Institute for Philosophical Research.

Great Books and popular works

With Robert Maynard Hutchins, Adler championed the Great Books movement in liberal education. As editor of Britannica's Great Books of the Western World (1952), he compiled the Syntopicon, a two-volume index of the great ideas of Western thought. His best-selling How to Read a Book (1940, revised 1972 with Charles Van Doren) became a classic guide to analytical reading. He later led the Paideia Proposal (1982) for reform of American public schooling around Socratic seminar discussion of classic works.

Later life

Adler wrote and lectured into his nineties, publishing Intellect: Mind Over Matter (1990) and other works, and converted to Roman Catholicism late in life. He died in 2001 at the age of 98.
