Giulio Racah

Giulio Racah (9 February 1909 – 28 August 1965) was an Italian–Israeli theoretical physicist who made foundational contributions to atomic and nuclear spectroscopy. He is best known for developing the mathematical theory of complex atomic spectra, including the Racah coefficients (W-coefficients) used to couple angular momenta in quantum mechanics, and the Racah parameters used in the theory of electron configurations.

Life and career

Racah was born in Florence, Italy, and studied at the University of Florence, taking his doctorate in 1931. He worked in Rome with Enrico Fermi's group and spent time in Zurich with Wolfgang Pauli before becoming professor of theoretical physics at the University of Pisa in 1937. Following the introduction of Italy's racial laws, he emigrated to Palestine in 1939 and joined the Hebrew University of Jerusalem, where he built a leading school of theoretical physics. He later served as the university's rector and acting president.

Scientific work

In a celebrated series of papers in the 1940s, "Theory of Complex Spectra", Racah applied group theory to classify the energy levels of atoms with many electrons, introducing tensor operators, coefficients of fractional parentage, and the W-coefficients that now bear his name. His methods transformed spectroscopy and were later extended to nuclear shell theory and particle physics. He also played a central role in establishing physics research in Israel and advised the Israeli defence establishment on scientific matters. He died in Florence in 1965 while travelling. The Racah Institute of Physics at the Hebrew University is named in his honour.
