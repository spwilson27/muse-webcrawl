Oktoberfest

LocationTheresienwiese, Munich, Germany
First held12 October 1810
Duration16–18 days (mid-September to first Sunday in October)
AttendanceAround 6–7 million visitors annually

Oktoberfest (German: Oktoberfest, Bavarian: Wiesn) is the world's largest Volksfest — a beer festival and travelling funfair — held annually in Munich, Bavaria, Germany, on the Theresienwiese fairground. Running from mid- or late-September to the first Sunday in October, it lasts 16 to 18 days and attracts around six million visitors from around the world, who consume roughly seven million litres of beer.

History

The festival originated on 12 October 1810, when citizens of Munich were invited to celebrate the wedding of Crown Prince Ludwig (later King Ludwig I) and Princess Therese of Saxe-Hildburghausen on the fields outside the city gates, named Theresienwiese ("Theresa's meadow") in her honour. Horse races closed the festivities and were repeated in following years, growing into an agricultural show and fair. By the late 19th century the festival had moved into September for the better weather, and large beer halls run by Munich's breweries became its centrepiece.

The festival

Only beer brewed within Munich's city limits by its six historic breweries — Augustiner, Hacker-Pschorr, Hofbräu, Löwenbräu, Paulaner and Spaten — and conforming to the Reinheitsgebot, at a strength of about 6%, may be served as Oktoberfest beer, in one-litre steins called Maß. The festival opens with the tapping of the first keg by the Mayor of Munich, who cries "O'zapft is!" ("It is tapped!"). Highlights include the landlords' and breweries' parade, the traditional costume and riflemen's parade, and the large tents seating thousands, alongside fairground rides, brass-band music, and traditional foods such as roast chicken (Hendl), pork knuckle, pretzels and gingerbread hearts. Many visitors wear Bavarian dress: lederhosen for men and dirndls for women.
