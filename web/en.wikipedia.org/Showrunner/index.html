Showrunner

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A showrunner is the leading producer of a television series in the United States and other countries where the role exists. Unlike films, where directors typically hold creative authority, in television the showrunner — usually the series' creator or a senior writer-producer — has overall creative authority and management responsibility for the production, combining the roles of head writer and executive producer.

Role

The showrunner oversees nearly every aspect of a series: developing story arcs, running the writers' room, assigning scripts, supervising casting and editing, managing the budget and schedule, and serving as the principal liaison with the network or streaming platform. The title is informal and not officially recognized by guilds; the credit a showrunner receives is typically "executive producer".

History

The term "showrunner" became widespread in the 1990s and 2000s as the trade press sought a term for the writer-producer who was the public face of a series. The rise of auteur-driven "prestige television" increased the prominence of figures such as David Chase (The Sopranos), Matthew Weiner (Mad Men), Shonda Rhimes (Grey's Anatomy) and Vince Gilligan (Breaking Bad), who became as associated with their shows as film directors are with their movies.

Outside the United States

In the United Kingdom, where series are shorter and often written by a single writer, the equivalent functions are divided between the lead writer, producer and script editor, though the showrunner model has been adopted for some larger productions.
