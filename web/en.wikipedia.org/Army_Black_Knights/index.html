Army Black Knights

The Army Black Knights are the athletic teams of the United States Military Academy at West Point, New York. The academy fields teams in NCAA Division I competition, with most teams competing in the Patriot League; the football team competes in the American Athletic Conference.

History

Intercollegiate athletics at West Point date to the nineteenth century. The football program became a national power in the 1940s under coach Earl "Red" Blaik, winning three consecutive national championships from 1944 to 1946, and produced Heisman Trophy winners Doc Blanchard, Glenn Davis, and Pete Dawkins.

Rivalries

The Black Knights' most famous rivalry is with the Navy Midshipmen, contested annually in the Army–Navy Game, one of the most celebrated fixtures in American college football. Army also competes with the Air Force Falcons for the Commander-in-Chief's Trophy.

Sports

The academy sponsors teams in football, basketball, baseball, ice hockey, lacrosse, wrestling, and many other sports. The ice hockey team competes in Atlantic Hockey, and the sprint football team is a perennial contender in its league.
