Doreen Valiente

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Doreen Edith Dominy Valiente (4 January 1922 – 1 September 1999) was an English author and occultist who was one of the first public figures of the modern pagan religion of Wicca. Initiated by Gerald Gardner in 1953, she became high priestess of his Bricket Wood coven and wrote much of the liturgy still used in Gardnerian Wicca, earning her the description "mother of modern witchcraft".

Life and work

Born Doreen Dominy in Mitcham, Surrey, she developed an early interest in magic and folklore. After reading newspaper reports about Gardner's coven, she contacted him and was initiated in 1953 at the home of the witch Edith Woodford-Grimes. Valiente rewrote Gardner's Book of Shadows, removing much of the material borrowed from Aleister Crowley and composing enduring texts such as the poetic form of the Charge of the Goddess and the Wiccan Rede's best-known expression. A dispute over publicity led her to leave Gardner in 1957; she later worked with other traditions, including Robert Cochrane's group.

Legacy

Valiente campaigned against sensationalism and fraud in the occult world, researched the historical roots of witchcraft, and amassed a major collection of ritual artefacts, now held by the Doreen Valiente Foundation in Brighton. Her books — including Where Witchcraft Lives (1962), An ABC of Witchcraft (1973) and Witchcraft for Tomorrow (1978) — remain standard references. In 2013 English Heritage placed a blue plaque on her Brighton home, the first such commemoration for a Wiccan.
