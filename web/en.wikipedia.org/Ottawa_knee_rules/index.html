Ottawa knee rules

TypeClinical prediction rule
Developed1990s, Ottawa, Canada
PurposeDecide whether knee X-ray is needed after injury

The Ottawa knee rules are a clinical prediction rule used in emergency medicine to determine whether a patient with an acute knee injury needs an X-ray to exclude a fracture. They were developed in Ottawa, Canada, by Ian Stiell and colleagues, alongside the better-known Ottawa ankle rules.

The rule

An X-ray is indicated if any of the following is present: age 55 or older; isolated tenderness of the patella; tenderness at the head of the fibula; inability to flex the knee to 90 degrees; or inability to bear weight both immediately after the injury and in the emergency department (four steps).

Performance

Validation studies have found the rules to be highly sensitive for clinically significant knee fractures, substantially reducing unnecessary radiographs without missing important injuries. They are widely taught and used internationally.
