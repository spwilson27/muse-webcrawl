Bed load

Bed load is particles in a flowing fluid, such as a river, that are transported along the bed. Bed load is in contrast to suspended load and wash load, which are carried in the water column.

Movement

Bed load moves by rolling, sliding and saltation (hopping). Its rate depends on flow velocity, particle size and density, and bed roughness, and is described by formulae such as the Meyer-Peter and Müller equation.

Significance

Bed load transport shapes river channels, forms bars and riffles, and is important in managing navigation, reservoirs and habitat. See Sediment transport.
