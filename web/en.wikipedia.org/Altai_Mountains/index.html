Altai Mountains

The Altai Mountains, also spelled Altay Mountains, are a mountain range in Central Asia and East Asia, where Russia, China, Mongolia, and Kazakhstan converge, and where the rivers Irtysh and Ob have their headwaters. The massif merges with the Sayan Mountains in the northeast and gradually becomes lower in the southeast, where it merges into the high plateau of the Gobi Desert. It spans from about 45° to 52° N and from about 84° to 99° E.

Geography

The highest summit is Mount Belukha (4,506 m), a twin-peaked glaciated massif on the Russian–Kazakh border. Other major peaks include Khüiten Peak in the Tavan Bogd group on the Mongolian frontier. The range gives rise to great Siberian rivers including the Ob, Irtysh, and Yenisei headwaters. The Golden Mountains of Altai in Russia are a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the region is noted for pristine lakes such as Lake Teletskoye and Lake Markakol.

Nature and archaeology

The Altai supports steppe, taiga, and alpine tundra, with wildlife including snow leopards, argali sheep, and Siberian ibex. Archaeologically it is famous for the frozen Pazyryk tombs of Scythian-era horsemen and for the Denisova Cave, where remains of the Denisovans and the first known Neanderthal–Denisovan hybrid were found.
