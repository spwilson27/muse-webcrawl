Damascus, Arkansas

Damascus, a small town in north-central Arkansas

Damascus is a town in Faulkner and Van Buren counties in the U.S. state of Arkansas. The population was 382 at the 2010 census. It is part of the Little Rock–North Little Rock–Conway Metropolitan Statistical Area. The town is best known nationally as the site of the 1980 Damascus Titan missile explosion, one of the most serious accidents involving a nuclear weapon in United States history.

History

Damascus was settled in the nineteenth century along the route between Little Rock and the northern hill country. Like many small Arkansas communities, it grew around farming, timber, and a stop on regional roads; U.S. Route 65 passes through the town, linking it to Conway to the south and Clinton to the north.

Titan missile explosion

On September 18–19, 1980, an accident at Launch Complex 374-7, a Titan II missile silo just north of Damascus, caused a major explosion. A dropped tool punctured the missile's first-stage fuel tank, and leaking fuel vapor ignited hours later, blowing the 740-ton silo door off and ejecting the missile's W-53 nuclear warhead. The warhead landed nearby but did not detonate, and its safety features prevented any radioactive contamination. One airman, Sergeant David Livingston, was killed and 21 others injured. The incident became the subject of the book and documentary film Command and Control by Eric Schlosser.

Geography

Damascus is located in northern Faulkner County and southern Van Buren County, in the foothills of the Ozarks. According to the United States Census Bureau, the town has a total area of about 5.0 km², all land.

Demographics

As of the census of 2010, there were 382 people residing in the town. The population has remained small and largely rural in character, with most residents commuting to larger employment centers such as Conway.
