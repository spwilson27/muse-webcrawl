Leaching (pedology)

In pedology, leaching is the loss of soluble substances and colloids from the upper layers of soil by percolating precipitation. As water moves downward through the soil profile it dissolves and carries away mineral salts, nutrients such as nitrogen, potassium and calcium, and fine clay particles, which may be redeposited in lower horizons — a process called illuviation — or lost to groundwater.

Leaching is most intense in humid climates with high rainfall and free drainage, and it contributes to soil acidification and the formation of strongly weathered, nutrient-poor soils such as laterites in the tropics. In agriculture, leaching of nitrate and phosphate fertilisers reduces fertility and can cause eutrophication of waterways; management practices such as cover crops, split fertiliser applications and buffer strips are used to limit it.

The rate of leaching depends on rainfall, soil texture, structure, vegetation cover and the solubility of the materials present.
