Islam is an Abrahamic monotheistic religion centred on the Quran, which Muslims believe to be the direct word of God (Allah) as revealed to the prophet Muhammad, and on Muhammad's teachings and example (the sunnah, recorded in hadith). It is the world's second-largest religion, with about 1.9 billion followers, known as Muslims, making up roughly a quarter of the world's population.

Beliefs

The core belief is that God is one and incomparable (tawhid) and that Muhammad is the final prophet. Muslims also believe in angels, revealed scriptures (including the Torah, Psalms and Gospel in their original forms), earlier prophets such as Abraham, Moses and Jesus, the Day of Judgment, and divine decree. The declaration of faith, the shahada, states: "There is no god but God, and Muhammad is the Messenger of God."

Practices

Religious duties include the Five Pillars: the shahada (declaration of faith), salah (prayer five times daily), zakat (almsgiving), sawm (fasting during Ramadan), and the hajj pilgrimage to Mecca, required once in a lifetime for those able. Mosques are the places of communal worship; Friday noon prayer is congregational.

History

According to Islamic tradition, Muhammad received his first revelation in 610 CE near Mecca. In 622 he migrated to Medina (the Hijra, year 1 of the Islamic calendar). After his death in 632, Islam expanded rapidly under the Rashidun and Umayyad caliphates across Arabia, Persia, the Levant, Egypt and North Africa, and later into Iberia, Anatolia, Central and South Asia and Southeast Asia. A schism over succession gave rise to the Sunni majority (about 87–90%) and Shia minority (about 10–13%).

Law and culture

Sharia, Islamic law derived from the Quran and sunnah, is interpreted by schools of jurisprudence (madhhab). Islamic civilization made major contributions to mathematics, astronomy, medicine, philosophy and architecture during the Islamic Golden Age.
