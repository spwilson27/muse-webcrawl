Antisolar point

The antisolar point is the abstract point in the sky directly opposite the Sun as seen by an observer — the point 180° from the Sun, below the horizon when the Sun is up. Geometrically, it is the point where the shadow of the observer's head falls; the antisolar point lies on the line extending from the Sun through the observer and continuing to the opposite side of the celestial sphere.

Associated phenomena

Many atmospheric optical phenomena are centered on or near the antisolar point. A rainbow is a circle centered on the antisolar point, of which observers see an arc. Glories — concentric colored rings seen around the shadow of an aircraft or mountaineer on clouds below — surround the antisolar point, as does the Brocken spectre. The heiligenschein brightens the ground around the shadow of the observer's head, and at night the gegenschein, a faint brightening of the zodiacal light, appears at the antisolar point.

Related points

The antisolar point is distinct from the anthelion, a halo phenomenon on the parhelic circle opposite the Sun, and from the sub-solar point on a planet's surface. During a total solar eclipse, the Moon's shadow converges toward the antisolar direction.
