Temple festival

A temple festival is a festival held at a temple, combining religious observance with communal celebration. In Hinduism, annual temple festivals — often called utsavam — are major events in which the temple deity is taken in procession on a chariot or palanquin, accompanied by music, dance, and fairs. Similar observances occur in Buddhism, Jainism, Shinto, and other traditions.

Hindu temple festivals

Major Hindu temple festivals include the Rath Yatra of Puri, in which the deities Jagannath, Balabhadra, and Subhadra are drawn through the streets on enormous wooden chariots; the Brahmotsavam at Tirupati; and the Panguni Uthiram and temple car festivals of Tamil Nadu. Festivals typically follow the lunar calendar and may last from a single day to several weeks, culminating in a procession and ritual bathing of the deity (abhisheka).

Other traditions

In Japan, a matsuri is a Shinto shrine festival featuring portable shrines (mikoshi) paraded through neighborhoods. Chinese temple fairs, held around Lunar New Year, combine worship with markets, opera, and folk performance. Buddhist temples observe festivals such as Vesak with processions and lantern displays.
