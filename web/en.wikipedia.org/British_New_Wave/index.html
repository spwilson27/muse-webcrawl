British New Wave

The British New Wave was a movement in British cinema of the late 1950s and early 1960s, characterised by social realism, working-class protagonists and northern English settings. Associated with the "kitchen sink" dramas and the Angry Young Men of literature and theatre, it brought a new frankness about class, sex and everyday life.

Films and directors

Key films include Room at the Top (1959), Saturday Night and Sunday Morning (1960), A Taste of Honey (1961), The Loneliness of the Long Distance Runner (1962) and This Sporting Life (1963). Leading directors included Tony Richardson, Karel Reisz, Lindsay Anderson and John Schlesinger, several of whom emerged from the Free Cinema documentary movement.
