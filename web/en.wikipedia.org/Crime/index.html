Crime

In ordinary language, a crime is an unlawful act punishable by a state or other authority. The term, in modern criminal law, has no simple universal definition, though statutory definitions have been provided for certain purposes. Crimes are generally categorized as offences against the person, property offences, public order offences, and offences against the state.

Elements

Most crimes require both a prohibited act (actus reus) and a culpable mental state (mens rea), though strict liability offences dispense with the mental element. Defences such as insanity, duress, and self-defence may negate liability.

Measurement and trends

Crime is measured through official statistics such as the FBI's Uniform Crime Reports and through victimization surveys. Recorded crime rose through much of the 20th century in many countries, then declined markedly from the 1990s in the United States and other Western nations — the "crime drop" — a trend whose causes remain debated among criminologists.
