Model railroad

A model railroad (American English) or railway modelling (British English) is a miniature representation of a railway system, built as a hobby or for professional display. A model railroad typically includes locomotives, rolling stock, track, scenery, buildings, and signalling, arranged on a layout whose realism ranges from simple toy train sets to meticulously researched re-creations of prototype locations.

Scales and gauges

Models are built to standard scales. The most popular worldwide is HO scale (1:87), followed by N scale (1:160), which allows extensive layouts in small spaces. Larger scales include O (1:48) and G (garden railways), while Z (1:220) is among the smallest. In Britain, OO scale (1:76) predominates. Standards organisations such as the NMRA in North America and MOROP (NERM standards) in Europe maintain interoperability standards.

Control systems

Traditional layouts vary track voltage to control train speed (analogue DC control). Modern layouts increasingly use Digital Command Control (DCC), in which each locomotive carries a decoder responding to digital commands sent through the rails, allowing independent control of multiple trains, sound, and lighting on the same track.

The hobby

Railway modelling combines skills in carpentry, electrical work, sculpture, painting, and historical research. Hobbyists may model a specific era and place, follow a prototype railway's operations, or create freelance railroads. Large club and exhibition layouts, and attractions such as Hamburg's Miniatur Wunderland — the world's largest model railway — draw millions of visitors. Magazines such as Model Railroader (founded 1934) anchor the hobby's literature.
