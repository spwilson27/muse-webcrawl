Bézout matrix

In mathematics, a Bézout matrix (or Bézoutian or Bezoutiant) is a special square matrix associated with two polynomials, introduced by Étienne Bézout in his work on elimination theory and named by James Joseph Sylvester. It is closely related to the resultant of two polynomials.

Definition

Let f(x) and g(x) be two polynomials of degree at most n. The Bézout matrix of order n is defined through the generating formula

[ f(x) g(y) − f(y) g(x) ] / (x − y) = Σ bij xi yj,

where the coefficients bij form a symmetric n × n matrix. Each entry is expressible in terms of the coefficients of the two polynomials.

Properties and applications

  • The determinant of the Bézout matrix equals the resultant of f and g; consequently the matrix is singular if and only if the polynomials have a common root.
  • The rank of the Bézoutian relates to the degree of the greatest common divisor of the two polynomials.
  • Bézout matrices appear in applications including root-counting and root-location problems for polynomials (via their inertia, using results analogous to Hermite's criterion), stability tests in control theory, and the study of continued fractions and Sturm sequences.
