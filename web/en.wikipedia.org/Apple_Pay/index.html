Apple Pay

DeveloperApple Inc.
Released20 October 2014
PlatformsiPhone, Apple Watch, iPad, Mac
TechnologyNFC contactless payment; tokenisation; Touch ID / Face ID authentication

Apple Pay is a mobile payment and digital wallet service by Apple Inc. that allows users to make payments in person, within apps, and on the web using an iPhone, Apple Watch, iPad, or Mac. Announced on 9 September 2014 and launched the following month in the United States, it works with contactless payment terminals using near-field communication (NFC), and it digitises payment cards stored in the Wallet app. By Apple's reports, the service is available in more than 80 countries and regions and is used by hundreds of millions of people.

How it works

When a user adds a credit or debit card, Apple Pay does not store the actual card number on the device or on Apple servers. Instead the card network assigns a Device Account Number — a token — which is encrypted and stored in the device's Secure Element chip. Each transaction is authorised with a one-time dynamic security code and the user's biometric authentication (Face ID or Touch ID) or passcode, so merchants never receive the customer's real card number. Apple charges card issuers a small fee, reported at about 0.15 percent of transaction value in the United States.

History and expansion

Apple Pay launched with six major US card issuers and support at some 220,000 locations, expanding to the United Kingdom in 2015 and to dozens of further markets in subsequent years. Variants include Apple Pay on the Web (2016), person-to-person payments via Apple Cash (2017, US), transit fare payment through Express Mode, and identity and key features in Wallet. The related Apple Card, a credit card issued with Goldman Sachs, followed in 2019.

Reception and regulation

Apple Pay helped mainstream contactless and tokenised payments and became the leading mobile wallet in many markets. Its requirement that NFC on iPhones be reserved for Apple's own service drew antitrust scrutiny: in 2024, following a European Commission investigation, Apple committed to opening iPhone NFC access to rival wallets in the European Economic Area, and the EU's Digital Markets Act imposed further obligations.
