Marvin Kaye

Marvin Nathan Kaye (March 10, 1938 – May 13, 2021) was an American writer, editor, anthologist and actor. A prolific figure in mystery, fantasy and horror publishing, he was the editor of numerous anthologies, a senior editor at The Magazine of Fantasy and Science Fiction's sister publications, and the long-time editor of H. P. Lovecraft's Magazine of Horror and Sherlock Holmes Mystery Magazine. He also co-founded and served as artistic director of The Open Book, a New York readers' theatre company.

Career

Kaye studied at Penn State and worked as a reporter and editor before turning to fiction. His novels include The Incredible Umbrella (1979) and its sequel The Amorous Umbrella, literary fantasies whose hero tours the worlds of classic fiction; the fantasy The Masters of Solitude (1978), written with Parke Godwin; and mystery novels including the Hilary Quayle series, beginning with The Laurel and Hardy Murders (1977). He wrote a novelisation related to the musical The Last Starfighter franchise and many short stories.

Editing and theatre

As an anthologist Kaye edited dozens of themed collections of fantasy, horror and mystery, often drawing on obscure classic stories, including The Resurrected Man, Forbidden Planets and volumes of ghost stories and devil tales. He taught creative writing at Mercy College in New York. With The Open Book (founded 1974 with his wife Saralee Kaye), he adapted and performed readers-theatre productions of classic literature in New York for decades. Kaye died in 2021 at the age of 83.
