Verizon Fios

Verizon Fios is a bundled internet access, telephone, and television service provided by Verizon Communications over a fiber-optic network in the United States. The name "Fios" is an acronym for "Fiber-Optic Service". Launched in 2005 in Keller, Texas, it was one of the first large-scale fiber-to-the-premises (FTTP) deployments by a major American telephone company and has consistently been rated among the fastest consumer internet services in the country.

History

Verizon began building its fiber network in 2004 under the Fios brand, passing millions of homes in its service territory in the Northeast and Mid-Atlantic. Internet service began in 2005, with television (Fios TV) following after the company negotiated local franchise agreements. In 2016 Verizon sold its wireline operations in California, Texas and Florida — including Fios customers there — to Frontier Communications. In 2024 Verizon announced an agreement to acquire Frontier, which would bring those fiber markets back under Verizon's control.

Services

Fios Internet offers symmetrical speeds up to gigabit class over optical network terminals installed at the customer premises. Fios TV delivers hundreds of channels over the same fiber using QAM and IP delivery, and Fios Digital Voice provides telephone service. The network largely uses GPON technology, with upgrades to faster standards planned to support multi-gigabit tiers.

Reception

Fios has frequently topped consumer satisfaction and speed surveys, including J.D. Power studies and the FCC's Measuring Broadband America reports, while also drawing the usual industry criticism over pricing, fees and customer service. Its footprint expansion slowed after about 2010 as Verizon shifted investment toward wireless, leaving availability concentrated in the Boston-to-Washington corridor and a few other markets.
