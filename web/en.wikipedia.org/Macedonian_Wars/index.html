Macedonian Wars

The Macedonian Wars (214–148 BC) were a series of conflicts fought by the Roman Republic and its Greek allies in the eastern Mediterranean against several different major Greek kingdoms. The result of the wars was the establishment of Roman hegemony over Macedonia, Greece and much of the eastern Mediterranean.

The four wars, fought between 214 and 148 BC, pitted Rome against the Antigonid kingdom of Macedon, ruled first by Philip V and later by his son Perseus. They coincided with Rome's wars against Carthage in the west and against the Seleucid king Antiochus III in the east.

First Macedonian War (214–205 BC)

The First Macedonian War broke out during the Second Punic War, when Philip V allied himself with Hannibal of Carthage after the Roman disaster at Cannae. Rome, preoccupied in Italy, relied on its allies the Aetolian League and Pergamum to contain Philip. The war ended inconclusively with the Treaty of Phoenice in 205 BC.

Second Macedonian War (200–197 BC)

In 200 BC Rome declared war on Philip again, responding to appeals from Pergamum and Rhodes and to Philip's expansion in the Aegean and Thrace. The decisive battle came at Cynoscephalae in 197 BC, where the legions of Titus Quinctius Flamininus defeated Philip's phalanx. The ensuing peace stripped Macedon of its possessions outside Macedonia proper, and Flamininus proclaimed the "freedom of the Greeks" at the Isthmian Games in 196 BC.

Third Macedonian War (171–168 BC)

Philip's son Perseus rebuilt Macedonian strength and influence, alarming Rome. War was declared in 171 BC, and after initial Roman difficulties the consul Lucius Aemilius Paullus crushed Perseus at the Battle of Pydna in 168 BC. Macedon was divided into four separate republics, and Perseus was carried to Rome in triumph.

Fourth Macedonian War (150–148 BC)

A pretender, Andriscus, claiming to be a son of Perseus, raised a revolt in 150 BC and defeated a Roman army. In 148 BC the praetor Quintus Caecilius Metellus defeated him at Pydna; Macedonia was then annexed as a Roman province, the first of the Greek world to be so absorbed.

Consequences

The wars ended the independence of Macedon and marked the beginning of direct Roman domination of Greece, completed in 146 BC with the Achaean War and the destruction of Corinth.
