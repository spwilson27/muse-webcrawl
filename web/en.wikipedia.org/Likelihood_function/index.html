Likelihood function

In statistical inference, the likelihood function measures how well a statistical model explains observed data. Given a parametric model, the likelihood of a set of parameter values is proportional to the probability of the observed data under those values: it is the joint probability (or density) of the observations, viewed as a function of the parameters.

Definition

For independent observations, the likelihood is the product of the individual densities evaluated at the data. Because products are unwieldy, the log-likelihood — the sum of the log densities — is usually used; it is maximised at the same parameter values. Parameters that make the observed data most probable are the maximum likelihood estimates.

Role in inference

The likelihood underlies maximum likelihood estimation, likelihood-ratio tests, and information criteria such as the AIC. In Bayesian statistics, the likelihood combines with a prior distribution through Bayes' theorem to yield the posterior. R. A. Fisher developed the formal concept of likelihood in the early 20th century, distinguishing it from probability.
