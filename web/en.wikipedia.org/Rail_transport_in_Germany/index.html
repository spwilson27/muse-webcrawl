Rail transport in Germany is provided by one of the densest railway networks in the world, with Deutsche Bahn (DB), a state-owned joint-stock company, as the dominant operator alongside several hundred private and regional companies. Germany's first railway opened in 1835 between Nuremberg and Fürth (the Bavarian Ludwigsbahn, worked by the locomotive Adler).

History

After a period of competing state and private railways in the 19th century, the lines were unified as the Deutsche Reichsbahn in 1920. Following the Second World War the network was divided between West Germany's Deutsche Bundesbahn and East Germany's Reichsbahn; the two merged in 1994 to form Deutsche Bahn AG as part of the German rail reform, which also opened the network to competition.

Services

Long-distance services are dominated by ICE (Intercity Express) high-speed trains, reaching up to 300 km/h on dedicated lines such as Cologne–Frankfurt, alongside Intercity and EuroCity trains. Regional services (Regional-Express, Regionalbahn) are ordered by the federal states, and most large cities have S-Bahn suburban networks and U-Bahn metros. Germany is a major freight corridor, and the 2023 introduction of the flat-rate Deutschlandticket boosted regional rail use.
