Hans Holbein the Younger

Bornc. 1497, Augsburg, Holy Roman Empire
Died1543, London, England
Known forPainting, portraiture, woodcut design
MovementNorthern Renaissance

Hans Holbein the Younger (c. 1497–1543) was a German artist and printmaker who worked in a Northern Renaissance style. He is considered one of the greatest portraitists of the sixteenth century and served as King's Painter to Henry VIII of England.

Early career

Holbein was born in Augsburg, the son of the painter Hans Holbein the Elder. He established himself in Basel, Switzerland, where he designed book illustrations, title pages, and woodcuts, including the famous Dance of Death series, and painted portraits of humanists such as Erasmus of Rotterdam.

England

Holbein first visited England in 1526 with an introduction to Sir Thomas More, and returned permanently in 1532. He painted merchants of the Steelyard, courtiers, and royalty. His celebrated works include The Ambassadors (1533), with its anamorphic skull, and portraits of Henry VIII that fixed the king's image for posterity, including the Whitehall mural (destroyed by fire in 1698).

Legacy

Holbein died in London in 1543, probably of plague. His portraits are prized for their precision, psychological insight, and meticulous rendering of textiles, jewellery, and objects that signal the sitter's status.
