Provinces of Italy

Italy is divided into 20 regions and roughly a hundred provinces and metropolitan cities.

The provinces of Italy (province d'Italia) are the second-level administrative divisions of the Italian Republic, between the regions and the municipalities (comuni). In recent decades their number has hovered around one hundred; following reforms, many provinces have been converted into "provincial-level" bodies with reduced powers, while the largest urban areas are administered as metropolitan cities (città metropolitane), such as Rome, Milan, Naples, Turin and Bologna.

Role and reform

Historically, provinces handled local roads, schools, environmental planning and coordination of municipalities, governed by an elected president and council. Austerity-era reforms — notably the 2014 Delrio law — stripped most ordinary-statute provinces of direct elections and many functions, turning provincial councils into bodies elected by local mayors and councillors. In the autonomous regions of Sicily and Sardinia, provinces were reorganised into free municipal consortia and new provincial entities respectively.

Examples

Provinces are usually named after their capital city: for example the Province of Florence (now the Metropolitan City of Florence), the Province of Verona in Veneto, the Province of Lecce in Apulia, and the Province of Trento — which, with Bolzano/South Tyrol, enjoys special autonomous status within Trentino-Alto Adige.
