Nafplio

Nafplio or Nauplia (Greek: Ναύπλιο) is a coastal city in the Peloponnese in Greece. It is the capital of the regional unit of Argolis. Nafplio was the capital of the First Hellenic Republic and of the Kingdom of Greece from 1821 until 1834, and is overlooked by the castle of Palamidi.

History

The area has been inhabited since prehistoric times; according to myth, the city was founded by Nauplius, son of Poseidon. Nafplio came under Venetian rule in the late 14th century and again in the late 17th century, when the Venetians built the Palamidi fortress (1711–1714). The Ottomans retook the city in 1715. During the Greek War of Independence, Nafplio was captured by Greek forces in 1822 and became the seat of the provisional government; Ioannis Kapodistrias, the first head of state of independent Greece, arrived there in 1828 and was assassinated in the city in 1831. King Otto made Nafplio his capital until the capital moved to Athens in 1834.

Landmarks

Principal sights include the Palamidi fortress, the island fortress of Bourtzi in the harbour, the Akronafplia walls, and the Archaeological Museum in Syntagma Square.
