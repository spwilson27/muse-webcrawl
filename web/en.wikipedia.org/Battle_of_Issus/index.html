The Battle of Issus was fought on 5 November 333 BC in south-eastern Anatolia between the Macedonian army of Alexander the Great and the Persian army of King Darius III. It was Alexander's second great victory over the Achaemenid Empire, following the Battle of the Granicus. Darius, having marched around behind Alexander and cut his supply lines, took position across the Pinarus River near the town of Issus. On the narrow coastal plain, where Persian numbers counted for little, Alexander's personally led cavalry charge broke the Persian left and drove at Darius himself, who fled the field; the Persian army collapsed.

Background

After defeating the Persian satraps at the Granicus in 334 BC, Alexander liberated the Greek cities of Asia Minor and advanced into Cilicia. Darius assembled a large royal army and marched from Babylon through Syria. In a bold move, the Persians crossed the Amanus mountains by a northern pass and descended on Issus, in Alexander's rear, massacring his sick and wounded left there. Alexander promptly turned back south to confront the king.

The battle

Darius drew up his army behind the Pinarus River, with Greek mercenary hoplites in the centre, Persian infantry (Cardaces) on either side, and cavalry massed on his right by the sea. Alexander placed Parmenion with the allied cavalry on the left along the coast, the phalanx in the centre, and himself with the Companion cavalry on the right against the foothills. After the phalanx struggled across the river against the Greek mercenaries in the centre, Alexander's cavalry smashed the Persian left and wheeled inward directly at Darius's chariot. Seeing his army collapsing around him and Alexander approaching, Darius fled, discarding his chariot, bow and royal mantle. The Persian right-wing cavalry, which had pressed Parmenion hard, withdrew on hearing of the flight. The pursuit continued until dark, and the Macedonians captured the Persian camp, including Darius's mother, wife and children, whom Alexander treated with conspicuous chivalry.

Aftermath

The victory opened Syria and Phoenicia to Alexander and gave him the Persian royal family's persons as leverage in later negotiations; Darius's subsequent offers of ransom, territory and marriage alliance were rejected. Alexander next reduced the Phoenician ports, culminating in the Siege of Tyre, and went on to Egypt before the final confrontation with Darius at the Battle of Gaugamela in 331 BC. The battle is immortalised in the Alexander Mosaic, a Roman copy of a Hellenistic painting, now in Naples.
