McLeod Ganj

McLeod Ganj is a suburb of Dharamshala in the Kangra district of Himachal Pradesh, India. Named after Sir Donald Friell McLeod, a lieutenant governor of Punjab under British rule, it is known as "Little Lhasa" because of its large Tibetan population.

History

A British hill station in the 19th century, McLeod Ganj became the residence of the 14th Dalai Lama after he fled Tibet following the 1959 uprising. The Tibetan government-in-exile is based in nearby Gangchen Kyishong, and the Tsuglagkhang Complex, with its temples and museum, is a major pilgrimage and tourist site.

Geography and culture

Perched in the Dhauladhar range at about 2,082 metres, the town is a centre for Tibetan Buddhism, handicrafts, and trekking, including the popular Triund trail.
