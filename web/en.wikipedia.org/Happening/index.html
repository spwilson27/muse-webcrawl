Happening

MediumPerformance art, theatre, visual art
PeriodLate 1950s–1970s
Key figureAllan Kaprow
Related movementsFluxus, performance art, environmental theatre

A happening is a performance, event, or situation art work, typically involving audience participation and elements of improvisation, that emerged in the late 1950s and flourished in the 1960s. The term was coined by the American artist Allan Kaprow, whose 1959 work 18 Happenings in 6 Parts, staged at the Reuben Gallery in New York, is usually credited as the first happening.

Characteristics

Happenings blurred the boundary between art and everyday life. They were often staged in lofts, courtyards, streets, or other non-theatrical spaces, followed loose scores or instructions rather than fixed scripts, and treated spectators as participants rather than observers. Props might be ordinary objects, sounds were often environmental, and chance played a central role — a debt to the composer John Cage, whose experimental classes at the New School had influenced Kaprow and many of his contemporaries.

Artists and legacy

Artists associated with happenings include Claes Oldenburg, Jim Dine, Robert Whitman, Carolee Schneemann, and Red Grooms in the United States, as well as the Fluxus network internationally. Happenings anticipated later developments in performance art, installation art, and immersive theatre, and the word itself entered popular usage during the 1960s as a general term for a spontaneous, fashionable event.
