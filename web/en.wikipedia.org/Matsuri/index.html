Matsuri

Japanese festivals

Matsuri (Japanese: 祭り) is the Japanese word for a festival or holiday, in origin a Shinto celebration honouring the kami (deities) of a shrine or marking a seasonal event. There are countless local matsuri across Japan — estimates run into the hundreds of thousands — most held in summer and autumn in connection with the rice cycle, though famous winter festivals exist as well.

Features

A typical matsuri centres on the home shrine: the kami is installed in a portable shrine (mikoshi) and processed through the neighbourhood, accompanied by music, dancing and floats (dashi). Around the sacred core grows a fairground of food stalls (yatai) selling takoyaki, yakisoba and kakigōri, games, and crowds in summer yukata. Celebrated examples include Kyoto's month-long Gion Matsuri (dating to 869), Osaka's Tenjin Matsuri with its river procession, Tokushima's Awa Odori dance festival, Aomori's Nebuta Matsuri with giant illuminated floats, and the Sapporo Snow Festival, a modern addition to the calendar.

Significance

Originally occasions to pray for harvests, health and protection from epidemics, matsuri remain anchor points of local identity and community organisation, requiring months of preparation by neighbourhood associations. Many have become major tourist attractions while retaining their religious core at the shrine itself.
