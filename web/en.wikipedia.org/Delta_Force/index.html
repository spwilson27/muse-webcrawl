Delta Force

Delta Force, formally the 1st Special Forces Operational Detachment–Delta (1st SFOD-D), is a special operations unit of the United States Army under the Joint Special Operations Command. It specialises in counterterrorism, hostage rescue, direct action and special reconnaissance.

History

The unit was founded in 1977 by Colonel Charles Beckwith, who modelled it on the British Special Air Service after serving with them in Malaya. Its first major mission, Operation Eagle Claw in 1980 — the attempted rescue of American hostages in Tehran — ended in disaster at the Desert One refuelling site and helped spur the creation of US Special Operations Command. Delta operators later fought in Panama, the Gulf War, Somalia (including the 1993 Battle of Mogadishu), the Balkans, Afghanistan and Iraq, and participated in operations against al-Qaeda and the Islamic State, including the 2019 raid that killed Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi.

Selection

Recruits, mostly experienced soldiers from the Ranger Regiment and Special Forces, undergo a gruelling selection course in the mountains of West Virginia followed by the six-month Operator Training Course at Fort Bragg (now Fort Liberty), North Carolina, covering marksmanship, demolitions, hostage rescue and tradecraft.
