Nerva (born Marcus Cocceius Nerva; 8 November 30 – 28 January 98) was Roman emperor from 96 to 98. Nerva assumed the throne after the assassination of Domitian, becoming the first of the so-called "Five Good Emperors" and founder of the Nerva–Antonine dynasty.

Early life and career

Nerva came from a distinguished senatorial family from Narni in Umbria, with ancestors among the jurists of the early Empire. He served as a trusted adviser under Nero (receiving triumphal honours for helping suppress the Pisonian conspiracy of 65) and held two consulships — under Vespasian in 71 and under Domitian in 90. Known as a mild, diplomatic elder statesman, he survived the reigns of several emperors, a remarkable feat for a high-profile senator.

Reign

Proclaimed emperor by the Senate on the day of Domitian's murder, Nerva sought to restore liberty and undo the terror of the previous regime: he released those accused of treason, recalled exiles, forbade new treason trials, and cut taxes and court expenditure. His lack of military standing, however, emboldened the Praetorian Guard, who in autumn 97 besieged the palace and forced him to surrender Domitian's assassins for execution. In response Nerva adopted the popular general Trajan as son and heir in October 97 — a move that stabilised the succession and began the adoptive tradition. He also launched the alimenta, a welfare scheme supporting poor children in Italy. Nerva died of natural causes in January 98 and was deified; Trajan succeeded him without disturbance.
