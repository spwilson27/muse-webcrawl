Kleinian group

Kleinian group

FieldComplex analysis / geometric group theory
Named afterFelix Klein

In mathematics, a Kleinian group is a discrete subgroup of the group of orientation-preserving isometries of three-dimensional hyperbolic space — equivalently, a discrete subgroup of PSL(2,ℂ) acting on the Riemann sphere by Möbius transformations. Named after Felix Klein, Kleinian groups generalise Fuchsian groups (discrete subgroups of PSL(2,ℝ)) from two dimensions to three and sit at the meeting point of complex dynamics, hyperbolic geometry and geometric group theory.

Limit sets and domains

A Kleinian group Γ divides the Riemann sphere into the limit set — the fractal set of accumulation points of orbits, where the action is chaotic — and the domain of discontinuity Ω, where the action is properly discontinuous and the quotient Ω/Γ is a Riemann surface. Famous examples include Schottky groups, whose limit sets are Cantor sets, and quasi-Fuchsian groups, whose limit sets are Jordan curves.

Significance

William Thurston’s work on hyperbolic 3-manifolds made Kleinian groups central to low-dimensional topology: most knots, links and 3-manifolds are governed by the geometry of Kleinian groups. The Ahlfors finiteness theorem, the ending lamination theorem (proved by Minsky, Brock, Canary and collaborators) and modern work on Cannon’s conjecture all concern the structure and deformation spaces of these groups.
