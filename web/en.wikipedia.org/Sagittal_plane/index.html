Sagittal plane

The sagittal plane divides the body into right and left portions.

The sagittal plane is an anatomical plane that divides the body into right and left sections. It is one of the three principal anatomical planes, alongside the coronal (frontal) and transverse (axial) planes. The term comes from the Latin sagitta, "arrow," recalling the sagittal suture of the skull, along which the plane passes.

Terminology

The midsagittal or median plane passes exactly through the midline, dividing the body into equal right and left halves. Any sagittal plane parallel to it, offset to one side, is a parasagittal plane. Movements occurring in the sagittal plane include flexion and extension, such as bending the knee or nodding the head. In medical imaging, sagittal views are produced by MRI and CT reconstructions and are standard in ultrasound and in lateral radiographs of the spine.

Applications

Beyond anatomy, the sagittal plane is used in biomechanics to analyze gait and lifting, in dentistry and orthodontics for cephalometric measurements, and in zoology — where, for bilaterally symmetric animals, it is the plane of symmetry. In quadrupeds it divides the body identically, though the orientation of the body axes differs from upright humans.
