Henryk Zygalski

Henryk Zygalski

Born: 15 July 1908, Posen (Poznań), German Empire

Died: 30 August 1978 (aged 70), Liss, Hampshire, England

Occupation: Mathematician, cryptologist

Known for: Zygalski sheets; breaking Enigma

Perforated (Zygalski) sheets, one of the Polish methods against Enigma

Henryk Zygalski (15 July 1908 – 30 August 1978) was a Polish mathematician and cryptologist who worked at breaking German Enigma ciphers before and during World War II at the Polish Cipher Bureau. With his Poznań University colleagues Marian Rejewski and Jerzy Różycki, he reconstructed the Enigma machine and developed methods to read its traffic.

Enigma work

Zygalski invented the perforated sheets (płachty Zygalskiego, "Zygalski sheets") in 1938: a set of large paper sheets, perforated according to the cycle characteristics of Enigma rotor permutations, which when stacked and moved over a light table revealed the daily rotor order and ring settings. The method exploited the doubled message key used by German operators. Sets of sheets were prepared for all six possible rotor orderings, though the German addition of two further rotors in December 1938 made the full requirement far larger.

Later life

After the fall of France in 1940 Zygalski worked at the "Cadix" centre in Vichy France and passed through Spain to Britain in 1943, but, like the other Polish cryptologists, was kept from the work at Bletchley Park after the Poles had shared their knowledge with the Allies in July 1939. He remained in England after the war, teaching mathematics at the Polish University Abroad and then at Battersea Polytechnic (later the University of Surrey). In 2000 he was posthumously awarded the Grand Cross of the Order of Polonia Restituta along with Rejewski and Różycki.
