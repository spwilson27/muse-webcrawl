François Achille Bazaine (13 February 1811 – 28 September 1888) was a French general and Marshal of France who served in Algeria, the Crimea, Italy, and Mexico. He is best known for surrendering the fortress city of Metz, with some 140,000–180,000 men, to Prussian forces during the Franco-Prussian War in October 1870.

Career

Rising from the ranks, Bazaine distinguished himself in Algeria and at Sevastopol, and commanded French expeditionary forces in Mexico, where he was made Marshal in 1864. In 1870 he took command of the Army of the Rhine.

Metz and trial

After defeats at Mars-la-Tour and Gravelotte, Bazaine withdrew into Metz and, after a siege, surrendered on 27 October 1870 — the largest capitulation of the war. In 1873 a court-martial convicted him of treason and sentenced him to death; the sentence was commuted to 20 years' imprisonment, from which he escaped in 1874 and lived in exile in Spain.
