Planetary phase

A planetary phase is a term used in astronomy to describe the illuminated portion of a planet as seen from a given vantage point, in analogy with the phases of the Moon. The effect is most striking for the inferior planets Mercury and Venus, which show a full cycle of phases as they orbit the Sun inside Earth's orbit.

Description

When an inferior planet is on the far side of the Sun it appears nearly fully illuminated but small; as it moves around its orbit it shows gibbous, half, crescent and finally new phases, growing in apparent size as it approaches Earth. Galileo Galilei's observations of the phases of Venus in 1610 were important evidence for the heliocentric model of the Solar System.

Superior planets

Planets beyond Earth's orbit are always nearly fully illuminated as seen from Earth, showing at most a slight gibbous phase near quadrature, as with Mars.
