Socialism with Chinese characteristics

Socialism with Chinese characteristics (Chinese: 中国特色社会主义) is the official ideology of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) and the People's Republic of China, holding that China is in the primary stage of socialism and that its policies must be adapted to Chinese conditions. The term was introduced by Deng Xiaoping in 1982 and underpins the era of "reform and opening up".

Deng Xiaoping Theory

Deng argued that China had to develop its productive forces before a mature socialism was possible, famously insisting it did not matter whether a cat was black or white so long as it caught mice. His program legitimated markets, private enterprise, and foreign investment within a system formally committed to socialism and led by the CCP — often summarized internationally as a "socialist market economy".

Later formulations

Successive leaderships appended doctrinal components: Jiang Zemin's "Three Represents", Hu Jintao's "Scientific Outlook on Development", and since 2012 Xi Jinping Thought on Socialism with Chinese Characteristics for a New Era, written into the party constitution in 2017. The ideology is officially presented as an application of Marxism–Leninism and Mao Zedong Thought to China's national conditions.

Interpretations and debate

Supporters credit the model with lifting hundreds of millions from poverty while preserving stability. Critics, including some Western scholars and Chinese dissidents, argue that China's economy is better described as state capitalism, and that the phrase serves mainly to legitimize one-party rule while accommodating market mechanisms.
