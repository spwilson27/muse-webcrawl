Self-portrait

A self-portrait is a portrait of an artist made by that artist. Self-portraits occur in painting, drawing, printmaking, sculpture and photography, and range from straightforward records of appearance to complex statements of identity and artistic ambition.

History

Although earlier examples exist, self-portraiture flourished in the Renaissance as artists' social status rose and mirrors improved. Albrecht Dürer made landmark self-portraits, including a painted self-portrait at age twenty-eight in 1500. Rembrandt painted, etched and drew himself throughout his life — over eighty works — creating an unparalleled visual autobiography. In modern art, artists such as Vincent van Gogh, Frida Kahlo and Cindy Sherman used self-portraiture to explore psychology and persona.

Self-portrait photography

Photographers adopted the genre from the medium's beginnings; Robert Cornelius made an early photographic self-portrait in 1839. In the twenty-first century the smartphone "selfie" made informal self-portraiture a mass everyday practice, raising new questions about self-presentation that artists continue to examine.
