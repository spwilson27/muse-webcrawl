Oghuz languages

The Oghuz languages are a sub-branch of the Turkic language family spoken by approximately 108 million native speakers, making them the most widely spoken group of Turkic languages. They descend from the language of the Oghuz Turks, who migrated westward from Central Asia beginning in the early medieval period. The principal modern Oghuz languages are Turkish, Azerbaijani, Turkmen, and Gagauz, together with regional varieties such as Qashqai, Khorasani Turkic, and Salar.

Classification and features

Oghuz languages share features such as the loss of initial h-, voicing of certain initial consonants, and distinctive verb morphology. They form a dialect continuum across Anatolia, the Caucasus, Iran, and Turkmenistan; western varieties (Turkish, Azerbaijani, Gagauz) and eastern varieties (Turkmen, Khorasani) differ in vowel systems and vocabulary borrowed from Persian, Arabic, and Russian.

History

The Oghuz confederation appeared on the steppes north of the Aral Sea by the eighth century. Under the Seljuks, Oghuz tribes carried their language into Persia and Anatolia in the eleventh century, laying the foundation for the Ottoman Turkish literary tradition and, later, the modern Turkish Republic's language reforms.
