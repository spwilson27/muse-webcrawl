Ejection fraction

Echocardiography is the most common method of measuring ejection fraction

Ejection fraction (EF) is the volumetric fraction of fluid (usually blood) ejected from a chamber (usually the heart) with each contraction (or heartbeat). It is most commonly applied to the left ventricle of the heart, where it is a key measure of systolic function.

Definition and measurement

Left ventricular ejection fraction is calculated as the stroke volume divided by the end-diastolic volume: EF = (EDV − ESV) / EDV, where ESV is the end-systolic volume. It is typically measured by echocardiography, and can also be assessed by cardiac magnetic resonance imaging, computed tomography, or ventriculography.

Clinical significance

A normal left ventricular ejection fraction is generally considered to be about 50–70%. A reduced ejection fraction (below about 40%) defines heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) and may result from myocardial infarction, dilated cardiomyopathy, or valvular disease. Heart failure can also occur with a preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF), in which diastolic dysfunction predominates. Ejection fraction guides treatment decisions, including eligibility for certain drugs and implantable cardioverter-defibrillators.
