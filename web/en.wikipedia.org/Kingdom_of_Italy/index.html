The Kingdom of Italy (Italian: Regno d'Italia) was a state that existed from 17 March 1861, when Victor Emmanuel II of Sardinia was proclaimed King of Italy, until 2 June 1946, when a referendum abolished the monarchy and civil discontent led King Umberto II into exile. The kingdom was the first Italian state in more than a millennium to unite the whole Italian peninsula, completing the process known as the Risorgimento.

Unification

Under Prime Minister Camillo Cavour, the Kingdom of Sardinia allied with France to defeat Austria in 1859, while Giuseppe Garibaldi's Expedition of the Thousand conquered Sicily and Naples in 1860. Venetia was annexed in 1866 and Rome in 1870, when it became the capital, leaving only the "unredeemed" territories of Trento and Trieste outside the kingdom.

Liberal era and empire

The liberal governments of the late 19th and early 20th centuries industrialised the north, extended the franchise, and built a colonial empire in Eritrea, Somalia, Libya, and the Dodecanese. Italy joined the Allies in the First World War in 1915 and gained Trentino, South Tyrol, and Istria, though nationalist disappointment at the "mutilated victory" fed postwar unrest.

Fascism and fall

In 1922 Benito Mussolini became prime minister and built a fascist dictatorship. His regime conquered Ethiopia in 1935–36, allied with Nazi Germany, and entered the Second World War in 1940. After the Allied invasion of Sicily, Victor Emmanuel III dismissed Mussolini in July 1943; the armistice of September 1943 split Italy between the Allied-backed Kingdom of the South and the German-backed Italian Social Republic. The monarchy, discredited by its association with fascism, lost the 1946 institutional referendum, and the Italian Republic was proclaimed.
