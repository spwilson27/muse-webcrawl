Sweatshop

A sweatshop is a factory or workshop, especially in the garment industry, where manual workers are employed at very low wages for long hours under poor and often hazardous conditions.

History

The term arose in the 19th-century clothing trades of Britain and the United States, where subcontracting systems squeezed piece rates. Disaster and scandal — from the Triangle Shirtwaist Factory fire of 1911 to the Rana Plaza collapse in Bangladesh in 2013, which killed more than 1,100 workers — have repeatedly spurred labour reform.

Debate

Anti-sweatshop movements press brands to monitor supply chains and pay living wages. Some economists counter that factory jobs, however harsh, may pay more than available alternatives, and argue for improving conditions rather than boycotts that eliminate jobs.
