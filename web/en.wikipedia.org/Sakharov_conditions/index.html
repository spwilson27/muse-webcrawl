Sakharov conditions

The Sakharov conditions, proposed by physicist Andrei Sakharov in 1967, are the three necessary conditions that a process must satisfy to generate a baryon asymmetry — the observed imbalance of matter over antimatter in the universe — through baryogenesis.

The three conditions

First, baryon number violation: interactions must be able to change the net baryon number. Second, C and CP violation: charge-conjugation and combined charge-parity symmetries must be broken, so that processes producing baryons are favoured over those producing antibaryons. Third, departure from thermal equilibrium: the reactions must occur out of equilibrium, otherwise CPT symmetry would drive the asymmetry back to zero.

Significance

The Standard Model contains all three ingredients to some degree, but its CP violation and its electroweak phase transition appear too weak to explain the observed asymmetry, motivating theories of baryogenesis and leptogenesis beyond the Standard Model.
