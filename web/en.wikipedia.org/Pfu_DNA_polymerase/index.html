Pfu DNA polymerase

Source organismPyrococcus furiosus
TypeFamily B DNA polymerase
Size775 amino acids; ~90 kDa
Properties3′→5′ exonuclease proofreading; thermostable; blunt-end products
Error rate~1.3 × 10−6 errors per base pair duplicated

Pfu DNA polymerase is a thermostable DNA polymerase isolated from the hyperthermophilic archaeon Pyrococcus furiosus. Because it possesses 3′→5′ exonuclease proofreading activity, it replicates DNA with much higher fidelity than Taq polymerase, making it a standard enzyme for PCR applications where accuracy is critical, such as cloning and site-directed mutagenesis.

Properties

P. furiosus grows optimally near 100 °C, so its polymerase readily withstands the repeated heating cycles of PCR. Pfu polymerase extends primers at roughly 1,000 nucleotides per minute at 72 °C — slower than Taq — and produces blunt-ended amplification products rather than the single 3′ adenine overhangs generated by Taq, which affects cloning strategy (blunt-end ligation or addition of A-overhangs for TA cloning).

Fidelity

The proofreading exonuclease removes misincorporated nucleotides during synthesis. The measured error rate of Pfu is around 1.3 × 10−6 per base pair per duplication, several-fold lower than Taq's approximately 8 × 10−6 to 2 × 10−5 under comparable conditions. Pfu can stall at uracil bases in the template ("uracil stalling"), a read-ahead function characteristic of archaeal family B polymerases; engineered variants such as PfuUltra and fusion polymerases address this and improve speed and processivity.

Applications

Pfu and its engineered derivatives are used in high-fidelity PCR, amplification of templates for sequencing and expression cloning, blunt-end PCR cloning, and whole-plasmid amplification for site-directed mutagenesis kits. Its higher cost and slower extension relative to Taq mean Taq remains preferred for routine, error-tolerant screening PCR.
