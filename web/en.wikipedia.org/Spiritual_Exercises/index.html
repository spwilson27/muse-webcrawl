Spiritual Exercises

The Spiritual Exercises (Exercitia spiritualia), composed 1522–1524 by the Spanish priest Saint Ignatius of Loyola, are a set of Christian meditations, contemplations and prayers originally designed to be carried out over about thirty days of retreat in silence. They form the foundational spiritual practice of the Society of Jesus (the Jesuits).

Structure

The Exercises are divided into four "weeks" of unequal length. The first week meditates on sin and God's mercy; the second contemplates the life of Christ up to Palm Sunday; the third focuses on the Passion; and the fourth on the Resurrection and God's love. Key elements include the daily examination of conscience (the Examen), imaginative contemplation of Gospel scenes, and the famous Principle and Foundation, which frames the goal of human life as praising and serving God.

Purpose

Ignatius intended the Exercises not as a book to be read but as a manual for a director guiding a retreatant, helping the exercitant discern God's will and make a free decision about the direction of their life — the "election". The retreat culminates in the Contemplation to Attain Love.

Influence

Approved by Pope Paul III in 1548, the Spiritual Exercises have influenced Catholic spirituality for nearly five centuries and are now widely practised by Christians of many traditions, often in adapted formats such as the "retreat in daily life" (the 19th Annotation).
