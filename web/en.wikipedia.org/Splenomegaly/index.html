Splenomegaly

Splenomegaly is an enlargement of the spleen. The spleen usually lies in the left upper quadrant (LUQ) of the human abdomen. Splenomegaly is one of the four cardinal signs of hypersplenism, together with some reduction in circulating blood cells.

Causes

Causes are grouped into infections (such as infectious mononucleosis and malaria), liver diseases causing portal hypertension (such as cirrhosis), hematologic malignancies (lymphomas and leukemias), hemolytic anemias, infiltrative disorders (such as amyloidosis and Gaucher disease), and autoimmune conditions. Massive splenomegaly — spleen weight over 1,000 g — is typically associated with chronic myeloid leukemia, myelofibrosis, malaria, or visceral leishmaniasis.

Diagnosis

An enlarged spleen may be felt on abdominal palpation, though normally the spleen is not palpable in adults. Ultrasound and CT imaging confirm size and help identify the cause; further workup includes blood tests and sometimes bone marrow biopsy.

Treatment

Treatment addresses the underlying cause. Splenectomy may be required if the enlargement causes severe cytopenias, pain, or risk of rupture; patients with splenomegaly are advised to avoid contact sports.
