The 332nd Fighter Group was a United States Army Air Forces unit composed primarily of African American pilots and support personnel, famously known as the Tuskegee Airmen or "Red Tails" for the distinctive crimson paint applied to their aircraft tails. It was the first unit of Black American military aviators in United States history.

Formation and training

The group was activated on 13 October 1942 at Tuskegee Army Air Field, Alabama, where Black pilots trained under a segregated program championed despite widespread institutional resistance. Its squadrons — the 99th, 100th, 301st and 302nd Fighter Squadrons — trained on the P-40 Warhawk and later aircraft.

Combat operations

Deployed to Italy in 1944, the group flew the P-47 Thunderbolt and then the P-51 Mustang on bomber escort missions over southern and central Europe with the Fifteenth Air Force. The Red Tails built a strong reputation for disciplined escort work, losing notably few bombers to enemy fighters compared with other escort groups. Pilots of the 332nd were credited with destroying more than 100 enemy aircraft in aerial combat and many more on the ground, and the group earned Distinguished Unit Citations.

Legacy

The success of the Tuskegee Airmen helped pave the way for the desegregation of the United States armed forces in 1948. The modern 332nd Air Expeditionary Wing carries the lineage of the group.
