Frito pie

Frito pie is a dish popular in the Midwestern, Southeastern, and Southwestern United States, whose basic ingredients are chili, cheese, and corn chips — especially Fritos. Variations include jalapeños, salsa, onions, sour cream, and refried beans. The dish can be served in a bowl or, famously, directly in a single-serving Fritos bag split down the side, a serving style sometimes called a "walking taco".

History

The origins of Frito pie are disputed between Texas and New Mexico. One account credits Daisy Doolin, mother of Fritos founder Charles Elmer Doolin, with an early recipe; the Frito-Lay company promoted chili-and-chips recipes in the decades after Fritos were introduced in 1932. In New Mexico, the pie is strongly associated with the Five & Dime store on the Santa Fe Plaza (later a Woolworth's), where it was served from the 1960s with a distinctive red-chile-based chili.

Regional versions

In Texas the dish is a staple at fairs, football concessions, and chili parlors. Southwestern versions may use New Mexican red or green chile sauce. Related dishes include the "walking taco" of the Midwest, made with Doritos or Fritos, and the Tostilocos of Tijuana.
