The South Shetland Islands are a group of Antarctic islands lying about 120 kilometres (75 mi) north of the Antarctic Peninsula, separated from it by the Bransfield Strait and from South America by the Drake Passage. The archipelago comprises eleven principal islands and numerous smaller ones, extending roughly 450 kilometres from southwest to northeast.

History

The islands were sighted in 1819 by the English merchant captain William Smith aboard the brig Williams, and sealers arrived soon afterwards, hunting the abundant fur seals nearly to extinction within a few seasons. Fabian Gottlieb von Bellingshausen visited in 1821. In the twentieth century the islands hosted whaling stations, most notably at Whalers Bay on Deception Island.

Research stations

Today the islands have no permanent population but host numerous scientific research stations, especially on King George Island, which is served by the Teniente Rodolfo Marsh Martin Airport operated by Chile. Stations are maintained by countries including Argentina, Brazil, Bulgaria, Chile, China, Ecuador, Peru, Poland, Russia, South Korea, Spain, Ukraine, the United States and Uruguay.

Wildlife

The maritime Antarctic climate supports mosses, lichens and two native flowering plants, along with breeding colonies of chinstrap, gentoo and macaroni penguins, southern giant petrels, and Antarctic fur seals and elephant seals along the shores.
