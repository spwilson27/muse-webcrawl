The Vistula Lagoon (Polish: Zalew Wiślany; Russian: Калининградский залив, Kaliningradskiy zaliv; German: Frisches Haff) is a brackish water lagoon on the Baltic Sea coast of Poland and Russia's Kaliningrad Oblast. It is separated from Gdańsk Bay by the Vistula Spit. The lagoon is a mouth of a few branches of the Vistula river — mainly the Nogat — and of the Pregolya river.

Geography

The lagoon is about 90 km long, 10 to 19 km wide, and up to 5.2 m deep, with a surface area of roughly 838 km². Its only natural outlet to the sea is the Strait of Baltiysk in the Russian section. Ports on the lagoon include Elbląg, Tolkmicko, Frombork and Krynica Morska in Poland and Kaliningrad in Russia, the latter reached by the Kaliningrad Sea Canal along the northern shore.

Vistula Spit canal

To give Polish ports direct access to the Baltic without passing through Russian waters, Poland constructed a shipping canal through the Vistula Spit near Nowy Świat. The canal, about 1.3 km long with locks and breakwaters, was officially opened on 17 September 2022.

History

Until 1945 the lagoon lay entirely within Germany (East Prussia); the post-war border settlement divided it between Poland and the Soviet Union, a division that persists today between Poland and Russia. In the winter of 1945 the frozen lagoon was an evacuation route for German refugees fleeing the Red Army.
