Maurice Halbwachs

Maurice Halbwachs (11 March 1877 – 16 March 1945) was a French philosopher and sociologist known for developing the concept of collective memory. A student and collaborator of Émile Durkheim, he argued that individual memory is framed and sustained by the social groups to which one belongs. His major work, Les cadres sociaux de la mémoire (The Social Frameworks of Memory, 1925), founded the modern study of social memory.

Work

Halbwachs taught at the universities of Strasbourg and Paris and held a chair at the Collège de France. Beyond memory, he wrote on suicide (following Durkheim), social morphology, urban life in Chicago, and the working-class standard of living. His posthumous La mémoire collective (1950) became influential in memory studies, history, and anthropology from the 1980s onward.

Death

Arrested by the Gestapo in 1944 after protesting the murder of his Jewish in-laws, Halbwachs was deported to Buchenwald, where he died of dysentery in March 1945.
