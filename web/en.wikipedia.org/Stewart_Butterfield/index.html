Stewart Butterfield

Daniel Stewart Butterfield (born 21 March 1973) is a Canadian entrepreneur and businessman, best known as a co-founder of Flickr and of Slack.

Daniel Stewart Butterfield (born 21 March 1973) is a Canadian entrepreneur who co-founded the photo-sharing website Flickr and the workplace messaging platform Slack. Both products grew out of tools built for online games that failed commercially.

Early life and education

Butterfield was born in Lund, British Columbia. He studied philosophy at the University of Victoria and earned a master's degree in philosophy from the University of Cambridge. He worked as a web designer in the late 1990s before moving into start-ups.

Ludicorp and Flickr

In 2002 Butterfield co-founded Ludicorp with Caterina Fake and Jason Classon to build Game Neverending, an online game. When the game was shelved in 2004, the team repurposed its photo-sharing tool as Flickr, which rapidly became the leading photo-sharing site on the web. Yahoo acquired Flickr in 2005, and Butterfield left Yahoo in 2008.

Slack

Butterfield's next company, Tiny Speck, built the whimsical game Glitch, which shut down in 2012. Its internal chat tool became Slack, launched in 2013. Slack grew into one of the most widely used business communication platforms, went public in 2019, and was acquired by Salesforce in 2021 for about $27.7 billion. Butterfield served as Slack's CEO until 2023.
