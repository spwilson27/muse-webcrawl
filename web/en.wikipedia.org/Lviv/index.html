Lviv

Lviv's historic centre, a UNESCO World Heritage Site

Country: Ukraine
Oblast: Lviv Oblast
Founded: mid-13th century
Population: ~717,000 (2022)

Lviv (Ukrainian: Львів) is the largest city in western Ukraine and the sixth-largest city in the country, with a population of about 717,000. Founded in the mid-13th century by Daniel of Galicia and named for his son Lev, Lviv was the capital of the Kingdom of Galicia–Volhynia and grew rich as a trading centre on routes between the Baltic and Black Seas.

History

Over its history, Lviv has belonged to Poland (as Lwów), the Austrian and Austro-Hungarian Empires (as Lemberg), the short-lived West Ukrainian People's Republic, interwar Poland again, the Soviet Union (as Lvov), and — since 1991 — independent Ukraine. Each regime left its mark on the city's architecture and institutions. The Second World War and the Holocaust destroyed Lviv's once-large Jewish community, and post-war population transfers removed most of its Polish inhabitants.

Culture and landmarks

The historic city centre, with its Renaissance, Baroque, and Secessionist architecture, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Landmarks include Rynok Square with its 65-metre town hall tower, the Lviv Theatre of Opera and Ballet, the Armenian Cathedral, the Dominican Church, and Lychakiv Cemetery. The city hosts Ivan Franko National University (founded 1661) and Lviv Polytechnic, and is known for its coffeehouse culture and festivals.

Modern era

Since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, Lviv — close to the Polish border — has served as a hub for displaced people, diplomacy, and humanitarian logistics, while itself suffering periodic missile and drone strikes.
