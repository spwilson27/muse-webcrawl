Ammonium chloride

FormulaNH4Cl
Molar mass53.49 g/mol
AppearanceWhite crystalline solid
Solubility in waterHighly soluble

Ammonium chloride is an inorganic compound with the formula NH4Cl and a white crystalline salt that is highly soluble in water. Solutions of ammonium chloride are mildly acidic. In its naturally occurring mineralogical form it is known as sal ammoniac. The mineral forms on burning coal dumps from condensation of coal-derived gases, and is also found around some volcanic vents.

Production and uses

Ammonium chloride is a product of the Solvay process used to produce sodium carbonate. Its principal applications are as a nitrogen source in fertilizers (for example, as an ingredient of chloroammonium phosphate) and as a flux in preparing metals to be tin-coated, galvanized, or soldered. It is also used as an expectorant in cough syrups, as an electrolyte in zinc–carbon batteries, as a food additive (E510) — notably to give salty liquorice its distinctive taste in Nordic countries — and as a component of many proprietary cold medicines.

History

The earliest mention of sal ammoniac dates to the geographer Strabo's description of a salt found near the temple of Jupiter Ammon in Libya, from which it took its name. In Asia it was historically obtained from the soot of burned dung and traded as far as China.
