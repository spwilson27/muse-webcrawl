IAU designated constellations

Number: 88
Adopted: 1922 (list); 1928 (boundaries)
Body: International Astronomical Union

The IAU designated constellations are the 88 modern constellations recognised by the International Astronomical Union. In 1922 the IAU adopted the standard list with three-letter abbreviations, and in 1928 it approved precise boundaries, drawn along lines of right ascension and declination by the Belgian astronomer Eugène Delporte, so that every point of the celestial sphere belongs to exactly one constellation.

History

Forty-eight constellations descend from the catalogue of Ptolemy's Almagest (2nd century AD). Others were added by Dutch navigators Pieter Dirkszoon Keyser and Frederick de Houtman in the southern sky, by Johannes Hevelius, and by Nicolas-Louis de Lacaille, who subdivided the huge Argo Navis into Carina, Puppis, and Vela and added fourteen southern figures.

Characteristics

Constellations are now regions of sky, not merely star patterns; traditional asterisms such as the Big Dipper lie within them. The largest is Hydra, the smallest Crux. Names are Latin, with a standard genitive form used in star designations (e.g., Alpha Centauri). Zodiacal constellations number thirteen on the ecliptic when Ophiuchus is counted.
