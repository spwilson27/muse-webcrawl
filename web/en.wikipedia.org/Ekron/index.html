Ekron

Ekron (Hebrew: עֶקְרוֹן, ʿEkrôn) was one of the five cities of the famed Philistine pentapolis, located in southwestern Canaan, near the border with the Kingdom of Judah. While now identified conclusively with Tel Miqne (Khirbet el-Muqanna), near the modern Israeli village of Mazkeret Batya, 35 kilometres west of Jerusalem, its location was once uncertain.

History

The site was inhabited from the Chalcolithic period onward, but rose to prominence in the Iron Age when the Philistines — a "Sea People" — settled there around 1200 BC. According to the Hebrew Bible, Ekron was allotted to the tribe of Dan and later to Judah, and it was the city to which the Philistines sent the ark of the Covenant, where its god Baal-zebub ("Lord of the Flies," from which Beelzebub derives) was consulted by King Ahaziah.

Assyrian and Babylonian periods

Under Assyrian suzerainty in the 7th century BC, Ekron flourished as a major centre of olive oil production; its industrial quarter contained more than 100 oil installations, making it one of the largest industrial centres of the ancient world. The city was destroyed by Nebuchadnezzar II in 604 BC during the Babylonian campaign in Philistia.

Ekron inscription

Excavations between 1981 and 1996 uncovered the Ekron Royal Dedicatory Inscription, a rare Phoenician-script text recording the dedication of a temple by Ikausu (Achish), son of Padi, confirming the city's identity and its line of kings known from Assyrian records.
