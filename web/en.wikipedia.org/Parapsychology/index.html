Parapsychology

Parapsychology is the study of alleged psychic phenomena — extrasensory perception (ESP), telepathy, clairvoyance, precognition, psychokinesis — and other paranormal claims, for example those related to near-death experiences, apparitions, and reincarnation. It is identified by mainstream science as a pseudoscience, as the field's findings have not been replicated reliably and its reported effects are generally tiny, inconsistent, or attributable to methodological flaws.

History

The field grew out of the nineteenth-century psychical research movement and the Society for Psychical Research (1882). In the 1930s, Joseph Banks Rhine at Duke University popularised the term "parapsychology" and attempted to bring the subject into the laboratory, using Zener cards to test ESP and dice-rolling to test psychokinesis. Later research programmes included the Ganzfeld experiments on telepathy, remote-viewing research funded by the United States government (the Stargate Project, terminated in 1995), and Daryl Bem's controversial 2011 precognition experiments, whose failure to replicate became an important episode in psychology's replication crisis.

Scientific status

The Parapsychological Association, founded in 1957, was admitted to affiliation with the American Association for the Advancement of Science in 1969. However, surveys of scientists show that a large majority regard parapsychology as unscientific, citing the absence of a plausible mechanism, the failure of effects to strengthen with better controls, publication bias, and episodes of fraud. Parapsychologists respond that their methods meet ordinary scientific standards and that the field is judged by a double standard.
