Totila

Totila
Reign: 541–552
Predecessor: Eraric
Died: 1 July 552, Taginae
Kingdom: Ostrogothic Kingdom of Italy

Totila, original name Baduila (died 1 July 552), was king of the Ostrogoths from 541 to 552. A skilled commander and energetic ruler, he came close to reversing the Byzantine reconquest of Italy during the Gothic War, retaking most of the peninsula from the empire of Justinian I before his final defeat.

Reign

Totila was elected king after the brief reign of Eraric, when the Gothic cause seemed lost following the capture of Ravenna and King Witiges in 540. Rallying the Goths, he defeated Byzantine forces in a string of campaigns — aided by his striking of propaganda coinage, leniency toward peasants and slaves, and systematic dismantling of city walls to deny the enemy strongholds. He captured Naples in 543 and Rome itself in 546, briefly emptying the city of its inhabitants, and again in 550.

Death

In 552 the Byzantine general Narses invaded Italy with a large army. At the Battle of Taginae (Busta Gallorum) in Umbria, Totila's cavalry charge was destroyed by Narses's dismounted archers; Totila was mortally wounded and died shortly afterwards. His successor Teia was killed within the year, and organised Gothic resistance in Italy collapsed soon after.
