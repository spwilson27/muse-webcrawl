The Ligurian Sea (Italian: Mar Ligure) is an arm of the Mediterranean Sea lying between the Italian Riviera (Liguria) and Tuscany to the north and east, and the French island of Corsica and the French Riviera to the west and south. The International Hydrographic Organization defines its limits from Cape Ferrat near Nice, around Corsica's northern capes, to the Tuscan archipelago and the Italian coast near Piombino.

Geography

The sea is bordered by the Maritime Alps and the Apennines, which plunge steeply into deep water close offshore; depths reach about 2,850 metres northwest of Corsica. The coast includes the famed resorts of the Italian Riviera — Portofino, the Cinque Terre and Sanremo — and the major commercial ports of Genoa, one of the Mediterranean's busiest, La Spezia (a naval base) and Livorno.

Conservation

The Ligurian Sea forms the core of the Pelagos Sanctuary for Mediterranean Marine Mammals, a large marine protected area established by France, Italy and Monaco notable for its population of fin whales, sperm whales, and several dolphin species. The waters off Portofino and the Cinque Terre are also protected as marine reserves.
