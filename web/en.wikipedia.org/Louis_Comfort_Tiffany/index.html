Louis Comfort Tiffany

Louis Comfort Tiffany (February 18, 1848 – January 17, 1933) was an American artist and designer, son of Charles Lewis Tiffany, founder of Tiffany & Co. He is celebrated for his work in stained glass — windows, lamps and mosaics — and was a leading figure of the Art Nouveau and Aesthetic movements in the United States.

Tiffany developed Favrile glass, an iridescent art glass patented in 1894, and his studios produced the celebrated leaded-glass lamps (including dragonfly and wisteria designs, many designed by Clara Driscoll and the "Tiffany Girls"). His firm decorated interiors including rooms in the White House under President Chester A. Arthur, and he built the Laurelton Hall estate on Long Island to house his collections.
