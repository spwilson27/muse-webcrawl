Tailplane

A tailplane, also known as a horizontal stabilizer, is a small lifting surface located on the tail (empennage) of an aircraft. It provides longitudinal stability and, through attached or integrated elevators, control of pitch.

Function

The tailplane generates a downward or upward force that balances the pitching moment of the wing, keeping the aircraft stable about its lateral axis. Elevators deflect to raise or lower the nose. Many airliners use an all-moving, trimmable stabilizer to adjust for changes in centre of gravity and speed.

Configurations

Most tailplanes mount low on the fuselage or on the vertical fin in a T-tail arrangement. Some aircraft use a canard foreplane instead of, or in addition to, a tailplane. The tailplane may include trim tabs and de-icing systems on its leading edge.
