LabVIEW

LabVIEW (Laboratory Virtual Instrument Engineering Workbench) is a graphical programming environment developed by National Instruments (now part of Emerson) and first released in 1986. Programs, called virtual instruments (VIs), are drawn as block diagrams in the "G" language, where dataflow between nodes determines execution.

Features

Each VI has a front panel (user interface) and a block diagram (code). LabVIEW integrates tightly with data-acquisition and instrument-control hardware — via standards such as GPIB, serial, and NI's own DAQ devices — making it widely used in test, measurement and laboratory automation.

Editions

LabVIEW runs on Windows, macOS and Linux, and specialised modules target real-time and FPGA hardware (CompactRIO). A free Community edition for non-commercial use was introduced in 2020, and the successor environment LabVIEW NXG was discontinued in favour of the classic product line.
