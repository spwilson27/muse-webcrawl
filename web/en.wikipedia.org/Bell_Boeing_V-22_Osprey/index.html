The Bell Boeing V-22 Osprey is an American multi-mission tiltrotor aircraft with vertical takeoff and landing (VTOL) and short takeoff and landing (STOL) capability. Its rotating engine nacelles let it take off like a helicopter and then fly like a turboprop aeroplane, combining helicopter versatility with the speed and range of a fixed-wing transport.

Development

The programme began in the 1980s after the failure of the Iran hostage rescue mission highlighted the need for a long-range vertical-lift aircraft. Development was long and controversial, with several fatal crashes during testing, but the MV-22 entered Marine Corps service in 2007 and has since deployed to Iraq, Afghanistan and elsewhere. The CMV-22B variant serves the US Navy as a carrier onboard delivery aircraft.

Design

Two Rolls-Royce T406 turboshafts drive three-bladed proprotors on tilting wingtip nacelles. The Osprey carries 24 troops or about 9,000 kg of internal cargo at roughly twice the speed of conventional helicopters.
