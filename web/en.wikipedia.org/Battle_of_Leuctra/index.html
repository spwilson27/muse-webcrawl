Battle of Leuctra

The Battle of Leuctra (Greek: Λεῦκτρα) was fought on 6 July 371 BC between the Boeotians, led by the Thebans, and the Spartans, along with their respective allies, near the village of Leuctra in Boeotia. The Theban victory, engineered by the general Epaminondas, shattered the myth of Spartan invincibility and destroyed Sparta's dominance over Greece, ushering in the brief period of Theban hegemony.

Background

After the Corinthian War and the King's Peace of 387 BC, Sparta continued to act as Greece's hegemon, but its high-handed treatment of Thebes—including the seizure of the Cadmea citadel in 382 BC—bred resentment. When negotiations at a peace conference in Sparta collapsed in 371 BC over Thebes' claim to sign for all Boeotia, King Cleombrotus I marched into Boeotia with some 10,000 hoplites.

The battle

Epaminondas deployed his army in a revolutionary formation. Tradition placed the best troops on the right wing in a line twelve shields deep, but Epaminondas massed his left wing fifty shields deep, strengthened by the elite Sacred Band under Pelopidas, and held back his weaker right in an oblique, echeloned advance. The overwhelming Theban left crushed the Spartan right, where the king and the full Spartan citizens (Spartiates) stood. Cleombrotus was killed along with some 400 of the 700 Spartiates present—a manpower loss Sparta, with its shrinking citizen body, could never replace.

Aftermath

Leuctra ended Spartan hegemony permanently. Epaminondas followed up by invading the Peloponnese and liberating Messenia, whose helot labour had underpinned the Spartan economy for centuries. The battle is a landmark in military history for its deliberate concentration of force at the decisive point and its oblique order of battle, a concept later revived by Frederick the Great.
