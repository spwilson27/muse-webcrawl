Laurentia

Laurentia is the ancient geological core — craton — of North America, made of Archaean and early Proterozoic rocks, centred on the Canadian Shield around Hudson Bay and extending under much of Canada, Greenland and the northern United States. Named for the Saint Lawrence River region, it is also called the North American craton.

Formation

Laurentia assembled between about 2.0 and 1.8 billion years ago in the Trans-Hudson and related orogenies, welding older provinces (Superior, Hearne, Rae, Slave, Wyoming) together. It later formed the core of the supercontinents Columbia, Rodinia and Pangaea, and grew by accretion along its margins, including the Grenville orogeny to the southeast about 1 billion years ago.

Significance

Much of the continent's mineral wealth and its oldest rocks — including the ~4-billion-year-old Acasta Gneiss — lie within Laurentia, which has remained tectonically stable for over a billion years.
