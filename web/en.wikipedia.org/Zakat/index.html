Zakat

Zakāt (Arabic: زكاة; "that which purifies") is a form of almsgiving, often collected by the Muslim community, and one of the Five Pillars of Islam. It is based on accumulated wealth and is obligatory for all Muslims who meet the necessary criteria of wealth, known as the nisab. Zakat is considered a religious obligation, and its payment is intended to purify the giver's income and wealth and to assist the poor and needy.

The standard rate is 2.5% of a Muslim's total savings and wealth above the nisab threshold, calculated over the course of a lunar year, though rates differ for agricultural produce, livestock and extracted minerals.

Quranic basis and recipients

The Quran names categories of zakat recipients in Surah At-Tawba (9:60): the poor, the needy, those employed to administer the funds, those whose hearts are to be reconciled, those in bondage, those in debt, those striving in the cause of God, and the stranded traveller. Zakat is distinct from voluntary charity, known as sadaqah.

Practice

In most Muslim-majority countries, zakat payment is voluntary and privately arranged, while in a few states it is collected or regulated by the government. Zakat is often paid during Ramadan. Modern estimates of global zakat flows run to hundreds of billions of dollars annually, making it a significant mechanism of redistribution and charitable finance.
