The Sonoran Desert is a hot desert and ecoregion covering large parts of the southwestern United States (Arizona and southeastern California) and northwestern Mexico (Sonora and the Baja California peninsula). Unusually lush for a desert, it is considered the most biologically diverse desert in North America—sometimes called the "green desert"—thanks to its two rainy seasons.

Climate and subdivisions

The desert receives rain both in winter, from Pacific frontal systems, and in summer, from the North American monsoon, allowing a long growing season. Botanists divide it into subdivisions including the Lower Colorado River Valley (the hottest, driest core), the Arizona Upland (the only part where the giant saguaro is common), the Plains of Sonora, and the Baja California regions.

Flora and fauna

Signature plants include the saguaro (Carnegiea gigantea), organ pipe and senita cacti, palo verde trees, ocotillo, creosote bush, and the boojum tree of Baja California. Animal life includes the Gila monster, desert tortoise, kangaroo rats, javelinas, roadrunners, and cactus wrens. The desert's Indigenous peoples, including the Tohono O'odham and Hohokam, developed sophisticated dryland farming and irrigation.

Conservation

Protected areas include Saguaro National Park, Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument, and Mexico's El Pinacate y Gran Desierto de Altar Biosphere Reserve. Pressures include urban growth around Phoenix and Tucson, groundwater pumping, invasive buffelgrass, and border infrastructure.
