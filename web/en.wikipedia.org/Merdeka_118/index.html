Merdeka 118

Merdeka 118, also known as Menara Warisan Merdeka and formerly PNB 118, is a 118-storey megatall skyscraper in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia. At 678.9 metres (2,227 ft) including its spire, it is the second-tallest structure in the world after Dubai's Burj Khalifa, and the tallest building in Southeast Asia. The name commemorates Malaysian independence (Merdeka) and the neighbouring Stadium Merdeka, where independence was proclaimed in 1957. It should not be confused with the KL Tower, the city's 421-metre telecommunications tower.

Design and construction

The tower was designed by Melbourne-based Fender Katsalidis; its faceted, diamond-like glass façade is said to be inspired by traditional Malay songket patterns and by the outstretched arm of Tunku Abdul Rahman proclaiming independence. Construction began in 2014; the spire was completed in late 2021, making the building taller than Shanghai Tower, and the tower was completed in 2023. The project is the centrepiece of a mixed-use precinct including a mall, residences and a mosque, adjacent to Stadium Merdeka and Stadium Negara.

Uses

The tower contains offices — PNB's headquarters occupies much of it — a Park Hyatt hotel in the upper levels, and an observation deck ("The View at 118") near the top, expected to be among the highest in the world. Anchor tenants announced include the hotel and various corporate offices.

Criticism

The project drew criticism over its cost (over RM 5 billion), its necessity amid a glut of Kuala Lumpur office space, and the use of public-linked investment funds (PNB) to finance it.
