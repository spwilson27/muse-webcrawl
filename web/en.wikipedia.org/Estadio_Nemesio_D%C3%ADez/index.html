Estadio Nemesio Díez Riega, nicknamed La Bombonera, is a football stadium in Toluca, Mexico, and the home ground of Deportivo Toluca. It is named after Nemesio Díez, the Spanish-Mexican businessman and former owner of the club.

History

The stadium was inaugurated on 8 August 1954 as Estadio Héctor Barraza, later renamed Estadio Luis Gutiérrez Dosal, and eventually for Nemesio Díez following his death. It was a venue for the FIFA World Cup in both 1970 and 1986, the only stadium outside Mexico City to host matches at both tournaments; its high altitude of 2,667 metres makes it one of the highest World Cup venues ever used.

Remodelling

Between 2015 and 2017 the stadium underwent a major reconstruction for Toluca's centenary year, expanding capacity to about 31,000, adding a roof and video screens in an English-style design with stands close to the pitch, while remaining on the same historic site. The rebuilt stadium was officially reopened in 2017.

Atmosphere

La Bombonera is known for its compact, vertical design and passionate crowds. A characteristic sight is the "El Matador" mural and the displays of the supporters' groups. Because of Toluca's sustained success — including ten league titles won largely in this era — the ground has a reputation as one of the most difficult away fixtures in Mexican football.
