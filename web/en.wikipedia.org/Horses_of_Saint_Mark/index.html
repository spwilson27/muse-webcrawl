Horses of Saint Mark

The Horses of Saint Mark are a set of four bronze statues of horses that form part of a quadriga monument. Of ancient origin — variously attributed to classical Greek or Roman sculptors — they adorned the Hippodrome of Constantinople before being looted by Venetian forces in the Fourth Crusade (1204) and installed on the loggia of St Mark's Basilica in Venice.

History

The horses, with their chariot, stood atop the Hippodrome in Constantinople for centuries. After the sack of the city in 1204, Doge Enrico Dandolo had them shipped to Venice, where they were placed above the central portal of St Mark's — trophies of the republic's new empire. Napoleon removed them to Paris in 1797 to crown the Arc de Triomphe du Carrousel; they were returned to Venice in 1815.

Conservation

Because of air pollution, the originals were taken inside in the 1980s and replaced on the façade by copies. Metallurgical analysis shows the statues are about 96–98% copper, cast by the lost-wax method, and were once gilded — explaining medieval descriptions of them as golden.
