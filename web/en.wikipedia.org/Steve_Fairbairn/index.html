Stephen Fairbairn (1862–1938) was an Australian rower and rowing coach whose methods transformed the sport in England. Educated at Cambridge, he rowed in the Boat Race and won events at Henley Royal Regatta.

Coaching philosophy

Fairbairn rejected the rigid "orthodox" style of his era, teaching instead that rowing should be natural and relaxed, emphasizing leg drive, rhythm and blade work over fixed body positions — an approach dubbed "Fairbairnism." He coached Jesus College, Cambridge, and Thames Rowing Club to repeated successes and influenced coaches worldwide. His aphorism "Mileage makes champions" remains famous, and he founded the Head of the River Race in 1925 on the model of bumps racing.

Legacy

Cambridge's Fairbairn Cup is named in his honour, and his writings, including Rowing Notes, are classics of coaching literature.
