Edible bird's nest

Edible bird's nests are the nests of several species of swiftlet, made almost entirely from solidified saliva, which are harvested for human consumption, most famously as the principal ingredient of bird's nest soup in Chinese cuisine. The nests are among the most expensive animal products sold by weight and have been prized in Chinese culture for centuries for their flavour and reputed health benefits.

The birds and their nests

The most valuable nests come from the edible-nest swiftlet and the black-nest swiftlet, which breed in colonies in dark caves and, increasingly, in purpose-built "swiftlet houses" in Southeast Asia. The male bird builds the half-cup nest against a vertical surface over about 35 days from strands of its own saliva, which harden on exposure to air.

Harvest and trade

Traditional cave harvesting, as in the Gomantong and Niah caves of Borneo, can be dangerous and is licensed to limit over-exploitation. Nests are graded by colour: rare reddish "blood nests" fetch the highest prices, followed by white nests. Indonesia is the world's largest producer. Concerns over sustainability, adulteration, and nitrite contamination have led to import regulations in several markets.

Culinary use

When soaked and simmered, the nest develops a gelatinous texture. It is usually served as a sweet dessert soup with rock sugar, or in savoury double-boiled preparations with chicken or ham. The flavour is subtle; the appeal lies mainly in texture and prestige.
