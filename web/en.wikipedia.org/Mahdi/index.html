Mahdi

The Mahdi (Arabic: ٱلْمَهْدِيّ, "the Guided One") is a prophesied redeemer in Islam who is expected to appear before the Day of Judgment to rid the world of evil and injustice and to restore true religion. Belief in the Mahdi is an important element of Islamic eschatology in both Sunni and Shia traditions, though the traditions differ in important respects.

In Shia Islam

In Twelver Shia Islam, the Mahdi is identified with Muhammad al-Mahdi, the twelfth Imam, who is believed to have entered occultation (ghayba) in the 9th century and to remain alive, hidden, until he reappears at the end of time. His return (zuhur) is awaited with devotion, and Friday prayers often include supplications for his reappearance. In Ismaili and other Shia branches, doctrines differ.

In Sunni Islam

Sunni tradition, drawing on hadith literature, expects a Mahdi from the Prophet Muhammad's family (often described as a descendant named Muhammad ibn Abdullah) who will rule for a period of years, fill the earth with justice, and precede or accompany the return of Jesus (Isa).

Claimants

Throughout history, various figures have claimed to be the Mahdi or have been proclaimed as such, including Ibn Tumart, founder of the Almohad movement; Muhammad Ahmad of Sudan, whose Mahdist state resisted Anglo-Egyptian rule in the 1880s; and Mirza Ghulam Ahmad, founder of the Ahmadiyya movement (whose claim is rejected by most other Muslims).
