Chenda Melam

Chenda Melam is a traditional percussion ensemble from Kerala in south India, centred on the chenda, a cylindrical wooden drum played with sticks. It accompanies temple festivals, processions and classical performing arts such as Kathakali and Theyyam.

Instrumentation

An ensemble typically combines chenda drums with ilathalam (hand cymbals), kuzhal (a double-reed wind instrument), kurumkuzhal and kombu (a long curved trumpet). Performances build from slow, measured passages to fast, dense climaxes.

Forms

Varieties of melam include the Panchari Melam, performed in temple festivals such as Thrissur Pooram with more than a hundred musicians, and the Pandi Melam, performed outside temple precincts. Tayambaka is a solo chenda form.

Cultural role

Chenda Melam is a centrepiece of Kerala's festival culture, accompanying caparisoned elephant processions and drawing large crowds; leading performers are celebrated masters of rhythm.
