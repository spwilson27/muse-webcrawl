New Line Cinema is an American film production studio founded in 1967 by Robert Shaye. Initially an independent distributor and producer known for low-budget genre films, it grew into a major studio, famously described as "the house that Freddy built" after the success of A Nightmare on Elm Street (1984).

History

New Line achieved major success in the 1990s with films such as Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles, Dumb and Dumber and The Mask. It was acquired by Turner Broadcasting in 1994 and became part of Time Warner in 1996. The studio's greatest triumph was Peter Jackson's The Lord of the Rings trilogy (2001–2003), followed by The Hobbit trilogy.

Later years

In 2008 New Line was folded into Warner Bros. Pictures as a production unit, retaining its name on genre releases. In later years it has been associated with the Conjuring horror franchise and films such as It (2017) and Shazam! (2019).
