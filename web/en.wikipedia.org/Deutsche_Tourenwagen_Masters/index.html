The Deutsche Tourenwagen Masters, commonly known as the DTM, is a sports car racing series based in Germany and organised by the ADAC. Founded in 1984 as the Deutsche Tourenwagen Meisterschaft, the original series ran until 1996, when spiralling costs ended its international offshoot, the ITC. The championship was relaunched in 2000 under the "Masters" name with silhouette prototype touring cars from Audi, Mercedes-Benz, Opel and later BMW. From 2021, the DTM switched to GT3-based cars, opening the series to many more manufacturers and privateer teams.

History

Original series (1984–1996)

The original DTM featured production-based Group A touring cars and grew into a high-technology contest between Alfa Romeo, Audi, BMW, Ford, Mercedes-Benz and Opel. Star drivers included Klaus Ludwig, Bernd Schneider and Nicola Larini. In 1995 the series split into a domestic championship and the FIA International Touring Car Championship (ITC); both folded after 1996 as costs soared.

Relaunch (2000–2020)

The new DTM debuted in 2000 with two-door silhouette coupés from Audi, Mercedes-Benz and Opel (Opel withdrew after 2005). Bernd Schneider won the first relaunched title, and went on to take five championships in all. The series developed into one of the world's leading touring car championships, with mandatory pit stops, DRS-like push-to-pass systems and races at circuits such as Hockenheimring, Norisring, Nürburgring, Zandvoort and Brands Hatch. BMW rejoined in 2012 and won the manufacturers' title at its first attempt. Audi withdrew at the end of 2020, ending the "Class One" prototype era.

GT3 era (2021–present)

From 2021 the DTM adopted GT3 regulations, attracting entries from Audi, BMW, Ferrari, Lamborghini, Mercedes-AMG, Porsche and others, with many independent teams. After the promoter ITR wound down, the ADAC took over the series for 2023. Champions of the GT3 era include Maximilian Götz (2021), Sheldon van der Linde (2022), Thomas Preining (2023) and Mirko Bortolotti (2024).

Format

A DTM weekend typically features two sprint races of about 55 minutes plus one lap, one on Saturday and one on Sunday, each preceded by its own qualifying session and including a mandatory pit stop for tyres. Points are awarded to the top finishers on the Formula One-style scale, with additional points for qualifying.
