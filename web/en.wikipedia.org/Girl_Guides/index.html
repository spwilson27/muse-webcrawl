Girl Guides (known as Girl Scouts in the United States and some other countries) is a worldwide movement for girls and young women, founded in the United Kingdom in 1910 by Robert Baden-Powell and his sister Agnes Baden-Powell. It developed from the Scouting movement Baden-Powell had founded for boys, after groups of girls began appearing at Scout rallies asking to join.

History

At the Crystal Palace Scout rally of 1909, Baden-Powell encountered patrols of girls calling themselves Girl Scouts. Concluding that a separate organisation was needed, he asked his sister Agnes to organise the Girl Guides, and in 1910 the first companies were registered. His wife, Olave Baden-Powell, later became the movement's world leader as Chief Guide. The movement spread rapidly through the British Empire and beyond; in the United States, Juliette Gordon Low founded the Girl Scouts of the USA in 1912.

Programme

Guiding aims to develop character, practical skills, and community service through activities such as camping, crafts, first aid, and international exchange. Members make a Promise and follow a set of Laws, progress through age sections — such as Rainbows, Brownies, Guides, and Rangers in the UK — and earn interest badges. Guiding and Scouting organisations in many countries are now co-educational, while others remain single-sex; national associations coordinate globally through the World Association of Girl Guides and Girl Scouts (WAGGGS), founded in 1928.
