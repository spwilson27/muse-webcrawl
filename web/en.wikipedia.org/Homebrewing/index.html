Homebrewing

Homebrewing is the brewing of beer and other alcoholic beverages on a small scale for personal, non-commercial purposes. Supplies, such as kits and fermentation tanks, can be purchased from specialty stores or online. Alcoholic beverages have been made at home since their origin, long before their industrial production.

Homebrewing was largely illegal or restricted in many countries during the 20th century. In the United States, homebrewing of beer was federally legalized in 1978 when President Jimmy Carter signed H.R. 1337, allowing households to produce up to 100 US gallons (about 380 litres) per adult per year, up to 200 gallons per household, without federal tax. States retained the right to regulate it; Mississippi and Alabama were the last states to legalize homebrewing, in 2013.

Process

Beer homebrewing typically follows the same basic steps as commercial brewing: preparing a sugary wort from malt (or using malt extract), boiling it with hops, cooling, fermenting with yeast in a fermenter fitted with an airlock, and then packaging in bottles or kegs, often with a priming sugar to carbonate the beer in the bottle. Sanitation is emphasized in homebrewing literature, since contamination by wild yeasts or bacteria can spoil a batch. Common batch sizes are 5 US gallons (19 litres).

Culture

The American Homebrewers Association, founded in 1978 by Charlie Papazian, promotes the hobby; Papazian's book The Complete Joy of Home Brewing introduced many to the pastime, along with his motto "Relax. Don't worry. Have a homebrew." Homebrew clubs and competitions are common, and many professional craft brewers began as homebrewers.
