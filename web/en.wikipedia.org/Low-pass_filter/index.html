Low-pass filter

Low-pass filter
Filter that passes signals below a cutoff frequency and attenuates signals above it.

A low-pass filter is a filter that passes signals with a frequency lower than a selected cutoff frequency and attenuates signals with frequencies higher than the cutoff. The exact frequency response depends on the filter design; the simplest form is an electronic RC circuit whose output is taken across the capacitor.

Characteristics

For a first-order RC filter, the cutoff is fc = 1/(2πRC), above which the gain falls at −20 dB per decade. Higher-order designs — Butterworth, Chebyshev, Bessel and elliptic — trade flatness of the passband against steepness of roll-off and phase behaviour. Digital low-pass filters, such as moving-average and FIR/IIR filters, perform the same function in sampled systems.

Applications

Low-pass filters are used as anti-aliasing filters before analogue-to-digital conversion, to smooth audio and remove hiss, in radio receivers, power-supply smoothing, image blurring, and sensor signal conditioning. In synthesisers, voltage-controlled low-pass filters (such as the Moog ladder filter) shape timbre.
