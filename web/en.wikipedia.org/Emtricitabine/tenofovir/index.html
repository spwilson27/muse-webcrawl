Emtricitabine/tenofovir

Emtricitabine/tenofovir, sold under the brand name Truvada among others, is a fixed-dose combination antiretroviral medication used to treat and prevent HIV/AIDS. It combines two nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors, emtricitabine and tenofovir disoproxil fumarate, in a single tablet taken by mouth.

Type:Combination drug
Components:Emtricitabine; Tenofovir disoproxil
Routes:By mouth

Medical uses

The combination is used with other antiretrovirals to treat HIV infection, and it is also approved for pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP) to reduce the risk of acquiring HIV in people at high risk; when taken consistently, it reduces the risk of sexual transmission by over 90 per cent. It is also active against hepatitis B virus, and abrupt discontinuation in co-infected patients can provoke a flare of hepatitis.

History

Truvada was developed by Gilead Sciences and approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration in 2004 for treatment and in 2012 for prevention — the first drug approved for PrEP. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines, and inexpensive generic versions are widely available.

Side effects

Common side effects include headache, nausea, and fatigue. More significant concerns include kidney dysfunction and reduced bone mineral density associated with tenofovir, which led to the development of a related formulation using tenofovir alafenamide.
