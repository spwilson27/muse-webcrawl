The Hospital

The Hospital
Directed by: Arthur Hiller
Written by: Paddy Chayefsky
Starring: George C. Scott, Diana Rigg
Release date: December 14, 1971
Running time: 103 minutes

The Hospital is a 1971 American black comedy satirical film directed by Arthur Hiller and written by Paddy Chayefsky, who won the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay for his script. The film stars George C. Scott as Dr. Herbert Bock, the suicidal chief of medicine at a Manhattan teaching hospital beset by mishaps, protests, and a series of mysterious deaths.

Plot

Over a chaotic few days, Dr. Bock — impotent, divorced, and contemplating suicide — struggles to run a hospital descending into farce: patients die from administrative mix-ups, staff stage protests, and a killer stalks the wards. Bock falls in love with Barbara Drummond (Diana Rigg), the daughter of a patient, who offers him a way out of his despair. The film's dark conclusion leaves Bock choosing to stay at the hospital out of necessity.

Production and themes

Chayefsky, who retained unusual creative control including casting approval, wrote the film as a furious satire of modern institutional medicine and bureaucracy. The screenplay's mordant dialogue and Scott's performance were widely praised, and the film is seen as a precursor to Chayefsky's later script for Network (1976).

Reception and awards

The film was a critical and commercial success. Chayefsky won the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay, and Scott was nominated for Best Actor. The Hospital was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry in 1995 and is consistently ranked among the funniest American screenplays by the Writers Guild of America.
