Self-defence

Self-defence is a countermeasure that involves defending the health and well-being of oneself from harm. It includes physical techniques for resisting an attack and, in law, the right to use reasonable force to protect oneself or others from imminent violence.

Physical self-defence

Physical self-defence training typically draws on martial arts and fighting systems such as boxing, judo, Brazilian jiu-jitsu and Krav Maga. Instruction often emphasizes awareness, avoidance and de-escalation first, then striking, grappling or escaping when confrontation is unavoidable. Some jurisdictions regulate defensive tools such as pepper spray and conducted energy weapons.

Legal doctrine

Most legal systems recognise a right of self-defence, generally requiring that the threat be imminent and the response proportionate, and many impose a duty to retreat where it is safe to do so; "stand your ground" and "castle doctrine" rules in parts of the United States modify those requirements. In international law, Article 51 of the United Nations Charter preserves the inherent right of individual or collective self-defence if an armed attack occurs against a member state.
