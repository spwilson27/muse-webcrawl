Swarming (honey bee)

Swarming is the process by which a honey bee colony reproduces: the old queen leaves the hive with roughly half the worker bees to establish a new nest, while a new queen takes over the original colony.

Process

Before departure, workers raise queen cells and the queen is slimmed for flight. The swarm pours out, clusters temporarily on a branch or other surface while scout bees search for a suitable cavity, then flies to the chosen site. Swarming typically occurs in spring when the colony is populous and forage abundant.

Beekeeping

Beekeepers manage swarming by providing space, splitting colonies and replacing ageing queens, since a swarm loses much of the season's honey-producing workforce. Captured swarms are a traditional way to stock new hives.
