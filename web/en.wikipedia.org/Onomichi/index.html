Onomichi

Japanese: 尾道市

Prefecture: Hiroshima

Region: Chūgoku (San'yō)

Area: approx. 285 km²

Population: approx. 130,000

Onomichi (尾道市, Onomichi-shi) is a city located in Hiroshima Prefecture, Japan, on the coast of the Seto Inland Sea. The city is known for its many temples, its steep hillside lanes crowded with old houses and roaming cats, its association with Japanese literature and cinema, and as one terminus of the Shimanami Kaidō cycling route across the islands of the Inland Sea to Shikoku.

Geography and history

Onomichi developed as a port from the twelfth century, when it served as a rice-shipping point for the estates of the region. During the Edo period it flourished as a trading harbour for merchants of the Inland Sea, and wealthy merchants endowed many of the temples that still line the hills. The historic centre climbs a narrow strip between the sea and steep slopes; paved lanes wind past Senkō-ji and other temples, with views over the Onomichi Channel and the shipyards and islands beyond. The city expanded through mergers with surrounding towns, including Innoshima and Setoda in 2006, bringing several offshore islands within the city limits.

Culture

Onomichi is celebrated as a town of literature and film. The novelist Fumiko Hayashi is commemorated there, and director Yasujirō Ozu set much of his 1953 film Tokyo Story in the city; Nobuhiko Obayashi, born in Onomichi, filmed his "Onomichi trilogy" there. The Cat Alley (Neko no Hosomichi) lane, decorated with painted cat stones, is a popular attraction. Onomichi ramen, a soy-sauce-based noodle soup topped with pork back fat, is the city's signature dish.

Transport

The Shimanami Kaidō — a series of bridges linking Honshu to Shikoku via islands such as Ikuchijima and Ōmishima — begins at Onomichi and is one of Japan's most famous cycling routes. The city is served by the San'yō Main Line and the San'yō Shinkansen at Shin-Onomichi Station, along with ferries to nearby islands.
