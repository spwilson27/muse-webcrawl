Number needed to harm

The number needed to harm (NNH) is an epidemiological measure indicating how many people, on average, need to be exposed to a risk factor or treatment for one additional person to experience a harmful outcome. It is the reciprocal of the absolute risk increase.

Calculation

If the incidence of an outcome is higher in the exposed group than in the control group, NNH = 1 / (incidence in exposed − incidence in controls). A smaller NNH indicates a greater risk. NNH is the counterpart of the number needed to treat (NNT), which measures benefit; clinicians compare the two to weigh a treatment's risks against its benefits.

Use

NNH is used in evidence-based medicine and drug-safety reporting to communicate side-effect risks in an intuitive form alongside relative risks and odds ratios.
