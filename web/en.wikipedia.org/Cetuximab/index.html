Cetuximab

Trade nameErbitux
TypeMonoclonal antibody (chimeric IgG1)
TargetEpidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)
RoutesIntravenous infusion
ApprovedFDA 2004

Cetuximab, sold under the brand name Erbitux, is a chimeric (mouse/human) monoclonal antibody used in the treatment of certain cancers. It binds to the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), blocking the binding of epidermal growth factor and other ligands, which inhibits tumor cell growth and survival signaling.

Medical uses

Cetuximab is used in the treatment of metastatic colorectal cancer and cancers of the head and neck, including squamous cell carcinoma of the head and neck, where it may be combined with chemotherapy or radiation therapy. Its benefit in colorectal cancer is restricted to patients whose tumors express wild-type KRAS, because mutations in KRAS render the tumor independent of EGFR signaling.

Mechanism of action

EGFR is a receptor tyrosine kinase overexpressed in many epithelial tumors. By binding the extracellular domain of EGFR, cetuximab prevents ligand-induced receptor activation and downstream signaling through pathways such as RAS–RAF–MEK–ERK and PI3K–AKT. The antibody's Fc region can also recruit immune effector cells, producing antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity.

Side effects

Common adverse effects include an acne-like skin rash, which correlates with treatment response, infusion reactions, hypomagnesemia, fatigue, and diarrhea. Severe infusion reactions, occasionally fatal, occur in a small percentage of patients. A notable regional pattern of severe allergic reactions in the southeastern United States has been linked to pre-existing IgE antibodies against galactose-alpha-1,3-galactose (alpha-gal).

History

Cetuximab was developed by ImClone Systems and approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration in 2004 for colorectal cancer, initially in combination with irinotecan. It later gained approvals for head and neck cancer and, with KRAS testing, became an early example of biomarker-guided cancer therapy.
