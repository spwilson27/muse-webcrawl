The American Federation of Labor and Congress of Industrial Organizations (AFL–CIO) is the largest federation of unions in the United States, made up of dozens of national and international unions representing millions of workers.

History

The federation was created in 1955 by the merger of the American Federation of Labor (AFL) and the Congress of Industrial Organizations (CIO), ending a two-decade split in the American labor movement. George Meany became its first president, serving until 1979.

Role

The AFL–CIO coordinates political action, lobbying, and organizing support for its affiliates, and represents U.S. labor internationally through bodies such as the International Trade Union Confederation.
