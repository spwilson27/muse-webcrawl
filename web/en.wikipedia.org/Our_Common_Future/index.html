Our Common Future, commonly known as the Brundtland Report, was published in 1987 by the United Nations through Oxford University Press. It was the product of the World Commission on Environment and Development, convened in 1983 and chaired by former Norwegian prime minister Gro Harlem Brundtland.

Sustainable development

The report is credited with popularizing and defining the concept of sustainable development: "development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs." It argued that poverty and environmental degradation are linked, that economic growth must change in quality, and that the environment and development cannot be treated as separate challenges. The report addressed population, food security, energy, industry, and the management of the global commons, including the oceans, outer space, and Antarctica.

Legacy

Our Common Future helped set the agenda for the 1992 Earth Summit in Rio de Janeiro (UNCED), which produced Agenda 21 and the framework conventions on climate change and biodiversity. The Brundtland definition of sustainable development remains the most widely cited formulation and a reference point for subsequent frameworks, including the UN Sustainable Development Goals of 2015.
