Operation Zarb-e-Azb (Urdu: ضربِ عضب, "strike of the sword") was a joint military offensive conducted by the Pakistan Armed Forces against armed militant groups in North Waziristan, in the Federally Administered Tribal Areas along the Afghan border. Launched on 15 June 2014, it was the largest operation of Pakistan's war against the Pakistani Taliban and marked a shift to targeting all militant factions in the area without distinction.

Background

North Waziristan had become a hub for the Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan (TTP), al-Qaeda, the East Turkestan Islamic Movement, the Islamic Movement of Uzbekistan, and the Haqqani network. Peace talks between the government of Nawaz Sharif and the TTP collapsed in early 2014 amid continued attacks; the immediate trigger for the offensive was the TTP's assault on Karachi's Jinnah International Airport on 8–9 June 2014, which killed dozens.

The offensive

The operation began with air strikes on militant hideouts, followed by a ground offensive by some 30,000 troops advancing on the towns of Miranshah and Mir Ali. The army reported destroying bomb-making factories, ammunition dumps, and command centres, and said thousands of militants were killed over the course of the operation, though independent verification was difficult because the area was closed to journalists. Operations later extended into the Shawal Valley and, in 2017, into the Rajgal and Khyber areas under the follow-up Operation Radd-ul-Fasaad.

Displacement and aftermath

Around a million residents were displaced from North Waziristan and housed in camps, principally around Bannu, before a phased repatriation began in 2015. The operation coincided with a marked decline in terrorist violence across Pakistan; the December 2014 massacre of schoolchildren in Peshawar by the TTP, which killed some 149 people, was carried out as militants were being squeezed from their sanctuaries. Critics noted that many militant leaders escaped into Afghanistan, contributing to cross-border tensions.
