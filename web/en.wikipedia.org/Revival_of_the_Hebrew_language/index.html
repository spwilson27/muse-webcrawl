Revival of the Hebrew language

The revival of the Hebrew language took place in Europe and the Levant and then Israel during the end of the 19th century and into the 20th century, in which the language of Hebrew changed from a mainly liturgical and literary language of Judaism to a spoken and written language used for daily life. It is the only truly successful example of a dead language being revived into a living vernacular with millions of native speakers.

For centuries Hebrew had been preserved in prayer, scripture and scholarship, while Jews spoke vernaculars such as Yiddish, Ladino and Arabic. The Haskalah (Jewish Enlightenment) expanded Hebrew literature, but the transformation into a spoken language is credited above all to Eliezer Ben-Yehuda (1858–1922), who settled in Jerusalem in 1881, insisted on speaking Hebrew in his home, and compiled a monumental dictionary, coining words for modern concepts from Semitic roots.

Eliezer Ben-Yehuda, the central figure of the Hebrew revival.

Process of revival

The revival advanced through three connected channels: Hebrew-medium schools in the agricultural settlements of the First and Second Aliyah, where a generation of children acquired Hebrew as a first language; a Hebrew press and literature; and standardisation bodies, beginning with the Hebrew Language Committee (1890) and continuing with today's Academy of the Hebrew Language. Ideological commitment in the Yishuv — including campaigns against Yiddish and other diaspora languages — made Hebrew the language of the street by the 1920s, and it became an official language of Mandatory Palestine in 1922.

Modern Hebrew

The result, Modern Hebrew, draws its vocabulary from Biblical, Mishnaic and medieval Hebrew, reanalysed through the grammar and phonology its revivalists brought from European and Middle Eastern languages. Linguists debate how much it constitutes a direct continuation of ancient Hebrew versus a hybrid "Israeli" language; in either case, Hebrew today is the everyday language of millions and the official language of Israel.
