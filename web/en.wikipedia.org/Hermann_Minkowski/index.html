Hermann Minkowski (1864–1909)

Hermann Minkowski (22 June 1864 – 12 January 1909) was a German mathematician and professor at the universities of Königsberg, Zürich and Göttingen. He created and developed the geometry of numbers and used geometrical methods to solve problems in number theory, mathematical physics, and the theory of relativity. He is best known for his foundational work on spacetime: in 1907 he showed that Albert Einstein's special theory of relativity could be understood geometrically as a theory of a four-dimensional space, now called Minkowski space or Minkowski spacetime.

Life

Minkowski was born in Aleksotas (then in the Russian Empire, now in Lithuania) to German-Jewish parents and grew up in Königsberg. At eighteen he shared the Paris Academy's Grand Prix for an essay on representing numbers as sums of five squares. He studied under David Hilbert, whom he befriended for life, and taught Einstein at the Zürich Polytechnic, reportedly without being especially impressed by him as a student. Minkowski died suddenly of a ruptured appendix in Göttingen in 1909, aged 44.

Work

His Geometrie der Zahlen (1896) founded the geometry of numbers, treating number-theoretic questions through convex bodies and lattices; Minkowski's theorem—that any convex symmetric set of volume greater than 2n in n-dimensional space contains a non-zero lattice point—remains a cornerstone of the field.

In his 1908 Cologne address "Space and Time" (Raum und Zeit), Minkowski declared: "Henceforth space by itself, and time by itself, are doomed to fade away into mere shadows, and only a kind of union of the two will preserve an independent reality." His four-dimensional formulation, including the invariant spacetime interval and the light cone, initially struck Einstein as superfluous mathematical adornment, but became indispensable to Einstein's later development of general relativity and to all subsequent relativistic physics.
