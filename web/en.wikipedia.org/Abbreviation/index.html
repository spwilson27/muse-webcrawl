Abbreviation

An abbreviation (from Latin brevis, meaning "short") is a shortened form of a word or phrase, by any method. It may consist of a group of letters or words taken from the full version. For example, the word abbreviation can itself be represented by the abbreviation abbr., abbrv., or abbrev.

Types

Common kinds include acronyms (formed from initial letters and pronounced as words, such as NATO), initialisms (pronounced letter by letter, such as FBI), contractions (such as don't), and truncations (such as exam for examination). Abbreviations are widely used in technical writing, addresses, titles (Mr., Dr.), and measurements (km, lb).

History and usage

Scribal abbreviations are ancient, appearing in Latin manuscripts and inscriptions, and expanded with printing and telegraphy. Style guides differ on punctuation and capitalization of abbreviations, and usage varies between British and American English.
