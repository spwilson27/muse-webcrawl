Nectar

Nectar is a viscous, sugar-rich liquid produced by plants in glands called nectaries, either within flowers (floral nectaries) to attract pollinating animals or on leaves and stems (extrafloral nectaries) to attract insects that defend the plant.

Composition and role

Nectar consists mostly of water and the sugars sucrose, glucose and fructose, with small amounts of amino acids, lipids, alkaloids and other compounds. Animals such as bees, hummingbirds, bats, butterflies and ants consume nectar as an energy source; in return, pollinators transfer pollen between flowers. Bees convert nectar into honey by evaporation and enzymatic action. Extrafloral nectar feeds ants and parasitoid wasps that attack herbivores, an indirect plant defence.

In mythology

In Greek mythology, nectar was the drink of the gods, paired with ambrosia as their food, conferring immortality.
