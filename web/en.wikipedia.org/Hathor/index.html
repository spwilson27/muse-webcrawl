Hathor was a major goddess in ancient Egyptian religion who embodied motherhood, love, beauty, sexuality, music, dancing, joy and fertility. She was commonly depicted as a cow, a woman wearing a headdress of cow horns and a sun disk, or a woman with cow's ears. Her principal cult centre was Dendera in Upper Egypt, where a great temple to her still stands.

Roles

Hathor was a sky goddess and the mother or consort of the sky god Horus and the sun god Ra; her name means "House of Horus". She welcomed the dead into the afterlife, nourished the living as a cow goddess who suckled the king, and protected miners in turquoise and copper regions such as Sinai, where she was called Lady of Turquoise. As a goddess of music she was associated with the sistrum, a rattle shaken in temple ritual.

Mythology

In one famous myth, the Destruction of Mankind, Ra sent Hathor in her fierce form as the lioness Sekhmet to punish rebellious humans; she was only stopped when beer dyed red was poured out and she drank it and fell asleep. Hathor also appears in the myth of the Distant Goddess, fleeing to Nubia and being persuaded to return to Egypt.

Worship

Worship of Hathor dates from at least the Old Kingdom and remained important for over three thousand years. Her temple at Dendera, with its Hathor-headed columns, is one of the best-preserved temple complexes in Egypt.
