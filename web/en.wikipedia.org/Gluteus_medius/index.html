Gluteus medius

The gluteus medius is one of the three gluteal muscles of the buttock, lying on the outer surface of the pelvis between the gluteus maximus and the gluteus minimus. It is a broad, fan-shaped muscle that is a principal abductor of the hip and an essential stabiliser of the pelvis during walking and running.

Gluteus medius
OriginOuter surface of the ilium
InsertionGreater trochanter of the femur
NerveSuperior gluteal nerve (L4–S1)
ActionsAbduction and rotation of the hip; pelvic stabilisation

Structure

The gluteus medius arises from the external surface of the ilium between the anterior and posterior gluteal lines. Its fibres converge into a tendon that inserts on the lateral surface of the greater trochanter of the femur. The muscle is supplied by the superior gluteal nerve and vessels, which run between it and the underlying gluteus minimus.

Function

When standing on one leg — as happens with every step of gait — the gluteus medius of the supporting side contracts to keep the pelvis level and prevent it dropping toward the unsupported side. Weakness or paralysis of the muscle, for instance from injury to the superior gluteal nerve, produces the characteristic Trendelenburg gait, in which the pelvis sags on the opposite side during walking. The anterior fibres of the muscle also assist in medial rotation and flexion of the hip, while the posterior fibres assist extension and lateral rotation.
