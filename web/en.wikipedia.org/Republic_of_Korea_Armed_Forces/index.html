Republic of Korea Armed Forces

Founded: 15 August 1948
Service branches: Army; Navy (incl. Marine Corps); Air Force
Headquarters: Yongsan District, Seoul
Active personnel: approx. 500,000
Conscription: 18–21 months for men

The Republic of Korea Armed Forces (Korean: 대한민국 국군), also known as the ROK Armed Forces, are the armed forces of South Korea. Founded in 1948 following the division of Korea, they comprise the Army, the Navy (including the Marine Corps), and the Air Force, with a large reserve force. The ROK military is one of the largest standing armed forces in the world, maintained primarily to deter and if necessary defeat aggression from North Korea.

The ROK Armed Forces are among the world's largest standing militaries.

History

The armed forces trace their origin to the Korean Liberation Army of the provisional government in exile and to the constabulary forces organized under the United States Army Military Government in Korea after 1945. The Army was formally established in 1948. During the Korean War (1950–1953), the ROK forces, nearly destroyed in the war's opening weeks, were rebuilt under United Nations Command and grew to over 600,000 men. South Korea later contributed major combat forces to the Vietnam War. The military dominated South Korean politics through the coups of 1961 and 1979 and only returned fully to civilian control after democratization in the late 1980s and the purge of the Hanahoe faction under President Kim Young-sam in 1993.

Organization and posture

The President of South Korea is commander-in-chief, acting through the Minister of National Defense and the Joint Chiefs of Staff. Due to the continuing threat from the North, all able-bodied South Korean men must complete 18 to 21 months of military service. South Korea fields modern equipment, including K2 Black Panther tanks, K9 Thunder self-propelled howitzers, Sejong the Great-class destroyers with Aegis combat systems, and F-35A fighters, and maintains a close alliance with the United States, which stations roughly 28,500 personnel in the country under US Forces Korea. Wartime operational control arrangements between the allies have been the subject of long-running transfer negotiations.
