The chickpea or chick pea (Cicer arietinum) is an annual legume of the family Fabaceae, subfamily Faboideae, cultivated for its edible seeds. It is also known as the garbanzo bean in Spanish-influenced usage. Chickpeas are a key ingredient in Mediterranean and Middle Eastern cuisines, used in dishes such as hummus, falafel, chana masala, and cocido madrileño. They are a nutrient-dense food, rich in protein, dietary fiber, folate, iron, and phosphorus.

History

Chickpeas were domesticated in the Fertile Crescent around 10,000 years ago, probably in southeastern Turkey and northern Syria, from the wild progenitor Cicer reticulatum. Charred remains have been found at Neolithic sites such as Jericho. The plant spread to the Mediterranean — praised by Roman authors, and its Latin name cicer is said to be the origin of Cicero's family name — and to India, where it became a staple pulse.

Description

The plant grows to 20–50 cm tall with feathery pinnate leaves and small white, pink, or purple flowers. Each pod contains one or two seeds. Two main varieties are cultivated: the large, pale, smooth Kabuli type common in the Mediterranean, and the smaller, darker, wrinkled Desi type predominant in India and Ethiopia.

Culinary uses

Chickpeas are eaten cooked in stews and curries, roasted as snacks (leblebi in Turkey), ground into flour (besan/gram flour used in pakoras and socca/farinata), pureed into hummus, and rolled into falafel. The cooking liquid, aquafaba, whips like egg white and is used as a vegan egg substitute in meringues and mayonnaise.

Agriculture

India is by far the largest producer. Like other legumes, chickpeas fix atmospheric nitrogen through symbiosis with Rhizobium bacteria, improving soil fertility. They are relatively drought-tolerant and suited to semi-arid climates, though yields are sensitive to drought and frost at flowering.

Nutrition

A 100-gram serving of cooked chickpeas provides roughly 160 kcal, about 9 g of protein, and substantial folate and manganese, making them a central protein source in vegetarian diets.
