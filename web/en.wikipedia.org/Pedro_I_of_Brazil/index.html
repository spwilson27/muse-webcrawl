Pedro I of Brazil

Pedro I (12 October 1798 – 24 September 1834), nicknamed "the Liberator", was the founder and first ruler of the Empire of Brazil. As King Pedro IV of Portugal, he briefly reigned over Portugal in 1826 before abdicating that throne in favour of his daughter Maria II.

Life and reign

Born in Lisbon, Pedro fled to Brazil with the Portuguese royal family in 1807–08 ahead of Napoleon's invasion. When his father, John VI, returned to Portugal in 1821, Pedro remained as regent. Pressed by Lisbon's demands to reduce Brazil to colonial status again, he declared Brazilian independence on 7 September 1822 — the famous "Cry of Ipiranga" — and was acclaimed emperor.

Pedro promulgated Brazil's first constitution in 1824 after dissolving the constituent assembly, a liberal charter that nonetheless granted the emperor a strong "moderating power". His reign saw the Cisplatine War with the United Provinces, which ended in the independence of Uruguay (1828), and growing political conflict that led him to abdicate the Brazilian throne on 7 April 1831 in favour of his five-year-old son, Pedro II.

Returning to Europe, Pedro fought as Duke of Braganza to restore his daughter Maria II against his absolutist brother Miguel in the Portuguese Liberal Wars. He died of tuberculosis in Lisbon in 1834, shortly after the liberal victory.
