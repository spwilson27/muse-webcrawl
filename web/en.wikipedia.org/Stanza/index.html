Stanza

A poem arranged in stanzas.

In poetry, a stanza is a named group of lines, usually recurring in meter and rhyme scheme, forming the basic recurring structural unit of a poem. The word comes from the Italian stanza, meaning "room", reflecting how each stanza functions like a room in a house. Stanzas are separated by blank lines and may be identified by their number of lines and rhyme pattern.

Common forms

Stanzas are named by line count: a couplet has two lines, a tercet three, a quatrain four (the most common), a cinquain five, a sestet six, a septet seven and an octave eight. Specific forms include the ballad stanza, the Spenserian stanza of nine lines used in The Faerie Queene, the ottava rima of eight lines used by Byron in Don Juan, and the intricate rhyme royal of Chaucer.

Function

Stanza divisions organize a poem's thoughts into units, create rhythm and expectation, and allow refrains and parallel structures. In song, the analogous unit is often called a verse. The concept is comparable to the paragraph in prose. Much as the lines of a musical staff organize notes, stanzas organize the visual and rhythmic layout of verse.

Isometric and heterometric stanzas

A poem is isometric when all its stanzas have the same structure, and heterometric when the stanzas vary. Fixed forms such as the sonnet may be printed as a single stanza or divided, as in the Petrarchan octave and sestet.
