Book of Rites

The Book of Rites, also known by its Chinese name Liji (禮記), is one of the Five Classics of Confucianism. It is a collection of texts describing the ceremonial rites, social forms, and governmental organisation of the Zhou dynasty, compiled during the Han dynasty from materials attributed to the late Warring States and early Han periods.

Contents

The received text, edited by Dai Sheng (the "Liji of Dai the Elder"), contains 49 chapters. Among its most influential sections are the Great Learning and the Doctrine of the Mean, later elevated by Zhu Xi as two of the Four Books; the Record of Music; chapters on mourning dress and funerary ritual; and the Monthly Ordinances correlating government action with the calendar.

Influence

The Liji codified the ritual order (li) at the heart of Confucian political thought, shaping Chinese law, court ceremony and family practice for two millennia. Together with the Rites of Zhou and the Etiquette and Ceremonial, it forms the "Three Rites" (Sanli).
