Learjet 23
Manufacturer: Learjet (Lear Jet Corporation)
First flight: 7 October 1963
Produced: 1964–1966

The Learjet 23 is an American six-to-eight-seat twinjet business aircraft manufactured by Lear Jet Corporation. Derived from the Swiss FFA P-16 ground-attack fighter design, it first flew on 7 October 1963 and was the first of the Learjet family — effectively creating the modern business jet market.

Design

The aircraft is a low-wing cantilever monoplane with two General Electric CJ610 turbojet engines mounted at the rear of the fuselage and distinctive wingtip fuel tanks. It was notably fast for its class, cruising at high subsonic speeds and climbing to 45,000 feet, performance that gave the Learjet name its reputation for speed.

Production

Production ran from 1964 to 1966 with 104 aircraft delivered before the improved Learjet 24 replaced it. Surviving examples are now rare and mostly preserved or flown privately.
