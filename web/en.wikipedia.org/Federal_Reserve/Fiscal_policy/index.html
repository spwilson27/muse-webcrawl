Fiscal policy

In the United States, fiscal policy is made by Congress and the President, not by the Federal Reserve.

Fiscal policy is the use of government revenue (taxation) and government expenditure to influence a country's economy. In the United States it is the responsibility of the elected branches — Congress, which holds the power of the purse, and the President — and it stands in deliberate contrast to monetary policy, which is conducted by the Federal Reserve. The distinction is fundamental to the Federal Reserve's role: the central bank sets interest rates and manages the money supply, but it does not tax, spend, or decide the federal budget.

Fiscal and monetary policy compared

Fiscal policy works through the government's budget: raising or cutting taxes, increasing or reducing spending on infrastructure, defense, welfare, and transfers, and running budget deficits or surpluses. Monetary policy, by contrast, works through interest rates and credit conditions, via tools such as the federal funds rate set by the Federal Open Market Committee. Because the Federal Reserve is independent in its operations, the two policies can at times push in opposite directions — for example, fiscal stimulus adding demand while the Fed raises rates to fight inflation, a tension visible in the early 1980s and in the debates of 2022–2023.

Interaction with the Federal Reserve

Although it does not control fiscal policy, the Fed is deeply affected by it. Large federal deficits increase the supply of Treasury securities, influence long-term interest rates, and shape the environment in which monetary policy operates; the Fed also holds a portfolio of Treasury securities bought in open market operations and remits its profits to the Treasury. Federal Reserve chairs regularly testify to Congress that the long-run federal debt path is unsustainable and that fiscal sustainability supports the central bank's ability to keep prices stable — while insisting that specific tax and spending choices belong to elected officials.

Keynesian background

The deliberate use of fiscal policy to stabilise the economy grew out of the economics of John Maynard Keynes, who argued during the Great Depression that government spending could offset collapses in private demand. Automatic stabilisers — falling tax receipts and rising unemployment benefits in a downturn — now cushion recessions without new legislation, while discretionary stimulus packages, such as those of 2009 and 2020–2021, are fiscal policy decisions debated between Congress and the administration.
