Federal funds rate

In the United States, the federal funds rate is the interest rate at which depository institutions lend reserve balances to other depository institutions overnight on an uncollateralised basis. The Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) sets a target range for the rate — since December 2008 expressed as a range of 25 basis points — and the Federal Reserve implements the target principally by setting the interest rate paid on reserve balances, supplemented by the overnight reverse repurchase agreement facility. The effective federal funds rate, published by the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, is the volume-weighted median of actual overnight transactions.

Significance

The federal funds rate is the Federal Reserve's principal instrument for implementing monetary policy. Changes in it ripple through other short-term rates — Treasury bills, commercial paper, adjustable-rate loans — and influence longer-term rates, asset prices, the exchange rate and ultimately employment and inflation. Banks set the prime rate at a fairly stable spread (historically about 300 basis points) above the funds rate, linking it to many consumer and business loans.

History

Trading in federal funds developed in the 1920s as banks with surplus reserves at the Fed lent to those needing to meet reserve requirements. Under Chairman Paul Volcker the rate peaked at 20 percent in June 1981 during the campaign against double-digit inflation. It fell to a 0–0.25 percent target range in December 2008 during the financial crisis, where it remained until December 2015, returned to that range in March 2020 during the COVID-19 pandemic, and rose rapidly in 2022–23 — to 5.25–5.50 percent — in response to the post-pandemic inflation surge.

Mechanism

Before 2020, the Fed steered the rate by adjusting the scarce supply of reserves through open market operations. In the current ample-reserves framework, it instead relies on administered rates: interest on reserve balances anchors the rate from above, and the overnight reverse repo facility provides a floor for money market rates.
