History of the Federal Reserve System

The Federal Reserve System was created by the Federal Reserve Act of 1913.

The history of the Federal Reserve System concerns the development of the central banking system of the United States. The Federal Reserve was created by the Federal Reserve Act, signed into law by President Woodrow Wilson on 23 December 1913, in response to a series of financial panics — particularly the Panic of 1907 — that had demonstrated the need for an elastic currency and a lender of last resort.

Before 1913, the United States had experimented with central banking twice: the First Bank of the United States (1791–1811) and the Second Bank of the United States (1816–1836), both of whose charters were allowed to lapse amid political opposition. During the National Banking era that followed, recurring panics exposed the fragility of a system without a central bank.

Creation and early years

The secret 1910 meeting of bankers and politicians at Jekyll Island, Georgia, drafted a plan that, after extensive debate, became the basis of the Federal Reserve Act. The system that emerged combined public oversight through a Federal Reserve Board in Washington with twelve regional Federal Reserve Banks owned by member banks.

Major episodes

The Fed's history includes its role in the Great Depression (criticised by Milton Friedman and Anna Schwartz for permitting bank failures and monetary contraction), the 1951 Treasury–Fed Accord restoring its independence, the Volcker disinflation of 1979–1982, the Great Moderation, the aggressive response to the 2007–2008 financial crisis under Ben Bernanke (quantitative easing and emergency lending), and the pandemic-era expansion of 2020. Debates continue over the Fed's dual mandate, its regulatory powers, and the size of its balance sheet.
