Federal Reserve Act

The Federal Reserve Act was an Act of the United States Congress, signed into law by President Woodrow Wilson on December 23, 1913, that created the Federal Reserve System, the central banking system of the United States. It provided for the establishment of Federal Reserve Banks to furnish an elastic currency and to supervise banking in the United States.

Background

The Panic of 1907 exposed the fragility of a banking system without a lender of last resort. A National Monetary Commission study and a compromise between agrarian and banking interests produced a plan for a decentralised central bank: twelve regional Reserve Banks owned by member banks, overseen by a presidentially appointed Federal Reserve Board in Washington.

Provisions and later changes

The Act required national banks to join the system, created the Federal Reserve note as currency, and empowered Reserve Banks to rediscount commercial paper. Major amendments followed, including the Banking Act of 1935, which concentrated authority in the Board of Governors, and later measures expanding the Fed's monetary policy mandate.
