The 54th Massachusetts Infantry Regiment was an infantry regiment of the Union Army during the American Civil War, famous as one of the first major American military units made up of Black soldiers. It was commanded by Colonel Robert Gould Shaw.

Formation

Authorized by Governor John A. Andrew after the Emancipation Proclamation, the regiment recruited free Black men from Massachusetts and across the North, including two sons of abolitionist Frederick Douglass. Recruits initially faced unequal pay — $10 a month minus a clothing deduction, versus $13 for white soldiers — and the men refused their pay in protest until Congress equalized wages in 1864.

Fort Wagner

On July 18, 1863, the 54th led the assault on Fort Wagner near Charleston, South Carolina. The regiment suffered roughly 270 casualties, including Shaw, who was killed and buried with his men by the Confederates. Although the fort was not taken, the regiment's bravery proved the fighting ability of Black troops and spurred further recruitment; some 180,000 Black men eventually served in the Union forces.

Legacy

The Saint-Gaudens Memorial on Boston Common honors Shaw and the regiment. Their story was dramatized in the 1989 film Glory, which won three Academy Awards.
