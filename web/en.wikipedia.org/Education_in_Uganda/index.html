Education in Uganda

Education in Uganda follows a system inherited in large part from the British colonial period, organised as seven years of primary education, four years of lower secondary (O-Level), two years of upper secondary (A-Level), and tertiary education at universities and other institutions. The Ministry of Education and Sports oversees the sector.

History and reforms

Formal schooling was introduced by Christian missionaries in the late nineteenth century, and institutions such as Makerere, founded as a technical school in 1922 and later Uganda's premier university, emerged under colonial rule.

In 1997 Uganda became one of the first countries in sub-Saharan Africa to introduce Universal Primary Education (UPE), abolishing tuition fees in government primary schools. Enrolment roughly doubled within a few years, straining classrooms, teacher numbers, and materials. Universal Secondary Education followed in 2007, again among the first such policies in Africa.

Structure and examinations

Pupils sit the Primary Leaving Examination (PLE) after Primary 7, the Uganda Certificate of Education (UCE) after Senior 4, and the Uganda Advanced Certificate of Education (UACE) after Senior 6, all administered by the Uganda National Examinations Board (UNEB). English is the medium of instruction, though policy encourages early teaching in local languages.

Challenges

The system faces high dropout rates — especially among girls — large class sizes, teacher shortages, and disparities between urban and rural areas. Despite these constraints, literacy has risen steadily, and Uganda's youthful population means education remains central to national development policy. The COVID-19 pandemic caused one of the world's longest school closures in Uganda (2020–2022), with lasting effects on learning.
