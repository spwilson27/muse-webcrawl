Petroleum industry in Mexico

State companyPemex (Petróleos Mexicanos)
Nationalised18 March 1938
Major fieldsCantarell, Ku-Maloob-Zaap
Peak productionapprox. 3.4 million bbl/day (2004)

The petroleum industry in Mexico is centred on the state-owned company Pemex, created after the Mexican oil expropriation of 1938, when President Lázaro Cárdenas nationalised the assets of foreign oil companies. Mexico has historically been one of the world's major crude producers and exporters, and oil revenue was long a pillar of federal finances.

Offshore platforms of the Cantarell complex, Bay of Campeche

Commercial production began in the early 1900s around Tampico and the "Golden Lane" fields of Veracruz and Tamaulipas. The industry was transformed in the 1970s by the discovery of the supergiant Cantarell field in the Bay of Campeche, which by the early 2000s was one of the largest producing fields on Earth. Cantarell's decline after 2004 drove a long fall in national output, partly offset by the neighbouring Ku-Maloob-Zaap complex.

Reform and recent history

Constitutional energy reforms in 2013–2014 ended Pemex's monopoly and opened exploration and production to private and foreign companies through licensing rounds, though subsequent administrations curtailed the opening and re-emphasised state control, prioritising domestic refining — including construction of the Olmeca (Dos Bocas) refinery in Tabasco. Pemex remains one of the world's most indebted oil companies, and debates over its finances, fuel theft ("huachicol") and the energy transition continue to shape Mexican politics.
