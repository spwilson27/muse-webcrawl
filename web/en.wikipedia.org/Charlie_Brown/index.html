Charlie Brown

Charles "Charlie" Brown is the principal character of the comic strip Peanuts, syndicated in daily and Sunday newspapers in numerous countries all over the world. Depicted as a "lovable loser", Charlie Brown is one of the great American archetypes and a popular and widely recognized cartoon character. Charlie Brown is characterized as a person who frequently suffers, and as a result, is usually nervous and lacks self-confidence. He shows both pessimistic and optimistic attitudes: on some days, he is apprehensive to even get out of bed because he is unable to face the day, but on others, he hopes for the best and tries as much as he can to accomplish things.

Character

Charlie Brown is drawn with a round head and a single curl of hair at the front. His trademark shirt bears a black zigzag stripe. He is the manager of a perennially losing baseball team and a kite-flyer whose kites invariably end up in the "kite-eating tree". His catchphrases include "Good grief!", and he is famous for never successfully kicking the football that Lucy van Pelt pulls away at the last moment.

History

The character's name was first used on May 30, 1948, in Schulz's earlier strip Li'l Folks. Charlie Brown officially debuted in Peanuts on October 2, 1950. Over the strip's nearly fifty-year run his personality evolved from a cheerful, prank-playing child into the anxious everyman of the strip's mature years. He has appeared in numerous animated television specials, beginning with A Charlie Brown Christmas (1965), and in feature films including The Peanuts Movie (2015).
