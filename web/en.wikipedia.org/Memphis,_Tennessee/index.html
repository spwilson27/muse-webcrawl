Memphis is a city in the U.S. state of Tennessee, situated on the Mississippi River in the state's southwestern corner. It is the second-most populous city in Tennessee and one of the largest cities of the southeastern United States. Founded in 1819 and named after Memphis, Egypt, the city grew as a major port and cotton market.

History

The bluff site was inhabited for millennia, including by the Chickasaw people, before European exploration. In the 19th century Memphis became a center of the cotton trade and slavery. The city suffered yellow fever epidemics in the 1870s and briefly lost its charter. In the 20th century it became a hub of blues, soul, and rock and roll music. Martin Luther King Jr. was assassinated in Memphis in 1968 while supporting striking sanitation workers.

Culture

Memphis is famed for Beale Street, Sun Studio (where Elvis Presley, Johnny Cash, and others first recorded), Stax Records, and Graceland, Presley's home. It is also known for Memphis-style barbecue and as a major logistics hub, home to the global headquarters of FedEx.

Economy

Transportation and distribution dominate the economy thanks to the city's river port, rail lines, interstate highways, and Memphis International Airport, long among the world's busiest cargo airports.
