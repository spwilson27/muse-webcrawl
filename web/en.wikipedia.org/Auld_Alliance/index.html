Auld Alliance

The Auld Alliance (Scots for "Old Alliance") was an alliance between the kingdoms of Scotland and France against England, first formalised in 1295 and maintained, with renewals, until 1560. It originated with the treaty concluded in Paris in October 1295 between John Balliol of Scotland and Philip IV of France, at a time when Edward I of England was asserting overlordship over Scotland.

The alliance linked Scotland and France against their common enemy, England.

History

Under the alliance, each kingdom agreed to attack England if the other were attacked, obliging England to fight on two fronts. Scottish armies campaigned in France during the Hundred Years' War — Scots guards served the French crown, and Joan of Arc's army included Scottish soldiers. The alliance drew Scotland into the disastrous Battle of Flodden in 1513, where James IV was killed invading England while Henry VIII was campaigning in France. It was renewed through the marriage of Mary, Queen of Scots, to the French dauphin Francis in 1558.

End and legacy

The alliance effectively ended with the Treaty of Edinburgh in 1560, as Protestant Scotland under John Knox aligned itself with England, and French troops withdrew. Nevertheless, cultural and legal traces persisted: Scots enjoyed dual citizenship rights in France for centuries, Scottish law retained French influences, and the phrase "Auld Alliance" still connotes friendly Franco-Scottish relations, invoked in sport and diplomacy.
