Heinz-Harald Frentzen (born 18 May 1967) is a German former racing driver who competed in Formula One between 1994 and 2003, winning three Grands Prix and finishing runner-up in the 1997 World Championship (after Michael Schumacher's exclusion) and third in 1999.

Early career

Frentzen rose through karting and German Formula Opel Lotus, and was a member of the Mercedes-Benz junior team in sports cars alongside Michael Schumacher and Karl Wendlinger. He also raced in Japan in Formula 3000 before entering Formula One.

Formula One

He made his debut with Sauber in 1994, impressing with points finishes in an uncompetitive car despite a difficult period following his team-mate Wendlinger's serious accident at Monaco. In 1997 he joined champions Williams, winning at Imola but struggling to match team-mate Jacques Villeneuve, who won the title. His best season came with Jordan in 1999, when victories in France and Italy made him an outside title contender and he finished third in the standings. Later stints with Prost, Arrows and Sauber yielded few results, and he left Formula One during 2003. He subsequently raced in the DTM touring car series and at Le Mans before retiring.
