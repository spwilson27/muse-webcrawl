The Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences (AMPAS) is a professional honorary organisation of film industry figures, based in Beverly Hills, California, best known as the body that presents the Academy Awards, popularly called the Oscars. Its stated purposes include advancing the arts and sciences of motion pictures, recognising outstanding achievement, and preserving the history of the medium.

Founding and functions

The Academy was conceived by Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer studio head Louis B. Mayer and founded in 1927 with 36 charter members; actor Douglas Fairbanks was its first president. Originally it also mediated industry labour disputes and promoted technical standards, but since the 1940s it has concentrated on the annual awards, education, and preservation through institutions such as the Margaret Herrick Library, the Academy Film Archive and its science and technology programmes. Membership, by invitation, is organised into branches for each craft (actors, directors, cinematographers and so on) and surpassed 10,000 members in the 2020s, following a major expansion and diversification drive after the 2015–2016 "#OscarsSoWhite" controversies. The Academy Museum of Motion Pictures opened in Los Angeles in 2021.

The Academy Awards

The first Academy Awards were presented on 16 May 1929 at a banquet in the Hollywood Roosevelt Hotel. The awards are now among the most watched entertainment broadcasts in the world. Nominees in most categories are chosen by the corresponding branch, while winners of Best Picture and most awards are decided by the membership at large.
