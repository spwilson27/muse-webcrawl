The Parable of the Prodigal Son (also called the Parable of the Lost Son) is one of the best-known parables of Jesus, found only in the Gospel of Luke. It follows the Parable of the Lost Sheep and the Parable of the Lost Coin in a trilogy about the recovery of the lost.

The story

A younger son demands his share of the inheritance, travels to a distant country and squanders everything in dissolute living. Reduced to feeding pigs and starving, he "comes to himself" and resolves to return and beg to be treated as a hired servant. While he is still far off, his father sees him, runs to embrace him, and orders a feast with the fatted calf. The elder son, who has stayed and worked obediently, resents the celebration; the father replies that the celebration is fitting, "for this your brother was dead, and is alive; he was lost, and is found."

Interpretation and influence

The parable is traditionally read as an image of divine forgiveness for the repentant, with the father's joy mirroring the shepherd's joy over the recovered sheep. The word "prodigal" (wastefully extravagant) entered English through this story. It has inspired famous depictions by Rembrandt, Murillo and others, and ballets, operas and films.
