The Good Shepherd is an image of Jesus found in the Gospel of John, in which Jesus declares: "I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep" (John 10:11). Unlike the hired hand, who flees when the wolf comes, the good shepherd knows his sheep by name and seeks out the one that is lost — imagery closely related to the Parable of the Lost Sheep in Matthew and Luke.

Background

Shepherd imagery runs through the Hebrew Bible: Psalm 23 ("The LORD is my shepherd"), Ezekiel 34, which promises that God himself will shepherd Israel, and the figures of Moses and David. In John's Gospel the saying is one of the seven "I am" statements.

In early Christian art

The Good Shepherd carrying a sheep across his shoulders is among the most frequent images in the earliest Christian art, appearing in the Roman catacombs and on sarcophagi from the 3rd century onward, modelled on the ancient Greek kriophoros (ram-bearer) figure. It later became a standard motif for Christ's care for the individual believer and for pastoral ministry.
