Parable of the Lost Sheep

The Parable of the Lost Sheep is one of the parables of Jesus. It appears in the Gospels of Matthew (18:12–14) and Luke (15:3–7) and concerns a shepherd who leaves ninety-nine sheep to search for a single one that has gone astray.

Text

In Luke's version, Jesus tells the parable in response to Pharisees who criticised him for eating with tax collectors and sinners. The shepherd, having found the lost sheep, lays it on his shoulders rejoicing and calls together his friends to celebrate. Jesus concludes that there will be more joy in heaven over one sinner who repents than over ninety-nine righteous persons who need no repentance. In Luke the parable is the first of a trilogy about redemption, followed by the Parable of the Lost Coin and the Parable of the Prodigal Son.

Interpretation

The parable is traditionally interpreted as expressing God's love for the individual sinner and the joy of repentance. The image of Christ as the Good Shepherd who seeks the lost draws on Old Testament imagery such as Psalm 23 and Ezekiel 34. The parable has been a frequent subject in Christian art.
