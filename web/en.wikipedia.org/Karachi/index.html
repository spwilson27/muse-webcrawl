Karachi

Country: Pakistan
Province: Sindh
Population: over 17 million (city proper); one of the world's largest metropolitan areas
Known as: City of Lights, City of the Quaid

Karachi (Urdu: کراچی; Sindhi: ڪراچي) is the capital city of the Pakistani province of Sindh and the largest city in Pakistan, as well as one of the most populous cities in the world. Ranked as a beta-global city, it is Pakistan's premier industrial and financial centre and the country's major seaport, located on the coast of the Arabian Sea northwest of the Indus Delta.

History

The region around Karachi has been inhabited for millennia; the modern city was founded as the fortified village of Kolachi in 1729 and developed by the Talpur Mirs. Captured by the British in 1839 and annexed in 1843, it grew rapidly under colonial rule as a port and garrison town, connected by rail to the Punjab in 1878. At Pakistan's independence in 1947 Karachi became the national capital — a status it held until Islamabad was developed in the 1960s — and absorbed hundreds of thousands of Muslim refugees (Muhajirs) from India.

Geography and climate

Karachi lies on a coastal plain with rocky outcroppings and hills, drained by the seasonal Lyari and Malir rivers. Its climate is arid but moderated by the sea, with hot summers, mild winters, and a monsoon season from July to September. The city has suffered severe heatwaves, notably in 2015.

Economy and demographics

Karachi generates the largest share of Pakistan's GDP and tax revenue. Its ports — the Port of Karachi and Port Qasim — handle most of the nation's cargo. The city is ethnically and linguistically diverse: while Urdu-speaking Muhajirs form a plurality, Sindhis, Pashtuns, Punjabis, Baloch, and many smaller communities are also present. Decades of rapid growth have strained infrastructure, water supply, and governance.
