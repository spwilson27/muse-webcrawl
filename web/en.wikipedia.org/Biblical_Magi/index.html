Biblical Magi

The biblical Magi (singular Magus), also known as the Three Wise Men or Three Kings, were distinguished foreigners who visited the infant Jesus after his birth, bearing gifts of gold, frankincense, and myrrh. Their visit is described in the Gospel of Matthew (Matthew 2), the only one of the four canonical gospels to mention them.

The account in Matthew

Matthew says that Magi "from the east" followed a star to Jerusalem, asking where "the king of the Jews" had been born. King Herod, alarmed, directed them to Bethlehem; they found the child, worshipped him, presented their gifts, and, warned in a dream, returned home by another route without reporting back to Herod.

Interpretation

The word magoi referred to members of a Persian priestly caste associated with astrology and dream interpretation, and the figures are often understood as astrologers or sages from Persia or Arabia. Matthew does not state their number; the tradition of three arises from the three gifts. Western Christian tradition later named them Caspar, Melchior, and Balthazar and depicted them as kings, partly in light of Old Testament prophecies of kings bearing gifts.

Feast and traditions

The visit is commemorated at Epiphany (6 January) in Western Christianity. Relics attributed to the Magi have been venerated at Cologne Cathedral since the 12th century. In many cultures, including Spain and Latin America, Three Kings' Day is a major gift-giving festival.
