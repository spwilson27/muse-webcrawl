Eviction

Eviction
AreaLandlord–tenant law
SynonymUnlawful detainer (US)

Eviction is the removal of a tenant from rental property by the landlord, typically through a legal process. In some jurisdictions it may also involve the removal of persons from premises that were foreclosed upon.

Grounds and process

Common grounds for eviction include non-payment of rent, violation of lease terms, damage to the property, and the expiry of a lease. Most jurisdictions require the landlord to give the tenant written notice — such as a notice to pay or quit — and to obtain a court order before the tenant can be removed; "self-help" evictions such as changing the locks or cutting off utilities are unlawful in many places.

Effects

Eviction is a major cause of housing instability and homelessness, and an eviction record can make it difficult for tenants to obtain housing in the future. Sociologist Matthew Desmond's book Evicted (2016) documented the role of eviction in perpetuating poverty in the United States.

Variations

Rules differ widely by country and by state or province. Many European countries provide strong security of tenure, while others permit "no-fault" evictions after notice. Temporary eviction moratoria were widely adopted during the COVID-19 pandemic.
