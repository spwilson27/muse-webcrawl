Music for Strings, Percussion and Celesta, Sz. 106, BB 114, is a composition by Hungarian composer Béla Bartók, commissioned by Paul Sacher to celebrate the tenth anniversary of the Basel Chamber Orchestra, which premiered it on 21 January 1937 under Sacher's direction. It is considered one of Bartók's greatest works.

The music

The ensemble consists of two string orchestras placed antiphonally, with percussion (including xylophone, snare drum, cymbals, tam-tam and bass drum), celesta, harp and piano. The four movements form a vast arch: a slow fugue built patiently from a chromatic subject; a fast, dance-like Allegro; a nocturnal Adagio of "night music" character, whose xylophone figures became famous through use in film; and a folk-dance finale. Proportions related to the Fibonacci sequence and golden ratio have been analysed in the first movement's structure.

Reception

The Adagio's eerie textures were used to striking effect in Stanley Kubrick's film The Shining (1980), introducing the work to a wide audience. The piece remains a staple of the modern orchestral repertoire.
