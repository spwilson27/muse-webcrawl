Fannie Mae

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Federal National Mortgage Association (FNMA), commonly known as Fannie Mae, is a United States government-sponsored enterprise (GSE) and, since 1968, a publicly traded company. Founded in 1938 during the Great Depression as part of President Franklin D. Roosevelt's New Deal, its purpose is to expand the secondary mortgage market by securitising mortgage loans, thereby increasing the supply of money available for home lending.

Business

Fannie Mae purchases mortgage loans from lenders, pools them, and issues mortgage-backed securities to investors, guaranteeing timely payment of principal and interest in exchange for guarantee fees. It does not originate loans or lend directly to homebuyers. Together with its sibling GSE Freddie Mac, Fannie Mae supports a large share of American residential mortgage finance and underpins the standard 30-year fixed-rate mortgage. The name "Fannie Mae" is a phonetic play on its acronym, FNMA, created to make it easier to refer to the association.

Conservatorship

During the 2008 financial crisis, mounting losses on mortgage guarantees brought Fannie Mae to the brink of insolvency. In September 2008 the Federal Housing Finance Agency placed Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac into federal conservatorship, and the US Treasury injected roughly $116 billion into Fannie Mae under a senior preferred stock agreement. The enterprises later returned to profitability and paid dividends to the Treasury exceeding the support received, but they remain in conservatorship, and proposals for reform or release have recurred in successive administrations.
