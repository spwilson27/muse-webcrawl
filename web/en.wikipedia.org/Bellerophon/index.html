Bellerophon is a hero of Greek mythology, famed as the tamer of the winged horse Pegasus and the slayer of the fire-breathing Chimera. His story, told in Homer's Iliad (Book 6) and later authors, is one of the earliest recorded uses of a written message—the "Bellerophontic letter"—ordering the bearer's death.

Myth

Falsely accused by Queen Anteia (Stheneboea) of assault, Bellerophon was sent by King Proetus to Lycia carrying a sealed letter instructing King Iobates to kill him. Iobates instead set him impossible tasks: slaying the Chimera (achieved from Pegasus's back, with a lead-tipped spear melted in the monster's fiery throat), defeating the Solymoi and the Amazons, and surviving an ambush. Victorious, he married the king's daughter and inherited half the kingdom.

Downfall

In later tradition, pride led Bellerophon to attempt to fly Pegasus to Mount Olympus; Zeus sent a gadfly that stung Pegasus, and Bellerophon fell to earth, wandering alone and lame thereafter—a cautionary tale about hubris. The spring of Peirene at Corinth, where he captured Pegasus with Athena's golden bridle, was sacred in his legend.
