Battle of Rossbach

The Battle of Rossbach took place on 5 November 1757 near the village of Rossbach (Roßbach) in Saxony, during the Seven Years' War. A Prussian army of about 22,000 men under King Frederick the Great defeated a combined Franco-Imperial army of some 41,000 under the Prince de Soubise and Prince Joseph of Saxe-Hildburghausen. The battle, which lasted barely ninety minutes, is regarded as one of Frederick's greatest victories and a classic demonstration of the oblique order of battle and rapid Prussian infantry fire discipline.

Background

In 1757 Prussia was threatened on several fronts: by Austria in Silesia, Russia in East Prussia, Sweden in Pomerania, and a French and Imperial army advancing from the west. Frederick resolved to defeat the western army first, before it could join the Austrians. The allied commanders, whose forces were of mixed quality and divided counsel, manoeuvred to cut Frederick off near the Saale river.

The battle

On 5 November the allies marched to turn the Prussian left flank, believing Frederick was retreating. Frederick, watching from the roof of a house in Rossbach, recognised the opportunity. The Prussians struck camp with remarkable speed, screened by hills, and fell on the allied columns as they deployed. General Friedrich Wilhelm von Seydlitz's cavalry charged and routed the allied horse, then wheeled against the infantry, while Prussian infantry advanced in echelon with rapid platoon fire. The allied army disintegrated in about 90 minutes, losing some 10,000 men against roughly 550 Prussian casualties.

Aftermath

Rossbach freed Frederick to march east, where exactly one month later he won the Battle of Leuthen against Austria. In France the defeat became a byword for military incompetence ("rossbach" entered slang), while in Germany the victory over the French made Frederick a national hero. The battle is often paired with Leuthen as the twin pillars of Frederick's reputation.
