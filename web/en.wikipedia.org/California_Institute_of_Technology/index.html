The California Institute of Technology (branded as Caltech) is a private research university in Pasadena, California. Known for its strength in science and engineering, it has an unusually small student body and an intense academic culture.

History

Caltech began in 1891 as Throop University, a vocational school founded by Amos G. Throop. Under astronomer George Ellery Hale, physicist Robert A. Millikan, and chemist Arthur Amos Noyes, it was remade into an institute focused on science and technology and renamed the California Institute of Technology in 1920. Millikan, a Nobel laureate, led the school through its formative decades.

Academics and research

Caltech is organized into six academic divisions: Biology and Biological Engineering; Chemistry and Chemical Engineering; Engineering and Applied Science; Geological and Planetary Sciences; Humanities and Social Sciences; and Physics, Mathematics and Astronomy. It manages the Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) for NASA, operates the Palomar Observatory and the Owens Valley Radio Observatory, and co-operates LIGO, whose first direct detection of gravitational waves was announced in 2016. As of recent counts, dozens of Nobel laureates have been Caltech faculty, alumni, or researchers.

Campus life

Undergraduates are sorted at arrival into one of eight Houses under the Residence Life system, and student conduct is governed by an Honor Code: "No member of the Caltech community shall take unfair advantage of any other member of the Caltech community." Annual traditions include Ditch Day and the interhouse games. Students are often called "Techers" and the teams the Beavers.
