Cabinet of Canada

The Cabinet of Canada (French: Cabinet du Canada) is a body of ministers of the Crown that, along with the Canadian monarch, forms the government of Canada. Chaired by the prime minister, the Cabinet is the senior decision-making body of the executive branch, setting government policy and priorities.

Composition

Cabinet ministers are chosen by the prime minister and formally appointed by the governor general. They are almost always members of Parliament — usually the House of Commons, occasionally the Senate — and each heads a government department or holds a portfolio such as finance, foreign affairs, health or defence. Cabinets have typically numbered between 25 and 40 members, with attention paid to regional, linguistic and gender representation.

Working

The Cabinet operates under the principles of cabinet solidarity and ministerial responsibility: ministers must publicly support government decisions and answer to Parliament for their departments. Full Cabinet and its committees, serviced by the Privy Council Office, coordinate legislation and spending. Formally, decisions are enacted through the Governor General in Council.
