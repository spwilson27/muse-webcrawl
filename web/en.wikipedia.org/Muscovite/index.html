Muscovite (also known as common mica, isinglass, or potash mica) is a hydrated phyllosilicate mineral of aluminium and potassium with formula KAl₂(AlSi₃O₁₀)(F,OH)₂, or (KF)₂(Al₂O₃)₃(SiO₂)₆. It has a highly perfect basal cleavage yielding remarkably thin laminae (sheets) which are often highly elastic.

Sheets of muscovite were used in Russia as a substitute for glass in windows (hence the name, from Muscovy glass) and it remains an important material as an electrical and thermal insulator. It is the most common member of the mica family, occurring in igneous rocks such as granite and pegmatites, and in metamorphic rocks such as schist and gneiss.

Properties and uses

Muscovite is colourless to pale brown, green or pink, with a pearly lustre. Because its sheets are transparent, flexible and resistant to heat and electricity, it has been used in furnace windows, electrical insulation, paints, plastics, cosmetics and as a filler. Large crystals occur in pegmatites; notable localities include India, Brazil, Russia and the United States.
