Logarithmic levels expressed in bels and decibels.

The bel (symbol: B) is a logarithmic unit used to express the ratio of two values of a power or root-power quantity, such as power, intensity, voltage or sound pressure. It is named in honour of Alexander Graham Bell, pioneer of telephony. One bel corresponds to a power ratio of 10:1.

Definition and usage

The level of a power quantity in bels is the base-10 logarithm of the ratio to a reference value. Because one bel is rather large for practical work, the decibel—one tenth of a bel—is used almost universally in acoustics, electronics and telecommunications to express gain, attenuation and sound pressure level.

History

The unit originated in the 1920s at Bell Telephone Laboratories as the "transmission unit" for measuring loss in telephone lines, and was renamed the bel in 1928. A related logarithmic unit, the neper, uses natural logarithms and is common in European engineering.
