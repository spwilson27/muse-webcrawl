Mohs scale

The Mohs scale of mineral hardness is a qualitative ordinal scale, from 1 to 10, characterizing scratch resistance of minerals through the ability of harder material to scratch softer material. It was proposed by German geologist Friedrich Mohs in 1812.

Mohs scale
CreatorFriedrich Mohs (1812)
Range1 (talc) – 10 (diamond)
TypeOrdinal, scratch test

The scale

Reference minerals: 1 talc, 2 gypsum, 3 calcite, 4 fluorite, 5 apatite, 6 orthoclase feldspar, 7 quartz, 8 topaz, 9 corundum, 10 diamond. Everyday comparisons include a fingernail (~2.5), copper coin (~3), steel nail (~5.5), and glass (~5.5).

Use and limits

Geologists use the scale for field identification. It is not linear: diamond is far harder in absolute terms than corundum, and quantitative scales such as Vickers measure indentation hardness instead.
