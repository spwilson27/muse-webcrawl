The Boat Race

The Boat Race is an annual set of rowing races between the Cambridge University Boat Club and the Oxford University Boat Club, traditionally rowed between eights of male and female students on the River Thames in London. The men's race was first held in 1829 and has been held annually since 1856, except during the First and Second World Wars and the COVID-19 pandemic. The women's race, first held in 1927, has taken place on the same course and the same day as the men's since 2015.

Course

The Championship Course runs 4 miles and 374 yards (6.8 km) over the tidal Thames from Putney to Mortlake, passing Fulham, Hammersmith and Barnes. Crews race on the ebb tide, and the contest for the favoured Middlesex or Surrey stations is decided by the toss of a 1829 gold sovereign. Winning times are typically between 16 and 20 minutes depending on conditions; the course record, set by Cambridge in 1998, is 16 minutes 19 seconds.

Rivalry

Cambridge row in light blue and Oxford in dark blue, giving rise to the names "the Light Blues" and "the Dark Blues". More than 250,000 spectators line the banks and millions watch on television. Notable incidents include sinkings by both crews (Cambridge in 1859 and 1978, Oxford in 1925 and 1951), a dead heat in 1877, and the 2012 race, which was halted by a swimmer protesting on the course and later marred by a clash of oars in the restart.
