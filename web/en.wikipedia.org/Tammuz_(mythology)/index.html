Tammuz (mythology)

Tammuz (Akkadian: Duʾzu, Dumuzi) was an ancient Mesopotamian god of shepherds and fertility, and the consort of the goddess Inanna (Ishtar). In Sumerian tradition, as Dumuzi, he was the fifth antediluvian king of Bad-tibira and the husband of Inanna.

Death and mourning

In the myth of Inanna's descent to the underworld, Dumuzi is given over to the demons of the underworld in exchange for Inanna's release. His death was ritually mourned each year in the month named after him — Tammuz, the fourth month of the Hebrew calendar — a practice condemned in Ezekiel 8:14, which describes women weeping for Tammuz at the Temple in Jerusalem.

Legacy

Tammuz became a classic example of the "dying-and-rising god" in comparative religion, a pattern popularized by James Frazer's The Golden Bough, though modern scholars debate the "rising" element.
