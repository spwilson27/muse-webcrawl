Saint Stephen's Day

Saint Stephen's Day, also called the Feast of Saint Stephen, is a Christian saint's day on 26 December (or 27 December in the Eastern tradition) commemorating Saint Stephen, the first Christian martyr or protomartyr. It falls on the second day of Christmastide and is a public holiday in many countries with historical Catholic, Anglican or Lutheran traditions.

Date26 December (Western); 27 December (Eastern)
CommemoratesSaint Stephen, first Christian martyr
Also known asBoxing Day (in many Commonwealth countries)

Saint Stephen

Stephen was one of the seven deacons chosen by the early Church in Jerusalem, according to the Acts of the Apostles. Accused of blasphemy, he delivered a long defence speech before the Sanhedrin and was stoned to death (c. 34 AD), with the future apostle Paul among those consenting to his death. His feast has been kept on the day after Christmas since at least the fourth century.

Observances

In Ireland the day is known for the Wren Day tradition, in which "wren boys" parade in costume. In Catalonia it is celebrated as Sant Esteve, a family holiday. In German-speaking countries the Stephanitag is a public holiday, and in Finland ("Tapaninpäivä") it traditionally involved sleigh rides and visiting. In Britain and much of the Commonwealth the same date is widely observed as Boxing Day, a bank holiday associated with sport and post-Christmas sales.
