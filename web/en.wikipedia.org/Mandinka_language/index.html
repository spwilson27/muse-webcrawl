Mandinka language

Mandinka (also called Mandingo) is a Mande language spoken by the Mandinka people of West Africa, principally in Senegal, the Gambia, and Guinea-Bissau, with speakers also in Mali and neighbouring countries. It is part of the closely related Manding language continuum that includes Bambara and Maninka.

Features

Mandinka is a tonal language with two basic tones, uses subject–object–verb word order, and has relatively simple phonology. It lacks the nasal vowels found in some sister languages and is known for its extensive use of proverbs in speech.

Writing

Mandinka is written in the Latin alphabet in official contexts in Senegal and the Gambia, in Arabic-based Ajami script for religious and older texts, and occasionally in the N'Ko script created in 1949.
