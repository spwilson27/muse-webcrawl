The Ute Indian Tribe, also known as the Uintah and Ouray Ute Tribe, is a federally recognized tribe of the Ute people based in Fort Duchesne, Utah. Its reservation, the Uintah and Ouray Indian Reservation, is the second-largest reservation in the United States, covering over 4.5 million acres in northeastern Utah's Uintah Basin.

History

The tribe descends from the Uintah, White River (Yampa), and Uncompahgre bands of the Ute people. In the 1880s these bands were removed to the Uintah Reservation, which had been established in 1861, and to the Uncompahgre Reservation created in 1882; the two were consolidated in the 1930s. The reservation's oil, gas, and mineral resources have been a major economic and legal matter, with long disputes over water rights, trespass, and jurisdiction.

Government and economy

The tribe is governed by a business committee under a constitution adopted in the 1930s. Revenues come from oil and gas development, the Ute Tribe's water and mineral management, agriculture, and enterprises including the Ute Crossing Grill and the tribe's ranching operations. The tribe operates its own fish and wildlife department, health services, and the Uintah River High School.
