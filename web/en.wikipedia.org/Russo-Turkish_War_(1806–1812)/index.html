The Russo-Turkish War of 1806–1812 was one of the many conflicts between the Russian and Ottoman Empires, fought over the Danubian Principalities and the Caucasus during the Napoleonic Wars. It ended with Russia's annexation of Bessarabia.

Course of the war

War began in 1806 when the Ottoman sultan, encouraged by France, deposed the pro-Russian hospodars of Moldavia and Wallachia; Russian troops under Ivan Michelson occupied the principalities. Fighting continued inconclusively along the Danube and in the Caucasus, punctuated by truces and by Russia's simultaneous wars with France, Sweden, and Persia. In 1811 Field Marshal Mikhail Kutuzov took command on the Danube, defeated the main Ottoman army at Ruse, and encircled it at Slobozia, forcing the Porte to negotiate.

Treaty of Bucharest

The Treaty of Bucharest, signed on 28 May 1812, ceded Bessarabia — the eastern half of Moldavia between the Prut and Dniester rivers — to Russia, while Wallachia and the rest of Moldavia were returned to Ottoman rule. The settlement freed Russian forces on the Danube just weeks before Napoleon's invasion of Russia in June 1812.
