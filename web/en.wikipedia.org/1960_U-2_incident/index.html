1960 U-2 incident

The 1960 U-2 incident occurred on 1 May 1960, when a United States U-2 spy plane was shot down by Soviet air defences over Soviet airspace near Sverdlovsk (now Yekaterinburg) while conducting photographic aerial reconnaissance. The pilot, Francis Gary Powers, survived, was captured, and was put on trial in Moscow. The incident caused the collapse of the Four Powers summit in Paris two weeks later and was a major embarrassment to the Eisenhower administration during the Cold War.

The flight

Powers, a pilot employed by the Central Intelligence Agency, took off from Peshawar, Pakistan, on a mission (GRAND SLAM) intended to photograph missile and plutonium production sites across the Soviet Union and land in Bodø, Norway. His Lockheed U-2C was flying at roughly 70,000 feet, believed to be beyond the reach of Soviet interceptors, but near Sverdlovsk an S-75 Dvina (SA-2 Guideline) surface-to-air missile detonated close behind the aircraft, disabling it. Powers parachuted to safety but did not use his optional suicide device; he was captured shortly after landing.

Diplomatic fallout

Assuming Powers had been killed, NASA issued a cover story about a missing weather research aircraft. Soviet premier Nikita Khrushchev then revealed that the pilot was alive and much of the wreckage intact, presenting captured film and equipment in Moscow. President Dwight D. Eisenhower was forced to admit that the United States had been conducting spy flights over the USSR for years. At the Paris summit opening on 16 May 1960, Khrushchev demanded an apology; Eisenhower refused, and the summit collapsed.

Powers's trial and exchange

Powers pleaded guilty to espionage and was sentenced to ten years' imprisonment. On 10 February 1962, after less than two years, he was exchanged at the Glienicke Bridge in Berlin for Soviet intelligence officer Rudolf Abel, in the first famous spy swap of its kind. Powers returned to the United States, was criticised for not destroying the aircraft or himself, but a Senate inquiry cleared him. He died in a helicopter crash in 1977 while working as a traffic reporter in Los Angeles.

Legacy

The incident ended the U-2 overflight programme over the Soviet Union, accelerated American reliance on reconnaissance satellites, and hardened Cold War tensions in the final year of Eisenhower's presidency. The exchange of Powers and Abel was dramatised in the 2015 film Bridge of Spies.
