Armed Forces of the Philippines

The Armed Forces of the Philippines (AFP; Filipino: Sandatahang Lakas ng Pilipinas) is the military force of the Philippines. It consists of three main service branches: the Philippine Army, the Philippine Air Force, and the Philippine Navy (including the Marine Corps). The President of the Philippines is the commander-in-chief, and the AFP is administered under the Department of National Defense, with headquarters at Camp Aguinaldo in Quezon City.

History

The AFP traces its origins to the Philippine Revolutionary Army founded in 1897 during the Philippine Revolution against Spain, and to the Philippine Constabulary established under American rule. The modern armed forces were formally organised under the National Defense Act of 1935, with Douglas MacArthur serving as Field Marshal of the Philippine Army. Philippine forces fought alongside the United States in World War II after the Japanese invasion of 1941–42, and later contributed troops to the Korean and Vietnam Wars. For decades the AFP's focus has been internal security against communist and Moro insurgencies, alongside a more recent shift toward territorial defence in the South China Sea (West Philippine Sea).

Organisation and modernisation

Active personnel number roughly 150,000, supplemented by reserves and the Citizen Armed Force Geographical Unit (CAFGU) militia. Since 2013, the Revised AFP Modernization Program has sought to acquire modern ships, aircraft, and air defence systems in phases known as "Horizons". The Philippines maintains a Mutual Defense Treaty with the United States dating to 1951 and regularly holds the Balikatan joint exercises.
