Louis Philippe I (6 October 1773 – 26 August 1850) was King of the French from 1830 to 1848, the last king of France. He came to power in the July Revolution of 1830 and ruled the July Monarchy, styling himself "King of the French" and cultivating a bourgeois, umbrella-carrying image that earned him the nickname the "Citizen King".

Early life

The son of Louis Philippe, Duke of Orléans (Philippe Égalité), he fought as a young revolutionary general at Valmy and Jemappes in 1792, then defected to the Austrians in 1793 and spent two decades in exile in Switzerland, Scandinavia, the United States and England, at times teaching at a school in Reichenau.

Reign and fall

His reign began liberally but grew steadily more conservative and corrupt; suffrage remained confined to a small wealthy electorate despite his minister Guizot's encouragement to "enrich yourselves". Economic crisis and the banned reform banquets of 1847–48 provoked the February Revolution of 1848. Louis Philippe abdicated in favour of his grandson, fled to England disguised as "Mr Smith", and died at Claremont in 1850.
