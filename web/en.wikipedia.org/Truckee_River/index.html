Truckee River

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Truckee River is a river in the U.S. states of California and Nevada, about 121 miles (195 km) long. It is the sole outlet of Lake Tahoe and flows generally northeast through the Sierra Nevada, through the city of Reno, and empties into Pyramid Lake in the Great Basin desert. Its watershed covers about 3,060 square miles.

Course and use

From Tahoe City on Lake Tahoe's northwestern shore, the river runs through Truckee, California, then down the eastern Sierra escarpment into Reno and Sparks, Nevada, before turning north across the lands of the Pyramid Lake Paiute Tribe to its terminus. The river supplies municipal water to the Reno–Sparks area, irrigation water in the Truckee Meadows and the Newlands Project downstream, and hydroelectric power. The Boca, Stampede, and Prosser Creek reservoirs regulate its flow.

Ecology and disputes

The river historically supported Lahontan cutthroat trout and the endangered cui-ui sucker of Pyramid Lake; diversions in the 20th century lowered Pyramid Lake's level and nearly extinguished both fish. The 1990 Truckee–Carson–Pyramid Lake Water Rights Settlement Act and the 2008 Truckee River Operating Agreement rebalanced allocations among cities, irrigators, and the Paiute Tribe, aiding the recovery of the lake's fishery.
