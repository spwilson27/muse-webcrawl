The history of London, the capital of England and the United Kingdom, extends over some 2,000 years. The Romans founded Londinium around AD 47–50 as a port and commercial centre on the River Thames. After Roman withdrawal the settlement declined, was re-established within the old walls by Alfred the Great in 886, and grew into England's largest town and chief port during the Middle Ages.

Early modern and Georgian London

Tudor and Stuart London expanded beyond the City walls into Westminster and the suburbs. The Great Plague of 1665 and the Great Fire of 1666 — which destroyed much of the medieval City — were followed by rebuilding in stone under architects such as Christopher Wren, whose St Paul's Cathedral became an enduring symbol. By the 18th century London was the largest city in Europe and a global trading hub.

Victorian era to the present

Victorian London, at the heart of the British Empire, built the first underground railway (1863), sewers, and embankments. The Blitz of 1940–41 devastated the city during the Second World War. Post-war decline was followed by reinvention as a global financial and cultural centre; Greater London's population surpassed its pre-war peak in the 2010s.
