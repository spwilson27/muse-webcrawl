Physical Vapor Deposition

Physical vapor deposition (PVD) describes a family of vacuum deposition methods used to produce thin films and coatings. In PVD, material is vaporized from a solid source in a vacuum environment and condenses atom by atom on a substrate. Film thicknesses range from a few nanometers to many micrometers.

Processes

Common PVD processes include sputtering (atoms are ejected from a target by ion bombardment), thermal and electron-beam evaporation, pulsed laser deposition, and cathodic arc deposition. Unlike chemical vapor deposition, PVD involves purely physical phase change rather than chemical reactions at the surface.

Applications

PVD coatings provide hard, wear- and corrosion-resistant layers (such as titanium nitride on cutting tools), optical coatings, decorative finishes, and metallization layers in semiconductor and solar-cell manufacture.
