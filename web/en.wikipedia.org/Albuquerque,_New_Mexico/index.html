Albuquerque (/ˈælbəkɜːrki/ AL-bə-kur-kee) is the most populous city in the U.S. state of New Mexico. Its population was 564,559 at the 2020 census, making it the 32nd most populous city in the United States; the metropolitan area has roughly one million residents. The city lies in the Albuquerque Basin along the Rio Grande, flanked by the Sandia Mountains to the east.

History

The area was inhabited for centuries by Pueblo peoples before Spanish colonists founded the Villa de Alburquerque in 1706, named for the Duke of Alburquerque, viceroy of New Spain (the first "r" was later dropped). After the Mexican–American War the territory passed to the United States in 1848. The arrival of the railroad in 1880 sparked a "New Town" east of the old plaza, and in the 20th century Route 66, Kirtland Air Force Base and Sandia National Laboratories drove rapid growth.

Geography and culture

Albuquerque has a high-desert climate with abundant sunshine, wide temperature swings and low humidity. Landmarks include the historic Old Town, the Sandia Peak Tramway — one of the longest aerial trams in the world — and the Petroglyph National Monument. The city is famous for the Albuquerque International Balloon Fiesta, held each October since 1972 and among the largest hot-air balloon festivals in the world. Its cuisine blends Spanish, Mexican and Native American influences, centred on New Mexico chile, and the city gained renewed fame as the setting of the television series Breaking Bad and Better Call Saul.
