Jacob's Ladder

Jacob's Ladder (Hebrew: Sulam Yaakov) is a ladder leading to heaven that was featured in a dream the biblical patriarch Jacob had during his flight from his brother Esau, as described in the Book of Genesis (Genesis 28:10–19).

The dream

Fleeing Beersheba for Harran, Jacob stopped for the night at a place he later named Bethel, using a stone for a pillow. He dreamed of a stairway resting on the earth with its top reaching to heaven, with angels of God ascending and descending on it, and God standing above it renewing the covenant made with Abraham and Isaac, promising Jacob's descendants the land and divine protection.

Interpretations

Jewish commentary treats the ladder as a symbol of the connection between heaven and earth and of the fortunes of nations; Christian interpretation often reads it as a type of Christ, following John 1:51. The image has inspired countless works of art, hymns such as "Nearer, My God, to Thee", and the African-American spiritual "We Are Climbing Jacob's Ladder".
