Hand, foot, and mouth disease

Hand, foot, and mouth disease (HFMD) is a common infection caused by a group of enteroviruses, most often coxsackievirus A16 and enterovirus 71. It mainly affects infants and children under five, causing fever, mouth sores and a skin rash.

Signs and symptoms

After an incubation of three to six days, fever and sore throat appear, followed by painful sores in the mouth and a rash with blisters on the hands, feet and sometimes buttocks. Most cases are mild and resolve within a week to ten days. Rarely, particularly with enterovirus 71, complications such as viral meningitis, encephalitis or myocarditis occur.

Transmission and prevention

The viruses spread through saliva, nasal mucus, blister fluid, stool and contaminated surfaces. Good hand hygiene and disinfecting surfaces reduce transmission; there is no specific antiviral treatment, and care is supportive, with fluids and pain relief. Inactivated enterovirus 71 vaccines are used in China.

Epidemiology

HFMD occurs worldwide, with large outbreaks in East and Southeast Asia. It is unrelated to foot-and-mouth disease of livestock.
