Dairy

A dairy is a place where milk is produced and processed, and the word also denotes the products made from milk. Dairy products include milk itself and foods manufactured from it, such as butter, cheese, yoghurt, cream, and ice cream. Most commercial dairy production uses cows' milk, but milk is also obtained from goats, sheep, buffalo, camels, and other mammals.

Production

Dairy farming ranges from smallholder systems — India produces more milk than any other country, much of it from small farms and buffalo — to large intensive operations with automated rotary milking parlours. Raw milk is cooled, transported to processing plants, pasteurised, and homogenised before packaging or conversion into other products. Fermentation by lactic acid bacteria yields yoghurt and many cheeses; churning separates butter; and casein and whey are extracted for food ingredients.

Health and diet

Dairy foods are a major source of calcium and high-quality protein, but they also contribute saturated fat, and a large share of the world's adults have lactose intolerance, limiting consumption in much of East Asia and Africa. Fermented products and aged cheeses contain little lactose and are often tolerated. Plant-based alternatives made from soy, oats, and nuts have grown rapidly in many markets.

Environment

Dairy farming is a significant source of methane and other greenhouse-gas emissions, land use, and water consumption, subjects of ongoing research and policy debate alongside animal-welfare questions concerning calf separation and slaughter of male calves.
