Dactyl (poetry)

In poetry, a dactyl is a metrical foot consisting of one stressed syllable followed by two unstressed syllables, as in the words "poetry" and "elephant". The name comes from the Greek daktylos, meaning finger, because a finger has one long segment and two shorter ones.

Classical use

In Greek and Latin quantitative verse, a dactyl is one long syllable followed by two short syllables. Six dactylic feet make the dactylic hexameter of epic poetry, used in Homer's Iliad and Odyssey and Virgil's Aeneid.

English use

English examples include Longfellow's Evangeline and the limerick's anapestic relatives; pure dactylic verse is relatively uncommon in English but appears in comic and light verse.
