Myofascial trigger points, also known simply as trigger points, are discrete, focal, hyperirritable spots located in a taut band of skeletal muscle. Pressure on a trigger point can produce local tenderness and pain that is referred to a characteristic region elsewhere in the body, as well as a transient contraction known as a local twitch response. Trigger points are the defining feature of myofascial pain syndrome.

Characteristics

Trigger points are classified as active, which produce spontaneous or easily provoked symptoms, or latent, which are tender only on palpation. Janet Travell, physician to President John F. Kennedy, and David Simons systematised the concept in their influential manual Myofascial Pain and Dysfunction: The Trigger Point Manual. Proposed mechanisms include sustained sarcomere contraction at motor endplates, local ischemia and sensitisation of nociceptors.

Treatment and controversy

Treatments include manual pressure and stretching, dry needling, trigger point injections, ultrasound and spray-and-stretch techniques. The scientific status of trigger points remains debated: critics note the absence of a gold-standard diagnostic test and variable inter-rater reliability of palpation, while proponents cite imaging and biochemical studies of the trigger point milieu showing lowered pH and elevated algogenic substances.
