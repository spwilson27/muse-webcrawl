Geometric progression

Areas of squares in geometric progression, illustrating growth by a constant ratio.

In mathematics, a geometric progression, also known as a geometric sequence, is a sequence of non-zero numbers where each term after the first is found by multiplying the previous one by a fixed, non-zero number called the common ratio. For example, the sequence 2, 6, 18, 54, ... is a geometric progression with common ratio 3. Similarly 10, 5, 2.5, 1.25, ... is a geometric sequence with common ratio 1/2.

Formulas

If the first term is a and the common ratio is r, then the n-th term is an = a·rn−1. The sum of the first n terms is Sn = a(1 − rn)/(1 − r) for r ≠ 1. When |r| < 1, the infinite series converges to a/(1 − r).

Properties

The terms of a geometric progression correspond to an exponential function. The geometric mean of two terms equidistant from an intermediate term equals that term. Geometric growth appears in compound interest, population models, and radioactive decay (with a ratio below one).

History

Geometric progressions were studied in ancient Egyptian and Babylonian mathematics; the Rhind Mathematical Papyrus contains a problem summing a geometric series of powers of 7. Euclid's Elements, Book IX, proves the formula for the sum of a geometric series.
