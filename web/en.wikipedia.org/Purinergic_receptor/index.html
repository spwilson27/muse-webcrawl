Purinergic receptor

Purinergic receptors are a family of cell-surface receptors activated by purines and related molecules, principally adenosine and the nucleotides ATP, ADP, UTP, and UDP. They mediate a wide range of physiological processes in the nervous, cardiovascular, immune, and other systems.

Classes

The receptors are divided into two main classes. P1 receptors respond to adenosine and comprise four G protein-coupled subtypes: A1, A2A, A2B, and A3. P2 receptors respond to nucleotides and are subdivided into the ionotropic P2X receptors — ligand-gated ion channels, seven subtypes — and the metabotropic P2Y receptors, eight G protein-coupled subtypes.

Functions

Extracellular ATP acts as a neurotransmitter and as a danger signal released from damaged cells, while adenosine generally exerts modulatory and protective effects, for example slowing heart rate and promoting sleep. Purinergic signalling is involved in pain perception, inflammation, platelet aggregation, smooth muscle contraction, and bone remodelling.

Clinical significance

Several important drugs act on purinergic receptors: caffeine antagonises adenosine receptors, the antiplatelet agent clopidogrel blocks the P2Y12 receptor after metabolic activation, and adenosine itself is used to treat certain cardiac arrhythmias.
