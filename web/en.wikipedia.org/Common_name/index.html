Common name

A common name (also called a vernacular name, folk name, or trivial name) is a name for a taxon or organism that is based in everyday language, as opposed to the scientific name used in binomial nomenclature. The common name "robin", for example, refers to entirely different birds in Europe (Erithacus rubecula) and North America (Turdus migratorius).

Characteristics

Common names arise locally, vary between languages and regions, and may apply one name to several species or several names to one species. They are not governed by the international codes of nomenclature, though organisations such as ornithological unions and the Entomological Society of America maintain standardised lists of official common names for their fields.

Uses and limits

Common names are essential for public communication, field guides, legislation, and trade, and often preserve ecological or cultural knowledge. Their ambiguity, however, makes scientific names indispensable in research: a binomial is unique, stable, and the same in every language.

Chemistry

The term is also used in chemistry, where a "trivial name" such as water or baking soda coexists with the systematic IUPAC name.
