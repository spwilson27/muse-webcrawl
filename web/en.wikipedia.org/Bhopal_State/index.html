Bhopal State

Established1707
StatusPrincely state of British India
CapitalBhopal
Merged1949 (into Madhya Bharat, India)
Notable rulersThe Begums of Bhopal

Bhopal State was a princely state in central India, founded in 1707 by the Afghan adventurer Dost Mohammad Khan. It became a British protectorate by treaty in 1818 and remained one of the larger princely states until Indian independence.

The Begums

Bhopal is remarkable for its succession of female rulers. Four women — Qudsia Begum, Sikandar Begum, Shah Jahan Begum and Sultan Jahan Begum — ruled the state for much of the period from 1819 to 1926, overseeing administrative reform, railways, education and the construction of landmarks such as the Taj-ul-Masajid mosque.

Accession

The last Nawab, Hamidullah Khan, acceded to India in 1949 after a period of negotiation, and Bhopal became a Part C state before being merged into Madhya Pradesh in 1956.
