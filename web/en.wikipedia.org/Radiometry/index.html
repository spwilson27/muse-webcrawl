Radiometry

Radiometry is a set of techniques for measuring electromagnetic radiation, including visible light. Radiometric techniques in optics characterize the distribution of the radiation's power in space, as opposed to photometric techniques, which weight the radiation by the sensitivity of the human eye. Radiometry is important in astronomy, remote sensing, illumination engineering, and computer graphics.

Radiant flux emitted, reflected, transmitted, or received per unit area and solid angle underlies the basic quantities of radiometry.

Integral relations

The quantities are related by integration over area and solid angle. For example, radiant flux is the integral of radiance over the emitting area and the hemisphere of directions, weighted by the cosine of the angle from the surface normal. Radiance is conserved along rays in a lossless medium, a fact fundamental to optical system design and to rendering algorithms in computer graphics.

Applications

In remote sensing, calibrated radiometric measurements from satellites monitor climate, vegetation, and ocean color. In computer graphics, the rendering equation formulated by James Kajiya in 1986 expresses image formation in radiometric terms. Radiometry also underpins photovoltaics, laser safety standards, and thermal imaging.
