The National High Magnetic Field Laboratory (MagLab) is a facility at Florida State University in Tallahassee, Florida, that is operated by a consortium of Florida State University, the University of Florida and Los Alamos National Laboratory. It is the largest and highest-powered magnet laboratory in the world and is funded primarily by the National Science Foundation.

Facilities

The laboratory operates three sites: Tallahassee, which houses DC field magnets including the 45-tesla hybrid magnet — long the world's most powerful continuous-field magnet; Gainesville at the University of Florida, which specializes in ultra-low temperature and high-field magnetic resonance; and Los Alamos, which operates pulsed-field magnets reaching up to 100 tesla in short bursts.

Research

Thousands of visiting scientists use the magnets each year for research in physics, chemistry, biology, materials science and engineering. Record fields enable studies of superconductivity, quantum phenomena, and the structure of proteins and other complex materials, and the lab also develops new magnet technology, including high-temperature superconducting cables.
