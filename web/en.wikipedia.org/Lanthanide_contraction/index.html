Lanthanide contraction

The lanthanide contraction is the greater-than-expected decrease in atomic and ionic radii of the elements in the lanthanide series from atomic number 57 (lanthanum) to 71 (lutetium), beyond what would be expected from a mere increase in atomic number within other periods.

Cause

The contraction arises because the 4f electrons being added across the series shield the increasing nuclear charge very poorly for one another. As each element adds a proton and a 4f electron, the effective nuclear charge experienced by the outer electrons increases, pulling the electron cloud inward. The ionic radius of the trivalent ions (Ln3+) falls from about 103 pm for La3+ to about 86 pm for Lu3+.

Consequences

The lanthanide contraction has profound chemical consequences. It makes the later lanthanides very similar in size and chemistry to one another, rendering their separation difficult; it causes the post-lanthanide 5d elements hafnium and tantalum to have almost the same radii as their 4d congeners zirconium and niobium (e.g., Zr4+ ≈ 84 pm, Hf4+ ≈ 83 pm), making those pairs notoriously hard to separate. It also contributes to the high densities of the 5d transition metals and to the similarity between yttrium and the heavy lanthanides such as holmium and erbium.

A related phenomenon, the actinide contraction, occurs in the actinide series due to poor shielding by 5f electrons.
