MASH

MASH most commonly refers to a Mobile Army Surgical Hospital, a United States Army medical unit concept developed after the Second World War to bring surgical care close to the front lines. MASH units were first deployed in the Korean War, where they achieved high survival rates for wounded soldiers who reached them alive.

In popular culture

The concept became famous through Richard Hooker's 1968 novel MASH: A Novel About Three Army Doctors, Robert Altman's 1970 film M*A*S*H, and the long-running television series M*A*S*H (1972–1983), which followed the staff of the fictional 4077th MASH during the Korean War. The series finale, "Goodbye, Farewell and Amen", was the most-watched single episode of television in the United States at the time of its broadcast.

Legacy

The U.S. Army gradually replaced MASH units with Combat Support Hospitals, and the last MASH unit was deactivated in 2006. The principles of forward surgical care pioneered by the MASH program continue in modern military medicine.
