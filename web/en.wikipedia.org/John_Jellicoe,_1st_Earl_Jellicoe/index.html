Admiral of the Fleet John Rushworth Jellicoe, 1st Earl Jellicoe, GCB, OM, GCVO (5 December 1859 – 20 November 1935) was a Royal Navy officer who commanded the British Grand Fleet at the Battle of Jutland in 1916 and later served as First Sea Lord and Governor-General of New Zealand.

Early career

Jellicoe entered the Royal Navy in 1872 and specialised in gunnery. He served in China during the Boxer Rebellion, where he was seriously wounded, and worked closely with Admiral Jackie Fisher on modernising the fleet.

Jutland

At the outbreak of the First World War Jellicoe took command of the Grand Fleet. At Jutland (31 May – 1 June 1916), the only great battleship clash of the war, his deployment of the fleet forced the German High Seas Fleet to retreat; his caution about torpedo risks was criticised, but Britain retained command of the sea. Churchill called him "the only man on either side who could lose the war in an afternoon".

Later career

As First Sea Lord (1916–1917) Jellicoe was slow to adopt convoys against the U-boat campaign and was removed by Lloyd George at Christmas 1917. He later served as Governor-General of New Zealand (1920–1924) and was created Earl Jellicoe in 1925.
