Aldgate

Aldgate is an area of central London and the name of one of the gates in the former London Wall, which gave its name to a ward of the City of London. The gate stood at the junction of Aldgate High Street, Leadenhall Street, and Fenchurch Street, on the road east out of the City towards Colchester and Essex.

History

The gate, of possible Roman origin, was rebuilt several times and finally demolished in 1761 as an obstruction to traffic. Geoffrey Chaucer lived in rooms above the gate in the 1370s–1380s while writing some of his early works. In the late 19th century the surrounding streets were associated with London's Jewish community and with the Whitechapel murders of 1888 — Mitre Square, where Catherine Eddowes was killed, lies just inside the old gate's line.

Today

The Aldgate area, at the eastern edge of the City's financial district, has been reshaped by office development and public-realm schemes such as Aldgate Square, opened in 2018. It is served by Aldgate and Aldgate East Underground stations. The name is usually explained from Old English ealu-geat, "ale gate", or as "old gate".
