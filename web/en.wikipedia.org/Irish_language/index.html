Irish (Standard Irish: Gaeilge), also called Irish Gaelic, is a Goidelic language of the Celtic family, native to Ireland. It was the dominant language of the Irish people for most of their recorded history, and the Irish brought it with them to other regions, notably Scotland and the Isle of Man, where it gave rise to Scottish Gaelic and Manx. Irish has the oldest vernacular literature of any language in western Europe.

History and decline

Irish is attested from Primitive Irish ogham inscriptions of the fourth century AD through Old and Middle Irish manuscript literature. It remained the majority language until the nineteenth century, when a combination of the national schools, official discouragement and above all the Great Famine — which fell hardest on Irish-speaking districts — accelerated a rapid shift to English. By 1891 Irish speakers were a minority concentrated in the western districts later designated the Gaeltacht.

Revival and status

The Gaelic League (founded 1893) led a cultural revival, and after independence in 1922 Irish became the first official language of the Irish state, compulsory in schools and required for many public jobs. Despite this support, the number of habitual speakers continued to shrink, and UNESCO classifies Irish as "definitely endangered". About 40% of Ireland's population report some ability in the language, but daily speakers outside the education system number only in the tens of thousands, concentrated in Gaeltacht areas of Donegal, Mayo, Galway, Kerry, Cork, Waterford and Meath. Irish-language media include the television channel TG4 and the radio service Raidió na Gaeltachta, and Irish has been a full official language of the European Union since 2007 (with a derogation that ended in 2022).

Features

Irish is a VSO (verb–subject–object) language with initial consonant mutations (lenition and eclipsis), a two-gender noun system, and a distinctive orthography in which letter combinations mark broad and slender consonant quality. Its modern standard, An Caighdeán Oifigiúil, coexists with three main dialect groups: Ulster, Connacht and Munster.
