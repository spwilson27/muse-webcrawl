Ernesto Cesàro

Born: 12 March 1859, Naples, Italy
Died: 12 September 1906, Torre Annunziata, Italy
Fields: Mathematics

Ernesto Cesàro (12 March 1859 – 12 September 1906) was an Italian mathematician who worked in the field of differential geometry. He is best known for the Cesàro summation method for assigning sums to divergent series.

Work

Cesàro wrote a celebrated textbook on intrinsic geometry and contributed to number theory, divergent series, and probability. Cesàro summation defines the sum of a series as the limit of the average of its partial sums, and agrees with the ordinary sum for convergent series while assigning finite values to some divergent ones, such as Grandi's series.

Cesàro's theorem

In probability theory, Cesàro's theorem (1888) states that the probability that two random integers are coprime is 6/π². He also gave his name to the Cesàro curve, a fractal construction related to the Lévy C curve.

Death

Cesàro died in 1906 at Torre Annunziata while attempting to rescue his son Manlio from drowning.
