Rashtrapati Bhavan

LocationRajpath, New Delhi, India
Construction1912–1929
ArchitectEdwin Lutyens
Residence ofPresident of India

Rashtrapati Bhavan ("Presidential House") is the official residence of the President of India, located at the western end of Rajpath (now Kartavya Path) in New Delhi. It is one of the largest presidential residences in the world, comprising a vast mansion with 340 rooms across four floors, set within an estate of about 320 acres that includes the famous Mughal Gardens (now Amrit Udyan).

History

The building was designed by British architect Edwin Lutyens as the residence of the Viceroy of India, after the decision to move the capital of British India from Calcutta to Delhi. Construction began in 1912 and was completed in 1929; the first Viceroy to occupy it was Lord Irwin. After Indian independence in 1947 it became Government House, and in 1950 it was renamed Rashtrapati Bhavan when Rajendra Prasad, India's first President, took up residence.

Architecture

The palace blends Western classical forms with Indian architectural motifs. Its most distinctive feature is a large copper dome inspired by the Great Stupa at Sanchi, and it incorporates elements such as chhatris, jaalis and chujjas drawn from Indian traditions. The Durbar Hall, Ashoka Hall and the marble halls are used for state ceremonies, including swearing-in ceremonies and investitures.

Mughal Gardens

The gardens behind the palace combine Mughal charbagh layouts with English flower gardens, and are opened to the public each spring.
