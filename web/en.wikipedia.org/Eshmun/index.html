Eshmun (Phoenician: ʿsmn) was a Phoenician god of healing and the tutelary god of Sidon. His name is thought to derive from a Semitic root meaning "oil", used in healing anointment, or from the word for "eight", as the eighth son in one version of his myth.

Mythology

According to the account preserved by Damascius and retold by Photius, Eshmun was a youth from Berytus (Beirut) who caught the attention of the goddess Astronoe (identified with Astarte). Pursued by her, he castrated himself with an axe and died; the goddess revived him with warmth and made him a god. This myth of death and rebirth linked him to other dying-and-rising deities of the Levant.

Cult

The great Temple of Eshmun near Sidon, built from the 7th century BCE onwards by kings including Eshmunazar II and Bodashtart, stood beside springs at Bustan el-Sheikh and remained a major pilgrimage and healing sanctuary into the Roman period. The Greeks identified Eshmun with Asclepius, and his cult spread across the Phoenician and Punic world, including Carthage and Sardinia.
