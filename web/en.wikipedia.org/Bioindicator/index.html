Bioindicator

A bioindicator is any species (an indicator species) or group of species whose function, population, or status can reveal the qualitative status of the environment. Bioindicators can tell us about the cumulative effects of different pollutants in an ecosystem and how long a problem may have persisted, information physical or chemical testing cannot provide on its own.

Types

Plant indicators, such as lichens and mosses, are sensitive to air pollution and heavy metals. Animal indicators include aquatic macroinvertebrates — mayfly, stonefly and caddisfly larvae are intolerant of water pollution, so their presence signals good water quality — as well as amphibians, whose permeable skin makes them sensitive to environmental change. Microbial indicators, such as coliform bacteria, are used to assess water safety.

Uses

Biomonitoring with indicator species is used in environmental impact assessment, conservation biology and the management of protected areas. The canary in the coal mine is a classic historical example of a sentinel species used to warn of toxic gas.
