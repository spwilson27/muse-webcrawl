Imad al-Din Zengi

Imad al-Din Zengi (Arabic: عماد الدین زنكي; c. 1085 – 1146), commonly rendered in Western sources as Zengi, was the Turkish atabeg of Mosul and Aleppo and the founder of the Zengid dynasty. His name is also transliterated Imad ad-Din, Emad ed-Din, and ʿImād al-Dīn. Zengi earned his place in history as the Muslim commander whose capture of Edessa in 1144 triggered the Second Crusade.

Career

The son of Aq Sunqur al-Hajib, the executed governor of Aleppo, Zengi rose through service to the Seljuk sultans of Iraq. As atabeg of Mosul (from 1127) and master of Aleppo (from 1128), he built a powerful principality spanning northern Iraq and Syria and waged war on the Artuqids, the Burid emirs of Damascus, and the Crusader states.

Capture of Edessa

In late 1144 Zengi exploited the absence of Count Joscelin II to besiege Edessa, the weakest of the Crusader states. The city fell on 24 December 1144, and the citadel days later. It was the first major Crusader capital to be retaken by Muslim forces and marked a turning point in the history of the Crusades; the news moved Pope Eugenius III to proclaim a new crusade in 1145.

Assassination and succession

Zengi was murdered in his sleep in 1146 by a slave he had threatened. His empire split: his second son Nur ad-Din succeeded in Aleppo and carried on the jihad against the Franks, unifying Syria and paving the way for Saladin's reconquest of Jerusalem in 1187.
