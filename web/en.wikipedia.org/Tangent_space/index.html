Tangent space

In mathematics, the tangent space of a manifold at a point is a vector space that generalises the notion of a tangent line to a curve or a tangent plane to a surface. It captures the directions in which one can move infinitesimally from that point while staying on the manifold, and it provides the linear approximation to the manifold near the point.

Definitions

Several equivalent definitions exist. For a smooth manifold embedded in Euclidean space, the tangent space at a point is the affine subspace tangent to the manifold there. Intrinsically, a tangent vector can be defined as an equivalence class of curves through the point (two curves being equivalent if they have the same velocity in any chart), or algebraically as a derivation: a linear map on the ring of smooth functions satisfying the Leibniz rule at the point. The dimension of the tangent space equals the dimension of the manifold.

Tangent bundle and vector fields

The disjoint union of the tangent spaces at all points of a manifold forms the tangent bundle, itself a manifold of twice the dimension. A smooth choice of a tangent vector at every point is a vector field. The differential (pushforward) of a smooth map between manifolds is a linear map between their tangent spaces, generalising the derivative of a function.

Applications

Tangent spaces are fundamental to differential geometry, the study of geodesics and curvature, and to physics: in general relativity, spacetime is a manifold whose tangent spaces carry the Minkowski metric of special relativity, and in mechanics the tangent bundle of a configuration space is the phase space of velocities and positions.
