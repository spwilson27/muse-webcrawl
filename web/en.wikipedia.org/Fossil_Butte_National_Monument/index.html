Fossil Butte National Monument

Fossil Butte National Monument is a United States National Monument in Lincoln County, Wyoming, managed by the National Park Service. It preserves part of the Green River Formation, one of the world's richest fossil deposits, centred on the sediments of Fossil Lake, an Eocene lake that existed about 52 million years ago. The monument protects over 8,000 acres of high desert sagebrush and the butte from which it takes its name.

Fossil Lake

During the Eocene epoch, subtropical Fossil Lake covered about 1,500 square kilometres in present-day Wyoming. Fine limestone sediments accumulating on its bottom entombed dead organisms in extraordinary detail, preserving complete articulated skeletons and even soft tissues. The fossils include more than twenty species of fish (herring-like Knightia is the Wyoming state fossil), stingrays, crocodiles, turtles, the earliest known bats (Icaronycteris and Onychonycteris), birds, insects, and plants such as palms. Periodic mass die-offs, perhaps from algal blooms or overturn of the lake's waters, produced dense layers of perfectly preserved fish.

History and visitor centre

Fossils from the area were collected from the 1870s onward; commercial quarries just outside the monument still supply fossil fish sold worldwide. The monument was authorised in 1972. Its visitor centre displays more than 300 fossils, including a 13-foot crocodile and a large rotating aquarium-style exhibit of fossil fish, and offers ranger programmes and a quarry talk during the summer season. Collecting fossils inside the monument is prohibited, but visitors can observe fossil preparation demonstrations.
