Cross-country skiing (sport)

Cross-country skiing as a competitive sport consists of ski races over groomed courses of varying length, contested in two techniques: the classic style, in which skiers move in parallel tracks using a diagonal stride, and the freestyle or skating technique, in which they push off with a motion similar to ice skating. It has been part of the Winter Olympic Games since the first edition in 1924 (men) and 1952 (women), and is governed internationally by the International Ski and Snowboard Federation (FIS).

Events

Standard competition formats include individual interval-start races (10 km and 15 km), mass-start races up to 50 km, the skiathlon (combining both techniques), sprint races of about 1.5 km, team sprints, and 4 × 10 km (men) and 4 × 5 km (women) relays. The FIS Cross-Country World Cup has been held annually since the early 1980s, and the Tour de Ski stage race is a highlight of the season.

History

Competitive skiing developed in 19th-century Norway and Sweden, where skiing was a practical means of winter transport and a military skill. Norwegian skier Sondre Norheim pioneered modern skiing techniques and equipment. Scandinavians dominated the sport's early decades, and Norway remains its most successful nation, with stars such as Bjørn Dæhlie and Marit Bjørgen among the most decorated Winter Olympians of all time.
