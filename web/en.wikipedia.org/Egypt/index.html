Egypt, officially the Arab Republic of Egypt, is a transcontinental country spanning north-eastern Africa and the Sinai Peninsula in western Asia. It is bordered by the Mediterranean Sea to the north, Libya to the west, Sudan to the south, the Red Sea to the east, and the Gaza Strip and Israel to the north-east. The great majority of its population lives along the fertile banks of the Nile — the longest river system in the world — and in its delta, amid a country otherwise dominated by desert.

History

Egypt has one of the longest histories of any country. The unification of Upper and Lower Egypt around 3100 BC began three millennia of pharaonic civilisation, which produced the pyramids of Giza, the temples of Luxor and Karnak, and hieroglyphic writing (see Egyptian chronology). Egypt was successively ruled by Persians, Greeks under Alexander the Great and the Ptolemaic dynasty, Romans, and from the seventh century AD by Arab Muslim dynasties including the Fatimids and the Mamluks, followed by the Ottomans. The Suez Canal opened in 1869. After a period as a British protectorate and then a kingdom, the monarchy was overthrown in 1952 and a republic proclaimed. Under Gamal Abdel Nasser Egypt nationalised the Suez Canal in 1956; Anwar Sadat made peace with Israel in 1979, following the October War of 1973. Hosni Mubarak ruled from 1981 until his resignation during the 2011 Egyptian revolution.

Geography and economy

Outside the Nile valley and delta, most of Egypt is part of the Sahara Desert; the Western Desert contains a chain of oases, while the Eastern Desert rises to the Red Sea hills and the Sinai hosts Mount Catherine, the country's highest peak. Egypt's economy ranks among the largest in Africa and the Middle East, drawing on petroleum and natural gas, agriculture in the Nile valley, tourism to its ancient monuments and Red Sea resorts, remittances from Egyptians abroad, and tolls from the Suez Canal, which links the Mediterranean with the Red Sea and the Indian Ocean.

Culture

Cairo, founded in 969 AD, is the largest city of the Arab world and a centre of Arabic literature, film and scholarship; al-Azhar University, founded in the tenth century, is one of the oldest universities in the world. Egypt is predominantly Sunni Muslim, with a substantial Coptic Christian minority. Its cuisine centres on bread, legumes such as ful medames and falafel (ta'ameya), and rice dishes such as koshari.
