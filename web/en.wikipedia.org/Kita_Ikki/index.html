Kita Ikki (1883–1937), born Kita Terujirō, was a Japanese author, intellectual and political philosopher whose writings expounded the ideology that inspired the radical young officers of the Imperial Japanese Army in the 1930s. His vision of a nationalist, emperor-centred, militarily-led socialist reorganisation of Japan made him the most influential ideologue of the Shōwa Restoration movement.

Ideas

Kita began as a socialist sympathiser and supporter of the Chinese Revolution of 1911, in which he participated alongside Song Jiaoren. His mature doctrine, set out in the Outline Plan for the Reorganisation of Japan (1919, banned and circulated in manuscript), called for a coup d’état by the army, suspension of the Meiji constitution, nationalisation of major industries with limits on private wealth, redistribution of land, and an expansionist foreign policy to liberate Asia from Western imperialism under a purified imperial Japan.

Death and influence

Although Kita took no part in the February 26 Incident of 1936, the army officers who led the attempted coup revered him, and he was convicted of ideological complicity in secret proceedings and executed in August 1937. Historians continue to debate whether his thought is best classed as fascist, national-socialist or a uniquely Japanese revolutionary nationalism.
