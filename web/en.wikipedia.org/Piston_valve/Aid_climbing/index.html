Aid climbing

Aid climbing is a style of rock climbing in which the climber makes progress by standing on or pulling on equipment placed in the rock, rather than using only natural hand- and footholds as in free climbing. It is used on steep, blank big-wall routes where free climbing is impossible or extremely difficult.

Technique

The leader places protection — nuts, camming devices, pitons, or specialised hooks and copperheads — tests each placement, then clips in etriers (webbing ladders) and ascends them before placing the next piece. Aid routes are graded from A0 to A5, reflecting the security of the placements and the consequences of a fall.
