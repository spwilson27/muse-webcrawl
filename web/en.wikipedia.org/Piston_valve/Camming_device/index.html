Camming device

A spring-loaded camming device (SLCD, often called a "cam" or "Friend") is a piece of rock-climbing protection consisting of two to four cam lobes mounted on an axle with a spring and a trigger. Placed in a crack, the device converts a downward pull into an outward force against the rock walls, holding a fall.

History

The principle derives from the constant-angle logarithmic spiral. Ray Jardine developed the first commercially successful units, marketed as Friends in the late 1970s, revolutionising crack climbing in places such as Yosemite. Modern designs include dual-axle units with wider expansion ranges.
