Piton

A piton is a flat or angled metal spike, usually of steel, that rock and ice climbers drive into a crack or seam in rock (or into ice) with a hammer, to serve as an anchor for protection or aid. A rope is clipped through an eye in the piton's head with a carabiner, so that a fall can be arrested or the climber can stand in a sling attached to the piton.

Types and use

Pitons come in many shapes for different cracks: thin blades and "knifeblades" for hairline seams, Lost Arrow pitons for medium cracks, and wide angle pitons for larger fissures; softer iron pitons deform to grip irregular European limestone, while hard chrome-molybdenum pitons suit American granite. Ringed and bong pitons serve still wider placements.

History and decline

Pitons were standard climbing equipment from the early twentieth century through the 1960s and made possible many classic ascents in the Alps, Dolomites and Yosemite. Repeated hammering and removal, however, scars rock, so from the 1970s "clean climbing" ethics and the advent of removable nuts and camming devices largely displaced pitons on free climbs. They remain in use on some aid climbs, big walls, winter routes and remote alpine terrain where cracks are too thin or iced for other gear.
