Pitch (music)

In music, pitch is the perceived fundamental frequency of a sound, the quality that allows sounds to be ordered on a scale from "low" to "high". It is one of the principal attributes of musical tones, along with duration, loudness and timbre. Pitch is closely related to frequency — measured in hertz — but is a perceptual property: the ear judges a tone's pitch mainly from its fundamental frequency, on an approximately logarithmic scale.

Standards and notation

Pitches are named with letters (A through G) plus accidentals, and organized into octaves: doubling the frequency raises a pitch by one octave. Modern concert pitch usually sets A4 (the A above middle C) to 440 Hz, an international standard adopted in the twentieth century, though orchestras and historical performers use values from about 415 to 466 Hz. In written notation, pitch is shown by a note's vertical position on the staff, modified by key signature and accidentals.

Perception

Most listeners perceive pitch relationships rather than absolute frequencies; people with perfect pitch, a rare ability, can identify tones without a reference. Pitch perception depends on the harmonic spectrum as well as the fundamental — the brain can infer a "missing fundamental" — and very low or high frequencies are judged less precisely.
