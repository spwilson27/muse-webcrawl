Mimesis (book)

Mimesis: The Representation of Reality in Western Literature
AuthorErich Auerbach
Original titleMimesis: Dargestellte Wirklichkeit in der abendländischen Literatur
Published1946 (Bern); English translation 1953
TranslatorWillard R. Trask
SubjectLiterary criticism, comparative literature

Mimesis: The Representation of Reality in Western Literature is a work of literary criticism by the German philologist Erich Auerbach, published in 1946. Written in Istanbul, where Auerbach had fled from Nazi Germany, it traces the changing representation of reality in Western literature from Homer and the Bible through Dante, Shakespeare, Cervantes and Flaubert to Virginia Woolf. It is regarded as one of the most influential works of twentieth-century comparative literature and the founding text of Auerbach's method of close stylistic reading.

Auerbach's study spans three thousand years of Western writing.

Method and argument

Each of the book's twenty chapters begins with a short passage (an Ansatzpunkt, or starting point) and unfolds from its syntax, diction and narrative technique a picture of the whole culture's way of apprehending reality. The famous first chapter, "Odysseus' Scar", contrasts Homer's fully externalised style — everything in the foreground, evenly illuminated — with the biblical narrative of the binding of Isaac, terse and "fraught with background", demanding interpretation. Auerbach argues that classical antiquity enforced a separation of styles (high style for tragedy and epic heroes, low style for comedy and everyday life), which the Christian doctrine of the Incarnation broke down: in the Bible and later in Dante's Comedy, ordinary and lowly people are treated with full tragic seriousness. The history of Western realism is, for Auerbach, the gradual conquest of this "mixing of styles", culminating in the nineteenth-century novel and, in the final chapter on Woolf's To the Lighthouse, in modernist narration of consciousness.

Composition and reception

Auerbach wrote the book between 1942 and 1945 while teaching at Istanbul University, famously noting in the epilogue that the absence of a rich research library obliged him to rely on the texts themselves. Published in Bern in 1946 and translated into English by Willard R. Trask in 1953, Mimesis helped establish comparative literature in the postwar academy. Critics have praised its learning and humane scope while questioning its canon (few women writers, no literature outside Europe) and its teleology of realism. Edward Said, who championed Auerbach, treated the book as an exemplary work of exilic humanism.
