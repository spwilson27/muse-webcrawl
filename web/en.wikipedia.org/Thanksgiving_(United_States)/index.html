Thanksgiving (United States)

A traditional Thanksgiving dinner

Thanksgiving is a national holiday celebrated in the United States on the fourth Thursday of November. It originated as a harvest festival and is centred on a large family meal, traditionally featuring roast turkey.

History

The holiday's origins are conventionally traced to the harvest feast shared in 1621 by the Plymouth colonists and the Wampanoag people. Days of thanksgiving were observed sporadically thereafter; George Washington proclaimed the first national day of thanksgiving in 1789. In 1863, during the Civil War, Abraham Lincoln proclaimed a national Thanksgiving Day to be held each November, following a long campaign by writer Sarah Josepha Hale. The date was briefly moved by Franklin D. Roosevelt in 1939 (the "Franksgiving" episode) before Congress fixed the fourth Thursday of November in 1941.

Traditions

The Thanksgiving meal typically includes turkey with stuffing, mashed potatoes, gravy, sweet potatoes, cranberry sauce, and pumpkin pie. Other customs include the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade in New York (held since 1924), televised American football, the presidential "pardoning" of a turkey, and volunteer work for the poor. The following day, Black Friday, begins the Christmas shopping season. For many Native Americans, the day is also observed as a National Day of Mourning, commemorating the consequences of colonization.
