Kingdom of Kush
Period: c. 1070 BC – AD 350
Capitals: Napata; later Meroë
Region: Nubia, Nile valley of present-day Sudan

The Kingdom of Kush was an ancient African kingdom in Nubia, centred along the Nile valley in what is now northern Sudan and southern Egypt. Emerging after the collapse of Egypt's New Kingdom around 1070 BC, Kush endured for more than a thousand years, at its height conquering Egypt itself and ruling as its Twenty-fifth Dynasty.

Napatan period

From their capital at Napata, near the Fourth Cataract, Kushite kings such as Kashta and Piye expanded northward; by about 744 BC Piye had subdued Egypt, and his successors — Shabaka, Shebitku and Taharqa — ruled a double kingdom from the Mediterranean to the Blue Nile. Defeated by Assyrian invasions in the 660s BC, the Kushites withdrew to Nubia but retained Egyptian-influenced religion, hieroglyphic writing and pyramid burial.

Meroitic period

Around the 4th century BC the royal court shifted south to Meroë, a centre of ironworking and trade between Africa and the Mediterranean. The Meroitic period saw the development of an indigenous alphabetic script (still only partly understood), powerful ruling queens — the Kandakes — and conflicts with Roman Egypt. The kingdom declined by the 4th century AD and Meroë was abandoned, traditionally after an invasion by the Aksumite king Ezana.

Legacy

Sudan contains more pyramids than Egypt — over 200 Kushite pyramids at sites including Meroë, Nuri and El-Kurru, now recognised as UNESCO World Heritage Sites.
