A brigade combat team (BCT) is the basic deployable unit of manoeuvre in the United States Army. Created during the Army's modular reorganization of the 2000s, a BCT is a combined-arms formation of roughly 4,400 soldiers that contains its own combat, fires, reconnaissance, engineer, and sustainment elements, allowing it to deploy and fight independently under a division headquarters.

Types

There are three types of BCT. Armored brigade combat teams (ABCTs) are built around Abrams tanks and Bradley fighting vehicles. Infantry brigade combat teams (IBCTs) are lighter foot-mobile formations, including airborne and air assault variants. Stryker brigade combat teams (SBCTs) are medium-weight forces mounted on the eight-wheeled Stryker family of vehicles, designed for rapid deployment.

Composition

A typical BCT has three combined-arms or infantry battalions (or Stryker infantry battalions), a cavalry (reconnaissance) squadron, a field artillery battalion, a brigade engineer battalion, and a brigade support battalion, all under a brigade headquarters company. Modular conversion grew the number of brigades available for rotations to Iraq and Afghanistan by restructuring divisions into self-contained teams.

Recent changes

As the Army refocused on large-scale combat operations against peer adversaries in the 2020s, divisions regained prominence as the principal tactical headquarters, and some BCT structure and enabler arrangements were revised, though the BCT remains the Army's principal brigade-level formation.
