Port Royal

Port Royal is a village located at the end of the Palisadoes, at the mouth of Kingston Harbour, in south-eastern Jamaica. Founded by the Spanish and captured by the English in 1655, it became one of the largest and wealthiest ports in the English Americas and a notorious haven for privateers and pirates, earning the reputation as the "wickedest city on Earth." Much of the town sank beneath the sea in the great earthquake of 1692.

History

Situated on a deep-water harbour near the shipping lanes of the Spanish Main, Port Royal grew rich on trade, privateering commissions against Spain, and the plunder brought in by buccaneers such as Henry Morgan. By the 1680s it had a population of some 6,500–10,000 and hundreds of taverns and brothels. On 7 June 1692 a massive earthquake caused about two-thirds of the town — some 13 hectares — to subside into the harbour, killing around 2,000 people; fire and disease killed many more in the aftermath.

Later years and archaeology

The town was rebuilt on a smaller scale but never recovered its commercial primacy; Kingston, across the harbour, took over its trade. Port Royal instead became a Royal Navy station, where Horatio Nelson served as a young officer, and was further damaged by an earthquake in 1907. Today it is a quiet fishing village. The submerged ruins of the sunken city are among the most important underwater archaeological sites in the Americas, preserving the layout of a seventeenth-century colonial port, and Jamaica has sought World Heritage recognition for the site.
