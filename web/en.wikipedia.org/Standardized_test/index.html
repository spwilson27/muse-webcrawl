Standardized test

A standardized test is a test that is administered and scored in a consistent, or "standard", manner. Standardized tests are designed so that the questions, conditions for administering, scoring procedures, and interpretations are consistent and predetermined, allowing results to be compared across test takers.

Types

Standardized tests include achievement tests, aptitude tests such as the SAT and ACT used in US college admissions, professional licensing examinations, and international assessments such as PISA. They are typically multiple-choice or short-answer and are often machine-scored.

History

Standardised examinations have roots in imperial China's civil service examinations. In the West, standardized testing expanded in the 19th and 20th centuries with mass schooling, intelligence testing, and large-scale university admissions.

Criticism

Critics argue that high-stakes standardized tests encourage teaching to the test, disadvantage some demographic groups, and measure a narrow range of abilities. Defenders argue they provide objective, comparable measures of achievement and can identify inequities between schools. Many universities have adopted test-optional admissions policies.
