Spin–orbit interaction

In quantum mechanics, the spin–orbit interaction (or spin–orbit coupling) is a relativistic interaction of a particle's spin with its orbital motion, which splits spectral lines into fine-structure components. In atoms it arises from the interaction between the electron's magnetic moment and the magnetic field the electron experiences as it moves through the electric field of the nucleus.

In atoms

Spin–orbit coupling explains part of the fine structure of atomic spectra, such as the splitting of the sodium D lines. Its strength grows rapidly with atomic number, approximately as Z⁴, making it much more important in heavy atoms.

In solids

In semiconductors, Rashba and Dresselhaus spin–orbit couplings are central to the field of spintronics, where the electron spin rather than its charge is used to process information.
