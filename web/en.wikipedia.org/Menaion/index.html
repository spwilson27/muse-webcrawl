Menaion

The Menaion (Greek: Μηναῖον; Slavonic: Минїѧ, Miníya) is the liturgical book used by the Eastern Orthodox Church and the Eastern Catholic Churches that follow the Byzantine Rite. It contains the propers — hymns, readings and prayers — for the fixed feasts of the liturgical year, arranged by month, and is therefore normally published in twelve volumes, one for each month from September to August.

Contents

For each day of the year the Menaion supplies the texts for Vespers, Matins (Orthros) and the Divine Liturgy commemorating the saints and feasts of that day: stichera, canons, troparia, kontakia and synaxarion notices summarising the lives of the saints. Days of greater rank receive fuller offices. The services in the Menaion are combined with those of the other liturgical books — the Octoechos for the weekly cycle and the Triodion and Pentecostarion for the movable cycle centred on Easter.

Editions

Printed editions of the Greek Menaion appeared in Venice in the 16th century, and Slavonic editions were produced in Moscow and Kyiv. The standard Slavonic edition is the Moscow "Green Menaion". English translations of the complete twelve volumes have been published in the 20th and 21st centuries, notably by St. John of Kronstadt Press and the Monastery of St. Job of Pochaev in Jordanville.

Related books

The General Menaion provides generic services for classes of saints (apostles, martyrs, hierarchs and so on) for use when a saint's proper service is not available or for local commemorations. The Festal Menaion is a selection containing the services for the twelve Great Feasts.
