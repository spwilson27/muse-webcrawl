Narrative mode

Narrative mode (also known as narrative point of view) is the method by which a narrator communicates a story to the audience — the set of choices about who speaks, what they know, and how they relate to the events narrated. It is a central concern of narratology, the study of narrative structure.

Main modes

The most common modes are classified by grammatical person and by the narrator's knowledge and participation:

  • First person — a character within the story tells it ("I"), and may be a protagonist, a witness, or an unreliable narrator.
  • Second person — the narration addresses the reader or a character as "you", rare outside experimental fiction and gamebooks.
  • Third person — an external narrator ("he/she/they"), who may be omniscient (knowing all thoughts and events), limited (restricted to one character's perspective), or objective (reporting only externals).

Related concepts

Analysts such as Gérard Genette distinguish the question "who speaks?" (voice) from "who sees?" (focalization). Techniques associated with particular modes include stream of consciousness, free indirect discourse, and the epistolary and frame-tale forms.
