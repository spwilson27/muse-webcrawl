The Italian buffalo, also known as the Italian Mediterranean buffalo (Italian: Bufala Mediterranea Italiana), is an Italian breed of water buffalo. It is of the river buffalo type and is the only indigenous water buffalo breed in Italy.

History

Theories about the arrival of the water buffalo in Italy include introduction by the Goths during the migrations after the fall of the Roman Empire, by the Normans from Sicily, or during the Arab invasions. The breed was traditionally raised in the marshy coastal plains of Campania and Lazio — the Pontine Marshes and the Sele plain — where its milk became the raw material for mozzarella di bufala. A herdbook was established in the late 20th century and the breed was officially recognised by the Italian ministry of agriculture.

Characteristics

Italian buffalo are generally black or dark grey with a sparse coat, long curved horns that sweep back and outward, and a large, deep body. Cows weigh roughly 500–600 kg and bulls more. The breed is hardy, long-lived, and well adapted to hot, humid environments and poor forage.

Use

The breed is raised principally for its milk, which has a higher fat and protein content than cow's milk and is used to produce Mozzarella di Bufala Campana, a protected designation of origin (PDO) cheese, as well as ricotta di bufala and other dairy products. Buffalo meat is also marketed, often as a lean specialty product. Annual milk yields average around 2,000–2,800 kg per lactation in recorded herds.
