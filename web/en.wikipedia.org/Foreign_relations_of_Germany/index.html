Foreign relations of Germany

Germany's foreign policy is anchored in European integration and the transatlantic alliance.

The foreign relations of Germany are conducted by the Federal Republic of Germany through the Federal Foreign Office (Auswärtiges Amt), headed by the Federal Minister for Foreign Affairs within the government led by the Chancellor. As Europe's largest economy and a founding member of the European Union, Germany pursues a foreign policy centred on multilateralism, European integration, the transatlantic partnership with the United States and NATO, and a rules-based international order. Germany maintains diplomatic relations with virtually all states and one of the world's largest networks of embassies and consulates.

Post-war foundations

After 1945 the Federal Republic (West Germany) anchored itself in the West: it joined NATO in 1955 and was a founding member of the European Coal and Steel Community and the European Economic Community, reconciling with France through the Élysée Treaty of 1963. Chancellor Willy Brandt's Ostpolitik in the early 1970s normalized relations with the Soviet Union, Poland, and East Germany. Following reunification in 1990 under the Two Plus Four Treaty, Germany regained full sovereignty and confirmed its eastern border with Poland.

Policy since reunification

Unified Germany deepened European integration through the Maastricht and Lisbon treaties and adopted the euro, while gradually expanding its security role: Bundeswehr deployments abroad, once controversial, included Kosovo (1999), Afghanistan (2001–2021), and anti-piracy and training missions. Germany's export-oriented economy made trade and energy central to its external policy, including a longstanding reliance on Russian natural gas via the Nord Stream pipelines — an approach sharply reassessed after Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, when Chancellor Olaf Scholz proclaimed a Zeitenwende ("turning point"), committing a €100 billion defence fund, increased military spending, and heavy weapons deliveries to Ukraine.

Key relationships and roles

The Franco-German partnership is described as the engine of the EU; relations with the United States remain the cornerstone of German security. Germany is the world's third-largest provider of development aid, a major contributor to the United Nations, and an advocate of EU enlargement and of reform of international institutions. Migration policy, notably during the 2015 refugee crisis, and debates over relations with China — Germany's largest trading partner for several years — are recurring themes in German foreign-policy debate.
