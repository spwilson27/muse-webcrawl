Augusto César Sandino (1895–1934)

Augusto César Sandino (full name: Augusto Nicolás Calderón de Sandino y José de María; 18 May 1895 – 21 February 1934) was a Nicaraguan revolutionary and leader of a rebellion between 1927 and 1933 against the United States occupation of Nicaragua. Despite being labeled a "bandit" by the United States government, his exploits made him a hero throughout much of Latin America, where he became a symbol of resistance to United States imperialism.

Early life

Sandino was born in Niquinohomo, Nicaragua, the illegitimate son of a landowner and an indigenous servant. He worked in Honduras, Guatemala, and Mexico, where he was exposed to labour movements and anti-imperialist ideas, before returning to Nicaragua in 1926.

Guerrilla war

When civil war broke out between liberals and conservatives, Sandino refused the 1927 peace settlement brokered by the United States and led the Ejército Defensor de la Soberanía Nacional from the Segovia mountains. His guerrillas fought the US Marine Corps and the Nicaraguan Guardia Nacional until the Marines withdrew in 1933. He is credited with pioneering tactics later associated with guerrilla warfare, and his forces were the target of early aerial bombing campaigns.

Death and legacy

After the election of Juan Bautista Sacasa, Sandino negotiated disarmament, but in February 1934 he was seized and executed on the orders of Anastasio Somoza García, head of the Guardia Nacional. Decades later, the Sandinista National Liberation Front (FSLN), which overthrew the Somoza dynasty in 1979, took its name from him.
