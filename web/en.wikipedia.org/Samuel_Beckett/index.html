Samuel Barclay Beckett (13 April 1906 – 22 December 1989) was an Irish novelist, playwright, short-story writer, theatre director, poet and literary translator. A resident of Paris for most of his adult life, he wrote in both French and English and is regarded as one of the most influential writers of the 20th century. His work — bleak, tragi-comic and formally radical — explores human existence in terms of endurance, failure and the urge to go on, crystallised in his famous line: “Ever tried. Ever failed. No matter. Try again. Fail again. Fail better.”

Life

Beckett was born in Foxrock, County Dublin, into a prosperous Protestant family, and educated at Portora Royal School and Trinity College Dublin, where he read French and Italian. In 1928 he went to Paris as a lecturer at the École Normale Supérieure and entered the circle of James Joyce, whom he assisted and admired. He settled permanently in Paris in 1937. During the Second World War he worked for the French Resistance; after the Gestapo broke up his cell he fled with his partner Suzanne Dechevaux-Dumesnil to the village of Roussillon in unoccupied France, an experience he drew on later.

Work

Beckett’s early fiction, including the novel Murphy (1938), is learned and comic; his masterpieces of prose came after the war in the trilogy Molloy (1951), Malone Dies (1951) and The Unnamable (1953), written in French and progressively stripping away plot, setting and identity. His play En attendant Godot (Paris, 1953; English Waiting for Godot, 1955) — in which two tramps wait by a bare tree for someone who never comes — became the defining work of the Theatre of the Absurd and one of the most famous plays ever written. Later plays include Endgame (1957), Krapp’s Last Tape (1958) and Happy Days (1961); his late prose and drama grow ever more compressed. He received the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1969 “for his writing, which — in new forms for the novel and drama — in the destitution of modern man acquires its elevation.”

Beckett died in Paris in December 1989 and is buried in Montparnasse Cemetery.
