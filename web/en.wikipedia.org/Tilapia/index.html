Tilapia

Tilapia
FamilyCichlidae (cichlids)
Native rangeFresh waters of Africa and the Levant
Key farmed speciesNile tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus)
SignificanceOne of the world's most important food fish; invasive in many regions

Tilapia is the common name for nearly a hundred species of cichlid fish native to fresh waters in Africa and the Middle East. Several species are among the most widely farmed fish in the world, valued for their fast growth, hardiness and mild flavour.

Biology

Tilapias are generally deep-bodied, laterally compressed fish, many of them maternal mouthbrooders: the female carries fertilised eggs and fry in her mouth for protection. They tolerate warm, low-oxygen water and varied diets, feeding largely on algae and plant matter — traits that suit them to pond aquaculture but also make them potent invaders when released.

Aquaculture

Fish farming of tilapia was practised in ancient Egypt, and the Nile tilapia is now produced in millions of tonnes annually, led by China, Indonesia, Egypt, Bangladesh and the Philippines. Tilapia has become one of the most consumed seafood products in the United States and a crucial affordable protein source in the developing world. Escaped farmed tilapia have established feral populations in warm waters worldwide, sometimes harming native ecosystems, and are listed among problematic invasive species in several countries.
