Honda CB750

Manufacturer: Honda
Production: 1969–2007 (with breaks)
Engine: 736 cc air-cooled inline-four (original)
Class: Standard / sport

The Honda CB750 is an air-cooled, transverse, in-line-four-cylinder engine motorcycle made by Honda over several generations from 1969 to 2007, with an upright riding posture and styling resembling the Universal Japanese Motorcycle (UJM) concept it helped define. The original CB750, unveiled at the Tokyo Motor Show in 1968 and released in 1969, is often called the first "superbike": it offered a 736 cc four-cylinder engine, an electric starter, and a front disc brake at a mass-market price, producing 67 horsepower and a top speed around 125 mph (201 km/h).

Impact

The CB750's combination of performance, reliability, and price transformed the motorcycle industry, eclipsing the British manufacturers that had dominated large motorcycles and establishing the template — transverse four, overhead camshafts, disc brakes — for the superbikes that followed. It was named "Machine of the Year" by Motor Cycle News in 1969 and is included in many lists of the most influential motorcycles ever made. Later generations included the DOHC CB750 of 1979 onward, the Nighthawk 750 of the 1990s, and a final retro-styled version sold in Japan in 2007.
