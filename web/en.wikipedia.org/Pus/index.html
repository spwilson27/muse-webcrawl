Pus

Pus is an exudate, typically white-yellow, yellow, or yellow-brown, formed at the site of inflammation during bacterial or fungal infection. It consists of a thin protein-rich liquid (liquor puris) and dead leukocytes — mainly neutrophils — from the body's immune response, together with bacteria and cellular debris.

Formation

When the immune system detects a pyogenic (pus-producing) infection, it recruits large numbers of neutrophils to the site. As these cells die after engulfing pathogens, they accumulate with necrotic tissue to form pus. A localised collection of pus within tissue is an abscess; pus accumulating in a body cavity is an empyema, and a pus-filled lesion on the skin may form a pustule or boil.

Clinical significance

The classical medical maxim ubi pus, ibi evacua ("where there is pus, evacuate it") reflects that drainage is often the key treatment for abscesses, alongside antibiotics. The colour and odour of pus can give clues to the causative organism: for example, the blue-green pus produced by Pseudomonas aeruginosa results from the pigment pyocyanin.
