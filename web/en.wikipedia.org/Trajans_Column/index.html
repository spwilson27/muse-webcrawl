Trajan's Column

LocationTrajan's Forum, Rome
DedicatedAD 113
Heightabout 30 m (38 m with pedestal)

Trajan's Column is a Roman triumphal column in Rome, Italy, that commemorates Emperor Trajan's victory in the Dacian Wars. It was completed in AD 113 and is famous for its spiral bas relief, which artistically represents the wars between the Romans and Dacians in 155 scenes along a frieze about 190 metres long.

Description

The column is composed of 29 blocks of Luna marble, with a spiral staircase of 185 steps inside leading to a viewing platform. Trajan appears 58 times in the relief, shown addressing troops, sacrificing, and directing operations. The pedestal held a golden urn containing Trajan's ashes. The statue of Trajan on top was replaced in 1587 by one of Saint Peter under Pope Sixtus V.

Influence

The column inspired later monuments such as the Column of Marcus Aurelius and Napoleon's Vendôme Column, and casts of its relief — notably at the Victoria and Albert Museum — remain key sources for Roman military equipment and the Dacian Wars.
