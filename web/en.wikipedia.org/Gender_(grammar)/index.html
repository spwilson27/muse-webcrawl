Grammatical gender

[ Image placeholder: Table of noun genders (masculine, feminine, neuter) in a German grammar book ]

In linguistics, a grammatical gender system is a specific form of a noun class system, where nouns are assigned to gender categories that are often not related to the real-world qualities of the entities denoted. Depending on the language and the word, this assignment can bear some relationship with the meaning of the noun (e.g. in sex or animacy), but is often arbitrary or determined by formal (morphological or phonological) factors. Gender is a property of nouns that controls agreement: articles, adjectives, pronouns and sometimes verbs must take forms matching the gender of the noun they refer to.

Types of systems

Common systems distinguish masculine and feminine (French, Spanish, Italian, Arabic, Hebrew); masculine, feminine and neuter (German, Russian, Latin, Greek, Sanskrit); or animate and inanimate (Algonquian languages). Some languages, including Danish and Dutch, contrast "common" and neuter. Many languages — English (apart from pronouns), Finnish, Turkish, Persian, Japanese and Chinese among them — have no grammatical gender for nouns. Bantu languages extend noun-class systems to a dozen or more classes.

Assignment and agreement

In Spanish, nouns ending in -o are usually masculine and in -a feminine, while in Russian gender is largely predictable from the nominative ending. Exceptions abound: German das Mädchen ("the girl") is neuter because the diminutive suffix -chen is always neuter. Agreement patterns historically allow linguists to reconstruct the gender system of Proto-Indo-European, which is generally held to have had masculine, feminine and neuter genders.

Gender and cognition

Psycholinguistic studies have explored whether grammatical gender subtly influences how speakers conceptualise objects, with contested results. Debates over gender-fair language have led some communities to reform usage — for example, proposals for gender-neutral forms in German and the Swedish pronoun hen.
