Intercession of saints

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The intercession of saints is a Christian doctrine, held by the Catholic Church, the Eastern Orthodox Church, the Oriental Orthodox churches, and some Anglicans, that saints may be asked to intercede (or pray) for others. The doctrine teaches that the saints in heaven, being alive in Christ, continue to pray for the Church on earth, and that the faithful may ask for their prayers just as they ask living fellow Christians to pray for them.

Basis and practice

Supporters cite passages of Scripture such as Revelation 5:8 and 8:3–4, in which the elders and angels offer the prayers of the saints before God, and point to early Christian inscriptions and the writings of the Church Fathers as evidence of the practice in the early Church. Devotions include the Litany of the Saints, prayers to patron saints of particular causes and places, pilgrimage to shrines, and feast days. The Virgin Mary holds a pre-eminent place as intercessor in Catholic and Orthodox devotion.

Distinctions

Catholic teaching distinguishes veneration of saints (dulia, and hyperdulia for Mary) from the worship due to God alone (latria), and understands Christ as the one mediator between God and humanity (1 Timothy 2:5), with the saints' intercession subordinate to and dependent upon his.

Criticism

Most Protestant traditions reject the invocation of saints, arguing that it lacks explicit biblical warrant and detracts from the sole mediatorship of Christ. The practice was abolished in churches of the Reformation, though some Lutherans and Anglicans retain a limited commemoration of saints and may accept their intercession without invoking them directly.

Other religions

Analogous practices exist elsewhere: in Islam, tawassul (seeking intercession, debated among schools) and veneration of Sufi saints; in some forms of Buddhism, devotion to bodhisattvas who aid others toward enlightenment.
