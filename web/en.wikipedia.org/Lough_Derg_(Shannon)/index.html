Lough Derg (Shannon)

Lough Derg (Irish: Loch Deirgeirt) is a freshwater lake in Ireland, the third largest on the island after Lough Neagh and Lough Corrib. It lies on the River Shannon, the last and largest of the great Shannon lakes, bordered by counties Clare, Galway, and Tipperary. The lake is about 38.6 km long and covers roughly 130 km², with its northern shore near Portumna and its southern end at Killaloe and Ballina.

Geography and use

The Shannon enters Lough Derg at Portumna and leaves at Killaloe, where a hydroelectric scheme at Ardnacrusha, built in the 1920s, uses the fall in the river for power generation. The lake is a major centre for boating, angling, and water sports, with marinas at Dromineer, Garrykennedy, and Terryglass. Holy Island (Inis Cealtra) in the lake preserves early monastic ruins and a round tower.

Distinction

This Lough Derg should not be confused with the smaller Lough Derg in County Donegal, site of the pilgrimage at St Patrick's Purgatory on Station Island.
