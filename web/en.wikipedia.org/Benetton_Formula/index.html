Benetton Formula Ltd., commonly referred to simply as Benetton, was a Formula One constructor that raced from 1986 to 2001. The team was owned by the Benetton family, whose clothing company had entered the sport as a sponsor, and it evolved from the Toleman team. Benetton is best known for launching the career of Michael Schumacher, who won his first two Drivers' Championships with the team in 1994 and 1995, the latter year also bringing Benetton its only Constructors' Championship.

History

Origins (1986–1990)

Benetton bought the Toleman team at the end of 1985. The first Benetton car, the B186 powered by a BMW turbo engine, won the 1986 Mexican Grand Prix with Gerhard Berger. After switching to Ford-Cosworth power, the team became a regular podium finisher with drivers including Thierry Boutsen, Alessandro Nannini, and the young Nelson Piquet, who won the team's races in 1990–91.

The Schumacher era (1991–1995)

Under team principal Flavio Briatore and technical director Ross Brawn, Benetton signed Michael Schumacher after his debut race with Jordan in 1991. Schumacher won at Spa in 1992 and took the 1994 Drivers' title in the B194 in a season shadowed by the deaths of Ayrton Senna and Roland Ratzenberger and by allegations of illegal driver aids. With Renault engines in 1995, Schumacher retained the title with nine wins and Benetton took the Constructors' Championship ahead of Williams.

Decline and sale (1996–2001)

Schumacher's departure to Ferrari, with Brawn and designer Rory Byrne, began a long decline. The team, racing under an Italian licence from 1996, won only once more — Berger's victory at the 1997 German Grand Prix — and was sold to Renault in 2000 for US$120 million. It raced as Benetton through 2001 before becoming the Renault F1 Team in 2002. Benetton's 27 wins, all by Schumacher (19), Piquet (3), Berger (2), Johnny Herbert (2), and Alesi's contemporaries, place it among the more successful of the "garagiste" teams of its era.
