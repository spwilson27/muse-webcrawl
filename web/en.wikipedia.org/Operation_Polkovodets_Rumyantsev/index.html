Operation Polkovodets Rumyantsev

Operation Polkovodets Rumyantsev
Date: 3–23 August 1943
Location: Belgorod–Kharkov region, Ukrainian SSR
Result: Soviet victory; Kharkov liberated

Operation Polkovodets Rumyantsev (Russian: Полководец Румянцев, "Commander Rumyantsev", after the 18th-century Russian field marshal Pyotr Rumyantsev) was a military operation conducted by the Red Army against the Wehrmacht on the Eastern Front during the Second World War. It was the Soviet counteroffensive that followed the Battle of Kursk, launched on 3 August 1943 by the Voronezh and Steppe Fronts under Generals Nikolai Vatutin and Ivan Konev.

The offensive

The operation, also known as the Belgorod–Kharkov offensive, deployed over 1.1 million men and more than 3,000 tanks against Field Marshal Erich von Manstein's Army Group South. Soviet forces liberated Belgorod on 5 August and, after heavy fighting around Bogodukhov and Akhtyrka, retook Kharkov on 23 August, the fourth and final time the city changed hands in the war.

Significance

Together with Operation Kutuzov against the Orel salient, Rumyantsev marked the first successful Soviet summer offensive of the war and transferred the strategic initiative on the Eastern Front permanently to the Red Army, opening the way to the liberation of Ukraine and the advance to the Dnieper.
