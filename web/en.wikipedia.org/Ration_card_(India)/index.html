A ration card in India is an official document issued by state governments to households that are eligible to purchase subsidised food grain and other commodities from fair price shops under the Public Distribution System (PDS), administered under the National Food Security Act, 2013. Ration cards also serve widely as proof of identity and residence for Indian citizens.

History

Rationing in India began during the Second World War and was institutionalised after independence through the PDS, initially concentrated in cities and food-deficit regions. The system was universal until 1997, when the Targeted Public Distribution System (TPDS) divided households into Above Poverty Line (APL) and Below Poverty Line (BPL) categories. The National Food Security Act 2013 recast the categories as Antyodaya Anna Yojana (AAY) households — the poorest of the poor — and Priority Households (PHH).

Types and entitlements

AAY households are entitled to 35 kg of food grains per family per month, while Priority Households receive 5 kg per person per month, at highly subsidised prices (rice, wheat, and coarse grains). State governments also issue non-priority cards for households outside the NFSA quotas, which may carry state-level subsidies such as for sugar and kerosene.

One Nation One Ration Card

The One Nation One Ration Card (ONORC) scheme, rolled out nationally by 2021–2022, makes ration cards portable across India, allowing migrant workers to draw their entitlements from any fair price shop in the country using Aadhaar-based biometric authentication.
