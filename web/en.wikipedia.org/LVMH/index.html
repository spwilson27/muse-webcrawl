LVMH

LVMH Moët Hennessy Louis Vuitton, commonly known as LVMH, is a French multinational holding company and conglomerate specializing in luxury goods. Headquartered in Paris, it controls around 75 houses (brands) across six business groups, including Louis Vuitton, Moët & Chandon, Hennessy, Dior, Fendi, Givenchy, Bulgari, Sephora and Tiffany & Co. It is the world's largest luxury group by revenue, and its chairman and CEO, Bernard Arnault, has frequently ranked among the world's wealthiest individuals.

History

LVMH was formed in 1987 by the merger of the fashion house Louis Vuitton (founded in 1854) with Moët Hennessy, itself a 1971 merger of the champagne producer Moët & Chandon and the cognac maker Hennessy. Bernard Arnault, with financing support, gained control of the group in the late 1980s after a celebrated corporate battle, and pursued an aggressive strategy of acquiring luxury brands: Christian Dior Couture, Givenchy, Fendi, Bulgari, and in 2021 the American jeweller Tiffany & Co. for about $15.8 billion, the group's largest acquisition.

Business groups

The group is organised into wines and spirits; fashion and leather goods (its largest and most profitable division, anchored by Louis Vuitton and Christian Dior); perfumes and cosmetics; watches and jewellery; selective retailing (Sephora, DFS, Le Bon Marché); and other activities including hotels and media. LVMH also holds a substantial stake in Hermès accumulated controversially in 2010–2014, which led to a settlement under which the shares were distributed to LVMH shareholders.

Scale

LVMH's revenue surpassed €86 billion in 2023, and in April 2023 it became the first European company to exceed a market capitalisation of $500 billion. The group employs more than 200,000 people and operates a retail network of several thousand stores worldwide, with Asia and the United States among its largest markets.
