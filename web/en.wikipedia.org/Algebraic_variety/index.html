Algebraic variety

Algebraic varieties are the central objects of study in algebraic geometry, a sub-field of mathematics. Classically, an algebraic variety is defined as the set of solutions of a system of polynomial equations over the real or complex numbers. Modern definitions generalize this concept in several different ways, while attempting to preserve the geometric intuition behind the original definition.

Conventions regarding the definition of an algebraic variety differ slightly. One modern approach defines them as integral schemes of finite type over a field, though older texts and many references still use more restrictive definitions.

Definitions

An affine variety over an algebraically closed field k is the zero locus in affine n-space of a set of polynomials in k[x1, …, xn]. The set of such points is given the Zariski topology, whose closed sets are precisely algebraic sets. A projective variety is defined analogously in projective space using homogeneous polynomials.

Examples

  • Algebraic curves, such as the twisted cubic and elliptic curves
  • Algebraic surfaces, such as quadric surfaces and K3 surfaces
  • Grassmannians and flag varieties
  • Abelian varieties, which are projective varieties with a group structure

Basic results

Hilbert's Nullstellensatz establishes a correspondence between ideals in polynomial rings and points of affine varieties over algebraically closed fields. A fundamental theorem states that the image of a projective variety under a regular map is closed, which implies that projective varieties behave like compact spaces in many respects.
