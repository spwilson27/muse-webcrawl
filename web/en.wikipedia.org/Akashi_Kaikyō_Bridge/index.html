Akashi Kaikyō Bridge

Akashi Kaikyō Bridge
Crosses: Akashi Strait, Japan
Total length: 3,911 m
Longest span: 1,991 m
Opened: 5 April 1998

The Akashi Kaikyō Bridge (Japanese: 明石海峡大橋) is a suspension bridge linking the city of Kobe on Honshu to Iwaya on Awaji Island, Japan. It crosses the busy Akashi Strait as part of the Honshu–Shikoku Highway. When completed in 1998 it had the longest central span of any suspension bridge in the world, at 1,991 metres — a record it held until 2022.

Construction

Building the bridge took about ten years and some two million workers. During construction, the Great Hanshin earthquake of January 1995 shifted the two towers nearly a metre apart, lengthening the span, but the structure under construction survived essentially undamaged. The bridge is designed to withstand winds of 286 km/h and earthquakes up to magnitude 8.5.

Significance

Before the bridge, ferries carried passengers across the strait, where storms had caused disasters such as the 1955 sinking of two ferries with 168 deaths — a tragedy that strengthened the case for a fixed link. The bridge's main cables use high-strength steel wire; laid end to end, its wire would circle the Earth several times.
