Qasigiannguit

Qasigiannguit on Disko Bay

Qasigiannguit (Danish: Christianshåb) is a town in the Qeqertalik municipality in western Greenland, located on the southeastern shore of Disko Bay. Founded in 1734 as a Danish trading post by the merchant Jacob Severin, it is one of the oldest towns in Greenland. Its population is about 1,000, making it one of the smaller towns in the country.

History and economy

The town's economy historically rested on shrimp fishing and processing, and the local shrimp factory long provided the main employment; halibut and other fishing are also important. The Qasigiannguit Museum, housed in some of the town's oldest buildings, presents the history of Inuit culture in the Disko Bay area, including the Saqqaq culture, and runs the "Project Living Village" of reconstructed turf huts. The town is served by a heliport, with connections through Aasiaat and Ilulissat, and by ferry in summer.

Geography

Qasigiannguit sits at the mouth of a fjord system southeast of Disko Island, at about 68.8° N. The surrounding mountains and the icebergs calved from the Sermeq Kujalleq glacier near Ilulissat dominate the landscape, and the area is known for whale watching and hiking.
