Tacoma, Washington

Tacoma is a mid-sized urban port city in and the county seat of Pierce County, Washington, United States. Located on Puget Sound's Commencement Bay, about 32 miles (51 km) southwest of Seattle, it is the third-largest city in the state. The Port of Tacoma is one of the largest container ports in North America, operated jointly with Seattle's as the Northwest Seaport Alliance.

History

The area was inhabited by the Puyallup people, whose name for the snow-capped mountain was Tahoma — the source of the city's name. European-American settlement began in the 1850s; Tacoma was platted in 1868 and in 1873 was chosen as the western terminus of the Northern Pacific Railway, prompting its slogan "City of Destiny". The rail boom made it a major lumber and shipping centre, though the city also saw the violent expulsion of its Chinese residents in 1885 (the "Tacoma method"). The Tacoma Narrows Bridge collapse of 1940 — "Galloping Gertie" — became a famous case study in engineering.

Geography and economy

Tacoma sits at the base of the Puget Sound lowlands beneath Mount Rainier. Its economy combines the deep-water port, Joint Base Lewis–McChord nearby, healthcare, and education (University of Washington Tacoma, founded 1990). Revitalisation has centred on the museum district, including the Museum of Glass (2002) and the LeMay – America's Car Museum.

Culture

Tacoma is known for its historic theatre district, the Tacoma Dome arena, and as the birthplace of glass artist Dale Chihuly and crooner Bing Crosby. It is linked by the Tacoma Narrows Bridge to the Kitsap Peninsula.
