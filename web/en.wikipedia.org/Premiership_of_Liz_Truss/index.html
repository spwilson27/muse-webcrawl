Premiership of Liz Truss

The premiership of Liz Truss began on 6 September 2022, when Truss accepted Queen Elizabeth II's invitation to form a government following her election as leader of the Conservative Party, and ended on 25 October 2022 upon her resignation. At 49 days (50 by some counts) in office, it was the shortest premiership in British history.

Mini-budget crisis

Two days after Truss took office, Queen Elizabeth II died, suspending politics during national mourning. On 23 September, Chancellor Kwasi Kwarteng presented a "mini-budget" of unfunded tax cuts, including abolishing the 45p top rate of income tax. Financial markets reacted sharply: the pound fell to record lows against the dollar, gilt yields surged, and the Bank of England intervened to support pension funds. Most of the measures were reversed within weeks; Kwarteng was dismissed on 14 October and replaced by Jeremy Hunt.

Resignation

After further turmoil, including the resignation of Home Secretary Suella Braverman and a chaotic parliamentary vote on fracking, Truss announced her resignation on 20 October 2022. She was succeeded by Rishi Sunak. Truss later defended her agenda in a memoir and public appearances, attributing her downfall to resistance within the economic establishment.
