Ski

A ski is a narrow strip of semi-rigid material worn underfoot to glide over snow. Substantially longer than wide and characteristically turned up at the front tip, skis are used with ski boots, connected by bindings, in the sport of skiing and for transport over snow.

Ski

Uses: Skiing, transport, hunting
Materials: Wood, fibreglass, carbon, metal edges
Origin: Scandinavia / Altai, prehistoric

History

The oldest preserved skis, found in Sweden and Russia, date back several thousand years; rock carvings in Norway depict skiers around 4000 BCE. Skis were essential for winter hunting and travel in northern Eurasia and were later adopted by armies. In the 19th century Sondre Norheim's innovations in Telemark skiing began their recreational transformation.

Types

Modern skis vary by use: narrow cross-country skis, cambered alpine skis with steel edges, wide powder skis, twin-tip freestyle skis and short skiboards. Most are built as laminated composites with a wood or foam core, sidecut geometry that helps them carve turns, and waxable or patterned bases.
