Dolomites

The Dolomites are a mountain range in northeastern Italy. They form part of the Southern Limestone Alps and extend across the regions of Trentino-Alto Adige/Südtirol and Veneto. The range features some of the most dramatic mountain scenery in Europe, with steep pale rock pinnacles, deep valleys, and numerous peaks over 3,000 metres, the highest being Marmolada at 3,343 metres.

Geology and name

The mountains are composed mainly of dolomite rock, a sedimentary carbonate named after the French geologist Déodat Gratet de Dolomieu, who described the mineral in the late 18th century. The rocks originated as coral reefs and lagoons in a tropical sea during the Triassic period and were later uplifted.

Tourism and history

The Dolomites are a major destination for skiing, climbing, and hiking; the Via Ferrata routes originated here, many built during the First World War, when the front line between Italy and Austria-Hungary ran through the range. In 2009 UNESCO inscribed the Dolomites as a World Heritage Site.
