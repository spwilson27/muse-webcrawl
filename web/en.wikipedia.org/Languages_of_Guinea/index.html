Languages of Guinea

The languages of Guinea include French, the official language inherited from colonial rule, and numerous indigenous languages of the Niger–Congo family. The most widely spoken are Fula (Pular), Maninka (Malinké) and Susu, each associated with a major region of the country.

Major languages

Pular predominates in the Fouta Djallon highlands, Maninka in Upper Guinea, and Susu in coastal Lower Guinea. Kissi, Kpelle and Toma are spoken in the Forest Region.

Language policy

Under President Ahmed Sékou Touré, national languages were promoted in education and media using the Latin script; French later regained dominance in schooling. Literacy campaigns have used both Latin and Arabic-based Ajami writing, as well as the indigenous N'Ko script for Maninka.
