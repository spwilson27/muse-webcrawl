Biophilia (album)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Biophilia is the seventh studio album by Icelandic singer-songwriter Björk, released in October 2011 by One Little Independent Records and Nonesuch. It was conceived as a multimedia project exploring connections between music, nature and technology.

Concept and release

Each of the ten tracks was accompanied by an interactive app for iOS devices — the album is often described as the first "app album" — with games, animations and visualisations linking musical structures to natural phenomena such as crystal formation, lunar phases, lightning and DNA replication. Several songs were written for unusual instruments built for the project, including a Tesla coil, a pendulum harp and a "gameleste" (a celesta–gamelan hybrid).

Reception

The album received generally favourable reviews and was supported by an educational programme and a residency-style concert series premiering at the Manchester International Festival. The Biophilia apps were later inducted into the Museum of Modern Art's collection.
