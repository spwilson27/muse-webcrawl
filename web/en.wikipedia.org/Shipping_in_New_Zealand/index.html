Shipping in New Zealand

Major ports: Auckland, Tauranga, Wellington, Lyttelton, Port Chalmers
Key link: Cook Strait ferries
Regulator: Maritime New Zealand

Shipping in New Zealand has been central to the country's development as an island nation. Māori voyaging canoes (waka) first settled the islands, and European shipping from the eighteenth century onwards carried trade, migrants and exports. As an export economy dependent on agricultural produce, New Zealand relied heavily on refrigerated shipping to distant markets, especially Britain.

History

Coastal shipping connected scattered settlements long before good roads existed, and companies such as the Union Steam Ship Company built large coastal and trans-Tasman networks. The first refrigerated shipment of meat, aboard the Dunedin in 1882, transformed the economy by making frozen exports viable.

Ports and ferries

Major ports include the Ports of Auckland, Port of Tauranga (the country's largest by volume), CentrePort Wellington, Lyttelton Port and Port Otago. Roll-on/roll-off ferries across Cook Strait, between Wellington and Picton, form a vital freight and passenger link between the North and South Islands.

Modern shipping

Today most international cargo is carried by foreign-flagged container ships, while coastal shipping competes with road and rail freight. Maritime safety and regulation are overseen by Maritime New Zealand.
