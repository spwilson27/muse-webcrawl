Green New Deal

The Green New Deal (GND) is a proposed package of United States legislation and, more broadly, a family of policy frameworks that aim to address climate change and economic inequality together. The name deliberately echoes the New Deal, the series of social and economic reforms enacted under President Franklin D. Roosevelt in response to the Great Depression.

Origins

The term was popularized in the late 2000s, appearing in the writing of journalist Thomas Friedman and in policy proposals by green parties and think tanks in the United States, the United Kingdom, and elsewhere. In November 2018, activists from the Sunrise Movement occupied the office of prospective House Speaker Nancy Pelosi to demand a Green New Deal, with newly elected Representative Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez joining them, which propelled the concept into mainstream American politics.

The 2019 resolution

On 7 February 2019, Representative Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez and Senator Ed Markey introduced a non-binding congressional resolution (H. Res. 109 / S. Res. 59) recognizing the duty of the federal government to create a Green New Deal. The resolution proposed a ten-year national mobilization to achieve net-zero greenhouse gas emissions, create millions of jobs, invest in infrastructure and industry, secure clean air and water and a sustainable environment, and promote justice and equity for frontline and vulnerable communities. A Senate vote in March 2019 failed, with most Democrats voting "present" in protest at the procedure.

Related proposals

Related frameworks have been advanced internationally, including the European Green Deal announced by the European Commission in 2019, which aims to make the European Union climate-neutral by 2050. In the United States, elements of Green New Deal thinking influenced later legislation such as the Inflation Reduction Act of 2022, though that law was considerably narrower in scope.

Criticism and debate

Supporters argue that climate policy and economic policy cannot be separated and that a large public investment program is needed to decarbonize at the required speed. Critics have raised concerns about cost, feasibility, and the breadth of the social programs attached to the proposal.
