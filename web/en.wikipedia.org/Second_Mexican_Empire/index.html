The Second Mexican Empire (Spanish: Segundo Imperio Mexicano) was a client state of the French Empire existing between 1864 and 1867. It was established by Napoleon III, who used France's military intervention in Mexico — begun in 1861 after President Benito Juárez suspended foreign debt payments — to install a Habsburg prince as emperor. The Second French Empire intended to replace the republican government of Juárez that France's army had dislodged from Mexico City, and to check the expanding power of the United States (distracted by its Civil War) by creating a French-aligned monarchy in Latin America.

Maximilian's reign

Archduke Ferdinand Maximilian of Austria, younger brother of Emperor Franz Joseph I, accepted the crown at Miramare in April 1864 and landed at Veracruz in May, entering Mexico City in June 1864 with his wife Charlotte (Empress Carlota). A liberal by instinct, Maximilian disappointed his Conservative backers by upholding many of Juárez's Reform Laws, abolishing peonage and attempting land and labour reforms, while never winning over republican Liberals, who regarded him as a foreign puppet. His regime depended entirely on French troops under Marshal Bazaine, and governed through a costly civil war against republican guerrillas and Juárez's itinerant government in the north.

Collapse

After 1865, the victorious United States pressed France under the Monroe Doctrine and supplied Juárez; facing war with Prussia at home, Napoleon III announced a phased withdrawal, completed in March 1867. Maximilian refused to abdicate or depart with the French, hoping for victory with his remaining Mexican Conservative forces. He was defeated and captured at Querétaro in May 1867 and, despite international pleas for clemency — from Victor Hugo and Garibaldi among others — was executed by firing squad with generals Miramón and Mejía on 19 June 1867. Juárez re-entered Mexico City on 15 July 1867, restoring the Republic. The episode inspired Édouard Manet's famous series of paintings of the execution.
