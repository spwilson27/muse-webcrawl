The Valiant was the first of the RAF's V-bombers to enter service.

The Vickers Valiant was a British high-altitude jet bomber designed to carry nuclear weapons, and part of the Royal Air Force's "V bomber" force in the 1950s and 1960s. The Valiant was the first of the three V-bombers to become operational, preceding the Avro Vulcan and Handley Page Victor. Developed in response to Specification B.35/46, it was the most conventional of the three designs, with a shoulder-wing layout and four Rolls-Royce Avon engines buried in the wing roots.

The prototype first flew on 18 May 1951, piloted by Vickers' chief test pilot "Mutt" Summers. The Valiant entered service in 1955, and in October 1956 a Valiant of No. 49 Squadron dropped Britain's first live atomic bomb at Maralinga, Australia; the following year Valiants dropped hydrogen bombs during Operation Grapple at Christmas Island. During the Suez Crisis of November 1956, Valiants bombed Egyptian airfields from Malta — the type's only conventional bombing operations.

Tanker role and fatigue crisis

As the V-force adopted low-level penetration tactics in the early 1960s, Valiants were increasingly used for aerial refuelling, reconnaissance, and electronic countermeasures. In 1964, fatigue cracks were found in the rear wing spars of several aircraft — a problem aggravated by low-level turbulence the structure had not been designed for. Repair was judged uneconomical, and the entire Valiant fleet was withdrawn in January 1965, the first V-bomber to be retired.

Legacy

Only one complete Valiant survives: XD818, the aircraft that dropped the first British hydrogen bomb, preserved at the Royal Air Force Museum Cosford. Though overshadowed by the Vulcan and Victor, the Valiant pioneered Britain's airborne nuclear deterrent and its tanker operations.
