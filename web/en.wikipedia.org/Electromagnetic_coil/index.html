Electromagnetic coil

An electromagnetic coil is an electrical conductor such as a wire in the shape of a coil (spiral or helix). Electromagnetic coils are used in electrical engineering, where electric currents interact with magnetic fields, in devices such as electric motors, generators, inductors, electromagnets, and transformers.

Principle

When current passes through the wire, it generates a magnetic field whose strength is proportional to the current and the number of turns. Winding the wire into a coil concentrates the field along the coil's axis; adding a ferromagnetic core greatly increases it.

Types

Coils may be air-cored or wound on iron or ferrite cores. A tightly wound cylindrical coil is a solenoid; a toroidal coil is wound on a ring-shaped core, confining flux within the core.

Applications

Coils serve as inductors in filters and resonant circuits, as windings in motors and transformers, as electromagnets for lifting and switching, and in sensors, relays, and ignition systems.
