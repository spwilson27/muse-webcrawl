Operation Dynamo

Operation Dynamo
Date: 26 May – 4 June 1940
Location: Dunkirk, France, and the English Channel
Result: 338,226 Allied troops evacuated

Operation Dynamo, the evacuation from Dunkirk, also known as the Miracle of Dunkirk, was the evacuation of Allied soldiers during World War II from the beaches and harbour of Dunkirk, in the north of France, between 26 May and 4 June 1940. The operation commenced after large numbers of British, French, and Belgian troops were cut off and surrounded by German troops during the six-week Battle of France.

The evacuation

The operation was directed by Vice-Admiral Bertram Ramsay from the tunnels beneath Dover Castle. Although planners initially hoped to rescue around 45,000 men, a flotilla of over 800 vessels — Royal Navy destroyers, merchant ships, fishing boats, pleasure craft, and lifeboats, remembered as the "Little Ships of Dunkirk" — crossed the Channel under air attack. In total 338,226 soldiers were evacuated, including some 198,000 British and 140,000 French troops.

Aftermath

The rescued forces formed the core around which Britain could rebuild its army, although nearly all heavy equipment had to be abandoned in France. Prime Minister Winston Churchill's "We shall fight on the beaches" speech acknowledged that "wars are not won by evacuations" while hailing the deliverance that made continued resistance possible.
