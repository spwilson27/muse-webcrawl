Hardwick Hall

Location: Doe Lea, Chesterfield, Derbyshire, England

Built: 1590–1597

Architect: Robert Smythson

Owner: National Trust

Hardwick Hall in Derbyshire is one of the most significant examples of Elizabethan architecture in England. Built between 1590 and 1597 for Bess of Hardwick, Countess of Shrewsbury — the richest woman in England after Queen Elizabeth I — it was designed by the architect Robert Smythson and is famous for its exceptionally large windows, giving rise to the local saying "Hardwick Hall, more glass than wall".

Architecture

The house is a symmetrical prodigy house crowned with the initials "ES" (Elizabeth Shrewsbury) in stone balustrades above its six towers. Inside, the state rooms occupy the top floor, culminating in the Long Gallery and the High Great Chamber, hung with the celebrated Gideon tapestries. Remarkably, an inventory of 1601 survives, and many of the furnishings it lists — tapestries, embroideries, and furniture — remain in the house today.

History

After Bess's death the hall passed through the Cavendish family, Earls and Dukes of Devonshire, whose main seat was nearby Chatsworth. In the 1950s the house was transferred to the National Trust in lieu of death duties. The ruins of the earlier Hardwick Old Hall stand nearby.
