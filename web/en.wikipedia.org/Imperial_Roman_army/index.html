Imperial Roman army

The Imperial Roman army was the military land force of the Roman Empire from the establishment of the Principate under Augustus in 27 BC until the division of the empire in AD 395 (and beyond, in modified form). It succeeded the army of the Roman Republic and was the first fully professional standing army of the Roman state.

Organization

Under Augustus the army was reorganized into legions of citizen heavy infantry — eventually about 30 legions of roughly 5,000 men each — supported by auxiliary regiments (auxilia) of non-citizen infantry and cavalry recruited from the provinces, who received citizenship on completion of 25 years of service. Specialized forces included the Praetorian Guard in Rome, the urban cohorts, and the fleets at Misenum and Ravenna.

Pay and service

Legionaries enlisted for 25 years (later 20 plus reserve service) and received a regular salary, donatives, and retirement bonuses in cash or land. The total establishment numbered around 150,000 legionaries and a comparable number of auxiliaries, deployed mainly along the frontiers — the Rhine, Danube, Euphrates, and Hadrian's Wall in Britain.

Later reforms

The reforms of Gallienus, Diocletian, and Constantine progressively divided the army into static frontier troops (limitanei) and mobile field armies (comitatenses), a structure that carried into the late Roman and early Byzantine periods.
