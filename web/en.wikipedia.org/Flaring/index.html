Flaring

Flaring refers to the combustion of gases through a gas flare, typically associated gas produced with crude oil. In petroleum extraction and refining, gas that cannot be processed or sold is burned off in flare stacks to prevent the buildup of dangerous pressures, and because methane venting is a more potent greenhouse gas than its combustion product, carbon dioxide.

Petroleum industry

Gas flares are used at oil wells, refineries, chemical plants, and landfills. They serve as safety devices to dispose of relief gases during unplanned over-pressuring of plant equipment, and routinely at production sites lacking gas-gathering infrastructure.

Environmental impact

Flaring releases large quantities of carbon dioxide and black carbon. The World Bank's "Zero Routine Flaring by 2030" initiative aims to eliminate routine flaring; satellite data show Russia, Iraq, the United States, Iran, and Algeria among the largest flaring countries.

Regulation

Many jurisdictions now restrict routine flaring. Norway banned routine flaring early, while the US EPA and state regulators have tightened limits, notably in the Permian Basin where flaring grew alongside shale oil production.
