The Delian League was a confederacy of Greek city-states, numbering between 150 and 330, founded in 478 BC under the leadership (hegemony) of Athens, whose purpose was to continue fighting the Persian Empire after the Greek victory in the Battle of Plataea at the end of the Second Persian invasion of Greece. The league's treasury was kept on the sacred island of Delos, where its synod also met — hence the modern name.

Formation and early campaigns

After the Spartan regent Pausanias alienated the Ionian allies, the Greek cities of the Aegean invited Athens to lead the continuing war against Persia. Members swore oaths to have the same friends and enemies, and contributed ships or money (phoros) assessed by Aristides "the Just". Athenian-led fleets won the decisive victory at the Eurymedon River (c. 466 BC) and campaigned in Cyprus and Egypt, where a disastrous expedition ended in 454 BC.

The Athenian empire

In 454 BC the treasury was transferred from Delos to Athens, officially for safety. Increasingly the league functioned as an Athenian empire: Athens suppressed revolts at Naxos, Thasos, Euboea, Samos and Mytilene, installed garrisons and cleruchies (settlements of Athenian citizens), and used league funds to build the Parthenon and other monuments. Allied "tribute lists" inscribed in Athens recorded a sixtieth of each payment dedicated to Athena. The Thirty Years' Peace with Sparta (445 BC) did not loosen Athens's grip.

Peloponnesian War and dissolution

During the Peloponnesian War (431–404 BC), Athens raised tribute sharply to fund the struggle, and several allies revolted after the Athenian catastrophe in Sicily. When Sparta's fleet, funded by Persia, destroyed Athens's navy at Aegospotami in 405 BC, the league collapsed; Athens surrendered in 404 BC and the league was formally dissolved. A more equal Second Athenian League was founded in 378 BC, explicitly designed to avoid the first league's abuses.
