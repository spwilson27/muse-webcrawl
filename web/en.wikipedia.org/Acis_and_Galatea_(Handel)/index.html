Acis and Galatea (Handel)

Acis and Galatea
Composer: George Frideric Handel
Catalogue: HWV 49
Premiere: 1718, Cannons
Libretto: Attributed to John Gay and others

Acis and Galatea (HWV 49) is a musical work by George Frideric Handel with an English text by John Gay and others. It has been described variously as a serenata, a masque, a pastoral opera, and — as the work is sometimes given in full costume — an opera in two acts.

History

Handel composed the first version in 1718 while resident at Cannons, the estate of the Duke of Chandos, adapting a story from Ovid's Metamorphoses. He later revised it substantially, including a bilingual version in 1732. It was Handel's most popular dramatic work during his lifetime.

Story

The shepherd Acis and the sea-nymph Galatea are in love, but the giant Polyphemus, enraged by jealousy, kills Acis with a boulder. Galatea transforms her dead lover into an everlasting fountain and stream.

Music

Famous numbers include the chorus "Wretched lovers!", Galatea's "Heart, the seat of soft delight", and Polyphemus's aria "O ruddier than the cherry".
