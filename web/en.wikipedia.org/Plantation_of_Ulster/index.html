Plantation of Ulster

The Plantation of Ulster (Irish: Plandáil Uladh; Ulster Scots: Plantin o Ulstèr) was the organised colonisation (plantation) of Ulster – a province of Ireland – by people from Great Britain during the reign of King James I. Most of the settlers (or planters) came from southern Scotland and northern England; their culture differed significantly from that of the native Irish. Small privately funded plantations by wealthy landowners began in 1606, while the official plantation sponsored by the government, administered by the City of London, began in 1609.

Background

The plantation followed the Flight of the Earls in 1607, when the Gaelic lords Hugh O'Neill and Rory O'Donnell fled Ireland, and the subsequent confiscation of their lands in counties Armagh, Coleraine (later Londonderry), Donegal, Fermanagh, Tyrone and Cavan. The scheme aimed to secure the province with loyal Protestant settlers.

Effects

Tens of thousands of British settlers arrived during the 17th century, transforming Ulster's demography and creating a distinct Protestant, British-identifying community. Native Irish dispossession contributed to rebellion in 1641 and left a lasting legacy in Irish history and in the divided identities of modern Northern Ireland.
