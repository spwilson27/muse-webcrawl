Mutual insurance

Policyholders own a mutual insurer collectively

A mutual insurance company is an insurance company owned entirely by its policyholders. Any profits earned are either retained within the company or distributed to policyholders as dividends or reduced future premiums, in contrast to a stock insurance company, which is owned by external shareholders.

History

Mutual insurance grew out of 18th- and 19th-century friendly societies and mutual fire insurance associations, in which communities pooled risks locally. Benjamin Franklin helped found one of the earliest American mutual fire insurers, the Philadelphia Contributionship, in 1752. Mutual life insurers became major financial institutions in the 19th and 20th centuries.

Demutualisation

From the 1980s onward, many large mutuals converted to stock ownership ("demutualised") to gain access to capital markets, including several major life insurers in the US, UK, and elsewhere. Many mutual insurers nevertheless remain, particularly in property and casualty lines and in farm mutuals, and mutuality continues to be advocates' model for aligning insurer and customer interests.
