Sound stage


Interior of a sound stage during a production

A sound stage (also written soundstage) is a large, soundproofed room or building in a film or television production facility, used for the construction of sets and the filming of productions. The term originates with the advent of sound films in the late 1920s, when stages had to be acoustically isolated so that dialogue could be recorded cleanly, free of outside noise.

Structure

Sound stages are typically windowless, hangar-like structures with high ceilings fitted with lighting grids and catwalks, heavy sound-insulated doors, and silent air-conditioning systems that can be shut off during takes. Floors are smooth and level to permit camera movement and the construction of sets. Many stages feature water tanks or pits for aquatic filming. Sizes vary from a few hundred to several thousand square metres; famous large stages include the 007 Stage at Pinewood Studios in England, one of the largest in the world.

Use

Filming on a stage gives a production complete control over lighting, weather, and sound, and allows sets to remain standing for the duration of a shoot. Stages are used alongside location shooting and backlots. Multiple stages grouped together form the core of a studio complex such as Warner Bros. Studios in Burbank or Leavesden in Hertfordshire.

Virtual production

Modern stages may incorporate LED volume walls displaying real-time computer-generated environments, allowing actors to be filmed "on location" within the stage — a technique popularised by productions such as The Mandalorian.
