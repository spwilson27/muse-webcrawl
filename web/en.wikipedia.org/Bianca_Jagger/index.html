Bianca Jagger

Bianca Jagger
Born: 2 May 1945, Managua, Nicaragua
Occupation: Human rights advocate, former actress
Spouse: Mick Jagger (m. 1971; div. 1978)

Bianca Jagger (born Bianca Pérez-Mora Macías, 2 May 1945) is a Nicaraguan social and human rights advocate and a former actress. She became internationally known in the 1970s during her marriage to Mick Jagger, lead singer of the Rolling Stones, and as a fixture of the Studio 54 social scene in New York.

Early life

Jagger was born in Managua, Nicaragua, the daughter of a successful import-export merchant and a housewife. After her parents divorced, she won a scholarship to study political science at the Institute of Political Studies in Paris. She was influenced by Gandhi's philosophy of nonviolence.

Marriage and public life

She married Mick Jagger in Saint-Tropez, France, in 1971; their daughter Jade was born the same year. The couple divorced in 1978. Bianca Jagger was photographed in some of the most iconic images of the disco era, including riding a white horse into Studio 54 on her 32nd birthday.

Human rights work

After the 1972 Nicaragua earthquake, Jagger became involved in humanitarian work in her home country. She has campaigned against the death penalty, for the rights of indigenous peoples, and against human rights abuses in Latin America. She founded the Bianca Jagger Human Rights Foundation and has served as chair of the Executive Committee of Amnesty International USA and as a goodwill ambassador for the Council of Europe.
