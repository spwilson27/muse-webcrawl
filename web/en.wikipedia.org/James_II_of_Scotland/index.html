James II (16 October 1430 – 3 August 1460) was King of Scots from 1437 until his death in 1460. Nicknamed "Fiery Face" because of a conspicuous red birthmark on his cheek, he succeeded his father James I as a six-year-old after the king's assassination, and as an adult became an energetic ruler who broke the power of the Black Douglas family and strengthened the Scottish crown.

James II of Scotland (1430–1460)

Minority

James was crowned at Holyrood Abbey in March 1437. His minority was dominated by struggles among the queen mother, Joan Beaufort, and powerful magnates, including Sir Alexander Livingston and William Crichton, who fought over custody of the young king. In 1440 the rivalry culminated in the "Black Dinner" at Edinburgh Castle, where the teenage Earl of Douglas and his brother were executed after a show trial in the king's presence.

Personal rule

In 1449 James married Mary of Guelders and assumed full control of government. He set out to destroy the over-mighty Black Douglases. In 1452, after inviting William, 8th Earl of Douglas, to Stirling Castle under safe conduct, James stabbed him during a quarrel, and courtiers finished the killing. War with the Douglas family followed, ending in 1455 with the royal victory at the Battle of Arkinholm and the forfeiture of the Douglas estates, which greatly enriched the crown. James also used artillery and diplomacy against the Lord of the Isles and asserted Scottish interests against England during the Wars of the Roses, supporting the Lancastrian cause.

Death

In 1460 James besieged Roxburgh Castle, one of the last Scottish strongholds still held by the English. On 3 August one of his own cannon — part of the great Flemish siege train of which he was so proud — exploded beside him, killing the king at the age of twenty-nine. He was buried at Holyrood Abbey and succeeded by his eight-year-old son, James III.
