Whitehorse is the capital and largest city of Yukon, Canada, and the only city in the territory. Located at kilometre 1,425 of the Alaska Highway, it lies in the valley of the Yukon River and is home to roughly 70 per cent of Yukon's population.

History

The site takes its name from the White Horse Rapids, whose foaming crests were said to resemble the manes of white horses; the rapids were drowned by a hydroelectric dam in 1958. Whitehorse boomed during the Klondike Gold Rush of 1897–98 as a staging point at the head of navigation on the Yukon River, and in 1900 the White Pass and Yukon Route railway linked it to Skagway, Alaska. The town became a major construction centre during the building of the Alaska Highway in 1942, and replaced Dawson City as Yukon's capital in 1953.

Geography and climate

Whitehorse sits in a relatively mild valley microclimate by northern standards, though winters are long and dark, with temperatures occasionally below −40 °C. In summer, daylight lasts nearly 20 hours. The city is a hub for tourism, mining services, and outdoor recreation, and is served by Erik Nielsen Whitehorse International Airport, named for the territory's long-serving MP.

The sternwheeler SS Klondike, preserved on the riverbank, is a National Historic Site recalling the riverboat era.
