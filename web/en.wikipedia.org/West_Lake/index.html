West Lake

West Lake (Xī Hú)
Location: Hangzhou, Zhejiang, China
Type: Freshwater lake
Surface area: about 6.5 km²
Average depth: about 2.3 m
UNESCO World Heritage Site: listed 2011

West Lake (Chinese: 西湖, pinyin: Xī Hú) is a freshwater lake in Hangzhou, the capital of Zhejiang Province in eastern China. Divided by causeways into several sections and dotted with temples, pagodas, gardens and artificial islands, it has inspired Chinese poets, scholars and painters for over a thousand years and is regarded as a paradigm of the fusion of landscape and culture.

History

The lake began as a lagoon of the Qiantang River and was progressively dredged and landscaped under successive dynasties. The Bai Causeway is associated with the Tang-dynasty poet-governor Bai Juyi, and the Su Causeway with the Song-dynasty statesman and poet Su Shi (Su Dongpo), who directed major dredging works in the eleventh century. Marco Polo described Hangzhou and its lake as among the finest in the world.

Ten Scenes

Since the Southern Song dynasty, the lake's scenery has been celebrated in the "Ten Scenes of West Lake", including "Spring Dawn at Su Causeway", "Leifeng Pagoda in Evening Glow" and "Three Pools Mirroring the Moon". In 2011 the West Lake Cultural Landscape of Hangzhou was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.
