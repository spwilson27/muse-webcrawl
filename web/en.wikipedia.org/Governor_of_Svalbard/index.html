Governor of Svalbard

The Governor of Svalbard (Norwegian: Sysselmesteren på Svalbard, formerly Sysselmannen) is the highest Norwegian state representative on the Arctic archipelago of Svalbard and serves as the territory's chief of police and administrative authority. The office was established in 1925 when Norway assumed sovereignty over the islands under the Svalbard Treaty of 1920. The office was renamed from Sysselmann to the gender-neutral Sysselmester in 2021.

Seat: Longyearbyen

Established: 1925

Appointer: Government of Norway (Ministry of Justice)

First holder: Johannes Gerckens Bassøe

Duties

The Governor's office (Sysselmesteren) combines several functions unusual on the Norwegian mainland: it is the police district for Svalbard, the county governor's environmental and administrative authority, the local passport and notarial office, and operator of search-and-rescue helicopter services. The Governor enforces Norwegian law as modified by the Svalbard Treaty, oversees environmental protection (most of the archipelago's land is protected), registers residents and vehicles, and issues permits for travel outside the settlements, including polar-bear safety measures.

Context

Svalbard's status derives from the 1920 treaty, which grants Norway sovereignty while preserving the economic rights of signatory states and keeping the islands demilitarized and tax-light. The Governor is based in the administrative centre of Longyearbyen and staff includes police officers, environmental advisers, and helicopter crews. The office also supervises the former mining settlements and research stations, including Ny-Ålesund and the Russian settlement of Barentsburg.

List note

Governors serve fixed terms, typically three years, and are appointed by the King in Council. In 2021 Lars Fause became the first officeholder under the new title of Sysselmester.
