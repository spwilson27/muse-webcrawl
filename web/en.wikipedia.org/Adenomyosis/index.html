Adenomyosis

SpecialtyGynaecology
SymptomsHeavy or painful periods, chronic pelvic pain; often none
Risk factorsPrior uterine surgery, childbirth, age over 40
DiagnosisTransvaginal ultrasound, MRI; confirmed by pathology after hysterectomy
TreatmentNSAIDs, hormonal therapy, hysterectomy

Adenomyosis is a medical condition characterised by the growth of cells that proliferate on the inside of the uterus (endometrium) atypically located among the cells of the uterine wall (myometrium), as a result of which the uterus becomes enlarged. The displaced glandular tissue is surrounded by reactive smooth-muscle hyperplasia, and the condition most often affects women of reproductive age, particularly those who have had children.

Symptoms, when present, include heavy menstrual bleeding (menorrhagia), painful periods (dysmenorrhoea), chronic pelvic pain and pain during intercourse. Many cases are asymptomatic and discovered incidentally. Adenomyosis frequently coexists with endometriosis and uterine fibroids, and it is associated with infertility and adverse pregnancy outcomes in some studies.

Diagnosis and treatment

Historically the diagnosis could only be made by examining the uterus after hysterectomy, but modern transvaginal ultrasound and magnetic resonance imaging can identify characteristic features with reasonable accuracy. Management depends on symptoms and whether the patient wishes to preserve fertility: options include non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, hormonal treatments such as the levonorgestrel intrauterine system and GnRH analogues, uterine artery embolisation, and conservative surgery. Hysterectomy remains the only definitive cure.
