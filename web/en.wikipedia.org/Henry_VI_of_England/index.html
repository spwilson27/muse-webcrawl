Henry VI of England

Henry VI

Reign: 31 August 1422 – 4 March 1461; 3 October 1470 – 11 April 1471

Predecessor: Henry V

Successor: Edward IV

Born: 6 December 1421, Windsor Castle

Died: 21 May 1471 (aged 49), Tower of London

House: Lancaster

Henry VI, king in infancy

Henry VI (6 December 1421 – 21 May 1471) was King of England from 1422 to 1461 and again from 1470 to 1471, and claimed King of France from 1422 to 1453. The only child of Henry V, he succeeded to the throne at nine months old — the youngest person ever to become English monarch — during the final phase of the Hundred Years' War.

Reign

During his minority England was governed by his uncles, while in France the war turned against the English after the intervention of Joan of Arc; by 1453 only Calais remained of England's French possessions. Henry, pious and gentle but politically ineffectual, married Margaret of Anjou in 1445. Patronage of favourites and the loss of France discredited the court. In 1453 Henry suffered a mental breakdown, and Richard, Duke of York was made Protector.

Wars of the Roses

Dynastic conflict between the houses of Lancaster and York erupted in 1455 in the Wars of the Roses. Henry was captured at Northampton in 1460 and deposed in 1461 by Edward IV. Briefly restored in 1470 (the "Readeption"), he was deposed again after the battles of Barnet and Tewkesbury in 1471, in which his son Edward of Westminster was killed. Henry died in the Tower of London on 21 May 1471, almost certainly murdered.

Legacy

Henry founded Eton College and King's College, Cambridge, whose chapels are monuments of late Gothic architecture. Popular veneration treated him as a saint, though he was never canonised. Shakespeare devoted three plays to his reign.
