Harvard Yard, the historic center of Harvard University.

Harvard Yard, in Cambridge, Massachusetts, is the oldest part of the Harvard University campus, its historic center, and its modern crossroads. The 22.4-acre (9.1-hectare) grassy area is bounded by fences and gates and contains freshman dormitories, libraries, classrooms, and the offices of the university president. It is divided principally into the Old Yard and Tercentenary Theatre.

Old Yard

The Old Yard, on the western side, is the site of Harvard's earliest buildings. It includes Massachusetts Hall (1720), the oldest surviving Harvard building; Harvard Hall (1766); Hollis, Stoughton, and Holworthy halls; and freshman dormitories such as Grays, Weld, Matthews, and Wigglesworth halls. The statue of John Harvard, sculpted by Daniel Chester French in 1884, stands before University Hall (1815) and is one of the most photographed monuments in the United States, despite depicting not Harvard himself but a model posing as him.

Tercentenary Theatre

The eastern part of the Yard, the Tercentenary Theatre, was created for the university's 300th anniversary celebrations in 1936. It is flanked by Widener Library (1915), the largest university library in the world by collection size, and Memorial Church (1932), built in memory of Harvard's dead of the First World War. The theatre is the site of Harvard's commencement exercises.

Gates

The Yard is enclosed by a fence pierced by 27 gates, including Johnston Gate (1889), the oldest, at the western entrance from Harvard Square, and the bicentennial and class gates given by alumni classes. Each gate carries inscriptions and class years.

Traditions

Freshmen live in the Yard dormitories and eat in Annenberg Hall in Memorial Hall, just north of the Yard. A well-known student tradition holds that one should enter the Yard by Johnston Gate, pass through the Porcellian Gate during one's studies, and leave by the Dexter Gate at graduation — entering and exiting only these few times.
