Central Japan Railway Company

Central Japan Railway Company (JR Central)
Founded: 1987 (privatisation of JNR)
Headquarters: Nagoya, Japan
Key line: Tōkaidō Shinkansen

The Central Japan Railway Company (Japanese: 東海旅客鉄道株式会社, Tōkai Ryokaku Tetsudō Kabushiki-gaisha), known as JR Central, is one of the seven companies of the Japan Railways Group. It operates railways in the Chūbu region of Japan and is best known for the Tōkaidō Shinkansen, the world's busiest high-speed rail line, linking Tokyo with Nagoya and Osaka.

History

JR Central was created on 1 April 1987 when the state-owned Japanese National Railways was broken up and privatised. It inherited the profitable Tōkaidō corridor, and its revenues from the Shinkansen have made it one of the most profitable railway companies in the world. The company was fully sold to private investors in 2006.

Operations

Besides the Tōkaidō Shinkansen, JR Central runs conventional lines around Nagoya and the Chūō, Kansai, and Kisei lines. It is developing the Chūō Shinkansen, a maglev line using superconducting technology tested at the Yamanashi test track, planned to connect Tokyo and Nagoya in about 40 minutes at speeds up to 500 km/h; construction began in 2014, though opening has been delayed by disputes over water use in Shizuoka Prefecture.
