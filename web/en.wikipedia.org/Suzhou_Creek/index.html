Suzhou Creek

Suzhou Creek (Chinese: 苏州河; pinyin: Sūzhōu Hé), also called the Wusong River (吴淞江), is a river in central Shanghai, China. About 125 km long overall, with roughly 54 km within Shanghai's urban area, it flows from Tai Lake in Jiangsu Province through Suzhou and Shanghai and joins the Huangpu River at the northern end of the Bund, near Waibaidu Bridge.

History

The river takes its name from Suzhou, the city it passes upstream; its older name, Wusong Jiang, reflects its former mouth near Wusong. In earlier centuries the Wusong was the main channel draining Tai Lake to the sea, before sedimentation made the Huangpu dominant. In the treaty-port era the creek formed the boundary between the British and American settlements that merged into the Shanghai International Settlement, and its banks were lined with warehouses, mills, and factories — including the Sihang Warehouse, famous for its defense during the 1937 Battle of Shanghai.

Pollution and rehabilitation

Industrialization left the creek notoriously polluted and malodorous. A major cleanup program begun in 1998 (the Suzhou Creek Rehabilitation Project) dredged the river, built sewage interception systems, and redeveloped its banks with promenades, parks and converted warehouse districts such as the M50 art district.

Geography

In Shanghai the creek meanders through Changning, Putuo, Jing'an and Zhabei districts and along the border of Huangpu District before its confluence with the Huangpu.
