George Edward Clinton (born July 22, 1941) is an American musician, singer, songwriter, bandleader, and record producer. His Parliament-Funkadelic collective developed a form of psychedelic-influenced funk music in the 1970s that became known as P-Funk, blending science-fiction themes, surreal humour, and elaborate stage shows.

Career

Clinton began in a doo-wop group, the Parliaments, which scored a hit with "(I Wanna) Testify" in 1967. In the early 1970s he reorganised the musicians into two main bands: Parliament, oriented toward horn-driven funk, and Funkadelic, oriented toward psychedelic rock guitar. Landmark albums include Funkadelic's Maggot Brain (1971) and Parliament's Mothership Connection (1975) and Funkentelechy vs. the Placebo Syndrome (1977), which produced the hit "Flash Light".

Solo work and influence

Clinton's 1982 solo album Computer Games yielded the hit single "Atomic Dog". His music was extensively sampled by hip hop artists, shaping the sound of G-funk, and he collaborated widely across funk, rock, and rap. Parliament-Funkadelic was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1997, and Clinton received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2019.
