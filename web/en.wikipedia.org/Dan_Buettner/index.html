Dan Buettner (born 1960) is an American author, explorer, and fellow of National Geographic. He is best known for identifying and popularizing the "Blue Zones" — regions of the world where people reportedly live much longer and healthier lives than average — and for writing a series of bestselling books about the lifestyle lessons drawn from those communities.

Blue Zones

Working with demographers and researchers, Buettner identified five Blue Zones: Okinawa (Japan), Sardinia (Italy), Ikaria (Greece), the Nicoya Peninsula (Costa Rica), and the Seventh-day Adventist community of Loma Linda, California (United States). His 2005 National Geographic cover story "The Secrets of a Long Life" and his 2008 book The Blue Zones brought the concept to a wide audience. Common factors he emphasizes include a plant-slant diet, regular natural movement, strong social ties, and a sense of purpose. The concept was further popularized by the 2023 Netflix documentary series Live to 100: Secrets of the Blue Zones.

Other work

Buettner has led cycling expeditions on several continents, setting Guinness World Records for transcontinental rides, and founded the Blue Zones Project, which works with cities to reshape environments for healthier living.
