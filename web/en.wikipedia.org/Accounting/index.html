Accounting, also known as accountancy, is the process of recording, classifying, summarising and reporting financial transactions to provide information that is useful in making business and economic decisions. Often called "the language of business", accounting measures the results of an organisation's economic activities and conveys this information to users such as investors, creditors, management and regulators.

History

Accounting records date back more than 7,000 years to ancient Mesopotamia, where clay tokens and later cuneiform tablets tracked goods and taxes. The modern discipline is usually traced to the Italian mathematician and Franciscan friar Luca Pacioli, whose Summa de arithmetica, geometria, proportioni et proportionalita (1494) contained the first printed description of the double-entry bookkeeping system used by Venetian merchants. In double entry, every transaction affects at least two accounts, and total debits always equal total credits.

Branches

Financial accounting

Financial accounting focuses on the preparation of financial statements — principally the balance sheet (statement of financial position), the income statement (statement of profit or loss), the statement of cash flows and the statement of changes in equity — for external users. It is governed by standards such as the International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) issued by the IASB and, in the United States, Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP) set by the FASB.

Management accounting

Management (or managerial) accounting provides information for internal decision-making, including budgeting, cost analysis, product pricing and performance measurement. Unlike financial accounting it is not bound by external standards.

Other branches

Tax accounting deals with compliance with tax law; auditing is the independent examination of financial statements to provide assurance on their reliability; forensic accounting investigates fraud and disputes; and governmental and non-profit accounting adapt the framework to public-sector and charitable organisations.

Fundamental concepts

The accounting equation — Assets = Liabilities + Equity — underlies the balance sheet. Other core concepts include the accrual basis (recognising transactions when they occur rather than when cash moves), the going-concern assumption, consistency, prudence and materiality. The main financial-accounting methods, such as depreciation of long-lived assets and inventory valuation (FIFO, LIFO, weighted average), shape reported results significantly.

Profession

Professional accountants typically hold qualifications such as Chartered Accountant (CA), Certified Public Accountant (CPA) or Chartered Certified Accountant (ACCA), and are subject to codes of ethics issued by bodies including the International Federation of Accountants. The largest global firms — Deloitte, PwC, EY and KPMG, the "Big Four" — dominate the audit market for listed companies.
