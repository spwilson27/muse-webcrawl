Kenneth Charles Marion Rexroth (22 December 1905 – 6 June 1982) was an American poet, translator, and critical essayist. A central figure of the San Francisco Renaissance, he is regarded as a father figure of the Beat Generation, although he disliked the label and kept his distance from the movement's celebrity.

Early life

Rexroth was born in South Bend, Indiana, and was largely self-educated after an unhappy childhood in Ohio and Chicago, where he frequented the bohemian and radical circles around the Dil Pickle Club. A conscientious objector during the Second World War, he settled in San Francisco in 1927 and became active in anarchist and pacifist politics, helping to found the Libertarian Circle.

Poetry

Rexroth's verse combines classical form, erotic and mystical themes, and a deep engagement with nature, especially the Sierra Nevada, which he hiked throughout his life. Collections such as In What Hour (1940), The Phoenix and the Tortoise (1944), and The Signature of All Things (1949) established his reputation. He also pioneered poetry readings accompanied by jazz, a practice later taken up by the Beats.

San Francisco Renaissance and the Beats

In October 1955 Rexroth acted as master of ceremonies at the famous Six Gallery reading, where Allen Ginsberg first performed Howl. Though he championed younger poets, he grew critical of the commercialisation of the Beat phenomenon and of what he saw as its literary shallowness.

Translations and criticism

Rexroth was a prolific translator from Chinese, Japanese, Greek, Latin, and French; his One Hundred Poems from the Chinese (1956) and translations of Du Fu and Li Qingzhao remain widely read. His essays, collected in volumes such as Classics Revisited, championed overlooked authors and world literature, and he served for years as a book reviewer and commentator on KPFA radio. He died in Santa Barbara in 1982.
