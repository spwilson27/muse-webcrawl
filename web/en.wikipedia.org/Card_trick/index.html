Card trick

A card trick is a feat of magic performed with playing cards, in which the magician appears to achieve an impossible outcome — locating a chosen card, transforming one card into another, or making cards vanish and reappear. Card tricks range from self-working mathematical effects requiring no skill to routines demanding years of practice in sleight of hand.

Techniques

Foundations of card magic include false shuffles and cuts that preserve the deck's order, forces that make a spectator's "free" choice predetermined, controls that bring a selected card to a known position, palms that secretly steal cards from the deck, and gimmicked apparatus such as double-backed cards, stripper decks and marked cards. Classic plots include the ambitious card, in which a signed card repeatedly rises to the top of the deck, and "Out of This World", devised by Paul Curry, in which a spectator separates red and black cards by intuition alone.

History

Card manipulation emerged after playing cards spread through Europe in the 14th century. The foundational text of modern card magic is Jean Hugard and Frederick Braué's The Royal Road to Card Magic (1948), and Dai Vernon's close-up work profoundly influenced the field. Cardistry — the purely aesthetic flourishing of cards — has developed into a related performance art of its own.
