Marcomannic Wars

The Marcomannic Wars (Latin: Bellum Germanicum et Sarmaticum) were a series of wars lasting from about AD 166 until 180. These wars pitted the Roman Empire against principally the Germanic Marcomanni and Quadi and the Sarmatian Iazyges, along with a wide range of other peoples, along the Empire's whole northern frontier on the Danube. Pressure from migrating peoples further north and east — including Goths moving toward the Black Sea — pushed these groups against the Roman border. The wars occupied much of the reign of Emperor Marcus Aurelius, who died on campaign in AD 180.

Course of the wars

The crisis began in 166–167 as the Empire was weakened by troops returning from the Parthian war who brought the Antonine Plague. Lombards and Ubii crossed the Danube; in 170 a great coalition swept through Pannonia, besieged Aquileia — the first time Italy itself was threatened in centuries — and raided as far as Greece, where the Costoboci sacked Eleusis. Marcus Aurelius, co-emperor with Lucius Verus until the latter's death in 169, established headquarters at Carnuntum and Aquileia, raised two new legions (II and III Italica), and even sold imperial treasures to fund the war. Roman counteroffensives carried the war across the Danube; the famous "rain miracle" of 174, in which a storm saved a surrounded Roman army, was celebrated on the Column of Marcus Aurelius.

Settlement

Marcus Aurelius intended to annex the lands of the Marcomanni and Iazyges as new provinces (Marcomannia and Sarmatia), but revolts elsewhere and his death at Vindobona (Vienna) or Sirmium in March 180 ended the plan. His son and successor Commodus quickly negotiated peace, restoring the status quo ante with buffer zones along the Danube, subsidies and recruitment arrangements; the frontier then held, broadly, for another half-century. The wars revealed both the resilience of the Antonine military system and the strain that simultaneous plague, fiscal pressure and frontier crisis placed on the Empire.
