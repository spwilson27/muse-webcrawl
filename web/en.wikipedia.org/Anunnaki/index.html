Anunnaki

The Anunnaki were a group of major deities in Mesopotamian religion

The Anunnaki (Sumerian: 𒀭𒀀𒉣𒈾, also transcribed Anunaki, Annunaki, or Anunna) were a group of deities in the religions of the ancient Sumerians, Akkadians, Assyrians, and Babylonians. The name is generally interpreted to mean "princely offspring" or "offspring of Anu," the sky god. In early Sumerian texts the Anunnaki appear as the great gods of heaven and earth who decreed the fates of humanity; in later Akkadian tradition they are sometimes contrasted with the Igigi and associated with the underworld as judges of the dead.

In Mesopotamian texts

The Anunnaki appear in myths including the Enūma Eliš, where Marduk assigns them their stations, and the Atrahasis epic, in which they impose labour on the lesser gods—the burden from which humanity is created to relieve them. Their number varies by text, with seven or fifty great gods mentioned, and lists include Anu, Enlil, Enki, Ninhursag, Nanna, Utu, and Inanna.

Pseudoarchaeology

In the twentieth century, writer Zecharia Sitchin popularized the claim that the Anunnaki were extraterrestrial beings who visited Earth from a planet called Nibiru and engineered humanity to mine gold. These claims, originating in his 1976 book The Twelfth Planet, are rejected by scholars and scientists as pseudoarchaeology, relying on mistranslations and unsupported readings of the texts.
