Mein Kampf

Mein Kampf (German for "My Struggle") is a 1925 autobiographical and political manifesto by Adolf Hitler, the leader of the Nazi Party. The book outlines Hitler's political ideology and future plans for Germany. Volume 1 was published on 18 July 1925 and Volume 2 in 1926. Much of it was dictated by Hitler to his deputy Rudolf Hess while Hitler was imprisoned at Landsberg Prison following the failed Beer Hall Putsch of November 1923.

The book combines elements of autobiography with an exposition of Hitler's ideas: antisemitism, anticommunism, German nationalism, and the doctrine of Lebensraum ("living space") in Eastern Europe. It became a bestseller in Germany after Hitler's rise to power in 1933; copies were given to soldiers and at weddings, and by 1945 some 12 million copies had been printed in German, with translations into many languages.

Contents

The first volume, subtitled "A Reckoning", covers Hitler's youth in Austria, his time in Vienna and Munich, his military service in the First World War, and his early political career. The second volume, "The National Socialist Movement", sets out the party programme and strategy. Throughout, Hitler presents history as a struggle between races and identifies Jews as the principal enemy of the German people.

Postwar status

After the Second World War, the Allied occupation authorities transferred the copyright to the Free State of Bavaria, which refused to allow republication in Germany for decades. When the copyright expired at the end of 2015, the Institute for Contemporary History in Munich published a heavily annotated scholarly edition in 2016, which became a bestseller. The book remains a subject of study for historians of Nazism and extremism.
