High Arctic relocation

The High Arctic relocation (French: La délocalisation du Haut-Arctique) was a relocation of Inuit families by the Government of Canada in the 1950s to the far north of the Arctic Archipelago. In 1953 and 1955, families from Inukjuak in northern Quebec (and some from Pond Inlet) were moved to new settlements at Resolute Bay and Grise Fiord on Cornwallis and Ellesmere Islands.

Background and motives

The government publicly justified the move as a response to overpopulation, declining game, and welfare dependency in Inukjuak, promising abundant wildlife and a chance to return within a year or two if the relocatees wished. Historians and Inuit organisations have long argued that asserting Canadian sovereignty over the High Arctic during the Cold War was also a motivating factor, a conclusion later supported by archival research, though the government maintained sovereignty was not the primary purpose.

Hardship and aftermath

The relocatees faced an unfamiliar environment, much colder winters, months of darkness, and species they had never hunted, and the promised return was not honoured for many years. Some families did not return to Inukjuak until the 1980s. In 2010 the Government of Canada issued a formal apology for the relocation and its consequences.
