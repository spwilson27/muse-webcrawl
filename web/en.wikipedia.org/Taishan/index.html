Taishan

Taishan (Chinese: 台山; pinyin: Táishān; Jyutping: Toi4saan1), formerly romanized as Toishan or Toisan, is a county-level city in southwestern Guangdong Province, China. It is administered as part of the prefecture-level city of Jiangmen. During the 19th and early 20th centuries, Taishan was a major source of Chinese emigration to North America, and the Taishanese dialect (Hoisan-wa) was once the predominant variety of Chinese spoken in Chinatowns across the United States and Canada.

Geography

Taishan lies on the coast of the South China Sea, west of the Pearl River Delta. The county-level city covers an area of roughly 3,286 square kilometres and includes several offshore islands, the largest of which are Shangchuan Island and Xiachuan Island. The terrain is predominantly hilly, with coastal plains along the shore.

History

The area was established as Xinning County in 1499 during the Ming dynasty. It was renamed Taishan in 1914 to avoid confusion with Xinning in Hunan Province. Following the First Opium War and the opening of treaty ports, large numbers of Taishanese labourers emigrated abroad, particularly to California during the Gold Rush and the construction of the transcontinental railroads. Remittances from overseas Chinese funded schools, hospitals, and the distinctive diaolou fortified towers found across the region.

Demographics and language

Taishan is known as the "First Home Town of Overseas Chinese" because of the large diaspora claiming ancestry there; it is estimated that more people of Taishanese descent live abroad than in Taishan itself. The local language is Taishanese, a Yue Chinese dialect related to but distinct from Cantonese.

Economy

The economy includes agriculture (the area is known for rice and eels), manufacturing, and tourism centred on hot springs and island resorts. Shangchuan Island is traditionally identified as the place where the Jesuit missionary Saint Francis Xavier died in 1552.
