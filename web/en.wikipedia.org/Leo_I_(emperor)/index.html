Leo I (emperor)

Leo I (Greek: Λέων, Leōn; c. 401 – 18 January 474), also known as Leo the Thracian, was Eastern Roman emperor from 457 to 474. A soldier of Thracian origin raised to the throne by the powerful general Aspar, Leo proved far from a puppet: he built up a rival power base of Isaurian troops, resisted Germanic domination of the army, and launched an ambitious but disastrous expedition against the Vandals in North Africa. He was the first emperor to be crowned by the Patriarch of Constantinople and the first to legislate in Greek.

Reign

On the death of Marcian in 457, the Alan general Aspar, who could not take the throne himself because of his Arian faith, installed Leo, an officer in his service. Leo's coronation by Patriarch Anatolius set a precedent for Byzantine inaugurations. Determined to escape Aspar's control, Leo promoted the Isaurians of southern Anatolia, led by Tarasicodissa — the future emperor Zeno, whom he married to his daughter Ariadne. In 471 Aspar and his son Ardabur were murdered in the palace, earning Leo the epithet "the Butcher" and ending Germanic ascendancy at court.

Foreign policy

In 468 Leo mounted a vast joint expedition with the West against Genseric's Vandal kingdom in Africa, commanded by his brother-in-law Basiliscus. The fleet was destroyed by Vandal fireships off Cape Bon — one of the most expensive failures of the age, said to have cost over 100,000 pounds of gold. Leo also intervened in Western imperial successions, installing Anthemius in 467, and fought the Ostrogoths and Huns in the Balkans. He died of dysentery in January 474 and was briefly succeeded by his young grandson Leo II.
