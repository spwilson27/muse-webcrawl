Sacramento metropolitan area


Image placeholder: Sacramento metropolitan area

The Sacramento metropolitan area, officially the Sacramento–Roseville–Folsom, CA Metropolitan Statistical Area, is a metropolitan area in Northern California centered on Sacramento, the state capital. As of the 2020 census its population was about 2.4 million, making it the fifth-largest metropolitan area in California. The core counties are Sacramento, Placer, El Dorado and Yolo, and the wider combined statistical area includes the Yuba City and Truckee–Grass Valley areas.

Geography and economy

The area lies at the confluence of the Sacramento and American rivers in the northern Central Valley, at the foot of the Sierra Nevada foothills. Sacramento's role as the seat of California state government makes public administration the dominant employer, alongside health care, education, technology and food processing. Major suburbs and satellite cities include Roseville, Elk Grove, Folsom, Citrus Heights, Rancho Cordova, Davis and Woodland. The region is served by Sacramento International Airport, Interstate 5 and Interstate 80, Amtrak's Capitol Corridor, and Sacramento Regional Transit light rail.

History

The area grew from Sutter's Fort (founded 1839) and the city of Sacramento, incorporated in 1850, which became the western terminus of the First Transcontinental Railroad in 1869. Suburban growth accelerated after the Second World War, and the eastern suburbs along the Highway 50 and Interstate 80 corridors expanded rapidly from the 1990s onward.
