National Gallery of Modern Art

The National Gallery of Modern Art (NGMA) is the premier art gallery in India devoted to modern and contemporary art. It is run by the Ministry of Culture, Government of India. The main museum is in New Delhi, with branches in Mumbai and Bengaluru.

History

The NGMA was established in 1954 in the former Jaipur House at India Gate, New Delhi, and inaugurated by Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru. Its founding collection drew on works by leading Indian artists, and it has since grown to hold more than 17,000 works.

Collection

The collection includes paintings, sculptures, prints and photographs by artists such as Raja Ravi Varma, Rabindranath Tagore, Abanindranath Tagore, Amrita Sher-Gil, Jamini Roy, Nandalal Bose and members of the Progressive Artists' Group, alongside international artists including Thomas Daniell and Jacob Epstein. The gallery also holds an important archive of photographs and a specialised art library.

Branches

The Mumbai branch opened in 1996 in the Sir Cowasji Jehangir Public Hall, and the Bengaluru branch opened in 2009 in the Manikyavelu Mansion on Palace Road.
