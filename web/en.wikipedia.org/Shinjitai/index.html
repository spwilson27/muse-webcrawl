Shinjitai

Shinjitai (新字体, "new character form") are the simplified forms of kanji used in Japan since reforms promulgated after the Second World War. The older, traditional forms are called kyūjitai (旧字体).

Reform

In 1946 the Japanese government published the tōyō kanji list with simplified standard forms, reducing stroke counts and regularising components — for example 國 → 国 (country), 學 → 学 (learn), 廣 → 広 (wide), and 龍 → 竜 (dragon). The jōyō kanji lists of 1981 and 2010 retained these forms. Simplification in Japan was generally less extensive than China's simplified characters, and many kyūjitai forms remain in personal names and historical texts.

Usage

Some simplified forms coincide with Chinese simplifications, others differ, and a number of characters were left unchanged. Extended shinjitai — informal further simplifications used in handwriting — exist for some characters not officially simplified.
