Merovingian dynasty

The Merovingian dynasty was the ruling family of the Franks from the middle of the 5th century until 751. They first appear as "Kings of the Franks" in the Roman army of northern Gaul. By the time of Childeric I (died 481/482), the son of Merovech — from whom the dynasty takes its name — they were established in Tournai. Childeric's son Clovis I became the first king of all the Franks and founder of the realm that would become France.

Clovis and his successors

Clovis (reigned 481–511) conquered most of Gaul, defeating Syagrius at Soissons, the Alemanni and the Visigoths at Vouillé, and converted to Nicene Christianity, traditionally dated to 496. On his death the kingdom was divided among his four sons in the Frankish custom of partible inheritance, producing the sub-kingdoms of Neustria, Austrasia, Burgundy and Aquitaine.

Mayors of the palace and decline

From the later seventh century real power passed to the mayors of the palace, particularly the Austrasian Pippinids. Charles Martel, mayor from 715 to 741, defeated the Umayyad invasion at Tours-Poitiers in 732. His son Pepin the Short deposed the last Merovingian king, Childeric III, in 751 with papal approval, founding the Carolingian dynasty. Later Merovingian kings were derided by Carolingian propaganda as rois fainéants — "do-nothing kings" — long-haired figureheads carried in ox-carts.
