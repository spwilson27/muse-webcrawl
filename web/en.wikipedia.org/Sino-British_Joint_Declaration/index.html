Sino-British Joint Declaration

Signed19 December 1984
LocationBeijing, China
SignatoriesUnited Kingdom; China
SubjectFuture of Hong Kong

The Sino-British Joint Declaration — formally the Joint Declaration of the Government of the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland and the Government of the People's Republic of China on the Question of Hong Kong — is an international treaty signed in Beijing on 19 December 1984. It settled the future of Hong Kong after the expiry of the British lease on the New Territories in 1997.

Terms

Under the declaration, the People's Republic of China would resume the exercise of sovereignty over Hong Kong (including Hong Kong Island, Kowloon and the New Territories) on 1 July 1997, while the United Kingdom agreed to restore Hong Kong to China on that date. China declared its basic policies: Hong Kong would become a Special Administrative Region enjoying a high degree of autonomy under the principle of "one country, two systems", and its existing social and economic systems, lifestyle, rights and freedoms would remain unchanged for 50 years.

Implementation

The declaration was registered with the United Nations in 1985. Its policies were elaborated in the Basic Law of Hong Kong, adopted in 1990, and the transfer of sovereignty took place at midnight on 30 June – 1 July 1997. The status of the declaration has since been disputed: China has described it as a historical document, while the United Kingdom maintains it remains a valid treaty whose commitments it monitors.
