Grumman F-14 Tomcat

The Grumman F-14 Tomcat is an American carrier-capable supersonic, twin-engine, two-seat, twin-tail, variable-sweep wing fighter aircraft. It was developed for the United States Navy's Naval Fighter Experimental program and entered service in 1974, replacing the McDonnell Douglas F-4 Phantom II as the Navy's primary maritime air superiority fighter and fleet defense interceptor.

An F-14 Tomcat of the United States Navy

Design and role

The F-14 was designed around the AWG-9 radar and the long-range AIM-54 Phoenix air-to-air missile, a combination intended to intercept Soviet bombers at great distances from the carrier battle group. Its variable-geometry wings sweep automatically between 20 and 68 degrees to suit speed and maneuvering requirements. Two crew — a pilot and a radar intercept officer — operate the aircraft.

Service history

Tomcats saw combat over Libya in 1981 and 1989, shooting down four Libyan aircraft, and served in the Gulf War of 1991. Later variants such as the F-14D added improved engines and the ability to deliver precision-guided munitions. The type was retired by the U.S. Navy in 2006, replaced by the F/A-18E/F Super Hornet. Iran, which bought F-14s in the 1970s, has continued to operate a dwindling fleet. The aircraft achieved wide popular fame through the 1986 film Top Gun.
