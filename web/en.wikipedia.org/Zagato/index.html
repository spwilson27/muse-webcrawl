Zagato

Zagato is an Italian coachbuilding company founded in Milan in 1919 by Ugo Zagato. It is celebrated for designing and hand-building lightweight, aerodynamic car bodies for manufacturers including Alfa Romeo, Aston Martin, Ferrari, Maserati and Lancia. The company's best-known design signature is the "double bubble" roof, in which the roofline carries two gentle humps above the occupants' heads to reduce frontal area while preserving headroom.

History

Ugo Zagato, who had worked in the aircraft industry during the First World War, applied aviation techniques — light alloy framing and streamlined shapes — to automobile coachwork. In the 1920s and 1930s, Zagato-bodied Alfa Romeos achieved repeated success in events such as the Mille Miglia and Targa Florio. Elio Zagato, Ugo's son, led the firm in the post-war period, producing celebrated gran turismo designs such as the Alfa Romeo Giulietta SZ and the Aston Martin DB4 GT Zagato of 1960, one of the most valuable collector cars in the world.

Design and later work

Under Andrea Zagato, the third generation of the family, the firm continued limited-production collaborations, including the Aston Martin V12 Zagato and the Iso Rivolta GTZ. Zagato models are typically produced in very small numbers, often as numbered collector editions, and the company also works in industrial and rail design from its atelier near Milan.
