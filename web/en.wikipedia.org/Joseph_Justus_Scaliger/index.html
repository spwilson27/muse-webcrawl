Joseph Justus Scaliger

Joseph Justus Scaliger (5 August 1540 – 21 January 1609) was a French Calvinist religious leader and scholar, known for expanding the notion of classical history into the study of ancient chronology. He is considered the founder of the science of chronology and one of the greatest scholars of the Renaissance.

Life

Scaliger was the tenth child of the Italian scholar Julius Caesar Scaliger. Largely self-taught in Greek and the classical authors, he traveled in France and Scotland, converted to Protestantism, and fought in the French Wars of Religion. After the St. Bartholomew's Day massacre he fled to Geneva, where he became a professor. In 1593 he accepted a chair at the University of Leiden in the Netherlands, where he remained until his death, attracting students from across Europe.

Work

His Opus de emendatione temporum (1583) revolutionized the dating of ancient history, and the posthumous Thesaurus temporum (1606) reconstructed the lost chronicle of Eusebius and synchronized Egyptian, Babylonian, Greek, and biblical timelines. Scaliger devised the Julian Period of 7,980 years, still used in astronomy as the basis of Julian day numbers. He also edited and commented on numerous classical and astronomical texts, including Manilius and Festus.
