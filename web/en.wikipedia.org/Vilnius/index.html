Vilnius

Vilnius
Capital and largest city of Lithuania
Population: about 600,000
Founded: traditionally 1323 (letter of Gediminas)
Known for: Baroque Old Town, a UNESCO World Heritage Site

Vilnius is the capital and largest city of Lithuania, situated at the confluence of the Vilnia and Neris rivers in the southeast of the country. Its Old Town, one of the largest surviving medieval old towns in Northern Europe, is celebrated for its Baroque architecture and its hundreds of churches.

History

Legend places its founding in a dream of Grand Duke Gediminas, whose letter of 1323 inviting foreign merchants and craftsmen is the city's first mention. Vilnius became capital of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania and, after 1569, a major city of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth. Vilnius University, founded in 1579, is among the oldest in Eastern Europe. The city was a great centre of Jewish scholarship — home of the Vilna Gaon — until the Holocaust destroyed its Jewish community (see Vilna Ghetto). Occupied in turn by Germany and the USSR during the Second World War, Vilnius became capital of restored independent Lithuania in 1990–91.

Culture

Landmarks include Gediminas Tower, the Cathedral, the Gate of Dawn, and the bohemian self-declared "Republic of Užupis". Vilnius was a European Capital of Culture in 2009.
