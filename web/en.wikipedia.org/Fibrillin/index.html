Fibrillin

Fibrillin
TypeGlycoprotein, extracellular matrix
GenesFBN1, FBN2, FBN3
StructureLarge cysteine-rich glycoprotein (~350 kDa)
FunctionForms microfibrils in elastic and non-elastic connective tissue
Associated diseasesMarfan syndrome, congenital contractural arachnodactyly, stiff skin syndrome

Fibrillin is a large glycoprotein of the extracellular matrix that serves as a structural component of calcium-binding microfibrils, which provide force-bearing structural support in elastic and non-elastic connective tissue throughout the body. Fibrillin microfibrils form the scaffold on which elastin is deposited in elastic fibres, and also occur independently in tissues such as the ciliary zonules of the eye. Mutations in the fibrillin-1 gene (FBN1) cause Marfan syndrome.

Types

Humans have three fibrillin genes: FBN1 (chromosome 15), encoding fibrillin-1; FBN2 (chromosome 5), encoding fibrillin-2, expressed mainly early in development; and FBN3 (chromosome 19), encoding fibrillin-3. The proteins are large — around 2,800 amino acids and 350 kDa — and are rich in cysteine, with numerous epidermal growth factor-like domains, most of which bind calcium, interspersed with transforming growth factor β-binding protein-like domains.

Function

Fibrillin molecules assemble head-to-tail into microfibrils about 10–12 nm in diameter with a characteristic "beads-on-a-string" appearance in electron microscopy. Beyond their structural role, microfibrils regulate the bioavailability of growth factors, particularly TGF-β, which is sequestered in the matrix bound to latent TGF-β-binding proteins associated with fibrillin. Loss of fibrillin-1 therefore leads both to weakened tissue and to excessive TGF-β signalling, a mechanism now understood to drive the aortic aneurysm of Marfan syndrome.

Clinical significance

More than a thousand FBN1 mutations have been associated with Marfan syndrome, an autosomal dominant connective-tissue disorder affecting the skeleton, eyes and cardiovascular system. Mutations in FBN2 cause congenital contractural arachnodactyly (Beals syndrome). Other FBN1-related phenotypes include stiff skin syndrome, acromicric and geleophysic dysplasias, and familial thoracic aortic aneurysm. The discovery of the TGF-β mechanism led to trials of angiotensin-receptor blockers such as losartan in Marfan patients.
