Mount Gerizim

Mount Gerizim is one of two mountains flanking the city of Nablus (biblical Shechem) in the West Bank, the other being Mount Ebal. Rising to about 881 metres above sea level, Gerizim is sacred to the Samaritans, who regard it — rather than Jerusalem — as the chosen place of God, the site of their temple and the centre of their worship to this day.

In the Bible

In Deuteronomy, the Israelites are commanded that on entering the Promised Land they should pronounce blessings on Mount Gerizim and curses on Mount Ebal; Joshua 8 describes the ceremony being carried out. In the Samaritan tradition, Gerizim is also identified with Mount Moriah, the place of the binding of Isaac, and the Samaritan Pentateuch names it as the place God has chosen for sacrifice.

Samaritan temple and history

A Samaritan temple stood on the summit from at least the Persian period, probably the fifth or fourth century BCE, until it was destroyed by the Hasmonean king John Hyrcanus around 110 BCE, a catastrophe that deepened the breach between Jews and Samaritans. Under Rome the site held a temple to Zeus Hypsistos, shown on local coins; a Byzantine octagonal church of the Theotokos was built on the summit in the fifth century CE, and its ruins, along with those of the Samaritan precinct and a later fortress, occupy the plateau.

Modern community

The surviving Samaritan community of a few hundred people lives mainly in the village of Kiryat Luza on the mountain's shoulder, where the Passover sacrifice is still offered annually on the summit plateau — one of the most distinctive continuing rituals of the ancient world.
