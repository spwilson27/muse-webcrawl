Tomas Garrigue Masaryk

Tomas Garrigue Masaryk (7 March 1850 – 14 September 1937) was a Czechoslovak statesman, philosopher and sociologist who founded Czechoslovakia and served as its first president from 1918 to 1935. He is regarded as the founding father of the state and is often called the "President Liberator".

Early life and scholarship

Born in Hodonin, Moravia, then part of the Austrian Empire, to a Slovak father and a Moravian mother, Masaryk studied philosophy in Vienna and Leipzig. He became a professor at Charles University in Prague and a member of the Austrian parliament, where he defended the rights of Czechs and other smaller nations. He took his middle name from his American wife, Charlotte Garrigue, whom he married in 1878. He gained renown for defending Leopold Hilsner, a Jew falsely accused of ritual murder, against antisemitic prejudice.

Founding of Czechoslovakia

During the First World War Masaryk went into exile, organising the Czechoslovak National Council and the Czechoslovak Legions and winning Allied recognition of an independent Czechoslovak state. He was elected president in November 1918 and re-elected three times, presiding over Central Europe's most durable interwar democracy until resigning due to ill health in 1935. He died two years later at his residence in Lany.
