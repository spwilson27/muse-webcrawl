Crisis in Venezuela

An ongoing socioeconomic and political crisis began in Venezuela during the presidency of Hugo Chávez and worsened under Nicolás Maduro, marked by hyperinflation, economic contraction, shortages of food and medicine, unemployment, and mass emigration.

Venezuela's economy depended heavily on oil exports; when world oil prices collapsed in 2014, state revenues fell sharply. Mismanagement of the state oil company PDVSA, price and currency controls, expropriations, and mounting debt compounded the shock. Between roughly 2013 and 2021 Venezuela's GDP contracted by about three-quarters, among the largest peacetime economic collapses recorded. Annual inflation reached hundreds of thousands of percent in 2018–2019 before easing after partial dollarisation.

Humanitarian impact and emigration

Shortages of essentials, the collapse of public services and electricity blackouts, and rising poverty drove more than seven million Venezuelans to leave the country, creating one of the world's largest displacement crises, with the largest numbers hosted by Colombia, Peru, and other regional states. A disputed 2018 presidential election produced a political standoff in 2019 when National Assembly president Juan Guaidó claimed the interim presidency and was recognised by dozens of countries, though Maduro retained power.
