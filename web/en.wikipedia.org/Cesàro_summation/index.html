Cesàro summation

In mathematical analysis, Cesàro summation (also known as the Cesàro mean) assigns values to some infinite series that do not converge in the usual sense. Named after the Italian mathematician Ernesto Cesàro (1859–1906), the method takes the limit of the arithmetic means of a series' partial sums.


Cesàro means converge to 1/2 for Grandi's series

Definition

For a series with partial sums s1, s2, …, the Cesàro mean is the average (s1 + … + sn)/n. If these averages converge to a limit A, the series is said to be Cesàro summable to A. Every convergent series is Cesàro summable to its ordinary sum, but some divergent series also acquire values: the series 1 − 1 + 1 − 1 + ⋯ (Grandi's series) is Cesàro summable to 1/2.

Generalizations

The idea extends to higher-order (C, k) means, and is closely related to Abel summation and to the summability methods used in Fourier analysis, where Fejér's theorem shows that the Cesàro means of a Fourier series converge to the function.
