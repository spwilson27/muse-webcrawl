Fulfulde

Fulfulde, also known as Fula, Fulani, Pulaar, or Peul, is a Senegambian language spoken as a first language by the Fula people across a broad belt of West and Central Africa, from Senegal and Mauritania in the west to Sudan in the east. With tens of millions of speakers, including many who use it as a lingua franca, it is among the most widely spread languages of Africa geographically.

Classification and dialects

Fulfulde belongs to the Senegambian branch of the Atlantic family within the Niger–Congo languages, and is closely related to Wolof and Serer. Major dialect clusters include Pulaar of Senegal and Mauritania, Pular of Guinea, Maasina Fulfulde of Mali, Nigerian Fulfulde, and Adamawa Fulfulde of Cameroon. The varieties are not all mutually intelligible. Like other Atlantic languages, Fulfulde has an elaborate noun class system with initial consonant mutation.

Writing

Fulfulde has been written for centuries in an Arabic-based script called Ajami, used for religious and poetic literature. Today the Latin alphabet is official in most countries, with special characters such as ɓ, ɗ, ŋ, and ƴ for implosive and glottalised consonants. In the 1980s the brothers Ibrahima and Abdoulaye Barry invented the Adlam script in Guinea, which has gained growing use and digital support for writing the language.
