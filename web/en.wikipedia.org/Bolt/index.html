Bolt (fastener)

ClassificationThreaded fastener
ComponentsHead, shank, external thread; usually used with a nut
Common typesHex bolt, carriage bolt, eye bolt, U-bolt, anchor bolt

A bolt is a form of threaded fastener with an external male thread, requiring a matching nut for most uses. Bolts are closely related to screws, and the distinction is often one of application: a bolt is generally inserted through unthreaded holes in assembled parts and secured with a nut, while a screw forms or mates with a thread in the part itself.

Design

A typical bolt consists of a head — most commonly hexagonal, for turning with a wrench — and a cylindrical shank that may be fully or partially threaded. Bolts are specified by diameter, thread pitch, length, and strength grade, under standards such as ISO metric and SAE/ASME inch series.

Materials and strength

Bolts are made from carbon steel, alloy steel, stainless steel, brass, titanium, and other metals, with property classes (for example ISO class 8.8 or 10.9) indicating tensile strength. Proper tightening to a specified torque creates preload that clamps the joint together.

Applications

Bolted joints are fundamental to construction, machinery, vehicles, bridges, and furniture. Specialised forms include carriage bolts for wood, eye bolts for lifting, U-bolts for pipes, and anchor bolts set in concrete.
