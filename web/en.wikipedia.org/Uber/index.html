Uber

Uber Technologies, Inc. is an American multinational transportation company that provides ride-hailing services, courier services, food delivery, and freight transport. Founded in 2009 in San Francisco by Garrett Camp and Travis Kalanick as "UberCab," it launched its app in 2010, allowing users to hail a car from a smartphone.

Uber pioneered the gig-economy model of contracting with independent drivers who use their own vehicles, with fares calculated dynamically through "surge pricing." It expanded rapidly worldwide, often clashing with taxi industries and regulators over licensing, safety, and the employment status of drivers. In 2017 Kalanick resigned as CEO amid controversies and was succeeded by Dara Khosrowshahi.

The company went public on the New York Stock Exchange in May 2019. Its businesses include the core Mobility segment, Uber Eats food delivery (launched 2014; it acquired Postmates in 2020), and Uber Freight. Uber sold its self-driving division, the Advanced Technologies Group, to Aurora Innovation in 2020. It operates in roughly 70 countries and thousands of cities, with well over 100 million monthly active users.
