Boeing X-48

The Boeing X-48 is an American experimental unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) built to investigate the characteristics of blended wing body (BWB) aircraft, a type of flying wing design in which the traditionally distinct wing and body are smoothly blended. The program was a joint effort of Boeing, NASA, and the United States Air Force, with the subscale testbeds built by Cranfield Aerospace in the United Kingdom.

Background

Studies by McDonnell Douglas and NASA in the 1990s suggested a blended wing body transport could offer major gains in fuel efficiency, noise, and payload volume compared with conventional tube-and-wing airliners. Boeing's Phantom Works pursued the concept, which produces lift across the entire airframe and places engines atop the rear fuselage, shielding their noise from the ground.

Test program

The 8.5%-scale X-48B, with a wingspan of about 6.2 m (20.4 ft), first flew in July 2007 at NASA's Dryden (now Armstrong) Flight Research Center at Edwards Air Force Base. Two X-48B airframes accumulated more than 120 flights exploring the low-speed handling of tailless blended designs, including stall behavior and engine-out control. The X-48C variant, modified with a reshaped aft deck and two engines in place of three, flew from 2012 to 2013 to study pitch control and further noise reductions. Trials concluded in April 2013.

Legacy

The data gathered informed NASA and industry work on future ultra-efficient transports, including later hybrid wing body concepts and wind-tunnel models, even though no full-scale BWB airliner has yet entered production.
