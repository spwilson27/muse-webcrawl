Prime lens

A prime lens is a photographic lens of fixed focal length, as opposed to a zoom lens, which has a variable focal length. Common prime focal lengths include 35 mm, 50 mm (the "nifty fifty"), and 85 mm for portraits.

Advantages

Because their optical design is optimized for a single focal length, prime lenses are generally sharper, smaller, lighter, and cheaper than zooms, and typically offer wider maximum apertures (such as f/1.8 or f/1.4), enabling better low-light performance and shallower depth of field.

Use

Photographers using a prime lens change composition by physically moving — "zooming with their feet." Primes are favored in street photography, portraiture, and cinema, where dedicated prime sets cover a range of fixed focal lengths.
