In mathematics, the modular group is the projective special linear group PSL(2, ℤ) of 2 × 2 matrices with integer entries and determinant 1, with matrices identified up to sign. It acts on the complex upper half-plane by fractional linear (Möbius) transformations, and is a fundamental object in the theory of modular forms, elliptic curves and hyperbolic geometry.

Generators and structure

The modular group is generated by the translation T: z ↦ z + 1 and the inversion S: z ↦ −1/z, subject to the relations S² = (ST)³ = identity. Consequently it is isomorphic to the free product of a cyclic group of order 2 with one of order 3. Every element is a matrix [[a, b], [c, d]] with ad − bc = 1, acting as z ↦ (az + b)/(cz + d).

Fundamental domain

The quotient of the upper half-plane by the modular group can be represented by the standard fundamental domain: the set of points z with |z| ≥ 1 and |Re(z)| ≤ 1/2. Images of this region under the group tile the half-plane, giving the half-plane the structure of a hyperbolic orbifold; points on the boundary of the domain are identified in pairs, and the two special points i and eiπ/3 have nontrivial stabilisers.

Subgroups and applications

Finite-index subgroups, especially the congruence subgroups Γ(N), Γ0(N) and Γ1(N) defined by congruence conditions on matrix entries, give rise to modular curves that classify elliptic curves with level structure. The modular group and its subgroups also appear in the theory of lattices, string theory, and the arithmetic of quadratic forms.
