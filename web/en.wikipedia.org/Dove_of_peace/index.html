Dove of peace


The dove with an olive branch is an international symbol of peace.

The dove of peace is a symbol representing peace, typically depicted as a white dove in flight, often carrying an olive branch in its beak. The image combines ancient, biblical, and modern associations and is used by peace movements, churches, and political organisations worldwide.

Origins

In the Book of Genesis, a dove released by Noah returns with an olive leaf, signalling that the Flood has ended and God's reconciliation with humanity. Early Christians adopted the dove as an image of the Holy Spirit and of peace. The olive branch itself derives from Greek and Roman symbolism of supplication and concord.

Modern use

The symbol's modern prominence dates to 1949, when Pablo Picasso's lithograph La Colombe was chosen as the emblem of the World Peace Congress in Paris. Picasso's stylised dove became a ubiquitous poster image of the postwar peace movement and appeared on stamps, banners, and the insignia of peace organisations. White doves are also released at ceremonies, including occasions at the Vatican and the Olympic Games.
