Effective field theory

In physics, an effective field theory is a type of approximation, or effective theory, for an underlying physical theory, such as a quantum field theory or a statistical mechanics model. An effective field theory includes only the degrees of freedom appropriate for describing physical phenomena occurring at a chosen length scale and energy scale, while ignoring substructure and processes at shorter distances (or, equivalently, at higher energies). Intuitively, one averages over behaviour at shorter length scales to obtain a simpler model at longer length scales, in the spirit of the renormalization group.

Principles

Effective field theories are constructed by writing down all interactions consistent with the symmetries of the problem, organised as an expansion in powers of the ratio of the energy of interest to a higher cutoff scale. The expansion coefficients (low-energy constants) encode the unknown short-distance physics and may be determined by matching to the underlying theory or by fitting to experiment. Because only a finite number of terms contribute at any given order, the theory is predictive despite containing infinitely many possible interactions — a resolution of the older concern that such theories are "non-renormalizable".

The Appelquist–Carazzone decoupling theorem underlies the approach: heavy particles affect low-energy physics only through renormalisation of couplings and through effects suppressed by powers of the heavy mass.

Examples

A classic example is Fermi's theory of beta decay, an effective theory of the weak interaction valid below the mass of the W boson. In nuclear and particle physics, chiral perturbation theory describes low-energy interactions of pions and nucleons. The Euler–Heisenberg Lagrangian describes light-by-light scattering below the electron mass. More recently, the Standard Model Effective Field Theory (SMEFT) parametrizes possible effects of physics beyond the Standard Model through higher-dimensional operators, and effective theories are also used in condensed matter physics and general relativity (for example, in modelling gravitational-wave sources).

Relation to renormalization

The modern Wilsonian picture of renormalization — in which a theory is defined with a cutoff and couplings "flow" as the cutoff is lowered — makes effective field theory the natural language for quantum field theory generally: every quantum field theory may be regarded as an effective theory valid below some scale.
