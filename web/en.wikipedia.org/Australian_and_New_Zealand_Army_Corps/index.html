Australian and New Zealand Army Corps

The Australian and New Zealand Army Corps (ANZAC) was a First World War army corps formed in Egypt in late 1914 from Australian Imperial Force and New Zealand Expeditionary Force units. Commanded initially by General William Birdwood, the corps is best known for its role in the Gallipoli Campaign.

Gallipoli and after

On 25 April 1915 ANZAC troops landed at what became known as Anzac Cove on the Gallipoli Peninsula in Türkiye, in an ultimately unsuccessful campaign against the Ottoman Empire that lasted until evacuation in December 1915 and January 1916. The landing date, Anzac Day, became the principal day of remembrance in Australia and New Zealand. After Gallipoli the corps was reorganised into I ANZAC Corps and II ANZAC Corps and fought on the Western Front in France and Belgium. In the Second World War the name was briefly revived for a corps in the Greek campaign of 1941.

Legacy

The "Anzac legend" — emphasising mateship, endurance and egalitarianism — became central to national identity in both countries, and the term Anzac is protected by law in Australia. The Anzac spirit is also invoked for joint Australian–New Zealand military cooperation to the present day.
