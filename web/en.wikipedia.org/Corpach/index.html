Corpach

Corpach (Scottish Gaelic: A' Chòrpach) is a large village north of Fort William, in the Scottish Highlands. It lies at the head of Loch Linnhe and at the western entrance of the Caledonian Canal. The name is thought to mean "place of the bodies", supposedly because corpses of island chiefs were rested here on their way to burial on Iona.

Geography and transport

Corpach is the site of the sea lock at the southern end of the Caledonian Canal, where it meets Loch Linnhe. Just east of the village is Neptune's Staircase, a remarkable flight of eight locks at Banavie raising the canal by 19 metres. The West Highland Line railway passes through Corpach station, and the A830 "Road to the Isles" runs through the village toward Arisaig and Mallaig.

Industry

The village is home to a large paper mill and pulp mill, established in the 1960s and long a major local employer, as well as a commercial port handling timber and other bulk cargo. Annat, adjacent to Corpach, hosts industrial estates serving the Lochaber area.

History

During the Second World War, HMS St Christopher, a Royal Navy coastal forces training base, was located at Corpach. The Treasures of the Earth museum in the village displays crystals, gemstones and minerals.
