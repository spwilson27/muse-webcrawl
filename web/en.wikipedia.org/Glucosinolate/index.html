Glucosinolate

Glucosinolates are natural organic compounds that occur in many plants of the order Brassicales, especially the cabbage family (Brassicaceae), including cabbage, broccoli, kale, mustard, horseradish, and rapeseed. They are responsible for the pungent flavours of these plants and serve as a chemical defence against herbivores: when plant tissue is damaged, the enzyme myrosinase hydrolyses glucosinolates into biologically active products such as isothiocyanates — the sharp compounds in mustard and wasabi.

Chemistry

All glucosinolates share a common structure: a β-thioglucose group linked through a sulfonated oxime to a variable side chain derived from an amino acid. More than 130 different glucosinolates have been identified. Well-known examples include sinigrin (in mustard seed and horseradish), glucoraphanin (in broccoli, precursor of sulforaphane), and progoitrin, whose breakdown product goitrin can interfere with thyroid iodine uptake.

Health effects

Isothiocyanates such as sulforaphane have attracted research interest because they induce phase II detoxification enzymes and show anti-cancer activity in cell and animal studies; epidemiological studies link high cruciferous vegetable consumption with modestly reduced cancer risk. Conversely, very high glucosinolate content in animal feed (for example in rapeseed meal) can be goitrogenic, which led plant breeders to develop low-glucosinolate "double-zero" canola varieties.
