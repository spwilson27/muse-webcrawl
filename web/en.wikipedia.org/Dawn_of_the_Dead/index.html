Dawn of the Dead

Dawn of the Dead is a 1978 zombie horror film written, directed, and edited by George A. Romero, and produced in collaboration with Italian filmmaker Dario Argento. It is the second entry in Romero's Night of the Living Dead series and follows four survivors of a zombie apocalypse who barricade themselves inside a suburban shopping mall.

Plot

As society collapses under a plague of reanimated corpses, television producer Francine, her boyfriend Stephen, and SWAT officers Roger and Peter flee Philadelphia by helicopter. They take refuge in a shopping mall, clear it of zombies, and enjoy its stores—until a biker gang raid and the undead overwhelm them, leaving Francine and Peter to escape with little fuel remaining.

Production

The film was shot largely at the Monroeville Mall in Pennsylvania during overnight hours in the winter of 1977–78, on a budget of about $640,000. Tom Savini provided the groundbreaking gore makeup effects and also acted in the film. Goblin composed much of the score for the international cut.

Themes and legacy

Dawn of the Dead is widely read as a satire of consumerism, with its zombies shuffling through the mall as parodies of shoppers. It grossed roughly $66 million worldwide and is considered one of the greatest horror films ever made. A remake directed by Zack Snyder was released in 2004, and Romero followed it with Day of the Dead (1985).
