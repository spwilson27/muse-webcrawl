Memorial University of Newfoundland

Memorial University of Newfoundland, also known as Memorial University or MUN, is a public research university based in St. John's, Newfoundland and Labrador, Canada. Founded in 1925 as Memorial University College, it was established as a living memorial to the Newfoundlanders and Labradorians who died in the First World War, and later also honours those who fell in the Second World War.

History and campuses

The college granted its first degrees in association with British universities and became a full university in 1949, the year Newfoundland joined Canada. Its main St. John's campus opened in 1961. Today Memorial has more than 18,000 students and several campuses: the St. John's campus, Grenfell Campus in Corner Brook, the Marine Institute, the Labrador Campus in Happy Valley-Goose Bay, and Harlow Campus in England — one of only two Canadian universities with a campus in the United Kingdom.

Academics

Memorial offers programs in humanities and social sciences, science, engineering, business, medicine, nursing, pharmacy, education, music and fine arts. It is particularly known for ocean and maritime studies, cold-ocean engineering and research related to the North Atlantic, and it operates the Ocean Sciences Centre and major research vessels.
