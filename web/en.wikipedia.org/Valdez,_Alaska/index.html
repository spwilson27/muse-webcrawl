Valdez (Alutiiq: Suacit) is a city in the Chugach Census Area in the U.S. state of Alaska. Located on an ice-free port on the northeastern shore of Prince William Sound, it is the southern terminus of the Trans-Alaska Pipeline System and one of the most important oil ports in the United States. The city is also known as the site badly damaged by the 1964 Alaska earthquake and as the port after which the tanker Exxon Valdez, responsible for the 1989 oil spill in Prince William Sound, was named.

History

The port was named in 1790 by the Spanish explorer Salvador Fidalgo for naval officer Antonio Valdés y Fernández Bazán. A settlement grew after 1898, when Valdez became a supply port for prospectors on the Valdez Trail to the Klondike and Interior gold fields, promoted (with exaggerated claims) as an "all-American route." The town prospered as a road and supply centre; the Richardson Highway, Alaska's first road, connected it to Fairbanks. The original townsite was destroyed by the Good Friday earthquake of 27 March 1964, whose tsunami and soil liquefaction killed 32 people in Valdez; the city was rebuilt on stable bedrock about 4 miles (6.4 km) away at the present site.

Oil terminal

In the 1970s Valdez was chosen as the marine terminus of the Trans-Alaska Pipeline from Prudhoe Bay. The Valdez Marine Terminal, across the port on the southern shore, stores and loads crude oil into tankers. On 24 March 1989 the tanker Exxon Valdez grounded on Bligh Reef after departing the terminal, spilling roughly 11 million gallons of crude oil into Prince William Sound — the largest oil spill in U.S. waters until the Deepwater Horizon disaster of 2010. The spill transformed tanker regulation, leading to the Oil Pollution Act of 1990 and double-hull requirements.

Geography and climate

Surrounded by the Chugach Mountains, Valdez receives enormous snowfall — an average of around 300 inches (760 cm) a year, among the highest of any U.S. city — and is a centre for heli-skiing in the nearby Thompson Pass. Its oceanic climate is wet and mild by Alaskan standards. The economy combines oil, commercial fishing (notably pink salmon), tourism (including tours to the Columbia Glacier) and government services. Valdez is connected by the Richardson Highway to the rest of Alaska and by state ferry to Whittier and Cordova; nearby waterfalls line the highway through Keystone Canyon.
