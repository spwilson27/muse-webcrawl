Foedus Cassianum

Treaties of early Rome were inscribed on bronze and publicly displayed.

The Foedus Cassianum (in English: Treaty of Cassius) was a treaty which formed an alliance between the Roman Republic and the Latin League in 493 BC after the Battle of Lake Regillus. It ended the war between the Latin League and Rome, placing both on an equal footing, and provided for mutual defence, joint military commands, and an equal division of the spoils of war.

Background

After the expulsion of Rome's last king, the Latin cities of Latium contested Roman leadership of the region. The struggle culminated in the Roman victory at Lake Regillus (traditionally dated 496 BC), celebrated in legend by the miraculous intervention of Castor and Pollux. Rather than attempting to crush the Latins, the Roman consul Spurius Cassius Vecellinus negotiated a settlement in 493 BC that made them partners.

Terms

According to Dionysius of Halicarnassus, the treaty provided that there should be peace between Romans and Latins "as long as heaven and earth endure"; that neither party should assist the other's enemies or allow enemies passage; that they should aid one another with all their strength when attacked; that spoils taken in joint wars should be divided equally; that private disputes between citizens of the two parties should be settled within ten days in the city where the contract was made; and that nothing could be added to or removed from the treaty without the consent of both Romans and Latins.

Significance

The treaty was inscribed on a bronze column that still stood in the Roman Forum in Cicero's day. It gave Rome a secure base and a reservoir of allied manpower that underpinned its conquest of Italy: the joint Roman–Latin armies campaigned together against the Aequi, Volsci and Hernici for more than a century. The alliance lasted until the Latin War of 341–338 BC, after which Rome dissolved the Latin League and bound the Latin cities to itself individually — the seed of the later system of Roman alliances (socii) across Italy.
