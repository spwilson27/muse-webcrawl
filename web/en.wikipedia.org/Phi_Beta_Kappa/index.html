Phi Beta Kappa

The Phi Beta Kappa Society (ΦΒΚ) is the oldest academic honor society in the United States and the most prestigious, due to its long history and academic selectivity. Founded on December 5, 1776, at the College of William & Mary in Virginia, it is widely considered the nation's most prestigious honor society.

History

Phi Beta Kappa was founded by five students at William & Mary — John Heath, who served as its first president, Thomas Smith, Richard Booker, Armistead Smith, and John Jones. It was the first collegiate society to bear a Greek-letter name and served as the model for the modern Greek system of fraternities and sororities. Originally a secret literary and debating society, it dropped its secrecy requirement in the 1830s and evolved into an honor society recognising excellence in the liberal arts and sciences.

Membership

Membership is by invitation only, typically extended to undergraduates in the top 10 percent of their class at institutions hosting a chapter. Membership is widely regarded as one of the highest honors for college graduates. Notable members have included U.S. presidents, Supreme Court justices, and Nobel laureates.

Symbols

The society's emblem is a gold key engraved with the Greek letters ΦΒΚ, a hand pointing to seven stars (representing the founding members and the aim of scholarship), with the date of founding on the reverse. The society's colors are pink and sky blue.
