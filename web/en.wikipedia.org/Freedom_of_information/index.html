Freedom of information

Freedom of information is the right of access to information held by public bodies, sometimes described as a right to know. It is an extension of freedom of speech and is recognised in international law as a fundamental human right. More than 100 countries around the world have implemented some form of freedom of information legislation.

History

Sweden's Freedom of the Press Act of 1766, associated with Anders Chydenius, is generally regarded as the first freedom of information law, establishing public access to government documents in Sweden and Finland. In the United States, the Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) was enacted in 1966 and took effect in 1967, allowing for the full or partial disclosure of previously unreleased information controlled by the federal government.

Principles

Freedom of information laws typically establish a presumption that records held by public authorities are public, with limited exemptions for matters such as national security, personal privacy, and ongoing law enforcement. Requesters usually need not give a reason for their request, and refusals can be appealed to an information commissioner or the courts.

International recognition

The United Nations recognised freedom of information as fundamental in a 1946 resolution describing freedom of information as "the touchstone of all the freedoms". UNESCO marks 28 September as the International Day for Universal Access to Information.
