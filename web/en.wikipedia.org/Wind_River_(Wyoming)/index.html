The Wind River is a river in west-central Wyoming, United States, about 185 miles (298 km) long. It is the upper course of the Bighorn River: the two names describe the same stream, the name changing at the "Wedding of the Waters" near Thermopolis, where the river enters Bighorn Canyon.

Course

The Wind River rises at Wind River Lake on Togwotee Pass in the Wind River Range and flows southeast through the Wind River Basin, past Dubois and through the Wind River Indian Reservation, home of the Eastern Shoshone and Northern Arapaho tribes. At Riverton it is joined by the Popo Agie River and turns north, flowing through Boysen Reservoir and the scenic Wind River Canyon before emerging at the Wedding of the Waters, from which point it is known as the Bighorn River.

History and use

The river's valley was a corridor for fur trappers and for emigrant trails in the 19th century. Today its waters support irrigation farming and ranching in the basin, and Boysen Dam provides hydroelectric power, flood control and recreation. Water rights on the river were the subject of major litigation under the Winters doctrine following the reservation's creation, notably in the long-running Big Horn adjudication.
