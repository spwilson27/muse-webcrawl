North Cape (Norway)

The North Cape (Norwegian: Nordkapplatået or Nordkapp) is a cape on the northern coast of the island of Magerøya in Northern Norway. The cape is in Nordkapp Municipality in Finnmark county. The European route E69 motorway (highway) has its northern terminus at the North Cape, which is a major tourist attraction. The cape includes a 307-metre-high (1,007 ft) cliff with a large flat plateau on top, where visitors can watch the midnight sun and views of the Barents Sea to the north.

Geography

The North Cape is often referred to as the northernmost point of Europe. In fact, the neighbouring Knivskjellodden point, just to the west, extends about 1,450 metres (4,760 ft) further north, and the northernmost point of mainland Europe is Cape Nordkinn (Kinnarodden). However, North Cape is the northernmost point reachable by road, connected to the international E-road network, and it is far more dramatic in appearance, which accounts for its fame.

History

The cape was named by the English explorer Richard Chancellor in 1553, when he passed it in search of the Northeast Passage. In 1943, the Battle of the North Cape was fought offshore between British and German naval forces, ending with the sinking of the German battleship Scharnhorst. Tourism began in the 19th century; visitors once arrived by boat and climbed the cliff by rope. The North Cape Hall, built into the plateau, opened in 1988 and contains exhibitions, a cinema, and "the northernmost post office".

Midnight sun and tourism

Because of its latitude (71°10′N), the sun does not set at the North Cape from about mid-May to the end of July, and does not rise for a corresponding period in winter. The site receives roughly 200,000 visitors a year. A steel globe sculpture on the plateau, erected in 1978, has become the symbol of the cape.
