Port of Antwerp

The Port of Antwerp is the port of the city of Antwerp, Belgium, located on the river Scheldt about 80 kilometres (50 mi) inland from the North Sea. It is Europe's second-largest seaport by cargo volume, after the Port of Rotterdam, and one of the largest ports in the world, handling over 230 million tonnes of international maritime cargo annually and more than 14 million TEU of containers. In 2022 the port merged with the Port of Zeebrugge to form the Port of Antwerp-Bruges.

History

Antwerp's rise as a trading centre dates to the 16th century, when it was briefly the richest trading city in Europe. Its modern port developed in the 19th and 20th centuries with the digging of large dock basins on both banks of the Scheldt and the construction of some of the world's largest locks, including the Berendrecht Lock and, in 2016, the Kieldrecht Lock, then the largest lock in the world.

Operations

The port is a leading hub for containers, breakbulk, liquid bulk, and one of the world's largest integrated chemical clusters, hosting major petrochemical and refining complexes. Its inland location places it close to the industrial heartlands of Belgium, the Netherlands, Germany, and northern France, and it is connected to the European hinterland by dense road, rail, and inland waterway networks. The port area covers more than 120 square kilometres and directly and indirectly employs well over 100,000 people.
