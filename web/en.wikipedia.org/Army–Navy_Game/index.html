Army–Navy Game

The Army–Navy Game is an annual American college football game between the Army Black Knights of the United States Military Academy at West Point and the Navy Midshipmen of the United States Naval Academy at Annapolis. First played in 1890, it is one of the most traditional and enduring rivalries in college football, and it traditionally closes the college football regular season.

Traditions

The game is marked by pageantry, including the formal "march-on" of the Corps of Cadets and the Brigade of Midshipmen before kickoff, flyovers by military aircraft, and the exchange of "prisoners" — students studying at the rival academy — at midfield. At the conclusion, both teams gather before each student section while the bands play the alma maters, with the winning team's school song sung second; the custom of singing second is a mark of mutual respect. The President of the United States has frequently attended, and the game is regularly played at neutral professional stadiums, most often in Philadelphia.

History

Navy won the first meeting in 1890, and the series has been played almost every year since 1899. The 1926 game, played at Soldier Field in Chicago, ended in a 21–21 tie that helped Army and Navy share honours in a celebrated season; the 1963 game was postponed following the assassination of President John F. Kennedy. The rivalry also forms one leg of the Commander-in-Chief's Trophy competition with the Air Force Academy.
