Wilkins Micawber

Wilkins Micawber is a fictional character in Charles Dickens's 1850 novel David Copperfield. He is a kind-hearted, improvident clerk perpetually in debt, yet eternally optimistic that "something will turn up." The character is generally considered to be based in part on Dickens's own father, John Dickens, who was imprisoned for debt in the Marshalsea prison.

Character

Micawber is David's landlord and friend during the boy's time in London. His financial philosophy is summed up in his famous formula: "Annual income twenty pounds, annual expenditure nineteen nineteen and six, result happiness. Annual income twenty pounds, annual expenditure twenty pounds ought and six, result misery." Despite repeated stays in debtors' prison, Micawber's grandiloquent cheerfulness never deserts him. Near the novel's end he dramatically exposes the fraud of Uriah Heep, and emigrates to Australia, where he finally prospers as a magistrate in the fictional settlement of Port Middlebay.

Legacy

The character gave English the term "Micawberism" for incurable optimism in the face of adversity, and the "Micawber principle" of living within one's means. He has been portrayed on screen by actors including W. C. Fields (1935), Ralph Richardson, Bob Hoskins, and Paul Whitehouse.
