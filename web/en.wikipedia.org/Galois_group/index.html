Galois group

In mathematics, in the area of abstract algebra known as Galois theory, a Galois group is the group of field automorphisms of a field extension that fix the base field. The concept was introduced by Évariste Galois in his study of polynomial equations, where it provided a criterion for when an equation can be solved by radicals.

Definition

If E/F is a field extension, the Galois group Gal(E/F) is the set of all automorphisms of E that fix every element of F, with composition as the group operation. When the extension is Galois — that is, normal and separable — the order of the group equals the degree of the extension, and the fundamental theorem of Galois theory gives a bijection between intermediate fields and subgroups of the Galois group.

Examples

The Galois group of a quadratic extension such as ℚ(√2)/ℚ is the cyclic group of order 2, generated by the automorphism sending √2 to −√2. The splitting field of a cubic polynomial typically has Galois group S3, while a general quintic has Galois group S5, which is not solvable — the group-theoretic reason no general radical formula exists for degree five.

Fundamental theorem

The fundamental theorem of Galois theory states that for a finite Galois extension E/F there is an inclusion-reversing correspondence between intermediate fields and subgroups of Gal(E/F); normal subgroups correspond to intermediate fields that are themselves Galois over F, with quotient groups as their Galois groups.

Applications

Galois groups underlie the classical results on solvability of polynomials, compass-and-straightedge constructions, and the insolvability of angle trisection. The absolute Galois group of the rational numbers is a central object of modern number theory and the Langlands program.
