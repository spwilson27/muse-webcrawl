Operation Entebbe

Operation Entebbe, also known as the Entebbe Raid or Operation Yonatan, was a counter-terrorist hostage-rescue mission carried out by the Israel Defense Forces at Entebbe Airport in Uganda on 4 July 1976.

A week earlier, Air France Flight 139 from Tel Aviv via Athens to Paris was hijacked by members of the Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine and the German Revolutionary Cells, and diverted to Entebbe with the complicity of Ugandan dictator Idi Amin. Hijackers released non-Jewish passengers and held 106 Israeli and Jewish hostages, threatening to kill them.

Israeli commandos from Sayeret Matkal flew some 4,000 km, landed at night, stormed the terminal in under an hour, killed all seven hijackers and rescued 102 hostages. Three hostages were killed, a fourth was later murdered in hospital, and the Israeli commander Yonatan Netanyahu — brother of Benjamin Netanyahu — was killed. Ugandan soldiers were killed and Ugandan fighter aircraft destroyed to prevent pursuit.
