Historic England is an executive non-departmental public body of the British Government, sponsored by the Department for Culture, Media and Sport. It is the government's statutory adviser on the historic environment of England — its listed buildings, scheduled monuments, registered parks, gardens and battlefields, conservation areas and historic places generally.

Origins

On 1 April 2015 English Heritage was split in two: a charity retaining the name English Heritage took over care of the National Heritage Collection of properties open to the public, while the statutory and advisory functions continued as the newly named Historic England. The arrangement aimed to let the property arm move toward self-financing while the regulator remained publicly funded.

Functions

Historic England maintains the National Heritage List for England, the official register of protected historic sites — some 400,000 entries. It advises ministers on listing and scheduling, gives expert advice to local planning authorities and owners, runs research and aerial photography programmes, keeps the Historic England Archive, and publishes the annual Heritage at Risk Register of sites threatened by neglect or decay.

Work

Its activities include grant aid for repairs, listing assessments prompted by public nomination, heritage schools programmes, and major research projects such as the study of England's historic landscapes and seaside towns. Counterparts elsewhere in the UK are Historic Environment Scotland, Cadw in Wales and the Historic Environment Division in Northern Ireland.
