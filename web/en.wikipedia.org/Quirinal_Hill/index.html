Quirinal Hill

LocationRome, Italy
Elevation~61 m (highest of Rome's hills)
Notable siteQuirinal Palace

The Quirinal Hill (Latin: Collis Quirinalis; Italian: Quirinale) is one of the Seven Hills of Rome, at the north-east of the city centre. It is the highest of the seven hills and is home to the Quirinal Palace, the official residence of the President of the Italian Republic. The name derives from the god Quirinus, who had a temple on the hill, or possibly from the Sabine town of Cures whose settlers lived there.

History

The hill was settled early, with evidence of an Iron Age village from the 8th century BC. In ancient Rome it was a residential district of temples and aristocratic houses; the Baths of Constantine and the Temple of Quirinus stood here. During the Renaissance and Baroque eras, popes built summer residences on the hill, which was considered healthier than the Vatican lowlands. The Quirinal Palace, begun in 1583 under Pope Gregory XIII, became a papal palace, then the residence of the Kings of Italy after 1870, and finally the presidential palace in 1946.

Landmarks

  • Quirinal Palace — official residence of the President of Italy, one of the largest palaces in the world.
  • Scuderie del Quirinale — former palace stables, now an exhibition space.
  • Piazza del Quirinale — with the Fountain of the Dioscuri, dominated by the colossal statues of Castor and Pollux.
  • Sant'Andrea al Quirinale — Bernini's celebrated Baroque church.
  • Palazzo Barberini — housing part of the Galleria Nazionale d'Arte Antica.
