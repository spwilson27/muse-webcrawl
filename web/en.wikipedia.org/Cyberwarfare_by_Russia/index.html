Cyberwarfare by Russia

Cyberwarfare by Russia comprises computer network operations attributed by Western governments and security researchers to the Russian state, including units of the military intelligence agency GRU, the foreign intelligence service SVR, and the Federal Security Service (FSB), as well as affiliated criminal and hacktivist groups. Russia has consistently denied responsibility for such operations.

Attributed operations

Frequently cited incidents include the 2007 distributed denial-of-service attacks on Estonia, cyber operations accompanying the 2008 Russo-Georgian War, intrusions into Ukrainian power grids in 2015 and 2016, the global NotPetya outbreak of 2017, the hack of the Democratic National Committee and interference in the 2016 United States elections, the SolarWinds supply-chain compromise discovered in 2020 (attributed to the SVR), and destructive wiper attacks against Ukrainian targets during the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine.

Groups

Named threat groups attributed to Russia include Sandworm (GRU Unit 74455), Fancy Bear / APT28 (GRU Unit 26165), Cozy Bear / APT29 (SVR) and Turla (FSB). Several GRU officers have been indicted in the United States and the Netherlands in connection with these campaigns.

Doctrine

Russian military writing treats cyberspace within the broader concept of "information confrontation", encompassing technical attacks, espionage and psychological influence operations. Western analysts contrast this with narrower Western definitions of cyber warfare.
