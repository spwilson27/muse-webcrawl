The Sámi Parliament of Norway (Northern Sámi: Sámediggi) is the representative body for people of Sámi heritage in Norway. It was opened in 1989 and is seated in Kárášjohka in Finnmark county.

History

The parliament followed the Alta controversy of the late 1970s and the subsequent Sámi Rights Commission. The Sámi Act of 1987 provided its legal basis, and the first election was held in 1989.

Structure

Its 39 members are elected every four years by Sámi registered on the Sámi electoral roll, across seven constituencies. It administers cultural, linguistic, and educational matters and is consulted on issues affecting Sámi land and resources.

Building

The parliament building, opened in 2000, is noted for its distinctive architecture in the shape of a traditional lávvu tent.
