Florida Senate

The Florida Senate is the upper house of the Florida Legislature, the state legislature of the U.S. state of Florida. It is composed of 40 members, each elected from a single-member district with a population of approximately 510,000 residents. Senators serve four-year terms and are limited to two terms (eight years) under the state's term-limits law.

History

Florida's first legislative body met in 1822 as the Legislative Council of the Territory of Florida. Upon statehood in 1845, the bicameral Florida Legislature was established, consisting of the Senate and the House of Representatives. The current chamber operates under the Florida Constitution of 1968, which established the modern framework of state government.

Composition

Senators are elected in partisan elections. Following the 2022 redistricting cycle, districts are drawn to be roughly equal in population. The Senate is presided over by the President of the Senate, chosen by the majority party caucus, and the President pro tempore. The presiding officer appoints committee chairs and members and refers bills to committees.

Powers and process

The Senate shares legislative power with the House and the Governor. It has the sole power to confirm many gubernatorial appointments and to try impeachments brought by the House. Sessions begin in early spring each year and last 60 days, with the passage of a general appropriations bill being the legislature's only constitutionally required duty.
