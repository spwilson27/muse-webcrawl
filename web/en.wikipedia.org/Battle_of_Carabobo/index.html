Battle of Carabobo

Battle of Carabobo
Date: 24 June 1821
Location: Carabobo, Venezuela
Result: Decisive patriot victory
Part of the Venezuelan War of Independence

The Battle of Carabobo, fought on 24 June 1821, was the decisive engagement of the Venezuelan War of Independence. The patriot army commanded by Simón Bolívar defeated the Spanish royalist forces of Field Marshal Miguel de la Torre on the plain of Carabobo southwest of Valencia, securing the independence of Venezuela.

The battle

Bolívar's army of roughly 6,500 men, including the British Legions and the llanero cavalry of José Antonio Páez, faced about 5,000 royalists drawn up in a strong position. Bolívar led the bulk of his force on a turning movement through rough ground onto the royalist right flank, while Páez's horsemen pressed the attack. The British Legion held its ground under heavy fire during a critical phase, allowing the patriot divisions of Ambrosio Plaza and Manuel Cedeño — both of whom were killed — to press home the assault. The royalist army collapsed; only a remnant under the Burgos battalion escaped into Puerto Cabello.

Aftermath

Caracas was liberated days later. Páez was proclaimed commander-in-chief on the field, and the victory is commemorated by the monumental Campo Carabobo and the Arch of Triumph of Carabobo in Venezuela. Spain retained only Puerto Cabello, which fell in 1823 after the naval Battle of Lake Maracaibo.
