Great Hall of the Cooper Union

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Great Hall of the Cooper Union is a historic lecture hall and auditorium in the basement of the Foundation Building of Cooper Union, at Cooper Square in the East Village of Manhattan, New York City. Completed in 1858–59 when the college founded by industrialist Peter Cooper opened, it seats about 900 people and is one of the most famous speaking venues in American history.

Famous addresses

On 27 February 1860 the hall hosted Abraham Lincoln's Cooper Union Address, the speech against the spread of slavery — ending "Let us to the end dare to do our duty as we understand it" — that made the Illinois lawyer a credible presidential candidate. Presidents including Ulysses S. Grant, William Howard Taft, Theodore Roosevelt, Woodrow Wilson, and Bill Clinton also spoke there, as did Frederick Douglass, Susan B. Anthony, Mark Twain (who introduced himself there in a celebrated 1867 lecture), and Victoria Woodhull, who in 1871 became the first woman to address a political gathering of that prominence on women's rights.

Role in public life

True to Peter Cooper's vision of free education and civic debate, the Great Hall hosted meetings that shaped the American labour movement, the founding of organisations such as the NAACP (whose early mass meetings were held there) and the American Red Cross ( Clara Barton addressed a relief meeting there in the 1880s), and generations of free public lectures. In 2009 President Barack Obama delivered a major speech on financial regulatory reform in the hall, marking its 150th anniversary year.
