Economic history

Economic history is the study of history using methodological tools from economics, or with a special attention to economic phenomena. Research is conducted using a combination of historical methods, statistical methods and the application of economic theory to historical situations and institutions. The field can encompass a wide variety of topics, including equality, finance, technology, labour, and business.

Scope

Economic historians study, among other subjects, the origins of money and markets, the economic growth of the ancient world, feudalism and the transition to capitalism, the Industrial Revolution, the spread of industrialization, the Great Depression, postwar growth, and the economic rise of East Asia. Cliometrics — the "new economic history" that emerged in the 1960s — applied quantitative methods and counterfactual analysis, exemplified by Robert Fogel's work on American railroads and slavery.

Methods and debates

The discipline sits at the intersection of history and economics. Debates have included the standard of living during industrialization, the economic causes and consequences of empire and slavery, the origins of the Great Divergence between Europe and Asia, and the role of institutions — emphasized by scholars such as Douglass North and Daron Acemoglu — in long-run development.
