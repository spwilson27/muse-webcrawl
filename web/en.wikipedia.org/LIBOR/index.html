LIBOR

LIBOR (London Interbank Offered Rate) was a benchmark interest rate calculated from estimates submitted by major banks of the rates at which they could borrow from other banks in the London interbank market. Published for several currencies and maturities, it underpinned an estimated hundreds of trillions of dollars of loans, mortgages and derivatives.

Scandal and reform

Investigations beginning in 2012 revealed that banks had manipulated submissions for profit and to appear creditworthy during the 2008 financial crisis, leading to billions of dollars in fines, criminal convictions and tightened administration — passing from the British Bankers' Association to ICE Benchmark Administration in 2014.

Discontinuation

Regulators required LIBOR's replacement by "risk-free rates" such as SOFR (US dollar), SONIA (sterling) and €STR (euro). Most LIBOR settings ceased at the end of 2021, and the remaining US dollar panels ended in June 2023, with synthetic rates published temporarily for legacy contracts.
