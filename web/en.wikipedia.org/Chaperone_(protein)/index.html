Chaperone (protein)

In molecular biology, molecular chaperones are proteins that assist the conformational folding, unfolding, assembly and disassembly of other macromolecular structures, without becoming part of the final structure themselves. They help newly synthesised proteins achieve their correct three-dimensional shape and prevent misfolded proteins from aggregating.

Function

Many chaperones are heat shock proteins, induced when cells are exposed to elevated temperatures or other stresses that threaten to denature proteins. Major families include Hsp60 (chaperonins such as GroEL/GroES in bacteria), Hsp70, Hsp90 and the small heat shock proteins. Chaperonins form barrel-shaped complexes that enclose a single unfolded protein, giving it an isolated environment in which to fold.

Medical relevance

Failure of protein quality control is implicated in diseases of protein misfolding, including Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease and cystic fibrosis. Pharmacological chaperones — small molecules that stabilise a specific mutant protein — are used therapeutically in some conditions, such as migalastat for Fabry disease.
