Atlantic Ocean Road


Storseisundet Bridge, the road's longest bridge

The Atlantic Ocean Road (Norwegian: Atlanterhavsveien) is an 8.3-kilometre (5.2 mi) section of Norwegian County Road 64 that runs across an archipelago of small islands and skerries between the island of Averøy and the mainland in Møre og Romsdal county, Norway. Connected by eight bridges, the road zigzags across the exposed edge of the Norwegian Sea and is one of Norway's most celebrated scenic routes.

Construction

Construction began in 1983 and was plagued by severe weather — workers endured a dozen hurricanes during the six years of building. The road opened on 7 July 1989. It was originally a toll road, with the tolls planned to pay off the investment over 15 years; strong traffic meant the debt was cleared by 1999 and the toll removed. The longest and most photographed of its bridges is the Storseisundet Bridge, whose dramatic curve creates an optical illusion that the road ends in mid-air when approached from certain angles.

Tourism

Designated one of Norway's National Scenic Routes, the road draws visitors who come for its combination of engineering and wild coastal scenery, particularly during autumn storms when waves break over the roadway. Rest areas and viewing platforms — including a raised walkway at Eldhusøya — line the route, and the surrounding waters are popular for sea fishing and boat trips to the fishing village of Håholmen. The road has featured in numerous car advertisements and films.
