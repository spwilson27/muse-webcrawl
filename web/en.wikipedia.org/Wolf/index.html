Wolf

The wolf (Canis lupus) is a large canine native to Eurasia and North America. It is the largest living member of the family Canidae and is distinguished from other Canis species by its less pointed ears and muzzle, shorter torso and longer tail. Wolves are highly social animals that live and hunt in family groups called packs, and they are the ancestors of the domestic dog.

Description

Adult wolves typically measure 105–160 cm (41–63 in) in length and weigh 30–50 kg (66–110 lb), with considerable variation among populations; northern wolves tend to be larger. The coat is generally grizzled grey, though it can range from nearly pure white to black, brown or reddish. Wolves possess powerful jaws, keen senses of smell and hearing, and great endurance, enabling them to travel long distances at a trotting pace of about 8–9 km/h and to sprint considerably faster.

Behaviour and ecology

Wolves live in packs, usually a breeding pair and their offspring of several years. The pack defends a territory, marking it with scent and howling. Wolves are apex predators whose diet consists mainly of ungulates such as deer, elk, moose and bison, supplemented by beaver, hare and other smaller prey. Cooperative hunting and food sharing are central to pack life. Pups are born in dens in spring after a gestation of about 63 days.

Distribution

Historically the wolf had the largest range of any terrestrial mammal, but persecution reduced it drastically. Wolves persist across much of Russia, Canada, Alaska and parts of the contiguous United States, Europe and Asia. Reintroduction to Yellowstone National Park in 1995 is a celebrated example of trophic cascade ecology, and recovering populations in western Europe have returned wolves to countries such as Germany, France and the Netherlands.

Relationship with humans

Wolves occupy a prominent place in mythology, folklore and literature, from Romulus and Remus to the Norse Fenrir. Domestication of wolves tens of thousands of years ago gave rise to the dog. Conflicts over livestock depredation have driven extermination campaigns, but attitudes have shifted toward coexistence and conservation in many regions.
