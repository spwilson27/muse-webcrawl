Béla Bartók

Béla Viktor János Bartók (25 March 1881 – 26 September 1945) was a Hungarian composer, pianist and ethnomusicologist. He is considered one of the most important composers of the 20th century and, with Franz Liszt, is regarded as Hungary's greatest composer.

Life and career

Through his collection and analytical study of folk music, Bartók was one of the founders of comparative musicology, which later became ethnomusicology. With Zoltán Kodály he travelled the Hungarian countryside recording peasant songs on phonograph cylinders.

Music

His works include the opera Duke Bluebeard's Castle, the ballet The Miraculous Mandarin, six string quartets, the Music for Strings, Percussion and Celesta, the Concerto for Orchestra, and the pedagogical piano collection Mikrokosmos. He emigrated to the United States in 1940 to escape the Second World War and died of leukemia in New York City.
