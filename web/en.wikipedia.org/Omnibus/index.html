Omnibus

Latinomnibus — "for all"
SensesBus; collected edition; multi-part legislation

Omnibus, from the Latin for "for all", is a word with several related senses revolving around the idea of a single vehicle or volume carrying many things.

Transport

Historically, an omnibus was a large horse-drawn public conveyance carrying passengers along a fixed route — introduced in cities such as Nantes, Paris (1828), and London (1829) — and the shortened form "bus" comes from it. The word survives in legal and formal usage for motor buses and in the names of some transport undertakings.

Publishing and media

An omnibus edition is a single volume collecting several works by one author or on one theme, such as a novelist's collected trilogy. In broadcasting, an omnibus is a compilation repeat of several episodes of a serial shown together, a usage common in British television scheduling.

Law and legislation

An omnibus bill combines many diverse measures into one legislative package voted on as a whole; see omnibus spending bill. An omnibus clause in a contract or insurance policy covers a broad category of cases not individually enumerated.
