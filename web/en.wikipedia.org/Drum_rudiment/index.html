Drum rudiment

In percussion music, a rudiment is one of a number of relatively small patterns which form the foundation for more extended and complex drumming patterns. The term "rudiment" is most closely associated with various forms of field drumming, where the snare drum plays a prominent role; the technique is also used on the drum kit and other percussion instruments.

The standard rudiments

From the hundreds of possible patterns, a standard list crystallised in the United States. In 1933 the National Association of Rudimental Drummers (NARD) published the "Standard 26 American Drum Rudiments", including the single stroke roll, double stroke roll, paradiddle, flam, drag, ratamacue and the flamacue. In 1984 the Percussive Arts Society (PAS) expanded the list to the 40 International Drum Rudiments, organised into four families: roll rudiments, diddle rudiments, flam rudiments and drag rudiments.

Examples

  • Single paradiddle: RLRR LRLL — an alternation of single and double strokes.
  • Flam: a grace note struck just before a main note, giving a fuller sound.
  • Drag (ruff): two grace notes before a main stroke.
  • Single stroke roll: continuous alternating single strokes.
  • Double stroke roll: each hand strikes twice in succession.

Rudiments descend from military drumming traditions: Swiss Basel drumming, French and British regimental calls, and the tabor accompaniments of early modern Europe.
