Art forgery

Art forgery is the creation and sale, usually for financial gain, of works of art that are falsely credited to other, usually more famous artists. It can involve copying an existing work, creating a new work in an artist's style, or falsifying the provenance of a genuine work.

Notable forgers

Famous cases include Han van Meegeren, whose fake Vermeers deceived Dutch experts and were sold during the Second World War; Elmyr de Hory, whose imitations of modern masters circulated widely; and Wolfgang Beltracchi, whose forged works in the style of early modernists earned millions before his conviction in 2011. The Knoedler Gallery scandal in New York, involving fake Abstract Expressionist paintings, closed one of America's oldest galleries in 2011.

Detection

Forgeries are detected through provenance research, connoisseurship, and scientific methods such as pigment analysis, X-radiography, dendrochronology of panels, and carbon dating. Provenance gaps — especially for works claimed to have surfaced from private collections — are a common warning sign.
