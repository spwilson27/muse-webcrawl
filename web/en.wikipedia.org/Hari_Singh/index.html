Hari Singh

Born23 September 1895, Jammu, British India
Died26 April 1961, Bombay, India
Reign23 September 1925 – 17 November 1952 (Maharaja of Jammu and Kashmir)
DynastyDogra
SuccessorKaran Singh (as Sadr-i-Riyasat)

Maharaja Sir Hari Singh (23 September 1895 – 26 April 1961) was the last ruling Maharaja of the princely state of Jammu and Kashmir in British India. A member of the Hindu Dogra dynasty, he succeeded his uncle Pratap Singh in 1925 and reigned over a large, religiously diverse state whose population was majority Muslim.

Reign

Hari Singh introduced reforms including compulsory primary education, laws against child marriage, and measures opening temples and public institutions to lower castes; he also suppressed political agitation, most notably during the 1931 uprising in Kashmir and the subsequent Glancy Commission reforms. He represented Indian princes at the Round Table Conferences in London and was a prominent figure in the Chamber of Princes.

Accession of Kashmir

At the Partition of India in 1947, Hari Singh sought to keep his state independent, signing a standstill agreement with Pakistan while deferring a decision on India. In October 1947, an invasion by tribal militias from Pakistan drove toward Srinagar; the Maharaja fled the capital and, on 26 October 1947, signed the Instrument of Accession to India in return for Indian military assistance — an accession whose legitimacy Pakistan has disputed ever since and which remains at the heart of the Kashmir conflict. In 1949 he appointed his son Karan Singh as regent, formally abdicated in 1952 when the monarchy was abolished, and spent his remaining years in Bombay.
