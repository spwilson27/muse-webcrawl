Eastern Armenian
Native to: Armenia, Artsakh, Georgia, Iran, Russia
Language family: Indo-European → Armenian
Writing system: Armenian alphabet
Official status: Armenia

Eastern Armenian is one of the two standardised forms of modern Armenian, the other being Western Armenian. It is the official language of the Republic of Armenia and is spoken in Armenia, Georgia, Iran, Russia and the wider Armenian diaspora originating from those regions.

History

Eastern Armenian developed in the eastern part of the Armenian highlands and was codified in the 19th century, notably through the writings of Khachatur Abovian, whose novel Wounds of Armenia (1841) is considered the first work of modern Eastern Armenian literature.

Orthography

In the 1920s, the Soviet Armenian government introduced a reformed spelling orthography, which is used in Armenia today. Armenian communities in Iran continue to use the traditional (classical) orthography. Western Armenian also retains the classical spelling.

Features

Eastern Armenian preserves a three-way distinction in stop consonants (voiced, voiceless unaspirated and voiceless aspirated), whereas Western Armenian has largely merged the voiced and unaspirated series. The two standards also differ in vocabulary, with Eastern Armenian containing more loanwords from Russian and Persian.
