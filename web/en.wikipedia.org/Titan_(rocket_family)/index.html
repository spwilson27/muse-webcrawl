Titan was a family of United States expendable rockets developed by the Martin Company for the U.S. Air Force, used between 1959 and 2005. The family began as the Titan I and Titan II intercontinental ballistic missiles, burning storable hypergolic propellants, and evolved into a series of space launchers that lofted military payloads, NASA planetary probes and the crewed Gemini spacecraft.

Development

Titan I was conceived in 1955 as a backup to the Atlas ICBM; the improved Titan II, with larger stages, storable propellants launchable from silos, and a much bigger warhead, became the largest ICBM ever deployed by the United States, remaining on alert from 1963 to 1987. Titan II's reliability and simplicity made it the booster for NASA's Gemini programme (1964–66), carrying ten crewed missions.

Space launcher variants

The Titan III family (1964–82) added strap-on solid rocket boosters and upper stages such as Transtage and Centaur, launching Vela nuclear-detection satellites and the twin Viking Mars landers and Voyager probes (on Titan IIIE-Centaur). The Titan IV (1989–2005), the largest of the family, launched the Cassini–Huygens mission to Saturn and heavy reconnaissance satellites; it was retired in favour of the Atlas V and Delta IV, with the final Titan flight on 19 October 2005.

Accidents

Storable-propellant Titan II missiles suffered two serious silo accidents: a 1978 fuel leak at Damascus, Arkansas that killed one airman, and a 1980 explosion at the same complex that blew off the silo door and ejected the warhead (which did not detonate).
