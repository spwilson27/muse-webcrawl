The Lamborghini Countach is a rear mid-engine, rear-wheel-drive sports car produced by the Italian automobile manufacturer Lamborghini from 1974 to 1990. It is one of the many exotic designs developed by Italian design house Bertone, which pioneered and popularised the sharply angled "Italian Wedge" shape. The Countach also popularised the scissor door, which became a Lamborghini signature.

History

Shown as the LP500 prototype at the 1971 Geneva Motor Show, the Countach stunned visitors with its futuristic wedge silhouette. The name is an exclamation of astonishment in the Piedmontese dialect. Production LP400 models, with a 3.9-litre V12, began in 1974. Successive versions included the LP400 S, LP500 S, the 5000 Quattrovalvole (with a 5.2-litre four-valve V12), and the 25th Anniversary edition of 1988, restyled by Horacio Pagani. In total, 1,983 cars were built.

Design and engineering

Designer Marcello Gandini adapted the cab-forward wedge of his Lancia Stratos Zero concept. The engine was mounted longitudinally behind the cabin, with the gearbox in front of it ("in uscita") to improve weight distribution and shorten the driveline. The aluminium bodywork covered a spaceframe chassis; later cars gained wide wheel-arch extensions, large rear wings, and prominent air intakes. Rear visibility was notoriously poor, and drivers commonly reversed while sitting on the sill with the door open.

Legacy

The Countach became the defining poster car of its era and a cultural icon of 1980s excess. In 2021 Lamborghini unveiled the Countach LPI 800-4, a limited hybrid tribute based on the Sián FKP 37. Original examples are among the most collectible Lamborghinis, with values rising sharply in the 2010s and 2020s.
