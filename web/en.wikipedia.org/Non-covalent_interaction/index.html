Non-covalent interaction

A non-covalent interaction is a type of chemical bond that, unlike a covalent bond, does not involve the sharing of electron pairs between atoms. Non-covalent interactions are typically much weaker than covalent bonds but are collectively crucial in supramolecular chemistry, protein folding, drug binding and the structure of DNA.

Types

The main classes are electrostatic interactions (ion–ion, ion–dipole and dipole–dipole forces, including hydrogen bonds and halogen bonds), van der Waals forces (dispersion and repulsion), π-effects such as π–π stacking, and the hydrophobic effect. Hydrogen bonds, though individually weak (typically 5–30 kJ/mol), direct the folding of proteins and the pairing of DNA bases; dispersion forces stabilise large molecular assemblies.
