The Palace of the Governors (Spanish: Palacio de los Gobernadores) is an adobe structure on Palace Avenue on the north side of the Santa Fe Plaza in Santa Fe, New Mexico. Built beginning in 1610, it served as the seat of government for centuries — under Spain, Mexico and the United States — and is widely regarded as the oldest continuously used public building in the continental United States.

History

Construction began in 1610 under Pedro de Peralta, governor of the Spanish province of Santa Fe de Nuevo México. During the Pueblo Revolt of 1680 the building was occupied by Pueblo peoples, who held Santa Fe until the Spanish reconquest under Diego de Vargas in 1692–93. It later housed Mexican governors after 1821 and American territorial governors after 1846; the writer Lew Wallace, territorial governor in the late 1870s, is said to have finished his novel Ben-Hur there. In 1909 the building became the home of the Museum of New Mexico.

The museum and artisans

Today the Palace forms part of the New Mexico History Museum complex. Under its long front portal (portal), Native American artisans sell jewellery, pottery and other crafts through the Native American Vendors Program — a daily market that has become one of Santa Fe's best-known sights and an important source of income for Pueblo and tribal artists.
