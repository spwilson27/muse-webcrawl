The Herschel Space Observatory was a space observatory built and operated by the European Space Agency, active from 2009 to 2013. It was the largest infrared telescope ever launched at the time, with a 3.5-metre primary mirror made of silicon carbide, and it observed in the far-infrared and submillimetre wavebands (55–672 µm) that are blocked by Earth's atmosphere. It is named after William Herschel, who discovered infrared radiation in 1800.

Mission

Herschel was launched on 14 May 2009 aboard an Ariane 5 from Kourou, together with the Planck spacecraft, and operated from a halo orbit around the Sun–Earth L2 point. Its instruments — the heterodyne spectrometer HIFI and the PACS and SPIRE cameras — were cooled to below 2 K by more than 2,300 litres of superfluid helium. The mission ended on 29 April 2013 when the helium was exhausted; the spacecraft was passivated and placed in a heliocentric disposal orbit.

Discoveries

Herschel made key observations of star formation, detecting water vapour throughout the universe, including in planet-forming discs; it traced the origin of water in comet Hartley 2 to an Earth-like isotopic composition, mapped cold dust in galaxies, and conducted large surveys of the Galactic plane that catalogued stellar nurseries across the Milky Way.
