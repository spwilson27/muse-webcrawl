United States Central Command

The United States Central Command (USCENTCOM or CENTCOM) is one of the eleven unified combatant commands of the U.S. Department of Defense. It was established in 1983, taking over the previous responsibilities of the Rapid Deployment Joint Task Force (RDJTF).

CENTCOM's area of responsibility covers the Middle East, parts of Central Asia and South Asia, and surrounding waters — a region of more than 4 million square miles stretching from Egypt in the west to Kazakhstan in the northeast. Its headquarters is located at MacDill Air Force Base in Tampa, Florida, with a forward headquarters at Al Udeid Air Base in Qatar.

History

The command was formed in response to concerns heightened by the Iranian Revolution and the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan in 1979. CENTCOM directed major operations including the Gulf War of 1991, the Iraq War beginning in 2003, and the War in Afghanistan, as well as ongoing operations against terrorist groups in the region. In 2021, Israel was transferred into CENTCOM's area of responsibility from European Command.

Component commands

  • U.S. Army Central (ARCENT)
  • U.S. Naval Forces Central Command (NAVCENT)
  • U.S. Air Forces Central (AFCENT)
  • U.S. Marine Corps Forces Central Command (MARCENT)
  • U.S. Special Operations Command Central (SOCCENT)
