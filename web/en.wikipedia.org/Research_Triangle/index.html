The Research Triangle, or simply the Triangle, is a region in the Piedmont of North Carolina in the United States, anchored by the cities of Raleigh and Durham and the town of Chapel Hill, home to three major research universities: North Carolina State University, Duke University, and the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill.

Research Triangle Park

The name originated in the 1950s with the creation of Research Triangle Park (RTP), a 7,000-acre research park situated between the three universities. Founded in 1959, RTP is one of the largest research parks in the world and is home to hundreds of companies, including science and technology firms, government agencies, academic institutions, and nonprofits, employing tens of thousands of workers.

Economy

The region is known for its concentration of high-technology, biotechnology, pharmaceutical, and academic employers. Major employers include IBM, Cisco, GlaxoSmithKline, and the universities themselves, along with the United States Environmental Protection Agency, which operates a large campus in the park.

Metropolitan areas

The Triangle encompasses two metropolitan statistical areas: the Raleigh-Cary metropolitan area and the Durham-Chapel Hill metropolitan area, which together form the Raleigh-Durham-Chapel Hill combined statistical area, with a population of over two million residents.
