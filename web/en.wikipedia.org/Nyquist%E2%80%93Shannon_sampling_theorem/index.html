Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem

The Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem is a theorem in signal processing which states that a continuous signal that does not contain any frequencies higher than a certain maximum can be perfectly reconstructed from its samples, provided the sampling rate is more than twice that maximum frequency.

Statement

If a function x(t) contains no frequencies higher than B hertz, it is completely determined by its ordinates at a series of points spaced 1/(2B) seconds apart. The minimum sampling rate 2B is called the Nyquist rate. Sampling below this rate causes aliasing, in which high frequencies masquerade as lower ones.

History

The theorem is named after Harry Nyquist and Claude Shannon, though it was also derived independently by E. T. Whittaker, Vladimir Kotelnikov and others. Shannon published the classic formulation in his 1949 paper "Communication in the Presence of Noise".

Applications

The theorem underlies all digital audio and signal acquisition: compact discs, for example, sample audio at 44.1 kHz, more than twice the roughly 20 kHz limit of human hearing.
