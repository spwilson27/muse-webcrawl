Tanner scale

The Tanner scale, also known as the Tanner stages or sexual maturity rating (SMR), is a scale of physical development in children, adolescents, and adults. Developed by the British paediatrician James Tanner from longitudinal growth studies begun in the 1940s and published in 1962, it defines five stages of puberty based on the development of primary and secondary sex characteristics.

The stages

Stage 1 is prepubertal. Stages 2 through 4 describe progressive development, and stage 5 represents full adult maturity. Separate criteria are given for breast development and pubic hair in females, and for genital development and pubic hair in males. In girls, stage 2 (thelarche, the appearance of breast buds) typically occurs between ages 8 and 13; in boys, stage 2 begins with testicular enlargement, typically between ages 9 and 14. The full progression through the stages usually takes several years.

Clinical use

Clinicians use Tanner staging to assess whether puberty is precocious or delayed, to interpret growth velocity and hormone levels, and in the evaluation of disorders of pubertal development. The scale is also used in research on adolescent health and in forensic age estimation, though with recognised limitations: development varies by individual and population, and staging based on appearance alone cannot determine chronological age precisely.

Criticism

The original reference population was small and ethnically homogeneous, and average ages of pubertal onset have shifted since Tanner's studies, leading to periodic recalibration. Self-assessment by adolescents, sometimes used in large surveys, is less reliable than clinical examination.
