OxyContin

OxyContin is a brand name for a controlled-release formulation of the opioid analgesic oxycodone, developed and marketed by Purdue Pharma. Approved by the US FDA in 1995, it was designed to provide 12 hours of pain relief for moderate to severe pain.

Role in the opioid epidemic

Aggressive marketing that downplayed addiction risk, together with the ease of defeating the time-release mechanism, made OxyContin a widely misused drug and a central symbol of the opioid epidemic in the United States. Purdue pleaded guilty to criminal charges in 2007 and again in 2020; the Sackler family owners faced extensive litigation, and Purdue entered bankruptcy in 2019.

Reformulation

In 2010 Purdue introduced an abuse-deterrent formulation that is harder to crush or dissolve, which reduced OxyContin misuse but was followed by shifts of some users to heroin and later illicit fentanyl.
