Haram

Ḥarām (Arabic: حَرَام) is an Arabic term meaning "forbidden" in Islamic law (Sharia). It designates any act, object, or food that is prohibited by God according to the Quran and the Sunnah, and is one of the five rulings (al-aḥkām al-khamsa) that classify human actions, opposite to ḥalāl ("permissible").

Categories of action

In classical jurisprudence, actions are divided into five categories: obligatory (farḍ/wājib), recommended (mustaḥabb), neutral (mubāḥ), reprehensible (makrūh), and forbidden (ḥarām). Committing a haram act is considered sinful, while abstaining from it out of obedience to God is rewarded.

Examples

Prohibitions classed as haram include consuming pork and intoxicants, gambling, usury or interest (ribā), theft, murder, and adultery. Rulings are derived from the Quran and hadith through the principles of Islamic jurisprudence (uṣūl al-fiqh), and details can vary between legal schools (madhhabs). Necessity (ḍarūra) may in certain circumstances permit otherwise forbidden acts, such as eating forbidden food when starving.

Related meanings

The same root ḥ-r-m also gives ḥaram in the sense of a sacred sanctuary — as in al-Masjid al-Ḥarām, the Great Mosque of Mecca — and is related to the word "harem", denoting a protected or inviolable space.
