Chromate conversion coating

Chromate conversion coating or alodine coating is a type of conversion coating used to passivate steel, aluminium, zinc, cadmium, copper, silver, titanium, magnesium, and tin alloys. The coating serves as a corrosion inhibitor, as a primer for paint and adhesives, as a decorative finish, and to retain electrical conductivity.

Process

The coating is applied by immersing the part in a bath containing hexavalent chromium compounds (chromic acid or dichromate salts) together with activators such as fluoride. The solution reacts with the metal surface to form a thin gel-like film of mixed metal oxides and chromates, typically a fraction of a micrometre to a few micrometres thick. On aluminium alloys the finish ranges from clear to iridescent gold or tan; on zinc-plated steel it produces the familiar yellow, olive-drab, or clear chromate finishes. Well-known trade names include Alodine, Iridite, and Chem Film.

Applications

Chromate conversion coatings are widely used in the aerospace and electronics industries because they protect aluminium while preserving electrical bonding. They are governed by specifications such as MIL-DTL-5541 for aluminium. The coating's reservoir of soluble chromate gives it a degree of "self-healing": chromate ions can migrate to scratches and re-passivate exposed metal.

Health and environmental concerns

Hexavalent chromium is carcinogenic and environmentally hazardous, so regulations such as the European Union's RoHS and REACH rules have driven the adoption of trivalent chromium passivates and other chromium-free alternatives, which have gradually replaced hexavalent processes in many applications.
