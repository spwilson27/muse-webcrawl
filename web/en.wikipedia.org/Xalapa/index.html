Xalapa, capital of the Mexican state of Veracruz.

Xalapa or Jalapa (officially Xalapa-Enríquez) is the capital city of the Mexican state of Veracruz and the seat of the municipality of the same name. It is located in the central part of the state, on the eastern slopes of the Sierra Madre Oriental, roughly 100 kilometres northwest of the port of Veracruz.

Name

The name comes from the Nahuatl Xāllapan, from xālli ("sand") and āpan ("place on the water"), often interpreted as "spring in the sand". The suffix honors Juan de la Luz Enríquez, a 19th-century governor of Veracruz.

Geography and climate

Xalapa sits at about 1,400 metres above sea level in a humid, mountainous region known for lush vegetation, frequent mist and mild temperatures year-round. Its abundant flowers and greenery earned it the nickname "City of Flowers", a name promoted by the naturalist Alexander von Humboldt, who visited in 1804.

History

The area was inhabited in pre-Hispanic times by Totonac peoples. After the Spanish conquest, Xalapa became an important staging post on the route between Veracruz and Mexico City, and later a center of trade fairs. It became the state capital in the 19th century (definitively in 1885).

Culture and education

Xalapa is home to the Universidad Veracruzana, one of Mexico's largest public universities, and to the Xalapa Symphony Orchestra, founded in 1929 and among the oldest in the country. The Museo de Antropología de Xalapa is famous for its collection of giant Olmec colossal heads and other Gulf Coast archaeology. The city gives its name to the jalapeño pepper.
