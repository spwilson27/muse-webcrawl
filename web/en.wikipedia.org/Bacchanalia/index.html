Bacchanalia

The Bacchanalia were unofficial, privately funded popular Roman festivals of Bacchus, the Greco-Roman god of wine, freedom, intoxication, and ecstasy. They were based on the Greek Dionysia and the Dionysian Mysteries. The festivals were introduced into Rome around 200 BC from the Greek colonies of southern Italy.

The scandal of 186 BC

According to the historian Livy, the cult spread to Rome and became associated — in the Senate's view — with secrecy, crime, and conspiracy across class and gender lines. In 186 BC, the Roman Senate issued the Senatus consultum de Bacchanalibus, a decree, surviving in an inscribed bronze copy, that severely restricted the cult: worship was placed under strict state control, meetings were limited in size, and unauthorised celebration was suppressed throughout Italy. Thousands were reportedly arrested and many executed.

Later usage

The suppression is often interpreted as an assertion of senatorial authority over Italy and private religion rather than a purely moral crackdown. In modern usage, "bacchanalia" has come to mean any uninhibited, drunken celebration or orgy.
