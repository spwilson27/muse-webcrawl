Butter tart

A butter tart is a small pastry tart, a quintessentially Canadian dessert, consisting of a flaky shell filled with a sweet mixture of butter, sugar, syrup and egg, baked until the filling is semi-solid with a crusty top.

Variations

Raisins, currants, walnuts or pecans are often added, though purists debate whether raisins belong at all. The filling's consistency — runny or firm — is a matter of regional and family pride. Maple syrup may replace corn syrup for a distinctively Canadian flavour.

History

The earliest published recipe dates to 1900 in Barrie, Ontario, though the tart descends from older British and French-Canadian sugar-based pastries. Ontario celebrates the treat with butter tart tours and festivals, and Canada Post issued a butter tart stamp in 2019.
