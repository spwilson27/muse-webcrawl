Renal biopsy

A percutaneous renal biopsy performed under real-time ultrasound guidance.

Renal biopsy (also called kidney biopsy) is a medical procedure in which a small piece of kidney tissue is removed for examination under a microscope. It is performed to diagnose, prognose, and guide treatment of kidney diseases that cannot be adequately characterised by blood and urine tests and imaging alone. The procedure is most often carried out percutaneously, with a spring-loaded needle passed through the skin of the back into the lower pole of the kidney under real-time ultrasound guidance.

Indications

Common indications include unexplained acute kidney injury, nephrotic syndrome, persistent proteinuria or haematuria of suspected glomerular origin, suspected systemic diseases affecting the kidney such as lupus nephritis, and dysfunction of a transplanted kidney. The obtained cores are examined by light microscopy, immunofluorescence, and electron microscopy, which together allow classification of glomerular diseases and assessment of chronic damage.

Risks

The principal risk is bleeding, which is usually minor and self-limiting, manifesting as transient visible haematuria or a small perinephric haematoma. Serious bleeding requiring transfusion or intervention occurs in a small percentage of cases, and loss of the kidney or death is rare. Blood pressure control, correction of clotting abnormalities, and withholding anticoagulant and antiplatelet drugs beforehand reduce the risk.
