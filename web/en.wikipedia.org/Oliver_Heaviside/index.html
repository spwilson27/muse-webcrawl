Oliver Heaviside FRS (18 May 1850 – 3 February 1925) was an English self-taught mathematician and physicist who invented a new technique for solving differential equations (equivalent to the Laplace transform), independently developed vector calculus, and rewrote Maxwell's equations in the form commonly used today.

Work

Working as a telegraph operator and later in private study, Heaviside advanced the theory of long-distance telephony and telegraphy, showing that adding inductance (loading coils) could remove distortion from telephone lines — an idea patented later by others. He formulated the telegrapher's equations, predicted the existence of an ionised layer in the upper atmosphere (the Kennelly–Heaviside layer) that reflects radio waves, and developed the operational calculus used by electrical engineers.

Recognition

Heaviside was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1891 and was the first recipient of the Faraday Medal in 1922. Much of his work was published in The Electrician and in his books Electromagnetic Theory and Electrical Papers.
