Electricity sector in Ireland
System operator: EirGrid (TSO)
Main utility: ESB
Largest fuel: Natural gas; wind a major share
Market: Single Electricity Market (all-island)

The electricity sector in Ireland covers generation, transmission, and supply on the island of Ireland. Since 2007 the Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland have shared the all-island Single Electricity Market (SEM), operated across both jurisdictions, with EirGrid as transmission system operator in the Republic and SONI in Northern Ireland.

History

The Electricity Supply Board (ESB) was founded in 1927 to build the Shannon Scheme at Ardnacrusha, then one of the largest hydroelectric projects in the world, which supplied most of the young Irish Free State's power. The ESB remained a vertically integrated state monopoly for most of the 20th century before market liberalization under EU directives unbundled generation, networks, and supply.

Generation

Natural gas provides the largest share of electricity, but Ireland has become a world leader in wind penetration: wind routinely supplies around a third of annual demand and at times the majority of instantaneous output — challenging for a small, weakly interconnected island grid. Moneypoint in County Clare, long the country's largest station and its last major coal burner, ended coal burning in 2025. Peat stations such as those in the midlands have closed. Ireland imports and exports power through interconnectors to Great Britain (the East–West and Moyle interconnectors), with the Celtic Interconnector to France under construction.

Policy challenges

Ireland's data-centre boom has made demand growth and security of supply politically prominent, while the climate target of up to 80 percent renewable electricity by 2030 drives offshore wind development in the Irish Sea.
