Victory Column (Berlin)

The Victory Column (German: Siegessäule) is a monument in Berlin, Germany. Designed by Heinrich Strack after 1864 to commemorate Prussia's victory over Denmark in the Second Schleswig War, it was inaugurated in 1873 and subsequently also marked the Prussian victories over Austria (1866) and France (1870–71), reflected in additional gilded cannon barrels decorating the column.

Description

The column stands in the Großer Tiergarten on the Großer Stern roundabout. Its total height is about 67 metres, crowned by an 8.3-metre gilded bronze statue of Victoria, the Roman goddess of victory, weighing about 35 tonnes and sculpted by Friedrich Drake. Berliners affectionately call the statue "Goldelse" ("Golden Lizzy").

History

Originally erected on the Königsplatz in front of the Reichstag, the column was moved to its present location in 1938–39 as part of the Nazi regime's replanning of Berlin. A viewing platform beneath the statue can be reached by climbing 285 steps inside the column.
