Diterpene

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A diterpene is a class of terpenes composed of four isoprene units, giving a carbon skeleton of 20 carbon atoms (C20). They are produced by plants, animals and fungi. Diterpenes are biosynthesised from geranylgeranyl pyrophosphate (GGPP), itself built from isopentenyl pyrophosphate and dimethylallyl pyrophosphate via the mevalonate pathway in animals and fungi or the MEP pathway in plants.

Types and examples

Diterpenes display great structural diversity: acyclic forms (phytol, the side chain of chlorophyll, and phytanic acid), bicyclic forms (labdanes such as sclareol, and clerodanes), tricyclic forms (abietanes such as abietic acid from pine resin, and pimaranes), and tetracyclic forms (kauranes such as steviol, the aglycone of the sweetener stevia, and gibberellins, plant hormones that regulate growth and germination).

Uses

Many diterpenes have pharmacological significance: the diterpenoid paclitaxel (Taxol), originally isolated from the bark of the Pacific yew, is a widely used anticancer drug; forskolin from Coleus forskohlii activates adenylate cyclase and is used in research and traditional medicine; and the diterpene alcohol retinol (vitamin A) and its derivatives play essential roles in vision and development.
