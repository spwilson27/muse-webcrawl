The President of Turkey (Turkish: Türkiye Cumhurbaşkanı) is the head of state and, since the constitutional changes of 2017, also the head of government of the Republic of Turkey. The President represents the Republic, ensures the implementation of the Constitution and the regular functioning of state organs, and serves as commander-in-chief of the Turkish Armed Forces.

History

The office was created with the proclamation of the Republic on 29 October 1923, when Mustafa Kemal Atatürk was elected its first president. Under the parliamentary system that lasted until 2018, presidents were largely ceremonial figures elected by the Grand National Assembly; a 2007 referendum introduced direct popular election. The 2017 constitutional referendum abolished the office of Prime Minister and transferred executive authority to the President, converting Turkey to a presidential system, implemented after the 2018 elections.

Powers

The President issues presidential decrees on executive matters, appoints and dismisses ministers and the Vice President, prepares the national budget, calls parliamentary and presidential elections, promulgates laws (with a suspensive veto), and grants pardons on grounds of chronic illness, disability, or old age. The President is elected by universal suffrage for a five-year term and may serve at most two terms, subject to an exception where Parliament calls early elections during a second term.

List of presidents

Twelve men have held the office, including Atatürk, İsmet İnönü, Celâl Bayar, Cemal Gürsel, Cevdet Sunay, Fahri Korutürk, Kenan Evren, Turgut Özal, Süleyman Demirel, Ahmet Necdet Sezer, Abdullah Gül, and Recep Tayyip Erdoğan.
