Subdivision (land)

A subdivision is an area of land divided from a larger parcel into smaller pieces — typically called lots or plots — usually for sale or development. The term is widely used in real estate and land use law. Subdivisions may be purely residential, commercial, industrial, or mixed-use, and are generally recorded on an official plat or subdivision map filed with the local government.

Legal aspects

In most jurisdictions, dividing land requires approval under local subdivision regulations, which may set minimum lot sizes, require street and utility improvements, mandate dedication of land for parks or schools, and impose environmental review. The process is closely tied to zoning ordinances that govern what may be built on each lot.

History

Modern subdivision practice grew with the expansion of cities and the railroad and streetcar suburbs of the 19th century. In the United States, mass-produced suburban subdivisions expanded dramatically after the Second World War, exemplified by developments such as Levittown, New York. Subdivision design shifted from gridiron street patterns toward curvilinear layouts with culs-de-sac in the later 20th century.

Types

Subdivisions range from simple lot splits to large master-planned communities containing thousands of homes along with schools, shops, and amenities. Condominium and planned unit development forms subdivide ownership interests in other ways, and many subdivisions are governed by homeowner associations enforcing covenants, conditions, and restrictions (CC&Rs).
