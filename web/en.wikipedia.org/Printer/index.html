In computing, a printer is a peripheral device that produces a physical, usually paper, copy of text and graphics stored in electronic form. Printers are among the most common computer accessories and are used in homes, offices, and industry. Their performance is typically measured by speed (pages per minute) and resolution (dots per inch, dpi).

Types

Inkjet printers propel tiny droplets of liquid ink onto paper; they are inexpensive and print high-quality colour photographs but have relatively costly consumables. Laser printers use a xerographic process with dry toner and a heated fuser, offering fast, sharp text output and dominating office use. Dot matrix printers, an impact technology, survive in niches requiring carbon copies. Thermal printers are ubiquitous for receipts and labels, and dye-sublimation and large-format plotters serve photographic and engineering applications. 3D printers, which build solid objects layer by layer, are a distinct class of device.

History

Computer printers originated in the 1950s with adapted teleprinters and line printers for mainframes. The first high-speed printer was Remington Rand's UNIPRINT (1953). Xerox's 9700 (1977) pioneered commercial laser printing, while the HP LaserJet (1984) brought it to the desktop alongside the rise of the personal computer and desktop publishing, aided by the PostScript page description language. Modern printers commonly combine scanning, copying, and fax in multi-function printers and connect wirelessly to computers and phones.
