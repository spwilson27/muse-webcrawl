Commoner

A commoner, also known as the common man, commoners, the common people or the masses, is an ordinary person in a community or nation who does not have any significant social status, especially one who is a member of neither royalty, nobility, nor any part of the aristocracy.

Historical usage

In feudal and early modern European societies, commoners formed the third estate, below the clergy and the nobility; the term covered everyone from peasants and artisans to wealthy merchants and professionals who lacked noble title. In the United Kingdom, a commoner is anyone who is neither the sovereign nor a peer, so that even members of the royal family without peerages are technically commoners; members of the House of Commons were historically drawn from commoners.

In imperial China, commoners (庶民) were distinguished from officials and the imperial clan, and in feudal Japan the commoner classes comprised peasants, artisans, and merchants below the samurai. The word "commoner" is also used in British universities, notably Oxford, for an undergraduate who does not hold a scholarship or exhibition.
