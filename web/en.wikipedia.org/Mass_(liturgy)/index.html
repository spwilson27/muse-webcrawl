Mass (liturgy)

Mass
Type: Christian liturgy
Traditions: Roman Catholic, Old Catholic, some Anglican and Lutheran
Central act: Eucharist (Holy Communion)

The Mass is the central liturgical service of the Eucharist in the Roman Catholic Church and in some other Western Christian traditions, including Old Catholicism, Anglo-Catholicism and some Lutheran churches. The term derives from the Latin dismissal formula Ite, missa est ("Go, it is the dismissal"). In Eastern Christianity, the corresponding service is generally called the Divine Liturgy.

Structure

Since the reforms of the Second Vatican Council, the Mass in the Roman Rite consists of two main parts. The Liturgy of the Word includes readings from Scripture, a homily, the Creed and prayers of intercession. The Liturgy of the Eucharist includes the presentation of bread and wine, the Eucharistic Prayer (including the consecration and the words of institution), and Communion. Introductory and concluding rites frame the service. Catholics believe that in the Eucharist the bread and wine become the body and blood of Christ (transubstantiation).

Forms and history

The Mass developed from the early Christian celebration of the Lord's Supper. The Tridentine Mass, codified after the Council of Trent in 1570 and celebrated in Latin, remained the standard form until the introduction of the Mass of Paul VI in 1969–1970, normally celebrated in the vernacular. The older form continues to be celebrated as an extraordinary form. Mass settings — the Ordinary (Kyrie, Gloria, Credo, Sanctus, Agnus Dei) and Propers — have inspired an enormous body of sacred music, from Gregorian chant through the polyphony of Palestrina and Byrd to the great Masses of Haydn, Mozart and Beethoven.
