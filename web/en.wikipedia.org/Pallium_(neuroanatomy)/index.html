Pallium (neuroanatomy)

Part ofTelencephalon (forebrain)
ComponentsCerebral cortex, hippocampus, amygdala, olfactory bulb
Contrasts withSubpallium

In neuroanatomy, the pallium is the dorsal region of the telencephalon (forebrain) in vertebrates. It gives rise to the cerebral cortex in mammals — including the neocortex, hippocampus, and olfactory cortex — as well as structures such as the amygdala and claustrum. The ventral telencephalon is called the subpallium and gives rise to the basal ganglia.

Divisions

Developmental studies divide the pallium into medial, dorsal, lateral, and ventral sectors. The medial pallium forms the hippocampal formation; the dorsal pallium forms the neocortex of mammals; the lateral pallium forms the olfactory (piriform) cortex; and the ventral pallium contributes the claustrum and parts of the amygdala.

Evolution

All vertebrates possess a pallium, though its organisation varies greatly: in amphibians and reptiles it is a thin three-layered sheet, while in birds it develops into the large hyperpallium and dorsal ventricular ridge, and in mammals into the six-layered neocortex. How homologous these structures are across groups is a longstanding question in comparative neuroscience.
