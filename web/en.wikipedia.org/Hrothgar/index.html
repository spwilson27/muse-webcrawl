Hrothgar

Hrothgar (Old English: Hrōðgār) is a legendary Danish king depicted in the Old English epic poem Beowulf and other early medieval Scandinavian and English sources. He is a member of the Scylding dynasty and the builder of Heorot, the great mead hall that is attacked by the monster Grendel.

In Beowulf

In Beowulf, Hrothgar is an aged and venerable king who, after success in war, builds the magnificent hall Heorot as a place to share treasure with his retainers. For twelve years the monster Grendel, provoked by the noise of celebration, raids the hall by night and kills Hrothgar's thanes, and the old king can do nothing to stop him. The Geatish hero Beowulf sails to Denmark, kills Grendel and Grendel's mother, and is richly rewarded. Hrothgar's speech of thanks — often called "Hrothgar's sermon" — warns the young hero against pride and the dangers of kingship. Hrothgar appears in the poem as a figure of wisdom, generosity, and the sorrow of old age.

Other sources

A Hrothgar (Norse Hróarr) also appears in Old Norse sagas such as Hrólfs saga kraka and Saxo Grammaticus's Gesta Danorum, as well as in the Old English poem Widsith. The details vary considerably between traditions. Some scholars have connected him with a historical Danish king of the 6th century, though he is generally treated as a figure of legend.
