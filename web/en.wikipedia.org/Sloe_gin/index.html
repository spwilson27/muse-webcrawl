Sloe gin


Homemade sloe gin, with the sloes steeping in the spirit

Sloe gin is a British red liqueur made with gin and sloes. Sloes are the fruit (drupe) of Prunus spinosa, the blackthorn plant, a relative of the plum. Typically, the fruit are steeped in gin together with sugar; the resulting liqueur has an alcohol content between 15 and 30 percent by volume. It is traditionally made in autumn and winter, after the first frosts soften the sloes, and is often consumed at Christmas.

Production

Sloe gin is made from ripe sloes, which are traditionally picked after the first frost of winter (late October to early November in the northern hemisphere). Each sloe is pricked—traditionally with a thorn taken from the blackthorn bush on which they grew, or a fork—and dropped into a wide-mouthed jar. The jar is filled with sugar and gin, sealed, and turned or shaken weekly during the first weeks. The liquid turns a deep ruby red as the fruit flavour infuses. After at least three months, often much longer, the gin is decanted and bottled.

An alternative method adds sugar syrup after the fruit has been steeped, which allows greater control over sweetness. Almond flavouring may be added, or the natural almond note comes from the sloe stones. Commercial producers frequently use less expensive neutral spirits and flavourings, though premium producers still macerate real sloes.

Legal definitions

Under European Union regulations, sloe gin is defined as a spirit drink produced by macerating sloes in gin, with a minimum alcoholic strength of 25% ABV and only natural flavourings permitted. In the United States, sloe gin may be made from grain-neutral spirits with a lower alcohol content, which has historically led to a perception of lower quality.

Serving

Sloe gin is traditionally served neat as a winter warmer or after-dinner drink. It is also used in cocktails, most famously the sloe gin fizz, which combines sloe gin with lemon juice, simple syrup, and soda water. It can be mixed with champagne for a festive drink, or added to mulled wine and cider.
