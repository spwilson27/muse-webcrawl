Large language model

A large language model (LLM) is a language model trained with self-supervised machine learning on a vast amount of text, designed for natural language processing tasks, especially language generation. LLMs are based on the transformer architecture and are trained to predict the next token in a sequence.

Training

LLMs are pre-trained on large corpora of text — books, articles, websites, and code — which can contain trillions of tokens. Model size is measured in parameters, ranging from millions to hundreds of billions or more. After pre-training, models are often fine-tuned with instruction data and aligned using techniques such as reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).

Capabilities

As models scale, they exhibit emergent abilities including translation, summarisation, question answering, reasoning, and code generation. LLMs power chatbots such as ChatGPT, as well as code assistants, search features, and document-analysis tools. They can also be adapted to specific domains through fine-tuning or retrieval-augmented generation (RAG).

Limitations and concerns

LLMs may produce plausible but false statements ("hallucinations"), reflect biases in their training data, and require enormous computational resources to train and run. Their environmental cost, impact on employment, and potential misuse for misinformation are subjects of ongoing debate.

Notable models

Well-known model families include OpenAI's GPT series, Google's Gemini, Anthropic's Claude, Meta's Llama, and BERT, an earlier encoder-only model widely used for understanding tasks.
