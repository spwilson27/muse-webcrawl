Starch or amylum is a polymeric carbohydrate consisting of numerous glucose units joined by glycosidic bonds. This polysaccharide is produced by most green plants for energy storage. Worldwide, it is the most common carbohydrate in human diets, and is contained in large amounts in staple foods such as wheat, potatoes, maize (corn), rice, and cassava. Pure starch is a white, tasteless and odourless powder that is insoluble in cold water or alcohol; it consists of two types of molecules: the linear and helical amylose and the branched amylopectin.

Structure and properties

Amylose is a linear chain of α(1→4)-linked glucose typically hundreds to thousands of units long, forming helices that give the blue colour with iodine — a classic test for starch. Amylopectin is much larger and highly branched, with α(1→6) branch points every roughly 24–30 glucose units. Depending on the plant, starch generally contains 20–25 percent amylose and 75–80 percent amylopectin by weight. In the plant, starch is stored in semi-crystalline granules within amyloplasts. When heated in water, granules swell and gelatinize, thickening the mixture; on cooling, retrogradation can firm gels and stale baked goods.

Digestion and nutrition

Humans digest starch with amylase enzymes in saliva and pancreatic juice, breaking it down to maltose and glucose, which is absorbed for energy. Fractions that escape small-intestinal digestion — resistant starch — are fermented by the gut microbiota and are studied for metabolic benefits. Starch provides about 4 kilocalories per gram and is the main energy source in most diets; staple starchy foods include bread, pasta, cereals, and tubers.

Industrial uses

Starch is processed into sugars such as glucose syrup and high-fructose corn syrup, fermented into ethanol, and used as a food thickener, adhesive (including corrugated board), paper coating and sizing agent, textile stiffener, and pharmaceutical excipient. Modified starches are chemically or physically treated to improve stability, viscosity, or freeze–thaw performance.
