Treaty of Thorn (1411)

The (First) Treaty of Thorn was a peace treaty formally ending the Polish–Lithuanian–Teutonic War (1409–1411). It was signed on 1 February 1411 in Thorn (Toruń), one of the southernmost cities of the Monastic State of the Teutonic Knights.

Treaty of Thorn (1411)
Signed1 February 1411, Thorn (Toruń)
PartiesPoland and Lithuania; the Teutonic Order
ContextAfter the Battle of Grunwald, 1410

Terms

Poland regained the Dobrzyń Land, Lithuania regained Samogitia — but only for the lifetimes of Jogaila (Władysław II Jagiełło) and Vytautas, after which it would revert to the Order. The Teutonic Order paid a large indemnity — some 100,000 kopas of Prague groschen — which strained its finances. Prisoners were released and captured standards returned in part.

Aftermath

Despite Grunwald's scale, the terms were comparatively lenient, partly because the siege of Marienburg had failed. Disputes over borders and Samogitia fed later conflicts — the Hunger War (1414) and Gollub War (1422) — settled more durably by the Treaty of Melno (1422). The indemnity helped spark internal revolt within the Order's state.
