Kalka–Shimla Railway

Kalka–Shimla Railway
Length: 96.6 km
Gauge: 762 mm (narrow gauge)
Opened: 1903
UNESCO World Heritage Site: 2008

The Kalka–Shimla Railway is a narrow-gauge railway in northern India that runs from Kalka in Haryana to Shimla, the capital of Himachal Pradesh, through the foothills of the Himalayas. Built under British rule and opened in 1903, it climbs about 1,400 metres over 96.6 kilometres, passing through 102 tunnels and over more than 800 bridges and viaducts. In 2008 it was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the Mountain Railways of India.

History

Shimla was the summer capital of British India, and a rail link was needed to move officials, mail, and supplies up from the plains. Construction began in 1898 under the Delhi-Ambala-Kalka Railway Company. The longest tunnel, at Barog, is associated with a well-known legend about the engineer Colonel Barog. The line's ruling gradient and tight curves demanded small locomotives and low speeds; the journey still takes over five hours.

Route and features

The line serves 18 stations, including Solan, Barog, Kandaghat, and Summer Hill. Its multi-arched stone viaducts, built in the style of Roman aqueducts, are celebrated engineering landmarks. Diesel railcars and the luxury Shivalik Deluxe Express operate alongside ordinary passenger trains, and the route is a popular tourist attraction for its mountain scenery.
