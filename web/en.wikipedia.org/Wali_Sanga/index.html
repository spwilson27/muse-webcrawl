Wali Sanga

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Wali Sanga (Javanese: "Nine Saints") are nine revered figures credited in Javanese tradition with spreading Islam in Java during the 15th and 16th centuries, as the Hindu-Buddhist Majapahit empire declined and the first Javanese Muslim sultanates arose. Their tombs remain major pilgrimage sites.

The nine saints

Lists vary, but the commonly cited nine are: Maulana Malik Ibrahim (Sunan Gresik), Sunan Ampel, Sunan Bonang, Sunan Drajat, Sunan Giri, Sunan Kalijaga, Sunan Kudus, Sunan Muria, and Sunan Gunung Jati. Many are said to have been of Arab, Persian, or Chinese descent or connected to earlier Muslim trading communities on Java's north coast (the Pasisir).

Methods and legend

Tradition portrays the saints as adapting Islam to Javanese culture rather than imposing it: Sunan Kalijaga is credited with using wayang shadow puppetry, gamelan music, and Javanese dress to teach Islamic ideas, and Sunan Kudus with respecting Hindu sensibilities about cattle. The saints are also woven into the founding legends of the Demak Sultanate, Java's first Muslim kingdom. Historical documentation is thin, and much of the narrative comes from later chronicles (babad) mixing fact and myth.

Legacy

The Wali Sanga embody the syncretic character of Javanese Islam. Pilgrimage (ziarah) to their graves draws millions of Indonesian Muslims, and institutions from mosques to the university UIN Walisongo in Semarang bear their name.
