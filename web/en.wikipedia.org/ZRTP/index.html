ZRTP

ZRTP (a portmanteau of "Z" and Real-time Transport Protocol) is a cryptographic key-agreement protocol for negotiating encryption keys between two endpoints of a voice over IP (VoIP) phone call. It was developed by Phil Zimmermann, the creator of Pretty Good Privacy (PGP), together with Jon Callas and others.

DeveloperPhil Zimmermann
TypeKey agreement protocol
StandardRFC 6189
TransportRTP media stream

Overview

Unlike protocols that rely on a public key infrastructure or on signalling-channel security, ZRTP performs a Diffie–Hellman key exchange directly in the media stream. Security against man-in-the-middle attacks is provided by a Short Authentication String (SAS) — a short sequence of words or characters that the two callers read aloud and compare.

Key continuity

ZRTP caches key material from previous calls between the same endpoints. If an attacker is absent from the first call, later calls are protected even if the SAS is not re-verified, a property sometimes called key continuity.

Adoption

ZRTP was published as RFC 6189 in 2011 and has been implemented in software such as Jitsi, Linphone, and various secure telephone applications.
