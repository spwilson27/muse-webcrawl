Nyaya

SchoolĀstika (orthodox) Hindu philosophy
Founder (trad.)Akshapada Gautama
Root textNyāya Sūtras (c. 2nd c. BCE – 2nd c. CE)
FocusLogic, epistemology, debate

Nyaya (Sanskrit: न्याय, Nyāya, "method, rule, logic") is one of the six orthodox (āstika) schools of Hindu philosophy, distinguished by its systematic development of logic and epistemology. Its foundational text, the Nyāya Sūtras attributed to Akshapada Gautama, organises inquiry around sixteen categories (padārtha) and the means of attaining liberation through true knowledge.

Epistemology

Nyaya recognises four valid means of knowledge (pramāṇa): perception (pratyakṣa), inference (anumāna), comparison (upamāna), and testimony (śabda). Its theory of inference is articulated in a five-membered syllogism: proposition, reason, example, application, and conclusion — classically illustrated by inferring fire on a hill from the perception of smoke. Nyaya is realist and pluralist: the self, God, and external objects are all real, and error is explained as misattribution (anyathākhyāti). Later Nyaya theologians such as Udayana offered elaborate logical proofs for the existence of God (Īśvara) as the efficient cause of the world.

Navya-Nyaya

From the 13th century, Gangeśa's Tattvacintāmaṇi inaugurated Navya-Nyāya ("New Logic"), centred in Mithila and later Navadvipa, which developed a highly technical formal language for analysing cognition, negation, and quantifier-like relations, and influenced all later Indian philosophy. Nyaya merged closely with the Vaiśeṣika school, whose categories of substance it largely adopted.
