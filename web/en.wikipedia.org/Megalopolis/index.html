Megalopolis

A megalopolis (sometimes called a megapolis or megalopolitan region) is a large, continuous urban region formed when several metropolitan areas grow together into a single networked system. The term was popularised by the French geographer Jean Gottmann in his 1961 study of the northeastern United States, where he used it for the urbanised corridor running from Boston through New York City and Philadelphia to Washington, D.C.

Definition and characteristics

A megalopolis is generally defined by a population of more than 10 million people spread across multiple, functionally linked metropolitan areas. The constituent cities retain separate identities and governments, but they are connected by dense transport corridors, shared labour markets, commuting flows, trade and infrastructure. Unlike a single conurbation, a megalopolis is polycentric: it has many urban cores rather than one dominant centre.

Examples

Frequently cited examples include the Northeast megalopolis (BosWash) in the United States; the Taiheiyō Belt in Japan, running from Tokyo to Fukuoka; the Blue Banana corridor in Western Europe, stretching from northwest England through the Low Countries and the Rhineland to northern Italy; and China's Yangtze River Delta and Pearl River Delta regions. In India, the corridor from Delhi through to Kolkata is sometimes described as an emerging megalopolitan system.

History of the term

The word derives from the Greek megas (great) and polis (city); it was also the name of an ancient city in Arcadia, Greece. Gottmann's Megalopolis: The Urbanized Northeastern Seaboard of the United States (1961) gave the term its modern urban-studies meaning. Later writers extended the concept to "megaregions" planned at national scale.

Criticism

Critics note that the boundaries of a megalopolis are arbitrary and depend on which linkages are measured. Others argue that the term overstates integration: governance remains fragmented among many states, provinces and municipalities, and the rural and suburban spaces between the cores are not truly urban.
