C86

C86 was a compilation album released in 1986 as a mail-order cassette supplement to the British music magazine NME. The title refers to the cassette format and year. The tape featured 22 tracks by then little-known British independent bands, including Primal Scream, The Pastels, The Wedding Present, The Soup Dragons, and The Mighty Lemon Drops.

The cassette

The tape was compiled by NME journalists (Roy Carr, Adrian Thrills, and Neil Taylor among them) as the latest in the magazine's series of mail-order cassettes, following C81. Side one (called "Side One" or, in some accounts, the "jangly" side) opens with Primal Scream's "Velocity Girl", frequently cited as one of the defining tracks of the era; side two tended toward noisier and more abrasive acts such as The Jesus and Mary Chain-influenced bands, Big Flame, and Stump.

Style and legacy

"C86" became shorthand for a whole genre of British indie music — jangly guitars, shambling amateurishness, and melodic pop indebted to The Byrds, The Velvet Underground, and post-punk — and for a fanzine-based culture around labels such as Sarah Records. Critics were divided: some celebrated its DIY ethos, others attacked it as twee and retrograde. In Britain the term was sometimes used pejoratively, though many of the featured bands, including Primal Scream and The Soup Dragons, went on to mainstream success.

Expanded editions

The album was issued on CD in 2006, and an expanded three-disc deluxe edition appeared in 2014, adding many more bands of the period. A 2016 book and documentary retrospectives marked its thirtieth anniversary.
