Order:Rosales

Rosales is an order of flowering plants belonging to the rosid clade of the eudicots. It is sister to a clade consisting of the orders Fagales and Cucurbitales. The order contains about 7,700 species distributed among some 260 genera in nine families, and includes many plants of great economic importance: roses, apples, pears, peaches, plums, strawberries, almonds, elms, figs, nettles, hops and cannabis.

Classification

Under the APG IV system, the following nine families are recognised in Rosales:

• Rosaceae — the rose family, by far the largest, with apples, pears, stone fruits, berries and ornamental roses.
• Rhamnaceae — the buckthorn family.
• Elaeagnaceae — the oleaster family.
• Ulmaceae — the elm family.
• Cannabaceae — including hops and cannabis.
• Moraceae — the mulberry and fig family.
• Urticaceae — the nettle family.
• Barbeyaceae and Dirachmaceae — two small families.

Older systems such as that of Cronquist included many more families in Rosales, but molecular phylogenetic studies have redrawn the order's boundaries substantially; several former members, such as the saxifrages, have been moved to other orders.

Characteristics

Members of Rosales are morphologically diverse, ranging from herbs and shrubs to large trees. Flowers are often showy with numerous stamens, and the fruit types vary widely—from the pomes of apples and drupes of cherries in Rosaceae to the multiple fruits of figs in Moraceae. Many families in the order are wind- or insect-pollinated, and nitrogen-fixing symbioses with Frankia bacteria occur in several lineages, including some Rosaceae and Elaeagnaceae.

Economic importance

Rosales supplies a large share of the world's temperate fruit crops, as well as timber (elm, cherry), ornamentals, fibres and herbal products. The Rosaceae alone ranks among the most economically significant plant families.
