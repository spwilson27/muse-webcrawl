Robert Penn Warren
Born: 24 April 1905, Guthrie, Kentucky
Died: 15 September 1989, Stratton, Vermont
Notable work: All the King's Men

Robert Penn Warren (1905–1989) was an American poet, novelist and literary critic, and the only person to have won Pulitzer Prizes for both fiction and poetry.

Life and work

Born in Kentucky and educated at Vanderbilt University, Warren was associated in his youth with the Fugitive poets and later the Southern Agrarians. With Cleanth Brooks he co-authored influential textbooks of the New Criticism, including Understanding Poetry (1938). His novel All the King's Men (1946), based in part on the career of Louisiana governor Huey Long, won the Pulitzer Prize for Fiction and was adapted into an Academy Award-winning film.

Honours

Warren won Pulitzer Prizes for poetry in 1958 and 1979 and in 1986 was named the first Poet Laureate Consultant in Poetry of the United States under that title.
