Douglas DC-8

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Douglas DC-8 (sometimes McDonnell Douglas DC-8) is an early long-range narrow-body jetliner designed and produced by the American Douglas Aircraft Company. Work began in 1952; it first flew on 30 May 1958 and entered service with Delta Air Lines and United Airlines in September 1959. A pioneering jet airliner, the DC-8 competed directly with Boeing's 707.

Development and variants

The DC-8 was offered in the initial Series 10–40 powered by various turbojets, then the longer-range Series 50 with Pratt & Whitney JT3D turbofans, and from 1967 the stretched "Super Sixties" (Series 61, 62 and 63), which could seat up to 259 passengers or carry freight. In total 556 DC-8s were delivered between 1959 and 1972. In the early 1980s many Series 60 aircraft were re-engined with CFM56 turbofans as the Series 70, greatly reducing noise and fuel burn.

Service history

A DC-8 became the first civilian jetliner to break the sound barrier, in a controlled dive during a test flight in August 1961. The type served with airlines worldwide as a passenger aircraft and later as a freighter; NASA used one as a flying laboratory. Although withdrawn from passenger service, a handful of DC-8s remain in cargo, charter and research use decades after production ended.
