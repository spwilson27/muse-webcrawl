Battle of Sheriffmuir

Battle of Sheriffmuir
Date13 November 1715
LocationSheriffmuir, near Dunblane, Scotland
ResultTactically inconclusive; strategic government victory
BelligerentsBritish government forces; Jacobites
CommandersJohn Campbell, 2nd Duke of Argyll; John Erskine, Earl of Mar

The Battle of Sheriffmuir was fought on 13 November 1715 on high moorland near Dunblane, the major Scottish engagement of the Jacobite rising of 1715. The Jacobite army under the Earl of Mar, though considerably larger, faced government forces commanded by the Duke of Argyll.

The battle became proverbially confused: each army's right wing routed the opposing left, so that both sides could claim to have won part of the field — a symmetry captured in the song "There was a battle in the north... and some say that we wan, and some say that they wan". Mar, however, failed to exploit his advantage and withdrew to Perth, surrendering the initiative.

Aftermath

Strategically Sheriffmuir ended the rising in Scotland. Coming on the same day as the Jacobite surrender at Preston in England, it doomed the rebellion; the Old Pretender, James Francis Edward Stuart, landed in December but abandoned the cause within weeks, sailing back to France in February 1716.
