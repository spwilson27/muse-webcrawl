Alternating series

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, an alternating series is an infinite series whose terms alternate in sign, of the form a1 − a2 + a3 − a4 + …, where each an is positive. A classic example is the alternating harmonic series 1 − 1/2 + 1/3 − 1/4 + …, which converges to ln 2.

Alternating series test

The alternating series test (Leibniz's test) states that if the terms decrease in absolute value monotonically to zero, the series converges. The error when stopping after a partial sum is no greater in magnitude than the first omitted term, and partial sums alternate around the limit.

Conditional convergence

Many alternating series are only conditionally convergent: rearranged, their terms can be made to sum to any value, as described by the Riemann series theorem.
