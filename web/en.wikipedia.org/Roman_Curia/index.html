The Roman Curia (Italian: Curia Romana) is the central administrative apparatus of the Holy See through which the pope conducts the affairs of the worldwide Catholic Church. Often compared to a civil service and diplomatic corps combined, it handles doctrine, bishops' appointments, canon law, finance, communications and relations with states.

Structure

The Curia's first office is the Secretariat of State, headed by the Cardinal Secretary of State — effectively the Holy See's prime minister — which handles political and diplomatic affairs and coordinates the other departments. The executive departments, called dicasteries, include those for the Doctrine of the Faith, Bishops, Divine Worship and the Discipline of the Sacraments, the Causes of Saints, Evangelization, and the Eastern Churches. Judicial functions lie with the tribunals — the Apostolic Penitentiary, the Supreme Tribunal of the Apostolic Signatura and the Roman Rota — while bodies such as the Secretariat for the Economy oversee finances, an area overhauled after the Vatileaks scandals and the London property affair.

History

The Curia grew from the papal chapel and chancery of the early Middle Ages; the College of Cardinals' consistories once did its work. Sixtus V's bull Immensa aeterni Dei (1588) organised the system of specialised congregations that lasted, with adjustments by Pius X (1908) and Paul VI (1967), into the modern era. John Paul II's Pastor Bonus (1988) governed the Curia until Pope Francis's constitution Praedicate evangelium (2022) re-founded it on evangelisation, opened dicastery leadership to any baptised Catholic (not only cardinals), merged several councils into dicasteries, and made the Dicastery for Evangelization the first-listed department, headed by the pope himself.

Criticism

The Curia has long been criticised — including by popes — for bureaucracy, careerism and resistance to reform; Pope Francis's 2014 Christmas address cataloguing its "spiritual diseases" became famous. Its officials serve at the pope's pleasure, and the institution's effectiveness depends heavily on the reigning pontiff's management.
