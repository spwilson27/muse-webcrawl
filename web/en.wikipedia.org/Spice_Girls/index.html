The Spice Girls are an English girl group formed in London in 1994. The group comprises Melanie Brown ("Scary Spice"), Melanie Chisholm ("Sporty Spice"), Emma Bunton ("Baby Spice"), Geri Halliwell ("Ginger Spice") and Victoria Beckham ("Posh Spice"). They are widely credited with leading the mid-1990s revival of teen pop and popularising the slogan "girl power".

Career

Their debut single "Wannabe" (1996) topped the charts in 37 countries, and their debut album Spice sold more than 31 million copies worldwide, becoming the best-selling album by an all-female group in history. The follow-up Spiceworld (1997) and the film of the same name cemented their global fame; in the UK they scored nine number-one singles, including three consecutive Christmas number ones.

Halliwell left the group in May 1998. The remaining four released Forever (2000) before going on indefinite hiatus in 2001. All five reunited for a world tour in 2007–2008 and performed at the closing ceremony of the 2012 London Olympics. In 2019 they toured UK and Irish stadiums as a four-piece, without Beckham.

"Girl power" and cultural impact

The group's carefully differentiated personas and emphasis on female friendship and self-confidence — packaged as "girl power" — made them a defining cultural phenomenon of the 1990s and influenced a generation of pop acts. Their commercial empire extended to films, dolls, and hundreds of licensed products. They remain the best-selling female group of all time, with over 100 million records sold.
