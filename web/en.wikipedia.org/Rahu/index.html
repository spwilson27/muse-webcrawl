Rahu

Rahu, the serpent head that swallows the Sun and Moon, causing eclipses.

Rahu (Sanskrit: राहु, Rāhu) is one of the nine major celestial bodies (navagraha) in Hindu texts and the king of meteors. It is the severed head of the asura Svarbhanu, who is believed to swallow the Sun and Moon, causing eclipses. Rahu is depicted as a serpent with no body, riding a chariot drawn by eight black horses, and is paired with Ketu, the remaining tail of the serpent, opposite in the sky.

Astronomically, Rahu and Ketu correspond to the ascending and descending lunar nodes — the two points where the Moon's orbit crosses the ecliptic — and thus to the places where eclipses can occur. In Vedic astrology, Rahu is a malefic shadow planet associated with materialism, obsession, foreign lands and sudden gains, and governs an 18-year period (dasha) in a person's chart.

Mythology

According to the myth of the churning of the Ocean of Milk (Samudra Manthana), the asura Svarbhanu disguised himself among the gods to drink the nectar of immortality, amrita. The Sun and Moon alerted Vishnu, who cut off the demon's head with his discus — but the head, having already tasted the nectar, became immortal as Rahu, while the body became Ketu. In revenge, Rahu periodically "swallows" the Sun and Moon, but the eclipse ends because the orbs pass out through his severed neck.

Worship and astrology

Rahu is worshipped particularly during solar and lunar eclipses and in temples dedicated to the navagraha, notably in Tamil Nadu. The period of the day ruled by Rahu, called Rahu Kalam, is considered inauspicious for beginning new ventures in South Indian tradition.
