Christian eschatology

Christian eschatology is the branch of Christian theology concerned with the "last things": death, judgement, the Second Coming of Christ, the resurrection of the dead, the end of the world, and the final destiny of humanity and creation. Its teachings draw chiefly on the Old and New Testaments, especially Daniel, the Synoptic Gospels, Paul's epistles, and the Book of Revelation.

Core themes

Central elements include the parousia (Second Coming), the general resurrection, the Last Judgement, and the establishment of the Kingdom of God in a renewed heaven and earth. Christians have differed over the intermediate state between death and resurrection, the nature of heaven and hell, and the timing and character of the millennium described in Revelation 20.

Millennial views

Interpretations of the millennium include premillennialism (Christ returns before a literal thousand-year reign), postmillennialism (the gospel progressively transforms the world before Christ returns), and amillennialism (the millennium symbolises the present church age). Dispensationalism, a 19th-century premillennial system, adds a distinct rapture of the church and a detailed prophetic timetable.

Historical development

Expectation of an imminent end was strong in the early church and recurred in movements throughout Christian history, from Montanism to medieval millenarian movements and the Millerite "Great Disappointment" of 1844.
