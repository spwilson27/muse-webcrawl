William of Wykeham (1320 or 1324 – 27 September 1404) was an English bishop, statesman and educational founder. As Bishop of Winchester and twice Lord Chancellor of England, he was one of the most powerful men in the kingdom under Edward III and Richard II, and he founded both Winchester College and New College, Oxford.

Rise

Born of humble parents at Wickham in Hampshire, Wykeham rose through skill in administration and building. Working for the Crown, he supervised major works at Windsor Castle and other royal residences, and his abilities brought him rapid promotion in both church and state: keeper of the privy seal, then Chancellor in 1367, and Bishop of Winchester — England's richest see — in 1366. His accumulation of offices and wealth drew attack from John of Gaunt's faction; in 1376 he was impeached, stripped of his temporalities, and briefly exiled, but was pardoned and restored the following year.

Foundations

Wykeham's lasting legacy is educational. He founded Winchester College in 1382 as a grammar school to prepare seventy poor scholars for New College, Oxford, which he had founded in 1379; the two institutions formed a single linked foundation, a model later copied by Eton and King's College, Cambridge. His motto, "Manners makyth man", remains that of both colleges. He also rebuilt the nave of Winchester Cathedral in the Perpendicular style, where his chantry tomb still stands.
