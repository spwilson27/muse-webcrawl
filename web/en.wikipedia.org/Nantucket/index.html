Nantucket

Nantucket is an island about 30 miles (48 km) south of Cape Cod in the U.S. state of Massachusetts, together with the small islands of Tuckernuck and Muskeget forming the Town and County of Nantucket. Its name derives from a Wampanoag word meaning "faraway land of water" or "place of peace".

History

English settlers purchased the island in 1659 from Thomas Mayhew. In the late 17th and 18th centuries Nantucket became a world centre of the whaling industry; its Quaker shipowners and Wampanoag and later Cape Verdean crews crewed voyages across the Pacific. Herman Melville's Moby-Dick features the island prominently, though the Pequod sails from Nantucket in fiction while the real whaling ship Essex — sunk by a sperm whale in 1820 — was a Nantucket vessel. The decline of whaling and the Great Fire of 1846 shrank the population sharply.

Today

Nantucket revived as a summer resort; its year-round population of roughly 14,000 swells several-fold in summer. The entire island is a National Historic Landmark District, noted for its unpainted grey-shingled houses, cobblestone Main Street, conservation land, and landmarks such as Brant Point Light. It is reached by ferry and by Nantucket Memorial Airport.
