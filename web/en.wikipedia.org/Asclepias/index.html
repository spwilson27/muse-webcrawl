Asclepias

Scientific classification
Kingdom:Plantae
Order:Gentianales
Family:Apocynaceae
Genus:Asclepias L.

Asclepias is a genus of herbaceous, perennial, flowering plants known as the milkweeds, named for their latex, a milky substance containing cardiac glycosides termed cardenolides, exuded where cells are damaged. Most species are toxic to humans and many other species, primarily due to the presence of cardenolides.

Common milkweed (Asclepias syriaca) in flower.

The genus contains over 200 species distributed widely across Africa, North America, and South America. Members of the genus are ecologically important as nectar sources and as the larval host plants of monarch butterflies (Danaus plexippus), whose caterpillars sequester the plant's toxins for their own defence.

Flowers and pollination

Milkweed flowers are highly specialised: the pollen is packaged into paired structures called pollinia, which attach to visiting insects. The distinctive crown-like corona and hooded petals give many species an ornate appearance. Seed pods split open to release seeds attached to silky floss, aiding wind dispersal.

Uses

Milkweed floss is buoyant and water-resistant and was used as a filling for life jackets during the Second World War. Common milkweed is increasingly planted in gardens and restoration projects to support monarch populations.
