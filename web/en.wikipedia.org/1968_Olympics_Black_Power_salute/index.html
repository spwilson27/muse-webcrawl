1968 Olympics Black Power salute

Date16 October 1968
VenueOlympic Stadium, Mexico City
ParticipantsTommie Smith, John Carlos, Peter Norman

The 1968 Olympics Black Power salute was a political demonstration conducted by African-American athletes Tommie Smith and John Carlos during their medal ceremony at the 1968 Summer Olympics in Mexico City on 16 October 1968.

The protest

After Smith won the 200-metre race in a world-record time and Carlos took bronze, the two men mounted the podium shoeless, wearing black socks and Olympic Project for Human Rights badges. As the United States national anthem played, each raised a black-gloved fist and kept it raised until the anthem finished. Australian silver medallist Peter Norman stood in solidarity, wearing a supporting badge.

Aftermath

The International Olympic Committee condemned the gesture, and Smith and Carlos were suspended from the US team and expelled from the Olympic Village. Both endured ostracism at home, but the image became one of the most iconic photographs of the 20th century, and the athletes were later honoured with statues and awards.
