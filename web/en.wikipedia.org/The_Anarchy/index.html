The Anarchy was a civil war in England and Normandy between 1138 and 1153, arising from a disputed succession to the throne of Henry I. The conflict between the supporters of King Stephen of Blois and those of his cousin the Empress Matilda produced a prolonged breakdown of royal authority.

Background

Henry I's only legitimate son, William Adelin, drowned in the White Ship disaster of 1120. Henry designated his daughter Matilda — widow of the Holy Roman Emperor Henry V and wife of Geoffrey of Anjou — as his heir and made his barons swear oaths to support her. On Henry's death in 1135, his nephew Stephen seized the crown instead, claiming the oaths had been coerced.

The war

Matilda landed in England in 1139. Stephen was captured at the Battle of Lincoln in 1141 and Matilda prepared for coronation in London, but was driven out by a hostile crowd; her forces were then defeated at Winchester and her half-brother Robert of Gloucester captured, forcing an exchange of prisoners. Matilda's dramatic escape from besieged Oxford Castle across the frozen Thames in December 1142 became the war's most famous episode. Geoffrey meanwhile conquered Normandy, and after 1148 Matilda left England, leaving the cause to her son Henry.

Settlement and aftermath

Exhausted by stalemate, the parties agreed the Treaty of Wallingford in 1153: Stephen would reign for life, and Henry would succeed him. Stephen died the following year and Henry II founded the Plantagenet dynasty, restoring royal government. The Anglo-Saxon Chronicle famously described the period as a time when "Christ and his saints slept", and the many unlicensed "adulterine" castles built during the war were demolished under Henry II.
