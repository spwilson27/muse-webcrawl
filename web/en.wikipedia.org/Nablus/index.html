Nablus (Arabic: نابلس) is a Palestinian city in the northern West Bank, lying in a valley between Mount Gerizim and Mount Ebal. It is one of the oldest cities in the region and a major commercial and cultural centre of the West Bank, home to An-Najah National University, one of Palestine's largest universities.

History

The city was founded by the Roman emperor Vespasian in AD 72 as Flavia Neapolis ("new city"), near the older site of biblical Shechem and Jacob's Well. It flourished under Byzantine, early Islamic, Crusader, Ayyubid, Mamluk, and Ottoman rule, becoming a centre of trade in olive oil, soap, and textiles. Earthquakes, notably in 1927, damaged much of the historic fabric. Nablus came under Israeli occupation in 1967 and Palestinian Authority administration in 1995 under the Oslo Accords.

Economy and culture

Nablus is famous for its traditional olive-oil soap, produced in historic soap factories in the old city, and for kunafa, the syrup-soaked cheese pastry associated with the city. The old city's souks, caravanserais, mosques, and Ottoman-era palaces form one of the largest historic urban centres in Palestine. A small Samaritan community lives on Mount Gerizim above the city.
