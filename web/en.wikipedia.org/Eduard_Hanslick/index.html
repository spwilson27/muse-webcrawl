Eduard Hanslick

Eduard Hanslick (11 September 1825 – 6 August 1904) was an influential Bohemian-Austrian music critic, aesthetician, and historian based in Vienna. He was a leading advocate of absolute music and a prominent champion of Johannes Brahms, and an equally prominent opponent of the music of Richard Wagner.

Life and career

Born in Prague, Hanslick studied law at Charles University and later the University of Vienna, where he took a doctorate in 1849. He largely taught himself music, studying piano with Tomášek. He joined the Austrian civil service while writing criticism, and in 1861 became the first professor of the history and aesthetics of music at the University of Vienna.

As chief music critic of the Neue Freie Presse, Hanslick wielded enormous influence over Viennese musical life. His friendship with Brahms and hostility to Wagner made him a central figure in the so-called "War of the Romantics." Wagner caricatured him in Die Meistersinger von Nürnberg as the pedantic critic Beckmesser (originally named "Hans Lick" in a draft).

On the Musically Beautiful

Hanslick's treatise Vom Musikalisch-Schönen (1854; On the Musically Beautiful) is a foundational text of musical aesthetics. Against the view that music's value lies in the feelings it expresses or arouses, Hanslick argued that the content of music is "forms moved in sound" (tönend bewegte Formen) and that musical beauty is inherent in those forms themselves. The book went through many editions and has shaped debates about musical meaning ever since.
