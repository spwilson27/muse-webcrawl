Hard news

FieldJournalism
ContrastSoft news (feature stories)
Typical topicsPolitics, crime, war, economics, disasters

Hard news is a term in journalism for timely, factual reporting on serious topics and events, such as politics, crime, war, disasters, and the economy. It is contrasted with soft news, which covers entertainment, lifestyle, human interest, and feature material.

Characteristics

Hard news stories are usually written in the inverted pyramid style, presenting the most important facts first, and are tied to a specific, recent event with clear public significance. They prioritise the "five Ws" — who, what, when, where, and why.

Role in media

Traditionally, hard news formed the core of newspaper front pages and broadcast bulletins. Studies of media economics suggest audiences often prefer soft news, and commercial pressures have pushed some outlets toward "infotainment". Scholars debate the democratic consequences, since hard news consumption is associated with political knowledge and civic engagement.

Related concepts

The distinction is one of degree rather than kind: many stories blend hard and soft elements, and the same event can be covered in either mode depending on framing, timing, and emphasis.
