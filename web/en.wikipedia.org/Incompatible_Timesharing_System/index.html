Incompatible Timesharing System

DeveloperMIT Artificial Intelligence Laboratory
Initial release1967
PlatformPDP-6, PDP-10
Written inAssembly language (MIDAS)

The Incompatible Timesharing System (ITS) is a time-sharing operating system developed principally by hackers at the MIT Artificial Intelligence Laboratory, first booted in 1967 for the Digital Equipment Corporation PDP-6 and later run on PDP-10 computers. Its name is a jibe at MIT's earlier Compatible Time-Sharing System (CTSS). ITS was a central incubator of hacker culture and of programs such as the original Emacs editor, the Macsyma computer algebra system, and early network software.

A PDP-10 of the type that ran ITS at the MIT AI Lab.

Features

ITS was written in assembly language and notable for its openness: it had no passwords, all files were publicly accessible, and any user could observe or even interfere with others' sessions — a deliberate embodiment of the hacker ethic. Distinctive features included device-independent I/O, a powerful debugger (DDT) that doubled as the system shell, and the ability to link to files on remote machines over the ARPANET; ITS systems were famously welcoming to network tourists.

Legacy

ITS hosted the first version of Emacs, the Zork predecessor MDL-based games, and much early work in artificial intelligence. The last original ITS machines at MIT were shut down in 1990. Modern enthusiasts have restored ITS to run on PDP-10 emulators, preserving its software library.
