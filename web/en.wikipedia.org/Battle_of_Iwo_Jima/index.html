The Battle of Iwo Jima (19 February – 26 March 1945) was a major battle of World War II in which the United States Marine Corps and Navy landed on and captured the island of Iwo Jima from the Imperial Japanese Army. The American invasion, designated Operation Detachment, aimed to seize the island's three airfields, including Motoyama, to provide a staging area for attacks on the Japanese main islands and a base for fighter escorts and emergency landings for B-29 bombers raiding Japan.

Background

The Japanese commander, General Tadamichi Kuribayashi, knowing he could not win, prepared an elaborate defence in depth: some 18 km of tunnels and thousands of caves, pillboxes, and bunkers, with orders that each soldier should kill ten Americans before dying. About 21,000 Japanese troops garrisoned the island. Three Marine divisions (3rd, 4th, and 5th) of the V Amphibious Corps, roughly 70,000 men, were assigned to the assault after months of air and naval bombardment.

Battle

The Marines landed on 19 February against initially light opposition on the black volcanic beaches, but as they moved inland the Japanese opened fire from concealed positions. On 23 February men of the 5th Marine Division raised the U.S. flag atop Mount Suribachi at the island's southern tip — the second, larger flag raising that day was photographed by Joe Rosenthal in what became one of the most famous images of the war. Fighting then ground northward through strongpoints such as the Motoyama plateau, "the Meat Grinder," and Hill 362, often using flamethrowers, demolition charges, and tanks. The island was declared secure on 26 March 1945.

Casualties and legacy

The battle was one of the bloodiest in Marine Corps history: 6,821 Americans were killed and about 19,000 wounded, while nearly the entire Japanese garrison died — only about 1,000 were taken prisoner. Twenty-seven Medals of Honor were awarded, the most for any single U.S. battle. Admiral Chester Nimitz wrote that among the Americans who served on Iwo Jima, "uncommon valor was a common virtue." The island's airfields were in use before the battle ended; Iwo Jima was returned to Japan in 1968. In 1985 the island was officially renamed Iōtō, its pre-war name. Clint Eastwood's paired films Flags of Our Fathers and Letters from Iwo Jima (2006) depict the battle from American and Japanese perspectives.
