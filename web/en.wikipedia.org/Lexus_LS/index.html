The Lexus LS is the flagship sedan that launched the Lexus brand in 1989.

The Lexus LS (Japanese: レクサス・LS) is a full-size luxury sedan that serves as the flagship model of Lexus, the luxury division of Toyota. It has been produced across five generations, all featuring V8 engines (until the fifth generation) and rear-wheel drive, with hybrid and all-wheel-drive variants. In Japan, it was sold as the Toyota Celsior until 2006.

Development and launch

The LS originated in Toyota's secret "Flagship One" (F1) project, launched in 1983 by chairman Eiji Toyoda to build the world's best luxury car. Some 60 designers, 1,400 engineers, and thousands of prototypes were involved over six years, at a cost of more than US$1 billion. The first LS 400 debuted at the North American International Auto Show in January 1989 and went on sale that September, launching the Lexus brand in the United States. Its combination of quietness, refinement, build quality, and a price well below German rivals shocked the industry; the LS 400's 4.0-litre V8 produced 250 hp with exceptionally low noise and vibration.

Generations

The second generation (1994) refined the original formula; the third-generation LS 430 (2000) added a 4.3-litre V8 and advanced features such as adaptive cruise control. The fourth-generation LS 460 (2006) introduced an eight-speed automatic transmission and the LS 600h hybrid, the first production V8 hybrid. The fifth generation (2017) switched to the GA-L platform, a twin-turbocharged V6, and a more expressive design with a coupe-like silhouette.

Significance

The LS repeatedly won quality and dependability awards and forced established European marques to improve their own flagship models. The name "LS" stands for "Luxury Sedan."
