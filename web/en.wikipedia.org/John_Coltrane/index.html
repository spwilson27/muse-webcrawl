John Coltrane

Born: 23 September 1926, Hamlet, North Carolina, U.S.
Died: 17 July 1967 (aged 40), Huntington, New York, U.S.
Genres: Hard bop, modal jazz, avant-garde jazz, free jazz
Instruments: Tenor saxophone, soprano saxophone, alto saxophone, flute
Years active: 1945–1967

John William Coltrane (September 23, 1926 – July 17, 1967) was an American jazz saxophonist, bandleader and composer. He is among the most influential and acclaimed figures in the history of jazz and 20th-century music.

Early career

Born in Hamlet, North Carolina, Coltrane moved to Philadelphia after high school and served in a U.S. Navy band in Hawaii. After the war he worked with Dizzy Gillespie, Earl Bostic and Johnny Hodges, developing his sound while struggling with heroin addiction.

Miles Davis and Monk

Coltrane joined Miles Davis's first great quintet in 1955, recording landmark albums such as 'Round About Midnight and later Kind of Blue (1959). In 1957 he worked with Thelonious Monk, an apprenticeship he credited with transforming his harmonic approach, and recorded his first album as leader, Blue Train, for Blue Note.

Classic Quartet and late period

With McCoy Tyner, Jimmy Garrison and Elvin Jones, Coltrane's classic quartet produced My Favorite Things (1961) and A Love Supreme (1964), a four-part suite expressing his spiritual awakening after overcoming addiction in 1957. His later work, including Ascension (1965), moved into free jazz. Coltrane died of liver cancer in 1967 at age 40.

Legacy

Coltrane's influence extends across jazz and popular music. He was canonised by the African Orthodox Church as Saint John William Coltrane, and received a posthumous Pulitzer Prize special citation in 2007.
