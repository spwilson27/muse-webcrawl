Maturidi

Maturidism (Arabic: الماتريدية) is one of the main schools of Sunni Islamic theology, founded by Abu Mansur al-Maturidi (d. 944 CE) of Samarkand. Alongside Ash'arism and Atharism, it is one of the three recognised orthodox creeds of Sunni Islam, and it is the predominant theological school among followers of the Hanafi school of law, including most Muslims of Turkey, the Balkans, Central Asia, and South Asia.

Doctrine

Maturidism occupies a middle position between the Mu'tazilite emphasis on reason and the traditionalist reliance on revelation alone. It holds that humans can know of God's existence and distinguish good from evil through reason, even before revelation, though revelation remains necessary for the details of worship and law. On faith (iman), Maturidis generally define it as assent of the heart, confession by the tongue, and — in the view of many — acts; unlike the Murji'ah they debated whether faith increases and decreases. The school affirms God's attributes as real but without likening them to created things, and allows metaphorical interpretation (ta'wil) of ambiguous scriptural expressions.

Al-Maturidi and his works

Imam Abu Mansur Muhammad ibn Muhammad al-Maturidi was born in Maturid, near Samarkand, in Transoxiana. His chief work, Kitab al-Tawhid ("The Book of Monotheism"), systematised Hanafi theology, and his Qur'an commentary Ta'wilat Ahl al-Sunnah remains influential.

Influence

The Ottoman Empire and the Mughal Empire both patronised Hanafi-Maturidi scholarship, spreading the school from Anatolia to India. In 2016 the Grozny conference on Sunni Islam listed Maturidis among the adherents of orthodox Sunnism.
