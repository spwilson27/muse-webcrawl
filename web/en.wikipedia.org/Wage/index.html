Wage

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A wage is a payment made by an employer to an employee in exchange for work done over a period of time. Wages are typically calculated by the hour, day, or piece and paid weekly or biweekly, distinguishing them from a salary, which is a fixed periodic amount. Along with rent, interest, and profit, wages are one of the classic categories of income in economics.

Determination

In standard economic theory, wages are set where the supply of and demand for labour meet, with a worker's wage tending toward their marginal productivity. Alternative accounts emphasise bargaining power, institutions, and monopsony: minimum-wage laws, collective bargaining by trade unions, and wage boards all modify market outcomes. Efficiency-wage theory notes that paying more can raise productivity, effort, and retention.

Forms and regulation

Compensation may include piece rates, overtime premiums, tips, bonuses, and benefits such as pensions and health insurance. Most countries regulate wages through minimum-wage statutes, working-time rules, and anti-discrimination law; measures such as the real wage (adjusted for prices) and the gender pay gap are widely used indicators. Historically, payment in kind or in company scrip (the truck system) was common and was restricted by legislation in many countries.

History

Wage labour existed in the ancient world but became the dominant form of work only with industrialisation, as workers separated from ownership of land and tools sold their labour power. The growth of factory employment generated the labour movement and modern employment law.
