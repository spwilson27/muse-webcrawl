Space weapon

Artist's concept of a space-based interceptor

Space weapons are weapons used in space warfare. They include weapons that can attack space systems in orbit (for example, anti-satellite weapons), attack targets on the earth from space, or disable missiles travelling through space. In the course of the militarisation of outer space, such weapons were developed mainly by the contesting superpowers during the Cold War, and some remain in development today.

Types

Space weapons are commonly grouped into earth-to-space weapons, such as direct-ascent anti-satellite missiles and ground-based lasers; space-to-space weapons, such as co-orbital interceptor satellites; and space-to-earth weapons, a largely conceptual category including orbital bombardment systems. The Soviet Almaz military stations carried a cannon, test-fired in 1974–75, the only known live firing of a weapon in orbit. The Outer Space Treaty prohibits placing weapons of mass destruction in orbit or stationing them in outer space, but does not ban conventional weapons.

History

Development intensified in the 1960s–1980s: the United States tested the ASM-135 anti-satellite missile in 1985, destroying the Solwind satellite, while the Soviet Union fielded the Istrebitel Sputnikov co-orbital system. The US Strategic Defense Initiative of 1983 proposed space-based missile defences, earning the nickname "Star Wars". More recent anti-satellite tests by China (2007), the United States (2008), India (2019) and Russia (2021) renewed concern about debris and the weaponisation of space.
