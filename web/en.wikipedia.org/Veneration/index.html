Veneration

Veneration (Latin: veneratio; Greek: προσκύνησις, proskynesis) is the act of showing great respect or reverence toward a person, object, or symbol regarded as holy. In Catholic, Orthodox, and some other Christian traditions, veneration is directed to saints, icons, and relics, and is distinguished from worship (latria), which is due to God alone.

Theology

The Second Council of Nicaea (787) affirmed the veneration of icons, teaching that the honour shown to an image passes to its prototype. Catholic theology distinguishes latria (worship of God), dulia (veneration of saints), and hyperdulia (special veneration of the Virgin Mary). Relics are venerated in classes: first-class (parts of a saint's body), second-class (items the saint owned), and third-class (objects touched to first-class relics).

Other traditions

Analogous practices exist in many religions: ancestor veneration in Chinese and African traditional religions, veneration of teachers and sacred objects in Buddhism and Hinduism, and respect for relics of the Prophet's companions in parts of Islam. Protestant traditions generally rejected the veneration of saints and relics during the Reformation, regarding it as idolatry.
