Yellowjacket
GeneraVespula, Dolichovespula
FamilyVespidae
DistributionNorthern Hemisphere; introduced widely

Yellowjacket or yellow jacket is the common name in North America for predatory social wasps of the genera Vespula and Dolichovespula. Members of these genera are known simply as "wasps" in other English-speaking countries. Most are black with yellow facial and abdominal markings and, like other vespines, build nests of a paper-like material made from chewed wood fibres.

Behaviour

Yellowjackets are social insects living in colonies of hundreds to thousands of workers ruled by a single queen. Nests may be underground, in wall voids, or, in the case of Dolichovespula, aerial. Workers forage for insects to feed the larvae and for carbohydrates such as nectar, fruit, and human food and drinks, which makes them a nuisance at outdoor meals in late summer. They can sting repeatedly, and colonies defend their nests aggressively.

Notable species

The German yellowjacket (Vespula germanica) and the common wasp (Vespula vulgaris), both native to Europe, have become invasive in North and South America, Australia, and New Zealand, where huge perennial colonies sometimes form. The eastern yellowjacket (Vespula maculifrons) is a common native North American species.
