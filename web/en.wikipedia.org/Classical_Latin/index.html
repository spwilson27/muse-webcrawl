Classical Latin is the form of the Latin language recognized as a standard by writers of the late Roman Republic and the Roman Empire. It was developed from Old Latin under the influence of Greek models and the self-conscious stylistic program of authors such as Cicero and Caesar, and it remained the model for literary and learned Latin for two millennia. In some later periods the term also includes the "Golden Age" prose and poetry of the 1st century BC and the "Silver Age" literature that followed.

Characteristics

Classical Latin is a highly inflected language. Nouns, adjectives, and pronouns are declined in five declensions across six cases (nominative, genitive, dative, accusative, ablative, vocative); verbs are conjugated in four conjugations across tense, mood, voice, person, and number. Word order is flexible — typically subject–object–verb — because grammatical relations are marked by inflection rather than position. The standard written language differed considerably from the spoken Latin of ordinary people, known as Vulgar Latin, from which the Romance languages descend.

The classical canon — Cicero, Caesar, Sallust, and Livy in prose; Lucretius, Catullus, Virgil, Horace, and Ovid in verse — fixed the vocabulary and syntax that later grammarians codified. Pronunciation has been reconstructed from meter, puns, inscriptions, and the testimony of Roman grammarians: notably, c was always hard /k/, v was pronounced /w/, and vowel length was phonemic.

Later history

After the classical period, written Latin evolved into Late Latin and then the Medieval Latin of the Church and scholarship, while Classical Latin itself was preserved as a standard and revived by Renaissance humanists such as Erasmus. It remains the language of the Roman Catholic Church's formal documents, of scientific binomial nomenclature, and of law, medicine, and academia's technical vocabularies. Roman type inscriptions in all capitals, with U written V, reflect the classical alphabet of 23 letters, which lacked J, U, and W as separate letters.
