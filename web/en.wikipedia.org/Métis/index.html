Métis

Regions: Canada (Prairies) and northern United States
Languages: English, French, Michif
Status: Recognised Indigenous people in Canada

The Métis are an Indigenous people in Canada and parts of the United States, descended primarily from unions between First Nations women and European (mainly French) fur traders, who developed a distinct culture, language and nation in the historic Northwest.

History

Centred on the Red River Settlement, the Métis resisted Canadian expansion in the Red River Resistance of 1869–70 led by Louis Riel, leading to the creation of Manitoba, and again in the North-West Resistance of 1885. Their language, Michif, combines French nouns with Cree verbs.

Recognition

The Métis are one of three recognised Aboriginal peoples under section 35 of Canada's Constitution Act, 1982. The infinity symbol on a blue or red field is their national flag.
