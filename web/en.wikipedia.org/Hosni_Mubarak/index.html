Muhammad Hosni El Sayed Mubarak (4 May 1928 – 25 February 2020) was an Egyptian politician and military officer who served as the fourth president of Egypt from 1981 to 2011. Before entering politics, Mubarak was a career officer in the Egyptian Air Force, rising to become its commander in 1972 and leading it during the Yom Kippur War of 1973.

Presidency

Mubarak was appointed vice president by Anwar Sadat in 1975 and became president following Sadat's assassination in October 1981. His nearly three-decade rule was marked by political stability, a close relationship with the United States, maintenance of the peace treaty with Israel, and economic liberalisation, but also by corruption, police brutality, political repression, and rule under a near-continuous state of emergency.

Resignation and trials

Mass protests during the Egyptian revolution of 2011 forced Mubarak to resign on 11 February 2011. He was subsequently tried and convicted of complicity in the killing of protesters, though his conviction was later overturned and he was released in 2017 after being acquitted on retrial. He died in Cairo in February 2020 and was given a military funeral.
