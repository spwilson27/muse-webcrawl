1seg (Japanese: ワンセグ, wansegu) is a mobile terrestrial digital audio/video and data broadcasting service in Japan. The service began on 1 April 2006 and is designed for reception on mobile devices such as mobile phones, car navigation systems, and portable game consoles.

The name derives from the fact that the service uses one of the thirteen segments of the bandwidth allocated to each ISDB-T television channel. Twelve segments are used for high-definition or standard-definition television for fixed receivers, while the remaining centre segment is used for the mobile service.

Technical details

1seg broadcasts use the H.264/AVC video codec at a resolution of 320×240 pixels (QVGA) and the AAC audio codec. The data rate is roughly 416 kbit/s in total. Because the signal occupies only a narrow part of the channel, receivers can be small, inexpensive, and economical with battery power, although picture quality is limited compared with full television broadcasts.

Reception and devices

Many Japanese mobile phones sold from the late 2000s onward included a built-in 1seg tuner, as did devices such as the Nintendo DS (with an accessory tuner) and various portable media players. The service carries the same programme schedule as the associated terrestrial channel, and some broadcasts include data services and emergency warning information.

Related services

A successor service, NOTTV, operated by mmbi, launched in 2012 using part of the frequency band vacated by analogue television, but it closed in 2016. Similar one-segment mobile services based on ISDB-T have been adopted in Brazil and other countries using the ISDB-Tb standard.
