Modern Indian painting

Modern Indian painting emerged in the late 19th and early 20th centuries as Indian artists negotiated between colonial academic art training, indigenous miniature and folk traditions, and the modernist movements of Europe. Its history is conventionally traced from the Company painting of the colonial period through the Bengal School to the assertive modernism of post-Independence India.

Bengal School

Under Abanindranath Tagore, with the support of the British art administrator E. B. Havell, the Bengal School rejected the oil-painting naturalism of Raja Ravi Varma in favour of wash techniques, Mughal and Rajput miniature models, and pan-Asian influences. Artists such as Nandalal Bose, who led the art school at Santiniketan, linked painting to the nationalist Swadeshi movement and to Indian craft traditions.

Toward modernism

Artists including Jamini Roy, who turned to Bengali folk idioms; Amrita Sher-Gil, who fused Parisian modernism with Indian subjects; Rabindranath Tagore, with his expressive ink works; and George Keyt in Ceylon, broadened the field between the wars. After Independence in 1947, the Bombay Progressive Artists' Group — including F. N. Souza, M. F. Husain, S. H. Raza, and K. H. Ara — championed an international modernism rooted in Indian experience.

Later developments

Subsequent generations — the Madras Art Movement around K. C. S. Paniker, the Baroda school's narrative painters such as Bhupen Khakhar, and contemporary artists working in installation and new media — diversified Indian art further. Since the 1990s, Indian modern and contemporary art has achieved major international recognition and record auction prices, with Husain and Raza among the most celebrated names.
