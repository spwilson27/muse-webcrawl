Catastrophe

A catastrophe is a sudden event causing widespread destruction, suffering or upheaval. The word derives from the Greek katastrophē, meaning an overturning or sudden turn, originally used in drama for the concluding turn of a plot. In modern usage it overlaps with disaster, encompassing natural events such as earthquakes, floods and volcanic eruptions as well as human-caused events such as wars, industrial accidents and financial collapses.

Usage and study

Governments and insurers classify catastrophes by their toll in lives and economic losses; reinsurance markets define catastrophe thresholds for events such as hurricanes and earthquakes. In mathematics, catastrophe theory studies how smooth changes in parameters can produce sudden discontinuous changes in a system's behaviour. In geology, catastrophism is the view that Earth's features were shaped by sudden violent events, contrasted historically with uniformitarianism.
