Brookings Institution

Brookings headquarters in Washington, D.C.

The Brookings Institution, commonly known as Brookings, is an American public policy organisation ("think tank") based in Washington, D.C. Founded in 1916, it conducts research in economics, foreign policy, governance, global development, and metropolitan policy, and is consistently ranked among the most influential think tanks in the world. Its stated mission is to conduct "in-depth, nonpartisan research" to improve policy and governance.

History

Brookings traces its origins to the Institute for Government Research (1916), established with support from St Louis philanthropist Robert S. Brookings, which merged with other bodies to form the Brookings Institution in 1927. Economists at Brookings contributed to debates over the New Deal (often critically), postwar monetary policy, and the design of the federal budget process; the institution helped develop the modern congressional budget framework and has advised administrations of both parties. It was the first private organisation devoted to analysing public policy at the national level.

Organisation and research

Brookings houses research programmes in Economic Studies, Foreign Policy, Governance Studies, Global Economy and Development, and Brookings Metro. Centres abroad have included Brookings Doha Center (2008–2021) and Brookings India (now the Centre for Social and Economic Progress, independent since 2020). The Brookings Institution Press publishes scholarly books and journals such as the Brookings Papers on Economic Activity. Recent presidents include Strobe Talbott (2002–2017), John R. Allen (2017–2022), and Amy Liu (interim, from 2022), followed by Cecilia Rouse, who became president in 2024.

Funding and influence

Brookings is funded by foundations, corporations, governments, and individuals, and publishes an annual list of donors. Its fellows frequently serve in government — the so-called "revolving door" — and its reports are widely cited in media and congressional testimony.
