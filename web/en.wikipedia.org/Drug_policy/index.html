Drug policy

Drug policy is the set of laws and government programs that regulate the production, distribution, sale, possession, and use of psychoactive drugs. Policies range from prohibition and criminal enforcement to regulation, decriminalization, and harm-reduction approaches, and are shaped internationally by treaties such as the 1961 Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs.

Approaches

Prohibition criminalizes some or all stages of the drug trade, exemplified by the "war on drugs" declared by U.S. President Richard Nixon in 1971. Decriminalization removes criminal penalties for personal possession while keeping supply illegal, as in Portugal's 2001 reform. Legalization and regulation bring production and sale under state rules, as several jurisdictions have done for cannabis. Harm reduction seeks to minimize health damage without necessarily reducing use, through needle exchanges, supervised consumption sites, naloxone distribution, and drug checking.

Debates

Supporters of liberalization argue prohibition fuels organized crime, mass incarceration, and unsafe products, while defenders of strict controls argue they limit availability and use. Public-health researchers often evaluate policies by their effects on overdose deaths, disease transmission, and drug use prevalence, and many countries have shifted emphasis toward treatment over time.
