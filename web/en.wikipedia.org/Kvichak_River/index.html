The Kvichak River (pronounced Kwee-jack) is a river in southwestern Alaska, flowing roughly 50 miles (80 km) southwest from Lake Iliamna, the largest lake in Alaska, to Kvichak Bay on Bristol Bay. It drains the Iliamna basin into the Bering Sea watershed and passes through or near the communities of Igiugig, at the lake outlet, and Levelock, near its mouth.

Salmon runs

The Kvichak is famous for its sockeye salmon runs, historically among the largest in the world. The river forms a key part of the Bristol Bay salmon fishery, the most valuable wild salmon fishery on Earth. Kvichak sockeye runs have fluctuated dramatically over the decades, from tens of millions of fish in strong years to much weaker returns in others, and the stock has at times been listed as a stock of concern by Alaskan fisheries managers. The river also supports rainbow trout, Arctic grayling and other species prized by sport anglers.

Names and history

The name is of Alaska Native origin and was recorded by Russian explorers in the 19th century in forms such as "Kvichek" or "Kuitchek". The river corridor has been used for centuries by Yup'ik and Dena'ina peoples for fishing camps and travel between the lake and the bay.

Conservation issues

The Kvichak watershed attracted wider attention during debates over the proposed Pebble Mine, a large copper-gold project in the headwaters region of Bristol Bay tributaries, which fishing groups and many local communities opposed over risks to salmon habitat.
