Japanese battleship Yamato

Yamato (大和)
Class: Yamato-class battleship
Laid down: 4 November 1937
Launched: 8 August 1940
Commissioned: 16 December 1941
Fate: Sunk by U.S. aircraft, 7 April 1945
Displacement: ~71,659 tonnes (full load)
Main armament: 9 × 46 cm (18.1 in) guns

Yamato (Japanese: 大和, named after the ancient Japanese Yamato Province) was the lead ship of her class of battleships built for the Imperial Japanese Navy (IJN) shortly before World War II. She and her sister ship Musashi were the heaviest and most powerfully armed battleships ever constructed, displacing nearly 72,000 tonnes at full load and armed with nine 46 cm (18.1 inch) Type 94 main guns — the largest caliber naval artillery ever mounted on a combat vessel.

Design and construction

Designed in secrecy to outmatch the numerically superior United States Navy, Yamato was laid down at the Kure Naval Arsenal in 1937 and commissioned a week after the attack on Pearl Harbor in December 1941. Her armor scheme was built around protection against 18-inch shells, and her hull form and machinery gave her a speed of 27 knots.

Service history

Yamato served as the flagship of Admiral Isoroku Yamamoto during the Battle of Midway in June 1942, though she did not engage the enemy directly. As the war turned against Japan and air power proved dominant, the great battleship saw little of the decisive surface battle for which she had been built. She was torpedoed by the submarine USS Skate in December 1943 and later took part in the Battle of Leyte Gulf in October 1944, where she fired her main guns at enemy ships for the only time.

Sinking

On 6 April 1945, Yamato sailed on Operation Ten-Go, a one-way mission to defend Okinawa with no air cover and insufficient fuel for a return. U.S. carrier aircraft intercepted her the following day and subjected her to waves of bombing and torpedo attacks. After taking numerous hits she capsized and exploded on 7 April 1945, sinking with the loss of some 2,500 of her crew of about 3,330 men. The wreck was located in 1985 beneath about 340 metres of water in the East China Sea.

Legacy

Yamato remains a powerful cultural symbol in Japan, remembered both as the pinnacle of battleship engineering and as an emblem of the futility of the war's final phase. A museum in Kure houses a 1:10 scale model of the ship.
