Armistice of Mudros

The Armistice of Mudros (Turkish: Mondros Mütarekesi) was an armistice signed on 30 October 1918 between the Ottoman Empire and the Allies of the First World War, ending hostilities in the Middle Eastern theatre. It was signed aboard HMS Agamemnon in Mudros harbour on the island of Lemnos by the Ottoman Minister of Marine Affairs, Rauf Bey (Rauf Orbay), and the British Admiral Sir Somerset Gough-Calthorpe.

Signed30 October 1918
LocationMudros, Lemnos, Greece
PartiesOttoman Empire; the Allies
Effective31 October 1918, noon

Terms

The armistice required the demobilization of the Ottoman army, the release of Allied prisoners, the opening of the Dardanelles and Bosphorus straits and Allied occupation of their forts, the surrender of Ottoman garrisons in Arabia, Mesopotamia, Syria and elsewhere, and Allied control of railways and telegraphs. Crucially, Clause 7 allowed the Allies to occupy any strategic points if their security were threatened, and Clause 24 permitted occupation of the six Armenian-populated eastern vilayets in case of disorder — provisions later used to justify occupations across Anatolia.

Consequences

The armistice began the Allied occupation and partition of the Ottoman Empire: Constantinople came under Allied occupation from November 1918, and French, British, Italian and (from May 1919) Greek forces landed in Anatolia. The perceived humiliation of the terms fuelled Turkish resistance under Mustafa Kemal, leading to the Turkish War of Independence, the Treaty of Lausanne (1923), and the proclamation of the Republic of Turkey.
