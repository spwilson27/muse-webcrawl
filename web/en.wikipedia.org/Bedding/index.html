Bedding

Bedding, also called bedclothes or bed linen, is the materials laid above the mattress of a bed for warmth, comfort, hygiene and decoration. A typical set includes a bottom (fitted or flat) sheet, a top sheet, blankets or a duvet/comforter with cover, and pillowcases, sometimes supplemented by bedspreads, quilts, throws and mattress protectors.

Components

Sheets are usually made from cotton, linen, polyester or blends, and are described by weave (percale, sateen, flannel) and thread count. Duvets and comforters are filled with down, feathers, wool, cotton or synthetic fibres, with warmth ratings such as the tog system used in Britain. Blankets may be woven wool, fleece or knitted. Pillowcases match the bedding set and cover pillows of various shapes.

Sizes

Bedding is made in sizes matching standard mattresses — single, double, queen, king and super king — though dimensions differ between countries; North American "queen" and "king" differ from their British counterparts. Hotels typically use white bedding for ease of laundering and inspection.

Care

Bedding is laundered regularly to remove sweat, skin cells and allergens; dust mites thrive in warm bedding, and wash temperatures of 60 °C are often recommended for allergy control. The word "bedding" can also refer to straw or similar material used as animal litter.
