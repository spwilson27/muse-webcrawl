Roscosmos

The State Corporation for Space Activities "Roscosmos" (Russian: Государственная корпорация по космической деятельности «Роскосмос») is a state corporation of the Russian Federation responsible for space flights, cosmonautics programs, and aerospace research.

History

Roscosmos was formed after the dissolution of the Soviet Union, inheriting the bulk of the Soviet space program, including the Soyuz and Progress spacecraft, the Mir space station (later deorbited in 2001), and the Baikonur Cosmodrome, which Russia leases from Kazakhstan. In 2015 the agency was abolished as a government body and reconstituted as a state corporation, absorbing most of the Russian space industry.

Programs

Roscosmos operates the Soyuz crewed spacecraft and Progress cargo vehicles, which since the retirement of the U.S. Space Shuttle in 2011 until 2020 provided the only means of crew transport to the International Space Station. It manages Russia's segment of the ISS, the GLONASS global navigation satellite system, and launch vehicles including the Soyuz-2 and Angara families from the Plesetsk and Vostochny cosmodromes.

Recent developments

The agency's international cooperation contracted sharply following Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine. Its Luna 25 lunar lander crashed into the Moon in August 2023. Russia announced plans for a new national station, the Russian Orbital Service Station, while continuing ISS participation through the late 2020s.
