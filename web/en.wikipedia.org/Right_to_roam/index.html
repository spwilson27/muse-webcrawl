Right to roam

The right to roam, also known as freedom to roam, is the general public's right to access certain public or privately owned land, lakes and rivers for recreation and exercise. The right is sometimes called the right of public access to the wilderness or the "right of access".

In Scotland, Finland, Iceland, Norway, Sweden, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, Austria, the Czech Republic and Switzerland, the freedom to roam takes the form of general public rights which are sometimes codified in law. In some Northern European countries the traditional right, known in Scandinavia as allemansrätten ("everyman's right"), has existed since ancient times.

England and Wales

In England and Wales, the Countryside and Rights of Way Act 2000 established a limited statutory right of access on foot to mapped areas of mountain, moor, heath, down and registered common land. The Dartmoor Commons Act 1985 had earlier given access rights over Dartmoor's commons. In 2023 the UK Supreme Court upheld a right to wild camp on parts of Dartmoor.

Scotland

The Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2003 formalised a universal right of access for recreation, education and passage over most land and inland water, subject to responsible behaviour governed by the Scottish Outdoor Access Code.
