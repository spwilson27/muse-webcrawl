Vagn Walfrid Ekman

Vagn Walfrid Ekman (1874–1954).

Vagn Walfrid Ekman (3 May 1874 – 9 March 1954) was a Swedish oceanographer, one of the founders of modern physical oceanography. Born in Stockholm, the son of oceanographer Fredrik Laurentz Ekman, he studied at Uppsala University and worked with Vilhelm Bjerknes.

The Ekman spiral

Ekman's most famous contribution arose from observations made during Fridtjof Nansen's Fram expedition (1893–1896), which found that Arctic ice drifted 20–40 degrees to the right of the wind direction. Ekman showed theoretically in 1902 and published fully in 1905 that this results from a balance between wind friction and the Coriolis effect: surface water moves at 45 degrees to the wind, and each deeper layer moves more slowly and further rotated, forming the Ekman spiral. The net transport of the Ekman layer is 90 degrees to the wind — to the right in the Northern Hemisphere — a result fundamental to understanding wind-driven ocean circulation, upwelling, and coastal currents.

Later work

Ekman also developed instruments including a reversing current meter and contributed to the theory of the "dead water" phenomenon that had hindered the Fram. He became professor of mechanics and mathematical physics at Lund University in 1910 and was elected to the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences. The Ekman number, Ekman layer, and Ekman transport are named for him.
