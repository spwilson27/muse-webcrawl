Protocol (diplomacy)

Diplomatic protocol is the set of rules, customs, and forms of etiquette governing relations between states and their representatives.

In international politics, protocol is the etiquette of diplomacy and affairs of state. It is a set of international courtesy rules that regulate the order of precedence, forms of address, ceremonial, and the conduct of official correspondence and meetings between heads of state, heads of government, and diplomats. Protocol rules are not mere formalities: they rest on the principle of the sovereign equality of states, and breaches can cause serious diplomatic incidents.

History

The modern system of diplomatic protocol developed in Europe during the early modern period as resident embassies became permanent. Precedence disputes were once capable of provoking violence; the Congress of Vienna in 1815 established the first broadly accepted rules on the classification of diplomatic representatives and their precedence. These arrangements were later codified in the Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations of 1961 and the Vienna Convention on Consular Relations of 1963.

Functions

State protocol offices, such as the Office of the Chief of Protocol in the United States Department of State, organise state visits, credential ceremonies, and international conferences. Protocol governs such matters as the order in which ambassadors are received, seating at state banquets, the exchange of gifts, and the use of national flags and anthems.

Order of precedence

Precedence among diplomats is generally determined by the date on which they presented their letters of credence to the receiving head of state. The senior ambassador, or doyen, is the longest-serving ambassador in a given capital. Among heads of state, precedence in multilateral settings is often determined alphabetically by country name or by length of tenure.
