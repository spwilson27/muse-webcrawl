Johns Hopkins Hospital

The Johns Hopkins Hospital (JHH) is the teaching hospital and biomedical research facility of Johns Hopkins School of Medicine, located in Baltimore, Maryland. Founded in 1889 from the bequest of philanthropist Johns Hopkins, it is widely regarded as one of the world's leading hospitals and played a central role in the development of modern American medicine.

History

Banker and philanthropist Johns Hopkins left $7 million in his will of 1873 — then the largest philanthropic gift in American history — to found a university and hospital. The hospital opened on May 7, 1889, and the School of Medicine followed in 1893, notable for admitting women on equal terms and for requiring a college degree for admission.

Innovations

Founding physicians William Osler, William Stewart Halsted, Howard Kelly and William Welch established the residency training system, the use of rubber gloves in surgery, and many other practices. The hospital was a pioneer in cardiac surgery (the Blalock–Thomas–Taussig shunt, 1944), neurosurgery, and the first sex-reassignment surgeries in the United States.

Today

The hospital is the flagship of Johns Hopkins Medicine and consistently ranks among the top hospitals in the United States. Its campus in East Baltimore includes the Sheikh Zayed Tower and the Charlotte R. Bloomberg Children's Center, opened in 2012.
