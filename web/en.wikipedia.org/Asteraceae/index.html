Asteraceae


The head (capitulum) of a sunflower is composed of many small florets.

Asteraceae is a large family of flowering plants, commonly known as the aster, daisy, composite or sunflower family. With more than 32,000 accepted species in over 1,900 genera, it is one of the two largest families of flowering plants, rivalling the orchids. Members occur on every continent except Antarctica.

Characteristics

The defining feature of the family is the capitulum: a dense head of many small flowers (florets) surrounded by an involucre of bracts, which together mimic a single large flower. Florets may be tubular disc florets, strap-shaped ray florets, or both. The fruit is a cypsela, often crowned with a pappus of hairs or scales that aids wind dispersal, as in the dandelion.

Importance

The family includes major food crops such as sunflower, lettuce, chicory, artichoke and safflower, popular ornamentals such as chrysanthemums, dahlias, marigolds and zinnias, and widespread weeds such as dandelions and thistles. Many species are important for pollinators late in the season.

Classification

Asteraceae is placed in the order Asterales within the asterid clade of eudicots. Subfamilies include Asteroideae, Cichorioideae and Carduoideae among others.
