West Hollywood is a city in Los Angeles County, California, United States. Incorporated in 1984, it is home to the Sunset Strip. As of the 2020 census, its population was 35,757. The city is bordered by Beverly Hills to the west and the city of Los Angeles — the districts of Hollywood, Hollywood Hills, and Fairfax — to the north, east and south.

History

The area was historically part of the Rancho La Brea and was later developed around the Pacific Electric Railway yards; the settlement that grew up there was known as Sherman, after railway executive Moses Sherman. In the early twentieth century, the unincorporated community became known as West Hollywood. Because it lay outside the city of Los Angeles, the area attracted nightclubs, casinos and restaurants during Prohibition and became a centre of the film industry's nightlife along the Sunset Strip.

In 1984, a coalition of gay activists, senior citizens facing rent increases, and Russian Jewish immigrants successfully campaigned for incorporation as an independent city. West Hollywood adopted one of the strongest rent-control ordinances in the country and became known for progressive policies.

Demographics and culture

West Hollywood has a large gay population and is sometimes referred to as "Boys Town" or the "Gay Village"; roughly one-third or more of its residents identify as LGBT. The city hosts one of the largest Halloween Carnavals in the United States and a long-running Pride celebration. Landmarks include the Pacific Design Center, the Whisky a Go Go, the Troubadour, the Viper Room, the Chateau Marmont (just at the city boundary) and the Comedy Store.

The city is also home to a significant Russian-speaking community, one of the largest concentrations of Russian immigrants in the United States.

Government

West Hollywood is governed by a five-member city council elected at large, with the mayoralty rotating among its members. The city contracts with the Los Angeles County Sheriff's Department for policing.
