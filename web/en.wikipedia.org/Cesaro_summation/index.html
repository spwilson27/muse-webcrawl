Cesàro summation

In mathematical analysis, Cesàro summation (also known as the Cesàro mean) is a technique for assigning values to some infinite sums that are not necessarily convergent in the usual sense. Named after the Italian analyst Ernesto Cesàro (1859–1906), the method considers the arithmetic mean of the first n partial sums of a series and takes the limit of those means.

Definition

For a series Σ an with partial sums sk = a1 + … + ak, the Cesàro sum is the limit as n→∞ of (s1 + … + sn)/n, if it exists. Cesàro summation is linear and regular: any series convergent in the ordinary sense is Cesàro summable to the same value, but the converse fails.

Examples

Grandi's series 1 − 1 + 1 − 1 + ⋯ has partial sums 1, 0, 1, 0, …, whose averages tend to 1/2, so its Cesàro sum is 1/2. Cesàro summation is a fundamental tool in Fourier analysis: Fejér's theorem states that the Cesàro means of the partial sums of the Fourier series of a continuous function converge uniformly to the function.
