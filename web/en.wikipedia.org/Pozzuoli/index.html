Pozzuoli

Pozzuoli (Italian: [potˈtswɔːli]; Neapolitan: Pezzulo) is a city and comune of the Metropolitan City of Naples, in the Italian region of Campania. It is the main city of the Phlegraean Peninsula, west of Naples, and lies within the caldera of the Campi Flegrei volcanic area.

History

Pozzuoli began as the Greek colony of Dicaearchia ("city of good government"), founded by settlers from Samos around 530 BC. Under the Romans it became Puteoli, one of the great commercial ports of the empire and the principal harbour for trade with the eastern Mediterranean; grain ships from Alexandria docked here, and the Apostle Paul is recorded as landing at Puteoli on his journey to Rome. The town's volcanic pozzolana sand was the key ingredient in Roman marine concrete, prized because it set underwater.

Sights

Major remains include the Flavian Amphitheatre, the third largest Roman amphitheatre in Italy, capable of seating some 20,000 spectators; the Macellum, a market building long known as the Temple of Serapis, whose columns bear borings from marine molluscs recording past changes in ground level; and the ancient port area and Rione Terra acropolis. Nearby are the Solfatara crater, Lake Avernus and the Cumaean Sibyl's cave at Cumae.

Bradyseism

Pozzuoli is famous for bradyseism — the slow rise and fall of the ground caused by magmatic and hydrothermal activity in the Campi Flegrei caldera. Crises in 1969–1972 and 1982–1984 lifted the town by nearly two metres and prompted the evacuation of the old centre; renewed uplift and earthquake swarms since the 2000s, accelerating in 2023–2024, have kept the area under close monitoring.
