CIA black sites

In the intelligence community, a black site is a location at which a secret operation or project is conducted with official deniability. The term came to wide public attention for the secret prisons operated by the US Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) after the September 11 attacks, used to detain suspected terrorists outside the United States and outside normal legal processes.

The programme

Following 9/11, the CIA established detention facilities in countries including Afghanistan, Thailand, Poland, Romania and Lithuania, and at Guantánamo Bay, as part of its Rendition, Detention and Interrogation programme. Detainees were held incommunicado and subjected to so-called "enhanced interrogation techniques", which investigations later characterised as torture.

Exposure and aftermath

The existence of the sites was reported by The Washington Post in November 2005 and confirmed by President George W. Bush in September 2006. President Barack Obama ordered the CIA detention facilities closed in January 2009. The US Senate Intelligence Committee's 2014 report on CIA torture documented the programme in detail, and the European Court of Human Rights ruled against several host states for complicity in detainee abuse.
