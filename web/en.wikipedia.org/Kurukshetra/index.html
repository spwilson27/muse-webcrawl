Kurukshetra (Sanskrit: कुरुक्षेत्र, "the field of the Kurus") is a city and sacred region in the Indian state of Haryana. In Hindu tradition it is the land where the Kurukshetra War between the Kauravas and the Pandavas, narrated in the epic Mahabharata, was fought, and where the god Krishna delivered the Bhagavad Gita to the warrior Arjuna on the battlefield. Kurukshetra is one of the most important pilgrimage centres of Hinduism.

Mythology and sacred geography

According to tradition, the region was named after King Kuru, an ancestor of the Kauravas and Pandavas, who performed penance on the banks of the Sarasvati River and made the land holy. The area described in the texts is a region of 48 kos (about 140 km in circuit) dotted with sacred ponds (tirthas), temples and sites associated with the Mahabharata. The Vamana Purana describes Kurukshetra as the altar of Brahma, where creation was set in motion.

Main sites

The most important sacred tank is Brahma Sarovar, associated with the creation of the universe by Brahma, where pilgrims bathe especially during solar eclipses. The nearby Sannihit Sarovar is believed to be the meeting point of seven sacred rivers. Jyotisar is venerated as the spot where Krishna delivered the Gita, marked by a banyan tree said to be an offshoot of the original. Other sites include Bhishma Kund, associated with the fallen patriarch Bhishma, and the Sheikh Chilli's Tomb complex of the Mughal period.

Modern city

Modern Kurukshetra is a district headquarters and university town, home to Kurukshetra University (founded 1956) and the National Institute of Technology Kurukshetra. The city hosts the International Gita Mahotsav festival, and a panorama and science centre presents the Mahabharata war in dioramas. A large statue of Arjuna's chariot stands in the city. Kurukshetra is well connected by rail and road to Delhi and Chandigarh.
