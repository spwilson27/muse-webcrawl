Crossroads (Cream song)

"Crossroads" is a song by the British rock supergroup Cream, released on their live album Wheels of Fire (1968). It is an adaptation of Robert Johnson's 1937 blues song "Cross Road Blues", reworked in a hard-rock arrangement dominated by Eric Clapton's guitar playing and singing. The recording was made live at the Winterland Ballroom in San Francisco in March 1968.

Background

Johnson's original, recorded in 1936 and released the following year, became wrapped in the legend that he sold his soul to the Devil at a Mississippi crossroads in exchange for his musical gifts. Cream, following the earlier example of Elmore James, accelerated the song and recast it for a power-trio format, and it became one of the signature numbers of their live sets.

Reception and legacy

Clapton's two guitar solos on the track are frequently cited among the greatest in rock music, and the song became a staple of classic-rock radio. It has been inducted into lists of the greatest songs of all time and the recording entered the Grammy Hall of Fame. Clapton later named his drug and alcohol rehabilitation centre in Antigua, and his series of guitar festivals, after the song.
