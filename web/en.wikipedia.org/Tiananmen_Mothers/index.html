Tiananmen Mothers

Formation1995
TypeHuman rights advocacy group
Key figureDing Zilin
PurposeAccountability and commemoration for victims of the 1989 crackdown

The Tiananmen Mothers (Chinese: 天安门母亲) is a group of Chinese democracy activists, mostly women, who are the relatives of people killed in the 1989 Tiananmen Square protests and massacre in Beijing. Led for many years by Ding Zilin, a retired professor whose 17-year-old son Jiang Jielian was killed on the night of 3–4 June 1989, the group campaigns for the Chinese government to acknowledge the deaths, end persecution of the families, and allow public mourning.

Formation and goals

The families began organizing in the years after 1989; the group adopted the name Tiananmen Mothers in 1995. In open letters issued annually, typically ahead of the June 4 anniversary, the group has put forward three demands: an official investigation and truthful accounting of the crackdown, accountability and punishment for those responsible, and consolation, compensation, and the right to mourn publicly for the victims' families. It has also called for a national memorial.

Documentation work

The group has laboriously identified and documented victims, compiling lists of the dead — by 2018 it had documented over 200 victims — together with testimonies recorded in works such as the documentary film The Tiananmen Mothers: Voices of Survivors and Families of the Victims and the book Witnesses: The Tiananmen Massacre. Its annual open letters are published abroad, often through the Hong Kong– and US-based group Human Rights in China.

Government response

Public commemoration of the events in mainland China remains prohibited, and members of the group are subject to surveillance and restrictions, particularly around anniversaries. Elderly members have continued issuing statements despite declining membership as parents of victims pass away. The group has received international human rights attention, and its letters are read at vigils abroad, notably the long-running candlelight vigil formerly held in Hong Kong's Victoria Park.
