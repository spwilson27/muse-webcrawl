Val Lewton (born Vladimir Ivanovich Leventon; 1904–1951) was a Russian-American novelist, film producer and screenwriter best known for a series of low-budget horror films produced for RKO Radio Pictures in the 1940s, including Cat People (1942) and I Walked with a Zombie (1943).

Early career

Born in Yalta, Lewton emigrated to the United States as a child and worked as a journalist and prolific novelist before joining MGM's publicity department and then becoming David O. Selznick's story editor and assistant, contributing uncredited work to films such as Gone with the Wind.

The RKO horror unit

In 1942 RKO hired Lewton to head a unit making cheap horror films to compete with Universal's monster movies. Given only titles, budgets under $150,000 and short running times, Lewton and directors Jacques Tourneur, Robert Wise and Mark Robson created atmospheric films that suggested horror rather than showing it — the famous "Lewton bus" shock cut and shadow-drenched set pieces influenced generations of filmmakers.

Later work and legacy

After RKO, Lewton produced a handful of films at Paramount, MGM and Universal, including the historical drama Please Believe Me and the Apache film Apache Drums. He died of a heart attack at 46. His restrained, literary approach to horror is now regarded as foundational to the psychological horror genre.
