Rail transport in India

Rail transport in India consists of a network of over 68,000 kilometres (42,000 miles) of track operated almost entirely by Indian Railways, a statutory body under the Ministry of Railways. It is one of the largest railway networks in the world, carrying more than eight billion passengers and over 1.5 billion tonnes of freight annually before the COVID-19 pandemic.

History

The first passenger railway in India ran from Bombay (Mumbai) to Thane in 1853, built by the Great Indian Peninsula Railway. Under British rule the network expanded rapidly, built largely with private capital guaranteed by the state. After independence in 1947, the railways were nationalized and consolidated into Indian Railways in 1951. Later decades saw the conversion of metre gauge to broad gauge under Project Unigauge, progressive electrification, and computerization of reservations.

Network and services

The system uses mainly 1,676 mm broad gauge. Premium services include the Vande Bharat Express semi-high-speed trains introduced in 2019, the Rajdhani and Shatabdi expresses, and luxury tourist trains such as the Palace on Wheels. Suburban railways serve Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, and other cities, and rapid transit (metro) systems operate in a growing number of urban areas. Dedicated Freight Corridors and a planned Mumbai–Ahmedabad high-speed line using Japanese Shinkansen technology are major ongoing projects.

Organization

Indian Railways is divided into 19 zones and employs about 1.2 million people, making it one of the world's largest employers. Manufacturing is carried out by in-house production units such as the Integral Coach Factory in Chennai and Chittaranjan Locomotive Works.
