Vaslav Nijinsky

Vaslav Nijinsky (12 March 1889 – 8 April 1950) was a Polish-born Russian ballet dancer and choreographer, widely regarded as the greatest male dancer of the early 20th century. Celebrated for his virtuosity, extraordinary leaps and expressive characterisations, he transformed male dancing and, as a choreographer, produced some of the most controversial works in ballet history.

Born12 March 1889, Kiev, Russian Empire
Died8 April 1950 (aged 61), London, England
OccupationBallet dancer, choreographer

Career

Born to Polish dancers, Nijinsky trained at the Imperial Ballet School in St Petersburg and joined the Mariinsky Ballet, quickly becoming its star. Sergei Diaghilev engaged him as the leading dancer of the Ballets Russes, where he created celebrated roles in Fokine's Le Spectre de la rose, Petrushka and Scheherazade. His own choreography — L'après-midi d'un faune (1912), Jeux (1913) and Stravinsky's The Rite of Spring (1913) — scandalised audiences with angular, anti-classical movement; the Rite premiere provoked a famous near-riot in Paris.

Later life

Nijinsky's marriage to Romola de Pulszky in 1913 led Diaghilev to dismiss him. During the First World War he was interned in Hungary; after release and an American tour, his mental health collapsed. Diagnosed with schizophrenia in 1919, he spent much of the rest of his life in sanatoria. His Diary, written in early 1919, was published in expurgated form in 1936 and in full in 1995. He died in London in 1950 and was later reburied near Diaghilev in Paris.
