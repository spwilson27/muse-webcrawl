Portishead (band)

Portishead is an English band formed in 1991 in Bristol by singer Beth Gibbons, multi-instrumentalist Geoff Barrow, and guitarist Adrian Utley, with engineer Dave McDonald as an early member. Named after the Somerset town of Portishead, near Bristol, the group became the most prominent act associated with trip hop, the downtempo style that emerged from Bristol in the 1990s, blending hip hop beats and samples with jazz, film-score atmospheres, and Gibbons's mournful vocals.

Career

Their debut album, Dummy (1994), won the 1995 Mercury Prize and is regarded as a landmark of the decade, with tracks such as "Sour Times" and "Glory Box" built on samples from Isaac Hayes and 1960s spy-film music. The darker, more abrasive Portishead (1997) and the live album Roseland NYC Live (1998), recorded with an orchestra, followed before the band withdrew from regular activity for a decade, releasing no studio material while Barrow pursued other projects.

Portishead returned with Third in 2008, which abandoned much of their signature sound for krautrock, folk, and electronic influences and was met with wide acclaim. Since then the band has performed only sporadically, curating festivals and making rare live appearances, including a 2022 benefit performance for War Child — their first concert in seven years.
