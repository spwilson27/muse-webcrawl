Tostig Godwinson
Born: c. 1029
Died: 25 September 1066, Stamford Bridge
Title: Earl of Northumbria (1055–1065)
Father: Godwin, Earl of Wessex
Brother: Harold Godwinson

Tostig Godwinson (c. 1029 – 25 September 1066) was an Anglo-Saxon nobleman, Earl of Northumbria and brother of King Harold Godwinson. His deposition and subsequent alliance with the Norwegian king Harald Hardrada set in motion the invasions that culminated in the Norman Conquest of England.

Earl of Northumbria

Son of the powerful Earl Godwin of Wessex, Tostig became Earl of Northumbria in 1055. His rule grew increasingly unpopular — heavy taxation and harsh justice provoked rebellion in 1065, when the Northumbrian thegns rose, killed his retainers and demanded his removal. King Edward the Confessor, unwilling or unable to fight, exiled Tostig, who fled to Flanders swearing revenge on Harold, whom he blamed for failing to support him.

Invasion and death

In 1066 Tostig raided the English coast, then joined Harald Hardrada's great Norwegian invasion fleet. The invaders defeated a northern English army at the Battle of Fulford near York, but on 25 September 1066 Harold Godwinson surprised them at the Battle of Stamford Bridge. Both Hardrada and Tostig were killed in the rout. Harold's exhausted army then marched south to face William of Normandy at Hastings, where Harold himself died three weeks later.
