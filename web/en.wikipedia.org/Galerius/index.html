Galerius

Gaius Galerius Valerius Maximianus (c. 258 – May 311) was a Roman emperor who ruled from 305 to 311. Rising from humble origins in the Balkans — he was said to have been a herdsman in his youth — he became a capable soldier and was appointed Caesar under Diocletian in 293 as part of the Tetrarchy, governing the eastern provinces.

Career

As Caesar, Galerius campaigned on the Danube and won a major victory over the Sasanian Persians in 298, following an initial defeat near Carrhae; the resulting Peace of Nisibis gained Rome territory in Mesopotamia and Armenia. When Diocletian and Maximian abdicated in 305, Galerius became Augustus of the East and the dominant figure in the empire, though the system soon broke down into civil war among rival tetrarchs.

Persecution of Christians

Galerius was the driving force behind the Diocletianic Persecution, the last and most severe persecution of Christians in the Roman Empire, begun in 303. Ancient sources suggest he pressed the reluctant Diocletian to act. Ironically, it was Galerius himself who ended the persecution: suffering from a painful terminal illness, he issued the Edict of Serdica on 30 April 311, granting Christians toleration and asking for their prayers. He died days later, in May 311.

Legacy

The Arch of Galerius and his Rotunda still stand in Thessaloniki, and the ruins of his palace complex, Felix Romuliana (Gamzigrad), in modern Serbia are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
