The Circuit Gilles Villeneuve, also spelled Circuit Gilles-Villeneuve in French, is a 4.361 km (2.710 mi) motor racing circuit in Montreal, Quebec, Canada. It is the venue for the FIA Formula One Canadian Grand Prix. It has previously hosted the FIA World Sportscar Championship, the Champ Car World Series (Grand Prix of Montreal), the NASCAR Canadian Tire Series, and the NASCAR Xfinity Series (NAPA Auto Parts 200).

History

The circuit lies on Île Notre-Dame, an artificial island in the St. Lawrence River built for Expo 67, within the Parc Jean-Drapeau. It was constructed in 1978 and originally named the Circuit Île Notre-Dame. After the death of Canadian driver Gilles Villeneuve in a qualifying crash at the 1982 Belgian Grand Prix, the circuit was renamed in his honour; Villeneuve had won the inaugural 1978 race there, his first Formula One victory.

Layout

The circuit is a fast, tight street-style course characterised by long straights, heavy braking zones, and chicanes flanked by unforgiving walls. The final chicane's exit wall is famously known as the "Wall of Champions" after several world champions crashed into it during the 1999 race. Other notable features include the Senna curves at the start and the hairpin (Virage de la Concorde/L'Épingle) at the eastern end.

Events

The Canadian Grand Prix has been held at the circuit almost continuously since 1978, with occasional interruptions, including cancellations in 2020 and 2021 owing to the COVID-19 pandemic. The circuit is also used by the public for cycling, rollerblading, and cross-country skiing when not in use for racing.
