16:9 aspect ratio

The 16:9 aspect ratio (also written 1.78:1) is a widescreen proportion for images and displays, 16 units wide for every 9 units of height. Since the late 2000s it has been the dominant standard for televisions, computer monitors, and smartphone-era video, and it is the standard format for high-definition television resolutions such as 1280×720 (720p) and 1920×1080 (1080p), as well as 4K UHD (3840×2160) and 8K UHD.

History

The ratio was proposed by Kerns H. Powers, a member of the SMPTE Working Group on High-Definition Electronic Production in the 1980s. Powers cut out rectangles of all the popular film and television aspect ratios of the time and found that 16:9 (mathematically the geometric mean of 4:3 and the cinematic 2.39:1) allowed all of them to be displayed with equal relative area of letterboxing or cropping.

Adoption

Europe promoted 16:9 broadcasting in the 1990s through its HD-MAC action plan, and the format gradually displaced 4:3. In 2008–2009 computer monitors shifted from 16:10 to 16:9 panels, and by the early 2010s the ratio had become near-universal for consumer displays. CinemaScope-style content wider than 16:9 is letterboxed on such screens; legacy 4:3 content is pillarboxed.

Common resolutions

Resolutions with a 16:9 ratio include 1024×576, 1152×648, 1280×720, 1366×768, 1600×900, 1920×1080, 2560×1440, 3840×2160 and 7680×4320.
