Classic of Poetry

Classic of Poetry
Chinese: 詩經 (Shījīng)
Poems: 305
Period covered: c. 11th–7th centuries BCE
Status: One of the Five Classics

The Classic of Poetry, also known as the Shijing or the Book of Songs, is the oldest existing collection of Chinese poetry. It comprises 305 works dating from the 11th to 7th centuries BCE and is one of the Five Classics traditionally said to have been compiled or edited by Confucius.

Contents

The poems are traditionally divided into four parts: the Guofeng ("Airs of the States"), folk songs from the various states of Zhou China; the Xiaoya ("Lesser Odes"); the Daya ("Greater Odes"), many of them court poems; and the Song ("Hymns"), sacrificial hymns performed in ancestral temples.

Style and themes

Most poems are four-character lines organized in rhymed stanzas. Their subjects include love and courtship, court life, agriculture and the seasons, warfare and hardship, and praise and complaint directed at rulers. Later Confucian interpretation read many of the poems allegorically as moral or political instruction.

Influence

The Shijing shaped the vocabulary and imagery of Chinese poetry for over two thousand years and was memorized by generations of students. Its folk-song sections are an important source for the study of early Chinese language, society, and daily life.
