Hyperbolic functions

In mathematics, hyperbolic functions are analogues of the ordinary trigonometric functions, but defined using the hyperbola rather than the circle. Just as the points (cos t, sin t) form a circle with unit radius, the points (cosh t, sinh t) form the right half of the unit hyperbola. The basic hyperbolic functions are the hyperbolic sine "sinh" and hyperbolic cosine "cosh", from which are derived the hyperbolic tangent "tanh", hyperbolic cosecant "csch", hyperbolic secant "sech", and hyperbolic cotangent "coth".

Definitions

sinh x = (ex − e−x)/2, and cosh x = (ex + e−x)/2. The hyperbolic tangent is tanh x = sinh x / cosh x. They satisfy identities analogous to the trigonometric ones, including cosh²x − sinh²x = 1. The inverse hyperbolic functions are the area functions arsinh, arcosh and artanh, expressible with logarithms.

Applications

Hyperbolic functions occur in the solutions of many differential equations, in the shape of a hanging cable or chain (the catenary, y = a cosh(x/a)), in hyperbolic geometry, in the rapidity description of special relativity and Lorentz transformations, and in the distribution of temperature and electric potential in certain geometries.

History

The functions were introduced in the 1760s independently by Vincenzo Riccati and Johann Heinrich Lambert. Lambert adopted the names and abbreviations that are essentially those in use today.
