Child and adolescent psychiatry
FocusMental health of children and adolescents
Common conditionsADHD, autism, anxiety, depression, eating disorders
TreatmentsPsychotherapy, family work, medication

Child and adolescent psychiatry (or paediatric psychiatry) is the branch of psychiatry specialising in the diagnosis and treatment of mental, emotional and behavioural disorders in children, adolescents and their families. Practitioners complete general psychiatry training followed by a fellowship in child and adolescent work.

Assessment and treatment

Assessment relies on interviews with the child and caregivers, school information and developmental history, because children's symptoms are interpreted in the context of normal development. Common presentations include attention deficit hyperactivity disorder, autism spectrum disorder, anxiety and depressive disorders, oppositional and conduct problems, tic disorders and early psychosis. Treatment is usually multimodal: evidence-based psychotherapies such as cognitive behavioural therapy and parent management training, school liaison, and medication where indicated — for example stimulants for ADHD or SSRIs for anxiety and depression, used with careful monitoring.

History

The field grew from early 20th-century child guidance clinics — the first, associated with William Healy in Chicago, opened in 1909 — and from figures such as Leo Kanner, who described autism in 1943. Professional bodies such as the American Academy of Child and Adolescent Psychiatry (founded 1953) set training standards. Contemporary debates concern rising reported rates of youth mental illness, access to services, and the balance of psychosocial and pharmacological treatments.
