Humphrey Bogart

Humphrey Bogart
Born: 25 December 1899, New York City
Died: 14 January 1957, Los Angeles
American film and stage actor, an icon of film noir.

Humphrey DeForest Bogart (December 25, 1899 – January 14, 1957), nicknamed Bogie, was an American film and stage actor. His performances in classic Hollywood cinema made him an American cultural icon. In 1999, the American Film Institute selected Bogart as the greatest male star of classic American cinema.

Career

Bogart began acting on Broadway in the 1920s and appeared in films throughout the 1930s, often typecast as a gangster in pictures such as The Petrified Forest (1936). His breakthrough came with High Sierra (1941) and The Maltese Falcon (1941), where his portrayal of private detective Sam Spade defined the hard-boiled screen hero. Casablanca (1942), in which he played Rick Blaine opposite Ingrid Bergman, became one of the most beloved films ever made and earned him his first Academy Award nomination.

Later work and legacy

Bogart starred in To Have and Have Not (1944), where he met Lauren Bacall, whom he married; they made four films together. He won the Academy Award for Best Actor for The African Queen (1951) and received further acclaim for The Caine Mutiny (1954) and Sabrina (1954). Off screen he was a founding member of the Rat Pack. Bogart died of esophageal cancer in 1957, aged 57. His distinctive, world-weary persona remains the archetype of the film-noir antihero.
