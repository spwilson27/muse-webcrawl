Bethel

Bethel (Hebrew: בֵּית אֵל, Beit El, "House of God") was an ancient Israelite city and sanctuary in the hill country north of Jerusalem. It is prominent in the Hebrew Bible as the place where Jacob dreamed of a ladder reaching to heaven (Genesis 28) and renamed the site, formerly called Luz, to Bethel.

Biblical history

Bethel appears repeatedly in the patriarchal narratives: Abraham built an altar nearby, and Jacob later returned and received the name Israel there. After the division of the kingdom, Jeroboam I made Bethel one of two royal sanctuaries of the northern Kingdom of Israel (with Dan), setting up a golden calf — an act condemned by the biblical prophets. The city is generally identified with the site near the modern Palestinian village of Beitin in the West Bank.

Other places

The biblical name inspired many settlements, including Bethel, Alaska; Bethel, Maine; Bethel, Ohio; and Bethel, Connecticut, as well as the modern Israeli settlement of Beit El. "Bethel" is also a common name for chapels and churches, originally in Welsh Nonconformist tradition.
