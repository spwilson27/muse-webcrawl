The California Desert Protection Act of 1994 (CDPA) is a United States federal law that established protections for large areas of the California desert, including the Mojave and Colorado deserts. It was among the largest land conservation measures passed by Congress in the continental United States.

Provisions

The Act upgraded Death Valley and Joshua Tree from national monuments to national parks and greatly enlarged both, created the Mojave National Preserve, and designated 69 wilderness areas covering millions of acres administered by the Bureau of Land Management and other agencies. It also established protections for desert tortoise habitat and transferred certain lands between federal agencies to consolidate management.

Legislative history

The bill was championed for years by Senator Alan Cranston and, after his retirement, by Senator Dianne Feinstein. Its passage followed a long campaign by conservation groups and extended negotiation with mining, ranching, off-road vehicle and military interests, whose existing uses were partly accommodated. President Bill Clinton signed the Act on October 31, 1994.

Later amendments

The California Desert Protection Act of 2010, also led by Feinstein, adjusted boundaries and added protections. The 2019 Dingell Act incorporated further Feinstein desert legislation, expanding Death Valley and Joshua Tree national parks and designating additional wilderness and off-highway vehicle recreation areas.
