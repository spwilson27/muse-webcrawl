Surtsey

Surtsey is a volcanic island located in the Vestmannaeyjar archipelago off the southern coast of Iceland. It was formed by an undersea volcanic eruption that began 130 meters below sea level and reached the surface on 14 November 1963. The eruption lasted until 5 June 1967, by which time the island measured about 2.7 km²; erosion by wind and waves has since reduced it to roughly 1.4 km². It is named after Surtr, the fire giant of Norse mythology.

Scientific significance

Surtsey offers a rare, pristine natural laboratory: because access has been strictly limited to scientists since its formation, researchers have been able to document how life colonizes new land from scratch. The first vascular plant was found in 1965; seeds arrived by sea currents, wind, and birds, followed by mosses, lichens, insects, and eventually breeding seabirds — fulmars and black guillemots in 1970, and a gull colony from 1985 that greatly accelerated soil formation. Erosion is expected to leave only the resistant palagonite core above water over coming centuries.

Surtsey was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2008.
