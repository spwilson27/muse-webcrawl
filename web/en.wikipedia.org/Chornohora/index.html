Chornohora

Chornohora (Ukrainian: Чорногора, "Black Mountain") is the highest mountain range in Ukraine, located in the Eastern Carpathians. It lies on the border of Ivano-Frankivsk and Zakarpattia oblasts and includes the country's highest peak, Mount Hoverla.

The main ridge of Chornohora

Geography

The main ridge of Chornohora stretches for about 40 km and contains all six of Ukraine's peaks above 2,000 m: Hoverla (2,061 m), Brebeneskul (2,035 m), Chorna Hora or Pip Ivan Chornohirskyi (2,028 m), Petros (2,020 m), Hutyn Tomnatyk (2,016 m), and Rebra (2,001 m). Glacial lakes such as Brebeneskul and Nesamovyte lie in cirques along the ridge. Much of the range is protected within the Carpathian Biosphere Reserve and the Carpathian National Nature Park.

Landmarks

On the summit of Pip Ivan stand the ruins of the White Elephant, a Polish astronomical and meteorological observatory built in 1938 and recently partially restored as a mountain rescue station. The range is a popular hiking destination, and the Prut and Tysa (Tisza) rivers have their sources on its slopes.
