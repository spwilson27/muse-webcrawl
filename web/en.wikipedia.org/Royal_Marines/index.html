Royal Marines

The Royal Marines are the United Kingdom's amphibious special operations capable commando force, and one of the five fighting arms of the Royal Navy. Founded in 1664 as the Duke of York and Albany's Maritime Regiment of Foot, the corps provides the UK's commando forces, specialising in amphibious warfare, arctic and mountain warfare, and rapid-reaction expeditionary operations. Its motto is Per Mare, Per Terram ("By Sea, By Land").

Organisation

The principal formation is the UK Commando Force (formerly 3 Commando Brigade), built around 40 Commando, 42 Commando and 45 Commando, together with 43 Commando Fleet Protection Group, 47 Commando (Raiding Group), the Commando Logistic Regiment, 30 Commando IX Group, and attached Army and Navy units. All Royal Marines undergo the Commando course at the Commando Training Centre, Lympstone, Devon — among the longest and most demanding infantry training courses in the world — earning the coveted green beret. Specialisations include the Mountain Leader cadre, whose members staff the Mountain and Arctic Warfare cadre tradition, and the Special Boat Service (SBS), the UK's naval special forces unit, recruited largely from the corps.

History

Marines served aboard warships throughout the age of sail, providing sharpshooters and boarding parties, and fought ashore in campaigns on every continent. They won special distinction at Belle Île (1761), Bunker Hill (1775), and in the Second World War, when the "Royal Marines Division" and the first Commandos were formed — the corps adopted commando role wholesale after the war. Royal Marines fought in Korea, Suez, the Falklands War (1982), where they were central to the amphibious landings and the advance on Stanley, and in Iraq and Afghanistan.
