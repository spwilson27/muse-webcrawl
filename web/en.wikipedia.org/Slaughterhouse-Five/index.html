Slaughterhouse-Five, or, The Children's Crusade: A Duty-Dance with Death is a 1969 semi-autobiographical science fiction-infused anti-war novel by Kurt Vonnegut. It tells the story of Billy Pilgrim, an American soldier who survives the firebombing of Dresden as a prisoner of war and becomes "unstuck in time," travelling randomly through episodes of his life, including an abduction by aliens from the planet Tralfamadore.

Plot and themes

The novel's nonlinear structure mirrors Billy's passive, fatalistic outlook. The Tralfamadorians perceive all moments simultaneously, and their response to death — "So it goes," repeated throughout the book after every mention of death — encapsulates the novel's stance toward inevitability and free will. Vonnegut drew on his own experience of surviving the Dresden bombing of February 1945, held by the Germans in an underground slaughterhouse meat locker — Schlachthof-fünf, Slaughterhouse Five.

Reception

Slaughterhouse-Five was a commercial and critical breakthrough for Vonnegut and is consistently ranked among the greatest novels of the twentieth century. It has also been a frequent target of book challenges and bans in the United States. A film adaptation directed by George Roy Hill was released in 1972.
