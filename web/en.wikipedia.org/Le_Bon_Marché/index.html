Le Bon Marché

Le Bon Marché is a department store in Paris, France, widely regarded as one of the first modern department stores in the world. Founded in 1838 and greatly expanded under Aristide Boucicaut from 1852, it stands on the Left Bank in the 7th arrondissement, at the corner of rue de Sèvres and rue du Bac.

Boucicaut introduced innovations that transformed retailing: fixed prices marked on goods, free entry without obligation to buy, seasonal sales, catalogues and home delivery, and a wide variety of departments under one roof. The store's grand iron-and-glass architecture, developed with engineers including Gustave Eiffel, became a model for department stores internationally.

Émile Zola's novel Au Bonheur des Dames (1883) was inspired by the store. Today Le Bon Marché is part of the LVMH group and is known for luxury fashion, design, and its food hall, La Grande Épicerie de Paris, in the adjoining building.
