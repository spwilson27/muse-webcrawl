Sonic Youth was an American rock band formed in New York City in 1981. Founded by Thurston Moore, Kim Gordon, and Lee Ranaldo, with drummer Steve Shelley joining in 1985, the band became a pivotal force in alternative and indie rock, famed for its use of alternate guitar tunings, prepared guitars, dissonance, and feedback drawn from the avant-garde and New York's no wave scene.

History

Emerging from the downtown art scene and influenced by Glenn Branca's guitar orchestras, the band released early records on independent labels before signing with SST for EVOL (1986) and Sister (1987). The double album Daydream Nation (1988) was a critical landmark, later selected for the US National Recording Registry. Signing to major label DGC in 1990, they released Goo and Dirty and famously championed younger bands, inviting the then-unknown Nirvana to tour and helping bring alternative rock into the mainstream.

Breakup and legacy

The band went on indefinite hiatus in 2011 following the separation of Moore and Gordon, effectively ending the group. Sonic Youth influenced generations of guitar bands, from Pavement and My Bloody Valentine to contemporary noise and indie acts, and are credited with popularising experimental tunings and textures in rock music.
