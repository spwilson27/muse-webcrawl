Ottoman–Venetian War (1570–1573)

The Ottoman–Venetian War of 1570–1573, also called the Fourth Ottoman–Venetian War or the War of Cyprus, was fought between the Ottoman Empire and the Republic of Venice, joined by the Holy League assembled by Pope Pius V. Its principal prize was Cyprus, then a Venetian colony.

Course of the war

In 1570 Ottoman forces under Lala Mustafa Pasha invaded Cyprus, capturing Nicosia and besieging Famagusta, which held out until August 1571; its commander Marcantonio Bragadin was flayed alive after surrendering. Meanwhile the Holy League's fleet, commanded by Don John of Austria, destroyed the Ottoman fleet at the Battle of Lepanto on 7 October 1571, one of the largest naval battles of the early modern era.

Outcome

Despite Lepanto, the Ottomans rebuilt their fleet within a year, and Venice, abandoned by its allies, signed a separate peace in March 1573, formally ceding Cyprus and paying an indemnity. Cyprus remained Ottoman until 1878.
