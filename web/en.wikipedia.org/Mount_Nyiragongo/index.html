Mount Nyiragongo

Mount Nyiragongo (/ˌnɪərəˈɡɒŋɡoʊ/) is an active stratovolcano with an elevation of 3,470 m (11,385 ft) in the Virunga Mountains, associated with the Albertine Rift, in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, about 12 km (7.5 mi) north of the city of Goma and Lake Kivu. It is famed for the large, persistent lava lake in its summit crater — for decades one of only a handful on Earth — and for lava of exceptionally low viscosity that can flow at speeds of up to 60 km/h (37 mph) downslope, an extreme hazard to nearby populations.

Geology

Nyiragongo is one of eight volcanoes in the Virunga chain on the western branch of the East African Rift. Its magma is an unusually silica-poor, alkali-rich nephelinite, which explains the extraordinary fluidity of its lavas. The summit crater, about 2 km wide, long contained a lava lake that drained catastrophically in 1977, refilled, and emptied again in 2002.

Nyiragongo's lava lake, among the largest ever observed, before the 2021 eruption.

Eruption of 1977

On 10 January 1977 the crater walls fractured and the lava lake drained in less than an hour, sending flows down the flanks at extraordinary speed. At least 70 people were killed by most accounts, with some reports of far higher numbers.

Eruption of 2002

On 17 January 2002, fissures opened on the southern flank and lava streamed into Goma, destroying thousands of homes and businesses, covering part of the airport runway, and forcing the evacuation of several hundred thousand people; around 250 people died from lava, gas, and related hazards.

Eruption of 2021

On 22 May 2021 flank fissures again fed lava toward Goma; the flow stopped short of the city centre but destroyed villages to the north and displaced tens of thousands. The eruption drained the summit lava lake.
