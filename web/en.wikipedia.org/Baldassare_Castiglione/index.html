Baldassare Castiglione
Born: 6 December 1478, Casatico, Italy
Died: 2 February 1529, Toledo, Spain
Occupation: Courtier, diplomat, author
Notable work: The Book of the Courtier (1528)

Baldassare Castiglione, Count of Casatico (6 December 1478 – 2 February 1529), was an Italian courtier, diplomat and author of the Renaissance, best known for The Book of the Courtier (Il Cortegiano), published in 1528.

Life

Born near Mantua, Castiglione served the Gonzaga and then the della Rovere court at Urbino, one of the most refined courts of Renaissance Italy. He later entered papal service and was appointed nuncio to Spain by Pope Clement VII. He died in Toledo in 1529; Charles V reportedly called him one of the finest gentlemen in the world. His portrait by Raphael is among the most famous of the Renaissance.

The Book of the Courtier

Castiglione's dialogue, set in the court of Urbino in 1507, describes the ideal courtier and lady of the court, coining the concept of sprezzatura — a studied nonchalance that makes accomplishments appear effortless. Translated widely, the book shaped manners and ideals of gentility across early modern Europe.
