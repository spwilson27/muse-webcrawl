All Souls' Day

Date2 November
Also calledCommemoration of All the Faithful Departed
Related toAll Saints' Day, Day of the Dead

All Souls' Day, also called the Commemoration of All the Faithful Departed, is a day of prayer and remembrance for the souls of those who have died, observed by Catholics and other Christian denominations on 2 November, following All Saints' Day on 1 November.

Belief and practice

In Catholic theology the day is dedicated to prayer for the dead who are in purgatory, that they may be purified and enter heaven. Customs include attending requiem Masses, praying the Office of the Dead, and visiting cemeteries to decorate graves with flowers and candles. The observance was promoted by Odilo of Cluny in the late 10th century and spread throughout the Church.

Around the world

In Mexico and other Latin American countries the date coincides with the Day of the Dead (Día de los Muertos), blending indigenous and Catholic traditions with altars (ofrendas), food offerings, and marigolds. Comparable commemorations of ancestors exist in many cultures, such as the Chinese Ghost Festival.
