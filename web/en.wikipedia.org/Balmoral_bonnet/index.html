Balmoral bonnet

The Balmoral bonnet (also called a Balmoral cap, and formerly a Kilmarnock bonnet) is a traditional Scottish cap of soft wool, usually dark blue, black, or green, dating to at least the sixteenth century. It is a principal item of both military and civilian Highland dress, worn with the kilt, and takes its modern name from Balmoral Castle, the royal residence in Aberdeenshire, in the nineteenth century.

Description

The bonnet is knitted or made of felted wool, soft and brimless, with a flat crown that is typically pulled forward and to the right side of the wearer's head. It is usually finished with a tourie — a woollen pom-pom — at the centre of the crown, ribbons or "tails" hanging at the back, and often a chequered or diced band around the lower edge and a badge or clan crest worn on the left side on a silk cockade.

History and use

Descended from the broad blue bonnet once common across lowland and Highland Scotland, the Balmoral was adopted by Scottish regiments of the British Army; a feather bonnet was worn for full dress by Highland regiments, while the plainer Balmoral and the similar Glengarry served for ordinary duty. It remains part of the uniform of the Royal Regiment of Scotland and of Commonwealth regiments with Scottish traditions, and is widely worn by pipe bands and by civilians as part of day Highland dress. In military use the badge is worn over the left eye and the ribbons at the back are traditionally tied in a bow or left hanging.
