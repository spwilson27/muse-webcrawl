Vipassanā

Vipassanā (Pāli) or vipaśyanā (Sanskrit) literally means "seeing things as they really are" and is commonly translated as insight. In Buddhism it denotes a clear awareness of exactly what is happening as it happens, and the direct insight into the three marks of existence: impermanence (anicca), suffering or unsatisfactoriness (dukkha), and non-self (anattā).

Practice

Vipassanā meditation is traditionally paired with samatha (calm abiding). Classic instructions appear in texts such as the Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta, which directs mindfulness to the body, feelings, mind, and mental phenomena. Modern Theravāda teachers such as Mahasi Sayadaw popularized a "noting" technique, while Ledi Sayadaw and his successors promoted lay meditation practice.

Modern movements

The 19th–20th century Burmese Vipassanā movement brought the practice to laypeople across Asia and the West. S. N. Goenka's ten-day retreats, taught worldwide from centres in the tradition of Sayagyi U Ba Khin, became especially influential. Vipassanā-based methods underpin modern mindfulness-based interventions such as MBSR, developed by Jon Kabat-Zinn.
