Horsehead Nebula

The Horsehead Nebula (also known as Barnard 33) is a small dark nebula in the constellation Orion, about 1,375 light-years from Earth. Its dark cloud of dust and gas is silhouetted against the bright emission nebula IC 434, giving it the unmistakable profile of a horse's head.

Discovery and appearance

Scottish astronomer Williamina Fleming identified the nebula on a photographic plate in 1888. The dark shape, roughly 3.5 light-years tall, is part of the Orion molecular cloud complex; its silhouette lasts only because a dense pillar of material resists the eroding radiation of the nearby massive star Sigma Orionis. In infrared images the nebula becomes partially transparent and reveals stars forming within.

Observing

Despite its fame from astrophotography, the Horsehead is a notoriously difficult visual target for amateur telescopes, requiring dark skies and large aperture. It lies just south of Alnitak, the easternmost star of Orion's Belt, close to the Flame Nebula and the great Orion Nebula.
