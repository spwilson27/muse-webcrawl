Edgar Allan Poe (19 January 1809 – 7 October 1849) was an American writer, poet, author, editor and literary critic who is best known for his poetry and short stories, particularly his tales involving mystery and the macabre. He is widely regarded as a central figure of Romanticism and Gothic genre fiction in American literature, and was one of the country's earliest practitioners of the short story. He is considered the inventor of the detective fiction genre and is further credited with contributing to the emerging genre of science fiction.

Life

Poe was born in Boston, the second child of actors. Orphaned young, he was taken in by John and Frances Allan of Richmond, Virginia. He attended the University of Virginia but left after a year for lack of money, then enlisted in the United States Army and later studied briefly at West Point before being dismissed. A career of editing literary journals in Richmond, New York, Philadelphia and Baltimore brought him critical recognition but little income. In 1836 he married his cousin Virginia Clemm; her death from tuberculosis in 1847 devastated him. Poe died in Baltimore on 7 October 1849, aged 40, under circumstances that remain mysterious.

Works

Poe's best-known fiction includes the Gothic tales "The Fall of the House of Usher", "The Masque of the Red Death", "The Tell-Tale Heart" and "The Cask of Amontillado"; the ratiocinative detective stories featuring C. Auguste Dupin — "The Murders in the Rue Morgue", "The Mystery of Marie Rogêt" and "The Purloined Letter" — which profoundly influenced Arthur Conan Doyle and later detective fiction; and the novel The Narrative of Arthur Gordon Pym of Nantucket (1838). His poem "The Raven" (1845) brought him national fame. His essay "The Philosophy of Composition" set out his doctrine of the unity of effect.

Legacy

Championed in France by Charles Baudelaire, whose translations made Poe famous across Europe, Poe influenced writers from Fyodor Dostoevsky and Jules Verne to H. P. Lovecraft. The Mystery Writers of America present annual Edgar Awards in his honour, and his homes in Baltimore, Philadelphia, Richmond and the Bronx are preserved as museums.
