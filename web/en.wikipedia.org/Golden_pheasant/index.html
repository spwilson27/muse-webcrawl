Golden pheasant

Scientific nameChrysolophus pictus
FamilyPhasianidae
Native rangeForests of mountainous western China
Conservation statusLeast Concern (IUCN)

The golden pheasant (Chrysolophus pictus), also known as the Chinese pheasant or rainbow pheasant, is a gamebird of the family Phasianidae native to forests in the mountainous regions of western China. It is one of the most popular pheasants in aviculture worldwide.

Description

The male is unmistakable: it has a golden-yellow crest and rump, a bright red body, an orange "cape" with black barring that can be spread in display, a scarlet breast, and a very long barred tail that accounts for most of its 90–105 cm total length. The female is far drabber, with mottled brown plumage and a shorter tail. Despite the male's brilliant colours, the birds are shy and difficult to spot in the wild.

Habitat and behaviour

Golden pheasants inhabit dense forest and woodland, feeding on the ground on grain, leaves, berries, and invertebrates. They roost in trees at night and, although capable of flight, prefer to run. The male's call is a metallic "chack".

Introduced populations

Escaped or released birds have established feral populations in parts of the United Kingdom, North America, and elsewhere. The species hybridises in captivity with the closely related Lady Amherst's pheasant. Legend in China has sometimes linked the golden pheasant to tales of the phoenix.
