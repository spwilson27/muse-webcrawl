A Secular Age

A Secular Age
Author: Charles Taylor
Published: 2007 (Harvard University Press)
Subject: Secularization, philosophy of religion
Based on: Gifford Lectures (1999)

A Secular Age is a book by Canadian philosopher Charles Taylor, published in 2007 by Harvard University Press. Running to nearly 900 pages, it is a monumental account of how Western societies moved from a world in 1500 in which belief in God was nearly unchallengeable to one in which belief is one option among others — and frequently not the easiest to embrace.

Argument

Taylor distinguishes three senses of secularity and focuses on the third: the conditions of belief. He rejects "subtraction stories" that explain secularization as science simply stripping away superstition. Instead he traces how Latin Christendom's "Reform" movements — within both Catholicism and Protestantism — disciplined populations, disenchanted the cosmos, and produced the "buffered self," insulated from spirits and forces that the pre-modern "porous self" experienced directly. This, together with the "modern moral order" and the rise of an "immanent frame," generated "exclusive humanism" and what Taylor calls the "nova effect": a proliferation of new spiritual and anti-spiritual positions. Believers and unbelievers alike are "cross-pressured" by awareness of alternatives.

Reception

The book was widely praised as a landmark of contemporary philosophy and religious studies, though some critics found its account Eurocentric and its Catholic sympathies intrusive. It grew out of Taylor's 1999 Gifford Lectures and his earlier work Sources of the Self (1989).
