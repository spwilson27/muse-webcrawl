Scottish Trades Union Congress

Founded: 1897

Headquarters: Glasgow, Scotland

Members: Around 600,000 (affiliated unions)

The Scottish Trades Union Congress (STUC) is Scotland's national trade union centre. Founded in 1897, it brings together affiliated trade unions and local trades union councils representing hundreds of thousands of workers across Scotland.

History

Scottish unions had previously affiliated to the London-based Trades Union Congress (TUC), but disagreements — including over political representation and a dispute involving the Glasgow Trades Council — led Scottish unions to form their own congress in 1897. The first STUC conference met in Glasgow.

Organisation

The STUC is governed by an annual congress of delegates from affiliated unions and trades councils, which elects a General Council. It campaigns on employment rights, pay, public services and equality, and represents Scottish workers to the Scottish Parliament and Government. Its headquarters are in Glasgow.

Role

While affiliated unions may also belong to the TUC, the STUC is the principal voice of the trade union movement within Scotland's devolved political system.
