Global Peace Index

The Global Peace Index (GPI) is an annual report produced by the Institute for Economics and Peace (IEP) that measures the relative peacefulness of countries and regions. First published in 2007, it ranks 163 independent states and territories covering over 99 percent of the world's population.

Methodology

The index is built from 23 qualitative and quantitative indicators grouped into three domains: ongoing domestic and international conflict, societal safety and security, and militarisation. Indicators include numbers of deaths from internal and external conflict, homicide rates, incarceration, political instability, weapons imports and military expenditure. Data are drawn from sources including the Economist Intelligence Unit, the United Nations and the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute.

Findings

Iceland has consistently ranked as the most peaceful country since the index began, while countries affected by major wars rank lowest. The reports have generally found a long-term decline in global peacefulness over the past decade, alongside an estimate of the economic impact of violence amounting to a substantial share of world GDP.
