Central Valley Project

The Central Valley Project (CVP) is a federal water project in California operated by the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation. Authorised in the 1930s, it comprises a system of dams, reservoirs, canals, and pumping plants that store water in Northern California and deliver it to the Central Valley and parts of the San Francisco Bay Area for irrigation, municipal use, and hydroelectric power.

Facilities

Major components include Shasta Dam on the Sacramento River, Folsom Dam on the American River, Friant Dam on the San Joaquin River, the Delta Cross Channel, the Delta–Mendota Canal, and the Tracy Pumping Plant. The system includes some 20 dams and reservoirs and hundreds of miles of canals, delivering on average about 5 million acre-feet of water a year to farms and cities.

Controversies

The CVP has been controversial since its inception. Friant Dam's diversions dried up long stretches of the San Joaquin River, prompting restoration litigation and a settlement to restore flows and salmon. Operations in the Sacramento–San Joaquin Delta affect endangered fish such as the delta smelt, and allocation disputes intensify during droughts.
