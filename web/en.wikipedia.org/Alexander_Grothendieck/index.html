Alexander Grothendieck

Born: 28 March 1928, Berlin, Germany
Died: 13 November 2014, Saint-Lizier, France
Field: Algebraic geometry
Awards: Fields Medal (1966)

Alexander Grothendieck (28 March 1928 – 13 November 2014) was a German-born French mathematician who became the leading figure in the creation of modern algebraic geometry. His research extended the scope of the field and introduced elements of category theory, homological algebra, and functional analysis into it. He is considered by many to be the greatest mathematician of the 20th century.

Early life

Grothendieck was born in Berlin to an anarchist writer father, Alexander "Sascha" Schapiro, and a journalist mother; his father perished at Auschwitz. The young Grothendieck was raised partly in France, survived internment camps as a stateless refugee, and taught himself mathematics before studying at Montpellier and Nancy, where he produced groundbreaking work on functional analysis.

Work at IHÉS

From 1958 Grothendieck worked at the newly founded Institut des hautes études scientifiques (IHÉS) near Paris. With collaborators including Jean-Pierre Serre, Pierre Deligne, and Jean Dieudonné, he rebuilt algebraic geometry on the theory of schemes, set out in the vast Éléments de géométrie algébrique (EGA) and the seminar volumes SGA. He introduced influential concepts including schemes, topoi, étale cohomology, and motives, and proved key cases of the Weil conjectures. He was awarded the Fields Medal in 1966.

Later life

A committed pacifist and environmentalist, Grothendieck left IHÉS in 1970 in protest at its acceptance of military funding. He progressively withdrew from public mathematics, wrote the autobiographical meditation Récoltes et Semailles (1980s), and in 1991 retired to a village in the Pyrenees, living in near-total seclusion until his death in 2014. His unpublished manuscripts, the "Grothendieck circle" papers, remain a subject of study and negotiation.
