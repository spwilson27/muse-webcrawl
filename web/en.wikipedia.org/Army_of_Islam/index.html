Army of Islam

The Army of Islam (Turkish: İslâm Ordusu), also referred to as the Caucasus Army of Islam, was an Ottoman military formation raised in 1918 during the First World War to secure the Caucasus and the oil fields of Baku. It was distinct from the better-known Palestine-based group namesakes of later decades (such as Jaysh al-Islam factions in Gaza and Syria).

Background

After the Russian Revolution of 1917 and Russia's withdrawal from the war under the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk, Ottoman forces advanced into the Caucasus to recover territories lost earlier and to reach the Caspian Sea. The Ottoman war minister Enver Pasha envisioned uniting the Turkish-speaking and Muslim peoples of the region and securing access to Baku's oil.

Formation and command

The Army of Islam was formally established in 1918 and placed under the command of Nuri Pasha, Enver's half-brother. It combined Ottoman regular divisions with Azerbaijani and Dagestani volunteer units, and its strength grew to roughly 12,000–14,000 men. Although nominally an independent formation, it reported effectively to the Ottoman high command.

Battle of Baku

The Army of Islam advanced through Azerbaijan, defeating Armenian and Bolshevik forces, and on 15 September 1918 captured Baku after the Battle of Baku, expelling the Centro-Caspian Dictatorship and its British Dunsterforce detachment. The capture was followed by reprisals against Armenian civilians, known as the September Days.

Dissolution

Following the Armistice of Mudros (30 October 1918), which ended hostilities between the Ottoman Empire and the Allies, Ottoman forces were required to withdraw from the Caucasus, and the Army of Islam was dissolved.
