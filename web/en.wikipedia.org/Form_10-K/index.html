Form 10-K

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A Form 10-K is an annual report required by the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) that gives a comprehensive summary of a company's financial performance. It must be filed by publicly traded companies (and certain other registrants) pursuant to Section 13 or 15(d) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934. The report is available to the public through the SEC's EDGAR database.

Contents

The 10-K is divided into four parts and fifteen items, including:

  • Part I — a description of the business, risk factors, properties, legal proceedings, and mine safety disclosures.
  • Part II — market information, audited financial statements, management's discussion and analysis of financial condition and results of operations (MD&A), disclosures about market risk, and controls and procedures.
  • Part III — directors and executive officers, executive compensation, security ownership, related transactions, and accountant fees (often incorporated by reference from the proxy statement).
  • Part IV — exhibits and schedules.

Deadlines

Filing deadlines depend on the size of the registrant's public float: large accelerated filers file within 60 days of fiscal year-end, accelerated filers within 75 days, and non-accelerated filers within 90 days. The 10-K is audited, unlike the quarterly Form 10-Q, and contrasts with the glossier annual report to shareholders, which is not filed with the SEC in the same form.

Related forms

Foreign private issuers file the analogous Form 20-F or 40-F. Smaller reporting companies may use scaled disclosure. Late filings are notified on Form 12b-25.
