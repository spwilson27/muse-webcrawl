Necronomicon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Necronomicon, also referred to as the Book of the Dead, or under a purported original Arabic title of Kitab al-Azif, is a fictional grimoire (textbook of magic) appearing in stories by the horror writer H. P. Lovecraft and his followers. It was first mentioned in Lovecraft's 1924 short story "The Hound", and its putative author, the "Mad Arab" Abdul Alhazred, had been quoted a year earlier in "The Nameless City".

Fictional history

Within the fiction, the book was written in Damascus around 730 AD, translated into Greek and Latin, and suppressed by church authorities; copies supposedly survive in libraries such as the British Museum and the fictional Miskatonic University in Arkham. Lovecraft, who invented the title — variously glossed as "Book of the Laws of the Dead" from Greek roots — privately admitted his creation in letters even while his deadpan style persuaded some readers the book was real.

Legacy

The Necronomicon became a cornerstone of the Cthulhu Mythos and of pop-culture occultism: hoax editions appeared in the 1970s (the "Simon Necronomicon"), it features in the Evil Dead films as the Necronomicon Ex-Mortis, and university catalogues have had to fend off prank records of its holdings.
