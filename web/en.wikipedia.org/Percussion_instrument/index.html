Percussion instrument

A percussion instrument is a musical instrument that is sounded by being struck or scraped by a beater including attached or enclosed beaters or rattles, struck, scraped or rubbed by hand, or struck against another similar instrument. The percussion family is believed to include the oldest musical instruments, following the human voice.

Classification

In the Hornbostel–Sachs system, percussion instruments are divided into idiophones, where the instrument body itself vibrates (cymbals, xylophone, triangle), and membranophones, where a stretched membrane vibrates (most drums). Percussion is also divided into pitched instruments, such as timpani, marimba and tubular bells, and unpitched instruments, such as snare drum, bass drum and cymbals.

In the orchestra

The percussion section of an orchestra typically includes timpani plus a wide assortment of drums, cymbals, mallet instruments and accessories played by several percussionists.

In popular music

The drum kit is the rhythmic backbone of most popular music genres. Latin percussion such as congas, bongos and timbales is widespread, as are hand instruments like shakers and tambourines.
