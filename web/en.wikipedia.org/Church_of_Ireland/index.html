The Church of Ireland (Irish: Eaglais na hÉireann) is a Christian church in Ireland and an autonomous province of the Anglican Communion. It is organised on an all-Ireland basis and is the second-largest Christian church on the island after the Roman Catholic Church.

History

The church traces its roots to the early Christian mission associated with Saint Patrick in the fifth century. During the English Reformation, the Irish church broke with Rome and was established by law; from 1801 to 1871 it was united with the Church of England as the United Church of England and Ireland. The Irish Church Act 1869 disestablished it, effective 1871, after which it reorganised as an independent voluntary body.

Structure

The church comprises two provinces, Armagh and Dublin, each headed by an archbishop; the Archbishop of Armagh is Primate of All Ireland. It is governed episcopally and by the General Synod, which includes bishops, clergy, and lay representatives. There are eleven dioceses following a 2022 restructuring.

Beliefs and worship

The Church of Ireland is broadly Reformed in doctrine and uses the Book of Common Prayer, first published in Irish translation in 1608 and most recently revised in 2004. It ordains women as priests (from 1990) and bishops (from 2013).
