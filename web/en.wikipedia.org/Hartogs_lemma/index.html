Hartogs's lemma

Hartogs's lemma concerns analytic continuation across a compact subset of a domain.

Hartogs's lemma, also known as Hartogs's extension theorem in one of its forms, is a fundamental result in the theory of functions of several complex variables, proved by the German mathematician Friedrich Hartogs (1874–1943). It illustrates the striking difference between holomorphic functions of one variable and of several variables: in several variables, holomorphic functions automatically extend across certain holes in their domain.

Statement

Let n ≥ 2 and let Ω be a domain in Cn. Suppose that K is a compact subset of Ω such that Ω \ K is connected. Then every holomorphic function on Ω \ K extends uniquely to a holomorphic function on all of Ω. In other words, a holomorphic function of several variables cannot have an isolated singularity or even a compact singular set: singularities must "reach the boundary" of the domain.

Context and consequences

The lemma is closely related to Hartogs's phenomenon and to the Hartogs figure, a subdomain of the unit bidisc in C2 such that every holomorphic function on the figure extends to the whole bidisc. Together with results such as the Riemann extension theorem and the theory of domains of holomorphy, Hartogs's lemma helped establish several complex variables as a distinctive field, in which phenomena like removable compact singularities have no analogue in one complex variable.

The result also implies that there are no isolated zeros accumulating in the same way, and it is a stepping stone toward the Cousin problems and the theory of Stein manifolds.
