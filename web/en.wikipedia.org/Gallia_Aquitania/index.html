Gallia Aquitania

Gallia Aquitania was a province of the Roman Empire, together with Gallia Lugdunensis, Gallia Belgica, and Gallia Narbonensis one of the four provinces into which Augustus divided Gaul. It corresponded roughly to the southwestern region of modern France, extending at its greatest extent from the Pyrenees and the Atlantic coast northward to the river Loire.

History

The original territory of the Aquitani, a people speaking a language related to Basque, lay between the Garonne and the Pyrenees and was subdued by Julius Caesar during the Gallic Wars and initially by Crassus in 56 BC. Augustus enlarged the province considerably by adding fourteen Celtic peoples north of the Garonne, giving it a population that was largely Celtic in culture despite the province's name.

The provincial capital was Mediolanum Santonum (Saintes) and later Burdigala (Bordeaux). In the reforms of Diocletian the province was divided into Aquitania Prima, Aquitania Secunda, and Novempopulania ("land of the nine peoples"), the latter corresponding more closely to the original Aquitanian territory. In the fifth century the region passed to the Visigoths, whose Kingdom of Toulouse controlled it until the Frankish victory at the Battle of Vouillé in 507.
