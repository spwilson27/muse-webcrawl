Colonial Office

The Colonial Office was a department of the British government responsible for administering the United Kingdom's colonial empire. Created in its modern form in 1854, it oversaw British colonies, protectorates, and mandates until it was merged into the Foreign and Commonwealth Office in 1968.

History

Responsibility for the colonies had passed among various offices since the 17th century. A separate Secretary of State for War and the Colonies existed from 1801; in 1854 the roles split, creating a dedicated Colonial Office. The office administered most of the empire outside India, which had its own India Office.

Decline and abolition

As decolonization accelerated after the Second World War, the Colonial Office shrank. In 1966 it merged with the Commonwealth Relations Office to form the Commonwealth Office, which itself merged with the Foreign Office in 1968, ending the Colonial Office's separate existence.

Legacy

The office's records, held at the UK National Archives, are a major source for the history of the British Empire and of the many nations that emerged from it.
