Wilfred Ruprecht Bion (1897–1979) was a British psychoanalyst who became president of the British Psychoanalytical Society from 1962 to 1965. Born in India and educated in England, he served as a tank officer in the First World War, winning the Distinguished Service Order, before studying medicine and entering psychoanalysis, where he was analysed by John Rickman and later by Melanie Klein.

Work on groups

At the Tavistock Clinic during and after the Second World War, Bion studied small groups of patients and officers, developing the theory that groups oscillate between a rational "work group" and unconscious "basic assumption" states characterised by dependency, fight–flight, and pairing. His papers were collected in Experiences in Groups (1961), a foundational text in group dynamics and organisational studies.

Theory of thinking

Bion later developed an influential, if famously abstract, theory of thought: raw sensory and emotional impressions ("beta elements") are transformed by "alpha function" — originally the mother's reverie, the "container" processing the infant's "contained" projections — into elements usable for dreaming and thinking. His books Learning from Experience (1962) and Elements of Psycho-analysis (1963), with its notation system "the Grid," strongly influenced Kleinian and post-Kleinian psychoanalysis. He spent his final decade practising in Los Angeles before returning to England shortly before his death.
