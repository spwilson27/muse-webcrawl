Operation Serval

Operation Serval (French: Opération Serval) was a French military operation in Mali. The aim of the operation was to oust Islamic militants from the north of Mali, who had begun a push into the centre of the country. Operation Serval followed UN Security Council Resolution 2085 of 20 December 2012 and an appeal by the Malian interim government for French military assistance. The operation began on 11 January 2013 and ended on 15 July 2014, when it was replaced by the broader regional Operation Barkhane.

Background

In 2012 Tuareg separatists of the MNLA, soon eclipsed by Islamist groups including al-Qaeda in the Islamic Maghreb (AQIM), Ansar Dine and MUJAO, seized Mali's three northern regions following a coup in Bamako. In January 2013 Islamist columns advanced south, capturing Konna and threatening Bamako; interim president Dioncounda Traoré requested French help, and President François Hollande authorised intervention the same day.

Course of the operation

French air strikes on 11 January halted the advance at Konna; a helicopter pilot was killed on the first day. French ground forces deployed from Chad and France, aided by allied airlift. With Malian and African troops they retook Gao and Timbuktu by the end of January, and in early February French paratroopers seized Kidal's airport. In February–March 2013, French, Malian and Chadian forces fought a hard mountain campaign in the Adrar des Ifoghas against entrenched jihadist fighters. MINUSMA, the UN peacekeeping mission, took over security responsibilities from July 2013, and Mali held elections later that year.

Aftermath

Serval was judged a tactical success: northern cities were recovered and jihadist infrastructure degraded, with relatively few French losses. However, the insurgency reconstituted over the following decade; Serval's successor, Operation Barkhane, continued until France withdrew from Mali in 2022 amid deteriorating relations with Mali's junta.
