Joint Security Area

The Joint Security Area (JSA, often referred to as the Truce Village or Panmunjom) is the only portion of the Korean Demilitarized Zone (DMZ) where North and South Korean forces stand face-to-face. It was established under the 1953 Korean Armistice Agreement as a neutral venue for negotiations between the United Nations Command and the Korean People's Army, and is the site of the iconic blue conference huts straddling the Military Demarcation Line.

Layout and function

The JSA lies about 800 metres south of the original village of Panmunjom. Its conference row consists of buildings administered alternately by the two sides; the central huts are bisected by the demarcation line, allowing delegates to cross between North and South within a single room. The area hosts Military Armistice Commission meetings and has been a site for talks, defections, and incidents, including the 1976 axe murder incident.

Recent history

Following the 2018 Panmunjom Declaration, the two Koreas and the UN Command withdrew firearms and guard posts from the JSA, intending it to become a demilitarized zone for tourism. Tours for civilians operated from both sides, though they have been periodically suspended amid tensions, including after a 2023 incident in which a U.S. soldier crossed into the North.
