In probability theory and statistics, covariance is a measure of the joint variability of two random variables. If the greater values of one variable mainly correspond with the greater values of the other variable, the covariance is positive; if greater values of one correspond with lesser values of the other, it is negative. The sign of the covariance shows the tendency in the linear relationship between the variables.

Definition and properties

For random variables X and Y with means μX and μY, the covariance is cov(X, Y) = E[(X − μX)(Y − μY)] = E[XY] − μXμY. The covariance of a variable with itself is its variance. Uncorrelated variables have covariance zero, though zero covariance does not in general imply independence. Unlike correlation, covariance is not scaled to lie between −1 and 1, which makes it harder to interpret.

Applications

Covariance matrices generalise the concept to vectors of variables and are fundamental in portfolio theory, principal component analysis, the Kalman filter, and multivariate statistics. In genetics, genetic covariance measures how genetic values influence two traits together.
