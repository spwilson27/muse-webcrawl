Anangu

Aṉangu is the name used by Aboriginal people of the Western Desert region of central Australia to refer to themselves. The term, from Western Desert languages such as Pitjantjatjara and Yankunytjatjara, means "human being" or "person". It is widely used in reference to the traditional owners of Uluṟu-Kata Tjuṯa National Park in the Northern Territory.

Country and culture

Aṉangu country covers a vast area of arid central Australia spanning parts of the Northern Territory, South Australia, and Western Australia. Aṉangu law and culture, known as Tjukurpa, encompasses law, spirituality, land ownership, and moral obligations, expressed through story, song, dance, and rock art.

Uluṟu

Aṉangu are the traditional custodians of Uluṟu and Kata Tjuṯa. Title to the park was handed back to Aṉangu in 1985, and the land is leased back to Parks Australia and jointly managed. At Aṉangu request, the climb of Uluṟu was permanently closed in October 2019 out of respect for the site's spiritual significance.

Languages

Most Aṉangu speak Pitjantjatjara or Yankunytjatjara as a first language, often alongside other Western Desert dialects and English. Language and cultural programs operate in communities such as Mutitjulu, near Uluṟu.
