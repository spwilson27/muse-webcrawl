Showrooming

Practice: Examining goods in store, buying online
Enabled by: Smartphones, price comparison
Countermeasures: Price matching, exclusive products, omnichannel retail

Showrooming is the practice of examining merchandise in a traditional brick-and-mortar retail store and then buying it online, often at a lower price. The spread of smartphones with barcode-scanning and price-comparison apps in the early 2010s made showrooming a widely discussed challenge for physical retailers.

Impact

Retailers complained that they bore the cost of shops and staff while online sellers — with lower overheads and, in some jurisdictions, tax advantages — captured the sale. The phenomenon was blamed for pressure on electronics and book retailers in particular.

Responses

Stores responded with price matching, exclusive or own-brand products, loyalty programmes, improved in-store service, and omnichannel strategies such as buy-online-pick-up-in-store. The opposite behaviour — researching online and buying in store — is called webrooming, and many retailers now design for both.
