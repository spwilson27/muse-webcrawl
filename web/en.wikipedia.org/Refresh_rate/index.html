Refresh rate

The refresh rate, also known as the vertical refresh rate or vertical scan rate, is the number of times per second that a raster-based display device displays a new image. It is usually expressed in hertz (Hz). For example, a display with a refresh rate of 60 Hz redraws the image 60 times each second.

CRT displays

On cathode-ray tube (CRT) displays, the refresh rate is the rate at which the electron beam redraws the whole screen. Low refresh rates on CRTs produce visible flicker, because the phosphors fade between scans; rates of 72 Hz and above were generally considered flicker-free for most viewers. Refresh rates were tied to the timing of the video signal and the vertical blanking interval.

LCD and modern displays

Liquid-crystal displays do not flicker in the same way, because each pixel holds its state until updated. Nevertheless, refresh rate still matters: higher rates reduce motion blur, improve the perceived smoothness of scrolling and animation, and lower input latency. Consumer displays commonly run at 60 Hz, while gaming monitors offer 144 Hz, 240 Hz, or more.

Variable refresh rate (VRR) technologies such as AMD FreeSync and NVIDIA G-Sync allow the display to synchronise its refresh to the frame rate produced by the graphics processor, reducing screen tearing and stutter.

Relation to frame rate

Refresh rate is a property of the display, whereas frame rate is a property of the content or rendering pipeline. If the frame rate exceeds the refresh rate without synchronisation, screen tearing can occur; if it falls below, judder or repeated frames result.
