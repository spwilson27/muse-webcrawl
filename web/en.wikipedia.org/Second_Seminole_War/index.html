Second Seminole War

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Seminoles used Florida's swamps and wetlands to resist removal for seven years.

The Second Seminole War, also known as the Florida War (1835–1842), was a conflict in Florida between the United States Army and groups of Native Americans collectively known as the Seminoles, consisting of Creek and Miccosukee peoples. It was part of the series of conflicts called the Seminole Wars, and it arose from American efforts to forcibly remove the Seminoles to Indian Territory west of the Mississippi River under the Indian Removal Act.

Background

Under the Treaty of Moultrie Creek (1823), the Seminoles had been confined to a reservation in central Florida. Pressure from white settlers, the Indian Removal Act of 1830, and the contested Treaty of Payne's Landing (1832) — which the United States claimed committed the Seminoles to emigrate — set the stage for war. Many Seminoles, encouraged by leaders such as Osceola, refused to leave.

Course of the war

The war began in late December 1835 with the Seminole ambush of Major Francis Dade's command — the "Dade Massacre" — and the killing of Indian agent Wiley Thompson by Osceola. The Seminoles waged guerrilla warfare in the swamps and hammocks, defeating or frustrating successive U.S. commanders. Osceola was captured under a flag of truce in 1837 and died in prison at Fort Moultrie in 1838, an act widely condemned. Leaders such as Billy Bowlegs and Abiaka (Sam Jones) continued resistance.

End and legacy

After seven years, the United States declared the war over in August 1842 without a formal treaty or surrender. Most Seminoles had been killed or removed to Oklahoma, but a few hundred remained in the Everglades and Big Cypress Swamp; their descendants, the Seminole Tribe of Florida and the Miccosukee Tribe, never signed a peace treaty with the United States. The war was one of the costliest Indian wars in U.S. history, claiming the lives of around 1,600 U.S. soldiers, many from disease, and costing tens of millions of dollars.
