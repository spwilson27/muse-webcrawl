Combined Charging System

Combined Charging System (CCS)
Type: EV charging connector
Variants: Combo 1 (North America), Combo 2 (Europe)
Charging: AC and DC fast charging
Max power: up to 350 kW (liquid-cooled)

The Combined Charging System (CCS) is a standard for charging electric vehicles that combines a connector for alternating current (AC) charging with two additional pins for direct current (DC) fast charging in a single inlet. It exists in two regional variants: Combo 1, built on the SAE J1772 (Type 1) connector used in North America, and Combo 2, built on the Type 2 (Mennekes) connector used in Europe.

History

CCS was developed by the CharIN association, founded by German and international automakers and suppliers, and was first demonstrated in 2011. It was adopted for the European Union's alternative fuels infrastructure requirements, making Combo 2 the standard DC fast-charging interface on new European charging stations.

Technology

The system communicates between vehicle and charger using power-line communication based on the HomePlug GreenPHY standard. Early implementations supported DC charging up to about 200 amps; high-power charging versions with liquid-cooled cables reach up to 500 amps and 1000 volts, enabling charging power of up to roughly 350–500 kW.

Competition

CCS competes with CHAdeMO, the Tesla-developed North American Charging Standard (NACS, standardized as SAE J3400), and China's GB/T standard. In 2023 several major automakers announced adoption of NACS in North America, while CCS remains dominant in Europe.
