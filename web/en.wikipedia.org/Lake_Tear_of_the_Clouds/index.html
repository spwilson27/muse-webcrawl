Lake Tear of the Clouds

Lake Tear of the Clouds is a small mountain lake in Essex County, New York, in the Adirondack Mountains. Lying in a col between Mount Marcy and Mount Skylight at an elevation of about 1,322 m (4,338 ft), it is often cited as the highest lake in the state and is regarded as the source of the Hudson River.

Geography

The lake drains via Feldspar Brook into the Opalescent River, which flows into the upper Hudson. Though Lake Tear of the Clouds is popularly described as the Hudson's source, the river's hydrologic source is more precisely Henderson Lake, further downstream along the same drainage. The lake was named by Verplanck Colvin, superintendent of the Adirondack Survey, who reached it during his surveys in the 1870s.

Theodore Roosevelt

The lake is famous in American history because of the events of 13–14 September 1901. Vice President Theodore Roosevelt had climbed Mount Marcy and was descending past the lake when a messenger arrived with the news that President William McKinley, shot eight days earlier in Buffalo, was dying. Roosevelt made a celebrated midnight buckboard ride from the Tahawus area to the railhead at North Creek, where he learned McKinley had died, and then travelled to Buffalo to be sworn in as the 26th President.
