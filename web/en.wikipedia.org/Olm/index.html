The olm or proteus (Proteus anguinus) is a blind amphibian endemic to the subterranean waters that flow through the limestone karst of the Dinaric Alps of southeastern Europe. It is the only exclusively cave-dwelling chordate found in Europe, and the only species in the genus Proteus. Locals once believed the pale creatures washed out of caves by floods were the offspring of dragons.

Description

The olm has an elongated, snake-like body 20–30 cm long, with short limbs (three toes on the front legs, two on the hind). Its skin lacks pigmentation and appears white-pink; because its eyes are undeveloped and covered by skin, it is blind. It retains larval features throughout life (neoteny), including feathery red external gills, and breathes through gills, lungs, and skin.

Senses and behavior

To navigate permanent darkness, the olm has extraordinary non-visual senses: acute smell and hearing, sensitivity to vibrations, and electroreception via ampullae of Lorenzini-like organs on its head, allowing it to detect the weak electric fields of prey. It feeds on small crustaceans, snails, and insect larvae. Olms are famously hardy: individuals in captivity have survived without food for years, and a 2010 study estimated a maximum lifespan exceeding 100 years, exceptional for so small an animal. They reproduce slowly, laying eggs or — in colder water — giving birth to live young, and may breed only once a decade.

Conservation

The olm is listed as Vulnerable by the IUCN and protected across its range. It is highly sensitive to pollution of the karst groundwater. A black, pigmented subspecies, P. a. parkelj, with developed eyes, was discovered in Slovenia in 1986. The olm features on Slovenian coins and is a symbol of the Postojna Cave, where a captive population is maintained.
