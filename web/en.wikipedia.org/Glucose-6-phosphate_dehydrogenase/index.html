Glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase

Glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) is an enzyme in the pentose phosphate pathway that produces NADPH and ribose-5-phosphate. It converts glucose-6-phosphate to 6-phosphoglucono-δ-lactone and is the rate-limiting step of the pathway.

G6PD
EC: 1.1.1.49
Gene: G6PD (Xq28)
Pathway: Pentose phosphate

Function

NADPH from G6PD maintains reduced glutathione, protecting red blood cells from oxidative damage, and supports reductive biosynthesis.

Deficiency

G6PD deficiency, an X-linked trait affecting ~400 million people, causes haemolytic anaemia triggered by fava beans (favism), infections and drugs such as primaquine. It likely persists due to protection against malaria. See X chromosome.
