Fragaria vesca, commonly called the woodland strawberry, wild strawberry, or alpine strawberry, is a perennial herbaceous plant in the rose family that grows naturally throughout much of the Northern Hemisphere. It produces small, intensely flavoured red fruit.

Description

The plant grows low to the ground with trifoliate leaves and small white flowers with five petals. It spreads by runners (stolons) as well as by seed. Unlike the modern garden strawberry, its fruit is small — usually under two centimetres — but noted for a strong, aromatic flavour prized by foragers and gourmet cooks.

Distribution and habitat

Fragaria vesca occurs in woods, banks, and grassy places across Europe, northern Asia, and North America. It has been consumed by humans since the Stone Age and was cultivated in Persian gardens from antiquity; it was the dominant cultivated strawberry in Europe until the 18th century, when it was displaced by hybrids of American species.

Genomics

The species is diploid with a small genome of about 240 million base pairs, sequenced in 2010, which makes it a useful model organism for the Rosaceae family.
