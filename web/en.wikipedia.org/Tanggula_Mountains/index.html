Tanggula Mountains

Tanggula Mountains (Tibetan: གདང་ལ་; Chinese: 唐古拉山) — a mountain range on the central Tibetan Plateau, forming the source region of the Yangtze River. Its highest peak, Geladaindong, rises 6,621 m. The Qinghai–Tibet railway crosses the range at the Tanggula Pass, 5,072 m.

The Tanggula Mountains (Tibetan: གདང་ལ་, Wylie: Gdang La; Chinese: 唐古拉山; pinyin: Tánggǔlāshān) are a mountain range in the central part of the Tibetan Plateau, straddling the border between the Tibet Autonomous Region and Qinghai Province, China. The range runs roughly east–west for several hundred kilometres at elevations mostly above 5,000 metres.

Geography

The main peak, Geladaindong (6,621 m), stands at the headwaters of the Tuotuo River, regarded as a source stream of the Yangtze. Other rivers rising in the range include tributaries of the Salween (Nu) and Mekong (Lancang). The range forms a watershed between rivers flowing east to the Pacific and those draining the interior plateau.

Transport

The Qinghai–Tibet Highway and the Qinghai–Tibet Railway cross the range at the Tanggula Pass (5,072 m / 16,640 ft). Tanggula railway station, at 5,068 m, is the highest railway station in the world. The region is sparsely inhabited, with permafrost, alpine steppe, and wildlife such as Tibetan antelope in surrounding reserves.
