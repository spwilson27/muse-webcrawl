The App Store is a digital distribution service operated by Apple Inc. for applications ("apps") on its operating systems. Launched on 10 July 2008 alongside the iPhone 3G and iPhone OS 2, it lets users browse and download apps developed with Apple's software development kits, distributed as free downloads or for a price, with Apple traditionally retaining a 30% commission (15% for many smaller developers and subscriptions after the first year).

History and scale

The store opened with about 500 applications and grew explosively: it passed 10 billion downloads in 2011 and has hosted millions of apps, becoming central to the "app economy". Apple reviews apps submitted by registered developers against its guidelines before they can be sold, a model that defined the modern mobile marketplace but also drew criticism for gatekeeping.

Controversies and regulation

Apple's commission and rules on in-app purchases have been challenged by developers — most prominently in Epic Games v. Apple (2020–2021) — and by regulators; the European Union's Digital Markets Act has required Apple to allow alternative app marketplaces and payment options in the EU. Surveys of the store's economic impact are regularly published by Apple and by independent analysts.
