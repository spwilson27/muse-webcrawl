Deep-sea exploration

The bathyscaphe Trieste, used for the first descent to Challenger Deep

Deep-sea exploration is the investigation of physical, chemical, and biological conditions on the seabed and in the deep ocean, for scientific or commercial purposes. It is conducted with crewed submersibles, remotely operated vehicles (ROVs), and autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs).

History

Milestones include the Challenger expedition (1872–76), the bathysphere dives of William Beebe and Otis Barton in the 1930s, and the 1960 descent of the bathyscaphe Trieste to Challenger Deep in the Mariana Trench. The submersible Alvin later enabled discoveries of hydrothermal vents in 1977 and exploration of the wreck of the Titanic.

Challenges

Extreme pressure, darkness, cold, and the corrosive nature of seawater make deep-sea exploration technically demanding and expensive.
