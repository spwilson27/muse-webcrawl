Twin-lens reflex camera
Abbreviation: TLR
Famous models: Rolleiflex, Rolleicord, Mamiya C series
Typical film: 120 roll film

A twin-lens reflex camera (TLR) is a type of camera with two objective lenses of the same focal length. One lens takes the photograph while the other is used for viewing: its image is reflected by a fixed mirror onto a ground-glass focusing screen viewed from above.

Design

The viewing and taking lenses are mounted together, usually on a panel that moves for focusing, so both remain matched. Because the viewing system uses a separate lens, the photographer sees the scene continuously, even during exposure, and there is no mirror blackout. Drawbacks include parallax error at close distances and a laterally reversed waist-level image.

History

The design was popularised by the German Rolleiflex of 1928 and remained a favourite of portrait and press photographers through the mid-20th century, prized for quiet operation and sharp medium-format negatives.
