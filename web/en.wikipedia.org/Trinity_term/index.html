Trinity term

Trinity term is the summer term at Oxford, when examinations are held and punting on the river is a traditional pastime.

Trinity term is the third and final term of the academic year at several British and Irish universities, most notably the University of Oxford and the University of Dublin (Trinity College Dublin). The name derives from Trinity Sunday, the feast around which the term historically began; it is the summer term, running roughly from late April to the end of June (at Oxford) and is the term in which most final examinations are held.

At Oxford

The University of Oxford's academic year is divided into three terms of eight weeks each:

TermApproximate dates
Michaelmas termOctober to December
Hilary termJanuary to March
Trinity termApril to June

At Oxford, Trinity term (sometimes called Trinity Full Term in the University's statutes) runs from the third Sunday after Easter — calculated with reference to Trinity Sunday, which falls after Pentecost — for eight weeks. It is the season of examinations ("Mods" and "Finals" in earlier eras), May Morning celebrations on 1 May, Eights Week rowing, Commemoration balls, and Encaenia, the ceremony at which honorary degrees are conferred.

Other uses

Trinity term is also one of the four terms of the legal year in England and Wales, running from late May or early June (after the Trinity law sitting break following Easter) to the end of July, when the courts sit before the long summer vacation. The term was historically one of the four "terms" — Hilary, Easter, Trinity and Michaelmas — during which the superior courts sat at Westminster.

At Cambridge the corresponding summer term is called Easter term, while several other universities in the UK, Ireland and the Commonwealth retain "Trinity term" for their third term. At Trinity College Dublin the academic year is divided into Michaelmas, Hilary and Trinity terms, mirroring Oxford.
