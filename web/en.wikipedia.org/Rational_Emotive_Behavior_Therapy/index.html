Rational Emotive Behavior Therapy (REBT) is a form of psychotherapy developed by the American psychologist Albert Ellis beginning in 1955. Originally called Rational Therapy and later Rational Emotive Therapy, it was among the earliest forms of cognitive behavioural therapy. REBT holds that emotional disturbance is caused largely not by events themselves but by the irrational beliefs people hold about those events.

The ABC model

REBT's central framework is the ABC model: an Activating event triggers Beliefs, which produce emotional and behavioural Consequences. Therapy adds Disputation of irrational beliefs and the development of an Effective new philosophy. Ellis identified core irrational beliefs such as the demand that one must be approved of by everyone, that one must be thoroughly competent at all times, and that the world must be fair and comfortable.

Techniques

REBT is an active-directive therapy. Techniques include cognitive disputation, rational emotive imagery, shame-attacking exercises (deliberately doing something mildly embarrassing to learn that disapproval is tolerable), and behavioural homework. Ellis distinguished "elegant" REBT, aimed at deep philosophical change, from general cognitive behavioural therapy focused on symptom relief.

Influence

Ellis founded the Albert Ellis Institute in New York and popularised REBT through books such as Reason and Emotion in Psychotherapy (1962) and, with Robert A. Harper, A Guide to Rational Living (1961). REBT influenced Aaron Beck's cognitive therapy, though the two approaches differ in style and philosophy.
