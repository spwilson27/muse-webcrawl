Anguilla

Anguilla (/æŋˈɡwɪlə/, ang-GWIL-ə) is a British Overseas Territory in the Caribbean, the most northerly of the Leeward Islands in the Lesser Antilles. It consists of the flat, low-lying coral and limestone main island of Anguilla itself, approximately 26 km (16 mi) long by 5 km (3 mi) wide, together with a number of much smaller islands and cays. The capital is The Valley, and the population is about 15,000. Renowned for its beaches, Anguilla's economy rests on luxury tourism, offshore banking and fishing. The territory is also notable for deriving significant government revenue from its .ai Internet country code domain, in demand from artificial-intelligence companies.

History

Amerindian peoples, including the Arawak, settled Anguilla long before Europeans arrived; the island's indigenous name was Malliouhana. English settlers from St Kitts colonised it from 1650. Administered as part of the St Kitts-Nevis-Anguilla colony, Anguillans resented rule from Basseterre, and in the Anguilla Revolution of 1967 they expelled the St Kitts police and twice declared independence under Ronald Webster. British troops restored order in 1969 (Operation Sheepskin), and the Anguilla Act 1971 placed the island under direct British rule; in 1980 it formally separated as a British crown colony, now an Overseas Territory — a rare case of a population revolting to remain British.

Geography and climate

The main island is flat and arid, composed of coral limestone with scrub vegetation, salt ponds, and 33 beaches frequently ranked among the world's finest, including Shoal Bay and Meads Bay. The climate is tropical, moderated by trade winds, with average temperatures around 27 °C and a hurricane season from June to November; Hurricane Irma devastated the island in September 2017. There are no rivers, and drinking water comes from wells, rainwater cisterns and desalination.

Economy

With few natural resources, Anguilla's economy depends on high-end tourism, offshore financial services, lobster fishing and remittances. Uniquely, the territory licenses the .ai domain; registrations surged with the AI boom, and domain fees have provided a substantial share of government revenue in the 2020s.

Politics and culture

Anguilla is governed under a 1982 constitution (revised 2019) with a House of Assembly and a Premier, while the Governor, appointed by the British monarch, retains responsibility for external affairs, defence and internal security. English is official, spoken alongside Anguillian Creole. Boat racing is the national sport, reflecting the island's seafaring heritage, and the summer carnival and Moonsplash music festival anchor the cultural calendar.
