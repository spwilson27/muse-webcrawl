Decision tree

TypeDecision support / predictive model
Related methodsRandom forest, gradient boosting

A decision tree is a decision support tool that uses a tree-like model of decisions and their possible consequences, including chance event outcomes, resource costs, and utility. In machine learning, decision trees are also widely used as predictive models that map observations about an item to conclusions about its target value.

Structure

A decision tree consists of internal nodes representing tests on attributes, branches representing the outcomes of those tests, and leaf nodes representing class labels or continuous values. The path from the root to a leaf constitutes a classification rule.

Learning

Tree-learning algorithms such as ID3, C4.5, and CART construct trees from training data by recursively choosing the attribute that best splits the data, commonly measured by information gain or Gini impurity. Pruning is used to reduce overfitting by removing branches that contribute little predictive power.

Advantages and limitations

Decision trees are simple to understand and interpret, require little data preparation, and can handle both numerical and categorical data. However, single trees can be unstable and prone to overfitting; ensemble methods such as random forests and gradient-boosted trees address these weaknesses.
