Palace of Versailles

The Palace of Versailles (French: Château de Versailles) is a former royal residence built by King Louis XIV located in Versailles, about 19 kilometres (12 mi) west of Paris, France. The palace is owned by the French Republic and has since 1995 been managed, under the direction of the French Ministry of Culture, by the Public Establishment of the Palace, Museum and National Estate of Versailles.

History

The site began as a hunting lodge built by Louis XIII in 1623, later enlarged into a château. His son, Louis XIV, the "Sun King", transformed Versailles into the centre of political power in France, moving the court there permanently in 1682. Successive expansions produced the great palace that housed the court and government until the French Revolution of 1789 forced the royal family back to Paris.

Under Louis XV and Louis XVI the palace continued to evolve, with the Petit Trianon built for Madame de Pompadour and later associated with Marie Antoinette, whose hamlet (the Hameau de la Reine) recreated a rustic village in the grounds. After the Revolution the palace was largely emptied of its furnishings, but in the 1830s Louis-Philippe converted it into a museum of French history.

The Palace

The palace comprises some 2,300 rooms spread over 63,154 m². Its most celebrated space is the Hall of Mirrors (Galerie des Glaces), 73 metres long, where 357 mirrors face seventeen arched windows overlooking the gardens; it was here that the Treaty of Versailles ending the First World War was signed in 1919. Other highlights include the Royal Opera, the Royal Chapel, and the Grand Apartments of the king and queen.

Gardens

The Gardens of Versailles, designed by André Le Nôtre, cover roughly 800 hectares and are the canonical example of the French formal garden, with geometric parterres, ornamental lakes, canals, and dozens of fountains fed by an elaborate hydraulic system. The Grand Canal stretches 1.6 km east–west across the park.
