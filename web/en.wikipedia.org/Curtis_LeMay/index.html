Curtis Emerson LeMay (November 15, 1906 – October 1, 1990) was a US Air Force general who implemented an effective but controversial strategic bombing campaign in the Pacific theatre of World War II. He later served as Chief of Staff of the United States Air Force from 1961 to 1965.

World War II

LeMay built a reputation in the European theatre commanding B-17 groups of the Eighth Air Force, where he developed formation and bombing tactics and led the Regensburg raid of August 1943. Transferred to the Pacific, he took command of XXI Bomber Command in January 1945 and shifted the B-29 campaign against Japan from high-altitude precision bombing to low-altitude night incendiary attacks, beginning with the firebombing of Tokyo on 9–10 March 1945 — one of the most destructive air raids in history. His command also laid naval mines in Operation Starvation and flew the atomic bomb missions' supporting operations.

Cold War

As commander of Strategic Air Command from 1948 to 1957, LeMay transformed it into the core of America's nuclear deterrent, emphasising readiness, standardisation and the ability to strike the Soviet Union. As Air Force Chief of Staff during the Cuban Missile Crisis he advocated stronger military action against Cuba, clashing with President Kennedy's civilian leadership.

Later life

In 1968 LeMay was the vice-presidential running mate of segregationist George Wallace on the American Independent Party ticket, a campaign that damaged his reputation. He died in 1990 at March Air Force Base, California. His blunt maxim that his job was to "kill people and break things" — and his reported remark about bombing North Vietnam "back into the Stone Age" — remain central to debates about air power and morality.
