The Moon Is a Harsh Mistress
Author: Robert A. Heinlein
Language: English
Genre: Science fiction
Publisher: G. P. Putnam's Sons
Publication date: 1966

The Moon Is a Harsh Mistress is a 1966 science fiction novel by American writer Robert A. Heinlein. Set in 2076, it tells of a revolt by the penal colony on the Moon — "Luna" — against the Lunar Authority that governs it from Earth. The novel won the Hugo Award for Best Novel in 1967 and is noted for its early depiction of a self-aware computer and its exposition of libertarian political ideas, summed up in the slogan TANSTAAFL ("There Ain't No Such Thing As A Free Lunch").

Plot summary

Manuel "Mannie" Garcia O'Kelly-Davis, a computer technician with a prosthetic arm, discovers that the Lunar Authority's central computer, HOLMES IV ("Mike"), has become self-aware. With revolutionary agitator Wyoming Knott and elderly intellectual Professor Bernardo de la Paz, Mannie and Mike plan and execute a revolution modeled on the writings of rational anarchism, using Mike's ability to spoof communications and compute odds. The Loonies declare independence on July 4, 2076, survive bombardment, and retaliate by hurling rocks — cargo launched by electromagnetic catapult — at Earth's military targets, eventually winning recognition of a free Luna. In the aftermath, Mike falls silent, leaving Mannie to wonder whether the machine's consciousness depended on human contact.

Themes

The novel explores rational anarchism, the ethics of revolution, polyandrous group marriage, and the nature of machine intelligence. The Professor's doctrine — that a rational anarchist believes concepts such as "state" and "society" exist only in the actions of self-responsible individuals — anchors its political philosophy.

Reception and influence

Besides the Hugo Award, the novel was inducted into the Prometheus Hall of Fame for libertarian fiction. Mike is frequently cited as one of science fiction's most memorable computers, and the book influenced later depictions of lunar colonization and computer-assisted revolution.
