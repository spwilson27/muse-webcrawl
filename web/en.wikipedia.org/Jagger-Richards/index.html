Jagger–Richards (also credited as "The Glimmer Twins") is the songwriting partnership of Mick Jagger and Keith Richards, the creative core of the English rock band the Rolling Stones. Together they have written the majority of the band's catalogue, including many of the most famous songs in rock music.

History

Manager Andrew Loog Oldham pushed Jagger and Richards to write their own material in 1963, famously locking them in a kitchen until they produced a song. Their early compositions, such as "As Tears Go By", were given to other artists, but from 1965 onward songs like "(I Can't Get No) Satisfaction", "Paint It Black", "Jumpin' Jack Flash", and "Sympathy for the Devil" defined the Stones' sound.

Style

Richards typically develops riffs and musical structures — famously the opening of "Satisfaction", which came to him in his sleep — while Jagger contributes lyrics and melodies, though the two roles often overlap. Their partnership is frequently ranked alongside Lennon–McCartney among rock's greatest songwriting teams.
