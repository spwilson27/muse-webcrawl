Drama Desk Award

The Drama Desk Awards are annual prizes recognizing excellence in New York theatre, presented by the Drama Desk organization. Founded in 1949 as the Vernon Rice Awards, they were renamed in 1963–64 after the Drama Desk, a group of theatre critics, editors, and reporters. The awards are unusual in that productions from Broadway, Off-Broadway, and Off-Off-Broadway compete against one another in the same categories.

History

The Drama Desk was founded in 1949 by a group of New York theatre critics, including New York Times drama editor Sam Zolotow. The first awards, named for critic Vernon Rice, were presented in 1949. In the 2012–13 season, the Drama Desk Awards were incorporated and the for-profit producing entity Drama Desk Awards LLC took over management of the ceremony, though a voting membership of critics and journalists continues to select the nominees and winners.

Categories

Awards are given in categories including Outstanding Play, Outstanding Musical, Outstanding Revival of a Play, Outstanding Revival of a Musical, Outstanding Actor and Actress in a Play and in a Musical, and numerous design categories such as set, costume, lighting, and sound design, as well as choreography, orchestration, and lyrics.

Significance

Because the Drama Desk considers Broadway and Off-Broadway productions together, its nominations sometimes spotlight smaller productions that later transfer to Broadway. The awards are typically announced in late spring, ahead of the Tony Awards, and are considered part of the New York theatre awards season alongside the Outer Critics Circle Awards and the Drama League Awards.
