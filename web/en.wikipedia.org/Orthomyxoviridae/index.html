Orthomyxoviridae is a family of negative-sense RNA viruses that includes the influenza viruses. The name derives from the Greek orthos ("straight") and myxa ("mucus"), referring to the viruses' affinity for mucus and their distinction from the paramyxoviruses. Their genomes consist of 6 to 8 linear segments of negative-sense single-stranded RNA, enclosed in a lipid envelope studded with glycoprotein spikes.

Influenza viruses

Four genera cause influenza: Influenzavirus A, whose natural reservoir is wild waterfowl and which causes pandemics in humans through antigenic shift; Influenzavirus B, largely restricted to humans; Influenzavirus C, causing mild illness; and Influenzavirus D, found in cattle and swine. The surface proteins haemagglutinin (H) and neuraminidase (N) define the subtypes of influenza A, such as H1N1 and H3N2.

Other genera

Isavirus causes infectious salmon anaemia, an economically important disease of farmed salmon. Thogotovirus and Quaranjavirus are tick-borne viruses that occasionally infect humans and livestock.

Replication

Uniquely among RNA viruses, orthomyxoviruses transcribe their genomes in the host cell nucleus, "snatching" caps from host mRNAs to prime synthesis. Reassortment of segments between co-infecting viruses can generate novel pandemic strains.
