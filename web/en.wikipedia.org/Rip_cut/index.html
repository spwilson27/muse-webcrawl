Rip cut

A rip cut is a type of cut in woodworking that runs along the grain of the wood, generally parallel to its longest dimension, as opposed to a crosscut, which runs across the grain. Ripping is most often used to reduce the width of a board or to cut timber to dimension.

Rip cutting a board along the grain using a table saw with a rip fence.

Tools and technique

Rip cuts are made by hand with a rip saw, whose teeth are filed in a chisel-like "rip" pattern with little or no set-driven bevel, shearing fibres along the grain; or by machine with a table saw fitted with a rip fence, a bandsaw, or a track saw. On a table saw the fence is set to the desired width and the stock is fed through the blade, ideally with push sticks and a riving knife to control kickback—the dangerous ejection of the work caused by the blade pinching the kerf. Because the fibres are split rather than severed, rip cuts typically leave smooth surfaces but can cause tear-out if the grain is irregular.

In sawmilling, ripping is also the process of sawing logs into boards lengthwise; the related term "rip" is used in metalworking and other trades for any straight cut along the length of a sheet material.
