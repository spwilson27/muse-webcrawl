The Federalist Society for Law and Public Policy Studies, commonly called the Federalist Society, is an American conservative and libertarian legal organization formed in 1982. It is composed of student chapters at law schools, lawyers chapters in major cities, and faculty divisions, and it describes itself as "a group of conservatives and libertarians interested in the current state of the legal order." The Society advocates the interpretive theories of originalism and textualism, separation of powers, and a limited role for the judiciary and federal government.

History

The Society was founded in 1982 by students at Yale Law School and the University of Chicago Law School, with early support from professors including Antonin Scalia and Robert Bork and funding from conservative foundations. It grew into a national network of some 200 student chapters and numerous lawyers chapters, holding an annual National Lawyers Convention in Washington, D.C. Although the Society officially takes no positions on legislation or candidates and does not endorse judicial nominees, its members and recommended lists have been highly influential in Republican judicial selection.

Influence

Members have included multiple justices of the United States Supreme Court, and network figures advised the George W. Bush and Donald Trump administrations on judicial appointments; President Trump publicly pledged to select Supreme Court nominees from lists associated with the Society's orbit, and Justices Neil Gorsuch, Brett Kavanaugh, and Amy Coney Barrett were all connected to the organization. Critics argue the Society functions as a pipeline and vetting mechanism for conservative judges, while supporters say it is a debating society that sharpened legal arguments across the profession.
