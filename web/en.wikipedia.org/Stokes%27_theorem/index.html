Stokes' theorem

Stokes' theorem is a statement in vector calculus about the integration of differential forms on manifolds. In its classical form it relates the surface integral of the curl of a vector field over a surface to the line integral of the field around the surface's boundary curve.

Classical statement

For a smooth surface Σ with boundary curve ∂Σ and a vector field F: the integral of (∇ × F) · dS over Σ equals the integral of F · dr around ∂Σ. Intuitively, the total "circulation density" inside a surface adds up to the circulation around its edge.

General form

The generalised Stokes theorem states that the integral of the exterior derivative of a differential form ω over a manifold M equals the integral of ω over the boundary of M. It unifies the fundamental theorem of calculus, Green's theorem, the classical Stokes theorem and the divergence theorem as special cases.

History

The theorem is named after George Gabriel Stokes, who set it as an examination question at Cambridge in 1854, though related results were known to Kelvin, Green and others.
