Otto Werner

Otto Werner (1 October 1849 – 26 June 1926) was a German physician and botanist known for describing the premature aging syndrome now called Werner syndrome (adult progeria).

Life and work

Werner was born in Husum, in Schleswig-Holstein. He studied medicine at the University of Kiel, where he later worked as a physician. In his 1904 doctoral thesis, Über Katarakt in Verbindung mit Sklerodermie ("On cataract in connection with scleroderma"), he described four siblings who developed cataracts, scleroderma-like skin changes, greying and thinning hair, short stature, and other signs of accelerated aging in young adulthood.

Werner syndrome

The condition Werner described is a rare autosomal recessive disorder caused by mutations in the WRN gene, which encodes a DNA helicase involved in DNA repair and maintenance of telomeres. Patients typically develop normally until puberty, then age prematurely, with increased risk of cancer, type 2 diabetes, osteoporosis, and cardiovascular disease. The syndrome was named in Werner's honor, and his 1904 thesis remains the foundational clinical description of the disorder.
