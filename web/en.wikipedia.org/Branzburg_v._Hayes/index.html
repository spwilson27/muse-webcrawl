Branzburg v. Hayes

Citation: 408 U.S. 665 (1972)
Decided: 29 June 1972
Court: Supreme Court of the United States
Issue: Reporters' privilege under the First Amendment
The U.S. Supreme Court

Branzburg v. Hayes, 408 U.S. 665 (1972), is a landmark decision of the United States Supreme Court holding that the First Amendment does not exempt journalists from the obligation of ordinary citizens to appear and testify before a grand jury regarding information received in confidence.

Background

The case consolidated appeals by reporters, including Earl Caldwell of The New York Times and Paul Branzburg, who had refused to testify before grand juries about confidential sources and activities — in Branzburg's case, his reporting on drug use and the Black Panthers in Kentucky — arguing for a constitutional "reporter's privilege".

Decision

In a 5–4 decision written by Justice Byron White, the Court held that requiring reporters to testify did not abridge freedom of the press. Justice Lewis Powell's concurrence, emphasising that courts could quash subpoenas issued in bad faith or without legitimate need, has been read by many lower courts as supporting a qualified privilege in some circumstances, and the case prompted many states to enact shield laws protecting journalists' sources.
