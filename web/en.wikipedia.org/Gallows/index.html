Gallows

A gallows (or, less precisely, gallows frame) is a frame or elevated beam, typically wooden, from which a noose is suspended for the execution of a person by hanging. Gallows have been used for judicial executions in many societies for centuries; in England, famous sites included Tyburn in London, where public executions were held until 1783.

Forms

Gallows took many forms: a single upright post with a projecting beam, frames with two uprights and a crossbeam, and later purpose-built scaffolds with a trapdoor, developed with the "long drop" method in the 19th century, in which the fall was calculated to break the neck quickly. In some places permanent stone or timber gibbet structures stood at crossroads or town boundaries, also used to display the bodies of executed criminals as a deterrent (gibbeting).

Decline

As capital punishment was abolished or restricted across most of the world during the 20th century, gallows fell out of use, though hanging remains a legal method of execution in a number of countries, and gallows are preserved at museums and former prison sites.
