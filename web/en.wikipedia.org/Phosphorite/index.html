Phosphorite

Phosphorite, or phosphate rock, is a sedimentary rock containing a high proportion of phosphate minerals, typically apatite. It is the principal source of phosphorus for the manufacture of phosphate fertilisers and is mined extensively in countries such as Morocco, China, the United States and Russia.

Formation

Phosphorites form mainly in marine environments where upwelling currents bring phosphorus-rich deep water to shallow shelves. Phosphorus from dead organisms accumulates and is precipitated as carbonate fluorapatite (francolite), often as nodules, pellets or replacement of carbonate sediments.

Uses

The overwhelming majority of mined phosphate rock is processed into phosphoric acid and fertilisers such as superphosphate and ammonium phosphates. Smaller amounts are used in animal feed supplements, detergents and industrial chemicals.

Reserves and concerns

World reserves are heavily concentrated in Morocco and Western Sahara. Because phosphorus is essential to agriculture and has no substitute, the long-term depletion of high-grade deposits — sometimes discussed under the heading of "peak phosphorus" — is a subject of ongoing study.
