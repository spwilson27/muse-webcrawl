Gmina

The gmina (Polish pronunciation: [ˈɡmʲina], plural gminy) is the principal unit of the lowest tier of territorial division and local government in Poland, comparable to a municipality or commune in other countries. The word derives from the German Gemeinde ("community"). As of recent counts there are 2,477 gminas in Poland.

Types

There are three types of gmina:

  • Urban gmina (gmina miejska) — consists of a single town or city. Poland's 66 largest cities additionally hold county status as "cities with powiat rights".
  • Urban-rural gmina (gmina miejsko-wiejska) — a town together with its surrounding villages and countryside.
  • Rural gmina (gmina wiejska) — villages and countryside only, with its seat usually in a village (occasionally in a town outside the gmina).

Government

Each gmina is governed by an elected council (rada gminy) and an executive: a wójt in rural gminas, a mayor (burmistrz) in urban-rural and smaller urban gminas, or a city president (prezydent miasta) in larger cities — since 2002 directly elected by residents. Gminas are responsible for local matters including primary schools, local roads, water supply, waste management, spatial planning, libraries and social welfare. Above the gmina sit the powiat (county) and the voivodeship (province).

Subdivisions

Rural areas within gminas are often organised into auxiliary units called sołectwa (singular sołectwo), each headed by a sołtys (village head); urban gminas may be divided into districts (dzielnice) or neighbourhoods (osiedla). The gmina system in its present form dates from the local government reforms of 1990, with the three-tier structure completed in 1999.
