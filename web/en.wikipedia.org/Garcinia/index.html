Garcinia

Garcinia is a genus of flowering plants in the family Clusiaceae, native to tropical regions of Asia, Africa, Australia, and the Pacific and Caribbean islands. The genus includes several hundred species of evergreen trees and shrubs, many of which bear edible fruit. The best-known species is the mangosteen (Garcinia mangostana), prized as a fruit throughout Southeast Asia.

Description

Garcinia species are typically dioecious evergreen trees with opposite, leathery leaves and a milky or yellow latex in the bark and fruit. The fruits are berries, often with a thick rind and juicy, segmented flesh. Many species are slow-growing and adapted to humid lowland rainforest.

Uses

Several species are used in cooking: the rind of Garcinia gummi-gutta (Malabar tamarind) and Garcinia atroviridis are used as souring agents in South and Southeast Asian cuisines, and kokum (Garcinia indica) is used in western Indian cooking and for its butter. Extracts of G. gummi-gutta, which contain hydroxycitric acid, are marketed in weight-loss supplements, although evidence for their efficacy is limited. Gamboge, a yellow pigment and traditional medicine, is obtained from the resin of several Garcinia species.
