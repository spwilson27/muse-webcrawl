Ducted fan

A ducted fan propulsion unit (placeholder image)

A ducted fan is a mechanical fan or propeller mounted within a cylindrical shroud or duct. The duct reduces losses at the blade tips, allowing the fan to produce more thrust than an open propeller of the same diameter, and it can improve safety by shielding the blades and reducing noise in certain frequency ranges. Ducted fans are used in aircraft, hovercraft, airships, drones and ventilation.

Operation

The duct's airfoil-shaped lip accelerates airflow into the fan, and the close clearance between blade tips and duct wall suppresses tip vortices, improving efficiency particularly at low speeds and static thrust conditions. The duct itself can generate additional thrust or lift. At high forward speeds, however, duct drag increases and open propellers become more efficient.

Applications

Ducted fans appear in the fenestron tail rotors of helicopters, in vertical take-off aircraft such as the Bell X-22, in hovercraft lift and propulsion fans, in radio-controlled model jets with electric ducted fans (EDF), and in personal air vehicles and unmanned aerial vehicles. Large turbofan jet engines are, in effect, a highly developed form of ducted fan.

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages include higher static thrust per unit diameter, reduced noise signature in some applications, and physical protection of blades and bystanders. Disadvantages include greater weight and complexity of the duct, sensitivity to crosswinds, and reduced efficiency at high cruise speeds.
