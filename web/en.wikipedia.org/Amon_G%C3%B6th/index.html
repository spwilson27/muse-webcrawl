Amon Leopold Göth (11 December 1908 – 13 September 1946) was an Austrian SS functionary and war criminal. He served as commandant of the Kraków-Płaszów concentration camp in German-occupied Poland during World War II and personally murdered prisoners.

Career

Göth joined the Austrian Nazi Party and SS in the early 1930s. After postings in Lublin, where he participated in Operation Reinhard, he became commandant of the Płaszów labor camp in February 1943 and oversaw its conversion into a concentration camp. He supervised the liquidation of the Kraków and Tarnów ghettos, during which thousands of Jews were murdered.

Arrest and trial

Arrested by the SS itself in 1944 on corruption charges, Göth was in a sanatorium when the war ended. Extradited to Poland, he was tried in Kraków in 1946, convicted of crimes including the murder of tens of thousands of people, and hanged near the site of the Płaszów camp.

In popular culture

Göth was portrayed by Ralph Fiennes in Steven Spielberg's 1993 film Schindler's List, a performance that brought the camp's history to a global audience.
