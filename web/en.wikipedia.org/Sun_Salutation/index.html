Sun Salutation

Sun Salutation, also called Surya Namaskar (Sanskrit: सूर्यनमस्कार, Sūrya Namaskāra, "Sun Salutation"), is a practice in yoga as exercise incorporating a flow sequence of some twelve gracefully linked asanas. The set of twelve poses is dedicated in honour of the Hindu solar deity Surya. In India, there are similar salutations to other deities, such as the Moon Salutation.

The sequence

The basic sequence involves moving from a standing position into Downward and Upward Dog and back again. A typical round begins and ends in Pranamasana (prayer pose), moving through Hasta Uttanasana (raised arms), Uttanasana (standing forward bend), Ashwa Sanchalanasana (equestrian pose), Dandasana (plank), Ashtanga Namaskara (eight-limbed salute), Bhujangasana (cobra), and Adho Mukha Svanasana (downward-facing dog), before reversing the sequence. Each movement is coordinated with an inhalation or exhalation.

History

Although often presented as ancient, the Sun Salutation as a fixed sequence is largely a modern creation, popularised in the 1920s–1930s by Bhawanrao Shriniwasrao Pant Pratinidhi, the Rajah of Aundh, who published it as a system of health exercise. It was subsequently incorporated into the flowing style of yoga taught by Krishnamacharya at Mysore and from there into Ashtanga Vinyasa and other modern schools.

Practice and benefits

The sequence is widely used as a warm-up and as a complete short practice, valued for stretching and strengthening much of the body, mobilising the spine, and synchronising breath with movement. Practitioners traditionally perform it at dawn facing the rising sun.
