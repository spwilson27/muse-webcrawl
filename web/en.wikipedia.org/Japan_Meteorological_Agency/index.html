Japan Meteorological Agency

Japan Meteorological Agency

Abbreviation: JMA

Founded: 1875

Headquarters: Minato, Tokyo, Japan

Parent agency: Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism

The Japan Meteorological Agency (気象庁, Kishō-chō), abbreviated JMA, is an agency of the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism of Japan. It is responsible for gathering and providing results from the measurement and observation of weather, earthquakes, volcanic activity and tsunamis.

History

The agency traces its origins to the Tokyo Meteorological Observatory, founded in 1875. Through a series of reorganizations it became the Central Meteorological Observatory and, in 1956, was renamed the Japan Meteorological Agency. Its headquarters moved to Toranomon in Minato, Tokyo in 2020.

Services

The JMA issues weather forecasts and warnings, operates weather radars and satellites including the Himawari geostationary series, and is the World Meteorological Organization-designated Regional Specialized Meteorological Centre for the northwest Pacific, responsible for naming tropical cyclones in that basin. It also operates the Earthquake Early Warning system, which has provided public alerts since 2007, and monitors Japan's active volcanoes.
