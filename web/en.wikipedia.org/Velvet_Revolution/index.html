The Velvet Revolution (Czech: sametová revoluce) was a non-violent transition of power in what was then Czechoslovakia, occurring from 17 November to 29 December 1989. Popular demonstrations against the one-party government of the Communist Party of Czechoslovakia included students and older dissidents. The result was the end of 41 years of one-party rule in Czechoslovakia, the subsequent dismantling of the command economy, and conversion to a parliamentary republic.

Background

After the suppression of the Warsaw Pact invasion of Czechoslovakia in 1968 and the policy of "normalization," Czechoslovakia remained one of the most conservative states in the Eastern Bloc. Dissident movements such as Charter 77, associated with the playwright Václav Havel, kept up a small but persistent opposition. By late 1989, communist governments across Eastern Europe were collapsing, following the fall of the Berlin Wall on 9 November.

17 November 1989

On 17 November, International Students' Day, a peaceful student demonstration in Prague commemorating a Czech student, Jan Opletal, who was killed by the Nazis in 1939, was violently suppressed by riot police. The event sparked a series of protests, theatre strikes, and the formation of the Civic Forum (Občanské fórum) in the Czech lands and Public Against Violence in Slovakia.

General strike and negotiations

Protests grew to hundreds of thousands in Prague's Wenceslas Square and Letná Plain. On 27 November a two-hour general strike involved most of the population. The Communist Party leadership under Miloš Jakeš resigned, and negotiations between the government and Civic Forum led to a new government and the removal of the party's constitutional leading role.

Outcome

On 29 December 1989, Václav Havel was unanimously elected president of Czechoslovakia by the Federal Assembly. Free elections followed in June 1990. The name "Velvet Revolution" reflected the peaceful character of the transition; it would later inspire the term "Velvet Divorce" for the peaceful dissolution of Czechoslovakia in 1993.
