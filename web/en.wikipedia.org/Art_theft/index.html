Art theft

Art theft, sometimes called artnapping, is the stealing of paintings, sculptures, or other forms of visual art from galleries, museums, or other public and private locations. Stolen art is often resold or used by criminals as collateral in the underworld, and famous works are generally too recognisable to sell openly.

Notable thefts

The 1911 theft of the Mona Lisa from the Louvre by Vincenzo Peruggia made the painting world-famous; it was recovered in Florence two years later. In 1990, thirteen works worth some $500 million were stolen from the Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum in Boston — the largest art theft in history, still unsolved, with the empty frames still hanging in the museum. The theft of Edvard Munch's The Scream in 1994 and again in 2004 also drew global attention; both versions were recovered.

Recovery and prevention

Specialised police units such as the FBI Art Crime Team and Scotland Yard's Art and Antiques Unit pursue stolen works, and databases including the Art Loss Register record stolen items. Wartime looting, notably by Nazi Germany, continues to generate restitution claims.
