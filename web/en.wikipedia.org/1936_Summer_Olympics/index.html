1936 Summer Olympics

The 1936 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XI Olympiad, were held from 1 to 16 August 1936 in Berlin, Germany. Awarded to Berlin in 1931 before the Nazi seizure of power, the Games were exploited by Adolf Hitler's regime as a showcase for Nazi Germany, even as they produced the immortal triumphs of the African-American sprinter and long jumper Jesse Owens.

Context

Calls for a boycott over Nazi racial policies were debated in several countries, most seriously in the United States, where the movement narrowly failed. The regime temporarily softened its antisemitic displays, removing anti-Jewish signs, while using the Games' scale, pageantry and Leni Riefenstahl's film Olympia to impress foreign visitors. The Berlin Games introduced the Olympic torch relay, invented for this edition with the flame carried from Olympia, Greece.

Competition

Forty-nine nations and 3,963 athletes took part — the largest Games yet. Jesse Owens won four gold medals (100 m, 200 m, long jump and 4×100 m relay), the outstanding individual performance, in a public refutation of Nazi racial ideology. His friendship with German long jumper Luz Long became emblematic. Germany topped the medal table overall, aided by a state-directed sports programme.

Sporting firsts

Basketball made its Olympic debut, played outdoors on clay courts, with the United States defeating Canada in a rain-soaked final. Handball and canoeing also appeared for the first time. Television coverage — transmitted to local viewing halls — made Berlin the first Games to be televised.
