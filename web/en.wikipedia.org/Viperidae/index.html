Viperidae

The Viperidae (vipers) are a family of venomous snakes found in most parts of the world, excluding Antarctica, Australia, Hawaii, Madagascar and various other isolated islands. All possess long, hinged fangs that permit deep penetration and injection of venom, which folds back against the roof of the mouth when not in use.

Characteristics

Vipers are generally heavy-bodied snakes with keeled scales and triangular heads distinct from the neck. They are divided into subfamilies including the Viperinae (Old World true vipers, such as adders and puff adders), Crotalinae (pit vipers, such as rattlesnakes, lanceheads and Asian pit vipers) and Azemiopinae. Pit vipers possess heat-sensing pit organs between the eye and nostril.

Venom and behaviour

Viper venom typically contains enzymes that cause local pain, swelling, tissue damage and disruption of blood clotting. Most vipers are ambush predators that strike and release prey, then track it by scent. Many species give birth to live young (ovoviviparity).
