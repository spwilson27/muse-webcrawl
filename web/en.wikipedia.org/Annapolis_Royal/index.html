Annapolis Royal is a town in Nova Scotia, Canada, located in the western part of the province on the Annapolis River. One of the oldest European settlements in Canada, it originated as the French habitation of Port-Royal in 1605. The town served as the capital of Acadia and later of the British colony of Nova Scotia until Halifax was founded in 1749.

History

The French settlement at Port-Royal, established by Pierre Dugua, Sieur de Mons and Samuel de Champlain, was one of the earliest permanent European colonies in North America north of Florida. Control of the site changed hands repeatedly between French and British (and briefly Scottish) forces in the 17th and early 18th centuries. After the British capture in 1710 it was renamed Annapolis Royal in honour of Queen Anne. The star fort at the site, now Fort Anne National Historic Site, is the oldest national historic site in Canada.

Today

Modern Annapolis Royal is a small service and tourism centre noted for its historic streetscape, the Annapolis Royal Historic Gardens, one of Canada's largest farmers' markets, and nearby attractions such as Port-Royal National Historic Site across the river. The town gained attention for the Annapolis Royal Tidal Power Station, for decades North America's only tidal generating station.
