Magnetic flux quantum

Symbol: Φ0
Value: h/2e ≈ 2.067833848 × 10−15 Wb
Named concepts: Josephson constant KJ = 2e/h

The magnetic flux quantum is the quantum of magnetic flux passing through a superconductor. Denoted Φ0 = h/2e, it equals approximately 2.068 × 10−15 webers, where h is the Planck constant and e is the elementary charge. The factor of two reflects that the charge carriers in a superconductor are Cooper pairs of electrons.

Flux quantisation

In a superconducting ring or hollow cylinder, the trapped magnetic flux can take only discrete values that are integer multiples of Φ0. Fritz London predicted flux quantisation in 1950 (estimating h/e), and it was observed experimentally in 1961 by Robert Doll and Martin Näbauer and, independently, by Bascom Deaver and William Fairbank.

Applications

Flux quantisation is central to SQUIDs (superconducting quantum interference devices), the most sensitive magnetometers known. The related Josephson effect links Φ0 to voltage and frequency standards: since the 2019 redefinition of the SI, the Josephson constant KJ = 1/Φ0 is exact. Vortices in type-II superconductors each carry a single flux quantum.
