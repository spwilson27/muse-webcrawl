Playa

A playa with desiccation-cracked mudflats

A playa (from the Spanish word for "beach") is a dry lake bed: a flat-bottomed depression found in interior desert basins that is periodically covered by water and subsequently desiccates. Playas are among the flattest known natural landforms, and are also called dry lakes, alkali flats or, in Australia, by related terms such as claypans. They form in endorheic basins where water flows into a depression but has no outlet, so it evaporates, leaving behind fine sediments and evaporite minerals.

Formation

Playas form in arid and semi-arid regions where precipitation is low and evaporation rates are high. Runoff from surrounding mountains carries dissolved salts and fine silt and clay into a closed basin. Standing water — sometimes only a few centimetres deep — deposits its sediment load evenly across the floor of the depression. When the water evaporates, salts such as halite, gypsum and borates precipitate, and the clay-rich mud shrinks and cracks into polygonal plates. Repeated cycles of flooding and drying over thousands of years build up the characteristic extremely flat surface; many large playas descend from pluvial lakes of the last ice age, such as Lake Bonneville in Utah, whose remnant is the Bonneville Salt Flats.

Ecology

Although dry for most of the year, playas support specialised life. Ephemeral pools that form after rain hatch dormant eggs of fairy shrimp and other crustaceans, attracting migratory birds. Because playas collect water in otherwise parched landscapes, they are important seasonal wetlands for wildlife across the American Southwest, Central Asia and Australia.

Human use

The hard, flat surfaces of large playas have been used as natural runways and proving grounds. Edwards Air Force Base in California uses Rogers Dry Lake for aircraft landings, including historic Space Shuttle landings, and the Black Rock Desert playa in Nevada hosts the annual Burning Man festival as well as land-speed record attempts. Playas are also sources of evaporite minerals: lithium-bearing brines beneath salt flats such as Bolivia's Salar de Uyuni — the world's largest salt flat — and Chile's Salar de Atacama are major sources of lithium for batteries. Playa dust, however, can be a significant source of airborne particulates and a public-health concern when it contains harmful salts or contaminants, as at Utah's Great Salt Lake bed and California's Salton Sea.
