Osamu Tezuka

Born3 November 1928, Toyonaka, Osaka, Japan
Died9 February 1989 (aged 60), Tokyo, Japan
OccupationManga artist, animator
Notable worksAstro Boy, Kimba the White Lion, Black Jack, Phoenix
StudiosMushi Production, Tezuka Productions

Osamu Tezuka (手塚 治虫, 3 November 1928 – 9 February 1989) was a Japanese manga artist, cartoonist and animator. Widely regarded as the "God of Manga" and a founding father of both modern manga and anime, he created an estimated 700 manga titles comprising some 150,000 pages, and pioneered techniques — cinematic panel layouts, long-form story manga and the large-eyed character style — that defined the medium.

Career

Tezuka, who trained and qualified as a physician but never practised, achieved his breakthrough with New Treasure Island (1947), which sold some 400,000 copies and established the postwar manga boom. His most famous creation, Astro Boy (Tetsuwan Atom, 1952), became Japan's first popular television anime series in 1963 through his studio Mushi Production, which also produced Kimba the White Lion. Mushi Production's bankruptcy in 1973 did not slow him; in the 1970s he produced acclaimed works for adult readers including Black Jack, about an unlicensed surgeon, the multi-volume Buddha, and his lifelong, unfinished epic Phoenix.

Legacy

Tezuka's themes — science, medicine, war, and the value of life — drew on his wartime experiences and medical training. The Tezuka Osamu Cultural Prize and the Osamu Tezuka Manga Museum in his hometown of Takarazuka commemorate him; a vast generation of creators, from Fujiko Fujio to Naoki Urasawa, cite him as their formative influence.
