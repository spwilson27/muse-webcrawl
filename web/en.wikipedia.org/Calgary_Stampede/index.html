Calgary Stampede

The Calgary Stampede is an annual rodeo, exhibition, and festival held every July in Calgary, Alberta, Canada. The ten-day event, which bills itself as "The Greatest Outdoor Show on Earth", attracts over one million visitors per year and features one of the world's largest rodeos.

History

The Stampede was organized in 1912 by American promoter Guy Weadick with backing from Calgary's "Big Four" businessmen — Patrick Burns, A. E. Cross, George Lane, and A. J. McLean — as a one-time frontier celebration. It was revived in 1919 as the Victory Stampede and, from 1923, merged with the Calgary Exhibition to become an annual event. The chuckwagon races, the "Rangeland Derby", were introduced in 1923 and became one of its most famous features.

Events

The festival opens with the Stampede Parade through downtown Calgary. The rodeo includes bull riding, barrel racing, steer wrestling, tie-down roping, saddle bronc riding, and bareback riding, with total prize money among the largest in the sport. Other attractions include the midway, an agricultural exhibition, the Grandstand Show, Indigenous programming at Elbow River Camp, concerts, and nightly fireworks. Free pancake breakfasts are served across the city during Stampede week.

Stampede Park

Events are centred on Stampede Park in the Beltline district, home to the Scotiabank Saddledome and the BMO Centre, whose major expansion opened in 2024. The 2013 event went ahead as "Come Hell or High Water" shortly after severe flooding of the Elbow and Bow rivers inundated the grounds.

Impact and criticism

The Stampede is a major engine of Calgary's tourism economy and a defining element of the city's western identity. It has also drawn criticism from animal welfare groups over injuries and deaths of rodeo and chuckwagon animals.
