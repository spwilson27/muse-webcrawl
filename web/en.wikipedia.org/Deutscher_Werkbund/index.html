The Deutscher Werkbund (German Association of Craftsmen) is a German association of artists, designers, architects, and industrialists founded in 1907 in Munich. Its purpose was to promote good design in mass production and architecture, elevating the quality of German manufactured goods by integrating traditional crafts with industrial technology.

History

Inspired in part by the English Arts and Crafts movement, Hermann Muthesius and colleagues sought the opposite conclusion: rather than rejecting the machine, German design should embrace it. The Werkbund's members included Peter Behrens, whose work for AEG became a landmark of corporate design, as well as Henry van de Velde, Josef Hoffmann, and Bruno Taut.

A famous debate at the 1914 Cologne exhibition opposed Muthesius's advocacy of standardisation (Typisierung) to van de Velde's defence of individual artistic expression. After the First World War the Werkbund turned toward modernism; its 1927 exhibition "Die Wohnung" in Stuttgart created the Weissenhof Estate, directed by Ludwig Mies van der Rohe, with houses by leading modernist architects including Le Corbusier.

The Werkbund was dissolved under the Nazi regime in 1934 and refounded after the Second World War. Its ideas strongly influenced the Bauhaus and the broader modern movement.
