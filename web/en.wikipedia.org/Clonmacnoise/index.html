Clonmacnoise

Clonmacnoise (Irish: Cluain Mhic Nóis, "Meadow of the Sons of Nós") is a ruined early Christian monastery situated in County Offaly, Ireland, on the River Shannon south of Athlone. Founded in 544 by Saint Ciarán, it became one of the great centres of religion, learning and craftsmanship in early medieval Ireland.

History

Ciarán founded the monastery at the crossroads of Ireland, where the Shannon met the Esker Riada, the main east–west route across the island. Clonmacnoise grew into a major monastic city with close ties to the kings of Connacht; several high kings of Ireland were buried there, including Rory O'Connor, the last high king. It was raided repeatedly by Vikings, rival Irish kingdoms and later the Normans, and declined after the 12th-century reform of the Irish church and the founding of Athlone. The site was finally devastated in 1552 by the English garrison from Athlone.

Remains

The site preserves two round towers, the ruins of several small churches including Temple Ciarán and the cathedral (rebuilt around 909 by King Fland Sinna and Abbot Colmán), and three celebrated high crosses — the Cross of the Scriptures, the North Cross and the South Cross — whose originals are now displayed in the on-site interpretative centre, with replicas standing outdoors. The annals known as the Annals of Tigernach and the Chronicle of Ireland were compiled at Clonmacnoise.

Modern significance

Pope John Paul II visited the site in 1979. It remains a place of pilgrimage and one of Ireland's most important national monuments.
