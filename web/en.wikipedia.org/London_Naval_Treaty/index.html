SubjectNaval armaments limitation

The London Naval Treaty, officially the Treaty for the Limitation and Reduction of Naval Armament, was an agreement concluded on 22 April 1930 between the United Kingdom, the United States, Japan, France and Italy. It extended and developed the system of naval limitation begun by the Washington Naval Treaty of 1922.

Provisions

The treaty set limits on cruisers, destroyers and submarines, distinguishing between heavy cruisers with 8-inch guns and light cruisers with 6.1-inch guns, and extended the battleship building "holiday" until 1936. Submarine warfare rules, including provisions on the treatment of merchant shipping, were also codified.

Consequences

The treaty was contentious in Japan, where naval officers objected to the ratios imposed on cruisers; the controversy contributed to the shooting of Prime Minister Hamaguchi Osachi in 1930. France and Italy declined to accept all parts of the agreement. A Second London Naval Treaty followed in 1936, but the system of naval arms control collapsed as Japan withdrew and international tensions rose.
