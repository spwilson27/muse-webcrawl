The Rub' al Khali ("Empty Quarter") is the largest contiguous sand desert (erg) in the world, encompassing most of the southern third of the Arabian Peninsula. It stretches across Saudi Arabia, Oman, the United Arab Emirates and Yemen, covering some 650,000 square kilometres.

Geography

The desert is roughly 1,000 km long and 500 km wide. Its terrain is covered by sand dunes reaching heights of up to 250 metres, interspersed with gravel and gypsum plains. It is one of the driest places on Earth, with annual rainfall below 35 mm and summer temperatures regularly exceeding 50 °C.

Exploration

Bertram Thomas made the first documented crossing by a Westerner in 1930–31, followed by Harry St John Philby in 1932. Wilfred Thesiger crossed the desert twice in the 1940s with Bedouin companions, journeys he described in his classic book Arabian Sands (1959).

Resources and settlement

Beneath the sands lie some of the world's largest oil fields, including Shaybah. Historically the desert was crossed by caravan routes carrying frankincense from southern Arabia; the legendary lost city of Iram (Ubar) is said to lie somewhere within or near it.
