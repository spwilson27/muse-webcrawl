Algonquin Peak

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Elevation: 5,115 ft (1,559 m)

Range: MacIntyre Range, Adirondack Mountains

Location: Essex County, New York, US

Listing: Adirondack High Peaks (2nd highest)

Algonquin Peak is a mountain in the MacIntyre Range of the Adirondack Mountains in Essex County, New York. At 5,115 feet (1,559 m) it is the second-highest mountain in New York State, after Mount Marcy, and one of the 46 Adirondack High Peaks traditionally counted by the Adirondack Forty-Sixers.

Description

The mountain rises south of Lake Placid, between Mount Marcy to the east and its companion summits Wright Peak and Iroquois Peak in the same range. Its broad, open summit of alpine tundra — one of the largest such areas in the Adirondacks — supports fragile arctic-alpine plants, and summit stewards are posted in summer to protect the vegetation and educate hikers. The name honours the Algonquian peoples, who historically hunted in the region, though the land was claimed by their rivals the Iroquois (Haudenosaunee).

Hiking

The standard route ascends from the Adirondak Loj trailhead on the Van Hoevenberg Trail and a spur up Algonquin's northeastern shoulder, a round trip of about eight miles with over 2,900 feet of elevation gain. The summit offers wide views across the High Peaks, including Mount Marcy, Mount Colden and Lake Placid. Algonquin is often combined with Wright Peak, site of a 1942 B-47 bomber crash, and Iroquois Peak in a single traverse.
