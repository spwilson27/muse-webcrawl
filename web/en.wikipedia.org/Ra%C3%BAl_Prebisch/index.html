Raúl Prebisch

Raúl Prebisch (1901–1986) was an Argentine economist known for his contribution to structuralist economics and the Prebisch–Singer hypothesis, which holds that the terms of trade for primary-commodity exporters tend to decline relative to manufactured goods over the long run.

Career

Prebisch helped found and directed Argentina's central bank before becoming executive secretary of the United Nations Economic Commission for Latin America (ECLAC) in 1950, where he shaped the school of Latin American structuralism. He later served as the first secretary-general of UNCTAD (1964–1969).

Ideas

With Hans Singer, who reached similar conclusions independently, Prebisch argued that the global economy divided into an industrial "center" and a commodity-producing "periphery," and that technical progress benefited the center disproportionately. His policy prescription — import-substitution industrialization — influenced development policy across Latin America for decades and underpins dependency theory, though it was later criticized for inefficiency and protectionism.
