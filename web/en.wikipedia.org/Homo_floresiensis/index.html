Homo floresiensis

Homo floresiensis is an extinct species of small archaic human that inhabited the island of Flores, Indonesia, until the arrival of modern humans about 50,000 years ago. The remains of an individual about 1.1 m (3 ft 7 in) in height were discovered in 2003 at Liang Bua cave on Flores. Partial skeletons of at least nine individuals have been recovered, including one complete skull (LB1). Because of its small stature the species has been nicknamed "the hobbit".

Discovery and debate

The discovery team, led by Indonesian and Australian researchers including Mike Morwood and R. P. Soejono, announced the find in 2004. The combination of tiny body and brain size — an estimated cranial capacity of roughly 380–426 cm³ — with relatively recent survival sparked intense debate: some researchers argued the fossils represented modern humans with a pathological condition such as microcephaly or Down syndrome, while the discoverers and most subsequent studies maintained that they constitute a distinct species, possibly descended from Homo erectus and shrunk by insular dwarfism. Older stone tools and small hominin fossils from Mata Menge, dated to around 700,000 years ago, support a long occupation of the island.

Extinction

Revised dating places the skeletal remains at about 100,000 to 60,000 years old, with the species disappearing from Liang Bua around 50,000 years ago, close to the time modern humans reached the region.
