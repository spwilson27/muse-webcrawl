Turing Award

The ACM A. M. Turing Award is an annual prize given by the Association for Computing Machinery (ACM) for contributions of lasting and major technical importance to computer science. It is generally recognized as the highest distinction in the field and is often referred to as the "Nobel Prize of Computing". The award is named for Alan Turing, the British mathematician and computer scientist.

History

The first recipient, in 1966, was Alan Perlis, recognized for his work on compilers. Subsequent laureates include Maurice Wilkes (1967), John McCarthy (1971, artificial intelligence), Edsger Dijkstra (1972), Donald Knuth (1974), Ken Thompson and Dennis Ritchie (1983, Unix and C), Tim Berners-Lee (2016, the World Wide Web), and Geoffrey Hinton, Yoshua Bengio, and Yann LeCun (2018, deep learning). Since 2014 the award has carried a prize of US$1 million, supported by Google.

Selection

Recipients are chosen by an ACM committee from nominations in any area of computer science; the citation emphasizes the lasting significance of the contribution. The award may be shared by up to three people for a joint contribution.
