Al Pacino

Alfredo James Pacino (born 25 April 1940) is an American actor considered among the greatest of his generation. Over a career spanning more than five decades, he became famous in the 1970s for roles in The Godfather trilogy, Serpico, Dog Day Afternoon and ...And Justice for All. He won the Academy Award for Best Actor for Scent of a Woman (1992) and is one of few performers to win the Triple Crown of Acting: an Academy Award, two Tony Awards and two Primetime Emmy Awards.

Early life

Pacino was born in East Harlem, New York City, to Italian-American parents who separated when he was two. Raised in the South Bronx by his mother and grandparents, he attended the High School of Performing Arts and later studied method acting under Lee Strasberg at the Actors Studio.

Career

After stage success, including a Tony Award for Does a Tiger Wear a Necktie? (1969), Pacino's film breakthrough came as Michael Corleone in The Godfather (1972). Through the 1970s he received four consecutive Academy Award nominations. Later landmarks include Scarface (1983), Heat (1995) with Robert De Niro, Donnie Brasco (1997), The Insider (1999), and The Irishman (2019), as well as television work in Angels in America (2003) and You Don't Know Jack (2010).
