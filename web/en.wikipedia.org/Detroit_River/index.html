Detroit River

Detroit River
Length: 51 km (32 mi)
Source: Lake St. Clair
Mouth: Lake Erie
Border: United States / Canada

The Detroit River is a 51-kilometre river in the Great Lakes system that flows south from Lake St. Clair to Lake Erie, forming part of the border between the American state of Michigan and the Canadian province of Ontario. Its name derives from the French Rivière du Détroit, "river of the strait". Together with the St. Clair River it connects Lake Huron to Lake Erie.

Geography

The river begins at the outlet of Lake St. Clair near Windmill Pointe and flows past the cities of Detroit, Michigan and Windsor, Ontario, which face each other across its busiest reach. It contains numerous islands, the largest being Grosse Ile; others include Belle Isle, Fighting Island and Grass Island. The river's average flow is among the largest of any river in North America relative to its length.

History

Indigenous peoples used the strait as a trade and travel corridor for millennia. French explorers charted it in the 17th century, and Antoine de Cadillac founded Fort Pontchartrain — modern Detroit — on its banks in 1701. The river was a theatre of the War of 1812 and later a famous crossing point on the Underground Railroad, with enslaved people escaping from the United States to Canada. During Prohibition it became a notorious rum-running route.

Crossings and ecology

The river is crossed by the Ambassador Bridge (1929), for decades the busiest international border crossing in North America by trade volume, the Detroit–Windsor Tunnel (1930), and the Gordie Howe International Bridge, completed in the mid-2020s. The lower river is protected as the Detroit River International Wildlife Refuge, established in 2001, an important habitat for migratory waterfowl and a recovering fishery for walleye and lake sturgeon.
