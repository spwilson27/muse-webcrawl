The Ottoman Empire, historically known in Western Europe as the Turkish Empire, was an empire created by Turkish tribes in Anatolia that grew to become one of the most powerful states in the world during the 15th and 16th centuries. The empire, founded at the end of the 13th century in northwestern Anatolia by the Turkoman tribal leader Osman I, endured for more than six centuries, from around 1299 to 1922. At its height it controlled much of Southeast Europe, Western Asia, and North Africa — some 5.2 million square kilometres — and stood astride the trade routes between Europe and Asia.

Rise and expansion

From a small frontier principality (beylik) on the Byzantine border, the Ottomans expanded across Anatolia and into the Balkans under Orhan, Murad I, and Bayezid I, winning landmark victories at Kosovo (1389) and Nicopolis (1396). Recovery from Timur's victory at Ankara (1402) was followed by the defining triumph of Mehmed II, who conquered Constantinople in 1453, ending the Byzantine Empire. Selim I (1512–1520) annexed the Mamluk Sultanate, taking Syria, Egypt, and the Hejaz — with Mecca and Medina — and Suleiman the Magnificent (1520–1566) extended Ottoman power to Hungary, Iraq, and the gates of Vienna (1529), making the empire a dominant naval power in the Mediterranean and Indian Ocean.

Government and society

The empire was centred on the sultan, who from Selim I's time also claimed the caliphate. Administration ran through the grand vizier and the Imperial Council (Divan), a professional bureaucracy, and a standing army whose elite infantry, the Janissaries, were recruited through the devşirme levy of Christian boys. Provincial cavalry were supported by the timar land-grant system. Non-Muslim communities were organised in semi-autonomous millets under their own religious leaders, and Ottoman cities were famously multilingual and multi-confessional. The empire produced outstanding achievements in architecture (notably the mosques of Mimar Sinan), calligraphy, ceramics, and literature.

Transformation and decline

From the late 16th century the empire underwent military, fiscal, and institutional transformation as it adapted to inflation, new infantry warfare, and shifting trade routes. Defeat at Vienna in 1683 and the Treaty of Karlowitz (1699) began a long loss of European territory to Austria and Russia. The 19th century brought the reform era of the Tanzimat, modernising armies, law codes, and schools, alongside growing European financial and political penetration and rising nationalism among subject peoples. The Balkan Wars (1912–13) stripped away most remaining European provinces.

First World War and dissolution

The empire entered the First World War allied with Germany. It won at Gallipoli and Kut but collapsed in 1918 after defeats in Palestine, Mesopotamia, and the Caucasus; during the war the Ottoman government perpetrated the Armenian genocide and other mass killings of Christian minorities. The Armistice of Mudros (October 1918) and the Treaty of Sèvres (1920) envisaged partition, but the Turkish War of Independence under Mustafa Kemal Atatürk overturned the settlement: the sultanate was abolished on 1 November 1922, the Republic of Turkey proclaimed in 1923, and the caliphate abolished in 1924.
