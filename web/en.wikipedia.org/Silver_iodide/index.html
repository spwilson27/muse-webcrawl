Silver iodide

Silver iodide is an inorganic compound with the formula AgI. The compound is a bright yellow solid, but samples almost always contain impurities of metallic silver that give a gray coloration. The silver contamination arises because AgI is highly photosensitive. This property is exploited in silver-based photography. Silver iodide is also used as an antiseptic and in cloud seeding.

Structure and properties

The structure adopted by silver iodide is temperature dependent, an unusual property for an ionic solid: below 147 °C it exists in the wurtzite (β) form, while above that temperature it converts to the α form, a superionic conductor in which silver cations move almost freely through the iodide lattice—AgI is a prototypical fast-ion conductor, a phenomenon studied since Michael Faraday. Like the other silver halides, AgI is extremely insoluble in water (Ksp ≈ 8.5 × 10−17).

Preparation and uses

Silver iodide is prepared by reaction of an iodide solution (e.g., potassium iodide) with a solution of silver ions (e.g., silver nitrate). Because the crystal structure of β-AgI closely resembles that of ice, fine particles of silver iodide serve as effective ice-nucleating agents and have been dispersed by aircraft and ground generators in cloud-seeding programmes intended to increase precipitation or suppress hail since the pioneering work of Vincent Schaefer and Bernard Vonnegut in the 1940s.
