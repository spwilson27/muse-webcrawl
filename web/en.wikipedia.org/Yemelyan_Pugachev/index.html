Yemelyan Ivanovich Pugachev (c. 1742 – 1775) was a Don Cossack who led the largest peasant revolt in Russian history, Pugachev's Rebellion (1773–1775), during the reign of Catherine the Great, claiming to be the murdered Emperor Peter III.

Pugachev, the Cossack pretender to the Russian throne.

Rebellion

A deserter from the army, Pugachev appeared among the Yaik Cossacks in 1773 declaring himself Peter III and promising land, freedom, and relief from taxes and military service. His movement drew Cossacks, serfs, Old Believers, and non-Russian peoples of the Volga and Urals. Rebels seized fortresses along the Yaik, besieged Orenburg, and in July 1774 captured and burned much of Kazan.

Defeat and execution

Imperial forces under generals including Alexander Suvorov defeated the rebels in August–September 1774; Pugachev was betrayed by his own Cossacks and taken to Moscow in an iron cage. He was beheaded and quartered on 21 January 1775. Catherine suppressed memory of the revolt, renaming the Yaik River the Ural.

Legacy

The rebellion inspired Pushkin's history of the revolt and his novel The Captain's Daughter. It remains a symbol of popular resistance in Russia.
