The Moncada Barracks was a military barracks in Santiago de Cuba, named after General Guillermo Moncada, a hero of the Cuban War of Independence. On 26 July 1953, the barracks was the site of an armed attack by a small group of revolutionaries led by Fidel Castro. The attack failed, but it is regarded as the beginning of the Cuban Revolution, and the date is commemorated in Cuba as the Día de la Rebeldía Nacional (Day of National Rebellion).

The attack

The assault was planned by Fidel Castro, then a young lawyer and activist opposed to the government of Fulgencio Batista, who had seized power in a coup in March 1952. Castro and his brother Raúl led a group of roughly 130 rebels in simultaneous attacks: the main force against the Moncada Barracks, the second-largest military garrison in Cuba, and smaller detachments against the barracks at Bayamo and targets such as the civil hospital and palace of justice in Santiago.

The attack, timed for the morning after the Santiago carnival in the hope that the garrison would be off guard, quickly went wrong. The rebels failed to capture the barracks' armoury, lost the element of surprise, and were outnumbered and outgunned. Many attackers were killed in the fighting or captured and summarily executed afterwards; the Castro brothers were among those imprisoned rather than killed.

Aftermath

Fidel Castro was tried and sentenced to 15 years in prison. His defence speech, published clandestinely as History Will Absolve Me, became the manifesto of the movement, which took its name — the 26th of July Movement — from the date of the attack. Following an amnesty in 1955, the Castros went into exile in Mexico, returned to Cuba aboard the yacht Granma in December 1956, and eventually triumphed in the Cuban Revolution on 1 January 1959.

The building today

After the revolution, the barracks was converted into a school and a museum, the Museo Histórico 26 de Julio, opened in the 1960s. The main facade still bears bullet holes from the 1953 attack, preserved as a memorial. The barracks complex now houses the Ciudad Escolar 26 de Julio (26th of July School City) alongside the museum.
