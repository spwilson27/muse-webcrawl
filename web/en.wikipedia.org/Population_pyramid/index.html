Population pyramid

A population pyramid (also called an age pyramid or age picture diagram) is a graphical illustration of the distribution of a population by age group and sex. It consists of two back-to-back bar charts — males conventionally on the left and females on the right — with the youngest cohorts at the base and the oldest at the top, making the shape of the distribution immediately visible.

Population pyramids reveal a population's age and sex structure.

Types

Three classic shapes are recognized. An expansive pyramid is broad at the base, indicating high birth rates and a young, growing population typical of many developing countries. A constrictive pyramid is narrower at the base than in the middle, reflecting below-replacement fertility and an aging population, as in much of Europe and Japan. A stationary pyramid shows roughly equal cohort sizes until old age, indicating long-stable low fertility and mortality. Irregularities in a pyramid preserve the marks of history — wars, baby booms, epidemics, and migration waves.

Uses

Demographers, governments, and businesses use pyramids to project school demand, labor-force size, pension and healthcare burdens, and dependency ratios, and to visualize the demographic transition from high to low birth and death rates.
