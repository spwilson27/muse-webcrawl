Bar Kokhba revolt
Date: 132–136 CE
Location: Judaea, Roman Empire
Result: Roman victory; mass devastation of Judaea

The Bar Kokhba revolt (132–136 CE) was a large-scale rebellion by the Jews of Judaea against the Roman Empire, led by Simon bar Kokhba. It was the third and last of the Jewish–Roman wars, and for a time established an independent Jewish state.

Causes

The revolt was provoked by Emperor Hadrian's decision to found the Roman colony of Aelia Capitolina on the ruins of Jerusalem, with a temple to Jupiter, and by decrees against Jewish religious practice, including circumcision.

Course of the war

The rebels, using guerrilla tactics and prepared cave and tunnel systems, inflicted heavy losses and ruled much of Judaea for about two and a half years, minting coins inscribed "For the Freedom of Jerusalem". Rabbi Akiva reportedly hailed Bar Kokhba as the Messiah. Rome eventually deployed overwhelming forces under Julius Severus; the fall of the fortress of Betar in 135, where Bar Kokhba died, effectively ended the revolt.

Aftermath

According to Cassius Dio, hundreds of thousands of Jews were killed and the population of Judaea was devastated; many survivors were enslaved or exiled. Jerusalem became a pagan Roman city closed to Jews, the province was renamed Syria Palaestina, and Jewish life recentred in Galilee — consequences that shaped Jewish history for centuries.
