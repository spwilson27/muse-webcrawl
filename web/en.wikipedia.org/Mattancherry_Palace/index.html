Mattancherry Palace

The Mattancherry Palace is a Portuguese palace popularly known as the Dutch Palace, in Mattancherry, Kochi, in the Indian state of Kerala. It features Kerala murals depicting portraits and exhibits of the Rajas of Kochi. The palace was included in the "tentative list" of UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

History

The palace was built around 1545 by the Portuguese as a present to Veera Kerala Varma, the Raja of Kochi, to appease him after a temple was plundered. It came into the possession of the Dutch in 1663, who carried out extensions and renovations — hence the name "Dutch Palace". The palace later reverted to the Rajas of Kochi, whose coronations were traditionally held there.

Architecture and murals

The two-storeyed palace is built in the traditional Kerala nalukettu style around a central courtyard with a small temple to the goddess Bhagavati. Its glory is its murals: large wall paintings executed in rich warm colours in tempera, depicting scenes from the Ramayana, Mahabharata and Puranic legends, as well as a gallery of portraits of the Kochi rulers. Exhibits include royal costumes, palanquins, weapons and the celebrated coronation robes and dresses.
