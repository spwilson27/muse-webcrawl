Lefevre Peninsula

The Lefevre Peninsula is a peninsula located about 15 kilometres northwest of the Adelaide city centre in South Australia, Australia. It juts into the Gulf St Vincent between the Port River estuary to the east and the gulf waters to the west, and forms the maritime gateway to Port Adelaide, the state's principal port.

Geography and suburbs

The peninsula runs roughly north–south and carries the coastal suburbs of (from south to north) Semaphore, Semaphore Park and Exeter areas along its flank, with Largs Bay, Taperoo, Osborne, North Haven and Outer Harbor toward its northern end. It is bounded by the Barker Inlet and Port River to the east. The northern tip, Outer Harbor, hosts Adelaide's passenger and container terminals, while Osborne is a major shipbuilding precinct, home to the ASC submarine and surface-ship yards.

History

The peninsula is named after Sir John George Shaw Lefevre, a British civil servant and one of the Colonization Commissioners for South Australia. Before European settlement the area was the country of the Kaurna people. Following colonisation in 1836, the peninsula developed around Port Adelaide's shipping trade, with Semaphore becoming a popular seaside resort; coastal defences such as Fort Largs and Fort Glanville were built in the late nineteenth century to guard the port approaches.
