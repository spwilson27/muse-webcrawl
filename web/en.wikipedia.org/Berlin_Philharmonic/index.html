Berlin Philharmonic

The Berlin Philharmonic (German: Berliner Philharmoniker) is a German orchestra based in Berlin. It is one of the most acclaimed orchestras in the world, regularly ranked among the finest ensembles in classical music. Founded in 1882 by 54 musicians who broke away from an earlier ensemble, the orchestra is famous for its self-governing structure: its members elect their own chief conductor.

History

Under conductors such as Hans von Bülow, Arthur Nikisch, and Wilhelm Furtwängler, the orchestra achieved international renown. Its longest-serving chief conductor was Herbert von Karajan (1955–1989), whose hundreds of recordings made the orchestra a global brand. He was succeeded by Claudio Abbado (1989–2002) and Simon Rattle (2002–2018), and then by Kirill Petrenko.

The Philharmonie

The orchestra's home since 1963 is the Berliner Philharmonie, designed by Hans Scharoun, whose vineyard-style seating — with the stage at the centre surrounded by terraced audience blocks — revolutionized concert hall design and was widely imitated, including in Los Angeles and Paris.

Digital Concert Hall

In 2008 the orchestra launched the Digital Concert Hall, a streaming platform broadcasting its concerts worldwide in high definition, which has become a model for orchestras internationally.
