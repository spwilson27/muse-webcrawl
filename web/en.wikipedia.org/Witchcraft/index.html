Witchcraft

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Witchcraft is the practice of magical skills, spells and abilities, traditionally understood as the use of supernatural or magical powers, often involving the invocation of spirits or deities and the performance of rituals. Belief in witchcraft is found in cultures around the world, though its meaning varies widely: it may refer to malevolent sorcery feared by a community, to folk healing and divination, or to modern religious movements such as Wicca that reclaim the term positively.

Historical witchcraft and witch hunts

In early modern Europe and colonial North America, witchcraft was prosecuted as a heresy and a pact with the Devil. The great witch hunts, peaking between roughly 1560 and 1650, led to tens of thousands of executions, most of the victims being women. Influential texts such as the Malleus Maleficarum (1487) codified beliefs about witches' sabbaths, familiars and maleficium. Famous trials include those at Trier, Würzburg, Pendle and Salem, Massachusetts (1692–93). The hunts declined as legal standards of evidence tightened and elite scepticism grew; the last executions in Europe took place in the eighteenth century.

Anthropological perspectives

Anthropologists have documented witchcraft beliefs in many societies, where accusations often function to explain misfortune — illness, crop failure, sudden death — and to police social tensions. E. E. Evans-Pritchard's classic study of the Azande of Sudan showed how witchcraft provided a coherent explanatory system alongside natural causation. In parts of Africa and Asia, accusations of witchcraft continue to lead to violence against accused persons, frequently elderly women or children.

Modern witchcraft

In the twentieth century, Gerald Gardner and others founded Wicca, a modern pagan religion centred on the worship of a Goddess and God, seasonal festivals and ritual magic, which its adherents identify as a revival of witchcraft. Contemporary witchcraft also encompasses eclectic and secular practices spread widely through books and social media, and the figure of the witch has become a symbol in feminist and popular culture, from The Wizard of Oz to Harry Potter.
