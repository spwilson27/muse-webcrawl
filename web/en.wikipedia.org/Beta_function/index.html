Beta function

In mathematics, the beta function, also called the Euler integral of the first kind, is a special function defined for complex numbers x and y with positive real parts by

B(x, y) = ∫₀¹ tx−1(1−t)y−1 dt.

It was studied by Leonhard Euler and Adrien-Marie Legendre and given its name by Jacques Binet.

Properties

The beta function is symmetric: B(x, y) = B(y, x). Its most important property connects it to the gamma function:

B(x, y) = Γ(x)Γ(y) / Γ(x + y).

For positive integers this reduces to a relation with factorials and the binomial coefficient: 1/((n+1) B(m+1, n+1)) equals the binomial coefficient "n+1 choose m+1". The beta function also satisfies recurrence relations such as B(x+1, y) = B(x, y) · x/(x+y), and an integral representation over the positive real axis that is useful in probability theory.

Incomplete beta function

The incomplete beta function generalizes the integral to a variable upper limit and is central to statistics: the cumulative distribution function of the beta distribution, and of the binomial distribution, can be expressed through the regularized incomplete beta function. It appears in Student's t-distribution and the F-distribution.

Applications

Beyond probability, the beta function occurs in combinatorics, in the evaluation of Feynman diagrams and string amplitudes (the Veneziano amplitude is built from the beta function), and in analytic number theory.
