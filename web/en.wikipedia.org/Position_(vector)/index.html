Position (vector)

In geometry, a position or position vector, also known as a location vector or radius vector, is a Euclidean vector that represents the position of a point P in space relative to an arbitrary reference origin O. Usually denoted x, r, or s, it corresponds to the straight line segment from O to P; in other words, it is the displacement of the point from the origin.

Representation

In a Cartesian coordinate system, the position vector of the point (x, y, z) is r = xî + yĵ + zk̂, where î, ĵ, and k̂ are unit vectors along the axes. Its magnitude |r| is the distance of the point from the origin. Position vectors may be expressed in any coordinate system — Cartesian, polar, cylindrical, or spherical — and in differential geometry they serve as the natural parametrisation of curves: a curve is the locus traced by the tip of r(t).

Applications

Position is the fundamental quantity of kinematics: velocity is its first time derivative and acceleration its second, so the equations of motion of a particle are equations for r(t). The difference of two position vectors is a displacement vector, which — unlike position itself — is independent of the choice of origin. In continuum mechanics and field theory, position vectors label material points and spatial locations, and the concept generalises to affine spaces, where points have no preferred origin, and to the four-position of special relativity in spacetime.
