The Behavioral Analysis Unit (BAU) is a department of the United States Federal Bureau of Investigation's National Center for the Analysis of Violent Crime. Based at the FBI Academy in Quantico, Virginia, its agents and analysts provide case-based operational support to federal, state and local law enforcement, applying knowledge of criminal behaviour, threat assessment and investigative strategy.

History and work

The unit descends from the Behavioral Science Unit established in the 1970s, whose agents — notably John E. Douglas and Robert Ressler — interviewed serial offenders and developed offender profiling methods. Today's BAU is organised into units covering crimes against adults, crimes against children, counterterrorism and threats, and cyber matters. Its work includes criminal investigative analysis, interview strategy, threat assessment and research; it also supports the ViCAP database of violent crimes.

In popular culture

The BAU was popularised by the television series Criminal Minds (2005–2020) and by books and portrayals such as Mindhunter. Academic debate continues over the empirical foundations of traditional profiling, and the FBI emphasises that its analysis is an investigative tool rather than a substitute for evidence.
