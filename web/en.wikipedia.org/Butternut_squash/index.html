Butternut squash
Species: Cucurbita moschata
Type: Winter squash
Cultivar origin: United States, 1940s

Butternut squash (Cucurbita moschata), known in Australia and New Zealand as butternut pumpkin, is a type of winter squash with a sweet, nutty taste similar to that of pumpkin.

Description

The fruit has a bell shape with a smooth, tan-yellow skin and deep orange flesh, with a small seed cavity in the bulbous end — a shape that gives a high proportion of usable flesh. It grows as a trailing annual vine and is harvested mature, when the rind has hardened, allowing storage for months.

History and cultivation

The modern butternut was developed in Massachusetts in the 1940s by Charles Hubbard, who crossed squash varieties to produce a compact, smooth fruit. It requires a long, warm growing season and full sun.

Culinary use

Butternut squash is roasted, sautéed, mashed or puréed into soups; its flesh is rich in vitamin A, fibre and carotenoids. The seeds can be roasted like pumpkin seeds, and the flowers are also edible.
