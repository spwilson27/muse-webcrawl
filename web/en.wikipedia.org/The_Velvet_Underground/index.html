The Velvet Underground

The Velvet Underground was an American rock band formed in New York City in 1964. Led by singer and guitarist Lou Reed and Welsh multi-instrumentalist John Cale, the band also included guitarist Sterling Morrison and drummer Moe Tucker. Although commercially unsuccessful during their career, they are now regarded as one of the most influential bands in the history of rock, punk and alternative music.

History

The band came under the management of artist Andy Warhol, who incorporated them into his Exploding Plastic Inevitable multimedia shows and produced their 1967 debut album, The Velvet Underground & Nico, famous for its Warhol banana cover art. The abrasive White Light/White Heat (1968) followed; Cale then departed, replaced by Doug Yule. The quieter The Velvet Underground (1969) and Loaded (1970), containing "Sweet Jane" and "Rock and Roll", closed their studio career before the band dissolved in the early 1970s.

Legacy

Brian Eno famously remarked that while few people bought their first album, everyone who did started a band. The group was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1996. See also Lou Reed, Andy Warhol and The Village Voice.
