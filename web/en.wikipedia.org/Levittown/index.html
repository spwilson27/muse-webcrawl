Levittown

Levittown is the name given to several large suburban housing developments created in the United States by William J. Levitt and his company Levitt & Sons. The first and most famous, Levittown, New York, on Long Island, was built between 1947 and 1951 and is widely regarded as the first mass-produced suburb, a model that transformed American housing after the Second World War.

History

Using assembly-line construction techniques — each worker performing a single specialised task as crews moved from house to house — Levitt & Sons built more than 17,000 houses in the New York development. The houses, initially Cape Cod and ranch styles, were sold at prices affordable to returning veterans, aided by low-interest GI Bill mortgages.

Criticism and legacy

Levittown's early racially restrictive leases, which excluded African American buyers, became a symbol of post-war housing discrimination and suburban segregation. Nevertheless, the development's methods reshaped suburban growth, home ownership, and the American landscape. Later Levittowns were built in Pennsylvania, New Jersey (later renamed Willingboro), and Puerto Rico.
