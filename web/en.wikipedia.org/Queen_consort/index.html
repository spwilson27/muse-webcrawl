Queen consort

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A queen consort is the wife of a reigning king, who usually shares her husband's rank and status and holds the feminine equivalent of his monarchical titles. Historical queens consort have typically been crowned alongside their husbands in a coronation ceremony, though they do not as a rule share the king's political or military powers. The term is distinguished from queen regnant, a female monarch who reigns in her own right, such as Elizabeth II.

Roles

Traditionally, a queen consort's central role was dynastic — producing heirs — together with ceremonial, charitable, and cultural patronage. Some consorts exercised substantial political influence, as regents during a husband's absence or a child's minority, or as informal advisers; famous examples include Eleanor of Aquitaine, Catherine de' Medici, and, in Romanian history, Queen Marie. A widowed queen consort is styled queen dowager, and the mother of a reigning monarch may be known as the queen mother.

Modern examples

In present-day monarchies the role is largely ceremonial and philanthropic. Current queens consort include Camilla of the United Kingdom, Letizia of Spain, Máxima of the Netherlands, and Rania of Jordan; the husband of a reigning queen is usually styled a prince consort, or in a few cases king consort.
