Actinic keratosis

Medical information
Specialty:Dermatology
Symptoms:Rough, scaly patch on sun-exposed skin
Causes:Ultraviolet radiation
Risks:May progress to squamous cell carcinoma

Actinic keratosis (AK), sometimes called solar keratosis or senile keratosis, is a pre-cancerous area of thick, scaly, or crusty skin. These growths are more common in fair-skinned people and those who are frequently in the sun. They are believed to form when skin gets damaged by ultraviolet (UV) radiation from the sun or from indoor tanning beds, usually over the course of decades. Given their pre-cancerous nature, if left untreated, a small proportion may eventually turn into squamous cell carcinoma, though estimates of the per-lesion annual risk are low (often quoted below 1%).

Signs and symptoms

Actinic keratoses commonly appear on sun-exposed areas such as the face, ears, scalp (especially in balding men), neck, forearms, and backs of the hands. Lesions are usually small rough or sandpapery patches that may be pink, red, brown, or skin-coloured, and are often felt more easily than seen. Variants include hypertrophic, pigmented, and lichenoid forms, and the cutaneous horn.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is usually clinical, sometimes supported by dermoscopy; biopsy is performed when squamous cell carcinoma is suspected. Treatment options include cryotherapy, curettage, topical agents such as 5-fluorouracil, imiquimod, diclofenac gel, or tirbanibulin, photodynamic therapy, chemical peels, and laser treatment. Field therapy treats broader sun-damaged areas with multiple subclinical lesions. Prevention centres on sun protection.
