Colleges of the University of Cambridge

The colleges of the University of Cambridge are the 31 constituent colleges through which the university's teaching, accommodation, and pastoral care are largely organized. Every student and most academics are members of a college. The colleges are independent, self-governing institutions with their own property, endowments, and fellows, while the central university confers degrees and provides faculties, departments, and examinations.

History

Peterhouse, founded in 1284 by Hugh de Balsham, bishop of Ely, is the oldest college. A wave of medieval and Tudor foundations followed — King's, Queens', St John's, Trinity, and others — often endowed by royal or noble patrons. Trinity, founded by Henry VIII in 1546, remains the largest and wealthiest. Women's colleges began with Girton (1869) and Newnham (1871); all colleges now admit men and women. Clare Hall and Darwin were founded in the 1960s for graduate students.

College life

Colleges provide accommodation, dining halls (including formal hall dinners), libraries, chapels, gardens, and sporting clubs. Undergraduates receive small-group teaching in supervisions arranged by colleges. Each college is headed by a Master (or equivalent) and governed by its fellows. College wealth varies greatly; traditional rankings such as the Tompkins Table compare undergraduate examination results.

List

The colleges include Christ's, Churchill, Clare, Corpus Christi, Downing, Emmanuel, Fitzwilliam, Gonville and Caius, Homerton, Hughes Hall, Jesus, King's, Lucy Cavendish, Magdalene, Murray Edwards, Pembroke, Peterhouse, Queens', Robinson, Selwyn, Sidney Sussex, St Catharine's, St Edmund's, St John's, Trinity, Trinity Hall, and Wolfson, among others.
