Pasargadae was the capital of the Achaemenid Empire under Cyrus the Great (559–530 BC), who ordered its construction. It is also the location of Cyrus's tomb. Located in present-day Fars Province, Iran, about 90 km (56 mi) northeast of the modern city of Shiraz, Pasargadae was the first capital of the Achaemenid Empire and an outstanding example of early Persian art and architecture.

History

Pasargadae was founded in the territory where Cyrus defeated the Median king Astyages in 550 BC. The city remained the ceremonial capital of the empire until Darius I moved the capital to Persepolis and Susa, though it retained great religious significance. Alexander the Great visited and ordered the restoration of Cyrus's tomb in 330 BC, after it had been looted.

Tomb of Cyrus

The most famous monument at Pasargadae is the Tomb of Cyrus, a limestone structure consisting of a gabled burial chamber raised on six receding tiers, about 11 m (36 ft) high overall. According to ancient writers, an inscription once read: "O man, whoever you are and wherever you come from, for I know you will come, I am Cyrus who won the Persians their empire. Do not therefore begrudge me this bit of earth that covers my bones."

Other structures

The site includes the fortified terrace of Tall-i Takht ("Solomon's Throne"), the palace complexes known as Palace P (the residential palace, with the earliest known fourfold garden layout, or chahar bagh), Palace S (the audience hall), and the limestone Gatehouse R bearing a relief of a winged figure wearing an Elamite robe — often associated with Cyrus himself. The Zendan-e Soleyman ("Prison of Solomon") is a square stone tower of uncertain function, similar to the Ka'ba-ye Zartosht at Naqsh-e Rostam.

Archaeology

The site was first explored by James Morier in 1812 and properly identified by George Curzon. Ernst Herzfeld conducted excavations in 1928, and David Stronach directed the British Institute of Persian Studies excavations of 1961–63. Pasargadae was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2004.
