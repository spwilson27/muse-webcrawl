Vienna Philharmonic

The Vienna Philharmonic (German: Wiener Philharmoniker) is an orchestra based in Vienna, Austria, widely regarded as one of the finest orchestras in the world. Its musicians are drawn exclusively from the orchestra of the Vienna State Opera, and the ensemble is unusual in being self-governing: it elects its own management and, distinctively, has no permanent chief conductor, engaging guest conductors by invitation.

History

The orchestra was founded in 1842 by the composer-conductor Otto Nicolai, who organised the first "Philharmonic" concert with musicians of the imperial court opera. After Nicolai's departure the orchestra adopted democratic self-governance, a structure it retains. Its golden age is associated with Hans Richter, and later with music directors and long-term collaborators such as Gustav Mahler (chief conductor of the parent opera), Richard Strauss, Wilhelm Furtwängler, Bruno Walter, Herbert von Karajan, Leonard Bernstein, and Claudio Abbado. The orchestra premiered major works including Brahms's Second and Third Symphonies and Bruckner's symphonies.

Sound and traditions

The "Vienna sound" is linked to distinctive instruments and playing traditions, including the Viennese oboe, rotary-valve trumpets, and the use of natural-horn technique in the horn section. The orchestra performs in the Musikverein's Golden Hall, famed for its acoustics. Its annual New Year's Concert, devoted to the Strauss family and broadcast worldwide from the Musikverein, is one of the most-watched classical events in the world. The orchestra controversially admitted its first female member only in 1997, and its history under National Socialism has been the subject of research the orchestra itself has commissioned and published.
