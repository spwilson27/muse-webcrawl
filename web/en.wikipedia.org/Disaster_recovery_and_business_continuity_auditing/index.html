Disaster recovery and business continuity auditing

Disaster recovery and business continuity auditing is the examination and verification of an organization's plans, procedures, and infrastructure for responding to disruptive events — such as natural disasters, cyberattacks, or equipment failures — and for maintaining or quickly resuming critical business functions.

Scope

An audit assesses whether a business continuity plan (BCP) and disaster recovery plan (DRP) exist, are current, and are tested. Auditors review business impact analyses, recovery time objectives (RTO) and recovery point objectives (RPO), backup and replication arrangements, alternate-site provisions, communication trees, and the assignment of responsibilities. Evidence typically includes test results from tabletop exercises and live failover drills.

Frameworks and standards

Audits are commonly conducted against frameworks such as ISO 22301 for business continuity management systems, NIST SP 800-34 for contingency planning, and COBIT for IT governance. Findings are graded by severity, and management responses with remediation timelines are tracked until closure. Regulatory regimes in finance and healthcare often mandate periodic independent audits of recovery capability.
