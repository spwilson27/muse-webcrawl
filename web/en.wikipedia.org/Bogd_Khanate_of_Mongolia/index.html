Bogd Khanate of Mongolia

The Bogd Khanate of Mongolia was a period of Mongolian history between 1911 and 1919, and a second time from 1921 to 1924, during which Mongolia was a theocratic khanate under the Bogd Khan, the eighth Jebtsundamba Khutughtu, the spiritual leader of Mongolian Buddhism.

Independence from Qing China

As the Qing dynasty collapsed in 1911, Mongolian nobles and lamas declared independence in December of that year and enthroned the Bogd Khan as ruler in the capital, Urga (now Ulaanbaatar). The new state was not widely recognized internationally. Under the 1915 Treaty of Kyakhta, agreed between Russia, China, and Mongolia, Outer Mongolia accepted Chinese suzerainty in return for autonomy.

Chinese occupation and restoration

In 1919 the Chinese general Xu Shuzheng occupied Urga and abolished Mongolian autonomy. In February 1921, the anti-Bolshevik Russian officer Roman von Ungern-Sternberg drove the Chinese out and restored the Bogd Khan. Months later, Mongolian revolutionaries allied with the Red Army defeated Ungern-Sternberg and established a constitutional government with the Khan as a limited monarch.

End of the khanate

The Bogd Khan died in May 1924. The Mongolian People's Republic was proclaimed later that year, ending the theocratic monarchy, and the search for a ninth reincarnation of the Jebtsundamba Khutughtu was officially forbidden during the socialist period.
