Māui (mythology)

Māui is a culture hero and trickster in Polynesian mythology. In Māori mythology he is the youngest of five brothers, born prematurely and cast into the sea wrapped in a tress of his mother's hair, from which he takes his full name, Māui-tikitiki-a-Taranga. Versions of his exploits are told across Polynesia, from Hawaiʻi to Tahiti to Tonga.

Feats in Māori tradition

Fishing up the North Island. Stowing away in his brothers' waka, Māui used an enchanted jawbone hook and his own blood as bait to haul a colossal fish from the ocean — Te Ika-a-Māui, "the fish of Māui", which became the North Island of New Zealand. The South Island is his canoe, Te Waipounamu, and Stewart Island his anchor stone.

Slowing the sun. Because the sun, Tama-nui-te-rā, crossed the sky too quickly for people to work, Māui and his brothers wove great flax ropes and snared the sun at dawn, beating it until it agreed to travel more slowly.

Bringing fire. Māui obtained fire from Mahuika, the goddess of fire and his ancestress, tricking her out of her burning fingernails one by one; fire survived, in her final act, in the seeds of certain trees such as the kaikōmako.

Death. Seeking to reverse death for humankind, Māui attempted to pass through the body of Hine-nui-te-pō, the goddess of death, but was crushed when a fantail's laughter woke her — which is why mortals die.

Across Polynesia

Hawaiian tradition credits Māui with raising the sky, fishing up islands, and restraining the sun from Haleakalā ("house of the sun"); his name survives in the island of Maui. The figure is among the most widely shared in Pacific oral literature.
