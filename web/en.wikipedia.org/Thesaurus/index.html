Thesaurus

A thesaurus (plural: thesauri or thesauruses) is a reference work that lists words grouped together according to similarity of meaning (containing synonyms and sometimes antonyms), in contrast to a dictionary, which provides definitions for words and generally lists them in alphabetical order. The word "thesaurus" comes from Latin thēsaurus, which in turn comes from Ancient Greek thēsaurós (θησαυρός), meaning "treasure, treasury, storehouse".

History

Collections of synonyms and conceptually related words are ancient: the Sanskrit Amarakosha, a thesaurus in verse by Amarasimha, dates to around the 4th century CE. In the modern era, the landmark English work is Roget's Thesaurus, compiled by Peter Mark Roget and first published in 1852. Roget organised the vocabulary of English into six broad classes — Abstract Relations, Space, Matter, Intellect, Volition, and Affections — subdivided into a hierarchy of concepts, an arrangement still used in modern editions. Unlike an alphabetical thesaurus, Roget's original concept-based organisation requires the user to think of an idea and then find words expressing it.

Types and uses

Thesauri may be organised alphabetically, like a dictionary with synonym lists under each headword, or conceptually, following Roget. In library and information science, a thesaurus is a controlled vocabulary used for indexing and information retrieval, in which terms are linked by hierarchical (broader/narrower), equivalence (synonym), and associative relationships; examples include the Art & Architecture Thesaurus and MeSH (Medical Subject Headings). Word processors and search engines incorporate thesaurus functions to help writers find alternative wording.
