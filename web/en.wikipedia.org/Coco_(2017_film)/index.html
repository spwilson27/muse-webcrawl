Coco (2017 film)

Coco is a 2017 American animated fantasy film produced by Pixar Animation Studios and released by Walt Disney Pictures. It was directed by Lee Unkrich and co-directed by Adrian Molina, from a story conceived by Unkrich. The film is inspired by the Mexican holiday Día de los Muertos (Day of the Dead).

Plot

Miguel Rivera, a 12-year-old boy in the town of Santa Cecilia, dreams of becoming a musician like his idol Ernesto de la Cruz, despite his family's generations-old ban on music. On Día de los Muertos, Miguel is transported to the Land of the Dead, where, with the help of the trickster skeleton Héctor, he seeks Ernesto's blessing and uncovers the true history of his family's ban. In the end, Miguel learns that Héctor is his great-great-grandfather and the true songwriter behind Ernesto's fame.

Music

The score was composed by Michael Giacchino, with songs including "Remember Me", written by Kristen Anderson-Lopez and Robert Lopez, which won the Academy Award for Best Original Song.

Reception and awards

Coco grossed over $814 million worldwide and was widely praised for its animation, story, music and respectful portrayal of Mexican culture. It won the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature and Best Original Song at the 90th Academy Awards, as well as the Golden Globe and BAFTA for Best Animated Film.
