2008 financial crisis

The 2008 financial crisis, also known as the global financial crisis (GFC), was a severe worldwide economic crisis that occurred in the late 2000s. It was the most serious financial crisis since the Great Depression and triggered the Great Recession.

The crisis had its roots in the United States housing market. Years of low interest rates, lax lending standards and a speculative bubble in house prices encouraged banks to issue large numbers of subprime mortgages to borrowers with poor credit. These mortgages were packaged into mortgage-backed securities (MBS) and collateralized debt obligations (CDOs), which received high ratings from credit rating agencies and were sold to investors worldwide.

Collapse

When house prices began to fall in 2006–2007, mortgage defaults rose sharply and the value of mortgage-linked securities collapsed. In March 2008 the investment bank Bear Stearns was rescued through a Federal Reserve-backed purchase by JPMorgan Chase. On 15 September 2008, Lehman Brothers filed for bankruptcy — the largest in US history — after the government declined to bail it out. Panic spread through global money markets, interbank lending froze, and stock markets plunged; the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 777.68 points on 29 September 2008.

Response

Governments and central banks responded with extraordinary measures. The United States enacted the Emergency Economic Stabilization Act of 2008, creating the $700 billion Troubled Asset Relief Program (TARP) to recapitalise banks. The Federal Reserve cut interest rates to near zero and began quantitative easing. Many European banks were nationalised or bailed out, and the crisis contributed to the subsequent European sovereign debt crisis.

Aftermath

Millions lost their homes to foreclosure and their jobs as unemployment in the US reached 10 percent in October 2009. Regulatory reforms included the Dodd–Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act of 2010 in the United States and the international Basel III capital standards.
