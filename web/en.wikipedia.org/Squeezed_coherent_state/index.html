Squeezed coherent state

In physics, a squeezed coherent state is a quantum state that is usually described by two non-commuting observables having continuous spectra of eigenvalues. Examples are position and momentum of a particle, and the (dimension-less) electric field in the amplitude (phase 0) and in the phase (phase 90°) of a light wave (the wave's quadratures).

Definition

The product of the uncertainties of two such operators must satisfy the uncertainty principle; in a squeezed state the uncertainty is redistributed so that one observable's variance falls below the standard quantum limit while the conjugate observable's variance increases correspondingly, keeping the product at or above the minimum. Squeezed states of light are typically generated by nonlinear optical processes such as parametric down-conversion or four-wave mixing.

Applications

Squeezed light is injected into the LIGO and Virgo gravitational-wave detectors to reduce quantum noise and improve sensitivity, and is used in quantum metrology and continuous-variable quantum information.
