Wikipedia Zero

Wikipedia Zero was an initiative of the Wikimedia Foundation that ran from 2012 to 2018, partnering with mobile telephone service providers to offer their subscribers free-of-charge access to Wikipedia on mobile devices — a practice known as zero-rating. Its goal was to reduce barriers to accessing free knowledge, particularly in the developing world, where data costs were often prohibitive.

History

The programme launched in 2012 with initial partners including Orange in Africa and the Middle East, and expanded to dozens of operators in more than 50 countries, among them Telenor, Airtel, Aircel, MTN and Digi. At its peak, the service reached hundreds of millions of mobile subscribers. A related earlier project, Wikipedia for Schools, addressed offline access, while the Foundation also promoted SMS and USSD access experiments such as Wikipedia SMS in Kenya.

Reception and criticism

Wikipedia Zero received the 2013 SXSW Interactive Activism Award. It also attracted criticism: zero-rating was argued to conflict with net neutrality principles, and in 2016 the Telecom Regulatory Authority of India banned discriminatory data tariffs, ending such programmes there. The Wikimedia Foundation defended the initiative as non-commercial and aimed purely at free access to knowledge, distinguishing it from commercial zero-rated bundles such as Facebook's Free Basics, which it declined to emulate in scope.

Closure

In February 2018 the Foundation announced the programme would be wound down during that year, citing declining usage, falling mobile data costs in target markets, and an intention to pursue new partnerships and approaches to raising awareness of Wikipedia instead.
