Alvin Plantinga

Alvin Carl Plantinga
Born: 15 November 1932, Ann Arbor, Michigan, U.S.
Era: Contemporary philosophy
Main interests: Epistemology, philosophy of religion, modal logic

Alvin Carl Plantinga (born 15 November 1932) is an American analytic philosopher widely regarded as one of the most important philosophers of religion of the twentieth century. He taught for many years at the University of Notre Dame and Calvin College. His work revived scholarly interest in theistic arguments and in Christian philosophy within analytic philosophy.

Major contributions

Plantinga is known for his "free will defense" against the problem of evil, presented in God, Freedom, and Evil (1974), which argues that it is logically possible that God could not create a world with significantly free creatures who never do wrong. His modal version of the ontological argument reformulated Anselm's reasoning using possible-worlds semantics. In epistemology, his three-volume Warrant trilogy developed a theory of knowledge as beliefs produced by properly functioning cognitive faculties, and argued that belief in God can be "properly basic" — held rationally without evidence — a position central to Reformed epistemology. He has also argued against philosophical naturalism with his evolutionary argument against naturalism.

Recognition

Plantinga was president of the American Philosophical Association (Western Division) and the Society of Christian Philosophers, which he co-founded. In 2017 he received the Templeton Prize. Time magazine described him in 1980 as "America's leading orthodox Protestant philosopher of God."
