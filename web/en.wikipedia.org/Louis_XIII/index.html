Louis XIII

Louis XIII (27 September 1601 – 14 May 1643), called "the Just", was King of France from 1610 until his death in 1643 and King of Navarre (as Louis II) from 1610 to 1620. His reign, dominated by his chief minister Cardinal Richelieu, saw France curb the power of the Habsburgs, reduce the domestic independence of the Huguenots and the great nobility, and emerge as the leading power of Europe.

Reign

Louis came to the throne at age eight after the assassination of his father, Henri IV; his mother, Marie de' Medici, acted as regent. In 1617 he asserted personal rule by having her favourite Concino Concini assassinated and exiling his mother. From 1624 his government was directed by Richelieu, whose programme centred on royal authority at home and opposition to Habsburg Spain and Austria abroad. Royal forces took the Huguenot stronghold of La Rochelle after a long siege in 1628, though the Peace of Alès (1629) preserved Protestant worship while ending their military autonomy. France intervened openly in the Thirty Years' War against the Habsburgs from 1635.

Death and succession

Louis died at Saint-Germain-en-Laye in May 1643, five months after Richelieu. He was succeeded by his four-year-old son, Louis XIV, under the regency of his widow, Anne of Austria.
