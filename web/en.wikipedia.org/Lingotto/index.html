Lingotto is a building complex in Turin, Italy, famous as the historic factory of the automobile manufacturer Fiat and as an icon of twentieth-century industrial architecture. The main building, some 500 metres long and five storeys high, was constructed between 1916 and 1923 to designs by the engineer Giacomo Matté-Trucco. Its most celebrated feature is the test track on its roof, an oval circuit with steeply banked ends roughly one kilometre in length.

Design

The factory embodied a vertical assembly line: raw materials entered at ground level and the cars ascended through successive production stages on the floors above, spiralling up internal ramps, until completed vehicles emerged onto the roof for testing. The reinforced-concrete frame, with its regular grid of columns, long ribbon windows and two helical ramps at either end, was admired by the architect Le Corbusier, who praised it in Vers une architecture (1923); it became a touchstone of modernist and Futurist architecture. The rooftop track appears in the 1969 film The Italian Job, in which Minis are chased across it.

Later history

Production ceased in 1982 as Fiat's manufacturing moved elsewhere. In the 1980s and 1990s the complex was converted, under the direction of architect Renzo Piano, into a multifunctional centre containing a shopping mall, concert hall (the Auditorium Giovanni Agnelli, designed with acoustician Yasuhisa Toyota), a hotel, a convention centre, a multiplex cinema, a university engineering faculty, and the Pinacoteca Giovanni e Marella Agnelli art gallery, housed in a steel "scrigno" (jewel box) structure on the roof. The rooftop track itself was retained and remains open for events. The district around the building, also called Lingotto, was the site of the Oval Lingotto speed-skating arena built for the 2006 Winter Olympics.
