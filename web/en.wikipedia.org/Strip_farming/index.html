Strip farming

Strip farming, or strip cropping, is a method of cultivating a field in long, narrow strips of alternating crops, typically aligned along contours or perpendicular to the prevailing wind. It is used to reduce soil erosion where a slope is too steep or too long, or when other methods of preventing erosion are impractical.

Method

Crops with good soil-holding roots, such as hay or legumes, are alternated with erosion-prone row crops such as corn or soybeans. The close-growing strips slow runoff water and trap sediment carried from the row-crop strips, while wind strips place tall or dense crops as windbreaks. Strip widths are chosen to suit machinery and slope.

History

The practice was promoted widely in the United States during the Dust Bowl of the 1930s by the Soil Conservation Service, alongside contour ploughing and terracing, and remains a standard conservation practice on erodible land. A related but distinct usage, strip farming in medieval Europe, referred to the division of open fields into long narrow strips held by different tenants.
