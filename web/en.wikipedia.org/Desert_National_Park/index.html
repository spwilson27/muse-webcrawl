Desert National Park

Desert National Park is a national park situated in the Indian state of Rajasthan, near the towns of Jaisalmer and Barmer. It is one of the largest national parks in India, covering an area of about 3,162 square kilometres (1,221 sq mi) of the Thar Desert. The park was gazetted in 1980 and protects a representative ecosystem of the Thar, including sand dunes, rocky outcrops, salt lake bottoms, and pavements.

Fauna

The park is best known as one of the last strongholds of the critically endangered great Indian bustard. Other notable fauna include the blackbuck, chinkara, Bengal fox, desert fox, wolf, desert cat, and the Indian spiny-tailed lizard. Migratory and resident birds include eagles, harriers, falcons, buzzards, kestrels, larks, shrikes, and demoiselle cranes, which arrive in winter.

Fossils

The Akal Wood Fossil Park, within the national park area, preserves fossilised tree trunks estimated to be around 180 million years old, evidence that the region once supported forests and lay under a sea in the Jurassic period. Marine fossils and fossilised shells are also found in the park.

Flora

Vegetation is sparse and adapted to aridity: khejri, ber, rohida, dhok, and sewan grass among the dunes provide fodder for livestock and habitat for wildlife.
