Imhotep

Lived: 27th century BC, ancient Egypt
Roles: Chancellor to Pharaoh Djoser; architect; physician; high priest
Known for: Designing the Step Pyramid at Saqqara

Imhotep (Egyptian: "the one who comes in peace") was an Egyptian polymath of the Third Dynasty who served under Pharaoh Djoser. He is credited as the architect of the Step Pyramid at Saqqara, the first monumental stone building in Egypt, and is among the very few non-royal Egyptians to have been deified after death.

Life and works

Imhotep held titles including chancellor of the King of Lower Egypt, high priest of Heliopolis, and chief sculptor. The Step Pyramid complex at Saqqara transformed Egyptian royal tomb architecture from mudbrick mastabas into a six-stepped stone pyramid surrounded by courtyards and temples. Later tradition credited Imhotep with wisdom writings and with foundational works of medicine; the Edwin Smith Papyrus is sometimes associated with him.

Deification and cult

By the New Kingdom, Imhotep was venerated as a god of medicine and healing, one of only two commoners (with Amenhotep, son of Hapu) to receive full divine status. Small bronze statues depict him seated with a scroll. His tomb has never been conclusively identified, though it is believed to lie at Saqqara.
