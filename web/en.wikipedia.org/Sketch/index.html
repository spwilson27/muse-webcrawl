Sketch (drawing)

Sketch Medium: Pencil, pen, charcoal, chalk
Purpose: Study, draft, visual note-taking

A sketch is a rapidly executed freehand drawing that is not usually intended as a finished work. A sketch may serve a number of purposes: it might record something that the artist sees, it might develop or record an idea for later use, or it might be used as a quick way of graphically demonstrating an image, idea or principle.

Technique

Sketches are typically made with dry, quick media such as graphite pencil, pen and ink, charcoal, chalk or silverpoint, favouring speed over detail. Lines are often loose and exploratory, with construction lines left visible and forms built up through hatching and cross-hatching. Artists keep sketchbooks for studies and daily practice, and courtroom sketch artists produce rapid likenesses in settings where cameras are prohibited.

Role in art and design

In the European academic tradition, studies and sketches — the Italian bozzetto and modello — were essential stages in preparing paintings and sculptures, allowing artists to resolve composition and detail before committing to the final work. Leonardo da Vinci's sketchbooks are celebrated examples of sketching as a tool of inquiry. Today sketching remains fundamental in industrial design, architecture and illustration, where rough thumbnail sketches are used to explore concepts quickly, and the term has been extended to digital sketching on graphics tablets.
