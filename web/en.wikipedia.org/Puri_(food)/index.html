Puri (food)

Puri (also spelled poori) is a deep-fried bread made from unleavened whole-wheat flour that originated in the Indian subcontinent. When lowered into hot oil, the small disc of dough puffs up into a hollow, balloon-like bread with a crisp exterior.

Preparation

A stiff dough of atta (whole-wheat flour), water, and salt — sometimes with a little semolina or oil — is rolled into small circles and deep-fried in oil or ghee. The steam generated inside makes the puri puff; it deflates somewhat as it cools. Puris are best eaten freshly fried.

Serving

Puri is commonly served at breakfast or as part of festive meals, accompanied by potato curry (puri bhaji), chole (spiced chickpeas), halwa, or kheer. It features in the thali meal and has many regional variants, including the Bengali luchi made with refined flour and stuffed versions such as radhaballabi. A miniature crisp puri forms the shell of pani puri / golgappa, the popular street snack.
