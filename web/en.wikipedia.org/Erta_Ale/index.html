Erta Ale (Afar for "smoking mountain") is a continuously active basaltic shield volcano in the Afar Region of northeastern Ethiopia, at the centre of the Erta Ale Range in the Danakil Depression. It is one of only a handful of volcanoes in the world — and the most famous in Africa — to host a persistent lava lake in its summit crater.

Geology

Erta Ale lies where the East African Rift is splitting the continent apart; the volcano sits in a depression below sea level, part of the Afar Triangle where three rift systems meet. The broad, low shield is about 50 km wide and rises only 613 m above sea level, but the summit caldera contains two pit craters, one of which holds an active lava lake that has been present, with interruptions, since at least 1906.

Activity

An eruption has been ongoing since 1967, making Erta Ale one of the longest-lived current eruptions on Earth. In January 2017 a major overflow episode sent lava streaming down the flanks and drained and refilled the lava lake, dramatically reshaping the summit. Earlier notable activity included explosions in 2005 that killed livestock and forced evacuations of nearby Afar communities.

Visiting

Despite extreme heat — the Danakil is among the hottest inhabited regions on Earth — Erta Ale is a popular if demanding tourist destination, usually reached by multi-day expeditions from Mekelle or Hamedela, with an overnight climb to view the lava lake. Security in the region has occasionally been a concern: in 2012 an armed attack at the volcano killed five tourists, and further incidents have occurred since.
