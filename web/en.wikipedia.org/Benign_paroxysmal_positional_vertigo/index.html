Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo

Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV) is a disorder of the inner ear in which brief episodes of vertigo occur with changes in head position. It is the most common cause of vertigo.

Cause

BPPV results when otoconia — calcium carbonate crystals normally in the utricle — become displaced into a semicircular canal, most often the posterior canal, where they move with head motion and falsely signal rotation (canalithiasis).

Diagnosis

The Dix–Hallpike test reproduces vertigo and a characteristic torsional nystagmus when the affected ear is placed downward.

Treatment

Particle-repositioning manoeuvres, especially the Epley manoeuvre, return the crystals to the utricle and resolve symptoms in most patients. Medication plays little role.
