Cingulata

Cingulata
Order of mammals
Includes: armadillos, glyptodonts, pampatheres

Cingulata is an order of New World placental mammals characterized by a leathery armored shell formed of bony plates called osteoderms. The only surviving members are the armadillos (family Dasypodidae and Chlamyphoridae); extinct members include the large club-tailed glyptodonts and the pampatheres.

Evolution

Cingulata belongs to the superorder Xenarthra, together with the anteaters and sloths. The group originated in South America during the Paleocene or earlier and diversified in isolation there. Glyptodonts grew to the size of small cars; the largest, Doedicurus, bore a spiked tail club. Cingulates reached North America during the Great American Interchange, and the nine-banded armadillo continues to expand its range in the United States.

Characteristics

The armor consists of a scapular shield, a pelvic shield, and—in armadillos—movable bands between them that allow flexibility. Most species are insectivorous or omnivorous diggers with strong claws and a low metabolic rate.
