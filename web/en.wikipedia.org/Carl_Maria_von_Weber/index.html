Carl Maria von Weber

Carl Maria von Weber (18 or 19 November 1786 – 5 June 1826) was a German composer, conductor, virtuoso pianist, guitarist and critic who was one of the first significant composers of the Romantic era. Best known for his operas Der Freischütz, Euryanthe and Oberon, Weber pioneered the German Romantic opera and greatly influenced later composers including Wagner.

Life

Born in Eutin, Weber travelled widely as a child with his father's theatre troupe. He worked in Salzburg, Munich, Prague and Dresden, where as Kapellmeister from 1817 he championed German opera against the Italian tradition. He died in London of tuberculosis, shortly after the premiere of Oberon at Covent Garden.

Music

Weber's orchestration, use of folk melody, and depiction of the supernatural — above all in the Wolf's Glen scene of Der Freischütz (1821) — established a model for Romantic opera. His clarinet concertos and the piano pieces such as Invitation to the Dance remain standard repertoire.
