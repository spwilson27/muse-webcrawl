The Federated States of Micronesia (FSM) is an island country in Oceania, consisting of four states — Yap, Chuuk, Pohnpei and Kosrae — spread across the western Pacific Ocean's Caroline Islands. Its 607 islands have a combined land area of only 702 km² but are scattered over some 2,700 km of ocean, giving the country one of the world's largest exclusive economic zones. The capital is Palikir, on the island of Pohnpei.

History

The islands were settled by Micronesian peoples thousands of years ago; the stone city of Nan Madol, off Pohnpei, was the ceremonial centre of the Saudeleur dynasty. After Spanish colonisation, the Carolines were sold to Germany in 1899, seized by Japan in 1914, and after the Second World War administered by the United States as part of the Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands. The FSM adopted its own constitution in 1979 and became independent in 1986 under a Compact of Free Association with the United States, which provides economic assistance and defence in exchange for strategic rights; the compact's funding provisions were renewed in 2023–24.

Geography and economy

The islands range from mountainous volcanic islands to low coral atolls and are highly vulnerable to climate change and sea-level rise. The economy depends on US compact assistance, fishing (especially tuna licence fees), subsistence agriculture and a small tourism sector. Chuuk Lagoon is famed for its Second World War wreck diving.
