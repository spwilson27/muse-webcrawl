Miter box

A miter box (also spelled mitre box) is a woodworking tool used to guide a hand saw to make precise miter cuts in a workpiece, most commonly for joining moulding, picture frames, and trim at corners. The box holds the material at a set angle while slots in its walls guide the saw blade.

Description

A traditional miter box is an open-ended, three-sided channel made of wood, plastic, or metal, with pre-cut slots at common angles — typically 90°, 45°, and sometimes 22.5° and 30° — on both the left and right sides. The workpiece is placed against the inside back wall and the saw is run through the matching pair of slots. Adjustable metal miter boxes with clamping mechanisms and angle scales allow cuts at arbitrary angles and were widely used before the spread of powered miter saws.

Use

Miter boxes are used with a backsaw, whose stiffened blade resists flexing in the guides. The tool remains common for small trim work and in situations where a powered compound miter saw is impractical.
