Il Lombardia, officially the Giro di Lombardia, is a one-day professional cycling race held annually in Lombardy, Italy, each autumn. It is one of the five Monuments of professional road cycling and is nicknamed La corsa delle foglie morte — "the race of the falling leaves". First run in 1905 as Milan–Milan, it traditionally closes the European road-racing season.

Course

The route has varied considerably, starting and finishing in Milan, Como, or Bergamo in different eras. Its signature climbs are around Lake Como: the Madonna del Ghisallo, whose chapel is a shrine to cycling, and the brutally steep Muro di Sormano, with gradients above 25%. The hilly course of roughly 240–250 km favours climbers and puncheurs rather than sprinters.

History and winners

The race was created by the newspaper La Gazzetta dello Sport. Fausto Coppi won a record five times between 1946 and 1954; Tadej Pogačar equalled that tally with five consecutive victories from 2021 to 2025. Other multiple winners include Alfredo Binda, Gino Bartali, Eddy Merckx (later stripped of one win), Sean Kelly, Damiano Cunego, and Philippe Gilbert. The race forms part of the autumn Italian classics series alongside races such as Milano–Torino and the Giro del Piemonte.
