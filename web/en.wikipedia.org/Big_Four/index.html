Big Four

Big Four is a nickname used to refer to the four largest professional services networks in the world: Deloitte, Ernst & Young (EY), KPMG, and PricewaterhouseCoopers (PwC). Together they audit the vast majority of publicly traded companies worldwide and dominate the market for accounting, assurance, tax, and advisory services. The term is also used in other fields for groups of four dominant entities, but its accounting usage is the most widespread.

The firms

Each Big Four network is a global organisation of legally separate member firms that share a common brand, standards, and quality controls. Deloitte is generally the largest by revenue, followed by PwC, EY, and KPMG. Collectively the four employ well over a million people and earn combined revenues in the hundreds of billions of US dollars annually.

History

The modern firms descend from accounting partnerships founded in the 19th and early 20th centuries in London and New York. A long series of mergers reduced an earlier "Big Eight" group of firms through the "Big Six" and "Big Five" stages. The collapse of Arthur Andersen in 2002, following its role as auditor of Enron during that company's accounting scandal, reduced the group to the present four. Andersen's surviving practices were largely absorbed by the remaining firms.

Services

Beyond their traditional audit and assurance work, the Big Four sell tax advice, consulting, financial advisory, risk management, and legal services in some jurisdictions. This expansion into consulting has periodically raised concerns about conflicts of interest between audit independence and advisory revenue, leading regulators to require separation or rotation in some markets.

Criticism and regulation

The concentration of the audit market in four firms has drawn scrutiny from competition authorities, particularly in the United Kingdom and the European Union. Critics argue that the firms' dominance reduces choice for large multinational clients and that audit failures in corporate collapses reveal shortcomings in audit quality. Proposed remedies have included joint audits, market-share caps, and structural separation of audit and non-audit businesses.

Other uses

"Big Four" also describes, among other groups, the four major American thrash metal bands (Metallica, Slayer, Megadeth, and Anthrax), the four largest British railway companies created by the 1923 grouping, and historically the principal Allied powers of the First World War.
