Library catalogue

A library catalogue (American English: library catalog) is a register of all bibliographic items found in a library or group of libraries. It enables users to locate books and other material by author, title, subject, or other access points.

Forms

Catalogues have taken many forms over time: handwritten lists, printed book catalogues, sheaf catalogues, and the card catalogue that dominated libraries through most of the 20th century. Since the late 20th century, the online public access catalogue (OPAC) has become the standard form, increasingly integrated with discovery services spanning physical and electronic collections.

Cataloguing rules

Cataloguers describe items according to shared standards so that records are consistent across libraries. Major codes have included Charles Ammi Cutter's rules (1876), the Anglo-American Cataloguing Rules (AACR2, 1978), and its successor Resource Description and Access (RDA, 2010). Bibliographic data has traditionally been encoded in the MARC format, while classification schemes such as Dewey Decimal and Library of Congress Classification arrange books on shelves.

Union catalogues

A union catalogue combines the records of many libraries, allowing users to locate items across institutions and request interlibrary loans. The largest example is WorldCat, maintained by OCLC, which aggregates holdings from libraries in many countries.
