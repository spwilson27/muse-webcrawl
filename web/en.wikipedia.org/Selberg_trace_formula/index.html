Selberg trace formula

In mathematics, the Selberg trace formula, introduced by Atle Selberg in 1956, is an expression for the character of the unitary representation of a group G on the space L² of square-integrable functions on a quotient of G. It generalizes the classical Poisson summation formula and plays a role in analytic number theory analogous to the relationship between the zeros of the Riemann zeta function and prime numbers.

The compact case

For a compact quotient such as a Riemann surface of genus greater than one, the formula equates a spectral side — a sum over the eigenvalues of the Laplacian — with a geometric side — a sum over closed geodesics grouped by conjugacy classes in the fundamental group.

Significance

Selberg used the formula to study the distribution of eigenvalues and to prove results on the Riemann zeta function. Its generalization to general reductive groups, developed by James Arthur as the Arthur–Selberg trace formula, became a central tool in the Langlands program and in the proof of cases of functoriality.
