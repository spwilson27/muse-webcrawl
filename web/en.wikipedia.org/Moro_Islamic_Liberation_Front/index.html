Moro Islamic Liberation Front

[Image: Members of the Moro Islamic Liberation Front in Maguindanao]

The Moro Islamic Liberation Front (MILF) is a Moro separatist group and political organization based in Mindanao in the southern Philippines. It was founded in the late 1970s by Salamat Hashim, who broke away from Nur Misuari's Moro National Liberation Front (MNLF) over ideological and ethnic disputes, formally adopting the MILF name in 1984. Its goal was the establishment of an Islamic state or autonomous homeland for the Bangsamoro people.

Insurgency

The MILF grew into the largest Moro armed movement, fielding an armed wing, the Bangsamoro Islamic Armed Forces, with camps such as Camp Abubakar in Maguindanao. After the 1996 peace agreement between the government and the MNLF, Manila opened talks with the MILF, interrupted by President Joseph Estrada's "all-out war" in 2000, which captured Camp Abubakar, and by renewed fighting in 2003 and 2008. A long-running ceasefire, monitored by an international team led by Malaysia, held through much of the 2010s.

Peace process

In 2014 the MILF and the Aquino government signed the Comprehensive Agreement on the Bangsamoro. Legislation stalled after the 2015 Mamasapano clash, but under President Rodrigo Duterte the Bangsamoro Organic Law was enacted in 2018. Following a plebiscite in 2019, the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM) was created, and MILF chairman Murad Ebrahim became interim chief minister of the transition government. The MILF's forces are undergoing phased decommissioning, and the organization is transforming itself into a political party while the transition to regular regional elections proceeds.
