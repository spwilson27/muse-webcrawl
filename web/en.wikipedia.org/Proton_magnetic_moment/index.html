Proton magnetic moment

The proton magnetic moment is the magnetic dipole moment of the proton, symbol μp, approximately 2.792847 nuclear magnetons, or about 1.4106 × 10−26 joules per tesla. The proton, like the electron, behaves as a tiny magnet because of its spin.

Anomalous value

If the proton were a simple point-like Dirac particle, its magnetic moment would be exactly one nuclear magneton (g-factor 2). The measured value of about 2.79 nuclear magnetons — an anomalous magnetic moment — was an early indication that the proton has internal structure, now understood in terms of its constituent quarks and gluons. The moment was first measured by Otto Stern and coworkers in the 1930s, a result that surprised theorists.

Significance

The proton's magnetic moment underlies nuclear magnetic resonance and thus MRI: hydrogen nuclei (protons) in the body precess in a magnetic field at the Larmor frequency, emitting signals used to form images. Precise values of μp are tabulated by CODATA among the fundamental physical constants.
