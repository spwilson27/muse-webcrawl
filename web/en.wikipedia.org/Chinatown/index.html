Chinatown
Chinese: 唐人街 / 中國城
Meaning: Ethnic enclave of Chinese people outside Greater China

A Chinatown is an ethnic enclave of Chinese people located outside Greater China, most often in an urban setting. Areas known as Chinatowns exist throughout the world, including in Europe, Asia, Africa, Oceania and the Americas, and developed as overseas Chinese communities settled abroad from the 19th century onward.

History

Some of the oldest Chinatowns grew around trading ports such as Binondo in Manila, founded in 1594. Large communities formed in San Francisco, New York City, London, Toronto and Sydney during gold rushes and railway construction, often in response to discrimination that concentrated residents in particular districts.

Characteristics

Chinatowns typically feature Chinese restaurants, grocery stores, bakeries, banks, herbal medicine shops and cultural institutions, often marked by a paifang gateway and bilingual signage. Many remain centres for newer immigrants while also becoming tourist attractions.

Notable Chinatowns

Major examples include those in San Francisco (the oldest in North America), Manhattan, Yokohama, Melbourne, Vancouver and London's Soho.
