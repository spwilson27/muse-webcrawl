Master-slave morality

ConceptMoral philosophy
Proposed byFriedrich Nietzsche
Key workOn the Genealogy of Morality (1887)

Master-slave morality is a central concept in the philosophy of Friedrich Nietzsche, developed in Beyond Good and Evil (1886) and On the Genealogy of Morality (1887). Nietzsche argued that cultures develop two basic moral frameworks: the master morality of the strong, who value pride, power, and nobility, and the slave morality of the weak, who value kindness, humility, and sympathy.

Slave revolt in morality

Nietzsche claimed that slave morality began as a creative reaction of the oppressed against master values, a revaluation in which the virtues of the powerless (meekness, patience) were recast as good and the qualities of the masters (strength, pride) condemned as evil.

Influence

The distinction remains a standard topic in moral philosophy and Nietzsche scholarship, though interpretations of Nietzsche's aims vary widely.
