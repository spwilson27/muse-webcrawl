Réunion


Placeholder image: Réunion

Réunion (French: La Réunion) is an island in the Indian Ocean, east of Madagascar and about 175 kilometres southwest of Mauritius. It is an overseas department and region of France, and forms part of the European Union as an outermost region. Its capital and largest city is Saint-Denis.

Geography

The island is volcanic in origin, formed by the Réunion hotspot. The Piton des Neiges shield volcano, 3,069 metres high, is the highest point in the Indian Ocean. The active Piton de la Fournaise is one of the world's most active volcanoes. The interior is mountainous, with three natural amphitheatres known as cirques: Salazie, Cilaos and Mafate.

History

The island was uninhabited when Portuguese explorers first recorded it in the early 16th century. France took possession in 1642 and settled it as Île Bourbon. Coffee and later sugar cane plantations relied on enslaved labour from Africa and Madagascar; slavery was abolished in 1848, after which indentured workers arrived from India, China and Africa. Réunion became an overseas department in 1946.

Demographics and culture

The population is about 870,000, of mixed African, European, Indian and Chinese ancestry. French is the official language; Réunion Creole is widely spoken. Hinduism, Christianity and Islam are all practised. The cuisine blends French, Malagasy, Indian and Chinese influences.

Economy

Sugar cane remains the main agricultural export, alongside rum and vanilla. Tourism is growing, and the island is a notable destination for hiking and for viewing volcanic eruptions. Réunion is also known for the Grand Raid ultramarathon.
