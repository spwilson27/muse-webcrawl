Paranormal Activity is a 2007 American supernatural horror film written, directed, photographed and edited by Oren Peli. Presented in the found-footage style, it centres on a young couple, Katie and Micah (Katie Featherston and Micah Sloat), who move into a suburban San Diego home and set up a video camera to record the increasingly violent nocturnal disturbances of a demonic presence that Katie says has followed her since childhood. It is widely cited as one of the most profitable films ever made relative to its budget.

Production

Peli, a first-time filmmaker, shot the film in his own house over about a week with a home video camera and a budget commonly reported as $15,000, using the actors' real first names and largely improvised dialogue. After screening at the Screamfest festival in 2007 and the Slamdance Film Festival in 2008, the film was acquired by Paramount Pictures and its DreamWorks studio; following test screenings, a new ending was shot at the suggestion of Steven Spielberg.

Release and reception

Paramount released the film through an innovative "demand it" marketing campaign in September–October 2009, letting audiences vote for screenings in their cities. It became a sleeper hit, grossing about $193 million worldwide. Critics praised its minimalist build-up of dread; comparisons were drawn to The Blair Witch Project, which had pioneered the found-footage marketing model a decade earlier.

Franchise

The film launched a franchise produced by Blumhouse, including Paranormal Activity 2 (2010), 3 (2011), 4 (2012), The Marked Ones (2014), The Ghost Dimension (2015) and the streaming sequel Next of Kin (2021), which together grossed close to $900 million worldwide.
