Manche (French pronunciation: [mɑ̃ʃ]) is a department in Normandy, northwestern France, named after the English Channel, which is known in French as La Manche ("the sleeve"). Its prefecture is Saint-Lô, and its largest city is Cherbourg-en-Cotentin. The department covers most of the Cotentin Peninsula and the mainland facing the Channel Islands.

Geography

Manche is surrounded by water on three sides: the English Channel to the west, north, and east, giving it some 350 kilometres of coastline — the longest of any French department's mainland. The landscape is one of bocage (small hedged fields), marais (marshland) around the Cotentin's base, and rocky headlands such as the Nez de Jobourg. The Chausey Islands lie offshore from Granville, and Mont-Saint-Michel, the celebrated tidal island and abbey — one of France's most visited sites — stands in the bay at the department's southwestern corner, bordering Brittany. The department borders Calvados, Orne, Mayenne, and Ille-et-Vilaine.

History

Manche was created on 4 March 1790 during the French Revolution, from part of the former province of Normandy. The area recalls William the Conqueror, who set out from Normandy to conquer England in 1066. Saint-Lô was almost completely destroyed in June 1944 during the Battle of Normandy following the D-Day landings; Utah Beach, where American forces landed on 6 June 1944, lies on Manche's east coast, and the department was a major battleground in the weeks that followed, including the Battle of Cherbourg and Operation Cobra. Because of its near-total destruction, Saint-Lô was nicknamed the "Capital of Ruins".

Economy

Agriculture and food processing are central: Manche is a leading producer of milk, butter, apples and cider, oysters (notably from Blainville-sur-Mer and Saint-Vaast-la-Hougue), mussels, and vegetables from the Créances coast. Naval shipbuilding at Cherbourg (Naval Group, builder of submarines) and the nuclear industry — the Flamanville nuclear power plant and the La Hague reprocessing plant — are major employers. Tourism centres on Mont-Saint-Michel, the D-Day beaches, the Cité de la Mer maritime museum in Cherbourg, and the Tatihou island fortifications of Vauban (a UNESCO World Heritage Site).
