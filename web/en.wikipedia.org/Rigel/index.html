Rigel

Rigel is a blue supergiant star in the constellation of Orion. It has the Bayer designation β Orionis, which is Latinized to Beta Orionis and abbreviated Beta Ori or β Ori. Although its designation is "beta", it is normally brighter than the "alpha" star, Betelgeuse, and is usually the brightest star in Orion and the seventh-brightest in the night sky.

Characteristics

Rigel is a massive blue supergiant that has exhausted the hydrogen in its core and expanded to roughly 75 times the radius of the Sun. Its luminosity is around 120,000 times that of the Sun, with considerable uncertainty due to its distance of roughly 860 light-years. It varies slightly in brightness, as is typical of blue supergiants in the Alpha Cygni class of variables, and it is losing mass through a stellar wind.

System

What appears as one star is a multiple system: the bright supergiant Rigel A is accompanied by a binary pair, Rigel B and C, of blue-white main-sequence stars, and a more distant faint companion has also been reported. The name Rigel derives from the Arabic Rijl Jauzah, "the foot of Jauzah" (Orion).

Future

Stars of Rigel's mass are expected to end their lives in core-collapse supernovae, leaving behind a neutron star or black hole.
