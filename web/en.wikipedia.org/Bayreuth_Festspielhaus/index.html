The Bayreuth Festspielhaus is an opera house in the town of Bayreuth in northern Bavaria, Germany, built by the composer Richard Wagner for the performance of his own operas. It is the home of the annual Bayreuth Festival, at which works of Wagner — above all the Ring cycle and Parsifal — are performed each summer.

Design

The foundation stone was laid on 22 May 1872 on the "Green Hill" outside the town, with financing secured in part by King Ludwig II of Bavaria. Wagner's design was revolutionary: a fan-shaped, amphitheatre-like auditorium with a single raked block of seats and no side boxes, a double proscenium, darkened house lights during performances, and a deep covered orchestra pit — the "mystic abyss" — that hides the musicians and blends the orchestral sound.

History

The theatre opened in August 1876 with the first complete performance of Der Ring des Nibelungen. Parsifal was written for its acoustics and premiered there in 1882; for decades it was performed nowhere else. After Wagner's death the festival was run by his widow Cosima, then by descendants including Winifred Wagner — whose friendship with Hitler darkened its interwar reputation — and after 1951 by Wieland and Wolfgang Wagner, whose abstract "New Bayreuth" productions transformed opera staging. It has remained in the hands of the Wagner family and the Richard Wagner Foundation.

Acoustics

The brick-and-wood interior, the covered pit and the continental seating produce a uniquely blended, warm sound long prized by singers and conductors.
