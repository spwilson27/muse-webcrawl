Politics (Aristotle)

Politics (Greek: Πολιτικά, Politika) is a work of political philosophy by Aristotle, written in the 4th century BCE. Composed of eight books, it examines the city-state (polis), citizenship, constitutions, slavery, education and the best practicable state.

Aristotle famously states that “man is by nature a political animal.” He classifies constitutions by who rules and whether they rule in the common interest — kingship, aristocracy and polity as correct forms, tyranny, oligarchy and democracy as deviations — and favors a large middle class as stabilizing.
