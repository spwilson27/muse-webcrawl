Polish–Soviet War

Date1919 – 1921
LocationCentral and Eastern Europe
BelligerentsPoland (with Ukrainian allies) vs. Soviet Russia
ResultPolish victory; Treaty of Riga (1921)

The Polish–Soviet War (late 1918/1919 – 18 March 1921) was an armed conflict between the Second Polish Republic and Ukrainian forces on one side and Soviet Russia and Soviet Ukraine on the other. It settled the eastern borders of the reborn Polish state and halted the westward expansion of the Russian Revolution.

Course of the war

Fighting began as Polish and Soviet forces moved into territories vacated by Germany after the First World War. In April 1920, Józef Piłsudski, allied with the Ukrainian leader Symon Petliura, launched the Kiev offensive, briefly taking the city. The Red Army under Mikhail Tukhachevsky counter-attacked and advanced to the gates of Warsaw.

Battle of Warsaw

In August 1920 the Polish counteroffensive from the Wieprz River — the so-called "Miracle on the Vistula" — routed the Red Army. Polish cryptologists' breaking of Soviet ciphers contributed decisively. Further Polish victories at Komarów and the Niemen forced Moscow to negotiate.

Outcome

The Peace of Riga (March 1921) partitioned Belarusian and Ukrainian lands between Poland and the Soviet republics, a settlement that lasted until 1939. The war established Poland as a regional power and, in the view of many historians, prevented the spread of communist revolution into a defeated and unstable Germany.
