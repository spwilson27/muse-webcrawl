Privatization

FieldEconomics; public policy
AntonymNationalization
MethodsShare issue; trade sale; voucher scheme; contracting out

Privatization is the transfer of ownership, property, or business from a government to the private sector, or the shift from public to private provision of services. It may involve selling state-owned enterprises outright, floating shares on a stock exchange, distributing vouchers to citizens, or contracting out services while retaining public ownership of assets.

History

Large-scale privatization became a defining policy of the 1980s under the government of Margaret Thatcher in the United Kingdom, which sold off industries including telecommunications, gas, electricity, water, steel, and airlines. The model spread through Latin America, and after 1989 the post-communist states of Eastern Europe and the former Soviet Union privatised vast state sectors, in Russia and Czechoslovakia partly through mass voucher schemes — with outcomes ranging from successful restructuring to asset stripping and the rise of oligarchs.

Arguments

Supporters argue that private ownership improves efficiency, investment, and consumer choice through competition and capital-market discipline, and that sales raise revenue and reduce public debt. Critics contend that privatised natural monopolies can exploit consumers, that essential services may suffer from underinvestment, and that public accountability is lost; several sectors, such as railways and water, have seen partial renationalisation or renewed public ownership in response.
