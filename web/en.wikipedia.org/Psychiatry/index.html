Psychiatry is the medical specialty devoted to the diagnosis, treatment, and prevention of mental disorders, including substance use disorders. Psychiatrists are physicians who evaluate patients' mental and physical symptoms, make diagnoses, and develop treatment plans, which may include psychotherapy, medications, psychosocial interventions, and medical procedures such as electroconvulsive therapy.

Scope

Psychiatric disorders commonly treated include schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, major depression, anxiety disorders, obsessive–compulsive disorder, post-traumatic stress disorder, eating disorders, and neurocognitive disorders. Subspecialties include child and adolescent psychiatry, geriatric psychiatry, forensic psychiatry, and addiction psychiatry.

History

The care of the mentally ill was centered for centuries in asylums; moral treatment reforms in the late 18th and 19th centuries were pioneered by figures such as Philippe Pinel and William Tuke. Emil Kraepelin's classification of psychoses in the late 19th century laid the groundwork for modern diagnostic systems. The mid-20th century saw the introduction of effective psychotropic medications — chlorpromazine (1952), lithium, and the first antidepressants — transforming treatment and enabling deinstitutionalization. The publication of the DSM-III in 1980 ushered in a standardized, criterion-based diagnostic era.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis relies on clinical interview, mental status examination, collateral history, and standardized criteria from the DSM-5 or ICD-11; there are few laboratory biomarkers for most disorders. Treatments include pharmacotherapy (antidepressants, antipsychotics, mood stabilizers, anxiolytics), psychotherapies often delivered with psychologists, brain stimulation methods (ECT, transcranial magnetic stimulation), and social supports. The biopsychosocial model is the prevailing integrative framework.

Controversies

Psychiatry has faced criticism regarding diagnostic validity and expansion, the influence of the pharmaceutical industry, involuntary treatment, and historical abuses — debates that include the anti-psychiatry movement of the 1960s and ongoing discussion of the medicalization of ordinary distress.
