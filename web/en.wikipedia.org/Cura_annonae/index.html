Cura annonae

Cura Annonae ("care for the grain supply") was the term for the Roman government's system of importing and distributing grain — chiefly wheat — to the residents of the city of Rome, and later Constantinople. From the late Republic it provided subsidised and then free grain (the frumentationes, or grain dole) to registered male citizens.

Empire: Roman Republic / Empire
Commodity: Wheat
Administered by: Praefectus annonae
Supply regions: Egypt, Africa, Sicily, Sardinia

Origins

State grain distributions began with the reforms of Gaius Gracchus in 123 BC, which sold grain below market price. In 58 BC Publius Clodius Pulcher made the distribution free. Julius Caesar and Augustus regularised the recipient rolls, fixing them at roughly 200,000–320,000 people.

Administration and logistics

Under the Empire the praefectus annonae, an equestrian official, supervised the annona: grain fleets sailed from Alexandria and Carthage to Ostia and Puteoli, feeding a capital of about one million people. Emperors took personal credit for the supply, and failures provoked riots, making the annona central to imperial legitimacy — part of the "bread and circuses" described by Juvenal.

Later history

Aurelian replaced the grain dole with bread distributions and added pork and oil. The system continued at Constantinople until the loss of Egypt in the 7th century AD.
