National Weather Service

The National Weather Service (NWS) is an agency of the United States federal government responsible for weather forecasting, warnings of hazardous weather, and related meteorological and hydrological services. It is part of the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) within the Department of Commerce.

History

Federal weather services began in 1870 with a joint resolution of Congress placing weather observation under the Army Signal Service. The civilian Weather Bureau was created in 1890, moved to the Department of Commerce in 1940, and was renamed the National Weather Service in 1970 when NOAA was formed.

Organisation and services

The NWS operates 122 Weather Forecast Offices, 13 river forecast centres, and national centres including the Storm Prediction Center, the National Hurricane Center, the Weather Prediction Center and the Space Weather Prediction Center. It issues public forecasts, severe weather watches and warnings, fire weather and aviation products, and operates the NEXRAD Doppler radar network and NOAA Weather Radio.
