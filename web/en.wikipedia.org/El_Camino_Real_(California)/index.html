El Camino Real (California)

Mission bell markers, first erected in 1906, mark the historic route

El Camino Real (Spanish for "The Royal Road," also known as the California Mission Trail) was a roughly 600-mile (965 km) road connecting the 21 Spanish missions in California, from Mission San Diego de Alcalá in the south to Mission San Francisco Solano in Sonoma in the north. The route also linked the missions with the four presidios and several pueblos of Alta California.

History

The trail developed between 1769 and 1823 as Franciscan missionaries under Junípero Serra and his successors founded the mission chain. The missions were placed about a day's journey apart along the route. After Mexico secularised the missions in the 1830s, the road continued as the principal north–south route of California. Much of its alignment is followed today by U.S. Route 101 and other modern roads.

Commemoration

In 1904, a campaign led by the California El Camino Real Association began marking the route with distinctive cast-iron mission bells suspended from shepherd's-crook-style staffs; the first marker was placed in Los Angeles in 1906. Hundreds of the bells remain along the route, and sections of the historic road are commemorated by markers and monuments.
