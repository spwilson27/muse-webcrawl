The NCAA Division I Football Championship Subdivision (FCS) is the second-highest level of college football in the United States, below the Football Bowl Subdivision (FBS). Governed by the National Collegiate Athletic Association, the FCS determines its national champion through a bracketed playoff tournament, unlike the FBS, whose champion is decided through the College Football Playoff and bowl games.

History

When the NCAA split Division I football in 1978, the lower tier was designated Division I-AA. The subdivision was renamed the Football Championship Subdivision in 2006. FCS programs typically operate with smaller budgets and stadiums than FBS schools and are limited to 63 full scholarship equivalents, compared with 85 in the FBS.

Playoffs

The FCS championship is decided by a single-elimination playoff, which has most recently featured 24 teams: conference champions receive automatic bids and the remaining spots are filled by at-large selections. The title game, the NCAA Division I Football Championship Game, has been held in Frisco, Texas, since 2010.

Notable programs

North Dakota State has been the subdivision's dominant program in the modern era, winning numerous national titles. Other historically successful programs include Georgia Southern, Appalachian State (which later moved to the FBS), Montana, Youngstown State, and South Dakota State.
