Doctor Faustus

Doctor Faustus, also rendered Doktor Faustus or Doctor Faustus, is the title of several works derived from the German folk figure Faust, the scholar-alchemist who makes a pact with the Devil (often represented as Mephistopheles). The name is used of Marlowe's Elizabethan play and, most prominently in modern literature, of Thomas Mann's 1947 novel Doktor Faustus: The Life of the German Composer Adrian Leverkühn, Told by a Friend, both of which present the figure in very different forms. For the broader legendary figure, see Faust.

Thomas Mann's novel

Mann's Doktor Faustus (1947) takes the form of a biography of the German composer Adrian Leverkühn, written by his loyal friend, the schoolteacher Serenus Zeitblom. Leverkühn, a brilliant but isolated musical genius, knowingly enters into a pact with the Devil—symbolised in the novel by syphilis infection and a hallucinatory conversation—in exchange for twenty-four years of creative genius. Through Leverkühn's life, Mann examines German culture, the collapse of Weimar democracy and the rise of Nazism; music in the novel is itself autonomous, rational and self-destructive. The work is considered one of the major novels of the twentieth century.

Other uses

The title is also borne by other works, including Ferruccio Busoni's operatic Doktor Faust (first performed 1925, unfinished), John Neumeier's ballet Doctor Faustus, and a small number of plays, poems and studies treating the Faust legend. Films and popular culture reimagine Doctor Faustus in fandom and fantasy, such as the Warhammer 40,000 character Fabius Bile, whose alias is "Doctor Faustus".
