Beehive tomb

A beehive tomb, also called a tholos tomb (plural tholoi), is a burial structure characterised by a false dome formed by corbelling — successive rings of stone each projecting slightly inward. The type is best known from Mycenaean Greece of the Late Bronze Age.

Characteristics

Tholos tombs were approached by an open passageway (the dromos) leading to a doorway (the stomion) and a circular chamber roofed by the corbelled dome, often covered by an earthen mound. They served as prestigious burials for elites between roughly 1500 and 1200 BCE.

Famous examples

The largest and best preserved is the so-called Treasury of Atreus at Mycenae, whose dome spans about 14.5 metres and whose lintel stone weighs over 100 tonnes. Similar tombs are known from Messenia, including the tholoi at Pylos, and in Thessaly and Crete.
