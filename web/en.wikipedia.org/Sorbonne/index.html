Sorbonne


The chapel of the Sorbonne, Paris

The Sorbonne (French: La Sorbonne) is a building in the Latin Quarter of Paris, France, which from 1253 onwards housed the College of Sorbonne, part of the University of Paris and one of the first universities in the world. The name is commonly used to refer to the historic University of Paris itself. The building is now shared by several Parisian universities, including Sorbonne University and Paris 1 Panthéon-Sorbonne University, and houses the Paris rectorate.

History

The college was founded in 1253 (confirmed 1257) by Robert de Sorbon, chaplain and confessor to King Louis IX, to provide lodging and theological instruction for poor students. It became the theological centre of the university, and its doctors were consulted as an authority on doctrine throughout Europe. During the French Revolution the university was suppressed, and the Sorbonne buildings were later restored to education under Napoleon.

The building

The current complex was rebuilt between 1885 and 1901 under architect Henri-Paul Nénot, in a grand academic style. The chapel, built in the 1630s under Cardinal Richelieu — whose tomb it contains — is the surviving element of the earlier buildings. The Sorbonne's grand amphitheatre (Grand Amphithéâtre), decorated with Puvis de Chavannes' mural The Sacred Grove, seats about a thousand and hosts ceremonies and lectures.

May 1968

The Sorbonne was famously occupied by students during the events of May 1968, beginning with protests at Nanterre and the closure of the Sorbonne on 3 May, which ignited a nationwide general strike.
