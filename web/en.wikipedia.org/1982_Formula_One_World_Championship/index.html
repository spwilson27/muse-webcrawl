The 1982 Formula One World Championship was the 36th season of FIA Formula One motor racing, contested over sixteen Grands Prix from 23 January to 25 September 1982. It is remembered as one of the most turbulent and tragic seasons in the sport's history: eleven different drivers won races, no driver won more than twice, two drivers were killed, and the championship was won by Finland's Keke Rosberg, who scored only one victory all year.

Season summary

The season was overshadowed by the FISA–FOCA war, which produced a driver strike threat at the opening race in South Africa and a FOCA-team boycott of the San Marino Grand Prix. At Imola, in a field of only fourteen cars, Ferrari's Didier Pironi won after defying an understanding with team-mate Gilles Villeneuve to hold position; Villeneuve vowed never to speak to Pironi again. Two weeks later, at Zolder on 8 May, Villeneuve was killed during qualifying when his Ferrari struck the March of Jochen Mass.

Riccardo Paletti died in a start-line crash at the Canadian Grand Prix in June, and Pironi's career was ended by a violent crash in practice at Hockenheim that broke both his legs — yet he still finished second in the championship. Amid the chaos, Rosberg's consistency in the normally aspirated Williams-Ford FW08, including victory in the Swiss Grand Prix at Dijon, carried him to the title, clinched with fifth place at the final race in Las Vegas. Ferrari won the Constructors' Championship despite its tragedies. John Watson and Pironi finished level on points behind Rosberg.
