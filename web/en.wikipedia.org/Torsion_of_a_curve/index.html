Torsion of a curve

Torsion of a curve
FieldDifferential geometry
Symbolτ (tau)
Related toCurvature, Frenet–Serret formulas

In the differential geometry of curves in three dimensions, the torsion of a curve measures how much the curve deviates from lying in a single plane. Together with the curvature, it forms a complete set of invariants for a space curve: a curve is determined up to rigid motion by its curvature and torsion as functions of arc length.

Definition

Let r(s) be a curve parametrized by arc length, with unit tangent T, unit normal N, and binormal B = T × N. The torsion τ is defined by the Frenet–Serret formula dB/ds = −τN. Equivalently, for a general parametrization, torsion can be computed from the first three derivatives of the position vector.

Interpretation

The torsion measures the rate at which the osculating plane — the plane spanned by the tangent and normal vectors — rotates about the tangent as one moves along the curve. A curve with zero torsion everywhere is a plane curve. A helix has constant nonzero torsion (and constant curvature), which is why it twists steadily out of any plane.

Sign conventions

The sign of the torsion depends on the handedness convention adopted. With the right-handed Frenet frame, a right-handed helix has positive torsion and a left-handed helix has negative torsion.

Applications

Torsion appears in the geometry of DNA supercoiling, the mechanics of elastic rods and cables, and in describing the paths of charged particles and light rays in curved media.
