Kaikōura is a town on the east coast of the South Island of New Zealand, located on State Highway 1 about 180 kilometres north of Christchurch, between the Seaward Kaikōura Range and the Pacific Ocean. Its Māori name, from kai (food) and kōura (crayfish), reflects the abundance of rock lobster in its waters.

Geography and wildlife

The Kaikōura Peninsula juts into a deep offshore canyon system that brings nutrient-rich water close to shore, supporting sperm whales, dusky dolphins, Hector's dolphins, orca, and a large New Zealand fur seal colony at Ōhau Point. Whale Watch Kaikoura, a Māori-owned operator founded in 1987, made the town an internationally known ecotourism destination, and birdwatchers visit to see albatrosses and other pelagic species on boat trips.

History and earthquake

The area was long occupied by Māori, including Waitaha, Ngāti Māmoe, and Ngāi Tahu, and in the nineteenth century became a whaling and sealing station. On 14 November 2016 a magnitude 7.8 earthquake centred near Culverden struck the district: the seabed along the coast was raised by up to several metres, the town's road and rail links were severed by landslides, and two people died in the wider event. State Highway 1 and the Main North Line railway reopened after a major rebuild in late 2017, and the uplifted coastline created new intertidal habitats that are now closely studied.
