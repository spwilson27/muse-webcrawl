Mexico City (Spanish: Ciudad de México, CDMX) is the capital and largest city of Mexico, and one of the largest Spanish-speaking cities in the world. It is a federal entity in its own right, divided into sixteen boroughs (alcaldías), and sits in the Valley of Mexico at high altitude on the drained bed of Lake Texcoco.

History

The city stands on the site of Tenochtitlan, the island capital of the Aztec (Mexica) Empire, founded in 1325 where, according to legend, an eagle perched on a cactus devouring a serpent — the image now on Mexico's flag. Tenochtitlan, with perhaps 200,000 inhabitants, was one of the largest cities on Earth when Hernán Cortés and his indigenous allies conquered and largely destroyed it in 1521. The Spanish rebuilt Mexico City on its ruins, and it became the capital of the viceroyalty of New Spain and, after 1821, of independent Mexico. The city endured occupation by the United States in 1847 and by France in the 1860s, revolutionary upheaval, and a century of explosive growth.

Geography and challenges

Because it rests on lakebed clays and pumps groundwater, the city is sinking by up to tens of centimetres a year in places, tilting colonial buildings like the Metropolitan Cathedral. The high basin traps air pollution, once notorious and now improved. Earthquakes are a recurring danger: the 1985 quake killed thousands, and another struck on 19 September 2017, the anniversary of the first. The metropolitan area spreads far into the State of Mexico, forming one of the world's largest urban agglomerations.

Culture and landmarks

The main square, the Zócalo, is flanked by the Metropolitan Cathedral, the National Palace (with Diego Rivera's murals) and the excavated Templo Mayor of the Aztecs. Chapultepec Park, one of the largest urban parks in the world, contains the National Museum of Anthropology — among the finest museums anywhere — and the only royal castle in the Americas. The Paseo de la Reforma leads to the Angel of Independence; the floating gardens of Xochimilco preserve Aztec-era canals. The historic centre and Xochimilco are UNESCO World Heritage Sites, as is the central campus of the National Autonomous University (UNAM). Mexico City hosted the 1968 Olympic Games and is renowned for its food, from street tacos to some of the world's top-ranked restaurants.
