Iraqi Kurdistan, officially the Kurdistan Region, is an autonomous region in northern Iraq, recognised by the Iraqi constitution of 2005. It borders Iran to the east, Turkey to the north, and Syria to the west. The region is governed by the Kurdistan Regional Government (KRG), seated in Erbil, and its population is predominantly Kurdish, with Turkmen, Assyrian, Arab, and other minorities.

History

Kurds in Iraq faced decades of marginalisation and violent repression under successive Baghdad governments. The Ba'athist regime of Saddam Hussein carried out the Anfal campaign of 1986–89, including the chemical attack on Halabja in March 1988, which killed thousands of civilians and is widely recognised as genocide. After Iraq's defeat in the 1991 Gulf War, a Kurdish uprising and a Western-enforced no-fly zone allowed the region to develop de facto self-rule; elections in 1992 formed the KRG. A civil war in the mid-1990s between the Kurdistan Democratic Party (KDP) and the Patriotic Union of Kurdistan (PUK) split the region into two administrations, later reunified.

Autonomy and politics

The 2005 Iraqi constitution formally recognised the Kurdistan Region as a federal region. It has its own parliament, president, prime minister, and security forces, the Peshmerga. The KDP, dominant in Erbil and Duhok, and the PUK, dominant in Sulaymaniyah, remain the principal parties, alongside opposition movements such as Gorran (Change). Disputes with Baghdad persist over oil exports, the regional budget, and disputed territories, above all oil-rich Kirkuk.

War against ISIL and the 2017 referendum

When the Islamic State (ISIL) swept across northern Iraq in 2014, Peshmerga forces, backed by a U.S.-led coalition, became front-line combatants and took control of Kirkuk and other disputed areas. In September 2017 the KRG held an independence referendum in which over 90 percent voted for secession; Baghdad, neighbouring states, and Western governments opposed the move, and Iraqi forces retook Kirkuk and most disputed territories the following month, forcing the KRG to freeze the referendum's result.

Economy

The economy depends heavily on oil and gas, exported mainly through a pipeline to the Turkish port of Ceyhan, as well as agriculture, tourism, and trade with Turkey and Iran. Erbil's construction boom in the 2000s reflected relative security compared with the rest of Iraq, though salary arrears, corruption, and dependence on oil revenues remain chronic problems.
