India is situated north of the equator between 8°4′ N and 37°6′ N latitude and 68°7′ E to 97°25′ E longitude. It is the seventh-largest country in the world, with a total area of 3,287,263 square kilometres. India measures 3,214 km from north to south and 2,933 km from east to west, and has a land frontier of 15,200 km and a coastline of 7,516.6 km.

Physical geography of India: the Himalaya to the north, the Indo-Gangetic plain, the Deccan plateau and coastal plains.

India shares borders with Pakistan, Afghanistan, China, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh and Myanmar, and maritime borders with Sri Lanka, the Maldives, Indonesia and Thailand.

Physical features

India's physiography is usually divided into six regions: the Northern Mountains (the Himalaya, the world's highest range, formed by the ongoing collision of the Indian Plate with Eurasia); the Indo-Gangetic Plain, the vast fertile alluvial plain of the Indus, Ganges and Brahmaputra systems; the Thar Desert in the northwest; the Central Highlands and Deccan Plateau of peninsular India, flanked by the Western and Eastern Ghats; the Coastal Plains; and the Islands — the Andaman and Nicobar archipelago in the Bay of Bengal and Lakshadweep in the Arabian Sea.

Rivers

The major Himalayan rivers are the Indus, the Ganges and the Brahmaputra; the principal peninsular rivers include the Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Narmada, Tapti and Mahanadi, flowing mostly eastward into the Bay of Bengal, except the Narmada and Tapti, which flow west through rift valleys.

Climate

India's climate is dominated by the South Asian monsoon: the southwest monsoon (June–September) brings most of the country's rainfall, and a retreating northeast monsoon affects the southeast coast. Climates range from alpine in the Himalaya through tropical monsoon in most of the country to arid in the Thar. Natural resources and environment

India has the world's fourth-largest coal reserves, significant iron ore, bauxite, mica and rare earths, and large arable land (about half its area is cultivated). Environmental issues include air and water pollution, groundwater depletion, deforestation and pressure on biodiversity; India is home to four global biodiversity hotspots.
