Natal was a province of South Africa from the formation of the Union of South Africa in 1910 until 1994, when it was merged with the KwaZulu bantustan to form the modern province of KwaZulu-Natal. Its territory lay on the Indian Ocean coast of southeastern South Africa, between the Tugela River and the Eastern Cape.

History

The name commemorates Vasco da Gama's sighting of the coast on Christmas Day 1497 (Natal being Portuguese for Christmas). Afrikaner Voortrekkers founded the short-lived Republic of Natalia in 1839 before Britain annexed the area in 1843; the Colony of Natal received responsible government in 1893. The region was the scene of the Anglo-Zulu War of 1879 and of fighting in the South African (Boer) War. After 1910 Natal was the most English-speaking and, politically, the most separatist-minded of the four provinces.

Geography and population

Natal stretched from the subtropical coast around the port of Durban inland to the Drakensberg escarpment. Its population combined a Zulu-speaking majority in the countryside with large English-speaking and Indian South African communities in the cities — Durban having one of the largest Indian populations of any city outside India, a legacy of indentured labour brought to work the sugar-cane fields from 1860.
