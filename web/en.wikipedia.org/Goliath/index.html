Goliath

Goliath (Hebrew: גָּלְיָת, Golyāṯ) is a Philistine giant in the Book of Samuel, described as a champion of the Philistine army from Gath. He is best known from the biblical narrative in 1 Samuel 17, in which the young Israelite shepherd David defeats him in single combat with a sling stone, an episode that has made "David and Goliath" a byword for an underdog victory against overwhelming odds.

The narrative

According to 1 Samuel, the Philistines and the Israelites under King Saul faced each other in the Valley of Elah. For forty days Goliath came forward morning and evening, challenging Israel to send out a champion to decide the battle in single combat. David, who had come to the camp bringing provisions for his brothers, volunteered. Refusing Saul's armour, he took his staff, five smooth stones from the stream, and his sling. He struck Goliath in the forehead with a stone, then used Goliath's own sword to behead him, whereupon the Philistines fled.

Height and armour

The Masoretic Text gives Goliath's height as "six cubits and a span" — roughly 2.9 metres (9 ft 9 in). Older witnesses, including a Dead Sea Scrolls manuscript of Samuel, the first-century historian Josephus, and the Septuagint, read "four cubits and a span", about 2.06 metres (6 ft 9 in) — still exceptionally tall for the ancient world. His bronze helmet, coat of mail weighing five thousand shekels of bronze (about 57 kg), bronze greaves, and massive spear are described in unusual detail.

Textual questions

A passage in 2 Samuel 21:19 states that Elhanan son of Jair killed "Goliath the Gittite", which appears to contradict the David story; 1 Chronicles 20:5 resolves this by making Elhanan's victim Lahmi, the brother of Goliath. Scholars have long debated whether the David–Goliath story was originally attached to another hero, and note that the narrative in 1 Samuel 17 is stylistically distinct from its surroundings.

Legacy

The phrase "David and Goliath" has entered common usage for contests between a much weaker and a much stronger party. The story has been retold in art — including sculptures by Donatello, Michelangelo, and Bernini — and in popular culture, and the name Goliath has been applied to everything from giant beetles and frogs to cranes, aircraft, and roller coasters.
