LGBT rights in Europe

LGBT rights in Europe are among the most developed in the world in many countries, though conditions vary from full legal equality in much of Western and Northern Europe to restrictive regimes in parts of Eastern Europe. Same-sex sexual activity is legal in every European country; the last criminal prohibitions were removed in the 1990s.

Relationship recognition

The Netherlands became the first country in the world to open marriage to same-sex couples in 2001. By the mid-2020s, marriage equality exists in more than twenty European states, including Belgium, Spain, Norway, Sweden, France, the United Kingdom, Germany, Switzerland, Greece and Estonia. Registered partnerships or civil unions are available in most remaining EU states except a handful, such as Poland, Slovakia, Romania, Bulgaria and Lithuania, where recognition is minimal or contested; the European Court of Human Rights has ruled that states must provide some legal framework for same-sex couples.

Anti-discrimination and other rights

EU law prohibits employment discrimination based on sexual orientation in all member states, and most European countries ban discrimination in goods and services as well. Joint adoption is legal in most marriage-equality countries. Transgender rights — legal gender recognition, and increasingly self-identification models as adopted in Spain, Germany, Ireland and the Nordic countries — remain a live policy debate in the United Kingdom and elsewhere.

Regional contrasts

Russia's 2013 "gay propaganda" law, expanded in 2022, and the designation of the "international LGBT movement" as extremist in 2023 mark the sharpest restrictions on the continent; Hungary has adopted related measures since 2021. ILGA-Europe's annual Rainbow Map ranks Malta, Iceland, Belgium and Spain among the highest-scoring countries and Russia, Azerbaijan and Turkey among the lowest in Europe.
