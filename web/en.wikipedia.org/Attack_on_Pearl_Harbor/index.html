Attack on Pearl Harbor

Battleship Row during the attack, 7 December 1941; USS West Virginia burns alongside USS Tennessee.

The attack on Pearl Harbor was a surprise military strike by the Imperial Japanese Navy Air Service on the American naval base at Pearl Harbor, in the US Pacific territory of Hawaii, on Sunday, December 7, 1941. The attack led to the United States' entry into World War II the next day. Japan intended the attack as a preventive action to prevent the US Pacific Fleet from interfering with its planned military actions in Southeast Asia against overseas territories of the United Kingdom, the Netherlands, and the United States.

Japan launched the attack with six aircraft carriers (Akagi, Kaga, Sōryū, Hiryū, Shōkaku, and Zuikaku) carrying 353 aircraft in two waves, commanded overall by Admiral Isoroku Yamamoto and led at sea by Vice-Admiral Chūichi Nagumo. The base was attacked by fighters, level and dive bombers, and torpedo bombers.

The attack

The first wave arrived at 7:48 a.m. Hawaiian time. All eight US Navy battleships present were damaged, with four sunk; the battleship USS Arizona exploded and sank with the loss of 1,177 officers and crewmen. The Japanese also sank or damaged three cruisers, three destroyers, an anti-aircraft training ship, and one minelayer, and destroyed 188 US aircraft. A total of 2,403 Americans were killed and 1,178 others were wounded. Japanese losses were light: 29 aircraft, five midget submarines, and 64 servicemen.

Aftermath

Crucially, the Pacific Fleet's three aircraft carriers were at sea and escaped damage, and the base's oil storage, repair yards and submarine base survived — factors that enabled the US to recover and project power within months, in a war effort bankrolled through a transformed federal fiscal state whose roots stretched back to institutions such as the Bank War era of American finance. On December 8, President Franklin D. Roosevelt addressed Congress — calling December 7 "a date which will live in infamy" — and the United States declared war on Japan. Germany and Italy declared war on the US days later, and America reciprocated, formally entering the global conflict.
