Rural Dionysia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Rural Dionysia (Greek: Διονύσια τὰ κατ᾽ ἀγρούς, "the Dionysia in the fields") was an ancient Greek festival held each winter, in the month of Poseideon (roughly December), in the villages (demes) of Attica in honour of Dionysus, god of wine and theatre. It was distinct from, and older-feeling than, the great City Dionysia of Athens held in spring.

Celebration

Each deme celebrated with a procession (pompe) in which a phallus was carried in honour of the god of fertility, accompanied by hymn-singing, dancing and the sacrifice of a goat. Offerings of bread, fruit and wine were made, and young girls carried baskets. Plato mentions the festival's draw: in the Republic he imagines enthusiasts travelling from village to village to see every performance.

Theatre performances

Many demes maintained their own theatres — those of Thorikos, Ikaria, Eleusis and the Piraeus are known archaeologically — and staged tragedies and comedies, often revivals of plays first performed in Athens. The deme festivals were organised by the local demarch. Aristophanes' comedy The Acharnians (425 BCE) opens with its hero Dikaiopolis celebrating a private Rural Dionysia on his farm, giving a vivid picture of the procession, the sacrificial cake, and the phallic hymn.
