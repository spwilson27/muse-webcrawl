Means of production

Means of production
ComponentsMeans of labour (tools, factories, infrastructure) and subjects of labour (raw materials, land)
Key theoristsKarl Marx, Friedrich Engels
Related conceptsMode of production, capital, private property

The means of production are the physical, non-human inputs used in producing goods and services: the instruments of labour — tools, machinery, factories, offices, infrastructure and technology — together with the subjects of labour, the raw materials and land on which work is performed. The concept is central to Marxist economics and to debates about ownership in political economy.

In Marxist theory

For Karl Marx, who owns the means of production, together with the social relations this creates, defines a mode of production. Under capitalism, the means of production are privately owned by the bourgeoisie while the proletariat, owning little beyond its labour power, must sell that labour to live. Marx and Friedrich Engels argued in the Communist Manifesto for the abolition of bourgeois private property in the means of production and their transfer to common or social ownership.

Broader usage

Economists outside the Marxist tradition use similar concepts such as capital goods and factors of production, though usually treating land, labour and capital as separate factors. Questions about ownership of the means of production remain central to arguments over socialism, cooperatives, public ownership and, in the digital era, the ownership of platforms and data.
