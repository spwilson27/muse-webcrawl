Air France–KLM

Air France–KLM S.A. is a Franco-Dutch airline holding company incorporated in France, formed by the merger of Air France and KLM Royal Dutch Airlines in 2004. Headquartered in Paris, the group is one of the largest airline groups in Europe by revenue and passengers carried. Its principal subsidiaries are Air France, KLM, and the low-cost carrier Transavia; the group also holds stakes in other airlines and operates the Flying Blue loyalty programme and a major cargo and maintenance business.

History

The merger, announced in 2003 and completed in May 2004, created the world's largest airline group by revenue at the time. The structure preserved both national brands and hubs — Paris Charles de Gaulle for Air France and Amsterdam Schiphol for KLM — under a single holding company listed in Paris. The French and Dutch states retained significant shareholdings. The group weathered the COVID-19 pandemic with multi-billion-euro state aid packages from both governments, accompanied by restructuring and environmental conditions, including cuts to domestic French short-haul flying where rail alternatives exist.

Operations

The group organizes its business into network airlines (passenger and cargo), low-cost operations (Transavia), and maintenance (Air France Industries KLM Engineering & Maintenance, one of the world's largest multi-product MRO providers). Its two main airlines coordinate schedules and revenue management across their twin hubs, and both are members of the SkyTeam alliance. In 2023 the group joined with partners in an agreement to take a minority stake in Scandinavian Airlines (SAS), which subsequently joined SkyTeam.
