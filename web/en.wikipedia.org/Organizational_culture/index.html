Organizational culture

Organizational culture, also known as corporate culture, encompasses values and behaviours that contribute to the unique social and psychological environment of an organisation. It comprises the organisation's expectations, experiences, philosophy, and values that hold it together, and is expressed in its self-image, inner workings, interactions with the outside world, and future expectations. Culture is based on shared attitudes, beliefs, customs, and written and unwritten rules that have been developed over time and are considered valid.

Models

Edgar Schein described culture at three levels: visible artifacts (structures, processes, dress), espoused beliefs and values (stated strategies and goals), and basic underlying assumptions (unconscious, taken-for-granted beliefs that determine behaviour). Geert Hofstede's research on national cultures influenced later work comparing organisational practices across countries. The Competing Values Framework, developed by Kim Cameron and Robert Quinn, classifies cultures into clan, adhocracy, market, and hierarchy types. Charles Handy's model distinguishes power, role, task, and person cultures.

Effects and change

Researchers link strong, coherent cultures to employee engagement and consistent performance, but a strong culture can also resist needed change and stifle diversity of thought. Organisational culture develops through founding leadership, hiring and socialisation practices, stories, rituals, and reward systems. Deliberate culture change is widely regarded as difficult and slow, typically requiring changes in leadership behaviour, structures, and incentives over several years. Subcultures often form within departments or professions and may differ from the dominant culture.
