Nuclear magneton

SymbolμN
Value5.0507837461(15) × 10−27 J·T−1
Definitioneħ / (2mp)

The nuclear magneton (symbol μN) is a physical constant of magnetic moment, defined in terms of the elementary charge, the reduced Planck constant, and the proton mass: μN = eħ/(2mp). It provides the natural unit for expressing the magnetic moments of atomic nuclei and nucleons.

Value and use

Its value is approximately 5.0507837461 × 10−27 joules per tesla. Because the proton is about 1,836 times heavier than the electron, the nuclear magneton is smaller than the corresponding Bohr magneton by the same factor, reflecting the far smaller magnetic moments of nuclei compared with electrons. The magnetic moments of the proton and neutron are quoted in these units: about +2.793 μN for the proton and −1.913 μN for the neutron. Their deviation from the naive Dirac values (1 and 0 respectively) was an early hint that nucleons have internal structure.

Applications

Nuclear magnetons are used throughout nuclear physics and NMR spectroscopy to characterise nuclear spins and magnetic properties, including hyperfine structure and nuclear g-factors.
