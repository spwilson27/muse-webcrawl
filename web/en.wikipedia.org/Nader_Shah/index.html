Nader Shah

Nader Shah Afshar (born Nadr Qoli; August 1688 – 20 June 1747) was the founder of the Afsharid dynasty of Iran and one of the most powerful rulers in Iranian history, ruling as shah of Iran from 1736 to 1747. A brilliant military commander, sometimes called the "Napoleon of Persia" or the "Second Alexander", he restored Iranian sovereignty after the Afghan occupation and created an empire stretching from the Caucasus and Central Asia to the Indus Valley.

Reign: 8 March 1736 – 20 June 1747

Coronation: 1736, Mughan Plain

Born: August 1688, Dastgerd, Safavid Iran

Died: 20 June 1747, Quchan

Rise to power

Of the Turkic Afshar tribe, Nader rose from obscure origins to become the chief commander of Tahmasp II, expelling the Hotak Afghans who had overthrown the Safavids — defeating them decisively at Damghan and Herat in 1729. He then drove the Ottomans and Russians from Iranian territory, deposed Tahmasp II, and in 1736 had himself crowned shah.

Campaigns

Nader's most famous campaign was his invasion of the Mughal Empire: after victory at Karnal in 1739 he occupied Delhi, where a massacre followed a local uprising, and returned to Iran with immense loot including the Peacock Throne, the Koh-i-Noor and Daria-i-Noor diamonds. His armies also campaigned in Central Asia, Dagestan, and Mesopotamia, and he built a navy in the Persian Gulf and the Caspian.

Assassination and legacy

Increasingly tyrannical and mentally unstable in his last years — he blinded his own son Reza Qoli on suspicion of a plot — Nader was assassinated by his own officers in 1747. His empire rapidly fragmented, though his military reforms and restoration of Iran are remembered as a decisive, if brutal, chapter in Iranian history.
