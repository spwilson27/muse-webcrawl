Ignaz Semmelweis

Born 1 July 1818, Buda, Hungary
Died 13 August 1865, Vienna
Known for Hand disinfection in obstetrics

Ignaz Philipp Semmelweis (1 July 1818 – 13 August 1865) was a Hungarian physician and scientist, now known as an early pioneer of antiseptic procedures. Working at the Vienna General Hospital, he discovered that requiring hand disinfection with chlorinated lime dramatically reduced deaths from puerperal ("childbed") fever in maternity clinics.

Discovery

In 1846–47 Semmelweis noticed that clinics staffed by medical students who performed autopsies had far higher maternal mortality than clinics staffed by midwives. He concluded that "cadaverous particles" were carried on hands, and in 1847 introduced mandatory handwashing, cutting mortality from about 18 percent to under 2 percent.

Reception and death

His findings conflicted with established medical opinion and were largely rejected during his lifetime. Semmelweis's mental health deteriorated; he was committed to an asylum in 1865 and died there two weeks later, probably from an infected wound. His ideas were vindicated with the later acceptance of germ theory.
