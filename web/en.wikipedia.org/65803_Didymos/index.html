65803 Didymos

65803 Didymos (provisional designation 1996 GT) is a binary near-Earth asteroid about 780 metres in diameter. Its moon, Dimorphos, was the target of NASA's Double Asteroid Redirection Test (DART) mission, the first demonstration of asteroid deflection by a kinetic impactor.

Discovery and naming

Didymos was discovered on 11 April 1996 by the Spacewatch survey at Kitt Peak National Observatory. Its name, Greek for "twin", was approved in 2003 in reference to its binary nature; the moonlet was named Dimorphos ("having two forms") in 2020.

Physical characteristics

Didymos rotates rapidly, once every 2.26 hours, and has an oblate, "spinning top" shape with an equatorial ridge, typical of rubble-pile asteroids spun up by the YORP effect. It is classified as an S-type (siliceous) asteroid.

The DART impact

On 26 September 2022 the DART spacecraft struck Dimorphos at about 22,500 km/h, shortening its orbital period around Didymos by roughly 33 minutes — substantially more than the predicted minimum — and ejecting a plume of debris observed by telescopes worldwide. ESA's Hera mission, launched in October 2024, is travelling to the system to survey the impact's effects in detail.
