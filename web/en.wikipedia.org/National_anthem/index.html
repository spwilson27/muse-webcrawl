National anthem

A national anthem is a musical composition of patriotic character that evokes and eulogises the history and traditions of a country and its people. Most national anthems are marches or hymns in style, and nearly all sovereign states have one, officially adopted or held by tradition.

History

The oldest surviving anthem is the Dutch Wilhelmus, written between 1568 and 1572. National anthems proliferated in the 19th and early 20th centuries with the rise of the nation-state. Some anthems were composed for the purpose; others began as party songs, hymns, or even drinking songs later elevated to official status.

Usage and customs

Anthems are played at state occasions, international diplomacy, and sporting events such as the Olympic Games and FIFA World Cup. Etiquette varies: standing, removing headgear, and silence are customary in many countries. A few anthems have no official lyrics — notably those of Spain, Bosnia and Herzegovina, and Kosovo — while South Africa's anthem famously combines five languages.

Shared melodies

Some tunes serve multiple nations: the melody of "God Save the King" is used (with different words) by Liechtenstein, and historically by many others. Greece has the longest anthem, with 158 stanzas, of which the first two are officially used.
