Anti-radiation missile

An anti-radiation missile (ARM) is a missile designed to detect and home in on an enemy radio emission source, typically a radar. ARMs are the principal weapon of SEAD (Suppression of Enemy Air Defenses) missions, flown by specialised aircraft — the United States Air Force's "Wild Weasel" units — to destroy surface-to-air missile batteries and air-defence radars.

History

The first operational ARM was the American AGM-45 Shrike, introduced during the Vietnam War; it was followed by the more capable AGM-78 Standard ARM and the high-speed AGM-88 HARM, which remains widely used and can remember a target's location even if the radar switches off. Other examples include the British ALARM, designed to loiter on a parachute until a radar reactivated, the French Martel, and Russian types such as the Kh-31 and Kh-58.

Countermeasures

Radar operators counter ARMs by shutting down or blinking their emissions, using decoy transmitters, and deploying dedicated short-range defences, making the contest between ARMs and air defences a continuing cycle of measure and countermeasure. Ground-based variants of the concept, and ARMs adapted to attack jamming aircraft ("home-on-jam"), extend the idea beyond radar targets. See also AGM-88 HARM.
