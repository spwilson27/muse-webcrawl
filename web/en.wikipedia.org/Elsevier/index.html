Elsevier

Elsevier is a Dutch academic publishing company specialising in scientific, technical and medical literature. A part of the RELX Group, it publishes more than 2,900 journals — including The Lancet and Cell — and tens of thousands of books, and operates platforms such as ScienceDirect, Scopus, SciVal and the reference manager Mendeley. It is one of the largest publishers of scholarly research in the world.

History

The modern company was founded in 1880 in Rotterdam, taking its name from the House of Elzevir, a family of Dutch printers active in the seventeenth century (to which the modern firm has no direct connection). Elsevier expanded internationally after the Second World War and merged in 1993 with Reed International to form Reed Elsevier, renamed RELX in 2015.

Business and products

Elsevier's journals cover the physical, life and health sciences and the social sciences and humanities. Its abstract and citation database Scopus, launched in 2004, competes with Web of Science. The company has increasingly described itself as an information analytics business, selling research-management and clinical decision-support tools to universities, hospitals and governments.

Criticism and open access debates

Elsevier has been a central figure in controversies over subscription prices — the "serials crisis" — and over open access. The 2012 "Cost of Knowledge" boycott, in which thousands of researchers pledged not to publish or review for its journals, drew wide attention. Universities and consortia in several countries have cancelled and renegotiated "big deal" contracts, sometimes after periods without access, while Plan S and other funder mandates have pushed the publisher toward transformative agreements.
