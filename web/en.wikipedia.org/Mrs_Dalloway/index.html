Mrs Dalloway is a novel by Virginia Woolf, published on 14 May 1925, that details a day in the life of Clarissa Dalloway, a fictional upper-class woman in post–First World War England. It is one of Woolf's best-known novels.

Plot and structure

The novel follows Clarissa as she prepares for a party she will host that evening, interwoven with the story of Septimus Warren Smith, a shell-shocked veteran who eventually kills himself, news of whose death reaches Clarissa at her party. Woolf employs stream of consciousness, moving fluidly between characters' interior monologues, so that past and present interpenetrate over the single June day.

Development

Mrs Dalloway evolved from two short stories, "Mrs Dalloway in Bond Street" and the unfinished "The Prime Minister", and grew out of Woolf's experiments in Jacob's Room. It is regarded as a landmark of modernist fiction, frequently compared with Joyce's Ulysses, which also unfolds over one day in June.

Adaptations and legacy

The novel was adapted into a 1997 film directed by Marleen Gorris. Michael Cunningham's Pulitzer Prize-winning 1998 novel The Hours and its 2002 film adaptation draw on Woolf's life and the writing of Mrs Dalloway.
