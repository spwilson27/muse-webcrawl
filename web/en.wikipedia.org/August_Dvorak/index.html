August Dvorak

August Dvorak (May 5, 1894 – October 10, 1975) was an American educational psychologist and professor of education at the University of Washington in Seattle, Washington. He is best known as the inventor, together with his brother-in-law William Dealey, of the Dvorak keyboard layout, patented in 1936 as an alternative to the QWERTY layout.

The Dvorak keyboard layout, designed to increase typing speed and reduce fatigue.

Life and career

Dvorak was of Czech descent and served in the United States Navy during both world wars, teaching in the submarine service — an experience he credited with interesting him in efficiency and error reduction. At the University of Washington he studied the physiology and psychology of typing, analysing films of typists and the frequency of English letter combinations.

The Dvorak keyboard

The Dvorak Simplified Keyboard places the most common letters and digraphs on the home row and under the strongest fingers, so that most typing is done without leaving the home row and the hands alternate frequently. Dvorak and Dealey received US patent 2,040,248 in 1936. Dvorak also designed single-hand layouts for the left and right hands. Despite its designers' claims of superior speed and comfort, the layout never displaced QWERTY, though it is supported natively by all major operating systems. Dvorak co-authored the 1936 book Typewriting Behavior summarising his research.
