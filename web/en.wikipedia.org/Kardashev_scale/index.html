Kardashev scale

The Kardashev scale is a method of measuring a civilization's level of technological advancement based on the amount of energy it is capable of harnessing and using. The scale was proposed by the Soviet astronomer Nikolai Kardashev in 1964.

Categories

  • Type I: A civilization able to access and store all of the energy available on its home planet — on the order of 1016 watts for Earth.
  • Type II: A civilization able to harness the total energy output of its home star, for example by means of a Dyson sphere — on the order of 1026 watts.
  • Type III: A civilization able to control energy on the scale of its entire host galaxy — on the order of 1036 watts.

Humanity's rating

Carl Sagan proposed interpolating the scale with fractional values; on that basis, human civilization in the early 21st century rates at roughly 0.7, using about 2×1013 watts. Later authors have extended the scale to Types IV and V (universe and multiverse scales) and below to Type 0.

Applications and criticism

The scale is widely used in SETI research and futurology as a framework for discussing advanced extraterrestrial civilizations and long-term human prospects. Critics note that energy use alone may not capture technological advancement, and that civilizations might pursue efficiency rather than ever-greater consumption.
