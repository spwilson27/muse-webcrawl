Lower Manhattan, also known as Downtown Manhattan or Downtown New York City, is the southernmost part of Manhattan, the central borough of New York City. It is the city's governmental seat and the oldest part of the metropolis, where New Amsterdam was founded in 1624. Lower Manhattan contains the Financial District and Wall Street, and forms the fourth-largest central business district in the United States.

Definition and neighborhoods

The term "Lower Manhattan" most broadly denotes everything south of 14th Street — including the Lower East Side, Chinatown, SoHo, Tribeca, the East and West Villages, and the Financial District — while in narrower usage it refers to the downtown business core south of Chambers Street. The street plan below Houston Street follows old Dutch and English colonial paths rather than the 1811 grid, producing the tight, irregular canyons famous in the Financial District.

History

The Dutch West India Company established New Amsterdam at Manhattan's southern tip in 1624, building Fort Amsterdam and Wall Street's defensive palisade (1653). The British took over in 1664, renaming the settlement New York. Here George Washington was inaugurated as the first U.S. president at Federal Hall in 1789, when the city briefly served as the national capital. During the 19th century the port and financial markets grew together; the Erie Canal (1825) made New York the nation's chief port, and landfilling progressively expanded the island's edges.

World Trade Center and recovery

The original World Trade Center, completed in the 1970s, symbolised the district. Its destruction in the September 11 attacks of 2001 killed nearly 3,000 people and devastated the area. The rebuilt site includes the National September 11 Memorial & Museum and One World Trade Center (1,776 ft / 541 m), completed in 2014 — the tallest building in the Western Hemisphere. Since 2001 the residential population of Lower Manhattan has more than doubled, converting it from a nine-to-five office district into a mixed-use neighbourhood.

Transportation

More subway lines converge here than almost anywhere in the city, at hubs such as Fulton Center. Ferries run from the Battery to Staten Island, Governors Island and New Jersey. The district suffered severe flooding during Hurricane Sandy in 2012, prompting ongoing resiliency projects along its waterfronts.
