National Historic Landmark

A National Historic Landmark (NHL) is a building, district, object, site or structure in the United States that is officially recognised by the federal government for its outstanding historical significance. More than 2,600 places have received the designation, which is administered by the National Park Service.

Criteria

Landmarks are chosen because they possess exceptional value in illustrating the heritage of the United States — for example, sites associated with events that shaped the nation's history, properties linked to significant historical figures, or outstanding examples of architecture and engineering. All NHLs are automatically listed on the National Register of Historic Places, though they form only a small fraction of that register.

Designation and protection

The Secretary of the Interior designates landmarks following studies by the National Park Service and review by the National Park System Advisory Board. Designation is largely honorary and does not by itself prevent a private owner from altering or demolishing a property, though federal involvement in a project triggers review under Section 106 of the National Historic Preservation Act of 1966. A small number of NHLs are ships, such as the USS Constitution.
