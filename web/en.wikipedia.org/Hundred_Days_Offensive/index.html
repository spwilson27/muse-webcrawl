Hundred Days Offensive

Hundred Days Offensive
Date: 8 August – 11 November 1918
Location: Picardy, France to Mons, Belgium
Result: Decisive Allied victory; end of First World War
Belligerents: Allies (France, British Empire, United States, Belgium) vs German Empire

The Hundred Days Offensive (8 August to 11 November 1918) was a series of massive Allied offensives that ended the First World War. Beginning with the Battle of Amiens, Allied forces advanced over 160 kilometres, pushing the Imperial German Army back behind the Hindenburg Line and forcing Germany to seek an armistice.

Background

Germany's Spring Offensive of 1918 had exhausted its best troops without achieving victory, while hundreds of thousands of American soldiers were arriving in France each month. Allied commander Ferdinand Foch planned coordinated, successive attacks to prevent Germany from resting or redeploying reserves.

Course

At Amiens on 8 August, British, Australian and Canadian forces with hundreds of tanks achieved complete surprise; Erich Ludendorff called it “the black day of the German Army”. French and American armies attacked at St Mihiel and in the Meuse–Argonne, the largest battle in American military history to that date. By late September the Allies breached the Hindenburg Line at the St Quentin Canal.

Outcome

Germany's allies collapsed — Bulgaria, the Ottoman Empire and Austria-Hungary all signed armistices — and revolution broke out in Germany. The Armistice of 11 November 1918 took effect at 11 a.m. The offensive cost the Allies roughly 1.07 million casualties and Germany about 1.17 million, including some 386,000 prisoners taken by the Allies.

Significance

The Hundred Days demonstrated mature combined-arms warfare — infantry, tanks, artillery, aircraft and logistics coordinated — and remain the British Army's greatest series of victories.
