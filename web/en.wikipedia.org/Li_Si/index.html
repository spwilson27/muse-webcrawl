Li Si

Born: c. 280 BC
Died: 208 BC
Office: Chancellor of the Qin dynasty

Li Si (Chinese: 李斯; c. 280 – 208 BC) was a Chinese calligrapher, philosopher, politician, and writer of the Qin dynasty. He served as chancellor under Qin Shi Huang, the First Emperor, and played a central role in unifying China.

Career

A student of Xunzi alongside Han Fei, Li Si came to Qin and rose to become its chief minister. He promoted Legalist policies: standardising script (the small seal script), weights, measures and coinage; abolishing feudal domains in favour of commanderies; and suppressing dissent, most notoriously in the "burning of books" of 213 BC.

Downfall

After the First Emperor's death in 210 BC, Li Si conspired with the eunuch Zhao Gao to place the younger son Huhai on the throne. Zhao Gao later turned on him, and Li Si was executed in 208 BC by the "five punishments" and waist chop.
