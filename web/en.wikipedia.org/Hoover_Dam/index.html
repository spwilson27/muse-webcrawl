Hoover Dam is a concrete arch-gravity dam in the Black Canyon of the Colorado River, on the border between the U.S. states of Nevada and Arizona. It was constructed between 1931 and 1936 during the Great Depression and was dedicated on September 30, 1935, by President Franklin D. Roosevelt. Its construction was the result of a massive effort involving thousands of workers, and cost over one hundred lives.

[Image: Hoover Dam viewed from the Mike O'Callaghan–Pat Tillman Memorial Bridge]

History

The dam was named after President Herbert Hoover, who played a key role in its authorization. Originally known as Boulder Dam, the name was officially changed to Hoover Dam by an act of Congress in 1947. The dam impounds Lake Mead, the largest reservoir in the United States by volume when full.

Construction

A consortium called Six Companies, Inc. won the bid to build the dam and began construction in 1931. Because the scale of the project was unprecedented, novel techniques were used, including the use of cooling pipes embedded in the concrete to dissipate the heat of curing. The dam was completed two years ahead of schedule. The nearby planned community of Boulder City was built to house the workers.

Power generation

The dam's generators provide power for public and private utilities in Nevada, Arizona, and California. Hoover Dam generates, on average, about 4 billion kilowatt-hours of hydroelectric power each year, serving around 1.3 million people.

Tourism

Lake Mead and Hoover Dam are major tourist attractions, drawing roughly seven million visitors a year to the dam and around ten million to the lake's recreation area.
