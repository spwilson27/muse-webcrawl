Surinamese cuisine

Surinamese cuisine is one of the most diverse in the Americas, reflecting the country's population, which descends from Indigenous peoples and from settlers and laborers from the Netherlands, Africa, India, Indonesia (especially Java), and China. These traditions blend freely: a single restaurant in Paramaribo may serve Javanese noodles, Indian roti, and Creole pom.

Dishes

Signature dishes include pom, a Creole oven dish of grated tayer root with chicken and citrus, often served at celebrations; roti with chicken curry and potatoes, eaten with the hands in the Indian style; Javanese bami goreng and nasi goreng (fried noodles and rice); saoto, a Javanese-style chicken soup with bean sprouts and egg; moksi meti ("mixed meats") of Chinese-Creole origin; and peanut soup, reflecting West African influence. Madame Jeanette peppers provide fierce heat, and cassava, plantain, rice, and saltfish are staple ingredients. Chinese tjauw min, bakabana (fried plantain with peanut sauce), and dawet, a Javanese coconut-milk drink, are also widespread.

Influence abroad

Because a large Surinamese diaspora lives in the Netherlands, Surinamese-Chinese-Javanese restaurants are common in Dutch cities, and dishes such as roti kip ("chicken roti") and Surinaamse broodjes have entered Dutch street food.
