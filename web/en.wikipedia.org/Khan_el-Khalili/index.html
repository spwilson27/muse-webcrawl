Khan el-Khalili

Khan el-Khalili (Arabic: خان الخليلي) is a famous bazaar and souq in the historic centre of Cairo, Egypt. Established as a centre of trade in the Mamluk era, the market is one of Cairo's main attractions for tourists and Egyptians alike, selling jewellery, spices, perfumes, lanterns, and traditional crafts.

History

The site was originally the location of the Za'afran Tomb, the burial place of the Fatimid caliphs. In 1382 the Mamluk emir Jaharks el-Khalili demolished the cemetery and built a large caravanserai (a khan) on the site to serve merchants and their caravans. The khan gave the market its name; parts of the original structure, including ornate gates and a domed market hall (the qaysariya), survive today.

Under the Ottomans the bazaar expanded, and by the early modern period it was the centre of foreign trade in Cairo, with quarters for Turkish, Greek and other merchants.

The bazaar today

The market district, adjacent to al-Azhar Mosque and the al-Hussein Mosque, is a maze of narrow lanes lined with workshops and shops. Its most famous coffeehouse, El Fishawy, has operated continuously for over two centuries and was a haunt of Nobel laureate Naguib Mahfouz, whose novel Midaq Alley is set nearby. Haggling is expected, and the bazaar remains a working market as well as a tourist destination.
