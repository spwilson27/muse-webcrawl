Gaza City (Arabic: غَزَّة, romanized: Ghazzah) is a Palestinian city in the Gaza Strip, with a population of 590,481 (in 2017), making it the largest city in the State of Palestine. Inhabited since at least the 15th century BCE, Gaza has been dominated by several different peoples and empires throughout its history. It was incorporated into the Ottoman Empire in the early 16th century, fell to the British during World War I, became part of Mandatory Palestine, was administered by Egypt after the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, and was captured by Israel in the Six-Day War in 1967. Gaza later was placed under the control of the Palestinian Authority and, in 2007, of Hamas.

History

Gaza was a major Philistine city, one of the five city-states of the Philistine pentapolis, and is mentioned in the Hebrew Bible as the place where Samson died destroying the temple of Dagon. The city was conquered by Alexander the Great in 332 BCE and subsequently came under Ptolemaic, Seleucid, Roman and Byzantine rule. It was an important trading post on the Incense Road between the Red Sea and the Mediterranean. Under Islamic rule from the 7th century CE, Gaza became a centre of Islamic scholarship, and in the early Ottoman period it was the capital of one of the largest districts of Ottoman Syria.

Geography

Gaza City is situated on the eastern Mediterranean coast, on a coastal plain roughly 50 metres above sea level. The old city core rises on a low hill. The city has a hot semi-arid climate with mild winters and hot, humid summers, and receives around 300–400 mm of rain annually, concentrated in the winter months.

Economy

Historically, Gaza was a centre for the weaving of cotton cloth (giving its name to the fabric "gauze"), trade in spices, perfumes and agricultural goods. In modern times its economy has centred on small industries, commerce, fishing and agriculture, and has been heavily affected by blockades and repeated conflicts.

Landmarks

Landmarks include the Great Omari Mosque, the Church of Saint Porphyrius, the Rashad Shawa Cultural Center, the Qasr al-Basha palace museum, and the ancient port area of Anthedon. The city is home to the Islamic University of Gaza, Al-Azhar University – Gaza, and Al-Aqsa University.
