Tyche

Tyche wearing a mural crown and holding a cornucopia.

Tyche (Ancient Greek: Τύχη, Túkhē, "Luck") is the Greek goddess of chance, fortune, and prosperity, and the presiding tutelary deity who governed the fortune and prosperity of a city and its destiny. In Classical Greek mythology, she is usually depicted as the daughter of Aphrodite and Hermes, or of Oceanus and Tethys, among the Oceanids. Her Roman equivalent is Fortuna.

Worship and iconography

Tyche became especially prominent in the Hellenistic period, when the fortunes of cities and kingdoms seemed to rise and fall capriciously. Cities adopted their own local Tyche, typically shown wearing a mural crown (a crown shaped like city walls) and carrying a cornucopia and a rudder, symbolizing abundance and her steering of destinies. The Tyche of Antioch, a famous statue by Eutychides, became one of the most copied images of antiquity.

Legacy

The name also survives in astronomy and science: 258 Tyche is a main-belt asteroid, and "Tycho" related names derive from the same root of luck.
