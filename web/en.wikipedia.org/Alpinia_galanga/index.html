Alpinia galanga

Alpinia galanga, commonly known as greater galangal, is a plant in the ginger family (Zingiberaceae) native to Southeast Asia. Its rhizome is a widely used spice in Indonesian, Thai, Malaysian and other Southeast Asian cuisines, where it is known by names such as lengkuas (Malay/Indonesian) and kha (Thai).

Description and uses

The plant grows to about two metres tall, with long lance-shaped leaves and racemes of greenish-white flowers. The cylindrical rhizomes are hard, woody and pale with darker ringed skin, and have a sharp citrusy, peppery flavour distinct from ginger. Fresh or dried galangal is an essential ingredient in dishes such as rendang, tom kha gai and many curry pastes and sambals; it is also used in traditional medicine in Indonesia (jamu) and elsewhere, and historically was known in medieval European cooking as "galingale".

Related species

Greater galangal is one of four plants called galangal; the others include lesser galangal (Alpinia officinarum), Kaempferia galanga and Boesenbergia rotunda. The genus Alpinia is named after the Italian botanist Prospero Alpini.
