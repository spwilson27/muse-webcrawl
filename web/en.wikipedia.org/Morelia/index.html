Morelia is the capital and largest city of the Mexican state of Michoacán, in the Guayangareo Valley of west-central Mexico. Built largely of pink cantera stone, its exceptionally preserved colonial centre was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1991.

Morelia Cathedral, built of pink cantera stone.

History

The city was founded in 1541 by order of Viceroy Antonio de Mendoza as Valladolid, a Spanish city intended to supplant the Purépecha centre at Pátzcuaro. It was the birthplace of José María Morelos, the priest and insurgent leader of the Mexican War of Independence, and of Agustín de Iturbide. In 1828 the city was renamed Morelia in Morelos's honour. Its university, the Universidad Michoacana de San Nicolás de Hidalgo, descends from a college founded in 1540 and claims to be among the oldest in the Americas.

Sights

The historic centre contains more than two hundred historic buildings, many in a distinctive Mexican Baroque style: the cathedral (built 1660–1744), the government palace, the Clavijero Palace and the long arcade of the former Jesuit college. An 18th-century aqueduct with 253 arches still runs into the city. Morelia hosts an internationally known film festival, founded in 2003, and its cuisine — including carnitas from nearby towns and sweets such as ate — is celebrated.
