Hokkaido University (北海道大学, Hokkaidō daigaku), abbreviated to Hokudai (北大), is a public research university in Sapporo, Hokkaido, Japan. It is one of Japan's former Imperial Universities and among the country's most prestigious institutions, with a main campus noted for its spacious grounds, poplar avenue and historic buildings.

History

The university was founded in 1876 as Sapporo Agricultural College, the first modern institution of higher education in Japan, with the American educator William S. Clark as its first vice-president. Clark's farewell words to his students — "Boys, be ambitious!" — became the university's unofficial motto, and a statue of Clark stands on the campus. In 1918 it became Hokkaido Imperial University, the fifth of the imperial universities, and in 1947 it took its present name.

Academics

Hokkaido University comprises twelve undergraduate faculties — including letters, education, law, economics, science, medicine, dentistry, veterinary medicine, engineering, agriculture, fisheries sciences and pharmaceutical sciences — and a large graduate school system. It is known for research in agriculture, low-temperature science (its Institute of Low Temperature Science was founded in 1941), catalysis, Slavic-Eurasian studies and Arctic research. Professor Akira Suzuki shared the 2010 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for the Suzuki coupling reaction, developed at the university.

Campus

The Sapporo campus, just north of Sapporo Station, covers about 180 hectares and is a popular public space, with the Furukawa Lecture Hall, the university museum, elm groves and the poplar avenue first planted in 1903. A second campus in Hakodate houses fisheries sciences.
