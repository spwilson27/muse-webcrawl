Joseph Jacques Omer Plante (17 January 1929 – 27 February 1986) was a Canadian professional ice hockey goaltender, regarded as one of the most important innovators in the sport. He is best known for popularizing the goalie mask, which changed hockey permanently.

Career

Plante starred for the Montreal Canadiens from 1953 to 1963, winning six Stanley Cups, including five in a row from 1956 to 1960. He won the Vezina Trophy seven times and the Hart Memorial Trophy as league MVP in 1962.

The mask

On 1 November 1959, after being struck in the face by a shot from Andy Bathgate of the New York Rangers, Plante returned to the ice wearing a practice mask that coach Toe Blake had previously forbidden in games. Blake relented, and Plante refused to play without it afterwards; the mask soon became standard equipment for goaltenders.

Style and influence

Plante pioneered roaming from the crease to play the puck, popularized the stand-up style's positional play, and wrote influential instructional books on goaltending. He was inducted into the Hockey Hall of Fame in 1978.
