Renegade (Styx song)

Artist: Styx
Album: Pieces of Eight
Released: 1978 (album); 1979 (single)
Genre: Hard rock, progressive rock
Songwriter: Tommy Shaw
Length: 4:13

"Renegade" is a 1978 song written by Tommy Shaw and recorded by the American rock band Styx for their eighth studio album Pieces of Eight. Released as a single in 1979, it reached No. 16 on the Billboard Hot 100. The song tells the story of an outlaw on the run who has been captured and faces hanging, pleading in the opening lines — sung a cappella with layered harmonies — "Oh mama, I'm in fear for my life from the long arm of the law."

"Renegade" became one of Styx's signature songs and concert staples.

Composition and reception

The song opens with a distinctive acapella vocal passage before the full band — Shaw's and James Young's guitars, Chuck and John Panozzo's rhythm section, and Dennis DeYoung's keyboards — drives into a hard-rock groove, with an extended guitar solo in the final section. Shaw originally conceived it as a slower, country-influenced piece before the band reworked it into a rocker. "Renegade" became a durable fixture of classic rock radio and of Styx's live shows, and it is regularly played at Pittsburgh Steelers home games, where the crowd waves Terrible Towels during the song.

In popular culture

The song has appeared in films, television series, and video games, and has been covered or sampled by later artists. Its narrative of a condemned fugitive has made it one of the best-known story songs in American arena rock.
