Biography

Charles Edward Haden (August 6, 1937 – July 11, 2014) was an American jazz double bass player, bandleader, composer and educator whose career spanned more than fifty years. He was best known for his long association with saxophonist Ornette Coleman, helping to pioneer the free jazz movement in the late 1950s.

Early life

Haden was born in Shenandoah, Iowa, into a musical family that performed country and folk music as the Haden Family Band. As a small child he sang on the family's radio show. A bout with polio in his teens damaged his singing voice and left his face partially paralysed, turning his attention to the bass. He moved to Los Angeles in 1957.

Career

In Los Angeles Haden played with Art Pepper, Hampton Hawes and Dexter Gordon before joining Ornette Coleman's quartet. The group's 1959 engagement at New York's Five Spot Café and albums such as The Shape of Jazz to Come (1959) changed the direction of jazz. Haden's warm, resonant tone and melodic approach to harmony made him one of the most influential bassists in the idiom.

In 1969 he founded the Liberation Music Orchestra, a big band with arrangements by Carla Bley, whose albums combined avant-garde jazz with political themes drawn from the Spanish Civil War and later conflicts. He co-founded the cooperative group Old and New Dreams, and from the 1980s led Quartet West, which evoked the atmosphere of 1940s Los Angeles noir. With guitarist Pat Metheny he recorded the Grammy-winning duet album Beyond the Missouri Sky (Short Stories) (1997).

Awards and death

Haden received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2013 and was named an NEA Jazz Master. He died in Los Angeles on 11 July 2014, aged 76, from complications of post-polio syndrome and liver disease.
