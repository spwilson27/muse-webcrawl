Anti-consumerism

Buy Nothing Day is a prominent anti-consumerist action

Anti-consumerism is a sociopolitical ideology that opposes consumerism — the continual buying and consuming of material goods. It criticises the economic system of mass consumption, arguing that advertising manufactures artificial needs, that consumption is linked to environmental destruction and personal dissatisfaction, and that corporate power distorts culture and politics.

Ideas and history

Critiques of luxury and accumulation date to antiquity and to religious traditions praising voluntary poverty. Modern anti-consumerism draws on Thorstein Veblen's analysis of "conspicuous consumption" (1899), mid-century critiques of the "affluent society", and works such as Naomi Klein's No Logo (2000) and the magazine Adbusters. Movements associated with anti-consumerism include voluntary simplicity, degrowth, freeganism, the slow movement, and campaigns like Buy Nothing Day, held on the day after U.S. Thanksgiving.

Arguments and criticism

Supporters link high consumption to climate change, resource depletion and waste, and cite research that well-being does not rise proportionally with income beyond a point. Critics respond that consumption drives economic growth and poverty reduction, that consumers exercise genuine choice, and that anti-consumerist messages can themselves become marketable brands.
