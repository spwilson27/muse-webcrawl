Bond event

A Bond event is a North Atlantic ice rafting event, identified in marine sediment cores, in which grains of hematite-stained glass, volcanic glass, and other debris—carried south by icebergs—mark episodes of cooler climate during the Holocene. Named after Gerard C. Bond of the Lamont–Doherty Earth Observatory, who described them in 1997, Bond events recur at intervals of roughly 1,500 years and have been linked, at least in their earlier occurrences, to the Dansgaard–Oeschger cycles of the last glacial period.

Evidence and chronology

Bond and colleagues found that sediment cores from across the North Atlantic contain layers enriched in ice-rafted debris—including distinctive hematite-stained grains traced to Hudson Bay and volcanic glass from Iceland—deposited when armadas of icebergs drifted far south and melted. Eight to nine such events were identified in the Holocene (Bond events 0 through 8), at approximate ages of 0.4, 1.4, 2.8, 4.2, 5.9, 8.1, 9.4, 10.3, and 11.1 thousand years ago. The most recent corresponds broadly to the Little Ice Age, and the 4.2 ka event has been associated with the collapse of the Akkadian Empire and is now the formal marker for the start of the Meghalayan Age.

Causes

The mechanism remains debated. Bond proposed a link to variations in solar activity, noting correlations with cosmogenic isotope records (carbon-14 and beryllium-10), suggesting that reduced solar output weakened Atlantic circulation and expanded sea ice. Others emphasise internal oscillations of the ocean–ice system, freshwater forcing, and the dynamics of the Atlantic meridional overturning circulation. The pacing (~1,470 years) also matches combination tones of known solar cycles, though critics caution that the Holocene signal is weaker and less regular than the glacial Dansgaard–Oeschger pattern.

Relation to other climate events

Bond events are sometimes presented as the Holocene continuation of glacial ice-rafting episodes known as Heinrich events and Dansgaard–Oeschger cycles, though the scale of iceberg discharge in the warm Holocene was far smaller. Several Bond events coincide with recorded regional climate shifts, droughts, and societal disruptions in the Mediterranean, Mesopotamia, and China.
