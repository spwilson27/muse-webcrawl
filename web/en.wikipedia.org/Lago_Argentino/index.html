Lake Argentino (Spanish: Lago Argentino) is a freshwater lake in the Austral Andes of Santa Cruz Province, Patagonia, Argentina. It is the largest lake in Argentina and the third-largest in South America, with a surface area of about 1,466 km² and a maximum depth near 500 metres.

Geography

The lake is fed by meltwater from several glaciers of the Southern Patagonian Ice Field, including the Perito Moreno, Upsala, and Spegazzini glaciers, which calve icebergs into its arms. The milky turquoise colour of the water comes from glacial rock flour. Its outlet is the Santa Cruz River, which flows east to the Atlantic Ocean.

Tourism

The town of El Calafate on the southern shore is the gateway to Los Glaciares National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The northern shore is served by El Chaltén. The lake is noted for fishing, particularly perch and trout.
