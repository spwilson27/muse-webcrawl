Deftones

Deftones is an American alternative metal band formed in Sacramento, California, in 1988. The band's line-up has most consistently featured vocalist Chino Moreno, guitarist Stephen Carpenter, drummer Abe Cunningham, and keyboardist and turntablist Frank Delgado. Bassist Chi Cheng was seriously injured in a car accident in 2008 and died in 2013; he was succeeded by Sergio Vega, who played with the band until 2021, after which Fred Sablan took over touring bass duties.

History

Deftones released their debut album, Adrenaline, in 1995, followed by Around the Fur (1997), which brought them wider attention within the rising nu metal scene. Their third album, White Pony (2000), is widely regarded as a landmark release: it broadened the band's sound with shoegaze, trip hop, and post-rock influences, and included the single "Change (In the House of Flies)" and the Grammy Award-winning track "Elite". Subsequent albums included Deftones (2003) and Saturday Night Wrist (2006). Sessions for an album titled Eros were shelved after Cheng's accident.

The band returned with Diamond Eyes (2010), followed by Koi No Yokan (2012), Gore (2016), Ohms (2020), and Private Music (2025). Although lumped in with nu metal early in their career, Deftones have been praised for an experimental approach that sets them apart from their peers, mixing heavy, downtuned guitar passages with atmospheric textures and Moreno's shift between screaming and soft, melodic singing.

Musical style and legacy

Critics frequently describe Deftones' sound as a blend of alternative metal, shoegaze, and post-metal. The band is frequently cited as an influence by later metal and alternative acts, and White Pony in particular is regularly ranked among the greatest metal albums of its era. The band has sold more than ten million albums worldwide.
