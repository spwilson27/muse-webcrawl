Warsaw Philharmonic

Founded: 1901
Location: Warsaw, Poland
Type: Symphony orchestra and concert hall

The Warsaw Philharmonic (Polish: Filharmonia Warszawska) is a Polish orchestra and national institution based in Warsaw. Founded in 1901, it is one of Poland's oldest musical institutions and is regarded as the country's leading symphony orchestra. Its home, the Warsaw Philharmonic Concert Hall, is located on Jasna Street in central Warsaw.

History

The orchestra gave its inaugural concert on 5 November 1901, conducted by co-founder Emil Młynarski, with Ignacy Jan Paderewski as piano soloist. The original concert hall, modelled on European philharmonic halls, was destroyed during the Siege of Warsaw in 1939 and rebuilt in a new style after the Second World War; the rebuilt hall was inaugurated in 1955.

Activities

The Warsaw Philharmonic performs a regular subscription season, tours internationally, and has made numerous recordings. It is closely associated with the International Chopin Piano Competition, founded in 1927, whose finalists traditionally perform with the orchestra. Distinguished music directors have included Grzegorz Fitelberg, Witold Rowicki, and Antoni Wit.
