Tax

Taxes are compulsory financial charges imposed by governments on individuals and organizations.

A tax is a compulsory financial charge or some other type of levy imposed on a taxpayer (an individual or legal entity) by a governmental organization to collectively fund government spending, public expenditures, or to regulate and reduce negative externalities. Tax compliance refers to policy actions and individual behaviour aimed at ensuring that taxpayers are paying the right amount of tax at the right time and securing the correct tax allowances and tax reliefs.

Overview

Taxes consist of direct or indirect taxes and may be paid in money or as unpaid labour. Direct taxes are levied on income or wealth, such as income tax, corporate tax, and property tax. Indirect taxes are levied on transactions, such as sales tax, value-added tax (VAT), and excise duties. The first known taxation took place in Ancient Egypt around 3000–2800 BC.

Purposes and effects

Taxes fund public goods and services such as infrastructure, defence, education, and healthcare. They are also used to influence behaviour — for example, taxes on tobacco, alcohol, and carbon emissions — and to redistribute wealth. Economists analyse the economic incidence of taxation, noting that the party who legally pays a tax is not always the party who bears its economic burden. Excessive or poorly designed taxation can create deadweight loss and incentives for avoidance or evasion.

Types of taxation

Common taxes include income tax, payroll tax, corporate tax, capital gains tax, inheritance tax, property tax, and consumption taxes such as VAT and sales tax. Tariffs are taxes on imports. Tax systems may be progressive, proportional, or regressive depending on how the average rate varies with the tax base.
