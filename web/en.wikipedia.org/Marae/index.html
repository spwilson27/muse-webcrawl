Marae

A marae in New Zealand, centred on a carved meeting house (wharenui).

A marae (in New Zealand Māori, Cook Islands Māori, Tahitian), malaʻe (in Tongan), malae (in Samoan), or mālaʻe (in Hawaiian) is a communal or sacred place that serves religious and social purposes in Polynesian societies. In all these languages, the word also means "cleared, free of weeds and trees". Marae generally consist of an open cleared area in front of a significant building, such as a meeting house. In New Zealand, the marae remains a vital focus of Māori community life, where formal welcomes, weddings, funerals (tangihanga) and tribal gatherings take place.

In New Zealand

A New Zealand marae complex typically comprises the open courtyard (marae ātea), a carved meeting house (wharenui, often personifying an ancestor), a dining hall (wharekai), kitchens and ablution blocks. The marae belongs to an iwi (tribe), hapū (sub-tribe) or whānau (extended family), and visitors are manuhiri who are formally welcomed in the pōwhiri ceremony, involving waiata (song), speeches (whaikōrero) and the hongi greeting. Marae operate under tikanga (customary protocol), with speaking rights and roles on the paepae (orators' bench) governed by local custom.

During the twentieth century, urban marae were established in cities for Māori who had migrated away from tribal lands, and marae are now also attached to schools, universities, hospitals and government institutions. Prominent examples include Waitangi in the Bay of Islands and Te Papa's Rongomaraeroa marae in Wellington.

In wider Polynesia

Elsewhere in Polynesia, marae were principally religious sites. In Tahiti and the Society Islands, stone marae with stepped altars (ahu) served as temples for the worship of gods such as ʻOro; the great marae of Taputapuātea on Raʻiātea, a centre of voyaging and religion, became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2017. In pre-Christian Hawaiʻi, the equivalent heiau temples fulfilled similar functions, while the modern Hawaiian use of mālaʻe is closer to the Samoan village malae, a ceremonial village green.
