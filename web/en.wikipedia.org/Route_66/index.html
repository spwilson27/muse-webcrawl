U.S. Route 66

U.S. Route 66 (also known as the Will Rogers Highway, and colloquially the Main Street of America or the Mother Road) was one of the original highways of the United States Numbered Highway System. Established on November 11, 1926, it ran 2,448 miles (3,940 km) from Chicago, Illinois, through Missouri, Kansas, Oklahoma, Texas, New Mexico and Arizona, to Santa Monica, California.

History

Route 66 became a symbol of American mobility. During the Dust Bowl of the 1930s it carried farming families west to California — a migration immortalised in John Steinbeck's The Grapes of Wrath (1939), which dubbed it the "Mother Road" — and after the Second World War it fed the boom in automobile tourism, lined with motels, diners, filling stations and roadside attractions. The 1946 song "(Get Your Kicks on) Route 66", written by Bobby Troup, cemented its place in popular culture.

Decline and preservation

The Interstate Highway System gradually bypassed Route 66; the last stretch was replaced by Interstate 40 at Williams, Arizona, in 1984, and the route was removed from the U.S. Highway System on June 27, 1985. Portions survive as "Historic Route 66", a National Scenic Byway in places, maintained by state and private preservation associations. Surviving landmarks such as the Wigwam Motels, the Blue Hole, Cadillac Ranch and the U-Drop Inn draw heritage tourists from around the world.
