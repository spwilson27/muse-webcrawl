Grunge (sometimes referred to as the Seattle sound) is an alternative rock genre and subculture which emerged during the mid-1980s in the U.S. state of Washington, particularly in Seattle and nearby towns. The style features heavy electric guitar distortion, often alternating quiet verses with loud choruses, and draws influences from hardcore punk and heavy metal.

History

The early grunge movement coalesced around the independent label Sub Pop and bands such as Green River, Mudhoney, Soundgarden, and the Melvins. Grunge achieved worldwide commercial success in the early 1990s with Nirvana's Nevermind (1991) — whose single "Smells Like Teen Spirit" became an anthem of Generation X — and Pearl Jam's Ten (1991), alongside major albums by Alice in Chains and Soundgarden. The genre's mainstream moment faded after the death of Nirvana's Kurt Cobain in 1994 and the decline of several leading bands, although its influence persisted in post-grunge and later alternative rock.

Characteristics

Grunge music is characterized by heavily distorted guitars, contrasting dynamics, feedback, and lyrics expressing alienation, apathy, social disillusionment and a desire for freedom. Grunge fashion — flannel shirts, ripped jeans, and thrift-store clothing — reflected the Pacific Northwest working-class aesthetic and an anti-consumerist stance, though it was rapidly commercialized by designers and retailers in 1992–93.

Legacy

Grunge is credited with displacing glam metal from mainstream rock and opening commercial space for alternative music generally. Many grunge bands continued recording into the 21st century, and reunions and new work by Pearl Jam, Alice in Chains and Soundgarden members sustained the genre's legacy.
