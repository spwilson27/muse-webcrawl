Fiat

Fiat Automobiles, Italy's largest car manufacturer

Fiat Automobiles S.p.A. is an Italian automobile manufacturer, formerly part of Fiat Chrysler Automobiles and since 2021 a brand of Stellantis. Founded in Turin in 1899 as Fabbrica Italiana Automobili Torino (FIAT) by Giovanni Agnelli and partners, Fiat was for much of the 20th century the largest car maker in Europe and Italy's dominant industrial group.

History

Fiat produced its first car, the 4 HP, in 1900 and grew into aircraft, trains and industrial vehicles. After the Second World War it motorised Italy with small cars such as the 500 ("Topolino" and later the Nuova 500 of 1957), the 600 and the 127. Fiat acquired Lancia, Alfa Romeo and Ferrari, and in 2014 merged with Chrysler to form Fiat Chrysler Automobiles, which merged with PSA Group in 2021 to create Stellantis.

Today

The Fiat brand now concentrates on city cars and small family cars, including the modern 500 (also sold as an electric model), the Panda and the Tipo, with strong sales in Europe and South America.
