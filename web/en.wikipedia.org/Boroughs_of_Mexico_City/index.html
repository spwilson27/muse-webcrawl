Boroughs of Mexico City

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mexico City is divided into 16 boroughs (Spanish: demarcaciones territoriales), formerly called delegaciones. Each borough is headed by a mayor (alcalde), elected since 2018 under the city's 2016 constitution, which transformed the former Federal District into a full federal entity with its own legislature and granted the boroughs a status comparable to municipalities.

The sixteen boroughs

  • Álvaro Obregón — western borough including San Ángel.
  • Azcapotzalco — dense northern industrial borough.
  • Benito Juárez — central, largely middle- and upper-income residential area.
  • Coyoacán — historic southern borough, home to Frida Kahlo's Blue House.
  • Cuajimalpa de Morelos — western borough including the Santa Fe business district.
  • Cuauhtémoc — the city center, containing the Zócalo and Historic Center.
  • Gustavo A. Madero — the most populous borough, containing the Basilica of Our Lady of Guadalupe.
  • Iztacalco — the smallest borough by area.
  • Iztapalapa — the most populous for parts of its history, in the east.
  • Magdalena Contreras — southwestern borough with extensive forest.
  • Miguel Hidalgo — containing Chapultepec Park and Polanco.
  • Milpa Alta — rural southern borough known for nopal cactus production.
  • Tláhuac — southeastern borough with chinampa agriculture and wetlands.
  • Tlalpan — the largest borough by area, extending to the Ajusco mountains.
  • Venustiano Carranza — containing the international airport and the Merced market.
  • Xochimilco — famous for its canals and floating gardens (chinampas), a UNESCO World Heritage site.

History

The borough system evolved from the municipalities into which the Federal District was reorganized in 1928, when the district's municipal governments were replaced by delegations administered by appointees of the federal government. Democratization came in stages: an elected Legislative Assembly in 1997, an elected Head of Government, and finally, under the Political Constitution of Mexico City promulgated in 2017, elected mayors and councils in each borough from 2018 onward.
