Fourth Dynasty of Egypt

The Giza pyramid complex, the supreme achievement of Fourth Dynasty builders.

The Fourth Dynasty of ancient Egypt (c. 2613–2494 BC) is the dynasty of the great pyramid builders. It forms the second dynasty of the Old Kingdom period and is often regarded as the golden age of ancient Egyptian civilization, producing the pyramids of Giza and the Great Sphinx.

Achievements

Under Sneferu Egyptian builders perfected the smooth-sided true pyramid, transitioning from Djoser's step pyramid through the Bent Pyramid to the Red Pyramid at Dahshur. His son Khufu raised the Great Pyramid, originally 146.6 metres tall, which remained the tallest human-made structure in the world for more than 3,800 years. The solar boat buried beside it — the Khufu ship — survives as one of the oldest intact vessels anywhere.

The dynasty also saw the full flowering of Old Kingdom royal sculpture, including the seated statue of Khafre in diorite and the triads of Menkaure, masterpieces of Egyptian art. Trade expeditions reached Nubia, the Sinai and the Levant, and the royal annals record raids and the import of cedar and other goods.

End of the dynasty

The dynasty ended with Shepseskaf and possibly a short-lived king Thamphthis known from later lists. Power passed to the Fifth Dynasty, whose kings were more closely associated with the sun cult of Ra and the sun temples of Abu Gorab.
