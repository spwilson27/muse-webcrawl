Gamma Draconis

Gamma Draconis (Eltanin)
Constellation: Draco
Apparent magnitude: 2.23
Distance: ~154 light-years
Spectral type: K5 III

Gamma Draconis (γ Draconis, abbreviated Gamma Dra, γ Dra), formally named Eltanin, is a star in the northern constellation of Draco. It is the brightest star in Draco, with an apparent visual magnitude of 2.23. Measurements from the Hipparcos satellite place it at a distance of about 154 light-years (47 parsecs) from the Sun.

Properties

Eltanin is an orange giant of spectral type K5 III, having exhausted the hydrogen at its core and expanded to roughly 40–50 times the Sun's radius, with a luminosity of several hundred times solar. It is approaching the Solar System; in about 1.5 million years it will pass within roughly 28 light-years, becoming the brightest star in the night sky for a time.

Historical significance

The star passes almost directly overhead at the latitude of London. In the 1720s James Bradley's observations of Gamma Draconis, undertaken while searching for stellar parallax, revealed instead the aberration of light — the first direct evidence of the Earth's orbital motion — published in 1729.

Nomenclature

The traditional name Eltanin derives from the Arabic At-Tinnin, "the Dragon". In 2016 the International Astronomical Union's Working Group on Star Names approved Eltanin as the proper name for Gamma Draconis.
