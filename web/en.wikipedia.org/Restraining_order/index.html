A restraining order or protective order is an order used by a court to protect a person in a situation often involving alleged domestic violence, child abuse and neglect, assault, harassment, stalking, or sexual assault. Restraining and personal protection order laws vary from one jurisdiction to another but all establish who can seek an order, what protection or relief a person can be given under an order, and how the order may be enforced.

The unprotected or harassed person can apply for such an order from the court. In most states, there are generally two types: temporary restraining orders (TROs) and permanent restraining orders. A TRO is generally granted without a full hearing, based on the applicant's affidavit, and lasts for a short period until a hearing can be held. A longer-term order follows a court hearing at which both parties may present evidence.

Restraining orders are issued by courts and enforced by law enforcement.

Provisions

A restraining order may require the restrained person to stay a specified distance away from the protected person, their home, workplace or school; to have no contact, including by phone, text or through third parties; to move out of a shared residence; and, in many jurisdictions, to surrender firearms. Violation of an order is typically a criminal offence, punishable by arrest, fines or imprisonment, and may also constitute contempt of court.

Domestic violence orders

In the United States, every state has some form of domestic violence restraining order law. Federal law under the Violence Against Women Act requires that a valid protective order granted by one state be enforced by law enforcement in other states under the full faith and credit clause. Respondents subject to qualifying orders are prohibited from purchasing or possessing firearms under federal law (18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(8)).

Criticism and debate

Supporters argue restraining orders are an essential, accessible tool for victims. Critics note that orders are sometimes issued on limited evidence at the temporary stage, can affect child custody and housing, and that enforcement and actual protection vary in practice. Research on effectiveness is mixed, though many protected persons report improved feelings of safety.
