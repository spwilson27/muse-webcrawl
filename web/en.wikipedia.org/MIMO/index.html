MIMO

Multiple-input and multiple-output (MIMO) is a method for multiplying the capacity of a radio link using multiple transmission and receiving antennas to exploit multipath propagation. MIMO has become an essential element of wireless communication standards including IEEE 802.11n (Wi-Fi 4), Wi-Fi 5 and 6, 4G LTE, and 5G NR.

Principles

In a MIMO system, data is split into multiple spatial streams transmitted simultaneously over the same frequency channel from different antennas. Because the streams arrive at the receiver antennas by different paths, signal processing (such as space-time coding, precoding, and spatial multiplexing) can separate them, increasing throughput roughly in proportion to the number of antenna pairs, or improving reliability through diversity gain.

Applications

Beyond consumer Wi-Fi and cellular networks, MIMO techniques are used in power-line communication, digital television, and military radio. "Massive MIMO", using arrays of dozens or hundreds of antennas, is a cornerstone of 5G base stations, enabling beamforming toward individual users.
