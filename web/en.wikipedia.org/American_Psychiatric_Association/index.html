The American Psychiatric Association (APA) is an organization of psychiatrists working together to ensure humane care and effective treatment for all persons with mental illness, including substance use disorders. It is the main professional organization of psychiatrists and trainee psychiatrists in the United States, and the largest psychiatric organization in the world.

Publications

The APA publishes the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM), used by clinicians, researchers, insurance companies, and policymakers as the standard classification of mental disorders in the United States. The fifth edition, DSM-5, was published in 2013, with a text revision (DSM-5-TR) in 2022. Its flagship journal is the American Journal of Psychiatry.

History

The association was founded in 1844 as the Association of Medical Superintendents of American Institutions for the Insane, adopting its current name in 1921. It should not be confused with the American Psychological Association, which shares the abbreviation APA.
