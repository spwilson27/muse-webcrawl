Salzburg Festival

The Großes Festspielhaus, main venue of the Salzburg Festival.

The Salzburg Festival (Salzburger Festspiele) is a prominent festival of music and drama held each summer in Salzburg, Austria, the birthplace of Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart. Founded in 1920 by Hugo von Hofmannsthal, Max Reinhardt and Richard Strauss, it is widely considered one of the world's most prestigious classical music festivals.

History

The festival began with Reinhardt's legendary open-air production of Hofmannsthal's Jedermann ("Everyman") on the cathedral square, a production still performed each year. Under conductors such as Arturo Toscanini, Bruno Walter and later Herbert von Karajan, Salzburg became synonymous with the highest standards in opera and orchestral performance. The Großes Festspielhaus opened in 1960.

Programme

Each summer the festival presents operas, orchestral concerts (anchored by the Vienna Philharmonic), chamber music, recitals and theatre over roughly six weeks, drawing around a quarter of a million visitors. Sister events include the Whitsun Festival (Salzburger Festspiele Pfingsten), directed by Cecilia Bartoli since 2012, and the Easter Festival, founded by Karajan in 1967.

Significance

Salzburg productions frequently set artistic benchmarks, and premieres and new stagings there attract international attention. The festival is also a major economic engine for the city of Salzburg.
