Pyrope

Pyrope is a magnesium aluminium silicate mineral of the garnet group, with the chemical formula Mg3Al2(SiO4)3. Its colour ranges from deep red to nearly black, and transparent specimens are prized as gemstones. The name derives from the Greek pyropos, "fiery-eyed," referring to its fiery red colour.

Pyrope
Group: Garnet
Formula: Mg3Al2(SiO4)3
Colour: Red to reddish black
Hardness: 7–7.5 (Mohs)
Crystal system: Cubic

Properties and occurrence

Pyrope is the only garnet species that is always red in natural samples. It forms almost a complete solid-solution series with almandine, the iron garnet; most natural "pyrope" gem material is in fact a pyrope–almandine mixture, including the variety rhodolite, which has a purplish-red hue. Pyrope is characteristic of high-pressure metamorphic rocks such as eclogite and of ultramafic rocks like peridotite; chromium-rich pyrope is an indicator mineral in diamond prospecting, occurring in kimberlite pipes.

Sources

Historic sources include the Bohemian garnet deposits of the Czech Republic; gem material also comes from Tanzania, Kenya, Madagascar, Sri Lanka, and the southwestern United States (the "Arizona ruby" or anthill garnets).
