Circuit quantum electrodynamics

Circuit quantum electrodynamics (circuit QED or cQED) is the study of the interaction between light (microwave photons) and matter (superconducting artificial atoms) in electrical circuits. It adapts the concepts of cavity quantum electrodynamics to superconducting resonators and qubits fabricated on chips.

Principles

In circuit QED, a superconducting qubit—such as a transmon, made from Josephson junctions—is coupled to a microwave resonator that acts as a cavity. Because the artificial atom and the resonator mode can be engineered to have very large dipole coupling, the system readily reaches the strong-coupling regime, in which energy oscillates coherently between qubit and photon. Readout is typically performed dispersively, measuring the shift of the resonator frequency that depends on the qubit state.

Applications

Circuit QED is the leading architecture for superconducting quantum computers developed by companies and universities worldwide, and is used to study quantum optics phenomena such as vacuum Rabi splitting on a chip.
