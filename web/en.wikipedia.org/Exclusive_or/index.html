Exclusive or

Exclusive or, exclusive disjunction, exclusive alternation, or XOR (pronounced variously) is a logical operator which is true only when the operands differ. The XOR of two operands is true if and only if exactly one of them is true. It is symbolized by the prefix operator J and by the infix operators XOR, EOR, EXOR, ∨̇, ⊕, ⊻, and ≢.

Definition and properties

In ordinary English, "or" usually means inclusive or, but in logic and mathematics the exclusive reading "either one or the other, but not both" is captured by XOR. XOR is commutative and associative, and self-inverse: A ⊕ A = False, and A ⊕ False = A. These properties make the XOR operation the addition operation of the field of two elements and a fundamental building block of digital circuits, where the XOR gate implements it in hardware.

Applications

Exclusive or is widely used in computer science. It is the operation performed by the half adder to produce the sum bit; it underpins parity bits and error-detecting and error-correcting codes such as RAID storage; it appears in cryptography, where the bitwise XOR of a message with a key forms the basis of the one-time pad and of stream ciphers; and it is used in graphics programming, random number generation, and the classic in-place swap of two variables in some programming idioms. In bitwise operations on integers, XOR compares corresponding bits and returns 1 where the bits differ.

In natural language

The English word "or" is often ambiguous between inclusive and exclusive senses; context usually disambiguates. Phrases such as "either ... or" strongly favour the exclusive reading, while "and/or" was coined to make the inclusive reading explicit.
