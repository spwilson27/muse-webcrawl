Xerxes I

Xerxes I (Old Persian: Xšayār̥šā; c. 518–465 BCE), called Xerxes the Great, was the fourth King of Kings of the Achaemenid Empire, ruling from 486 BCE until his assassination in 465 BCE. He was the son of Darius I.

Reign

Xerxes crushed revolts in Egypt and Babylon, then mounted a vast invasion of Greece in 480 BCE to avenge his father's defeat at Marathon. His forces won at Thermopylae and sacked Athens, but the Persian fleet was destroyed at Salamis (480) and the army decisively beaten at Plataea (479), ending the invasion.

Legacy

Xerxes completed building projects at Persepolis and Susa. Greek sources portray him as proud and capricious — Herodotus famously describes him lashing the Hellespont — though modern historians read this critically. He is often identified with King Ahasuerus in the Book of Esther. He was assassinated by the commander Artabanus and succeeded by Artaxerxes I.
