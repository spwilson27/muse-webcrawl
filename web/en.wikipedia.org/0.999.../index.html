The repeating decimal 0.999... equals 1.

In mathematics, 0.999... (also written 0.9) is a repeating decimal consisting of infinitely many 9s after the decimal point. It denotes exactly the same real number as 1: in symbols, 0.999... = 1. This equality has long been accepted by professional mathematicians and is taught in textbooks, yet it is often counterintuitive to students because of the distinct notations.

Proofs

A common algebraic argument runs: let x = 0.999...; then 10x = 9.999..., and subtracting gives 9x = 9, so x = 1. More rigorously, 0.999... is the sum of the geometric series 9/10 + 9/100 + 9/1000 + ..., which converges to 1. Real analysis defines real numbers so that no nonzero infinitesimal difference can exist between 0.999... and 1.

Intuition and education

Scepticism about the equality has been studied by mathematics educators: students often expect 0.999... to be "just below" 1, or to keep growing as more 9s are added. The equality follows from the absence of nonzero infinitesimals in the real number system and the fact that there is no number between 0.999... and 1. Alternative number systems, such as the hyperreals, handle infinitesimals differently but still treat the standard repeating decimal as equal to 1.
