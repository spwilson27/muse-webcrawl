Tomato

KingdomPlantae
FamilySolanaceae
GenusSolanum
SpeciesS. lycopersicum

The tomato (Solanum lycopersicum) is a plant in the nightshade family whose berry is eaten worldwide as a vegetable. Native to western South America and Central America, it was domesticated in Mexico — the name derives from the Nahuatl word tomatl — and spread around the globe after the Spanish colonisation of the Americas.

Botany

Tomato plants are vines typically grown as annuals, with serrated leaves and yellow flowers. The fruit is classified botanically as a berry; its colour usually comes from the pigment lycopene. Thousands of cultivars exist, from tiny cherry tomatoes to ribbed beefsteak types and heirloom varieties in yellow, green and purple.

Culinary use

Tomatoes are eaten raw in salads and sandwiches and cooked into sauces, soups, juices and pastes; they are a cornerstone of Italian, Spanish, Mexican and Indian cooking. Because the plant is a nightshade, Europeans initially suspected the fruit of being poisonous, and it was grown as an ornamental for generations before becoming a food staple.

Production

China is the world's largest producer, followed by India and Turkey. In 1887 the tomato was famously at the centre of the United States Supreme Court case Nix v. Hedden, which ruled it a vegetable for customs purposes while acknowledging it was botanically a fruit.
