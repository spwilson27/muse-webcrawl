Hadamar Killing Centre

The Hadamar Killing Centre (German: NS-Tötungsanstalt Hadamar) was a psychiatric hospital in Hadamar, Hesse, used by the Nazi regime as one of six killing facilities under the Aktion T4 programme of mass murder of people with disabilities. Between January and August 1941, some 10,000 people were gassed there; after the programme's official halt, killing continued by lethal injection and starvation until March 1945, claiming thousands more victims, including Jewish children, forced labourers, and tubercular patients.

History

The facility was established in a former sanatorium. Victims arrived by bus and were murdered in a gas chamber disguised as a shower room; their bodies were cremated on site, and families received falsified death certificates and urns of ashes.

Trials and memorial

In October 1945, American military authorities tried seven members of the Hadamar staff in one of the first post-war war crimes trials held in Germany, focused on the murder of several hundred Polish and Soviet forced labourers. Today the site is a memorial and museum documenting the crimes of Nazi "euthanasia".
