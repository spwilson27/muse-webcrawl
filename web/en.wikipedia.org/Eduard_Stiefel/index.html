Eduard Stiefel

Eduard L. Stiefel (21 April 1909 – 25 November 1978) was a Swiss mathematician. Together with Magnus Hestenes, he invented the conjugate gradient method, and gave (partially) what is now understood as the Stiefel–Whitney class.

Life and work

Stiefel studied at ETH Zurich, receiving his doctorate in 1935 under Heinz Hopf. He spent time at Princeton's Institute for Advanced Study before returning to ETH, where he became a professor and founded the Institute for Applied Mathematics in 1948.

Recognising the need for automatic computation in applied mathematics, Stiefel travelled to the United States in 1948–49 to study American computer projects, and on his return arranged for ETH to acquire Konrad Zuse's Z4 computer in 1950, making Zurich one of the earliest European centres of electronic computing.

Contributions

In 1952 Stiefel and Magnus Hestenes independently developed the conjugate gradient method for solving large systems of linear equations, now a cornerstone of numerical linear algebra. His earlier work with Hassler Whitney on characteristic classes produced the Stiefel–Whitney classes in algebraic topology, and the Stiefel manifold of orthonormal frames bears his name. He also worked on celestial mechanics, including satellite orbit computation, and directed numerical weather prediction projects in Switzerland.
