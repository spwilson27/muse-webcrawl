Nolan Kay Bushnell (born February 5, 1943) is an American businessman and electrical engineer. He established Atari, Inc. and the Chuck E. Cheese chain of family entertainment centers, and is widely regarded as one of the founding fathers of the video game industry. In 1971 he co-created Computer Space, the first commercially sold coin-operated video game, and in 1972 co-founded Atari with Ted Dabney, launching Pong, the game that ignited the arcade video game business.

Nolan Bushnell, founder of Atari and Chuck E. Cheese

Early life

Bushnell was born in Clearfield, Utah, and studied electrical engineering at the University of Utah, where he encountered Spacewar! on the university's mainframe — an experience that convinced him of the commercial potential of video games. He worked at Ampex and at Nutting Associates, where he developed Computer Space.

Atari

Bushnell and Ted Dabney founded Atari in Sunnyvale, California, in June 1972. Their first product, Pong, became a sensation in bars and arcades. Atari expanded into home consoles with the Home Pong and the Atari 2600 (1977). Bushnell sold Atari to Warner Communications in 1976 for an estimated $28 million and left the company in 1979 following disagreements over direction. Atari famously gave a start to Apple co-founders Steve Jobs and Steve Wozniak; Bushnell declined to invest $50,000 in Apple for a one-third stake.

Chuck E. Cheese and later ventures

In 1977 Bushnell opened the first Pizza Time Theatre, combining pizza, arcade games, and animatronic entertainment; the chain became Chuck E. Cheese. He subsequently founded more than twenty companies through his Catalyst Technologies incubator, including ventures in robotics, mapping (Etak), and education (BrainRush). Bushnell was inducted into the Video Game Hall of Fame and received the BAFTA Fellowship for his contributions to the industry.
