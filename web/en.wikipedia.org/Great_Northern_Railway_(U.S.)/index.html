Great Northern Railway (U.S.)

Reporting mark: GN

Operated: 1889–1970

Headquarters: St. Paul, Minnesota

Key person: James J. Hill

Successor: Burlington Northern Railroad

The Great Northern Railway (reporting mark GN) was an American Class I railroad running from Saint Paul, Minnesota, to Seattle, Washington. It was the creation of the 19th-century railroad entrepreneur James J. Hill and, at its completion in 1893, the northernmost transcontinental railroad in the United States.

History

Hill and his partners acquired the bankrupt St. Paul and Pacific Railroad in 1878 and rebuilt it as the St. Paul, Minneapolis and Manitoba Railway, extending westward across North Dakota and Montana while promoting settlement and agriculture along the line. Unlike other transcontinentals, the Great Northern was built largely without federal land grants, and Hill emphasised sound engineering and low grades: the line crossed the Rocky Mountains at Marias Pass (5,213 ft), the lowest crossing in the northern United States, and later replaced its original Cascade crossing with the Cascade Tunnel, opened in 1929 and at 7.8 miles still one of the longest railway tunnels in the US.

The completed route ran from the Twin Cities through Fargo, Minot, Great Falls, and Spokane to Everett and Seattle, with branches to the iron ranges of Minnesota, the Pacific Northwest ports, and north into Canada.

Operations and trains

The railway's freight traffic included iron ore, grain, timber and merchandise. Its premier passenger train was the Empire Builder, inaugurated in 1929 and named for Hill himself, the "Empire Builder". The Great Northern was noted for its mountain goat logo — "Rocky" — inspired by the goats of Glacier National Park, which the railway promoted as a tourist destination and where it built hotels and chalets.

Merger

After decades of attempts, the Great Northern merged in 1970 with the Northern Pacific Railway, the Chicago, Burlington and Quincy Railroad and the Spokane, Portland and Seattle Railway to form the Burlington Northern Railroad, today part of BNSF Railway. Amtrak's Empire Builder still runs over much of the former Great Northern route.
