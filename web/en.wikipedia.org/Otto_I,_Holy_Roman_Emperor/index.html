Otto I, Holy Roman Emperor


Born: 23 November 912, Wallhausen
Died: 7 May 973 (aged 60), Memleben
Reign: King of Germany 936–973; Emperor 962–973
Spouse: Eadgyth of England; Adelaide of Italy

Otto I (23 November 912 – 7 May 973), known as Otto the Great, was East Frankish (German) king from 936 and Holy Roman Emperor from 962 until his death. He is considered the founder and first emperor of the Holy Roman Empire, reviving the imperial title in the West more than a century after its collapse in Italy.

Consolidation of the kingdom

The son of Henry the Fowler, Otto subdued the great stem duchies by appointing family members and churchmen to office, building the "Ottonian church system" in which bishops served as pillars of royal government. He ended the Magyar threat to Europe with a decisive victory at the Battle of Lechfeld in 955, and extended German influence eastward over the Slavs, founding bishoprics such as Magdeburg as centres of mission and rule.

Emperor

Intervening in Italy, Otto married Adelaide, widow of King Lothair II, took the Lombard crown, and in 962 was crowned emperor in Rome by Pope John XII, who in return received the Diploma Ottonianum confirming papal territories. When John plotted against him, Otto deposed him — asserting imperial authority over the papacy. His reign saw a cultural flowering, the Ottonian Renaissance, and his dynasty ruled the empire until 1024. He died at Memleben in 973 and was buried in Magdeburg Cathedral beside his first wife, Eadgyth.
