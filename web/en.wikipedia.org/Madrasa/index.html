Madrasa

The courtyard of a madrasa, with an iwan and student cells.

A madrasa (Arabic: مدرسة, madrasah, pl. مدارس, madāris) is the Arabic word for any type of educational institution, secular or religious (of any religion), whether for elementary education or higher learning. In the West, the word usually refers to a specific type of religious school or college for the study of the religion of Islam, although this may not be the only subject studied.

In countries where Arabic is not the primary language, the term is often loaned for Islamic schools: medrese in Turkish, madrasah in Indonesian and Malay, madrasa in Urdu, Bengali, and Persian.

History

Early Qur'anic schools attached to mosques existed from the first centuries of Islam, but the madrasa as an endowed institution is usually traced to the 9th–11th centuries in Khurasan and Persia. The most famous early example is the Nizamiyya network founded by the Seljuk vizier Nizam al-Mulk in the 11th century, including the Nizamiyya of Baghdad (1065), where the theologian al-Ghazali taught.

Madrasas were funded through charitable endowments (waqf), which paid professors and provided stipends, food, and lodging for students. The curriculum centred on the Qur'an, hadith, Islamic jurisprudence (fiqh), Arabic grammar, and theology, with some institutions also teaching logic, mathematics, astronomy, and medicine.

Architecture

Classical madrasa architecture, especially in Persia and Central Asia, employed a four-iwan plan: a rectangular courtyard surrounded by vaulted halls (iwans) and two storeys of student cells, often attached to a mosque and the tomb of the founder. Notable examples include the Madrasa al-Mustansiriya in Baghdad, the madrasas of the Registan in Samarkand, and the Bou Inania Madrasa in Fez.

The madrasa in the modern world

Today madrasas operate in many Muslim-majority and minority communities, ranging from informal Qur'an memorisation schools to large boarding institutions such as those of South Asia (including the Deobandi and Barelvi networks) and the pesantren of Indonesia. In several countries, state-supervised madrasa systems combine religious and general subjects.
