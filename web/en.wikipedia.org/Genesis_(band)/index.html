Genesis (band)

Genesis were an English rock band formed at Charterhouse School, Godalming, Surrey, in 1967. The band's longest-lasting and most commercially successful line-up consisted of keyboardist Tony Banks, bassist/guitarist Mike Rutherford, and drummer/singer Phil Collins. The earlier line-up that achieved critical and cult success in the 1970s featured singer Peter Gabriel and guitarist Steve Hackett.

History

Genesis began as a group of schoolfriends writing pop songs, and after signing to Charisma Records developed into one of the leading progressive rock bands of the 1970s with albums such as Foxtrot (1972), Selling England by the Pound (1973), and the concept album The Lamb Lies Down on Broadway (1974), noted for Gabriel's theatrical stage costumes and storytelling.

Gabriel left in 1975, and Collins, previously the drummer, became lead vocalist. Hackett departed in 1977, leaving the trio whose more concise, pop-oriented sound brought enormous commercial success: Duke (1980), Abacab (1981), Genesis (1983), and Invisible Touch (1986), whose title track reached number one in the United States. Collins left in 1996; one further album, Calling All Stations (1997) with singer Ray Wilson, preceded a long hiatus. The Banks–Rutherford–Collins line-up reunited for tours in 2007 and again for "The Last Domino?" tour of 2021–2022, the band's farewell.

Legacy and sales

Genesis have sold an estimated 100 million albums worldwide and were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2010. Their career is frequently described as two distinct bands — a progressive rock group fronted by Gabriel and a pop-rock trio under Collins — both commercially and critically significant.
