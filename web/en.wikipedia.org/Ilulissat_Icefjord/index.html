The Ilulissat Icefjord (Greenlandic: Ilulissat Kangerlua) is a fjord in western Greenland near the town of Ilulissat. It is filled with icebergs calved from the Jakobshavn Glacier (Sermeq Kujalleq), one of the fastest-moving and most productive glaciers in the world, draining about 6.5% of the Greenland ice sheet.

Geography

The fjord runs about 40 km from the Greenland ice sheet to Disko Bay. A shallow sill at its mouth grounds many large bergs, which remain in the fjord until melted or forced out by ice behind them.

Scientific significance

Studied for over 250 years, the icefjord has helped scientists understand ice-cap dynamics and climate change. It was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2004. The Ilulissat Icefjord Centre (Kangiata Illorsua), designed by Dorte Mandrup, opened in 2021.

Tourism

Boardwalks to the fjord's edge and boat tours among the bergs make it one of Greenland's most visited natural sites.
