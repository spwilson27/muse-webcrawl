Long non-coding RNA

Long non-coding RNAs (long ncRNAs, lncRNA) are a type of RNA, generally defined as transcripts more than 200 nucleotides long that are not translated into protein. Tens of thousands of lncRNAs have been catalogued in the human genome, although the fraction with demonstrated function remains debated.

Functions

Known lncRNAs regulate gene expression at many levels: by recruiting chromatin-modifying complexes, guiding transcription factors, acting as scaffolds for nuclear bodies, or serving as decoys for microRNAs and proteins. Well-studied examples include Xist, which coats one X chromosome during X-inactivation; HOTAIR, which recruits Polycomb repressive complexes; and MALAT1, associated with nuclear speckles and cancer prognosis.

Characteristics

Most lncRNAs are transcribed by RNA polymerase II, capped, spliced and polyadenylated like mRNAs, but typically show lower expression, weaker sequence conservation and more tissue-specific patterns. Dysregulation of lncRNAs has been linked to cancer, neurological disease and developmental disorders, making them an active field of molecular biology.
