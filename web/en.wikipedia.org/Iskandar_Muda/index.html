Iskandar Muda (c. 1583 – 27 December 1636) was the twelfth Sultan of Aceh in northern Sumatra and the greatest ruler of the Aceh Sultanate, which under him became a major commercial and Islamic power in Southeast Asia, dominating the pepper trade and much of the Malay world.

Reign

Coming to the throne in 1607 after a palace coup, Iskandar Muda centralised the administration, subordinating the hereditary orang kaya nobility and building a formidable standing army and fleet equipped with large galleys and heavy cannon from Ottoman sources. He extended Acehnese control along both coasts of Sumatra and across the strait to Kedah, Perak, Pahang and Johor, and punished local rulers who resisted his monopoly of the pepper trade. European visitors, such as the English factors and the French admiral Augustin de Beaulieu, described a glittering but terrifying court in which discipline was maintained by draconian punishments.

Malacca campaign

His greatest ambition, the conquest of Portuguese Malacca, ended in disaster in 1629, when his armada was destroyed by a coalition of Portuguese, Johor and Patani forces, with enormous losses. The sultanate never recovered its former strength, though it remained wealthy and independent.

Legacy

Iskandar Muda's era is remembered as Aceh's golden age: Islam and Malay letters flourished, scholars such as Hamzah Fansuri's successors taught at court, and the administrative code Adat Meukuta Alam bears his name. He died in 1636 and was succeeded by his son-in-law Iskandar Thani. Sultan Iskandar Muda International Airport in Banda Aceh is named after him.
