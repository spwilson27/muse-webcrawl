Lacrosse is a contact team sport played with a lacrosse stick and a lacrosse ball. It is the oldest organized sport in North America, originating with the Indigenous peoples of the Eastern Woodlands and the Plains, for whom it held ceremonial, social and sometimes martial significance. Players use the head of the stick, which is strung with a mesh pocket, to carry, pass, catch and shoot the ball into the opposing team's goal.

The modern game was codified in 1867 by the Canadian dentist William George Beers. Field lacrosse is played outdoors on a field roughly 110 yards long, with ten players per side. Canada popularised an indoor variant, box lacrosse, played in ice hockey rinks in the summer. A shortened six-a-side version, "sixes", was developed for international play and will return lacrosse to the Olympic Games at Los Angeles in 2028.

History

Versions of the stickball game were played by Algonquian, Iroquoian, and Southeastern tribes for centuries. The Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) called it deyhontsigwa'ehs ("they bump hips") and regarded it as a gift from the Creator, played to settle disputes, train warriors, and for healing and thanksgiving. French Jesuit missionaries in the 1630s observed the game and named it la crosse, after a bishop's crosier. Dr. William George Beers of Montreal wrote the first rulebook in 1867, standardising team size and field dimensions, and the game spread through the Commonwealth and the United States.

Variations

  • Field lacrosse — men's and women's versions, played outdoors; the women's game historically involved less protective equipment and different stick rules.
  • Box lacrosse — indoor, six-a-side, played mainly in Canada and by the Haudenosaunee; governed by a 30-second shot clock.
  • Intercrosse / softcrosse — a non-contact co-ed version using plastic sticks and a soft ball, used in schools.
  • Wheelchair lacrosse — played by athletes in sport wheelchairs, mainly in the United States.
  • Sixes — a fast-paced six-a-side outdoor format adopted for the Olympics.

International competition

World Lacrosse (founded 1974 as the International Lacrosse Federation) organises world championships for men's and women's field, box, and under-20 teams. The Haudenosaunee Nationals compete as a sovereign team and are perennial medal contenders alongside the United States and Canada. The sport appeared at the 1904 and 1908 Olympic Games, where Canadian teams won gold.
