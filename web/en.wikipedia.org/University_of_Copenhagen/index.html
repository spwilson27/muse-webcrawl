The University of Copenhagen (Danish: Københavns Universitet, abbreviated KU) is a public research university in Copenhagen, Denmark. Founded in 1479 by King Christian I, it is the oldest university in Denmark and one of the oldest in Northern Europe. With around 37,000 students and 10,000 employees, it is also the country's largest institution of research and education.

History

The university was inaugurated on 1 June 1479 after Pope Sixtus IV issued a bull authorising its creation. It was re-founded in 1537 as a Lutheran institution following the Reformation, becoming the centre of theological and scientific learning in Denmark–Norway. Notable early figures include the astronomer Tycho Brahe, who studied there, and Ole Rømer, who measured the speed of light. Niels Bohr, who won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1922, founded the university's Institute for Theoretical Physics (now the Niels Bohr Institute) in 1921.

Organisation

The university has six faculties: Health and Medical Sciences, Humanities, Law, Science, Social Sciences, and Theology. Its campuses — City Campus, North Campus, South Campus, and Frederiksberg Campus — are spread across the Copenhagen area. Teaching was conducted in Latin until the early 19th century; most bachelor's teaching is now in Danish, with many master's programmes in English.

Rankings and alumni

KU is a member of the International Alliance of Research Universities (IARU) and LERU, and is regularly ranked among the top universities in Europe. Ten Nobel laureates have been associated with the university, including Niels Bohr, Aage Bohr, and Ben Mottelson.
