Online auction

An online auction (also electronic auction or e-auction) is an auction conducted over the internet, in which participants bid for products or services through a website or application. Online auctions extend the traditional auction by removing geographic limits, running continuously, and automating bidding and payment.

Types

The most common form is the forward (English) auction, in which prices rise until a deadline; platforms such as eBay use proxy bidding, automatically raising a bidder's offer to the minimum needed to stay ahead, up to a maximum. Other forms include Dutch auctions (descending price), reverse auctions (sellers compete for a buyer's business, common in procurement), sealed-bid auctions, penny auctions, and live-streamed auctions of collectibles and art.

History

Internet auctions date to the mid-1990s: eBay (founded 1995 as AuctionWeb) and Onsale popularized consumer online auctions, while business-to-business exchanges adopted electronic bidding around the same period. Established auction houses such as Sotheby's and Christie's later added online-only and hybrid sales.

Issues

Online auctions face problems including fraud and non-delivery, shill bidding (fake bids by the seller), bid sniping in the final seconds, and last-second bidding wars that push prices above comparable fixed prices. Feedback and reputation systems, escrow services, and buyer-protection programs mitigate some risks.
