Halukka

TypeCharitable distribution system
RegionOttoman Palestine (the Four Holy Cities)
PeriodEarly modern era to 20th century

The halukka (or chalukah) was a system of collecting charitable funds in Jewish communities of the diaspora and distributing them to poor Jewish residents of Palestine, particularly those living in the Four Holy Cities: Jerusalem, Hebron, Safed, and Tiberias. The name derives from a Hebrew word meaning "distribution".

Organisation

Funds were gathered by emissaries (meshulachim) sent to communities across Europe, North Africa, and elsewhere, and were divided among the recipients according to their community of origin (organised in kolels) and family size. The system supported a largely scholarly and elderly population in the Old Yishuv devoted to Torah study and prayer.

Criticism and decline

The halukka was criticised by maskilim, productivisation advocates, and early Zionists, who argued it discouraged labour and self-sufficiency. From the late 19th century, new settlements and institutions increasingly directed support toward schools, hospitals, and productive enterprises, and the traditional halukka declined in importance, especially after the First World War disrupted collections.
