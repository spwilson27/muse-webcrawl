Bengal School of Art

The Bengal School of Art was an influential art movement in early 20th-century India that promoted a distinctly Indian, nationalist style of painting, rejecting the academic Western art then taught in colonial art schools.

Origins

The movement was led by Abanindranath Tagore, nephew of Rabindranath Tagore, and supported by E. B. Havell, principal of the Government School of Art in Calcutta. Abanindranath's Bharat Mata (1905) became its iconic image.

Style

Artists drew on Mughal and Rajput miniatures, Ajanta murals and Japanese wash techniques, producing soft, lyrical watercolours on Indian themes — mythology, history and rural life.

Artists

Key figures included Nandalal Bose, Kshitindranath Majumdar, Asit Kumar Haldar and K. Venkatappa. The school shaped modern Indian art and the art programme at Santiniketan.
