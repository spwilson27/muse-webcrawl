Salinity

Salinity is the saltiness or amount of salt dissolved in a body of water, called saline water (compare with salt content). It is usually measured in g/L or g/kg (grams of salt per litre or kilogram of water — the latter being dimensionless and equal to ‰, parts per thousand). Salinity is an important factor in determining many aspects of the chemistry of natural waters and of biological processes within them, and is a thermodynamic state variable that, along with temperature and pressure, governs physical characteristics like the density and heat capacity of water.

Definitions and measurement

Oceanographers historically measured salinity in practical salinity units (PSU) from electrical conductivity, based on the Practical Salinity Scale 1978; the current TEOS-10 standard defines Absolute Salinity in g/kg. Average ocean salinity is about 35 g/kg (3.5 percent), though it varies from below 10 g/kg near river mouths to over 40 g/kg in the Red Sea. Waters are classified as fresh (below 0.5 ‰), brackish (0.5–30 ‰), saline (30–50 ‰), or brine (above 50 ‰).

Significance

Salinity affects the density of seawater and hence ocean circulation, including the thermohaline circulation driven by differences in temperature and salinity. It constrains which organisms can live in a body of water, and salinization of soils and fresh water is a major environmental problem in irrigation and in coastal areas affected by seawater intrusion.
