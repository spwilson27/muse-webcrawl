Dragon boat

A dragon boat is a human-powered watercraft originating from the Pearl River Delta region of China's southern Guangdong Province. Traditionally made of wood and decorated with a dragon head and tail, it is propelled by a crew of paddlers rowing in unison to the beat of a drum. Dragon boat racing is the best-known use and forms part of the traditional Chinese Duanwu Festival (Dragon Boat Festival).

History

Dragon boat racing dates back more than 2,000 years. Popular legend associates the festival with the poet and minister Qu Yuan (c. 340–278 BC), who drowned himself in the Miluo River; locals raced out in boats to recover his body and dropped rice dumplings (zongzi) into the water to keep fish away. Ritual racing was believed to ward off evil spirits and encourage rains for prosperous crops.

The boat and crew

A standard racing dragon boat is about 12.5 metres long and carries 20 paddlers seated in pairs, a drummer at the bow who sets the stroke rhythm, and a steerer (sweep) at the stern operating a long steering oar. Boats are traditionally blessed and the dragon's eyes "dotted" in an awakening ceremony before competition.

Modern sport

Modern dragon boat racing began in Hong Kong in 1976 and is now governed internationally by the International Dragon Boat Federation (founded 1991). Festivals and regattas are held worldwide, and dragon boating has become popular as a corporate team-building activity and among breast-cancer survivor crews.
