Popular Science (also known as PopSci) is an American science and technology magazine and digital publication founded in 1872 by Edward L. Youmans. It is devoted to reporting on new technology, scientific discoveries, and innovations for a general audience.

History

The magazine began as The Popular Science Monthly, a scholarly periodical that published articles by leading scientists of the day, including Charles Darwin, Thomas Henry Huxley, and Louis Pasteur. In 1915 the scholarly content was spun off as The Scientific Monthly, while Popular Science continued as a magazine focused on practical science, inventions, and do-it-yourself projects.

Through the twentieth century the magazine became famous for its futuristic cover art and its predictions about automobiles, aviation, spaceflight, and home technology. In 2020 the print edition was discontinued and the publication became digital-only.

Content

Popular Science covers consumer technology, space exploration, health, the environment, and military innovation. Its annual "Best of What's New" awards, first presented in 1988, recognize notable new products and technologies.
