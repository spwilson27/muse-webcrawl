Volatile organic compound

Volatile organic compounds (VOCs)
DefinitionOrganic chemicals with high vapour pressure at room temperature
ExamplesBenzene, toluene, formaldehyde, acetone, xylene
SourcesPaints, solvents, fuels, vehicles, vegetation
EffectsSmog formation; irritation; some carcinogenic

Volatile organic compounds (VOCs) are organic chemicals that have a high vapour pressure at ordinary room temperature, causing significant numbers of molecules to evaporate into the surrounding air. The term covers thousands of substances, both natural (emitted by vegetation, e.g. isoprene and terpenes) and synthetic. Regulatory definitions vary: the U.S. EPA, for example, defines VOCs by their photochemical reactivity, exempting compounds of negligible reactivity such as acetone.

Sources

Common indoor sources include paints, varnishes, adhesives, cleaning products, pressed-wood furniture (formaldehyde), cosmetics, and tobacco smoke. Outdoor sources include vehicle exhaust, petrol evaporation, industrial solvents, and by far the largest global source — plant emissions.

Health and environmental effects

In sunlight, VOCs react with nitrogen oxides to form ground-level ozone and photochemical smog. Indoors, elevated VOC levels are associated with eye, nose, and throat irritation and "sick building syndrome"; specific VOCs carry specific hazards — benzene and formaldehyde are known human carcinogens. Regulation of VOC content in paints and coatings, and of industrial and vehicle emissions, is a standard air-quality policy in many countries.
