Five Strengths

The Five Strengths (Sanskrit: pañcabalāni; Pali: pañca balāni) are a set of qualities in Buddhism that, when developed, become powers that cannot be shaken by their opposites. They are:

  1. Faith / conviction (saddhā bala)
  2. Energy / effort / persistence (viriya bala)
  3. Mindfulness (sati bala)
  4. Concentration (samādhi bala)
  5. Wisdom / discernment (paññā bala)

Relation to the Five Faculties

The Five Strengths correspond closely to the Five Spiritual Faculties (pañca indriyāni): the same five qualities are called "faculties" while they are still being developed and vulnerable, and "strengths" or "powers" when they have become firmly established and unshakeable. In the traditional scheme, faith is balanced by wisdom, and energy by concentration, with mindfulness serving as the balancing and protecting faculty overall.

Place in the path

The Five Strengths are included among the thirty-seven aids to awakening (bodhipakkhiyā dhammā) and are frequently taught as a summary of the qualities a practitioner must cultivate to progress toward enlightenment.
