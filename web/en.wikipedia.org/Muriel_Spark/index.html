Muriel Spark

Dame Muriel Sarah Spark (née Camberg; 1 February 1918 – 13 April 2006) was a Scottish novelist, short story writer, poet and essayist. She is best known for her novel The Prime of Miss Jean Brodie (1961), the story of an unconventional Edinburgh schoolteacher and her influence on a group of schoolgirls.

Life

Born in Edinburgh, Spark worked as a secretary and in wartime intelligence during the Second World War. After years in Southern Rhodesia and London, where she edited poetry journals and struggled financially, she converted to Roman Catholicism in 1954, a faith that deeply shaped her fiction. Success with novels such as Memento Mori (1959) and The Prime of Miss Jean Brodie allowed her to write full time; she later lived in New York and, for many years, in Tuscany, Italy.

Work

Spark wrote 22 novels noted for their wit, economy and blend of realism with metaphysical and sometimes macabre themes. Other works include The Ballad of Peckham Rye, The Girls of Slender Means and Loitering with Intent. She was appointed DBE in 1993.
