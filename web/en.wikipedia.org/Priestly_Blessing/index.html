Priestly Blessing

The Priestly Blessing (Hebrew: Birkat Kohanim), also known in rabbinic literature as the raising of the hands, is a Hebrew prayer recited by Kohanim (Jewish priests) to bless the congregation. Its text comes from Numbers 6:24–26: "The LORD bless you and keep you; the LORD make his face shine upon you and be gracious to you; the LORD lift up his countenance upon you and give you peace."

Ritual

During the blessing, the Kohanim stand facing the congregation with their hands raised, fingers arranged to form the Hebrew letter shin, and their prayer shawls draped over their heads. In Israel the blessing is recited daily in many congregations; in the Diaspora it is commonly reserved for festivals.

Antiquity

The blessing is among the oldest biblical texts known: it appears on two silver amulets from Ketef Hinnom in Jerusalem dated to around 600 BCE. Variants of the blessing are also used in Christian liturgy.
