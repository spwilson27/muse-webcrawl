Albert of Prussia (German: Albrecht von Preussen; 17 May 1490 – 20 March 1568) was the last Grand Master of the Teutonic Order and the first Duke of Prussia. In 1525 he secularised the Teutonic Order's Prussian territory, converting to Lutheranism and transforming it into the hereditary Duchy of Prussia, a fief of the Kingdom of Poland — an act that made Prussia the first openly Protestant state.

Grand Master

A member of the Franconian line of the House of Hohenzollern, Albert was elected Grand Master in 1510. He resisted Polish demands of homage, leading to the Polish–Teutonic War of 1519–1521, which ended in a four-year truce. During journeys through Germany he met Martin Luther, who advised him to dissolve the Order's rule, marry, and rule Prussia as a hereditary duke.

Duke of Prussia

By the Treaty of Kraków (1525) and the Prussian Homage in Kraków, Albert received the duchy from his uncle, King Sigismund I of Poland. He introduced the Reformation, issued a church order, promoted printing in German, Polish and Old Prussian, and founded schools. In 1544 he founded the University of Königsberg, the Albertina, as a Protestant university. He married Dorothea of Denmark in 1526 and, after her death, Anna Maria of Brunswick.

Later years and legacy

Albert's later reign was troubled by theological disputes (the Osiandrian controversy), court intrigue and plague. He died at Tapiau in 1568 and was succeeded by his son Albert Frederick. His transformation of the monastic state into a secular duchy laid the groundwork for the later Brandenburg-Prussian state.
