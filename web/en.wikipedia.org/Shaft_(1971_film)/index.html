Shaft is a 1971 American blaxploitation crime action film directed by Gordon Parks and starring Richard Roundtree as the private detective John Shaft. Adapted by Ernest Tidyman and John D. F. Black from Tidyman's 1970 novel of the same name, the film tells the story of a private detective hired by a Harlem mobster to rescue his kidnapped daughter from Italian mafiosi.

Plot

John Shaft, a New York private investigator, is recruited by Harlem crime boss Bumpy Jonas to find his daughter Marcy, who has been kidnapped. Shaft works through a network of contacts, including militant activist Ben Buford, while navigating between the police, Jonas's organisation and the Mafia, and eventually leads a rescue mission to free Marcy.

Music and legacy

The film's soundtrack by Isaac Hayes, especially the "Theme from Shaft", became iconic; Hayes won the Academy Award for Best Original Song, the first African American to win in that category. Shaft was a major commercial success and is credited with helping to launch the blaxploitation genre. It spawned sequels including Shaft's Big Score! (1972) and Shaft in Africa (1973), a short-lived television series, and later films in 2000 and 2019. In 2000 the film was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry.
