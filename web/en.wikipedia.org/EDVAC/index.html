The EDVAC computer at the Ballistic Research Laboratory.

EDVAC (Electronic Discrete Variable Automatic Computer) was one of the earliest electronic computers. It was built by the Moore School of Electrical Engineering at the University of Pennsylvania for the United States Army's Ballistic Research Laboratory at Aberdeen Proving Ground, as a successor to the ENIAC.

Design

Unlike ENIAC, EDVAC was a binary rather than decimal machine, and it was designed to be a stored-program computer. Its design was famously described in John von Neumann's 1945 First Draft of a Report on the EDVAC, which circulated the stored-program concept, thereafter known as the von Neumann architecture, to researchers worldwide. EDVAC used mercury delay-line memory holding about 1,024 44-bit words and roughly 6,000 vacuum tubes; input and output were by magnetic wire and later magnetic tape.

History

Design work began in 1944, while ENIAC was still under construction, with J. Presper Eckert and John Mauchly leading the engineering. After the designers left the university to found their own company, progress slowed, and EDVAC was not delivered until 1949 and not fully operational until 1951. It ran ballistic and scientific calculations until 1962.
