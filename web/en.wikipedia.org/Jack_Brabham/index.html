Sir John Arthur Brabham (2 April 1926 – 19 May 2014) was an Australian racing driver who won the Formula One World Championship in 1959, 1960, and 1966. He remains the only driver to win the championship in a car bearing his own name, built by the Brabham team he founded with designer Ron Tauranac.

Career

Brabham began racing midget cars in Australia after wartime service as an RAAF mechanic, then moved to England and joined Cooper. Driving Cooper's revolutionary rear-engined cars, he won back-to-back titles in 1959 — famously pushing his car across the line at Sebring when it ran out of fuel — and 1960, helping establish the rear-engined layout as standard.

Frustrated with Cooper, he founded Motor Racing Developments in 1961. In 1966, with the Australian Repco V8 engine, the light, reliable Brabham BT19 carried him to his third title at age 40. He retired at the end of 1970; his team, later owned by Bernie Ecclestone, won further championships under Nelson Piquet.

Legacy

Knighted in 1979, Brabham was renowned as an engineer-driver whose mechanical sympathy and development skill shaped his cars. His sons Geoff, Gary, and David also raced, David winning the 2009 Le Mans 24 Hours.
