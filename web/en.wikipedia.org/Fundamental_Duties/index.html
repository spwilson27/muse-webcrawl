Fundamental Duties

The Fundamental Duties are a set of moral obligations of the citizens of India, set out in Part IVA (Article 51A) of the Constitution of India. They were not part of the original Constitution of 1950; ten duties were added by the 42nd Amendment in 1976, on the recommendation of the Swaran Singh Committee, and an eleventh was added by the 86th Amendment in 2002.

The duties

Article 51A states that it shall be the duty of every citizen of India, among other things: to abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals, the national flag and the national anthem; to cherish the ideals of the freedom struggle; to uphold the sovereignty, unity, and integrity of India; to defend the country; to promote harmony and renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women; to value and preserve the composite culture of India; to protect the natural environment; to develop scientific temper, humanism, and the spirit of inquiry and reform; to safeguard public property and abjure violence; to strive towards excellence; and (added in 2002) for a parent or guardian to provide opportunities for education to their child between the ages of six and fourteen.

Legal status

Unlike the Fundamental Rights, the Fundamental Duties are non-justiciable: they cannot be enforced by courts, and no citizen can be penalised solely for failing to observe them. Parliament may, however, provide by law for penalties for failure to fulfil specific duties. The Supreme Court of India has used the duties as an aid to interpreting statutes and assessing the constitutionality of legislation.

Background

The idea of constitutional duties was drawn in part from the constitution of the former Soviet Union. The Swaran Singh Committee recommended eight duties in 1976; the government added ten, and the eleventh, on children's education, accompanied the introduction of the right to education as a Fundamental Right under Article 21A.
