Guy Standing (born 9 February 1948) is a British labour economist and professorial research associate at SOAS University of London. He is best known for his book The Precariat: The New Dangerous Class (2011), which argues that globalisation has produced a growing class of people living in insecure employment, lacking occupational identity and social protections.

Standing worked for many years at the International Labour Organization (ILO), where he directed the Socio-Economic Security Programme. He has been a prominent advocate of unconditional basic income and co-founded the Basic Income Earth Network (BIEN) in 1986. He has participated in basic income pilot projects in Namibia and India, the latter conducted with the Self Employed Women's Association (SEWA) in Madhya Pradesh.

Main works

His books include Beyond the New Paternalism (2002), Work after Globalization: Building Occupational Citizenship (2009), The Precariat (2011), A Precariat Charter (2014), The Corruption of Capitalism (2016), Basic Income: And How We Can Make It Happen (2017), Plunder of the Commons (2019), and The Blue Commons (2022).

Ideas

Standing argues that the precariat constitutes a distinct socio-economic class-in-the-making, characterised by insecure labour, unstable income and loss of citizenship rights. He contends that without policies such as basic income and a revival of the commons, the precariat could be drawn toward populist and authoritarian politics.
