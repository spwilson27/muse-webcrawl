Psychometrics

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Psychometrics is a field of study within psychology concerned with the theory and technique of measurement. It generally refers to the field in psychology and education that is devoted to testing, measurement, assessment, and related activities. Psychometrics is concerned with the objective measurement of latent constructs that cannot be directly observed, such as intelligence, personality traits, abilities, and attitudes.

The field involves two major research tasks: constructing instruments and procedures for measurement, and developing and refining theoretical approaches to measurement. Key concepts include reliability (the consistency of a measure), validity (whether a test measures what it claims to measure), standardization, and fairness or bias in testing.

History

Psychometrics has roots in the work of Francis Galton, who pioneered individual-differences research and correlation, and in early mental testing by James McKeen Cattell and Alfred Binet. Charles Spearman's work on factor analysis and the g factor of intelligence, Louis Leon Thurstone's multiple-factor theory, and the development of classical test theory in the 20th century established the field's statistical foundations. The founding of the Psychometric Society and the journal Psychometrika in the 1930s consolidated it as a discipline.

Theoretical approaches

  • Classical test theory models an observed score as a true score plus error, and underlies measures such as Cronbach's alpha.
  • Item response theory (IRT) models the probability of a response as a function of a latent trait and item parameters, enabling adaptive testing and item banking.
  • Factor analysis and structural equation modelling are used to investigate the structure of psychological attributes.
  • Rasch measurement provides models for constructing interval-level measures from ordinal data.

Applications

Psychometric methods underpin intelligence tests (such as the Wechsler scales), personality inventories (such as the Big Five inventories and the MMPI), educational assessments, occupational selection testing, and clinical screening instruments. Practitioners who specialize in the field are called psychometricians.

Criticism

Psychometrics has been criticized for reifying latent constructs, for cultural and demographic bias in some tests, and for the social consequences of high-stakes testing. Debates continue over the interpretation of group differences, test fairness, and the validity of particular constructs.
