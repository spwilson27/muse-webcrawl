The Boeing EA-18G Growler is an American carrier-based electronic warfare aircraft, a specialized version of the two-seat F/A-18F Super Hornet. It replaced the Northrop Grumman EA-6B Prowler in service with the United States Navy, providing tactical jamming and electronic protection for strike aircraft and ground forces.

Design and capabilities

The Growler retains most of the Super Hornet's airframe and flight performance but replaces the internal cannon with electronic systems and adds wingtip receiver pods (ALQ-218) and underwing jamming pods (ALQ-99, being supplemented by the Next Generation Jammer, AN/ALQ-249). Its crew of two — a pilot and an electronic countermeasures officer — can detect, identify, and jam enemy radar and communications, and can attack radar sites with AGM-88 HARM anti-radiation missiles.

Operational history

The EA-18G first deployed in 2010 and saw its first combat use during Operation Odyssey Dawn over Libya in 2011, subsequently supporting operations in Iraq and Syria against the Islamic State and flying air-defense suppression missions in contested environments. The Royal Australian Air Force operates the Growler — the type's only export customer — from RAAF Base Amberley.
