Naropa (Prakrit: Naḍapāda; Sanskrit: Nāropā; Tibetan: ནཱ་རོ་པ་, 1016–1100) was an Indian Buddhist monk, scholar, and mahasiddha. A leading teacher at the great monastic university of Nalanda, he abandoned scholarship after a visionary encounter and became the foremost disciple of the mahasiddha Tilopa. He is best known as the compiler and namesake of the Six Dharmas of Naropa (often called the Six Yogas), a set of advanced tantric practices central to the Kagyu school of Tibetan Buddhism.

Life

Traditional biographies recount that Naropa, originally named Samantabhadra, rose to become a gatekeeper-scholar (pandita) of Nalanda's northern gate, famed for debating. A dakini appeared to him in the form of an old woman and showed him the limits of book learning, directing him to seek the teacher Tilopa. After finding Tilopa, Naropa endured the famous twelve great and twelve minor hardships — ordeals testing his devotion — before receiving the full transmission of the teachings.

Six Dharmas

The Six Dharmas comprise the yogas of inner heat (tummo), illusory body, dream, clear light, bardo (intermediate state), and transference of consciousness (phowa). Naropa transmitted them to his Tibetan disciple Marpa, from whom they passed to Milarepa and into the Kagyu lineages. Naropa University in Boulder, Colorado, founded by Chögyam Trungpa, is named in his honour.
