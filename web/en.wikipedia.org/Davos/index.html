Davos

Davos (Romansh: Tavau) is a town in the Swiss canton of the Grisons, located in the district of Prättigau/Davos at an elevation of about 1,560 metres (5,118 ft) — among the highest towns in Europe. It is internationally known as a ski resort and as the host of the annual meeting of the World Economic Forum.

History

The high valley was settled by Romansh speakers and, from the 13th century, by Walser German settlers whose dialect still predominates. In the 19th century Davos became a famous health resort: its dry, sunny mountain air was prescribed for tuberculosis patients, and sanatoria — later the setting of Thomas Mann's novel The Magic Mountain (1924) — attracted visitors from across Europe, including Robert Louis Stevenson, who completed Treasure Island there.

Tourism and sport

With the decline of the sanatorium era, Davos reinvented itself as a winter sports centre, with the Parsenn, Jakobshorn, Pischa, Madrisa, and Rinerhorn ski areas, and the natural ice rink that hosted early speed-skating championships. The town is home to HC Davos, one of Switzerland's most successful ice hockey clubs, which hosts the annual Spengler Cup, the world's oldest invitational ice hockey tournament, founded in 1923.

World Economic Forum

Since 1971 Davos has hosted the Annual Meeting of the World Economic Forum, founded by Klaus Schwab, which gathers political leaders, executives, and civil-society figures each January and has made "Davos" a byword for global elite gatherings — and a focus of anti-globalization protest.
