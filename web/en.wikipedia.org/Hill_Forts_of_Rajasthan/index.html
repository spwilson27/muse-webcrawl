Hill Forts of Rajasthan

The Hill Forts of Rajasthan are a group of six fortified sites in the Indian state of Rajasthan collectively inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2013. Built chiefly between the eighth and eighteenth centuries by the Rajput kingdoms, they exemplify the military architecture and court culture of medieval Rajasthan within the Aravalli mountain range.

Component forts

The serial nomination comprises Chittorgarh Fort, Kumbhalgarh Fort — whose perimeter wall is among the longest in the world — Ranthambore Fort, Gagron Fort, Amber Fort near Jaipur, and Jaisalmer Fort, one of the world's few living forts, still inhabited within its golden sandstone walls. Each fort combines massive defensive walls exploiting the hill terrain with palaces, temples, reservoirs, and urban settlements inside.

Significance

UNESCO recognised the forts as an outstanding example of Rajput military hill architecture, reflecting the power and courtly culture of the Rajput states that controlled trade routes across northwestern India. The sites incorporate elaborate water-harvesting systems suited to the arid climate, and their palaces display rich sculpture, painting, and mirror work. They remain major tourist attractions and venues for festivals such as the Jaisalmer Desert Festival.
