Privilegium Maius

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Privilegium Maius manuscript, Austrian National Library

The Privilegium Maius (Latin for "the greater privilege") was a set of medieval documents forged in 1358 or 1359 by Duke Rudolf IV of Austria of the House of Habsburg. It was a modified version of the Privilegium Minus issued by Emperor Frederick Barbarossa in 1156, which had raised the Margraviate of Austria to a duchy. The forged documents granted Austria and its rulers extraordinary rights and privileges within the Holy Roman Empire, including the hereditary title of Archduke for Habsburg rulers.

Contents

The forgery consisted of five documents purporting to date from Roman antiquity and the early Middle Ages, including fabricated privileges supposedly granted by Julius Caesar and Nero to the region of Austria. Among the rights claimed were that Austria was indivisible, that its ruler held the title of Palatine Archduke, ranked immediately after the prince-electors, could not be summoned before imperial courts, and that primogeniture governed the succession.

Exposure and recognition

The Italian scholar and poet Petrarch, consulted by Emperor Charles IV, denounced the documents as forgeries. Charles refused to confirm them, but Charles's successor Frederick III, himself a Habsburg, recognised the Privilegium Maius in 1453, giving the claimed privileges legal force within the Empire. The archducal title was thereafter borne by all Habsburg rulers, and the forged privileges shaped Austria's constitutional position for centuries.
