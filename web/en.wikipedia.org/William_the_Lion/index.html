William the Lion

William the Lion (Uilliam an Leòmhann)
Born: c. 1142
Died: 4 December 1214, Stirling
Reign: King of Scots, 1165–1214

William the Lion (Scottish Gaelic: Uilliam an Leòmhann; c. 1142 – 4 December 1214), sometimes styled William I, was King of Scots from 1165 to 1214, the second-longest reign in Scottish history before the Union of the Crowns. His reign saw both a humiliating subordination to England and, in later years, consolidation and development of the Scottish kingdom.

Early reign and the Treaty of Falaise

William succeeded his brother Malcolm IV in 1165. Determined to recover Northumbria, which his family had lost, he joined the revolt of Henry II's sons in 1173–74 and invaded northern England. In July 1174 he was captured at the Battle of Alnwick while besieging the castle. As the price of release he signed the Treaty of Falaise (1174), acknowledging Henry II as overlord of Scotland and surrendering key castles.

Treaty of Quitclaim and later reign

In 1189 Richard I, needing money for his crusade, released Scotland from the Falaise terms by the Quitclaim of Canterbury in exchange for 10,000 marks. William's later reign focused on internal consolidation: he suppressed revolts in Galloway and the north, founded the burgh and abbey landscape that shaped medieval Scotland, and established Arbroath Abbey (1178), dedicated to Thomas Becket. The lion rampant became associated with his royal arms, giving him his epithet and Scotland its enduring heraldic symbol.

William married Ermengarde de Beaumont in 1186; their son succeeded him as Alexander II. He died at Stirling in 1214 and was buried at Arbroath Abbey.
