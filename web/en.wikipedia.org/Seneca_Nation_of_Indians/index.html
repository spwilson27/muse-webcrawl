Seneca Nation of Indians

The Seneca Nation of Indians is a federally recognized Native American tribe in New York based in western New York. Based at the Cattaraugus Reservation, the Seneca Nation of Indians is one of two federally recognized Seneca tribes; the other is the Tonawanda Seneca Nation. The Seneca are one of the original five nations of the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois Confederacy), in which they are known as the "keepers of the western door".

The Seneca Nation administers five territories in western New York: the Cattaraugus, Allegany, and Oil Springs reservations, and the Niagara Falls and Buffalo territories held for gaming purposes. Enrolled membership is about 8,000 citizens.

History

The Seneca historically lived south of Lake Ontario, with villages along the Genesee River and westward. They were the most populous of the Five Nations and leading participants in the fur trade. After the American Revolutionary War—during which the Sullivan Expedition of 1779 destroyed Seneca towns—the Seneca ceded much of their land by treaties including the Treaty of Big Tree (1797), retaining reservations in western New York.

In 1848 the Seneca adopted a written constitution and an elective form of government, replacing rule by hereditary chiefs; a traditionalist faction later broke away to form the Tonawanda Band. In the 1960s the construction of the Kinzua Dam flooded over 10,000 acres of the Allegany Reservation despite the 1794 Treaty of Canandaigua's guarantee of the land, forcing the relocation of some 600 Seneca members.

Government

The Seneca Nation is governed by an elected president, treasurer, clerk, and a council of sixteen councillors, elected biennially by citizens of the nation, along with courts and a marshals service. The capital rotates between the Cattaraugus and Allegany territories.

Economy

The Nation operates gaming enterprises through Seneca Gaming Corporation, including Seneca Niagara Resort & Casino in Niagara Falls and Seneca Allegany Resort & Casino in Salamanca. It also has enterprises in fuel distribution, construction, and retail, and has litigated disputes with New York State over taxation and gaming compact payments.
