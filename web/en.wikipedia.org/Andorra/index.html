Andorra, officially the Principality of Andorra (Catalan: Principat d'Andorra), is a landlocked microstate in the eastern Pyrenees, bordered by France to the north and Spain to the south. It is one of Europe's smallest states, with an area of 468 square kilometres and a population of roughly 80,000. Its capital, Andorra la Vella, is the highest capital city in Europe, at about 1,023 metres above sea level.

History

Tradition credits Charlemagne with granting Andorra its independence; its political system took shape with the Paréages of 1278 and 1288, which established the unique co-principality: sovereignty is shared between the Bishop of Urgell (in Spain) and the head of the French state — today the President of France. This arrangement, one of the oldest surviving political systems in the world, endured for centuries while Andorra remained a rural, isolated valley community. The adoption of a constitution in 1993 established Andorra as a parliamentary democracy, with the co-princes remaining joint heads of state.

Geography

Andorra is entirely mountainous, comprising steep valleys of the Pyrenees; its highest point is Coma Pedrosa at 2,942 metres. The climate is alpine with snowy winters, making the principality a major ski destination, with resorts such as Grandvalira and Vallnord.

Economy and society

Tourism dominates the economy, accounting for about 80% of GDP; Andorra attracts some 8–10 million visitors a year, drawn by skiing and duty-free shopping. Banking and finance are also significant. Andorra has no income tax for most purposes historically and remains known for low taxes, though reforms since the 2010s introduced a modest corporate and income tax. Catalan is the sole official language — the only country where it is official nationwide — though Spanish, Portuguese and French are widely spoken. Andorra is not a member of the European Union but uses the euro by agreement.
