The 2021 Taliban offensive was a military campaign by the Taliban and allied groups against the government of Afghanistan that began on 1 May 2021, coinciding with the withdrawal of United States and NATO forces under the Doha Agreement timetable.

Course of the offensive

The Taliban first seized rural districts across the country, then, from 6 August, began capturing provincial capitals in rapid succession, including Zaranj, Kandahar, Herat, Mazar-i-Sharif and Jalalabad, often as government forces surrendered or withdrew. Afghan security forces, undermined by low morale, corruption, logistics failures and the loss of US air support, largely collapsed.

Fall of Kabul

On 15 August 2021 Taliban fighters entered Kabul; President Ashraf Ghani fled the country, and the Islamic Republic collapsed. Chaotic evacuation scenes followed at Hamid Karzai International Airport as tens of thousands of Afghans and foreign nationals sought to leave; a suicide bombing at the airport on 26 August killed around 170 Afghans and 13 US service members. The last US forces departed on 30 August, ending the twenty-year war. The Taliban declared the restored Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan.
