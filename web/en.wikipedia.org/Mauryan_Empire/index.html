Mauryan Empire

The Mauryan Empire (also spelled Maurya Empire) was the first great empire of ancient India, ruling from about 322 to 185 BCE. Founded by Chandragupta Maurya, it unified most of South Asia for the first time under a single administration.

Rulers

Its major emperors were Chandragupta (r. c. 321–297 BCE), Bindusara (r. c. 297–273 BCE), and Ashoka (r. c. 268–232 BCE), whose reign is remembered for the spread of Buddhism and the policy of dhamma.

Legacy

Mauryan art and architecture, including the polished sandstone pillars of Ashoka topped by animal capitals, influenced later Indian art. The lion capital at Sarnath became the national emblem of India, and the Ashoka Chakra appears on the Indian flag.
