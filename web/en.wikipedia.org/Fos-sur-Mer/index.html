Fos-sur-Mer

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fos-sur-Mer (Provençal: Fòs de Mar) is a commune in the Bouches-du-Rhône department in southern France, located about 50 km (31 mi) northwest of Marseille, at the eastern end of the Golfe de Fos on the Mediterranean coast, near the mouth of the Rhône. Its population is approximately 15,000.

Port and industry

Fos-sur-Mer is best known as the site of a vast deep-water port and industrial zone developed from the late 1960s as an extension of the Port of Marseille (today the Marseille-Fos port). The zone hosts container terminals, oil refineries and petrochemical plants, liquefied natural gas terminals (Fos Tonkin and Fos Cavaou), and steelworks, and is one of the largest port-industrial complexes in the Mediterranean. The port handles crude oil, containers, ore, and gas, and is a principal gateway for imports into France and southern Europe.

History and setting

The name derives from the Fossae Marianae, a canal dug by the Roman general Gaius Marius around 102 BC to bypass the silting Rhône delta. The old village, crowned by the medieval church of Saint-Sauveur on a rocky outcrop, overlooks the modern industrial plain and the Étang de Berre to the northeast. The surrounding Crau plain and Camargue wetlands contrast with the heavy industry, and the environmental and public-health effects of the industrial zone have been the subject of long-running local debate and study.

Sport

The commune is home to Fos Provence Basket, a professional basketball club, and to sporting facilities serving the western Marseille metropolitan area.
