Community service

Community service
Forms: court-ordered unpaid work; volunteering; school requirements
Purposes: rehabilitation, reparation, civic engagement

Community service is unpaid work performed by a person for the benefit of their local community. It may be voluntary, required by schools or employers, or imposed by courts as an alternative to, or as part of, a criminal sentence.

Court-ordered service

As a sentence, community service is intended to combine punishment with reparation and rehabilitation. Offenders perform work such as litter removal, maintenance of public spaces or assisting charities, supervised by probation services; in England and Wales it forms part of the community order as "unpaid work".

Voluntary and educational service

Many schools require students to complete service hours, and national programmes such as AmeriCorps in the United States organise longer-term service. Religious and civic traditions worldwide include communal labour obligations and volunteering.

Benefits and criticism

Research associates structured community service with lower reoffending and stronger civic engagement, though critics question coerced "volunteering" and the quality of some placements.
