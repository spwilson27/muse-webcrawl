Diego Rivera (December 8, 1886 – November 24, 1957) was a prominent Mexican painter whose large frescoes helped establish the Mexican muralism movement in Mexican art and influenced mural painting internationally.

Life and work

Trained in Mexico and Europe, Rivera spent a decade in Paris working in a Cubist idiom before returning to Mexico in 1921. There he painted sweeping murals on public buildings — including the National Palace in Mexico City — depicting Mexican history, indigenous culture, and social struggle from a Marxist perspective.

Work in the United States

Between 1930 and 1934 Rivera painted murals in San Francisco, Detroit, and New York. His Detroit Industry Murals are regarded as masterpieces. His Rockefeller Center mural, Man at the Crossroads, was destroyed in 1934 because it included a portrait of Lenin; he later repainted it in Mexico City as Man, Controller of the Universe.

Personal life

Rivera was married to the artist Frida Kahlo, with whom he shared a turbulent, famous relationship. His studio-house in San Ángel, designed by Juan O'Gorman, is now a museum.
