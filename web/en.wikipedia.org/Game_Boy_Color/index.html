The Game Boy Color (often abbreviated as GBC) is an 8-bit handheld game console developed by Nintendo and released in Japan on October 21, 1998, and to international markets that November. It is the successor to the original Game Boy and can display up to 56 colors at once from a palette of 32,768. The console is slightly thicker and taller than the Game Boy Pocket and features a faster processor and more memory.

Features

The GBC is fully backward compatible with the original Game Boy library, automatically colorizing older monochrome cartridges with selectable palettes — a first for a Nintendo handheld and a major selling point. New cartridges were produced in both dual-compatible and GBC-only formats, the latter in clear plastic shells. An infrared port enabled limited data exchange between systems in a handful of games.

Library and legacy

Signature titles include Pokémon Gold and Silver, The Legend of Zelda: Oracle of Ages and Oracle of Seasons, and the Game Boy Color edition of Tetris DX. Combined sales of the Game Boy and Game Boy Color reached 118.69 million units, making the line one of the best-selling console families ever. Production ended in 2003, two years after the Game Boy Advance launched.
