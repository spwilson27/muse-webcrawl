Keoladeo National Park
Location: Bharatpur, Rajasthan, India
Area: about 29 km²
Established: 1982 (national park)
Status: UNESCO World Heritage Site (1985); Ramsar site

Keoladeo National Park, formerly known as the Bharatpur Bird Sanctuary, is a famous avifauna sanctuary in Bharatpur, Rajasthan, India, that hosts thousands of birds, especially during the winter season. It is a man-made and man-managed wetland, created in the 18th and 19th centuries as a hunting reserve for the maharajas of Bharatpur, whose bunds and sluices flooded a natural depression to attract waterfowl.

History

The sanctuary takes its name from the Keoladeo (Shiva) temple within its boundaries. For over a century the marshes hosted extravagant duck shoots; a plaque in the park records bags from hunts attended by viceroys and princes. The area was declared a bird sanctuary in 1956, a national park in 1982, a Ramsar site, and in 1985 a UNESCO World Heritage Site for its importance to migratory birds on the Central Asian flyway.

Fauna and flora

Over 370 bird species have been recorded. Keoladeo was the only known Indian wintering ground of the critically endangered Siberian crane, which ceased to visit in the early 2000s. Heronries in the flooded acacia woodland host painted storks, spoonbills, ibises and egrets, while raptors, including greater spotted and imperial eagles, winter in large numbers. Mammals include nilgai, sambar, chital, wild boar, jungle cat and golden jackal; large Indian rock pythons sun themselves near the water in winter.

Management issues

Because the wetland is artificial, it depends on water releases from reservoirs and canals outside the park, and drought years have repeatedly sparked conflict with farmers over allocation. Grazing by village cattle was banned after the park's creation, which led to ecological changes in the grasslands, and the invasive mesquite Prosopis juliflora and water hyacinth require active control.
