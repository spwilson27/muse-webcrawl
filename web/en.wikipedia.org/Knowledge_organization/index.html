Knowledge organization


Knowledge organization is a field of library and information science.

Knowledge organization (KO) is a field of study concerned with the structure, processing and use of knowledge, particularly the organization of documents, document representations, works and concepts for retrieval. It is a central subject within library and information science and is closely connected with classification, indexing, cataloguing and bibliography.

Scope

Knowledge organization covers activities such as document description, indexing and classification performed in libraries, bibliographical databases, archives and museums, as well as the design of the systems used for those purposes — classification schemes such as the Dewey Decimal Classification and the Library of Congress Classification, subject heading lists, thesauri, taxonomies and ontologies.

The term was used by figures such as Henry Evelyn Bliss, whose works on the organization of knowledge in libraries appeared in the early 20th century. The professional society for the field, the International Society for Knowledge Organization (ISKO), was founded in 1989 and publishes the journal Knowledge Organization.

Approaches

Scholars distinguish traditional approaches centred on universal classification systems from user-oriented, cognitive and domain-analytic approaches, the latter associated with Birger Hjørland, which examine how knowledge is organized within particular scholarly disciplines and professional communities.
