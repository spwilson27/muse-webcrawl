Double eagle

The Saint-Gaudens double eagle, first struck in 1907

The double eagle is a gold coin of the United States with a denomination of $20. First authorized by the Coinage Act of 1849, regular production ran from 1850 until 1933, when the United States abandoned the gold standard. It was the largest denomination of United States coin struck for general circulation.

Types

The first type, the Liberty Head double eagle designed by James B. Longacre, was struck from 1850 to 1907. It was succeeded by the Saint-Gaudens double eagle (1907–1933), designed by Augustus Saint-Gaudens at the request of President Theodore Roosevelt and widely considered one of the most beautiful American coin designs.

The 1933 issue

Nearly half a million 1933 double eagles were minted but never officially issued after President Franklin D. Roosevelt ended gold coinage; all but a handful were melted. The few surviving examples are among the most valuable coins in the world: one specimen sold at auction in 2021 for $18.9 million, a record for a coin.

Modern issues

Since 1986 the United States Mint's American Gold Eagle bullion coins have revived the Saint-Gaudens design, and in 2009 the Mint issued an ultra-high-relief double eagle for collectors.
