Masnavi

The Masnavi, or Masnavi-ye-Ma'navi (Persian: مثنوی معنوی, "The Spiritual Couplets"), is an extensive poem written in Persian by Jalal al-Din Muhammad Rumi, also known as Rumi. It is one of the best known and most influential works of Sufism. The Masnavi is a series of six books of poetry that together amount to around 25,000 verses or 50,000 lines.

Content

Rumi began the Masnavi around 1258 at the request of his disciple Husam al-Din Chalabi and continued it until his death in 1273. The poem weaves together Quranic interpretation, fables, animal tales, anecdotes and profound theological and mystical teaching, aiming to instruct Sufi disciples in how the soul may reach union with God, the Beloved.

It opens with the famous "Song of the Reed", in which a reed flute cut from its reed bed laments its separation — a metaphor for the soul's longing to return to its source.

Significance

The Masnavi has been called "the Quran in the Persian tongue" by the poet Jami. It has shaped Persian, Turkish, Urdu and Pashto literature and remains widely read in Iran, Afghanistan, Tajikistan and South Asia. English translations include Reynold A. Nicholson's complete scholarly edition and popular versions by Coleman Barks.
