Dilemma

A dilemma is a problem offering two possibilities, neither of which is unambiguously acceptable or preferable. The possibilities are termed the horns of the dilemma, a clichéd expression being "on the horns of a dilemma". In classical rhetoric and logic, a dilemma is an argument presenting an opponent with two alternatives, both of which lead to a conclusion damaging to their position.

In logic

Logical dilemmas take constructive and destructive forms. A constructive dilemma infers a disjunction of consequents from a disjunction of antecedents and two conditionals; a destructive dilemma infers the negation of a disjunction of antecedents. Responding to a dilemma argument classically involves "grasping a horn" (denying one conditional), "escaping between the horns" (denying the disjunction by finding a third option), or constructing a counter-dilemma.

False dilemma

A false dilemma (false dichotomy) is a logical fallacy presenting only two options when others exist, or framing choices as mutually exclusive when they are not. It is common in rhetoric, politics and advertising.

Moral dilemmas

In ethics, a moral dilemma is a situation in which an agent must choose between conflicting moral requirements and cannot satisfy all of them. Philosophical thought experiments such as the trolley problem explore how people judge such cases. Related concepts include the prisoner's dilemma in game theory — a situation in which individually rational choices produce a collectively worse outcome — named for its framing as two prisoners separately deciding whether to betray each other.
