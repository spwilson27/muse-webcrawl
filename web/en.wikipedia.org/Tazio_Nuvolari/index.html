Tazio Nuvolari

Tazio Giorgio Nuvolari (16 November 1892 – 11 August 1953) was an Italian motorcycle and racing car driver, widely regarded as one of the greatest drivers of the pre-Formula One era. Known as Il Mantovano Volante ("The Flying Mantuan") and Nivola, he was revered for his extraordinary car control, daring overtaking, and ability to win in inferior machinery, and was champion of Europe in 1932.

Career

Nuvolari began racing motorcycles in 1920 before switching largely to cars in the later 1920s. With Alfa Romeo and Enzo Ferrari's Scuderia he won the Mille Miglia (1930, 1933), consecutive Targa Florios (1931, 1932), the 1932 European Championship with victories at Monza, Reims, and the Nürburgring, and the 1933 Le Mans 24 Hours with Raymond Sommer, driving the final stints with a leaking fuel tank plugged with chewing gum.

The 1935 German Grand Prix

His most celebrated victory came at the Nürburgring in 1935, where, before a crowd of Nazi officials, his outdated Alfa Romeo P3 — giving away some 75 horsepower to the Mercedes-Benz and Auto Union entries — beat nine German cars after a legendary final-lap pass of Manfred von Brauchitsch, whose tyre had burst. The organisers, expecting a German win, reportedly had no Italian anthem ready.

Nuvolari joined Auto Union in 1938, winning the Italian and Donington Grands Prix, and won the final pre-war Grand Prix at Belgrade in 1939. After the war, in failing health and driving with a blood-soaked handkerchief against respiratory problems, he finished a heroic second in the 1946 Milan Grand Prix and won at Albi. He died of a stroke in 1953; all of Mantua reportedly closed for his funeral, and his coffin was carried on a car chassis through streets lined with mourners.

Legacy

Ferdinand Porsche called Nuvolari "the greatest driver of the past, the present, and the future". His four-wheel-drift technique and never-say-die style made him Italy's first great sporting hero, and he remains the benchmark against whom pre-war drivers are measured.
