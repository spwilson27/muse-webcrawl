Tacrolimus

Tacrolimus (FK-506)
Class: Calcineurin inhibitor
Formula: C₄₄H₆₉NO₁₂
Uses: transplant immunosuppression, eczema
Trade name: Prograf, Protopic

Tacrolimus, sold under the brand name Prograf among others, is an immunosuppressive drug. It is used after organ transplantation to lower the risk of organ rejection, and in ointment form to treat moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis (eczema). It is also used for severe uveitis and other autoimmune conditions.

Tacrolimus is a macrolide calcineurin inhibitor discovered in 1984 in a Japanese soil sample containing the bacterium Streptomyces tsukubaensis. Like ciclosporin, it blocks calcineurin phosphatase activity in T cells, suppressing interleukin-2 production and T-cell activation — but it is substantially more potent by weight.

Side effects

Important adverse effects include kidney damage (nephrotoxicity), tremor and other neurotoxicity, high blood sugar and diabetes, hypertension, and increased susceptibility to infection and malignancy. Blood levels are monitored closely because the drug has a narrow therapeutic index and is metabolised by the liver enzyme CYP3A.
