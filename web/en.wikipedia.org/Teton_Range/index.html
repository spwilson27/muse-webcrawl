Teton Range

Teton Range
Location: Wyoming and Idaho, United States
Length: about 40 mi (64 km) N–S
Highest peak: Grand Teton, 13,775 ft (4,199 m)
Parent range: Rocky Mountains

The Teton Range is a mountain range of the Rocky Mountains in North America, located in northwestern Wyoming and extending slightly into Idaho. Stretching about 40 miles (64 km) from north to south, the range rises abruptly from the flat valley floor of Jackson Hole on its east, with no foothills, making it one of the most dramatic mountain fronts in the United States. Most of the range lies within Grand Teton National Park. Its highest summit is Grand Teton at 13,775 feet (4,199 m).

Geology

The Tetons are among the youngest mountain ranges in North America, uplifted beginning roughly 9–13 million years ago along the Teton fault, a normal fault on the range's eastern side. As the block west of the fault rose, the Jackson Hole block dropped, creating about 30,000 feet of vertical displacement in total; erosion and glaciation carved the jagged peaks from ancient Precambrian granite and gneiss nearly 2.7 billion years old — some of the oldest rock exposed in any American national park. Pleistocene glaciers sculpted U-shaped canyons and left moraine-dammed lakes such as Jenny Lake and Jackson Lake.

Peaks

The principal peaks, collectively called the Cathedral Group, include Grand Teton (13,775 ft), Mount Owen (12,928 ft), Teewinot Mountain (12,325 ft), Middle Teton (12,804 ft), and South Teton (12,514 ft); Mount Moran (12,605 ft) dominates the northern part of the range. The name "Teton" comes from French trappers' les trois tétons ("the three breasts/teats"), referring to the three prominent peaks seen from the west.

History and recreation

Indigenous peoples, including Shoshone groups, lived in and travelled through the region for thousands of years. Fur trappers and the Hayden expeditions of the 1870s — whose photographs by William Henry Jackson and paintings by Thomas Moran publicised the range — followed in the 19th century. Grand Teton was first climbed in 1898 (or possibly 1872, a disputed claim). The range is now a major centre for mountaineering, hiking, and skiing, with Jackson Hole Mountain Resort on its slopes; the Exum Mountain Guides, founded by Glenn Exum in the 1930s, pioneered American rock climbing instruction here.
