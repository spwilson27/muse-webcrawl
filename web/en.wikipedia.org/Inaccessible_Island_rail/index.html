The Inaccessible Island rail (Laterallus rogersi, formerly placed in the genus Atlantisia) is a small flightless bird of the rail family, Rallidae. Endemic to Inaccessible Island in the Tristan da Cunha archipelago in the remote South Atlantic, it is the smallest extant flightless bird in the world, measuring about 13 to 15.5 cm (5.1–6.1 in) and weighing around 34 to 49 g (1.2–1.7 oz).

The world's smallest flightless bird, found only on Inaccessible Island.

Description and behaviour

The rail has brown upperparts, dark grey underparts, a short black bill, and red eyes. It occupies most of the island's habitats, from tussock grassland to the plateau, feeding on invertebrates such as earthworms, moths, and berries and seeds. It breeds between October and January, laying two eggs. Its flightlessness is thought to have evolved in the absence of mammalian predators.

Taxonomy and conservation

The species was described by Percy Lowe in 1923. Genetic studies place it within the genus Laterallus, its ancestors presumably having flown from South America some 1.5 million years ago. The population is estimated in the thousands and considered stable, but the species is classified as Vulnerable because its entire range is a single small island; the accidental introduction of rats or other predators would be catastrophic.
