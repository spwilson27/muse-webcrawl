Quadripoint

A quadripoint is a point on Earth where four distinct political territories meet. The territories may be national or subnational. Quadripoints are rare: while many tripoints exist where three territories meet, borders rarely converge in a single point of four, and some apparent quadripoints turn out on closer inspection to be pairs of nearby tripoints.

Quadripoint
DefinitionPoint where four political territories meet
Famous exampleFour Corners (US states AZ, CO, NM, UT)
International exampleNear Kazungula, Zambezi River (disputed)

Examples

The best-known quadripoint is the Four Corners in the United States, where Arizona, Colorado, New Mexico, and Utah meet at a single point marked by a monument — the only such point between US states. Internationally, a short boundary segment in the Zambezi River near Kazungula separates Botswana, Namibia, Zambia, and Zimbabwe; whether the four meet at a true single quadripoint or at two tripoints about 150 metres apart has long been disputed.

Historical quadripoints

Historically, Moresnet, the Netherlands, Belgium, and Prussia met at a quadripoint in the 19th century, and other former quadripoints have disappeared with border changes in post-colonial Africa and elsewhere.
