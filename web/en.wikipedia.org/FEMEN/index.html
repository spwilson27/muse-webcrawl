FEMEN

FEMEN (Ukrainian: Фемен) is a radical feminist activist group founded in Kyiv, Ukraine, in 2008 by Anna Hutsol, together with Oksana Shachko and Alexandra Shevchenko. The group became internationally known for staging topless protests — with slogans painted on their bodies and flower wreaths in their hair — against sex tourism, religious institutions, sexism, homophobia and other social and political targets.

History and tactics

FEMEN's stated ideology combines atheism, feminism and opposition to the sex industry, which the group describes as "sextremism". Its protests, designed to attract media attention, have targeted events and figures including the Vatican, the Euro 2012 football championship, Vladimir Putin, Silvio Berlusconi and the Tunisian government. Activist Amina Tyler's case in Tunisia in 2013 prompted an international "Topless Jihad Day" of solidarity protests.

Organisation and controversies

The movement established branches in several countries, notably France, where co-founder Inna Shevchenko settled after fleeing Ukraine in 2012; her likeness was used for a French postage stamp of Marianne in 2013. The group has drawn criticism from other feminists for its confrontational style, its protests at religious sites, and — following a 2013 documentary — allegations that male consultant Victor Svyatski had exercised manipulative influence over the group, which FEMEN members disputed in part. Oksana Shachko died in Paris in 2018. Activity declined in the late 2010s, though branches continued occasional actions.
