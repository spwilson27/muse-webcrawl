France and weapons of mass destruction

France is one of the five nuclear-weapon states recognized under the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons (NPT). It developed an independent nuclear deterrent, the force de frappe, beginning in the late 1950s, and conducted its first nuclear test, Gerboise bleue, on 13 February 1960 in the Sahara Desert in Algeria.

Nuclear programme

Under President Charles de Gaulle, France built a full nuclear triad of land-based missiles, submarine-launched ballistic missiles, and air-delivered weapons. France conducted 210 nuclear tests between 1960 and 1996, first in Algeria and then at the Moruroa and Fangataufa atolls in French Polynesia. The final test series in 1995–96, ordered by President Jacques Chirac, drew international protest; France then signed the Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty in 1996. France dismantled its land-based missiles on the Plateau d'Albion and now maintains a dyad of ballistic missile submarines (the Triomphant class) and air-launched cruise missiles (ASMPA), with an arsenal estimated at roughly 290 warheads.

Chemical and biological weapons

France used chemical weapons during the First World War and maintained stockpiles into the Cold War era, but it ratified the Geneva Protocol in 1926, the Biological Weapons Convention in 1984, and the Chemical Weapons Convention in 1993, under which its declared chemical stockpiles were destroyed. France acceded to the NPT in 1992, later than the other recognized nuclear-weapon states.
