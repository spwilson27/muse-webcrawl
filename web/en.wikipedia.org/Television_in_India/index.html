Television in India

The television industry in India is very diverse and produces thousands of programs in many of India's official languages. Television in India began experimentally in 1959 in New Delhi, and regular daily transmission started in 1965 as part of All India Radio. The state-owned broadcaster Doordarshan held a monopoly until the economic liberalization of the early 1990s opened the market to private satellite channels.

History

Doordarshan expanded rapidly in the 1980s, aided by the INSAT satellite system and the broadcast of popular serials such as Hum Log, Ramayan, and Mahabharat, which drew enormous audiences. The arrival of CNN coverage of the Gulf War in 1991 spurred the growth of satellite television, and channels such as Zee TV, Star Plus, and Sun TV emerged.

Broadcasting today

India has several hundred television channels, broadcasting news, entertainment, sports, and regional-language programming. Direct-to-home (DTH) satellite services and cable networks reach hundreds of millions of viewers, while streaming platforms have grown rapidly since the mid-2010s. Cricket broadcasts are among the most watched television events in the country.

Regulation

The Ministry of Information and Broadcasting oversees television policy, and the Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI) regulates aspects of broadcasting and cable distribution.
