Giant lock

Oversized locks appear as votive objects and as symbolic grave goods in several traditions.

A giant lock is an oversized functional or ceremonial lock, far larger than required for ordinary use. Giant locks are found in several contexts: as masterpieces demonstrating a locksmith's skill, as votive offerings hung at shrines, as symbols of security displayed by lock-making towns and firms, and as advertising landmarks.

Craft and guild traditions

In European guild tradition, a journeyman locksmith might produce a masterwork (Meisterstück) to qualify as a master; ambitious examples included very large and elaborately ornamented locks displaying multiple mechanisms in a single case. Such locks survive in museum collections as demonstrations of pre-industrial metalworking skill.

Religious and folk uses

At shrines in Japan and elsewhere in Asia, worshippers hang locks — sometimes very large ones — as offerings symbolising the securing of a wish, fidelity, or protection. In parts of Central and West Africa, large locks have been placed on tombs and shrines as symbols that the dead, or a secret, is sealed away.

Records and landmarks

Manufacturers and towns periodically commission record-breaking locks for publicity. Among the best known are the enormous working padlocks displayed in lock-making centres such as Willenhall in England, historically the centre of the British lock trade.
