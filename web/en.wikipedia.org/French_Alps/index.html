French Alps

The French Alps are the portion of the Alps mountain range that stands within France, located in the regions of Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes and Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur. While some of the ranges of the French Alps are entirely within France, others, such as the Mont Blanc massif, are shared with Switzerland and Italy. At 4,808 metres, Mont Blanc, on the French-Italian border, is the highest mountain in the Alps and in Western Europe.

Geography

The French Alps stretch from Lake Geneva in the north to the Mediterranean coast near Nice in the south. Major massifs include the Mont Blanc massif, the Écrins, the Vanoise, the Queyras, and the Mercantour. Important rivers rising in the region include the Rhône, Isère, Durance, and Arc. Large glacial lakes such as Lake Annecy and Lake Bourget lie at the mountain front.

The range is generally divided into the Northern Alps, with higher, glaciated peaks and a wetter climate, and the Southern Alps, which are lower and drier under Mediterranean influence.

Tourism and sport

The French Alps are one of the world's leading destinations for skiing and mountaineering. Chamonix, at the foot of Mont Blanc, hosted the first Winter Olympic Games in 1924; Grenoble (1968) and Albertville (1992) also hosted Winter Olympics. Famous ski resorts include Val d'Isère, Courchevel, Méribel, Les Deux Alpes, and Alpe d'Huez, the last also renowned as a Tour de France cycling climb. In summer the region attracts hikers, climbers, and paragliders.

Environment

Protected areas include the Vanoise, Écrins, and Mercantour national parks, home to alpine ibex, chamois, marmots, and bearded vultures. The glaciers of the French Alps have retreated markedly under climate change, affecting water supplies and winter tourism.
