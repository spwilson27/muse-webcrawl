Gingerbread house

A gingerbread house is a novelty confectionery building made of gingerbread — cookie dough baked in flat pieces and assembled with icing or melted sugar — and decorated with candies, chocolate, and royal icing. Gingerbread houses are a popular Christmas tradition in many countries, particularly in Germany, Scandinavia, and North America.

History

Gingerbread has been baked in Europe since the Middle Ages, and decorated gingerbread figures and models were made by specialist bakers' guilds in Germany. The tradition of gingerbread houses is often linked to the Brothers Grimm fairy tale Hansel and Gretel (1812), in which the children find a house made of bread and sweets in the forest; after the tale's publication, German bakers began making small decorated houses known as Lebkuchenhaus or Hexenhaus ("witch's house"). German immigrants carried the custom to the United States, where it became widespread.

Construction and traditions

House pieces are cut from rolled dough before or after baking, joined with stiff royal icing, and decorated to imitate roofs, windows, chimneys, and snowy landscapes. Ready-made kits are widely sold. Gingerbread-house decorating is a common family and school activity during the Christmas season, and competitions are held for elaborate creations; in Norway, the city of Bergen builds "Pepperkakebyen", a large miniature town of gingerbread houses, each year. Some bakers have set records for the largest gingerbread houses, large enough to walk through.
