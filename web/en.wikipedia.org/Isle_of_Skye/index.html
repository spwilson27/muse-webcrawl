The Isle of Skye (Scottish Gaelic: An t-Eilean Sgitheanach), commonly known as Skye, is the largest and northernmost of the major islands in the Inner Hebrides of Scotland. Its peninsulas radiate from a mountainous centre dominated by the Cuillin range, the rocky slopes of which provide some of the most dramatic mountain scenery in the country. The island has been inhabited since the Mesolithic period and has a rich history steeped in Norse and clan traditions.

Geography

Skye is about 80 kilometres (50 mi) long and up to 40 kilometres (25 mi) wide; its deeply indented coastline means no point is more than about 8 kilometres from the sea. The Black Cuillin, formed of gabbro and basalt, include twelve Munros and present some of Britain's most challenging climbing; the Red Cuillin to the east are lower, more rounded granite hills. In the north, the Trotternish peninsula hosts the island's most famous landscapes: the landslip formations of the Quiraing, the pinnacle of the Old Man of Storr, and Kilt Rock. The capital and largest settlement is Portree, with a picturesque harbour.

History

Skye was inhabited by Norse settlers from the 8th century, and Norse influence persists in many place names. In the later Middle Ages the island was divided between Clan MacLeod, based at Dunvegan Castle — among the oldest continuously inhabited castles in Scotland — and Clan MacDonald of Sleat. After the Jacobite rising of 1745, in which Skye's clans played a mixed role, Flora MacDonald famously helped the fugitive Charles Edward Stuart ("Bonnie Prince Charlie") escape to Skye from Benbecula in 1746, disguised as her maid — the episode immortalised in "The Skye Boat Song". The 19th century brought clearances and mass emigration, reducing the population from over 23,000 in the early 1800s to under 8,000 by the 1970s; since then the population has recovered to around 10,000.

Economy and culture

Tourism, crofting, fishing, and whisky are central to the economy. Talisker distillery at Carbost (founded 1830) is the island's oldest working distillery, and Torabhaig opened in 2017. Skye is a stronghold of Scottish Gaelic, spoken by a significant minority, and hosts Sabhal Mòr Ostaig, the Gaelic college of the University of the Highlands and Islands. The island is connected to the mainland by the Skye Bridge, opened in 1995, replacing the ferry from Kyle of Lochalsh to Kyleakin. Ferries also run from Armadale to Mallaig and from Uig to the Outer Hebrides.

Climate and wildlife

Skye has a mild, wet maritime climate influenced by the Atlantic. Wildlife includes golden eagles and white-tailed eagles, red deer, otters, and seals; the surrounding waters host basking sharks and dolphins in summer.
