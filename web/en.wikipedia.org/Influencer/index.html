Influencer

An influencer is a person who has built a reputation for their knowledge and expertise on a specific topic and uses social media or other online platforms to influence the purchasing decisions or opinions of their audience. Influencers regularly post content to a substantial following and are able to sway consumer behaviour through their perceived authenticity and relationship with their audience.

The rise of influencer marketing in the 2010s and 2020s transformed influencers into a significant feature of digital advertising. Brands collaborate with influencers to promote products through sponsored posts, reviews, and endorsements, often disclosing the commercial relationship in accordance with advertising regulations.

History

While celebrity endorsement predates the internet by decades, the modern influencer emerged with blogging and, later, platforms such as YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, and Twitch. Early "mommy bloggers" and YouTube creators demonstrated that non-celebrities could amass audiences rivalling traditional media outlets. By the late 2010s, influencer marketing had grown into a multibillion-dollar industry, with talent agencies, multi-channel networks, and analytics platforms supporting the ecosystem.

Categories

Influencers are commonly classified by follower count: nano-influencers (1,000–10,000 followers), micro-influencers (10,000–100,000), macro-influencers (100,000–1 million), and mega-influencers (over 1 million). They may also be categorised by niche, including beauty, fashion, gaming, fitness, travel, food, and finance. "Virtual influencers" — computer-generated characters such as Lil Miquela — constitute a further category.

Influencer marketing

Influencer marketing is a form of social media marketing involving endorsements and product placement from influencers. Marketers value influencers for their engagement rates and access to niche demographics. Regulators in several countries require disclosure of paid partnerships, typically with labels such as "#ad" or platform-provided "paid partnership" tags.

Criticism

Critics have raised concerns about deceptive advertising, the promotion of unrealistic lifestyles, undisclosed sponsorships, purchased followers, and the psychological effects of influencer culture on young audiences. High-profile failures, such as the fraudulent Fyre Festival of 2017, which was promoted heavily by influencers, drew widespread attention to the risks of the practice.
