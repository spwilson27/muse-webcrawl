Audrey Hepburn

Born: Audrey Kathleen Ruston, 4 May 1929, Ixelles, Brussels, Belgium

Died: 20 January 1993 (aged 63), Tolochenaz, Switzerland

Occupation: Actress, humanitarian

Years active: 1948–1989

Audrey Hepburn (born Audrey Kathleen Ruston; 4 May 1929 – 20 January 1993) was a British actress. Recognised as a film and fashion icon, she was ranked by the American Film Institute as the third-greatest female screen legend from the Classical Hollywood cinema. Born in Ixelles, Brussels, Hepburn spent parts of her youth in Belgium, England and the Netherlands, where she experienced the hardships of the German occupation during the Second World War.

Career

Hepburn's breakthrough came with Roman Holiday (1953), for which she won the Academy Award for Best Actress — the first actress to win an Academy Award, a Golden Globe Award and a BAFTA Award for a single performance. She went on to star in Sabrina (1954), Funny Face (1957), The Nun's Story (1959), Breakfast at Tiffany's (1961), in which her portrayal of Holly Golightly became iconic, Charade (1963), My Fair Lady (1964), Wait Until Dark (1967) and Robin and Marian (1976).

Humanitarian work

From the late 1960s Hepburn acted only occasionally, devoting much of her later life to UNICEF, for which she had contributed since 1954. She was appointed a Goodwill Ambassador in 1989 and travelled to some of the world's poorest communities. She received the United States Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1992 and died of appendiceal cancer at her home in Switzerland in January 1993.

Legacy

Hepburn remains one of the few performers to have won Academy, Emmy, Grammy and Tony Awards (EGOT). Her style — epitomised by the little black dress designed by Hubert de Givenchy for Breakfast at Tiffany's — continues to influence fashion.
