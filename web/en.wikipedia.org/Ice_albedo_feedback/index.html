Ice albedo feedback

Ice albedo feedback
[Image: Diagram showing how retreating sea ice exposes dark ocean water, increasing absorption of sunlight]
TypePositive climate feedback
MechanismLoss of reflective ice and snow cover lowers planetary albedo, increasing absorbed solar radiation

Ice albedo feedback is a positive feedback climate process in which a change in the area of ice caps, glaciers, and sea ice alters the albedo (surface reflectivity) of a planet or region. Ice and snow are bright and reflect most incoming sunlight back to space, whereas the land and open water exposed when ice melts are much darker and absorb more solar energy.

Mechanism

When temperatures rise, ice and snow melt, exposing darker surfaces beneath. The darker surface absorbs more sunlight, warming the surroundings further, which melts still more ice. The cycle therefore amplifies the initial warming. The feedback also operates in reverse: cooling promotes ice growth, which raises albedo and amplifies the cooling. Fresh snow has an albedo of up to about 0.9, sea ice roughly 0.5–0.7, while open ocean can be as low as 0.06, so the contrast between frozen and unfrozen surfaces is large.

Role in climate change

The ice albedo feedback is a major contributor to Arctic amplification, the phenomenon by which the Arctic warms several times faster than the global average. Declining Arctic sea ice since the late 1970s has been accompanied by reduced snow cover on northern land areas, both of which reduce the region's reflectivity. On longer timescales the feedback helped drive the great ice-age cycles, and runaway versions of it have been invoked in "Snowball Earth" hypotheses for severe glaciations in the distant past.
