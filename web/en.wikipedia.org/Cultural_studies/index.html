Cultural studies

Cultural studies is a politically engaged, post-disciplinary academic field that explores the dynamics of contemporary culture and its social and historical foundations. Cultural studies researchers generally investigate how cultural practices relate to wider systems of power associated with social class, race, national identity, ethnicity, sexuality, and gender. The field combines feminist theory, social theory, political theory, history, philosophy, literary theory, media theory, film studies, communication studies, political economy, and anthropology.

History

The field emerged in Britain in the 1950s and 1960s with the work of Richard Hoggart (The Uses of Literacy, 1957), Raymond Williams (Culture and Society, 1958), and E. P. Thompson. In 1964 Hoggart founded the Centre for Contemporary Cultural Studies (CCCS) at the University of Birmingham, and under Stuart Hall's directorship from 1969 the centre became the intellectual home of the "Birmingham School." Hall's work on encoding and decoding in media discourse, and the CCCS studies of youth subcultures such as Resistance Through Rituals (1976), defined the field's approach of taking popular culture seriously as a site of ideological struggle and hegemonic negotiation.

Themes and methods

Key themes include hegemony (drawing on Antonio Gramsci), ideology, representation, audience reception, subcultures, and the politics of identity. Cultural studies scholars use methods including textual analysis, ethnography, and historical research, and they analyze phenomena ranging from advertising and television to fashion, fandom, and everyday consumption. From the 1980s the field spread internationally — to the United States, Australia, and beyond — absorbing influences from poststructuralism, postcolonial theory, and globalization studies.

Criticism

Critics have charged cultural studies with political bias, jargon, and a celebratory attitude toward popular culture that neglects political economy. Others defend it as a vital interdisciplinary project linking the study of culture to questions of power and social change.
