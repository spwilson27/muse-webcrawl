William McKinley

William McKinley
Born: January 29, 1843, Niles, Ohio
Died: September 14, 1901, Buffalo, New York
Office: 25th President of the United States (1897–1901)

William McKinley (January 29, 1843 – September 14, 1901) was the 25th president of the United States, serving from 1897 until his assassination in 1901. A Republican, he led the nation to victory in the Spanish–American War, raised protective tariffs to promote American industry, and maintained the gold standard.

Early life and career

McKinley was born in Niles, Ohio, and was the last president to serve in the Civil War, rising from private to brevet major in the Union Army. He practised law in Canton, Ohio, served in Congress (1877–1891), where he authored the McKinley Tariff of 1890, and was Governor of Ohio from 1892 to 1896.

Presidency

McKinley defeated William Jennings Bryan in the landmark 1896 election, realigning American politics around industrialisation, sound money, and protectionism. In 1898 the United States declared war on Spain; the Treaty of Paris ceded Puerto Rico, Guam, and the Philippines to the United States and set Cuba on the path to independence, while Hawaii was annexed the same year. McKinley was re-elected in 1900, again defeating Bryan.

Assassination

On September 6, 1901, while attending the Pan-American Exposition in Buffalo, New York, McKinley was shot by Leon Czolgosz, an anarchist. He died of gangrene from his wounds on September 14 and was succeeded by Vice President Theodore Roosevelt. North America's highest peak bore his name as Mount McKinley from 1917 until it was restored to Denali in 2015 (a renaming revisited by executive order in 2025).
