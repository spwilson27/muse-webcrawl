Sketchpad

Sketchpad
DeveloperIvan Sutherland
Released1963
PlatformTX-2 (MIT Lincoln Laboratory)
TypeInteractive computer graphics / CAD
SignificanceFirst graphical user interface program; ancestor of CAD and OOP concepts

Sketchpad was a revolutionary computer program written by Ivan Sutherland in 1963 as part of his PhD thesis at MIT. It is widely regarded as the ancestor of modern computer-aided design (CAD) programs and a major breakthrough in the development of computer graphics and graphical user interfaces.

Running on the TX-2 computer at Lincoln Laboratory, Sketchpad allowed a user to draw directly on a CRT display with a light pen, manipulate the drawing, and impose geometric constraints — for example, that two lines be parallel, or that a point lie exactly on a circle — which the system then maintained automatically. It also introduced the ideas of "master" drawings and "instances", an early form of the object-and-instance model later used in object-oriented programming, and of direct manipulation with zooming and windowing.

Influence

Sketchpad influenced Douglas Engelbart's oN-Line System, Alan Kay's work on the graphical user interface and object-oriented programming, and the development of CAD software used in engineering and architecture. Sutherland received the ACM Turing Award in 1988 for Sketchpad and his other contributions to computer graphics.
