Change to Win Federation


The Change to Win Federation, a US labor federation founded in 2005.

The Change to Win Federation (CtW) is an American labor federation founded in 2005 by unions that broke away from the AFL–CIO. Its founding members argued that the American labor movement should devote far more resources to organizing new members rather than to electoral politics. At its founding the federation represented about six million workers through seven unions, including the Service Employees International Union (SEIU), the Teamsters, the United Food and Commercial Workers, UNITE HERE, the Laborers, the Carpenters and the United Farm Workers.

Founding

Change to Win grew out of the New Unity Partnership, a reform caucus formed in 2003. Frustrated by declining union density and by the AFL–CIO leadership's spending priorities, the dissident unions announced their own federation in June 2005 and formally disaffiliated around the AFL–CIO's convention that July. Anna Burger of the SEIU served as its first chair.

Strategy

The federation promoted industry-wide organizing campaigns, coordinated bargaining and a focus on large service-sector employers. Its member unions were involved in prominent campaigns to organize janitors, hotel workers, warehouse employees and security guards.

Later developments

Several founding unions later departed or returned to the AFL–CIO: the Carpenters had already left the older federation's orbit earlier, the Laborers rejoined the AFL–CIO in 2010, UNITE HERE's factions reconciled in 2009, and the UFCW returned in 2013. The federation nonetheless continued to operate with core affiliates including the SEIU and the Teamsters, and in 2022 the Teamsters also rejoined the AFL–CIO. Change to Win maintained its emphasis on strategic research and corporate campaigns through its strategic organizing center.
