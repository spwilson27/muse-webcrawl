Insurrection

An insurrection is a violent uprising against an authority or government. It is generally a limited and lesser form of rebellion: while a rebellion may seek the overthrow of a government or authority, an insurrection may be more localised or spontaneous and does not necessarily aim at complete regime change. The term has specific meanings in law in several countries.

Legal usage

In United States law, insurrection is a federal crime under 18 U.S.C. § 2383, punishing anyone who incites, sets on foot, assists, or engages in rebellion or insurrection against the authority of the United States. The Insurrection Act of 1807 authorises the President to deploy the military within the United States in certain circumstances to suppress insurrection and enforce the law.

Historical examples

Notable insurrections include the Whiskey Rebellion (1791–1794) in the United States, the Paris Commune of 1871, the Easter Rising of 1916 in Ireland, and the Warsaw Uprising of 1944. The storming of the United States Capitol on 6 January 2021 has been widely described as an insurrection, and some participants were convicted of seditious conspiracy.
