The Golden Gate Bridge is a suspension bridge spanning the Golden Gate, the one-mile-wide (1.6 km) strait connecting San Francisco Bay and the Pacific Ocean. It links the city of San Francisco, California, with Marin County, carrying both U.S. Route 101 and California State Route 1 across the strait. It is one of the most internationally recognized symbols of San Francisco and of the United States, and has been declared one of the Wonders of the Modern World by the American Society of Civil Engineers.

History

Before the bridge, the only practical route between San Francisco and Marin County was by ferry across the bay. A bridge had been proposed as early as 1872, but strong currents, deep water, frequent fog, and powerful winds made the strait seem impossible to span. In 1923 the California legislature created the Golden Gate Bridge and Highway District to design and build the bridge. Chief engineer Joseph B. Strauss, working with engineers including Charles Alton Ellis and Leon Moisseiff, developed a suspension design suited to the site.

Construction began on January 5, 1933, during the Great Depression. The project was completed ahead of schedule and under its $35 million budget. A pioneering safety achievement, the bridge used safety netting that saved the lives of 19 workers, who formed the "Half-Way-to-Hell Club". Eleven men died during construction. The bridge opened to pedestrians on May 27, 1937, and to vehicles the following day.

Design

The bridge's main span of 4,200 feet (1,280 m) was the longest suspension bridge main span in the world until 1964. Its two towers rise 746 feet (227 m) above the water. The distinctive color, International Orange, was chosen partly for visibility in the strait's frequent fog and partly for its harmony with the surrounding landscape; it was originally intended as only a primer coat. The Art Deco detailing was designed by Irving Morrow.

Traffic and tolls

The bridge carries roughly 100,000 vehicles per day. Tolls are collected only in the southbound direction, into San Francisco, and since 2013 tolling has been fully electronic. Pedestrians may cross on the east sidewalk, and cyclists use both sidewalks at designated times.

Suicide barrier

The Golden Gate Bridge has long been a frequent site for suicides. After decades of debate, a steel net suicide deterrent extending 20 feet out from the walkway on both sides was completed in January 2024.
