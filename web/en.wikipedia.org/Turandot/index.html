Turandot is an opera in three acts by Giacomo Puccini, completed posthumously by Franco Alfano, with an Italian libretto by Giuseppe Adami and Renato Simoni. Set in Peking (Beijing), China, it tells of the icy Princess Turandot, who will marry only a suitor who can answer her three riddles—and has all who fail executed. The opera contains one of the most famous tenor arias ever written, "Nessun dorma".

History

The story derives from Carlo Gozzi's 1762 play, itself drawn from the Persian collection of tales The Book of One Thousand and One Days; the name Turandot means "daughter of Turan". Puccini worked on the opera from 1920 but died in 1924 leaving the final duet unfinished; Alfano completed it from Puccini's sketches. At the premiere at La Scala on 25 April 1926, conductor Arturo Toscanini laid down his baton at the point where Puccini's music ends, saying, "Here the opera ends, because at this point the maestro died." Later performances use the Alfano ending.

Synopsis

Prince Calaf, seeing Turandot, falls in love and strikes the gong to announce himself as a suitor, despite the pleas of his father Timur and the slave girl Liù, who loves him. He answers the three riddles correctly. Turandot recoils, so Calaf offers her a riddle in return: discover his name by dawn and he will die. Turandot decrees that no one in Peking shall sleep ("Nessun dorma" — "None shall sleep") until the name is found. Liù, tortured, kills herself rather than reveal it. Her death melts Turandot's heart; at dawn she announces that the stranger's name is "Love", and the two are united.

Music

Puccini's score draws on Chinese musical themes, including the folk melody "Mòlìhuā" (Jasmine Flower), and demands exceptionally large orchestral and choral forces and a dramatic soprano of unusual power in the title role.
