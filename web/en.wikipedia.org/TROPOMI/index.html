TROPOMI (the Tropospheric Monitoring Instrument) is a satellite spectrometer aboard the European Space Agency's Sentinel-5 Precursor satellite, part of the European Union's Copernicus programme. It measures key atmospheric trace gases and aerosols relevant to air quality, climate, and the ozone layer.

Instrument

TROPOMI is a push-broom imaging spectrometer developed in the Netherlands under the responsibility of the Netherlands Space Office (NSO), with Airbus Defence and Space Netherlands as prime contractor. It observes in ultraviolet, visible, near-infrared, and shortwave-infrared bands, imaging a swath about 2,600 km wide that gives near-daily global coverage at a spatial resolution of a few kilometres.

Measurements

The instrument retrieves concentrations of nitrogen dioxide, ozone, formaldehyde, sulfur dioxide, methane, carbon monoxide, and aerosols, as well as cloud properties. Its fine resolution allows individual pollution sources such as cities, industrial complexes, and ships to be distinguished. During the COVID-19 lockdowns of 2020, TROPOMI data were widely used to document sharp reductions in nitrogen dioxide emissions, and the instrument has also been used to detect large methane leaks from oil and gas infrastructure.

Mission

Sentinel-5 Precursor was launched on 13 October 2017 from Plesetsk Cosmodrome. TROPOMI bridges the gap between the earlier OMI instrument on NASA's Aura satellite and the future Sentinel-5 instrument on Metop-SG satellites.
