Taipa

Taipa (Chinese: 氹仔; Cantonese: Tahm Jai) is an island in Macau, a special administrative region of China, located south of the Macau Peninsula and connected to it by three bridges. Historically a quiet island of villages, duck farms, and firecracker factories, Taipa has been transformed by land reclamation and the development of the Cotai casino strip, which physically joined Taipa to the island of Coloane.

History

Taipa was settled by Chinese fishermen and farmers and came under Portuguese administration in the 19th century; it long retained a village character of shipyards, firecracker workshops, and the church of Our Lady of Carmel (1885). The Governor Nobre de Carvalho Bridge (1974) first linked Taipa to the peninsula, followed by the Friendship Bridge (1994) and the Sai Van Bridge (2004). Macau International Airport, opened in 1995 partly on reclaimed land and a runway strip built on piles in the sea, is located at Taipa's eastern end.

Development

Reclamation of the channel between Taipa and Coloane created Cotai, home since 2007 to mega-resorts such as The Venetian Macao. Away from the strip, Taipa Village preserves Portuguese colonial houses, the Taipa Houses Museum, and a food street on Rua do Cunha. The island also hosts the Macau Jockey Club, the Macau Stadium, and campuses including the Macau University of Science and Technology. Taipa is served by the Macau Light Rapid Transit (opened 2019).
