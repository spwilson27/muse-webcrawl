3GPP2

The Third Generation Partnership Project 2 (3GPP2) was a standards organisation established in December 1998 to develop third-generation (3G) mobile telephony specifications based on the CDMA2000 family of standards, which evolved from the cdmaOne (IS-95) technology. It was the counterpart to the 3GPP, which developed the UMTS/W-CDMA standards from GSM.

Membership and scope

3GPP2 brought together five regional standards development organisations — including ARIB and TTC (Japan), CCSA (China), TIA (North America), and TTA (South Korea) — along with industry members such as Qualcomm, Motorola, and Ericsson. Its specifications covered the cdma2000 1x radio interface, the EV-DO (Evolution-Data Optimized) packet data standards, and their network architecture, as well as the short-lived Ultra Mobile Broadband (UMB) proposal for a fourth-generation system.

Decline

As operators worldwide converged on LTE, developed within 3GPP, as the single 4G standard, major CDMA2000 operators announced migrations to LTE and work on UMB was abandoned in favour of LTE. Development activity in 3GPP2 wound down through the 2010s, and CDMA2000 networks themselves were progressively shut down in the early 2020s as subscribers moved to LTE and 5G services.
