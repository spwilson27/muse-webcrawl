František Palacký (14 June 1798 – 26 May 1876) was a Czech historian and politician, the most influential figure of the Czech National Revival. His monumental history of the Czech lands reshaped Czech national identity, and he is honoured with the title "Father of the Nation" (Otec národa).

Life and work

Palacký was born in Hodslavice, Moravia, the son of a schoolteacher from a family of the secret Protestant tradition. Educated in Pressburg (Bratislava), he settled in Prague in 1823 as archivist and historian to the noble Sternberg family. In 1827 he founded the journal of the Bohemian Museum, and he campaigned for a bilingual Czech-German scholarly culture in Bohemia.

His life's work, the five-volume History of the Czech Nation in Bohemia and Moravia (published in German from 1836 and in Czech from 1848), presented Czech history as a long encounter and conflict between Slavs and Germans, with the Hussite movement as its moral centre.

Politics

In the revolutionary year 1848 Palacký famously declined an invitation to the Frankfurt Parliament, arguing in his open letter that the Czechs belonged with a reformed Austrian state — the doctrine known as Austroslavism, summed up in his remark that if Austria did not exist, it would have to be created in the interest of Europe. He presided over the Prague Slav Congress and later led the Czech National Party (the "Old Czechs") in the Bohemian Diet and the Austrian Reichsrat. His portrait appears on the Czech 1,000-koruna banknote.
