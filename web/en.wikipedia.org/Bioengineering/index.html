Biological engineering

Biological engineering, or bioengineering, is the application of engineering principles and life-science knowledge to design systems, products and processes using living organisms or biological molecules. It overlaps with biomedical engineering, biotechnology, genetic engineering and synthetic biology.

Scope

Bioengineers design medical devices and diagnostics, tissue-engineered implants, biopharmaceutical production processes, engineered microbes for chemicals and fuels, agricultural traits, and biomaterials. Work spans molecular scales to whole ecosystems and manufacturing plants.

History

The field coalesced in the mid-20th century around fermentation engineering and biomedical devices, and expanded with recombinant DNA in the 1970s, genomics, and CRISPR-based editing. Dedicated university departments and professional societies formed from the 1960s onward.

Methods

Core tools include mathematical modelling, systems biology, bioreactor design, biomechanics, biomaterials science and design-build-test cycles for engineered cells. Ethical, safety and regulatory considerations — biosafety, dual-use risk and environmental release — are integral to practice.
