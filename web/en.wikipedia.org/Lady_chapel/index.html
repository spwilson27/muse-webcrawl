Lady chapel

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A Lady chapel or Marian chapel is a chapel dedicated to the Virgin Mary ("Our Lady") within a large church or cathedral. Lady chapels are a traditional feature of British and French cathedrals and large churches, reflecting the strong medieval devotion to Mary.

Position and architecture

The Lady chapel is most often the largest chapel of the church, and is usually situated east of the high altar, at the extreme eastern end of the building, projecting beyond the main structure — as at Ely, Lichfield, and Westminster Abbey, where Henry VII's Lady Chapel is a masterpiece of Perpendicular Gothic fan vaulting. In some churches the chapel occupies a separate or lateral position.

History

The daily singing of the Lady Mass and antiphons in honour of the Virgin made the Lady chapel a liturgical focus of medieval religious houses and cathedrals. Many were richly endowed and decorated, and they frequently contain the tombs of founders and benefactors.
