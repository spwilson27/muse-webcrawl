Codon

A codon is a sequence of three nucleotides (a triplet) in messenger RNA (mRNA) that specifies a particular amino acid, or signals the start or end of protein synthesis, during translation. The set of correspondences between codons and amino acids is known as the genetic code.

The standard code

Because there are four RNA bases (A, U, G and C), there are 4³ = 64 possible codons. Of these, 61 are sense codons specifying the 20 standard amino acids, and three — UAA, UAG and UGA — are stop codons. The codon AUG both codes for methionine and serves as the usual start codon. Since 61 codons encode only 20 amino acids, the code is degenerate: most amino acids are specified by more than one codon, differing mainly in the third position (the "wobble" position).

Translation

During translation, the ribosome moves along the mRNA reading codons in sequence. Each codon is recognised by the anticodon of a transfer RNA (tRNA) molecule carrying the corresponding amino acid, which is added to the growing polypeptide chain until a stop codon is reached.
