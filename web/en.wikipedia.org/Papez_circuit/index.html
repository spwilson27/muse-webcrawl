Papez circuit

The Papez circuit is a neural pathway in the brain that forms a major part of the limbic system. It was described by neuroanatomist James Papez in 1937, who proposed that the circuit provides the anatomical basis for emotion.

Pathway

The circuit begins in the hippocampus. Fibres travel through the fornix to the mammillary bodies of the hypothalamus. From there, the mammillothalamic tract carries signals to the anterior nuclei of the thalamus, which project to the cingulate cortex. The cingulate cortex connects via the cingulum back to the parahippocampal gyrus and entorhinal cortex, which in turn project back to the hippocampus, closing the loop.

Function

Papez originally proposed the circuit as the substrate of emotional experience. Later work has shown that the circuit is also important in memory: damage to structures along the pathway, particularly the mammillary bodies and anterior thalamic nuclei, is associated with amnesia, as seen in Korsakoff syndrome.
