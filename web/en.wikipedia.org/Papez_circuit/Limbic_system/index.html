Limbic system

Limbic system
ComponentsHippocampus, amygdala, cingulate cortex, hypothalamus, fornix, mammillary bodies
FunctionsEmotion, motivation, memory, olfaction

The limbic system is a set of brain structures located on both sides of the thalamus, immediately beneath the medial temporal lobe of the cerebrum. It supports a variety of functions including emotion, behaviour, motivation, long-term memory and olfaction. The term derives from the Latin limbus ("border"), coined by Paul Broca for the ring of cortex around the corpus callosum.

The Papez circuit

In 1937 James Papez proposed a closed circuit as the anatomical basis of emotion: the hippocampus projects via the fornix to the mammillary bodies; from there fibres reach the anterior thalamus, then the cingulate cortex, and finally back to the hippocampus. The circuit is now understood to be as important for memory as for emotion — damage at its diencephalic nodes causes the amnesia of Korsakoff syndrome.

Structures

Core structures include the hippocampal formation, amygdala, cingulate and parahippocampal gyri, hypothalamus, mammillary bodies, septal nuclei and anterior thalamic nuclei. Modern neuroscientists debate whether the "limbic system" is a coherent system, since its structures also serve non-emotional functions, but the term remains widely used as a convenient grouping.
