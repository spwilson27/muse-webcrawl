Since 1922 the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland has been made up of four countries: England, Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland. Each retains distinct legal, educational, and cultural traditions, and three of the four have their own devolved governments.

England

England is the largest country, with over 80 percent of the UK's population. It has no devolved government of its own and is governed directly by the UK Parliament and Government at Westminster, a situation that gives rise to the "West Lothian question" about legislative fairness.

Scotland and Wales

Scotland has its own legal system, church, and — since 1999 — a devolved Parliament in Edinburgh with wide powers including health, education, and some taxation. Wales, legally joined with England for centuries, now has the Senedd (Welsh Parliament) in Cardiff with growing legislative powers, and Welsh enjoys official status alongside English.

Northern Ireland

Northern Ireland, created by the partition of Ireland in 1921, has the Northern Ireland Assembly at Stormont under a power-sharing executive, established by the Good Friday Agreement of 1998. Its constitutional status — remaining in the UK or joining a united Ireland — is governed by the principle of consent.
