Rethymno (Greek: Ρέθυμνο [ˈreθimno], also Rethimno, Rethymnon, and Rhithymnos) is a city in Greece on the island of Crete. It is the capital of the Rethymno regional unit, and has a population of about 35,000 (city) and 60,000 in the wider municipality. The city was built during the Minoan civilisation as ancient Rhithymna, and flourished later under Venetian rule, when many of its Renaissance-era buildings were constructed.

Rethymno's old town, largely built by the Venetians, is one of the best-preserved in Greece, with narrow lanes, Venetian and Ottoman houses, loggias and fountains. The city is also the seat of the University of Crete's humanities campus, and its economy rests on tourism, agriculture and services.

History

The site was inhabited in Minoan times as Rhithymna, which minted its own coins but was never a major power. Under the Venetians, who ruled Crete from 1204, the town grew into an intermediate trading port between Heraklion and Chania and acquired its walls, the large Fortezza fortress (built 1573–1580 against pirate and Ottoman attacks) and the Rimondi Fountain (1626). The Ottomans captured Rethymno in 1646 during the Cretan War; they added minarets and the Egyptian-style lighthouse at the harbour. Crete became autonomous in 1898 and united with Greece in 1913.

Landmarks

  • Fortezza — the large Venetian citadel on the Paleokastro hill, one of the largest Venetian fortifications in Crete.
  • Old town — with the Venetian Loggia, the Rimondi Fountain, and Ottoman mosques such as Neratze Mosque.
  • Old harbour — lined with fish taverns, guarded by a 19th-century lighthouse.
  • Archaeological Museum — housed in the church of St. Francis.

Culture

Rethymno hosts the annual Renaissance Festival, celebrating the city's Venetian-era heritage of music and theatre, and a wine festival in summer. The nearby Monastery of Arkadi, a symbol of Cretan resistance in 1866, lies in the municipality's hinterland.
