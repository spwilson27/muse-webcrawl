A Eurogame, also called a German-style board game or designer board game, is a class of tabletop games that generally emphasise strategy and resource management over luck and direct conflict. The style emerged from postwar German game design and reached international prominence in the 1990s.

Characteristics

Typical features include indirect player interaction (competing for resources rather than attacking one another), no elimination of players before the end, relatively low randomness, abstracted themes applied over strong mechanics, scoring by victory points often kept partially hidden, and playing times of roughly 30–120 minutes. Games are usually credited to a named designer on the box.

History

Germany's Spiel des Jahres award (founded 1978) nurtured a strong domestic design culture. Klaus Teuber's Catan (1995) became a worldwide hit and a gateway title, followed by games such as Carcassonne, Ticket to Ride, Puerto Rico and Agricola. In recent decades the Euro style has blended with American thematic design into hybrid games, and the term is now used loosely for strategy board games in this tradition.
