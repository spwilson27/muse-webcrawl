Embrace, extend, and extinguish

"Embrace, extend, and extinguish" (EEE), also known as "embrace, extend, and exterminate", is a phrase that the U.S. Department of Justice found was used internally by Microsoft to describe its strategy for entering product categories involving widely used standards, extending those standards with proprietary capabilities, and then using those differences in order to strongly disadvantage its competitors.

The three phases

The strategy consists of three phases: embrace — publicly adopting a standard or competing technology; extend — adding proprietary extensions that create incompatibilities; and extinguish — using market dominance to marginalise the original standard and its other implementers. Internal Microsoft testimony and documents relating to HTML, Kerberos, and other technologies surfaced during the United States v. Microsoft Corp. antitrust proceedings.

Legacy

The phrase has since been applied more broadly, by critics and commentators, to strategies of dominant platform companies toward open standards, open-source projects, and interoperable protocols.
