A newspaper is a periodical publication containing written information about current events and is often typed in black ink with a white or gray background. Newspapers can cover a wide variety of fields such as politics, business, sports, arts, and science, and they often include materials such as opinion columns, weather forecasts, reviews, obituaries, birth notices, crosswords, and advertisements.

History

The first printed newspaper is generally considered to be the Relation, published by Johann Carolus in Strasbourg in 1605. Newspapers spread across Europe in the seventeenth century, and the first English daily appeared in 1702. The steam press, telegraph, and cheap wood-pulp paper enabled mass-circulation "penny press" papers in the nineteenth century. By the late twentieth century, most developed countries had several competing national and local dailies.

Formats and organization

Print newspapers are typically produced in broadsheet, tabloid, or Berliner formats and organized into sections — news, business, sport, culture — produced by reporters and editors under an editor-in-chief. Traditionally newspapers earned most revenue from advertising and the rest from sales and subscriptions.

Decline of print

Since the 2000s, the rise of the internet and social media has sharply reduced print circulation and advertising revenue in most countries, leading to closures and consolidation, while major titles have shifted to digital subscriptions and online editions.
