Egg production

Egg production is the farming of eggs, principally chicken eggs, for human consumption. Eggs are laid by female animals of many species, but by far the most commonly consumed are those of the chicken, followed by ducks and quails. Global production of chicken eggs exceeded 80 million tonnes in recent years, with China producing roughly a third of the world's supply, followed by the United States, India, and Indonesia.

History

Chickens were domesticated from the red junglefowl of Southeast Asia several thousand years ago and gradually selected for laying. Egg production was a small-scale farm activity until the twentieth century, when selective breeding produced specialised laying hybrids capable of more than 300 eggs per year, and production became industrialised with large indoor flocks, automated feeding, egg collection, grading, and packing.

Housing systems

  • Battery cages: small wire cages housing several hens; banned in the European Union since 2012 in their conventional ("barren") form, but still widely used elsewhere.
  • Enriched (furnished) cages: larger cages providing a nest, perch, and scratch area.
  • Barn (aviary) systems: hens housed indoors with litter floors and sometimes multiple tiers, free to move within the shed.
  • Free-range: hens have daytime access to outdoor runs.
  • Organic: free-range conditions plus organic feed and additional welfare requirements.

The production cycle

Pullets begin laying at around 18–20 weeks of age and are typically kept for one laying cycle of about a year before being replaced ("spent hens"). Laying hens are fed diets rich in calcium for shell formation. Artificial lighting regimes maintain day lengths that stimulate laying. Eggs are collected daily, washed or cleaned where permitted, candled to detect defects, graded by weight, stamped with production codes in many jurisdictions, packed, and refrigerated through the supply chain.

Welfare and environmental issues

Egg production raises animal-welfare concerns including confinement, beak trimming, the culling of male chicks at hatch (increasingly addressed by in-ovo sexing technology), and the fate of spent hens. Environmental issues include manure management, ammonia emissions, feed sourcing, and vulnerability to disease outbreaks such as highly pathogenic avian influenza, which caused severe supply disruptions and price spikes in the 2020s. Consumer and legislative pressure has driven a worldwide, if uneven, shift toward cage-free production.
