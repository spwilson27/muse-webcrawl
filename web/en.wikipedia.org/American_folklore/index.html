American folklore

American folklore encompasses the traditional beliefs, legends, tales, and oral traditions that have been passed down within American culture. It draws on Native American traditions, the folklore of European settlers, African traditions carried by enslaved people, and the experiences of later immigrant communities.

Tall tales and folk heroes

The tall tale tradition produced figures such as Paul Bunyan, the giant lumberjack; Pecos Bill of the cowboy frontier; John Henry, the steel-driving man who raced a steam drill; and Johnny Appleseed, based on the real nurseryman John Chapman. Regional traditions include Br'er Rabbit trickster tales, Appalachian Jack tales, and Louisiana's Cajun and Creole lore.

Study

The American Folklore Society was founded in 1888, and collectors such as John and Alan Lomax documented folk song traditions, while the Federal Writers' Project gathered life histories and folklore during the 1930s.
