Standard 8 mm film

Standard 8 mm film, also known as Regular 8 or Double 8, is a motion picture film format developed by the Eastman Kodak company and released in 1932. It was created to provide a cheaper home movie alternative to 16 mm film.

Format

The film is actually 16 mm wide stock with twice the normal number of perforations. It is loaded into the camera and run through twice, exposing one half of the width on each pass. After processing, the film is slit lengthwise and the two halves are spliced together, yielding 8 mm wide finished film.

History

Standard 8 was the dominant amateur format for decades until Kodak introduced Super 8 film in 1965, which offered a larger picture area and convenient cartridges. Standard 8 remained in use by dedicated hobbyists long afterwards.
