Qajar dynasty

The Qajar dynasty (Persian: دودمان قاجار) was an Iranian royal dynasty of Turkic origin, specifically from the Qajar tribe, ruling over Iran from 1789 to 1925. The Qajar family took full control of Iran in 1794, having previously come to power under the Safavid and Afsharid dynasties; they deposed Lotf 'Ali Khan, the last Shah of the Zand dynasty, and re-asserted Iranian sovereignty over large parts of the Caucasus.

History

Agha Mohammad Khan, crowned in 1796 and founder of Tehran as the capital, was assassinated in 1797 and succeeded by his nephew Fath-Ali Shah. Under Fath-Ali, Iran lost its Caucasian territories (modern Georgia, Armenia, Azerbaijan and Dagestan) to the Russian Empire after the Russo-Persian Wars, confirmed by the treaties of Gulistan (1813) and Turkmenchay (1828).

During the 19th century, under Mohammad Shah and Naser al-Din Shah, Iran faced mounting pressure from Russia and Britain — the "Great Game" — while attempting internal reform, notably under the prime minister Amir Kabir. The Qajar era saw the Tobacco Protest of 1891–92 and ultimately the Persian Constitutional Revolution of 1905–1911, which established a parliament (Majlis) and limited royal power.

Fall

Weakened by World War I occupations and internal disorder, the dynasty was effectively ended when Reza Khan, commander of the Cossack Brigade, seized power; in 1925 the Majlis deposed Ahmad Shah Qajar and Reza Khan founded the Pahlavi dynasty.

Legacy

Qajar art, especially portrait painting and photography under Naser al-Din Shah, and Qajar architecture in Tehran (such as the Golestan Palace) remain culturally significant.
