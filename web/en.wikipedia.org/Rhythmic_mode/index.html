Rhythmic mode

The rhythmic modes were a system of rhythmic patterns used in the polyphonic music of the Notre Dame school in the late 12th and 13th centuries. The modes, described by theorists such as Johannes de Garlandia and Anonymous IV, are the earliest systematic notation of measured rhythm in Western music.

The six modes

Notes were grouped in ligatures whose shapes indicated one of six patterns, each built from longs and breves in triple metre, analogous to poetic feet: Mode 1 (trochaic): long–breve; Mode 2 (iambic): breve–long; Mode 3: long followed by three breves; Mode 4: two breves then a long; Mode 5: all longs; Mode 6: all breves. The patterns repeated in regular cycles called ordines.

Use and legacy

The modes governed the tenor and upper voices of organa, clausulae, conductus and early motets of composers such as Léonin and Pérotin. As rhythms grew more varied in the later 13th century, the modal system was superseded by Franconian notation, which assigned specific durations to individual note shapes.
