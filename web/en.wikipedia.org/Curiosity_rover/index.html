Curiosity (rover)

Curiosity is a car-sized Mars rover that has explored Gale crater on Mars since 2012 as part of NASA's Mars Science Laboratory (MSL) mission. It was launched from Cape Canaveral on 26 November 2011 and landed on Aeolis Palus, inside Gale crater, on 6 August 2012, using a novel sky-crane landing system.

Mission

The rover's goals are to investigate Mars's climate and geology, assess whether Gale crater ever offered conditions favourable for microbial life, and study the planet's habitability in preparation for human exploration. It is powered by a multi-mission radioisotope thermoelectric generator (MMRTG) using plutonium-238, which frees it from dependence on solar power.

Findings

Curiosity found rounded pebbles indicating an ancient stream bed, evidence of an ancient freshwater lake in Yellowknife Bay, organic molecules preserved in mudstone, seasonal variations in atmospheric methane, and complex geology on the slopes of Mount Sharp (Aeolis Mons), which it has been climbing since 2014. Its radiation measurements helped characterise risks for future crewed missions.

Instruments

The payload includes the Mastcam cameras, the ChemCam laser spectrometer, the SAM (Sample Analysis at Mars) laboratory suite, CheMin X-ray diffraction, the RAD radiation detector, and a robotic arm carrying a drill and the MAHLI and APXS instruments.
