Mr.

Mr. (pronounced /ˈmɪstər/), is an English form of address for men, usually used as a title or prefix. It is derived from the earlier forms of master, as the equivalent formal title for a man was Master. The abbreviation Mr. is used (in American English with a period, in British English often without: "Mr Smith").

"Mr." used as a prefix before a surname

Usage

Mr. is a title used before a surname or full name of a male, regardless of marital status. It is a shortened version of Mister, itself derived from "master". Historically, "Master" was reserved for men of high rank, while "Mister" was a general form of address for gentlemen. The plural form is Messrs. (from French messieurs).

Equivalents

Equivalent female titles include Miss, Mrs. and Ms. The gender-neutral title Mx. has also come into use. In professional and military contexts, titles indicating rank or office (Doctor, Professor, Captain) generally replace "Mr." as a prefix.
