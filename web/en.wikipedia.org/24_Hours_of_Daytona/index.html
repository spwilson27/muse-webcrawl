24 Hours of Daytona

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Night racing on the Daytona road course during the Rolex 24.

The 24 Hours of Daytona, also known as the Rolex 24 At Daytona for sponsorship reasons, is a 24-hour sports car endurance race held annually at Daytona International Speedway in Daytona Beach, Florida. It is run on a 3.56-mile (5.73 km) combined road course, utilising portions of the NASCAR tri-oval and an infield road course. The race has been held since 1966 and is the first major automobile race of the year in the United States.

The race is sanctioned by IMSA and is the opening round of the IMSA SportsCar Championship. Along with the 24 Hours of Le Mans and the 12 Hours of Sebring, it forms part of the informal Triple Crown of endurance racing.

History

Daytona's first major endurance event was a three-hour Continental race in 1962, won by Dan Gurney in a Lotus 19. The event grew to 2,000 kilometres in 1964 and became a full 24-hour race in 1966, the same year it joined the World Sportscar Championship calendar. The 1966 race was won by Ken Miles and Lloyd Ruby in a Ford GT40 Mk II, leading a famous 1-2-3 finish for Ford.

From 1982, the race became the centrepiece of the IMSA GT Championship. In 1998 it came under Grand-Am sanctioning, and following the merger of Grand-Am and the American Le Mans Series, it returned to IMSA as the flagship event of the unified SportsCar Championship in 2014. Rolex has been the title sponsor since 1992, and winning drivers receive Rolex Cosmograph Daytona watches.

Format

The race traditionally starts on the last Saturday of January or the first Saturday of February, beginning in the afternoon and running through the night — roughly half the race takes place in darkness — before finishing 24 hours later. Teams field multiple classes simultaneously, including Grand Touring Prototype (GTP), Le Mans Prototype 2 (LMP2), and two GT classes based on GT3 machinery. Each car is driven by a team of three to five drivers.

Notable winners

Hurley Haywood and Scott Pruett share the record for most overall victories, with five wins each. Porsche is the most successful manufacturer in the race's history. Famous winners include A. J. Foyt, Mario Andretti, and Brian Redman. The 1966, 1967, and 1970 races were won by factory Ford and Ferrari entries during the height of the Ford–Ferrari rivalry.

Records

The race distance record was set in 2018, when the winning Action Express Racing Cadillac covered 808 laps and 2,876.85 miles (4,629.84 km). The event attracts fields of around 60 cars and crowds exceeding 100,000 spectators across the race week, which also includes the Roar Before the Rolex 24 test weekend held earlier in January.
