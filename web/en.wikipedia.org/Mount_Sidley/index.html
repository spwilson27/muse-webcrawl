Mount Sidley

Elevation4,285 m (14,058 ft)
LocationMarie Byrd Land, Antarctica
RangeExecutive Committee Range
TypeShield volcano

Mount Sidley is the highest dormant volcano in Antarctica, rising 4,285 metres in the Executive Committee Range of Marie Byrd Land. It is a massive, mainly snow-covered shield volcano with a spectacular horseshoe-shaped caldera about 5 kilometres wide, whose cliffs drop more than a kilometre to the caldera floor. Sidley is the highest of the "Volcanic Seven Summits" — the highest volcanoes on each continent.

Discovery and exploration

The volcano was discovered on 18 November 1934 during a flight by Rear Admiral Richard E. Byrd, who named it for Mabelle Sidley, daughter of a contributor to his Antarctic expeditions. Despite being Antarctica's highest volcano, it is far less known than the continent's highest peak, Vinson Massif, and was not climbed until 11 January 1990, when New Zealander Bill Atkinson reached the summit as part of a United States Antarctic Program geological party. Only a handful of ascents have been made since, all by ski-equipped expeditions flown into remote Marie Byrd Land.

Geology

Sidley belongs to a chain of shield volcanoes formed as the West Antarctic lithosphere moved over a mantle plume. Its oldest lavas date to about 6 million years ago, with the youngest activity roughly 4.2 million years ago; the volcano is considered dormant rather than extinct. The caldera's amphitheatre-shaped wall is a result of sector collapse or caldera subsidence.
