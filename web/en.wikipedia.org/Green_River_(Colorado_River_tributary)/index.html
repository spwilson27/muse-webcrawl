The Green River, located in the western United States, is the chief tributary of the Colorado River. It rises in the Wind River Range of western Wyoming, flows south through Utah, briefly into Colorado, and joins the Colorado River in Canyonlands National Park after a course of about 730 miles.

Course and features

The river is impounded by Flaming Gorge Dam in northeastern Utah, forming Flaming Gorge Reservoir, and flows through Dinosaur National Monument, where it meets its largest tributary, the Yampa River, at Echo Park. Below, it traverses Desolation Canyon and Gray Canyon before crossing the San Rafael Swell country and entering Canyonlands.

History

The Green River valley was a corridor for fur trappers in the 1820s, including at rendezvous sites near present-day Daniel, Wyoming. John Wesley Powell began his famous 1869 expedition down the Green and Colorado rivers at Green River, Wyoming. The river basin is also noted for the Green River Formation, oil-shale-bearing sediments preserving Eocene fossils, and for the trona deposits near Green River, Wyoming.
