Linseed oil

Linseed oil, also known as flaxseed oil or flax oil (in its edible form), is a colourless to yellowish oil obtained from the dried, ripened seeds of the flax plant (Linum usitatissimum). The oil is obtained by pressing, sometimes followed by solvent extraction. Owing to its polymer-forming properties, linseed oil is used on its own or blended with other oils, resins or solvents as an impregnator, drying oil finish or varnish in wood finishing, as a pigment binder in oil paints, and as a component of linoleum.

Properties

Linseed oil is a drying oil: exposed to air, it undergoes exothermic polymerization and gradually hardens into a solid film. It is rich in alpha-linolenic acid, an omega-3 fatty acid, which accounts for its high drying capacity. Raw, boiled and stand oils differ in their degree of heat treatment and drying time; "boiled" linseed oil is typically treated with metallic dryers to speed curing.

Uses

Applications include oil paints, putty, wood finishes, linoleum flooring, gilding, and as a nutritional supplement in its cold-pressed edible form. Spontaneous combustion of oily rags is a known fire hazard because the polymerization reaction releases heat; rags soaked in linseed oil should be stored in sealed metal containers or spread flat to dry.
