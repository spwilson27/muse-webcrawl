Our Lady of Mount Carmel

Title ofMary, mother of Jesus
Feast day16 July
Associated orderCarmelites
DevotionBrown Scapular

Our Lady of Mount Carmel is a title given to the Virgin Mary as patroness of the Carmelite Order. It recalls the hermits who settled on Mount Carmel in the Holy Land in the twelfth century and dedicated their first chapel to Mary. Her feast is celebrated on 16 July.

The scapular tradition

According to Carmelite tradition, Mary appeared to Saint Simon Stock in Cambridge in 1251 and gave him the Brown Scapular, with a promise of salvation for those who die wearing it devoutly. The scapular devotion became one of the most widespread in Catholic practice and is often summarised in the Sabbatine privilege, a later tradition concerning release from purgatory.

Patronage

Our Lady of Mount Carmel is patroness of Chile, where she is venerated as the country's patron saint, and of many towns, fishermen, and religious congregations. Processions and festivals on her feast day are held across the Catholic world, notably in Haifa at the Stella Maris Monastery on Mount Carmel.
