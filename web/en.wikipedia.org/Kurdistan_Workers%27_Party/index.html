Kurdistan Workers' Party

Native name: Partiya Karkerên Kurdistanê (PKK)
Founded: 27 November 1978
Leader: Abdullah Öcalan (imprisoned)
Headquarters: Qandil Mountains

The Kurdistan Workers' Party, commonly known by its Kurdish acronym PKK, is a Kurdish militant political organization and armed guerrilla movement founded in Turkey in 1978 by Abdullah Öcalan. It has waged an insurgency against the Turkish state since 1984, initially seeking an independent Kurdish state and later Kurdish autonomy and cultural rights.

History

The PKK was founded in the village of Fis in Diyarbakır Province. It launched its armed campaign on 15 August 1984 with attacks in Eruh and Şemdinli. Öcalan was captured in Kenya in 1999 and imprisoned on İmralı Island; from prison he developed the ideology of democratic confederalism.

Designation

The PKK is designated a terrorist organization by Turkey, the United States, the European Union and several other countries. The conflict has claimed tens of thousands of lives, and peace efforts, including a 2013–2015 ceasefire period, have repeatedly broken down.
