Senegal

Senegal, officially the Republic of Senegal, is the westernmost country in West Africa, situated on the Atlantic Ocean coastline. It borders Mauritania to the north, Mali to the east, Guinea to the southeast and Guinea-Bissau to the southwest, and surrounds The Gambia.

History

Senegal was home to kingdoms including Jolof, and to French colonial trading posts at Saint-Louis and Gorée. It gained independence from France in 1960 under first president Léopold Sédar Senghor, a poet and theorist of Négritude. It briefly formed the Senegambia Confederation with The Gambia (1982-1989).

Geography and culture

The country is largely flat Sahelian plain, with the Cap-Vert peninsula at its west. Islam, mainly Sufi brotherhoods, is the predominant religion. Music such as mbalax, wrestling, and football are central to culture.

Politics

Senegal is regarded as one of West Africa's more stable democracies, though the regional crisis in The Gambia and domestic protests in 2021-2024 tested institutions. Bassirou Diomaye Faye became president in 2024.
