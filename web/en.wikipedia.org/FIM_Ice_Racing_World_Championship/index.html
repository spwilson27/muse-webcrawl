FIM Ice Racing World Championship

The FIM Ice Racing World Championship is the world championship series for ice speedway, a form of motorcycle racing on frozen oval tracks, sanctioned by the Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme (FIM). The individual championship has been held since 1966, and a team championship for national squads is also contested.

Ice speedway riders leaning into a turn

The sport

Ice speedway motorcycles are purpose-built machines fitted with tyres carrying hundreds of sharp steel spikes — up to 28 millimetres long — which provide grip on the ice. Riders race counter-clockwise around oval tracks of frozen water, typically 260 to 425 metres long, leaning at extreme angles with their left leg extended and protected by a steel shoe. The bikes have no brakes, and speeds can exceed 100 km/h.

History and champions

The championship has historically been dominated by riders from the Soviet Union and Russia, owing to the sport's popularity and long winters there; Soviet rider Gabdrakhman Kadyrov won the first world title in 1966, and Russian riders won the overwhelming majority of titles in subsequent decades, with Swedish and Finnish riders among the few other nations' champions. Rounds are traditionally held in Russia, Sweden, Finland, Germany, and the Netherlands.
