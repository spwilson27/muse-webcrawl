Bakumatsu

Bakumatsu
Period: 1853–1868
Country: Japan (Tokugawa shogunate)
Followed by: Meiji period

Bakumatsu (Japanese: 幕末, "end of the bakufu") refers to the final years of the Edo period of Japan, between 1853 and 1868, when the Tokugawa shogunate ended. It was a time of great turmoil as Japan's policy of national isolation (sakoku) collapsed and the country stood between feudal tradition and forced modernisation.

Opening of Japan

In 1853, Commodore Matthew Perry of the United States Navy arrived with his "Black Ships" and demanded Japan open to trade. The Convention of Kanagawa (1854) and subsequent unequal treaties with Western powers weakened the shogunate and sparked the sonnō jōi movement — "revere the emperor, expel the barbarians."

Conflict and civil war

Reformist domains such as Satsuma and Chōshū modernised their armies and allied against the shogunate. After the last shogun, Tokugawa Yoshinobu, nominally returned power to Emperor Meiji in 1867, the Boshin War (1868–1869) was fought between imperial and shogunal forces, ending in the shogunate's defeat.

Legacy

The Bakumatsu ended with the Meiji Restoration, which transformed Japan into a modern industrial state. Figures such as Sakamoto Ryōma and Saigō Takamori remain celebrated in Japanese popular culture, and the era is a favourite setting for historical drama.
