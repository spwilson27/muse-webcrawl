Prickly pear

GenusOpuntia
FamilyCactaceae
Native rangeThe Americas
Edible partsFruit (tuna); pads (nopal)

Prickly pear is the common name for cacti of the genus Opuntia, characterised by flat, fleshy, paddle-shaped stem segments (cladodes) studded with areoles bearing spines and tiny barbed bristles called glochids. Native to the Americas, prickly pears have been spread worldwide and are naturalised — sometimes invasively — in arid regions of Australia, Africa, and the Mediterranean.

Description

Plants range from low groundcovers to tree-like forms several metres tall. The large, showy flowers are typically yellow, orange, or red, and are followed by fleshy fruits, known in Spanish as tuna, which range in colour from green to deep magenta and are covered in glochids that must be removed before eating.

Uses

Prickly pears have been cultivated in Mexico for millennia. The young pads (nopales) are eaten as a vegetable, and the fruits are consumed fresh or made into juices, jams, and candies. The plants host the cochineal insect, source of the red dye carmine, and a nopal cactus appears on the flag and coat of arms of Mexico, commemorating the Aztec founding legend of Tenochtitlan. The most widely grown species is Opuntia ficus-indica.

Invasion and control

In Australia, prickly pears overran vast areas of farmland in the early twentieth century before being brought under control by the introduction of the moth Cactoblastis cactorum, one of the most celebrated successes of biological pest control.
