Sergei Prokofiev

Sergei Sergeyevich Prokofiev (27 April 1891 – 5 March 1953) was a Russian composer, pianist, and conductor, later Soviet. One of the major composers of the 20th century, his works include such widely heard pieces as the March from The Love for Three Oranges, the suite Lieutenant Kijé, the ballet Romeo and Juliet, and Peter and the Wolf.

Life

Born in Sontsovka (now Sontsivka, Ukraine), Prokofiev studied at the Saint Petersburg Conservatory. After the Russian Revolution he spent nearly two decades abroad, in the United States, Germany, and Paris, before returning permanently to the Soviet Union in 1936. In 1948 he was condemned, along with Shostakovich and others, in the Zhdanov decree against "formalism". He died in Moscow on the same day as Joseph Stalin.

Works

Prokofiev composed seven completed operas, including The Gambler and War and Peace; seven symphonies; eight ballets, including Cinderella; five piano concertos; two violin concertos; the cello concerto and Symphony-Concerto; and noted film scores for Sergei Eisenstein's Alexander Nevsky and Ivan the Terrible. His nine piano sonatas remain central to the instrument's repertoire.

Style

Prokofiev himself classified elements of his style as classical, modern, motoric (toccata), lyrical, and "grotesque" lines. His music combines biting dissonance and rhythmic drive with passages of expansive lyricism.
