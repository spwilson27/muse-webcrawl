Quebec roads

The road network of Quebec, Canada, comprises more than 185,000 km of roads of all types, of which roughly 31,000 km form the provincial highway system (réseau routier supérieur) administered by Transports Québec. The remainder consists of municipal streets and resource roads.

Provincial system

The provincial network is divided into:

  • Autoroutes — controlled-access freeways, numbered 1–99 with three-digit spurs (e.g. A-640), shown on blue shields;
  • National and regional routes — numbered highways shown on green shields, including Routes 101–399; east–west routes tend to be even-numbered and north–south routes odd-numbered, generally increasing eastward;
  • Collector roads and access roads to remote communities and resources.

Notable roads

Major corridors include the Trans-Canada Highway across the province (A-20, A-85 and A-40 in parts), Route 132 along the south shore of the St. Lawrence, Route 138 along the north shore to the Côte-Nord, Route 117 through the Laurentian and Abitibi regions, and the gravel James Bay Road (Route de la Baie James) and Trans-Taiga Road serving the hydroelectric developments of the north.

Signage and language

Quebec road signs are in French and rely heavily on pictograms; Quebec was a pioneer of symbol-based signage in North America. Winter conditions strongly influence the network, with mandated winter tires from 1 December to 15 March.
