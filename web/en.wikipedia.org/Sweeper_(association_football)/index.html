Sweeper (association football)

A sweeper is a defensive position in association football, occupied by a player who plays behind the line of central defenders and "sweeps up" loose balls, covering teammates who are beaten by opponents. The role, known in Italian as the libero ("free man"), was a defining feature of defensive systems from the 1930s through the 1990s.

History

The position developed from experiments in Switzerland and Austria and was popularised in Italy, where Helenio Herrera's catenaccio system at Internazionale in the 1960s used a sweeper behind a man-marking defence. Franz Beckenbauer reinterpreted the role at Bayern Munich and with West Germany, turning the sweeper into an attacking playmaker who stepped forward with the ball to launch attacks — the archetype of the attacking libero. Other celebrated liberos included Gaetano Scirea, Franco Baresi and Lothar Matthäus in his later years.

Decline

The sweeper largely disappeared from elite football in the 1990s. Changes to the offside law, the back-pass rule of 1992, and the spread of zonal marking and high pressing made a spare defender behind the line inefficient; modern teams instead use a flat back four and rely on the goalkeeper to sweep behind a high defensive line — the "sweeper-keeper" exemplified by Manuel Neuer.

Characteristics

Sweepers required exceptional reading of the game, composure on the ball and passing ability, since — having no direct opponent to mark — they were often their team's first distributor of possession.
