Eighty Years' War

Date1568–1648
LocationThe Low Countries
ResultPeace of Münster; Dutch independence recognised

The Eighty Years' War or Dutch Revolt (1568–1648) was an armed conflict in the Habsburg Netherlands between disparate groups of rebels and the Spanish government. The causes of the war included the Reformation, centralisation, taxation, and the rights and privileges of the nobility and cities.

Led initially by William of Orange, the northern provinces formed the Dutch Republic, which emerged as a world maritime and economic power. Spain retained control of the southern Netherlands (roughly modern Belgium).

Course

The conflict included the Spanish Fury at Antwerp, the Dutch capture of sea ports by the Sea Beggars, and a Twelve Years' Truce (1609–1621) before fighting resumed as part of the wider Thirty Years' War.

Conclusion

The Peace of Münster, ratified in 1648 as part of the Peace of Westphalia, formally recognised the independence of the Dutch Republic.
