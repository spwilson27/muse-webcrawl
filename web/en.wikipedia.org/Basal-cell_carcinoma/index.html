Basal-cell carcinoma

Basal-cell carcinoma

SymptomsPainless raised area of skin, visible blood vessels, non-healing sore
Risk factorsUltraviolet light exposure, fair skin, immunosuppression

Basal-cell carcinoma (BCC), also known as basal-cell cancer, is the most common type of skin cancer. It arises from the basal cells of the epidermis and typically appears as a painless, raised, pearly or waxy area of skin, often with visible small blood vessels, or as a sore that does not heal.

Causes and risk factors

The principal cause of BCC is exposure to ultraviolet (UV) radiation from sunlight and tanning beds, which damages DNA in skin cells; mutations in the PTCH1 tumor suppressor gene and the hedgehog signaling pathway are characteristic. Risk is highest in fair-skinned individuals, older adults, people with a history of sunburns, and those who are immunosuppressed.

Signs and course

BCC grows slowly and very rarely metastasizes, but it can invade and destroy surrounding tissue if untreated, particularly on the face. The most common subtype, nodular BCC, presents as a translucent papule or nodule; superficial, morpheaform, and pigmented variants also occur.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is confirmed by skin biopsy. Treatment usually involves surgical removal — standard excision or Mohs micrographic surgery for high-risk facial lesions — while topical medications, photodynamic therapy, curettage, cryotherapy, or radiation may be used in selected cases. Hedgehog pathway inhibitors such as vismodegib are used for rare advanced or metastatic disease. Prognosis after complete removal is excellent, though patients remain at risk of new lesions and require sun protection and follow-up.
