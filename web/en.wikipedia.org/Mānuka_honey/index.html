Mānuka honey is prized for its antibacterial activity and commands high prices.

Mānuka honey is a monofloral honey produced from the nectar of the mānuka tree, Leptospermum scoparium, native to New Zealand and southeast Australia. It is noted for strong antibacterial activity beyond that of ordinary honeys, attributed mainly to the compound methylglyoxal (MGO), which forms in the honey from dihydroxyacetone in the nectar.

Properties and grading

The antibacterial potency of mānuka honey is marketed under grading systems such as the Unique Mānuka Factor (UMF), which tests for marker compounds including leptosperin, dihydroxyacetone and methylglyoxal, and direct MGO ratings. Medical-grade mānuka honey preparations are used in wound dressings, where studies support antibacterial and wound-healing effects; claims for many other oral health benefits have weaker evidence.

Industry and authenticity

Mānuka honey is among the world's most expensive honeys and a significant New Zealand export. Its high value has produced problems of adulteration and mislabelling — more honey is sold worldwide as mānuka than is produced — prompting New Zealand government export standards requiring chemical and DNA marker testing to authenticate the product.
