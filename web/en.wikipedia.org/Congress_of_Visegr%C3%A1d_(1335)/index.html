Congress of Visegrád (1335)

The First Congress of Visegrád was a 1335 summit in Visegrád, Hungary, attended by Charles I of Hungary, Casimir III of Poland, and John of Bohemia. The three kings negotiated an anti-Habsburg alliance and settled disputes among their kingdoms.

Agreements

At the congress, Casimir III renounced his claim to Silesia in favor of Bohemia — a renunciation later formalized in the Treaty of Trentschin — while John of Bohemia renounced his claim to the Polish crown in exchange for a payment of 20,000 Prague groschen. The rulers also agreed on new trade routes that bypassed the staple right of Vienna, directing commerce through Brno and other towns so their merchants could avoid Habsburg tolls.

Legacy

A second congress followed in 1339, at which the succession to the Polish crown was discussed. The 1335 meeting is remembered as the inspiration for the Visegrád Group, the modern alliance of Hungary, Poland, the Czech Republic, and Slovakia founded in 1991 in the same town.
