BE-4

Manufacturer Blue Origin
Propellant LOX / liquefied natural gas (methane)
Cycle Oxygen-rich staged combustion
Thrust (sea level) approx. 550,000 lbf (2.4 MN)

The BE-4 (Blue Engine 4) is a reusable liquid rocket engine developed by the American aerospace company Blue Origin. Burning liquid oxygen and liquefied natural gas (primarily methane), it uses an oxygen-rich staged combustion cycle and produces about 550,000 pounds-force (2.4 meganewtons) of thrust at sea level.

Development

Blue Origin began work on the BE-4 in 2011 and publicly announced the engine in 2014, in partnership with United Launch Alliance (ULA), which selected it to power its Vulcan Centaur rocket. Development included extensive component and full-engine hot-fire testing, and production moved to a purpose-built factory in Huntsville, Alabama.

Applications

Two BE-4 engines power the first stage of ULA's Vulcan Centaur, which first flew in January 2024. Seven BE-4s power the first stage of Blue Origin's own New Glenn heavy-lift launch vehicle, which made its maiden flight in January 2025. The engine is designed for reuse and for moderate manufacturing cost compared with earlier high-performance engines.

Design

The use of liquefied natural gas offers advantages over kerosene (less coking in cooling channels) and over hydrogen (higher density and cheaper handling). The oxygen-rich staged combustion cycle, long used in Russian engines, drives the turbopumps with the full flow of one propellant through preburners.
