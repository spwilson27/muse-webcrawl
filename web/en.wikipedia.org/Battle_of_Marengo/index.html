Battle of Marengo

The Battle of Marengo was fought on 14 June 1800 between French forces under First Consul Napoleon Bonaparte and Austrian forces under General Michael von Melas, near the village of Marengo south of Alessandria in Piedmont, Italy. It was the decisive engagement of Napoleon's Italian campaign of 1800 in the War of the Second Coalition. After being driven back all day and facing defeat, the French were saved by the return of General Louis Desaix, whose counterattack — in which Desaix was killed — smashed the Austrian army and forced Austria to evacuate most of northern Italy.

Course of the battle

Having crossed the Alps via the Great St Bernard Pass, Napoleon threatened Austrian communications but dispersed his army; Melas seized the initiative and attacked across the Bormida on 14 June. Victor's corps was pushed back from Marengo village to San Giuliano, and by mid-afternoon the French were retreating in apparent defeat — Melas, elderly and slightly wounded, handed command to subordinates and retired to Alessandria. Around 17:00 Desaix arrived with Boudet's fresh division, reporting, in the famous (probably embellished) phrase, "This battle is completely lost. However, there is time to win another." A massed artillery battery under Marmont, Desaix's infantry assault and Kellermann's perfectly timed cavalry charge into the Austrian flank destroyed the pursuing column of grenadiers; the Austrian army collapsed and lost some 14,000 men. The next day Melas signed the Convention of Alessandria, surrendering Italy west of the Mincio. The victory consolidated Napoleon's political power as First Consul.
