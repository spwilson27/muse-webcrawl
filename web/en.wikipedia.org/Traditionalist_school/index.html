Traditionalist school


René Guénon (1886–1951)

The Traditionalist school, also known as perennialism, is a group of 20th- and 21st-century thinkers who regard all the world's orthodox religions as sharing a single, metaphysical truth or origin, from which — it is held — all esoteric and exoteric knowledge and doctrine has grown. Its principal exponents were the French metaphysician René Guénon (1886–1951), the Ceylonese-British scholar Ananda Coomaraswamy, and the Swiss philosopher Frithjof Schuon.

Traditionalists draw on the concept of the philosophia perennis ("perennial philosophy") and emphasise primordial Tradition — a body of truth transmitted from the origins of each religious form. They sharply criticise modernity, progressivism and secularism, which they view as deviations and a "reign of quantity" divorced from sacred principles.

The school influenced comparative religion, art history (through Coomaraswamy) and Sufi and esoteric circles; writers associated with it include Julius Evola, Titus Burckhardt, Martin Lings, Seyyed Hossein Nasr and Huston Smith. Its works underpin the Sophia Perennis school of religious studies, though the school's anti-modernism and some of its political associations, particularly in Evola's case, remain controversial.
