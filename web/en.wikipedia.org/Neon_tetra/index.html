Neon tetra

Neon tetra
Scientific name: Paracheirodon innesi
Family: Characidae
Origin: Amazon basin (Brazil, Colombia, Peru)
Size: ~3 cm (1.2 in)

The neon tetra (Paracheirodon innesi) is a freshwater fish of the characin family (Characidae) native to blackwater and clearwater streams in the Amazon basin of South America. It is one of the most popular aquarium fish in the world, prized for its brilliant iridescent blue stripe running from the nose to the adipose fin and the vivid red coloration of its rear half. It is closely related to the cardinal tetra (Paracheirodon axelrodi), which has a longer red stripe.

Distribution and ecology

Wild neon tetras inhabit slow-moving, shaded tributaries in Brazil, Colombia, and Peru, in soft, acidic water often stained dark by tannins. They are schooling fish, feeding on small invertebrates and organic matter. Wild fisheries around Barcelos, Brazil, supply part of the aquarium trade and are promoted as sustainable livelihoods, though the great majority of neon tetras sold are bred commercially in Southeast Asia, Florida, and Eastern Europe.

In the aquarium

Neon tetras are peaceful community fish best kept in groups of six or more, in water around 20–26 °C, slightly acidic and soft. They were introduced to the aquarium hobby in the 1930s and named for William T. Innes, editor of The Aquarium. The species is susceptible to "neon tetra disease," an incurable microsporidian infection (Pleistophora hyphessobryconis). A silvery-gold aquarium strain, the "gold neon tetra," has also been developed.
