Amelogenesis

Amelogenesis is the formation of enamel on teeth and begins when the crown is forming during the advanced bell stage of tooth development after dentinogenesis forms a bit of dentin. Although dentin must be present for enamel to form, enamel must also be present for dentin to continue to form, since ameloblasts signal the odontoblasts.

Stages

Amelogenesis is divided into three stages: the presecretory stage, the secretory stage, and the maturation stage. During the secretory stage, ameloblasts deposit enamel matrix proteins including amelogenin, ameloblastin, enamelin, and tuftelin. During the maturation stage, the ameloblasts transport substances used in the formation of enamel, and the matrix becomes highly mineralized.

Clinical significance

Defects in amelogenesis cause amelogenesis imperfecta, a group of hereditary conditions in which enamel is abnormally thin, soft, or discolored.
