Helmuth von Moltke the Elder

Helmuth von Moltke

Born: 26 October 1800, Parchim, Mecklenburg-Schwerin

Died: 24 April 1891 (aged 90), Berlin, German Empire

Service: Prussian Army; Imperial German Army

Rank: Field Marshal (Generalfeldmarschall)

Commands: Chief of the German General Staff, 1857–1888

Moltke in 1870

Helmuth Karl Bernhard Graf von Moltke (26 October 1800 – 24 April 1891) was a Prussian field marshal and chief of staff of the Prussian and later German armies for thirty years. He is regarded as the architect of Germany's wars of unification and one of the great strategists of the 19th century. He is often referred to as Moltke the Elder to distinguish him from his nephew, Helmuth von Moltke the Younger, who commanded the German Army at the outbreak of the First World War.

Early life

Moltke was born in Parchim, Mecklenburg-Schwerin, and initially commissioned into Danish service before transferring to the Prussian Army in 1822. He served as an adviser to the Ottoman Empire from 1835 to 1839 and published accounts of his travels in Anatolia. His translation work and writings on railways and strategy brought him to the attention of the Prussian high command.

Chief of the General Staff

Appointed chief of the Great General Staff in 1857, Moltke rebuilt the staff system, institutionalised war planning by railway timetable, and made the staff ride and war game central to officer education. Working with Chancellor Otto von Bismarck and War Minister Albrecht von Roon, he directed the Prussian victories in the Second Schleswig War (1864), the Austro-Prussian War (1866, decided at Königgrätz), and the Franco-Prussian War (1870–71), culminating in the proclamation of the German Empire.

Strategic thought

Moltke held that strategy is a "system of expedients" and that no plan survives first contact with the enemy — a maxim commonly paraphrased as "no plan survives contact with the enemy." He pioneered Auftragstaktik, directive command in which subordinates were given missions and expected to use their initiative. He retired in 1888 and died in Berlin in 1891.
