William Michael Harnett (August 10, 1848 – November 29, 1892) was an Irish-American painter best known for his hyper-realistic still-life paintings in the trompe-l'œil ("fool the eye") style.

Life and work

Born in Ireland and raised in Philadelphia, Harnett trained as an engraver before studying painting. He became famous for meticulously rendered arrangements of everyday objects — musical instruments, books, firearms, currency, and hanging game — painted with such precision that viewers reportedly tried to touch the objects. After the Hunt (1885) and The Old Violin (1886) are among his most celebrated works. He spent several years in Europe in the 1880s before returning to New York, where chronic illness curtailed his career.
