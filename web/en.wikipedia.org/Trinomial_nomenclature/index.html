Trinomial nomenclature
Three-part scientific names for taxa below the rank of species

In biology, trinomial nomenclature is used to name taxa below the rank of species: a name in three parts. It extends the binomial system of Carl Linnaeus, in which each species receives a two-part name consisting of the genus and the specific epithet. In zoology, the third part denotes a subspecies, as in Gorilla beringei beringei, the mountain gorilla.

Usage

In zoology, the trinomen is the complete name of a subspecies. In botany, trinomial names can denote subspecies, varieties or forms, with the rank indicated by an abbreviation such as "subsp." or "var." placed between the second and third parts. Cultivated plants use an additional system governed by the Cultivated Plant Code.
