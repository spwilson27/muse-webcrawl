Citadel Miniatures is a British manufacturer of miniature figurines for tabletop wargames, best known as the producer of the miniatures used in Games Workshop's games, including Warhammer 40,000, Warhammer Age of Sigmar (and its predecessor Warhammer Fantasy Battle), and licensed The Lord of the Rings ranges. Citadel is a subsidiary brand of Games Workshop.

History

Citadel Miniatures was founded in 1979 as the miniatures-producing arm associated with Games Workshop, which had been founded in 1975 by Ian Livingstone, Steve Jackson, and John Peake. Early Citadel figures, sculpted by artists including Bryan Ansell, Alan and Michael Perry, and Jes Goodwin, were cast in metal and sold through Games Workshop stores and mail order. Citadel's miniatures were promoted through White Dwarf magazine, which included catalogues such as the famous Citadel Compendiums and Journals of the 1980s.

Materials and production

Citadel figures were originally produced in lead alloy and later in white metal ("Warhammer metal") after lead was phased out in the 1990s. From the late 1980s onward the company increasingly used injection-moulded plastic kits, which allowed larger, multi-part models. In 2011 Games Workshop introduced "Finecast", a resin material, for many former metal models; it was later phased out in favour of plastic and the Forge World resin range.

Citadel Colour

The brand also encompasses the Citadel Colour range of hobby paints, brushes, and tools designed for painting the miniatures, including the Contrast paint line introduced in 2019, which was widely noted in the hobby press.
