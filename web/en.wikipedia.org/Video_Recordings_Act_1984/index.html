Video Recordings Act 1984

Video Recordings Act 1984
Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom
Designated authority: British Board of Film Classification
Context: the "video nasties" moral panic

The Video Recordings Act 1984 is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that established a statutory classification and licensing system for video works (initially VHS and Betamax tapes) supplied to the public. It designated the British Board of Film Classification (BBFC) as the authority responsible for classifying videos, usually more strictly than cinema releases.

Background

The Act followed the early-1980s "video nasties" panic, when unregulated horror videos — including titles later prosecuted under the Obscene Publications Act — were blamed for corrupting young viewers. Campaigners such as Mary Whitehouse pressed Parliament to bring home video under control.

Provisions and later history

Supplying an unclassified video, or a classified video to a person below its age rating, became a criminal offence. In 2009 it was discovered that a procedural failure — the Act had never been notified to the European Commission as required — made it unenforceable; prosecutions were halted until the Video Recordings Act 2010 repealed and revived it in corrected form.
