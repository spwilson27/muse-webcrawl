Nikolai Fyodorovich Vatutin (December 16, 1901 – April 15, 1944) was a Soviet military commander during World War II, one of the Red Army's most capable front commanders. He led the Southwestern and Voronezh Fronts in the encirclement at Stalingrad and the defence at Kursk, and as commander of the 1st Ukrainian Front he liberated Kiev in November 1943.

Vatutin joined the Red Army in 1920 and rose rapidly through staff appointments, escaping the Great Purge. At the Battle of Kursk in July 1943 his Voronezh Front withstood the German southern offensive. His forces then crossed the Dnieper and freed Kiev on November 6, 1943.

Death

On February 29, 1944, Vatutin's staff car was ambushed by Ukrainian Insurgent Army (UPA) fighters near the village of Mylyatyn in western Ukraine. He was wounded in the thigh and died of his wounds (gangrene/sepsis) in Kiev on April 15, 1944. He was posthumously made a Hero of the Soviet Union in 1965 and is buried in Kiev's Mariinskyi Park.
