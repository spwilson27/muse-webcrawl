Child Ballads

The Child Ballads are 305 traditional ballads from England and Scotland, and their American variants, collected by Harvard professor Francis James Child in the late 19th century. His monumental collection, The English and Scottish Popular Ballads (1882–1898), gives each ballad a "Child number," by which the songs are still universally cited.

The collection

Child gathered variants of each ballad from manuscripts, broadsides, and earlier printed collections, printing them with extensive scholarly notes and comparisons to ballads from other European traditions. Although he did little fieldwork himself, his rigorously edited texts became the standard reference for folk-song scholarship.

Contents and themes

The ballads cover romance and tragedy ("Barbara Allen," Child 84), supernatural encounters ("Tam Lin," Child 39), Robin Hood tales (a large block of numbers), border raids, historical events, riddles, and religious legends. Well-known Child ballads include "Lord Randall" (12), "The Elfin Knight" (2, ancestor of "Scarborough Fair"), "Edward" (13), and "Sir Patrick Spens" (58).

Influence

The collection profoundly shaped the folk revivals of the 20th century in Britain and America: singers such as Joan Baez, and the British folk-rock movement, drew heavily on it. The Roud Folk Song Index cross-references Child numbers, and later collections (such as those of Cecil Sharp) supplemented Child's textual work with melodies and field recordings.
