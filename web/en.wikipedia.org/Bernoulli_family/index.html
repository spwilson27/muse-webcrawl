Bernoulli family

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Bernoulli family of Basel was a patrician family, originally from Antwerp, known for having produced eight gifted academics who, among them, contributed substantially to the development of mathematics and physics during the early modern period.

Origins

The family, of Huguenot descent, fled Antwerp in 1583 to escape Spanish persecution, settling first in Frankfurt and then in Basel, Switzerland, where they became successful merchants and scholars. Leon Bernoulli was a physician in Antwerp; his descendant Jacob Bernoulli (1594–1659) settled in Basel and married into the city's patriciate.

Notable members

The most famous members are the brothers Jacob Bernoulli (1655–1705) and Johann Bernoulli (1667–1748), pioneers of Leibnizian calculus, the calculus of variations and probability theory — Jacob's posthumous Ars Conjectandi founded the mathematical theory of probability. Johann's son Daniel Bernoulli (1700–1782) founded hydrodynamics and derived the Bernoulli principle in fluid mechanics. Other notable members include Nicolaus I, Nicolaus II and Johann II Bernoulli, and the astronomer Jean Bernoulli. Family life was marked by intense intellectual rivalry, notably between Jacob and Johann and between Johann and his son Daniel.

The name also appears across science in Bernoulli numbers, Bernoulli's equation, Bernoulli trials and Bernoulli distributions, all named for members of the family.
