Honduras

Honduras, officially the Republic of Honduras (Spanish: República de Honduras), is a country in Central America, bordered by Guatemala to the northwest, El Salvador to the southwest, and Nicaragua to the southeast, with coastlines on the Caribbean Sea to the north and a short Pacific coast on the Gulf of Fonseca to the south. Its capital and largest city is Tegucigalpa. With an area of about 112,492 km² and a population of roughly 10.6 million, Honduras is a predominantly mountainous country known for the Maya ruins of Copán, the Bay Islands' coral reefs, and an economy long associated with bananas and coffee — the origin of the term "banana republic".

History

Western Honduras was part of the Maya world: Copán, near the Guatemalan border, was a major Classic-period city-state (5th–9th centuries CE) renowned for its carved stelae and the Hieroglyphic Stairway, while Lenca and other peoples inhabited the interior. Columbus reached the Honduran coast in 1502 on his fourth voyage; Spanish conquest under Hernán Cortés and Pedro de Alvarado followed in the 1520s, resisted famously by the Lenca chief Lempira, after whom the currency is named. As a province of the Captaincy General of Guatemala, colonial Honduras exported silver from mines around Tegucigalpa and Comayagua. Independence came in 1821; Honduras joined and then left the Federal Republic of Central America (1838) and endured a turbulent 19th century of caudillo politics. In the late 19th and 20th centuries, US fruit companies — United Fruit and Standard Fruit — built vast banana enclaves on the north coast, dominating the economy and politics and prompting O. Henry's coinage "banana republic". The 20th century saw military governments, the brief 1969 "Soccer War" with El Salvador, and a role as a base for US-backed Contra forces in the 1980s. A 2009 coup that removed President Manuel Zelaya drew international condemnation. Xiomara Castro, elected in 2021, became the country's first woman president in January 2022.

Geography and biodiversity

Honduras is the most mountainous country in Central America: rugged highlands cover most of the interior (highest peak Cerro Las Minas, 2,870 m), with fertile valleys and a narrow Pacific plain. The Caribbean lowlands of the northeast — La Mosquitia — contain Central America's largest remaining tropical rainforest, part of the Río Plátano Biosphere Reserve, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The Bay Islands (Roatán, Útila, Guanaja) sit on the Mesoamerican Barrier Reef, the second-largest coral reef system in the world, and are major diving destinations. The climate is tropical on the coasts and temperate in the highlands; the country is highly vulnerable to hurricanes, most devastatingly Mitch in 1998, which killed thousands and caused billions of dollars in damage.

Economy and society

Honduras is a lower-middle-income country whose economy rests on agriculture (coffee, bananas, African palm oil, melons), apparel assembly in maquiladoras around San Pedro Sula, and remittances from emigrants — a major share of GDP. Poverty and inequality are high, and gang violence drove homicide rates that peaked among the world's worst around 2011 before declining substantially. The population is predominantly mestizo, with Indigenous (Lenca, Miskito, Maya Ch'orti', Tolupan, Pech, Tawahka), Afro-Honduran Garífuna, and white minorities; Spanish is universal, with Indigenous and Creole English languages on the Caribbean coast and islands. Roman Catholicism and evangelical Protestantism are the main religions.

Government

Honduras is a unitary presidential republic under the 1982 constitution. The president is both head of state and government, elected to a single four-year term (re-election was long prohibited and remains politically contentious). The unicameral National Congress has 128 deputies, and the judiciary is headed by a Supreme Court elected by Congress. Politics was dominated for decades by the Liberal and National parties; the LIBRE party's 2021 victory marked a significant realignment.
