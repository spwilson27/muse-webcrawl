2022–2023 mpox outbreak

The 2022–2023 mpox outbreak was a global outbreak of mpox (formerly known as monkeypox) caused by the clade IIb variant of the monkeypox virus. Beginning in May 2022, cases were reported in countries where the disease was not endemic, and the outbreak spread to more than 100 countries.

Course of the outbreak

The first cases outside Africa were confirmed in the United Kingdom in May 2022. Transmission occurred mainly through close physical contact, and cases were concentrated among men who have sex with men, though the virus can spread to anyone. On 23 July 2022, the World Health Organization declared the outbreak a public health emergency of international concern.

Response

Countries deployed vaccination campaigns using the JYNNEOS/Imvanex smallpox vaccine for at-risk groups, alongside testing, contact tracing and public health messaging. In November 2022, WHO adopted the name "mpox" as a synonym for monkeypox to reduce stigma.

End of the emergency

Cases declined sharply by early 2023 due to vaccination, behaviour change and infection-derived immunity. WHO ended the public health emergency declaration on 11 May 2023, after more than 87,000 confirmed cases and over 100 deaths had been reported worldwide.
