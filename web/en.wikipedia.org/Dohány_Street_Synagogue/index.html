Dohány Street Synagogue

The Dohány Street Synagogue (Hungarian: Dohány utcai zsinagóga), also known as the Great Synagogue, is a historical synagogue in Erzsébetváros, the seventh district of Budapest, Hungary. It is the largest synagogue in Europe and one of the largest functioning synagogues in the world, seating about 3,000 people.

Architecture

Built between 1854 and 1859 in the Moorish Revival style, the synagogue was designed by the Viennese architect Ludwig Förster. Its twin onion-domed towers, rose window and richly patterned brick façade recall Islamic and medieval Spanish architecture. The interior, partly designed by Frigyes Feszl, includes a large organ — played in its early years by Franz Liszt and Camille Saint-Saëns — and some features unusual in synagogues, reflecting the Neolog Judaism of its congregation. Theodor Herzl, the founder of modern Zionism, was born in an adjoining house on Dohány Street.

History

The synagogue stands at the boundary of the former Budapest ghetto, where tens of thousands of Jews were confined in 1944–45; thousands who died there were buried in the synagogue's courtyard. Damaged during the Second World War and neglected under communist rule, the building was extensively restored in the 1990s, partly with donations from the Hungarian-American community including Estée Lauder and Tony Curtis. The complex includes the Hungarian Jewish Museum, a Holocaust memorial hall, and the Raoul Wallenberg Holocaust Memorial Park, with Imre Varga's weeping-willow memorial to Hungarian Holocaust victims.
