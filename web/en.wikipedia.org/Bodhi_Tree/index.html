Bodhi Tree

Bodhi Tree
Species: Ficus religiosa (sacred fig)
Location: Bodh Gaya, Bihar, India
Significance: site of the Buddha's enlightenment

The Bodhi Tree ("tree of enlightenment") at Bodh Gaya is a large sacred fig tree under which Siddhartha Gautama, the Buddha, is said to have attained enlightenment (bodhi) around the 5th century BCE.

History

Ashoka venerated the tree in the 3rd century BCE and built the Mahabodhi Temple nearby. The present tree descends from the original; a sapling taken by Ashoka's daughter Sanghamitta to Sri Lanka in the 3rd century BCE became the Jaya Sri Maha Bodhi at Anuradhapura, among the oldest living human-planted trees with a known planting date (288 BCE).

Significance

Bodhi trees are planted at Buddhist monasteries worldwide; their heart-shaped leaves are a symbol of awakening. The site is one of Buddhism's four holy pilgrimage places.
