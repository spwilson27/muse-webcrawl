Richard Mather

Richard Mather (1596 – 22 April 1669) was a Puritan minister in colonial New England, patriarch of the Mather dynasty of clergy. He was the father of Increase Mather and grandfather of Cotton Mather.

Life

Born in Lowton in Lancashire, England, Mather studied at Brasenose College, Oxford, and was ordained, but his nonconformist views led to suspension from preaching. In 1635 he emigrated to Massachusetts Bay Colony, becoming teacher of the church at Dorchester, a post he held for the rest of his life.

Works and influence

Mather helped prepare the Bay Psalm Book (1640), the first book printed in British North America, and wrote the preface. He was a leading defender of the Half-Way Covenant of 1662, which allowed baptism of the grandchildren of church members, and a principal author of the Cambridge Platform (1648) defining Congregational church government.

Death

He died in Dorchester in 1669 and was buried in the Dorchester North Burying Ground.
