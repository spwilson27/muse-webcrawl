August Wöhler

Born: 22 June 1819, Soltau, Hanover
Died: 21 March 1914, Hannover
Known for: Fatigue (material) research; Wöhler curve

August Wöhler (1819–1914) was a German engineer best known for systematic research into the fatigue of metals. Working for the railways, he investigated axle failures and conducted repeated-loading tests on iron and steel.

Wöhler curve

His results, published from the 1850s onwards, produced the stress–number of cycles (S–N) diagram still called the Wöhler curve, showing that materials can fail below their static strength under cyclic loading and that a fatigue limit exists for some steels.

Legacy

Wöhler's standardised test methods laid the foundations of fatigue design and materials testing in mechanical engineering.
