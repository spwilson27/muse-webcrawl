Peer group

A peer group is both a social group and a primary group of people who have similar interests, age, background or social status. The members of this group are likely to influence the person's beliefs and behaviour. Peer groups are especially influential during adolescence, when individuals seek belonging and identity outside the family.

Peer group
Field: Sociology, developmental psychology
Key influence period: Adolescence

Development

In childhood, peer groups are usually organized by adults and based on shared activities. During adolescence they become more autonomous and structured—often described in terms of cliques (small, close-knit groups) and crowds (larger reputation-based groupings)—and peer approval becomes a powerful motivator.

Peer influence

Peer pressure can encourage positive behaviours such as academic effort and sport, or risky behaviours such as smoking and delinquency. Research increasingly distinguishes peer influence from peer selection: adolescents also choose friends similar to themselves.

In organizations

The term is also used for sets of comparable companies ("peer group analysis" in finance) and for workplace colleagues of equal rank.
