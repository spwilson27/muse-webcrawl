Thebes (Ancient Egyptian: Waset; Greek: Θῆβαι, Thēbai) was an ancient Egyptian city on the east bank of the Nile, about 800 kilometres south of the Mediterranean, at the site of modern Luxor. It was the capital of Egypt during much of the Middle Kingdom and the New Kingdom, and the religious centre of the god Amun.

History

Thebes rose to prominence under the 11th Dynasty, whose Theban kings reunified Egypt to begin the Middle Kingdom. After the expulsion of the Hyksos by the Theban 17th and 18th Dynasties, Thebes became capital of an empire stretching from Nubia to the Euphrates. Its wealth grew immense, fed by tribute and by the estates of the temple of Amun at Karnak. The city later declined as political power shifted north, though it remained a religious centre; it was sacked by the Assyrians in 663 BC.

Sites

  • Karnak — the vast temple complex of Amun-Re, enlarged over 2,000 years, with its great hypostyle hall.
  • Luxor Temple — built mainly under Amenhotep III and Ramesses II, linked to Karnak by an avenue of sphinxes.
  • Valley of the Kings — on the west bank, the rock-cut tombs of New Kingdom pharaohs, including Tutankhamun (KV62).
  • Valley of the Queens, the mortuary temple of Hatshepsut at Deir el-Bahari, and the workers' village of Deir el-Medina.

Homer called it "hundred-gated Thebes" in the Iliad, a mark of its fame in the ancient world. The ruins were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1979.
