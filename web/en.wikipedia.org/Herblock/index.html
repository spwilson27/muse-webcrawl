Herblock

Herbert Lawrence Block (October 13, 1909 – October 7, 2001), commonly known as Herblock, was an American editorial cartoonist and author best known for his commentaries on national domestic and foreign policy.

Career

Block worked for the Washington Post from 1946 until his death, after earlier stints at the Chicago Daily News and the Newspaper Enterprise Association. He won three Pulitzer Prizes for editorial cartooning (1942, 1954 and 1979) and shared a fourth for public service in 1973 for his Watergate cartoons. He coined the term "McCarthyism" in a 1950 cartoon, and his drawings relentlessly attacked Senator Joseph McCarthy, President Richard Nixon and other figures he believed abused power.

Herblock's simple, bold style and his defence of civil liberties made him one of the most influential editorial cartoonists of the 20th century. The Library of Congress holds a major collection of his work.
