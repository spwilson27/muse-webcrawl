Basketball is a team sport in which two teams of five players oppose each other on a rectangular court, competing to shoot a ball through the defender's hoop — a basket 46 cm in diameter mounted 3.048 m (10 feet) high — while preventing the opposing team from scoring in their own hoop.

History

The game was invented in December 1891 by Canadian physical education instructor James Naismith in Springfield, Massachusetts, as an indoor sport to keep athletes fit during winter. The first game used a soccer ball and two peach baskets as goals, and Naismith's original thirteen rules were published in January 1892. The sport spread rapidly through YMCAs and colleges; the Basketball Association of America, which became the NBA, was founded in 1946. Basketball became an Olympic sport in 1936, and the women's game was added in 1976.

Rules and gameplay

Players advance the ball by dribbling or passing. Field goals are worth two points, or three from beyond the three-point arc; free throws after fouls are worth one point. A shot clock limits the time a team may possess the ball (24 seconds in the NBA and FIBA play). Violations include traveling, double dribbling, and goaltending; personal fouls penalize illegal physical contact. Standard positions are point guard, shooting guard, small forward, power forward, and center.

Global popularity

Basketball is among the world's most popular sports, with professional leagues in Europe, Asia, and the Americas, the FIBA World Cup, and the NBA — whose stars such as Michael Jordan, LeBron James, and Magic Johnson became global icons — drawing a worldwide audience.
