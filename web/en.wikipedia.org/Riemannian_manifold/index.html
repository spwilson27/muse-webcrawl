Riemannian manifold

In differential geometry, a Riemannian manifold is a geometric space on which many geometric notions, such as distance, angles, length, volume, and curvature, are locally defined. More precisely, a Riemannian manifold is a smooth manifold equipped with a Riemannian metric: a smoothly varying positive-definite inner product on each tangent space. The concept is named after Bernhard Riemann, who introduced it in his 1854 habilitation lecture "On the Hypotheses which lie at the Bases of Geometry".

Metric and geometry

The metric tensor g determines the length of curves by integration of the norm of their velocity, and hence a distance function making the manifold a metric space. It also defines angles, orthogonality, the volume form, and a gradient for functions. The Levi-Civita connection is the unique torsion-free connection compatible with the metric; its parallel transport and geodesic curves — locally shortest paths — generalise straight lines.

Curvature

Curvature measures how the geometry deviates from flat Euclidean space. The Riemann curvature tensor yields the sectional curvature of two-dimensional planes, the Ricci curvature (its trace), and the scalar curvature. Classical theorems include Gauss's Theorema Egregium, the Gauss–Bonnet theorem relating total curvature to topology, and comparison theorems of Rauch, Toponogov, and Bishop–Gromov.

Examples and applications

Examples include Euclidean space, spheres, hyperbolic space, the flat torus, and any smooth surface embedded in three-dimensional space with the induced metric. Riemannian geometry underlies general relativity (which uses the Lorentzian analogue, pseudo-Riemannian manifolds), geometric analysis such as Perelman's proof of the Poincaré conjecture via Ricci flow, and modern applications in machine learning and data geometry.
