In ring theory, a branch of abstract algebra, a quotient ring, also known as a factor ring or residue class ring, is a construction analogous to the quotient group in group theory and the quotient space in linear algebra. It is formed by taking a ring R and a two-sided ideal I in R and "dividing out" by I: the elements of the quotient ring R/I are the cosets of I in R, with addition and multiplication defined on representatives.

Definition

Given a ring R and an ideal I, the quotient ring is the set of cosets

R/I = { a + I : a ∈ R },

with operations (a + I) + (b + I) = (a + b) + I and (a + I)(b + I) = ab + I, which are well-defined precisely because I is an ideal. There is a natural surjective ring homomorphism π: R → R/I with kernel I.

Examples

  • The integers modulo n: ℤ/nℤ, the ring of residues used in modular arithmetic; it is a field when n is prime.
  • Polynomial quotient: F[x]/(p(x)) for an irreducible polynomial p over a field F gives a field extension, e.g. ℝ[x]/(x² + 1) ≅ ℂ.
  • Quotienting a ring of functions on a space by the ideal of functions vanishing on a subset gives functions on that subset.

Properties

The first isomorphism theorem states that the image of any ring homomorphism R → S is isomorphic to R/ker(φ). R/I is a field if and only if I is a maximal ideal, and an integral domain if and only if I is a prime ideal. Quotient rings are fundamental in algebraic geometry, where coordinate rings of varieties are quotients of polynomial rings.
