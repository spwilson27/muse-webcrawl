Mobile messaging

Mobile messaging is the exchange of text and multimedia messages between mobile devices. It began with the Short Message Service (SMS) of GSM networks and has expanded to include multimedia messaging (MMS) and a wide range of Internet-based instant messaging applications.

SMS and MMS

The first SMS message was sent in December 1992, and text messaging grew explosively in the late 1990s and 2000s, becoming a major revenue source for carriers despite its 160-character limit. MMS, introduced in the early 2000s, allowed photos, audio, and video, but saw limited adoption compared with later app-based services.

App-based messaging

From around 2010, over-the-top (OTT) messaging applications running over data connections displaced SMS in many markets. Major platforms include:

  • WhatsApp (founded 2009) — the most widely used messenger globally, with end-to-end encryption by default.
  • WeChat — dominant in China, combining messaging with payments and mini-programs.
  • iMessage — Apple's service integrated with SMS fallback on its devices.
  • Telegram and Signal — known respectively for large groups/channels and strong privacy guarantees.
  • Facebook Messenger, LINE, KakaoTalk, and Viber — regionally strong services.

The Rich Communication Services (RCS) standard was developed as a carrier-backed successor to SMS with read receipts, typing indicators, and larger media, and gained momentum with adoption by Google and, in 2024, Apple.

Impact

Mobile messaging has reshaped personal communication, business customer service, political campaigning, and commerce (conversational commerce), while raising policy questions about encryption, misinformation, and platform moderation.
