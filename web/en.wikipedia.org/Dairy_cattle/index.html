Dairy cattle

Dairy cattle (also called dairy cows) are cattle bred for the ability to produce large quantities of milk, from which dairy products are made. Dairy cattle generally are of the species Bos taurus.

History

Dairy cattle were domesticated from wild aurochs thousands of years ago. Historically, there was little distinction between dairy cattle and beef cattle, with the same stock often used for both meat and milk production. Today, the bovine industry is more specialized and most dairy cattle have been bred to produce large volumes of milk. Artificial insemination, first developed in the early 20th century, allowed rapid genetic improvement of herds through the widespread use of semen from elite bulls.

Breeds

The Holstein Friesian is the most common breed of dairy cattle in the United States, Europe, and much of the world, known for its very high milk yield and distinctive black-and-white markings. Other important dairy breeds include the Jersey, valued for high butterfat content; the Brown Swiss; the Ayrshire; the Guernsey; and the Milking Shorthorn. In India, zebu breeds such as the Gir and Sahiwal are widely kept.

Management

Dairy cows are typically milked two or three times per day. A cow must calve to begin producing milk; the lactation period lasts roughly 305 days, after which the cow is "dried off" for about two months before calving again. Cows are commonly fed grass, silage (such as corn silage or haylage), and grain-based concentrates. Modern operations may house cattle in free-stall barns, tie-stall barns, or on pasture, and automated robotic milking systems are increasingly used.

Milk production

The largest producers of cow's milk include the United States, India, China, Germany, Brazil, and Russia. Average annual milk yield per cow varies enormously by country and management system, from a few hundred litres in low-input systems to over 10,000 litres in intensive Holstein herds. Milk is processed into fluid milk, cheese, butter, yogurt, ice cream, and milk powder.

Animal welfare

Dairy farming has been criticized over issues including the separation of calves from their mothers shortly after birth, the fate of male calves (often raised for veal or slaughtered young), lameness, mastitis, and the relatively short productive lifespan of high-yielding cows. These concerns have driven welfare standards, audits, and growth in pasture-based and organic dairy systems.
