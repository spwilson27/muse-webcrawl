Snowy Mountains Scheme


Talbingo Dam, one of the scheme's major dams

The Snowy Mountains Scheme, also known as the Snowy Hydro scheme, is a hydroelectricity and irrigation complex in south-east Australia. Near the border of New South Wales and Victoria, the scheme consists of sixteen major dams, nine power stations, two pumping stations, and 225 kilometres (140 mi) of tunnels, pipelines and aqueducts, built between 1949 and 1974 under the supervision of the Snowy Mountains Hydro-electric Authority. It remains the largest engineering project undertaken in Australia.

Purpose and operation

The scheme collects and stores the waters of the Snowy River and its tributaries, which would otherwise flow to the sea, and diverts them westward through the Great Dividing Range into the Murray and Murrumbidgee river systems, providing irrigation water to inland agricultural regions and generating peaking hydroelectric power for New South Wales, Victoria, and the Australian Capital Territory. Its total generating capacity is about 4,100 megawatts.

Construction

Construction employed more than 100,000 people over 25 years, many of them post-war immigrants from more than 30 countries; about two-thirds of the workforce were migrants, and the scheme is regarded as a landmark of multicultural Australia. It set records in tunnelling, and its towns, such as Cabramurra—Australia's highest town—were built for workers. A total of 121 people died in industrial accidents during construction. The completed scheme was officially opened in 1972 and finished in 1974.

Snowy 2.0

A major expansion, Snowy 2.0, links the Tantangara and Talbingo reservoirs with 27 km of tunnels and a new underground pumped-hydro power station of about 2,000 megawatts capacity, intended to provide large-scale energy storage for the National Electricity Market. The project was announced in 2017 and has been under construction since 2019.
