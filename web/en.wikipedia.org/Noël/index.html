Christmas (Noël)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia — see also Noël, the French word for Christmas

Noël is the French word for Christmas, the annual festival commemorating the birth of Jesus Christ, observed on 25 December as a religious and cultural celebration among billions of people around the world. The word comes through Old French nael from the Latin nātālis (diēs), "day of birth." Related forms survive in other languages and in English carols; in English, "Nowell" was a common historical spelling, and "The First Nowell" remains one of the best-known traditional carols.

The name

In French-speaking regions, Joyeux Noël is the standard Christmas greeting. The name Noël is also given to boys born at Christmas time, and figuratively the word is used for Christmas songs (chants de Noël) and for Père Noël, the French counterpart of Father Christmas or Santa Claus. In medieval English usage, "Nowel" was cried out as a shout of joy at the Nativity, and the phrase appears in carols and in the writings of Chaucer's era.

Christmas itself

Christmas is a central feast of the Christian liturgical year, preceded by the season of Advent and followed by Christmastide. Popular customs include attending church services, exchanging gifts, decorating Christmas trees and homes with lights, sharing festive meals, and singing carols. The date of 25 December was established in the Roman Empire by the 4th century, coinciding with the winter solstice festival season; the actual date of Jesus's birth is unknown.

Christmas traditions vary widely by country: in France, the réveillon feast is eaten late on Christmas Eve, often featuring oysters, foie gras, roast poultry, and the bûche de Noël, a chocolate Yule-log cake, while children leave their shoes by the fireplace for Père Noël. In English-speaking countries, Christmas Day dinner, carol services, and cracker-pulling are characteristic, and figures such as Santa Claus dominate the season's commercial culture.
