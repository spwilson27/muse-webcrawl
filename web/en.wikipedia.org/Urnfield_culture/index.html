Urnfield culture
Period: Late Bronze Age
Dates: c. 1300–750 BC
Region: Central Europe

The Urnfield culture was a late Bronze Age culture of Central Europe, named after its distinctive burial custom: cremating the dead and placing their ashes in urns buried in flat cemeteries, or urnfields. It flourished roughly from 1300 to 750 BC.

Characteristics

Urnfield communities were skilled bronze-workers, producing swords, helmets, and ornate pottery. Settlements were often fortified hilltop sites. The culture is associated with the spread of cremation burial across Europe and is linked by some scholars to early Celtic and Italic peoples.

Decline

The Urnfield culture gradually gave way to the Iron Age Hallstatt culture in the west and other regional cultures elsewhere.
