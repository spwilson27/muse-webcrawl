The Young Turk Revolution of July 1908 was the overthrow of the autocratic rule of Ottoman Sultan Abdul Hamid II by the Young Turk movement, centred on the Committee of Union and Progress (CUP). It restored the Ottoman constitution of 1876 and the parliament that the sultan had suspended for three decades, inaugurating the Second Constitutional Era.

Background

Disaffection with Abdul Hamid's censorship, spies and autocratic government had produced exile opposition groups since the 1890s. By 1907–08 the movement had fused with secret committees of army officers in Macedonia, alarmed by the empire's weakness and by the prospect — discussed at the Reval meeting of June 1908 — of Anglo-Russian intervention in Macedonia.

The revolt

In early July 1908, officers including Ahmed Niyazi Bey and Enver Bey took to the hills of Macedonia with their troops, demanding restoration of the constitution. Mutinies spread through the Third Army, and on 23 July the CUP proclaimed the constitution restored in Salonica and Monastir. Facing the loss of his army, Abdul Hamid capitulated on 24 July, announcing the recall of parliament.

Aftermath

The revolution was initially greeted with euphoric scenes of fraternity among the empire's Muslims, Christians and Jews. Elections followed, but the new order was fragile: a counter-revolutionary rising in April 1909 (the 31 March Incident) was suppressed by troops from Salonica, and Abdul Hamid was deposed in favour of Mehmed V. The CUP progressively tightened its grip, culminating after the 1913 Raid on the Sublime Porte in the triumvirate of Enver, Talaat and Cemal Pashas, who led the empire into the First World War.
