Bolivia

Bolivia, officially the Plurinational State of Bolivia, is a landlocked country in western-central South America, bordered by Brazil to the north and east, Paraguay to the southeast, Argentina to the south, Chile to the southwest, and Peru to the west. It has a population of roughly 12 million and an area of 1,098,581 square kilometres, making it the fifth-largest country in South America. The seat of government and executive capital is La Paz, while the constitutional capital is Sucre. The largest city is Santa Cruz de la Sierra, in the eastern lowlands.

Salar de Uyuni, the world's largest salt flat, lies in Bolivia's southwest.

Geography

Bolivia's terrain divides between the high Andean Altiplano in the west—home to La Paz, Lake Titicaca (the world's highest navigable lake, shared with Peru), and the vast Salar de Uyuni salt flat—and the tropical Amazonian and Chaco lowlands covering two-thirds of the country in the east. Two parallel cordilleras run through the west, with peaks exceeding 6,000 metres, including Sajama (6,542 m), the country's highest point.

History

The region was home to the Tiwanaku civilisation, centred near Lake Titicaca, and later became part of the Inca Empire. Spanish colonisation from the 1530s made "Upper Peru" a centre of silver mining: the Cerro Rico of Potosí was one of the richest silver deposits in the world. Bolivia won independence in 1825 under the leadership associated with Simón Bolívar, for whom it is named, and Antonio José de Sucre. In the War of the Pacific (1879–84) Bolivia lost its Pacific coastline to Chile—a grievance that remains central to Bolivian politics and diplomacy. Further territorial losses followed in the Chaco War with Paraguay (1932–35) and the cession of Acre to Brazil.

The 1952 National Revolution introduced universal suffrage and nationalised the tin mines. After decades of coups and instability, democracy was restored in 1982. Evo Morales, elected in 2005 as the country's first indigenous president, oversaw a 2009 constitution declaring Bolivia a plurinational state and nationalising key industries during a commodity boom; his disputed 2019 re-election triggered a political crisis, and Luis Arce of the MAS party won the 2020 election.

Economy

Bolivia's economy rests on natural gas, minerals (tin, silver, zinc, and some of the world's largest lithium reserves in the Salar de Uyuni), soy and other agriculture, and remittances. Despite growth during the 2000s–2010s, Bolivia remains one of South America's poorer countries, with a large informal economy and significant indigenous population—among the highest proportions in the Americas.

Culture

Bolivian culture blends indigenous Andean and Spanish influences: the Carnaval de Oruro is a UNESCO-recognised festival, Andean textiles and music (charango, zampoña) are distinctive, and La Paz's cable-car network, Mi Teleférico, is the largest urban gondola system in the world.
