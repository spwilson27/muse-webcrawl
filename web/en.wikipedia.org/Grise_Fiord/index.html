Grise Fiord

Grise Fiord (Inuktitut: ᐊᐅᔪᐃᑦᑐᖅ, Aujuittuq, "place that never thaws") is an Inuit hamlet on the southern tip of Ellesmere Island in the Qikiqtani Region of Nunavut, Canada. It is Canada's northernmost public community, with a population of roughly 150 people. The hamlet lies about 1,160 km (720 mi) north of the Arctic Circle.

History

Grise Fiord was established in 1953 as part of the High Arctic relocation, in which the Canadian government moved Inuit families from Inukjuak in northern Quebec (and from Pond Inlet) to new settlements at Grise Fiord and Resolute. The government stated the move would improve living conditions and support sovereignty in the High Arctic, promising families they could return after two years if they wished — a promise that was not honoured for decades. The relocations caused great hardship, as families had to adapt to an unfamiliar environment, extreme cold, and months of polar darkness. In 2010, the Government of Canada formally apologised for the relocation and its treatment of the relocatees.

The community's English name comes from the Norwegian explorer Otto Sverdrup, who named the fiord "Grise Fiord" ("pig fiord") in 1899 after the grunting sounds of a nearby walrus herd.

Geography and life

The hamlet sits on the shore of Jones Sound, surrounded by mountains and glaciers. There are no roads connecting it to other communities; access is by air (Grise Fiord Airport) and by annual sealift in summer. Residents rely on hunting and fishing — narwhal, beluga, seal, walrus, and polar bear — alongside wage employment. The community has a school, a health centre, a co-operative store, and an RCMP detachment.
