Ralph Henry Baer (born Rudolf Heinrich Baer; March 8, 1922 – December 6, 2014) was a German-born American engineer and inventor widely regarded as the "Father of Video Games." Working at Sanders Associates in the 1960s, he conceived of playing games on a standard television set and led the team that built the "Brown Box," the prototype of the first home video game console.

Early life

Baer was born to a Jewish family in Germany; his family fled Nazi persecution to the Netherlands and then to the United States in 1938. He was drafted into the U.S. Army in 1943 and, after the Second World War, used the G.I. Bill to earn a degree in television engineering in 1949.

The Brown Box and the Odyssey

In 1966 Baer wrote a four-page proposal for a "game box" that would plug into a television. With colleagues Bill Harrison and Bill Rusch, he developed a series of prototypes culminating in the Brown Box in 1967–68, which played games including table tennis, handball and target shooting with a light gun. The design was licensed to Magnavox and released in 1972 as the Magnavox Odyssey, the first home video game console. The Odyssey's table tennis game inspired Atari's Pong, leading to landmark patent litigation that Baer's patents won.

Later inventions

Baer co-created the electronic memory game Simon (1978) with Howard Morrison, a major hit for Milton Bradley, along with other electronic toys and games. He held more than 150 patents.

Recognition

Baer received the National Medal of Technology in 2004 and the IEEE Edison Medal in 2014, and was inducted into the National Inventors Hall of Fame in 2010. His papers and prototypes are held by the Smithsonian Institution.
