Major chord

A major chord is a chord consisting of a root note, a major third, and a perfect fifth. In music theory, major chords are typically associated with a bright, stable, or happy sound, in contrast to the darker quality of minor chords.

Structure

A major triad contains three notes: the root, the note a major third (four semitones) above the root, and the note a perfect fifth (seven semitones) above the root. For example, a C major chord consists of the notes C, E, and G.

Notation

Major chords are commonly denoted by the root note alone (e.g., "C" for C major) or with "maj", "M", or a triangle symbol. In figured bass and jazz notation, extended major chords include the major seventh chord (maj7) and major ninth chord.

Use

The major chord is one of the foundational sonorities of Western tonal music, forming the tonic chord (I) in major keys and appearing throughout classical, popular, and folk music.
