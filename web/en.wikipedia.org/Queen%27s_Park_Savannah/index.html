Queen's Park Savannah

LocationPort of Spain, Trinidad and Tobago
AreaAbout 110 hectares (260 acres)
TypePublic park

The Queen's Park Savannah is a large park in Port of Spain, Trinidad and Tobago. Occupying about 110 hectares (260 acres) of flat grassland ringed by the Circular Road, it is one of the largest open urban spaces in the Caribbean and is often described as the world's largest traffic roundabout.

History

The land formed part of the Peschier family's sugar estate in the early 19th century. Governor Sir Ralph Woodford purchased it in 1817 for use as pasture and public recreation ground, and it gradually became the city's principal park. A grandstand was built for horse racing, which took place there from the mid-19th century.

Features

Around the Savannah stand several of Port of Spain's landmarks, including the "Magnificent Seven" colonial mansions along Maraval Road — among them Stollmeyer's Castle and the Archbishop's House — as well as the Queen's Park Cricket Club ground, founded in 1896, which hosted first-class and Test cricket.

Uses

The Savannah is used for sports, jogging, picnics, and food stalls selling coconut water and local snacks. It plays a central role in Trinidad Carnival, where masquerade bands cross the stage at the Grand Stand, and hosts concerts and national celebrations.
