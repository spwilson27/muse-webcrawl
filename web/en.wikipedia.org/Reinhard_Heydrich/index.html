Reinhard Heydrich

Reinhard Tristan Eugen Heydrich (7 March 1904 – 4 June 1942) was a high-ranking German SS and police official during the Nazi era and a principal architect of the Holocaust. He was chief of the Reich Security Main Office (RSHA), which included the Gestapo, Kripo, and SD, and chaired the January 1942 Wannsee Conference, which formalised plans for the deportation and genocide of all Jews in German-occupied Europe.

Early life and rise

Heydrich was born in Halle to a musical family; his father founded a conservatory. After service as a naval officer ended in dismissal in 1931, he joined the Nazi Party and the SS, where Heinrich Himmler tasked him with building an intelligence service, the Sicherheitsdienst (SD). He helped orchestrate the 1934 purge known as the Night of the Long Knives and, with the outbreak of war in 1939, organised the Einsatzgruppen mobile killing squads that followed the army into Poland and later the Soviet Union.

Bohemia and Moravia

In September 1941 Heydrich was appointed Acting Reich Protector of Bohemia and Moravia, where he combined brutal repression with increased food rations for workers — the policy of "whip and sugar" — while deporting Czech Jews and suppressing resistance.

Assassination

On 27 May 1942, Czechoslovak agents Jozef Gabčík and Jan Kubiš, trained by the British SOE, ambushed Heydrich in Prague (Operation Anthropoid). He died of his wounds on 4 June. The Nazis responded with savage reprisals, destroying the villages of Lidice and Ležáky and murdering thousands of Czechs. Heydrich was one of the highest-ranking Nazi officials to be assassinated during the war.
