Gain (electronics)

DefinitionRatio of output to input signal magnitude
UnitsDimensionless, or decibels (dB)
TypesVoltage, current, power gain

In electronics, gain is a measure of the ability of a two-port circuit — often an amplifier — to increase the power, voltage, or current of a signal from its input to its output. It is defined as the ratio of a signal quantity at the output port to the corresponding quantity at the input port. A gain greater than one represents amplification; a gain less than one is attenuation, sometimes described as a loss.

Definitions

Voltage gain is Av = Vout/Vin, current gain is Ai = Iout/Iin, and power gain is Ap = Pout/Pin. When input and output impedances are equal, power gain equals the square of voltage gain. In logarithmic units, power gain in decibels is 10 log10(Pout/Pin), while voltage and current gains are expressed as 20 log10 of their ratios.

Frequency dependence

Real amplifiers do not amplify all frequencies equally: gain varies with frequency, and the range over which it remains within 3 dB of its midband value defines the amplifier's bandwidth. The gain–bandwidth product of many amplifier stages is approximately constant. In feedback amplifiers, applying negative feedback reduces and stabilises gain while extending bandwidth and reducing distortion.

Antennas

The term also applies to antennas, where gain describes how strongly an antenna directs radiation in a given direction relative to a reference (an isotropic radiator, dBi, or a dipole, dBd).
