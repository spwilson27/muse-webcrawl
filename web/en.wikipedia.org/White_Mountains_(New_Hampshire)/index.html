White Mountains (New Hampshire)

The White Mountains are a mountain range covering about a quarter of the state of New Hampshire and a small portion of western Maine in the United States. They are part of the northern Appalachian Mountains and include Mount Washington, the highest peak in the north-eastern United States.

Geography

The range contains several sub-ranges, the most famous being the Presidential Range, whose peaks are named after US presidents. Forty-eight peaks exceed 4,000 feet and are collectively known as the "four-thousand footers", a popular goal for hikers. The White Mountain National Forest, established in 1918, covers most of the range.

Weather

The White Mountains are noted for severe weather. The summit of Mount Washington held the world record for a directly measured surface wind gust — 372 km/h (231 mph) in 1934 — for over sixty years, and its summit observatory describes conditions as among the worst in the world.

Tourism and recreation

The mountains attract hikers, skiers and leaf-peepers. The Appalachian Trail crosses the range, and the Mount Washington Cog Railway, opened in 1869, was the world's first mountain-climbing cog railway. Notable notches (passes) include Crawford Notch, Pinkham Notch and Franconia Notch, the latter formerly the site of the Old Man of the Mountain rock formation, which collapsed in 2003.
