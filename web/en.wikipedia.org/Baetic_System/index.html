Baetic System

The Baetic System (Spanish: Sistema Bético) is a mountain system in southern and eastern Spain, extending from the province of Cádiz in the southwest to the Valencian Community in the northeast, with a continuation beneath the Alboran Sea toward the Balearic Islands. Together with the Rif mountains of Morocco it forms the westernmost part of the Alpine mountain system around the Mediterranean.

Geology

The ranges were raised during the Alpine orogeny as the African plate pressed against the Iberian microplate, folding and thrusting Mesozoic and Cenozoic sediments. Geologists divide the system into an internal zone of metamorphic rocks near the coast and external zones of folded sedimentary rocks, including limestones that form dramatic karst landscapes such as the Torcal de Antequera.

Principal ranges

The system comprises many individual sierras, the most famous being the Sierra Nevada, which contains Mulhacén, the highest peak of mainland Spain at 3,479 metres. Other ranges include the Sierra de Grazalema, noted for its heavy rainfall, the Sierras de Cazorla, and the Sierra Morena's eastern neighbours. The ranges border the Guadalquivir valley and the Intrabetic depressions, and influence the climate of Andalusia by trapping moist Atlantic air.
