Cough

SpecialtyPulmonology, otorhinolaryngology
ICD-10R05
TypesAcute, subacute, chronic; productive or dry

A cough is a sudden expulsion of air through the large breathing passages, intended to clear the airways of secretions, irritants, foreign particles, and microbes. It is one of the most common reasons for visiting a doctor. A cough can be voluntary or, more usually, a reflex arc involving receptors in the airways, the vagus nerve, a medullary cough centre, and the respiratory muscles.

Mechanism

The cough reflex has three phases: an inspiratory phase, a compressive phase in which the glottis closes and intrathoracic pressure rises sharply, and an expulsive phase in which the glottis opens and air is expelled at high velocity, carrying material out of the airways.

Causes

Acute cough (under three weeks) is most often caused by viral upper respiratory infections such as the common cold. Subacute cough frequently follows an infection. Chronic cough (over eight weeks in adults) is commonly due to upper airway cough syndrome (post-nasal drip), asthma, gastro-oesophageal reflux disease, smoking, or ACE-inhibitor medication. Serious causes include pneumonia, tuberculosis, chronic bronchitis, heart failure, and lung cancer, and a persistent cough warrants medical evaluation.

Treatment

Treatment is directed at the underlying cause. Symptomatic remedies include demulcents such as honey, expectorants, and antitussives such as dextromethorphan or codeine, though evidence for over-the-counter cough medicines is limited. Antibiotics are not indicated for uncomplicated viral infections.
