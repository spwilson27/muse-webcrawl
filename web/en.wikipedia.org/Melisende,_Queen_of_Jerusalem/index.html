Melisende, Queen of Jerusalem

Melisende (c. 1105 – 11 September 1161) was Queen of Jerusalem from 1131 until her death, the eldest daughter of King Baldwin II and Morphia of Melitene, and the first woman to inherit and rule the crusader Kingdom of Jerusalem in her own right.

Reign

Melisende was designated her father's heir and married Fulk V of Anjou in 1129. After Baldwin II's death in 1131, she and Fulk were crowned joint rulers. Fulk's attempt to exclude her from power provoked a revolt by her supporters under her cousin Hugh II of Jaffa in 1134; thereafter she was restored to full partnership in government, appearing in charters and coinage as co-ruler. After Fulk's death in a hunting accident in 1143, Melisende ruled as queen and regent for her son Baldwin III, coronating him as co-king.

Conflict and later life

When Baldwin III came of age, mother and son clashed over power, and in 1152 the kingdom was briefly divided before Baldwin forced her retirement from active rule, though she retained Nablus and continued to advise. A celebrated patron of the Church and the arts, Melisende commissioned the Melisende Psalter, founded the convent of Bethany, and supported the Church of the Holy Sepulchre. She died in 1161 and was buried in the Church of St Mary of the Valley of Jehosaphat.
