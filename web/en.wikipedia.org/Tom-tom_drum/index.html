Tom-tom drum

A tom-tom drum (also written tom tom) is a cylindrical drum without snares, found in drum kits, orchestras, marching bands and many traditional musics around the world. Tom-toms produce a pitched but indefinite tone, higher or lower depending on their diameter and depth, and they are used for rhythmic fills, accents and melodic patterns in drumming. In a typical drum kit they sit above the bass drum (rack toms) and on legs or a stand beside it (the floor tom).

Types

Modern drum kits usually include two or three tom-toms. Rack toms are mounted over the bass drum, commonly 10–13 inches in diameter; the floor tom (14–18 inches) stands on three legs to the drummer's side. Concert toms, used in orchestras and marching percussion, may be single-headed, while kit toms are usually double-headed with a batter head struck by the stick and a resonant head below. Rototoms can be tuned by rotating the drum in its frame, and octobans are long, narrow, high-pitched toms used for melodic effects.

Construction and tuning

Shells are traditionally built from plies of maple, birch or mahogany, though acrylic, fibreglass and metal shells exist. Heads are tensioned evenly by lugs around the hoop; the interval between the batter and resonant heads shapes sustain and pitch bend. Drummers tune sets of toms to relative pitches — often fourths or fifths apart — so fills descend or ascend across the kit in a musical sequence.

History

The term "tom-tom" was borrowed from Anglo-Indian and Sinhala usage for hand drums, and was applied by early jazz drummers to the Chinese and Native American drums incorporated into the first drum sets. Tunable, purpose-built toms became standard in American swing kits of the 1930s under manufacturers such as Ludwig, Slingerland and Gretsch, and the multi-tom setups of rock and fusion drummers from the 1960s onward made the tom-tom a lead voice of the drum kit. Similar instruments — from the Brazilian tom-tom of samba batteries to the tuned toms of Chinese and Korean ensembles — appear in many drumming traditions.
