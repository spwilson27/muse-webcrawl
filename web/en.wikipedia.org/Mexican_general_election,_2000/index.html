2000 Mexican general election

Date: 2 July 2000
Winner: Vicente Fox (PAN)
Runner-up: Francisco Labastida (PRI)
Turnout: ~64%

General elections were held in Mexico on 2 July 2000. The presidential election was won by Vicente Fox of the National Action Party (PAN), ending 71 years of uninterrupted rule by the Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI) and marking Mexico's transition to competitive democracy.

Results

Fox, a former Coca-Cola executive and governor of Guanajuato running for the Alliance for Change, won about 42.5% of the vote. The PRI's Francisco Labastida took about 36.1%, and Cuauhtémoc Cárdenas of the PRD-led Alliance for Mexico about 16.6%. President Ernesto Zedillo promptly acknowledged Fox's victory on election night, ensuring a peaceful transfer of power on 1 December 2000.

Significance

The election followed electoral reforms that made the Federal Electoral Institute (IFE) genuinely autonomous, professionalizing vote counting after the disputed 1988 election. It is widely regarded as the first fully free and fair presidential election in modern Mexican history and a landmark of the "third wave" of democratization in Latin America. Congressional elections the same day left no party with a majority, beginning an era of divided government.
