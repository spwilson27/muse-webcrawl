Line of Control

The Line of Control (LoC) is a military control line between the Indian- and Pakistani-controlled parts of the former princely state of Jammu and Kashmir — a line which does not constitute a legally recognized international boundary, but serves as the de facto border. It was established as part of the Simla Agreement at the end of the Indo-Pakistani War of 1971. Both parties agreed to respect the line without prejudice to their differing recognized positions.

The line originally marked the ceasefire line of the 1947–48 Indo-Pakistani War, drawn at Karachi in 1949 under United Nations supervision. After the 1971 war, the two countries renamed it the Line of Control and pledged to resolve their differences peacefully. The Indian part of the former princely state is made up of the union territories of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh, while the Pakistani part consists of Azad Kashmir and Gilgit-Baltistan.

Geography

The LoC runs for roughly 740 kilometres (460 miles), from the International Border near Jammu in the south to the NJ9842 grid point in the north, beyond which begins the Line of Actual Control and the Siachen Glacier. It passes through mountainous terrain in northern Kashmir, including peaks above 5,000 metres. India erected a lengthy fence, the Kashmir barrier, along much of its side of the line to prevent infiltration.

Crossings

Designated crossing points have at times included Chakothi–Salamabad (Muzaffarabad–Srinagar), Rawalakot–Poonch, and the Wagah–Attari route in Punjab. Passenger bus services between Srinagar and Muzaffarabad began in 2005, the first such link in nearly sixty years, though cross-line travel and trade have been repeatedly suspended following tensions and attacks.
