Hook-and-loop fastener

A hook-and-loop fastener is a textile closure made of two components: a strip covered in tiny hooks and a strip covered in tiny loops. Pressed together, the hooks catch in the loops and bind strongly; peeled apart, they separate with a characteristic ripping sound. The best-known brand is Velcro, a name often used generically.

Invention

Swiss engineer George de Mestral invented the fastener after examining burdock burrs clinging to his dog's fur following a walk in 1941. Under a microscope he saw hundreds of tiny hooks that grabbed loops of fabric and fur. He spent years developing a practical manufacturing process, patenting the invention in 1955.

Uses

Hook-and-loop tape is used in clothing and shoes, blood-pressure cuffs, luggage, and cable ties. NASA adopted it extensively — for holding equipment in zero gravity and inside helmets — which greatly raised its public profile during the Apollo era. Strengths include ease of use and adjustability; weaknesses are lint accumulation, noise, and limited load-bearing strength.
