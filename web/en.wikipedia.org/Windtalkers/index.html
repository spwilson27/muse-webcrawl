Windtalkers is a 2002 American war film directed by John Woo, starring Nicolas Cage, about the Navajo code talkers of the United States Marine Corps during the Second World War.

Plot

Cage plays Sergeant Joe Enders, a Marine scarred by combat in the Solomon Islands, who is assigned to protect Private Ben Yahzee (Adam Beach), a Navajo code talker deployed at the Battle of Saipan in 1944. His orders are to protect the code at all costs — even, implicitly, by killing Yahzee rather than letting him be captured. The film dramatises the friendship between the two men and the contribution of the code talkers, whose Navajo-based code was never broken by the Japanese.

Reception

The film performed modestly at the box office, grossing about $77 million against a large production budget, and received mixed reviews: the battle scenes were praised, but critics faulted the focus on Cage's character over the Navajo Marines themselves. The real code talkers' story had remained classified until 1968; the surviving original 29 code talkers were later awarded Congressional Gold Medals in 2001.
