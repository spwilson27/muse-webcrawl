Bundesrat

Type: Upper house (federal council)
Seats: 69
Seat: Prussian House of Lords building, Berlin
Established: 23 May 1949

The Bundesrat (German for "Federal Council") is the legislative body that represents the sixteen states (Länder) of Germany at the federal level. Together with the Bundestag, it participates in federal legislation and in matters concerning the European Union. Its members are not elected directly; they are members of the state governments, which appoint and recall them, and each state delegation must cast its votes as a bloc.

Composition

The Bundesrat has 69 votes. Each state has at least three votes; states with more than two million inhabitants have four, those with more than six million have five, and those with more than seven million have six. North Rhine-Westphalia, Bavaria, Baden-Württemberg, and Lower Saxony hold six votes each, while the smallest states, such as Bremen, hold three. The presidency of the Bundesrat rotates annually among the minister-presidents of the states in an order fixed by population (the Königstein agreement).

Powers

Laws affecting state interests — especially those touching state finances or administration, roughly half of all federal legislation — require the Bundesrat's consent and can be vetoed absolutely. For other laws, the Bundesrat may lodge an objection that the Bundestag can override. A joint mediation committee (Vermittlungsausschuss) resolves disagreements. The Bundesrat also elects half the judges of the Federal Constitutional Court and participates in federal supervision of the states.

History

The institution descends from the Bundesrat of the North German Confederation (1867) and the German Empire, and from the Reichsrat of the Weimar Republic. The present body was created by the Basic Law of 1949 and met first in Bonn before moving to Berlin in 2000, into the former Prussian House of Lords building.
