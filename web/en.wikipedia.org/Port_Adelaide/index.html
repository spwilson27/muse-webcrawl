Port Adelaide

Port Adelaide is a port-side region of Adelaide, South Australia, about 14 kilometres (8.7 mi) northwest of the Adelaide city centre. It was the main port of the colony of South Australia from its founding and is home to one of Australia's best-preserved 19th-century port precincts, with a large collection of heritage-listed pubs, warehouses, and civic buildings. The wider local government area, the City of Port Adelaide Enfield, is one of the largest in metropolitan Adelaide.

History

Before European settlement the area was home to the Kaurna people. Colonel William Light selected the port site on the Port River inlet in 1836–37, though its mangrove swamps and distance from the city led some settlers to call it "Port Misery". Despite this, it became the colony's gateway: migrants, mail, and cargo arrived here throughout the 19th century, and copper from Burra and grain from the wheat belt left through its wharves. Shipbuilding and repair yards flourished, and the township gained a reputation for its many hotels and its strong maritime labour movement. Containerisation in the 20th century moved much shipping to deeper facilities at Outer Harbor, while the inner port saw gradual decline followed by heritage protection and urban renewal programs.

Attractions and culture

The port is home to the South Australian Maritime Museum, the National Railway Museum, and the South Australian Aviation Museum. A resident pod of Indo-Pacific bottlenose dolphins lives in the Port River, protected within the Adelaide Dolphin Sanctuary. The Port Adelaide Football Club, founded in 1870 and nicknamed the Magpies (and "Power" in the AFL), is one of Australia's oldest and most successful football clubs.
