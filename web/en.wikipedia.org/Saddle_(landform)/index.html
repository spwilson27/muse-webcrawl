Saddle (landform)

The saddle between two hills or mountains is the region surrounding the saddle point, the lowest point on the ridge between two summits and the highest point on the route between them. It is also known as a col, gap, or notch, and is a feature typical of mountainous terrain. A saddle is the point on the landscape that is simultaneously the highest point on the line of descent between two valleys and the lowest point on the ridge between two peaks.

Terminology

Names for saddles vary by region and language: in the Alps and elsewhere in Europe they are frequently called a col (French), passo (Italian), or Joch (German); in Britain, terms such as bealach (Scottish Gaelic) and bwlch (Welsh) are used. Where a road or railway crosses a saddle, it is generally known as a mountain pass.

Topography

In topography, the saddle is a classic example of a saddle point in mathematics: the surface curves upward in one direction (along the ridge) and downward in the perpendicular direction (toward the valleys). Saddles are important in determining the topographic prominence of peaks: the prominence of a summit is measured relative to the key col connecting it to higher terrain.
