Fishing net

A fishing net is a net used for fishing. Nets are devices made from fibres woven in a grid-like structure. Some fishing nets are also called fish traps — for example fyke nets, which are cage-like. Nets are usually formed by tying a relatively thin thread. Early nets were woven from grasses, flaxes and other fibrous plant material; later cotton was used, and modern nets are made of synthetic polyamides such as nylon.

History

Fishing nets date to prehistoric times; a net found with fishing tackle in Finland, the Antrea Net, is dated to about 8300 BC. Nets were used by ancient Egyptians, Greeks and Romans, and by Indigenous peoples throughout the Americas and the Pacific.

Types

Principal types include seine nets, drawn around a school of fish; gill nets, in which fish are entangled by their gills in a vertical wall of mesh; trawl nets, towed behind vessels; cast nets, thrown by hand over shallow water; lift nets, raised from below; and fyke and trap nets that guide fish into enclosures. Mesh size determines the size of fish caught and is regulated to protect juvenile stocks.

Environmental issues

Lost or abandoned nets — "ghost nets" — continue to catch fish, turtles, seabirds and marine mammals and are a major source of ocean plastic pollution. Mitigation includes biodegradable materials, net retrieval programmes and restrictions on drifting fish aggregating devices and indiscriminate drift nets.
