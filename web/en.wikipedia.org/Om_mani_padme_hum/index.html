Om mani padme hum

Sanskritॐ मणि पद्मे हूं (oṃ maṇi padme hūṃ)
TraditionMahayana and Tibetan Buddhism

Om mani padme hum (Sanskrit: ॐ मणि पद्मे हूं, IPA: [õːː mɐɳɪ pɐdmeː ɦũː]) is the six-syllabled Sanskrit mantra particularly associated with the four-armed Shadakshari form of Avalokiteshvara, the bodhisattva of compassion. It first appeared in the Mahayana Karandavyuha Sutra, where it is also referred to as the sadaksara (six syllables) and the paramahrdaya, or "innermost heart" of Avalokiteshvara.

In this text the mantra is seen as the condensed form of all the Buddhist teachings. The first word Om is a sacred syllable in Indian religions. The word Mani means "jewel" or "bead", Padme is the "lotus flower" (the Buddhist sacred flower), and Hum represents the spirit of enlightenment.

Meaning

The mantra is commonly translated as "praise to the jewel in the lotus", though this rendering is contested by scholars; the middle part of the mantra, maṇipadme, is often interpreted as a locative ("in the lotus jewel") or as the name of a being. The 14th Dalai Lama has described the mantra as a means of transforming one's impure body, speech and mind into the pure exalted body, speech and mind of a Buddha, with each syllable purifying one of the six realms of existence.

Practice

Because of its ubiquity in Tibetan Buddhism, the mantra is found carved into rocks as mani stones, printed on prayer flags, and embossed on prayer wheels, whose spinning is believed to accumulate merit equivalent to reciting the mantra aloud. Pilgrims recite it while circumambulating stupas, and it is recited countless times by practitioners using prayer beads (malas) of 108 beads.
