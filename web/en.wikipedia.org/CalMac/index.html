Caledonian MacBrayne

Caledonian MacBrayne, usually shortened to CalMac, is the principal operator of passenger and vehicle ferries to the Hebridean and Clyde islands of Scotland. It operates 33 vessels on 28 routes serving 52 ports and harbours, carrying several million passengers each year.

History

The company's origins lie in the 1851 shipping business of David Hutcheson & Co., which passed to David MacBrayne and later became David MacBrayne Ltd. In 1973 MacBrayne's Clyde and west Highland services were merged with the Caledonian Steam Packet Company to form Caledonian MacBrayne. In 2006 the undertaking was split: Caledonian Maritime Assets Ltd (CMAL) owns the vessels and ports, while CalMac Ferries Ltd operates the services under contract to the Scottish Government. CalMac also operates the Northern Isles ferry services under a separate contract (marketed as NorthLink Ferries) since 2012 in a later period under Serco; CalMac itself retained the Clyde and Hebrides Ferry Services (CHFS) contract.

Fleet and livery

CalMac vessels are painted with black hulls, white superstructure, and red funnels bearing the lion rampant emblem. Major vessels include MV Caledonian Isles, MV Isle of Lewis, MV Loch Seaforth, MV Finlaggan, MV Hebrides, and the dual-fuel MV Glen Sannox, which entered service on the Arran route in 2025 after a long-delayed construction at Ferguson Marine.

Routes

Routes radiate from mainland ports such as Oban, Uig, Mallaig, Ullapool, Scrabster, Wemyss Bay, and Ardrossan to islands including Arran, Islay, Mull, Skye, Lewis, North and South Uist, Barra, Tiree, Coll, and the Small Isles. Many crossings are designated "lifeline" services essential to island communities.
