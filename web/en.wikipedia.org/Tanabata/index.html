Tanabata

Tanabata
Observed byJapan
Date7 July (some regions: August, by lunar calendar)
Also calledStar Festival

Tanabata (Japanese: たなばた or 七夕, "Evening of the seventh"), also known as the Star Festival, is a Japanese festival originating from the Chinese Qixi Festival. It celebrates the meeting of the deities Orihime and Hikoboshi, represented by the stars Vega and Altair, lovers separated by the Milky Way who meet only once a year on the seventh day of the seventh lunar month.

Customs

People write wishes on small strips of paper called tanzaku and hang them on bamboo branches, sometimes with other decorations. The bamboo and wishes are often set afloat on a river or burned after the festival.

Sendai Tanabata

The largest celebration is the Sendai Tanabata Matsuri, held in August, famous for its elaborate hanging streamers (fukinagashi) displayed through the city's shopping arcades.
