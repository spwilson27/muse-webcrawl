The PalmPilot was a line of personal digital assistants (PDAs) produced by Palm, Inc., beginning in 1996. Small, inexpensive and focused on a few core tasks — calendar, contacts, to-do lists and notes — synchronised with a desktop computer, the PalmPilot succeeded where earlier handhelds such as Apple's Newton had struggled, and it defined the PDA category for a decade.

Development

The device was designed by Jeff Hawkins, who founded Palm Computing in 1992. Hawkins famously carried a block of wood cut to the device's intended size in his pocket to test the form factor during design. Rather than recognising free handwriting, the Pilot used Graffiti, a simplified set of single-stroke gestures that users learned quickly and the machine could recognise reliably. The first models, the Pilot 1000 (128 KB of memory) and Pilot 5000 (512 KB), were launched in March 1996 by U.S. Robotics, which had acquired Palm; after 3Com acquired U.S. Robotics, the devices were renamed PalmPilot following a trademark dispute with the Pilot Pen Corporation.

Success and legacy

The PalmPilot sold a million units within eighteen months, faster at the time than the VCR or mobile phone. Successors included the Palm III, V (with a slim metal case), and colour models, and Palm OS was licensed to makers including Handspring, Sony and IBM. The PDA market was eventually absorbed by the smartphone, in whose evolution Palm participated with the Treo line and, in 2009, the webOS-based Palm Pre.
