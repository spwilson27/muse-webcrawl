The Northern Ireland Assembly (Irish: Tionól Thuaisceart Éireann; Ulster Scots: Norlin Airlan Assemblie) is the devolved, unicameral legislature of Northern Ireland. It has power to legislate in areas not explicitly reserved to the Parliament of the United Kingdom, and to appoint the Northern Ireland Executive, the region's devolved government.

Origins

The Assembly was established as part of the Belfast (Good Friday) Agreement of 1998, which ended the period of conflict known as the Troubles. Its members — Members of the Legislative Assembly (MLAs) — are elected by the single transferable vote from multi-member constituencies. Since 2017 there have been 90 MLAs, five returned from each of 18 constituencies.

Power-sharing

Uniquely, the Assembly operates on a mandatory coalition basis designed to guarantee cross-community power-sharing. MLAs register a designation as "unionist", "nationalist" or "other". The First Minister and deputy First Minister — a joint office of equal status — are nominated by the largest parties of the largest designations, and certain key decisions require cross-community support. Any 30 MLAs may also lodge a petition of concern, triggering a cross-community vote on a measure.

Suspensions

Devolution has been interrupted several times. The Assembly was suspended by the UK government between 2002 and 2007 over disputes arising from the peace process, restored after the St Andrews Agreement, and it collapsed again from 2017 to 2020 after a breakdown between the governing parties, and from 2022 until February 2024 amid disputes over post-Brexit trading arrangements.
