Yamagata Aritomo

Prince Yamagata Aritomo

Prince Yamagata Aritomo (山縣 有朋, 14 June 1838 – 1 February 1922) was a Japanese statesman and military commander. He was one of the most prominent of the genrō, the unofficial oligarchs who advised the Emperor in Meiji and Taishō Japan, and he twice served as Prime Minister of Japan (1889–1891 and 1898–1900). Yamagata is regarded as the architect of the modern Imperial Japanese Army and a principal shaper of Japan's military and political institutions.

Born in Hagi in Chōshū Domain to a low-ranking samurai family, Yamagata participated in the pro-imperial movement that led to the Meiji Restoration. He commanded forces in the Boshin War and afterwards travelled to Europe to study Western military systems. On his return, he organised the new conscript Imperial Japanese Army, defeating the Satsuma Rebellion led by Saigō Takamori in 1877.

Military career

As Minister of War and later Chief of the Army General Staff, Yamagata professionalised the army along Prussian lines, promoting merit-based advancement and political independence for the military. He commanded the Japanese First Army in the First Sino-Japanese War (1894–1895) and played a leading organisational role during the Russo-Japanese War (1904–1905).

Political career

Yamagata served as Home Minister, where he established Japan's local government and police systems, and as Prime Minister in two cabinets. A deeply conservative figure, he opposed party government and worked to insulate the military and bureaucracy from parliamentary control, notably through the rule that service ministers be active-duty officers. His influence as the most powerful of the genrō endured until his death in 1922.
