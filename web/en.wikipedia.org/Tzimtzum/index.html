Tzimtzum

Tzimtzum (Hebrew: צמצום, "contraction" or "constriction") is a concept in Lurianic Kabbalah describing how God contracted His infinite light (Ohr Ein Sof) in order to make room for the creation of the world.

Doctrine

According to the teaching of Isaac Luria (the Ari), before creation the infinite divine light filled all existence. For a finite world to exist, God "withdrew" Himself, leaving an empty space (the chalal hapanui) into which a ray of light (the kav) could enter and the process of emanation and creation could unfold.

Interpretations

Rabbinic authorities have debated whether the tzimtzum should be understood literally or as a metaphor for concealment: Chabad Hasidism teaches that God's presence never actually departed, and that the withdrawal is only from the perspective of the created beings.

Consequences

The doctrine is closely linked to the "breaking of the vessels" (Shevirat HaKelim), in which the divine light shattered the vessels meant to contain it, scattering sparks that humanity can redeem through observance and prayer (tikkun olam).
