John Knox

John Knox (c. 1513 – 24 November 1572) was a Scottish minister, theologian, and writer who was a leader of Scotland's Reformation. He founded the Presbyterian Church of Scotland and is considered one of the most influential figures in Scottish history.

Early life and exile

Born near Haddington, Knox was ordained a Catholic priest and worked as a notary and tutor. Influenced by the executed reformer George Wishart, he joined the movement at St Andrews Castle and was captured by French forces, serving as a galley slave. After his release he preached in England under Edward VI and later in Frankfurt and Geneva, where he encountered John Calvin, whom he called his mentor.

Scottish Reformation

Returning to Scotland in 1559, Knox led the Protestant nobility against the Catholic regent Mary of Guise. In 1560 the Scottish Parliament adopted a Reformed confession of faith and broke with the papacy. Knox drafted the First Book of Discipline and the Book of Common Order, and served as minister of St Giles' Cathedral in Edinburgh. His First Blast of the Trumpet Against the Monstrous Regiment of Women (1558) attacked female rulers, and his confrontations with Mary, Queen of Scots, are legendary.

Legacy

Knox's History of the Reformation in Scotland remains a primary source. His advocacy of education for all and of resistance to unjust rulers shaped Scottish Presbyterianism and influenced democratic thought.
