Second London Naval Treaty

The Second London Naval Treaty was an international agreement on naval arms limitation signed on 25 March 1936 by the United States, the United Kingdom and France, following the Second London Naval Conference of 1935–36.

Terms

Unlike the Washington Treaty of 1922 and the first London Treaty of 1930, the 1936 treaty set no quantitative limits on fleet sizes — Japan had withdrawn from the conference and Italy declined to sign. Instead it imposed qualitative limits: capital ships limited to 35,000 tons displacement and 14-inch guns (with an escalator clause permitting 16-inch guns if others did not agree by 1937), aircraft carriers to 23,000 tons, and light cruisers to 8,000 tons with 6.1-inch guns. Submarine warfare rules were also restated, requiring submarines to observe prize rules toward merchant ships.

Outcome

The escalator clause was invoked in 1938, raising the battleship limit to 45,000 tons. The treaty system collapsed with the approach of the Second World War, and the agreement effectively lapsed by 1939.
