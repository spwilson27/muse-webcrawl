Mascara

Mascara is a cosmetic applied to the eyelashes to darken, lengthen, thicken, and define them. It is typically sold as a liquid in a tube with a brush applicator wand, in colors most commonly black or brown.

History

Eye darkening with kohl dates to ancient Egypt. Modern mascara is credited to the 19th century: perfumer Eugène Rimmel popularized a petroleum-jelly-and-coal-dust preparation in Europe, while in 1915 T. L. Williams created a similar product for his sister Mabel — the origin of Maybelline. Cake mascara applied with a wet brush dominated until Helena Rubinstein and others introduced the wand-in-tube format in the late 1950s.

Formulation and types

Mascara consists of pigments (iron oxides or carbon black), waxes, oils, and film-forming polymers. Varieties include volumizing, lengthening, curling, and waterproof formulas, the latter using volatile silicones and stronger film formers that require oil-based removers. Mascara has a short recommended shelf life — commonly three months — because the wand repeatedly introduces bacteria to the tube; eye infections are the main safety concern.
