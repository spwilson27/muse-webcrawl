The San Francisco Botanical Garden, formerly the Strybing Arboretum, is a 55-acre botanical garden in Golden Gate Park in San Francisco, California. It displays nearly 8,000 different kinds of plants from around the world, with particular strengths in magnolias, cloud-forest species from Mesoamerica and Southeast Asia, and plants from Mediterranean-climate regions.

History

The garden was planned in the nineteenth century by park superintendent John McLaren but not funded until a bequest from Helene Strybing in the 1920s. Ground was broken in 1937 and the arboretum opened in 1940.

Collections

Areas include the Garden of Fragrance, a Moon-Viewing Garden, an Ancient Plant Garden, the Mesoamerican Cloud Forest, a redwood grove, and one of the most significant magnolia collections outside China. The Helen Crocker Russell Library of Horticulture, the largest horticultural library in northern California, is located at the main entrance.
