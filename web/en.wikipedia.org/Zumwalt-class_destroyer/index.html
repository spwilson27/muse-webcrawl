The Zumwalt-class destroyer is a class of three United States Navy guided-missile destroyers designed as multi-mission surface combatants with an emphasis on land attack. The class was the product of the DD(X) programme and is named for Admiral Elmo Zumwalt, a former Chief of Naval Operations. With a displacement of more than 15,000 tons, the ships are the largest destroyers ever built for the US Navy.

USS Zumwalt (DDG 1000), lead ship of the class, with its distinctive wave-piercing tumblehome hull.

Design

The class features a wave-piercing tumblehome hull and composite deckhouse designed to minimise radar cross-section, giving the ships a radar signature comparable to a small fishing boat. An Integrated Power System provides about 78 megawatts of electrical power for propulsion, sensors and weapons. The ships were built around the Advanced Gun System (AGS), two 155 mm guns intended to fire the Long Range Land Attack Projectile; when that projectile was cancelled on cost grounds, the Navy re-purposed the class, and plans call for the gun mounts to be replaced by launchers for the Conventional Prompt Strike hypersonic missile.

Ships in class

The class comprises USS Zumwalt (DDG 1000), commissioned in 2016; USS Michael Monsoor (DDG 1001), commissioned in 2019; and USS Lyndon B. Johnson (DDG 1002), launched in 2018. The programme was truncated from a planned 32 ships to three, and total programme costs exceeded $22 billion, making each ship one of the most expensive destroyers ever built.
