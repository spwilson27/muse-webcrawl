Wedgwood is an English fine china, porcelain and luxury accessories manufacturer founded on 1 May 1759 by potter and entrepreneur Josiah Wedgwood. Based in Staffordshire, it became one of the most famous names in ceramics, renowned for its creamware ("Queen's Ware") and for jasperware — the matte stoneware, most famously in "Wedgwood blue" with white classical reliefs, introduced in the 1770s.

History

Josiah Wedgwood combined scientific experiment in ceramic bodies and glazes with innovative marketing — showrooms, catalogues in several languages, and royal patronage — to build a global brand. His Etruria works (1769) applied factory division of labour to pottery production. The company supplied services to Catherine the Great of Russia and to royal and presidential households worldwide.

Later years

In 1987 Wedgwood merged with Waterford Crystal to form Waterford Wedgwood, which entered administration in 2009. The brand was acquired by KPS Capital Partners and in 2015 by the Finnish Fiskars Group. Production of fine bone china continues at Barlaston, Stoke-on-Trent, and the Wedgwood Museum collection is recognised by UNESCO for its documentary importance.

Notable wares

Besides jasperware, Wedgwood is known for black basalt ware, Queen's Ware creamware, and for the anti-slavery medallion "Am I Not a Man and a Brother" (1787), one of the most widely reproduced images of the abolition movement.
