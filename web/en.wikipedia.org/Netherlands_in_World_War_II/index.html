Netherlands in World War II

The Netherlands in World War II covers the experiences of the Netherlands and its colonial empire from the German invasion of May 1940 to liberation in May 1945. Although the Netherlands had declared neutrality, Germany invaded on 10 May 1940. After the bombing of Rotterdam, the Dutch government capitulated on 15 May; Queen Wilhelmina and the government escaped to London, forming a government-in-exile. The country remained under German occupation, administered by Reichskommissar Arthur Seyss-Inquart, until the final months of the war.

Occupation and persecution

Occupation policies grew harsher over time. Dutch Jews were progressively isolated, and from 1942 deportations to extermination camps began; of roughly 140,000 Jews in the Netherlands, about 102,000 — nearly three-quarters — were murdered, the highest proportion in Western Europe. Among those deported was Anne Frank, whose diary became world-famous after the war. Dutch workers were conscripted for forced labour in Germany (Arbeitseinsatz). Resistance took forms ranging from the February Strike of 1941 in Amsterdam — a protest against the first anti-Jewish raids — to armed underground groups, illegal newspapers, and hiding people (onderduikers), while a minority collaborated, including Dutch volunteers in the Waffen-SS.

Liberation and the Hunger Winter

Allied forces liberated the south of the country in autumn 1944, including during Operation Market Garden, the failed airborne attempt to seize Rhine bridges at Arnhem. The north and west remained occupied through the Hongerwinter (Hunger Winter) of 1944–45, when a German embargo and a harsh winter caused severe famine that killed an estimated 18,000–22,000 people. Canadian forces played the leading role in the final liberation; German forces in the Netherlands surrendered on 5 May 1945, celebrated today as Liberation Day.

The East Indies

The Dutch East Indies were occupied by Japan from 1942 after the Battle of the Java Sea. Dutch and Indonesian civilians were interned in camps, and the war accelerated the Indonesian independence movement; Sukarno and Hatta proclaimed Indonesian independence on 17 August 1945, leading to the Indonesian National Revolution before Dutch recognition of sovereignty in 1949.
