Tagma (military)

The tagmata (singular tagma) were the elite professional regiments of the Byzantine army, created in the 8th century as a central, loyal striking force based in and around Constantinople, in contrast to the provincial thematic armies.

History

Emperor Constantine V formed the first tagmata around 765 from loyal troops, partly as a counterweight to the themes (military provinces) whose generals often rebelled. The core regiments included the Scholai, Exkoubitoi, Arithmos (Vigla), and Hikanatoi, supplemented later by foreign guard units such as the Varangian Guard.

Organisation

Each tagma was commanded by a domestikos or similar officer and numbered from a few hundred to several thousand men.quartered near the capital, they accompanied the emperor on campaign and served as the army's heavy cavalry core.

Decline

The tagmata suffered heavy losses at the Battle of Manzikert in 1071 and during the Fourth Crusade's sack of Constantinople in 1204, after which the system faded as Byzantine military structures changed.
