Halite

FormulaNaCl
Crystal systemCubic
Hardness2.5 (Mohs)
ColourColourless or white; also blue, purple, pink

Halite, commonly known as rock salt, is the mineral form of sodium chloride (NaCl). It forms isometric crystals, typically colourless or white, and occurs in extensive sedimentary beds where ancient seas or salt lakes evaporated. It is mined for table salt, food preservation, chemical feedstock, and de-icing roads.

Occurrence

Major deposits occur in the Silurian Salina Group beneath the Great Lakes region of North America, in Cheshire in England, in the Zechstein basin of northern Europe, and at Wieliczka in Poland, where salt has been mined since prehistoric times. Halite has a distinctive salty taste, perfect cubic cleavage, and low hardness, distinguishing it from similar minerals such as sylvite.
