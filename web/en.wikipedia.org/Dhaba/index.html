Dhaba

A dhaba is a roadside restaurant in the Indian subcontinent, mainly found along national and state highways. Dhabas serve local cuisine, and traditionally provided food and rest for travellers and truck drivers. The word originates from the Punjab region.

History and character

Dhabas began as simple eateries catering to truck drivers travelling long distances on India's highways, offering inexpensive, hearty food and a place to rest. Seating is often outdoors, traditionally on woven cots (charpoys), and cooking is done in the open, frequently in a clay tandoor oven.

Cuisine

Punjabi dhabas are famous for dishes such as dal makhani, sarson da saag with makki di roti, tandoori roti and naan, stuffed parathas served with white butter, and sweet or salted lassi. Food is typically prepared fresh, generously spiced, and served in a homestyle manner quite distinct from urban restaurant cooking.

In popular culture

The dhaba has become a cultural institution in India; many urban restaurants imitate the "dhaba style" of food and décor. Dhabas also feature prominently in Bollywood films and travel literature as symbols of authentic regional cooking.
