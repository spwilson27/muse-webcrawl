Ibn Fadlan

Aḥmad ibn Faḍlān ibn al-ʿAbbās ibn Rāshid ibn Ḥammād, commonly known as Ibn Fadlan, was a 10th-century Arab Muslim traveler, famous for his account of his travels as a member of an embassy of the Abbasid Caliph al-Muqtadir of Baghdad to the king of the Volga Bulgars, written in a work known as the Risala.

The journey

The embassy departed Baghdad in 921 CE, travelling through Bukhara and Khwarazm, across the steppe, and reaching the Volga Bulgar ruler in May 922. The mission's purpose was to instruct the newly converted Bulgars in Islamic law and to fund a fortress against the Khazars.

Account of the Rus

Ibn Fadlan's narrative is prized for its famous eyewitness description of the Rus — Scandinavian traders on the Volga — including a detailed account of a ship burial in which a slave girl was sacrificed to accompany her master. His descriptions of the customs, dress and hygiene (or lack of it) of the peoples he encountered remain a key primary source for the history of Eastern Europe and Central Asia in the early Middle Ages.
