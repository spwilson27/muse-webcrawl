Timeline of social media

The following is a timeline of social media, tracing significant launches and milestones in online social networking services. Social media traces its roots to bulletin board systems and early online services of the late 1970s and 1980s.

Pre-internet and early online services

  • 1973 – PLATO's Talkomatic provides early multi-user chat rooms at the University of Illinois.
  • 1978 – The first computer bulletin board system (CBBS) goes online in Chicago.
  • 1980 – Usenet is established, enabling distributed discussion groups.
  • 1985 – The WELL, an influential online community, is founded.
  • 1988 – Internet Relay Chat (IRC) is created by Jarkko Oikarinen.

1990s – The first social networks

  • 1994 – GeoCities lets users build personal homepages in themed "neighborhoods".
  • 1995 – Classmates.com connects former schoolmates; dating and profile sites proliferate.
  • 1997 – Six Degrees launches, often credited as the first modern social networking site with profiles and friend lists; AOL Instant Messenger (AIM) debuts.
  • 1999 – LiveJournal and Blogger popularize personal blogging.

2000s – The boom

  • 2002 – Friendster launches and rapidly grows.
  • 2003 – Myspace, LinkedIn, and WordPress launch; Skype introduces internet voice and video.
  • 2004 – Facebook is founded at Harvard; Flickr and Digg launch.
  • 2005 – YouTube is founded; Reddit launches.
  • 2006 – Twitter launches with its 140-character format; Facebook opens to the general public.
  • 2007 – Tumblr launches; the iPhone's release accelerates mobile social media.
  • 2009 – WhatsApp is founded; Facebook becomes the most visited social network.

2010s – Mobile and visual platforms

  • 2010 – Instagram and Pinterest launch; Google Buzz fails to gain traction.
  • 2011 – Snapchat introduces ephemeral photos; Google+ launches (shut down in 2019).
  • 2012 – Facebook acquires Instagram and reaches one billion users; Tinder popularizes swipe-based dating.
  • 2014 – Facebook acquires WhatsApp for about $19 billion.
  • 2016 – Musical.ly and the short-video format rise; Instagram Stories copies Snapchat's format.
  • 2017 – TikTok launches internationally (merged with Musical.ly in 2018).
  • 2018 – The Cambridge Analytica scandal triggers a global debate over data privacy.

2020s

  • 2020 – Clubhouse popularizes drop-in audio chat; pandemic lockdowns boost usage across platforms.
  • 2021 – Facebook's parent company rebrands as Meta, emphasizing the "metaverse".
  • 2022 – Elon Musk acquires Twitter and later rebrands it as X (2023).
  • 2023 – Meta launches Threads, gaining 100 million users in five days; decentralized networks such as Bluesky and Mastodon grow.
