Sir Tim Rice, lyricist

Sir Timothy Miles Bindon Rice (born 10 November 1944) is an English lyricist and author. He is best known for his collaborations with Andrew Lloyd Webber — Joseph and the Amazing Technicolor Dreamcoat, Jesus Christ Superstar and Evita — and with Elton John and Alan Menken on Disney films including Aladdin and The Lion King. He is one of a small number of artists to have won Emmy, Grammy, Oscar and Tony ("EGOT") awards.

Career

Rice abandoned a legal training for music publishing at EMI, where he met Lloyd Webber in 1965. Their concept album Jesus Christ Superstar (1970), with hits such as "Superstar" and "I Don't Know How to Love Him", became a stage phenomenon; Evita (1976 concept album, 1978 London stage) produced "Don't Cry for Me Argentina". With ABBA's Benny Andersson and Björn Ulvaeus he wrote the musical Chess (1984), including the hit single "One Night in Bangkok" and the duet "I Know Him So Well".

Film work

For Disney, Rice completed lyrics for Aladdin (1992) after Howard Ashman's death, winning an Academy Award for "A Whole New World", and wrote songs with Elton John for The Lion King (1994), winning an Oscar for "Can You Feel the Love Tonight". He also wrote lyrics for the stage musicals Beauty and the Beast (film songs with Ashman) and Aida (2000, with Elton John).

Other interests

Rice is a well-known cricket enthusiast — president of the MCC in 2002 and founder of the Heartaches Cricket Club — and a frequent broadcaster and quiz-show guest. He was knighted in 1994 for services to music.
