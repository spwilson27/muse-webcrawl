The onager (Equus hemionus), also known as the hemione or Asiatic wild ass, is a large member of the horse family native to the deserts and steppes of Asia. Sandy-brown with a pale belly and a short dark dorsal stripe, it is among the fastest land mammals in Asia, capable of running 60–70 km/h (37–43 mph), and it has never been truly domesticated.

Subspecies and distribution

Recognized subspecies include the Mongolian wild ass (khulan, E. h. hemionus), the Turkmenian kulan (E. h. kulan), the Persian onager (E. h. onager), the Indian wild ass or khur (E. h. khur), and the Anatolian and Syrian wild asses, the latter two extinct — the last Syrian wild ass died in 1927 at Vienna's Schönbrunn Zoo. Onagers survive in Iran, Turkmenistan, Kazakhstan (where they have been reintroduced), Mongolia, China, and India's Little Rann of Kutch.

Behavior and ecology

Onagers graze on grasses and browse shrubs, obtaining much of their moisture from food but requiring access to water within roughly 30 km. Stallions may hold territories around water sources or roam with bachelor groups; mares and foals form herds that gather in the hundreds during migration in Mongolia. Historically they were hunted for meat and hides and displaced by livestock competition for water and forage.

Conservation

The species is listed as Near Threatened overall, while the Persian onager and Turkmenian kulan are Endangered. Reintroduction programs have returned onagers to Kazakhstan's steppes and, remarkably, to Israel's Negev desert, where Persian onagers released from 1982 stand in for the extinct Syrian subspecies.
