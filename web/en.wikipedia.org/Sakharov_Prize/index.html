Sakharov Prize

The Sakharov Prize for Freedom of Thought is an annual award established by the European Parliament in 1988 to honour individuals and organisations defending human rights and fundamental freedoms. It is named after the Soviet physicist and dissident Andrei Sakharov. The prize, worth €50,000, is presented each December at a ceremony in Strasbourg around the anniversary of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights. It is regarded as one of the most prominent human-rights awards in the world.

Selection

Nominations are made by political groups of the European Parliament or by groups of at least 40 members. The Foreign Affairs and Development committees draw up a shortlist of three finalists, from which the Parliament's Conference of Presidents selects the laureate each October. The prize ceremony takes place during the December plenary session in Strasbourg.

Notable laureates

The first recipients in 1988 were Nelson Mandela and the Soviet dissident Anatoly Marchenko (posthumously). Subsequent laureates have included Aung San Suu Kyi (1990), the Mothers of the Plaza de Mayo, Reporters Without Borders, Malala Yousafzai (2013), the Congolese gynaecologist Denis Mukwege (2014), the Yazidi activists Nadia Murad and Lamiya Aji Bashar (2016), the Venezuelan democratic opposition (2017), the Ukrainian filmmaker Oleg Sentsov (2018), Alexei Navalny (2021), and the people of Ukraine (2022). Several laureates, such as Aung San Suu Kyi and Navalny, were imprisoned at the time of their award and could not collect the prize in person.

Sakharov Prize Network

Laureates form the Sakharov Prize Network, which works with the European Parliament to promote human rights, support laureates at risk, and organise events such as the Sakharov Fellowship for young human-rights defenders.
