Geryon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In Greek mythology, Geryon (Greek: Γηρυών, Gēryṓn), also Geryone, son of Chrysaor and Callirrhoe, was a fearsome giant who dwelt on the island Erytheia in the far west of the Mediterranean, near the edge of the world at the Straits of Gibraltar. The stealing of his magnificent red cattle formed the tenth of the Twelve Labours of Heracles.

Description

Ancient authors differ on Geryon's form. In the most common version he had three bodies joined at the waist, with three heads and six arms; other accounts give him one body with three heads, or wings as well. The early poet Stesichorus, in his Geryoneis (c. 6th century BC), of which substantial fragments survive on papyrus, described him as having six hands, six feet, and wings. Hesiod in the Theogony calls him simply a great warrior slain by Heracles beyond the Ocean.

The tenth labour

Eurystheus commanded Heracles to bring him the cattle of Geryon. These were a herd of beautiful red oxen guarded by the two-headed hound Orthrus — brother of Cerberus — and the herdsman Eurytion. Heracles travelled to the far west, borrowing the golden cup of the sun god Helios to sail across the Ocean, and on arriving in Erytheia he killed Orthrus with his club and slew Eurytion. As he drove the cattle away, Geryon confronted him, and Heracles killed him with an arrow dipped in the venom of the Hydra. The journey back to Greece, herding the cattle through Europe, generated numerous subsidiary legends, including encounters in Italy with the giant Cacus, who stole part of the herd.

In later literature

Geryon's peripheral, sun-drenched homeland near Tartessos made him a fixture of the geography of the mythical west. Dante placed a very different Geryon in the Inferno: the monster of fraud, with the face of an honest man, the body of a serpent, and a scorpion's sting, who carries Dante and Virgil down from the seventh to the eighth circle of Hell (cantos XVI–XVII). The Stesichorean fragments, recovered in the 20th century, inspired Anne Carson's verse novel Autobiography of Red (1998), a modern retelling in which Geryon is a sensitive, winged, red adolescent.
