Strathspey (dance)

Strathspey
GenreScottish country dance / ballroom
Time signature4/4 (slow, stately)
OriginStrathspey valley, Scotland, 18th century
MusicStrathspey tunes with "Scotch snap"

A strathspey is a type of dance tune in 4/4 time, named after the Strathspey region of Scotland, in the valley of the River Spey. Together with the reel and the jig, it is one of the three principal tempos of Scottish country dancing, of which it is the slowest and most stately.

Musical character

Strathspey music is characterised by the "Scotch snap", a short note followed by a dotted note, the reverse of the more common Lombard rhythm. Tunes are played at a dignified tempo — considerably slower than a reel — allowing for elegant, precise footwork and the distinctive strathspey travelling and setting steps.

Steps and figures

The strathspey travelling step combines a step, close and hop with pointed feet and turnout. Dances are performed either in longwise sets, like reels and jigs, or in the square "set and rotate" formations. Well-known strathspeys include "The Glasgow Highlanders", with its unique two-couple square formation, and "Monymusk". Strathspeys are also central to Highland dancing, where the Highland Fling is danced to strathspey tempo before speeding into a reel.

Tunes

Many strathspey tunes were composed by fiddlers such as William Marshall and James Scott Skinner; famous examples include "Tullochgorum" and "The Miller of Drone". The form also strongly influenced the music of Cape Breton Island in Canada.
