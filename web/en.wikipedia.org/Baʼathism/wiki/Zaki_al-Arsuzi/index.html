Zaki al-Arsuzi

Zaki al-Arsuzi (Arabic: زكي الأرسوزي; 1899 – 1968) was a Syrian philosopher, philologist, sociologist, historian, and Arab nationalist. He was one of the founders of Arab nationalist thought in its modern form and is regarded, particularly by the Syrian branch of the Ba'ath Party, as a founding father of Ba'athism, though his role was contested by Michel Aflaq's supporters.

Life and thought

Arsuzi was born in Latakia to an Alawite family and studied philosophy at the Sorbonne in Paris. Returning to Syria, he taught in Antioch and Aleppo and became active in nationalist politics, campaigning against the cession of the Sanjak of Alexandretta to Turkey in the late 1930s. In 1940 he founded a group called the Arab Ba'ath, several years before Aflaq and al-Bitar's organization of the same name; the two currents merged at the 1947 founding congress.

Ideas

Arsuzi's writings placed the Arabic language at the heart of nationhood: language, he argued, is the living bond and creative spirit of the Arab nation. Works such as The Genius of Arabic in Its Tongue developed a philological, almost mystical theory of Arab identity. His ideas strongly influenced Alawite and Syrian officers who later dominated the Syrian Ba'ath.

Legacy

After the Ba'ath took power in Syria in 1963, Arsuzi was honored as the movement's philosopher; after Aflaq's fall in 1966, Syrian Ba'athists elevated Arsuzi as the true founder of Ba'athism, a claim the Iraqi branch rejected. He died in Damascus in 1968.
