Adverse yaw

Adverse yaw is the natural and undesirable tendency for an aircraft to yaw in the opposite direction of a roll. It is caused by the difference in lift and drag of each wing during a turn, which produces a yawing moment away from the direction of the turn.

Adverse yaw
Domain: Aerodynamics
Cause: Differential induced drag
Countered by: Rudder, differential ailerons, Frise ailerons

Mechanism

When ailerons deflect to initiate a roll, the rising wing generates more lift and therefore more induced drag than the descending wing. The extra drag pulls the nose toward the rising wing — opposite to the intended direction of turn.

Mitigation

Pilots counter adverse yaw with coordinated rudder input. Designers reduce it with differential ailerons, which deflect upward more than downward; Frise-type ailerons, which add drag on the rising wing; and coupled control systems or yaw dampers.

See also: Aileron, Rudder.
