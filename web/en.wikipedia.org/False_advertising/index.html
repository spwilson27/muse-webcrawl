False advertising

False advertising is the act of publishing, transmitting or otherwise publicly circulating an advertisement containing a false claim, or a claim that is misleading or deceptive such that it is likely to influence purchasing decisions. It is prohibited by consumer protection laws in most jurisdictions.

Forms

Common forms include bait-and-switch pricing, hidden fees, false "was" prices, unsubstantiated health or performance claims, misleading labels and imagery, false endorsements, and deceptive omission of material facts. "Puffery" — vague, exaggerated praise that no reasonable consumer would take literally — is generally permitted.

Regulation

In the United States, the Federal Trade Commission enforces the FTC Act against unfair or deceptive advertising, and the Lanham Act allows competitors to sue over false claims in commerce; statutes such as the Wheeler-Lea Act and various state consumer protection acts also apply. The United Kingdom regulates misleading advertising under consumer protection regulations enforced alongside the Advertising Standards Authority's codes, and the European Union prohibits misleading and certain comparative advertising by directive. Sanctions range from corrective advertising and fines to criminal penalties.
