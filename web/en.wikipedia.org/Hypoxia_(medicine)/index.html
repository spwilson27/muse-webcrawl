Hypoxia (medicine)

Hypoxia
A condition in which the body or a region of the body is deprived of adequate oxygen supply at the tissue level.

Hypoxia is a condition in which the body or a region of the body is deprived of adequate oxygen supply at the tissue level. Hypoxia may be classified as either generalised, affecting the whole body, or local, affecting a specific region. It differs from hypoxemia, which refers specifically to low oxygen content in arterial blood, though the two often occur together.

Types and causes

A traditional classification distinguishes four types:

  • Hypoxemic hypoxia — low arterial oxygen from lung disease, shunts, or low inspired oxygen at altitude.
  • Anemic hypoxia — reduced oxygen-carrying capacity, as in anaemia or carbon monoxide poisoning.
  • Stagnant (circulatory) hypoxia — inadequate blood flow, as in shock, including the circulatory failure of severe hypovolemia.
  • Histotoxic hypoxia — cells cannot use delivered oxygen, classically in cyanide poisoning.

Signs and effects

Early symptoms include breathlessness, rapid heart rate, headache, restlessness and impaired judgement — a particular danger in aviation, where insidious altitude hypoxia can incapacitate pilots. Cyanosis (bluish discolouration of skin) may appear. The brain is exquisitely sensitive: minutes of severe hypoxia cause loss of consciousness and permanent injury. Cells sense oxygen through hypoxia-inducible factors (HIFs), whose discovery was recognised by the 2019 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine; HIF activation increases red-cell production and new blood-vessel growth, adaptations seen in acclimatisation to altitude.

Treatment

Treatment addresses the underlying cause and restores oxygenation — supplemental oxygen, positive-pressure ventilation, transfusion, or hyperbaric oxygen therapy in selected cases. In spaceflight and high-altitude aviation, prevention through pressurisation and oxygen systems is paramount.
