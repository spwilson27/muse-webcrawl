Heart of palm

Main sourcesPeach palm, coconut palm, açaí palm, juçara palm
Alternative namesPalm heart, palm cabbage, palmito
RegionCentral and South America, Southeast Asia

Heart of palm is a vegetable harvested from the inner core and growing bud of certain palm trees, most notably the peach palm (Bactris gasipaes), coconut palm (Cocos nucifera), açaí palm (Euterpe oleracea) and juçara palm (Euterpe edulis). It is prized for its delicate flavour and crisp, ivory-white flesh.

Harvesting

Harvesting the heart of a single-stemmed palm kills the tree, since the growing tip is removed. For this reason wild harvesting has threatened species such as the juçara palm in Brazil's Atlantic Forest. Multi-stemmed species and cultivated varieties such as the peach palm can be harvested more sustainably, because individual stems can be cut while the clump survives and produces new shoots.

Culinary use

Hearts of palm are usually sold canned or jarred in brine, though fresh hearts are eaten in producing regions. They are sliced into salads, blended into dips, used as a seafood or pasta substitute in vegan cooking, and served in dishes across Latin American and French-influenced cuisines. Costa Rica and Ecuador are major exporters of cultivated palm hearts.

Nutrition

The vegetable is low in calories and fat and provides dietary fibre, potassium, iron, zinc, phosphorus and vitamin B6.
