Basra Governorate is a governorate in southern Iraq, bordering Kuwait to the south and Iran to the east, with a short coastline on the Persian Gulf. Its capital is the city of Basra, Iraq's principal port and second-largest city. The governorate sits at the heart of Iraq's southern oil industry.

Geography

The governorate occupies the lower Mesopotamian plain where the Tigris and Euphrates join to form the Shatt al-Arab waterway, which flows to the Gulf. The terrain is flat and arid, moderated by marshland to the north and date palm groves along the rivers. Ports include Umm Qasr, Iraq's main deep-water port, and the oil terminals of the northern Gulf.

Economy

Basra Governorate contains some of Iraq's largest oil fields, including Rumaila and West Qurna, and accounts for the great majority of the country's oil production and exports. Agriculture — notably dates — fishing, and port activity are also important, though salinity, water scarcity, and pollution are persistent challenges.

History

The city of Basra was founded in 636 AD as a garrison town of the early Islamic caliphate and became a major center of trade and Arabic scholarship. The governorate was a frontline region during the Iran–Iraq War (1980–88) and the 1991 Gulf War, and saw heavy fighting and unrest in the years after 2003, including protests over corruption, electricity, and drinking-water quality in 2018.
