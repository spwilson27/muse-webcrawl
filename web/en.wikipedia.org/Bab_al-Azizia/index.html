Bab al-Azizia

Bab al-Azizia (Arabic for "The Splendid Gate") was a military barracks and compound in the southern suburbs of Tripoli, Libya, best known as the principal residence of Libyan leader Muammar Gaddafi from his seizure of power until the compound's capture by rebel forces in August 2011.

Description

The roughly six-square-kilometre complex contained barracks, government buildings, Gaddafi's private residences and the Bedouin-style tent in which he received visiting dignitaries. It was fortified with walls, guard towers and underground tunnels and bunkers.

History

The site was originally an Italian military base and later a royal residence before Gaddafi took it over. On 15 April 1986 it was bombed by United States aircraft in Operation El Dorado Canyon; a ruined building and a monument depicting a fist crushing an American warplane were preserved as symbols of resistance. During the 2011 Libyan Civil War the compound was repeatedly struck by NATO air strikes before falling to National Transitional Council fighters on 23 August 2011, who looted and vandalised it. Much of the site was subsequently demolished.
