London Calling (song)

“London Calling”
Song by the Clash
Album: London Calling
Released: 7 December 1979
Genre: Punk rock
Length: 3:19
Writers: Joe Strummer, Mick Jones

“London Calling” is a song by the British punk rock band the Clash. It was released as a single from the band's 1979 double album of the same name. Written by Joe Strummer and Mick Jones, the song takes its name from the BBC World Service station identification phrase used during and after the Second World War.

Lyrics and themes

Strummer's lyrics express anxiety about world events in the late 1970s: nuclear danger after the Three Mile Island accident, rising unemployment, racial conflict, police violence, and the fear that the Thames might flood London — “London is drowning / And I live by the river.” The song also mocks the post-punk media hype and the band's own position.

Reception and legacy

The single reached No. 11 on the UK Singles Chart. It is widely regarded as one of the greatest songs of all time, placing highly in Rolling Stone's list of the 500 Greatest Songs, and its bassline by Paul Simonon is frequently cited among the best in rock music. The song was performed at the closing ceremony of the 2012 London Olympics in recorded form and has become an anthem associated with London.
