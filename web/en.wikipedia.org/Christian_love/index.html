Christian love

Christian love refers to the love taught and exemplified in Christianity, centred on the Greek word agape — a self-giving, unconditional love directed toward God and neighbour. Jesus summarised the law in the command to love God with all one's heart and to "love your neighbour as yourself", and the New Testament presents God's love, shown in Christ, as the pattern for human love.

Biblical teaching

The apostle Paul's hymn to love in 1 Corinthians 13 describes love as patient and kind, not envious or boastful, keeping no record of wrongs, and enduring all things. The First Epistle of John declares that "God is love" and links love of God inseparably to love of others. The parable of the Good Samaritan extends "neighbour" beyond ethnic and religious boundaries.

In theology and practice

Theologians distinguish agape from eros (desire) and philia (friendship); Anders Nygren's Agape and Eros influentially argued that agape is wholly God's initiative. In practice, Christian love is expressed through charity, hospitality, forgiveness, care for the poor and sick, and, in its most demanding form, love of enemies.
