Video essay

Video essay
MediumOnline video (YouTube, Vimeo)
PrecursorsEssay film
Common subjectsFilm criticism, media analysis, culture, history

A video essay is a piece of video content that, like a written essay, presents an argument, analysis, or personal reflection, typically combining narration (voice-over), edited footage, images, music, and text. The term is most often applied to online works — especially on YouTube — that analyse films, television, video games, and broader cultural topics, though it descends from the older tradition of the essay film.

History

The essay film tradition includes works by Chris Marker (Sans Soleil, 1983), Harun Farocki, and Jean-Luc Godard. With the advent of consumer editing software and video platforms in the 2000s–2010s, the form flourished online. Channels such as Every Frame a Painting (Tony Zhou), Nerdwriter1, Lindsay Ellis, and ContraPoints popularised long- and short-form video essays on cinema and culture, and the format has been adopted in academic contexts as a form of "videographic criticism," championed by journals such as [in]Transition.

Characteristics

Video essays typically use existing footage under fair-use-style criticism, a structured thesis, and an engaging spoken delivery. Lengths range from a few minutes to multi-hour deep dives. The format's popularity has raised recurring debates about copyright claims on monetised commentary, algorithmic incentives toward length, and the boundary between criticism and entertainment.
