Marie-Claire Blais (1939–2021) was a Canadian writer, novelist, poet and playwright, and one of the most acclaimed French-language authors of her generation. She published more than twenty novels as well as plays, poetry and essays.

Career

Blais published her first novel, La Belle Bête (1959), at the age of twenty. Her breakthrough came with Une saison dans la vie d'Emmanuel (1965; A Season in the Life of Emmanuel), a bleak portrait of a poor rural Quebec family that won the Prix Médicis in France. She won the Governor General's Award for French-language fiction four times.

Later work

From the 1990s she spent much of her time in Key West, Florida, where she wrote the ten-volume cycle beginning with Soifs (1995; These Festive Nights), a sweeping portrait of American society. She was made a Companion of the Order of Canada.
