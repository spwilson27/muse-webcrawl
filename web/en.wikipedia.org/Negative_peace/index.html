Negative peace

Negative peace is the absence of direct violence, war, and physical conflict, without necessarily addressing the underlying conditions — injustice, inequality, and structural violence — that produce conflict. The concept is associated with the peace researcher Johan Galtung, who contrasted it with positive peace.

Background

Galtung introduced the distinction in the 1960s. A ceasefire or a heavily policed calm may end shooting while leaving the causes of conflict intact; such a peace is "negative" because it is defined by what is absent. Martin Luther King Jr. used a similar idea in his 1963 Letter from Birmingham Jail, criticizing a "negative peace which is the absence of tension" in favour of a "positive peace which is the presence of justice."

Criticism and use

The concept is widely used in peace and conflict studies to evaluate peace agreements and post-conflict settlements. Critics note that the boundary between the two peaces is blurry and that positive peace, if defined as the absence of all structural violence, can become so broad as to be utopian.
