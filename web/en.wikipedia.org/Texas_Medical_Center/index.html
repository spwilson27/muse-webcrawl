Texas Medical Center

The Texas Medical Center (TMC) is a medical district and neighbourhood of Houston, Texas, United States, southeast of Rice University and south of the Museum District. It is the largest medical complex in the world, comprising dozens of member institutions — hospitals, medical schools, nursing schools, and research organisations — clustered on roughly 1,345 acres (5.4 km²) of land. Over 106,000 people are employed in the district, and its institutions collectively receive millions of patient visits each year.

History

The centre traces its origins to the M.D. Anderson Foundation, created in 1936 by Monroe Dunaway Anderson, which in the 1940s offered land and grants to attract medical institutions to Houston. Baylor College of Medicine relocated from Dallas in 1943, and the Texas Medical Center was formally chartered in 1945. The M.D. Anderson Cancer Center, Texas Children's Hospital, Houston Methodist Hospital and Memorial Hermann hospital grew into nationally prominent institutions within the district.

Member institutions

  • MD Anderson Cancer Center — among the world's largest cancer centres.
  • Texas Children's Hospital — one of the largest paediatric hospitals in the United States.
  • Houston Methodist Hospital and Memorial Hermann–Texas Medical Center.
  • Baylor College of Medicine and the McGovern Medical School of UTHealth Houston.
  • Texas A&M, University of Houston and Texas Woman's University health programs.
  • The Texas Heart Institute, founded by surgeon Denton Cooley in 1962.

TMC Innovation and expansion

In the 2010s and 2020s the centre developed the TMC Innovation Institute and the TMC3 (Helix Park) life-sciences campus, a collaborative research district intended to anchor Houston's biotechnology industry.
