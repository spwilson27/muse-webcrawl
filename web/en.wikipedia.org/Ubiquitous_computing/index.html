Ubiquitous computing

Computation embedded invisibly in everyday environments.

Ubiquitous computing (or "ubicomp") is a concept in software engineering, hardware engineering, and computer science where computing is made to appear anytime and everywhere. In contrast to desktop computing, ubiquitous computing can occur using any device, in any location, and in any format. A user interacts with the computer, which can be in many different forms — laptops, tablets, and terminals in everyday objects such as a refrigerator or a pair of glasses.

Origins

The term was coined by Mark Weiser of Xerox PARC in 1988 and developed in his influential 1991 article "The Computer for the 21st Century", which envisioned tabs, pads, and boards — devices of many sizes woven invisibly into daily life. Related concepts include pervasive computing, ambient intelligence, the Internet of Things, and calm technology.

Research themes

Key challenges include context awareness, privacy, scalable networking, natural interaction, and designing systems that recede into the background of attention. Modern smartphones, wearables, voice assistants, and smart-home devices are regarded as partial realizations of Weiser's vision.
