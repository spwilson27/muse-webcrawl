Emerald Tablet

The Emerald Tablet, also known as the Tabula Smaragdina, is a compact and cryptic Hermetic text that was highly regarded by Islamic and European alchemists as the foundation of their art. It is attributed to Hermes Trismegistus and was said to have been inscribed on a tablet of emerald.

The earliest surviving versions appear in Arabic sources from the 8th or 9th century, notably in the Book of the Secret of Creation attributed to Balinas (pseudo-Apollonius of Tyana). It was translated into Latin in the 12th century and commented upon by figures including Albertus Magnus and Isaac Newton, who produced his own translation.

The tablet's most famous phrase is the axiom "as above, so below" — in Latin, quod est inferius est sicut quod est superius — expressing the correspondence between macrocosm and microcosm. The text describes the operation of the Sun and Moon and the generation of the one thing, and it remains central to Hermeticism and occult philosophy.
