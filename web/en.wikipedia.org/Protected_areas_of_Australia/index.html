Protected areas of Australia

Australia's reserve system covers a significant share of the continent.

The protected areas of Australia comprise thousands of reserves managed by the Commonwealth, state and territory governments, Indigenous communities, and private organizations. Together they form the National Reserve System, Australia's network of terrestrial and marine protected areas, which covers well over a fifth of the continent's land area and large areas of its marine jurisdiction, including the Great Barrier Reef Marine Park.

Types of reserves

Categories include national parks, nature reserves, conservation parks, Indigenous Protected Areas, and marine parks and sanctuaries. The Commonwealth manages parks such as Kakadu and Uluru-Kata Tjuta in the Northern Territory (jointly with their Aboriginal traditional owners), while states manage most national parks, beginning with Royal National Park near Sydney in 1879, the second national park declared in the world.

Indigenous Protected Areas

Indigenous Protected Areas, voluntarily declared by Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander landowners and managed under IUCN categories, now make up roughly half of the National Reserve System by area, combining conservation with Indigenous land management practices such as cultural burning.
