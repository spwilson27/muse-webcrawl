United Church of Christ (UCC)
Founded: June 25, 1957
Headquarters: Cleveland, Ohio
Tradition: Mainline Protestant (Reformed, Congregational)

The United Church of Christ (UCC) is a mainline Protestant Christian denomination in the United States, formed in 1957 by the merger of the Evangelical and Reformed Church and the Congregational Christian Churches. Its roots reach back to the Pilgrims and Puritans of New England, German and Swiss Reformed immigrants, and the early American frontier.

Beliefs and polity

The UCC has a congregational polity: local churches own their property and call their own pastors, covenanting voluntarily with wider settings of the church. The denomination affirms the autonomy of conscience, uses slogans such as "God is still speaking", and is theologically among the most liberal US churches; in 2005 its General Synod endorsed same-sex marriage, and it was the first major US denomination to ordain an openly gay man, William R. Johnson, in 1972. Membership, like that of other mainline bodies, has declined in recent decades to under one million members in roughly 4,800 congregations.
