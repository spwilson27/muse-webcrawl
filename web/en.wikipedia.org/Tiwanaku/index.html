Tiwanaku

Tiwanaku (also spelled Tiahuanaco) was a Pre-Columbian polity and archaeological site in western Bolivia, near the southern shore of Lake Titicaca. It was the capital of one of the most important civilisations of the Andes, flourishing between about 600 and 1000 CE, several centuries before the Inca Empire.

The site

The monumental core includes the Akapana stepped pyramid, the Kalasasaya platform temple, and the famous Gateway of the Sun, a massive stone portal carved from a single block of andesite and decorated with a central figure often called the Staff God, surrounded by winged attendants and a calendrical frieze. The masonry, much of it transported from quarries many kilometres away, is renowned for its precision.

The state

Tiwanaku's influence spread across the south-central Andes through colonies, trade and religious prestige, supported by raised-bed (chinampa-like suka kollus) agriculture around Lake Titicaca that could support a large urban population. The state declined around 1000 CE, probably in connection with prolonged drought. The site was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2000.
