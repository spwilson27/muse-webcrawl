Apodiformes

KingdomAnimalia
PhylumChordata
ClassAves
OrderApodiformes
FamiliesApodidae (swifts), Hemiprocnidae (treeswifts), Trochilidae (hummingbirds)

The Apodiformes are an order of birds comprising the swifts, treeswifts, and hummingbirds — some 450 species in total. The name means "footless", referring to the birds' very small feet, suited to perching rather than walking.

Characteristics

Apodiform birds share distinctive wing anatomy: a short humerus and long hand bones, adaptations for fast, agile flight. Swifts are aerial specialists that feed, mate, and even sleep on the wing; hummingbirds are the smallest birds and can hover by beating their wings up to 80 times per second, feeding on nectar and insects.

Evolution

Fossils such as Eocypselus from the early Eocene show that stem apodiforms lived about 50 million years ago. Molecular studies place the group within the nightjar-like clade Strisores (Daedalornithes), with the owlet-nightjars as their closest relatives.
