The Aṣṭādhyāyī (Devanagari: अष्टाध्यायी, "Eight Chapters") is a celebrated Sanskrit treatise on grammar written by the ancient Indian scholar Pāṇini, variously dated to the 6th–4th centuries BC. It set the linguistic standards for classical Sanskrit and is widely regarded as one of the greatest intellectual achievements of ancient India — a complete, rule-based generative grammar of the language composed in roughly 4,000 terse aphorisms (sūtras).

Structure

The work consists of eight chapters (adhyāya), each divided into four sections (pāda), containing about 4,000 rules in total. Its astonishing brevity is achieved by an elaborate metalanguage: abbreviated class terms (pratyāhāras) drawn from the introductory Śiva sūtras, marker letters (anubandhas), rule inheritance (anuvṛtti), and principles for resolving conflicts between rules. Operating together, the rules generate correct Sanskrit word forms from roots and affixes in a manner often compared to a formal algorithm or computer program.

Content

The Aṣṭādhyāyī covers phonetics, sandhi (sound changes at word boundaries), morphology, compounding, derivation, and aspects of syntax and accent for both the literary language and, where noted, the older Vedic language.

Commentarial tradition

The text generated an immense commentary literature. Kātyāyana's critical notes (Vārttikas, c. 3rd century BC) and Patañjali's great commentary, the Mahābhāṣya (c. 2nd century BC), are known together with Pāṇini's work as the work of the "three sages" (trimuni) of Sanskrit grammar. Later works such as the Kāśikā commentary systematized the tradition, which remained the foundation of Sanskrit learning in India for over two millennia.

Influence

Pāṇini's grammar became known to European scholars in the early 19th century and profoundly influenced the development of modern linguistics, informing ideas about phonology, morphology, and formal grammar. Its rule system has drawn sustained interest from logicians and computer scientists as an early example of a formal generative system.
