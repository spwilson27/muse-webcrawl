Asylum seeker

An asylum seeker is a person who leaves their country of residence, enters another country, and makes a formal application for the right of asylum — international protection — in that country. While the application is being decided, the person remains an asylum seeker; if the claim is accepted, they are recognized as a refugee.

Legal basis

The 1951 Refugee Convention and its 1967 Protocol define a refugee as someone outside their country owing to a well-founded fear of persecution for reasons of race, religion, nationality, membership of a particular social group, or political opinion. The principle of non-refoulement forbids returning a person to a place where they face serious threats to life or freedom.

Determination procedures

States and the UNHCR assess claims through interviews and evidence. Applicants may wait months or years for a decision, often with restricted rights to work, and those whose claims fail may be removed, though some receive subsidiary or humanitarian protection.

Statistics and politics

Global forced displacement has exceeded 100 million people in recent years, driven by conflicts in Syria, Ukraine, Afghanistan, Venezuela, and elsewhere. Asylum policy is a major political issue in many receiving countries, with debates over processing speed, detention, and burden-sharing.
