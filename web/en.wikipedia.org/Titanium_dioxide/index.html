Titanium dioxide

Titanium dioxide
Formula: TiO2
Appearance: White solid
Uses: Pigment, sunscreen, photocatalyst

Titanium dioxide, also known as titanium(IV) oxide or titania, is the inorganic compound with the formula TiO2. It is the most widely used white pigment because of its brightness and very high refractive index, which give it exceptional opacity and covering power.

Occurrence and production

Titanium dioxide occurs naturally in the minerals rutile, anatase, and brookite. Commercial production, mainly from ilmenite and rutile ores, proceeds by the older sulfate process or the chloride process, in which the ore is converted through titanium tetrachloride. World production amounts to several million tonnes per year.

Applications

The pigment, designated Pigment White 6 (CI 77891), is used in paints, plastics, paper, inks, coatings, and cosmetics. Ultrafine grades serve as ultraviolet absorbers in sunscreens. Titanium dioxide is also a photocatalyst under ultraviolet light, used in self-cleaning surfaces and air and water purification, and it was formerly used as the food colouring E171, which the European Union withdrew in 2022 over genotoxicity concerns that could not be excluded for nanoparticles.
