Chandrayaan programme

The Chandrayaan programme (from Sanskrit chandra, "Moon", and yāna, "craft") is a series of lunar exploration missions conducted by the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). It has included orbiters, impactors, landers and rovers exploring the Moon.

Missions

Chandrayaan-1 (2008) was India's first lunar probe. Its Moon Impact Probe struck the lunar south polar region, and data from its instruments helped confirm the presence of water molecules in lunar soil.

Chandrayaan-2 (2019) carried an orbiter, the Vikram lander and the Pragyan rover. The orbiter succeeded and continued operating for years, but the lander crashed during its descent attempt.

Chandrayaan-3 (2023) achieved a successful soft landing near the lunar south pole on 23 August 2023, making India the fourth country to land on the Moon and the first to land in the south polar region. Its rover conducted experiments on the lunar surface for about two weeks.

Future

Further missions, including a proposed lunar sample-return mission (Chandrayaan-4) and the joint LUPEX mission with Japan, are in development.
