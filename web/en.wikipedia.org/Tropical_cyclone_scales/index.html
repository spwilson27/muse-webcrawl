Tropical cyclone scales

Tropical cyclone scales are systems used by meteorological agencies to classify tropical cyclones by their maximum sustained wind speeds and sometimes by central pressure or potential damage. Because different ocean basins developed their own conventions, several scales are in use worldwide.

Major scales

The Saffir–Simpson hurricane wind scale, used in the Atlantic and northeastern Pacific, classifies hurricanes into Categories 1–5 based on sustained winds, from 119 km/h (74 mph) upward, with Category 5 above 252 km/h (157 mph). The western North Pacific uses its own categories (tropical storm, severe tropical storm, typhoon, very strong typhoon, violent typhoon), while the North Indian Ocean scale of the India Meteorological Department runs from depression to super cyclonic storm. The Australian scale (Bureau of Meteorology) uses Categories 1–5, and Météo-France, for the southwest Indian Ocean, uses terms such as intense tropical cyclone and very intense tropical cyclone.

Damage and storm surge

Wind category correlates only loosely with the danger from storm surge and flooding, which depend on storm size, forward speed, and coastline shape; this has motivated supplementary scales and risk communication approaches.
