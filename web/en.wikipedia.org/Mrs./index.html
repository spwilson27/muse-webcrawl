Mrs.

Mrs. (American English) or Mrs (British English, where full stops are typically omitted in abbreviations) is a commonly used English-language honorific for a married woman, as well as for a woman who has been married. The title originated as a contraction of the word mistress, the feminine form of master, which was formerly applied to women regardless of marital status.

History

Historically, Mrs. was the abbreviation of "mistress", a title used for all adult women from the seventeenth century onward. By the late eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, usage had narrowed so that Mrs. came to denote a married woman, while Miss — also derived from "mistress" — was reserved for unmarried women. The full pronunciation "missus" (or "missis") persisted in speech even as the written form became purely an abbreviation.

Usage

Mrs. is traditionally combined with the surname of the woman's husband, though many married women who keep their birth name also use it. Etiquette varies: some widows and divorcées retain Mrs., while others return to Miss or adopt Ms., a title introduced in the twentieth century that does not specify marital status. In British usage the abbreviation is written without a full stop (Mrs), while American style retains the period (Mrs.).
