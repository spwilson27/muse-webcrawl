Economist

An economist is a professional and practitioner in the social science discipline of economics. Economists study, develop, and apply theories and concepts from economics and write about economic policy. Within this field there are many sub-fields, ranging from broad philosophical theories to the focused study of minutiae within specific markets, macroeconomic analysis, microeconomic analysis, or financial statement analysis.

Work

Economists work in academia, government, central banks, international organizations such as the World Bank and the International Monetary Fund, and the private sector, including banks, consultancies, and large corporations. Their tasks include forecasting, market research, data analysis, and advising on decisions involving resource allocation, pricing, taxation, interest rates, and regulation.

Education

Professional economists typically hold a bachelor's degree in economics at minimum; research and senior policy positions usually require a master's degree or a PhD. The economics profession has been increasingly recognized in the 20th and 21st centuries, and the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences, established in 1968, is the field's most prominent award.

Notable economists

Historical figures include Adam Smith, David Ricardo, Karl Marx, John Maynard Keynes, and Milton Friedman. Contemporary economics is sometimes criticized for overreliance on mathematical models and for failures such as not predicting the 2008 financial crisis.
