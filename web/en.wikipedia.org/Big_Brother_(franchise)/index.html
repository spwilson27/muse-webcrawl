Big Brother (franchise)

Big Brother is a Dutch reality competition television franchise created by John de Mol Jr., first broadcast in the Netherlands in 1999. The format has been adapted in many countries around the world. In the show, contestants (called "housemates" or "houseguests") live together in a specially constructed house isolated from the outside world, under constant surveillance by cameras and microphones. The name is inspired by Big Brother, the fictional dictator in George Orwell's novel Nineteen Eighty-Four.

Format

Housemates are periodically required to nominate one another for eviction, and viewers (or, in the American version, the houseguests themselves) vote to evict until a winner remains and receives a cash prize. Editions vary internationally: the British version relied on public voting, while the American and Canadian versions developed a strategic competition format with a Head of Household and Power of Veto. Many countries also produce celebrity editions, such as Celebrity Big Brother.

International versions

The franchise has aired in more than 60 countries, including the United Kingdom, the United States, Australia, India (as Bigg Boss), Brazil, Germany, Spain (as Gran Hermano), the Philippines, and numerous African and Latin American editions. At its peak in the mid-2000s the show was one of the most successful television formats in the world, and several versions continue to air decades after the format's debut.
