National parks of Lithuania

Lithuania has five national parks, established to protect the country's most valuable natural and cultural landscapes. The first was founded in 1974, and the system took its present shape shortly after Lithuania regained independence in the early 1990s. Together the parks cover roughly 2.3% of the national territory.

The five parks

  • Aukštaitija National Park (established 1974) — the oldest park, in northeastern Lithuania, known for its lakes, pine forests, and traditional wooden villages.
  • Curonian Spit National Park (1991) — protects the Lithuanian half of the narrow Curonian Spit peninsula between the Curonian Lagoon and the Baltic Sea, famed for its drifting sand dunes; it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site shared with Russia.
  • Dzūkija National Park (1991) — the largest park, in the south, covering extensive pine forests, inland dunes, and ethnographic villages along the Nemunas and Merkys rivers.
  • Žemaitija National Park (1991) — in the Samogitian highlands of the northwest, centred on Lake Plateliai.
  • Trakai Historical National Park (1992) — unique in Europe as a historical park, protecting the lakeside town of Trakai and its island castle, once a seat of the Grand Dukes of Lithuania.

Administration

The parks are administered by directorates under the State Service for Protected Areas of the Ministry of Environment. In addition to the national parks, Lithuania maintains a network of regional parks and strict nature reserves.
