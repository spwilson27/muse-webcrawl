Bi (jade)

A bi is a type of circular Chinese jade artefact, a flat disc with a circular hole in the centre. The earliest bi were produced by the Neolithic Liangzhu culture (3400–2250 BCE) and were often buried with the dead as symbols of status and of a connection to heaven.

Symbolism

In later Chinese ritual tradition, codified in texts such as the Rites of Zhou, the round bi was associated with heaven, in contrast to the square cong associated with earth; a "round heaven and square earth" cosmology. Bi discs were offered in worship of the heavens and were among the six ritual jades.

Production

Because jade is extremely hard, bi were shaped by abrasion with quartz sand and other abrasives rather than cutting, a slow and labour-intensive process reflecting their prestige value. Later bi, from the Han dynasty onward, were often intricately carved with grain patterns or glass inlay.
