Hunting dog

Summary

Main types: Hounds, gun dogs, terriers, dachshunds, curs

Gun dog subtypes: Retrievers, spaniels, pointers and setters

Hound subtypes: Sighthounds, scent hounds

A hunting dog is a dog bred and trained to assist humans in hunting game. Dogs have hunted alongside humans for thousands of years, and different breeds have been developed for specialised tasks such as tracking scent, pursuing game by sight, flushing birds, retrieving shot game, or confronting quarry underground or at bay.

Types

Hounds hunt primarily by scent or sight. Scent hounds, such as the Bloodhound, Beagle, and Basset Hound, follow ground scent, often giving voice as they track. Sighthounds, such as the Greyhound, Saluki, and Irish Wolfhound, pursue prey by sight and speed. Gun dogs work with hunters carrying firearms: pointers and setters locate game and indicate it by pointing; spaniels flush birds from cover; and retrievers, such as the Labrador Retriever and Golden Retriever, recover shot birds from land or water.

Other working hunting dogs

Terriers were developed to pursue foxes, badgers, and vermin, often going to ground; Dachshunds were bred in Germany for badger hunting. In North America, cur-type dogs are used for hunting raccoons, squirrels, and large game. Lurchers — sighthound crosses — are traditional hunting companions in Britain and Ireland.

Training and modern use

Hunting dogs are trained to respond to voice, whistle, and hand signals, and to work under control around game and gunfire. While many hunting breeds are now kept primarily as pets, organised field trials and hunting tests continue to evaluate and preserve their working abilities.
