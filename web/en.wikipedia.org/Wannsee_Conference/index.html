Wannsee Conference

The Wannsee Conference (German: Wannseekonferenz) was a meeting of senior government officials of Nazi Germany and Schutzstaffel (SS) leaders, held in the Berlin suburb of Wannsee on 20 January 1942. The purpose of the conference, called by SS-Obergruppenführer Reinhard Heydrich, was to ensure the cooperation of administrative departments in implementing the Final Solution to the Jewish question.

Background

By late 1941, mass murder of Jews was already under way: Einsatzgruppen had killed hundreds of thousands in the occupied Soviet Union, and the first extermination camp at Chełmno had begun operations in December 1941. Heydrich, chief of the Reich Security Main Office, had been authorised by Hermann Göring in July 1941 to prepare a “total solution of the Jewish question” in German-controlled Europe. The conference, originally scheduled for December 1941, was postponed following the German declaration of war on the United States.

The meeting

Fifteen officials attended, including Adolf Eichmann, who prepared the minutes, and representatives of the Foreign Office, the Interior Ministry, the Justice Ministry, the General Government and the occupied eastern territories. Heydrich outlined plans to deport some 11 million Jews — a figure covering all of Europe, including countries not under German control — to the east, where they would be worked to death, and survivors would be “treated accordingly”.

The Protocol

The meeting’s minutes, the Wannsee Protocol, were written in euphemistic bureaucratic language. Thirty copies were distributed; only one survived the war, that of Undersecretary Martin Luther of the Foreign Office, found in 1947 by American investigators. The Protocol became key evidence of high-level coordination of the genocide, though historians debate whether the conference itself took the decision for extermination or formalised and coordinated a policy already in motion.

Legacy

The villa at Am Großen Wannsee 56–58 is now a memorial and museum, the House of the Wannsee Conference. The meeting has been dramatised in the films Conspiracy (2001) and The Conference (2022).
