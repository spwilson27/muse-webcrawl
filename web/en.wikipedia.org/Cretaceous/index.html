Cretaceous

Cretaceous Period
145–66 million years ago
Preceded by: Jurassic
Followed by: Paleogene
Third and final period of the Mesozoic Era

The Cretaceous is a geological period that lasted from about 145 to 66 million years ago (Mya). It is the third and final period of the Mesozoic Era, as well as the longest period of the Phanerozoic Eon. The name is derived from the Latin creta, "chalk", which is abundant in the latter half of the period. The Cretaceous ended with the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event, a large mass extinction in which many groups, including non-avian dinosaurs, pterosaurs, and large marine reptiles, disappeared.

A Late Cretaceous landscape in North America

Geology

During the Cretaceous, the late-Paleozoic-to-early-Mesozoic supercontinent of Pangaea completed its tectonic breakup into present-day continents, although their positions were substantially different. The Atlantic Ocean widened, and high global sea levels created shallow epicontinental seas across the interiors of continents; the Western Interior Seaway split North America into the landmasses of Laramidia and Appalachia. Extensive chalk deposits were laid down in these warm shallow seas.

Climate

The climate was generally warm throughout the period, with no permanent polar ice caps. Atmospheric carbon dioxide levels were much higher than today, and sea levels reached some of the highest of the Phanerozoic, flooding large areas of the continents. The Late Cretaceous saw a gradual cooling trend, though temperatures remained well above those of the present day.

Life

On land, dinosaurs remained the dominant large vertebrates, including tyrannosaurids, hadrosaurs, and ceratopsians in the north and titanosaurs in the south. Flowering plants (angiosperms) appeared early in the period and became dominant by its end, transforming terrestrial ecosystems and co-evolving with pollinating insects. Birds and mammals diversified, though mammals remained mostly small. In the seas, mosasaurs, plesiosaurs, and ammonites flourished. The period ended abruptly 66 million years ago when a large asteroid impact at Chicxulub, combined with massive volcanism in the Deccan Traps, triggered a mass extinction that eliminated roughly three-quarters of plant and animal species.

Subdivisions

The Cretaceous is divided into two epochs: the Early Cretaceous (145–100.5 Mya) and the Late Cretaceous (100.5–66 Mya), comprising twelve ages from the Berriasian to the Maastrichtian.
