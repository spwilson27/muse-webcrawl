The poblano (Capsicum annuum) is a mild chili pepper originating in the state of Puebla, Mexico. Dried, it is called ancho or chile ancho, from the Spanish word ancho ("wide"). Stuffed fresh and roasted, it is popular in chiles rellenos poblanos.

While poblanos tend to have a mild flavor, occasionally and unpredictably they can have significant heat. Different peppers from the same plant have been reported to vary substantially in heat intensity. The unripe poblano is dark green; the ripened red poblano is significantly hotter and sweeter than the unripe green form.

Description

The poblano grows to about 4 inches long and 2 to 3 inches wide, with thick, dark green flesh. Its size and thick walls make it especially suited to stuffing. On the Scoville scale, poblanos measure roughly 1,000 to 1,500 Scoville Heat Units, milder than a jalapeño.

Culinary use

Poblanos are a staple of central Mexican cuisine. Common preparations include chiles rellenos (stuffed, battered, and fried), chiles en nogada (stuffed poblanos in walnut sauce, a dish associated with Mexican independence celebrations in Puebla), and rajas con crema (strips of roasted poblano in cream). In dried ancho form they are a key ingredient in mole sauces, including mole poblano.
