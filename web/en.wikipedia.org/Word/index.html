Word

A word is a basic unit of language consisting of one or more morphemes, roughly corresponding to a set of sounds or written symbols that conveys a distinct meaning. Words are the smallest elements that can be uttered in isolation with objective or practical meaning, and they combine according to the rules of syntax into phrases, clauses and sentences.

Defining a word

Despite its familiarity, the word is notoriously hard to define precisely, and linguists distinguish several concepts: the orthographic word, a sequence of letters bounded by spaces; the phonological word, a unit of pronunciation; the lexeme, the abstract dictionary form (so that "run", "runs" and "ran" are forms of one lexeme); and the morpheme, the smallest meaningful unit, of which words may contain several. Writing systems differ too: Chinese characters typically represent syllable-morphemes, while languages such as Turkish or Inuktitut build long, sentence-like words from many morphemes.

Word classes and formation

Words are grouped into classes such as nouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs, divided broadly into open classes, which readily accept new members, and closed classes such as pronouns and conjunctions. New words enter a language through derivation, compounding, borrowing, blending, clipping and coinage — processes studied in morphology and lexicology. Vocabulary size varies by counting method, but large dictionaries of English record several hundred thousand words.
