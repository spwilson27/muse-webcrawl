Strawberry Hill House

Strawberry Hill House — often called simply Strawberry Hill — is a Gothic Revival villa built in Twickenham, London, from 1749 by the author Horace Walpole, son of Britain's first prime minister. It is the type specimen of the "Strawberry Hill Gothic" style and a foundational monument of the Gothic Revival in architecture.

History

Walpole transformed a modest cottage into a fantastical little castle with battlements, pinnacles, stained glass, fan-vaulted galleries, and rooms modeled on cathedral tombs and chapter houses, assisted by friends including John Chute and Richard Bentley. The house displayed his vast collections of antiquities and curiosities, described in his own printed guidebook. Strawberry Hill also housed Walpole's private printing press, the Strawberry Hill Press, and inspired his novel The Castle of Otranto (1764), which launched Gothic fiction.

Later history and restoration

After Walpole's death in 1797 the house passed through several owners and periods of decay; its contents were famously dispersed in an 1842 sale. A major restoration, completed in 2010 with support from the World Monuments Fund and the Heritage Lottery Fund, recreated many of Walpole's interiors, and the house and garden are now open to the public under the Strawberry Hill Trust.
