Vilna Ghetto

Vilna Ghetto
Location: Vilnius (Vilna), German-occupied Lithuania
Established: 6 September 1941
Liquidated: September 1943
Head of Judenrat: Jacob Gens

The Vilna Ghetto was a Jewish ghetto established by Nazi Germany in the old town of Vilnius (Vilna), Lithuania, during the Holocaust. Some 40,000 Jews were confined in two ghettos in September 1941; most were murdered in the Ponary massacre outside the city, and the ghetto was liquidated in September 1943.

Life and resistance

Despite starvation and terror, the ghetto sustained schools, a library, and a famous theatre. Under Judenrat chief Jacob Gens, the administration pursued a strategy of "work for life". In January 1942 the United Partisan Organization (Fareynikte Partizaner Organizatsye, FPO) was founded — the first Jewish resistance organisation in Nazi-occupied Europe — led by Yitzhak Wittenberg and Abba Kovner, whose manifesto first called on Jews not to "go like sheep to the slaughter".

Liquidation

After the Wittenberg affair of July 1943, the Gestapo demanded the resistance leader; Gens was himself executed in September 1943 as the ghetto was destroyed. Remaining inhabitants were deported to camps in Estonia and Latvia or murdered at Ponary. Of Vilnius's pre-war Jewish population of some 55,000–60,000 — the "Jerusalem of Lithuania" — only a few thousand survived.
