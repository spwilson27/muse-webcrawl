Karlovy Vary (German: Karlsbad) is a spa city in the Karlovy Vary Region of the Czech Republic. It lies on the confluence of the rivers Ohře and Teplá, about 106 km west of Prague. It is named after Emperor Charles IV, who founded the city in the 14th century.

Hot springs

The city is famous for its numerous thermal springs — thirteen main springs and several hundred smaller ones — whose waters, reaching up to about 73 °C at the Vřídlo geyser, have been used for therapeutic bathing and drinking cures for centuries. Elegant colonnades, including the Mill Colonnade and Market Colonnade, shelter the spring outlets in the historic spa district.

History and culture

Karlovy Vary became one of Europe's most fashionable spa resorts in the 18th and 19th centuries, visited by aristocracy and figures such as Goethe, Beethoven, and Marx. In 2021 it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as part of the Great Spa Towns of Europe. Since 1946 the city has hosted the Karlovy Vary International Film Festival, one of the oldest film festivals in the world. Local products include Becherovka herbal liqueur, Moser glass, and spa wafers (oplatky).
