Korean idol

Idol groups are known for synchronised choreography and fan engagement.

A Korean idol, or simply idol (Korean: 아이돌), is a type of celebrity working in the field of K-pop in fandom culture in South Korea, either as a member of a group or as a solo act. Idols are typically manufactured and produced by entertainment agencies through a rigorous trainee system of auditions, training in singing, dance and languages, and carefully managed public images.

Trainee system

Aspiring idols, often recruited as children or teenagers, sign contracts with agencies such as SM Entertainment, YG Entertainment, JYP Entertainment and Hybe. Trainees may train for years before debuting in groups such as BTS, Blackpink, Twice or EXO. The system has drawn criticism over long contracts, strict dieting and control of personal life, prompting regulatory attention by the Korea Fair Trade Commission.

Fan culture

Idols cultivate intense parasocial relationships with fans through social media, fan meetings and merchandise. Organised fan clubs adopt official colours and light sticks, and fan support activities include coordinated streaming, voting and charity projects.
