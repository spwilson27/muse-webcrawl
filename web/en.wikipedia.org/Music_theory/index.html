Music theory

Music theory is the study of the practices and possibilities of music. It examines the fundamental elements of music — melody, harmony, rhythm, form, timbre and notation — and the systems by which music is composed, analysed and understood. The term covers both descriptive analysis of particular works and normative rules of compositional practice.

Fundamentals

Core concepts include pitch and scales, intervals, chords and chord progressions, key and tonality, metre and rhythm, and musical notation. In Western tonal theory, harmony is built largely from triads and their extensions organised by function around a tonic; other traditions, from Indian raga to Arab maqam, organise pitch and melody by different principles.

History

Theoretical writing dates to ancient China, India, Mesopotamia and Greece, where Pythagoras and his followers investigated the mathematical ratios of consonance. Medieval theorists codified modes and counterpoint; the Baroque era developed thoroughbass practice; the 19th and 20th centuries produced systematic harmonic theory, Schenkerian analysis, set theory and serialism. Modern music theory is an academic discipline studied in conservatoires and universities and applied in composition, performance, production and music technology.
