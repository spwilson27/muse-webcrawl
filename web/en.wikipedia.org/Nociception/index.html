Nociception

Nociception is the sensory nervous system's process of encoding noxious stimuli, such as tissue damage, extreme temperatures or chemical irritation. It is initiated by specialised sensory neurons called nociceptors. Nociception underlies the conscious experience of pain but is not identical to it: nociceptive signals can occur without pain being felt, and pain can arise without peripheral nociception, as in neuropathic pain.

Nociceptors

Nociceptors are free nerve endings found in skin, muscle, joints and internal organs. They respond to mechanical, thermal and chemical threats, and are served by two main fibre classes: fast, myelinated Aδ fibres carrying sharp "first pain", and slow unmyelinated C fibres carrying dull, aching "second pain". Many nociceptors are polymodal, and their thresholds can be lowered by inflammatory mediators — a sensitisation that produces tenderness at injured sites.

Pathway

Signals travel to the dorsal horn of the spinal cord, where reflex withdrawal can be triggered, and ascend chiefly via the spinothalamic tract to the thalamus and somatosensory cortex, with parallel pathways to limbic structures that supply pain's emotional character.
