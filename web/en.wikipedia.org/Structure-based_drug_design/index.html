Structure-based drug design
Field: Medicinal chemistry, computational biology
Methods: X-ray crystallography, NMR, docking

Structure-based drug design (SBDD) is the process of designing drug molecules using knowledge of the three-dimensional structure of a biological target, usually a protein, obtained by X-ray crystallography, NMR spectroscopy or cryo-electron microscopy.

Process

Researchers identify a binding pocket, then design or dock candidate ligands that fit it with complementary shape and chemistry, iterating through synthesis and testing. Computational docking and scoring functions estimate binding affinity before compounds are made.

Successes

Notable structure-based drugs include HIV protease inhibitors, the influenza drug zanamivir, and imatinib-related kinase inhibitors. SBDD is contrasted with ligand-based design, which relies on properties of known active compounds.
