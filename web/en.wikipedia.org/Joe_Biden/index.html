Joseph Robinette Biden Jr. (born 20 November 1942) is an American politician who served as the 46th president of the United States from 2021 to 2025. A member of the Democratic Party, he was the 47th vice president under Barack Obama from 2009 to 2017 and represented Delaware in the U.S. Senate from 1973 to 2009.

Early life

Biden was born in Scranton, Pennsylvania, and his family moved to Delaware when he was a child. He studied history and political science at the University of Delaware and earned a law degree from Syracuse University. In 1972, shortly after he was elected to the Senate at age 29, his wife Neilia and daughter Naomi were killed in a car accident; his sons Beau and Hunter were injured. He was sworn in at their hospital bedside and commuted by train from Wilmington to Washington throughout his Senate career.

Senate career

Biden chaired the Senate Foreign Relations Committee and the Judiciary Committee, which he led during the contentious confirmations of Robert Bork and Clarence Thomas. He was a principal author of the Violence Against Women Act (1994) and supported the 1994 crime bill and the 2002 authorization for the Iraq War. He sought the Democratic presidential nomination in 1988 and 2008 without success. His son Beau, Delaware's attorney general, died of brain cancer in 2015.

Vice presidency and presidency

As Obama's running mate he was elected vice president in 2008 and re-elected in 2012, overseeing the economic stimulus implementation and playing a role in foreign policy including the Iraq drawdown. In 2020 he won the Democratic nomination and defeated incumbent Donald Trump in the general election, with Kamala Harris as his running mate, becoming the oldest president at inauguration.

His presidency (2021–2025) included the American Rescue Plan, the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act, the CHIPS and Science Act and the Inflation Reduction Act; the U.S. withdrawal from Afghanistan in 2021; and leadership of the Western response to Russia's invasion of Ukraine from 2022. Facing concerns about age and after a poor debate performance in June 2024, he withdrew from the 2024 re-election race in July 2024 and endorsed Harris, who lost to Trump. He left office on 20 January 2025.
