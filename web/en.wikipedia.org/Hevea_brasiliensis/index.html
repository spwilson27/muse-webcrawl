Hevea brasiliensis

Hevea brasiliensis, the Pará rubber tree, is a species of flowering plant in the spurge family Euphorbiaceae, native to the Amazon basin. It is by far the most important commercial source of natural rubber: its milky latex is harvested by tapping the bark and coagulated into rubber used worldwide, above all for vehicle tires.

Hevea brasiliensis
Family: Euphorbiaceae
Native range: Amazon basin
Product: Natural rubber (latex)

Cultivation and history

In the 1870s, seeds collected in Brazil by Henry Wickham were germinated at Kew Gardens and the seedlings sent to Ceylon and Malaya, founding the Southeast Asian plantation industry that soon eclipsed Amazonian production — in part because South American leaf blight (Microcyclus ulei) devastates dense plantings in the tree's native range. Today Thailand, Indonesia, and Vietnam are leading producers.

Harvesting

Tappers shave a thin spiral cut into the bark, allowing latex to flow into a cup; trees are tapped for roughly 25–30 years, after which the timber ("rubberwood") is also used for furniture. See also latex and rubber.
