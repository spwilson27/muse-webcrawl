Food loss and waste

Food loss and waste is food that is discarded, lost or uneaten along the food supply chain. By convention, food loss occurs before the retail stage — during production, storage, processing and distribution — while food waste occurs at retail and consumption. The Food and Agriculture Organization has estimated that roughly 14 percent of the world's food is lost between harvest and retail, and the UN Environment Programme estimates a further 17 percent is wasted by households, restaurants and shops, meaning about one third of all food produced is never eaten.

Causes

In lower-income countries, losses are dominated by inadequate storage, refrigeration and transport infrastructure, and by pests and spoilage. In higher-income countries, waste predominates at the consumer end: over-purchasing, confusion over date labels, cosmetic standards for produce, and large portion sizes all contribute. Surplus edible food is also discarded because of market gluts and contractual requirements.

Impacts

Food loss and waste account for an estimated 8–10 percent of global greenhouse gas emissions, as well as wasted land, water and fertiliser. Food decomposing in landfill generates methane. The issue is addressed by Sustainable Development Goal 12.3, which aims to halve retail and consumer food waste and reduce food losses by 2030.

Mitigation

Responses include redistribution of surplus food through food banks, date-label reform, improved cold chains, anaerobic digestion and composting of unavoidable waste, and public awareness campaigns. The food recovery hierarchy prioritises prevention, then feeding people, feeding animals, industrial uses, composting, and landfill last.
