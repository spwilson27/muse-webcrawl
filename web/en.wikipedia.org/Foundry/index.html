Foundry

Foundry
Industry: Metalworking
Process: Melting and casting metal
Products: Castings for machinery, engines, pipes, cookware
Furnaces: Cupola, induction, crucible

A foundry is a factory that produces metal castings. Metals are cast into shapes by melting them into a liquid, pouring the metal into a mould, and removing the mould material after the metal has solidified as it cools. The most common metals processed are aluminium and cast iron, though steel, bronze, brass, and zinc are also cast.

Process

The workflow includes patternmaking, mould and core making (often in sand), melting in cupola or induction furnaces, pouring, cooling, shakeout, and finishing operations such as grinding, heat treatment, and machining.

Types of foundries

Foundries are classed as ferrous (iron and steel) or non-ferrous (aluminium, copper alloys, magnesium, zinc). "Jobbing" foundries produce varied short runs, while captive foundries serve a single manufacturer, such as an automaker.

History

Metal casting dates to prehistoric bronze working; the industrial foundry developed with Abraham Darby's coke-fired smelting at Coalbrookdale (1709), enabling mass-produced cast iron.
