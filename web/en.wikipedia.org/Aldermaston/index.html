Aldermaston

Aldermaston is a village and civil parish in Berkshire, England, about 13 kilometres (8 miles) southwest of Reading. It is most widely known for the nearby Atomic Weapons Establishment (AWE), the facility responsible for designing and manufacturing the warheads for the United Kingdom's nuclear deterrent, and for the Aldermaston Marches of the late 1950s and early 1960s, among the earliest major protests of the Campaign for Nuclear Disarmament (CND).

History

The village is recorded in the Domesday Book and has a parish church, St Mary the Virgin, dating in part to the twelfth century. The Aldermaston Court estate was rebuilt in the nineteenth century after a fire. During the Second World War an airfield, RAF Aldermaston, was built nearby and used by the RAF and USAAF.

Atomic Weapons Establishment

In 1950 the airfield site became the Atomic Weapons Research Establishment, where Britain's first atomic bomb was developed. Now operated as AWE Aldermaston, together with AWE Burghfield it maintains the warheads for the Trident submarine-launched missile system.

Aldermaston Marches

From 1958, anti-nuclear campaigners marched between London and Aldermaston at Easter. The first march, organised by the Direct Action Committee and supported by the newly formed CND, drew thousands and helped make the CND symbol — later known worldwide as the peace sign — famous.
