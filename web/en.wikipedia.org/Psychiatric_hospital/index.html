Psychiatric hospital

Bethlem Royal Hospital in London, founded in 1247, is among the oldest institutions for the care of people with mental illness.

A psychiatric hospital is a hospital specialising in the treatment of serious mental disorders, such as schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, major depression, and acute psychosis. Modern psychiatric hospitals provide inpatient assessment and treatment, usually for episodes of acute illness, and may include secure units for patients detained under mental-health legislation. They evolved from the lunatic asylums of earlier centuries, becoming medical institutions in the 19th and 20th centuries as psychiatry developed as a medical specialty.

History

Institutions for people with mental illness existed in the medieval Islamic world (bimaristans) and in Europe, where London's Bethlem Royal Hospital — the origin of the word "bedlam" — admitted psychiatric patients from the 14th century. The 18th and 19th centuries saw the growth of purpose-built county and state asylums, many intended to practise the humane "moral treatment" pioneered at the York Retreat (founded 1796). Asylums grew into very large custodial institutions in the early 20th century, and abuses within them drove reform movements.

Deinstitutionalisation

From the 1950s, the arrival of antipsychotic medication, changing social attitudes, and official inquiries into asylum conditions fuelled deinstitutionalisation: the closure of large asylums and a shift toward community-based care, shorter inpatient stays, and treatment in general hospitals' psychiatric units or stand-alone acute hospitals. Italy's Basaglia Law (1978) went furthest, mandating closure of asylums nationwide. Critics argue the policy was often implemented without adequate community services, contributing to homelessness and the criminalisation of mental illness in some countries.

Modern services

Today's psychiatric hospitals typically offer acute admission wards, psychiatric intensive care units, rehabilitation and forensic (secure) services, electroconvulsive therapy suites, and outpatient and day-patient programmes. Admission may be voluntary or involuntary under laws such as the Mental Health Act 1983 in England and Wales. Care is delivered by multidisciplinary teams including psychiatrists, psychiatric nurses, clinical psychologists, occupational therapists, and social workers.
