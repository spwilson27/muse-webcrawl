The United States Intelligence Community (IC) is a group of separate U.S. federal government intelligence agencies and subordinate organizations that work both separately and collectively to conduct intelligence activities in support of the foreign policy and national security interests of the United States. Member organizations include intelligence agencies, military intelligence, and civilian intelligence and analysis offices within federal executive departments.

The IC is overseen by the Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI), headed by the Director of National Intelligence (DNI), who reports to the president of the United States. The term "Intelligence Community" was given statutory form by Executive Order 12333, signed by President Ronald Reagan on 4 December 1981.

History

Before the creation of the DNI, the IC was headed by the Director of Central Intelligence, who simultaneously directed the CIA. The Intelligence Reform and Terrorism Prevention Act of 2004, enacted in response to the 9/11 Commission's findings, created the ODNI to improve coordination and information sharing among agencies. The first DNI, John Negroponte, took office in 2005.

Members

The community comprises 18 elements, including:

  • Independent agency: Central Intelligence Agency (CIA)
  • Department of Defense: National Security Agency (NSA), Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA), National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency (NGA), National Reconnaissance Office (NRO), and the intelligence elements of the Army, Navy, Air Force, Marine Corps, and Space Force
  • Other departments: the State Department's Bureau of Intelligence and Research (INR), the FBI's Intelligence Branch, the Department of Energy's Office of Intelligence and Counterintelligence, the Treasury's Office of Intelligence and Analysis, the DEA's intelligence division, DHS intelligence offices, and the Coast Guard's intelligence element

Activities and budget

The IC collects information through signals intelligence (SIGINT), human intelligence (HUMINT), geospatial intelligence (GEOINT), measurement and signature intelligence (MASINT), and open-source intelligence (OSINT), and produces analysis including the President's Daily Brief and National Intelligence Estimates. The disclosed aggregate budget request for the National Intelligence Program has been in the range of $70 billion annually in recent years, with a separate Military Intelligence Program of roughly $25–30 billion.
