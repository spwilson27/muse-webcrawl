Adirondack Mountains

The Adirondack Mountains are a mountain range in northeastern New York State, forming a circular dome of some 160 kilometres across. The highest peak is Mount Marcy at 1,629 metres. Much of the range lies within the Adirondack Park, a protected area of about six million acres — the largest publicly protected area in the contiguous United States.

Geology

The mountains are an uplifted dome of ancient Precambrian rock, geologically related to Canada's Laurentian Mountains rather than to the younger Appalachians to the south.

Ecology and recreation

The region contains thousands of lakes and ponds, including Lake Placid, and extensive mixed forests. It is popular for hiking (the 46 "High Peaks"), canoeing, fishing and winter sports.
