Federigo Enriques

Federigo Enriques (5 January 1871 – 14 June 1946) was an Italian mathematician, now known principally as the first to give a classification of algebraic surfaces in birational geometry, and for his work in the history and philosophy of science. He was a leading figure of the Italian school of algebraic geometry.

Born5 January 1871, Livorno, Italy
Died14 June 1946, Rome, Italy
FieldsAlgebraic geometry, philosophy of science
InstitutionsUniversity of Bologna, University of Rome

Work

After graduating from the Scuola Normale Superiore in Pisa, Enriques taught at Bologna and then Rome. With Guido Castelnuovo and Francesco Severi he developed the theory of algebraic surfaces, culminating in the Enriques–Kodaira classification, in which Enriques surfaces — a class he discovered in 1910 — form a key family. He collaborated with Castelnuovo on a famous memoir of 1914 classifying surfaces by their genera.

Later life

Enriques also wrote widely on the foundations and history of mathematics and edited the journal Scientia. As a Jew, he was forced from his chair under the fascist racial laws of 1938 and taught at a clandestine school for Jewish students in Rome; he was reinstated after the war but died soon after, in 1946.
