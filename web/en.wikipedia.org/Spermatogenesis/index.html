Spermatogenesis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The stages of sperm production in the testis

Spermatogenesis is the process by which haploid spermatozoa develop from germ cells in the seminiferous tubules of the testis. Beginning at puberty, diploid spermatogonia divide by mitosis; some of their descendants become primary spermatocytes, which undergo meiosis to produce haploid spermatids. The spermatids then mature through spermiogenesis — acquiring a flagellum, condensing their nucleus and forming an acrosome — into mature spermatozoa.

Stages

The process is conventionally divided into three phases: spermatocytogenesis (mitotic proliferation of spermatogonia), meiosis (division of spermatocytes to yield spermatids), and spermiogenesis (differentiation of spermatids into spermatozoa). In humans the full process takes approximately 74 days, and millions of sperm are produced daily. A further period of maturation occurs in the epididymis.

Regulation

Spermatogenesis is supported by Sertoli cells within the seminiferous tubules and is regulated by the hormones follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and testosterone, coordinated by the hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis. It requires a temperature slightly below core body temperature, which the scrotum provides.
