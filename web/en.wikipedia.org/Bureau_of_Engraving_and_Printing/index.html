The Bureau of Engraving and Printing (BEP) is a bureau of the United States Department of the Treasury that designs and produces a variety of security products for the United States government, most notably the Federal Reserve Notes — the paper currency of the United States. In addition to currency, the BEP produces Treasury securities, military commissions, award certificates, invitations and admission cards, and many types of identification cards, forms, and other special security documents for a variety of government agencies.

The BEP operates two facilities: one in Washington, D.C., and another in Fort Worth, Texas. The Washington facility, completed in 1914, sits adjacent to the Tidal Basin near the Washington Monument, while the Western Currency Facility in Fort Worth opened in 1991 to meet growing demand and to provide geographic redundancy.

History

The Bureau traces its roots to August 29, 1862, when a small number of employees began work in the attic of the Treasury Building, sealing by hand paper money printed by private banknote companies. The operation grew out of the federal government's decision during the Civil War to issue its own paper currency, the Demand Notes and later United States Notes. By the 1870s the Bureau had taken over the full production of United States notes, bonds, and revenue stamps from private contractors, and in 1877 it became the sole producer of all United States currency.

Postage stamps were produced by the Bureau beginning in 1894, a role it held for more than a century until stamp production passed fully to the private sector in the 2000s. The Bureau also printed the food coupons of the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program until that program converted to electronic benefit cards.

Currency production

United States paper currency is printed on a cotton-linen substrate manufactured under contract by Crane Currency of Dalton, Massachusetts, which has supplied the paper since 1879. Modern Federal Reserve Notes incorporate security features including color-shifting ink, embedded security threads, watermarks, microprinting, and raised intaglio printing. The largest-denomination note currently produced is the $100 bill; denominations of $500 and above were last printed in 1945 and discontinued in 1969.

The Bureau prints billions of notes each year. Currency production is financed not by appropriation but through the Federal Reserve System, which purchases notes at production cost. Notes printed at Fort Worth carry a small "FW" mark next to the face plate number.

Public tours

The Washington facility offers public tours that allow visitors to observe the printing process from an elevated gallery. The visitor center displays exhibits on the history of United States currency, including a "Billion Dollar Exhibit" of currency and a collection of printing plates.
