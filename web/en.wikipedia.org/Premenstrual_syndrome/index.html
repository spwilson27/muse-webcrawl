Premenstrual syndrome

Premenstrual syndrome
Specialty: Gynaecology, psychiatry
Symptoms: Irritability, mood swings, bloating, breast tenderness, fatigue
Onset: Luteal phase of the menstrual cycle

PMS symptoms occur in the luteal phase and resolve shortly after menstruation begins.

Premenstrual syndrome (PMS) is a pattern of physical and emotional symptoms occurring in the one to two weeks before a menstrual period — the luteal phase of the menstrual cycle — and resolving within a few days after the period starts. Common symptoms include irritability, anxiety, depressed mood, mood swings, fatigue, bloating, breast tenderness, headaches, food cravings and sleep disturbance. Some degree of premenstrual symptoms is reported by a large proportion of women of reproductive age, while roughly 20–30% meet clinical criteria for PMS and about 3–8% experience the severe form, premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD).

Causes and diagnosis

The cause is not fully understood; symptoms are linked to cyclical changes in ovarian hormones (oestrogen and progesterone) interacting with neurotransmitter systems, particularly serotonin, rather than to abnormal hormone levels themselves. Diagnosis is based on a symptom diary kept over at least two cycles, confirming the luteal-phase timing and symptom-free interval after menstruation.

Treatment

Management ranges from lifestyle measures (exercise, sleep, stress reduction) and cognitive behavioural therapy to medications. Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs), taken continuously or only during the luteal phase, are the best-supported pharmacological treatment; combined oral contraceptives — particularly drospirenone-containing formulations — and, in refractory cases, GnRH analogues are also used. Calcium supplementation has modest evidence of benefit.
