Xi'an

Xi'an (西安; pinyin: Xī'ān) is the capital of Shaanxi province in northwestern China and one of the oldest cities in the country, with a history spanning more than 3,100 years. Under its former name Chang'an, it served as the capital of several of China's most important dynasties, including the Western Han and the Tang. It marks the eastern terminus of the Silk Road.

History

The area around Xi'an was inhabited by Neolithic farmers of the Yangshao culture; the Banpo site lies within the modern city. The Western Zhou founded their capital at Fenghao nearby, and the Qin built Xianyang just across the Wei River. Liu Bang established Chang'an as the Han dynasty capital in 202 BC. Tang-dynasty Chang'an (618–907) was among the largest and most cosmopolitan cities in the world, with a population of roughly one million inside the walls and a grid plan that influenced Kyoto and other East Asian capitals.

The city declined after the Tang but revived under the Ming, who rebuilt its massive city wall (1370s) — the most complete surviving city wall in China — and renamed it Xi'an, "Western Peace." In December 1936 the city was the site of the Xi'an Incident, when Zhang Xueliang detained Chiang Kai-shek to force a united front against Japan.

Landmarks

  • Terracotta Army — thousands of life-size clay soldiers guarding the mausoleum of Qin Shi Huang, discovered by farmers in 1974, about 40 km east of the city.
  • Giant Wild Goose Pagoda (652 AD) — built to house Buddhist scriptures brought from India by Xuanzang.
  • City Wall — Ming dynasty fortifications, 13.7 km in circumference.
  • Muslim Quarter and the Great Mosque, reflecting the city's Hui community and Silk Road heritage.
  • Bell Tower and Drum Tower — Ming-era landmarks at the city center.

Modern city

Modern Xi'an is a major center for education, aerospace, defense research, and tourism, with a population of roughly 13 million in the metropolitan area. It is a hub of China's technology industry in the northwest and a starting point for travel across Shaanxi.
