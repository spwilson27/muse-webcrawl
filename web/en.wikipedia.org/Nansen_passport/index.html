The Nansen passport was an internationally recognised travel document issued to stateless refugees by the League of Nations between 1922 and 1942. It was devised by Fridtjof Nansen, the League's High Commissioner for Refugees, originally for Russians displaced by revolution and civil war.

History

Introduced by an intergovernmental agreement of July 1922, the certificate allowed refugees who had lost the protection of their home state to prove their identity and travel legally. Over the following two decades some 450,000 Nansen passports were issued, first to Russian and Armenian refugees and later to others fleeing persecution. Holders included Igor Stravinsky, Robert Capa, and Marc Chagall.

Legacy

The document was the direct ancestor of the travel papers created under the 1951 Refugee Convention, and Nansen's humanitarian work — commemorated since 1954 by the Nansen Refugee Award — earned him the Nobel Peace Prize in 1922.
