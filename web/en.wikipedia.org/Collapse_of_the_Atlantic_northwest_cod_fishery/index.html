Collapse of the Atlantic northwest cod fishery

The collapse of the Atlantic northwest cod fishery refers to the catastrophic decline of Atlantic cod (Gadus morhua) stocks off the coasts of Newfoundland and Labrador and in the Gulf of St. Lawrence, culminating in a Canadian government moratorium on cod fishing in 1992. It is one of the most famous cases of overfishing and fishery mismanagement, devastating the economy and culture of coastal communities built around cod for five centuries.

Background

Cod from the Grand Banks had been exported to Europe since the early 1500s. After the Second World War, industrial factory trawlers — first foreign, then Canadian — massively increased catching power. Landings peaked in 1968 at about 810,000 tonnes. Canada extended jurisdiction to 200 nautical miles in 1977, but domestic fleet expansion, optimistic stock assessments, and political pressure to maintain jobs kept quotas too high.

Collapse and moratorium

By the late 1980s, inshore fishers reported shrinking fish while offshore survey data lagged behind reality; biomass estimates were later found to have been badly overestimated. In 1992 the northern cod stock was found to have fallen to roughly 1% of earlier levels, and Fisheries Minister John Crosbie announced an indefinite moratorium, throwing some 30,000 to 40,000 people out of work in Newfoundland and Labrador — the largest industrial layoff in Canadian history. Compensation and retraining programs followed, and many outport communities lost population permanently.

Causes and legacy

Analyses blame chronic overfishing, destructive bottom trawling, management that ignored scientific advice and environmental signals, cold water conditions, and changes in the ecosystem, including capelin declines and increased seal predation. Recovery was extraordinarily slow — food-web changes may have created a new stable state — though modest signs of rebuilding led Canada to lift the northern cod moratorium for a small commercial fishery in 2024, after 32 years. The collapse is a standard case study in the tragedy of the commons and precautionary fisheries management.
