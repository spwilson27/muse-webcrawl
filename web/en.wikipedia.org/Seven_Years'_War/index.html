Seven Years' War

Seven Years' War

Date1756–1763
LocationEurope, North America, South America, Africa, Asia
BelligerentsGreat Britain, Prussia, Portugal vs. France, Austria, Russia, Spain, Sweden
ResultTreaties of Paris and Hubertusburg (1763); Britain the dominant colonial power

The Seven Years' War (1756–1763) was a global conflict between Great Britain and France and their respective allies, fought across five continents. Winston Churchill called it the "first world war." In North America its theatre is known as the French and Indian War; in Germany as the Third Silesian War; and in India as the Third Carnatic War.

Causes

The war arose from the unresolved rivalry of Britain and France for colonial supremacy, and from Austria's determination to recover Silesia, seized by Prussia under Frederick the Great during the War of the Austrian Succession. The "Diplomatic Revolution" of 1756 realigned Europe's alliances, pairing Austria with France and Prussia with Britain. Fighting had already begun in North America in 1754, where a young George Washington's clash at Jumonville Glen was an early spark.

Course

Prussia survived against Austria, Russia, France, and Sweden through victories such as Rossbach and Leuthen (1757) and British subsidies, despite crushing defeats and the Russian occupation of Berlin; Russia's withdrawal after the death of Tsarina Elizabeth in 1762 — the "Miracle of the House of Brandenburg" — saved Frederick. Britain, under William Pitt the Elder, won its annus mirabilis in 1759: Quebec fell to James Wolfe at the Plains of Abraham, and naval victories at Lagos and Quiberon Bay destroyed French sea power. In India, Robert Clive's victory at Plassey (1757) laid the foundation of British rule. Spain's late entry cost it Havana and Manila, temporarily captured by Britain.

Outcome

The Treaty of Paris (1763) ceded Canada and French claims east of the Mississippi to Britain, transferred Louisiana to Spain, and confirmed Britain as the world's leading colonial power. The Treaty of Hubertusburg restored the status quo in central Europe. The war's enormous debts drove British attempts to tax the American colonies, helping precipitate the American Revolution.
