Alberto Santos-Dumont
Born20 July 1873, Palmira (now Santos Dumont), Brazil
Died23 July 1932, Guarujá, Brazil
Known forAirships; 14-bis; Demoiselle

Alberto Santos-Dumont (20 July 1873 – 23 July 1932) was a Brazilian aviation pioneer, widely celebrated in Brazil as the father of flight. Heir to a wealthy coffee-growing family, he devoted his fortune to aeronautics in Paris.

Airships

Santos-Dumont built a series of small airships, and in 1901 won the Deutsch de la Meurthe prize by flying his No. 6 airship from Saint-Cloud around the Eiffel Tower and back. He became a celebrity in Paris and a popularizer of flight.

Heavier-than-air flight

On 23 October 1906, his 14-bis made a short powered hop at Bagatelle, Paris, before official observers — the first public airplane flight in Europe; on 12 November he flew 220 metres to win the Archdeacon Prize. His later Demoiselle (1909) was one of the first light aircraft built in quantity. In Brazil he is regarded by many as having achieved the first true airplane flight, since his takeoffs were unassisted and publicly verified.

He also popularized the wristwatch, asking jeweller Louis Cartier to make one so he could time his flights hands-free.
