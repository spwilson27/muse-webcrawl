Photocopier

A photocopier is a machine that makes copies of documents and other visual images onto paper or plastic film quickly and cheaply. Most modern photocopiers use xerography, a dry printing process based on electrostatic charges, invented by Chester Carlson and commercialised by the Xerox Corporation.

How xerography works

A photoconductive drum is given a uniform electrostatic charge, then exposed to light reflected from the original document. The charge is neutralised where light falls, leaving a latent image that attracts toner particles; the toner is transferred to paper and fused by heat.

History

The Xerox 914, introduced in 1959, was the first successful plain-paper copier and transformed office work. Modern multifunction devices combine copying with printing, scanning and faxing.
