Precision dance

Precision dance is a style of dance performance in which a troupe of dancers executes choreographed movements in exact unison, emphasising synchronised lines, formations and gestures rather than solo virtuosity. The form is characterised by matched costumes, precise arm and head positions, and signature manoeuvres such as the eye-high kick line.

History

Precision dance developed from British music-hall troupes such as the Tiller Girls, founded by John Tiller in Manchester in the 1890s, whose dancers performed in perfectly matched lines. In the United States the style was popularised by the Rockettes, founded in St. Louis in 1925 as the Missouri Rockets and, after moving to New York, resident at Radio City Music Hall from 1932.

Technique

Dancers are selected for similar height and build so that lines appear uniform. Choreography relies on counting, spatial grids and rehearsed transitions; famous routines include the Rockettes' "Parade of the Wooden Soldiers", performed since 1933, with its controlled slow fall in a domino line. Precision teams also feature in university dance teams, drill teams and competitive pom and kick divisions in the United States.
