Felix Yusupov

Prince Felix Felixovich Yusupov, Count Sumarokov-Elston (23 March 1887 – 27 September 1967) was a Russian aristocrat, prince of the Yusupov family and heir to one of the largest fortunes in Imperial Russia. He is best known as a principal participant in the murder of Grigori Rasputin, the mystic who had gained influence over the family of Tsar Nicholas II, in December 1916.

Early life

Yusupov was born in Saint Petersburg to Princess Zinaida Yusupova, the last of her line, and Count Felix Sumarokov-Elston, who assumed the Yusupov name on marriage. The family owned estates, mines, factories and palaces, including the Moika Palace in Saint Petersburg and the estate of Arkhangelskoye near Moscow. His elder brother Nicholas was killed in a duel in 1908, leaving Felix as heir. He studied at Oxford University from 1909 to 1912, where he founded the Oxford Russian Club. In February 1914 he married Princess Irina Alexandrovna, the Tsar's niece and only daughter of Grand Duchess Xenia Alexandrovna.

Murder of Rasputin

On the night of 29–30 December 1916 (16–17 December Old Style), Yusupov, together with Grand Duke Dmitri Pavlovich, the politician Vladimir Purishkevich and others, murdered Rasputin at the Moika Palace. In his memoirs Yusupov claimed Rasputin survived poisoned cakes and wine, was shot, and finally died only after being bound and thrown through a hole in the ice of the Malaya Nevka River. The killing failed in its aim of saving the monarchy; the Tsar exiled Yusupov to his estate at Rakitnoye, a punishment that, by removing him from the capital, likely saved his life in the Revolution.

Exile and later life

After the October Revolution, Yusupov returned briefly to Petrograd, salvaged paintings by Rembrandt and family jewels from the Moika Palace, and escaped Russia in 1919 aboard the British battleship HMS Marlborough with other Romanov relatives. He settled in Paris, where he and Irina ran a short-lived fashion house, Irfé. In 1927 he published his account of the Rasputin murder, and in 1932 the couple won a libel action against MGM in London over the film Rasputin and the Empress, an award that helped sustain them for years and prompted the standard Hollywood disclaimer that characters are fictitious. Yusupov died in Paris in 1967 and is buried at the Sainte-Geneviève-des-Bois Russian Cemetery.
