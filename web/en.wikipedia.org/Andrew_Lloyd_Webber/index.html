Andrew Lloyd Webber, Baron Lloyd-Webber (born 22 March 1948) is an English composer and impresario of musical theatre. Several of his musicals have run for more than a decade in the West End and on Broadway, and he has composed music for 21 musicals, film scores and a requiem. He is one of the few artists to achieve EGOT status — winning Emmy, Grammy, Oscar and Tony awards.

Career

The son of composer William Lloyd Webber, he began writing music young and found success collaborating with lyricist Tim Rice: Joseph and the Amazing Technicolor Dreamcoat (1968), the rock opera Jesus Christ Superstar (1970) and Evita (1976), whose song "Don't Cry for Me Argentina" became a major hit. His 1981 musical Cats, based on T. S. Eliot's poems, ran for 21 years in London. Starlight Express (1984), The Phantom of the Opera (1986) — which became Broadway's longest-running show, closing in 2023 after 35 years — Aspects of Love (1989), Sunset Boulevard (1993) and School of Rock (2015) followed. His Really Useful Group is one of the largest theatre operators in London.

Honours

Lloyd Webber was knighted in 1992 and created a life peer in 1997. He received a Kennedy Center Honor in 2006, and in 2018 a special Tony Award for lifetime achievement completed his EGOT. Songs such as "Memory", "I Don't Know How to Love Him" and "The Music of the Night" have entered the popular standard repertoire.

Personal life

He has been married three times, most recently to Madeleine Gurdon since 1991, and is a noted art collector, particularly of Pre-Raphaelite and Victorian painting. Through the Andrew Lloyd Webber Foundation he supports arts education and heritage projects.
