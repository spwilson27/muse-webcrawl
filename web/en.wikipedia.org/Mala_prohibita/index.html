Malum prohibitum

Malum prohibitum (plural mala prohibita, from Latin "wrong because prohibited") is a legal term describing an act that is wrong only because it is prohibited by law, as distinct from malum in se, an act that is considered wrong in itself.

Concept

Offences classed as mala prohibita are typically regulatory or statutory offences — such as traffic violations, licensing breaches, or violations of administrative rules — where the underlying conduct is not inherently immoral. By contrast, offences such as murder and theft are mala in se.

Legal significance

The distinction matters in areas such as mens rea and strict liability: many mala prohibita offences are strict liability offences requiring no proof of intent. The classification has been debated by legal scholars, since some prohibited acts, such as drug possession, are argued by critics of the distinction to blur the two categories.
