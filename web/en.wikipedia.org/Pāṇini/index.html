Pāṇini

Pāṇini was an ancient Indian grammarian, probably of the 5th–4th century BC, from Śalātura in the Gandhara region (in present-day Pakistan). His grammar of Sanskrit, the Aṣṭādhyāyī ("Eight Chapters"), is one of the most remarkable intellectual achievements of the ancient world and founded the Indian grammatical tradition.

The Aṣṭādhyāyī

The work consists of some 3,959 aphoristic rules (sūtras) that generate correct Sanskrit words and sentences from roots and affixes. Using an algebraic notation of markers, metarules and rule ordering, Pāṇini describes the language with a completeness and economy that modern scholars compare to a formal system or computer program; his system anticipates concepts of generative grammar and the Backus–Naur form used for programming languages. The grammar presupposes the Śivasūtras, a phonetic classification of Sanskrit sounds, and accompanying lists of roots (dhātupāṭha) and word groups (gaṇapāṭha).

Influence

Commentators such as Kātyāyana and Patañjali (author of the Mahābhāṣya) elaborated Pāṇini's system, and mastery of his grammar defined classical Sanskrit learning for two millennia. In the 19th century his work influenced the development of comparative and structural linguistics in Europe.
