Drøbak

Drøbak is a town and the administrative centre of Frogn Municipality in Akershus county, Norway. Located at the narrowest point of the Oslofjord, about 30 kilometres south of Oslo, it has a population of roughly 13,000 and is a popular summer destination known for its wooden houses, harbour, and Christmas House.

History

Drøbak developed as a winter harbour for Oslo: when the inner fjord froze, ships unloaded at Drøbak and goods were carried overland. It received town status in 1842. The town is closely associated with Oscarsborg Fortress, built on islands in the narrows opposite the town; on 9 April 1940 the fortress sank the German heavy cruiser Blücher during the invasion of Norway, delaying the occupation of Oslo.

Attractions

Drøbak is home to Tregaardens Julehus (the Christmas House) and a year-round Santa Claus post office, reflecting the tradition that Santa lives in Drøbak. Other attractions include the Drøbak Aquarium, galleries, beaches, and the marina. The town's protected wooden architecture and seaside setting draw many visitors from the capital region in summer.
