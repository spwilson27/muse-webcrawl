Arthur's Seat is an ancient extinct volcano and the main peak of the hills in Holyrood Park at the centre of Edinburgh, Scotland, about a mile east of Edinburgh Castle. Rising to 251 metres, it offers panoramic views over the city and is one of its best-known landmarks.

Geology and history

The hill is the eroded remnant of a volcano that erupted about 350 million years ago in the Carboniferous period; glaciation plucked its eastern side and exposed the crag-and-tail structure that also underlies the Castle Rock. A hill fort occupied the summit area in prehistoric times. The name's origin is uncertain — it is popularly linked to King Arthur, though it may derive from Gaelic forms such as Àrd-na-Said, "height of arrows".

Legends and events

In 1836 workmen found seventeen miniature coffins hidden in a cave on the hill, a mystery never resolved and often linked in folklore to witchcraft or the Burke and Hare murders. The Radical Road, built along Salisbury Crags by unemployed weavers in the 1820s, and the traditional May Day dew-washing on the summit remain part of the hill's cultural history. Arthur's Seat is a Site of Special Scientific Interest and a favourite walk for residents and visitors alike.
