First Battle of Panipat

The First Battle of Panipat, fought on 21 April 1526 near the town of Panipat in present-day Haryana, India, was decisive in the founding of the Mughal Empire. The forces of the Timurid prince Babur, invading from Kabul, defeated the much larger army of Ibrahim Lodi, the last Sultan of Delhi.

Background

Babur, a descendant of Timur and Genghis Khan, had lost his ancestral kingdom in Central Asia and established himself in Kabul. Invited by disaffected Afghan nobles and by Daulat Khan Lodi, governor of Punjab, he invaded northern India in 1525–26 with an army of roughly 12,000–25,000 men, far outnumbered by Lodi's forces, which may have numbered 50,000–100,000 with hundreds of war elephants.

The battle

Babur anchored his position with a defensive line of carts lashed together in the Ottoman fashion, behind which his matchlockmen and field artillery — commanded by Ustad Ali Quli — could fire in safety. His cavalry used the tulughma tactic, wheeling around both flanks to envelop the enemy. Lodi's army, compressed and unable to manoeuvre, was shattered by gunfire and encirclement; Ibrahim Lodi himself was killed on the field with some 15,000–20,000 of his men.

Significance

The battle was one of the earliest in India decided by gunpowder firearms and field artillery. Babur occupied Delhi and Agra, and his victory laid the foundation of Mughal rule in India, which endured, with interruptions, until the 19th century. Two later battles — in 1556 and 1761 — were also fought at Panipat, making it one of the most consequential battlefields in Indian history.
