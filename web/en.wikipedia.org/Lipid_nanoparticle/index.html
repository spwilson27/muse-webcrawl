Lipid nanoparticle

Lipid nanoparticles (LNPs) are nanoparticles composed of lipids. They are a novel pharmaceutical drug delivery system and a pharmaceutical formulation. They were approved for human use in 2018 with the siRNA drug Onpattro (patisiran), and they gained global prominence as the delivery vehicle for the Pfizer–BioNTech and Moderna mRNA COVID-19 vaccines.

Composition

Typical LNPs for nucleic acid delivery contain four components: an ionizable cationic lipid, which binds the negatively charged RNA and becomes neutral at physiological pH; a phospholipid (helper lipid); cholesterol, which stabilises the particle; and a polyethylene glycol (PEG)-lipid, which reduces aggregation and immune clearance. Particles are usually 80–100 nm in diameter, formed by rapid mixing of an ethanolic lipid phase with an aqueous RNA phase.

Mechanism and applications

After injection, LNPs are taken up by cells via endocytosis. Acidification of the endosome protonates the ionizable lipid, which disrupts the endosomal membrane and releases the RNA cargo into the cytosol, where it can be translated (for mRNA) or engage the RNAi machinery (for siRNA). Beyond vaccines, LNPs are being developed for protein replacement therapies, gene editing, and rare-disease treatments.
