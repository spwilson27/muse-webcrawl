Warren Beatty

Henry Warren Beatty (born March 30, 1937) is an American actor and filmmaker. His career has spanned over six decades, and he has received numerous accolades, including an Academy Award and three Golden Globe Awards. He is the only person to have been nominated for acting in, directing, writing, and producing the same film — and he did so twice: first for Heaven Can Wait (1978) and again for Reds (1981), for which he won the Academy Award for Best Director.

Career

Beatty was born in Richmond, Virginia, the younger brother of actress Shirley MacLaine. He made his film debut in Elia Kazan's Splendor in the Grass (1961). A decade later he produced and starred in Bonnie and Clyde (1967), a landmark of the New Hollywood era. His other notable films include McCabe & Mrs. Miller (1971), Shampoo (1975), Dick Tracy (1990), Bugsy (1991), and Bulworth (1998). Known for his perfectionism and for taking long breaks between projects, he returned in 2016 with Rules Don't Apply.

Personal life and politics

Beatty has been married to actress Annette Bening since 1992; they have four children. Long active in Democratic politics, he campaigned for Robert F. Kennedy and George McGovern, and has often been discussed as a potential candidate for office, though he has never run.
