Proto-Indo-European mythology

Proto-Indo-European mythology is the reconstructed system of myths and deities of the speakers of Proto-Indo-European, the prehistoric parent language of the Indo-European family. Since the Proto-Indo-Europeans left no writing, their religion is recovered by the comparative method, matching cognate gods, formulas, and stories across descendant traditions including Vedic, Greek, Roman, Norse, Celtic, and Baltic mythology.

Reconstructed deities and themes

  • *Dyēus — the bright sky father, reflected in Zeus, Jupiter, Dyaus, and Týr
  • Perkwunos — a storm god who slays a serpent, echoed in Thor, Perun, and Indra's defeat of Vritra
  • The Dawn goddess (*H2éwsōs) — Greek Eos, Roman Aurora, Vedic Ushas
  • The Divine Twins — youthful horsemen and rescuers, such as the Greek Dioscuri and Vedic Ashvins
  • A creation myth involving the sacrifice and dismemberment of a primordial being or twin (compare Ymir and Yama)
  • A dragon- or serpent-slaying myth underlying many heroic tales

Scholarship

Comparative mythology developed in the 19th century and was given a systematic framework by Georges Dumézil, who proposed a trifunctional ideology dividing society into sovereignty, warfare, and fertility/production. Modern scholars debate the details while continuing to refine reconstructions using linguistic and archaeological evidence.
