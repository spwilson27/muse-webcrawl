Prime Minister of Iceland

The Prime Minister of Iceland (Icelandic: Forsætisráðherra Íslands) is the head of government of Iceland. The prime minister is appointed by the President of Iceland and leads the cabinet, exercising executive authority together with the other ministers. Because Iceland's governments are almost always coalitions, the prime minister is usually the leader of the largest party in a multi-party governing alliance formed after elections to the Althing, Iceland's parliament.

Stjórnarráðshúsið, the prime minister's office in Reykjavík.

History

The office originated with home rule in 1904, when Hannes Hafstein became Minister for Iceland within the Danish government. When Iceland became a sovereign state in personal union with Denmark in 1918, the post became prime minister of a separate cabinet, and with the founding of the republic in 1944, the modern office took shape. Notable holders include Jóhanna Sigurðardóttir, who in 2009 became the world's first openly lesbian head of government, and Katrín Jakobsdóttir, prime minister from 2017 to 2024. Geir Haarde was prosecuted before a special court over the 2008 financial crisis, the only Icelandic minister to be tried in this way.

Role

The prime minister chairs cabinet meetings, allocates portfolios, coordinates government policy, and represents Iceland abroad, including at the European Council-level meetings with Nordic and other partners. The prime minister's office is located at Stjórnarráðshúsið in central Reykjavík; the official residence is Þingvallir and the summer residence at Bessastaðir belongs to the president.
