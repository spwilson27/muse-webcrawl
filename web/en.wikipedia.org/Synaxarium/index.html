Synaxarium

A synaxarium (Greek: Synaxarion; plural synaxaria) is a collection of brief lives of the saints, arranged in calendar order according to their feast days, used in the Eastern Orthodox and Oriental Orthodox churches. Unlike a longer hagiography or a full menologium, the synaxarium gives short notices suitable for reading aloud at the morning office (Orthros/Matins) after the sixth ode of the canon.

Forms

The classical Byzantine text is the Synaxarion of Constantinople, compiled in the 10th century, traditionally in the circle of Emperor Constantine VII; its notices underlie most later Orthodox calendars. The Coptic Synaxarium, in Arabic, commemorates the martyrs and saints of the Coptic Church and remains in daily use, organised by the Coptic calendar's months from Thout to Mesori. The Ethiopian Synaxarium, translated from Arabic sources and greatly expanded, adds numerous native Ethiopian saints and is one of the largest such collections. Armenian and Syriac traditions preserve their own synaxaria.

Use

Selections from the synaxarium are read liturgically, printed in the Menaia and in popular daily devotionals such as the Prologue of Ohrid by St Nikolai Velimirovich, which reworked the Slavonic synaxarium for modern readers.
