Sandra Harding

Sandra G. Harding (born March 29, 1935) is an American philosopher of science and a leading figure in feminist epistemology and science and technology studies. She is a Distinguished Research Professor Emerita of education and gender studies at the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA), and previously served as director of the UCLA Center for the Study of Women.

Harding is best known for developing the theory of standpoint epistemology in the philosophy of science and for introducing the concept of strong objectivity. Her work argues that inquiry which starts from the lives of marginalized people can produce less partial and distorted accounts of the natural and social world than inquiry that begins from dominant groups' experiences.

Life and career

Harding received her bachelor's degree from Douglass College of Rutgers University in 1956 and her Ph.D. in philosophy from New York University in 1973. She taught at the State University of New York at Albany, the University of Delaware, and UCLA. She has also held visiting positions internationally and consulted with organizations including the United Nations on gender and science policy.

Major works

Her influential books include The Science Question in Feminism (1986), Whose Science? Whose Knowledge? Thinking from Women's Lives (1991), Is Science Multicultural? Postcolonialisms, Feminisms, and Epistemologies (1998), and Sciences from Below: Feminisms, Postcolonialities, and Modernities (2008). She also edited the collection The Feminist Standpoint Theory Reader (2004) and co-edited several handbooks on feminist research methodologies.

Standpoint theory and strong objectivity

Harding's standpoint theory holds that knowledge is socially situated, and that starting research from marginalized standpoints can reveal assumptions and questions invisible from positions of privilege. "Strong objectivity" extends the norms of scientific objectivity by requiring critical scrutiny of the social values and interests that shape research agendas, concepts, and evidence — not merely of research results.
