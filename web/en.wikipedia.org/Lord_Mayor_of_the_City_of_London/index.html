Lord Mayor of the City of London

The Lord Mayor's Show

The Lord Mayor of the City of London is the mayor of the City of London and the leader of the City of London Corporation. The office is distinct from the directly elected Mayor of London, who governs Greater London. Within the City, the Lord Mayor is treated as the first citizen and takes precedence over all others, including members of the Royal Family apart from the sovereign.

History and election

The office dates to 1189, when Henry FitzAilwin became the first recorded Mayor of London; the title "Lord Mayor" came into use in the 16th century. The most famous holder is Richard "Dick" Whittington, who served multiple terms in the late 14th and early 15th centuries. Candidates must be aldermen of the City and former sheriffs. Each year the liverymen of the City's livery companies elect two candidates at Common Hall on Michaelmas Day, from whom the Court of Aldermen chooses one to serve a one-year term beginning in November.

Role

The Lord Mayor presides over the Court of Aldermen and Court of Common Council, resides at the Mansion House, and acts as ambassador for the UK's financial and professional services sector, travelling extensively to promote the City. Ceremonial highlights include the Silent Ceremony of admission and the Lord Mayor's Show, a procession dating back centuries, and the Lord Mayor's Banquet at Guildhall, where the Prime Minister traditionally gives a major foreign policy speech.
