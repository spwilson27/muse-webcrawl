Franz Viktor Werfel (10 September 1890 – 26 August 1945) was an Austrian-Bohemian novelist, playwright, poet and essayist, associated with the expressionist movement and the literary circle of Max Brod and Franz Kafka in Prague. His novels reached a worldwide audience, particularly The Forty Days of Musa Dagh and The Song of Bernadette.

Life

Werfel was born in Prague into a Jewish family and served as a soldier and press officer on the Eastern Front in the First World War. He moved in the Viennese literary world and in 1929 married Alma Mahler, widow of the composer Gustav Mahler. After the Anschluss of 1938 the couple fled Austria; in 1940 they escaped France over the Pyrenees — a route he shared with Heinrich Mann — and reached the United States.

Works

Werfel first won fame as a lyric poet (Der Weltfreund, 1911) and playwright (The Trojan Women, 1915). His novel The Forty Days of Musa Dagh (1933), about the Armenian genocide and the resistance at Musa Dagh in 1915, became an international bestseller and an enduring symbol for Armenians. In Lourdes in 1940, hiding from the Nazis, he vowed to write the story of Saint Bernadette if he escaped; the resulting novel, The Song of Bernadette (1941), was a bestseller and was filmed by Hollywood in 1943, winning four Academy Awards. Werfel died of a heart attack in Beverly Hills in 1945.
