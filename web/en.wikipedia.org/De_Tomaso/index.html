De Tomaso

De Tomaso Modena S.p.A. is an Italian sports car manufacturer founded in Modena in 1959 by the Argentine businessman and racing driver Alejandro de Tomaso. The company is best known for pairing Italian design with American Ford V8 engines in mid-engined sports cars, most famously the Pantera.

History

Alejandro de Tomaso began by building racing cars, including a brief Formula One effort that later supplied cars raced by Frank Williams in 1970. The first road car was the small mid-engined Vallelunga (1963), followed by the striking Giorgetto Giugiaro-designed Mangusta (1967), powered by a Ford V8. The Pantera (1971), styled by Tom Tjaarda at Ghia and powered by a Ford 351 Cleveland V8, was sold through Lincoln-Mercury dealers in the United States and remained in production, in evolving forms, into the early 1990s — over 7,000 were built, making it the marque's greatest success.

Corporate expansion and decline

With Ford backing and Italian government support, de Tomaso acquired Ghia, Vignale, Innocenti, and in 1976 Maserati, plus motorcycle maker Moto Guzzi. The group's fortunes faded in the 1980s–90s; the Maserati stake was sold to Fiat, and Alejandro de Tomaso's stroke in 1993 was followed by decline. The company went into liquidation in 2004, a year after his death. Subsequent revival attempts collapsed amid scandal in 2012.

Revival

In 2014 the brand was acquired by Hong Kong-based Ideal Team Ventures, which unveiled the retro-styled, Apollo-derived De Tomaso P72 at the 2019 Goodwood Festival of Speed, followed by the track-focused P900.
