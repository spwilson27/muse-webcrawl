County Limerick (Irish: Contae Luimnigh) is a western county in the Republic of Ireland, in the province of Munster. It is named after the city of Limerick, which is its county town and administrative capital. The county's fertile plains form part of the Golden Vale, one of Ireland's richest agricultural regions, and its western boundary runs along the Shannon Estuary.

Geography

County Limerick covers about 2,756 km². The River Shannon forms its northern boundary, broadening into the Shannon Estuary in the northwest. Principal rivers include the Maigue, Deel, and Mulkear. The Ballyhoura Mountains rise on the southern border with County Cork, Mount Seefin being among the county's highest points, while the extinct volcano of Knockfierna overlooks the central plain. Other towns include Newcastle West, the county's second largest, Abbeyfeale, Rathkeale, Kilmallock, Adare, and Askeaton.

History

The area has prehistoric settlement evident at Lough Gur, one of Ireland's most important archaeological sites, with stone circles and lake dwellings dating to the Neolithic. Viking Limerick was founded in the 9th century and the region was later a stronghold of the O'Brien kings of Thomond before Anglo-Norman settlement fortified towns such as Kilmallock and Askeaton. The county saw fighting in the Confederate Wars, the Williamite War — including the sieges of Limerick of 1690–91 and the Treaty of Limerick — and the Irish War of Independence and Civil War.

Sport and culture

Limerick is renowned for hurling, winning multiple All-Ireland Senior Hurling Championships, including a celebrated run of titles in the early 2020s, and for rugby, with Thomond Park home to Munster Rugby. The county's sporting motto, and the famous line "There once was...", nod to the limerick verse form, whose connection to the county is traditional though disputed. Adare, with its thatched cottages and manor house, is often called Ireland's prettiest village.
