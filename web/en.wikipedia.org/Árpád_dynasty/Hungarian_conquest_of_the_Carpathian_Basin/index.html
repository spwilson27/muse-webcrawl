Hungarian conquest of the Carpathian Basin

The Hungarian conquest of the Carpathian Basin, also known in Hungary as the Honfoglalás ("taking of the homeland"), was the series of events in which the Magyar (Hungarian) tribes, led by Árpád, settled in the Carpathian Basin at the end of the 9th century, principally in 895–896. The conquest created the Principality of Hungary, the predecessor state of the Kingdom of Hungary.

Background and course

Before the conquest, the Magyars lived in Etelköz, a territory between the Dnieper and the Danube. Pressured by Pecheneg attacks and involved in wars between Bulgaria and the Byzantine Empire, the seven tribes crossed the Carpathian passes — including the Verecke Pass — and occupied the plains of the basin, which were then under the fragmented control of Moravia, Bulgaria and local Slavic and Avar-descended populations. The conquest was consolidated by victories such as the Battle of Pressburg in 907.
