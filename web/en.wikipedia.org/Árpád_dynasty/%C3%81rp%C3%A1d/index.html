Árpád

Árpád (c. 845 – c. 907) was the head of the confederation of the Magyar tribes at the end of the 9th and the beginning of the 10th centuries. According to Hungarian chronicles, he was the son of Álmos, the first head of the confederation, and was elected leader of the Hungarians around 895. Under his leadership the Magyars carried out the Hungarian conquest of the Carpathian Basin (the Honfoglalás), the foundational event of Hungarian history.

Life and legacy

Most information about Árpád comes from later chronicles such as the Gesta Hungarorum and the Chronicon Pictum, whose details are partly legendary. He is regarded as the founder of the Árpád dynasty, whose members ruled Hungary until 1301. Árpád died around 907, reportedly in the aftermath of the Battle of Pressburg, and was succeeded by his son Zolta.
