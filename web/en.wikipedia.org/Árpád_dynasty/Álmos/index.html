Álmos (Hungarian pronunciation: [ˈaːlmoʃ]; c. 820 – c. 895) was — according to Hungarian chronicles — the first head of the federation of the seven Hungarian tribes, and the father of Árpád, founder of the Árpád dynasty that ruled Hungary until 1301. He led the Magyars during their migration westward from Etelköz toward the Carpathian Basin, but, according to legend, did not himself enter the new homeland.

Life in the chronicles

The main sources are the Gesta Hungarorum of the anonymous notary of King Béla (c. 1200) and the chronicles of Simon of Kéza and the Illuminated Chronicle. They relate that Álmos was the son of Ügyek and Emese; his name — from the Hungarian word álom, "dream" — derives from the legend of Emese's dream, in which a divine turul bird foretold that her descendants would be glorious kings.

According to the Gesta Hungarorum, the seven chieftains elected Álmos as their leader in Etelköz and swore a blood oath to him and his line. Historians debate whether his actual title was kende (sacral prince) or gyula (military commander) within the dual kingship the Magyars are believed to have adopted from the Khazars.

Death and the conquest

Tradition holds that Álmos was killed — or ritually sacrificed — in Transylvania around 895, on the threshold of the Hungarian conquest of the Carpathian Basin, because, in Simon of Kéza's account, "he could not enter Pannonia", as it was ordained he should die before the conquest. His son Árpád succeeded him and led the tribes into their new homeland. Some historians consider Álmos a historical figure whose sacral-king role ended with a ritual regicide customary among steppe peoples; others treat many details as later legend.
