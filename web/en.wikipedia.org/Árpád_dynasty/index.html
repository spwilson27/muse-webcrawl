Árpád dynasty

The Árpád dynasty was the ruling royal house of the Principality and later Kingdom of Hungary from about 895 to 1301. Founded by Árpád, the grand prince who led the Magyar tribes into the Carpathian Basin during the Hungarian conquest, the dynasty produced Hungary's first kings and a remarkable number of saints, and it ended with the death of Andrew III in 1301.

History

After the conquest of the Carpathian Basin, Árpád's descendants ruled as grand princes. His great-great-grandson Stephen I was crowned king in 1000 or 1001 and founded the Christian Kingdom of Hungary, organizing its counties and church hierarchy; he was canonized in 1083. Later Árpádian kings included Ladislaus I, Coloman the Learned, and Béla IV, who rebuilt the kingdom after the Mongol invasion of 1241–42. The dynasty also gave the Church several saints, including Emeric, Margaret, and Elizabeth of Hungary.

End of the dynasty

The male line died out when Andrew III died in 1301 without a male heir. A succession struggle followed, and the throne eventually passed to the Angevin Charles Robert, who claimed descent through the female line. The Árpáds remain central to Hungarian national identity, symbolized by the red-and-white Árpád stripes used in Hungarian heraldry.
