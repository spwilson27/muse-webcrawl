Árpád dynasty

The Árpád dynasty (Hungarian: Árpád-ház) was the ruling dynasty of the Principality of Hungary in the 9th and 10th centuries and of the Kingdom of Hungary from 1000 to 1301. It was founded by Árpád, leader of the Magyar tribes during the Hungarian conquest of the Carpathian Basin. The dynasty is named after him and is also sometimes called the dynasty of the "holy kings", because several of its members — including Stephen I, the first king of Hungary, and Ladislaus I — were canonized.

History

Árpád's descendants ruled first as grand princes of the Hungarians. Vajk, baptized Stephen, was crowned king in 1000 or 1001 and organized the Christian kingdom. The male line of the dynasty died out with Andrew III in 1301, after which Hungary passed — after a period of interregnum and competing claims — to the Angevin Charles Robert, whose claim descended through the female line of the Árpáds.
