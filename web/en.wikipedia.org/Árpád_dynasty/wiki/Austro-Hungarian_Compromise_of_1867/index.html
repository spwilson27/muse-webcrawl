Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867

The Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867 (German: Ausgleich; Hungarian: kiegyezés) established the dual monarchy of Austria-Hungary. The Compromise partially re-established the former pre-1848 sovereignty and status of the Kingdom of Hungary, which had been deprived of its de facto existence following the Hungarian Revolution of 1848.

Terms

Negotiated principally by Ferenc Deák for Hungary and accepted by Emperor Franz Joseph I, the settlement created two states under one monarch: the Austrian Empire (Cisleithania) and the Kingdom of Hungary (Transleithania), each with its own parliament, government and capital, while foreign affairs, defence and the finances for common affairs remained joint, supervised by delegations of the two parliaments. Franz Joseph was crowned King of Hungary in Buda in June 1867.

Consequences

The Compromise stabilised the Habsburg monarchy for fifty years and underpinned rapid Hungarian economic development, but it entrenched Magyar predominance within Hungary and left the claims of other nationalities unresolved — tensions that contributed to the monarchy's collapse in 1918.
