Álmos

ReignGrand Prince of the Hungarians, c. 850 – c. 895
SonÁrpád

Álmos (c. 820 – c. 895) was — according to the medieval Hungarian chronicles — the first head of the confederation of the Magyar tribes and the father of Árpád, leader of the Hungarian conquest of the Carpathian Basin. The Gesta Hungarorum of the anonymous notary of King Béla describes Álmos as elected leader of the seven Magyar chieftains in Etelköz, sealed by a blood oath.

Legend and history

His name, meaning "the dreamy one" or "dreamt-of", is linked in the chronicle tradition to the Turul legend: his mother Emese dreamed that a turul bird impregnated her and prophesied that her descendants would be glorious kings. Constantine VII's De Administrando Imperio, by contrast, does not name Álmos as ruler but says Árpád was raised on a shield as prince. Whether Álmos led the Magyars westward himself or died — possibly by ritual sacrifice — before the occupation of the Basin around 895 remains debated by historians.

Legacy

Álmos is remembered as the dynastic founder from whom the Árpád dynasty claimed descent; the kings of Hungary traced their line through his son Árpád to the last Árpád king, Andrew III (died 1301).
