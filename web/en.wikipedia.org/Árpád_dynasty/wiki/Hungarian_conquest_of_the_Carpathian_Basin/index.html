Hungarian conquest of the Carpathian Basin

The Honfoglalás: Hungarian tribes crossing the Carpathians into their new homeland.

The Hungarian conquest of the Carpathian Basin, also known as the Hungarian conquest or simply Honfoglalás ("taking of the homeland"), was the series of events at the end of the 9th century in which the Hungarian tribes, led by Árpád, settled in the Carpathian Basin and founded the Principality of Hungary.

Background

The Hungarians (Magyars) were a semi-nomadic people from the steppes east of the Carpathians who had lived in Etelköz, between the Dnieper and the Danube, under Khazar influence. Pressured by the Pechenegs and drawn into the wars between Bulgaria and the Byzantine Empire, the seven Hungarian tribes concluded a blood oath, elected Álmos and then his son Árpád as their leader, and migrated westward.

The conquest

Around 895 the Hungarians crossed the passes of the north-eastern Carpathians and occupied the lowlands of the basin, defeating or displacing the local Slavic, Avar remnant and Moravian populations and the Bulgarian garrisons south of the Danube. Over the following decades Hungarian forces consolidated control of the whole basin. From their new homeland, mounted raiders struck deep into Western Europe until their defeat at the Battle of Lechfeld in 955 ended the raids and turned the principality toward settled statehood and Christianisation under Géza and Stephen I.
