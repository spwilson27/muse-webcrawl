Stephen I of Hungary

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Stephen I (Hungarian: I. István; born Vajk, c. 975 – 15 August 1038), also known as Saint Stephen, was the last Grand Prince of the Hungarians and the first King of Hungary, reigning from 1000 or 1001 until his death. He founded the Kingdom of Hungary, established its Christian church organization, and is regarded as Hungary's founding statesman and patron saint.

Reign

Stephen succeeded his father Géza in 997 and defeated rival pagan claimants, notably his relative Koppány, to consolidate power. Crowned with a crown traditionally said to have been sent by Pope Sylvester II, he organized the kingdom into royal counties, founded bishoprics and monasteries such as Pannonhalma Abbey, issued law codes, and promoted the conversion of the Hungarians to Christianity. He married Gisela of Bavaria, sister of Emperor Henry II.

Canonization and legacy

Stephen was canonized in 1083 by Pope Gregory VII, together with his son Emeric, who had predeceased him. His feast day, 20 August, is Hungary's principal national holiday, celebrating the foundation of the state. The Holy Crown and the preserved relic of his right hand remain objects of national devotion.
