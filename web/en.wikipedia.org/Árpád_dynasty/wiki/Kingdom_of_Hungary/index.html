Kingdom of Hungary

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Kingdom of Hungary was a monarchy in Central Europe that existed from the coronation of Stephen I in 1000 (or 1001) until 1946, with interruptions, making it one of Europe's longest-lived states. For much of the Middle Ages it was a major power encompassing modern Hungary, Slovakia, Transylvania, Croatia, and parts of neighboring regions, ruled first by the Árpád dynasty and later by Angevin, Luxembourg, Jagiellonian, and Habsburg kings.

History

Stephen I founded the Christian kingdom and organized its counties and dioceses. Under the Angevins in the 14th century and Matthias Corvinus in the 15th, Hungary flourished as a regional great power, but the defeat at Mohács in 1526 led to partition between the Ottoman Empire, the Habsburg monarchy, and Transylvania. Reunification under the Habsburgs followed in the late 17th century. The Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867 made Hungary an equal partner in the dual monarchy of Austria-Hungary. After 1918 the kingdom shrank drastically under the Treaty of Trianon; it persisted as a regency under Miklós Horthy until the monarchy was abolished in 1946.

Legacy

The Holy Crown of Hungary, the historic counties, and the concept of the "Lands of the Crown of Saint Stephen" remain central symbols of Hungarian national identity.
