The Árpád dynasty (Hungarian: Árpádok) was the ruling dynasty of the Principality of Hungary in the 9th and 10th centuries and of the Kingdom of Hungary from 1000 to 1301. It is named after Árpád, the leader who, according to tradition, led the Magyar tribes into the Carpathian Basin during the Hungarian conquest around 895. The dynasty's members founded the Christian Hungarian kingdom, and several of its kings and family members were canonised, giving the house a reputation as a dynasty of saints.

Origins

According to the medieval chronicles, notably the Gesta Hungarorum, the dynasty descended from Álmos, the first head of the federation of Magyar tribes. Hungarian tradition held that Álmos was descended from Attila the Hun, and the dynasty's eponymous hero, his son Árpád, became grand prince of the Hungarians. Modern historians treat much of this genealogy as legend, while accepting the central role of Árpád in the conquest of the Carpathian Basin.

Grand princes

Árpád's descendants ruled as grand princes of the Hungarians through the 10th century. His successors included Zoltán, Fajsz, Taksony and Géza. Grand Prince Géza (r. c. 971–997) strengthened central authority, opened relations with the Holy Roman Empire, and began the Christianisation of Hungary, having his son baptised under the name Stephen.

Kings of Hungary

Stephen I was crowned the first king of Hungary in 1000 or 1001 and is venerated as Saint Stephen, the founder of the Christian kingdom. After his death the succession was repeatedly contested, but the throne remained with Árpád descendants: among the dynasty's notable kings were Saint Ladislaus I, Coloman the Learned, Béla III, Andrew II (issuer of the Golden Bull of 1222), and Béla IV, who rebuilt the kingdom after the Mongol invasion of 1241–42. Ladislaus IV was the last effective ruler of the line's senior branch; the throne passed to Andrew III, the "Venetian", the last male member of the dynasty.

Extinction and legacy

With the death of Andrew III in 1301, the male line of the Árpád dynasty became extinct. After a period of interregnum and competing claims, the throne passed — through descent in the female line — to Charles Robert of the Capetian House of Anjou. The Árpáds left a lasting mark: they founded the Hungarian state, its counties and church organisation, and produced more canonised saints than any other medieval royal house, including Saint Stephen, Saint Emeric, Saint Ladislaus, Saint Elizabeth of Hungary, and Saint Margaret of Hungary. The red-and-white barry shield associated with the dynasty — the "Árpád stripes" — remains a historic element of Hungarian heraldry.
