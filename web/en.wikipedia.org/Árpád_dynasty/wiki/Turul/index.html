Turul

The Turul is a mythological bird of prey, mostly depicted as a falcon, in Hungarian tradition and a national symbol of Hungary. According to legend, the Turul appeared in a dream to Emese, the mother of Álmos, and prophesied that her descendants would become glorious rulers — the origin myth of the Árpád dynasty.

Legend

The Gesta Hungarorum and other medieval chronicles recount that Emese dreamed of a Turul bird which impregnated her and foretold a line of great kings. The bird also guided the Hungarians during their migration: a Turul is said to have led the tribal chiefs toward the Carpathian Basin, dropping a sword or showing the way when the tribes hesitated.

Symbolism

The Turul remains a prominent Hungarian emblem. Monumental statues stand at Tatabánya — one of the largest bird statues in Europe — and at the Buda Castle in Budapest. The image appears on coats of arms and was historically associated with the Árpád dynasty's divine right to rule.
