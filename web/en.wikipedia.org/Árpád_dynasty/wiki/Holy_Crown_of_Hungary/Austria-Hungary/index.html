Austria-Hungary and the Holy Crown of Hungary

Austria-Hungary, formally the Austro-Hungarian Monarchy (1867–1918), was structured as a real union of two states: the Austrian Empire and the Kingdom of Hungary. In Hungarian constitutional doctrine the latter was identified with the Holy Crown of Hungary — Transleithania comprised, officially, the "lands of the Holy Crown of Hungary", and the crown itself symbolised the continuity of Hungarian statehood reaching back to the medieval kingdom founded under the Árpád dynasty.

The 1867 settlement

The Compromise of 1867 made the doctrine practical law: Emperor Franz Joseph was crowned with the Holy Crown in Buda in June 1867, swearing to uphold Hungary's constitution and territorial integrity. Hungarian governments insisted that common Austro-Hungarian institutions rested on the consent embodied in Hungarian Law XII of 1867, renewed economically every ten years.

Symbolism

The crown appeared on Hungarian coinage, arms and official seals throughout the dualist era; the Crown Guard in Budapest watched over the regalia. After Charles IV's last coronation in 1916 and the monarchy's fall in 1918, the doctrine of the Holy Crown continued to shape Hungarian political thought well into the twentieth century.
