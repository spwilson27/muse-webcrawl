Hungarian conquest of the Carpathian Basin

Datec. 895–907
LocationCarpathian Basin (Pannonian Plain)
ResultHungarian settlement; foundation of the Principality of Hungary

The Hungarian conquest of the Carpathian Basin, known in Hungarian as the honfoglalás ("taking of the homeland"), was the series of events at the end of the 9th century in which the Magyar tribal confederation, led first by Álmos and then by his son Árpád, settled in the Carpathian Basin.

Background

The Hungarians had lived in Etelköz, between the Dnieper and the Danube, before pressure from the Pechenegs and a Bulgarian attack pushed them westward across the Carpathian passes, traditionally dated to 895.

The conquest

The incoming tribes defeated or absorbed the local powers of the region — Moravian, Frankish and Bulgarian forces — and occupied the whole basin by about 907, a consolidation sealed by the Battle of Pressburg (Pozsony), where a Bavarian army was destroyed. The conquest created the Principality of Hungary, the direct predecessor of the Kingdom of Hungary.
