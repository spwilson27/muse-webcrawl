Álmos

Álmos, semi-legendary first leader of the confederation of Hungarian tribes.

Álmos (c. 820 – c. 895) was — according to the medieval Hungarian chronicles — the first head of the confederation of the seven Hungarian tribes. The Hungarian chronicles describe him as the father of Árpád, founder of the Árpád dynasty and of the Principality of Hungary. His historicity is debated, and the traditions about him are woven with legend.

Legend and sources

The Gesta Hungarorum of the anonymous notary of King Béla tells that Álmos's mother, Emese, dreamed of a turul bird — a mythical falcon — which prophesied that her descendants would be glorious kings. His very name derives from the Hungarian word álom, "dream". The chronicles relate that the chieftains of the seven tribes swore a blood oath at Etelköz, mingling their blood in a vessel, and elected Álmos as their leader.

Death

According to tradition, Álmos did not enter the Carpathian Basin: he was killed, or ritually sacrificed, in Transylvania at the moment of the conquest, so that his son Árpád led the Hungarians into their new homeland. Modern historians treat many of these details cautiously, since the written sources are centuries later than the events.
