Habsburg monarchy

The Habsburg monarchy, also known as the Danubian monarchy or the Austrian monarchy, was the collection of empires, kingdoms, duchies, counties and other polities ruled by the Austrian branch of the House of Habsburg from the late 13th century until the monarchy's dissolution in 1918. It was not a single unified state but a composite monarchy held together by the person of the ruler.

Formation and growth

The monarchy's core formed with Rudolf I's acquisition of Austria in 1278–1282. It grew decisively in 1526, when Ferdinand I inherited Bohemia and Hungary after the death of Louis II at Mohács. Through the following centuries the Habsburgs ruled Spain and its empire (under the Spanish branch), the Austrian Netherlands, much of Italy, and (after 1867) the dual monarchy of Austria-Hungary.

Character and end

The monarchy was famously multilingual and multi-confessional, administered from Vienna. The Hungarian lands, including those claimed by the Árpád-founded kingdom, formed one of its historic pillars — a relationship formalised by the 1867 Compromise. Defeat in the First World War and the secession of its constituent nations ended the monarchy in November 1918.
