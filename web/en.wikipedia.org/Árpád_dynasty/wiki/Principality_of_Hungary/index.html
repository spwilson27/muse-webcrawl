The Principality of Hungary was the earliest documented Hungarian state, formed in the Carpathian Basin after the Hungarian conquest of the region at the end of the 9th century. It existed from about 895 until 1000, when Stephen I was crowned king and the Christian Kingdom of Hungary was established.

History

Under Grand Prince Árpád, the seven Magyar tribes and their allies occupied the Carpathian Basin in the conquest known in Hungarian as the honfoglalás. In the following decades Hungarian mounted warriors conducted raids across Europe, reaching as far as Iberia and Italy, until their defeat at the Battle of Lechfeld in 955 ended the western campaigns.

Grand Prince Géza (reigned c. 972–997) began orienting the principality toward Christian Europe, inviting missionaries and establishing relations with the Holy Roman Empire. His son Stephen completed the transformation, founding the kingdom in 1000 or 1001.

Organisation

The principality grew out of a tribal confederation headed by the Árpád dynasty. The Grand Prince's authority gradually strengthened over the tribal chieftains, laying the foundations of the medieval Hungarian state and its county system.
