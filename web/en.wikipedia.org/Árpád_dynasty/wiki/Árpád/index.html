Árpád

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Árpád (c. 845 – c. 907) was the head of the confederation of the Hungarian tribes at the turn of the 9th and 10th centuries and is traditionally regarded as the founder of the Hungarian nation and of the Árpád dynasty, which ruled Hungary until 1301. According to the medieval chronicles, he was the son of Álmos and was elected leader by the chieftains of the seven Magyar tribes, who swore the famous blood oath at Etelköz.

The Conquest

Under Árpád's leadership, the Hungarians crossed the Carpathians around 895 and conquered the Carpathian Basin, the event known in Hungarian as the Honfoglalás ("the Taking of the Homeland"). The Gesta Hungarorum of Anonymus credits Árpád with a central role in organizing the settlement and distributing lands among the tribes, though the chronicles were written centuries later and mix fact with legend.

Legacy

Árpád is one of the most revered figures in Hungarian history; his statue stands at the center of the colonnade of Heroes' Square in Budapest. The dynasty he founded produced Hungary's kings for four centuries, including Saint Stephen, the kingdom's founder.
