The Holy Crown of Hungary within Austria-Hungary

The Holy Crown of Hungary (Hungarian: Szent Korona), the coronation crown of the Kingdom of Hungary, held a unique constitutional position within Austria-Hungary, the dual monarchy formed by the Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867. Under the doctrine of the Holy Crown, the crown was not mere regalia but the embodiment of Hungarian statehood and sovereignty: the "lands of the Holy Crown" (a Szent Korona országai) constituted the Hungarian half of the monarchy, known formally as Transleithania.

Constitutional role

Franz Joseph I was crowned King of Hungary with the Holy Crown in Buda in June 1867, restoring Hungary's constitutional government. Hungarian public law held that legitimate kingship depended on coronation with this crown, performed by the Archbishop of Esztergom at Székesfehérvár or, later, Budapest. Charles IV, the last emperor-king, was crowned with it in December 1916.

Fate after 1918

After the monarchy's collapse the crown remained in Budapest, was seized and transported abroad at the end of the Second World War, and was held by the United States until its ceremonial return to Hungary in 1978. It is now displayed in the Hungarian Parliament Building. See also the Holy Crown of Hungary.
