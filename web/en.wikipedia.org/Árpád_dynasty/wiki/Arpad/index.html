Árpád

Bornc. 845
Diedc. 907
TitleGrand Prince of the Hungarians
DynastyÁrpád dynasty

Árpád (Hungarian pronunciation: [ˈaːrpaːd]; c. 845 – c. 907) was the head of the confederation of the Magyar tribes at the turn of the ninth and tenth centuries and is traditionally regarded as the founder of Hungary. He led the Hungarians in their conquest of the Carpathian Basin (the Honfoglalás, "taking of the homeland") around 895, and the royal house he founded — the Árpád dynasty — ruled the Kingdom of Hungary until 1301.

Life

Árpád was the son of Álmos, the first supreme leader of the Magyar tribal confederation. According to the medieval Hungarian chronicles, notably the Gesta Hungarorum of the notary Anonymus, the seven chieftains of the Hungarian tribes elected Árpád as their leader and sealed their covenant by mixing their blood in a single vessel (the vérszerződés, or blood oath). Historians debate how literally these accounts should be read, but Árpád's leadership of the migration westward is broadly accepted.

Under pressure from the Pechenegs and in alliance at first with the Byzantine Empire against Bulgaria, the Magyars crossed the Carpathian passes around 895 and occupied the basin, defeating the region's Moravian, Frankish and Bulgar forces in a series of campaigns over the following decade. Árpád himself is usually given the title of Grand Prince (nagyfejedelem), though some sources suggest he held the lesser office of kende or gyula alongside a sacral ruler.

Death and legacy

Árpád died around 907, possibly in connection with the Battle of Pressburg, in which Hungarian forces destroyed a Bavarian army. He was succeeded by his son Zoltán. His descendants included Saint Stephen I, the first crowned King of Hungary (1000/1001), and the dynasty named after him ruled Hungary for four centuries. Árpád is commemorated by the central figure group of the Millennium Monument in Heroes' Square, Budapest, and by countless place-names, schools and statues throughout Hungary.
