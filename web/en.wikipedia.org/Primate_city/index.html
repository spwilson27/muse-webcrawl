Primate city

A primate city is a city that is much larger than any other in its country or region and dominates its economic, political and cultural life. The term was coined by geographer Mark Jefferson in 1939.

Examples

Classic examples include Bangkok in Thailand, which is many times larger than the country's second city; Buenos Aires in Argentina; Cairo in Egypt; and Mexico City in Mexico. Paris and London are often cited as primate cities of their respective countries.

Causes and effects

Primate cities often arise in centralised states and former colonies where administration and trade were concentrated in one port or capital. Their dominance can cause regional imbalance and congestion but also agglomeration benefits.
