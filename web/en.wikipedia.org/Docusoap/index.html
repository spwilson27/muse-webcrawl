Docusoap

A docusoap is a hybrid television genre that combines the observational style of the documentary with the serialised storytelling and character-driven narratives of the soap opera. Docusoaps follow real people as they go about their lives or work—often in a single workplace or location—over multiple episodes, with storylines developed through editing, recurring characters and cliffhangers. The genre became prominent in British television in the 1990s and contributed to the rise of reality television.

History

Precursors include the American series An American Family (1973), which followed the Loud family, and British fly-on-the-wall documentaries of the 1970s and 1980s such as The Family (1974). The genre took off in Britain in the mid-1990s with fixed-rig and observational series such as Airport (1996), following staff at Heathrow, Driving School (1997), whose participant Maureen Rees became a celebrity, and The Clampers, Lakesiders and Paddington Green. These programmes were inexpensive to produce and drew large audiences.

Characteristics

Typical features include a fixed location or institution, a returning cast of real-life "characters", narration that identifies story arcs, and editing that highlights conflict, humour and personal drama. Because producers shape stories in the edit, the genre blurs the boundary between observation and construction, a criticism also levelled at later reality formats. Docusoaps are distinguished from mockumentaries, which are scripted comedies, and from structured reality shows such as The Only Way Is Essex, which go further in manipulating situations.

Influence

The docusoap format paved the way for celebrity reality series, workplace formats such as The Hotel and One Born Every Minute, and internationally franchised shows. In the United States, series like The Real World and later Deadliest Catch and Ice Road Truckers adopted related conventions. Many ordinary participants became tabloid celebrities, raising questions about duty of care that later became a major issue for reality television generally.
