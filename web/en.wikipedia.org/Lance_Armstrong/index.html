Lance Edward Armstrong (born 18 September 1971) is an American former professional road racing cyclist. He was the dominant stage racer of his era, winning the Tour de France a record seven consecutive times from 1999 to 2005 after recovering from testicular cancer. In 2012 the United States Anti-Doping Agency (USADA) concluded that Armstrong had used performance-enhancing drugs throughout his career and had led "the most sophisticated, professionalized and successful doping program that sport has ever seen"; he was stripped of all results from August 1998 onward and banned for life from sanctioned sport. In January 2013 he admitted to doping in a televised interview with Oprah Winfrey.

Career

Born in Plano, Texas, Armstrong began as a triathlete before turning professional in cycling in 1992. He won the World Road Race Championship in Oslo in 1993. In October 1996 he was diagnosed with metastatic testicular cancer that had spread to his lungs, abdomen and brain; given a poor prognosis, he survived after aggressive chemotherapy chosen to preserve his lung capacity. He returned to racing with the U.S. Postal Service team and won the Tours of 1999–2005, a comeback widely celebrated as one of sport's great stories. After a first retirement he returned in 2009, finishing third in that year's Tour, and retired again in 2011.

Doping case and fallout

Allegations followed Armstrong for years — notably in the 2004 book L.A. Confidentiel and in testimony by former teammate Floyd Landis in 2010. A federal investigation was closed without charges in 2012, but USADA's "Reasoned Decision" that October assembled testimony from 11 former teammates describing systematic EPO use, blood transfusions and cortisone within the U.S. Postal team. The UCI ratified the sanctions; the IOC stripped Armstrong's 2000 Olympic bronze medal. Sponsors abandoned him, he resigned from the Livestrong cancer foundation he had founded in 1997, and he later settled a U.S. government civil suit for $5 million in 2018.

Legacy

Armstrong's story transformed public awareness of testicular cancer and raised hundreds of millions of dollars through Livestrong's yellow wristband campaign, while his downfall became the emblematic case of doping in endurance sport and of the intimidation of whistleblowers by a star athlete.
