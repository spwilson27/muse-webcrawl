Battle of the River Plate

Date13 December 1939
LocationOff the estuary of the River Plate, South Atlantic
ResultBritish strategic victory; Admiral Graf Spee scuttled
BelligerentsUnited Kingdom and New Zealand vs. Nazi Germany
CommandersCommodore Henry Harwood vs. Kapitän zur See Hans Langsdorff (died by suicide)

The Battle of the River Plate was the first major naval battle of the Second World War, fought on 13 December 1939 in the South Atlantic. The German commerce raider Admiral Graf Spee, a "pocket battleship", was engaged off the River Plate estuary by three smaller Royal Navy cruisers under Commodore Henry Harwood. Damaged, Graf Spee took refuge in neutral Montevideo and, convinced that overwhelming forces awaited her, was scuttled by her captain on 17 December.

Harwood's cruisers took on a far more powerful opponent.

Background

Since September 1939 Admiral Graf Spee, captained by Hans Langsdorff, had sunk nine merchant ships in the South Atlantic and Indian Ocean without loss of life among their crews. Harwood, commanding the South American Division, guessed that Langsdorff would strike at the busy shipping lanes off the River Plate and concentrated his force — the heavy cruiser Exeter and light cruisers Ajax and Achilles — there. His judgement was vindicated on the morning of 13 December.

The battle

Harwood split his force to divide the German ship's fire. Graf Spee's 11-inch guns badly mauled Exeter, knocking out most of her armament and killing her captain on the bridge's staff among 61 dead; Ajax and Achilles were also hit. But the cruisers inflicted significant damage in return, including to Graf Spee's fuel purification system, which limited her ability to return to Germany. Langsdorff broke off the action and made for Montevideo, shadowed by the surviving cruisers.

Montevideo and the scuttling

Diplomatic pressure and British deception — broadcasting reports suggesting the battleship Renown and carrier Ark Royal were arriving — convinced Langsdorff that escape was hopeless. On 17 December, before a huge crowd, he took Graf Spee out of harbour and scuttled her in the estuary. Three days later, in Buenos Aires, Langsdorff shot himself. The battle was a major early morale victory for Britain.
