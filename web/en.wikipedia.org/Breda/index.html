Breda

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Grote Kerk, Breda

Breda is a city and municipality in the province of North Brabant in the southern Netherlands. It lies near the Dutch-Belgian border at the confluence of the rivers Mark and Aa of Weerijs. The municipality has a population of roughly 185,000, making it one of the larger cities of North Brabant. Breda has long-standing ties to the Dutch royal house of Orange-Nassau, whose ancestors held the lordship of Breda from the 15th century.

History

Breda received city rights in 1252 and developed as a fortified trading town. It played a significant role in the Eighty Years' War: it was captured by Spanish forces in 1581, famously retaken in 1590 by a Dutch force hidden in a peat barge, besieged and captured again by Spinola in 1625 — the event depicted in Velázquez's painting The Surrender of Breda — and finally secured by Frederick Henry in 1637. The Treaty of Breda (1667) ended the Second Anglo-Dutch War. During the Second World War, Breda was liberated in October 1944 by the Polish 1st Armoured Division under General Maczek, who is commemorated in the city.

Landmarks

The city centre is dominated by the Grote Kerk (Great Church), a Brabantine Gothic church containing the tombs of early members of the Nassau dynasty. Other notable sites include Breda Castle, which houses the Royal Military Academy, the Begijnhof, and the Spanjaardsgat water gate. The football club NAC Breda is based in the city.
