Form 10-Q

Form 10-Q, (also known as a 10-Q or Quarterly Report) is a quarterly report mandated by the United States federal Securities and Exchange Commission, to be filed by publicly traded corporations. Pursuant to Section 13 or 15(d) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, the 10-Q must be filed within 40 days (for large accelerated filers and accelerated filers) or 45 days (for non-accelerated filers) after the end of each of the first three fiscal quarters of a company. It is not required for the fourth quarter, when the annual Form 10-K is filed instead.

Contents

The report contains unaudited financial statements, management's discussion and analysis of financial condition and results of operations (MD&A), disclosures about market risk, and information about controls and procedures. It provides a continuing view of the company's financial position during the year, though the financial information is generally less detailed than that in the annual Form 10-K.

Related forms

Form 10-Q is one of a family of SEC periodic reports, alongside the annual Form 10-K and the current report Form 8-K, which is used to report specified material events between periodic filings.
