Charente (French pronunciation: [ʃaʁɑ̃t]) is a department in western France, in the Nouvelle-Aquitaine region. It is named after the Charente River, which flows through it from east to west, passing the prefecture, Angoulême, and the town of Cognac before reaching the Atlantic near Rochefort (in neighbouring Charente-Maritime).

Geography and economy

The department is largely rural, with rolling limestone countryside, vineyards, and farmland. It is best known as the home of Cognac, the celebrated brandy produced around the town of the same name and in surrounding districts. Angoulême is a centre of paper-making historically and of animated film and comics today, hosting the annual Angoulême International Comics Festival.

History

Charente was created in 1790 during the French Revolution, largely from the former province of Angoumois together with parts of Poitou, Limousin, and Saintonge. The area is rich in prehistoric sites, and Angoumois was contested between French and English crowns during the Hundred Years' War.
