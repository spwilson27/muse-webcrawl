Clear aligners

Clear aligners are orthodontic devices that are transparent, plastic forms of dental braces used to adjust teeth. A series of custom-made, removable trays is fabricated from digital scans or impressions of the patient's teeth; each tray is worn for roughly one to two weeks and moves the teeth in small increments toward the planned final positions.

History

The concept grew from H. D. Kesling's tooth positioner (1945) and later vacuum-formed retainers. Align Technology, founded in 1997 by Zia Chishti and Kelsey Wirth, commercialised computer-planned mass-produced aligners under the Invisalign brand, launching in 1999. Numerous competitor systems have since appeared, and direct-to-consumer models emerged in the 2010s.

Advantages and limitations

Aligners are less conspicuous than fixed braces and can be removed for eating and cleaning, but they require compliance of about 20–22 hours per day and may be unsuitable for certain complex movements; attachments (small bonded composite bumps) and interproximal reduction are often used to improve control.
