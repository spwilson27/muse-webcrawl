Pride and Prejudice

Pride and Prejudice is an 1813 novel of manners by the English author Jane Austen. The novel follows Elizabeth Bennet, the intelligent second of five sisters, as she navigates courtship, class, reputation, and marriage in the landed gentry of Regency-era England, above all her sparring relationship with the proud, wealthy Mr. Darcy, whose eventual proposal she first rejects before prejudice and misunderstanding on both sides give way to love.

Plot

When the wealthy Mr. Bingley rents Netherfield Park, bringing his friend Mr. Darcy, the Bennet family—whose estate is entailed away to a cousin, Mr. Collins—becomes entangled in a series of balls, misunderstandings, and scandals: Jane Bennet's courtship with Bingley, Elizabeth's near-romance with the charming but treacherous Wickham, and Lydia Bennet's elopement, which Darcy secretly redeems. The novel opens with one of the most famous lines in English literature: "It is a truth universally acknowledged, that a single man in possession of a good fortune, must be in want of a wife."

Reception and influence

Written between 1796 and 1797 in an early version titled First Impressions and published anonymously in 1813, the novel has never been out of print. It is consistently ranked among the most beloved English novels, has sold more than 20 million copies, and has inspired numerous film and television adaptations, notably the 1995 BBC serial starring Colin Firth and the 2005 film with Keira Knightley.
