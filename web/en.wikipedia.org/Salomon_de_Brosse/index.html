Salomon de Brosse

Bornc. 1571, Verneuil-sur-Oise, France
Died8 December 1626, Paris
OccupationArchitect
Notable workLuxembourg Palace, Paris

Salomon de Brosse (c. 1571 – 8 December 1626) was a French architect of the early Baroque period who played a major role in establishing the classical French style of the seventeenth century. He came from a distinguished family of artists and architects: his grandfather was Jacques I Androuet du Cerceau and his uncle Jacques II Androuet du Cerceau, both leading figures of the French Renaissance.

Career

De Brosse succeeded his uncle as court architect and worked for Henry IV and then for the regent Marie de' Medici. His most celebrated commission was the Luxembourg Palace in Paris (begun 1615), designed for Marie de' Medici as a Parisian counterpart to the Palazzo Pitti in her native Florence. Merging French traditions — notably the steep slate roofs and U-shaped plan of a château — with Italian rustication and classical orders, it became a landmark of French architecture and today houses the French Senate.

Other works

His other works include the château of Blérancourt, the Château de Coulommiers, the aqueduct of Arcueil (rebuilding a Roman structure to supply the Luxembourg gardens), the façade of the church of Saint-Gervais in Paris (1616) — among the first French church façades in the classical orders — and the Parlement of Brittany in Rennes. De Brosse's synthesis of Renaissance precedent and Baroque monumentality prepared the way for François Mansart and the golden age of French classicism.
