Oromia (Oromo: Oromiyaa) is a regional state in Ethiopia and the homeland of the Oromo people, the country's largest ethnic group. It is Ethiopia's largest region by both area and population, surrounding the capital Addis Ababa (called Finfinne in Oromo), which serves as its capital while also being the federal capital.

Geography

Oromia spans the central, western and southern parts of Ethiopia, bordering every other region except Tigray, as well as Kenya and South Sudan. Its terrain ranges from the high peaks of the Bale Mountains — including Mount Tullu Demtu, Ethiopia's second-highest point — to the lowlands of the Rift Valley with its chain of lakes, and the coffee-growing forests of the west. Major towns include Adama, Jimma, Nekemte, Ambo and Bishoftu.

Economy and culture

Agriculture dominates: Oromia produces most of Ethiopia's coffee, a crop the Oromo are traditionally credited with first cultivating in the Kaffa area, as well as teff, wheat and chat. The region's working language is Oromo, written in the Latin-based Qubee script. The traditional Gadaa system of democratic age-grade governance, practised for centuries, is inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
