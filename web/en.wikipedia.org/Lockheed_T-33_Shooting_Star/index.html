The Lockheed T-33 Shooting Star (or T-Bird) is an American jet trainer aircraft produced by Lockheed. It was developed from the single-seat P-80/F-80 Shooting Star fighter by lengthening the fuselage slightly and adding a second seat, and it became the most widely used advanced jet trainer in the world.

Development and service

The prototype, converted from an F-80C, first flew on 22 March 1948. Production T-33s were powered by a single Allison J33 turbojet. The aircraft entered service with the United States Air Force in 1948 and with the U.S. Navy (as the TV-2, later T-33B) shortly afterward. The last USAF T-33s were retired in 1997, an exceptionally long service life.

More than 6,500 T-33s were built, including license production in Japan by Kawasaki (210 aircraft) and in Canada by Canadair as the CT-133 Silver Star (656 aircraft), powered by the Rolls-Royce Nene engine. The type served with the air forces of more than 40 nations; some remained in service into the 21st century, including with the Bolivian Air Force, which retired its aircraft in 2017.

Variants and derivatives

Variants included reconnaissance (RT-33), target-tug and drone-director versions. The T-33 also served as the basis for the F-94 Starfire interceptor, which used the two-seat trainer airframe fitted with radar and armament. A naval carrier-capable derivative, the T2V SeaStar, featured a redesigned fuselage and blown flaps.

Specifications (T-33A)

  • Length: 11.48 m (37 ft 8 in)

  • Range: 2,050 km (1,275 mi)
  • Service ceiling: 14,600 m (48,000 ft)
