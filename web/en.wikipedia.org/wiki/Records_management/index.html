Records management

Records management, also known as records and information management, is an organizational function devoted to the management of information in an organization throughout its life cycle, from the time of creation or receipt to its eventual disposition. This includes identifying, classifying, storing, securing, retrieving, tracking and destroying or permanently preserving records.

Concepts

A record is defined in ISO 15489 as "information created, received, and maintained as evidence and as an asset by an organization or person, in pursuit of legal obligations or in the transaction of business." Records differ from ordinary documents in that they serve as evidence of activities and must retain integrity, authenticity, reliability and usability over time. The records life cycle describes stages from creation through active use, semi-active storage and final disposition — either destruction or transfer to an archives for permanent preservation.

Practices

Core tools of the discipline include retention and disposition schedules, which specify how long categories of records must be kept in line with legal, fiscal and operational requirements; file classification schemes and metadata; and access controls protecting confidentiality. Electronic records management systems (ERMS) and enterprise content management platforms apply these rules to digital records, email and databases. Records management supports legal discovery, regulatory compliance, freedom-of-information obligations, privacy rights such as data-protection erasure requests, and institutional memory.

Standards

The key international standard is ISO 15489, Information and documentation — Records management, first issued in 2001 and revised in 2016, supplemented by the ISO 30300 series on management systems for records and the DoD 5015.2 standard in the United States.
