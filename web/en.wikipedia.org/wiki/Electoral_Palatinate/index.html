The Electoral Palatinate (German: Kurpfalz) was a historic territory of the Holy Roman Empire along the upper Rhine, ruled by the Counts Palatine of the Rhine of the House of Wittelsbach. Its ruler was one of the seven prince-electors, confirmed by the Golden Bull of 1356.

Geography

The territory comprised lands on both banks of the Rhine around Heidelberg and Mannheim (the Lower Palatinate) plus the Upper Palatinate in present-day Bavaria, separated after divisions among Wittelsbach lines.

History

The Palatinate became a Calvinist center under Elector Frederick III in the 1560s; the Heidelberg Catechism (1563) was produced there. Elector Frederick V's acceptance of the Bohemian crown in 1619 sparked the Thirty Years' War and cost him his lands and electoral title (transferred to Bavaria in 1623). A restored Lower Palatinate with a new electoral vote followed the Peace of Westphalia (1648). Devastated again in the Nine Years' War, the electorate ended with mediatisation in 1803.

Legacy

"Palatine" emigrants to Britain and America in the 18th century carried the name abroad. Heidelberg University, founded in 1386, is Germany's oldest.
