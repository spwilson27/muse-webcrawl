Police brutality

Police brutality is the excessive and unwarranted use of force by law enforcement personnel against a person or group. It is a form of police misconduct that may involve physical assault, beatings, shootings, the improper use of tasers and restraints, as well as psychological intimidation and other abuse. The term is also applied to systemic practices in which violence by police is tolerated or covered up by institutions. Police brutality is considered a violation of civil and human rights in most legal systems and under international instruments such as the UN Convention Against Torture and the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights.

Causes and patterns

Research associates brutality with factors including the militarisation of police, weak accountability and discipline systems, qualified immunity and prosecutorial reluctance to charge officers, poor training in de-escalation, and broader patterns of discrimination. In many countries, brutality falls disproportionately on ethnic minorities, the poor, protesters, and other marginalised groups. Databases such as Mapping Police Violence and the Washington Post's fatal shootings database have attempted to quantify police killings in the United States, where roughly a thousand people are shot and killed by police each year; the US government itself lacks a complete official count.

Notable cases and movements

Widely publicised incidents have repeatedly triggered protest movements: the beating of Rodney King in Los Angeles in 1991 and the acquittal of the officers involved preceded the 1992 Los Angeles riots; the deaths of Eric Garner (2014), Michael Brown (2014), and many others fuelled the Black Lives Matter movement; and the murder of George Floyd by Minneapolis officer Derek Chauvin in May 2020 sparked worldwide protests and renewed demands for police reform, including bans on chokeholds, duty-to-intervene policies, and greater use of body cameras. Outside the United States, brutality has been a central grievance in movements from the 2019–2020 Hong Kong protests to Nigeria's #EndSARS protests against the Special Anti-Robbery Squad in 2020.

Accountability and reform

Proposed and adopted remedies include independent investigations of deaths in custody, civilian review boards, body-worn cameras, early-intervention systems tracking officers' complaints, reform of use-of-force standards toward necessity and proportionality, and, from some activists, defunding or abolition of police in favour of alternative responders for mental-health and other calls. Progress and outcomes vary widely by jurisdiction.
