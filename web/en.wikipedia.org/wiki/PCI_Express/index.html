PCI Express (Peripheral Component Interconnect Express), officially abbreviated as PCIe or PCI-e, is a high-speed serial computer expansion bus standard, designed to replace the older PCI, PCI-X and AGP bus standards. It is the common motherboard interface for personal computers' graphics cards, capture cards, sound cards, hard disk drive host adapters, SSDs, Wi-Fi and Ethernet hardware connections. PCIe has numerous improvements over the older standards, including higher maximum system bus throughput, lower I/O pin count and smaller physical footprint, better performance-scaling for bus devices, a more detailed error detection and reporting mechanism (Advanced Error Reporting, AER), and native hot-swap functionality.

Architecture

In contrast to the shared parallel bus of PCI, PCIe uses point-to-point serial links called lanes. Each lane consists of two differential signaling pairs — one for receiving and one for transmitting data — and lanes can be grouped (x1, x2, x4, x8, x16) to scale bandwidth. Data is sent in packets with a layered protocol architecture (transaction, data link and physical layers). A PCIe fabric is organized around a root complex connecting the CPU and memory to a tree of endpoints and switches, while retaining software compatibility with the PCI configuration model.

Each generation has roughly doubled the data rate. PCIe 6.0 moved to PAM4 signaling and a flow control unit (FLIT) mode with forward error correction, while remaining backward compatible with earlier generations of hardware. The specification is developed and maintained by the PCI-SIG (PCI Special Interest Group), a group of more than 900 companies.

Form factors and applications

The most familiar PCIe connectors are the edge-connector slots on desktop motherboards, from the short x1 slot to the full-length x16 slot used by graphics cards; cards can typically operate in wider slots ("up-plugging") and lane widths negotiate automatically. Derivatives and relatives include Mini PCI Express and M.2 for laptops and SSDs, ExpressCard, OCuLink, U.2/U.3 storage connectors, and external cabled PCIe. Protocols such as NVMe, AHCI and Thunderbolt build on or tunnel PCIe, making it the de facto internal interconnect of modern personal computers and servers.
