British royal family

The British royal family comprises Charles III and his close relations. There is no strict legal or formal definition of who is or is not a member, although the Royal Household has issued lists outlining who is a member of the royal family. Members often support the monarch in undertaking public engagements, pursue charitable work, and represent the United Kingdom abroad.

Members

The core members include the King and Queen Camilla; William, Prince of Wales, and Catherine, Princess of Wales; Prince Harry, Duke of Sussex; Anne, Princess Royal; and other working royals. The family descends from the House of Windsor, renamed from Saxe-Coburg and Gotha by George V in 1917 during the First World War.

Roles and duties

Members undertake thousands of engagements each year, support charities and military units, and conduct state visits. Their work is funded partly through the Sovereign Grant. See also Buckingham Palace, Monarchy of the United Kingdom, and Cabinet of the United Kingdom.

History

The modern royal family traces its lineage through centuries of English and Scottish monarchs, united under one crown in 1603 and one kingdom in 1707. Queen Elizabeth II, who reigned from 1952 to 2022, was the longest-reigning British monarch.
