The National Crime Information Center (NCIC) is the United States' central computerised index of criminal justice information, operated by the FBI's Criminal Justice Information Services (CJIS) Division. It gives police officers, sheriffs, dispatchers and other authorised agencies near-instant access to records on wanted persons, missing persons, stolen property and other law-enforcement data, and it is queried millions of times a day.

Files and operation

The system is divided into person files and property files. Person files include wanted persons, missing persons, unidentified persons, gang members, known or appropriately suspected terrorists, supervised releasees and people subject to protection orders. Property files cover stolen vehicles, boats, guns, licence plates, securities and other identifiable articles. When an officer runs a name or plate during a traffic stop, the query travels through state systems to NCIC and returns any "hit", which the entering agency must then confirm before action is taken.

History

NCIC went live in January 1967 with five files and a few hundred thousand records; it has been repeatedly upgraded since, most notably with NCIC 2000, which added mugshot and fingerprint image capability. The system holds tens of millions of records and forms the backbone of routine records checks across American policing, though audits have repeatedly flagged problems with accuracy and completeness — especially in the missing persons and protection order files — and with records wrongly entered or never removed.
