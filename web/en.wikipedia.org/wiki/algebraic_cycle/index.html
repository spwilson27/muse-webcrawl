Algebraic cycle

In mathematics, an algebraic cycle on an algebraic variety is a formal linear combination of subvarieties of the variety. Algebraic cycles are the algebraic-geometry analogue of cycles in algebraic topology, and they play a central role in the formulation of conjectures about the relationship between the geometry of a variety and its cohomology.

Definition

Let X be an algebraic variety. A cycle of codimension p (or dimension k) on X is a finite formal sum Σ ni[Vi], where the Vi are irreducible closed subvarieties of the given codimension (or dimension) and the coefficients ni are integers. The free abelian group generated by such cycles is denoted Zp(X).

Equivalence relations

Various equivalence relations are imposed on cycles to form the different groups used in intersection theory and related fields, including rational equivalence (giving the Chow groups), algebraic equivalence, homological equivalence, and numerical equivalence. The quotient of cycles by rational equivalence gives the Chow group of the variety.

Conjectures

Algebraic cycles lie at the heart of the Hodge conjecture, one of the Millennium Prize Problems, which asks whether certain cohomology classes of a smooth projective variety are combinations of classes of algebraic cycles. Related topics include the standard conjectures on algebraic cycles of Alexander Grothendieck and the theory of motives.
