Rhythm changes

Rhythm changes are a common 32-bar chord progression in jazz, derived from the changes to George Gershwin's song "I Got Rhythm" (1930). They form the basis of hundreds of jazz compositions, called contrafacts, and are a standard framework for jam sessions and improvisation.

Structure

The form is AABA, each section eight bars long. In B-flat major, the A sections typically cycle through I–VI–II–V turnarounds, while the bridge (B section) consists of dominant seventh chords descending in a cycle of fifths: III7–VI7–II7–V7. Common variants substitute chords freely, especially in later bebop practice.

Famous contrafacts

Songs built on rhythm changes include "Anthropology" by Charlie Parker and Dizzy Gillespie, "Oleo" by Sonny Rollins, "Moose the Mooche", "Dexterity", "Cottontail" by Duke Ellington, and the theme to The Flintstones, "Meet the Flintstones".
