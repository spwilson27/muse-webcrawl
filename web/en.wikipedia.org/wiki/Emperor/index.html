Emperor

An emperor (from Latin imperator, via Old French empereor) is a monarch, usually the sovereign ruler of an empire or another type of imperial realm. Empress, the female equivalent, may indicate an emperor's wife (empress consort), mother (empress dowager), or a woman who rules in her own right (empress regnant). Emperors are generally recognized to be of the highest monarchic rank, above kings.

Origins

The title derives from the Roman imperator, originally a military acclamation for a victorious commander. Augustus, the first Roman emperor (r. 27 BC – AD 14), accumulated republican powers while avoiding the title of king, and his successors came to be known as emperors. The Latin title was later revived in the Byzantine Empire (in Greek, basileus), the Holy Roman Empire, and states claiming the Roman succession.

Use of the title

In Europe, the question of who could legitimately bear the imperial title was politically charged: the Holy Roman Emperors, the Russian tsars (from Latin Caesar), the Ottoman sultans, and Napoleon (Emperor of the French) all asserted imperial rank. Outside Europe, the title is the standard English translation for the rulers of China (huángdì), Japan (tennō), Persia, Ethiopia, and other great monarchies. The Emperor of Japan is today the only reigning monarch with the title of emperor.
