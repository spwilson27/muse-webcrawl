Madison Square Garden, colloquially known as the Garden or MSG, is a multi-purpose indoor arena in New York City. It is located in Midtown Manhattan between Seventh and Eighth Avenues from 31st to 33rd Street above Pennsylvania Station. The current Garden, opened in 1968, is the fourth venue to bear the name and bills itself as "The World's Most Famous Arena".

History

The name derives from Madison Square, at 23rd Street and Fifth Avenue, where the first two Gardens stood. The first, an open-air venue converted by P. T. Barnum in 1879, hosted circuses and exhibitions. The second, a Moorish-style building designed by Stanford White that opened in 1890, was the scene of the 1906 murder of White by Harry Thaw — one of the era's most notorious crimes. The third Garden, at 50th Street and Eighth Avenue (1925–1968), was built by boxing promoter Tex Rickard and became synonymous with boxing, hosting championship fights, the Ringling Bros. circus and political conventions.

The fourth and current Garden, built atop the tracks of Pennsylvania Station after the demolition of its original head house, opened on 11 February 1968. It underwent a major three-phase renovation between 2011 and 2013 that rebuilt the interior bowl, concourses and seating while games continued to be played.

Events and tenants

The Garden is home to the New York Knicks of the NBA and the New York Rangers of the NHL, and hosts the Big East men's basketball tournament. It has staged landmark events including the "Fight of the Century" between Muhammad Ali and Joe Frazier in 1971, the Concert for Bangladesh (1971), WrestleMania events, the 1994 Rangers Stanley Cup victory, and countless concerts — Billy Joel holds a long-running monthly residency there. The Westminster Kennel Club Dog Show was long held at the Garden.
