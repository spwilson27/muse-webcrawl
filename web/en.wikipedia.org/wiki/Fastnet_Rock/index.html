Fastnet Rock, or simply Fastnet (Irish: Carraig Aonair, meaning "lonely rock"), is a small islet in the Atlantic Ocean and the southernmost point of Ireland. It lies some 6.5 kilometres (4 miles) southwest of Cape Clear Island and about 13 kilometres (8 miles) from the County Cork mainland. The rock is famous for its lighthouse, often called the "Teardrop of Ireland" because it was the last part of Ireland seen by emigrants sailing to America.

Lighthouse

The first lighthouse on Fastnet Rock began operating in 1854. The present granite tower, designed by William Douglass, was built between 1897 and 1904 and remains one of the most impressive feats of lighthouse engineering, withstanding immense Atlantic storms. The light was automated in 1989.

Fastnet Race

Fastnet Rock is the turning mark of the Fastnet Race, a famous biennial offshore yacht race run from Cowes on the Isle of Wight around the rock and back to Plymouth (now finishing at Cherbourg). The 1979 race was struck by a violent storm in which 15 sailors died, one of the worst disasters in ocean racing history.
