Erdal Arıkan

Born: 1958, Ankara, Turkey
Known for: Polar codes
Institution: Bilkent University
Awards: IEEE Richard W. Hamming Medal (2018)

Erdal Arıkan (born 1958) is a Turkish professor of electrical and electronics engineering at Bilkent University in Ankara. He is best known as the inventor of polar codes, the first family of error-correcting codes with an explicit construction proven to achieve the Shannon capacity of symmetric binary-input channels with low-complexity encoding and decoding.

Career

Arıkan studied electrical engineering at Middle East Technical University and the California Institute of Technology, then completed his PhD at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in 1986, with research on trellis coding. After a brief period at the University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign, he returned to Turkey to join the newly founded Bilkent University.

Polar codes

His 2009 paper "Channel Polarization: A Method for Constructing Capacity-Achieving Codes for Symmetric Binary-Input Memoryless Channels" introduced channel polarization: by recursively combining copies of a channel, synthetic channels polarize toward being either nearly perfect or nearly useless, so data can be sent over the good ones. Polar codes were adopted as the coding scheme for the control channels of the 5G NR standard by the 3GPP in 2016. Arıkan received the IEEE Richard W. Hamming Medal in 2018, and polar coding's role in 5G was cited prominently in industry awards, including Huawei's recognition of Arıkan in 2018.
