Hand washing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hand washing (or handwashing), also known as hand hygiene, is the act of cleaning one's hands with soap or handwash and water to remove viruses, bacteria, microorganisms, dirt, grease and other harmful or unwanted substances. It is one of the most important measures for preventing the spread of infectious diseases, including diarrhoeal diseases and respiratory infections.

History

Washing for ritual purity is ancient in many religions. Its medical importance was demonstrated in the 1840s by the Hungarian physician Ignaz Semmelweis in Vienna, who showed that requiring doctors to disinfect their hands with chlorinated lime dramatically reduced deaths from puerperal fever in maternity wards; his ideas were initially rejected, and he died before germ theory vindicated him. The nurse Florence Nightingale likewise promoted hand washing in hospitals during the same era.

Method

Public health guidance recommends wetting hands, applying soap, scrubbing all surfaces—including the backs of hands, between fingers and under nails—for at least 20 seconds, rinsing under clean running water and drying with a clean towel or air dryer. When soap and water are unavailable, alcohol-based hand sanitizer is an alternative for hands that are not visibly dirty.

In healthcare

The World Health Organization's "Five Moments for Hand Hygiene" framework defines when healthcare workers should clean their hands, including before patient contact and after exposure to bodily fluids. Despite its proven effectiveness, compliance in hospitals worldwide remains imperfect, and campaigns such as the WHO's SAVE LIVES: Clean Your Hands initiative work to improve it. Global Handwashing Day is observed each year on 15 October.
