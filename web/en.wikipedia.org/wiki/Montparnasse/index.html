Montparnasse

Montparnasse is an area of Paris, France, on the left bank of the Seine, centred on the crossroads of the Boulevard du Montparnasse and the Boulevard Raspail, largely within the 14th arrondissement. The name, "Mount Parnassus," was coined by students who came there in the 17th century to recite poetry on a mound of rubble.

Artistic centre

In the first decades of the 20th century, especially during the années folles between the World Wars, Montparnasse displaced Montmartre as the heart of artistic and intellectual Paris. Its famous cafés and brasseries — Le Dôme, La Coupole, La Rotonde, Le Select and La Closerie des Lilas — drew artists and writers including Picasso, Modigliani, Man Ray, Kiki de Montparnasse, Hemingway and Cocteau. Cheap rents attracted an international bohemia, and the district's academies, such as the Académie de la Grande Chaumière, taught generations of artists.

Modern district

Today the area is known for the Gare Montparnasse railway station, the 210-metre Tour Montparnasse office tower (1973) — long Paris's only skyscraper — the Montparnasse Cemetery, and the underground ossuaries of the Catacombs at nearby Denfert-Rochereau.
