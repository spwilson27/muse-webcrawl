Panama Canal

[Image: A ship in the Gatún Locks of the Panama Canal]
A ship in the Gatún Locks of the Panama Canal

The Panama Canal is an artificial waterway in Panama, about 82 kilometres long, that connects the Atlantic Ocean (via the Caribbean Sea) with the Pacific Ocean, cutting across the Isthmus of Panama. Opened in 1914 by the United States after an earlier French attempt failed, it is one of the largest and most difficult engineering projects ever undertaken.

Ships are raised and lowered 26 metres through systems of locks at each end, crossing the artificial Gatún Lake. Enlarged locks opened in 2016 allow much bigger 'Neopanamax' vessels. The canal was transferred from US to Panamanian control at the end of 1999 under the Torrijos-Carter Treaties. Severe drought in 2023-2024 sharply restricted transits because the locks depend on freshwater from Gatún Lake.
