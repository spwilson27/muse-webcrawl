Flat-top guitar

A flat-top guitar is a type of acoustic guitar with a flat soundboard (top) and steel strings, distinguished from the archtop guitar, whose top is carved or pressed into an arch. The flat-top design, most commonly associated with instruments developed by C. F. Martin & Co. in the 19th and 20th centuries, became the dominant form of the steel-string acoustic guitar used in folk, country, blues, bluegrass, and popular music.

ClassificationString instrument (chordophone)

Construction

Flat-top guitars are built around a hollow wooden body whose flat top, usually spruce or cedar, carries a glued bridge to which steel strings are anchored. To withstand the tension of steel strings, the underside of the top is reinforced with bracing; the X-bracing pattern patented and popularized by Martin became the industry standard. Body sizes range from small parlor and concert models to the large dreadnought (introduced by Martin in 1916) and jumbo shapes.

Sound and playing

Compared with nylon-string classical guitars, flat-tops have a brighter, louder tone with strong projection, suited both to fingerstyle playing and to strumming with a pick. The design spread through American folk and country music in the early 20th century and became a staple of rock and pop recording from the 1950s onward.
