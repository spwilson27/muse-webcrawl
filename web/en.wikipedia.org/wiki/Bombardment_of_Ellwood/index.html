Bombardment of Ellwood

Bombardment of Ellwood
Date: 23 February 1942
Location: Ellwood, California, United States
Part of: World War II, American Theater
Attacker: Japanese submarine I-17

The Bombardment of Ellwood was a naval attack by a Japanese submarine against United States mainland targets on the West Coast during World War II. On 23 February 1942, the Imperial Japanese Navy submarine I-17 surfaced near Santa Barbara, California and fired shells at the Ellwood Oil Field.

Background

Following the attack on Pearl Harbor, Japanese submarines operated off the American West Coast. I-17 under Commander Kozo Nishino had previously sunk American merchant vessels.

Bombardment

At about 7:15 pm, I-17 surfaced and fired 16–25 rounds from its 14 cm deck gun toward the Ellwood oil refinery and pier. Damage was minimal: a derrick and pumping station were slightly damaged, with losses estimated at $500. No casualties were reported.

Aftermath

The attack caused widespread fear of a Japanese invasion of the West Coast and contributed to the Battle of Los Angeles the following night. It also influenced the decision to intern Japanese Americans.
