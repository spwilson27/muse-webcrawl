Coachella (officially the Coachella Valley Music and Arts Festival) is an annual music and arts festival held at the Empire Polo Club in Indio, California, in the Coachella Valley of the Colorado Desert. Founded in 1999 by Paul Tollett and Rick Van Santen of Goldenvoice, it is one of the largest, most famous, and most profitable music festivals in the world.

Overview

Since 2012 the festival spans two consecutive three-day weekends in April, with (mostly) identical line-ups. It presents artists across rock, pop, hip-hop, electronic dance music, and indie genres, alongside large-scale art installations and sculptures. Each festival weekend draws around 125,000 attendees per day.

History

The inaugural 1999 edition, headlined by Beck, Tool, and Rage Against the Machine, lost money despite acclaim; after skipping 2000 it returned in 2001 and grew steadily. Landmark moments include Daft Punk's 2006 "pyramid" performance, Prince's 2008 set, the hologram "Tupac" appearance in 2012, Beyoncé's acclaimed 2018 headline set ("Beychella"), and Bad Bunny becoming the first Latin headliner in 2023. Coachella was postponed and ultimately held in April 2022 after the COVID-19 pandemic.

Cultural impact

Coachella is a bellwether for festival fashion and music-industry trends, famous for surprise guest appearances, reunion bookings, and its influencer-driven social media presence.
