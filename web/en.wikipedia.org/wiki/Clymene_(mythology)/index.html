Clymene (mythology)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Clymene, in Greek myth the name of several figures; the best known is the mother of Phaethon by Helios.

Clymene (Ancient Greek: Κλυμένη, "famous") is the name of several figures in Greek mythology.

Clymene, mother of Phaethon

The most famous Clymene was an Oceanid, one of the three thousand daughters of Oceanus and Tethys, or in some versions a daughter of King Minyas of Orchomenus. She was the wife of the Ethiopian king Merops and the mother, by the sun god Helios, of Phaethon and the Heliades. In Ovid's Metamorphoses, Phaethon, taunted that his father was not really the Sun, travels to Helios's palace to confirm his parentage; Clymene swears to her son's divine birth and directs him to his father, setting in motion the fatal chariot ride that nearly burned the earth.

Other figures

  • Clymene, daughter of Minyas — wife of Phylacus or of Cephalus, and mother of Iphiclus and possibly of Atalanta in some traditions.
  • Clymene, an Amazon — listed among the warrior women of myth.
  • Clymene, attendant of Helen — a handmaid who accompanied Helen to Troy; in the Iliad she is named among Helen's attendants.
  • Clymene, daughter of Catreus — mother of Palamedes by Nauplius in one tradition.
  • Clymene, wife of Dictys — adopted mother figure of Perseus on Seriphos in some accounts.

Name usage

The name has been given to the asteroid 104 Klymene, discovered in 1868, and appears in literature as a classical allusion to fame and to the mother of Phaethon.
