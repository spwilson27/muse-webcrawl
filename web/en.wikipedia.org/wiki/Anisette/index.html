Anisette, or Anis, is an anise-flavoured liqueur consumed mainly in Mediterranean countries, including France, Spain, Italy, Portugal and Turkey. It is sweeter and generally lower in alcohol than other anise spirits such as pastis, ouzo or rakı.

Production

Unlike pastis, which is made by maceration, true anisette is produced by distilling neutral spirit with aniseed and other botanicals, then sweetening — by traditional definition with a substantial quantity of sugar (in the EU aniseed-flavoured liqueurs of this type contain at least 100 grams of sugar per litre). Star anise, fennel and coriander may also be used; the characteristic flavour compound is anethole.

Serving

Anisette is usually drunk diluted with cold water, which turns it milky white — the "louche", caused by anethole coming out of solution — or taken in coffee (as in the Spanish sol y sombra, mixed with brandy). Well-known brands include Marie Brizard, founded in Bordeaux in 1755, and the Spanish Anís del Mono.

History

Anise-flavoured drinks are ancient in the Mediterranean; commercial anisette production grew in 18th-century France, and anisette and its cousins became popular substitutes after absinthe was banned in the early 20th century.
