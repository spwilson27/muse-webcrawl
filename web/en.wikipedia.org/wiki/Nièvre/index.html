Nièvre

Nièvre (French pronunciation: [njɛvʁ]) is a department in the Bourgogne-Franche-Comté region of central France, named after the river Nièvre. Its prefecture is Nevers. The department is one of the least densely populated in France and is largely rural, with landscapes of forests, river valleys and rolling hills.

History

Nièvre was created on 4 March 1790 during the French Revolution, from most of the old province of Nivernais. The département is known historically for its ironworks, pottery — notably the faience of Nevers — and its forests, which supplied timber and charcoal to Paris via the Loire and its tributaries.

Geography

The department is crossed by the river Loire on its western border and includes part of the Morvan massif in the east, a granite upland of forests and lakes now partly protected as a regional natural park. The Canal du Nivernais links the Loire basin to the Yonne and hence to Paris.

Main towns

Besides Nevers, principal communes include Cosne-Cours-sur-Loire, Decize, Clamecy, Varennes-Vauzelles and Château-Chinon, a small town in the Morvan long associated with François Mitterrand, who was its mayor before becoming President of France.
