Master's degree

Graduates in the academic dress of a master's degree.

A master's degree (from Latin magister) is a postgraduate academic degree awarded by universities or colleges upon completion of a course of study demonstrating mastery or a high-order overview of a specific field of study or area of professional practice. A master's degree normally requires previous study at the bachelor's level, either as a separate degree or as part of an integrated course. Master's graduates are expected to possess advanced knowledge of a specialized body of theoretical and applied topics, a high order of skill in analysis, critical evaluation, and professional application, and the ability to solve complex problems.

History

The master's degree dates to the medieval universities of Europe, where the licentia docendi (licence to teach) was granted to graduates of the higher faculties. In the Bologna process, the master's degree is the qualification of the second cycle, typically requiring 90–120 ECTS credits — one to two years of full-time study after a bachelor's degree.

Types

  • Master of Arts (MA) and Master of Science (MS/MSc) — the most common general master's degrees, in humanities/social sciences and sciences respectively.
  • Master of Business Administration (MBA) — a professional degree in management.
  • Master of Laws (LLM), Master of Engineering (MEng), Master of Education (MEd), Master of Public Health (MPH), Master of Fine Arts (MFA) and many other specialized titles.
  • Research master's (e.g., MPhil, MRes) — dominated by an extended research project and thesis, often preparatory to a doctorate.
  • Integrated master's — combined bachelor–master programmes, common in UK science and engineering.
  • Undergraduate master's — in Scotland and at Oxford and Cambridge, the MA is awarded to first-degree graduates, historically without further study.

Structure

Programmes usually combine taught coursework with a substantial dissertation or capstone project. In the United States, most master's degrees require 30–60 semester credit hours. Admission normally requires a bachelor's degree with a minimum grade point average, and professional programmes may require work experience.
