Margaret Geller

Margaret J. Geller (born 1947)

Margaret Joan Geller (born December 8, 1947) is an American astronomer and professor at Harvard University and the Harvard–Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics. She is renowned for her pioneering maps of the large-scale structure of the universe, including the discovery of the "Great Wall" of galaxies, one of the largest known structures in the cosmos.

Education and career

Geller was born in Ithaca, New York, earned her bachelor's degree from the University of California, Berkeley in 1970, and her PhD in physics from Princeton University in 1975, working on the theory of galaxy clustering. After positions at Cambridge and Harvard, she joined the Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory.

The Great Wall

With John Huchra, Geller led the CfA Redshift Survey, measuring the distances of thousands of galaxies in slices of the sky. Their 1986 "slice of the universe" maps first revealed that galaxies are arranged in thin sheets and filaments surrounding huge voids, rather than distributed uniformly. A deeper survey announced in 1989 revealed the CfA2 Great Wall, a structure of galaxies some 600 million light-years long — at the time the largest known structure in the universe. The results transformed understanding of cosmic structure and cosmology.

Honours

Geller received a MacArthur Fellowship in 1990 and is a member of the National Academy of Sciences. She is also known for science communication, including the film Where the Galaxies Are, and for later surveys of galaxy clusters and the SHELS and HectoMAP projects.
