Health effects of tea

The health effects of tea have been examined in numerous studies. Tea — from the leaves of Camellia sinensis — contains caffeine, the amino acid L-theanine, and polyphenols known as catechins (notably epigallocatechin gallate, EGCG, in green tea) and theaflavins (in black tea). Regular tea drinking has been associated in observational research with modest reductions in cardiovascular disease risk, but claims of broad disease-prevention benefits remain incompletely proven, and health authorities such as the U.S. Food and Drug Administration have declined to approve qualified health claims for green tea and cancer risk.

Constituents and mechanisms

Catechins act as antioxidants in vitro and may influence endothelial function and lipid metabolism. Caffeine provides mild stimulant effects; L-theanine is reported to promote relaxation without drowsiness, and the combination is associated with improved alertness. Tea also contributes fluoride and small amounts of minerals to the diet.

Evidence

  • Cardiovascular: cohort studies generally associate habitual tea consumption with slightly lower risks of stroke and cardiovascular mortality; trials show small reductions in LDL cholesterol and blood pressure for green tea extracts.
  • Cancer: evidence is inconsistent; agencies including the FDA and EFSA have found the data insufficient for health claims.
  • Cognition and metabolism: modest, short-term effects on alertness from caffeine are established; claims for weight loss from extracts are small and inconsistent.

Adverse effects

Excess consumption can cause caffeine-related effects (insomnia, anxiety). Concentrated green tea extracts have been linked in case reports to liver injury, prompting safety warnings from EFSA regarding high-dose EGCG supplements. Tea can inhibit non-haem iron absorption when drunk with meals, and very hot beverages (tea included) consumed above about 65 °C are classified by IARC as probably carcinogenic to the oesophagus — an effect of temperature, not of tea itself.
