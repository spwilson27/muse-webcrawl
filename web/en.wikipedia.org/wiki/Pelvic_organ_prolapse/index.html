Pelvic organ prolapse

Pelvic organ prolapse is a condition in which one or more of the pelvic organs — the bladder, uterus, vagina, small bowel, or rectum — descend from their normal position and bulge into or out of the vagina, owing to weakening of the pelvic floor muscles and connective tissues.

Types and symptoms

Forms include cystocele (bladder), rectocele (rectum), uterine prolapse, and vaginal vault prolapse after hysterectomy. Symptoms include a sensation of pelvic pressure or a vaginal bulge, urinary or bowel difficulties, and discomfort; many mild cases are asymptomatic. Risk factors include vaginal childbirth, aging and menopause, obesity, chronic coughing, and heavy lifting.

Treatment

Management ranges from watchful waiting and pelvic floor exercises to vaginal pessaries and surgery, including native-tissue repair, hysterectomy, and (increasingly controversially) mesh procedures. Severity is commonly graded using the POP-Q staging system.
