Metallurgical assaying

Metallurgical assaying is the quantitative determination of the composition of an ore, alloy or other metallurgical material, particularly its content of valuable metals such as gold, silver and platinum-group elements. Assay results guide mining, smelting, refining and trade.

Fire assay

The classical method is fire assay, in use for thousands of years. A weighed sample is fused with fluxes and a lead collector in a crucible; the lead button that collects the precious metals is then cupelled — oxidised in a porous cupel — leaving a bead of noble metal. The bead is parted in nitric acid to separate silver from gold, and weighed to determine the fineness.

Modern methods

Modern laboratories supplement fire assay with instrumental techniques including atomic absorption spectrometry (AAS), inductively coupled plasma emission and mass spectrometry (ICP-OES, ICP-MS), and X-ray fluorescence (XRF). Proper sampling — reducing a bulk lot to a representative test portion — is critical, since precious-metal ores are often highly heterogeneous.
