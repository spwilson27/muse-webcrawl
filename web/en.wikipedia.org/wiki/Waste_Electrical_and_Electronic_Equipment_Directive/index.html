Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment Directive

The Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment Directive (WEEE Directive) is a European Union directive — originally 2002/96/EC, recast as 2012/19/EU — governing the collection, recycling and recovery of waste electrical and electronic equipment (e-waste). Together with the companion RoHS Directive restricting hazardous substances in new equipment, it aims to reduce the environmental impact of discarded electronics.

Provisions

The directive makes producers financially responsible for the end-of-life management of their products (extended producer responsibility), requires member states to provide free collection points where consumers can return WEEE separately from household waste, and sets binding targets for collection, recovery and recycling by weight across categories of equipment — from large household appliances and IT equipment to lamps, tools and toys. Products placed on the EU market must bear the crossed-out wheeled-bin symbol to indicate they must not be disposed of as unsorted waste.

Implementation

The recast directive of 2012 widened the scope to essentially all electrical and electronic equipment from 2018 and raised collection targets — to 65% of the average weight of equipment placed on the market in the preceding three years, or 85% of WEEE generated. Member states implement the scheme through national regulations and producer-compliance schemes; the United Kingdom retained equivalent WEEE Regulations after leaving the EU.
