Voluntary Service Overseas

Voluntary Service Overseas (VSO) is a not-for-profit international development organisation founded in 1958 by Alec and Mora Dickson in the United Kingdom. VSO recruits and places skilled professional volunteers — teachers, health workers, engineers, business advisers and others — to work with communities in developing countries, sharing skills to fight poverty.

History

VSO began by sending British school leavers to work in Commonwealth countries during their gap year. Over time its focus shifted to experienced professionals, typically with at least several years' work experience, on placements of one to two years. Volunteers receive a modest local living allowance rather than a salary, living at the standard of the communities they serve. VSO's model emphasises partnership: host organisations request placements and volunteers work alongside local colleagues.

Work

VSO operates in Africa and Asia in three main programme areas: inclusive education, resilient health services (including sexual and reproductive health), and resilient livelihoods. It is a member of the International Forum for Volunteering in Development and leads the International Citizen Service consortium historically funded by the UK government. In recent years VSO has broadened to recruit volunteers from many countries, including national volunteers within programme countries, and supports youth volunteering and community volunteering.

Reception

VSO is registered as a charity in England and Wales. Alumni include politicians and public figures; the organisation has placed tens of thousands of volunteers since its founding.
