Karluks

The Karluks (also Qarlugs or Karluqs) were a prominent nomadic Turkic tribe in Central Asia between the 7th and 12th centuries. Originally part of the Western Turkic Khaganate, they established their own khaganate in the region between Lake Balkhash and the Altai Mountains — the "Jetysu" or Seven Rivers region — and later played a central role in founding the Karakhanid Khanate, the first Turkic Muslim state of Central Asia.

History

Chinese sources record the Karluks ("Geluolu") as three clans west of the Altai. In 751 they fought alongside the Abbasid forces against Tang China at the Battle of Talas, a turning point that consolidated Muslim influence in Transoxiana. Around 766 the Karluks overthrew the Türgesh and established their dominance in Jetysu, nominally recognising Uyghur suzerainty. Their chief took the title yabghu and later khan.

Karakhanid era and legacy

In the 9th and 10th centuries the Karluks, together with the Chigils and Yaghmas, formed the core of the Karakhanid confederation, whose ruler Satuq Bughra Khan converted to Islam around 934. The Karluk branch of Turkic languages survives today in Uzbek and Uyghur. Under the Mongols, the Karluk lands fell within the Chagatai Khanate, and Karluk-descended groups like the Hazara may partly trace ancestry to them, though this remains debated.
