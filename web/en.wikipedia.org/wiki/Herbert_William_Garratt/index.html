Herbert William Garratt (8 June 1864 – 25 September 1913) was an English steam locomotive engineer and the inventor of the Garratt system of articulated locomotives, widely used on narrow-gauge and heavy freight railways around the world.

Career

After an apprenticeship at the North London Railway's Bow Works, Garratt worked on railways in Argentina, Cuba, Lagos (Nigeria) and Peru, gaining experience with the limitations of conventional locomotives on lightly built colonial lines. His experiences led him to design an articulated locomotive with the boiler cradled between two separate engine units, distributing weight while allowing a large firebox and boiler unrestricted by driving wheels.

The Garratt locomotive

Garratt patented his design in 1907 and licensed it to Beyer, Peacock & Company of Manchester, who built nearly all Garratt locomotives. The first examples went to Tasmania and New South Wales in 1909. Garratts later became mainstays in Africa, Asia, Australia and South America; the largest were among the most powerful steam locomotives ever built. Garratt died in 1913, before the type's greatest successes.
