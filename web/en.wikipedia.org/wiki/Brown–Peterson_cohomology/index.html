Brown–Peterson cohomology

The Brown–Peterson spectrum BP is fundamental to chromatic homotopy theory.

In mathematics, Brown–Peterson cohomology is a generalized cohomology theory introduced by Edgar H. Brown and Franklin P. Peterson in 1966. Its representing spectrum, denoted BP, is a summand of the complex cobordism spectrum MU localized at a prime p, and it plays a central role in the study of formal group laws and chromatic homotopy theory.

Definition

Fix a prime p. The coefficient ring of Brown–Peterson cohomology is a polynomial algebra BP* = Z(p)[v1, v2, ...], where the generator vn has degree 2(pn − 1). The spectrum BP is characterized by the property that its formal group law is universal among p-typical formal group laws over its coefficient ring.

Properties and applications

Brown–Peterson cohomology is Landweber exact, so it can be used to construct many other cohomology theories. Quillen's theorem identifies BP*BP, the Hopf algebroid of cooperations, with the algebraic structure governing p-typical formal group laws and their coordinate changes. The Adams–Novikov spectral sequence, based on BP, is one of the principal tools for computing stable homotopy groups of spheres.

Closely related theories include Johnson–Wilson cohomology E(n), Morava K-theory K(n) and Morava E-theory, organized by the chromatic filtration of stable homotopy theory.
