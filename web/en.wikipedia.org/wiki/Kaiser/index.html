Kaiser

Kaiser is the German word for "emperor", derived from the Latin Caesar. In general usage it denotes an emperor of any empire; in English historiography, "the Kaiser" most specifically refers to the German emperors, especially Wilhelm II.

Historical usage

The title was borne by the rulers of the Holy Roman Empire, and from 1804 by the emperors of Austria (beginning with Francis II, who assumed the title Kaiser von Österreich). During the nineteenth-century unification of Germany, the title Deutscher Kaiser ("German Emperor") was created by the imperial constitution of 1871 for the King of Prussia, who also held the federal presidency of the empire. The three German Kaisers of 1871–1918 were Wilhelm I, Frederick III and Wilhelm II.

Usage in English

During the First World War, "the Kaiser" became a byword for Wilhelm II in Allied propaganda. Related titles in other languages derived from Caesar include the Russian Tsar and the Bulgarian and Serbian Tsar. In German, Kaiser remains the generic rendering of any emperor, e.g. the Emperor of Japan or of China.
