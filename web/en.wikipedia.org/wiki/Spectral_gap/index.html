Spectral gap

In mathematics, the spectral gap is the difference between the moduli of the two largest eigenvalues of a matrix or operator. The term is used in several related senses across mathematics and physics:

  • For a Markov chain or a graph, the gap between the largest eigenvalue (typically 1, for a stochastic matrix, or the largest eigenvalue of an adjacency matrix) and the second-largest in absolute value.
  • For a self-adjoint operator such as a Hamiltonian in quantum mechanics, the difference between the smallest eigenvalue (the ground-state energy) and the next eigenvalue.
  • For an operator on a Hilbert space, the distance in the spectrum between an isolated part of the spectrum — for instance the ground-state eigenvalue — and the rest of the spectrum.

Markov chains and expander graphs

For an ergodic Markov chain with transition matrix P, the spectral gap 1 − |λ2|, where λ2 is the second-largest eigenvalue in absolute value, controls the chain's mixing time: the larger the gap, the more rapidly the chain converges to its stationary distribution. In graph theory, the spectral gap of a regular graph is related to its expansion properties; families of graphs with a uniform spectral gap bounded away from zero are expander graphs, which have important applications in computer science, coding theory, and derandomization. Cheeger's inequality relates the spectral gap of the graph Laplacian to the graph's isoperimetric (Cheeger) constant.

Quantum mechanics and condensed matter

In quantum many-body physics, a system is said to be gapped if its Hamiltonian has a spectral gap above the ground state that persists in the thermodynamic limit, and gapless otherwise. The presence or absence of a gap has profound consequences: gapped systems exhibit exponential decay of correlations and robustness of ground-state properties to small perturbations, underlying phenomena such as the quantum Hall effect and topological order. Proving that the spectral gap of a general quantum Hamiltonian is non-zero is famously difficult; in 2015 it was shown that the spectral gap problem for two-dimensional lattice models is undecidable in general.

Other uses

The term also appears in the theory of automorphic forms and number theory, where a spectral gap towards Selberg's eigenvalue conjecture concerns the smallest positive eigenvalue of the Laplacian on hyperbolic surfaces, and in Poincaré inequalities, where the spectral gap of a diffusion operator gives the optimal constant controlling variance by the Dirichlet form.
