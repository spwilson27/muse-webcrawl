Lake Vänern

Lake Vänern (Swedish pronunciation: [ˈvɛ̂ːnɛɳ]) is the largest lake in Sweden, the largest lake in the European Union, and the third-largest lake in Europe after Lakes Ladoga and Onega in Russia. It is located in the provinces of Västergötland, Dalsland and Värmland in southwestern Sweden, and its shores include the towns of Karlstad, Kristinehamn, Mariestad, Lidköping, Vänersborg and Åmål.

The lake is divided by the Kålland peninsula and the island of Kållandsö into a western basin (Dalbosjön) and an eastern basin (Värmlandssjön). It contains some 22,000 islands and skerries, including Torsö, Brommö, Djurö and Lurö archipelagos. Its only outlet is the Göta älv, which flows to Gothenburg and the Kattegat and is navigable by large vessels via the Trollhätte Canal.

Geology and history

The Vänern basin was scoured by Quaternary ice sheets; after the last ice age the lake was initially part of the sea, becoming isolated as a freshwater lake roughly 11,500 years ago as the land rebounded. The lake is mentioned in Norse tradition, and the plateau mountain Kinnekulle on its southern shore is famous for its layered sedimentary geology and rare flora.

Environment and fishery

Vänern supports a significant commercial and recreational fishery — including salmon, trout, pike-perch, vendace (whose roe, löjrom, is a prized delicacy) and whitefish — and is an important source of drinking water. Parts of the lake are protected as nature reserves and within the Lake Vänern biosphere reserve recognised by UNESCO.
