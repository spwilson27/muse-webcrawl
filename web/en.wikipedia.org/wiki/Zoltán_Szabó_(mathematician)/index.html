Zoltán Szabó (mathematician)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Zoltán Szabó (born 1965) is a Hungarian-American mathematician, professor at Princeton University, known for his work in topology, in particular in Heegaard Floer homology and Khovanov homology. Together with Peter Ozsváth, he developed Heegaard Floer homology, a powerful invariant of three-manifolds and knots that transformed low-dimensional topology, and the knot Floer homology used to study concordance and contact structures.

Career

Szabó received his doctorate from Rutgers University in 1994. After positions elsewhere, he joined the faculty of Princeton University, where he has spent most of his career. With Ozsváth he also constructed grid diagrams for knot Floer homology, giving a combinatorial formulation of the theory.

Awards

Szabó and Ozsváth shared the Oswald Veblen Prize in Geometry in 2007 for their work on Heegaard Floer homology. He is a fellow of the American Mathematical Society.
