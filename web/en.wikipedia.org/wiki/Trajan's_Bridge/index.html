Trajan's Bridge (Romanian: Podul lui Traian; Serbian: Trajanov most), also called the Bridge of Apollodorus, was a Roman segmental arch bridge across the lower Danube, east of the Iron Gates, near the modern cities of Drobeta-Turnu Severin in Romania and Kladovo in Serbia. Built between AD 103 and 105 on the orders of the Emperor Trajan, it was designed by his architect Apollodorus of Damascus to supply Roman forces in the Dacian Wars. For more than a thousand years it was the longest arch bridge in the world, in both total and span length.

Construction

The bridge carried a wooden superstructure of segmental arches resting on twenty massive masonry piers founded in the riverbed — a remarkable feat of engineering given the Danube's depth, current and winter ice. The wooden arches each spanned about 51 metres. The crossing connected the Roman road system on the south bank with Drobeta and the new province of Dacia on the north bank. The construction is depicted, stylized, among the reliefs of Trajan's Column in Rome.

Destruction and remains

The wooden superstructure was dismantled in the mid-3rd century — Cassius Dio reports that Hadrian removed it out of caution against barbarian crossings, though it is more often attributed to Aurelian's abandonment of Dacia around AD 271. The piers survived in the river for centuries; several were demolished in the 19th and 20th centuries as hazards to navigation. Two piers and abutment remains survive today, protected as monuments on both banks.
