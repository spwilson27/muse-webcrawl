Gregory II the Martyr lover (Armenian: Գրիգոր Բ Վկայասէր, Grigor II Vkayaser) was the Catholicos of the Armenian Apostolic Church from 1065 to 1105. His epithet, Vkayaser — "lover of martyrs" — reflects his devotion to collecting and translating the lives and acts of the martyrs and saints of the Church.

Life

Gregory was the son of Gregory Magistros, the Armenian scholar-prince of the Pahlavuni family, and thus a scion of the house descended from Gregory the Illuminator. He succeeded his uncle Khachig II as Catholicos in 1065, at a time when Armenia was being overrun by the Seljuk Turks and the seat of the Catholicosate was forced into exile, moving from place to place as Byzantine and then shifting circumstances allowed. Gregory spent much of his pontificate in Cilicia and its borderlands, where Armenian princes such as the Rubenids were carving out principalities. He ordained bishops, worked to preserve Armenian ecclesiastical learning, and compiled martyrologies, translating Greek and Syriac accounts of the saints into Armenian.

Death and succession

In his last years Gregory, worn by travel and ill health, took up residence at the monastery of Karmir Vank (the Red Monastery) in the region of Kesun. He died in 1105. Because the succession was contested amid the dispersal of the Armenian hierarchy, the Catholicosate entered a period in which rival lines emerged, including the catholicosate of Aghtamar; the seat eventually settled at Hromkla and later at Sis in Cilicia.
