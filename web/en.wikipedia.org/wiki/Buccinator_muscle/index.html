Buccinator muscle

OriginAlveolar processes of maxilla and mandible; pterygomandibular raphe
InsertionOrbicularis oris, angle of the mouth
NerveBuccal branches of the facial nerve (CN VII)
ActionsCompresses the cheek; aids chewing, blowing, sucking

The buccinator is a thin quadrilateral muscle occupying the interval between the maxilla and the mandible at the side of the face. It forms the anterior part of the cheek and the lateral wall of the oral cavity. Its name derives from the Latin buccina, a trumpet — it is the muscle used in blowing a trumpet.

Structure and function

The muscle arises from the outer surfaces of the alveolar processes of the maxilla and mandible and from the pterygomandibular raphe behind; its fibres converge toward the angle of the mouth, where they interlace with the orbicularis oris. The duct of the parotid gland pierces it to open into the mouth opposite the second upper molar.

During chewing the buccinator presses the cheek against the teeth and works with the tongue to keep food between the grinding surfaces, preventing it from collecting in the vestibule. It is also essential for whistling, sucking, and blowing, and in infants for nursing.
