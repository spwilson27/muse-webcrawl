Leucippus (Greek: Λεύκιππος, Leukippos; fl. 5th century BC) was a pre-Socratic Greek philosopher traditionally credited as the founder of atomism — the doctrine that the universe consists of indivisible particles, the atoms, moving in empty space, the void. He was the teacher of Democritus, who systematised and popularised the theory.

Life and attributions

Almost nothing certain is known of his life; ancient sources place his birth variously at Miletus, Elea or Abdera, and he is said to have studied with Zeno of Elea. His historical existence was doubted already in antiquity — Epicurus reportedly denied there had been such a philosopher — but Aristotle and Theophrastus consistently treat him as the originator of atomism, and modern scholarship generally accepts him as a real figure whose writings were absorbed into those of Democritus. A work called the Great World-System and a treatise On Mind are attributed to him; only one verbatim fragment survives: "Nothing happens at random; whatever comes about is by reason and necessity."

Philosophy

Responding to the Eleatic denial of change and plurality, Leucippus postulated innumerable solid, indivisible atoms differing in shape, arrangement and position, moving perpetually in the void. Collisions and entanglements of atoms form vortices that generate worlds; all qualitative change reduces to atomic rearrangement. This mechanical, deterministic picture profoundly influenced later thought through Democritus and Epicurus.
