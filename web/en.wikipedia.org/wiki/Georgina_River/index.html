The Georgina River is one of the three major rivers of the Channel Country of western Queensland and the Northern Territory, Australia, forming part of the Lake Eyre basin — one of the largest internally drained river systems in the world.

Course

The river rises in the Barkly Tableland of the Northern Territory, north-west of Mount Isa, and flows generally south and south-east into Queensland, fed by tributaries such as the Buckley, Ranken, Sandover and Pituri creeks. Near Bedourie its waters join Eyre Creek, and in very wet years floodwaters pass through Goyder Lagoon and ultimately reach Lake Eyre (Kati Thanda) in South Australia — though in most years the flow dissipates in the channels and floodplains of the Channel Country.

Environment and use

The Georgina basin supports extensive cattle grazing on natural Mitchell grass pastures watered by the river's seasonal flows; towns along or near the river include Camooweal, Urandangi, Boulia and Bedourie. The river's wetlands are habitat for large populations of waterbirds during flood events.
