The Pratt & Whitney PW4000 is a family of high-bypass turbofan aircraft engines with thrust ratings from 52,000 to 99,040 pounds-force. It was the first commercial engine offered with dual full-authority digital engine control (FADEC) and entered service in 1987 on the Airbus A310.

Variants

The family comprises three sub-families distinguished by fan diameter: the 94-inch fan models power the Airbus A300, A310, and A330, the Boeing 747-400 and 767, and the McDonnell Douglas MD-11; the 100-inch fan (PW4090 and derivatives) was developed for the Boeing 777; and the 112-inch fan Advantage70 powered later A330s. A derivative core also underlies the Engine Alliance GP7000 for the Airbus A380.

Service history

PW4000 engines have accumulated tens of millions of flight hours. The type has been involved in notable uncontained fan-blade failures on Boeing 777s, including United Airlines Flight 328 in 2021, which led to inspections and design changes to fan blades and inlet structures.
