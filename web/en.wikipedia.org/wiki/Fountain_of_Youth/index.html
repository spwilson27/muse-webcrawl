Fountain of Youth

The Fountain of Youth is a mythical spring which, according to legend, restores the youth of anyone who drinks or bathes in its waters. Tales of such a fountain have been recounted around the world for thousands of years, appearing in the writings of Herodotus (5th century BC), in the Alexander romance (3rd century AD), and in the stories of Prester John (early Crusades, 11th/12th centuries AD).

Ponce de León

The legend became strongly attached to the Spanish explorer Juan Ponce de León, first governor of Puerto Rico, who in 1513 led an expedition to Florida. Later chroniclers claimed he was searching for the Fountain of Youth on an island called Bimini, said to lie north of Cuba. Historians doubt that finding the fountain was his actual motive; the association grew in the decades after his death and was popularised by writers such as Washington Irving. Ponce de León never found Florida's fountain, though his landing gave Florida its name (La Pascua Florida, "the feast of flowers").

Earlier sources

Herodotus described a special kind of water in the land of the Macrobians that gave them exceptional longevity. Medieval Alexander romances told of the Water of Life, sought by Alexander the Great, and similar rejuvenating waters appear in Japanese and Caribbean folklore — the latter speaking of the mythical land of Bimini.

In popular culture

The fountain appears throughout literature, art, and film, including Lucas Cranach the Elder's painting The Fountain of Youth (1546) and the film Pirates of the Caribbean: On Stranger Tides (2011). St. Augustine, Florida, hosts a tourist attraction named the Fountain of Youth Archaeological Park at the presumed landing site.
