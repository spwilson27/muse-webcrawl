Philippines campaign (1944–45)

Date: October 20, 1944 – September 2, 1945
Location: Philippine Islands
Result: Allied victory
Commanders: Douglas MacArthur; Tomoyuki Yamashita

U.S. forces returned to the Philippines at Leyte in October 1944.

The Philippines campaign of 1944–45 was the American and Allied campaign to liberate the Philippines from Japanese occupation during the Second World War. Having promised "I shall return" after his 1942 departure, General Douglas MacArthur directed the campaign, which began with landings on Leyte on 20 October 1944 and continued until the end of the war in September 1945.

Course of the campaign

The Leyte landings provoked the Battle of Leyte Gulf, one of the largest naval battles in history, in which Japan's fleet was effectively destroyed as a fighting force and the first organised kamikaze attacks were made. Ground forces secured Leyte and Mindoro, then landed at Lingayen Gulf on Luzon in January 1945. The month-long Battle of Manila in February 1945 devastated the capital and killed an estimated 100,000 Filipino civilians. Fighting continued across Luzon and the southern islands against General Tomoyuki Yamashita's troops, thousands of whom held out in mountain redoubts until Japan's surrender.

Aftermath

The campaign restored the Philippine Commonwealth government and fulfilled American pledges of independence, granted on 4 July 1946. Losses were heavy on all sides: hundreds of thousands of Japanese soldiers, over ten thousand Americans, and a very large number of Filipino civilians perished.
