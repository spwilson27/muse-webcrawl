220 film

220 film is a medium-format roll film introduced by Kodak in 1965 as a companion to 120 film. Like 120, it is 61 mm wide and is wound on the same type of spool, but it has no full-length backing paper — only a short paper leader and trailer. Because of this, a roll of 220 holds twice as much film as a roll of 120 of the same camera format, typically 24 exposures in 6×6 format instead of 12.

Characteristics

The absence of backing paper means 220 film cannot use the red-window frame counters found on older cameras; it requires cameras with mechanical frame counting designed for 220, or dual-mode cameras and interchangeable backs that can switch pressure-plate position between 120 and 220. The film is also thinner when wound, so pressure-plate adjustment matters for focus accuracy.

Availability

Production of 220 film declined sharply in the 2000s as medium-format use fell. Kodak discontinued its last 220 emulsions around 2015, and Fujifilm ended its 220 production shortly afterwards, leaving the format effectively obsolete, though expired stock continues to circulate.
