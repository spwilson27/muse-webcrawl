The Iranian peoples, or Iranic peoples, are a diverse grouping of Indo-European peoples who speak Iranian languages and share related cultural traditions. They descend from Proto-Iranians who spread from Central Asia in the 2nd millennium BC, and historically inhabited a vast region from the Caucasus and Anatolia to the Indus valley and the Eurasian steppe.

History

Ancient Iranian peoples included the Medes and Persians, who founded the Achaemenid Empire (550–330 BC), as well as the Parthians, Sogdians, Bactrians, Khwarazmians and the nomadic Scythians, Sarmatians and Alans of the steppe. Successive empires — Achaemenid, Parthian and Sasanian — spread Iranian languages and culture. After the Muslim conquest of Persia in the 7th century, Iranian culture was Islamised but not Arabised, and a New Persian literary renaissance flourished under dynasties such as the Samanids.

Modern peoples

The largest modern Iranian peoples are the Persians of Iran and the closely related Tajiks of Tajikistan and Afghanistan; the Pashtuns of Afghanistan and Pakistan; the Kurds of Turkey, Iraq, Iran and Syria; the Baloch of Pakistan, Iran and Afghanistan; and smaller groups including the Ossetians of the Caucasus (descended from the Alans), the Talysh, the Lurs, the Mazandaranis and Gilaks, the Hazaras, and the Pamiri peoples. The term "Iranian" in this ethnolinguistic sense is broader than citizenship of the modern state of Iran.
