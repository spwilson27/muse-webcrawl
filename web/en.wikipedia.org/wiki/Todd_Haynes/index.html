Todd Haynes (born January 2, 1961) is an American film director, screenwriter and producer. A central figure of the New Queer Cinema movement of the early 1990s, he is known for films that explore social outsiders, gender and identity, and that frequently pay homage to classic Hollywood melodrama.

Early career

Haynes gained attention with the short film Superstar: The Karen Carpenter Story (1987), which retold the life of singer Karen Carpenter using Barbie dolls; it became a cult work despite legal restrictions on its distribution. His feature debut Poison (1991) won the Grand Jury Prize at the Sundance Film Festival. Safe (1995), starring Julianne Moore as a housewife afflicted by a mysterious environmental illness, was voted the best film of the 1990s in a Village Voice critics' poll.

Later work

Velvet Goldmine (1998) explored glam rock; Far from Heaven (2002), a loving recreation of Douglas Sirk's 1950s melodramas, earned four Academy Award nominations. I'm Not There (2007) cast six actors, including Cate Blanchett, as facets of Bob Dylan. Haynes received widespread acclaim for Carol (2015), adapted from Patricia Highsmith's novel, and later directed Wonderstruck (2017), the legal thriller Dark Waters (2019), the documentary The Velvet Underground (2021), and May December (2023).
