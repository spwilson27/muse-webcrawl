Exponential smoothing

Exponential smoothing is a rule of thumb technique for smoothing time series data using an exponential window function. Whereas in the simple moving average the past observations are weighted equally, exponential functions assign exponentially decreasing weights over time. It is an easily learned and easily applied procedure for making determinations based on prior assumptions about the data and is widely used in forecasting, especially in inventory management.

Exponential smoothing weights recent observations more heavily than older ones.

TypeForecasting / time-series method
OriginatorsRobert Goodell Brown; Charles C. Holt; Peter Winters
Key parameterSmoothing factor α (0 < α < 1)

Simple exponential smoothing

In the simplest form, the smoothed value is a weighted average of the current observation and the previous smoothed value: st = αxt + (1 − α)st−1. Values of α close to 1 track the data closely; small values produce a smoother, slower-reacting series. The method works best for data without trend or seasonality.

Extensions

Holt's linear method adds a trend component with its own smoothing parameter. Holt–Winters further adds a seasonal component, in additive or multiplicative form, and remains a standard for seasonal demand forecasting. The family of models was unified in the ETS (Error, Trend, Seasonal) state-space framework by Hyndman and colleagues, which also provides prediction intervals and automatic model selection by information criteria.

Applications

Exponential smoothing underlies much operational forecasting: retail and manufacturing demand planning, call-centre volume prediction, and inventory control, where its simplicity and low data requirements are major advantages.
