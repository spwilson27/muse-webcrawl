Theodor Leschetizky

Theodor Leschetizky
Born: 22 June 1830, Łańcut, Austrian Empire (now Poland)
Died: 14 November 1915 (aged 85), Dresden, German Empire
Occupations: pianist, composer, piano teacher
Known for: teaching many of the leading pianists of the early 20th century
Notable students: Ignacy Jan Paderewski, Artur Schnabel, Mieczysław Horszowski, Benno Moiseiwitsch

Theodor Leschetizky (Polish: Teodor Leszetycki; 22 June 1830 – 14 November 1915) was a Polish pianist, professor, and composer born in Łańcut, then part of the Austrian Empire. He is regarded as one of the most influential piano teachers of all time, the founder of the so-called Leschetizky method, and the teacher of many of the most celebrated pianists of the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.

Life

Leschetizky's father, a capable amateur pianist, gave him his first lessons. The boy showed exceptional talent and made his debut in Lwów (Lemberg) at the age of nine, playing a concerto by Czerny. The family moved to Vienna, where the young Leschetizky studied piano with Carl Czerny — himself a pupil of Beethoven — and composition with Simon Sechter.

By his teens he was teaching and performing. In 1852 he travelled to St. Petersburg, where his playing at court led to a long residence in Russia. He became head of piano studies at the newly founded Saint Petersburg Conservatory under Anton Rubinstein, remaining there until 1878.

Returning to Vienna, Leschetizky established himself as the most sought-after piano teacher in Europe. Over the following decades, pupils came to him from all over the world. Among the most famous were Ignacy Jan Paderewski, Artur Schnabel, Mieczysław Horszowski, Benno Moiseiwitsch, Elly Ney, and many others; by common estimate he taught well over a thousand pupils, dozens of whom became internationally known concert pianists.

Teaching

Although later codified by his assistants (notably Marie Prentner, whose book The Leschetizky Method appeared with his approval), Leschetizky himself insisted that he had no fixed "method" beyond adapting his teaching to the individuality of each student. In practice his teaching emphasised a beautiful singing tone, a relaxed arm and supple wrist, economical movements, and careful attention to phrasing and musical character rather than mechanical drill. He prepared pupils for performance through regular musical evenings at his Vienna home, where students played before demanding audiences of fellow pupils.

Compositions and recordings

Leschetizky composed piano pieces, songs, chamber music, and an opera, Die erste Falte. His piano works are now seldom performed, though the Andante finale from his arrangements after Donizetti's Lucia di Lammermoor for the left hand enjoyed some popularity. In 1906 he recorded a small number of piano rolls for the Welte-Mignon reproducing piano, which preserve something of his own playing.

He died in Dresden in 1915. His influence persisted through generations of pupils and grand-pupils worldwide.
