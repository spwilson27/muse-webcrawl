Renal papillary necrosis

SpecialtyNephrology
DefinitionNecrosis of the renal papillae
CausesDiabetes, analgesic nephropathy, sickle cell disease, pyelonephritis, urinary obstruction

Renal papillary necrosis is a form of nephropathy involving the necrosis of the renal papillae, the points where the collecting ducts enter the renal pelvis. The papillae are vulnerable because the medullary blood supply is relatively sparse; ischemia causes the tissue to die and slough into the urine.

Causes

Common causes are recalled by the mnemonic POSTCARDS: pyelonephritis, obstruction, sickle cell disease, tuberculosis, cirrhosis/chronic liver disease, analgesic nephropathy, renal vein thrombosis, diabetes mellitus, and systemic vasculitis. Diabetes mellitus and long-term analgesic use are the most frequent associations; in many patients several factors coexist.

Signs and presentation

Patients may present with flank pain, hematuria, renal colic from sloughed papillae obstructing the ureter, fever from infection, or progressive chronic kidney disease. Imaging may show characteristic "ring shadows" or clubbed calyces where papillae have detached.

Treatment

Management focuses on treating the underlying cause — controlling diabetes, stopping offending analgesics, relieving obstruction, and treating infection — since established necrosis is irreversible.
