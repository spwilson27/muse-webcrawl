Warsaw, New York

Warsaw is a town in Wyoming County, New York, United States, and the county seat. The town, which includes the village of Warsaw, had a population of about 5,300 at recent censuses.

History

Warsaw was settled from 1803 and formed from the town of Batavia in 1808. The village became a noted centre of abolitionism: the Liberty Party was organised at Warsaw in 1839, and local resident Seth M. Gates, a congressman and newspaper editor, was a prominent antislavery campaigner. The Warsaw station area was also a stop associated with the Underground Railroad.

Geography

The town lies in the Oatka Creek valley in western New York, east of Letchworth State Park. U.S. Route 20A crosses the town east–west; the Wyoming County government offices and courthouse are in the village.
