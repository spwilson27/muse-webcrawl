Bock is a strong lager of German origin. Several substyles exist, including the traditional Bockbier, the paler Maibock (also called Helles Bock), the stronger Doppelbock, and Eisbock, made by partially freezing the beer and removing the ice. Bock is historically associated with special occasions and seasonal festivals, particularly Lent and spring.

History

Bock originated in the Hanseatic town of Einbeck in Lower Saxony, whose exported beer was prized in the Middle Ages; the name is a corruption of "Einbeck" in the Bavarian dialect. Brewers in Munich later adopted and refined the style. Monks of the Paulaner order brewed the original Doppelbock, "Salvator", as "liquid bread" for Lenten fasts; many doppelbocks still carry names ending in "-ator".

Styles

Traditional bock is dark, malty and lightly hopped at 6.3–7.2% alcohol. Maibock is a paler, more hop-forward version brewed for spring festivals. Doppelbock starts at 7% and is richer and maltier still; Weizenbock is a wheat version brewed with ale yeast. Eisbock, created in Kulmbach in 1990, reaches 9–14% by freeze distillation.
