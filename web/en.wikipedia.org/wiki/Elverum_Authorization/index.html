Elverum Authorization

The Elverum Authorization (Norwegian: Elverumsfullmakten) was a resolution adopted by the Norwegian Parliament (Storting) on 9 April 1940, the day Nazi Germany invaded Norway. The resolution granted the Norwegian government full authority to make decisions on behalf of the parliament until the Storting could convene again, in order to safeguard the independence and security of the country.

Background

The Storting had gathered at Hamar following the German attack, and then moved to Elverum as German forces advanced. There, the parliament unanimously adopted the authorization, drafted by Storting president C. J. Hambro. It empowered the cabinet of Johan Nygaardsvold to exercise full powers "where these are needed to safeguard the country's interests" until the Storting could reassemble.

Significance

The authorization provided the legal basis for the Norwegian government-in-exile's decisions during World War II, including control of the merchant fleet (Nortraship) and Norway's gold reserves. Its constitutionality has been debated, since the Norwegian Constitution contains no provision for such delegation, but it was widely accepted as a necessity of the emergency.
