Dandong

Dandong (Chinese: 丹东; pinyin: Dāndōng), formerly known as Andong, is a prefecture-level city in southeastern Liaoning province, China, on the north bank of the Yalu River, facing the North Korean city of Sinuiju across the border. It is the largest Chinese city on the border with North Korea, and a principal gateway for trade between the two countries.

Geography

Dandong sits at the mouth of the Yalu on the Korea Bay coast of the Yellow Sea. The prefecture administers a largely mountainous hinterland rising toward the Changbai Mountains, and enjoys a humid continental climate tempered by the sea, with hot, rainy summers and cold, dry winters.

History

The region was fortified from the Han dynasty onward; the eastern terminus of a Ming-dynasty continuation of the Great Wall, the Hushan ("Tiger Mountain") section, lies within the prefecture. Opened as a treaty port under the name Andong in the early 20th century, the city grew as a commercial and timber-trading centre. During the Korean War, Dandong was the main crossing point for Chinese People's Volunteer Army troops and supplies entering Korea, and was bombed by US aircraft; the destroyed Yalu River Bridge, preserved in its broken state, became a war memorial. The parallel Sino–Korean Friendship Bridge (1943) still carries most China–North Korea trade.

Economy

Dandong's economy rests on border trade, light industry, seafood and tourism, including visitors viewing North Korea from the riverfront promenade and Broken Bridge. The new Yalu River Bridge downstream, completed by China in 2014, long awaited North Korean road connections.
