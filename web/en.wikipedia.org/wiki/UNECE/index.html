United Nations Economic Commission for Europe

The United Nations Economic Commission for Europe (UNECE or ECE) is one of five regional commissions under the jurisdiction of the United Nations Economic and Social Council. It was established in 1947 to promote pan-European economic integration.

History

UNECE was founded in 1947, initially to aid the reconstruction of war-devastated Europe. During the Cold War it served as a rare bridge between East and West, and after 1989 it assisted the transition economies of Central and Eastern Europe and Central Asia.

Membership and work

UNECE has 56 member States, including countries in Europe, North America (Canada and the United States), Central Asia and Israel. Headquartered at the Palais des Nations in Geneva, Switzerland, it works on trade, transport, environment, energy, statistics, housing and forests.

UNECE is known for technical standards and conventions, including vehicle regulations, the TIR customs convention, the Aarhus Convention on environmental democracy, and transport agreements such as the European Agreement concerning the International Carriage of Dangerous Goods by Road (ADR).
