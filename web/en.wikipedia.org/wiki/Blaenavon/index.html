Blaenavon (Welsh: Blaenafon) is a town and community in Torfaen county borough, Wales, high on the side of a spur of the Brecon Beacons at the head of the Afon Lwyd valley. The town grew around an ironworks opened in 1788, and its landscape of mines, quarries, furnaces and workers' housing was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2000.

History

The Blaenavon Ironworks were built by Thomas Hill, Thomas Hopkins and Benjamin Pratt, who leased land from Lord Abergavenny and began smelting iron in 1789. Coal and iron ore were extracted from the surrounding hillsides, and in 1816 the company opened the Pwll Du tunnel to haul materials. The town's rapid growth made it one of the birthplaces of the Industrial Revolution in South Wales. The ironworks closed in 1904, though coal mining continued at Big Pit until 1980.

Landmarks

The preserved Blaenavon Ironworks, the Big Pit National Coal Museum and the Blaenavon Heritage Railway are major visitor attractions. St Peter's Church, built in 1804, contains a cast-iron font made at the works.
