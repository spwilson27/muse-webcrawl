The Maltese Falcon (1941 film)

The Maltese Falcon is a 1941 American film noir directed by John Huston in his directorial debut, starring Humphrey Bogart as private investigator Sam Spade and Mary Astor as femme fatale Brigid O'Shaughnessy. It is based on Dashiell Hammett's 1930 novel.

Plot

San Francisco private detectives Sam Spade and Miles Archer are hired by a woman calling herself Ruth Wonderly to follow Floyd Thursby. Archer and Thursby are soon murdered. The case draws Spade into a web of criminals — the perfumed Joel Cairo, the urbane Kasper Gutman ("the Fat Man") and their young gunsel Wilmer — all hunting a jewel-encrusted statuette of a falcon, a legendary tribute gift from the Knights of Malta. When the coveted bird finally surfaces it proves a worthless lead fake — "the stuff that dreams are made of" — and Spade turns the lying Brigid, with whom he has fallen in love, over to the police for Archer's murder.

Production

Huston's screenplay followed Hammett's novel unusually faithfully, retaining its dialogue and objective point of view. It was the third film version of the novel, after versions in 1931 and 1936 (Satan Met a Lady). Sydney Greenstreet made his film debut at 61 as Gutman.

Legacy

The film was nominated for three Academy Awards, including Best Picture, and established Bogart as a leading man. It is regarded as one of the first major film noirs and one of the greatest films ever made; it was among the first 25 films selected for the National Film Registry in 1989.
