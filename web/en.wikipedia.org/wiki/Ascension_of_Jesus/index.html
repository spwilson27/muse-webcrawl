Ascension of Jesus

Observed by: Christians worldwide

Date: 39 days after Easter Sunday (the 40th day of Easter)

The Ascension of Jesus is the Christian belief, embodied in the major creeds and confessional statements, that Jesus ascended bodily into Heaven in the presence of his disciples, forty days after his resurrection. In the biblical narrative, an angel tells the watching disciples that Jesus' second coming will take place in the same manner as his ascension.

Biblical accounts

The ascension is described in Mark 16:19, Luke 24:50–53 and Acts 1:3–11, and is alluded to elsewhere in the New Testament. According to Acts, after the resurrection Jesus appeared to his followers over forty days, then was taken up and hidden by a cloud on the Mount of Olives near Bethany. Two men in white told the disciples, "This same Jesus, who has been taken from you into heaven, will come back in the same way you have seen him go into heaven."

Feast of the Ascension

The Feast of the Ascension is one of the principal feasts of the Christian year, celebrated on Ascension Thursday, the fortieth day of Easter (counting inclusively), though in many jurisdictions it is transferred to the following Sunday. It is a public holiday in numerous European and other countries. The feast ranks with Christmas, Easter and Pentecost among the oldest observances of the Church.

In art

The Ascension is a frequent subject in Christian art, often showing Christ rising with hands raised in blessing, sometimes within a mandorla, while the apostles and the Virgin Mary look on. Notable depictions include works by Giotto, Pietro Perugino, John Singleton Copley and Rembrandt van Rijn.
