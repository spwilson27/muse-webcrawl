Cambridge is a city and university city in Cambridgeshire, England, on the River Cam approximately 55 miles (89 km) north of London. As of the 2021 census, the population of the City of Cambridge was 145,700. The city is best known as the home of the University of Cambridge, founded in 1209 and consistently ranked among the best universities in the world.

History

The area was settled in the Bronze and Iron Ages, and the Romans built a fort town, Duroliponte, on Castle Hill around AD 70. After the Roman withdrawal the town passed through Anglo-Saxon and Viking hands before the Norman Conquest; Cambridge Castle was built in 1068. In 1209, students fleeing hostility in Oxford founded the university that would transform the town. Cambridge received its city charter in 1951.

The university

The University of Cambridge is a collegiate university of 31 colleges, including King's College, whose chapel (1446–1515) is a masterpiece of late Gothic architecture, and Trinity College, the largest. University figures range from Isaac Newton and Charles Darwin to Alan Turing and Stephen Hawking. The university's museums, botanic garden, and Fitzwilliam Museum are major cultural institutions.

Economy

Cambridge is the centre of the "Silicon Fen", a cluster of high-technology and biotechnology companies — including ARM, AstraZeneca's global headquarters, and numerous start-ups spun out of the university — making it one of Europe's most important technology centres. Tourism and education are also major employers.

Transport and culture

The city is famous for cycling, with one of the highest levels of bicycle use in the United Kingdom. Punting on the River Cam past the college "Backs" is a signature activity. Cambridge railway station connects the city to London King's Cross in under an hour. Annual events include the Cambridge Folk Festival and the university's May Balls.
