Jeroboam II (Hebrew: יָרָבְעָם, romanized: Yaraḇəʿām) was the son and successor of Jehoash and the thirteenth king of the northern Kingdom of Israel, over which he ruled for forty-one years in the eighth century BC. His reign, conventionally dated to about 793–753 BC (including a co-regency), was the most prosperous period in the northern kingdom's history.

Reign

Taking advantage of Assyria's temporary weakness and the decline of Aram-Damascus, Jeroboam restored Israel's borders "from Lebo Hamath to the Sea of the Arabah" (2 Kings 14:25), recovering territory not held since the days of Solomon. Archaeological evidence — including the monumental building at Samaria and Megiddo and the Samaria ostraca recording shipments of wine and oil — points to considerable wealth and administrative sophistication during his reign.

Prophetic criticism

The prosperity of Jeroboam's reign rested on heavy burdens for the poor, and the prophets Amos and Hosea, active in his kingdom, fiercely denounced the corruption, luxury and social injustice of the elite. Amos prophesied the fall of the "house of Jeroboam" by the sword.

Death and succession

Jeroboam was succeeded by his son Zechariah, the last king of the dynasty of Jehu, who was assassinated after six months, plunging Israel into a period of instability that ended with the Assyrian conquest of 722 BC.
