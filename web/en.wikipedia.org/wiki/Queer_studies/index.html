Queer studies

Queer studies
Field: Critical theory, cultural studies, sociology
Emergence: Early 1990s
Key figures: Judith Butler, Eve Kosofsky Sedgwick, Michel Foucault

Queer studies is an interdisciplinary academic field devoted to the study of sexual orientation, gender identity, and the cultures of lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender, and queer people, together with the social norms that define some sexualities and genders as normal and others as deviant. It is closely related to, but distinct from, LGBT studies and women's and gender studies: where identity-based fields often take identity categories as their starting point, queer studies tends to interrogate the categories themselves.

Origins

The term "queer theory" was coined by Teresa de Lauretis at a 1990 conference at the University of California, Santa Cruz. The field drew foundational inspiration from Michel Foucault's History of Sexuality (1976–1984), which treated sexuality as a historically produced discourse, and from works such as Eve Kosofsky Sedgwick's Between Men (1985) and Epistemology of the Closet (1990) and Judith Butler's Gender Trouble (1990), which proposed that gender is performative — produced by repeated acts rather than expressing an inner essence.

Themes

Recurring concerns include the social construction of sexual identity; heteronormativity, the assumption that heterosexuality is the default and natural order; the closet and the politics of visibility and coming out; intersections of sexuality with race, class, and nation; queer temporality and kinship; and resistance to normalisation, including within mainstream gay culture itself.

Institutional history

Queer studies programmes, centres, and journals grew rapidly in American and British universities during the 1990s and 2000s, often out of earlier gay and lesbian studies and women's studies units. The field has been politically contentious, drawing criticism both from conservatives opposed to its subject matter and from scholars who argue it has become overly textual or detached from material politics.
