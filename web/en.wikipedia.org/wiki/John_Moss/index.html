John Moss

John Edward Moss (April 13, 1915 – December 5, 2002) was an American politician who served as a Democratic member of the United States House of Representatives from California from 1953 to 1978. He is remembered as a leading advocate of open government and consumer protection, and as a principal architect of the Freedom of Information Act.

Early life and career

Born in Hiawatha, Utah, Moss grew up in Utah and California. He worked in business and served in the California State Assembly before his election to Congress in 1952.

Congressional service

Moss chaired a special subcommittee on government information, where he spent more than a decade pressing federal agencies to release information to the public. His efforts culminated in the passage of the Freedom of Information Act in 1966. He also championed consumer safety legislation, including measures strengthening the Consumer Product Safety Commission, and played a role in the creation of the Moss–Magnuson Act and other regulatory reforms of the 1970s.

Legacy

After retiring in 1978, Moss remained active in civic affairs in Sacramento. He is widely credited with helping to establish the principle that government records belong to the public.
