Sonny & Cher

Sonny & Cher
Origin: Los Angeles, California, US
Genres: Pop, pop rock, folk rock
Years active: 1964–1977
Members: Sonny Bono, Cher

Sonny & Cher were an American pop duo made up of husband-and-wife team Sonny Bono and Cher. They rose to fame in 1965 with the hit "I Got You Babe" and sold an estimated 40 million records worldwide before becoming television stars in the 1970s.

Music career

Bono, a songwriter and producer who had worked with Phil Spector, began recording with Cher (born Cherilyn Sarkisian) in 1964, first as "Caesar and Cleo". As Sonny & Cher they scored hits with "I Got You Babe" (1965), "The Beat Goes On" (1967) and "All I Ever Need Is You" (1971). Their harmony singing, hippie fashion and playful stage banter made them emblematic of the era.

Television

The Sonny & Cher Comedy Hour (CBS, 1971–1974) was a top-rated variety show built around their bickering comic exchanges and Cher's extravagant Bob Mackie gowns. After their divorce in 1975, they briefly reunited for The Sonny and Cher Show (1976–1977). Bono later entered politics, serving as mayor of Palm Springs and as a US Congressman; Cher continued a hugely successful solo career in music and film. The duo were inducted into the Hollywood Walk of Fame in 1998, shortly after Bono's death in a skiing accident.
