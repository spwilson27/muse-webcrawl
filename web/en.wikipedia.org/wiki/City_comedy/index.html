City comedy

City comedy (also called citizen comedy) is a genre of early modern English drama, flourishing roughly from the 1590s to the 1630s. Set in contemporary London, these plays satirised the manners, commerce, sexual intrigue and social climbing of the city's merchants, apprentices, gallants and prostitutes.

Characteristics

Unlike the romances and histories of the public stage, city comedies stick to realistic urban settings and money-driven plots: sharpers trick gulls, wives outwit jealous husbands, and ambitious citizens chase gentility. The genre relies on multiple interwoven plots, topical references and a playfully moralising exposure of vice.

Major works and playwrights

Leading practitioners include Ben Jonson (Bartholomew Fair, The Alchemist, Volpone in its Italian setting), Thomas Middleton (A Chaste Maid in Cheapside, A Trick to Catch the Old One), Thomas Dekker (The Shoemaker's Holiday), and John Webster and Philip Massinger in the genre's later phase. The form influenced Restoration comedy and the modern situation comedy's ensemble structure.
