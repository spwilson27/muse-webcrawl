Sunyaev–Zeldovich effect

The Sunyaev–Zeldovich effect (named after Rashid Sunyaev and Yakov B. Zeldovich, often abbreviated as the SZ effect, SZE, or SZ) is the spectral distortion of the cosmic microwave background (CMB) through inverse Compton scattering by high-energy electrons in hot intracluster gas. First predicted in papers published in 1969–1972, the effect allows astronomers to detect galaxy clusters nearly independently of their redshift, because the surface brightness of the distortion does not dim with distance the way ordinary emission does.

CMB photons are up-scattered by hot electrons in the intracluster medium, shifting the CMB spectrum.

Mechanism

Photons of the CMB passing through a galaxy cluster encounter electrons in the intracluster medium heated to tens of millions of kelvin. Through inverse Compton scattering, the photons on average gain energy, producing a characteristic spectral signature: a decrease in CMB brightness at frequencies below about 217 GHz and an increase above it. This is the thermal SZ effect. A smaller kinetic SZ effect arises from the bulk motion of the cluster relative to the CMB rest frame, shifting the spectrum without distorting its shape.

Observations

The effect was first reliably measured in the 1980s and has since become a major tool of observational cosmology. Surveys by the Atacama Cosmology Telescope, the South Pole Telescope, and the Planck satellite have catalogued hundreds of clusters through their SZ signatures. Combined with X-ray observations, the effect can be used to estimate distances and the Hubble constant, measure the baryon content of clusters, and constrain cosmological parameters.
