Messier 54 (also known as M54 or NGC 6715) is a globular cluster in the constellation Sagittarius. It was discovered by Charles Messier in 1778 and subsequently included in his catalogue of comet-like objects.

Previously thought to belong to the Milky Way, it was discovered in 1994 that M54 most likely belongs to the Sagittarius Dwarf Spheroidal Galaxy (Sgr dSph), making it one of the first extragalactic globular clusters to be identified, albeit unknowingly for over two centuries. Because it lies at the centre of the Sgr dSph, some astronomers think it may actually be the nucleus (core remnant) of that dwarf galaxy rather than a true globular cluster.

Properties

M54 is about 87,000 light-years away, has an absolute magnitude around −10.0, and is among the most luminous and massive globular clusters known, with a mass of roughly 1.4 million solar masses. It has a dense, concentrated profile (class III) and a radius of about 150 light-years including its tidal extent. Its population shows evidence of multiple stellar generations with a spread in metallicity, supporting the interpretation that it formed as a galactic nucleus, possibly incorporating the separate cluster Terzan 8 nearby in the same system.

Observation

With an apparent magnitude of 8.4, M54 can be glimpsed in binoculars and small telescopes as a small, bright, fuzzy star; resolving its stars requires larger apertures because of its great distance. It is located about 0.5° southwest of the star Zeta Sagittarii (Ascella) and is best observed in summer from northern temperate latitudes.
