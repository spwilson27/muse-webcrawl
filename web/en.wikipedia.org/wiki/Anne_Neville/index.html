Anne Neville (11 June 1456 – 16 March 1485) was Queen of England from 1483 until her death, as the wife of King Richard III. She was the younger daughter of Richard Neville, 16th Earl of Warwick, known as "the Kingmaker", and a central figure in the dynastic struggles of the Wars of the Roses.

Life

Anne spent much of her childhood at Middleham Castle in Yorkshire, where she met her future husband, Richard, Duke of Gloucester, who was raised in her father's household. In 1470 she was married to Edward of Westminster, the Lancastrian Prince of Wales, as part of her father's alliance with Margaret of Anjou. Edward was killed at the Battle of Tewkesbury in 1471, shortly after her father's death at the Battle of Barnet, leaving Anne a widow at fourteen.

In 1472 Anne married Richard, Duke of Gloucester. Their only child, Edward of Middleham, died in 1484. When Richard seized the throne in 1483, Anne was crowned queen alongside him at Westminster Abbey. She died on 16 March 1485, possibly of tuberculosis, and was buried in Westminster Abbey. Richard was killed at the Battle of Bosworth Field five months later.

In literature

Anne appears as a character in Shakespeare's Richard III, famously wooed by Richard over the corpse of her father-in-law, Henry VI. She is also the subject of several modern historical novels, including Philippa Gregory's The Kingmaker's Daughter (2012).
