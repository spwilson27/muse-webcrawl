Arch Madness

Arch Madness is the popular name of the Missouri Valley Conference men's basketball tournament, the postseason tournament of the Missouri Valley Conference (MVC) in NCAA Division I college basketball. It is held annually in St. Louis, Missouri — whose landmark Gateway Arch gives the event its nickname — and the tournament champion receives the conference's automatic bid to the NCAA Division I men's basketball tournament.

History

The Missouri Valley Conference first held a postseason tournament in 1977; the format was discontinued after 1980 and revived in 1991. In 1992 the tournament settled in St. Louis, and it has remained there ever since, played at venues including the Kiel Auditorium, the Kiel Center (later Savvis Center), and, since 2008's expanded era, the Enterprise Center (formerly the Scottrade Center). St. Louis is one of the longest-running hosts of any conference tournament in the United States, and "Arch Madness" has become a fixture of the first weekend of the college basketball postseason calendar.

Format

All conference teams qualify for the single-elimination tournament and are seeded by regular-season conference record, with tiebreakers applied as needed. Higher seeds receive byes into the quarterfinals while the lowest seeds play first-round games on Thursday; semifinals follow on Saturday and the championship game on Sunday, traditionally broadcast nationally on CBS.

Notable champions

The tournament has produced several celebrated Cinderella runs. Northern Iowa won the event five times, including back-to-back titles in 2009 and 2010. Bradley captured dramatic titles in 2019 and 2020. Loyola Chicago's 2018 Arch Madness crown launched the Ramblers' famous run to the Final Four of the 2018 NCAA tournament. Drake, Indiana State, Illinois State, Southern Illinois, Wichita State (before its departure to the American Athletic Conference), and Creighton (before departing for the Big East) have all won multiple tournament championships.
