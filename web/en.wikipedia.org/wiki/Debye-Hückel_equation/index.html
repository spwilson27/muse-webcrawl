Debye–Hückel equation

Named afterPeter Debye; Erich Hückel
Published1923
FieldPhysical chemistry (electrolyte solutions)

The Debye–Hückel equation, proposed by Peter Debye and Erich Hückel in 1923, gives the activity coefficients of ions in dilute electrolyte solutions. It models each ion as surrounded by an "ionic atmosphere" of oppositely charged ions, allowing the deviations of real solutions from ideal behaviour to be calculated from electrostatics.

Limiting law

In its limiting form, the logarithm of the mean ionic activity coefficient is proportional to the square root of the ionic strength and to the product of the ionic charges. The Debye–Hückel limiting law is exact only at very low concentrations, but it correctly captures the characteristic square-root dependence verified experimentally.

Extensions

Extended forms of the equation introduce an ion-size parameter to improve accuracy at moderate concentrations, and later models such as the Pitzer equations build on the theory for concentrated solutions used in geochemistry and chemical engineering. A related result describes the relaxation and electrophoretic effects on the conductivity of electrolytes (the Debye–Hückel–Onsager equation).
