Eleanor Lambert (August 10, 1903 – October 7, 2003) was an American fashion publicist widely credited with establishing New York as a world fashion capital and with putting American fashion on the global map. Over a career spanning more than 75 years she founded New York Fashion Week, the Council of Fashion Designers of America (CFDA), the Met Gala as it is known today, and the International Best-Dressed List.

Career

Born in Crawfordsville, Indiana, Lambert studied sculpture briefly before moving to New York, where she worked at an art gallery and then in publicity. In the 1930s she became the first press director of the Whitney Museum of American Art and helped found the Museum of Modern Art's publicity approach. During the Second World War, when Paris couture was cut off by occupation, Lambert seized the moment for American designers: in 1943 she organised the first "Press Week" in New York, the direct ancestor of New York Fashion Week, persuading journalists to cover American collections in their own right.

Institutions and legacy

In 1948 Lambert organised the first Costume Institute benefit at the Metropolitan Museum of Art, beginning the tradition that grew into the Met Gala. She founded the CFDA in 1962, created the Coty Awards to honour American design, and originated the International Best-Dressed List (later transferred to Vanity Fair). In 1973 she staged the "Battle of Versailles" fashion show, pitting American designers against French couturiers and triumphing decisively for the Americans. She worked into her late nineties and was honoured by the CFDA with a lifetime achievement distinction created in her name. She died in Manhattan in 2003, aged 100, and is remembered as the "godmother of American fashion".
