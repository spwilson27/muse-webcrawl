Train

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A modern high-speed train in white and blue livery at a station platform]
A high-speed train.

A train is a series of connected vehicles (railcars or wagons) travelling on rail tracks to transport passengers or freight, usually pulled or pushed by a locomotive, or self-propelled in multiple units. Trains were the dominant form of land transport during and after the Industrial Revolution, beginning with steam locomotives in early 19th-century Britain, and remain vital today, from urban metros to high-speed rail and heavy freight.

Contents1. History
2. Types

History

The first public steam railway, the Stockton and Darlington Railway, opened in 1825, followed by the Liverpool and Manchester Railway (1830). Rail networks spread worldwide; diesel and electric traction replaced steam in the 20th century, and Japan's Shinkansen (1964) launched the high-speed era, with trains in China and Europe now routinely running at 300–350 km/h.

Types

Freight trains move bulk goods in wagons; passenger trains range from commuter and metro systems to sleeper trains. Special forms include monorails, maglev trains (levitated by magnets, fastest at Shanghai's airport line) and funiculars.
