Bit error rate

In digital transmission, the bit error rate (BER) is the number of bit errors per unit time or per number of bits transmitted. The bit error ratio (also BER) is the number of bit errors divided by the total number of transferred bits during a studied time interval. Bit error ratio is a unitless performance measure, often expressed as a percentage.

Definition

The bit error probability pe is the expected value of the bit error ratio. For example, a transmission with a BER of 10−6 is expected to have, on average, one erroneous bit in every million bits received. BER is commonly measured by transmitting a known pseudorandom bit sequence through a system and counting the errors at the receiver; such instruments are called bit error rate testers (BERTs).

Factors affecting BER

BER is degraded by noise, interference, distortion, bit synchronisation errors, attenuation and multipath fading in wireless channels. It improves with higher signal-to-noise ratio, stronger transmit power, and more robust modulation or error-correction schemes. Forward error correction codes reduce the effective BER at the cost of additional overhead bits.

Typical values

Fibre-optic systems commonly operate at BERs of 10−9 to 10−12 or better, while wireless links may accept 10−3 to 10−6 before error correction. Voice systems tolerate higher error rates than data systems, which may require error-free delivery guaranteed by retransmission protocols.
