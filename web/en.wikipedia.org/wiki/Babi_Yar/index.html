Babi Yar

Babi Yar
Ukrainian: Бабин Яр (Babyn Yar)
Location: Kyiv, Ukraine
Event: Massacres, 1941–1943
Victims: Jews, Roma, Soviet POWs and others
Perpetrators: Einsatzgruppen, SS, police auxiliaries

Babi Yar (Babyn Yar) is a ravine in Kyiv, Ukraine, site of mass murders carried out by Nazi German forces during the Second World War. In the largest single massacre, on 29–30 September 1941, 33,771 Jews from Kyiv were shot by Sonderkommando 4a of Einsatzgruppe C with SS, police and Ukrainian auxiliary support.

Massacres

Following the German occupation of Kyiv, Jews were ordered to assemble with documents and valuables, marched to the ravine, stripped and shot in groups. Killings at Babi Yar continued through 1943, murdering Roma, Soviet prisoners of war, Ukrainian nationalists, psychiatric patients and others; total victims are estimated at 100,000 or more. In 1943 the Nazis exhumed and burned bodies in an attempt to hide the crime.

Memory

Soviet authorities long suppressed the specifically Jewish identity of the victims. Yevgeny Yevtushenko's 1961 poem "Babi Yar" and Shostakovich's Thirteenth Symphony protested this silence and antisemitism. Memorials now stand at the site, and the Babyn Yar Holocaust Memorial Center was established to document the victims; the site was damaged by a Russian missile strike in March 2022.
