Ochota massacre

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Ochota massacre (Polish: Rzeź Ochoty, "the Ochota slaughter") was a series of mass murders and atrocities committed by German forces against the civilian population of the Ochota district of Warsaw, Poland, during the Warsaw Uprising, beginning on 4 August 1944. The killings were carried out principally by the SS RONA brigade (Kaminski Brigade), a collaborationist formation of Soviet citizens commanded by Bronislav Kaminski, acting alongside German police and SS units.

Events

After the uprising began on 1 August 1944, German forces moved to clear the southern districts of Warsaw. From 4 August, RONA troops swept through Ochota, looting, raping and murdering inhabitants, burning houses and attacking institutions, including the Maria Skłodowska-Curie Institute of Oncology (Radium Institute), where patients and staff were robbed, raped and killed. Residents were driven from their homes into transit points such as the Zieleniak marketplace, where many were shot, and from there deported to concentration camps or forced labour.

Death toll

Estimates of the victims in Ochota vary; commonly cited figures suggest that around 10,000 residents of the district were murdered during the pacification, with many more deported. The Ochota killings are often discussed together with the even larger Wola massacre, which took place at the same time on the other side of the city centre.

Aftermath

Kaminski himself was summoned by the Germans and executed by the SS later in August 1944, officially for unauthorised looting. After the war, the events in Ochota were documented by Polish investigators, and the sites of the killings are commemorated by memorial plaques in the district.
