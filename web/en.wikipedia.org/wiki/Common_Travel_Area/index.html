The Common Travel Area (CTA) is an open borders area comprising the United Kingdom, Ireland, the Isle of Man, and the Channel Islands. Citizens of the UK and Ireland may travel, live, and work freely throughout the area without routine passport controls, and enjoy associated rights including access to social welfare and, in many cases, voting rights.

History

The arrangement originated in 1923, after the establishment of the Irish Free State, when the two governments agreed to maintain a shared travel zone rather than impose border controls. It has survived Ireland's departure from the Commonwealth, both countries' membership of the European Union, and Brexit, after which the UK and Ireland reaffirmed the CTA in a 2019 memorandum of understanding.

Operation

There are generally no immigration controls for travel within the CTA, although identity documents may be required by carriers. Special arrangements keep the land border between Ireland and Northern Ireland free of routine checks.
