Louis-Joseph de Montcalm-Gozon, Marquis de Montcalm de Saint Véran (28 February 1712 – 14 September 1759) was a French soldier who commanded the French forces in North America during the Seven Years' War (the French and Indian War). He won notable victories at Oswego, Fort William Henry, and Carillon, but was mortally wounded at the Battle of the Plains of Abraham before Quebec, whose fall decided the fate of New France.

Early career

Montcalm entered the army at fifteen and served with distinction in the Wars of Polish and Austrian Succession, being wounded and captured at Piacenza (1746). Promoted major general, he was sent to Canada in 1756 to command the regular troops, where he immediately quarrelled with the governor, the Marquis de Vaudreuil, over strategy and the use of Indigenous allies and Canadian militia.

Campaigns in North America

In August 1756 Montcalm captured Fort Oswego; in 1757 he took Fort William Henry, an operation stained by the killing of British prisoners by France's Indigenous allies after the surrender — an event dramatised in The Last of the Mohicans. His greatest triumph came on 8 July 1758 at Carillon (Ticonderoga), where with about 3,600 men he repelled frontal assaults by some 16,000 British under Abercromby. From 1758, however, British sea power and numbers steadily overwhelmed the colony.

Quebec and death

In 1759 a British expedition under James Wolfe sailed up the St Lawrence and besieged Quebec. After a summer of stalemate, Wolfe's troops scaled the cliffs west of the city on the night of 12–13 September. Montcalm marched out to meet them on the Plains of Abraham and was defeated in a brief pitched battle; both he and Wolfe were mortally wounded. Montcalm died the next morning, reportedly saying he was glad not to live to see Quebec surrender. Quebec capitulated days later; Montreal fell in 1760, and by the Treaty of Paris (1763) Canada passed to Britain.

Legacy

Montcalm is commemorated with Wolfe by the obelisk in Quebec City honouring both generals, and he remains a central figure — criticised for his feuds and his decision to fight at the Plains, admired for Carillon and his personal courage — in the history of French Canada.
