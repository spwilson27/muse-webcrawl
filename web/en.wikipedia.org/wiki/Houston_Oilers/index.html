The Houston Oilers were a professional American football team based in Houston, Texas, from 1960 to 1996. Founded by oilman Bud Adams as a charter member of the American Football League (AFL), the Oilers won the first two AFL championships in 1960 and 1961. After the AFL–NFL merger they competed in the National Football League (NFL) as members of the AFC Central division. The franchise relocated to Tennessee in 1997 and is now the Tennessee Titans.

Early success and the Luv Ya Blue era

Led by quarterback George Blanda, the early Oilers starred in the AFL's first championship games. In the late 1970s, coach Bum Phillips, running back Earl Campbell and quarterback Dan Pastorini inspired the beloved "Luv Ya Blue" teams, which twice reached the AFC Championship Game, losing both times to the Pittsburgh Steelers.

Run-and-shoot years and The Comeback

With Warren Moon at quarterback, the run-and-shoot Oilers of the late 1980s and early 1990s were prolific but heartbreak-prone: on 3 January 1993 they surrendered a 35–3 lead to the Buffalo Bills in a wild card playoff game, losing 41–38 in overtime — the largest comeback in NFL history at the time, remembered simply as "The Comeback".

Relocation

After failing to secure a new stadium in Houston, Adams moved the team to Tennessee in 1997, where it played two seasons as the Tennessee Oilers before becoming the Titans in 1999. Houston returned to the NFL in 2002 with the expansion Texans. The Titans organization retains the Oilers' history and records, and has periodically worn Oilers throwback uniforms.
