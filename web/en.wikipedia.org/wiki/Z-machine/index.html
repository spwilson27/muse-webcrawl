Z-machine

LocationSandia National Laboratories, Albuquerque, New Mexico, USA
TypePulsed power / z-pinch facility
Peak current~26–27 million amperes
Peak power~290 terawatts
PurposeFusion research, materials science, stockpile stewardship

The Z-machine — formally the Z Pulsed Power Facility — is the world's largest high-frequency electromagnetic wave generator and the largest X-ray generator in the world, located at Sandia National Laboratories in Albuquerque, New Mexico, United States. It is designed to test materials in conditions of extreme temperature and pressure, principally for inertial confinement fusion research and the stewardship of the US nuclear weapons stockpile without underground testing.

Operation

The machine stores energy in banks of Marx generators and discharges it in a pulse lasting about 100 nanoseconds, delivering some 26–27 million amperes through a small cylindrical array of hundreds of fine tungsten wires. The enormous current vaporises the wires into plasma and generates an immense magnetic field that pinches the plasma inward — a configuration known as a z-pinch. The imploding plasma stagnates on axis, producing an intense burst of X-rays (up to about 290 terawatts peak power) that heats a target hohlraum to temperatures exceeding 2 billion kelvin, among the highest temperatures produced in a laboratory on Earth.

History and research

Originally built as the Particle Beam Fusion Accelerator (PBFA) in the 1980s, the facility was redesigned around the z-pinch approach in the 1990s and renamed Z. In 2003 Z experiments with an iron plasma attracted wide attention by reporting temperatures above 2 billion kelvin, hotter than the interiors of most stars. A major refurbishment ("ZR", completed in 2007) increased its current and shot rate. Z is also used to study the equation of state of materials at pressures of millions of atmospheres, radiation effects, and magnetised liner inertial fusion (MagLIF), a hybrid fusion concept developed at Sandia. In a typical year the facility fires on the order of 200 shots.

See also

Related facilities include the National Ignition Facility in California, which pursues laser-driven inertial confinement fusion.
