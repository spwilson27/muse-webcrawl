Equaliser (mathematics)

In category theory, an equaliser is a morphism that generalises the notion of the solution set of an equation between two parallel morphisms. Given two parallel morphisms f, g : X → Y, an equaliser is a morphism e : E → X such that f ∘ e = g ∘ e, and which is universal with this property: any other morphism with the same property factors uniquely through e.

Examples

In the category of sets, the equaliser of two functions f, g : X → Y is the subset {x ∈ X | f(x) = g(x)} together with its inclusion map into X. In the category of groups or modules, the equaliser is the kernel of the difference map f − g. In the category of topological spaces it is the corresponding subspace given the subspace topology.

Properties

Every equaliser is a monomorphism; an equaliser that is also an epimorphism is an isomorphism. A category with all equalisers and finite products is said to have all finite limits. The dual concept is the coequaliser.
