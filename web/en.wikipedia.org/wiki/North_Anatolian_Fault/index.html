North Anatolian Fault

The North Anatolian Fault (NAF; Turkish: Kuzey Anadolu Fay Hattı) is an active right-lateral strike-slip fault in northern Turkey, running about 1,500 km from the Karlıova triple junction in the east to the Gulf of Saros in the Aegean Sea. It accommodates the westward motion of the Anatolian Plate away from the Arabia–Eurasia collision zone.

North Anatolian Fault
Length: ~1,500 km
Type: Right-lateral strike-slip
Slip rate: ~20 mm/yr
Notable quakes: 1939 Erzincan; 1999 İzmit

Earthquake sequence

During the 20th century the fault ruptured in a famous westward-migrating sequence of large earthquakes, beginning with the devastating 1939 Erzincan earthquake (M ~7.8) and continuing through 1942, 1943, 1944, 1957, 1967, and finally the 1999 İzmit (M 7.6) and Düzce events near Istanbul. This pattern of sequential stress transfer is a classic case study in earthquake science.

Risk

The segment beneath the Sea of Marmara, offshore Istanbul, has not ruptured in a major event since 1766 and is regarded as a seismic gap posing significant risk to the Istanbul metropolitan area.
