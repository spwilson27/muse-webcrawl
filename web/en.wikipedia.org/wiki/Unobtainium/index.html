Unobtainium

Unobtainium, the mythical perfect material

Unobtainium is a term used in fiction, engineering, and science fiction to refer to a material that is extremely rare, costly, or physically impossible to obtain, yet which would be ideally suited to some practical purpose. The word is a joking portmanteau of "unobtainable" and the suffix "-ium", commonly used for chemical elements.

History

The term has been in informal use since at least the 1950s, when it was reportedly used by engineers at Lockheed's Skunk Works and in aerospace circles to describe materials with impossible or contradictory properties, such as being simultaneously very light and very strong. During the Cold War it was occasionally applied tongue-in-cheek to materials required for advanced aircraft and spacecraft designs.

Use in fiction

Unobtainium appears in numerous works as a MacGuffin or plot device. A prominent example is the 2009 film Avatar, in which humans mine a room-temperature superconductor called unobtanium on the moon Pandora. Similar fictional substances, such as "scrith", "adamantium", and "kryptonite", serve comparable narrative roles. The concept allows writers to hand-wave the physical limitations that would otherwise prevent a story's technology from working.
