Thermoplastic elastomer

Thermoplastic elastomer
Abbreviation: TPE, TPR
Class: Copolymers / polymer blends

Thermoplastic elastomers (TPE), sometimes referred to as thermoplastic rubbers (TPR), are a class of copolymers or physical mixes of polymers (usually a plastic and a rubber) that consist of materials with both thermoplastic and elastomeric properties. Unlike vulcanised rubbers, they can be melted, moulded, and recycled like ordinary thermoplastics while behaving like rubber in service.

Types

The main commercial families are styrenic block copolymers (TPE-S, e.g. SBS and SEBS), thermoplastic polyolefin blends (TPE-O or TPO), thermoplastic vulcanisates (TPV), thermoplastic polyurethanes (TPU), copolyesters (TPE-E), and polyamide block copolymers (TPE-A).

Properties and uses

TPEs are soft, flexible, and resilient, with good resistance to abrasion and weathering depending on type. They are used for soft-touch grips, seals and gaskets, footwear soles, cable insulation, medical tubing, and automotive components. Because they need no vulcanisation, processing by injection moulding or extrusion is fast and scrap can be reprocessed.

History

Thermoplastic polyurethanes were among the first to be commercialised in the late 1950s, followed by styrenic block copolymers in the 1960s and polyolefin blends and vulcanisates in the 1970s and 1980s.
