Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind (Japanese: 風の谷のナウシカ, Hepburn: Kaze no Tani no Naushika) is a 1984 Japanese animated post-apocalyptic fantasy film written and directed by Hayao Miyazaki, based on his 1982 manga of the same name. It was produced by Topcraft and distributed by Toei Company. Joe Hisaishi, in his first collaboration with Miyazaki, composed the score. The film stars the voices of Sumi Shimamoto, Gorō Naya, Yōji Matsuda, Yoshiko Sakakibara and Iemasa Kayumi.

Set in a post-apocalyptic world a thousand years after a catastrophic war known as the "Seven Days of Fire", the film tells the story of Nausicaä, the young princess of the Valley of the Wind, who becomes embroiled in a struggle between kingdoms competing for control of a dormant bioweapon while the toxic jungle known as the Sea of Corruption spreads across the land.

Plot

A thousand years after the "Seven Days of Fire", a cataclysmic war that destroyed human civilization and most of the Earth's ecosystems, scattered human settlements survive on the fringes of the Sea of Corruption, a vast toxic jungle populated by giant mutant insects, among them the enormous trilobite-like Ohmu. The Valley of the Wind is a small kingdom protected from the jungle's spores by ocean winds.

When a Tolmekian airship crashes in the valley carrying the embryo of a Giant Warrior, one of the bioweapons that caused the Seven Days of Fire, the valley is drawn into the conflict between Tolmekia and Pejite. Princess Nausicaä, who can communicate with the insects of the jungle, sets out to understand the true nature of the Sea of Corruption and to stop the fighting before the Giant Warrior is revived. In the climax, Nausicaä sacrifices herself to calm a stampeding herd of Ohmu and is resurrected by them, fulfilling an ancient prophecy of a saviour who would reunite humanity with the lost world.

Production

Miyazaki began the Nausicaä manga in 1982 for the magazine Animage, published by Tokuma Shoten. The film adaptation followed after the manga proved popular. The film was animated by Topcraft, whose core staff later formed the nucleus of Studio Ghibli, founded in 1985. Miyazaki adapted the first sixteen chapters of the manga, though the story continued in print until 1994 and its ending differs substantially from the film's.

Reception and legacy

The film was a critical and commercial success in Japan and is frequently ranked among the greatest animated films ever made. It is credited with helping to establish the anime fandom in Japan and abroad, and its environmental themes — humanity's relationship with nature, and the futility of war — became hallmarks of Miyazaki's later work. An early, heavily edited English-language version released in the United States in 1985 as Warriors of the Wind cut roughly twenty-two minutes of footage; Miyazaki was so displeased that Studio Ghibli later insisted on a strict "no cuts" policy for foreign releases. A faithful English dub, featuring the voices of Alison Lohman, Patrick Stewart, Shia LaBeouf and Uma Thurman, was released by Disney in 2005.
