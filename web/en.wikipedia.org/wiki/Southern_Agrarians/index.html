Southern Agrarians

The Southern Agrarians were a group of twelve American writers and academics, mostly associated with Vanderbilt University in Nashville, Tennessee, who published the manifesto I'll Take My Stand in 1930. The "Twelve Southerners" defended the agrarian traditions of the American South against industrial capitalism and what they saw as the dehumanising effects of modern industrial life. Members included John Crowe Ransom, Allen Tate, Robert Penn Warren, and Donald Davidson; several had earlier been part of the Fugitive poetry group.

I'll Take My Stand

The symposium's essays praised subsistence farming, localism, religion, and the "Southern tradition" as a humane alternative to the industrial North. The book attracted wide attention during the Great Depression but was also criticised, both then and later, for romanticising the South and largely evading the central role of slavery and racial injustice in the society it defended; the youngest contributor, Robert Penn Warren, would later repudiate aspects of the project in his civil-rights-era writings.

Legacy

The Agrarians influenced the New Criticism in literary studies (Ransom, Tate, Warren, and Cleanth Brooks reshaped how literature was taught) and continue to be cited by communitarian, localist, and conservative critics of industrial modernity.
