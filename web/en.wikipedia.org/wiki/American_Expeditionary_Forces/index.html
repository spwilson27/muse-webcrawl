The American Expeditionary Forces (AEF) were the United States armed forces sent to Europe during World War I. Commanded by General John J. Pershing, the AEF fought alongside British, French, and other Allied forces on the Western Front from 1917 until the Armistice of 11 November 1918.

Build-up

The United States declared war on Germany in April 1917 with a tiny regular army; the AEF was built through conscription under the Selective Service Act, a vast shipping effort, and an enormous logistical system of ports, railways, and depots in France. By the summer of 1918 American troops were arriving at a rate of some 10,000 per day.

Combat

Pershing insisted the AEF fight as an independent army rather than be amalgamated into Allied units. AEF divisions fought at Cantigny, Belleau Wood, Château-Thierry, and in the Second Battle of the Marne in 1918, then undertook the reduction of the Saint-Mihiel salient and the Meuse-Argonne Offensive — the largest battle in American military history to that date, involving over a million U.S. soldiers. About 116,000 American servicemen died in the war, many from the influenza pandemic.

Aftermath

After the Armistice, part of the AEF formed the Army of Occupation in the Rhineland. The headquarters was dissolved in 1920. A separate American force also served in North Russia and Siberia.
