Suharto

Suharto (8 June 1921 – 27 January 2008) was an Indonesian army officer and politician who served as the second and longest-serving president of Indonesia, ruling from 1967 to 1998. His authoritarian "New Order" regime pursued economic development and political stability, but was marked by corruption, nepotism and repression.

Rise to power

A career soldier who fought in the Indonesian National Revolution, Suharto manoeuvred to power after the abortive 30 September Movement of 1965, which was followed by mass killings of alleged communists in which hundreds of thousands died. He progressively sidelined President Sukarno and assumed the presidency in 1968.

New Order

Suharto's New Order stabilised the economy with Western-backed technocrats, achieving decades of growth and poverty reduction, while the military's dual function entrenched its political role. His government invaded and occupied East Timor in 1975. The Asian financial crisis of 1997–98 triggered riots and mass protests that forced his resignation on 21 May 1998. He died in Jakarta in 2008 without standing trial, having faced corruption charges that were dropped on health grounds.
