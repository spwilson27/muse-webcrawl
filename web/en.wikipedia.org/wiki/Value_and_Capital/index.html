Value and Capital

Value and Capital, subtitled An Inquiry into Some Fundamental Principles of Economic Theory, is a book by the British economist John Richard Hicks, published in 1939. It is regarded as one of the most influential works of twentieth-century microeconomic theory, systematising the ordinalist revolution in demand theory and the theory of general equilibrium.

Contents

Part I restates consumer theory using indifference curves and ordinal utility instead of measurable utility. Hicks (developing work of R. G. D. Allen) decomposed the effect of a price change into the substitution effect and the income effect — the Slutsky equation in its "Hicksian" form — and introduced the concepts of Hicksian (compensated) and Marshallian demand.

Part II treats general equilibrium and stability, developing the analysis of expectations and intertemporal equilibrium that Hicks credited in part to the Swedish school. Parts III and IV apply the framework to capital, interest, and trade cycles. The book helped bring Walrasian general equilibrium and Pareto's ideas into mainstream English-language economics, and it laid groundwork later used by Samuelson's Foundations of Economic Analysis.
