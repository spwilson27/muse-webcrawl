Rafael del Riego

Rafael del Riego (1784–1823), Spanish liberal officer.

Rafael del Riego y Flórez (7 April 1784 – 7 November 1823) was a Spanish general and liberal politician who led the 1820 military uprising (pronunciamiento) that forced King Ferdinand VII to restore the liberal Constitution of 1812, inaugurating the Trienio Liberal.

Career

Born in Tuña, Asturias, Riego fought in the Peninsular War and was imprisoned in France. After the war he conspired against Ferdinand VII's absolutist restoration. On 1 January 1820, at Cabezas de San Juan near Cádiz, he proclaimed the Constitution of 1812 among troops assembled for an expedition to suppress the Spanish American wars of independence. The revolt spread, and in March 1820 Ferdinand swore allegiance to the constitution.

Downfall and death

During the Trienio Liberal, Riego became a leading figure of the radical liberal (exaltado) faction and served as president of the Cortes in 1822. When a French army — the Hundred Thousand Sons of Saint Louis — invaded in 1823 to restore absolutism, Riego was captured, tried for treason, and hanged in Madrid on 7 November 1823.

Legacy

The Himno de Riego, a military march associated with his name, became the anthem of Spanish liberalism and the official anthem of the Second Spanish Republic. See also Trienio Liberal and Spanish Constitution of 1812.
