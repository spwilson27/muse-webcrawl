Garage rock

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Garage rock (sometimes called garage punk or '60s punk) is a raw and energetic style of rock music that flourished in the United States and Canada between 1963 and 1968. Inspired by British Invasion bands such as the Beatles, the Rolling Stones and the Kinks, thousands of amateur bands — often literally rehearsing in suburban garages — recorded energetic songs with basic chord structures, distorted guitars, organs and snarling vocals.

Characteristics and history

Regional hits such as "Louie Louie" by the Kingsmen (1963) defined the style. Other notable garage acts included the Sonics, the Seeds, the Standells, the Count Five ("Psychotic Reaction"), Question Mark and the Mysterians ("96 Tears"), and the 13th Floor Elevators, pioneers of psychedelic rock. Most bands released only a single or two on local labels and vanished, but their records became prized by collectors.

The 1972 compilation Nuggets, assembled by Lenny Kaye, revived interest in the genre and helped inspire 1970s punk rock, whose musicians admired garage rock's simplicity and aggression. A garage revival followed in the 1980s, and the style resurfaced again in the 2000s with bands such as the Strokes, the White Stripes, the Black Keys and Thee Oh Sees.
