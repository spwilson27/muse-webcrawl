Night Tube

The Night Tube is a service pattern on the London Underground and London Overground providing all-night through-the-night services on Friday and Saturday nights. It was launched on 19 August 2016 on the Central and Victoria lines, later extending to the Jubilee, Northern and Piccadilly lines and to parts of the Overground's East London line.

History

All-night Tube services were first proposed in 2013 as part of a package that also included the controversial closure of ticket offices. The launch, originally planned for September 2015, was delayed by industrial disputes with rail unions over pay and working conditions for night staff. Following an agreement, Night Tube began on the Central and Victoria lines in August 2016, with the Jubilee line following in October, the Northern line in November and the Piccadilly line in December 2016. Night Overground services on the East London line began in December 2017.

Service pattern

Trains typically run every 10 to 20 minutes through the night. The Northern line service operates on the Charing Cross branch and between Edgware and Morden via Bank on alternate patterns; the Piccadilly line service does not serve Heathrow Terminal 4 or the branch between Acton Town and Uxbridge. Standard fares apply, and daily fare capping treats journeys after 04:30 as part of the new day.

Impact

Transport for London estimated the service would support thousands of jobs and add hundreds of millions of pounds to London's night-time economy over its first decade. Surveys showed strong use by shift workers in hospitality, health care and security as well as leisure passengers. Night Tube was suspended in March 2020 during the COVID-19 pandemic; services resumed progressively from November 2021, beginning with the Central and Victoria lines.
