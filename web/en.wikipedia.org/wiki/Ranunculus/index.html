Ranunculus

FamilyRanunculaceae
Common namesButtercups, spearworts, water crowfoots
SpeciesAbout 1,700–1,800
DistributionWorldwide

Ranunculus is a large genus of flowering plants in the family Ranunculaceae, commonly known as buttercups. With roughly 1,700 to 1,800 species, it is the largest genus in the family and is distributed worldwide, in habitats ranging from meadows and woodlands to wetlands and alpine zones; the water crowfoots are fully aquatic.

Description

Most species are herbaceous perennials with five-petalled flowers, typically glossy yellow (some white, orange or red). The glossiness of buttercup petals results from a special epidermal layer that reflects light and helps attract pollinators. The name derives from Latin rana ("frog") with the diminutive -unculus, "little frog", referring to the wet habitats of many species.

Toxicity

Buttercups are poisonous when fresh because they contain ranunculin, which breaks down into the blistering agent protoanemonin; they are a hazard to grazing livestock, although drying (as in hay) destroys the toxin. Familiar species include the meadow buttercup (R. acris), creeping buttercup (R. repens) and bulbous buttercup (R. bulbosus).
