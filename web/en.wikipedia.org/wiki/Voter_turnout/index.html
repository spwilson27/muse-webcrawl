Voter turnout

Voter turnout is the percentage of eligible voters who cast a ballot in an election. High turnout is generally seen as a sign of democratic legitimacy, though its interpretation is debated.

Factors

Turnout tends to be higher with compulsory voting (as in Australia and Belgium), proportional representation, easy registration, weekend or holiday voting, and competitive, salient elections. It tends to be lower in second-order elections, among younger voters, and where registration is burdensome.

Trends

Many established democracies saw turnout decline from the mid-20th century, with notable recent rebounds in some countries. US presidential elections typically draw around 60% of eligible voters, midterms substantially less.
