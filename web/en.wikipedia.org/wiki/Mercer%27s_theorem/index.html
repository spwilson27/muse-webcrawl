Mercer's theorem is a result in functional analysis, proved by James Mercer in 1909, stating that a continuous symmetric positive-definite kernel on a compact interval can be represented as a uniformly convergent series of products of its eigenfunctions.

Statement

If K is a continuous symmetric kernel such that the associated integral operator is positive (that is, all its eigenvalues are non-negative), then K(x, y) equals the sum over all eigenfunctions φi of λi φi(x) φi(y), the series converging absolutely and uniformly.

Applications

Mercer's theorem underlies the theory of reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces and justifies the "kernel trick" in machine learning: positive-definite kernels correspond to inner products in some feature space, enabling support vector machines and kernel methods to operate in high-dimensional spaces implicitly.
