The Bourtzi (Greek: Μπούρτζι, from Ottoman Turkish burç, "tower") is a small Venetian water castle on an islet in the harbour of Nafplio, in the Argolic Gulf, Greece. Along with the Palamidi fortress and the Acronauplia, it forms the trio of fortifications that defended the town, one of the principal ports of the Venetian and later Ottoman Peloponnese.

History

The fortress was built in 1473 by the Venetians, to designs by the architect Antonio Gambello, on the islet of Agioi Theodoroi just off the harbour entrance. It guarded the approach to the port, which could be closed at night by a chain stretched between the Bourtzi and the shore bastion. The Venetians called it the Castello dello Soglio. The castle was renovated under the second Venetian occupation and again in the early 19th century.

During the Greek War of Independence the Bourtzi changed hands several times and served as a refuge for the Greek government. From 1830 to 1865 it was the residence of the town executioner — a posting so shunned that the location offshore was considered appropriate — and after 1865 it stood empty until it was converted into a luxury hotel in the 1930s, operating until around 1970. Today the Bourtzi is a protected monument and a symbol of Nafplio, reachable by boat from the harbour, and used for cultural events and music festivals in summer.

Architecture

The fortress is a low, elongated hexagonal tower-fort closely fitted to the shape of the islet, with a central tower, gun platforms at sea level and crenellated walls. Its three levels were connected by internal staircases, and its cisterns collected rainwater for the garrison.
