Memorabilia

Collected memorabilia on display

Memorabilia are objects kept or collected because of their historical interest, especially those associated with memorable people or events. The word derives from the Latin memorabilis, meaning "memorable". Memorabilia are distinct from ordinary collectables in that their value comes chiefly from their connection to a person, event, or era rather than from their intrinsic properties.

Categories

Popular categories of memorabilia include sports memorabilia (signed balls, jerseys, trading cards, and game-used equipment), music memorabilia (instruments, costumes, autographs, and concert posters), film and television props and costumes, political and historical documents, and space and aviation artefacts. Military memorabilia and militaria also form a significant collecting field.

Collecting and value

The value of a piece of memorabilia depends on rarity, condition, provenance, and the fame of the person or event associated with it. Authentication is central to the market: certificates of authenticity and expert verification help protect buyers from the large number of forged autographs and replicated artefacts in circulation. Record prices have been achieved at auction for items such as game-worn jerseys, original film props, and documents signed by historical figures.

Preservation

Museums and archives preserve memorabilia as part of cultural heritage, while private collectors often display items in frames with ultraviolet-filtering glass and climate-controlled storage to prevent fading and deterioration. Institutions such as the Smithsonian and national sports halls of fame hold major memorabilia collections.
