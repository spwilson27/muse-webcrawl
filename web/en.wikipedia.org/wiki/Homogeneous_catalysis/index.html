Homogeneous catalysis

In chemistry, homogeneous catalysis is catalysis in which the catalyst is in the same phase as the reactants, typically dissolved in the same liquid solution. It is contrasted with heterogeneous catalysis, where the catalyst is in a different phase (usually a solid acting on gases or liquids). Homogeneous catalysis encompasses acid–base catalysis in solution, catalysis by soluble transition-metal complexes — a major field of organometallic chemistry — and enzymatic catalysis.

Transition-metal catalysis

Soluble metal complexes allow fine control of reactivity and selectivity through the choice of metal and ligands, and their mechanisms can be followed spectroscopically. Landmark industrial processes include hydroformylation (the oxo process, using cobalt or rhodium catalysts to convert alkenes to aldehydes); the Monsanto and Cativa processes, in which rhodium or iridium complexes catalyse the carbonylation of methanol to acetic acid; the Wacker process for oxidising ethylene to acetaldehyde with palladium chloride; and Ziegler–Natta and single-site catalysts for olefin polymerisation. In synthesis, homogeneous catalysts underpin hydrogenation (for example Wilkinson's catalyst), cross-coupling reactions (Suzuki, Heck, Negishi), olefin metathesis and asymmetric catalysis, recognised by the Nobel Prizes in Chemistry in 2001 (asymmetric catalysis) and 2005 (metathesis), among others.

Advantages and disadvantages

Because every catalyst molecule is accessible and the reaction environment is uniform, homogeneous catalysts often show high activity and selectivity under mild conditions, and are easier to study and tune than surfaces. Their main drawbacks are practical: separating a dissolved catalyst from the products can be difficult and costly, thermal stability may be limited, and catalyst recycling is frequently required for economic and environmental reasons. Immobilising molecular catalysts on supports ("heterogenisation") and biphasic systems such as the aqueous biphasic Ruhrchemie/Rhône-Poulenc hydroformylation process are strategies to combine homogeneous activity with easy separation.
