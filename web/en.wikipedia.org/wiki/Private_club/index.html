Private club

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A private club is an organisation whose premises and facilities are restricted to members and their guests, rather than open to the general public. Private clubs take many forms, including social clubs, country clubs centred on golf or tennis, dining clubs, university clubs, working men's clubs, and exclusive metropolitan gentlemen's clubs. Membership is usually by application, nomination, or election, and is paid for through initiation fees and periodic dues.

Types

  • Social clubs — organisations formed around fellowship, dining, and shared interests.
  • Country clubs — typically feature golf courses, tennis, swimming, and dining facilities.
  • Gentlemen's clubs — a tradition originating in eighteenth-century London coffee houses, combining dining, libraries, and accommodation.
  • University and alumni clubs — serve graduates of particular institutions.
  • Working men's clubs — community social and drinking establishments, historically associated with the labour movement in Britain.

Legal status

Because genuinely private clubs are not "public accommodations", in many jurisdictions they have historically been exempt from certain anti-discrimination laws that apply to businesses open to the public, though this position has been contested in court cases and narrowed by legislation in several countries. Courts generally examine how selective the membership process is, whether the club is genuinely private or effectively operates as a business, and the degree of public involvement.
