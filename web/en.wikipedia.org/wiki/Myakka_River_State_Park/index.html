Myakka River State Park

Myakka River State Park is a Florida State Park located nine miles east of Sarasota, off State Road 72, on the Gulf Coast of Florida. At about 37,000 acres (58 square miles), it is one of the state's oldest and largest state parks, established in 1942. The park protects the Myakka River and its floodplain, dry prairies, pine flatwoods, wetlands and hammocks, and is well known for its large population of American alligators and other wildlife, including wading birds, wild turkeys, deer, bobcats and the Florida scrub-jay.

Features

The Myakka River flows through 12 miles of the park, widening into Upper Myakka Lake and Lower Myakka Lake. A popular attraction is the canopy walkway, completed in 2000 — a suspension bridge through the oak hammock canopy reaching a 74-foot tower with views over the surrounding prairie. Visitors can take boat tours aboard the park's large airboats, ride a safari tram, fish, canoe and kayak, and hike more than 39 miles of trails; the park also has campsites and historic log cabins built by the Civilian Conservation Corps in the 1930s, before the park was formally opened.

History and ecology

Much of the land was purchased from the estate of Bertha Palmer, a prominent Sarasota landowner, and development was carried out by the Civilian Conservation Corps. The park's dry prairie is among the largest remaining fragments of a globally imperilled ecosystem, maintained by prescribed burning, and the wetlands include the Deep Hole, a sinkhole in Lower Myakka Lake famous for congregations of alligators in the dry season.
