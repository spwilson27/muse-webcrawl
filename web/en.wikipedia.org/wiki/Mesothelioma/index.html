Mesothelioma

SymptomsShortness of breath, chest pain, cough, fatigue, weight loss
CausesAsbestos exposure (major risk factor)

TypesPleural, peritoneal, pericardial, testicular

Mesothelioma is a type of cancer that develops from the thin layer of tissue that covers many of the internal organs (known as the mesothelium). The most common area affected is the lining of the lungs and chest wall (pleural mesothelioma); less commonly the lining of the abdomen (peritoneal mesothelioma), and rarely the sac surrounding the heart or the testes may be affected. Signs and symptoms may include shortness of breath due to fluid around the lung, a swollen abdomen, chest wall pain, cough, feeling tired, and weight loss.

Cause

The primary risk factor is exposure to asbestos, a group of fibrous silicate minerals once widely used in insulation, shipbuilding, construction, and brake linings. Symptoms may not appear until 20 to 50 years after exposure. Other risk factors include genetics and infection with the simian virus 40 (SV40). The causal link between asbestos and mesothelioma was established in landmark studies in the 1960s, notably among crocidolite miners in South Africa's Northern Cape.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis may be suspected from chest X-ray or CT imaging showing pleural thickening and effusion, and is confirmed by examining fluid from around the lung and by tissue biopsy, which typically requires immunohistochemical staining to distinguish mesothelioma from other cancers. Histological subtypes include epithelioid, sarcomatoid, and biphasic, of which epithelioid carries the best prognosis.

Treatment options may include surgery (such as extrapleural pneumonectomy or pleurectomy/decortication), chemotherapy (commonly pemetrexed with cisplatin), radiation therapy, and immunotherapy (such as nivolumab with ipilimumab). Despite treatment, prognosis is generally poor, which has made mesothelioma a major focus of occupational-health regulation and of asbestos litigation in many countries.
