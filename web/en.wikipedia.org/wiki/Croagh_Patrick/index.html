Croagh Patrick

Elevation: 764 m (2,507 ft)

Location: County Mayo, Ireland

Irish: Cruach Phádraig, "Patrick's stack"

Nearest town: Westport

Croagh Patrick (Irish: Cruach Phádraig, meaning "Patrick's stack"), nicknamed "the Reek", is a mountain 764 metres (2,507 ft) high in County Mayo in western Ireland. It rises above Clew Bay near the town of Westport and is considered the holiest mountain in Ireland, an important site of pilgrimage since pre-Christian times.

Pilgrimage

According to tradition, Saint Patrick fasted on the summit for forty days in the fifth century AD, and the mountain became associated with his mission to Ireland. On Reek Sunday — the last Sunday in July — tens of thousands of pilgrims climb the mountain, many traditionally doing so barefoot as an act of penance. Mass is celebrated in a small chapel on the summit, built in 1905. Pilgrims also climb on Good Friday and on 15 August, the feast of the Assumption.

The pilgrimage has roots in the pre-Christian festival of Lughnasa, held at the beginning of the harvest season, and Croagh Patrick is one of several Irish mountains where Lughnasa assemblies were held. Archaeological evidence around the mountain, including the Boheh Stone with its rock art and a possible stone alignment, suggests ritual significance extending back to the Bronze Age.

Geology

The mountain's distinctive conical shape is formed of quartzite, a metamorphosed sandstone, which forms a hard cap over weaker schists. Glacial action during the last ice age sculpted the peak and deposited the scree slopes that make the final ascent steep and difficult.
