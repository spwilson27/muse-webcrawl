Professional mourning

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Professional mourning is the practice of hiring people — historically usually women — to grieve at funerals, wailing, lamenting and praising the deceased in order to honour the dead, express the family's grief and demonstrate its status. Such mourners have existed in many cultures since antiquity.

History

Professional mourners are depicted in ancient Egyptian tomb art and mentioned in the Bible and in classical Greek and Roman sources, where Roman praeficae led funeral lamentations; Roman sumptuary laws at times restricted their numbers. In China, hired mourners and funeral bands have a long history and persist in some regions as performance professionals. Ireland and Scotland had keening women who improvised laments (caoineadh) over the corpse — a tradition that survived into the 20th century. Comparable practices include Sardinian attitadoras, Mediterranean and Middle Eastern wailing women, and Ghanaian funeral specialists.

Modern practice

The practice declined in most of Europe as funeral customs became more restrained, but it persists in parts of Asia, Africa and the Middle East. In the United Kingdom, a much-reported modern service ("Rent a Mourner", founded in 2013) supplied discreet attendees to boost funeral congregations — a light-hearted echo of the older tradition.

In culture

Hired mourners appear across literature and film as symbols of performed grief, prompting reflection on the sincerity and social theatre of mourning rituals.
