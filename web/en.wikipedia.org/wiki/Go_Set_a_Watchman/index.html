Go Set a Watchman is a novel by Harper Lee that was published on July 14, 2015, by HarperCollins in the United States and William Heinemann in the United Kingdom. It was written before Lee's first and previously only published novel, the Pulitzer Prize-winning To Kill a Mockingbird (1960). The title comes from Isaiah 21:6: "For thus hath the Lord said unto me, Go, set a watchman, let him declare what he seeth."

Although initially promoted as a sequel, reviewers noted that Go Set a Watchman is actually the first draft of To Kill a Mockingbird, with several passages in the earlier-crafted work appearing nearly identically in the later famous novel, and key scenes from the draft appearing in the published version in a substantively altered form.

Plot

The novel follows an adult Jean Louise "Scout" Finch, now 26, as she returns to her hometown of Maycomb, Alabama, from New York City, where she is living. Her father, Atticus Finch, is 72 and suffering from rheumatoid arthritis. During the visit, Jean Louise discovers that Atticus has associated himself with a segregationist citizens' council, shattering her idealized image of him and forcing her to grapple with the racism of the town she grew up in during the tense years of the civil rights movement.

Publication controversy

The book's discovery, announced in February 2015, was widely reported, as was skepticism as to whether the elderly and reclusive Lee had genuinely consented to its publication. The Alabama Department of Human Resources investigated and found no evidence of elder abuse. The novel became one of the most pre-ordered books in history and was the best-selling book of 2015 in the United States, though critical reception was mixed, with much discussion centering on the altered portrayal of Atticus Finch.
