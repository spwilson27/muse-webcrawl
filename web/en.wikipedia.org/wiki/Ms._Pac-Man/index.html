Overview

Ms. Pac-Man is a 1982 maze arcade video game developed by General Computer Corporation (GCC) and published by Midway in North America. It is the first sequel to Pac-Man (1980) and introduced a female protagonist — Ms. Pac-Man, distinguished by a red bow, lipstick and a beauty mark — making her one of the first prominent female characters in video games.

Gameplay and origins

The game began as Crazy Otto, an enhancement kit GCC built for Pac-Man; after settling a lawsuit with Atari, GCC offered the project to Midway, the American distributor of Pac-Man, which developed it into an official sequel with Namco's blessing. Gameplay refines the original: four different maze designs and colours instead of one, warp tunnels, ghosts with partly randomised movement that prevents the perfect pattern play of the original, and bonus fruits that bounce through the maze rather than sitting still. Animated intermissions show Ms. Pac-Man and Pac-Man meeting and falling in love.

Reception and legacy

Ms. Pac-Man was an enormous commercial success, selling around 115,000 arcade cabinets in the United States and becoming one of the best-selling American-produced arcade games. Critics then and now often rate it above the original for its variety and improved ghost behaviour. It was ported to virtually every home platform, from the Atari 2600 and 5200 to modern compilations. Rights complications between Bandai Namco and the GCC-derived royalty holder AtGames later led to Ms. Pac-Man being replaced by a new character, "Pac-Mom", in some re-releases.
