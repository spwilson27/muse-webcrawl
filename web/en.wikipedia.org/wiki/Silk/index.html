Silk

Silk
SourceSilkworm (Bombyx mori) cocoons
OriginNeolithic China (sericulture)
Trade routeThe Silk Road
[Image: Shimmering bolts of silk fabric in rich colours at a market stall]

Silk is a natural protein fibre produced by silkworm larvae to form cocoons, woven into a lustrous, strong textile prized for millennia. Sericulture began in Neolithic China — legend credits the empress Leizu — and silk was a state secret and a supreme luxury export.

The Silk Road

Silk gave its name to the network of trade routes linking China with Central Asia, Byzantium and Europe, along which paper, gunpowder, religions and plagues (see the Black Death in the wider crawl) also travelled. Smuggled silkworm eggs let Byzantium and later Italy and France build their own industries.
