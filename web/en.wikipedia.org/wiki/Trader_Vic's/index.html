Trader Vic's

Trader Vic's is a Polynesian-themed restaurant and bar chain founded by Victor Jules Bergeron Jr. (1902–1984), who styled himself "Trader Vic." The first restaurant opened in 1934 in Oakland, California, as Hinky Dink's, and was renamed Trader Vic's in 1937 as Bergeron adopted a South Seas theme of tiki carvings, outrigger canoes, and exotic rum drinks.

The Mai Tai and tiki culture

Bergeron claimed to have invented the Mai Tai in 1944 for friends visiting from Tahiti — a claim rivalled by Donn Beach (Don the Beachcomber), his great competitor in founding tiki culture. Trader Vic's popularised drinks served in ceramic tiki mugs and vessels such as the scorpion bowl, alongside a menu of "Polynesian" and Chinese-oven-roasted dishes adapted for American tastes.

Expansion and legacy

The chain expanded internationally from the 1950s, often inside Hilton hotels — including the long-running London Hilton Park Lane location — and Bergeron's 1947 Bartender's Guide became a standard reference. Although most US locations closed as tiki fashion faded, a small number of restaurants (including Emeryville, California, near the original site, and several overseas) continue to operate, and the brand is frequently credited, with Don the Beachcomber, with launching the mid-century tiki phenomenon.
