Northallerton is a market town and civil parish in North Yorkshire, England. It is the county town of North Yorkshire and was the administrative centre of the former Hambleton district. The town lies in the Vale of Mowbray, between the North York Moors to the east and the Yorkshire Dales to the west.

History

The town grew around a Roman road and a crossing of the Sun Beck. Nearby, in 1138, the Battle of the Standard was fought between English and Scottish forces, ending in a Scottish defeat. Northallerton was granted a market charter in 1200 and its Wednesday and Saturday markets continue. Because of its position on the Great North Road, the town became an important coaching stop with numerous inns.

Transport and economy

Northallerton station stands on the East Coast Main Line, with fast services to London, York, Newcastle and Edinburgh, and trains on the Northallerton–Eaglescliffe line towards Middlesbrough. The town is a local service and administrative centre; County Hall houses North Yorkshire Council. The surrounding Vale of Mowbray is noted for agriculture and the production of Wensleydale cheese nearby.

Landmarks

All Saints' Church dates largely from the 12th to 15th centuries. The town also hosts the Friarage Hospital, founded on the site of a Carmelite friary, and an annual agricultural show. Nearby stands Mount Grace Priory, one of the best-preserved Carthusian monasteries in England.
