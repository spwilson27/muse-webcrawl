Jhansi is a historic city in the Indian state of Uttar Pradesh, in the region of Bundelkhand on the banks of the Pahuj River. It lies roughly 450 kilometres (280 mi) south of New Delhi and serves as the administrative headquarters of Jhansi district and the Jhansi division.

Jhansi is most famous for its association with Rani Lakshmibai, the queen who became a leading figure of the Indian Rebellion of 1857. The massive Jhansi Fort, built in 1613 by Raja Bir Singh Deo of Orchha, dominates the city from a rocky hill.

History

The city grew around the fort constructed in the early 17th century. In the 18th century Jhansi came under Maratha control; the Maratha general Naru Shankar founded the modern town in the 1740s. After the death of Maharaja Gangadhar Rao in 1853, the British East India Company annexed the state under the Doctrine of Lapse, refusing to recognize the adopted heir — a grievance that drove Rani Lakshmibai to join the 1857 rebellion. Jhansi was besieged and captured by British forces under Sir Hugh Rose in April 1858; the Rani escaped and died fighting at Gwalior in June 1858.

Geography and climate

Jhansi is situated on a rocky plateau in the southwestern part of Uttar Pradesh, bordering Madhya Pradesh. It experiences a humid subtropical climate with very hot summers, a monsoon season from July to September, and mild winters.

Transport

Jhansi Junction is one of India's major railway junctions, on the Delhi–Chennai and Delhi–Mumbai main lines. The city is connected by National Highways 27, 39, and 44 (the North–South Corridor).
