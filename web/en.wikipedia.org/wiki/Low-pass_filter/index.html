Low-pass filter

A low-pass filter is a filter that passes signals with a frequency lower than a selected cutoff frequency and attenuates signals with frequencies higher than the cutoff frequency. The exact frequency response of the filter depends on the filter design. The filter is sometimes called a high-cut filter, or treble-cut filter in audio applications. A low-pass filter is the complement of a high-pass filter.

Applications

Low-pass filters exist in many forms, including electronic circuits (such as a hiss filter used in audio), anti-aliasing filters for converting analog signals to digital, digital signal processing, acoustic barriers, blurring of images, and so on. In audio, low-pass filters are used in subwoofer crossovers and in the tone controls of amplifiers. In radio, they suppress harmonics of transmissions. The moving-average filter used in financial analysis is a particular kind of low-pass finite impulse response filter.

Electronic realization

The simplest passive low-pass filter is the RC circuit: a resistor in series with the load and a capacitor in parallel with it. Its cutoff frequency (in hertz) is fc = 1 / (2πRC), and its phase shift and 20 dB-per-decade roll-off above cutoff make it a first-order filter. Higher-order filters with steeper roll-off can be built with inductors (RLC circuits) or active components such as operational amplifiers, following designs such as Butterworth, Chebyshev and Bessel responses.

Digital filters

In digital signal processing, low-pass filters are implemented with finite impulse response (FIR) or infinite impulse response (IIR) structures, and are fundamental to decimation, smoothing and noise reduction.
