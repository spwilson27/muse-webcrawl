Butch Cassidy and the Sundance Kid

Butch Cassidy and the Sundance Kid is a 1969 American Western film directed by George Roy Hill and written by William Goldman. It stars Paul Newman as Butch Cassidy and Robert Redford as the Sundance Kid, with Katharine Ross as Etta Place. Loosely based on the lives of the outlaws Robert LeRoy Parker (Butch Cassidy) and Harry Longabaugh (the Sundance Kid), the film follows the pair as they flee the United States for Bolivia to escape a relentless posse.

Plot

Butch Cassidy leads the Hole-in-the-Wall Gang; his closest partner is the laconic gunman Harry "Sundance" Longabaugh. After a train robbery goes wrong — Butch uses too much dynamite and blows up the baggage car — the railroad hires a relentless superposse to hunt them. Worn down by the pursuit (immortalised in the recurring line "Who are those guys?"), Butch and Sundance, accompanied by Sundance's lover Etta Place, flee to Bolivia, where they resume robbing banks despite barely speaking Spanish. Eventually cornered by Bolivian soldiers in the village of San Vicente, the wounded pair charge out in a blaze of gunfire, and the film freezes on their image as the sound of the volley rolls.

Production and music

Goldman's screenplay, sold for a then-record sum, is noted for its witty, anachronistic banter. The film features the famous scene of the pair riding a bicycle to Burt Bacharach and Hal David's "Raindrops Keep Fallin' on My Head", sung by B. J. Thomas, which won the Academy Award for Best Original Song. Conrad Hall's cinematography also won an Oscar; the film received seven nominations and won four Academy Awards in total, including Best Original Screenplay.

Reception and legacy

The film was the top-grossing release of 1969 and one of the most successful Westerns ever made. It cemented Newman and Redford as a screen duo (they reunited in The Sting, 1973) and helped inspire the name of Redford's Sundance Institute and Film Festival. In 2003 it was selected for preservation in the US National Film Registry, and the American Film Institute ranked it among the greatest American films.
