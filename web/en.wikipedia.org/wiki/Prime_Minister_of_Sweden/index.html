Prime Minister of Sweden

Prime Minister of Sweden (statsminister)
Residence: Sager House, Stockholm
Appointer: Riksdag (on nomination by the Speaker)
Formation: 20 March 1876

The Prime Minister of Sweden (Swedish: statsminister, literally "Minister of State") is the head of government of the Kingdom of Sweden. The prime minister and the cabinet, collectively the Government, exercise executive authority and are accountable to the Riksdag.

Appointment

After an election, the Speaker of the Riksdag consults party leaders and proposes a candidate for prime minister. The candidate is approved unless an absolute majority of the Riksdag votes against — a system of "negative parliamentarism" in which a candidate need only be tolerated, not actively supported. The prime minister then appoints the other ministers.

History

The office was created in 1876, when Louis Gerhard De Geer became the first prime minister. Before that, executive power had been headed by the monarch. Sweden's modern constitutional framework, the 1974 Instrument of Government, formalised the prime minister's central role and removed the monarch from government formation entirely.

Residence and office

The prime minister's official residence is Sager House (Sagerska huset) in central Stockholm, and the government offices are at Rosenbad. Harpsund, a country estate, serves as a retreat analogous to Chequers or Camp David.
