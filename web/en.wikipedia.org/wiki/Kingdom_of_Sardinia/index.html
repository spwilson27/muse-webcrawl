Kingdom of Sardinia

Kingdom of Sardinia Capital: Turin (de facto), Cagliari (de jure)
Established: 1720 (Treaty of The Hague)
Dissolved: 1861 (proclamation of the Kingdom of Italy)
Ruling house: House of Savoy
Languages: Italian, French, Sardinian, Piedmontese

The Kingdom of Sardinia was a state in Southern Europe from the early 18th century until the Italian unification in 1861. Ruled by the House of Savoy from its mainland capital at Turin, it comprised the island of Sardinia together with the mainland territories of Piedmont, Savoy, Nice and Liguria. Because its political and economic centre lay in Piedmont, the state is often called Piedmont-Sardinia, and it became the nucleus around which the Kingdom of Italy was formed.

History

The kingdom was created in 1720 when Victor Amadeus II of Savoy exchanged Sicily for Sardinia under the terms of the Treaty of The Hague, which ended the War of the Quadruple Alliance. Although Sardinia was the poorer and more peripheral part of the realm, its royal title gave the Savoy dukes the rank of kings they had long sought.

During the Napoleonic Wars the mainland territories were annexed by France, and King Victor Emmanuel I ruled only the island from Cagliari. After the Congress of Vienna in 1815 the kingdom regained its mainland possessions and acquired the former Republic of Genoa, giving it an important port.

Under Charles Albert the kingdom granted a constitution, the Statuto Albertino of 1848, which was retained after unification and served as the fundamental law of Italy until 1948. In the First Italian War of Independence (1848–49) Sardinian forces were defeated by Austria at Custoza and Novara, and Charles Albert abdicated in favour of Victor Emmanuel II.

Unification of Italy

Under Prime Minister Camillo Benso, Count of Cavour, the kingdom allied with France in the Crimean War and then in the Second Italian War of Independence of 1859, defeating Austria and annexing Lombardy. In 1860 Giuseppe Garibaldi's Expedition of the Thousand conquered the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies, while Sardinian troops occupied the Papal States' eastern territories. On 17 March 1861 Victor Emmanuel II was proclaimed King of Italy, and the Kingdom of Sardinia formally ceased to exist as a separate state, its institutions becoming those of the new Italian kingdom.
