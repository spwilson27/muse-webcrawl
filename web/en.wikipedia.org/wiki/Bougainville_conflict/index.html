Bougainville conflict

The Bougainville conflict, also known as the Bougainville Civil War, was a multi-layered armed conflict fought from 1988 to 1998 in the North Solomons Province of Papua New Guinea (PNG) between the PNG government and the secessionist forces of the Bougainville Revolutionary Army (BRA).

Bougainville conflict
Date: 1988–1998
Location: Bougainville, Papua New Guinea
Result: Bougainville Peace Agreement; autonomy and independence referendum

Causes

The conflict was triggered by grievances over the Panguna copper mine, one of the largest in the world. Local landowners, led by Francis Ona, demanded compensation for environmental destruction of land and rivers and a greater share of revenues, most of which went to the PNG government and the mine's operator, Bougainville Copper Limited. When demands were refused, saboteurs attacked mine installations in late 1988, forcing the mine to close in 1989.

War

PNG security forces' heavy-handed response turned the dispute into a general secessionist war. PNG imposed a blockade of the island that lasted years and caused severe shortages of medicine and supplies; estimates of deaths range from 15,000 to 20,000, most from disease and deprivation. The Sandline affair of 1997 — PNG's hiring of mercenaries — brought down the government of Julius Chan and opened the way to peace talks.

Peace and aftermath

A truce in 1997 led to the Bougainville Peace Agreement of 2001, providing for an autonomous Bougainville government and a referendum on independence. In the 2019 referendum, 97.7% of voters chose independence, subject to negotiation and ratification by the PNG parliament.
