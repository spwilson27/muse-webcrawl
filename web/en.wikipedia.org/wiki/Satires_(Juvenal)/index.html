Satires (Juvenal)

The Satires are a collection of satirical poems by the Roman author Juvenal, written in the late 1st and early 2nd centuries AD. Sixteen satires in dactylic hexameter, divided into five books, survey the corruption, folly and vice of Roman society with famous indignation.

Author: Juvenal

Contents

Satire I is a programme piece: "it is hard not to write satire". Satire III laments life in Rome. Satire VI, the longest, attacks women and marriage. Satire X, on the vanity of human wishes, was imitated by Samuel Johnson in The Vanity of Human Wishes. Satire VII addresses patronage of poets.

Famous phrases

Juvenal coined "bread and circuses" (panem et circenses, Satire X), "a healthy mind in a healthy body" (mens sana in corpore sano), and "who will guard the guards themselves?" (quis custodiet ipsos custodes?, Satire VI).

Influence

The Satires defined the "Juvenalian" mode of formal verse satire — indignant and moralising, as opposed to Horace's gentler manner — influencing writers from Johnson to modern satirists.
