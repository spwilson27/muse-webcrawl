Heliotrope (building)

The Heliotrope (German: Heliotrop) is a rotating, energy-positive solar house designed by architect Rolf Disch and built near Freiburg im Breisgau, Germany, in 1994. The cylindrical house rotates to follow the sun, optimising heat gain in winter and minimising it in summer, and is considered the first building in the world to produce more energy than it consumes.

The Heliotrope in Freiburg

Design

The house is mounted on a central column and can rotate through a full circuit, driven by a small motor using little more power than the energy needed to turn it once or twice a day. A large triple-glazed facade faces the sun during the heating season to maximise passive solar gain; in summer the insulated side of the building is turned toward the sun to reduce overheating. A rooftop photovoltaic array of about 54 m² generates electricity, while solar thermal collectors supply hot water and a balcony railing of solar tubes provides additional heat. Rainwater collection, a grey-water system and composting of waste are integrated into the design.

PlusEnergy concept

The Heliotrope was the prototype for Disch's "PlusEnergy" (Plusenergie) concept, later applied on a neighbourhood scale in the Solar Settlement (Solarsiedlung) at Schlierberg in Freiburg, a community of homes that feed surplus electricity into the grid.
