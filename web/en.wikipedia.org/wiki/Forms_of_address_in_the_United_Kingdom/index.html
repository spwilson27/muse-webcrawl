Forms of address in the United Kingdom

Forms of address in the United Kingdom comprise the styles, titles, and modes of address used for the monarch, royalty, the peerage, clergy, judiciary, and public officials, both in speech and in correspondence. Many derive from centuries of court and church custom and are set out in reference works such as Debrett's.

Royalty and nobility

The monarch is addressed as "Your Majesty" (after the first use, "Sir" or "Ma'am") and referred to as "His/Her Majesty". Princes and princesses who are Royal Highnesses are addressed as "Your Royal Highness", dukes as "Your Grace", and other peers as "My Lord" or "Your Lordship". Wives, widows, and children of peers take courtesy styles according to rank, such as "Lady Mary" for a daughter of a duke, marquess, or earl.

Clergy and judiciary

Anglican bishops are "My Lord" and addressed in writing as "The Right Reverend"; archbishops are "Your Grace" / "The Most Reverend". Roman Catholic cardinals are "Your Eminence". High Court judges are addressed in court as "My Lord" or "My Lady"; circuit judges as "Your Honour"; magistrates as "Your Worship" or "Sir/Madam".

Officials and correspondence

Members of Parliament are addressed by name ("Mr/Ms + surname") and in the House of Commons refer to each other as "the honourable Member". Envelopes traditionally carry post-nominal styles such as "Esq." and honorific prefixes ("The Rt Hon." for Privy Counsellors), and letters to officials formally open "Dear Sir/Madam" and close "Yours faithfully".
