Dr Liet-Kynes is a fictional character in Frank Herbert's Dune (1965) and its prequels. He is the Imperial Planetologist on Arrakis and, secretly, the leader of the Fremen under his Fremen name, Liet. He is the son of Pardot Kynes, the first planetologist, and the father of Chani, who becomes the concubine of Paul Atreides.

Role in Dune

Liet-Kynes inherited his father's ecological project: a centuries-long programme, carried out by the Fremen, to transform the desert planet Arrakis into a green world with open water — "a man's dream" concealed from the Imperium. As Judge of the Change, he oversees the transfer of the Arrakis fief from House Harkonnen to House Atreides, and is torn between Imperial duty and his Fremen identity. He escorts Duke Leto Atreides to observe spice harvesting, famously describing the sandworm's relationship to the spice cycle.

After the Harkonnen–Sardaukar attack destroys House Atreides, Kynes is captured; the Harkonnens abandon him in the desert without a stillsuit or water. Dying, he hallucinates his father's lectures on ecology, and is killed when a spice blow erupts beneath him. His daughter Chani and his dream are carried forward by Paul and, later, by Paul's son Leto II, who completes the transformation of Arrakis.

Portrayals

Kynes was played by Max von Sydow in David Lynch's Dune (1984). In Denis Villeneuve's Dune (2021) the character was reimagined as a woman, Dr Liet Kynes, played by Sharon Duncan-Brewster.
