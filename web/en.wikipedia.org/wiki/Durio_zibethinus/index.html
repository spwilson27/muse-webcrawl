Durio zibethinus is the most common and commercially important species of durian tree, best known as the source of the durian fruit. Native to Borneo and Sumatra, it is widely cultivated across Southeast Asia, including Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand and the Philippines.

Description

The tree can grow to about 25–50 metres tall, with oblong leaves and clusters of pale flowers that open at night and are pollinated largely by bats. The fruit is large, ovoid, and covered with a thick, thorny rind; inside, the cream-coloured flesh surrounds several large seeds.

The fruit

Durian is often called the "king of fruits" in Southeast Asia. Its flesh is rich and custard-like, but the fruit is famous for its powerful odour, which many find unpleasant, and it is banned from some hotels and public transport systems. Hundreds of named cultivars exist, prized ones including Malaysia's Musang King and Thailand's Monthong. The flesh is eaten fresh and used in ice cream, cakes and traditional desserts; the seeds can be cooked.

Cultivation

The species requires a humid tropical climate with abundant rainfall and is propagated by grafting to preserve cultivar traits. Thailand and Malaysia are among the largest producers and exporters, with fresh and frozen durian in strong demand in China.
