LocationEastern Nepal, flowing into Bihar, India

The Sapta Kosi (Nepali: सप्तकोशी, "Seven Rivers") is a major river system of eastern Nepal and northern India. It is formed by the confluence of seven Himalayan rivers — the "seven Kosis" — traditionally listed as the Sun Kosi, Arun, Tamur, Bhote Kosi, Dudh Kosi, Indravati and Tama Kosi (with some lists substituting other tributaries, such as the Likhu). The Sapta Kosi is the largest tributary system of the Kosi River, which joins the Ganges in Bihar, India.

Course

The main tributaries rise in the high Himalayas of Nepal and Tibet. The Arun, the largest single contributor of water and sediment, rises in Tibet (where it is called the Phung Chu) and cuts through the Himalaya in a deep gorge between Mount Everest and Makalu. The Sun Kosi drains central-eastern Nepal, and the Tamur drains the Kanchenjunga region in the east. The three great rivers meet near Tribeni in eastern Nepal, from which the combined Sapta Kosi flows south through the Chatra gorge onto the plains of Bihar.

Flooding and engineering

The Kosi is notorious for carrying an enormous sediment load and for shifting course across its alluvial fan, earning it the name "Sorrow of Bihar". The Kosi Barrage near Bhimnagar, built under a 1954 agreement between Nepal and India, provides irrigation and flood moderation, and the Kosi embankments have been repeatedly tested — most seriously in the 2008 Kosi flood, when a breach at Kusaha in Nepal sent the river down an old course, inundating large areas of Nepal and Bihar and displacing millions. Proposals for a high dam (the Sapta Kosi High Dam) at Barahakshetra have been debated for decades for flood control, irrigation and hydropower.

Significance

The Sapta Kosi basin includes the Sagarmatha (Everest) region and supports rafting tourism on the Sun Kosi, major hydropower development, and intensive agriculture on the plains of eastern Nepal and Bihar.
