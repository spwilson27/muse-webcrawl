Jimmy Smith (musician)

DiedFebruary 8, 2005, Scottsdale, Arizona
GenresJazz, hard bop, soul jazz, jazz-funk

James Oscar Smith (December 8, 1925, or 1928 – February 8, 2005) was an American jazz musician who achieved the rare distinction of releasing a series of influential albums on the Blue Note label in the 1950s and 1960s that helped popularize the Hammond B-3 organ in jazz and soul music. Often billed as "The Incredible Jimmy Smith," he pioneered the soul jazz style, using the organ's drawbars and the bass pedals to create a distinctive trio sound without a bassist.

Career

Smith, who was largely self-taught on piano, turned to the Hammond organ in the mid-1950s and signed with Blue Note in 1956. Albums such as The Champ (1956), The Sermon! (1958), Back at the Chicken Shack (1960), and Midnight Special (1960) made him a leading figure in hard bop and soul jazz. Moving to Verve Records in the 1960s, he recorded with big bands arranged by Oliver Nelson, including The Cat (1964), and helped bring jazz organ to a wider audience. His later work embraced jazz-funk and fusion, and he continued to perform and record until shortly before his death.

Influence

Smith's virtuosic, blues-inflected organ playing influenced generations of organists and keyboard players, and his records were foundational for the acid jazz movement. He was named an NEA Jazz Master in 2005.
