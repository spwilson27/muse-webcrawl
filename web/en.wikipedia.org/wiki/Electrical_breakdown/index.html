Electrical breakdown is the process by which an electrically insulating material, subjected to a sufficiently strong electric field, suddenly becomes a conductor and an electric current flows through it. During breakdown, free charge carriers are generated by ionization, the resistance of the material collapses, and phenomena such as sparks, arcs, and lightning may result. The field strength at which breakdown occurs is called the dielectric strength or breakdown strength of the material.

Mechanism

In gases, a stray free electron accelerated by the field can ionize neutral molecules in collisions, releasing further electrons and producing an avalanche (Townsend discharge). The voltage at which breakdown occurs in a gas depends on the product of pressure and electrode gap — a relationship described by Paschen's law — and on the geometry of the electrodes. Once a conductive plasma channel forms, it can develop into a spark or a sustained arc.

In liquids and solids, breakdown may involve impact ionization, thermal runaway, electromechanical stress, and degradation along defects or moisture paths ("tracking"). In semiconductors, controlled avalanche breakdown and Zener breakdown are exploited in devices such as Zener diodes and avalanche photodiodes.

Breakdown strengths

Approximate dielectric strengths: dry air ≈ 3 MV/m; porcelain and glass ≈ 10–20 MV/m; transformer oil ≈ 10–15 MV/m; mica and many polymers ≈ 100 MV/m or more; and silicon dioxide thin films several hundred MV/m. Values depend strongly on thickness, temperature, humidity, and field uniformity.

Applications and hazards

Engineers design insulation systems, clearances, and surge protection to prevent unwanted breakdown, which can destroy equipment and start fires. Controlled breakdown is harnessed in spark plugs, gas-discharge lamps, spark gaps, electrostatic precipitators, and overvoltage protection devices. Corona discharge — partial breakdown around high-voltage conductors — is generally undesirable in power transmission but is used in photocopiers and air ionizers.
