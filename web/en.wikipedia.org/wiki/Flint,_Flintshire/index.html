Flint (Welsh: Y Fflint) is a town and community in Flintshire, Wales, lying on the estuary of the River Dee. It was formerly Flintshire's county town and is notable for Flint Castle, the first of the great castles built by Edward I during his conquest of Wales.

Flint Castle, begun in 1277.

History

Edward I began building Flint Castle in 1277, making it the first of the "iron ring" of fortresses intended to encircle the Welsh principality of Gwynedd. The town received its charter in 1284, the oldest borough charter in Wales. Richard II was captured at Flint Castle in 1399 and surrendered the crown to Henry Bolingbroke, a scene dramatised in Shakespeare's Richard II.

During the Industrial Revolution Flint developed as a port and centre for lead smelting, alkali and chemical works, supplied by coal from nearby mines. The town was also a point of embarkation for emigrants to America.

Geography and transport

Flint lies on the north bank of the Dee Estuary. The town is served by Flint railway station on the North Wales Coast Line, and the A548 coast road runs through it. The Flintshire Bridge, opened in 1998, carries the A548 across the Dee to the Wirral side.

Sport

The town's football club, Flint Town United, nicknamed the Silkmen after the town's former silk industry connections and shirt colours, plays in the Welsh football league system.
