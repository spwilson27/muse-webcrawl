Big five game

Big five game is a term for the five species of large African game animals that big-game hunters considered the greatest prizes and the most dangerous to pursue: lion, leopard, elephant, rhinoceros, and buffalo. In lowercase usage, the term appears widely in hunting literature, conservation writing, and safari marketing.

Origin

The phrase originated with 19th- and early 20th-century trophy hunters, for whom the big five represented the supreme test of hunting skill because each animal could kill a careless hunter. "Dangerous game" hunting of these species became tightly regulated under national laws and international agreements such as CITES.

Modern usage

Today the term is used mostly in wildlife tourism, where seeing all of the big five is a goal of many safari visitors, and in conservation discussions about the charismatic megafauna of African savannas. This article is the lowercase-titled counterpart of Big Five game.
