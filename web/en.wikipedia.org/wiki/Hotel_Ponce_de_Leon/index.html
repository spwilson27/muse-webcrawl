Hotel Ponce de Leon

Hotel Ponce de Leon
Location: St. Augustine, Florida
Built: 1885–1887; opened January 1888
Developer: Henry M. Flagler
Architects: John Carrère and Thomas Hastings
Style: Spanish Renaissance Revival
Now: Flagler College

The Hotel Ponce de Leon was a luxury hotel in St. Augustine, Florida, built by railroad magnate Henry M. Flagler and opened in 1888. A masterpiece of Spanish Renaissance Revival architecture constructed in poured concrete mixed with coquina shell, it was among the first major buildings with electricity installed by Thomas Edison's company. Today the building forms the centrepiece of Flagler College.

History

Flagler, a co-founder of Standard Oil, envisioned St. Augustine as an "American Riviera" winter resort. Young architects John Carrère and Thomas Hastings designed the hotel in an opulent Spanish style; interiors featured Tiffany stained glass and murals by George W. Maynard. The hotel hosted presidents and celebrities through the Gilded Age; it survived the Depression by catering to a loyal clientele and was used as a Coast Guard training centre during World War II. It closed in 1967 and was acquired the following year by the newly founded Flagler College.

Designation

The building is a U.S. National Historic Landmark. Its 79 Tiffany windows in the dining hall (now the Flagler Room) constitute one of the largest collections of Tiffany glass in their original setting.
