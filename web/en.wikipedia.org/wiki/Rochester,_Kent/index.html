Rochester, Kent

Rochester is a town in the unitary authority of Medway, in Kent, England. It is at the lowest bridging point of the River Medway, about 30 miles (48 km) southeast of London.

The town grew around a Roman settlement, Durobrivae, on Watling Street. Rochester Castle, built in the 12th century, has one of the best-preserved keeps in England. Rochester Cathedral, founded in 604, is the second-oldest cathedral foundation in England.

Dickens connection

Charles Dickens lived nearby at Gads Hill Place, Higham, and set several works in Rochester: it appears as Cloisterham in The Mystery of Edwin Drood and features in Great Expectations and The Pickwick Papers. The town historically held city status from 1211 until 1998, when an administrative error during local government reorganisation caused the status to lapse; it was the birthplace of the tradition of the Medway towns alongside Chatham and Gillingham.
