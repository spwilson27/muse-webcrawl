Omeprazole

Omeprazole
Trade names: Prilosec, Losec
Class: Proton-pump inhibitor
Formula: C17H19N3O3S

Omeprazole, sold under the brand names Prilosec and Losec among others, is a medication used in the treatment of gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), peptic ulcer disease, and Zollinger–Ellison syndrome. It is a proton-pump inhibitor (PPI) and works by blocking the hydrogen/potassium ATPase enzyme system of the gastric parietal cell, thereby suppressing stomach acid production.

Medical uses

Omeprazole is used to treat heartburn and acid reflux, to heal erosive esophagitis, and, in combination with antibiotics, to eradicate Helicobacter pylori in peptic ulcer disease. It is available both by prescription and over the counter.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include headache, abdominal pain, nausea, diarrhoea, and vomiting. Long-term use has been associated with increased risk of bone fractures, vitamin B12 deficiency, low magnesium, and certain infections. Omeprazole is metabolized in the liver, chiefly by CYP2C19, and can interact with drugs such as clopidogrel.

History

Omeprazole was first made in 1979 by Swedish scientists at AB Hässle (part of Astra), patented in 1978, and approved for medical use in 1988. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines and is available as a generic medication.
