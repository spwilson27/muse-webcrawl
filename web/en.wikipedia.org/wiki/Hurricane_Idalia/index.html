Hurricane Idalia was a powerful Category 4 hurricane that caused significant damage across parts of the southeastern United States, particularly in the Big Bend region of Florida, in late August 2023. The ninth named storm, third hurricane, and second major hurricane of the 2023 Atlantic hurricane season, Idalia developed from a tropical wave in the eastern Caribbean, crossed the Yucatán Peninsula, and rapidly intensified over the exceptionally warm waters of the eastern Gulf of Mexico.

Idalia approaching landfall on 30 August 2023.

Meteorological history

Tropical Depression Ten formed near the Yucatán Channel on 26 August and became Tropical Storm Idalia the next day. After drifting in the northwestern Caribbean, it struck western Cuba as a strengthening tropical storm, then entered the Gulf of Mexico and intensified rapidly, reaching Category 4 strength early on 30 August with peak winds of 130 mph (215 km/h) and a minimum pressure of 940 mbar. Slight weakening followed from an eyewall replacement cycle, and Idalia made landfall near Keaton Beach, Florida, at 11:45 UTC on 30 August as a high-end Category 3 hurricane with winds of 125 mph (205 km/h) — the strongest landfall on record in the Big Bend region.

Impacts

Idalia brought a storm surge of roughly 8–12 feet to parts of the Big Bend coast, inundating communities such as Cedar Key, Steinhatchee, and Horseshoe Beach, and drove record coastal flooding as far south as Tampa Bay. The storm then crossed northern Florida into Georgia and the Carolinas as a weakening tropical storm, causing widespread power outages, flooding rains, and tornadoes. Damage was estimated at about US$3.6 billion, and twelve deaths were attributed to the storm. The name Idalia was not retired and remains on the rotating list for future seasons.
