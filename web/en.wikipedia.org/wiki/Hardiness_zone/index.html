Hardiness zone

A hardiness zone is a geographic area defined as having a certain average annual minimum winter temperature, a factor relevant to the survival of many plants. The best-known system is the USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map, maintained by the United States Department of Agriculture.

USDA zones

The USDA system divides North America into 13 primary zones (1–13), each separated by 10 °F (5.6 °C) of average annual extreme minimum temperature, with subzones a and b. The map was updated in 2012 and again in 2023 using more recent climate data, shifting many areas to warmer zones.

Other systems

Similar maps exist for Europe, Australia, and other regions. Hardiness zones do not account for summer heat, rainfall, humidity or microclimates, so they are only one guide to plant survival. See also Hardiness (plants).
