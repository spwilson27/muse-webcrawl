Pedigree chart

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A pedigree chart is a diagram that shows the occurrence of phenotypes of a particular gene or organism and its ancestors from one generation to the next, most commonly used in humans, show dogs, and race horses. The word pedigree is a corruption of the French pied de grue ("crane's foot"), after the branched lines used in genealogical tables.

Symbols

By convention, in a human pedigree males are drawn as squares and females as circles; individuals affected by the trait under study are shaded, and known carriers of recessive traits may be half-shaded or marked with a central dot. The individual through whom the family came to attention is the proband, indicated by an arrow. Matings are shown by a horizontal line between partners, a double line denotes a consanguineous mating, and children hang below in birth order.

Uses in genetics

Geneticists and genetic counsellors use pedigree analysis to infer the mode of inheritance of a trait — autosomal dominant, autosomal recessive, X-linked or mitochondrial — and to estimate recurrence risks. Patterns are distinctive: a dominant trait appears in every generation, while a recessive trait can skip generations and is more likely to appear among the children of related parents. Pedigrees were central to the delineation of disorders such as Huntington's disease, cystic fibrosis and haemophilia, the last famously traced through the descendants of Queen Victoria.

In animal breeding

In livestock, dog and horse breeding a pedigree is a written record of ancestry, usually maintained by a breed registry, used to certify purebred status and to plan matings that avoid inbreeding or concentrate desirable traits.
