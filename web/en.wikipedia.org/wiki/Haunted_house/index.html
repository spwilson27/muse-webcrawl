Haunted house

A haunted house, spook house or ghost house in ghostlore is a house or other building often perceived as being inhabited by disembodied spirits of the deceased, typically former residents or people familiar with the property. Parapsychologists often attribute haunting to events such as violent death, tragedy, or strong emotion associated with the house.

The stereotypical haunted house: an isolated, decaying mansion.

Reported phenomena

Accounts of hauntings commonly describe apparitions, unexplained sounds such as footsteps and knocking, cold spots, moving objects (often associated with poltergeist activity), and feelings of dread. Skeptical investigators have proposed explanations including carbon monoxide poisoning, infrasound, mould toxins, sleep paralysis, suggestion, and hoaxes; surveys regularly find that a substantial minority of the public believes in haunted houses.

In culture

The haunted house is a central motif of Gothic and horror fiction, from Horace Walpole's The Castle of Otranto (1764) through Shirley Jackson's The Haunting of Hill House (1959) and Stephen King's The Shining. Separately, "haunted house" attractions — staged walk-through experiences with actors and effects, popular at Halloween — form a significant entertainment industry, originating in early 20th-century spook shows and elaborate modern productions. Famous allegedly haunted locations include the Winchester Mystery House in California, Borley Rectory in England, and the Amityville house in New York.
