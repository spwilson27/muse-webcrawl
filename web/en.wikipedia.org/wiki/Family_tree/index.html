Family tree

A family tree, also called a genealogy, pedigree chart, or genogram, is a chart representing family relationships in a conventional tree structure. More detailed family trees, used in medicine and social work, are called genograms; family trees are central tools of genealogy.

Representations

A family tree can present either ancestors (an ascending pedigree, with the subject at the bottom and forebears branching upward, or with the subject at the top) or descendants. Conventions typically place the oldest generation at the top, join partners with horizontal lines, and link children to parents with vertical lines; siblings are usually ordered by age from left to right. Standard symbols in genograms denote sex, birth, death, marriage, and divorce, and can encode medical information.

History

Genealogical charts have ancient roots, including the biblical genealogies and the family trees of Chinese clans, and were elaborated in medieval Europe in connection with heraldry and succession. Modern practice uses software and online databases to record and visualise large trees; genetic genealogy combines trees with DNA testing.
