Vilafranca del Penedès is a municipality in the province of Barcelona, Catalonia, Spain, and the capital of the comarca of Alt Penedès. It is the centre of the Penedès wine region, famous for still wines and for cava, the Catalan sparkling wine.

The basilica of Santa Maria in Vilafranca del Penedès.

History

The town grew up in the 12th century on the frontier between Christian Catalonia and Muslim al-Andalus, along the old Via Augusta. Counts of Barcelona granted it charters to attract settlers, and it became a market town for the surrounding agricultural plain. Notable buildings include the Gothic basilica of Santa Maria, the royal palace (Palau Reial), now home to the Vinseum wine museum, and the church of Sant Joan.

Wine and culture

Vilafranca sits among the vineyards of the Penedès denominación de origen; many famous bodegas have their historic cellars in the town. The town is also one of the great centres of the Catalan tradition of castells (human towers): its team, the Castellers de Vilafranca, founded in 1948, is among the most successful in history, famed for its green shirts and for first achieving several of the most difficult towers. The town's festa major, at the end of August in honour of Sant Fèlix, is one of Catalonia's most celebrated festivals.
