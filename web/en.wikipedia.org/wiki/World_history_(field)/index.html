World history (field)

World history, also called global history or transnational history, is a field of historical study that examines history from a global perspective. Rather than taking the nation-state or a single civilisation as the unit of analysis, world historians study patterns, connections and processes that transcend regional and cultural boundaries, comparing societies and tracing exchanges between them across long periods of time.

Origins and development

Although universal histories were written in antiquity and the medieval period — by authors such as Polybius, Sima Qian, Rashid al-Din and Ibn Khaldun — world history emerged as a distinct academic field in the late twentieth century. It grew out of dissatisfaction with purely national histories and with the Eurocentric framing of earlier "Western civilisation" courses taught in North American universities.

The field gained institutional form with the founding of the World History Association in 1982 and the launch of the Journal of World History in 1990. Scholars such as William H. McNeill, whose The Rise of the West (1963) emphasised contact and diffusion between civilisations, and Jerry Bentley helped define its methods and scope.

Themes and approaches

  • Cross-cultural interaction – trade, migration, conquest, missionary activity and other encounters between societies.
  • Comparative history – systematic comparison of institutions, economies and cultures across regions.
  • Large-scale processes – agriculture, industrialisation, disease transmission, empire-building and environmental change.
  • Networks and systems – influenced by world-systems theory, which analyses core, semi-peripheral and peripheral regions in a global division of labour.

World history is closely related to big history, which extends the timescale to the origins of the universe, and to environmental and economic history. Critics have questioned whether global narratives risk oversimplification or the loss of local detail, and debate continues over periodisation and the appropriate scale of analysis.

Teaching

World history is taught widely in schools and universities, including as the Advanced Placement World History course in the United States, which surveys global developments from c. 1200 CE to the present.
