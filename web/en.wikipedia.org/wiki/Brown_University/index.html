Brown University

Brown University is a private Ivy League research university in Providence, Rhode Island. Founded in 1764, it is the seventh-oldest institution of higher education in the United States and one of the nine Colonial Colleges chartered before the American Revolution. Brown is known for its Open Curriculum, adopted in 1969, which allows undergraduates to study without core requirements outside their concentration.

History

Brown was founded in Warren, Rhode Island, as the College in the English Colony of Rhode Island, a Baptist institution — the first American college to admit students regardless of religious affiliation. It moved to Providence in 1770 and was renamed in 1804 for Nicholas Brown Jr., a benefactor. Pembroke College, its coordinate women's college, merged fully in 1971. The 2006 report of its Steering Committee on Slavery and Justice examined the university's historical ties to the slave trade, including the Brown family's.

Academics

The university comprises the College, the Graduate School, the Warren Alpert Medical School, the School of Public Health, the School of Engineering, and the Watson Institute for International and Public Affairs. The Open Curriculum, developed from a 1969 student-authored report (the "Magaziner-Maxwell Report"), lets students take most courses satisfactory/no credit and design their own programmes. Brown is among the most selective universities in the United States, with an acceptance rate in the low single digits in recent years.

Campus and people

The main campus, on College Hill in Providence, contains many Georgian and Colonial Revival buildings; University Hall (1770) served as barracks and hospital during the Revolutionary War. Notable alumni include John D. Rockefeller Jr., Ted Turner, Emma Watson and Federal Reserve chair Janet Yellen. Brown's athletic teams, the Bears, compete in NCAA Division I.
