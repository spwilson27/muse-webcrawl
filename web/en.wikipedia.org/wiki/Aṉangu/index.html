Aṉangu

Aṉangu
Aṉangu country, central Australia
RegionCentral Australia (NT, SA, WA tri-state area)
LanguagesPitjantjatjara, Yankunytjatjara and related Western Desert languages
Meaning"People" / "human beings" in Pitjantjatjara–Yankunytjatjara

Aṉangu is the name by which the Aboriginal peoples of the Western Desert region of central Australia — principally speakers of Pitjantjatjara and Yankunytjatjara — refer to themselves; the word simply means "people" or "human beings". Aṉangu are the traditional owners of some of Australia's most famous landscapes, including Uluṟu and Kata Tjuṯa.

Culture and Tjukurpa

Aṉangu life is governed by Tjukurpa, the body of law, creation stories and moral teaching left by ancestral beings who shaped the land. Tjukurpa explains the features of sacred sites, prescribes ceremonies, kinship and land care, and is expressed in song, dance, body painting and rock art. Many Tjukurpa stories are restricted, and Aṉangu ask visitors not to photograph certain sacred places.

Land rights

Under the Aboriginal Land Rights (Northern Territory) Act 1976 and South Australia's Pitjantjatjara Land Rights Act 1981, Aṉangu won title to much of their country. The most celebrated handback came on 26 October 1985, when title to Uluṟu-Kata Tjuṯa was returned to its traditional owners, who immediately leased it back for joint management as a national park. The community of Mutitjulu sits near the base of Uluṟu.

Contemporary life

Today Aṉangu communities span the Aṉangu Pitjantjatjara Yankunytjatjara (APY) Lands of South Australia and communities in the Northern Territory and Western Australia. Aṉangu rangers and artists are prominent in land management and in the internationally successful Western Desert art movement, while communities continue to face the health, housing and education challenges common to remote Indigenous Australia.
