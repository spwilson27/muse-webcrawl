Laboratory flask

Laboratory flasks are vessels used in scientific laboratories to hold, mix, heat, and measure liquids. Usually made of borosilicate glass for its thermal and chemical resistance — or of plastic for some applications — flasks come in a variety of shapes, each adapted to a particular purpose.

Types

  • Erlenmeyer flask (conical flask): a flat-bottomed cone with a cylindrical neck, ideal for swirling mixtures without spilling and for titrations.
  • Volumetric flask: a flat-bottomed bulb with a long neck bearing a calibration mark, used to prepare solutions of precisely known volume.
  • Round-bottom flask: a sphere with a neck, heated evenly for distillation and reflux, held in a cork ring or clamp.
  • Florence flask: a round body with a flat bottom, used for boiling liquids.
  • Filter (Büchner) flask: a heavy-walled conical flask with a side arm for vacuum filtration.
  • Fernbach flask: a wide flask used in cell culture to maximize surface area.

Use and safety

Flasks are often graduated, though only volumetric flasks are precision measurement instruments. Round-bottom flasks must be supported and never set directly on a bench; vacuum flasks require inspection for cracks because of implosion risk. Stoppers, ground-glass joints, and condenser fittings standardize connections between flasks and other laboratory glassware.
