Tax relief


Tax relief reduces the amount of tax a person or business must pay

Tax relief is a general term for any programme, policy, or provision that reduces the amount of tax owed by an individual, household, or business. Relief may be general — such as a cut in tax rates or an increase in allowances — or targeted at particular groups, activities, or circumstances, for example pension saving, charitable giving, research and development, or taxpayers in financial hardship.

Forms of relief

  • Allowances and thresholds: raising the amount of income that can be earned tax-free (for example, a personal allowance) benefits all taxpayers below the cap.
  • Deductions: permitting certain expenses to be subtracted from taxable income, such as mortgage interest (historically in some countries) or business costs.
  • Tax credits: amounts subtracted directly from the tax bill, sometimes refundable if they exceed liability; examples include child tax credits and the Earned Income Tax Credit in the United States.
  • Exemptions and reliefs for specific activities: for instance, relief on pension contributions, capital allowances for business investment, or entrepreneurial reliefs on business disposals.
  • Hardship and debt relief: arrangements under which tax authorities accept reduced payment, instalment plans, or settlement of tax debts, such as the IRS "offer in compromise" programme in the United States.
  • Temporary relief: measures such as stamp-duty holidays, disaster relief postponements of filing deadlines, or pandemic-era deferrals.

Policy considerations

Tax reliefs are a form of public spending delivered through the tax system, sometimes called "tax expenditures". Governments use them to encourage behaviour (saving, investment, home ownership, giving), to support particular groups, or to respond to economic shocks. Critics argue that reliefs complicate the tax code, can disproportionately benefit higher earners, and escape the scrutiny applied to direct spending; periodic reviews in several countries have recommended pruning them in exchange for lower general rates.
