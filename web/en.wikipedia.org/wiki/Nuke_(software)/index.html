Nuke (software)

Nuke is a node-based digital compositing and visual effects application developed by Foundry. It is an industry standard for film and television post-production, used to combine computer-generated imagery with live-action footage, build digital environments, and perform tasks such as keying, rotoscoping, tracking, colour grading and 3D compositing.

History

Nuke originated at the visual effects studio Digital Domain, where it was developed in the early 1990s for in-house use, winning an Academy Award for technical achievement. In 2007 the software was acquired by The Foundry (now Foundry), a London-based developer, which commercialised and expanded it. The product family grew to include NukeX, with additional toolsets, and Nuke Studio, which adds editorial timeline functionality.

Features

Unlike layer-based editors, Nuke represents a composite as a directed graph of nodes, each performing an operation such as merging images, applying colour corrections or warping. It supports multi-channel EXR workflows, a full 3D environment with cameras, lights and geometry, deep compositing data, and scripting in Python and Tcl. Its extensibility and non-destructive workflow have made it the dominant compositing tool in high-end film production.
