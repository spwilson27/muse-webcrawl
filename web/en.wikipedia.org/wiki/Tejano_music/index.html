Tejano music

Tejano music (Spanish: música tejana), also known as Tex-Mex music, is a fusion of Mexican, European (especially German and Czech polka), and American musical traditions developed by Tejanos — the Mexican-American population of Texas. Its core instrumentation includes the button accordion and the bajo sexto (twelve-string bass guitar), introduced through conjunto bands in the early 20th century.

History

Tejano music grew out of the conjunto tradition of South Texas, pioneered by accordionists such as Narciso Martínez and Santiago Jiménez Sr. in the 1930s. In the postwar decades, Beto Villa and others added full orchestras (orquestas), blending big-band and ranchera sounds. The 1970s and 1980s brought synthesisers and a modern pop sensibility through groups such as Little Joe y La Familia, La Mafia and Mazz.

Golden age and Selena

The genre reached its commercial peak in the early 1990s, driven above all by Selena Quintanilla, the "Queen of Tejano music", whose crossover success brought the style to a national audience before her murder in 1995. The Tejano Music Awards, established in 1981, remain the genre's principal honours.

Styles

Tejano encompasses conjunto, orquesta, and progressive/ggrupero offshoots drawing on cumbia, country, rock and R&B — reflecting the bicultural experience of its audience.
