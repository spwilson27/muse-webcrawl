Square pyramidal number

In mathematics, a square pyramidal number (or pyramidal number) represents the number of stacked spheres in a pyramid with a square base and four triangular sides. These numbers are the figurate numbers obtained by summing the first n square numbers.

A square pyramid of spheres; the count of spheres is a square pyramidal number

Formula

The n-th square pyramidal number equals the sum of the first n squares:

Pn = 12 + 22 + ... + n2 = n(n + 1)(2n + 1) / 6

The first square pyramidal numbers are 1, 5, 14, 30, 55, 91, 140, 204, 285, 385, ... (sequence A000330 in the OEIS).

Properties and problems

The only numbers that are simultaneously square and square pyramidal are 1 and 4900 — this is the famous cannonball problem, posed by Édouard Lucas in 1875 and proved by G. N. Watson in 1918. The solution 4900 = 702 = 12 + 22 + ... + 242 means a square arrangement of cannonballs on the ground can be re-stacked into a square pyramid only in this nontrivial case.
