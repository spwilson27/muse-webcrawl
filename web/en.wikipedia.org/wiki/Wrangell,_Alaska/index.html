Wrangell is a city and borough in Southeast Alaska, United States, located on the northern tip of Wrangell Island in the Alexander Archipelago. With a population of about 2,100 at the 2020 census, it is one of Alaska's oldest non-Native settlements and the only US community to have been governed under four nations' flags: Tlingit, Russia, Great Britain and the United States.

History

The site was long a Tlingit settlement near the mouth of the Stikine River, a major trade route to the interior. The Russians built Redoubt Saint Dionysius there in 1834; the British Hudson's Bay Company leased the area and renamed it Fort Stikine. The town was named for Ferdinand von Wrangel, a Russian administrator. Wrangell boomed as a supply point during the Cassiar and Klondike gold rushes and later depended on fishing, timber and tourism.

Attractions

Chief Shakes Island, a Tlingit tribal house site in the harbour, and the Petroglyph Beach State Historic Site, with dozens of ancient rock carvings, are among its best-known landmarks. Wrangell is a port of call on the Alaska Marine Highway.
