Chicago Theatre

Address: 175 N. State Street, Chicago, Illinois
Opened: October 26, 1921
Capacity: 3,600

The Chicago Theatre, originally known as the Balaban and Katz Chicago Theatre, is a landmark theater located on North State Street in the Loop area of Chicago, Illinois. Built in 1921, the theater was a flagship for the Balaban and Katz (B&K) chain of movie palaces.

Architecture

The building was designed by architects Cornelius W. Rapp and George L. Rapp in the French Baroque (neo-Baroque) style. Its most famous feature is the iconic vertical sign spelling "CHICAGO," nearly six stories tall, which has become an unofficial emblem of the city and appears frequently in films and photographs. The marquee and sign use thousands of light bulbs; the sign was declared a Chicago Landmark.

History

Opened on October 26, 1921, the theater presented movies accompanied by live stage shows and a 29-piece orchestra. In later decades it declined as a single-screen cinema and was closed in 1985. After restoration, it reopened in 1986 as a performing arts venue hosting concerts, comedy, dance, and theatrical productions. It was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 1979.
