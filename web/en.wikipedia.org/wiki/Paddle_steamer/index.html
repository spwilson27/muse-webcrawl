Paddle steamer

A paddle steamer is a steamship or steamboat powered by a steam engine driving paddle wheels to propel the craft through the water. Paddle steamers dominated commercial steam navigation in the early 19th century before being largely superseded by the more efficient screw propeller.

History

Robert Fulton's North River Steamboat (the "Clermont") of 1807 demonstrated commercial viability on the Hudson River. Paddle steamers soon became the workhorses of American rivers such as the Mississippi and Ohio, and of coastal and cross-Channel routes in Europe. Side-wheelers carried wheels on each side; stern-wheelers, common on shallow western rivers, carried a single wheel at the stern.

Decline

Screw propulsion proved more efficient, seaworthy, and less vulnerable than exposed paddle wheels, and by the late 19th century ocean-going paddle steamers had mostly disappeared. Surviving vessels such as the PS Waverley now serve as excursion ships and floating museums.
