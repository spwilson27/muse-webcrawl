National Trust for Scotland

Founded: 1931
Headquarters: Edinburgh, Scotland
Type: Conservation charity
Properties: Castles, gardens, coastlines and battlefields across Scotland

The National Trust for Scotland (Scottish Gaelic: Urras Nàiseanta na h-Alba) is a Scottish conservation charity that protects and promotes Scotland's natural and cultural heritage. Founded in 1931, it is Scotland's largest conservation charity and one of its largest membership organisations.

Properties

The Trust cares for well over a hundred properties, including castles such as Culzean, Craigievar and Brodick; historic houses and gardens; the battlefield of Culloden; Robert Burns's birthplace cottage in Alloway; and wild landscapes including Ben Lawers, Glencoe (part), St Kilda (owned by the Trust since 1957) and several Hebridean islands. It also protects coastline, mountains and designed landscapes, and runs the gardens and estate visitor centres attached to many properties.

Organisation

The Trust is an independent charity, governed by its own Acts of Parliament, and is not part of the (England, Wales and Northern Ireland) National Trust, though reciprocal visiting arrangements exist between the two. Its work is funded by membership subscriptions, donations, legacies and visitor income. It should not be confused with the American National Trust for Historic Preservation.
