Amateur radio satellite

An amateur radio satellite is an artificial satellite built and used by amateur radio operators. It typically carries one or more transponders or repeaters that receive signals on an uplink frequency and retransmit them on a downlink frequency, allowing any licensed amateur with suitable ground equipment to communicate over intercontinental distances during the satellite's passes. Amateur satellites are designated in the OSCAR (Orbiting Satellite Carrying Amateur Radio) series once they reach orbit successfully.

The first amateur satellite, OSCAR 1, was launched on 12 December 1961, barely four years after Sputnik 1, and simply transmitted a "HI" beacon in Morse code. Since then, amateur organizations coordinated by AMSAT groups have flown dozens of satellites, from low-orbit FM and linear-transponder "birds" to the Phase 3 satellites in highly elliptical orbits and the geostationary QO-100 (Es'hail-2) payload, whose transponders cover a third of the Earth continuously.

History

OSCAR 1 was built by a group of California amateurs and launched as a secondary payload on a US Air Force mission from Vandenberg. OSCAR 3 (1965) carried the first linear transponder, enabling multiple simultaneous SSB and CW contacts; OSCAR 7 (1974) astonishingly returned to intermittent life decades later when its batteries shorted and its solar panels powered it in sunlight. From the 1990s, microsatellites and then CubeSats — many built by universities and AMSAT groups, such as the FUNcube series with educational telemetry — greatly increased launch opportunities.

How amateur satellites work

Ground stations compensate for the satellite's rapid motion, including significant Doppler shift, and steer antennas to track passes lasting about ten minutes for low orbits. Modes include FM repeaters, inverting linear transponders for SSB/CW, packet radio and digital store-and-forward mailboxes. The amateur satellite service has internationally allocated frequencies, principally in the 144–146 MHz and 435–438 MHz bands and at microwaves.

Notable satellites

AO-7 (OSCAR 7), AO-10 and AO-13 (the Phase 3 high-orbit series), UO-11, the Saudi SO-50, ISS amateur station (ARISS contacts with schools), and QO-100, the first amateur transponder in geostationary orbit, launched aboard Es'hail-2 in 2018.
