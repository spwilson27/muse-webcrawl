William Daniel Leahy (May 6, 1875 – July 20, 1959) was a United States Navy officer and the first person to hold the five-star rank of fleet admiral. He served as Chief of Naval Operations from 1937 to 1939 and as ambassador to France from 1941 to 1942. During World War II, Leahy served as chief of staff to President Franklin D. Roosevelt, functioning as his principal military adviser and effectively as the de facto chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff.

Early life and naval career

Leahy was born in Hampton, Iowa, and graduated from the United States Naval Academy in 1897. He served in the Spanish–American War aboard the battleship USS Oregon during its famous voyage around South America. Over the following decades he rose through the ranks, commanding ships and serving in staff positions. From 1927 to 1931 he was chief of the Bureau of Navigation, and later commanded the Battle Force of the U.S. Fleet.

Chief of Naval Operations

Leahy became Chief of Naval Operations in January 1937. He oversaw the expansion of the Navy as war approached and retired from active duty in August 1939 with the rank of admiral.

Ambassador and governor

After retirement, Leahy served as governor of Puerto Rico from 1939 to 1940. In late 1940 Roosevelt appointed him ambassador to Vichy France, a difficult post in which he sought to keep France from deepening its collaboration with Nazi Germany. He was recalled in 1942.

World War II

In July 1942 Roosevelt recalled Leahy to active duty as Chief of Staff to the Commander in Chief, a post created to coordinate the Army and Navy chiefs. Leahy chaired meetings of the Joint Chiefs of Staff, attended the major wartime conferences including Quebec, Tehran, Yalta, and Potsdam, and was present at President Roosevelt's death in April 1945. In December 1944 he was promoted to fleet admiral, the first of the Navy's five-star officers. He continued to serve under President Harry S. Truman until stepping down in 1949.

Death and legacy

Leahy died at Bethesda Naval Hospital in 1959. He was the first American naval officer to hold a five-star rank, and his role as the president's chief military adviser helped shape the modern National Security Council staff system.
