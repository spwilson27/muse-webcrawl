Sensitization

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sensitization
FieldPsychology, neuroscience, immunology
DefinitionIncreased response following repeated exposure to a stimulus
OppositeHabituation, desensitization

Sensitization is a non-associative learning process in which repeated administration of a stimulus results in the progressive amplification of a response. Sensitization often is characterized by an enhancement of response to a whole class of stimuli in addition to the one that is repeated. For example, repetition of a painful stimulus may make an organism respond more vigorously to a loud noise.

Neural mechanisms

Short-term sensitization of the gill-withdrawal reflex in the sea slug Aplysia, studied by Eric Kandel and colleagues, results from facilitation of synaptic transmission at sensory–motor synapses, mediated by serotonin and increased presynaptic neurotransmitter release; long-term sensitization additionally requires gene transcription and the growth of new synaptic connections. This work helped establish the cellular basis of learning and memory.

Related uses

In psychology, sensitization underlies phenomena such as the kindling model of mood disorders and incentive-sensitization in addiction, in which repeated drug exposure produces heightened motivational "wanting". In immunology, sensitization refers to the production of specific antibodies (such as IgE in allergy) after first exposure to an antigen, so that later exposures trigger an amplified immune response — the basis of allergic disease and of Rh sensitization in pregnancy.
