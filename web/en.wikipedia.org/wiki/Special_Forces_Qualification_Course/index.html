Special Forces Qualification Course

The Special Forces Qualification Course (SFQC or "Q Course") is the formal training program for entry into the United States Army Special Forces, commonly known as the Green Berets. The course is conducted by the U.S. Army John F. Kennedy Special Warfare Center and School at Fort Liberty (formerly Fort Bragg), North Carolina, with much of the field training taking place at Camp Mackall.

Overview

The Q Course prepares soldiers for assignment to a 12-man Operational Detachment Alpha (ODA), the basic operational element of a Special Forces company. Most candidates arrive from the Special Forces Assessment and Selection (SFAS) program, while in-service volunteers, 18X-ray enlistees, and National Guard candidates follow related pipelines. Training can last from under a year to more than two years depending on the candidate's military occupational specialty (MOS) and language assignment.

Phases

Historically the course has been organized into phases covering:

  • Orientation and history — introduction to unconventional warfare and the Special Forces heritage.
  • MOS training — instruction in one of the 18-series specialties: weapons (18B), engineer (18C), medical (18D), communications (18E), or intelligence (18F); officers train as detachment commanders (18A).
  • Collective training and the Culmination Exercise — small-unit tactics, survival, evasion, resistance and escape (SERE), and the large field exercise known as Robin Sage, in which students conduct an unconventional warfare campaign in the fictional country of "Pineland" across rural North Carolina.
  • Language and culture training — intensive instruction in a target language and regional orientation.
  • Regiment orientation — airborne operations, military free-fall for some assignments, and first-regiment indoctrination before graduation and the award of the Green Beret and Special Forces Tab.

Robin Sage

Robin Sage is the culminating unconventional warfare exercise of the Q Course and has been conducted since 1974 (as Cherokee Trail and later Gobbler Woods before taking its current name). Students are inserted into a simulated denied area, link up with a guerrilla force played by role-players and other trainees, and train, advise, and assist that force. Civilian volunteers from surrounding communities have supported the exercise for decades.
