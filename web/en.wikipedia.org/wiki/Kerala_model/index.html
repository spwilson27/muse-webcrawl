Kerala model
RegionKerala, India
FocusHuman development: education, health, land reform, public action
Noted forHigh literacy and life expectancy despite modest income

The Kerala model refers to the pattern of economic and social development in the Indian state of Kerala, characterised by achieving high levels of human development — literacy, life expectancy, low infant mortality and low population growth — at comparatively modest levels of per-capita income. The model has been widely studied by development economists, including Amartya Sen, as evidence that public action in education and health can deliver quality-of-life outcomes normally associated with much richer societies.

Features

Key features associated with the Kerala model include near-universal literacy (the highest in India, over 90 per cent since the 1991 census), life expectancy well above the Indian average, a sex ratio favourable to women compared with most of India, comprehensive land reforms carried out in the 1960s and 1970s, an extensive public distribution system for food, and accessible public health and education networks reaching into rural areas. Historical factors cited include the social reform movements of the 19th and early 20th centuries, the policies of the princely states of Travancore and Cochin, missionary activity, high caste–class political mobilisation and the influence of left-wing governments after statehood in 1956.

Criticism

Critics note persistent weaknesses: high unemployment, dependence on remittances from Keralites working in the Gulf states, low industrial growth, fiscal stress, and social problems including high reported rates of alcoholism and suicide. Scholars also debate how much of Kerala's achievement reflects redistribution and remittances rather than a reproducible development path.
