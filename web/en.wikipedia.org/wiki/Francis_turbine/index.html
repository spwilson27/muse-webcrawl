Francis turbine

The Francis turbine is a type of water turbine developed by James B. Francis in the 1840s at Lowell, Massachusetts. It is a reaction turbine that combines radial and axial flow concepts, and is the most common water turbine in use today.

Design

Water enters through a spiral volute casing and adjustable guide vanes (wicket gates) that direct it tangentially onto the runner — a wheel with fixed curved blades. Pressure drops as water passes through the runner, transferring energy from both pressure and velocity. A draft tube recovers energy at the exit.

Applications

Francis turbines suit medium heads (roughly 10–350 m) and are installed at major hydroelectric stations worldwide, including the largest units at the Three Gorges Dam. They can reach efficiencies above 95% and, in reversible form, serve in pumped-storage plants as both turbine and pump.
