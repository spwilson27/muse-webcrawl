National Development and Reform Commission
Agency overview
Formed: 2003 (successor to the State Development Planning Commission)
Jurisdiction: People's Republic of China
Parent agency: State Council

The National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC) is a macroeconomic management agency under the State Council of the People's Republic of China. It has broad administrative and planning control over the Chinese economy and has been described as a "mini-State Council" because of the breadth of its functions.

Functions

The NDRC formulates and implements strategies for national economic and social development, drafts China's five-year plans, approves major investment projects, and oversees price policy for key commodities and utilities. It also plays a leading role in industrial policy, energy policy, and climate policy, and it supervises anti-monopoly enforcement in pricing matters.

History

The NDRC descends from the State Planning Commission established in the 1950s, which directed China's centrally planned economy. It was renamed the State Development Planning Commission in 1998 and took its present name in 2003, when it absorbed functions related to industrial policy and enterprise reform.
