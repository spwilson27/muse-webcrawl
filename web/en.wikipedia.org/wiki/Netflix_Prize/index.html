Netflix Prize

The Netflix Prize was an open competition, launched in October 2006, offering a grand prize of US$1,000,000 for the best collaborative filtering algorithm to predict user ratings for films, based on previous ratings without any other information about the users or films. To win, a team had to improve on Netflix's own Cinematch system by at least 10% in root mean square error (RMSE) on a held-out test set.

Dataset and contest

Netflix released over 100 million ratings from some 480,000 anonymous users on nearly 18,000 movie titles. The contest drew more than 40,000 teams. Annual Progress Prizes of $50,000 rewarded interim leaders, and methods such as matrix factorization (SVD), restricted Boltzmann machines and large ensembles came to dominate.

Result

In 2009 the grand prize was awarded to "BellKor's Pragmatic Chaos," a merger of several leading teams, which achieved a 10.06% improvement. A planned sequel prize was cancelled in 2010 after privacy researchers showed the "anonymous" dataset could be de-anonymized, prompting an FTC inquiry and lawsuit.
