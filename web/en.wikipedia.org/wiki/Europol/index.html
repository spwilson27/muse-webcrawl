Europol, officially the European Union Agency for Law Enforcement Cooperation, is the law enforcement agency of the European Union (EU). Established in 1998 and based in The Hague, Netherlands, it serves as a centre for combating serious international crime and terrorism, supporting the law enforcement authorities of the EU member states through information exchange, intelligence analysis, and operational coordination.

History

The idea of a European police office was discussed from the 1970s onwards and was endorsed at the 1991 European Council in Luxembourg. The Europol Convention was signed in 1995 and entered into force in 1998; Europol began full activities on 1 July 1999, taking over the work of the Europol Drugs Unit. In 2010 it became an agency of the EU funded from the Union budget, and a new Europol Regulation applied from May 2017 expanded and clarified its mandate.

Mandate and work

Europol supports investigations into serious crime affecting two or more member states, including drug trafficking, human trafficking, migrant smuggling, money laundering, organised fraud, cybercrime, and terrorism. It has no executive powers: Europol officers cannot make arrests or conduct investigations independently, but they can participate in joint investigation teams. The agency operates secure information systems, including the Europol Information System and the SIENA communication network, and produces strategic threat assessments such as the Serious and Organised Crime Threat Assessment (SOCTA) and the Internet Organised Crime Threat Assessment (IOCTA).

Structure

Europol is headed by an Executive Director and governed by a Management Board with representatives of the member states and the European Commission. National units in each member state act as the liaison bodies between Europol and national authorities, and liaison officers from member states and third countries are based at the headquarters in The Hague. Within Europol, specialised centres include the European Cybercrime Centre (EC3), the European Counter Terrorism Centre (ECTC), and the European Migrant Smuggling Centre.

Cooperation

Europol cooperates with EU bodies such as Eurojust and Frontex, with international organisations including Interpol, and with non-EU countries under cooperation agreements. Its activities are subject to oversight by the European Parliament and national parliaments, and to data-protection supervision by the European Data Protection Supervisor.
