Self-injury Awareness Day

Self-injury Awareness Day (SIAD), also known as Self-Harm Awareness Day, is an annual event held on 1 March dedicated to raising awareness of self-harm and self-injury, reducing the stigma surrounding them, and encouraging people who self-injure to seek support.

ObservedAnnually on 1 March
TypeAwareness day
Also calledSelf-Harm Awareness Day
SymbolOrange ribbon

Observance

The day is marked by mental health charities, schools, and community organizations through educational campaigns, social media initiatives, and fundraising events. Supporters traditionally wear an orange ribbon — half orange and half another colour in some variants — as a symbol of awareness and hope. Some participants write the word "LOVE" on their arm or draw a butterfly as part of related awareness projects.

Purpose

Self-injury is often misunderstood as attention-seeking or as equivalent to suicidal behaviour, whereas clinicians generally understand it as a maladaptive coping mechanism for emotional distress. Awareness Day campaigns aim to correct misconceptions, to make it easier for people to disclose self-injury without shame, and to train educators, health professionals, and families to respond supportively. The day has been observed since around the early 2000s, though its exact origins are unclear.
