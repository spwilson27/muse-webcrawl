Deep reactive-ion etching

Deep reactive-ion etching (DRIE) is a highly anisotropic plasma etching process used in microfabrication to create deep, steep-sided trenches and holes in wafers, especially silicon.

Bosch process

The most common form alternates etching with sulfur hexafluoride and passivation with octafluorocyclobutane, producing characteristic scalloped sidewalls.

Applications

DRIE is essential for MEMS devices, accelerometers, gyroscopes, microfluidics and through-silicon vias.

Alternatives

Cryogenic etching uses very low temperatures to achieve smooth sidewalls.
