Sai Kung District

Location: New Territories, Hong Kong
Area: approx. 137 km²
Population: approx. 490,000 (2021)
Main settlements: Sai Kung Town, Tseung Kwan O

Sai Kung District is one of the 18 districts of Hong Kong, located in the eastern New Territories. It is the second largest district by area and is known for its unspoilt coastline, islands, country parks and seafood restaurants, sometimes described as the "back garden of Hong Kong".

Geography

The district covers the Sai Kung Peninsula and the southern half of the New Territories east of Kowloon, including the new town of Tseung Kwan O, which contains most of the district's population, and more than 70 islands including Kau Sai Chau, Sharp Island and the Ninepin Group. Large parts lie within the Sai Kung East and Sai Kung West Country Parks, and the district hosts much of the Hong Kong UNESCO Global Geopark.

History and economy

Sai Kung Town grew around its harbour and fishing community and remains a centre for seafood restaurants and boat trips to beaches such as those in Tai Long Wan. Fishing and farming declined after the 1970s, when the High Island Reservoir was built, flooding two villages, while Tseung Kwan O was developed as a new town from the 1980s onward.
