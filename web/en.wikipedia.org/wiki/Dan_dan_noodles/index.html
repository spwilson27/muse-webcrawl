Dan dan noodles

Dan dan noodles (Chinese: 擔擔麵; pinyin: dàndànmiàn) are a noodle dish originating from Chinese Sichuan cuisine. The dish consists of a spicy sauce, usually containing chili oil and Sichuan pepper, served over noodles, often topped with minced pork, preserved vegetables, and chopped peanuts or scallions.

Name and origin

The name refers to the carrying pole (dan dan) that street vendors used to carry their wares through the streets of Chengdu: baskets of noodles and sauce hung from either end of a bamboo pole shouldered by the hawker. Because the vendors sold the noodles cheaply to passers-by, the dish became a beloved, inexpensive street food of Sichuan's capital.

Preparation

The hallmark of the dish is mala — the hot-and-numbing flavour combination of dried chillies and Sichuan peppercorns. A typical bowl starts with a sauce of chili oil with its sediment, Sichuan pepper, black vinegar, soy sauce, and sometimes sesame paste, placed in the bottom of the bowl. Boiled thin wheat noodles are laid over the sauce and topped with crispy fried minced pork, ya cai (pickled mustard greens from Yibin), crushed roasted peanuts and spring onion. Traditionally the dish is served nearly dry, with only enough sauce to coat the noodles, though soup versions exist.

Variations abroad

In Japan, the dish inspired tantanmen, a ramen-style soup version with sesame and often a rich broth. In American Chinese restaurants, dan dan noodles are frequently made with a larger share of sesame or peanut paste and less chili heat, and are often served in soup form quite different from the Chengdu original.
