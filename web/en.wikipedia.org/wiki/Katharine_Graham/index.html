Katharine Graham

Katharine Graham
Born16 June 1917, New York City
Died17 July 2001
OccupationPublisher, Washington Post
[Image: Graham in the Post's newsroom in the 1970s]

Katharine Graham (1917–2001) was the American publisher who led the Washington Post Company for three decades, overseeing the paper through the Pentagon Papers and Watergate — decisions that made her, in her own title's phrase, the reluctant but resolute head of a great newspaper. Her memoir Personal History (1997) won the Pulitzer Prize for biography.

Tenure

Graham took control after her husband Philip Graham's death in 1963, took the company public, hired Ben Bradlee, and backed the newsroom against the Nixon White House's threats — the defining publisher-editor partnership of the era.
