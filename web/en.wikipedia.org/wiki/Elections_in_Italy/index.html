Elections in Italy

Elections in Italy give information on elections and election results in Italy. Italy elects at the national level a parliament consisting of two houses, the Chamber of Deputies and the Senate of the Republic. Since a 2020 constitutional reform, the Chamber has 400 members and the Senate 200 elected members; both are elected for five-year terms. The President of the Republic is elected for a seven-year term by an electoral college of parliamentarians and regional delegates.

Electoral system

The current law, the Rosatellum (2017), is a mixed system: about 37% of seats are filled by first-past-the-post in single-member districts and the remainder by proportional representation with a 3% national threshold for parties. Earlier postwar systems were predominantly proportional, and Italy has repeatedly reformed its electoral law, including the Mattarellum (1993) and the Porcellum (2005), the latter partly struck down by the Constitutional Court.

Other elections

Italians also elect regional councils and presidents, provincial and municipal governments, and Italy's members of the European Parliament. Referendums, both abrogative and constitutional, are a significant feature of Italian democracy. Voting is open to citizens aged 18 and over for both houses (the Senate voting age was lowered from 25 in 2021), and Italians resident abroad elect representatives in an overseas constituency.

Political parties

Postwar politics was dominated by Christian Democracy until the party system's collapse in the early 1990s. Subsequent decades saw competition between centre-left and centre-right coalitions, the rise of the Five Star Movement, and the 2022 victory of a right-wing coalition led by Giorgia Meloni's Brothers of Italy.
