Green–Tao theorem

The Green–Tao theorem, proved by Ben Green and Terence Tao in 2004, states that the sequence of prime numbers contains arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions. In other words, for every natural number k, there exist infinitely many arithmetic progressions of primes of length k. The theorem is an extension of Szemerédi's theorem to sets of positive density, generalized here to subsets of the primes of positive relative density.

Background

Questions about patterns in the primes are old. The van der Waerden theorem (1927) showed that any finite colouring of the integers contains arbitrarily long monochromatic arithmetic progressions, and Szemerédi's theorem (1975) showed that any set of integers of positive upper density contains arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions. The primes have density zero, however, so neither result applies directly. Furstenberg and Katznelson proved a multidimensional version using ergodic theory, and in 2004 Green and Tao combined ergodic-theoretic ideas with analytic number theory.

Statement and proof idea

Green and Tao developed a "transference principle": they embedded the primes in a pseudorandom superset (the "almost primes" weighted by a restriction of the von Mangoldt function, developed with work of Goldston and Yıldırım) and proved a relative Szemerédi theorem. The counting of progressions uses the Gowers uniformity norms, following the approach of Gowers's proof of Szemerédi's theorem.

Extensions

Subsequent work generalized the result to polynomial progressions in the primes (Tao and Ziegler) and to other sparse sets. In 2010 Green, Tao and Ziegler announced a proof of the inverse conjecture for the Gowers norms, completing the programme of describing all linear patterns in the primes (the "Möbius and nilsequences" conjecture).

Concrete examples include the 27-term prime arithmetic progression found by David Broadhurst and collaborators in 2019, the longest currently known.
