Acetylacetone

Acetylacetone is an organic compound with the chemical formula CH3–CO–CH2–CO–CH3. It is a colorless liquid, classified as a 1,3-diketone. It exists in equilibrium with a tautomer, CH3C(O)CH=(OH)CH3. Acetylacetone is a precursor to the important ligand acetylacetonate (acac), a bidentate ligand.

Properties

Acetylacetone has a pleasant odour and a boiling point of about 140 °C. The keto and enol tautomers interconvert rapidly; the enol form is stabilised by an intramolecular hydrogen bond and predominates in non-polar solvents and the gas phase. The central CH2 group is acidic (pKa ≈ 9), so the compound readily loses a proton to give the acetylacetonate anion.

Metal complexes

The acetylacetonate anion binds metal ions through both oxygen atoms, forming a six-membered chelate ring. Such complexes — for example tris(acetylacetonato)iron(III) and copper(II) acetylacetonate — are typically soluble in organic solvents and have been used as catalysts, NMR shift reagents and precursors in materials deposition.

Synthesis and uses

Industrially, acetylacetone is prepared by the thermal rearrangement of isopropenyl acetate, and in the laboratory by a Claisen condensation of acetone with ethyl acetate. Beyond coordination chemistry, it is used as a solvent additive, a fuel additive and an intermediate in the synthesis of heterocycles such as pyrazoles and pyrimidines.
