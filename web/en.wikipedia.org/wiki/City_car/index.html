City car

Class: A-segment
Also called: Micro car / kei car (Japan)
Examples: Fiat 500, Toyota Aygo, Hyundai i10, Smart Fortwo

A city car is a small car designed primarily for use in urban areas. It corresponds approximately to the European A-segment vehicle classification and, in Japan, to the kei car class (though kei cars are defined by strict legal limits on size and engine displacement). City cars are typically between about 3.4 and 3.7 metres long, are inexpensive to buy and run, and are easy to park in congested streets.

Characteristics

City cars usually have hatchback bodywork, front-wheel drive, and small petrol, hybrid, or electric powertrains. Interiors prioritise four seats within a minimal footprint, often with upright seating and a tall roofline. Low purchase price, fuel economy, and manoeuvrability are prioritised over performance and long-distance comfort.

History

The concept dates to post-war European economy cars such as the Fiat 500 (1957) and the original Mini (1959, usually classed slightly larger). Modern city cars proliferated from the 1980s and 1990s — for example the Fiat Panda, Peugeot 106, and Ford Ka. Joint-venture models such as the Peugeot 108, Citroën C1, and Toyota Aygo triplets exemplified platform sharing in the segment. In the 2010s and 2020s, some manufacturers withdrew from the segment as emissions rules raised costs, while electric models such as the Fiat 500e and Dacia Spring revived the format with battery power.
