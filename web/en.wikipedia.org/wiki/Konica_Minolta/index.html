Konica Minolta, Inc. (Japanese: コニカミノルタ株式会社) is a Japanese multinational technology company headquartered in Tokyo. It was formed in 2003 by the merger of Konica Corporation and Minolta Co., two long-established makers of cameras, copiers and photographic materials. Today the company focuses on office and production printing equipment and services under the bizhub brand, IT and workplace services, medical imaging, and precision measuring instruments.

History

Konica traced its origins to 1873, when Rokusaburo Sugiura began selling photographic materials in Tokyo; it later became Konishiroku and released Japan's first domestically produced roll film and colour film. Minolta was founded in Osaka in 1928 as Nichi-Doku Shashinki Shōten by Kazuo Tashima, and became known for cameras including the first integrated autofocus SLR system (the Maxxum/Dynax series, 1985) and for office copiers. The two companies merged in 2003 under a holding company, adopting the single Konica Minolta name in 2004.

Exit from photography

In January 2006 Konica Minolta announced its withdrawal from the camera and consumer photo businesses; its digital SLR camera assets, including the Maxxum/Dynax lens mount, were transferred to Sony, which developed them into its Alpha line. Photo paper and film production also ended, completing the company's shift to business technologies.

Business areas

The company's segments include Digital Workplace (multifunction peripherals and IT services), Professional Print (production and industrial printing, including the Accurio range), Healthcare (digital radiography and diagnostic imaging), and Industry (sensing, optical components and materials). Konica Minolta is also a title sponsor of the Konica Minolta Big Wave Classic and supports the planetarium "Konica Minolta Planetaria" venues in Tokyo.
