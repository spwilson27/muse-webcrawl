Lake Travis is a reservoir on the Colorado River in central Texas in the United States. It is named in honor of William B. Travis, the commander of the Alamo. The lake was formed in 1942 by the construction of Mansfield Dam by the Lower Colorado River Authority (LCRA) for purposes of flood control, water supply, electrical power generation and recreation.

Geography and history

Lake Travis is the largest of the Texas Highland Lakes, which also include Lake Buchanan, Inks Lake, Lake LBJ, Lake Marble Falls and Lake Austin. It stretches 65 miles upstream from Mansfield Dam, west of Austin. With 270 miles of shoreline, it is a popular recreation destination for boating, fishing, swimming, scuba diving, camping and picnicking.

The lake's water level is highly variable depending on rainfall in the watershed. Its historic high level was 717.19 feet above mean sea level during the Christmas flood of 1991, and its historic low was 614.2 feet during the drought of the 1950s. During the 2010s Texas drought the lake fell severely before recovering after floods in 2015 and 2018.

Recreation

The most popular site on the lake is Hippie Hollow Park, the only legally recognized clothing-optional public park in Texas, located near the city of Lago Vista. Pace Bend Park, Arkansas Bend Park and other LCRA and Travis County parks line the shore. The lake is stocked with largemouth bass, guadalupe bass, white bass, striped bass, catfish and sunfish.
