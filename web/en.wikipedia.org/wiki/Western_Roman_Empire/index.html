Western Roman Empire

Capital: Mediolanum (286–402), Ravenna (402–476)
Period: AD 285/395 – 476
Last emperor: Romulus Augustulus

In modern historiography, the Western Roman Empire refers to the western half of the Roman Empire, administered separately from the Eastern Roman Empire following the reforms of Diocletian and, definitively, the death of Theodosius I in 395, when the empire was divided between his sons Honorius (west) and Arcadius (east).

Decline and fall

During the 5th century the western court, based at Ravenna, lost control of most of its provinces to migrating Germanic peoples: the Visigoths sacked Rome in 410, the Vandals in 455, and Africa, Gaul, Hispania and eventually Italy itself passed to successor kingdoms. Power increasingly lay with barbarian-born generals such as Stilicho, Aëtius and Ricimer, who made and unmade emperors. In 476 the Germanic leader Odoacer deposed the last western emperor, Romulus Augustulus, and sent the imperial regalia to Constantinople — the conventional date for the "fall of Rome". Modern historians debate the causes and character of this transformation, from catastrophic collapse to gradual late-antique transition.
