Richard Norman Shaw

Richard Norman Shaw RA (7 May 1831 – 17 November 1912), sometimes known simply as Norman Shaw, was a British architect who was a leading figure in late Victorian domestic architecture and a pioneer of the Queen Anne Revival and Arts and Crafts styles.

Career

Born in Edinburgh, Shaw trained in London and worked for William Burn and George Edmund Street. He travelled in France, Italy, and Germany on a Royal Academy scholarship. In partnership and later alone, he designed country houses such as Cragside in Northumberland for Lord Armstrong, notable for its picturesque massing, half-timbering, and advanced services.

Style and works

Shaw was central to the development of Bedford Park in Chiswick, the first garden suburb, and designed houses in Hampstead and Chelsea including the Swan House. His later work turned toward classicism, including the Piccadilly Hotel and the former New Scotland Yard on the Victoria Embankment, built for the Metropolitan Police.

Influence

His office trained architects including Ernest Newton and Edward Prior, and influenced Edwin Lutyens. Shaw's blend of vernacular revival, comfort, and informal planning shaped English domestic architecture for decades.
