Resolvin

Resolvins are specialised pro-resolving mediators (SPMs) — signalling molecules formed from the omega-3 fatty acids eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA) — that actively promote the resolution of inflammation rather than merely suppressing it. The name derives from their identification in the resolution phase of inflammatory exudates.

Families

Resolvins are grouped into series according to their precursor: the E-series resolvins (RvE1, RvE2, RvE3) derive from EPA, while the D-series resolvins (RvD1–RvD6) derive from DHA. Members of the D-series formed in the presence of aspirin, via acetylated cyclooxygenase-2, are termed aspirin-triggered resolvins (AT-RvDs). Related SPM families include the protectins, maresins and lipoxins.

Biological actions

In experimental models, resolvins limit further neutrophil infiltration into inflamed tissue, stimulate macrophages to clear dead cells and debris (efferocytosis), reduce pain signalling, and promote tissue repair and return to homeostasis. They act through specific G-protein-coupled receptors, such as ChemR23/ERV1 for RvE1 and ALX/FPR2 and GPR32 for RvD1.

Research

Because unresolved inflammation underlies many chronic diseases, resolvins and stable analogues are under investigation for conditions including periodontitis, dry eye disease, asthma and atherosclerosis. A synthetic analogue of RvE1 reached clinical trials for ocular inflammation. The field was founded largely through the work of Charles Serhan and collaborators, who identified the first resolvins in the early 2000s.
