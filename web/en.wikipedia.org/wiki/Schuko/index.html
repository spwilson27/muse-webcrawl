Schuko

Schuko is a plug and socket system for alternating current power, officially known as the Type F system. The name is short for Schutzkontakt, German for "protective contact" — the earthing contact of the system. It was patented in 1926 by Albert Büttner and is the standard domestic plug across much of continental Europe.

Design

The Schuko plug has two round pins, 4.8 mm in diameter, spaced 19 mm apart, for line and neutral. Earthing is provided not by a third pin but by two metal clips on the sides of the plug that engage contacts in the socket recess. Because of this symmetrical earthing arrangement the plug can be inserted either way up, making it unpolarised. The matching socket is recessed, so the pins are fully shrouded before contact is made.

Use

Type F is used throughout Germany, Austria, the Netherlands, Spain, Scandinavia and most of eastern Europe, as well as in countries such as South Korea and Indonesia. It is rated at 16 A and 230 V. France, Belgium and several other countries use the similar Type E system with an earthing pin; many modern plugs are hybrid E/F designs compatible with both sockets.
