Chicago Stadium was an indoor arena in Chicago, Illinois, that opened in 1929 and was demolished in 1995. It was nicknamed the "Madhouse on Madison" for the deafening roar of its crowds, aided by its massive Barton theater organ.

History

The stadium was the home of the Chicago Blackhawks of the National Hockey League from 1929 to 1994 and of the Chicago Bulls of the National Basketball Association from 1967 to 1994. It hosted three Democratic National Conventions and numerous boxing matches, concerts and political rallies.

Replacement

In 1994 both the Blackhawks and Bulls moved across the street to the new United Center, and the Stadium was demolished the following year. Its site became a parking lot for the United Center.
