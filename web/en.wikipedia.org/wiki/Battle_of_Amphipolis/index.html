Battle of Amphipolis

The Battle of Amphipolis was fought in 422 BC during the Peloponnesian War between Athens and Sparta. It was the culmination of events that began in 424 BC with the capture of Amphipolis by the Spartan general Brasidas. The Athenian general Cleon brought an expedition to retake the city in 422 BC; in the ensuing battle both Cleon and Brasidas were killed, removing the two leading opponents of peace and paving the way for the Peace of Nicias the following year.

Background

Amphipolis, an Athenian colony on the Strymon River in Thrace, controlled valuable timber, gold mines, and the route into the Macedonian interior. In the winter of 424/423 BC the Spartan commander Brasidas seized it in a brilliant surprise march. The Athenian historian Thucydides, then a general, arrived too late to save the city and was exiled for twenty years — during which he wrote his history of the war. Athens' failed attempts to recover the city culminated in Cleon's expedition of 422 BC.

Battle

Cleon advanced on Amphipolis and, finding Brasidas unwilling to come out, began to withdraw. Brasidas seized the moment, bursting from the city gates with 150 picked hoplites against the exposed Athenian center while Clearidas attacked from another gate with the main force. The Athenian army collapsed; Cleon was killed while fleeing, and around 600 Athenians died. Brasidas was mortally wounded and died shortly after being carried back into the city, where he was honored as a hero and founder.

Aftermath

With the war's two most aggressive leaders dead, Athens and Sparta concluded the Peace of Nicias in 421 BC. The peace proved unstable, but Amphipolis itself was never restored to Athens despite the treaty terms, remaining a lasting grievance.
