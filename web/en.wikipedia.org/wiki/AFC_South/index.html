The AFC South is one of the four divisions of the American Football Conference (AFC) in the National Football League (NFL). It was created ahead of the 2002 season, when the NFL realigned its divisions into eight groups of four teams each following the admission of the Houston Texans as an expansion franchise.

Teams

  • Houston Texans — joined the league as an expansion team in 2002 and were placed in the new division.
  • Indianapolis Colts — moved from the AFC East; the franchise had previously played in Baltimore before relocating in 1984.
  • Jacksonville Jaguars — moved from the AFC Central, where they had played since their 1995 debut.
  • Tennessee Titans — moved from the AFC Central; formerly the Houston Oilers before relocating to Tennessee in 1997.

History

The Colts dominated the division in its first decade behind quarterback Peyton Manning, winning seven of the first nine titles and Super Bowl XLI in the 2006 season. The Titans were the division's first champions in 2002 and reached the playoffs as a wild card in several seasons, while the Texans emerged in the 2010s under defensive end J. J. Watt and won multiple division titles. The Jaguars' division crowns include the 2017 season, when they reached the AFC Championship Game, and the 2022 season. The division champion receives an automatic playoff berth and home-field advantage in the wild-card or divisional round.
