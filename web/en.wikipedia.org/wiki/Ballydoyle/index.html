Ballydoyle

Racehorses on the gallops at Ballydoyle.

Ballydoyle is a racehorse training facility in County Tipperary, Ireland, near Rosegreen and Cashel. It is one of the most famous and successful thoroughbred training establishments in the world and is the training base of Aidan O'Brien, who has trained there for the Coolmore Stud partnership since 1996.

History

Ballydoyle was established by the legendary Irish trainer Vincent O'Brien (no relation to Aidan), who began training there in the early 1950s after moving from Churchtown, County Cork. Under Vincent O'Brien, Ballydoyle produced a succession of champion racehorses, including Nijinsky, the last winner of the English Triple Crown in 1970, as well as The Minstrel, Alleged, El Gran Senor and Sadler's Wells, who became a dominant stallion at Coolmore.

Aidan O'Brien era

Aidan O'Brien took over the licence at Ballydoyle in 1996 and has since trained a record number of Group 1 winners, including Galileo, Montjeu, Giant's Causeway, Rock of Gibraltar, High Chaparral, Dylan Thomas, Camelot, Australia and City of Troy. He has broken world records for Group/Grade 1 winners in a single season and is the leading trainer in the history of several of Europe's greatest races.

Facilities

The yard is noted for its private gallops modelled on famous racecourses, its own equine swimming pool and other specialist facilities. Most of the horses trained at Ballydoyle are owned by the Coolmore partners — John Magnier, Michael Tabor and Derrick Smith — and their families.
