Yamagata Prefecture

Yamagata Prefecture (山形県)
Capital: Yamagata
Region: Tōhoku
Island: Honshu
Governor: Mieko Yoshimura
Area: approx. 9,323 km²
Population: approx. 1.07 million

Yamagata Prefecture is a prefecture of Japan located in the Tōhoku region of Honshu, on the coast of the Sea of Japan. Its capital and largest city is Yamagata. Together with Akita it formed the southern part of the historical Dewa Province.

Geography

Yamagata is a mountainous prefecture. The Ōu Mountains run along its eastern border with Miyagi and Fukushima, while the Dewa range borders Akita to the north; Niigata lies to the south. The Mogami River flows from the south through the center of the prefecture into the Sea of Japan at Sakata. Much of the population lives in inland basins — Yamagata, Yonezawa, Shinjō, and Tendō — while the Shōnai plain on the coast is a major rice-growing area. Winters are snowy, especially in the mountains, and the Zaō range is famous for its "snow monsters" (juhyō), ice-caked fir trees.

Economy and culture

Yamagata is Japan's leading producer of cherries, supplying roughly 70 percent of the national crop, and is also known for pears (La France), grapes, and Yonezawa beef, one of the great wagyu brands. Tourist draws include the hot springs of Ginzan Onsen and Zaō Onsen, the mountain temples of the Dewa Sanzan (Haguro, Gassan, and Yudono), and the Yamadera temple, where the haiku poet Matsuo Bashō composed a famous verse about stillness and cicadas in 1689. The prefecture's largest festival is the Hanagasa Matsuri, held each August in Yamagata City.

Transport

The Yamagata Shinkansen connects the capital with Fukushima and Tokyo, and the prefecture is served by Yamagata Airport and Shōnai Airport. See also Japanese shrine culture for related festival traditions elsewhere in Japan.
