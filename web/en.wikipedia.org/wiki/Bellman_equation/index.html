Bellman equation

FieldDynamic programming, control theory, economics, reinforcement learning
Named afterRichard Bellman
Introduced1950s
Related conceptsValue function, Markov decision process, Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation

A Bellman equation, named after the American mathematician Richard Bellman, is a necessary condition for optimality in the mathematical optimisation method known as dynamic programming. It expresses the value of an optimal decision problem at one point in time in terms of the payoff from a first decision and the value of the remaining (optimal) sub-problem: "an optimal policy has the property that whatever the initial state and initial decision are, the remaining decisions must constitute an optimal policy with regard to the state resulting from the first decision" — Bellman's Principle of Optimality.

Formulation

For a discrete-time Markov decision process with states s, actions a, rewards r, transition probabilities P and discount factor γ, the optimal value function satisfies:

V*(s) = maxa [ r(s,a) + γ Σs′ P(s′|s,a) V*(s′) ]

The analogous equation for the action-value function Q* underlies the reinforcement-learning algorithm Q-learning, in which the equation is used as an update target rather than solved directly. The Bellman expectation equation gives the value of a fixed policy, and the optimality equation above gives the value of the best policy. In continuous time, the counterpart is the Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman partial differential equation of optimal control theory.

Applications

Bellman equations are foundational in economics (optimal growth and search theory, where they describe asset values and reservation wages), operations research (inventory and scheduling), computer science (sequence alignment and shortest paths, via dynamic programming recurrences), and artificial intelligence, where the temporal-difference learning methods of reinforcement learning are built directly on the equation.
