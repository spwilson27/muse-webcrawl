Infrastructure Leakage Index

The Infrastructure Leakage Index (ILI) is a performance indicator for real (physical) losses from water distribution systems. Developed through the International Water Association (IWA), it is the ratio of Current Annual Real Losses (CARL) to Unavoidable Annual Real Losses (UARL): ILI = CARL / UARL.

Interpretation

An ILI of 1.0 would mean real losses equal the theoretical minimum unavoidable level, which is not economically realistic; well-managed systems in developed countries often target an ILI between 1.0 and 3.0, while systems with poor condition and management can have values well above 8. Unlike percentage-loss figures, the ILI accounts for system pressure, length of mains, and number of service connections, allowing fairer comparisons between utilities.

Unavoidable Annual Real Losses

The UARL formula estimates background leakage from mains, service connections, and storage, based on components' lengths, connection counts, and average operating pressure. It acknowledges that even a perfectly maintained network loses some water.

Use

The ILI is used by regulators and utilities for benchmarking, target setting, and prioritizing investment in non-revenue water reduction, pressure management, and active leakage control.
