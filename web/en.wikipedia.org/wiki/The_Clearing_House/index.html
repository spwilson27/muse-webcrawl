The Clearing House

The Clearing House (TCH) is a banking association and payments company owned by the largest commercial banks in the United States. Founded in 1853 as the New York Clearing House Association, it operates core payment systems including CHIPS and the RTP real-time payments network.

Founded1853
HeadquartersNew York City, U.S.
TypeBanking association / payments company
Key systemsCHIPS, RTP, EPN (ACH)

History

The New York Clearing House was established to simplify the daily exchange of checks among the city's banks. During 19th- and early 20th-century financial panics, clearing houses issued emergency loan certificates that functioned as a private lender-of-last-resort mechanism, a role later assumed by the Federal Reserve.

Payment systems

TCH operates CHIPS, the largest private-sector USD clearing system for international payments; the Electronic Payments Network (EPN), an automated clearing house; and RTP, launched in 2017 as the first new core US payments infrastructure in more than 40 years, providing instant settlement around the clock. It also operates image-based check exchange services.
