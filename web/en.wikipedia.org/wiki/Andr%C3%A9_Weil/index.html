André Weil (6 May 1906 – 6 August 1998) was a French mathematician, known for his foundational work in number theory and algebraic geometry. He was one of the most influential figures in twentieth-century mathematics and a founding member of the Nicolas Bourbaki group.

Life

Born in Paris, Weil studied at the École Normale Supérieure and travelled widely, including to India, where he taught at Aligarh Muslim University. A conscientious objector, he fled France during the Second World War, was imprisoned in Finland and France, and eventually emigrated to the United States. He taught at the University of Chicago and later at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton. His sister was the philosopher Simone Weil.

Work

Weil's proof of the Riemann hypothesis for curves over finite fields and his formulation of the Weil conjectures shaped modern algebraic geometry; the conjectures were ultimately proved by Pierre Deligne. He also contributed to the theory of algebraic groups, abelian varieties, and the formulation of the Langlands programme's context.

Honours

Weil received the Wolf Prize in Mathematics in 1979 and was a member of numerous academies.
