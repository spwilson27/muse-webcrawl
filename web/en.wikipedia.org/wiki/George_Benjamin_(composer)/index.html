Sir George William John Benjamin, CBE (born 31 January 1960) is an English composer of contemporary classical music. He is also a conductor, pianist, and teacher. He is widely regarded as one of the foremost British composers of his generation, best known internationally for his operas, particularly Written on Skin (2012), composed to a libretto by Martin Crimp, which has been performed worldwide and hailed as one of the most successful operas of the 21st century.

Early life and studies

Born in London, Benjamin began composing at the age of seven and studied piano and composition. As a teenager he travelled to Paris to study with Olivier Messiaen at the Paris Conservatoire — he was among Messiaen's last pupils — and then read music at King's College, Cambridge, where he studied with Alexander Goehr. His orchestral work Ringed by the Flat Horizon was performed at the BBC Proms in 1980 while he was still a student, making him the youngest living composer to have a work played at the Proms at that time.

Career

Benjamin's meticulously crafted output is comparatively small, reflecting a perfectionist working method. Major works include At First Light (1982), Antara (1987, for ensemble and electronics, composed at IRCAM), Three Inventions for chamber orchestra (1995), Palimpsests (2000–2002), and the Dance Figures (2004). His first operatic collaboration with Martin Crimp, the short Into the Little Hill (2006), was followed by Written on Skin (premiered at the Aix-en-Provence Festival in 2012), Lessons in Love and Violence (Royal Opera House, 2018), and Picture a day like this (2023).

As a conductor he has worked with leading orchestras and ensembles including the London Sinfonietta, the Ensemble Modern, the Concertgebouw Orchestra, and the Berlin Philharmonic. He has taught composition at the Royal College of Music and, since 2001, as Henry Purcell Professor of Composition at King's College London. He was knighted in the 2017 Birthday Honours.
