Sid Grauman

BornMarch 17, 1879, Indianapolis
DiedMarch 5, 1950, Los Angeles
OccupationTheatre impresario

Sidney Patrick Grauman (1879–1950) was an American showman and theatre impresario who built some of Hollywood's most famous movie palaces, including the Egyptian Theatre (1922) and Grauman's Chinese Theatre (1927) on Hollywood Boulevard.

Career

Grauman pioneered the lavish film premiere and the elaborate live "prologue" staged before screenings. The Chinese Theatre's forecourt, where celebrities left handprints and footprints in cement — beginning, by legend, with Norma Talmadge accidentally stepping in wet concrete — became a world-famous tourist attraction.

Legacy

Grauman also co-founded the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences and the Roosevelt Hotel. He received an honorary Academy Award in 1949.
