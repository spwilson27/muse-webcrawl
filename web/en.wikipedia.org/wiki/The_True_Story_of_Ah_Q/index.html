The True Story of Ah Q (阿Q正傳) is a novella by Lu Xun, the leading figure of modern Chinese literature. Serialized weekly from December 1921 to February 1922, it is one of the most famous and influential works of the May Fourth era and a founding text of modern Chinese fiction.

Synopsis

Ah Q is an illiterate, landless day labourer in the village of Weizhuang around the time of the 1911 Revolution. Bullied and beaten by everyone above him in the village hierarchy, he sustains himself through "spiritual victories": reinterpreting every humiliation as a triumph, and bullying those weaker than himself in turn. When the revolution reaches Weizhuang, Ah Q dreams of joining the revolutionaries and settling scores, but is shut out. Falsely implicated in a robbery of the Zhao household, he is arrested and executed by firing squad, still convinced of his own dignity, to the disappointment of a crowd that had hoped for a more dramatic beheading.

Themes and legacy

Lu Xun satirizes what he saw as flaws of the Chinese national character: self-deception, passivity, bullying the weak while fearing the strong, and a revolution that changed slogans without changing society. "Ah Q-ism" (Ah Q jingshen) became a durable Chinese term for self-deluding rationalization of defeat. The novella has been translated into many languages and is widely taught as a classic of world literature.
