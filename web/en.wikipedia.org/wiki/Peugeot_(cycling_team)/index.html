Peugeot was a French professional road cycling team sponsored by the Peugeot automobile and bicycle manufacturer. One of the oldest and most successful teams in the history of the sport, it existed in various forms from the beginning of the twentieth century until 1986. Its riders won the Tour de France ten times, and the team's black-and-white chequerboard jersey became one of the most recognisable in the peloton.

History

Peugeot began manufacturing bicycles in the 1880s and was sponsoring individual riders in the earliest Tours de France, when competitors rode as individuals rather than in national or trade teams. Peugeot riders dominated the early Tours: Louis Trousselier, Lucien Petit-Breton (twice), Philippe Thys (three times), Firmin Lambot (twice) and Jean Alavoine all won the race on Peugeot machines in the pre-team era.

When trade teams returned to the Tour after the Second World War, Peugeot fielded its own squad. In the 1960s and 1970s the team won the Tour with Roger Pingeon (1967) and Bernard Thévenet, whose victories in 1975 and 1977 — the first ending the reign of Eddy Merckx — were the team's modern high points. The squad's famous chequerboard ("damier") jersey design was introduced in 1963 and retained, with variations, for the rest of the team's existence.

Final years

In the 1980s the team developed young talent, including the Irish rider Stephen Roche and the Australian Phil Anderson, the first non-European to wear the yellow jersey, in 1981. Under directeur sportif Roger Legeay the team remained competitive, but Peugeot withdrew from sponsorship at the end of 1986. The structure continued under new sponsors, first as Z–Peugeot and then as Z, which won the 1990 Tour de France with Greg LeMond.

Notable riders

  • Bernard Thévenet — Tour de France winner 1975, 1977
  • Roger Pingeon — Tour de France winner 1967
  • Tom Simpson — world road race champion 1965
  • Phil Anderson — first non-European yellow jersey wearer
  • Stephen Roche — later Giro d'Italia, Tour de France and world champion
