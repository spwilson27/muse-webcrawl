Nam tiến

Nam tiến (Vietnamese: 南進; "southward advance" or "march to the south") refers to the historical southward expansion of Vietnam's territory from the 11th to the 19th century. Over roughly eight centuries, the Vietnamese state expanded from its heartland in the Red River Delta to encompass the central coast and the Mekong Delta.

History

The process began under the Lý and Trần dynasties through wars and diplomacy with the Cham kingdom of Champa. Major milestones included the 1471 campaign of Emperor Lê Thánh Tông, which destroyed the Cham capital of Vijaya, and the progressive annexation of remaining Cham territories in the 17th–19th centuries under the Nguyễn lords and the Nguyễn dynasty.

In the south, Vietnamese settlers moved into the Mekong Delta, territory previously under Cambodian (Khmer) control. By the mid-19th century, Vietnam had reached approximately its modern borders, incorporating lands formerly belonging to Champa and the Khmer Empire.

Consequences

Nam tiến displaced or assimilated Cham and Khmer populations, spread Vietnamese administration, language and wet-rice agriculture, and left lasting ethnic and territorial legacies in central and southern Vietnam.
