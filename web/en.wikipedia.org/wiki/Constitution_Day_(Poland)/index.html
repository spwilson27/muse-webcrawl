Constitution Day (Polish: Święto Konstytucji 3 Maja) is a Polish national and public holiday celebrated on 3 May. It commemorates the adoption of the Constitution of 3 May 1791 by the Great Sejm of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth — generally regarded as the first modern, codified national constitution in Europe and the second in the world, after that of the United States.

History

The 1791 constitution sought to reform the Commonwealth's dysfunctional politics: it abolished the liberum veto, introduced majority voting and a hereditary constitutional monarchy, granted townspeople new rights, and placed the peasantry under government protection. It survived barely a year before the Targowica Confederation and Russian invasion overthrew it, leading to the Second and Third Partitions that erased Poland from the map in 1795. The anniversary was nevertheless celebrated clandestinely under the partitions and was formally recognised as a holiday in the reborn Polish Republic in 1919. The communist authorities suppressed the celebration after 1946, preferring the 1 May workers' holiday, but the day was restored as a national holiday in 1990 following the fall of communism. It is an occasion for military parades, concerts, flag-flying and patriotic observances in Poland and in Polish diaspora communities; together with the 2 May Flag Day it forms the sequence of "May holidays" (majówka).
