Swatch Group

The Swatch Group AG is a Swiss manufacturer of watches and jewellery, and the world's largest watch company. It was formed in 1983 from the merger of the Swiss watchmaking firms ASUAG and SSIH, engineered under the leadership of Nicolas G. Hayek, and takes its name from the Swatch brand of colourful plastic quartz watches launched the same year, which helped rescue the Swiss industry from the quartz crisis.

Brands and business

The group owns brands across every price segment, from Flik Flak and Swatch through Tissot, Longines, and Rado to high-end marques such as Omega, Breguet, Blancpain, Glashütte Original, Jaquet Droz, and Harry Winston. It also supplies movements and components to much of the wider industry through subsidiaries such as ETA, Nivarox, and EM Microelectronic, a position that has periodically drawn competition scrutiny. The group is listed on the SIX Swiss Exchange and remains controlled by the Hayek family and related interests.

History

Swiss watchmaking had collapsed in the 1970s under competition from Japanese quartz movements. Hayek's strategy combined the merged groups' luxury brands with the automated, low-cost Swatch — a watch with only about 51 components, marketed as a fashion accessory — restoring profits and Swiss market leadership. The group's Omega brand serves as official timekeeper of the Olympic Games; in 2022 the "MoonSwatch" collaboration between Omega and Swatch became a global retail phenomenon.
