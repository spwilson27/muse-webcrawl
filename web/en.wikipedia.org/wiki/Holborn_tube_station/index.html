Holborn tube station

Holborn is a London Underground station in Holborn, central London, served by the Central and Piccadilly lines. Located at the junction of High Holborn and Kingsway, it is one of the busiest stations on the network and the only interchange between the two lines.

History

The station was opened by the Great Northern, Piccadilly and Brompton Railway on 15 December 1906, with platforms for what is now the Piccadilly line. The Central line platforms followed on 25 September 1933, allowing the closure of the nearby British Museum station the previous day. Holborn was also the terminus of the Piccadilly line's short Aldwych branch until its closure in 1994; one branch platform survives and is used for filming and events.

Escalators replaced the original lifts in the 1930s rebuilding, which also produced the modernised street-level building designed in the style of Charles Holden.

Layout and services

The Central line platforms lie above and roughly perpendicular to the Piccadilly line platforms, connected by escalators and passages. The station serves the London School of Economics, Lincoln's Inn Fields, and the British Museum area.
