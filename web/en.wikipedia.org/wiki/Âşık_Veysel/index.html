Âşık Veysel Şatıroğlu (25 October 1894 – 21 March 1973) was a Turkish Alevi folk poet, singer and virtuoso of the bağlama, and one of the most celebrated figures of the Turkish âşık (minstrel) tradition. Blinded by smallpox as a child, he composed and performed songs of love, mortality, nature and unity that became central to Turkey's folk repertoire.

Life

Veysel was born in the village of Sivrialan in Sivas province and lost the sight of both eyes by the age of seven, following smallpox and an accident. His father gave him a bağlama to console him, and he was taught by local minstrels. Brought to national attention in the 1930s after meeting the folklorist Ahmet Kutsi Tecer, he travelled widely, taught bağlama at Village Institutes, and broadcast on Turkish radio.

Work and legacy

His best-known song, "Uzun İnce Bir Yoldayım" ("I am on a long, narrow road"), is a meditation on life as a journey between two doors. Other songs such as "Kara Toprak" ("Black Earth") express humility before nature and death. In 1965 the Turkish parliament granted him a pension in recognition of his service to the Turkish language and national culture. His village home in Sivrialan is now a museum, and his songs have been recorded by generations of Turkish folk and rock musicians.
