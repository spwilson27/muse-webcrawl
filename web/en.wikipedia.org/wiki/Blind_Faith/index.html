Blind Faith

OriginLondon, England
Years active1968–1969
GenresBlues rock, folk rock
MembersEric Clapton, Steve Winwood, Ginger Baker, Ric Grech

Blind Faith were an English rock supergroup formed in London in 1968 by guitarist Eric Clapton and singer/keyboardist Steve Winwood, with drummer Ginger Baker and bassist Ric Grech. Clapton and Baker had played together in Cream, while Winwood came from the Spencer Davis Group and Traffic. The band released one self-titled album in August 1969, which topped the charts in the United States and the United Kingdom, before disbanding after a single tour.

History

Clapton and Winwood began jamming together at Winwood's cottage in Berkshire after the break-up of Cream. Baker, Clapton's bandmate in Cream, joined despite Clapton's reservations about recreating Cream's dynamic, and Grech of Family completed the line-up. The band made its public debut with a free concert in Hyde Park, London, on 7 June 1969 before an estimated 100,000 people.

Album

The album Blind Faith (1969) was recorded at Morgan and Olympic Studios in London. It contains six tracks, including Winwood's "Can't Find My Way Home" and "Had to Cry Today", Clapton's "Presence of the Lord", and a cover of Buddy Holly's "Well All Right". The original UK cover photograph, showing a young topless girl holding a model spaceship, was controversial in the United States, where the record was issued with an alternative cover showing the band.

Tour and break-up

A tour of Scandinavia and the United States followed the album sessions, but the band found itself expected to perform Cream and Traffic material, and the musicians' differing directions led Clapton to withdraw. Blind Faith played their final concert in Hawaii in August 1969 and split shortly afterwards. Winwood re-formed Traffic, while Clapton went on to Derek and the Dominos.
