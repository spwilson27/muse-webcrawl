The Four Corners is a region of the southwestern United States consisting of the southwestern corner of Colorado, the southeastern corner of Utah, the northeastern corner of Arizona and the northwestern corner of New Mexico. It is the only point in the United States where the boundaries of four states intersect, and gives its name to the surrounding region on the Colorado Plateau.

The monument

The quadripoint is marked by the Four Corners Monument, managed by the Navajo Nation, where visitors can stand in four states at once. The point was first surveyed in 1868 and formally marked by federal surveyors in the 1870s; despite periodic reports that the marker is misplaced, U.S. authorities have consistently held that the monument marks the legally recognised corner as originally surveyed.

The region

The wider Four Corners region is noted for its archaeology, including the cliff dwellings of Mesa Verde and the great houses of Chaco Canyon, and is composed largely of tribal lands of the Navajo, Hopi, Ute and Zuni peoples.
