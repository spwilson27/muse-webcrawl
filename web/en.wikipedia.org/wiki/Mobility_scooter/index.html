Mobility scooter

A typical four-wheeled mobility scooter

A mobility scooter is an electric personal transporter designed for people with physical disabilities or limited mobility who are able to sit upright and operate simple controls. It typically has three or four wheels, a seat mounted over a flat base, handlebars or a tiller for steering, and is powered by rechargeable batteries.

History

The first mobility scooter is generally credited to Allan Thieme, who built one in 1968 in Bridgeport, Michigan, for a family member who had multiple sclerosis; his company, Amigo Mobility, commercialised the design. Scooters became common from the 1980s onwards as battery technology improved and prices fell.

Types and regulation

Scooters range from small, lightweight travel models that dismantle for transport in a car, to heavy-duty road-going models. In the United Kingdom, Class 2 scooters are limited to 4 mph and used on pavements, while Class 3 scooters may travel at up to 8 mph on the road and must be registered. Rules differ widely in other countries; in many places mobility scooters are treated as pedestrian devices rather than vehicles.

Use and issues

Mobility scooters are widely used by older people and people with conditions affecting walking. Debates around their use concern pavement safety, speed, access to public transport, and the lack of mandatory training or insurance in some jurisdictions.
