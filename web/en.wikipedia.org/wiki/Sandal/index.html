Sandal

A sandal is an open type of footwear consisting of a sole held to the foot by straps passing over the instep and sometimes around the ankle. Sandals are among the oldest forms of shoe, worn in warm climates and for casual wear worldwide.

History

The oldest known sandals, made of sagebrush bark and found in Fort Rock Cave, Oregon, date to roughly 10,000 years ago. Ancient Egyptians made sandals from papyrus and palm leaves; Greeks and Romans developed leather sandals, including the Roman soldier's caliga. In Asia, wooden geta and straw zōri were traditional in Japan, and paduka in India.

Construction

Sandals may be made of leather, rubber, rope, wood or synthetic materials. Styles range from simple flip-flops (thongs) with a post between the toes, to strapped hiking sandals and heeled fashion sandals. Because they expose much of the foot, sandals suit hot weather but offer little protection.

Modern use

Today sandals are worn globally as casual summer footwear, beachwear, and in some religious and traditional dress. Waterproof sports sandals are used for hiking and water activities.
