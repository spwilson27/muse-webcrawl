Airbnb, Inc. is an American company operating an online marketplace for short- and long-term homestays and experiences in various countries and regions. The company acts as a broker, receiving commissions from each booking; it does not own the properties listed. Founded in 2008 by Brian Chesky, Joe Gebbia and Nathan Blecharczyk, Airbnb grew from renting out air mattresses in the founders' San Francisco flat — hence the original name "AirBed & Breakfast" — into one of the world's largest accommodation platforms.

History

The company joined the Y Combinator accelerator in 2009 and expanded internationally in the early 2010s, opening offices in Europe and beyond. It diversified into "Experiences" (hosted activities) in 2016 and luxury and hotel offerings thereafter. Airbnb's initial public offering took place in December 2020, one of the largest of that year, despite the severe impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on travel. It was added to the S&P 500 index in 2023.

Business model

Hosts list spare rooms or entire homes; guests book and pay through the platform, which charges service fees to both sides. A system of two-way reviews, host guarantees (AirCover) and identity checks underpin trust between strangers. Listings number in the millions across more than 220 countries and regions.

Criticism and regulation

Airbnb has been criticised for contributing to rising rents and housing shortages in tourist cities, for removing long-term rental stock, and for operating in a regulatory grey zone. Many cities — including New York, Paris, Barcelona and Amsterdam — have imposed registration requirements, night limits or outright restrictions on short-term rentals in response.
