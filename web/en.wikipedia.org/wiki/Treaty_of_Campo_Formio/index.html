Treaty of Campo Formio

The Treaty of Campo Formio was signed on 17 October 1797 between Revolutionary France, represented by General Napoleon Bonaparte, and the Habsburg Monarchy of Austria, ending the War of the First Coalition.

Terms

Austria recognized French possession of the Austrian Netherlands (Belgium) and French control of the left bank of the Rhine, and accepted the Cisalpine Republic—a French client state in Lombardy. In compensation Austria received the territory of the defunct Republic of Venice, including the city of Venice itself, Venetian lands east of the Adige, Istria, and Dalmatia, the republic having been partitioned between the two signatories. Venice, independent for over a thousand years, ceased to exist. Secret articles promised Austria further German territory and committed France to support arrangements at the forthcoming Congress of Rastatt.

Consequences

The treaty redrew the map of Italy and Germany, confirmed France's "natural frontiers" policy, and enormously boosted Bonaparte's prestige at home. Austria, humiliated but compensated, entered the War of the Second Coalition in 1799 after the Rastatt negotiations collapsed.
