Johann Kaspar Lavater

Born: 15 November 1741, Zurich, Switzerland

Died: 2 January 1801 (aged 59), Zurich

Occupation: Pastor, writer, philosopher

Known for: Popularising physiognomy

Johann Kaspar (or Jean-Gaspard) Lavater (15 November 1741 – 2 January 1801) was a Swiss poet, writer, philosopher and pastor, best known for his work on physiognomy, the supposed art of judging character from facial features.

Life and work

Ordained a minister in the Swiss Reformed Church, Lavater served as pastor in Zurich and became a celebrated preacher and correspondent of intellectuals across Europe, including Goethe and Herder. His Essays on Physiognomy (published in German from 1775 to 1778) were lavishly illustrated and enormously popular in translation, sparking a Europe-wide fashion for silhouette portraits and physiognomic study.

Legacy

Although physiognomy was later discredited as a pseudoscience, Lavater's work influenced literature and art of the Romantic period and remains a landmark in the cultural history of the face. He was shot by a French soldier during the occupation of Zurich in 1799 and died of his wounds some fifteen months later.
