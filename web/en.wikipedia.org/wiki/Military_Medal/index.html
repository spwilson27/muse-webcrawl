Military Medal

Type: Military decoration
Awarded for: Acts of gallantry and devotion to duty under fire
Country: United Kingdom
Established: 25 March 1916
Total awarded: More than 115,000 (First World War era the majority)

The Military Medal (MM) was a military decoration awarded to personnel of the British Army and other armed forces, and to personnel of other Commonwealth countries, below commissioned rank, for bravery in battle on land. Instituted on 25 March 1916, it was the other ranks' equivalent of the Military Cross (MC), which was awarded to commissioned officers and warrant officers.

History

The medal was established during the First World War to recognize acts of gallantry by non-commissioned officers and men for which existing awards were unsuitable. Recipients were entitled to use the post-nominal letters "MM". During the First World War alone, well over 115,000 Military Medals were awarded, along with several thousand bars for subsequent acts. Women were eligible, and the medal was awarded to a number of nurses and members of women's auxiliary services; the first women recipients were decorated for bravery during the Battle of the Somme.

Awards continued through the Second World War and later conflicts. The medal was discontinued in 1993, when the distinction between officers and other ranks in gallantry awards was removed and the Military Cross was opened to all ranks.

Description

The medal is a silver disc bearing the effigy of the reigning monarch on the obverse. The reverse shows the inscription "For Bravery in the Field" in four lines, surrounded by a laurel wreath and surmounted by the royal cypher. The ribbon is dark blue, red, and white in vertical stripes. A recipient of further awards wore a silver bar on the ribbon.
