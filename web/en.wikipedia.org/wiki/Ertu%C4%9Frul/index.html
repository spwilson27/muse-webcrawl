Ertuğrul

Ertuğrul
Died: c. 1280, Söğüt
Spouse: Halime Hatun (legendary)
Children: Osman I, Saru Batu Savcı Bey, Gündüz Bey
Father: Suleyman Shah (legendary) or Gündüz Alp
Tribe: Kayı tribe of the Oghuz Turks

Ertuğrul (also spelled Ertoğrul; died c. 1280) was a Turkic tribal leader of the Kayı tribe and the father of Osman I, the founder of the Ottoman Empire. According to Ottoman tradition, he led his tribe into Anatolia and received the town of Söğüt as a fief from the Seljuk Sultanate of Rum, laying the groundwork for the Ottoman state.

Biography

Very little is known with certainty about Ertuğrul's life. Ottoman chronicles written more than a century after his death relate that the Kayı tribe migrated westward from Central Asia, settled first near Ahlat and Erzincan, and that Ertuğrul was granted land around Söğüt, near the Byzantine frontier, after rendering military service to the Seljuk sultan Alaeddin Kayqubad I. From Söğüt, Ertuğrul expanded his domain at the expense of his Byzantine neighbours. His tomb at Söğüt remains a place of commemoration, and an annual festival honours him there.

Historicity

Modern historians treat much of the traditional account with caution, as contemporary documents are scarce; coins attributed to Osman naming his father are among the few material traces. Nevertheless, Ertuğrul is a revered figure in Turkish culture, celebrated in the popular television series Diriliş: Ertuğrul (2014–2019), and the Ottoman frigate Ertuğrul, lost off Japan in 1890, was named in his honour.
