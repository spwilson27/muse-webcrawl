Atalanta Bergamasca Calcio, commonly referred to as Atalanta, is a professional football club based in Bergamo, Lombardy, Italy, founded in 1907 and named after the mythological huntress Atalanta. The club plays in black-and-blue vertically striped shirts and has spent most of its history in Serie A, earning the nickname Regina delle provinciali ("Queen of the provincial clubs") as the most successful Italian side from outside a regional capital.

History

For most of the 20th century Atalanta were a mid-table Serie A club, winning their first major trophy, the Coppa Italia, in 1963. Their youth academy became famous for producing internationals, and the team reached the semi-finals of the European Cup Winners' Cup in 1988 while in Serie B.

Under coach Gian Piero Gasperini, appointed in 2016, Atalanta transformed into one of Europe's most admired attacking sides despite a modest budget. They finished third in Serie A three seasons running, reached the quarter-finals of the UEFA Champions League in 2020, and on 22 May 2024 won the UEFA Europa League in Dublin, defeating Bayer Leverkusen 3–0 with an Ademola Lookman hat-trick — the club's first European trophy and only the second major title in its history.

Stadium and supporters

Atalanta play at the Gewiss Stadium (formerly Stadio Atleti Azzurri d'Italia), which the club purchased from the city in 2017 and extensively rebuilt. The club's most heated rivalry is with neighbouring Brescia.

Honours

  • UEFA Europa League: 2023–24
  • Coppa Italia: 1962–63
  • Serie B titles: six, most recently 2010–11
