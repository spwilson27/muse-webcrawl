Côte d'Ivoire, also known as Ivory Coast and officially the Republic of Côte d'Ivoire, is a country on the southern coast of West Africa. It borders Liberia and Guinea to the west, Mali and Burkina Faso to the north, Ghana to the east, and the Gulf of Guinea (Atlantic Ocean) to the south. Its political capital is Yamoussoukro, while its largest city and economic centre is the port of Abidjan. Côte d'Ivoire is the world's largest producer of cocoa beans, which anchor an economy also based on coffee, cashews, palm oil, rubber, and petroleum.

History

The territory was home to a succession of African states — including the Kong Empire and, in the east, Akan kingdoms such as the Baoulé and the Ashanti-influenced Anyi — before France established protectorates in the nineteenth century and formally made Côte d'Ivoire a colony in 1893. It became part of French West Africa, developing plantation agriculture based on coffee and cocoa with forced labour that persisted in practice well into the colonial era.

The planter and politician Félix Houphouët-Boigny led the country to independence on 7 August 1960 and ruled until his death in 1993. His pro-Western, market-oriented policies produced the "Ivorian miracle" of rapid growth in the 1960s–1970s, drawing millions of immigrant workers from neighbouring states. Houphouët-Boigny moved the capital to his home town of Yamoussoukro, where he built the Basilica of Our Lady of Peace, among the world's largest churches. After his death, struggles over succession and the divisive doctrine of ivoirité — used to exclude northern, largely Muslim Ivorians and the presidential candidate Alassane Ouattara — led to a coup (1999), a civil war (2002–2007) that split the country between a rebel-held north and government south, and a second crisis after the disputed 2010 election, when incumbent Laurent Gbagbo refused to cede power to Ouattara; fighting ended in April 2011 with Gbagbo's arrest, aided by French and UN forces. Ouattara has since presided over strong economic growth and relative stability, winning re-election in 2015 and 2020 under a new (2016) constitution.

Geography and economy

The south is a belt of coastal lagoons and dense rainforest giving way to savanna in the north; Mount Nimba (1,752 m) in the west is the highest point. The economy is the largest in francophone West Africa and a regional hub through the port of Abidjan. Côte d'Ivoire produces roughly 40% of the world's cocoa (from Theobroma cacao), sold on world commodity markets, and is also a leading exporter of cashew nuts and a significant producer of coffee, rubber, palm oil, gold, and offshore oil and gas.

Demographics and culture

The population of about 29 million comprises more than sixty ethnic groups — among them the Akan (including Baoulé), Krou, Southern and Northern Mandé, and Voltaic (Gur) peoples — and a large immigrant population from Burkina Faso, Mali, and Guinea. French is the official language; Dioula serves as a trade language. Religions are divided mainly between Islam (especially in the north), Christianity (especially in the south), and indigenous beliefs. Ivorian culture is internationally known through music styles such as zouglou and coupé-décalé, and the national football team, the Elephants, won the Africa Cup of Nations in 1992, 2015, and 2023 (hosted at home).
