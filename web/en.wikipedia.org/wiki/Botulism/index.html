Botulism

Botulism is a rare but serious paralytic illness caused by botulinum toxin produced by the bacterium Clostridium botulinum (and related species). The toxin blocks acetylcholine release, causing flaccid paralysis.

Botulism
CauseBotulinum toxin
TypesFoodborne, wound, infant, iatrogenic
TreatmentAntitoxin, supportive care, ventilation

Signs and causes

Symptoms start with double vision, drooping eyelids, difficulty speaking and swallowing, then descending paralysis that can cause respiratory failure. Foodborne botulism comes from improperly canned or preserved foods; infant botulism is linked to spores (e.g. in honey); wound botulism affects people who inject drugs.

Prevention and treatment

Proper canning, heating food, and not feeding honey to infants under one year prevent most cases. Treatment is urgent antitoxin plus intensive care. The same toxin, in tiny controlled doses, is used medically and cosmetically as Botox.
