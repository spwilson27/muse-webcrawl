Intact forest landscape

An intact forest landscape (IFL) is an unbroken natural landscape of a forest ecosystem and its habitat–plant community components, within a current forest area, showing no signs of significant human activity, and large enough that all native biodiversity, including viable populations of wide-ranging species, can be maintained.

The concept was developed by a consortium including Greenpeace and the World Resources Institute, which map IFLs globally using satellite imagery. Areas qualify if they are at least 500 km², at least 10 km wide, and free of remotely sensed signs of human disturbance such as settlements, roads, logging, or agriculture.

Studies have found that the world's IFL area declined by roughly 7% between 2000 and 2013, with losses concentrated in the tropics, driven by logging, agriculture, and infrastructure expansion.
