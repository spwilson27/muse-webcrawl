Tinamiformes

Tinamiformes (tinamous)
Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Chordata
Class: Aves
Order: Tinamiformes
Family: Tinamidae
Species: about 46

The tinamous, order Tinamiformes and family Tinamidae, are ground-dwelling birds native to Mexico, Central America and South America. Although they superficially resemble quail and grouse, they are palaeognaths related to the flightless ratites such as ostriches and emus; unlike their relatives, tinamous can fly, though weakly and reluctantly.

Description

Tinamous range from the small dwarf tinamou, about 20 cm long, to the grey tinamou at nearly 50 cm. They have compact bodies, short rounded wings, cryptic brown or grey plumage, and strong legs adapted for running. Their glossy, porcelain-like eggs are strikingly coloured, and in most species the male incubates the eggs and raises the chicks.

Habitat and behaviour

They inhabit environments from tropical rainforest to high Andean grassland, feeding on seeds, fruit and small invertebrates. Tinamous are shy and are more often heard — their flute-like whistles are characteristic sounds of Neotropical forests — than seen. Several species are threatened by hunting and habitat loss.
