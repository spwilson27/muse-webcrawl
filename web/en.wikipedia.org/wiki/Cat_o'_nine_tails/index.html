Cat o' nine tails

The cat o’ nine tails was a naval flogging instrument.

The cat o’ nine tails, commonly shortened to the cat, is a multi-tailed whip used as an instrument of corporal punishment, most famously in the British Royal Navy. It consists of a handle of rope or wood with nine knotted cords, typically of cotton or leather, designed to lacerate the skin of the back.

Naval use

Flogging with the cat was a formal punishment for offences aboard ship from the age of sail into the 19th century. Sentences were imposed under naval discipline and carried out before the assembled crew; the number of lashes varied with the offence, historically reaching into the hundreds in severe cases. The ship’s cat was traditionally kept in a red baize bag—the origin sometimes suggested for the phrase “let the cat out of the bag.”

Construction and practice

The nine tails were often made from the strands of a rope unlaid into three groups, each cord knotted near its end to increase injury. A fresh cat might be made for a punishment, and naval surgeons attended floggings and could halt them on medical grounds.

Abolition

Corporal punishment was progressively restricted during the 19th century; the Royal Navy suspended flogging in 1879 and it was later formally abolished, while the British Army abolished it in 1881. The cat survives mainly in museums, naval tradition, and figurative expressions such as “no room to swing a cat.”
