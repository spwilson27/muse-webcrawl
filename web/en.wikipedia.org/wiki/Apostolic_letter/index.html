Apostolic letter

An apostolic letter (litterae apostolicae) is a formal document issued by the pope in his capacity as bishop of Rome and head of the Catholic Church. The term covers several kinds of papal correspondence and legislation, ranging from brief letters to individuals to documents addressed to the whole Church.

Forms

Apostolic letters may be issued motu proprio, on the pope's own initiative, often to make legal or administrative changes. An apostolic exhortation is a letter encouraging the faithful on a pastoral theme, frequently following a synod of bishops; well-known examples include Evangelii gaudium. Letters in the form of a brief are shorter and less solemn than bulls.

Use

Popes use apostolic letters to proclaim saints and blesseds, erect dioceses, appoint holidays, clarify teaching and mark anniversaries. In the New Testament, “apostolic letters” can also refer more loosely to epistles attributed to apostles.

Hierarchy of documents

In solemnity, apostolic letters generally rank below apostolic constitutions and encyclicals but above rescripts and private correspondence. Their authority depends on their content and intended audience.
