Cave painting

Cave paintings are a type of parietal art found on the walls or ceilings of caves worldwide, dating predominantly to prehistoric times. The oldest known cave paintings are more than 40,000 years old.

Famous sites

Celebrated examples include the Lascaux and Chauvet caves in France, Altamira in Spain, and caves in Sulawesi, Indonesia, where a warty pig painting dated to at least about 45,500 years ago is among the earliest figurative art known. Subjects are usually large wild animals — bison, horses, deer — human hands (stencils and prints), and abstract signs.

Techniques and interpretation

Pigments were made from ochre, charcoal, manganese dioxide and hematite, applied by finger, brush or blowing through tubes. Interpretations have included hunting magic, shamanic ritual and early symbolic communication, though purpose remains debated.
