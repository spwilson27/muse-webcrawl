Warm front

A warm front is a density discontinuity located at the leading edge of a homogeneous warm air mass, and is typically located on the equator-facing edge of an isotherm gradient. Warm fronts lie within broader areas of low pressure and move from the southwest to the northeast in the Northern Hemisphere, with the air behind the front warmer and more moist than the air ahead of it.

Characteristics

As warm air advances, it slides gently upward over the retreating wedge of cold air along a shallow slope. This produces a characteristic sequence of clouds ahead of the surface front: cirrus, cirrostratus, altostratus, and finally nimbostratus bringing steady, widespread precipitation. After the front passes, skies clear, temperatures rise, humidity increases and winds shift (from easterly or southeasterly to southerly or southwesterly in the Northern Hemisphere). Fog often forms in the cold air ahead of the front.

Symbol

On weather maps a warm front is drawn as a solid line with semicircles pointing in the direction of movement, traditionally coloured red.
