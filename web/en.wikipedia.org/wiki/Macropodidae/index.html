Macropodidae

Macropodidae is a family of marsupials known as macropods, comprising the kangaroos, wallabies, tree-kangaroos, wallaroos, pademelons and quokkas, as well as several extinct genera. Native to Australia and New Guinea, macropods range in size from the tiny musky rat-kangaroo's relatives to the red kangaroo, the largest living marsupial.

Characteristics

Macropods are characterised by large hind feet — the family name means "large foot" — long muscular tails used for balance, and a hopping gait that is remarkably energy-efficient at speed. Most are herbivorous grazers or browsers, with specialised stomachs for fermenting plant material.

Reproduction

Females carry a single young, born at a very early stage of development, in a forward-opening pouch; many species exhibit embryonic diapause, delaying development of a second embryo until the pouch is vacated.
