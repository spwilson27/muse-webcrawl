Overview

The Bogd Khan (1869–1924), born Agvaanlobsangchoijinyamdanzanvanchug, was the eighth Jebtsundamba Khutuktu, the highest lama of Tibetan Buddhism in Mongolia, and the last khan of Mongolia, reigning from the declaration of independence from Qing China in 1911 until his death in 1924.

Reign

Proclaimed khan when Mongolia broke from the collapsing Qing, the Bogd Khan ruled a theocratic monarchy recognised fully only by Tibet and Russia. Chinese troops under Xu Shuzheng abolished Mongolian autonomy in 1919; in 1921 the White Russian adventurer Roman von Ungern-Sternberg seized Urga and restored him, before Mongolian partisans with Red Army support took the capital and kept the Khan as a constitutional figurehead until his death. The Mongolian People's Republic was then proclaimed (1924), and the search for his reincarnation was forbidden — the ninth Jebtsundamba was recognised only after socialism fell.
