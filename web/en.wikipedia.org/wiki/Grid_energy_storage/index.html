Grid energy storage

Grid energy storage (also called large-scale energy storage) is a collection of methods used for energy storage on a large scale within an electrical power grid. Electrical energy is stored during times when electricity is plentiful and inexpensive (especially from variable renewable sources such as wind and solar power) or when demand is low, and later returned to the grid when demand is high or generation is low, buying and selling in effect at different times.

Need for storage

Electricity networks must balance supply and demand instant by instant. Historically this was done by adjusting the output of dispatchable fossil-fuel generators. As grids incorporate more variable renewable energy, storage provides services including frequency regulation, peak shaving, load shifting, energy arbitrage, spinning reserve, and deferral of transmission upgrades. Storage also supports black-start capability after blackouts.

Forms of storage

Pumped hydroelectricity

Pumped-storage hydroelectricity accounts for the overwhelming majority of stored energy worldwide. Water is pumped uphill to a reservoir when power is cheap and released through turbines when needed. Round-trip efficiency is typically 70–85%.

Batteries

Grid-scale battery systems, predominantly lithium-ion, have grown rapidly since the late 2010s, with installations such as the Hornsdale Power Reserve in Australia demonstrating sub-second frequency response. Flow batteries store energy in liquid electrolytes and suit longer durations.

Other technologies

Compressed air energy storage (CAES) stores air in underground caverns; flywheels provide very fast, short-duration response; thermal storage, including molten salt at concentrated solar plants, stores heat; and power-to-gas converts surplus electricity to hydrogen or methane. Gravity-based systems lift and lower heavy masses.

Economics and deployment

The levelised cost of storage has fallen sharply with battery prices, making storage increasingly competitive with gas peaker plants for short durations. By the mid-2020s, global grid battery capacity was growing at tens of gigawatts per year, led by China, the United States and Europe. Long-duration energy storage (over 8–10 hours) remains an active area of policy and research for deep decarbonisation.
