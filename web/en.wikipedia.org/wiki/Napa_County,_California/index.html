Napa County, California

Napa County is a county in the U.S. state of California, north of San Francisco Bay. Its county seat and largest city is Napa. With a population of about 138,000 (2020 census), it is one of California's original 27 counties, created in 1850. The county is world-famous for the Napa Valley wine region.

Geography and wine

The county covers about 2,040 square kilometres, encompassing the Napa Valley floor — framed by the Mayacamas Mountains to the west and the Vaca Mountains to the east — and drained by the Napa River. The valley's Mediterranean climate and varied soils support the Napa Valley AVA, established in 1981, whose Cabernet Sauvignon in particular ranks among the world's most celebrated and expensive wines. The 1976 Judgment of Paris tasting, in which Napa wines defeated French competitors, brought the region international fame.

Communities and economy

Besides Napa, towns include St. Helena, Calistoga — known for its hot springs and geysers — Yountville, and American Canyon. Wine production, tourism and hospitality dominate the economy, though the county has repeatedly suffered destructive wildfires, notably in 2017 and 2020, which burned significant areas and damaged wineries.
