Intercontinental Cup

The Intercontinental Cup is the name borne by several club football competitions contested between the champions of different confederations. Most famously, from 1960 to 2004 it matched the winners of the European Cup (later UEFA Champions League) against the winners of South America's Copa Libertadores, and was regarded in its era as the world club championship.

The original competition (1960–2004)

Founded in 1960, the cup was first played over two legs, with a play-off if needed; early editions were marked by brilliant football and notorious violence, notably the 1969 tie between Estudiantes and Milan. From 1980 the final became a single match in Tokyo, sponsored by Toyota, which stabilized the fixture. Peñarol, Nacional, Boca Juniors, Milan and Real Madrid were among the most successful clubs. In 2005 the competition was absorbed into the FIFA Club World Cup. In 2017 FIFA formally recognized all Intercontinental Cup winners as world club champions, and in 2024 the name was revived for a new annual FIFA competition whose final pits the UEFA Champions League winners against the champions emerging from the other confederations' play-offs.

Other uses

The name has also been used in other sports and contexts, including cricket's ICC Intercontinental Cup for non-Test nations, and the Afro-Asian Club Championship and similar defunct competitions in football.
