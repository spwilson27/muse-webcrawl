RCA Records is an American record label owned by Sony Music Entertainment, a subsidiary of Sony Corporation of America. It is one of Sony Music's four flagship labels, alongside Columbia Records, Epic Records and Arista Records. The label's history traces back to the Victor Talking Machine Company, founded in 1901, and it took its name from the Radio Corporation of America (RCA), which acquired Victor in 1929.

RCA has been home to many of the most successful recording artists of the twentieth and twenty-first centuries, including Elvis Presley, David Bowie, Dolly Parton, and in later decades Britney Spears, Justin Timberlake, Alicia Keys, and Miley Cyrus.

History

Origins

The Victor Talking Machine Company was founded in Camden, New Jersey, in 1901 by Eldridge R. Johnson, building on Emile Berliner's gramophone patents. Its "His Master's Voice" trademark, showing the dog Nipper listening to a gramophone, became one of the world's best-known brand images. In 1929 the Radio Corporation of America bought Victor, forming RCA Victor.

Golden era

RCA Victor was a leading label in the big band, country, and early rock and roll eras. In 1956 it released Elvis Presley's self-titled debut album, the first rock and roll album to top the Billboard chart. The label also pioneered the 45 rpm single format in 1949 and the Dynagroove recording process in the 1960s.

Later ownership

Following RCA Corporation's acquisition by General Electric in 1986, the record division was sold to Bertelsmann (BMG). In 2004 BMG merged with Sony to form Sony BMG, and in 2008 Sony acquired Bertelsmann's stake, placing RCA under Sony Music Entertainment. In 2011 the label was restructured, absorbing the Jive and Arista rosters into RCA.
