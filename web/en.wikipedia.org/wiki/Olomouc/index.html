Olomouc (German: Olmütz) is a city in the Czech Republic, in the eastern province of Moravia, situated on the Morava River. It is the sixth-largest Czech city and the administrative centre of the Olomouc Region. Historically the capital of Moravia and seat of a bishopric founded in 1063, Olomouc is today a university city — Palacký University, founded in 1573, is the second-oldest university in the Czech lands — and is celebrated for its Baroque monuments and six ornate fountains.

History

A Slavic fortified settlement existed here by the 9th–10th centuries under Great Moravia. The appanage principality and bishopric made Olomouc a principal Moravian centre through the Middle Ages, rivalling Brno. The city suffered heavily in the Thirty Years' War and was occupied by Sweden from 1642 to 1650, after which the Moravian capital shifted to Brno; Maria Theresa later made it a fortress. King Wenceslaus III was murdered in Olomouc in 1306, ending the Přemyslid dynasty. In 1848 the young Franz Joseph I was crowned emperor in the archbishop's palace here, and in 1850 the Punctation of Olmütz humbled Prussia in its rivalry with Austria.

Sights

The historic centre, largely preserved, is a national heritage reservation. Its centrepiece is the Holy Trinity Column, inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2000 as a supreme example of Central European Baroque; the Upper Square also holds the astronomical clock, rebuilt in socialist-realist style in the 1950s. Other landmarks include St. Wenceslas Cathedral with its 100-metre south tower, the Archdiocesan Museum, the Church of St. Maurice, and the six Baroque fountains depicting Neptune, Hercules, Jupiter, Triton, Caesar, and Mercury. Olomouc is also known for Olomoucké tvarůžky, a pungent ripened soft cheese made in nearby Loštice since the 15th century.
