In Greek mythology, Oceanus (Greek: Ὠκεανός, Ōkeanós) was a Titan son of Uranus (Sky) and Gaia (Earth), and the personification of the sea — conceived in early Greek thought as a great river encircling the flat disc of the world. With his sister-consort Tethys he fathered the river gods and the Oceanids, the spirits of springs, streams and seas. The modern word "ocean" derives from his name.

In Homer and Hesiod

In the Iliad, Oceanus is called the origin of all things — even of the gods — and Hera speaks of visiting "Oceanus, from whom the gods sprang, and mother Tethys" at the ends of the earth. Hesiod's Theogony makes him the eldest Titan, firstborn of Uranus and Gaia, and lists his three thousand daughters, the Oceanids, and his sons the rivers. Notably, Oceanus did not join the other Titans in their war against Zeus, and in some traditions he mediated disputes among the gods.

Role in myth

Oceanus appears as a wise, peaceable elder. In Aeschylus's Prometheus Bound he visits the chained Prometheus on a winged steed and counsels moderation. His daughter Styx became the river by which the gods swore their most binding oaths. As Greek geography expanded, the encircling river was pushed to the margins of the known world and eventually identified with the outer sea beyond the Pillars of Hercules.

Iconography

In Hellenistic and Roman art Oceanus is depicted as a mature, bearded man, often reclining with a ship's rudder or oar, a cornucopia, and marine attributes such as bull's horns, crab claws in his hair, and water streaming from his beard. Grand Oceanus masks are frequent in Roman mosaics and fountains, including the famous Trevi Fountain in Rome, where Oceanus commands the central niche.

Oceanus and geography

Early Greek maps, such as that associated with Hecataeus, drew the inhabited world as a circle surrounded by Ocean. Herodotus doubted the river's existence, but the conception persisted into Roman geography and medieval mappaemundi, which retained an encircling ocean around the three known continents.
