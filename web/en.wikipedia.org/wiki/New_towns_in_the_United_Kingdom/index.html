The new towns in the United Kingdom are towns that were planned and built under the authority of the New Towns Act 1946 and later legislation, mainly to relocate populations from overcrowded, bomb-damaged cities such as London and Glasgow. They were developed by publicly owned development corporations and were influenced by the garden city movement of Ebenezer Howard.

Waves of designation

The programme is usually divided into three waves. The first, in the late 1940s, created a ring of towns around London — including Stevenage (the first, designated in 1946), Crawley, Hemel Hempstead, Harlow, and Basildon — plus towns elsewhere such as East Kilbride in Scotland and Peterlee in County Durham. A second wave in the early 1960s included Skelmersdale, Runcorn, and Washington. The third and largest wave, from the mid-1960s, created Milton Keynes, Northampton, Peterborough, Redditch, Telford, and Warrington.

Legacy

Some 32 new towns were designated across the United Kingdom, housing millions of people. The development corporations were wound up from the late 1970s onward and their assets transferred or sold. Later initiatives, such as the "eco-towns" announced in the 2000s and garden community programmes, consciously echoed the new towns model, and Milton Keynes received city status in 2022.
