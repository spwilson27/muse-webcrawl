Farm cat

A farm cat resting near a barn; farm cats are kept primarily for pest control.

A farm cat, also known as a barn cat, is a domestic cat, usually of mixed breed, that lives primarily outdoors on agricultural properties and functions as a form of pest control. Farm cats may be feral or semi-feral, though many are socialised to people to varying degrees.

Role

Farm cats hunt rats, mice, and other small vermin that would otherwise damage stored grain, feed, and crops and spread disease among livestock. In exchange, farmers traditionally provide shelter in barns or outbuildings, some supplemental food, and basic care. Even well-fed cats continue to hunt, which makes them more effective than their numbers alone would suggest.

History

Cats were likely first domesticated in the Near East roughly 9,000–10,000 years ago in association with early grain farming, as wildcats were drawn to rodents in grain stores. The relationship between cats and farms is therefore as old as agriculture itself. Cats were also carried aboard ships as "ship's cats" for the same rodent-control reason.

Care and welfare

Modern animal welfare guidance encourages farmers to provide farm cats with adequate food, clean water, shelter, veterinary care, and spaying or neutering to control population growth. Working-cat adoption programmes run by animal shelters place unsocialised cats in barns, warehouses, and workshops as an alternative to euthanasia.

Related animals

Farm cats belong to the broader category of working animals, alongside mousers kept in shops, distilleries, stables, and historic buildings, including the famous mousers of government residences and breweries.
