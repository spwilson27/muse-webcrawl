Fuel cell vehicle

A fuel cell vehicle (FCV) or fuel cell electric vehicle (FCEV) is an electric vehicle that uses a fuel cell, usually a proton-exchange membrane fuel cell fed with hydrogen, to generate electricity for its motors instead of relying primarily on a battery. The fuel cell combines hydrogen with oxygen from the air, producing electricity and water vapour as the only tailpipe emission.

Technology

Hydrogen is stored on board in high-pressure tanks (typically 350 or 700 bar). A small buffer battery or supercapacitor handles acceleration peaks and regenerative braking. Compared with battery-electric vehicles, FCEVs refuel in a few minutes and can offer long range, but hydrogen production, compression, distribution and the round-trip efficiency of the hydrogen pathway are less favourable, and refuelling infrastructure is sparse.

Production models and deployment

Early demonstrations included GM's 1966 Electrovan. The first mass-market FCEV cars were the Hyundai ix35 Fuel Cell (2013), the Toyota Mirai (2014) and the Honda Clarity Fuel Cell (2016); Hyundai later launched the Nexo. Sales have concentrated in California, Japan and South Korea, and the technology has found growing interest in buses, trucks, forklifts and trains, where fast refuelling and range weigh more heavily. Critics argue passenger cars are better served by batteries, while proponents emphasize decarbonization of heavy transport.
