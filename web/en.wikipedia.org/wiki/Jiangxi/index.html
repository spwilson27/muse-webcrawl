Jiangxi

Jiangxi is a landlocked province in the south-east of the People's Republic of China, with its capital at Nanchang. The name means "west of the Yangtze" and dates from the Tang dynasty circuit of Jiangnanxi. Spanning some 167,000 square kilometres with about 45 million people, the province is drained by the Gan River, which flows north through its centre into Poyang Lake, China's largest freshwater lake, and thence to the Yangtze.

Geography and economy

Jiangxi is ringed by mountains — including the Wuyi range on the Fujian border and the Luoxiao mountains to the west — while the central and northern parts form the Gan valley and the Poyang plain, a major rice-growing region. The province is a significant producer of copper (the Dexing mine is among Asia's largest), tungsten and rare earths. Its best-known city internationally is Jingdezhen, the "Porcelain Capital", which supplied imperial and export porcelain for a thousand years. Lushan (Mount Lu), a celebrated summer retreat and UNESCO site, rises above the Yangtze near Jiujiang.

History

Jiangxi occupies a central place in the history of the Chinese Communist movement: the Nanchang Uprising of 1 August 1927 is commemorated as the founding of the People's Liberation Army, and the Chinese Soviet Republic was proclaimed at Ruijin in 1931, with its capital there until the start of the Long March in 1934. The Jinggang Mountains on the Hunan border, where Mao Zedong established an early revolutionary base in 1927, are likewise a major "red tourism" destination.
