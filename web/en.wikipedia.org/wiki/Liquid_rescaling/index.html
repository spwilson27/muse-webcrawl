Liquid rescaling

Type: Content-aware image resizing technique

Also known as: Seam carving (related)

Field: Computer graphics, image processing

Liquid rescaling is a content-aware image resizing technique in which an image is resized non-uniformly, with less visually important regions being compressed or expanded more than salient regions. The best-known algorithm of this family is seam carving, introduced by Shai Avidan and Ariel Shamir in 2007, in which a connected path of pixels (a "seam") of minimal total energy is repeatedly removed or inserted, preserving the main subjects of the photograph while changing its aspect ratio.

Method

An energy map is computed from the image, typically based on gradient magnitude, so that edges and detailed areas receive high energy. Dynamic programming finds the minimal-energy connected seam from one side of the image to the other. Removing seams shrinks the image; duplicating seams enlarges it. More advanced variants combine seam carving with forward energy (measuring the artifacts introduced by removal) and with object detection to protect faces and other important content.

Applications

Liquid rescaling was popularized by its inclusion in Adobe Photoshop (as "Content-Aware Scaling") and in the GIMP through plugins. It is used for retargeting images to different aspect ratios for web and mobile layouts.
