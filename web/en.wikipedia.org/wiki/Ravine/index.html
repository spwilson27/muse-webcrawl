Ravine

A ravine is a landform that is narrower than a canyon and is often the product of streambank erosion. Ravines are typically classified as larger in scale than gullies, although smaller than valleys. Ravines may also be called a cleuch, dell, ghout, gill or gorge in regional usage.

A ravine is generally a fluvial slope landform of relatively steep (cross-sectional) sides, on the order of twenty to seventy percent in gradient. Ravines may or may not have active streams flowing along the downslope channel which originally formed them; moreover, often they are characterised by intermittent streams, since their geographic scale may not be sufficiently large to support a perennial watercourse.

Formation

Ravines form where flowing water incises into soil or soft rock, particularly in areas of soft sedimentary strata or unconsolidated deposits. They can develop rapidly during heavy rainfall events and are a significant feature of badlands terrain and of urbanising landscapes where vegetation has been cleared.
