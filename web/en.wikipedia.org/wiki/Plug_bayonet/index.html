Plug bayonet

A seventeenth-century plug bayonet

The plug bayonet is an early form of bayonet, a blade designed to be attached to the muzzle of a musket or rifle. Unlike later designs, the plug bayonet has a tapered, round hilt that is wedged directly into the gun's barrel, converting the firearm into a short spear or pike for close combat.

History

Plug bayonets appeared in the mid-seventeenth century, reportedly first in south-western France — the bayonet takes its name from the city of Bayonne. Their great drawback was that with the bayonet fitted the musket could neither be loaded nor fired, and the loose friction fit meant blades could become stuck or fall out. During the Battle of Killiecrankie in 1689, government troops were caught while fixing plug bayonets after firing, a disaster that hastened their replacement. The socket bayonet, which slips over the barrel and locks to a lug, leaving the bore clear, was developed in the 1690s — commonly credited to the French engineer Vauban — and by the early eighteenth century had superseded the plug design in European armies.
