Satellite phone
TypeMobile telephone
ConnectionCommunications satellites (LEO, MEO or GEO)
Major networksIridium, Inmarsat, Thuraya, Globalstar
CoverageRegional to global, including oceans and poles

A satellite phone is a type of mobile phone that connects to other phones or the telephone network by radio through orbiting communications satellites instead of terrestrial cell sites. Satellite phones can therefore work in most remote locations on Earth where cellular coverage is absent, including oceans, polar regions and disaster zones.

Networks

Low Earth orbit (LEO) systems such as Iridium and Globalstar use constellations of dozens of satellites to give global coverage with small handsets, though signals may be briefly interrupted as satellites pass. Geostationary systems such as Inmarsat and Thuraya use a small number of large satellites fixed over the equator, offering regional coverage with near-broadband data but poor service at high latitudes.

Uses

Satellite phones are used by maritime and aviation operators, journalists, aid workers, military forces, remote industries such as mining and oil exploration, and emergency services — famously after hurricanes and earthquakes destroy terrestrial networks. Because they require line of sight to the sky, they work poorly indoors. Call costs are far higher than cellular, and some countries restrict or ban their use for security reasons.

Recent developments

Since the early 2020s, ordinary smartphones have begun to offer limited satellite messaging and emergency SOS features, and direct-to-cell satellite services are in development, blurring the line between the satellite phone and the conventional handset.
