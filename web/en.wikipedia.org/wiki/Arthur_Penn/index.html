Arthur Penn

Arthur Penn
Born: September 27, 1922, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, U.S.
Died: September 28, 2010 (aged 88), New York City, U.S.
Occupation: Film and theatre director
Years active: 1950s–2010

Arthur Hiller Penn (September 27, 1922 – September 28, 2010) was an American film and theatre director and producer. A central figure of the New Hollywood movement, he is best known for directing Bonnie and Clyde (1967), a landmark of American cinema, as well as the film version of The Miracle Worker (1962), which he had earlier directed on stage.

Early life and theatre

Penn was born in Philadelphia, the younger brother of photographer Irving Penn. He served in the United States Army during the Second World War and became involved in theatre while stationed in Europe. After studying at the Actors Studio in New York, he directed live television drama during the medium's golden age, then achieved Broadway success with productions including Two for the Seesaw, The Miracle Worker (winning the Tony Award for Best Direction) and All the Way Home.

Film career

Penn's first feature was the Western The Left Handed Gun (1958), starring Paul Newman as Billy the Kid. The Miracle Worker (1962) earned Academy Awards for its stars Anne Bancroft and Patty Duke. After Mickey One (1965) and The Chase (1966), he directed Bonnie and Clyde (1967), whose unprecedented violence and sympathetic treatment of its outlaw protagonists helped inaugurate the New Hollywood era. Subsequent films include Alice's Restaurant (1969), Little Big Man (1970), Night Moves (1975), The Missouri Breaks (1976), Four Friends (1981) and Penn & Teller Get Killed (1989).

Legacy

Penn was nominated three times for the Academy Award for Best Director. He died of congestive heart failure in Manhattan on September 28, 2010, the day after his 88th birthday. His influence on the generation of filmmakers who transformed American cinema in the late 1960s and 1970s is widely acknowledged.
