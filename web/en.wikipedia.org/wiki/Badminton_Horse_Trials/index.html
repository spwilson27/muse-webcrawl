The Badminton Horse Trials is a five-star international three-day event (eventing) competition held annually in the parkland of Badminton House, Gloucestershire, England, the seat of the Duke of Beaufort. First run in 1949, it is one of the most prestigious events in the equestrian calendar and one of only seven CCI5*-L competitions worldwide, forming part of the Rolex Grand Slam of Eventing together with Burghley and Kentucky.

History

The event was founded by the 10th Duke of Beaufort, who, inspired by the equestrian events at the 1948 London Olympics, offered his estate so British riders could train and compete at the highest level. The first Badminton in 1949 drew 22 starters. It has since grown into one of the largest spectator sporting events in Britain, drawing crowds of around a quarter of a million over the competition days, particularly on cross-country day.

Format

The competition follows the classic eventing format: dressage on the opening days; a demanding cross-country course of around 30 fences over some 6,700 metres of parkland; and show jumping on the final day, after a veterinary inspection. Famous cross-country obstacles include the Lake and Huntsman's Close. Notable multiple winners include Lucinda Green (six wins), Mark Todd, Ginny Elliot, William Fox-Pitt, and Andrew Nicholson, who won in 2017 after a record number of completions.
