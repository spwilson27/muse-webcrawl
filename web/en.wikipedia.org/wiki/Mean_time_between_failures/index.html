Mean time between failures

Mean time between failures (MTBF) is the predicted elapsed time between inherent failures of a mechanical or electronic system during normal system operation. MTBF can be calculated as the arithmetic mean (average) time between failures of a system. The term is used for repairable systems, while mean time to failure (MTTF) denotes the expected time to failure for a non-repairable system.

Definition

MTBF is typically defined as the total operational time divided by the total number of failures observed in a population of identical items under stated conditions: MTBF = Σ(start of uptime − start of downtime) / number of failures. For a constant failure rate λ during the useful-life period (the flat part of the bathtub curve), MTBF is the reciprocal of the failure rate, MTBF = 1/λ. MTBF is related to, but distinct from, mean time to repair (MTTR); together, MTBF and MTTR determine system availability.

Applications

MTBF figures are used in reliability engineering to plan maintenance, stock spare parts, and compare designs. For example, hard disk drive datasheets commonly quote MTBF values of several hundred thousand to over a million hours — a figure derived from accelerated testing of large populations, not a prediction that any individual drive will last that long. Critics note that MTBF is often misunderstood and can be misleading when failure rates are not constant or when wear-out mechanisms dominate.
