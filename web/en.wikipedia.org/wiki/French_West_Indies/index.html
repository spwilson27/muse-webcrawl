French West Indies

The French West Indies or French Antilles (French: Antilles françaises) are the French-administered islands in the Caribbean: principally Guadeloupe, Martinique, Saint Barthélemy and Saint Martin, with smaller dependencies. Guadeloupe and Martinique are overseas departments and regions of France.


Territories: Guadeloupe, Martinique, Saint Barthélemy, Saint Martin
Languages: French, Antillean Creole

History

France colonised the islands from the 17th century, developing sugar plantations worked by enslaved Africans. Slavery was abolished in French colonies in 1848 under Victor Schœlcher. Departmentalisation in 1946 made Guadeloupe and Martinique integral parts of France.

Culture

The islands blend French, African and Caribbean influences in language, cuisine, music such as zouk and gwoka, and literature — Martinique was home to poet Aimé Césaire, a founder of négritude. See also French Guiana.
