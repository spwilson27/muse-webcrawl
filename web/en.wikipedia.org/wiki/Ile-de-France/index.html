Paris, capital of France, is the centre of the Île-de-France region.

The Île-de-France (literally "Island of France") is the most populous of the eighteen regions of France and includes the capital city, Paris. Centred on the French capital, it is located in the north-central part of the country and is often called the Région parisienne ("Paris Region"). With roughly a fifth of France's population on about 2 percent of its area, it is the country's political, economic and cultural heartland.

History

The name derives from the old royal province — the historic domain of the Capetian kings — bounded by the rivers Seine, Marne, Oise and Aisne, making it a figurative "island". The modern region was created in 1976 (as the Paris Region, renamed Île-de-France in 1977) from the territory of the former Seine and Seine-et-Oise departments and neighbours. It is governed by a Regional Council seated in Saint-Ouen-sur-Seine (formerly Paris).

Geography and departments

The region comprises eight departments: Paris itself; the inner ring (petite couronne) of Hauts-de-Seine, Seine-Saint-Denis and Val-de-Marne; and the outer ring (grande couronne) of Seine-et-Marne, Yvelines, Essonne and Val-d'Oise. Major sub-centres include the business district of La Défense, Versailles, Saint-Denis, and the new towns built from the 1960s (Cergy-Pontoise, Évry, Marne-la-Vallée, Saint-Quentin-en-Yvelines, Sénart).

Economy and transport

Île-de-France produces roughly 30 percent of France's GDP. Transport is organised by Île-de-France Mobilités and includes the Métro, RER, Transilien suburban rail, trams and buses; the Grand Paris Express project is adding some 200 km of automated metro lines around the capital. Charles de Gaulle and Orly airports serve the region, and Disneyland Paris in Marne-la-Vallée is Europe's most visited tourist attraction.
