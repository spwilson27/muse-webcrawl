The Spallation Neutron Source (SNS) is an accelerator-based neutron source at Oak Ridge National Laboratory in Tennessee, United States. Together with the laboratory's High Flux Isotope Reactor, it forms one of the world's leading centres for neutron scattering research, serving thousands of visiting scientists each year who study the structure and dynamics of materials at the atomic scale.

How it works

An ion source produces negatively charged hydrogen ions, which are accelerated in a linear accelerator and a proton accumulator ring to an energy of 1 GeV. The beam is fired in short pulses — 60 times per second — at a target of liquid mercury. Each proton striking a mercury nucleus "spalls" off some twenty neutrons. The neutrons are slowed in moderators (water and liquid hydrogen) to useful energies and delivered to a suite of instruments arranged around the target station.

Research

Neutron scattering at the SNS probes the positions and motions of atoms in materials ranging from superconductors and magnetic materials to polymers, proteins, and engineering alloys. Because neutrons carry a magnetic moment and interact strongly with light elements such as hydrogen, they reveal structures that X-rays and electrons cannot. Experiments support work in energy storage, drug design, structural biology, and quantum materials.

History and upgrades

Construction began in 1999 and the facility produced its first neutrons in April 2006, achieving design power in the following years. A Proton Power Upgrade project subsequently increased beam power and prepared for a Second Target Station, which will add a suite of instruments optimised for very cold, bright neutron beams.
