Pattern hair loss

Placeholder image

Pattern hair loss, also known as androgenetic alopecia or androgenic alopecia, is a hair loss condition caused by an underlying susceptibility of hair follicles to androgenic miniaturization. It is the most common cause of hair loss in both men and women.

Patterns and causes

In men, the condition typically follows a pattern of receding hairline and balding at the crown (male-pattern baldness, graded on the Norwood scale). In women, it usually presents as diffuse thinning over the top of the scalp with preservation of the frontal hairline (graded on the Ludwig scale). Genetics and the androgen dihydrotestosterone (DHT) are central to the condition; susceptible follicles progressively produce finer, shorter hairs.

Treatment

Commonly used treatments include topical minoxidil and oral finasteride, which can slow progression and in some cases promote regrowth. Surgical hair transplantation redistributes follicles from resistant areas. Many other products are marketed for the condition with varying levels of evidence.
