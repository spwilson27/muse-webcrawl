4K resolution

4K resolution refers to a horizontal display resolution of approximately 4,000 pixels. Digital television and digital cinematography commonly use several different 4K resolutions. In television and consumer media, 3840 × 2160 (4K UHD) is the dominant 4K standard, whereas the movie industry uses 4096 × 2160 (DCI 4K).

History

The first commercially available 4K camera for cinematographic purposes was the Dalsa Origin, released in 2003. YouTube began supporting 4K for video uploads in 2010 as a result of popular manufacturers releasing 4K cameras. The first 4K television sets were shown at trade fairs in the early 2010s, and prices fell rapidly through the decade. By the late 2010s, 4K UHD resolution had gone mainstream in television receivers, monitors, game consoles and streaming services, succeeding 1080p as the prevailing high-end consumer resolution.

Resolutions

3840 × 2160 (4K UHD)

The resolution of 3840 × 2160 is the dominant 4K resolution used in the television industry, in the computer industry as monitors, and in home video. It has an aspect ratio of 16:9 and exactly four times as many pixels as Full HD (1920 × 1080), with twice the linear resolution, producing a total of 8,294,400 pixels. It is standardised by ITU-R as UHDTV format, and is also used by the HDMI specification.

4096 × 2160 (DCI 4K)

The Digital Cinema Initiatives (DCI) specification for digital cinema defines 4K as 4096 × 2160 pixels, an aspect ratio of approximately 17:9, used for theatrical projection. Projectors and cameras in the cinema industry generally use this resolution, while cropping or letterboxing to match the aspect ratio of the film.

Other resolutions

Other resolutions are sometimes described as 4K, including 4096 × 1716 for anamorphic or scope projection, 3996 × 2160, and various ultra-wide display formats such as 5120 × 2160, which is marketed as "5K" in some contexts despite also being described as 4K ultrawide elsewhere.
