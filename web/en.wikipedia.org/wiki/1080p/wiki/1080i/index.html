1080i

1080i (also known as Full HD or BT.709) is a combination of frame resolution and scan type. 1080i is used in high-definition television (HDTV) and high-definition video. The number "1080" refers to the number of horizontal lines on the screen. The "i" stands for interlaced, which was a scan mode used in older televisions.

Specifications

In the designation 1080i, the i stands for interlaced scan. A frame of 1080i video consists of 1080 horizontal lines of pixels; a full frame is drawn as two fields, one containing the odd-numbered lines and the other the even-numbered lines, transmitted in sequence. It assumes a widescreen aspect ratio of 16:9 and a frame resolution of 1920 × 1080 pixels, yielding, after the two interlaced fields are combined, a total pixel resolution of 2,073,600.

The field rate (in frames per second) is usually not specified, with the frame rate explicitly shown in most cases. A 1080i signal can be carried by the major digital television broadcast formats ATSC, DVB and ISDB. Most television networks using 1080i have a native resolution of 1920 × 1080i, although some broadcast at 1440 × 1080i or even 1280 × 1080i.

1080i versus 1080p

1080i differs from 1080p, where the p stands for progressive scan, in that 1080p transmits and displays each frame whole rather than as two interlaced fields. At the same frame rate, 1080i requires the same data bandwidth as 720p but with a higher effective resolution; on a fixed-pixel display, 1080i video is typically deinterlaced for display, and the trade-off between the two formats—temporal resolution against spatial resolution in moving images—was a common subject of debate in the early years of HDTV. Interlaced scanning was retained in HD broadcasting largely for backward compatibility with the analogue television standards, which had used interlacing to double the perceived frame rate without increasing bandwidth.
