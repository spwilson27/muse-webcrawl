Lazar Hrebeljanović (Serbian Cyrillic: Лазар Хребељановић; c. 1329 – 15 June 1389) was a medieval Serbian ruler who created the largest and most powerful state on the territory of the disintegrated Serbian Empire. His realm, known in historiography as Moravian Serbia, was centred on the Morava river basin. Lazar was killed at the Battle of Kosovo in 1389, fighting the Ottoman army of Sultan Murad I, and is venerated as a saint and martyr in the Serbian Orthodox Church.

Rise to power

Lazar was born into a family of court nobility; his father Pribac served as chancellor and seal-bearer to Emperor Stefan Dušan. Lazar himself entered the court of Emperor Stefan Uroš V, receiving the title of knez (prince). After Uroš's death in 1371 ended the Nemanjić dynasty, Lazar consolidated power in the Morava valley, made Kruševac his capital, and by marriage to Milica — a descendant of the Nemanjić line — strengthened his legitimacy. Through diplomacy and alliances, including with the Bosnian king Tvrtko I, he became the most powerful Serbian regional lord.

Church and building

Lazar worked to heal the schism between the Serbian Patriarchate and Constantinople, achieving reconciliation in 1375. He founded the monasteries of Ravanica, his burial church, and the Lazarica church in Kruševac, and supported Athonite monasticism and the hesychast movement.

Battle of Kosovo and legacy

As Ottoman raids intensified, Lazar led the Serbian forces that checked the Turks at Pločnik (1386) and Bileća (1388). On 15 June 1389 (28 June, Gregorian), he confronted Sultan Murad's army on Kosovo Polje. Both rulers died in the battle. Lazar was canonised soon after his death; his relics, kept at Ravanica, became a focus of the Kosovo myth — the body of epic poetry and national legend in which Lazar chooses the "heavenly kingdom" over earthly victory, making him the central martyr figure of Serbian national consciousness.
