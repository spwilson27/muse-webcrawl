Periodic limb movement disorder

Periodic limb movement disorder (PLMD) is a sleep disorder characterised by repetitive, stereotyped limb movements during sleep — typically flexion of the leg at the hip and knee with dorsiflexion of the foot — that occur in episodes lasting from seconds to minutes and repeat at intervals of roughly 20 to 40 seconds, mainly during non-REM sleep. The movements can fragment sleep and cause daytime sleepiness or insomnia.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis requires polysomnography showing a periodic limb movement index (movements per hour of sleep) above a threshold — more than 15 per hour in adults — together with clinical sleep disturbance not better explained by another disorder. PLMD must be distinguished from the periodic limb movements that commonly accompany restless legs syndrome, obstructive sleep apnoea and narcolepsy; many people have periodic limb movements in sleep without having the disorder.

Causes and associations

PLMD may be primary or secondary to conditions such as iron deficiency, kidney failure, diabetes or neurological disease, and to medications including some antidepressants. It frequently coexists with restless legs syndrome, and genetic and dopaminergic factors are thought to contribute.

Treatment

Management addresses underlying causes, such as correcting iron deficiency, and may include dopamine agonists, gabapentinoids or other agents used for restless legs syndrome, though evidence specific to PLMD is limited. Sleep hygiene measures are commonly recommended.
