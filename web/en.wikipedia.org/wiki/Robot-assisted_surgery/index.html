Robot-assisted surgery

Robot-assisted surgery or robotic surgery comprises surgical procedures performed using robotic systems controlled by a surgeon. Developed to overcome the limitations of minimally invasive surgery and to enhance the capabilities of surgeons performing open surgery, these systems give the operator magnified three-dimensional vision, motion scaling, tremor filtration and wristed instruments with greater range of motion than the human hand.

History

The first robot used in surgery, the Arthrobot, assisted in orthopaedic procedures in Vancouver in 1983. The PUMA 560 arm was used for a neurosurgical biopsy in 1985, and the PROBOT performed prostate surgery at Guy's Hospital, London, in the early 1990s. The dominant commercial platform, the da Vinci Surgical System from Intuitive Surgical, was cleared by the FDA in 2000 and has since been used in millions of procedures, including prostatectomy, hysterectomy and cardiac valve repair.

Advantages and disadvantages

Reported benefits include smaller incisions, reduced blood loss, shorter hospital stays and faster recovery. Against these stand the very high purchase and maintenance costs of the systems, longer operating times in some procedures, loss of tactile (haptic) feedback for the surgeon, and — in many specialties — a lack of clear evidence of superior long-term outcomes compared with conventional laparoscopy.
