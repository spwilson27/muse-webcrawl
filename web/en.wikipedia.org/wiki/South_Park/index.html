South Park is an American animated sitcom created by Trey Parker and Matt Stone for Comedy Central. Set in the fictional Colorado mountain town of South Park, the series follows four schoolboys — Stan Marsh, Kyle Broflovski, Eric Cartman and Kenny McCormick — and their often surreal and profane adventures, satirising American culture, current events, religion and politics.

Origins and production

Parker and Stone developed the show from two animated shorts, The Spirit of Christmas (1992 and 1995), which went viral in the early internet era. The series premiered on August 13, 1997, and became one of Comedy Central's flagship programmes. Episodes are produced in an unusually short cycle — typically six days — using computer animation that imitates the original cut-paper stop-motion style, allowing the show to respond rapidly to current events.

Characters and themes

Cartman's bigotry and scheming, Kenny's muffled speech and repeated deaths in early seasons ("Oh my God, they killed Kenny!"), and the boys' encounters with celebrities, cults and conspiracies are recurring features. The show is noted for its libertarian-leaning satire targeting all sides of cultural and political debates, and for episodes that have provoked genuine controversy, including depictions of religious figures and responses to censorship.

Films and other media

The feature film South Park: Bigger, Longer & Uncut (1999) was a critical and commercial success and earned an Academy Award nomination for its song "Blame Canada". The franchise includes video games such as The Stick of Truth (2014) and The Fractured but Whole (2017), and streaming specials produced for Paramount+. In 2021 Parker and Stone signed a large deal extending the series on Comedy Central and producing films for Paramount+.
