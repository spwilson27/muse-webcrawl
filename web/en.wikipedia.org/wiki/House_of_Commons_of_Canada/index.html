House of Commons of Canada

House of Commons
French: Chambre des communes
Type: Lower house of the Parliament of Canada
Seats: 338 (343 after 2025 redistribution takes effect)
Meeting place: West Block, Parliament Hill, Ottawa (temporary, during Centre Block renovation)
Presiding officer: Speaker of the House of Commons

The House of Commons of Canada (French: Chambre des communes du Canada) is the lower house of the bicameral Parliament of Canada, the other being the Senate of Canada. It is composed of members elected by Canadians in federal general elections, each representing a single-member electoral district (riding).

Role

As in the Westminster system, the government — the prime minister and cabinet — is drawn from and accountable to the Commons, and most legislation originates there. The chamber sits on Parliament Hill in Ottawa; its traditional room in Centre Block, recognisable by its green furnishings after the British Commons, is under long-term renovation, and members meet in the West Block.

Composition

Seat allocation by province follows population with constitutional guarantees; Ontario, Quebec, British Columbia, and Alberta hold the largest shares. Elections use first-past-the-post. The Official Opposition is the largest party not in government.

History

The Commons was established by the British North America Act, 1867 (now the Constitution Act, 1867), modelled on the British House of Commons.
