Urewe

RegionLake Victoria basin, East Africa
Periodc. 500 BCE – 500 CE (Early Iron Age)
Type siteUrewe, Nyanza Province, Kenya
Known forEarliest farming and ironworking culture of the Great Lakes region
Urewe ware: dimple-based pottery of the Early Iron Age Great Lakes

Urewe is an Early Iron Age archaeological culture of the Lake Victoria basin in East Africa, dating roughly from the mid-first millennium BCE to the mid-first millennium CE. It is named after the site of Urewe in Nyanza Province, Kenya, where its distinctive pottery was first described by Mary Leakey in the 1940s. Urewe is associated with the earliest farming communities of the African Great Lakes region, widely linked to the spread of Bantu-speaking peoples into the area.

Characteristics

The hallmark of the culture is Urewe ware: finely made, often burnished pottery, frequently with a characteristic dimpled or concave base, bevelled rims, and incised or stamped decoration including bands of oblique lines. Urewe sites also yield evidence of iron smelting — furnaces, tuyères, and slag — indicating that these early farmers were also among the region's first ironworkers, producing tools that supported forest clearance and cultivation. Settlements were typically located on hilltops and slopes with good rainfall around the lake's northern and western shores, in present-day Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda, and Burundi.

Significance and debate

Urewe has been central to debates about the Bantu expansion: linguistic reconstructions place an early centre of Great Lakes Bantu around the same region and period, and Urewe pottery is often taken as the material signature of those first farming communities. Its descendants or successors in the region include the later "Urewe tradition" industries and, eventually, the more complex societies of the later Iron Age. Peter Robertshaw, Merrick Posnansky, and others refined its chronology with radiocarbon dates; some scholars divide it into regional facies or regard "Urewe" as a broad tradition rather than a single culture.
