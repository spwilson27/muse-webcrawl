Lens space

Lens space
FieldTopology
Dimension3 (classical); generalized to all odd dimensions

A lens space is an example of a topological space, considered in mathematics. The term most commonly refers to a specific class of 3-manifolds, but lens spaces in general can be defined for any odd dimension. A three-dimensional lens space L(p, q) is obtained by gluing two solid tori together along their boundary in a specific way, or equivalently as the quotient of the 3-sphere S3 by a free action of the cyclic group Z/p.

Definition

Let p ≥ 2 and q be integers with q relatively prime to p. Consider the 3-sphere as the unit sphere in C2. The generator of the cyclic group acts by multiplying the two complex coordinates by primitive p-th roots of unity with weights 1 and q. The quotient space is the lens space L(p, q). The name comes from the fact that a fundamental domain for the action can be chosen in the shape of a lens, the intersection of two hemispheres.

Properties

Lens spaces were among the first known examples of 3-manifolds that have the same homology and fundamental group but are not homeomorphic. Their fundamental group is cyclic of order p, and their universal cover is the 3-sphere. Two lens spaces L(p, q) and L(p, q′) are homeomorphic if and only if q′ ≡ ±q±1 (mod p), while they are homotopy equivalent if and only if qq′ ≡ ±n2 (mod p) for some integer n. This finer distinction was historically important in the development of Reidemeister torsion.

Special cases include real projective 3-space, L(2, 1), and the Poincaré homology sphere is not a lens space but shares the feature of being a spherical space form. Lens spaces are the simplest examples of spherical space forms with cyclic fundamental group.

Generalizations

Higher-dimensional lens spaces are quotients of odd-dimensional spheres S2n−1 by free actions of cyclic groups, defined using several weight parameters. Weighted projective spaces and quotient singularities provide related generalizations in algebraic geometry.
