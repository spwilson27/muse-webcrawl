Planetary science

Planetary science (or more rarely, planetology) is the scientific study of planets (including Earth), celestial bodies such as moons, asteroids and comets, and planetary systems, in particular those of the Solar System. It is an interdisciplinary field drawing on astronomy, geology, atmospheric science, chemistry, physics, geophysics and biology, and examines the origin, evolution, composition, internal structure, surfaces and atmospheres of planetary bodies.

Comparative planetology: the planets and larger moons of the Solar System are studied as variations on common physical processes.

History

Planetary science began with telescopic astronomy: Galileo's observations of the Moon's surface, the phases of Venus and Jupiter's moons in 1609–1610 first treated other worlds as physical places. The discipline transformed in the space age. The Mariner, Venera, Viking, Voyager, Galileo, Cassini–Huygens, and Mars rover missions returned in-situ measurements, while the study of meteorites — fragments of asteroids and of Mars and the Moon — provided laboratory samples. The discovery from 1995 onward of thousands of exoplanets extended planetary science beyond the Solar System.

Sub-disciplines

  • Planetary geology — surfaces, volcanism, tectonics, cratering and interiors of rocky bodies.
  • Planetary atmospheres — structure, circulation, climate and escape of atmospheres.
  • Cosmochemistry — the chemistry of Solar System materials as recorded in meteorites and returned samples.
  • Comparative planetology — using one body to understand another, including Earth.
  • Astrobiology — the conditions for the origin and survival of life on planetary bodies.
  • Exoplanet science — detection and characterisation of planets around other stars.

Methods

Planetary scientists combine remote sensing from ground- and space-based telescopes, robotic spacecraft (orbiters, landers, rovers and sample-return missions), laboratory experiments simulating planetary conditions, fieldwork at terrestrial analogue sites, and numerical modelling of interiors, atmospheres and impacts.
