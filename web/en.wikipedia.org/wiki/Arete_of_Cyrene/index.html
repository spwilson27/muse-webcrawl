Arete of Cyrene (Ancient Greek: Ἀρήτη) was a Cyrenaic philosopher who lived in the late fifth and fourth centuries BC in Cyrene, Libya. She was the daughter of Aristippus of Cyrene, the founder of the Cyrenaic school of hedonistic philosophy.

Life and teaching

According to ancient sources, Arete learned philosophy from her father and in turn taught her own son, Aristippus the Younger, who is credited with systematising the Cyrenaic doctrine that pleasure is the sole good and pain the sole evil. Diogenes Laertius records that she was taught by her father and succeeded him in leading the school — making her among the earliest named women philosophers in the Western tradition.

Sources

No writings by Arete survive; what is known of her comes from later doxographers such as Diogenes Laertius, Strabo, and Aelian.
