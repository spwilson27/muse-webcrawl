Max-flow min-cut theorem

Max-flow min-cut theorem
Field: Network flow theory / graph theory
Proved by: L. R. Ford Jr. and D. R. Fulkerson (1950s)

In computer science and optimization theory, the max-flow min-cut theorem states that in a flow network, the maximum amount of flow passing from the source to the sink is equal to the total weight of the edges in a minimum cut, i.e., the smallest total weight of the edges which if removed would disconnect the source from the sink.

Statement

For a network with capacities on its edges, the value of a maximum flow equals the capacity of a minimum s–t cut. The theorem is a special case of linear programming duality and underlies augmenting-path algorithms such as the Ford–Fulkerson method and the Edmonds–Karp algorithm.

Applications

The theorem has applications in transportation and logistics, telecommunications, bipartite matching, project selection, image segmentation in computer vision, and sports elimination problems. Generalizations include Menger's theorem on vertex and edge connectivity.
