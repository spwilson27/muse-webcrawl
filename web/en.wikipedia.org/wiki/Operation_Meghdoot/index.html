Operation Meghdoot (lit. "Operation Cloud Messenger", after a Sanskrit play by Kalidasa) was the codename for the Indian Armed Forces operation to seize control of the Siachen Glacier in Kashmir, precipitating the Siachen conflict. Executed on the morning of 13 April 1984 by the Indian Army and Indian Air Force, it was the highest-altitude operation of its kind in history and pre-empted Pakistan's own planned operation, Ababeel, by a single day.

Background

The 1949 Karachi Agreement and the 1972 Simla Agreement had left the Line of Control in Kashmir undemarcated beyond point NJ9842, stating only that it would run "thence north to the glaciers". Through the 1970s and early 1980s Pakistan asserted a claim to the Siachen region, licensed foreign mountaineering expeditions there, and — according to Indian intelligence — prepared to occupy the glacier's dominating passes in the spring of 1984. An Indian expedition in 1978 and subsequent patrols confirmed the military significance of the Saltoro Ridge.

The operation

Under Northern Army Commander Lt. Gen. M. L. Chibber and executed by Brig. Vijay Channa's task force, Indian troops were airlifted by helicopter to the Bilafond La, Sia La and Gyong La passes on 13 April 1984, landing a platoon on Bilafond La just before Pakistani troops arrived. By the end of April India controlled the three passes and the commanding heights of the Saltoro Ridge; Pakistani attempts to dislodge them, including major assaults in 1987, failed.

Consequences

The operation began a decades-long military standoff on the "world's highest battlefield", where most casualties have been caused by altitude, avalanche and weather rather than combat. India retains control of the glacier itself and the Saltoro passes, while Pakistan holds the lower ground to the west. Repeated diplomatic efforts to demilitarise the glacier have not produced an agreement.
