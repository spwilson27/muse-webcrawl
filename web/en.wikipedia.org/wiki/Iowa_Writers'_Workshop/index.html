The Iowa Writers' Workshop is a two-year graduate-level creative writing program at the University of Iowa in Iowa City, and the oldest and most famous creative writing degree program in the United States. Founded in 1936, it grants the Master of Fine Arts degree and is widely credited with shaping modern American fiction and poetry through its workshop method, in which students critique one another's manuscripts under a faculty writer's direction.

History

Creative writing teaching at Iowa grew from the 1920s, when the university began accepting creative work for graduate degrees; the Workshop took formal shape in 1936. Under director Paul Engle (1941–1965) it raised funds privately, built an international reputation, and seeded the model later copied by hundreds of MFA programs. Faculty have included Robert Lowell, John Berryman, Kurt Vonnegut, Philip Roth and Marilynne Robinson; the program is housed in the restored Dey House.

Influence

Alumni and teachers of the Workshop have won numerous Pulitzer Prizes and National Book Awards — among them Robert Penn Warren, Flannery O'Connor, John Cheever (as a teacher), Michael Cunningham and Marilynne Robinson, whose novel Gilead (2004) won the Pulitzer Prize. Critics of "program fiction" debate the Workshop's influence on the style of contemporary American writing, but its centrality to American letters is undisputed; it was a key reason Iowa City was named a UNESCO City of Literature.
