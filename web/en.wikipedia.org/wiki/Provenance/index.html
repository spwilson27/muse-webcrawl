Provenance

Provenance (from French provenir, "to come from/forth") is the chronology of the ownership, custody or location of a historical object. The term was originally mostly used in relation to works of art, but is now used in a wide range of fields, including archaeology, palaeontology, archives, manuscripts, printed books, and science — in the tracking of data lineage and of the origin of materials.

Art and cultural property

A documented provenance can confirm that a painting attributed to a famous artist is genuinely his, corroborate its dating, and greatly increase its value; conversely, gaps in provenance can depress value or raise suspicion of forgery or theft. Provenance research became especially prominent after the Second World War and again from the 1990s, as museums and claimants investigated works looted by the Nazi regime (1933–45) or displaced during the war. The 1998 Washington Conference Principles on Nazi-Confiscated Art encouraged institutions to research their collections and resolve claims justly.

Science and data

In science, provenance refers to the documented origin and processing history of data or specimens, ensuring reproducibility and authenticity. Information systems model data provenance (lineage) to record how outputs were derived; the W3C PROV standard formalises this. In geology, provenance analysis studies sediment sources to reconstruct past environments.

Other uses

In archives, the principle of provenance (respect des fonds) requires that records be kept in the order and context of their creator. In the food and wine trades, provenance denotes origin and traceability of products.
