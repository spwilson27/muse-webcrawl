The March of Montferrat was a medieval and early modern margraviate in Piedmont, north-western Italy, roughly corresponding to the modern Monferrato region. It was ruled successively by the Aleramici dynasty, the Byzantine Palaiologoi and the Gonzaga of Mantua, before being raised to the Duchy of Montferrat in 1574.

History

The march originated in the tenth century as one of the frontier marches of the Kingdom of Italy, granted to Aleramo, the legendary first marquess. His descendants, the Aleramici, ruled Montferrat for centuries and played a prominent role in the Crusades: members of the family became kings of Jerusalem and Thessalonica and despots in the Latin East.

When the Aleramici male line failed in 1305, the march passed through the female line to the Palaiologoi of Byzantium. In 1533, on the death of the last Palaiologos marquess, it came under Spanish occupation before being awarded to Federico II Gonzaga of Mantua in 1536. In 1574 Maximilian's successor Guglielmo Gonzaga obtained its elevation to a duchy.
