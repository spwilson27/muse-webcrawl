Mary (Turkmen: Mary) is a city in Turkmenistan and the capital of Mary Province. It lies in an oasis of the Karakum Desert on the Murghab River and had a population of around 125,000 in the early twenty-first century. Mary is a major center of Turkmenistan's natural gas and cotton industries and a hub for electricity generation.

History

The modern city was founded in 1884 by the Russian Empire near the ruins of ancient Merv, one of the great cities of the Islamic world and an important stop on the Silk Road. Merv, about 30 kilometres east of the modern city, was destroyed by the Mongols under Tolui in 1221 in one of the bloodiest episodes of the Mongol conquests. The archaeological site of ancient Merv is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Under Russian and later Soviet rule, Mary grew as an administrative and military center of the Merv oasis, and the Karakum Canal made large-scale cotton cultivation possible. After Turkmen independence in 1991, the city was redeveloped with new government buildings, mosques, a stadium, and museums in the monumental style favored by the independent state.

Economy and transport

Mary's economy rests on natural gas — the nearby Galkynysh field is among the largest in the world — together with cotton ginning, food processing, and one of Turkmenistan's largest power stations. The city is served by an international airport and lies on the Trans-Caspian railway linking Ashgabat with Turkmenbashi and the east of the country.
