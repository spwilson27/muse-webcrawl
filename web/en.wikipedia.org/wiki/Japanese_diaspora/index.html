Japanese diaspora

[Image: Japanese Immigration Memorial, São Paulo]

The Japanese diaspora consists of the Japanese and their descendants living outside Japan. Emigration began in large numbers in the late nineteenth century, after the Meiji Restoration lifted restrictions on leaving the country. Today, people of Japanese descent are estimated to number over three million, with the largest communities in Brazil, the United States, and Peru.

History

The first major wave of emigrants were contract labourers who travelled to Hawaii in the 1880s and to the United States mainland in the following decades, working on plantations and railways. From 1908, Japan sponsored emigration to Brazil, where labourers worked on coffee plantations; the community in São Paulo grew into the largest Japanese population outside Japan.

Brazil

Japanese immigration to Brazil began with the arrival of the ship Kasato Maru at Santos in 1908, carrying 781 labourers. Despite initial hardships, the community prospered in agriculture and later in urban commerce. Brazil is now home to an estimated 1.5 million people of Japanese descent, concentrated in São Paulo.

United States

Japanese Americans numbered about 120,000 on the West Coast at the outbreak of World War II, when they were subjected to forced removal and incarceration (see Japanese American redress and court cases). After the war, the community rebuilt, and Japanese Americans became prominent in politics, science, and the arts.

Other communities

Significant populations of Japanese descent also exist in Peru, Canada, the Philippines, Argentina, and the United Kingdom. The Japanese government uses the term Nikkei (日系) for people of Japanese ancestry living abroad.
