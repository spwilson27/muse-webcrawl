Dixie Dean

William Ralph "Dixie" Dean (22 January 1907 – 1 March 1980) was an English footballer who played as a centre forward. He is best remembered for his years with Everton, where his phenomenal goalscoring — above all his record 60 league goals in the 1927–28 season — made him one of the most celebrated players in English football history.

Career

Dean began his career with Tranmere Rovers before joining Everton in 1925. In 1927–28 he scored 60 league goals in 39 matches — a record for a single English top-flight season that still stands — helping Everton to the league championship; he won another title in 1931–32 and the FA Cup in 1933. In total he scored 383 goals in 433 appearances for Everton, including a record 37 hat-tricks for the club. Despite a serious motorcycle accident in 1926 that fractured his skull, he returned to play within months.

International career

Dean won 16 caps for England between 1927 and 1932, scoring 18 goals, though his international career was curtailed by the selection practices of the era.

Later life and legacy

After football he ran a pub in Chester and worked at Littlewoods. He died of a heart attack at Goodison Park in 1980 while watching Everton play Liverpool. A statue of Dean stands outside Goodison Park, and he was an inaugural inductee into the English Football Hall of Fame in 2002. His nickname "Dixie" reportedly came from his dark complexion and curly hair being likened to people of the southern United States.
