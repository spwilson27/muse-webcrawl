Fresnel lantern

A Fresnel lantern (pronounced fray-NEL) is a common type of stage lighting instrument, named after Augustin-Jean Fresnel, who developed the stepped lens for lighthouses. It consists of a lamp, a reflector and a Fresnel lens, mounted in a housing with a focusing mechanism that slides the lamp and reflector toward or away from the lens to vary the beam from a narrow spot to a broad flood.

Characteristics

The Fresnel lens produces a beam with a soft, diffused edge, making fresnels ideal for general stage wash lighting where the beams of several lanterns must blend unobtrusively. The beam angle can typically be varied over a wide range (for example from about 10° to 60°). Accessories such as barn doors are used to shape the beam, and colour filters (gels) can be fitted in a frame at the front.
