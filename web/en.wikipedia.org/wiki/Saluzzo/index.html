Saluzzo (Piedmontese: Salusse) is a town and former principality in the province of Cuneo in the Italian region of Piedmont. It lies at the foot of Monviso, near the source of the Po River, and is noted for its well-preserved medieval old town.

History

Saluzzo was the seat of the Marquises of Saluzzo, a dynasty founded in the 12th century that ruled the surrounding marquisate for roughly four centuries. The town flourished under Ludovico II in the late 15th century, when it became a notable Renaissance court. After periods of French and Savoyard control, the marquisate was ceded to the House of Savoy by the Treaty of Lyon in 1601.

Sights

Landmarks include the Cathedral of Santa Maria Assunta (1491–1501), the castle known as the Castiglia, the church of San Giovanni with the marquises' burial chapel, and the Casa Cavassa, a Renaissance palace now housing the civic museum. Saluzzo is also the birthplace of the typographer Giambattista Bodoni (1740–1813) and the writer Silvio Pellico.
