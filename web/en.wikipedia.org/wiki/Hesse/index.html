Hesse

Hesse (German: Hessen) is a state (Land) of Germany, located in the west-central part of the country. Its capital is Wiesbaden, and its largest city is Frankfurt am Main, Germany's financial centre and home to the European Central Bank. Hesse has a population of about 6.3 million and an area of roughly 21,115 km².

Geography

Hesse is a hilly, heavily forested state crossed by the Rhine, Main, Fulda, and Lahn rivers. Its uplands include the Taunus, Rhön, and Odenwald ranges. The Rhine-Main metropolitan region in the south is one of Germany's most densely populated and economically productive areas.

History

The name derives from the Hessian tribe, descended from the ancient Chatti. The medieval Landgraviate of Hesse was divided in 1567 among the sons of Philip the Magnanimous, creating lines including Hesse-Kassel and Hesse-Darmstadt. Hessian soldiers famously served as auxiliaries of the British Army during the American Revolutionary War. The modern state was formed in 1945–46 from the merger of Hesse-Darmstadt with Prussian territories including Hesse-Nassau.

Economy

Hesse is among Germany's wealthiest states, driven by Frankfurt's banking and finance sector, Frankfurt Airport (one of Europe's busiest), the chemical and pharmaceutical industries around Darmstadt and the Rhine-Main area, and firms such as the software and printing industries of the region.
