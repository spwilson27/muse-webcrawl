Clos Montmartre

Clos Montmartre is a vineyard in the Montmartre district of the 18th arrondissement of Paris, France. Located at the corner of rue des Saules and rue Saint-Vincent, it covers about 1,556 square metres (0.38 acres) and is the most famous of the vineyards surviving within the city of Paris.

History

Vines were cultivated on the hill of Montmartre from Roman times, and the area's vineyards were associated with the abbey of Montmartre. Most of the plots disappeared as the district was urbanised, but in 1933 the City of Paris replanted the present vineyard to prevent the land being developed and to preserve Montmartre's village character. The planting was championed by local artists, including the painter Francisque Poulbot.

Wine

The vineyard contains some 1,760 vines of 27 grape varieties, predominantly Gamay and Pinot noir. It produces roughly 400–500 litres of wine (about 1,800 bottles) per year. The wine is sold at auction during the annual Fête des Vendanges (harvest festival), held each October, with proceeds supporting social works in the 18th arrondissement administered by the Comité de la République de Montmartre.
