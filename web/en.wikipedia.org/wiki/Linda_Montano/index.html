Linda Montano

Linda Mary Montano (born January 18, 1942) is an American performance artist. Her work, which has spanned more than five decades, explores the boundaries between art and life through extreme endurance performances, often inspired by her Catholic upbringing, Zen meditation practice and interests in health and ritual.

Early life

Montano was raised in a devout Catholic family in Saugerties, New York. She entered a convent as a novice for two years before leaving to study art, an experience that deeply shaped her later work's engagement with ritual, discipline and devotion.

Career

Beginning in the 1970s Montano produced performances centred on transformation and endurance. In Handcuffed (1973) she spent time physically bound to another artist. Her best-known work is the Rope Piece (1983–84), performed with Tehching Hsieh, in which the two artists were tied together at the waist by an eight-foot rope for one full year without touching each other.

In Seven Years of Living Art (1984–1991), Montano structured each year around a different chakra and colour, wearing only clothes of that colour, listening to a designated musical tone, and spending time each month in a correspondingly painted room. The work was followed by Another Seven Years of Living Art.

Montano has also worked as "Mother Theresa" and other personas in performance, founded the Art/Life Institute in Kingston, New York, and written books on performance art, including Art in Everyday Life and Performance Artists Talking in the Eighties.
