Royal Armoured Corps

The Royal Armoured Corps (RAC) is the armoured arm of the British Army. It was created on 4 April 1939, on the eve of the Second World War, by combining the mechanised cavalry regiments of the line with the Royal Tank Corps (renamed the Royal Tank Regiment). The Corps today comprises regular and reserve regiments operating main battle tanks and armoured reconnaissance vehicles, and includes both historic cavalry regiments and the Royal Tank Regiment.

History

The mechanisation of the British cavalry during the 1930s converted horsed regiments to armoured cars and light tanks. The formation of the Royal Armoured Corps in 1939 brought these regiments together with the Royal Tank Regiment under a single corps for training and administration, while individual regiments retained their identities, uniforms and traditions. During the Second World War, RAC regiments fought in every theatre, from France and North Africa to Burma and Normandy. The corps expanded enormously with wartime-raised battalions of infantry regiments converted to armour, and shrank again through amalgamations after 1945 and especially after the Cold War.

Modern regiments

The regular RAC today includes the Household Cavalry (Life Guards and Blues and Royals) in the armoured cavalry role, the Royal Tank Regiment, the Queen's Royal Hussars, the Royal Scots Dragoon Guards, the King's Royal Hussars, the Royal Dragoon Guards, the Queen's Dragoon Guards, the Light Dragoons and the Royal Lancers, together with the Royal Yeomanry and Queen's Own Yeomanry in the Army Reserve. Regiments variously field the Challenger 2 main battle tank, the Warrior infantry fighting vehicle (in reconnaissance formations), and the Scimitar and (increasingly) Ajax family of armoured reconnaissance vehicles. The corps' home is Bovington Garrison in Dorset, site of the Tank Museum and the Armour Centre.

Cap badge and traditions

The corps cap badge depicts a mailed fist encircled by a laurel wreath, surmounted by a crown, symbolising armoured shock action. Regiments of the corps carry numerous battle honours and maintain strong county and cavalry traditions despite repeated amalgamations.
