The Catskill Park is a protected area in southeastern New York State, encompassing some 700,000 acres (2,800 km²) of the Catskill Mountains across parts of Greene, Ulster, Delaware and Sullivan counties. Unlike a conventional park, it is a mosaic of state-owned Forest Preserve land and private land; nearly 300,000 acres are state-owned and constitutionally protected as "forever wild" under Article XIV of the New York State Constitution.

History

The state Forest Preserve was created in 1885 after heavy logging and bark-peeling for the tanning industry had devastated the region's hemlock forests. In 1894 voters approved the "forever wild" constitutional clause, prohibiting logging or sale of Forest Preserve land. The Catskill Park boundary was formally drawn in 1904. Several reservoirs of the New York City water supply system — including the Ashokan, Schoharie and Pepacton reservoirs — lie within or near the park and supply drinking water to millions downstate.

Geography and recreation

The park contains 35 peaks above 3,500 feet, known collectively as the Catskill High Peaks; the tallest is Slide Mountain (4,180 ft). Popular activities include hiking, camping, fishing in famed trout streams such as the Esopus Creek and Beaver Kill, hunting, and skiing at areas including Hunter Mountain and Belleayre. The park is also known for Kaaterskill Falls, one of New York's tallest waterfalls, and for its association with Washington Irving's story "Rip Van Winkle" and the Hudson River School painters.

Management

The state lands are administered by the New York State Department of Environmental Conservation, while the Catskill Center and other organisations work on conservation and tourism across both public and private land.
