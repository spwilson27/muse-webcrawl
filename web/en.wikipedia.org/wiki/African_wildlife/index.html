African wildlife refers to the fauna of the African continent, among the richest and most famous in the world. Unlike other continents, Africa retained most of its Pleistocene megafauna — elephants, rhinoceroses, hippopotamuses, giraffes and large antelope herds — probably because African animals coevolved with early humans and learned to avoid them. The continent's wildlife is a major focus of global conservation and ecotourism.

Savanna fauna

The East and Southern African savannas support the celebrated "big five" — lion, leopard, elephant, rhinoceros and Cape buffalo — together with cheetahs, spotted hyenas, giraffes, zebras, wildebeest and dozens of antelope species. The annual Great Migration of some 1.5 million wildebeest between Tanzania's Serengeti and Kenya's Maasai Mara is one of the largest terrestrial mammal movements on Earth.

Forests, deserts and islands

The Congo Basin rainforest shelters gorillas, chimpanzees, bonobos, okapis and forest elephants. The Sahara and Namib deserts host specialised species such as the fennec fox, addax and oryx. Madagascar, isolated for tens of millions of years, has extraordinary endemism, including lemurs, fossas and chameleons found nowhere else.

Conservation

Africa's wildlife is protected in extensive national park and reserve systems, but faces pressure from poaching for ivory and rhino horn, land conversion, and conflict with growing human populations. Community conservancies and tourism revenue are central to modern conservation strategies.
