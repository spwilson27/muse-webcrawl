B-side

The B-side is the reverse side of a phonograph record, cassette, or CD single, historically carrying a secondary recording regarded as less commercial than the featured A-side. The terms derive from 78 rpm shellac discs of the early 20th century, whose two sides were designated by record companies, and the convention persisted through the eras of the 7-inch vinyl single, the cassette single, and the CD single.

History

In the early recording industry, both sides of a disc could be marketed equally, but by the 1950s the A-side/B-side hierarchy was firmly established: the A-side received the promotion, the radio airplay, and the chart placement, while the B-side was often a lesser track, an instrumental version, or a song considered unsuitable for release as a single in its own right. Elvis Presley's "Don't Be Cruel" / "Hound Dog" (1956) was a famous exception in which both sides topped the charts as a double A-side.

Famous B-sides

B-sides sometimes eclipsed their A-sides. Gloria Gaynor's "I Will Survive" began life as the B-side of "Substitute" (1978) before disc jockeys flipped the record. Other celebrated B-sides include the Beatles' "Rain" and "Revolution", Queen's "We Will Rock You" (the B-side of "We Are the Champions"), and Oasis's "Acquiesce". Some artists, notably the Beatles and the Smiths, treated B-sides as an outlet for some of their most admired non-album work, and B-side compilations later became common.

In the digital era

With downloads and streaming, the physical distinction vanished, but the term survives: artists still release "B-sides" as bonus tracks, alternate versions, or non-album songs accompanying a single, and the word has entered general usage as a metaphor for the lesser-known counterpart of something.
