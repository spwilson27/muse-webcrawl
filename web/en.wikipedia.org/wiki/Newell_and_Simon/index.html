Newell and Simon

Allen Newell (1927–1992) and Herbert A. Simon (1916–2001) were American computer and cognitive scientists whose decades-long collaboration at Carnegie Mellon University (and earlier at the RAND Corporation) founded much of the field of artificial intelligence and the information-processing approach to psychology.

Work

With programmer J. C. Shaw, Newell and Simon built the Logic Theorist (1956), often called the first artificial intelligence program, which proved theorems in Russell and Whitehead's Principia Mathematica, and the General Problem Solver (1957), which modelled human problem-solving as means–ends analysis. They developed the list-processing language IPL and championed the physical symbol system hypothesis — the claim that a system of symbols manipulating expressions is necessary and sufficient for general intelligent action.

Recognition

Together they received the ACM Turing Award in 1975 "for their basic contributions to artificial intelligence, the psychology of human cognition, and list processing". Simon also received the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences in 1978 for his work on bounded rationality and decision-making in organisations.
