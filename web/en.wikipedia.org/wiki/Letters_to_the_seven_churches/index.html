Letters to the seven churches

SourceBook of Revelation, chapters 2–3
ChurchesEphesus, Smyrna, Pergamum, Thyatira, Sardis, Philadelphia, Laodicea
SettingRoman province of Asia (western Anatolia)
VisionaryJohn of Patmos

The letters to the seven churches are messages recorded in chapters 2 and 3 of the Book of Revelation, dictated — in the vision of John of Patmos — by the risen Christ to seven Christian congregations in the Roman province of Asia: Ephesus, Smyrna, Pergamum, Thyatira, Sardis, Philadelphia and Laodicea. They follow the order of a plausible postal route through the cities of western Anatolia.

Form and content

Each letter is addressed to the "angel" of the church and follows a common pattern: a self-description of Christ drawn from the opening vision, praise for the church's strengths ("I know your works"), criticism of its faults, a call to repentance, and a promise "to the one who conquers", closing with the refrain "He who has an ear, let him hear what the Spirit says to the churches." Ephesus is rebuked for abandoning its first love; Smyrna and Philadelphia receive no criticism; Sardis is called spiritually dead; and Laodicea is famously condemned as "lukewarm — neither hot nor cold".

Interpretation

Interpreters have read the letters as addressed to literal first-century congregations facing persecution, compromise and false teaching (such as the "Nicolaitans" and the prophetess "Jezebel" at Thyatira); historicist readings additionally map them onto successive ages of church history, while many modern scholars stress their function as a pastoral audit of real communities in Asia Minor.
