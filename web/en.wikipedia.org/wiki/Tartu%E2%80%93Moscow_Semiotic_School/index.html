Tartu–Moscow Semiotic School

The Tartu–Moscow Semiotic School was a scientific movement in semiotics established in the 1960s, centred at the University of Tartu in Estonia and involving scholars in Moscow. Its leading figure was the philologist and cultural theorist Yuri Lotman.

Ideas

The school developed the study of culture as a system of signs and texts, introducing concepts such as the semiosphere — the total space of semiosis analogous to the biosphere — and the notion of culture as a "secondary modelling system" built upon natural language. Members applied structural and information-theoretic methods to literature, film, myth and everyday behaviour.

Members and legacy

Associated scholars included Boris Uspensky, Vyacheslav Ivanov, Vladimir Toporov and Boris Gasparov. The journal Sign Systems Studies, founded in Tartu in 1964, remains a leading outlet for semiotic research, and the school's summer schools at Kääriku became famous intellectual gatherings in the Soviet Union.
