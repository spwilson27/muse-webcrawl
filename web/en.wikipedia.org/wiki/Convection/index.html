Convection

Convection
TypeHeat and mass transfer by fluid motion
FormsNatural (free), forced
Key parameterRayleigh number, Nusselt number

Convection is the transfer of heat (or other quantities) by the bulk movement of a fluid — gas or liquid — as opposed to conduction, where transfer is by molecular diffusion. It is one of the three main modes of heat transfer, alongside conduction and radiation.

Overview

Convection combines advection (bulk transport) and diffusion. Newton's law of cooling, q = hAΔT, describes convective heat flux with h the heat transfer coefficient.

Natural convection

In natural (free) convection, buoyancy drives flow: heated fluid expands, becomes less dense, and rises. Its onset in a layer heated from below is governed by the Rayleigh number, producing Bénard cells.

Forced convection

In forced convection, fans, pumps, or wind impose the flow — as in radiators, heat exchangers and atmospheric circulation.

Examples

Sea breezes, boiling water, thunderstorm updrafts, mantle convection driving plate tectonics, and cooling of electronic chips are all convective phenomena.
