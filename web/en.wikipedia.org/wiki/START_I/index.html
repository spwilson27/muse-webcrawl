START I (Strategic Arms Reduction Treaty) was a bilateral treaty between the United States and the Soviet Union on the reduction and limitation of strategic offensive arms. Signed by Presidents George H. W. Bush and Mikhail Gorbachev on 31 July 1991, months before the Soviet Union dissolved, it was the largest and most complex arms control treaty in history to that date and the first to require actual reductions in strategic nuclear weapons rather than merely ceilings.

Terms and implementation

The treaty limited each side to 1,600 deployed strategic nuclear delivery vehicles (ICBMs, SLBMs and heavy bombers) carrying no more than 6,000 accountable warheads, with sub-limits on ballistic-missile warheads and throw-weight. Verification relied on an elaborate regime of data exchanges, notifications, exhibitions and on-site inspections. After the USSR's collapse, the 1992 Lisbon Protocol made Russia, Belarus, Kazakhstan and Ukraine parties; the latter three returned their inherited nuclear weapons to Russia and joined the Non-Proliferation Treaty as non-nuclear states. START I entered into force on 5 December 1994, reductions were completed by December 2001, and the treaty expired on 5 December 2009, to be succeeded in 2010 by New START. See also nuclear disarmament.

Origins

The START process began with President Ronald Reagan's 1982 proposal, a shift from SALT's limits on launchers toward deep cuts in warheads, and progressed through the Reykjavík summit of 1986 and years of negotiation in Geneva before final agreement in 1991.
