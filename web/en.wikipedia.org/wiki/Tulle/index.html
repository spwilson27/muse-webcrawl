Tulle

Tulle is a fine, lightweight, stiff netting or mesh fabric made of silk, nylon, rayon or other fibres. It is also the name of a commune in central France — Tulle, prefecture of the Corrèze department — from which the fabric takes its name.

The fabric

Tulle is produced by wrapping weft threads around warp threads in a hexagonal mesh, a technique perfected by machine in the early 19th century following the invention of the bobbinet machine. It is widely used for bridal veils and gowns, ballet tutus, evening dresses, gift wrapping and decorative purposes. Finer, softer silk tulle drapes well, while nylon tulle is stiffer and cheaper.

The town

The town of Tulle lies on the Corrèze River and is known for its cathedral, its lace-making tradition (point de Tulle) and accordion manufacture. In June 1944, during the Second World War, 99 men of the town were hanged by the German 2nd SS Panzer Division in reprisal — the Tulle massacre.
