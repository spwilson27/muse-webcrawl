UPN

The United Paramount Network (UPN) was an American broadcast television network that operated from January 16, 1995, to September 15, 2006. It was owned by United Television and later by Viacom and CBS Corporation, and was launched in partnership between Paramount Television and Chris-Craft Industries as a competitor to The WB, which debuted the same month.

History

UPN grew out of Paramount's earlier attempts to launch a television network, including the planned Paramount Television Service of the late 1970s, which was shelved and whose flagship idea became Star Trek: The Motion Picture. When UPN launched in 1995, its flagship series was Star Trek: Voyager, the fourth television series in the Star Trek franchise. The network built its early schedule around science fiction, action series, and comedies aimed largely at African American audiences, including Moesha, The Parkers, Girlfriends, and Everybody Hates Chris. It also carried the wrestling program WWF SmackDown!, one of its most-watched shows.

Closure

UPN struggled financially throughout its existence and never matched the reach or ratings of the larger networks. In January 2006, CBS Corporation and Warner Bros. announced that UPN and The WB would shut down and merge their strongest programming into a new network, The CW. UPN ceased operations on September 15, 2006, and The CW launched days later, carrying over series such as America's Next Top Model, Veronica Mars, and Friday Night SmackDown.

Programming

Notable UPN series included Star Trek: Voyager and Star Trek: Enterprise, Buffy the Vampire Slayer (in its final two seasons), Roswell, Moesha, Girlfriends, Half & Half, All of Us, and America's Next Top Model.
