Bohr–van Leeuwen theorem

The Bohr–van Leeuwen theorem states that when statistical mechanics and classical mechanics are applied consistently, the thermal average of the magnetization is always zero. This makes magnetism in solids solely a quantum mechanical effect and means that classical physics cannot account for paramagnetism, diamagnetism or ferromagnetism. The theorem is named after Niels Bohr and Hendrika Johanna van Leeuwen.

History

Bohr derived the result in his 1911 doctoral dissertation, and van Leeuwen independently rediscovered and generalised it in her 1919 thesis. The intuition is that in thermal equilibrium, the magnetic moments from electrons orbiting in one sense are exactly cancelled by those at the boundary of the material, so no net magnetisation survives averaging.

Significance

The theorem is a clean demonstration that certain macroscopic phenomena require quantum mechanics: magnetic order arises from quantised angular momentum and electron spin, neither of which exists in classical physics.
