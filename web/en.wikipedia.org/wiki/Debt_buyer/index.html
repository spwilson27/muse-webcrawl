Debt buyer

[Image: Portfolio listing of charged-off consumer accounts offered for sale]

A debt buyer is a company, sometimes a collection agency or a private debt collection law firm, that purchases delinquent or charged-off debts from creditors—typically credit card issuers, banks, and other lenders—at a fraction of the face value of the debt, and then attempts to collect the full amount, either directly, through collection agencies, or through litigation. In the United States, purchased consumer debt commonly trades for only a few cents on the dollar, with price depending on the age and type of the accounts.

Business model

Original creditors sell charged-off accounts to remove them from their books and recover something immediately. Debt buyers profit if they collect more, in aggregate, than the purchase price plus collection costs. Portfolios are usually sold with minimal documentation, which has generated controversy when buyers sue consumers and cannot prove they own the debt or the amount claimed.

Regulation and criticism

In the United States, debt buyers collecting consumer debts are generally treated as "debt collectors" under the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act and are also overseen by the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau; regulators have taken enforcement actions over misleading suits and collection of time-barred debt. The industry grew rapidly from the 1990s, and large publicly traded buyers such as Encore Capital Group and PRA Group purchase portfolios worldwide.
