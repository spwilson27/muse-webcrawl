The presidency of Jair Bolsonaro began on 1 January 2019, when Jair Bolsonaro was inaugurated as the 38th president of Brazil, and ended on 1 January 2023. A former army captain and longtime federal deputy, Bolsonaro won the 2018 presidential election as a right-wing populist, defeating Fernando Haddad of the Workers' Party in the second round.

Domestic policy

His government, with Paulo Guedes as economy minister, pursued liberal economic reforms, including a major pension reform in 2019, expanded privatization and concessions, and central bank autonomy. It loosened gun laws, adopted a hardline public-security stance, and cut funding and oversight in environmental enforcement, during which deforestation in the Amazon rose sharply, drawing international criticism.

COVID-19 pandemic

Bolsonaro's handling of the COVID-19 pandemic was widely criticized: he downplayed the virus, opposed lockdowns and mask mandates, promoted ineffective remedies such as hydroxychloroquine, and delayed vaccine procurement. Brazil recorded one of the world's highest death tolls, with over 700,000 deaths. A Senate parliamentary inquiry in 2021 recommended criminal charges against him, which were not pursued by the prosecutor general.

2022 election and aftermath

Bolsonaro sought re-election in 2022, repeatedly casting doubt on Brazil's electronic voting system. He lost the run-off to Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva on 30 October 2022, the first incumbent president to lose re-election in Brazil. He left for the United States shortly before Lula's inauguration on 1 January 2023, when supporters who rejected the result stormed the seats of the three branches of government in Brasília on 8 January 2023. In June 2023, the Superior Electoral Court barred him from holding office until 2030 for abuse of power in attacking the electoral system.
