Benoit Blanc is a fictional private detective created by filmmaker Rian Johnson and portrayed by Daniel Craig in the Knives Out film series: Knives Out (2019), Glass Onion: A Knives Out Mystery (2022) and Wake Up Dead Man (2025). A celebrated "gentleman sleuth" with a pronounced Southern American drawl, Blanc is hired on baffling murder cases whose solutions he unravels in set-piece final revelations, in the tradition of classic whodunit fiction.

Character

Johnson conceived Blanc as an American counterpart to Agatha Christie's Hercule Poirot — a famous detective invited into a closed circle of suspects. Blanc is vain about his reputation, theatrical in method and fond of food metaphors: he describes the central mystery of Knives Out as a doughnut with a hole in its centre, and a case in Glass Onion as an onion to be peeled layer by layer. Beneath the flamboyance he is methodical, patient and humane, typically working through an honest amateur partner rather than the police.

The name "Benoit Blanc" alliteratively echoes other fictional detectives. In Glass Onion it is shown that Blanc lives with a partner, Phillip, and that he spent the COVID-19 lockdown restlessly playing the game Among Us with friends including Angela Lansbury and Stephen Sondheim.

Appearances

Knives Out (2019)

Blanc is anonymously hired to investigate the death of bestselling novelist Harlan Thrombey, found dead the morning after his 85th birthday party at his Massachusetts mansion. Working alongside nurse Marta Cabrera — who cannot lie without vomiting — Blanc exposes the plot of Harlan's grandson Ransom Drysdale.

Glass Onion (2022)

Blanc, uninvited, joins tech billionaire Miles Bron's murder-mystery weekend on his private Greek island. The party game turns real as Blanc reconstructs Bron's theft of his company from co-founder Andi Brand, the murder of Andi, and the destruction of Bron's glass onion mansion.

Wake Up Dead Man (2025)

In the third film Blanc investigates a murder connected with a Catholic parish in upstate New York, in a darker, gothic register recalling the work of Edgar Allan Poe.

Reception

Craig's performance, including his deliberately extravagant Kentucky-style accent, has been widely praised and become the series' signature. Critics have credited Blanc with reviving the star-detective whodunit for modern cinema, and the character's ensembles and structure have drawn frequent comparison to Christie's Poirot stories.
