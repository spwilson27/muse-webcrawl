Gettysburg Campaign

Movements of the armies during the campaign, June–July 1863

The Gettysburg Campaign was a military invasion of Pennsylvania, Maryland and Virginia by the Confederate Army of Northern Virginia under General Robert E. Lee, conducted in June and July 1863 during the American Civil War. Lee hoped to relieve pressure on Virginia, gather supplies, win a decisive victory on Northern soil and influence Northern public opinion toward a negotiated peace.

Course of the campaign

Following his victory at Chancellorsville, Lee marched north through the Shenandoah Valley, screened by J. E. B. Stuart's cavalry, which rode around the Union army and was absent at the critical moment. The Union Army of the Potomac, first under Joseph Hooker and from 28 June under George G. Meade, pursued. The armies collided at the Battle of Gettysburg (1–3 July 1863), the bloodiest battle of the war, ending in a decisive Union victory.

Retreat and aftermath

Lee retreated to Virginia, harassed but not destroyed, crossing the Potomac on 13–14 July. Combined with the fall of Vicksburg on 4 July, Gettysburg marked a turning point of the war: the Confederacy never again mounted a major invasion of the North. Lincoln's Gettysburg Address in November 1863 rededicated the nation to the war's purposes.
