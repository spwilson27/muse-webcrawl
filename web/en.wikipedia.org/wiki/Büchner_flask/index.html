Büchner flask

Placeholder image: a Büchner flask under a Büchner funnel.

A Büchner flask, also known as a vacuum flask, filter flask, suction flask or Kitasato flask, is a thick-walled Erlenmeyer flask with a short glass tube and hose barb on its neck. It is used in the laboratory for vacuum filtration together with a Büchner funnel. The flask is named after the German industrial chemist Ernst Büchner (1850–1925); in some countries it bears the name of the Japanese bacteriologist Kitasato Shibasaburō.

Design and use

The thick glass walls withstand the pressure difference when the flask is evacuated through its side arm, which is connected by heavy-walled rubber tubing to a vacuum source such as an aspirator or pump. A Büchner funnel fitted with a rubber cone or adapter seals into the neck; filter paper inside the funnel retains the solid while the filtrate collects in the flask. The arrangement greatly speeds filtration of precipitates compared with gravity filtration.

Safety

Because an evacuated flask is under compressive stress, flasks with cracks or scratches must not be used, and a safety trap is commonly placed between flask and vacuum source to prevent suck-back of water into the filtrate.
