Overview

Buganda is a traditional kingdom within Uganda, home of the Baganda (Ganda people), the country's largest ethnic group. Its king is the Kabaka, and its cultural and political centre is Kampala, Uganda's capital, which grew from the hills of the Buganda court (Mengo, Lubaga, Namirembe).

History and status

Buganda expanded in the 18th–19th centuries into the dominant kingdom of the Great Lakes region. The 1900 Uganda Agreement defined its status under the protectorate. Kingdoms were abolished by Milton Obote in 1966–67 — after the storming of the Lubiri palace — and restored as cultural institutions in 1993; Ronald Muwenda Mutebi II is the reigning Kabaka. The Kasubi Tombs, burial place of four kabakas, are a UNESCO World Heritage Site (damaged by fire in 2010 and since under restoration).
