Rustavi is a city in the south-east of Georgia, in the province of Kvemo Kartli, situated on the Mtkvari (Kura) River about 25 kilometres from the capital Tbilisi. It is one of Georgia's largest cities and an important industrial centre.

History

Rustavi has ancient roots as a fortress town guarding a crossing of the Kura, and it appears in the legend of the founding of Georgia's early kingdoms, but the old town was destroyed in the 13th century during the Mongol invasions and never fully recovered. The modern city was founded in 1948 as a purpose-built industrial settlement for the workers of the Rustavi Metallurgical Plant, one of the largest steelworks in the Caucasus, built with the labour of German prisoners of war.

Economy and culture

After the collapse of the Soviet Union the steel plant and associated chemical factories declined sharply, bringing unemployment and population loss, although steel production later resumed under new ownership. Rustavi is also known for its international motor racing circuit, the Rustavi International Motorpark, the only purpose-built race track in the Caucasus region.
