Shrewsbury

Shrewsbury sits in a loop of the River Severn.

Shrewsbury is a market town and the county town of Shropshire, England. It is situated on the River Severn, 150 miles (240 km) north-west of London. The town centre is largely unspoilt medieval in character, enclosed for the most part within a loop of the Severn, and has a largely unaltered medieval street plan with over 660 listed buildings. At the 2021 census the town had a population of about 76,000.

History

The town grew as a Saxon settlement and was a major point in the Welsh Marches, the borderland between England and Wales. Shrewsbury Castle was founded around 1070 by Roger de Montgomery, Earl of Shrewsbury. In 1403 the Battle of Shrewsbury was fought just north of the town, in which Henry IV defeated the rebel forces of Henry "Hotspur" Percy; the battle is dramatised in Shakespeare's Henry IV, Part 1. Shrewsbury Abbey, a Benedictine monastery founded in 1083, was dissolved in 1540, though its church survives.

The town prospered as a centre of the wool trade, and later produced flax and linen. During the English Civil War it was a Royalist stronghold, surrendering to Parliament in 1645.

Notable people

Charles Darwin, the naturalist who developed the theory of evolution by natural selection, was born in Shrewsbury in 1809 and educated at Shrewsbury School. Other notable residents include the poet Wilfred Owen, who spent part of his childhood in the town, and Robert Clive ("Clive of India"), who served as mayor and MP for the borough.

Landmarks

Landmarks include Shrewsbury Castle, now home to the Shropshire Regimental Museum; the Old Market Hall of 1596; the English and Welsh bridges across the Severn; Shrewsbury Abbey; and The Quarry, a 29-acre riverside park that hosts the annual Shrewsbury Flower Show, one of the largest horticultural events in Britain.

Transport and economy

Shrewsbury railway station has services to London, Birmingham, Manchester, Cardiff and Chester. The town is bypassed by the A5 and A49 trunk roads. Historically a market and agricultural centre, the modern economy is dominated by services, public administration, light industry and tourism.
