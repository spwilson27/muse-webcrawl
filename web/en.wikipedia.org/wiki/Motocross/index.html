Motocross

Motocross is a form of off-road motorcycle racing held on enclosed off-road circuits featuring dirt tracks with jumps, berms, and whoops. The sport evolved from motorcycle trials competitions in the United Kingdom and took its name from a contraction of the French moto and the English cross country. Riders compete over a set number of laps, most often in two races ("motos") whose combined results determine the overall winner.

History

The first known scramble race was held at Camberley, Surrey, England, in 1924. The sport grew in popularity in Britain and continental Europe after the Second World War, with the Motocross des Nations team event founded in 1947 and a European — later World — Championship established in the 1950s. Belgium and Sweden produced dominant riders, and specialized machines from Husqvarna, CZ, and later Japanese manufacturers transformed the sport. In the United States, motocross boomed in the late 1960s and 1970s, propelled by promoter Edison Dye and the creation of the AMA Motocross Championship in 1972. The indoor stadium variant, supercross, began in 1972 at the Los Angeles Coliseum and became a major American spectator sport.

Major championships

  • MXGP — the FIM Motocross World Championship, with MXGP (450 cc) and MX2 (250 cc) classes.
  • AMA Supercross and Pro Motocross — the premier American series, combined into the SuperMotocross World Championship from 2023.
  • Motocross des Nations — the annual "Olympics of motocross" for national teams.

Bikes and equipment

Modern motocross bikes are single-cylinder four-strokes of 250 cc or 450 cc (with 125 cc and 250 cc two-strokes still raced in some classes), featuring long-travel suspension, knobby tyres, and minimal weight. Riders wear helmets, goggles, chest protectors, boots, and increasingly neck braces, reflecting the sport's high injury risk.

Notable riders

Stefan Everts holds ten world championships; Antonio Cairoli won nine. In America, Ricky Carmichael — nicknamed "the GOAT" — dominated the early 2000s with ten AMA titles, followed by stars such as Jeremy McGrath (supercross), James Stewart, Ryan Dungey, Eli Tomac, and Jett Lawrence.
