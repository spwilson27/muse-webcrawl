Antonio Gramsci (22 January 1891 – 27 April 1937) was an Italian Marxist philosopher, writer and politician. A founding member and leader of the Communist Party of Italy, he was imprisoned by Benito Mussolini's Fascist regime in 1926 and remained incarcerated until shortly before his death. His Prison Notebooks, some 3,000 pages written in confinement, develop the theory of cultural hegemony — how a ruling class maintains dominance through consent and culture, not coercion alone — and remain foundational for political theory and cultural studies.

Life

Born in Ales, Sardinia, to a poor family, Gramsci suffered ill health from childhood, including a spinal deformity. A brilliant student, he won a scholarship to the University of Turin, where he joined the Italian Socialist Party and became a journalist. During Turin's factory council movement of 1919–20 (biennio rosso), he edited the paper L'Ordine Nuovo. In 1921 he co-founded the Communist Party of Italy at Livorno, became its leader in 1924 and a deputy in parliament. Despite parliamentary immunity he was arrested in November 1926; the prosecutor demanded that "this brain" be stopped from functioning for twenty years. He received a sentence of over 20 years and was held mainly at Turi prison, where, in deteriorating health, he wrote the Notebooks. Released to a clinic in 1934, he died in Rome in 1937.

Thought

Gramsci asked why revolution had failed in the West despite capitalism's crises. He argued that in advanced societies the state is supplemented by "civil society" — schools, churches, media, associations — through which the ruling class secures "hegemony": active consent to its world-view as common sense. Countering it requires a long "war of position" rather than a frontal "war of manoeuvre", led by "organic intellectuals" arising from the subaltern classes themselves. His concepts — hegemony, civil society, the national-popular, passive revolution, and his analysis of the Southern Question and of Americanism and Fordism — have influenced fields from political science to postcolonial studies.
