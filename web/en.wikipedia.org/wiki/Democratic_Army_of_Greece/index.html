The Democratic Army of Greece (Greek: Δημοκρατικός Στρατός Ελλάδας, Dimokratikos Stratos Elladas, abbreviated DSE) was the military force established by the Communist Party of Greece (KKE) during the Greek Civil War of 1946–1949. It was officially founded on 28 October 1946, building on earlier guerrilla activity by former members of the wartime resistance organisation ELAS who had taken to the mountains in the face of persecution by government forces and right-wing bands.

History

The DSE was commanded militarily by Markos Vafeiadis, a veteran ELAS commander, under the political direction of KKE general secretary Nikos Zachariadis. In December 1947 the communists proclaimed a Provisional Democratic Government, with Vafeiadis as prime minister, though no foreign state recognised it. The DSE received substantial support — supplies, training, and sanctuary — from Greece's northern communist neighbours, above all Yugoslavia, as well as Albania and Bulgaria.

Initially the DSE waged guerrilla warfare across the Greek countryside, but under Zachariadis's influence it progressively adopted conventional positional warfare, attempting to seize and hold towns such as Konitsa and Karditsa. At its peak in 1948–1949 the DSE fielded roughly 25,000–30,000 fighters, including a notable proportion of women and of Slavic-speaking Macedonians.

Defeat

The tide turned in 1949. The Tito–Stalin split led Yugoslavia to close its border to the DSE in July 1949, cutting off its principal supply route. Reorganised and heavily supported by the United States under the Truman Doctrine, the Greek National Army launched successful offensives — notably Operation Torch — against the DSE strongholds in the Grammos and Vitsi mountains. By October 1949 Zachariadis announced a ceasefire, and the surviving fighters fled into exile in Eastern Europe and the Soviet Union, where many remained for decades. The civil war ended with the defeat of the communists and Greece's alignment with the Western bloc.

Legacy

The DSE and the civil war left deep scars on Greek society, including the exile of tens of thousands of refugees and the controversial evacuation of children from the war zones. Official recognition of the DSE's fighters as part of the national resistance came only in 1989, when a government of national reconciliation legislated the civil war's end and recognised ELAS and DSE veterans.
