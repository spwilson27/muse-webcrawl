Flat-coated Retriever

The flat-coated retriever (also capitalised Flat-Coated Retriever) is a British retriever breed of gun dog, recognised by kennel clubs under the Sporting/Gundog group. It was bred to retrieve shot game from land and water during a day's shooting.

Appearance

Dogs stand roughly 59–62 cm at the withers and bitches slightly less; weights run about 27–36 kg for dogs and 25–32 kg for bitches. The coat is dense, flat and glossy, forming moderate feathering on the legs and tail, in solid black or solid liver only. The head is long and cleanly moulded, with dark almond-shaped eyes giving an intelligent, kind expression.

Temperament and use

Flat-coated retrievers are cheerful, outgoing and devoted dogs with a strong tail-wagging, optimistic character. Bred as dual-purpose working and show dogs, they retain a strong retrieving instinct and love of water, and they compete in field trials, obedience, agility and tracking as well as in the show ring. Their slow maturity and boisterous youth make them demanding but rewarding companions.

Background

The breed emerged in nineteenth-century England from crosses involving the St. John's water dog of Newfoundland with setters, sheepdogs and retriever types. For several decades it was Britain's most numerous retriever before the Labrador and Golden retrievers eclipsed it; it remains comparatively uncommon today.
