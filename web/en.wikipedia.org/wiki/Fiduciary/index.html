Fiduciary

A fiduciary is a person who holds a legal or ethical relationship of trust with another person (the principal or beneficiary) and is bound to act in that person's best interests when managing money or other assets. The word derives from the Latin fiducia, meaning trust. Fiduciary duties are among the most demanding obligations known to common law: as Justice Benjamin Cardozo wrote in Meinhard v. Salmon (1928), a fiduciary is held to "the punctilio of an honor the most sensitive".

Fiduciary duties

The two core duties are the duty of loyalty — the fiduciary must act solely in the beneficiary's interest, avoiding conflicts of interest and self-dealing — and the duty of care (or prudence) — the fiduciary must manage the entrusted affairs with the diligence of a prudent person, informing themselves and following the terms of the arrangement. Depending on the relationship, further duties may include obedience to instructions, full disclosure, confidentiality, and accounting for all property handled. Breach of fiduciary duty can result in damages, disgorgement of profits, removal, and in some cases criminal liability.

Examples of fiduciaries

  • Trustees administering a trust for beneficiaries.
  • Executors and administrators of a deceased person's estate, and guardians of minors or incapacitated persons.
  • Corporate directors and officers, who owe fiduciary duties to the corporation and its shareholders.
  • Agents acting for principals, including attorneys-in-fact under a power of attorney.
  • Investment advisers registered under securities law, pension plan trustees (in the US, under ERISA), and in some circumstances lawyers toward their clients and partners toward one another.

In finance

Debate over the "fiduciary standard" versus the weaker "suitability standard" has shaped financial regulation: registered investment advisers must place clients' interests first, while broker-dealers were historically held only to recommend suitable products; the US Department of Labor's fiduciary rule for retirement accounts (2016, vacated in 2018) and the SEC's Regulation Best Interest (2019) were attempts to narrow the gap. In corporate law, directors' duties of loyalty and care are policed through doctrines such as the business judgment rule and enhanced scrutiny of takeover defences.

Origins

Fiduciary law grew out of the English Court of Chancery's supervision of trusts and uses of land, and its concepts of conscience and good faith were generalised to other relationships of confidence. Civil law jurisdictions reach similar results through good-faith and mandate doctrines, though usually without an identical umbrella concept.
