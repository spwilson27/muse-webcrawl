The Indian Coast Guard (ICG) is a maritime law enforcement and search and rescue agency of India with jurisdiction over its territorial waters, including its contiguous zone and exclusive economic zone. It was formally established on 18 August 1978 by the Coast Guard Act, 1978 of the Parliament of India, and operates under the Ministry of Defence.

Duties

The Coast Guard's responsibilities include protecting India's maritime interests and enforcing maritime law, with jurisdiction over the territorial waters of India (12 nautical miles) and the exclusive economic zone (200 nautical miles). Its duties include offshore security, anti-smuggling and anti-poaching operations, protection of fishermen, search and rescue, marine pollution response, and assistance to customs and fisheries authorities.

Organisation

The service is headed by the Director General Indian Coast Guard, and is organised into regional headquarters (North-West, West, East, North-East, and Andaman & Nicobar), supported by district and station headquarters along the coast and island territories. It works in close coordination with the Indian Navy, the Department of Fisheries, state marine police, and customs.

Equipment

The ICG operates a fleet of offshore patrol vessels, fast patrol vessels, air cushion vehicles (hovercraft), and interceptor boats, together with aircraft including Dornier 228 maritime patrol aircraft and HAL Dhruv and Chetak helicopters. Its aviation arm was notably expanded following the 2008 Mumbai attacks, which prompted a major strengthening of coastal security.
