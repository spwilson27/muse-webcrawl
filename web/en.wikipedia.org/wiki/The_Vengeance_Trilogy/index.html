Overview

The Vengeance Trilogy (also known as the Revenge Trilogy) is a series of three South Korean films directed by Park Chan-wook, thematically united by their explorations of revenge, violence, and the moral costs of retribution. The films are not connected by plot or characters but by theme and style.

The films

Sympathy for Mr. Vengeance (2002) tells of a deaf-mute steelworker whose desperate attempt to obtain a kidney for his dying sister spirals into kidnapping and a chain of killings. Oldboy (2003), loosely based on a Japanese manga, follows Oh Dae-su, imprisoned without explanation for fifteen years and released to find his captor; it won the Grand Prix at the 2004 Cannes Film Festival and became an international cult classic, famous for its corridor fight scene filmed in a single take.

Lady Vengeance (2005) concludes the trilogy with the story of Lee Geum-ja, released from prison after thirteen years for a murder she did not commit, who carefully plots revenge against the real killer. Together the three films are credited with bringing South Korean cinema to worldwide attention and exemplify the extreme, stylized filmmaking of the Korean New Wave.
