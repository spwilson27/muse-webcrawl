Force Z was a British naval squadron sent to Singapore in late 1941 as a deterrent to Japanese aggression in the Far East. It consisted principally of the battleship HMS Prince of Wales and the battlecruiser HMS Repulse, escorted by four destroyers, under the command of Admiral Sir Tom Phillips. Both capital ships were sunk by Japanese naval aircraft on 10 December 1941 in one of the defining naval disasters of the Second World War.

Background

In the pre-war "Singapore strategy", Britain planned to send a fleet east in the event of war with Japan. By 1941, with Britain fully engaged against Germany and Italy, only a small force could be spared. Prime Minister Winston Churchill pressed for modern capital ships to be sent as a political deterrent, despite Admiralty doubts. The aircraft carrier HMS Indomitable, intended to provide air cover, ran aground in the Caribbean and never joined the force.

Loss

Force Z arrived at Singapore on 2 December 1941. When Japan invaded Malaya on 8 December, Phillips sailed to intercept the invasion convoys, without air cover and maintaining radio silence. The force was sighted by a Japanese submarine and then by reconnaissance aircraft, and on 10 December waves of land-based bombers and torpedo bombers of the Imperial Japanese Navy attacked off Kuantan. Repulse and Prince of Wales were both sunk, with the loss of 840 men, including Phillips and Captain John Leach. They were the first capital ships in history to be sunk by air attack alone while at sea and free to manoeuvre — a landmark in the ascendancy of naval air power over the battleship.

Aftermath

The loss of Force Z removed the principal Allied naval deterrent in the region, clearing the way for the Japanese advance on Singapore, which fell in February 1942. The wrecks, lying in the South China Sea, are designated war graves and protected sites.
