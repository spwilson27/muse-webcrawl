Quiberon

Quiberon (Breton: Kiberen) is a commune in the Morbihan department in Brittany in north-western France. It occupies the southern end of the Quiberon Peninsula (Presqu'île de Quiberon), a narrow strip of land extending into the Atlantic, connected to the mainland by a sandy isthmus. The commune is a popular seaside resort and the departure point for ferries to Belle-Île-en-Mer and other islands of Morbihan.

The Côte Sauvage on the Quiberon Peninsula

History

In June–July 1795, during the French Revolutionary Wars and the Chouannerie, French royalist émigrés supported by Britain landed on the peninsula in an attempt to stir a counter-revolutionary rising; the Quiberon expedition ended in defeat at the hands of General Lazare Hoche, and hundreds of prisoners were executed. The peninsula's dramatic western cliffs, the Côte Sauvage, contrast with the sheltered sandy beaches on its eastern side.

In the 19th and 20th centuries, sardine fishing and canning were mainstays of the local economy; the port of Port-Maria remains active. Today tourism, thalassotherapy, and sailing dominate, and the peninsula is a noted site for surfing and windsurfing.
