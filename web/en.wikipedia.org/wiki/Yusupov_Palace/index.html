The Yusupov Palace (Russian: Юсуповский дворец), also known as the Moika Palace, is a former residence of the Yusupov family in Saint Petersburg, Russia. Situated on the embankment of the Moika River, it is best known as the site of the murder of Grigori Rasputin in December 1916.

History

The palace was built in the 1770s to a design by the French architect Jean-Baptiste Vallin de la Mothe. It passed to Prince Nikolai Yusupov in 1830, and the fabulously wealthy Yusupov family remodelled the interiors in a succession of revival styles, adding a private theatre, a Moorish drawing room, and extensive art collections. By the early 20th century the family fortune rivalled that of the imperial family itself.

Murder of Rasputin

On the night of 29–30 December 1916 (16–17 December Old Style), Prince Felix Yusupov and his co-conspirators, including Grand Duke Dmitri Pavlovich and the politician Vladimir Purishkevich, lured the mystic Grigori Rasputin to the palace basement, where he was shot; his body was thrown into the Malaya Nevka River. The basement room is now a museum exhibit with wax figures reconstructing the scene.

Museum

After the Russian Revolution the palace was nationalised. It survived the Second World War comparatively intact and today operates as a museum and cultural centre, noted for its remarkably preserved 19th-century interiors.
