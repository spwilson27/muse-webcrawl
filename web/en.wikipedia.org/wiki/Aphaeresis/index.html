Aphaeresis

Aphaeresis
Also spelledApheresis (esp. American English)
FieldPhonology / historical linguistics
DefinitionLoss of one or more sounds at the beginning of a word

Aphaeresis (British spelling; American apheresis) is, in phonetics and historical linguistics, the loss of one or more sounds from the beginning of a word, especially the loss of an unstressed initial vowel. The term comes from Greek aphaíresis, "a taking away".

Examples

English examples include 'round from around, 'cause from because, cute from acute, and spy from Old French espier. In some words the shortened form has fully replaced the original, as with squire from esquire and sample from example. Aphaeresis is common in informal speech, in poetry for metrical reasons, and in the historical development of many languages.

Related processes

Aphaeresis is the initial counterpart of two other sound-loss processes: syncope, the loss of sounds from the middle of a word (as in family pronounced "fam'ly"), and apocope, the loss of sounds from the end of a word. Together these are grouped under the broader phenomenon of elision.
