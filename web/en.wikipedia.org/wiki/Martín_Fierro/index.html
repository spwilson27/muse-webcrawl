GenreEpic poem, gauchesque literature

Martín Fierro, also known as El Gaucho Martín Fierro, is a 2,316-line epic poem by the Argentine writer José Hernández. First published in 1872, with a sequel, La vuelta de Martín Fierro ("The Return of Martín Fierro"), in 1879, it is widely considered the national epic of Argentina and a masterpiece of gauchesque literature.

Plot

The poem is narrated in the first person by Martín Fierro, a gaucho who is torn from his home and family by forced conscription into the frontier army fighting indigenous peoples. Deserted and abused, he deserts, returns to find his home destroyed and his family scattered, and becomes a gaucho matrero — an outlaw. After killing men in duels and fleeing the police, he escapes into the desert with his companion Cruz. In the sequel, Fierro returns from captivity among the indigenous peoples and is reunited with his sons, to whom he delivers his famous counsel on work, family, and justice.

Themes and significance

Written in the language and meter of the payadores (gaucho minstrels), the poem protests the corruption and injustice of conscription and the destruction of the gaucho way of life during Argentina's modernization. Initially popular literature sold in country stores, it was elevated in the early twentieth century by critics such as Leopoldo Lugones, who proclaimed it the national epic. It has been translated into dozens of languages and adapted for film, theatre, and music.
