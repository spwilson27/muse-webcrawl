CURB-65

CURB-65, also known as the CURB criteria, is a clinical prediction rule that has been validated for predicting mortality in community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) and in infection of any site. The CURB-65 is based on the earlier CURB score and is recommended by the British Thoracic Society for the assessment of severity of pneumonia. It was developed in 2002 by Lim and colleagues from a study of over 1,000 patients.

The score

One point is given for each of the following features present in a patient with pneumonia:

  • C — Confusion: new mental confusion (AMTS ≤ 8)
  • U — Urea: blood urea nitrogen > 7 mmol/L (19 mg/dL)
  • R — Respiratory rate ≥ 30 breaths per minute
  • B — Blood pressure: systolic < 90 mmHg and/or diastolic ≤ 60 mmHg
  • 65 — Age ≥ 65 years

A simplified version, CRB-65, omits the urea measurement and can be used in primary care where blood tests are unavailable.

Comparison

The other widely used severity rule, the Pneumonia Severity Index (PSI/PORT score), uses 20 variables and stratifies patients into five risk classes; it is more accurate at identifying low-risk patients but more complex to calculate. Guidelines differ: British practice favors CURB-65, while North American guidelines historically favored the PSI, with recent ATS/IDSA guidance de-emphasizing routine use of severity scores beyond clinical judgment plus either tool.
