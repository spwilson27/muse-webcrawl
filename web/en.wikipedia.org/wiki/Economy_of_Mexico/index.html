The economy of Mexico is a developing mixed-market economy. It is among the fifteen largest economies in the world by nominal GDP and is the second-largest economy in Latin America after Brazil. Mexico is heavily integrated with the United States through trade, and is a member of the United States–Mexico–Canada Agreement (USMCA), the successor to NAFTA.

Mexico is a major manufacturing exporter, particularly of automobiles, auto parts, electronics and machinery. It is also a significant producer of petroleum, although oil's share of exports and government revenue has declined. Remittances from Mexicans working abroad, especially in the United States, are one of the country's largest sources of foreign income.

History

Following the Mexican Revolution, the state played a large role in the economy. The period from about 1940 to 1970, known as the Mexican miracle, saw sustained growth under import-substitution industrialisation. A debt crisis in 1982 led to bank nationalisation and a "lost decade" of stagnation. In the 1980s and 1990s Mexico liberalised trade, privatised state firms and joined NAFTA in 1994. The Tequila Crisis of 1994–95 caused a sharp recession, followed by export-led recovery.

Sectors

Manufacturing

Mexico is one of the world's leading exporters of automobiles and flat-screen displays. Assembly plants, historically called maquiladoras, cluster near the northern border and in central industrial states.

Petroleum

The state oil company Pemex, founded after the 1938 expropriation of foreign oil companies, long dominated the sector. Production peaked in 2004 and has since declined. A 2013 energy reform opened the sector to private investment, though later policy partially reversed course.

Tourism

Mexico is one of the most visited countries in the world, with destinations including Cancún, the Riviera Maya, Mexico City and colonial towns. Tourism is a major source of employment and foreign exchange.

Trade

The United States takes the great majority of Mexican exports. In 2023 Mexico became the United States' largest goods trading partner, ahead of China and Canada, aided by nearshoring trends as firms moved production closer to the US market.
