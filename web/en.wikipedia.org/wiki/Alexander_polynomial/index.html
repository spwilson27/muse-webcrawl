In knot theory, the Alexander polynomial is a knot invariant which assigns a polynomial with integer coefficients to each knot type. Introduced by James Waddell Alexander II in 1928, it was the first knot polynomial discovered, and it can be computed algorithmically from any knot diagram.

Definition

For an oriented knot K, the Alexander polynomial ΔK(t) is defined up to multiplication by units ±tn from the homology of the infinite cyclic covering space of the knot complement. In practice it is often computed using a Seifert matrix V associated to a Seifert surface: ΔK(t) is proportional to det(V − tVT). John Conway later found a simpler skein-relation description using a normalised two-variable-free form, the Alexander–Conway polynomial ∇(z), related by the substitution z = t1/2 − t−1/2.

Examples

The unknot has Δ(t) = 1. The trefoil knot has Δ(t) = t − 1 + t−1 (in symmetric Laurent form), while the figure-eight knot has Δ(t) = −t + 3 − t−1. The Alexander polynomial cannot distinguish mutant knots, nor in general a knot from its mirror image, limitations later addressed by the Jones polynomial (1984) and Floer homology theories. The genus of a knot bounds the polynomial's degree: 2·genus ≥ deg Δ.
