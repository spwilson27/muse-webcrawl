Thomas Cubitt

Born25 February 1788, Buxton, Norfolk, England
Died20 December 1855 (aged 67), Dorking, Surrey
OccupationMaster builder, developer
Known forDeveloping Belgravia, Bloomsbury, Pimlico; Osborne House

Thomas Cubitt (25 February 1788 – 20 December 1855) was an English master builder celebrated for his development of large districts of London, including Belgravia, Bloomsbury, and Pimlico. He is often described as the foremost builder of Georgian and early Victorian London.

Career

Born in Norfolk, the son of a carpenter, Cubitt travelled to India as a ship's carpenter before returning to London around 1810 and establishing his building business. He revolutionised the trade by employing all the building trades directly in his own large workshops on the Isle of Dogs, rather than relying on subcontractors — an early model of the integrated construction firm. His workshops could produce bricks, joinery, ironwork, and stucco ornament at scale.

Major developments

Working on leases from great landowners such as the Grosvenor and Bedford estates, Cubitt laid out the stuccoed terraces and squares of Belgravia and Pimlico, much of Bloomsbury, and streets in Islington and elsewhere. Queen Victoria and Prince Albert engaged him to build Osborne House on the Isle of Wight (1845–1851), and he also built the east front of Buckingham Palace (1847–1850). His brother Lewis Cubitt was a noted architect, designer of King's Cross station, and his younger brother William Cubitt was a prominent civil engineer.

Legacy

Cubitt was noted for decent treatment of his workforce, providing amenities unusual for the time, and he championed public parks and baths for working people. He died at his home in Dorking in 1855, leaving a substantial fortune. Statues of him stand in Pimlico and Dorking, and the firm he founded survived, in various forms, into the modern era.
