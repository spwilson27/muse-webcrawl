Jacobus de Voragine (Italian: Giacomo da Varazze; c. 1230 – 13/14 July 1298) was an Italian chronicler and Archbishop of Genoa. He was the author, or compiler, of the Legenda Aurea, the Golden Legend, a collection of lives of the saints that became one of the most popular and widely read books of the later Middle Ages. He was beatified by Pope Pius VII in 1816.

Life

Jacobus was born at Varazze (Voragine in Latin) near Genoa. He entered the Dominican Order in 1244, the year of its arrival in Genoa, and rose through its ranks, serving as prior and as provincial of Lombardy from 1267 to 1286. In 1292 he was elected Archbishop of Genoa. As archbishop he worked to reconcile the feuding Guelph and Ghibelline factions of the city, achieving a peace in 1295, and supported the city's poor and religious houses.

The Golden Legend

Compiled around 1260, the Golden Legend arranged some 180 lives of saints and accounts of feast days according to the liturgical calendar. Drawing on earlier collections and adding popular etymologies and miraculous tales, it went through more editions than the Bible in the late Middle Ages — over a thousand manuscripts survive, and it was among the first books William Caxton printed in English (1483). Though criticized by Renaissance humanists and banned in some Protestant contexts for its credulous miracle stories, it profoundly shaped medieval art, providing the iconography by which saints were depicted for centuries.

Other works

Jacobus also wrote a chronicle of Genoa (Chronicon Januense), sermons, and a life of the Virgin. He died in Genoa in 1298 and was buried in the Dominican church there.
