Kohima is the capital of the Indian state of Nagaland. It lies in the foothills of the Japfü mountain range in the Naga Hills at an elevation of about 1,444 metres (4,738 ft). With a population of about 99,000, it is the second-largest city in the state after Dimapur. Kohima is the home of the Angami Naga tribe and headquarters of Kohima District.

History

Kohima became the headquarters of the Naga Hills District under British administration in the 1870s–1880s. During the Second World War it was the site of the Battle of Kohima (April–June 1944), in which British and Indian forces halted the Japanese U-Go offensive into India; the battle, together with Imphal, is often called the "Stalingrad of the East". The Kohima War Cemetery, maintained by the Commonwealth War Graves Commission, bears the famous Kohima Epitaph: "When you go home, tell them of us and say, for your tomorrow, we gave our today."

Culture

Each December, Kohima hosts the Hornbill Festival at the Naga Heritage Village in Kisama, a major celebration of the cultures of Nagaland's tribes. The city is also known for the Kohima Cathedral, one of the largest churches in Northeast India, and for the State Museum's ethnographic collections.
