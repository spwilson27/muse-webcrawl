The 7.92×57mm Mauser (designated as the 8mm Mauser or 8×57mm by the SAAMI, and 8×57 IS by the C.I.P.) is a rimless bottlenecked rifle cartridge. Developed by the German Gewehr-Prüfungskommission, it was the standard service cartridge of Germany through both world wars.

Development

The cartridge descends from the Patrone 88 of 1888, chambered in the Gewehr 88. In 1903 Germany adopted the improved S Patrone with a lighter, pointed spitzer bullet of larger diameter (.323 in), requiring barrels to be re-bored; rifles so converted were marked accordingly. The resulting combination served in the Gewehr 98 and Karabiner 98k rifles and in machine guns from the MG 08 to the MG 42.

Sporting use

After 1945 the cartridge remained popular in Europe for hunting deer and boar. To avoid confusion with the older .318 in bore "J" version, sporting ammunition is sold as 8×57 IS (or JS), while the older loading is 8×57 I (J). It remains in production by most major European ammunition makers.
