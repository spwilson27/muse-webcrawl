Sunday school

A Sunday school is an educational institution, usually (but not always) Christian in character, that provides religious instruction to children and sometimes adults on Sundays, typically before or after church services. The movement originated in eighteenth-century Britain as a means of educating working children who were employed in factories during the week.

History

Sunday schools are generally credited to Robert Raikes, a Gloucester newspaper publisher, who began classes for poor children in 1780 and publicised the idea in his journal in 1783. The schools taught reading — using the Bible as a textbook — along with writing and arithmetic. The movement spread rapidly through Britain and North America, supported by interdenominational Sunday school unions.

Modern practice

As state-funded weekday education became universal, Sunday schools shifted from general literacy to religious teaching: Bible stories, hymns, catechism and moral instruction, usually led by lay volunteers. Many churches now hold children's classes during the main service, and the term is also used for similar institutions in Judaism, Islam and other traditions held on their respective days of worship.
