'Ndrangheta

The 'Ndrangheta (from Greek andragathía, "heroism" or "manly virtue") is a prominent Italian Mafia-type organized crime syndicate based in the region of Calabria in Southern Italy. Originating in the 18th century, it has since expanded to many countries worldwide and is widely considered the most powerful Italian criminal organization, surpassing the Sicilian Cosa Nostra and the Neapolitan Camorra in global reach.

Structure

Unlike other Italian mafias, the 'Ndrangheta is organized around blood ties and marriages between families. Its basic unit is the 'ndrina, a clan based on kinship, which controls a territory known as a locale. Horizontal coordination bodies such as the Camera di Controllo and the Crimine oversee inter-clan disputes.

Activities

The syndicate is heavily involved in international cocaine trafficking, extortion, money laundering, arms trafficking and waste dumping. Europol and Italian authorities estimate its annual revenue in the tens of billions of euros. The 2007 Duisburg massacre in Germany brought international attention to its expansion beyond Italy.
