Liothyronine

Trade namesCytomel, Tertroxin, others
ClassThyroid hormone
FormulaC15H12I3NO4
Elimination half-life~2.5 days

Liothyronine is a manufactured form of the thyroid hormone triiodothyronine (T3). It is most commonly used to treat hypothyroidism and myxedema coma. It may also be used as an adjunct in the management of certain thyroid cancers and in diagnostic suppression tests.

Medical use

Most people with hypothyroidism are treated with levothyroxine (T4) alone; liothyronine is faster-acting and more potent than levothyroxine, with a shorter half-life, which makes dosing less forgiving. Its clearest indications are myxedema coma, where rapid effect is needed, and preparation of patients with thyroid cancer for radioiodine scanning, where it shortens the period of hypothyroid symptoms. Combination T4/T3 therapy for ordinary hypothyroidism remains controversial, and major guidelines do not recommend it routinely.

Side effects

Excess dosing produces symptoms of hyperthyroidism: palpitations, tremor, anxiety, heat intolerance, weight loss and, with chronic overtreatment, atrial fibrillation and loss of bone mineral density.

History

T3 was identified in 1952 by Jack Gross and Rosalind Pitt-Rivers. Synthetic liothyronine (as the sodium salt) was marketed from the 1950s under names such as Cytomel.
