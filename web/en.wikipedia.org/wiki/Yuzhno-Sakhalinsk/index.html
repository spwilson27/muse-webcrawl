Yuzhno-Sakhalinsk (Russian: Ю́жно-Сахали́нск, IPA: [ˈjuʐnə səxɐˈlʲinsk], lit. "South Sakhalin") is a city and the administrative center of Sakhalin Oblast, Russia. It is located in the south of Sakhalin Island near Susuya Bay, about 25 kilometres (16 mi) north of Aniva Bay, and is the largest city on the island.

History

The settlement began as the small Russian village of Vladimirovka in 1882. Under the Treaty of Portsmouth (1905) southern Sakhalin passed to Japan, and the city, renamed Toyohara (豊原, "bountiful plain"), was planned and built as the capital of Karafuto Prefecture from 1937. Soviet forces retook the city in August 1945, and in 1946 it was renamed Yuzhno-Sakhalinsk. The Japanese and Korean populations were repatriated, though many ethnic Koreans remained.

Today the city is the center of Sakhalin's oil and gas industry, hosting offices of companies involved in the Sakhalin-1 and Sakhalin-2 projects. The city is served by Yuzhno-Sakhalinsk Airport (Khomutovo) and the Sakhalin Railway, and suffers severe winters; the 1995 Neftegorsk earthquake struck the north of the island, while Yuzhno-Sakhalinsk itself is known for its mudflows and heavy snow.
