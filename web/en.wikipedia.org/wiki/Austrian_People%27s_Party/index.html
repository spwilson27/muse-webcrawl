Austrian People's Party (ÖVP)
Founded: 17 April 1945
Ideology: Conservatism, Christian democracy, liberal conservatism
Political position: Centre-right
European affiliation: European People's Party
Colour: Turquoise (from 2017)

The Austrian People's Party (German: Österreichische Volkspartei, ÖVP) is a Christian-democratic and liberal-conservative political party in Austria. Founded in 1945 as the successor in spirit to the pre-war Christian Social Party, it has been one of the two dominant parties of the Second Republic, frequently governing in coalition or alone.

History

The ÖVP won Austria's first post-war election in 1945 under Leopold Figl and led governments for much of the following decades, often in grand coalition with the Social Democratic Party. Under Sebastian Kurz, who became leader in 2017 and rebranded the party with the colour turquoise, the ÖVP finished first in the 2017 and 2019 elections and Kurz became Chancellor. Kurz resigned in 2021 amid a corruption investigation; Karl Nehammer subsequently became party leader and Chancellor.
