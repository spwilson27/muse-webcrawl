Research Councils UK

Research Councils UK (RCUK) was, from 2002 to 2018, the umbrella partnership of the United Kingdom's seven research councils, the publicly funded agencies that invest in science, engineering, medicine, the social sciences, and the arts and humanities. It coordinated strategy and shared services across the councils and represented them collectively to government. On 1 April 2018 its functions, together with those of the individual councils and Innovate UK, were brought together in a new body, UK Research and Innovation (UKRI), created by the Higher Education and Research Act 2017.

Member councils

The seven councils were the Arts and Humanities Research Council (AHRC), the Biotechnology and Biological Sciences Research Council (BBSRC), the Economic and Social Research Council (ESRC), the Engineering and Physical Sciences Research Council (EPSRC), the Medical Research Council (MRC), the Natural Environment Research Council (NERC) and the Science and Technology Facilities Council (STFC). Together they invested roughly £3 billion a year in university research grants, studentships, institutes and major facilities such as the Diamond Light Source.

Role

RCUK promoted joint working on cross-disciplinary themes (such as energy, digital economy and lifelong health), ran shared administrative services from its Swindon base, engaged internationally on open access and research integrity, and published common policies, including the RCUK policy on open access to research publications. The councils operated under the Haldane principle that decisions on individual research proposals should be taken by researchers through peer review, at arm's length from ministers.
