Finswimming

Finswimming is an underwater sport consisting of four techniques involving swimming with the use of fins either on the water's surface using a snorkel with either monofins or bifins or underwater with monofin either by holding one's breath or using open circuit scuba diving equipment. Events exist over distances similar to those in swimming competitions for both swimming pools and open water venues. Competition at world and continental level is organised by the Confédération Mondiale des Activités Subaquatiques (CMAS). The sport's first world championships were held in 1976.

History

Finswimming developed in Europe during the 1930s after the development of the first commercially available rubber swimfins by Frenchman Louis de Corlieu in 1933. During the 1950s and 1960s, the sport grew in popularity in the Soviet Union and parts of Europe, with the first European championships held in 1967 and the first world championships in Hanover, Germany, in 1976.

Techniques

Surface finswimming

Surface finswimming (SF) uses a monofin and a snorkel, swimming on the surface with a dolphin kick.

Apnoea finswimming

Apnoea finswimming (AP) is performed underwater in a pool over 50 metres while holding the breath.

Immersion finswimming

Immersion finswimming (IM) uses scuba equipment with compressed air, performed underwater over distances up to 1,000 metres.

Bi-fins

Bi-fins (BF) is performed on the surface using two fins, one on each foot, with a snorkel and a front crawl style arm stroke.

Governing body

The international governing body is CMAS, which recognises finswimming within its underwater sports commission. The sport is also recognised by the International Olympic Committee, and competitions are held at the World Games.
