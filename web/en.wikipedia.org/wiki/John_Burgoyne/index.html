John Burgoyne

John Burgoyne (24 February 1722 – 4 August 1792) was a British Army officer, dramatist and politician best known for surrendering his army at the Battle of Saratoga in 1777 during the American Revolutionary War, a defeat that helped bring France into the war on the American side.

Early career

Burgoyne served in the Seven Years' War, distinguishing himself in Portugal in 1762, and sat in Parliament for much of his life. He was also a successful playwright; his comedy The Heiress (1786) was popular in London. He arrived in Boston in 1775 and was present at the Battle of Bunker Hill.

Saratoga campaign

In 1777 Burgoyne led an army south from Canada intending to cut New England off from the other colonies. After capturing Fort Ticonderoga, his advance stalled amid supply problems, the defeat of a foraging detachment at Bennington, and growing American resistance under Horatio Gates. Surrounded at Saratoga, he surrendered some 6,000 men on 17 October 1777 — the turning point of the war.

Later life

Paroled home, Burgoyne defended his conduct in print and Parliament, briefly served as commander-in-chief in Ireland, and devoted his later years to the theatre. He died in London in 1792.
