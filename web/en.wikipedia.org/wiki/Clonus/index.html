Clonus

Clonus is a series of rhythmic, involuntary muscular contractions and relaxations, typically elicited by suddenly stretching a muscle and maintaining the stretch. It is a sign of upper motor neuron dysfunction and is most commonly tested at the ankle, where rapid dorsiflexion of the foot produces repetitive plantar-flexion beats.

Mechanism

Clonus arises when descending inhibitory control over the spinal stretch reflex is lost. Stretch of the muscle spindle triggers a reflex contraction; the contraction stretches the antagonist, and the loop oscillates, usually at 5–8 Hz. A few beats of unsustained clonus can occur in anxious or healthy individuals, but sustained clonus — commonly defined as more than three beats, and especially if asymmetric — is pathological.

Causes

Clonus accompanies conditions that damage the corticospinal tracts, including stroke, multiple sclerosis, spinal cord injury, and cerebral palsy. It may also appear in serotonin syndrome, severe hepatic encephalopathy, and eclampsia, where generalised hyperexcitability is present. In serotonin syndrome, spontaneous and inducible clonus — especially ocular clonus — are key diagnostic features in the Hunter criteria.

Related signs

Clonus is assessed alongside hyperreflexia, spasticity, and the Babinski sign as part of the upper motor neuron syndrome.
