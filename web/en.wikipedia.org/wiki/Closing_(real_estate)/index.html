Closing (real estate)

Closing (also known as completion or settlement) is the final step in executing a real estate transaction in the United States and several other jurisdictions. It is the process in which the title to the property passes from the seller to the buyer, the purchase price is paid and disbursed, and the transaction documents are signed and recorded. The closing date is generally set in the purchase agreement, typically 30 to 60 days after the offer is accepted, subject to the satisfaction of contingencies such as financing, inspection, and title search.

Process

Before closing, the buyer usually performs a final walk-through to confirm the property's condition, and a title search or title insurance commitment establishes that the seller can convey marketable title. At the closing meeting — conducted by an escrow agent, attorney, or title company depending on the state — the parties sign the deed, the mortgage or deed of trust and promissory note (if the purchase is financed), and disclosure forms. Since 2015, borrowers in the United States receive a Closing Disclosure under the TILA-RESPA Integrated Disclosure rule, itemising loan terms and costs at least three business days before closing.

Closing costs

Closing costs are the fees and expenses beyond the purchase price, typically amounting to 2–5% of the price. They may include loan origination and appraisal fees, title search and title insurance premiums, escrow fees, attorney fees, recording charges, transfer taxes, and prepaid items such as property taxes and homeowner's insurance. Which party pays each cost is a matter of contract and local custom. After funds are disbursed and the deed is recorded with the county, the buyer receives the keys and the transaction is complete.
