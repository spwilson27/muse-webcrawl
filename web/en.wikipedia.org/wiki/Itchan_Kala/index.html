Itchan Kala

The walled inner city of Khiva, with the Kalta Minor minaret.

Itchan Kala (Uzbek: Ichan qalʼa) is the walled inner town of the oasis city of Khiva in Uzbekistan. It was the first site in Uzbekistan to be inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List, in 1990, recognised as an outstanding and well-preserved example of the Muslim architecture of Central Asia.

Description

The rectangular fortress covers about 26 hectares and is enclosed by mud-brick walls up to 10 metres high, pierced by four gates oriented to the cardinal directions. Within are some 50 historic monuments and hundreds of traditional houses. Landmarks include the Djuma Mosque, with its forest of carved wooden columns; the Tash Hauli palace of the khans; the mausoleum of Pahlavan Mahmud, the city's patron saint; and the Islam Hoja minaret, the tallest in Uzbekistan. The unfinished Kalta Minor minaret, clad in turquoise tiles, was intended to be far taller still.

History

Khiva was a major staging post on the Silk Road and the capital of the Khanate of Khiva. Most of Itchan Kala's surviving buildings date from the 18th and 19th centuries, when the khanate flourished on trade — including a notorious slave market — before becoming a Russian protectorate in 1873. The inner city has been preserved as an open-air museum, though residents still live within its walls.
