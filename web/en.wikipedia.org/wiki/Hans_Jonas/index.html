Hans Jonas
Born: 10 May 1903, Mönchengladbach, Germany
Died: 5 February 1993, New Rochelle, New York
Known for: The Imperative of Responsibility, studies of Gnosticism
Teachers: Martin Heidegger, Rudolf Bultmann

Hans Jonas (10 May 1903 – 5 February 1993) was a German-born American Jewish philosopher. A student of Martin Heidegger, he is best known for his work on Gnosticism and for The Imperative of Responsibility (1979), a foundational text of environmental ethics.

Life

Jonas studied philosophy and theology at Freiburg, Berlin and Marburg, earning his doctorate under Heidegger and Bultmann. He emigrated after the Nazi rise to power, served in the British Army's Jewish Brigade, and later taught at the New School for Social Research in New York.

Philosophy

In The Phenomenon of Life (1966) Jonas developed a philosophical biology. His ethics of responsibility argued that modern technology creates obligations toward future generations and the biosphere, reformulating Kant's imperative: act so that the effects of your action are compatible with the permanence of genuine human life.
