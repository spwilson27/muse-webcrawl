Operation Pegasus (1968)

Date: 1–14 April 1968

Location: Quảng Trị Province, South Vietnam

Part of: Vietnam War

Belligerents: United States, South Vietnam vs. North Vietnam

Result: Siege of Khe Sanh lifted; Route 9 reopened

Operation Pegasus was a military operation carried out by US and South Vietnamese forces from 1 to 14 April 1968 during the Vietnam War. Its purpose was to relieve the siege of the US Marine Corps combat base at Khe Sanh, which had been under attack and encircled by the People's Army of Vietnam (PAVN) since January, in what became one of the most publicized battles of the war.

Background

The siege of Khe Sanh, beginning on 21 January 1968 during the Tet Offensive, drew intense attention in the United States, with President Lyndon Johnson fearing a repetition of the French defeat at Dien Bien Phu. The base was supplied by air while Operation Niagara delivered massive air support. When the siege appeared to wane in March, MACV commander General William Westmoreland ordered a ground relief operation by the 1st Cavalry Division (Airmobile), under Major General John Tolson.

The operation

Beginning on 1 April, some 20,000 troops advanced westward along Route 9 from Ca Lu toward Khe Sanh, using heliborne assaults to seize dominating terrain along the road while engineers repaired bridges and rebuilt the route. PAVN resistance was comparatively light; the first relief column reached the Khe Sanh perimeter on 6 April, and Route 9 was declared open on 14 April when the 1st Cavalry linked up with the Marines and Operation Pegasus was terminated, succeeded by Operation Scotland II in the Khe Sanh area.

Aftermath

The relief of Khe Sanh was announced as a success, but within weeks the United States decided to abandon the base: in June–July 1968 the combat base was evacuated and demolished under Operation Charlie, as American strategy shifted away from holding the position. The episode remains debated among historians, both for the significance of the battle itself — including whether the siege was a genuine effort to take the base or a diversion for Tet — and for the decision to abandon Khe Sanh so soon after its relief.
