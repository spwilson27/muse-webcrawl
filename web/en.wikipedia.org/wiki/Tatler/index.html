Tatler

Tatler, the British magazine of fashion, society and the upper classes

Tatler is a British magazine published by Condé Nast Publications focusing on fashion, lifestyle, high society and politics among the British upper classes. Founded in 1909 in its modern form, it takes its name from the original Tatler, a literary and society periodical founded by Richard Steele in 1709. It is known for its coverage of the aristocracy, debutantes, social events such as Royal Ascot, and features such as the "Little Black Book" of eligible singles.

History

The original Tatler was launched by Richard Steele on 12 April 1709 and ran until 1711, presenting essays on manners, literature and society from the persona of "Isaac Bickerstaff". The name was revived several times over the following two centuries. The modern magazine dates from 3 July 1909, when it was launched (drawing on the earlier Sphere stable of illustrated papers) as an illustrated journal of society and the drama. It survived mergers and format changes through the 20th century, was acquired into the Condé Nast group, and repositioned as a glossy society magazine.

Content and editions

Tatler's content mixes fashion photography, society reporting, travel, food and satirical commentary on upper-class life, alongside long-running features on schools, weddings and social seasons. Its social reporting is exemplified by the Tatler "Little Black Book", an annual list of Britain's most eligible men and women, and by diaries covering parties and charity events.

International editions, most prominently Tatler Asia (formerly Hong Kong Tatler and sister titles in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, Taiwan, mainland China and the Philippines), publish similar society and luxury content for Asian markets. The British edition is edited in London and maintains a substantial digital and social-media presence.

Editors

Notable editors have included Tina Brown, who revitalised the magazine in the late 1970s and early 1980s before moving to Vanity Fair, and, in more recent decades, Geordie Greig, Kate Reardon, Catherine Ostler and Richard Dennen.
