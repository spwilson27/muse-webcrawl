Maranhão is a state in Brazil's Northeast Region, bordered by Piauí, Tocantins, and Pará, with a long Atlantic coastline. Its capital is São Luís, founded by the French in 1612 and famous for its Portuguese colonial tile-fronted centre, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Geography

The state's landscapes range from Amazonian forest in the west to Cerrado in the south and the extraordinary Lençóis Maranhenses National Park on the coast, where rain-filled lagoons form among vast white sand dunes. The Alcântara Launch Center, Brazil's spaceport, sits on the coast opposite São Luís, near the equator.

Culture

Maranhão is the heartland of the June festival tradition of Bumba Meu Boi, recognised by UNESCO as Intangible Cultural Heritage, and of reggae culture in São Luís, sometimes called the "Brazilian Jamaica". The state has historically had some of Brazil's lowest income and literacy indicators, alongside rich Afro-Brazilian traditions such as Tambor de Crioula.

Economy

The economy includes the Itaqui port and alumina/aluminium complex near São Luís, iron ore shipped from the Carajás railway terminus, agriculture (soy advancing in the south), and tourism to Lençóis and the historic capital.
