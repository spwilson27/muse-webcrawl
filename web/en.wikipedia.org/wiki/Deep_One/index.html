Deep One

The Deep Ones are fictional creatures in the Cthulhu Mythos of H. P. Lovecraft and derivative works. They are an ancient, ocean-dwelling race of amphibious fish-frog humanoids, introduced in Lovecraft's novella The Shadow over Innsmouth (written 1931, published 1936). The Deep Ones are immortal, worship the Great Old Ones Cthulhu and the sea deities Father Dagon and Mother Hydra, and interbreed with humans to produce hybrid offspring.

Description

Lovecraft describes the Deep Ones as having greyish-green, glistening bodies with white bellies, scaly skin, finned backs, webbed clawed hands, and bulging, unblinking eyes on fish-like heads. They move with a shambling, half-hopping gait on land but are graceful swimmers. They inhabit undersea cities such as Y'ha-nthlei, beneath the waters off Innsmouth, Massachusetts, near the reef known as Devil's Reef.

Role in the Mythos

In The Shadow over Innsmouth, Captain Obed Marsh brought the Deep Ones' cult to Innsmouth from the South Seas, founding the Esoteric Order of Dagon. In exchange for gold and abundant fish, the townspeople made pacts with the creatures and intermarried with them. Hybrid children appear human at first but gradually acquire the "Innsmouth look" — narrow heads, bulging eyes, and shrinking ears — until they transform fully and take to the sea. The narrator famously discovers his own hybrid ancestry at the story's end. Deep Ones have since appeared throughout Mythos fiction, games such as Call of Cthulhu, and popular culture as archetypal fish-people.
