The Spain national football team (Spanish: Selección Española de Fútbol) represents Spain in men's international football and is governed by the Royal Spanish Football Federation. Nicknamed La Roja, Spain is one of the most successful national teams in history, having won the FIFA World Cup in 2010 and a record four UEFA European Championships (1964, 2008, 2012, 2024), plus the UEFA Nations League in 2023.

History

Spain played its first match at the 1920 Antwerp Olympics, winning silver. For decades the team underachieved despite a strong domestic league, winning Euro 1964 on home soil but reaching only one World Cup semi-final before 2010. Under Luis Aragonés and then Vicente del Bosque, a golden generation built around Xavi, Andrés Iniesta, Iker Casillas and Sergio Ramos perfected the possession-based tiki-taka style, winning Euro 2008, the 2010 World Cup in South Africa (beating the Netherlands 1–0 in the final, Iniesta scoring in extra time) and Euro 2012 — an unprecedented three consecutive major titles.

Recent years

After a period of transition and early World Cup exits in 2014, 2018 and 2022, a young side coached by Luis de la Fuente won the 2023 Nations League and Euro 2024 in Germany, defeating England 2–1 in the final with breakout stars Lamine Yamal and Nico Williams, restoring Spain to the top of European football.

Style and identity

Spain traditionally wear red shirts and dark blue shorts. Their tiki-taka philosophy — short passing, positional rotation and dominance of possession — influenced club and national football worldwide.
