Something Else by the Kinks

Something Else by the Kinks, often referred to simply as Something Else, is the fifth studio album by the English rock band the Kinks, released on 15 September 1967 by Pye Records. It marked a shift toward a gentler, more reflective sound, rich in harpsichord, piano, and organ played in part by Ray Davies's wife Rasa and session musician Nicky Hopkins, and it introduced the songwriting of guitarist Dave Davies, whose "Death of a Clown" was a hit single. The album contains "Waterloo Sunset", one of Ray Davies's most celebrated songs.

Background and music

The album was Ray Davies's first as the group's effective producer, with Shel Talmy still nominally involved. Its mood is autumnal and nostalgic, centred on English life — afternoon tea, cricket, suburban routines ("Afternoon Tea", "End of the Season", "Situation Vacant"), and gently satirical character sketches. Three songs were written by Dave Davies, including "Death of a Clown" (released under his own name and a UK No. 3 single) and "Love Me Till the Sun Shines". The closing track, "Waterloo Sunset", reached No. 2 in the UK and is routinely ranked among the greatest songs of the era. "David Watts" later became better known through the Jam's 1978 cover.

Reception

On release the album sold poorly in Britain (reaching only No. 35) and in the United States, where the Kinks were still barred from touring. Retrospectively it is regarded as a classic: Rolling Stone placed it at No. 289 on its 2003 list of the 500 greatest albums of all time (and in later revisions of the list), and critics praise its cohesion, melodic craft, and quintessentially English sensibility as the beginning of the band's acclaimed late-1960s run that continued with The Kinks Are the Village Green Preservation Society (1968).
