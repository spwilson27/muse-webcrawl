Soraya Tarzi (24 November 1899 – 20 April 1968) was the queen consort of Afghanistan in the 1920s as the wife of King Amanullah Khan. Together with her husband she pursued an ambitious programme of modernisation and women's emancipation that made her one of the most visible and controversial public figures of her era in the Muslim world.

Early life

Soraya was born in Damascus, where her father, the Afghan intellectual and diplomat Mahmud Tarzi, lived in exile. Tarzi was a leading reformist thinker who exposed his children to Western education and ideas. In 1913 Soraya married the Afghan prince Amanullah, who became emir in 1919 and king in 1926.

Queen of Afghanistan

Unlike earlier Afghan queens, Soraya appeared in public alongside her husband, addressed audiences, and took an active role in reform. She founded the first girls' school in Afghanistan (Masturat School, 1921), encouraged women to abandon purdah and the veil, and in 1928 publicly removed her veil during an official ceremony — an act applauded by reformers and denounced by conservatives. She also supported sending Afghan girls to study in Turkey and promoted women's health and education.

Exile

The pace of Amanullah's reforms provoked conservative and tribal backlash, and in 1929 the royal couple were forced into exile. They settled in Rome, where Soraya lived for the remainder of her life. She died there in 1968; her remains were later returned to Afghanistan and buried in Jalalabad beside her husband.

Legacy

Soraya Tarzi is remembered as a pioneering advocate of women's rights in Afghanistan, and Afghan girls' schools and institutions have been named in her honour.
