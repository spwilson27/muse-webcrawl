Varietal

A varietal wine is a wine made primarily from a single named grape variety, and which typically displays the name of that variety on the wine label. Examples include Cabernet Sauvignon, Chardonnay, Pinot noir, Riesling, and Merlot wines.

The practice of naming wines by grape variety became especially prominent in the New World, in contrast to Old World wines traditionally named after their region of origin, such as Bordeaux or Chianti. Legal requirements vary by country: in the United States a varietal wine must generally contain at least 75% of the named grape, while the European Union generally requires 85%.

Labelling

Varietal labelling helps consumers identify the style of wine they are buying. Some varieties, such as Pinot noir and Riesling, are strongly associated with the term. The word "varietal" is also used more broadly for other agricultural products, such as coffee and chocolate, made from a single variety.
