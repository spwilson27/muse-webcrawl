Crawl space

A crawl space (or crawlspace) is a shallow, unfinished space beneath the first floor of a building, typically one to three feet (0.3 to 0.9 m) high, large enough only to crawl through — hence the name. It provides access to plumbing, wiring, ductwork and other building systems for inspection and repair, while raising the structure above the ground.

Purpose and construction

Houses with crawl spaces sit on a foundation of perimeter walls or piers rather than a full basement or a concrete slab. The arrangement is cheaper than a basement, keeps the living floor off damp ground, and can help protect against termites and, in some regions, minor flooding. Crawl spaces are common in the southern and coastal United States and in other areas with high water tables or expansive soils where basements are impractical.

Ventilation and moisture

Traditionally, crawl spaces were vented to the outdoors through openings in the foundation walls, on the theory that airflow would dry out moisture. More recent building science often favours sealed ("conditioned" or "encapsulated") crawl spaces: vents are closed, a heavy vapour barrier covers the soil, walls are insulated, and the space is kept dry with conditioned air or a dehumidifier. Unvented designs aim to prevent humid outdoor air from condensing on cool surfaces, which can cause mould, wood rot and pest problems. Building codes commonly require a minimum access opening and clearance beneath joists and girders.
