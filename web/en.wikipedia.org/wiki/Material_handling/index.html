Material handling

Material handling involves the movement, protection, storage and control of materials and products throughout manufacturing, warehousing, distribution, consumption and disposal. The process incorporates a range of manual, semi-automated and automated equipment and systems that support logistics and make the supply chain work.

Overview

Material handling is a core concern of industrial engineering and warehouse design. Well-designed handling systems reduce labour costs, decrease product damage, improve throughput, and enhance worker safety. A common industry maxim holds that material handling adds cost but no value to a product, so engineers aim to minimise handling wherever possible.

Types of equipment

Handling equipment is usually grouped into four categories. Transport equipment moves materials between locations and includes conveyor belts, forklifts, cranes, hoists, industrial trucks and automated guided vehicles. Positioning equipment, such as lift tables and manipulators, handles material at a single location, typically to feed a machine or workstation. Unit load formation equipment, including pallets, bins, crates and stretch wrap, restrains materials so they can be moved as a single unit. Storage equipment, such as racks, shelving and mezzanines, holds materials over a period of time.

Unit loads

A unit load is a collection of items arranged so that the mass can be handled as a single entity. The pallet is the most common unit load platform, standardised in sizes such as the 48-by-40-inch GMA pallet used in North America and the EUR pallet in Europe. Unit loads reduce the number of handling operations and protect goods in transit.

Automation

Modern distribution centres increasingly use automated storage and retrieval systems (AS/RS), robotic picking arms, conveyor sortation systems and warehouse management software to coordinate the flow of goods with minimal manual intervention.
