Schott AG is a German multinational glass company specialising in the manufacture of special-purpose glass and glass-ceramics. Founded in Jena in 1884 by glass chemist Otto Schott, the company is headquartered in Mainz and is wholly owned by the Carl Zeiss Foundation. Schott produces components and materials for the pharmaceutical, electronics, optics, automotive, aviation and home appliance industries.

History

Otto Schott, working with Ernst Abbe and Carl Zeiss in Jena, systematically developed new glass types with defined optical and thermal properties, founding the Glastechnisches Laboratorium Schott & Genossen in 1884. His borosilicate glass, marketed from 1893, resisted heat and chemical attack and became the basis of laboratory glassware (later the Duran brand) and heat-resistant household glass. After the Second World War and the division of Germany, the company was re-established in the West, with headquarters moved to Mainz in 1952, while the Jena works became part of East Germany's VEB Jenaer Glaswerk; the two were reunited after German reunification in 1991.

Products

Well-known Schott products include Zerodur, a glass-ceramic with near-zero thermal expansion used for telescope mirror substrates — including segments for extremely large telescopes — and laser gyroscopes; Ceran glass-ceramic cooktop panels; Borofloat and Duran borosilicate glass; FIOLAX glass tubing and syringes, vials and cartridges for pharmaceutical packaging; and optical glasses for lenses and photonics.

Organisation

Schott operates production sites and sales offices in more than 30 countries. As a foundation company, its sole shareholder is the Carl Zeiss Foundation, which also owns the optics company Carl Zeiss AG; dividends support the foundation's promotion of science.
