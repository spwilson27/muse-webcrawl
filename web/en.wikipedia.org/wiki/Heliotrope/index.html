Heliotrope

Heliotrope may refer to several related things. Most commonly it denotes plants of the genus Heliotropium in the borage family Boraginaceae, named from the Greek for "sun-turning", after the old belief that their flowers turn toward the sun. The common garden heliotrope, Heliotropium arborescens, is a tender perennial grown for its clusters of purple or white flowers with a sweet, cherry-pie scent.

Other uses

The name is also given to bloodstone, a dark green chalcedony flecked with red spots, historically called heliotrope; to a surveying instrument invented in the early 19th century that uses a mirror to reflect sunlight over long distances, used by Carl Friedrich Gauss in geodetic surveys; and to a shade of purple between magenta and violet.

Cultivation

Garden heliotropes prefer full sun and well-drained soil, are grown as annuals in cold climates, and contain alkaloids toxic to livestock if eaten in quantity.
