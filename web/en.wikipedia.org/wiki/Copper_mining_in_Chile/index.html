Copper mining in Chile

Copper mining in Chile is a mainstay of the Chilean economy. Chile is the world's largest producer of copper, accounting for roughly a quarter to a third of global mine output. The industry is concentrated in the arid north of the country, in the Atacama Desert regions of Antofagasta, Atacama, and Coquimbo, where large porphyry copper deposits occur along the Andes.

History

Copper was mined in the region in pre-Columbian times and on a growing scale under Spanish rule. Large-scale industrial mining began in the early 20th century with American companies — Kennecott at El Teniente and Anaconda at Chuquicamata. In 1971, under President Salvador Allende, Chile nationalized the large copper mines; the state company Codelco was formally created in 1976 and remains the world's largest copper producer.

Major operations

Escondida, operated by BHP, is the world's largest copper mine. Other major producers include Collahuasi, Anglo American Sur, and Antofagasta Minerals. El Teniente in central Chile is the world's largest underground copper mine.

Economic and environmental issues

Copper revenues fund a significant share of the national budget, and Chile operates sovereign wealth stabilization funds fed by copper income. Challenges include declining ore grades, water scarcity in the Atacama, energy costs, and labour disputes.
