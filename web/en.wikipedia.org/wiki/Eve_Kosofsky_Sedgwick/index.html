Eve Kosofsky Sedgwick (1950–2009) was an American academic scholar in the fields of gender studies, queer theory and critical theory. She is regarded as one of the founders of queer studies, and her work influenced literary criticism, performance studies and affect theory.

Major works

Her book Between Men: English Literature and Male Homosocial Desire (1985) introduced the concept of male homosocial desire, and Epistemology of the Closet (1990) analysed the homo/heterosexual definition as a structuring tension of modern Western culture. Later works, including Tendencies (1993) and Touching Feeling (2003), explored affect, shame and reparative reading.

Career and later life

Sedgwick taught at several universities, most prominently Duke University and the Graduate Center of the City University of New York. Diagnosed with breast cancer in 1991, she also wrote memoir and poetry, including A Dialogue on Love (1999), and studied Buddhism and textile arts in her later years.
