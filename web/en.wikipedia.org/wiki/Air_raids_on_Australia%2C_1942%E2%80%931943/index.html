Air raids on Australia, 1942–1943

Between February 1942 and November 1943, the Imperial Japanese Navy Air Service and Imperial Japanese Army Air Service carried out at least 97 air raids on Australia and its territories during the Pacific War. The attacks targeted ports, airfields, and towns across northern Australia — from Exmouth Gulf in Western Australia to Horn Island in Queensland — as well as installations in Papua.

The Darwin raids

The largest and deadliest attacks were the Bombing of Darwin on 19 February 1942, when 242 Japanese aircraft — launched from four aircraft carriers and land bases — attacked in two waves, killing at least 235 people, sinking eight ships in the harbour, and destroying numerous aircraft and buildings. It was the largest single attack ever mounted against Australia. Darwin was bombed 63 more times, and other towns attacked included Broome (where a raid on 3 March 1942 killed at least 88 people, many of them Dutch refugees in flying boats), Townsville, Katherine, Wyndham, Port Hedland, and Exmouth Gulf.

Course and effects

The raids aimed to neutralize northern bases, support Japanese operations in the Netherlands East Indies and New Guinea, and tie down Allied forces. Australian and American fighters, including Spitfires of No. 1 Wing RAAF from 1943, and anti-aircraft defences contested later raids with increasing success. The last raid struck Darwin on 12 November 1943. The attacks prompted major defensive preparations and evacuation of civilians from northern towns but are generally judged to have had limited strategic effect.
