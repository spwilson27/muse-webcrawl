Janiculum

The Janiculum (Italian: Gianicolo) is a hill in western Rome, Italy. Lying across the Tiber from the historic centre, it is not counted among the traditional Seven Hills of Rome, but it was enclosed within the city's Aurelian Walls and is famous for its panoramic views.

History

The hill takes its name from the god Janus, who was said to have founded a city there. In ancient Rome it was a strategic point guarding the crossing of the Tiber, and was fortified early. In 1849 the Janiculum was the scene of fierce fighting when Giuseppe Garibaldi's forces defended the Roman Republic against French troops; monuments to Garibaldi and to his wife Anita stand on the hill.

Sights

Landmarks include the church of San Pietro in Montorio, with Bramante's Tempietto (1502), the Fontana dell'Acqua Paola (1612), the American Academy in Rome and the Villa Lante. A cannon on the hill is still fired at noon each day, a tradition dating to 1847.
