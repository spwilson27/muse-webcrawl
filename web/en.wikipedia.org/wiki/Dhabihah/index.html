Dhabihah

Dhabihah-slaughtered meat is labelled halal

Dhabihah (Arabic: ذبيحة, romanised dhabīḥah, "slaughtered animal") is the term for the Islamic method of slaughtering animals for food, and by extension for meat produced by that method. The word is a variant transliteration of the same Arabic term also spelled dhabiha.

Method

Under dhabihah, a permitted animal is slaughtered by a single swift cut across the throat with a sharp blade, severing the windpipe, oesophagus and major blood vessels, after the invocation of God's name, and the blood is allowed to drain fully. Animals that are dead before slaughter, improperly bled, or slaughtered in another's name are not halal. Whether pre-slaughter stunning is permissible is debated among jurists and certification authorities; labels such as "hand slaughtered" and "zabiha halal" are used in some markets to distinguish stricter standards.
