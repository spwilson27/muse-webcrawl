Arthur Percival

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Lieutenant-General Arthur Percival

Lieutenant-General Arthur Ernest Percival, CB, DSO & Bar, OBE, MC, DL (26 December 1887 – 31 January 1966) was a British Army officer. He saw service in the First World War and built a successful military career during the interwar period but is most noted for his defeat in the Second World War, when he commanded British Commonwealth forces during the Battle of Singapore.

Fall of Singapore

As General Officer Commanding Malaya Command from April 1941, Percival was in an unenviable position. He lacked sufficient striking forces: the British were prioritizing other fronts, leaving his command with inadequate air support and no tanks. Japanese forces under General Tomoyuki Yamashita invaded the Malay Peninsula in December 1941 and advanced rapidly down it.

On 15 February 1942, Percival surrendered the island fortress of Singapore along with some 80,000 troops — the largest capitulation in British military history. He spent the rest of the war as a prisoner of the Japanese, in Singapore and later in Manchuria, and was present on the USS Missouri for the Japanese surrender ceremony in Tokyo Bay in September 1945.

Later life

After the war Percival retired from the Army. His memoir, The War in Malaya, was published in 1949. He died in 1966. Historians continue to debate the extent to which the defeat was his own failing versus the result of systemic under-preparation and misallocation of resources.
