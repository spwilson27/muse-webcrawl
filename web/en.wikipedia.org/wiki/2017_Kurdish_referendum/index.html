The 2017 Kurdish referendum was an independence referendum held on 25 September 2017 in the Kurdistan Region of Iraq and in Kurdish-held disputed territories, including Kirkuk. Organised by the Kurdistan Regional Government (KRG) under President Masoud Barzani, the vote asked whether the region should become an independent state. Despite warnings from Baghdad, Turkey, Iran and the United States, the referendum proceeded; official results showed 92.7 per cent voting in favour on a turnout of about 72 per cent. Iraq's federal government rejected the vote as unconstitutional, and its military response in October 2017 reversed Kurdish control of Kirkuk and most disputed areas.

Background

The Kurdistan Region had enjoyed autonomy within federal Iraq since 2005. Relations with Baghdad deteriorated over oil exports, budget shares and the status of disputed territories, which Kurdish Peshmerga forces had largely taken over in 2014 as the Iraqi army collapsed before the Islamic State offensive. Barzani argued that the partnership with Baghdad had failed and announced the referendum in June 2017.

Vote and reactions

Voting took place across the three KRG governorates and disputed districts. Celebrations followed the announcement of the overwhelming "Yes" result. Iraq's parliament demanded the deployment of troops to disputed areas and the takeover of border crossings and oilfields; Turkey and Iran, fearing encouragement to their own Kurdish minorities, threatened sanctions and closed airspace to KRG airports, and international flights to Erbil and Sulaymaniyah were suspended.

Aftermath

On 16 October 2017, Iraqi federal forces and allied paramilitaries retook Kirkuk and its oilfields almost without resistance, and within days the KRG lost nearly all disputed territory gained since 2014. The KRG offered to "freeze" the referendum's results, which Baghdad rejected; in November Iraq's Federal Supreme Court ruled the vote unconstitutional. Barzani stepped down as president on 1 November 2017. The episode is generally regarded as a strategic setback that set back the Kurdish independence project by years.
