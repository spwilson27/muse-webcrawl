Hermann of Reichenau

Hermann of Reichenau (18 July 1013 – 24 September 1054), also called Hermannus Contractus ("Hermann the Lame"), was an 11th-century Benedictine monk and scholar of Reichenau Abbey. Despite a severe physical disability that left him barely able to move or speak, he became one of the most learned men of his age, writing on history, mathematics, astronomy, and music theory.

Works

His Chronicon, a world chronicle from the birth of Christ to 1054, is an important source for 11th-century history and was continued after his death by his pupil Berthold of Reichenau. Hermann wrote treatises on the astrolabe and the quadrant, on computus (calendar reckoning), and on music, including a system of letter notation. Tradition attributes to him the beloved Marian antiphons Salve Regina and Alma Redemptoris Mater, though the attribution is not certain.

Veneration

Hermann was beatified by the Catholic Church in 1863; his feast day is 25 September.
