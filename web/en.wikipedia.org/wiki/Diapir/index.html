Diapir

Diapir
Field: Geology
Materials: Salt, shale, magma
Related: Salt dome, Anticline

A diapir is a type of geological intrusion in which a more mobile, ductile and deformable material is forced into brittle overlying rocks. Because the intruding material is less dense, buoyancy drives it upward.

Salt diapirs

The most common are salt diapirs, forming salt domes in sedimentary basins such as the Gulf of Mexico and the North Sea. Rising salt traps oil and gas in surrounding strata, making diapirs economically important exploration targets, and mined salt caverns are used for storage.

Other types

Shale and mud diapirs can produce mud volcanoes. Magmatic diapirs were once thought to be a main mechanism for granite pluton ascent, though dike transport is now favored for much magma movement.

Formation

Differential loading — uneven sediment deposition over a low-density layer — triggers instability, and the layer rises in bulbous, mushroom-shaped bodies over geological time.
