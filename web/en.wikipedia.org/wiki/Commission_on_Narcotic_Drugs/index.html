The Commission on Narcotic Drugs (CND) is one of the functional commissions of the United Nations Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC) and is the central drug policy-making body of the United Nations system. Established by ECOSOC resolution 9(I) in 1946 — taking over functions of the League of Nations' advisory committee on opium — it meets annually in Vienna and is serviced by the United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime.

Functions

Under the three international drug control treaties, the CND decides — on the recommendation of the World Health Organization for narcotic drugs and psychotropic substances, and of the International Narcotics Control Board for precursor chemicals — whether to add, transfer or delete substances from the treaty schedules. It also reviews global drug trends, adopts resolutions on drug policy, and serves as the governing body of the UN drug control programme, approving the budget of the UNODC's drug programme fund.

Notable decisions

In December 2020 the CND voted, by 27 to 25 with one abstention, to remove cannabis and cannabis resin from Schedule IV of the 1961 Single Convention — the category reserved for the most dangerous substances — following WHO recommendations, while keeping cannabis in Schedule I. The vote was widely reported as formal UN recognition of cannabis's medical utility, even though it remained prohibited for non-medical use. The Commission also organises ministerial segments, including the 2019 Ministerial Declaration and preparations toward the 2029 review of global drug policy commitments.
