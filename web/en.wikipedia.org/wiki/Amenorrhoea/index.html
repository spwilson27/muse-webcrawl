Amenorrhoea

Field: Gynaecology, endocrinology
Types: Primary, secondary
Causes: Pregnancy, hormonal disorders, stress, weight change, anatomical abnormalities

Amenorrhoea is the absence of menstrual periods in a woman of reproductive age. Primary amenorrhoea is the failure of menstruation to begin by about age 15–16 in a girl with otherwise normal development, while secondary amenorrhoea is the cessation of periods for three months or more in a woman who previously menstruated regularly. Amenorrhoea is a symptom rather than a disease, and it is normal during pregnancy, breastfeeding and after the menopause.

Causes

Causes are commonly grouped by the level of the reproductive axis affected. Hypothalamic causes include functional hypothalamic amenorrhoea from stress, weight loss, excessive exercise or eating disorders. Pituitary causes include prolactinoma and other tumours. Ovarian causes include polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), the commonest cause of secondary amenorrhoea, and primary ovarian insufficiency. Uterine causes include Asherman's syndrome (intrauterine scarring) and, in primary amenorrhoea, congenital absence of the uterus or an imperforate hymen; chromosomal conditions such as Turner syndrome and androgen insensitivity also present with primary amenorrhoea.

Diagnosis and treatment

Evaluation begins by excluding pregnancy, followed by history, examination and hormone tests — typically thyroid function, prolactin, androgens and gonadotropins — with imaging as indicated. Treatment targets the underlying cause: restoring energy balance in hypothalamic amenorrhoea, medication or surgery for prolactinoma, hormonal management for PCOS, and oestrogen replacement where ovarian failure or prolonged hypo-oestrogenism threatens bone health. Persistent amenorrhoea warrants medical assessment because of implications for fertility and bone density.
