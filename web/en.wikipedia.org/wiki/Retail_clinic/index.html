A clinic inside a retail store

A retail clinic is a category of walk-in health clinic located inside retail stores such as pharmacies, supermarkets, and department stores. They treat minor illnesses and provide preventive services, usually staffed by nurse practitioners or physician assistants.

Services

Retail clinics typically treat conditions such as colds, flu, ear infections, minor sprains, and skin conditions, and offer vaccinations, physical examinations, and screening tests. Prices are usually posted in advance, visits are short, and no appointment is needed.

Development

The model began in the United States around 2000, with chains such as MinuteClinic (later acquired by CVS) expanding rapidly. Supporters cite convenience, transparent pricing, and lower cost than emergency departments; critics raise concerns about fragmentation of care and conflicts of interest where the clinic's owner also sells prescriptions.
