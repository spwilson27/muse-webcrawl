The Anglo-Russian Convention of 1907, also known as the Convention between the United Kingdom and Russia relating to Persia, Afghanistan, and Tibet, was signed on 31 August 1907 in Saint Petersburg. It ended the long-standing rivalry between the two empires in Central Asia — the so-called Great Game — and delimited their respective spheres of influence in Persia, Afghanistan, and Tibet.

Terms

Persia was divided into a Russian sphere of influence in the north, a British sphere in the southeast, and a neutral zone between them. Russia recognised Afghanistan as lying within the British sphere of influence, while both powers agreed to respect the territorial integrity of Tibet and to abstain from interference in its internal affairs, recognising Chinese suzerainty there.

Significance

The Convention was not a formal alliance, but together with the Franco-Russian Alliance and the Entente Cordiale between Britain and France, it completed the diplomatic alignment known as the Triple Entente, which faced the Triple Alliance of Germany, Austria-Hungary, and Italy in the First World War. In Persia, the agreement was received with anger by nationalists and the newly established parliament, since Persia itself had not been consulted.
