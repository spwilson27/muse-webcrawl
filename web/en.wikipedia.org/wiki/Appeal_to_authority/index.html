Appeal to authority

An appeal to authority (Latin: argumentum ad verecundiam) is an argument in which a claim is presented as true because an authority or expert says it is true. Appeals to genuine, relevant expertise can be a reasonable form of inductive support, but the form becomes fallacious when the authority is irrelevant, unqualified, anonymous, biased, or when expert consensus is misrepresented.

Forms

Common fallacious variants include citing a celebrity on a scientific topic, invoking an unnamed “experts say” source, treating an authority in one field as authoritative in another, and insisting a claim must be accepted merely because of a person's status. A related move is the appeal to false authority, where the cited figure lacks the claimed credentials.

Assessment

Evaluating an appeal to authority involves checking whether the source is a recognised expert in the relevant field, whether there is consensus among such experts, whether the source is impartial, and whether the claim can be independently verified. Scientific practice treats expert opinion as provisional evidence, not proof.
