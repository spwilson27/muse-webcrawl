Yasaka Shrine

Yasaka Shrine (八坂神社)
Location: Gion, Higashiyama, Kyoto
Deity: Susanoo-no-Mikoto
Founded: traditionally 656 CE
Festival: Gion Matsuri (July)

Yasaka Shrine (八坂神社, Yasaka-jinja), once called Gion Shrine (祇園神社), is a Shinto shrine in the Gion district of Kyoto, Japan, at the eastern end of Shijō Avenue. It enshrines Susanoo-no-Mikoto together with his consort Kushinadahime and their children, the Yahashira-no-Mikogami. It is the head shrine of a network of roughly 2,300 Yasaka shrines across Japan.

History

Tradition dates the founding to 656 CE, when a monk from Goguryeo is said to have enshrined the deity Gozu Tennō, a figure later identified with Susanoo. In 869, during an epidemic in the capital, the shrine's portable shrines were paraded through the city in a rite — the Gion Goryō-e — that grew into the Gion Matsuri, today among Japan's most famous festivals, with its month of July observances and the great Yamaboko Junko processions of floats. During the Meiji period's separation of Shinto and Buddhism, the shrine was renamed from Gion-sha/Gion Tenjin to Yasaka Shrine (1871).

Grounds

The main hall, an Important Cultural Property, combines shrine hall and worship hall under one roof in the gion-zukuri style, covered by a cypress-bark roof. In front of it stands the dance stage (maidono), strung with hundreds of lanterns donated by Gion businesses that are lit at night — one of Kyoto's iconic views. The west gate (rōmon) faces Shijō Avenue; Maruyama Park adjoins the grounds to the east. The shrine is a popular site for hatsumōde, the first shrine visit of the New Year, and for the Ōmisoka bell-ringing season at year-end. Nearby stands the five-story pagoda of Hōkan-ji, known as Yasaka-no-tō, a landmark of the Higashiyama skyline.
