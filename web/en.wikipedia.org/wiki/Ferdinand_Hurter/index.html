Ferdinand Hurter

Ferdinand Hurter (15 March 1844 – 12 March 1898) was a Swiss industrial chemist who settled in England, where he became a leading figure in the alkali industry and, with Vero Charles Driffield, a pioneer of photographic sensitometry.

Career

Born in Schaffhausen, Switzerland, Hurter studied chemistry and worked in Zurich before moving to Widnes, England, in 1867 to work for the alkali manufacturers Gaskell, Deacon and Co. He became a prominent advocate of scientific methods in chemical manufacture and of technical education.

Hurter and Driffield

Beginning in the 1880s, Hurter collaborated with Vero Charles Driffield on the quantitative study of photographic emulsions. Their 1890 paper introduced the characteristic curve of photographic density versus exposure — the Hurter–Driffield (H&D) curve — and a system of speed numbers for plates, founding the science of sensitometry and the basis of modern film speed measurement.
