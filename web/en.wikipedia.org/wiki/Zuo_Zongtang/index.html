Zuo Zongtang

Zuo Zongtang (Chinese: 左宗棠; 10 November 1812 – 5 September 1885), courtesy name Jigao, was a Chinese statesman and military leader of the late Qing dynasty. One of the most important officials of the Tongzhi Restoration era, he is best known for suppressing rebellions in western China and for reconquering Xinjiang from the forces of Yakub Beg in the 1870s, which led to Xinjiang's establishment as a province in 1884. In the West he is also popularly, if spuriously, associated with the dish "General Tso's chicken".

Early life

Zuo was born in Wenjiachong, Xiangyin County, Hunan. Though he repeatedly failed the highest imperial examinations, he gained expertise in geography, agriculture, and military strategy, and worked as a tutor and local secretary. His talents came to the attention of senior officials, including Lin Zexu, who is said to have entrusted him with his maps and plans concerning the northwest frontier.

Military career

During the Taiping Rebellion, Zuo served on the staff of Hunan's governor and in 1860 raised his own force, the Chu Army, under the patronage of Zeng Guofan. He campaigned successfully in Jiangxi, Zhejiang, and Fujian against Taiping and Nian forces and became governor-general of Fujian and Zhejiang, founding the Fuzhou Arsenal and a naval school as part of the Self-Strengthening Movement. Transferred to Shaanxi and Gansu, he suppressed the Dungan Revolt (1862–1877) through a methodical strategy of securing logistics before advancing.

Reconquest of Xinjiang

Appointed imperial commissioner for Xinjiang in 1875, Zuo undertook a carefully prepared campaign against Yakub Beg, who had established a state in Kashgaria and received recognition from Britain and Russia. Zuo's forces retook Ürümqi in 1876 and Kashgar by the end of 1877, restoring Qing control. He also advocateded recovering the Ili valley from Russian occupation, achieved diplomatically by the Treaty of Saint Petersburg (1881). Xinjiang was made a province in 1884, with Zuo's recommendation.

Later life and legacy

Zuo subsequently served as governor-general in Nanjing and on the Grand Council, and was recalled to oversee coastal defence during the Sino-French War; he died in Fuzhou in 1885. He is remembered in China as a national hero for preserving territorial integrity. The American-Chinese dish "General Tso's chicken" was named in his honour in the 20th century, though he never knew it.
