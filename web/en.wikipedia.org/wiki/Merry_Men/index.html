Merry Men

The Merry Men, outlaws of Sherwood Forest in the Robin Hood ballads.

The Merry Men are the band of outlaws who follow Robin Hood in the English legends of Sherwood Forest. Living as fugitives from the Sheriff of Nottingham, they are traditionally depicted robbing the rich to give to the poor, feasting on the king's deer, and wearing Lincoln green. The phrase "merry men" originally meant simply a lord's retainers or companions, but through the ballads it became inseparably attached to Robin's company.

Principal members

Little John — Robin's huge, loyal second-in-command, recruited in the ballads after a quarterstaff fight on a bridge which Robin loses. Friar Tuck — the jovial, combative cleric, a later addition to the legend. Will Scarlet — a hot-tempered young swordsman, sometimes Robin's nephew. Much the Miller's Son — a yeoman outlawed for poaching. Alan-a-Dale — the wandering minstrel of the band, another later addition. Maid Marian, Robin's beloved, became associated with the outlaws through May Games and later retellings and is sometimes counted among the company.

In the ballads

The early ballads, such as A Gest of Robyn Hode (c. 1450), imagine a band of some 140 "wight yeomen", but the named characters accumulated gradually over centuries of ballad, play, novel and film. Each recruit typically joins after besting or being bested by Robin — in archery, wrestling, swordplay or quarterstaff — in a ritual of mutual respect. Walter Scott's Ivanhoe (1819) fixed much of the modern picture, and twentieth-century films, from Errol Flynn's The Adventures of Robin Hood (1938) onward, cemented the band's membership and cheerful camaraderie.
