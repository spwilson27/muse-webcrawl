United Nations list of non-self-governing territories

The United Nations list of non-self-governing territories records territories which the United Nations considers have not yet attained a full measure of self-government. The list derives from Chapter XI of the UN Charter, the "Declaration regarding Non-Self-Governing Territories", under which administering member states accept an obligation to promote the well-being and political advancement of the peoples of territories they administer, and to transmit information about them to the Secretary-General.

History

In 1946 eight member states listed 72 territories as non-self-governing. Dozens were removed as they became independent or freely associated states, a process driven by the 1960 Declaration on the Granting of Independence to Colonial Countries and Peoples (Resolution 1514). The Special Committee on Decolonization (C-24), created in 1961, reviews the list annually. Territories have occasionally been re-inscribed — French Polynesia was restored to the list in 2013 — or removed, as with Puerto Rico in 1953 after attaining commonwealth status, a decision that remains debated.

The current list

As of the mid-2020s the list contains 17 territories. Administered by the United Kingdom are Anguilla, Bermuda, the British Virgin Islands, the Cayman Islands, the Falkland Islands, Gibraltar, Montserrat, Pitcairn, Saint Helena and the Turks and Caicos Islands. The United States administers American Samoa, Guam and the US Virgin Islands. France administers French Polynesia and New Caledonia; New Zealand administers Tokelau; and Western Sahara, largely controlled by Morocco, is listed without an administering power recognised by the UN.

Some administering powers dispute the listing of particular territories, arguing their peoples have freely chosen their status through democratic processes; the General Assembly nonetheless retains them pending acts of self-determination it considers satisfactory.
