Flight information region

A flight information region (FIR) is an airspace of defined dimensions within which flight information service and alerting service are provided. FIRs are established by states under the framework of the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO), which divides the world's airspace into FIRs so that every part of the atmosphere has an authority responsible for air traffic services.

Organisation

Each FIR is managed by an area control centre, which provides air traffic control to flights in controlled airspace within the region. FIR boundaries usually follow national borders but often extend over international waters and may be delegated between states by agreement. The upper limit is commonly capped, with upper information regions (UIRs) above flight level 195 or 245 in some regions.

Services

Flight information service provides advice and information for the safe and efficient conduct of flights, while alerting service notifies search-and-rescue organisations about aircraft in distress. All aircraft in an FIR, not only those under control, are entitled to these services.
