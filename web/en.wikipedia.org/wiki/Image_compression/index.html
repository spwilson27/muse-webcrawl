Image compression

Image compression is a type of data compression applied to digital images, to reduce their cost for storage or transmission. Algorithms may take advantage of visual perception and of the statistical properties of image data to provide superior results compared with generic data compression methods which are used for other digital data.

Lossless and lossy compression

Image compression may be lossy or lossless. Lossless compression is preferred for archival purposes and often for medical imaging, technical drawings, clip art, or comics. Lossless methods include the deflate algorithm used by PNG, run-length encoding and predictive coding schemes.

Lossy compression methods, especially when used at low bit rates, introduce compression artifacts. They typically work by transforming the image into a frequency domain representation — most famously the discrete cosine transform used by JPEG — discarding high-frequency information to which human vision is less sensitive, and quantising the remainder. Lossy methods are especially suitable for natural images such as photographs in applications where a minor loss of fidelity is acceptable to achieve a substantial reduction in bit rate.

Formats and methods

Common compressed image formats include JPEG, JPEG 2000, WebP, AVIF and HEIF for lossy photographic compression, and PNG, GIF and TIFF for lossless or palette-based compression. Modern methods increasingly use wavelet transforms and learned (neural network based) codecs. Quality is commonly measured by peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and by perceptual metrics such as SSIM.

Applications

Image compression is essential to digital photography, web publishing, medical imaging, satellite imagery and video streaming, where it is combined with inter-frame prediction in video compression standards such as H.264 and HEVC.
