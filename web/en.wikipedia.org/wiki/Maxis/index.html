Maxis

Maxis is an American video game developer and a label of Electronic Arts (EA). Founded in 1987 by Will Wright and Jeff Braun, the studio is best known for the Sim series of simulation games, above all SimCity (1989) and The Sims (2000), one of the best-selling video game franchises of all time.

History

Wright and Braun founded Maxis in Orinda, California, after Braun agreed to publish Wright's city-building game SimCity, which mainstream publishers had repeatedly rejected. The game's success funded a series of spin-offs — SimEarth, SimAnt, SimFarm, SimCity 2000 and others — through the early 1990s. In 1997 Electronic Arts acquired Maxis. After several commercially weak titles, Wright's dollhouse game The Sims (2000) became a phenomenon, spawning sequels and expansion packs that made it one of the best-selling PC franchises ever.

Later years

Wright's ambitious evolution game Spore appeared in 2008; he left Maxis in 2009. The Emeryville studio continued with The Sims 3 (2009) and The Sims 4 (2014), while the 2013 reboot of SimCity was marred by server problems at launch. In 2015 EA closed the Maxis Emeryville studio, consolidating Sims development at other EA locations; the Maxis name survives as a label, with The Sims Studio (Redwood Shores) developing the franchise.

Legacy

Maxis pioneered open-ended "software toys" without fixed winning conditions, influencing sandbox and simulation design across the industry. Its games are noted for modelling complex systems — urban growth, ecosystems, household life — in playful, accessible forms.
