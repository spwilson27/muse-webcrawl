Anne E. Wojcicki (born July 28, 1973) is an American entrepreneur and biologist who co-founded and led 23andMe, the direct-to-consumer genetic testing company that popularised personal genome services. She has been a prominent advocate of giving individuals direct access to their own genetic information.

Early life and education

Wojcicki was born in Palo Alto, California, the youngest of three sisters; her sister Susan became an early Google employee and later CEO of YouTube, and her sister Janet is an epidemiologist. Her mother, Esther Wojcicki, is an educator and journalist, and her father, Stanley Wojcicki, was a physics professor at Stanford. She graduated from Yale University in 1996 with a degree in biology and worked as a health-care investment analyst on Wall Street before leaving, disillusioned with the incentives of the health-care system, to focus on consumer-driven health information.

23andMe

In 2006 Wojcicki co-founded 23andMe with Linda Avey and Paul Cusenza. The company's saliva-based test offered customers information about ancestry and inherited traits, and later health risks. The service drew regulatory scrutiny: in 2013 the U.S. Food and Drug Administration ordered the company to stop marketing health reports until it obtained authorisation, which it progressively regained from 2015 onward. 23andMe went public in 2021. After the company filed for bankruptcy in 2025, Wojcicki sought to reacquire its assets through her nonprofit TTAM Research Institute.

Personal life

Wojcicki married Google co-founder Sergey Brin in 2007; they have two children and divorced in 2015. She supports open-access health data initiatives and is a co-founder of the Breakthrough Prize in Life Sciences.
