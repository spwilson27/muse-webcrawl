Chief security officer

A chief security officer (CSO) is a company's most senior executive accountable for the development and oversight of policies and programs intended for the mitigation of security risks to an organisation's personnel, physical property and information. The role typically encompasses both physical security and information security, although in many organisations the latter is led separately by a chief information security officer (CISO).

Responsibilities

The CSO's responsibilities commonly include risk assessment, corporate security strategy, physical security of facilities, executive protection, crisis management and business continuity, investigations, and compliance with security-related regulations. As cyber threats grew in prominence from the 1990s onward, many organisations merged physical and logical security leadership under a single CSO, a trend known as security convergence.

Related roles

The CSO title should not be confused with the chief information security officer, who focuses on information and cyber security, or with the chief safety officer, who is responsible for workplace safety and, in pharmaceutical companies, for monitoring the safety of clinical trials. In some companies the term "chief security officer" has also been used for the executive responsible for product and platform trust and safety.
