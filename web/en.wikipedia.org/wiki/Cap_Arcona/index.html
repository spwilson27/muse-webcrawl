SS Cap Arcona

Type: Ocean liner / prison ship
Launched: 1927
Operator: Hamburg Süd
Tonnage: 27,561 GRT
Fate: Sunk by RAF air attack, 3 May 1945
Named for: Cape Arkona, Rügen

SS Cap Arcona, named after Cape Arkona on the island of Rügen, was a large German ocean liner and the flagship of the Hamburg Südamerikanische Dampfschifffahrts-Gesellschaft ("Hamburg–South America Line"). She made her maiden voyage on 29 October 1927, carrying passengers and emigrants between Hamburg and South American ports, principally Buenos Aires, and was considered one of the most beautiful ships of her era.

Second World War

During the Second World War the ship was requisitioned by the Kriegsmarine and used as an accommodation ship at Gotenhafen (Gdynia). In 1942 she served as a set for the German propaganda film Titanic. In early 1945 she took part in the evacuation of German troops and civilians from East Prussia across the Baltic Sea.

Sinking

In April and May 1945, as Nazi Germany collapsed, the SS loaded thousands of concentration camp prisoners — evacuated principally from Neuengamme camp — aboard Cap Arcona and the freighters Thielbek and Athen in Lübeck Bay, near Neustadt in Holstein. On 3 May 1945, days before the end of the war in Europe, Royal Air Force Hawker Typhoon aircraft, believing the ships to be carrying fleeing SS troops, attacked them with rockets, bombs and cannon fire. Cap Arcona caught fire and capsized; Thielbek sank quickly.

Of the roughly 7,000 prisoners aboard Cap Arcona, only about 350 survived; thousands more died aboard the other vessels or were shot in the water and on the beaches. In total the disaster killed some 7,000 people, making it one of the worst maritime disasters of the war. The wreck was scrapped in 1949, and memorials at Neustadt and along the Lübeck Bay coast commemorate the victims; human remains continued to wash ashore for decades afterwards.
