South Holland

South Holland (Dutch: Zuid-Holland, pronounced [ˈzœyt ˈɦɔlɑnt]) is a province of the Netherlands with a population of over 3.8 million as of 2023 and a population density of about 1,410/km², making it the country's most populous province and one of the most densely populated areas in the world. Situated on the North Sea in the west of the Netherlands, South Holland covers an area of 3,308 km², of which 609 km² is water. The provincial capital is The Hague, while its largest city is Rotterdam, home to Europe's largest seaport.

Geography

South Holland lies in the delta of the Rhine, Meuse and Scheldt rivers. Much of the province lies below sea level and is protected by an extensive system of dikes, dunes and pumping stations, and forms part of the Randstad conurbation together with North Holland and Utrecht. Other major cities include Dordrecht, Leiden, Delft, Gouda and Zoetermeer. The bulb-growing region around Lisse, famous for the Keukenhof gardens, lies in the province's north.

History

The area formed the southern part of the historical County of Holland, which gave its name to the whole country in common usage. The province of Holland was split into North Holland and South Holland in 1840, largely because of the dominance of Amsterdam and the size of the old province. Dordrecht, the oldest city in Holland, hosted the first gathering of the States of Holland in 1572 during the Dutch Revolt.

Economy

The Port of Rotterdam is the largest port in Europe and a pillar of the provincial economy, alongside petrochemical industry, logistics, horticulture and greenhouse farming in the Westland. The Hague hosts the Dutch government and royal court, numerous international courts including the International Court of Justice, and many embassies and international organizations.

Municipalities

As of 2023 the province consists of 50 municipalities. The most populous are Rotterdam, The Hague, Zoetermeer, Leiden, Dordrecht, Westland, Delft and Alphen aan den Rijn.
