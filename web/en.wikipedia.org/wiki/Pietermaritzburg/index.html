Pietermaritzburg is the capital and second-largest city in the province of KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa. It was founded in 1839 by Voortrekkers and is known for its Victorian architecture, its role in the early life of Mahatma Gandhi, and as the home of the University of KwaZulu-Natal's founding campus.

History

The city was founded by Voortrekkers after the Battle of Blood River and named in honour of the trek leaders Piet Retief and Gerrit Maritz. It served as the capital of the short-lived Natalia Republic and then of the Colony of Natal. In 1893, a young Mohandas Gandhi was ejected from a train at Pietermaritzburg station after refusing to move from a first-class compartment — an experience he later described as a turning point in his life; a statue of Gandhi stands in Church Street.

Geography and climate

The city lies in a basin of the Msunduzi River, surrounded by hills. It has a humid subtropical climate, with hot, rainy summers and mild, dry winters.

Education

Pietermaritzburg is home to a major campus of the University of KwaZulu-Natal (founded as the University of Natal in 1910) and a campus of Durban University of Technology, together with noted schools such as Maritzburg College and St. John's Diocesan School for Girls.

Sport and events

The city is associated with two famous endurance events: the Comrades Marathon, the world's largest ultramarathon, which alternates direction between Pietermaritzburg and Durban, and the Dusi Canoe Marathon on the Msunduzi and Mgeni rivers.
