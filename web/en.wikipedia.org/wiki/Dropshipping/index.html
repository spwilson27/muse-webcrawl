Dropshipping

Dropshipping is a retail fulfilment method where a store does not keep the products it sells in stock. Instead, when a store sells a product using the dropshipping model, it purchases the item from a third party — typically a wholesaler or manufacturer — and has it shipped directly to the customer. The seller does not handle the product directly.

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages include low start-up costs, no inventory holding and a wide possible product range. Disadvantages include low margins, little control over shipping times, quality and branding, inventory synchronisation problems and liability for supplier errors. The model expanded rapidly with e-commerce platforms and marketplaces in the 2010s, and is also associated with counterfeit goods and misleading advertising in some cases.

Procedure

The retailer lists products, the customer orders and pays, the retailer forwards the order to the dropship supplier, and the supplier ships directly to the customer, often in the retailer's packaging.
