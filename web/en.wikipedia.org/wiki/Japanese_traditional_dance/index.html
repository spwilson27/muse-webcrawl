Japanese traditional dance

Japanese traditional dance describes the diverse performing arts of dance in Japan, from ancient court and shrine dances to folk dances and the classical Nihon buyō developed from Kabuki and Noh. Dance (mai and odori) is integral to Japanese ritual, festival and theatre life.

Japanese traditional dance
Types: Bugaku, Kagura, Nihon buyō, Bon odori
Settings: Imperial court, shrines, festivals, theatre

Ancient and court dance

Myth in the Kojiki credits the goddess Ame-no-Uzume with the first dance, origin of shrine kagura. Bugaku, elegant court dance imported from China and Korea from the 7th century, is still performed by the Imperial Household's music department, with masked dancers in rich brocade. Gigaku, an earlier masked procession dance, has largely died out except in preserved masks.

Folk and festival dance

Bon odori is danced in circles at summer Obon festivals honouring ancestors, with regional variants such as Awa Odori in Tokushima — Japan's largest dance festival — and Gujō Odori in Gifu. Other folk forms include harvest and lion dances (shishimai) performed at New Year.

Nihon buyō

Nihon buyō is the classical dance refined in the Edo period from the dance portions of Kabuki, performed by Kabuki actors and by geisha and professional dance schools (ryū) such as Fujima and Hanayagi. Dancers in kimono use fans, umbrellas and hand gestures to mime stories, accompanied by nagauta singing and shamisen. It contrasts mai — slow, gliding, inward movement — with the livelier, foot-stamping odori. See Japanese theatre.
