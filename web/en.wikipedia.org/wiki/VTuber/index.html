VTuber

A VTuber (ブイチューバー, buichūbā), or virtual YouTuber, is an online entertainer who uses a virtual avatar generated using computer graphics and real-time motion capture. VTubers livestream and post videos — playing games, singing, chatting with viewers and collaborating with other creators — while remaining anonymous behind characters that are most often drawn in an anime art style.

History

Avatar-based streaming appeared in Japan in the early 2010s, but the phenomenon took off in late 2016 when Kizuna AI began posting videos as a self-described "virtual YouTuber". A boom followed in 2017–2018 with the rise of the "Four Heavenly Kings" (Kizuna AI, Kaguya Luna, Mirai Akari and the duo Siro/Nekomiya Hinata), and talent agencies industrialized the model.

Industry

The two largest agencies are Cover Corporation's hololive production and Anycolor's Nijisanji, each managing dozens of performers, including English- and Indonesian-language branches launched from 2020 that carried VTubing to a global audience. Avatars are typically 2D models animated with Live2D software or full 3D models tracked by cameras and body trackers. Revenue comes from Super Chat donations, memberships, merchandise, concerts and sponsorships. Retirements are called "graduations"; notable events include the graduation of hololive's Kiryu Coco (2021) and the record-breaking charity streams of independent and agency VTubers alike.
