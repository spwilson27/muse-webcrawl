Kansas City Southern Railway

The Kansas City Southern Railway Company (reporting mark KCS), often shortened to KCS, was a Class I railroad in the United States, headquartered in Kansas City, Missouri. Founded in 1887 by Arthur E. Stilwell, it was the smallest of the major North American railroads but strategically important for its cross-border network linking the U.S. Midwest and Gulf Coast with Mexico. In 2023 it merged with Canadian Pacific Railway to form Canadian Pacific Kansas City (CPKC), creating the first single-line railway connecting Canada, the United States, and Mexico.

History

Stilwell built the Kansas City, Pittsburg and Gulf Railroad in the 1890s to reach the Gulf of Mexico at Port Arthur, Texas — a port city he founded and named after himself. Bankrupt and reorganised in 1900 as the Kansas City Southern Railway, the company grew through lines to Shreveport, New Orleans, Dallas, and Houston, and for a time operated the elegant Southern Belle passenger train between Kansas City and New Orleans.

In 1996 KCS won a concession to operate Mexico's Northeast Railway, forming Transportación Ferroviaria Mexicana (later Kansas City Southern de México), which linked Monterrey, Mexico City, and the Pacific port of Lázaro Cárdenas. The combined "NAFTA Railway" made KCS an attractive merger target despite its modest size.

Merger

In 2021 Canadian Pacific and Canadian National fought a high-profile bidding war for KCS; Canadian Pacific prevailed with a deal valued at about US$31 billion. The merger closed in trust in December 2021 and, after approval by the U.S. Surface Transportation Board, was consummated on 14 April 2023, forming Canadian Pacific Kansas City. The KCS name and its "Southern Belle" colours survive in heritage locomotives of the combined system.
