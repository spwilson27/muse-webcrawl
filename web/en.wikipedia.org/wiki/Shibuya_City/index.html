Shibuya (渋谷区, Shibuya-ku) is a special ward in Tokyo, Japan. It is a major commercial and finance center, home to the two busiest railway stations in the world (Shinjuku Station is partly in Shibuya, and Shibuya Station), and is famous as a center of fashion and nightlife, particularly for young people.

Shibuya was historically the site of a castle town on the outskirts of Edo and a post station on routes leading into the city. In the Meiji era it developed as a rail terminus, and after the Second World War it became one of Tokyo's principal sub-centers (fukutoshin). The ward was formally established on March 15, 1947.

Geography

Shibuya is bordered by the special wards of Shinjuku, Minato, Meguro, Setagaya, Shinagawa, Suginami, and Nakano. Notable districts include Harajuku, Ebisu, Daikanyama, Yoyogi, Sendagaya, and the Shibuya station area itself.

Landmarks

The ward is known for the Shibuya Scramble Crossing in front of Shibuya Station's Hachikō exit, one of the busiest pedestrian crossings in the world, where as many as 3,000 people may cross at a time. A bronze statue of Hachikō, the famously loyal dog, stands nearby. Other landmarks include Yoyogi Park, the Meiji Shrine (in Yoyogi-Kamizonochō), Takeshita Street in Harajuku, Omotesandō, and the Shibuya Sky observation deck atop Shibuya Scramble Square, completed in 2019. NHK's broadcasting center and the Yoyogi National Gymnasium, built for the 1964 Tokyo Olympics, are also located in the ward.

Economy

Shibuya hosts the headquarters of numerous companies, including the East Japan Railway Company's offices near Shinjuku, and a concentration of information-technology firms in the area nicknamed "Bit Valley". Major retailers and department stores such as Shibuya Parco, Shibuya 109, and Tokyu and Seibu department stores anchor its retail economy.

Government

As a special ward, Shibuya has an elected assembly and a directly elected mayor. In 2015 the ward became the first municipality in Japan to issue partnership certificates recognizing same-sex couples.
