Yakov Zeldovich

Born: 8 March 1914, Minsk, Russian Empire
Died: 2 December 1987, Moscow, Soviet Union
Fields: Physics, astrophysics, cosmology

Yakov Borisovich Zeldovich (Russian: Я́ков Бори́сович Зельдо́вич; 8 March 1914 – 2 December 1987) was a Soviet physicist of Belarusian Jewish origin, regarded as one of the most versatile scientists of the 20th century. Largely self-taught in physics, he made foundational contributions to chemical physics, nuclear weapons design, particle physics, and physical cosmology, and founded an influential school of relativistic astrophysics.

Career

Zeldovich began work at the Institute of Chemical Physics in Leningrad, earning his doctorate with research on oxidation and adsorption; he developed the theory of chain reactions and detonation (the ZND detonation model, with von Neumann and Döring). From 1947 he was a key figure in the Soviet atomic bomb project, later decorated as a three-time Hero of Socialist Labour.

Scientific contributions

In later decades he turned to astrophysics: he predicted the Sunyaev–Zeldovich effect (with Rashid Sunyaev), in which cosmic microwave background photons are scattered by hot galaxy-cluster gas; developed the Zeldovich approximation ("pancake theory") for the formation of large-scale cosmic structure; and wrote influential work on black holes, neutron stars, and the early universe, including early analysis of Hawking radiation precursors. Stephen Hawking remarked that, on meeting him, he realized Zeldovich "was a real person" behind the many papers credited to his name. His students included Sunyaev, Igor Novikov, and other leading cosmologists.
