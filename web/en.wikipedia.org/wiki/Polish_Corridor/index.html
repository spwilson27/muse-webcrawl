Polish Corridor

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Polish Corridor (German: Polnischer Korridor; Polish: Pomorze Gdańskie / Korytarz polski) was a strip of territory in the Second Polish Republic that, between 1920 and 1939, separated the German exclave of East Prussia from the main body of Germany while giving Poland access to the Baltic Sea. Created by the Treaty of Versailles after the First World War from territory formerly in the Prussian provinces of West Prussia and Posen, the corridor ran along the lower Vistula to the coast near the Free City of Danzig (Gdańsk), which itself was not part of Poland but under League of Nations protection.

Background

The region, Eastern Pomerania (Pomerelia), had been part of the medieval Polish kingdom before its seizure by the Teutonic Knights in 1308–09; Royal Prussia returned to Poland in 1466, and the area was annexed by Prussia in the First Partition of Poland (1772). President Woodrow Wilson's Fourteen Points promised Poland "free and secure access to the sea", and the Versailles settlement implemented this by transferring the largely Polish- and Kashubian-speaking coastal districts to the reborn Polish state, while the mainly German city of Danzig became a free city.

Interwar tensions

Successive German governments refused to accept the loss of the corridor and Danzig; revision of the eastern border was a central aim of Weimar and then Nazi foreign policy. Trade and transit disputes between Germany and Poland, including the customs war of 1925–34, kept the region in crisis. Poland built the new port of Gdynia just north of Danzig, which by the 1930s handled more trade than Danzig itself. In late 1938 and 1939 Hitler demanded the return of Danzig and an extraterritorial road and rail link across the corridor; Poland's refusal, backed by British and French guarantees, formed the immediate diplomatic prelude to the German invasion of Poland on 1 September 1939, beginning the Second World War.

Aftermath

After 1945 the entire region, including Danzig/Gdańsk, was placed within Poland's postwar borders, and its German population was expelled westward. The territory today forms part of Poland's Pomeranian Voivodeship.
