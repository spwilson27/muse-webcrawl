The Yarkent Khanate, also known as the Yarkand Khanate or the Kashgar Khanate, was a Turkic-Mongol Sunni Muslim state ruled by descendants of Chagatai Khan. It existed from 1514 to 1705 and controlled the western Tarim Basin, including the oasis cities of Kashgar, Yarkand, Khotan, Aksu and Yangihissar, in what is now southern Xinjiang, China.

History

Founding

The khanate was founded by Sultan Said Khan, a descendant of the Chagatai khans of Moghulistan. In 1514 Said Khan seized Kashgar from Mirza Abu Bakr Dughlat, ending the Dughlat emirs' rule over the western Tarim Basin, and established his capital at Yarkand. His campaigns reunified much of the Tarim Basin and extended into Ladakh and the Pamirs.

Golden age

Under Said Khan's son Abdurashid Khan (r. 1533–1560), the khanate enjoyed stability and cultural flourishing. The court attracted musicians, poets and historians; the celebrated musician and poet Amannisa Khan, wife of Abdurashid, is credited with preserving the Twelve Muqam musical tradition of the Uyghurs.

Khoja influence and decline

From the late 16th century, Sufi Khoja lineages — descendants of the Naqshbandi master Ahmad Kasani — gained increasing influence at court. Rivalry between the "White Mountain" (Aq Taghliqs) and "Black Mountain" (Qara Taghliqs) Khoja factions weakened the khans' authority. In 1678–1680 the Aq Taghliq leader Afaq Khoja invited the Dzungar Mongols to intervene; the Dzungars conquered the khanate and installed the Khojas as their vassals, ending effective Chagatai rule by 1705.

Culture

The Yarkent court spoke Chagatai Turkic and patronised Persianate Islamic culture. Sufi shrines, madrasas and mausoleums were built across the oasis cities, and the period produced important Chagatai-language chronicles such as the Tarikh-i Rashidi of Mirza Muhammad Haidar Dughlat, a cousin of Babur.
