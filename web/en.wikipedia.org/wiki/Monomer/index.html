Monomer

A monomer (from Greek mono "one" and meros "part") is a molecule that can react with other monomer molecules to form a larger polymer chain or three-dimensional network in a process called polymerization. Monomers are the building blocks of polymers; a polymer made from a single type of monomer is a homopolymer, while one made from two or more types is a copolymer. Short chains of a few monomers are called oligomers.

Examples

  • Ethylene polymerises to polyethylene; styrene to polystyrene; vinyl chloride to PVC; tetrafluoroethylene to Teflon (PTFE); methyl methacrylate to acrylic glass (PMMA).
  • Natural monomers include amino acids, which polymerise into proteins; nucleotides, which form DNA and RNA; and glucose, which forms starch and cellulose.
  • Isoprene is the monomer of natural rubber (polyisoprene).

Requirements for polymerisation

To form polymers, monomers generally need a functionality of at least two: a double bond that can open (as in vinyl monomers), or two reactive groups that can link by condensation, often releasing a small molecule such as water. Monomers with functionality greater than two (for example glycerol or phenol-formaldehyde systems) produce branched or cross-linked networks.

Classification

Monomers may be classified as natural or synthetic, and by their polymerisation chemistry: addition (chain-growth) monomers, such as alkenes, and condensation (step-growth) monomers, such as diols, diamines and dicarboxylic acids used to make polyesters and nylons.
