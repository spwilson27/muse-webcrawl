Will Wright (game designer)

Born: January 20, 1960, Atlanta, Georgia

Occupation: Video game designer

Known for: SimCity, The Sims, Spore

William R. Wright (born January 20, 1960) is an American video game designer and co-founder of the game development company Maxis. Wright is best known for designing groundbreaking simulation games that lack conventional win conditions, which he has called "software toys".

His first commercial success was Raid on Bungeling Bay (1984), but while designing its levels he discovered he enjoyed building the cities more than destroying them — the insight that led to SimCity (1989), one of the most influential games ever made. At Maxis he followed it with games including SimEarth, SimAnt, and SimCity 2000.

The Sims and later work

Wright's The Sims (2000), a simulation of suburban household life, became the best-selling PC game in history at the time and spawned one of the highest-grossing franchises in the industry. His ambitious evolution game Spore followed in 2008. Wright left Maxis/Electronic Arts in 2009 to run the entertainment think tank Stupid Fun Club, and has since worked on projects exploring memory and personal data.

Recognition

Wright was the first person inducted into the Academy of Interactive Arts and Sciences' Hall of Fame in 1998 (after its founding class), received a BAFTA Fellowship in 2007 — the first game designer so honoured — and is widely credited with broadening the audience and subject matter of video games.
