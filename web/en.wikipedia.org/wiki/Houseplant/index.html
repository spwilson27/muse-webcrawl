Houseplant

A houseplant, also known as a pot plant, potted plant, or indoor plant, is a plant grown indoors in residences and offices. Houseplants are commonly grown for decorative purposes, and sometimes for their purported positive psychological effects and air-purifying qualities.

Cultivation

Most houseplants are tropical or semi-tropical species adapted to warm, stable temperatures of 15–25 °C and to the low light of forest understories. Care centres on appropriate light, watering (overwatering being the most common cause of death), potting compost with good drainage, humidity, feeding during the growing season, and periodic repotting.

Common species

Popular houseplants include pothos (Epipremnum aureum), snake plant (Dracaena trifasciata), peace lily (Spathiphyllum), spider plant (Chlorophytum comosum), Monstera deliciosa, Ficus species, orchids (especially Phalaenopsis), succulents and cacti.

History

Potted plants were kept in ancient Egypt, China and Rome; the practice flourished in Europe from the 17th century with orangeries and Wardian cases that enabled tender exotics to be transported and grown at home, and became a mass domestic custom in the Victorian era and again in the 1970s and 2010s indoor-gardening booms.

Effects

Studies popularly cite houseplants for reducing stress and improving wellbeing; claims of large air-purification effects from individual plants (following a 1989 NASA study) are considered exaggerated under realistic indoor conditions.
