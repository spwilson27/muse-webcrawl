Prince Eugene of Savoy

Prince Eugene Francis of Savoy-Carignano (18 October 1663 – 21 April 1736), known as Prince Eugene, was a field marshal in the army of the Habsburg monarchy and one of the most successful military commanders of his era. Born in Paris and rejected for service by Louis XIV of France, he entered Imperial service and rose to fame fighting the Ottoman Empire, winning the decisive Battle of Zenta (1697). Together with the Duke of Marlborough he won the great victories of the War of the Spanish Succession — Blenheim (1704), Oudenarde (1708) and Malplaquet (1709) — and later defeated the Ottomans again at Petrovaradin (1716) and Belgrade (1717).

Early life

Eugene was born at the Hôtel de Soissons in Paris, the youngest son of Eugene Maurice, Count of Soissons, of the Savoy-Carignano line, and Olympia Mancini, a niece of Cardinal Mazarin. Destined by his family for the Church, he instead sought a military career; rebuffed by Louis XIV, he left France in 1683 and volunteered for the defence of Vienna against the Ottomans.

Career

Eugene rose rapidly through the Imperial ranks during the Great Turkish War, becoming field marshal in 1693. His victory at Zenta destroyed Ottoman power in Hungary and led to the Treaty of Karlowitz (1699). In the War of the Spanish Succession he campaigned in Italy and, in partnership with Marlborough, in the Low Countries and Germany. As president of the Imperial War Council from 1703, he was effectively Austria's leading soldier-statesman, and he reshaped the Habsburg army and finances. His later victories over the Ottomans extended Habsburg rule in the Balkans.

Legacy

Eugene built the Belvedere Palace in Vienna, amassed a famous library and art collection, and acted as patron of scholars including Leibniz. Napoleon ranked him among the great captains of history. The German battleship and several warships — including the Italian cruiser Eugenio di Savoia and the German heavy cruiser Prinz Eugen — were named in his honour.
