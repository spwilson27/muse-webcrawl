Boho-chic

Boho-chic is a style of fashion drawing on various bohemian and hippie influences, which reached its commercial peak in the mid-2000s in association with actress Sienna Miller and model Kate Moss. Its hallmarks include flowing "floaty" skirts, peasant blouses, embroidered tunics, fur or faux-fur gilets, cowboy boots, chunky jewellery, and layered, eclectic accessories.

Origins of the term

The adjective "boho"—short for bohemian—was applied to fashion from the early 1990s, but "boho-chic" became ubiquitous in 2004–2005 when Sienna Miller's Glastonbury festival outfits were widely photographed and copied by high-street retailers. The look drew on earlier precedents: the bohemian dress of the Pre-Raphaelites, 1960s counterculture, and the 1970s styles of Anita Pallenberg and Talitha Getty.

Decline and revivals

By 2006–2007 fashion commentators had declared boho-chic passé as cleaner, more structured silhouettes took over, though its elements—maxi dresses, festival fashion, ethnic embroidery—have recurred cyclically, including in the "boho 2.0" revivals of the 2010s and 2020s. Critics have noted tensions between the style's anti-consumerist bohemian roots and its mass-market commercialization.
