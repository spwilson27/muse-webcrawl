Politics of Gibraltar

The Rock of Gibraltar

The politics of Gibraltar take place within a framework of a parliamentary representative democratic British Overseas Territory, in which the monarch of the United Kingdom is represented by a governor and the chief minister is the head of the elected government. Gibraltar has internal self-government in all matters except defence, external affairs, and internal security, which remain the responsibility of the governor.

Constitution

The current Gibraltar Constitution Order dates from 2006 and was approved by referendum. It modernised the relationship with the United Kingdom while affirming that Gibraltar would remain British for as long as its people wish. The Gibraltar Parliament consists of 17 elected members plus a Speaker; elections are held at least every four years, with voters able to vote for up to ten candidates.

Sovereignty dispute

Spain claims sovereignty over Gibraltar, ceded by Spain to Britain in perpetuity under the Treaty of Utrecht (1713). Gibraltarians have overwhelmingly rejected Spanish sovereignty or shared sovereignty in referendums in 1967 and 2002. The status of Gibraltar has also featured in negotiations following Brexit, since Gibraltar voted strongly to remain in the European Union and depends on a fluid border with Spain, where thousands of workers cross daily.

Parties and government

The principal parties are the Gibraltar Socialist Labour Party (GSLP), the Gibraltar Social Democrats (GSD), and the Liberal Party of Gibraltar. Since 2011 the chief minister has been Fabian Picardo of the GSLP, governing in alliance with the Liberal Party. Gibraltar elects no members to the UK Parliament but was, until Brexit, represented in the European Parliament as part of the South West England constituency.
