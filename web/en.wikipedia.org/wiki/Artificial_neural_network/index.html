Artificial neural networks (ANNs) are computational models used in machine learning, composed of interconnected nodes ("artificial neurons") organized in layers. Each connection carries a weight that is adjusted during training so that the network learns a mapping from inputs to outputs. Neural networks underpin modern deep learning and are used in image recognition, speech recognition, natural language processing, and generative modelling.

Structure

A typical feedforward network has an input layer, one or more hidden layers, and an output layer. Each neuron computes a weighted sum of its inputs, adds a bias, and applies a non-linear activation function such as the sigmoid, tanh, or rectified linear unit (ReLU). By the universal approximation theorem, a network with a single hidden layer of sufficient width can approximate any continuous function on a compact domain, though deeper networks are usually far more efficient in practice.

Training

Networks are trained by minimizing a loss function that measures the discrepancy between predictions and target values. The standard algorithm is backpropagation, which applies the chain rule to compute gradients of the loss with respect to every weight, combined with an optimizer such as stochastic gradient descent or Adam. Large datasets, graphics processing units, and techniques such as dropout and batch normalization made training very deep networks practical from the 2010s onward.

History

The field traces to the McCulloch–Pitts neuron (1943) and Frank Rosenblatt's perceptron (1958). Limitations of single-layer perceptrons, highlighted by Minsky and Papert in 1969, contributed to a decline in funding ("AI winter"). Backpropagation was popularized in 1986 by Rumelhart, Hinton, and Williams. Milestones include convolutional networks for handwriting recognition (LeNet, 1990s), long short-term memory (1997), AlexNet's victory in the 2012 ImageNet competition — often taken as the start of the deep learning era — and the transformer architecture (2017), which underlies large language models.

Types

Major families include multilayer perceptrons, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for spatial data, recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and LSTMs for sequences, generative adversarial networks, autoencoders, and transformers. Applications range from medical diagnosis and translation to protein structure prediction (AlphaFold) and game playing (AlphaGo).
