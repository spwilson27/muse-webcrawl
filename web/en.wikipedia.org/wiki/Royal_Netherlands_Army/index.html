Royal Netherlands Army

Soldiers of the Royal Netherlands Army

The Royal Netherlands Army (Dutch: Koninklijke Landmacht) is the land forces element of the Netherlands Armed Forces. Founded on 9 January 1814, it is charged with the defence of the Kingdom of the Netherlands and contributes to international peacekeeping and crisis-management operations.

History

The army traces its origins to the forces raised during the Dutch Revolt against Spain, and in its modern form to the army of the United Kingdom of the Netherlands established after the Napoleonic Wars. Dutch troops fought at Waterloo in 1815. In May 1940 the army resisted the German invasion for five days before capitulation. After the war the army formed part of NATO's Northern Army Group in West Germany during the Cold War; conscription was suspended in 1997, making it an all-volunteer force.

Structure

The army's operational units include mechanised and light brigades — among them 43 Mechanised Brigade, 13 Light Brigade and 11 Airmobile Brigade — together with the Korps Commandotroepen special forces and support commands for logistics, engineering, signals and medical services. German-Dutch cooperation is extensive: the Dutch 43 Mechanised Brigade is integrated into a German division, and the joint 1 (German/Netherlands) Corps is based in Münster.

Operations

The army has served in Korea, Lebanon, the former Yugoslavia, Iraq, Afghanistan, Mali and Lithuania, and provides the land component of NATO's enhanced Forward Presence.
