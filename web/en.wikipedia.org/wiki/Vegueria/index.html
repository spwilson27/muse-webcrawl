A vegueria (plural vegueries; Spanish: veguería) is a historical administrative division of Catalonia, in north-eastern Spain. Vegueries were the basic territorial units of the Principality of Catalonia from the Middle Ages until the early 18th century, each governed by a royal official called a veguer.

History

The institution developed from the Carolingian vicaria and became the standard framework of royal administration in Catalonia during the 12th and 13th centuries. The veguer exercised judicial and administrative authority on behalf of the Count of Barcelona and later the Crown of Aragon. Vegueries were abolished by the Nueva Planta decrees of Philip V in 1716, which replaced them with Castilian-style corregimientos.

Modern proposals

The idea of restoring vegueries returned in the 20th and 21st centuries as part of debates over Catalonia's territorial organisation. The Statute of Autonomy of Catalonia of 2006 envisaged vegueries as a tier of local government that would eventually replace the four provinces (Barcelona, Girona, Lleida and Tarragona). A 2010 law of the Parliament of Catalonia created seven vegueries — Alt Pirineu, Barcelona, Camp de Tarragona, Catalunya Central, Girona, Lleida and Terres de l'Ebre — with an eighth, Penedès, added in 2017, although their full implementation as governing bodies has remained incomplete.
