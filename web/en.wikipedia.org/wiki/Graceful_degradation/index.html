Graceful degradation

A gracefully degrading system sheds features in an orderly sequence instead of collapsing.

Graceful degradation is the ability of a system — physical, electronic, or software — to maintain limited functionality when a part of it fails, is overloaded, or becomes unavailable. Rather than crashing or failing catastrophically, the system continues to operate at a reduced level of performance or with reduced features, in a controlled and predictable manner.

Concept

The term originates in engineering, where it describes fault-tolerant design: a bridge, power grid, or aircraft control system built with redundancy so that individual component failures reduce capacity gradually rather than causing total collapse. In computing, graceful degradation is closely related to fault tolerance, fail-soft operation, and resilience engineering, and is contrasted with brittle systems that work perfectly until they fail completely.

Typical strategies include:

  • Redundant components that take over when a primary component fails.
  • Shedding non-essential load or features first when resources are scarce.
  • Serving cached or lower-quality output (for example, lower-resolution video) when bandwidth drops.
  • Isolating faults so that one failing module does not propagate errors to others.

In web design

In web development, graceful degradation describes the practice of designing a website or web application for the newest browsers and capabilities, while ensuring that it remains functional — if plainer — in older browsers or environments without scripting, advanced CSS, or plug-ins. Content should stay accessible even if presentation layers or interactive enhancements fail. The complementary philosophy, progressive enhancement, starts from a simple, universally working baseline and adds enhancements for capable browsers; the two approaches converge on the same practical goal of robustness across environments.

Examples

  • A telephone network that can still carry calls, at reduced capacity, when a switching centre fails.
  • A streaming service that lowers image resolution rather than stopping playback.
  • A website whose order form still works with JavaScript disabled.
  • A power grid that islands a damaged section to protect the rest of the network.
