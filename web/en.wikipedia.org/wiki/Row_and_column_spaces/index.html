Row and column spaces

In linear algebra, the column space of a matrix A is the span of its column vectors, and the row space is the span of its row vectors. Both are subspaces of Euclidean space (or of a general vector space's dual), and they always have the same dimension, called the rank of the matrix.

Definitions

For an m × n matrix A, the column space lies in Fm and consists of all linear combinations of the columns — equivalently, all vectors Ax, the image of the linear map. The row space lies in Fn and consists of all linear combinations of the rows. Row operations do not change the row space, though they change the column space; the non-zero rows of the row echelon form give a basis for the row space, and the pivot columns of the original matrix give a basis for the column space.

Rank theorem

The equality dim(row space) = dim(column space) = rank A is a central result, and together with the null space and left null space these form the four fundamental subspaces of Gilbert Strang's terminology, related by the rank–nullity theorem.
