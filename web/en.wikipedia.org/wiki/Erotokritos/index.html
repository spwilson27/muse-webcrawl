Erotokritos
Author: Vitsentzos Kornaros
Language: Cretan Greek
Genre: Romance epic poem
Published: c. 1640s (manuscript); 1713 (first printed edition, Venice)
Length: 10,012 fifteen-syllable verses

The Erotokritos is a romantic epic poem composed by the Cretan poet Vitsentzos Kornaros in the early 17th century, probably in the 1640s. Written in the Cretan dialect of Greek in rhyming fifteen-syllable couplets, it consists of 10,012 verses and is regarded as the masterpiece of Cretan literature and a classic of modern Greek literature.

Plot

The poem tells the story of the love between Erotokritos (Rotokritos), the son of a counsellor of King Herakles of Athens, and Aretousa, the king's daughter. Because of the difference in their social stations, Erotokritos wooes her in secret. When his identity as the mysterious singer beneath her window is discovered, he is exiled. After years of trials, disguises, tournaments and war against the Vlachs, the lovers are finally united in marriage.

Sources and influences

The plot derives from the French medieval romance Paris et Vienne, known to Kornaros through an Italian translation, but the poem transforms its source with elements of the Cretan theatre and folklore of the time. Parts of the Erotokritos passed into Greek oral tradition, and verses from it remain popular in Cretan music today.
