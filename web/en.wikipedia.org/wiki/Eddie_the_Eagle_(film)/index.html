Eddie the Eagle is a 2016 biographical sports comedy-drama film directed by Dexter Fletcher, loosely based on the life of British ski jumper Michael "Eddie" Edwards, who became a folk hero at the 1988 Winter Olympics in Calgary. Taron Egerton stars as Edwards, with Hugh Jackman as Bronson Peary, a fictionalised American coach — a washed-up former ski-jumping champion who reluctantly trains him.

Plot

Determined from childhood to reach the Olympics, the bespectacled plasterer Eddie Edwards fails at sport after sport before taking up ski jumping, a discipline with no British entrant since the 1920s. Training in Germany and enduring ridicule and injury, he qualifies by the standards of the day, competes at Calgary and finishes last in both events — but wins the crowd's affection and the admiration of the Olympic movement for embodying its amateur spirit. The film takes substantial liberties with Edwards's biography: his real coaches were Americans John Viscome and Chuck Berghorn, and the character of Peary is an invention.

Reception

The film premiered at the 2016 Sundance Film Festival and was received warmly by critics, who praised Egerton's performance and the film's feel-good tone; it grossed about US$46 million worldwide. Edwards himself endorsed the film while noting that only a small fraction of it was literally true.
