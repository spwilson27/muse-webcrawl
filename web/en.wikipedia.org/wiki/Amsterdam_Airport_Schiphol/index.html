Amsterdam Airport Schiphol (IATA: AMS, ICAO: EHAM), known informally as Schiphol Airport, is the main international airport of the Netherlands. It is located 9 kilometres southwest of Amsterdam, in the municipality of Haarlemmermeer, on land reclaimed from the former Haarlemmermeer lake — making it one of the world's lowest airports, about 3 metres below sea level. The name derives from a former fortification and, folk etymology holds, a "ships' hell" where vessels wrecked on the lake.

Operations

Schiphol is the principal hub of KLM and a major European hub for the SkyTeam alliance. It operates with a single large terminal divided into three departure halls, an arrangement introduced in 1967, and has six runways, including the Polderbaan (18R/36L), which lies 5 km from the terminal and requires long taxi times. It is consistently among Europe's busiest airports by passenger traffic and aircraft movements, and one of the world's leading cargo airports.

History and features

The airport began as a military airbase in 1916 and became KLM's home base in the 1920s. The present terminal complex dates from 1967, designed by Marius Duintjer and Benno Wissing. Schiphol is known for amenities unusual in airports, including an annex of the Rijksmuseum, a library, and extensive shopping; its railway station beneath the terminal links it to Amsterdam and the Dutch rail network. Environmental concerns over noise and nitrogen emissions have led the Dutch government to propose capping flight movements in recent years.
