Międzymorze (Polish pronunciation: [mʲɛn.d͡zɨˈmɔ.ʐɛ], meaning "Between the Seas"), known in English as the Intermarium, was a political plan pursued after the First World War by Polish leader Józef Piłsudski for a federation — or a close alliance — of Central and Eastern European countries. The proposed union would stretch between the Baltic, Black, and Adriatic seas (hence "between the seas"), encompassing some or all of Poland, Lithuania, Latvia, Estonia, Belarus, Ukraine, Czechoslovakia, Hungary, Romania, Yugoslavia, and possibly others.

Background

The concept drew on the memory of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, a multi-ethnic state that had dominated the region until the partitions of Poland in the late eighteenth century. After Poland regained independence in 1918, Piłsudski sought to create a bloc capable of resisting both German and Russian (later Soviet) domination. The federation was also intended to secure the newly independent states that had emerged from the collapsed Russian, German and Austro-Hungarian empires.

Attempts at implementation

Piłsudski's efforts included the Polish–Ukrainian alliance of 1920 with Symon Petliura and the Kiev offensive during the Polish–Soviet War, which ended with the Peace of Riga (1921) dividing Ukraine and Belarus between Poland and the Soviets. Relations with Lithuania were poisoned by the dispute over Vilnius (Wilno), seized by Poland in 1920, and Czechoslovakia likewise clashed with Poland over Cieszyn Silesia. By the mid-1920s the federal project had effectively failed, and Poland instead pursued a network of bilateral alliances (with France and Romania) and, after Piłsudski's 1926 coup, the policy of Prometheism — support for non-Russian independence movements within the USSR. Polish foreign minister Józef Beck later floated a related, looser "Third Europe" concept in the late 1930s.

Later echoes

The Intermarium idea has been repeatedly invoked since the Cold War. After 1989, and especially following Russia's annexation of Crimea in 2014, analysts and politicians revived the notion of closer cooperation among the states between the Baltic and Black seas. The Three Seas Initiative, launched in 2015–2016 by the presidents of Croatia and Poland, is a forum of European Union member states focused on north–south infrastructure, energy and digital connectivity; it is sometimes described as a modern, EU-based descendant of the Intermarium concept, though its sponsors emphasise its economic character.
