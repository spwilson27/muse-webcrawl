Sobibor (Russian: Собибор) is a 2018 Russian war drama film directed by and starring Konstantin Khabensky, in his directorial debut. The film dramatises the uprising and mass escape of prisoners from the Nazi extermination camp at Sobibor in German-occupied Poland on 14 October 1943 — the most successful prisoner revolt at any Nazi death camp. It was selected as the Russian entry for the Best Foreign Language Film at the 91st Academy Awards, but was not nominated.

Plot

The film follows Alexander "Sasha" Pechersky, a Jewish officer in the Red Army captured by the Germans and deported to Sobibor, where most new arrivals are murdered in gas chambers on arrival. Selected for slave labour, Pechersky is persuaded by fellow prisoners — including the Dutch jeweller's daughter Luka and the inmate leader Leon Feldhendler — to organise an escape. Over several weeks the prisoners secretly craft weapons and plan to kill the SS officers one by one. On 14 October 1943 the plan is set in motion: several SS men are lured to workshops and killed, and hundreds of prisoners storm the fences under fire. About 300 escape into the surrounding forest; Pechersky survives to join Soviet partisans.

Background and reception

The historical Pechersky was a Soviet lieutenant who led the revolt alongside Polish Jewish prisoner Leon Feldhendler; of the roughly 600 prisoners in the camp, about 300 escaped, and around 50 survived the war. After the revolt the Germans dismantled Sobibor and planted the site with trees. The film was made with the support of the Russian Ministry of Culture and premiered in Warsaw and Moscow in spring 2018. Critics praised its harrowing depiction of the camp and Khabensky's performance, though some noted its melodramatic embellishments of the historical record. The story had previously been filmed as the 1987 television film Escape from Sobibor, starring Alan Arkin and Rutger Hauer.
