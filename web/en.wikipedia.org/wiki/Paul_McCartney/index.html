Paul McCartney

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Paul McCartney performing in 2010, Höfner bass in hand, leading the crowd in a singalong at age 68]

Sir James Paul McCartney (born 18 June 1942) is an English singer, songwriter and musician, bassist and co-frontman of the Beatles and the most commercially successful songwriter in popular music history. With John Lennon he formed the Lennon–McCartney partnership; after the Beatles he founded Wings (1971–81) with his wife Linda, and has sustained a solo career into his eighties, from McCartney (1970) to stadium tours that remain among the world's biggest draws.

His compositions for the Beatles include 'Yesterday' — among the most covered songs ever written — 'Eleanor Rigby', 'Penny Lane', 'Hey Jude', 'Let It Be' and 'The Long and Winding Road'; with Wings, 'Band on the Run' and 'Live and Let Die' (1973). He has also composed classical works (the Liverpool Oratorio, 1991), electronic music as the Fireman, and film scores.

Beatles bedrock

Born in Liverpool, taught piano by ear and trumpet briefly, McCartney met Lennon at a church fête in 1957 and joined the Quarrymen. In the Beatles his melodic gift, harmonic daring and perfectionism balanced Lennon's edge; he was, by most accounts, the group's musical director in the studio years, and 'Yesterday' (1965) — which came to him in a dream, he said, complete — announced the balladeer inside the rocker.

After the Beatles

McCartney's 1970 departure and lawsuit dissolved the partnership in law (1975) and made him, briefly, pop's villain; Wings' mid-seventies peak (the Band on the Run album and the 1976 world tour) restored him. Linda's death in 1998, the record-breaking tours of the 2000s–2020s, a knighthood (1997) and the Companion of Honour (2017) mark the long Indian summer of a man the Guinness records name the most successful songwriter of all time.
