Rail transport in Turkey is operated by the Turkish State Railways (TCDD) and its passenger/freight subsidiary TCDD Taşımacılık. The network centres on Ankara and connects Istanbul, İzmir, Konya, Eskişehir and eastern provinces, with international links toward Europe, the Caucasus and Iran.

History

The first railway in Anatolia opened in 1856 between İzmir and Aydın under Ottoman rule; German-backed projects such as the Baghdad Railway followed. The Republic nationalised the lines from the 1920s, but investment lagged behind road building for much of the 20th century.

High-speed rail

Turkey launched its first high-speed service (YHT) between Ankara and Eskişehir in 2009, extended to Istanbul and Konya, with further lines under construction. In Istanbul, the Marmaray tunnel under the Bosphorus (2013) links the European and Asian suburban networks and carries freight between continents.
