FasTracks

FasTracks built out much of Denver's commuter rail network, including the A Line to Denver International Airport.

FasTracks is a multi-billion dollar public transit expansion plan for the Denver metropolitan area in Colorado, United States, administered by the Regional Transportation District (RTD). Approved by voters in November 2004, the programme was funded in part by a 0.4 percent sales tax increase across the eight-county RTD district.

Programme

The plan originally envisioned 122 miles (196 km) of new commuter rail and light rail, 18 miles (29 km) of bus rapid transit, 21,000 new parking spaces at transit stations, expanded bus service, and the redevelopment of Denver Union Station as a multimodal hub, at an estimated cost of about $4.7 billion.

Completed projects

Major components completed include the West (W) Line light rail to Golden (2013), the A Line commuter rail to Denver International Airport (2016), the B Line to Westminster, the G Line to Wheat Ridge and Arvada (2019), and the R Line through Aurora. Union Station reopened as a transit hub in 2014. Several elements have operated under public-private partnership arrangements, notably the Eagle P3 concession.

Challenges

Rising construction costs, the economic downturn of 2008, and lower-than-expected sales tax revenue pushed total costs well above the original estimate and left some corridors — most prominently the full North Metro (N) Line and extensions to Boulder and Longmont — delayed or unfinished, prompting continuing debate over further tax measures to complete the programme.
