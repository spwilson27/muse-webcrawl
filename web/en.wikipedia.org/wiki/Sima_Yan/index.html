Sima Yan (司馬炎; 236 – 16 May 290), courtesy name Anshi, posthumously known as Emperor Wu of Jin, was the founding emperor of the Jin dynasty of China. The grandson of Sima Yi and son of Sima Zhao, the power behind the throne of Cao Wei, he forced the last Wei emperor, Cao Huan, to abdicate in 266 and proclaimed the Jin dynasty. In 280 he conquered Eastern Wu, reuniting China after nearly a century of division since the end of the Han dynasty.

Emperor Wu of Jin, founder of the Jin dynasty and reunifier of China.

Reign

As regent of Wei from 265, Sima Yan moved quickly: within months he took the throne himself, treating the deposed Cao Huan leniently. After years of preparation he launched the conquest of Wu in 279–280; Jin fleets descended the Yangtze, Wu's capital Jianye fell, and its last emperor Sun Hao surrendered. For the first decade of reunification, Emperor Wu's reign (the Taikang era, 280–289) was remembered as prosperous, and he promulgated a new legal code and land system.

His later reign, however, was marked by extravagance — his harem was said to number in the thousands — and by fateful political decisions. He enfeoffed many imperial princes with large appanages and military commands, a policy intended to protect the dynasty that instead armed the princes for civil war. Worse, his heir, Sima Zhong (Emperor Hui), was widely regarded as developmentally disabled; over advice to depose him, Emperor Wu kept him in the succession. Within a year of Wu's death in 290, the War of the Eight Princes erupted, devastating northern China and fatally weakening the dynasty.
