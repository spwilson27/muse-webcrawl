Battle of Custoza (1866)

The Battle of Custoza took place on 24 June 1866 during the Third Italian War of Independence, fought between the Kingdom of Italy and the Austrian Empire. It was a notable Austrian victory, in which the army of Archduke Albrecht defeated a numerically superior Italian force commanded by General Alfonso La Marmora near the village of Custoza, southwest of Verona in Venetia.

Background

Italy, allied with Prussia in the Austro-Prussian War, sought to annex Venetia from Austria. The main Italian army crossed the Mincio river and advanced into the Quadrilatero region, a heavily fortified area guarded by the Austrian fortresses of Mantua, Peschiera, Verona and Legnago. Archduke Albrecht, commanding the Austrian South Army, concentrated his forces and planned to strike the Italian columns as they deployed east of the Mincio.

The battle

On the morning of 24 June, Italian divisions advancing piecemeal encountered the Austrian army descending from the hills around Custoza and Santa Lucia. Fighting centred on the Monte Torre and Monte Croce heights and the villages of Oliosi, Sommacampagna and Villafranca. Austrian infantry, supported by effective artillery and bold cavalry charges, repeatedly drove the Italians from the high ground. La Marmora, poorly informed and believing himself outnumbered, ordered a general retreat across the Mincio in the evening.

Aftermath

Italian losses were roughly 8,000 men killed, wounded and captured, against about 5,600 Austrian casualties. Despite the defeat, Italy still gained Venetia at the peace settlement, because Prussia's victory over Austria at Königgrätz forced Vienna to cede the province. The battle, together with the naval defeat at Lissa a month later, exposed serious weaknesses in Italian command and organisation, and Custoza became a byword in Italy for military humiliation; an earlier battle at the same village in 1848 had also ended in Austrian victory.
