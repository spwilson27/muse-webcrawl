Subsun

A subsun (also called a lower sun) is an atmospheric optical phenomenon in which a glowing spot appears below the Sun, seen most often from aircraft or mountain tops above a layer of cloud or ice fog. It is in effect a mirror image of the Sun reflected by a vast number of flat, horizontally oriented ice crystals.

Formation

The subsun is produced by sunlight reflecting off the upper faces of plate-shaped ice crystals falling with their large flat faces horizontal, acting together like a giant, imperfect mirror. Because the reflection occurs on the underside of the observer's position relative to the crystals, the image appears directly below the Sun, at an angle below the horizon equal to the Sun's altitude above it. When turbulence causes the crystals to wobble, the subsun stretches vertically into a light pillar-like column; when the crystals are well aligned, it appears as a compact, brilliant oval.

Observation

Subsuns are frequently seen by airline passengers looking down at cloud decks composed of ice crystals, and they can accompany other halo phenomena such as sun dogs and the subparhelion. From the ground, a subsun may occasionally be seen reflected from mountain ice fields or observed in diamond dust conditions. The phenomenon is distinct from a glory, which is centered on the antisolar point; the two can appear together from aircraft.
