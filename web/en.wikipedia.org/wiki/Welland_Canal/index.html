Welland Canal


A ship transiting the locks that lift vessels over the Niagara Escarpment.

The Welland Canal is a ship canal in Ontario, Canada, that connects Lake Ontario and Lake Erie. It runs from Port Colborne on Lake Erie to Port Weller on Lake Ontario, a distance of about 43.4 kilometres (27 miles), and forms a key section of the St. Lawrence Seaway and the Great Lakes Waterway. The canal enables ships to bypass Niagara Falls and ascend or descend the Niagara Escarpment — a height difference of about 99.5 metres (326 feet) — through eight locks.

History

The first Welland Canal was built between 1824 and 1829 under the direction of William Hamilton Merritt, originally to provide water power and a shipping route around the falls. Successive enlargements produced the second (1842–1846) and third (1887) canals. The present (fourth) canal was constructed between 1913 and 1932, and was deepened and modernised as part of the St. Lawrence Seaway project in the 1950s and 1970s, including the Welland By-Pass realignment opened in 1973.

Operations

The canal is operated by the St. Lawrence Seaway Management Corporation. Most traffic consists of "laker" bulk carriers moving iron ore, grain, coal, and other cargoes between the upper lakes and the St. Lawrence. Navigating the full length takes a ship roughly eleven hours. The canal closes in winter, typically from late December to late March.
