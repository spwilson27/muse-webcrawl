One country, two systems

Chinese一國兩制 (一国两制)
PinyinYīguó liǎngzhì
Proposed byDeng Xiaoping
Applied toHong Kong (1997), Macau (1999)
Guarantee period50 years from handover

"One country, two systems" (Chinese: 一國兩制) is a constitutional principle of the People's Republic of China under which the socialist system of mainland China would not be extended to certain territories, which would retain their capitalist systems and a high degree of autonomy for 50 years. Formulated by Deng Xiaoping in the early 1980s, it was designed for the planned reunification with Taiwan and was first applied to Hong Kong and Macau.

Origins

Deng developed the formula during negotiations over Hong Kong's future ahead of the 1997 expiry of the New Territories lease. Deng assured interlocutors that Hong Kong's way of life would remain unchanged for fifty years after the handover. The principle was codified in the Sino-British Joint Declaration (1984) and written into the Hong Kong Basic Law, and it was repeated in the Sino-Portuguese Joint Declaration (1987) for Macau.

Hong Kong

Under the Basic Law, Hong Kong became a Special Administrative Region on 1 July 1997, keeping its common-law legal system, its own currency (the Hong Kong dollar), immigration controls, and its capitalist economy, while Beijing assumed responsibility for defence and foreign affairs. Beijing guaranteed the arrangement until 2047. The framework came under strain during the 2014 Umbrella Movement and the 2019–20 protests, and the imposition of the Hong Kong National Security Law by Beijing in 2020 led critics, including the British government, to declare the Joint Declaration breached; China maintains the law restored stability within the "one country" framework.

Macau

Macau became a Special Administrative Region on 20 December 1999 after more than four centuries of Portuguese administration. Its economy, dominated by legal casino gambling, continued under its own Basic Law, with the guarantee running to 2049. Macau's transition has been comparatively uneventful politically.

Taiwan proposal

The formula was originally framed with Taiwan in mind: under it, Taiwan would retain its armed forces, political system, and economy under nominal PRC sovereignty. Successive governments in Taipei have rejected the proposal, and Taiwanese public opinion has consistently opposed it, especially after developments in Hong Kong.

Criticism and developments

Critics argue the principle has been hollowed out as Beijing asserted interpretive authority over the Basic Law, electoral vetting, and national-security legislation. Supporters in Beijing respond that Hong Kong and Macau remain economically distinct and prosperous, and that "one country" is the precondition of "two systems." The phrase remains central to China's stated policy toward Taiwan.
