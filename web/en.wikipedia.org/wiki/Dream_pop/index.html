Dream pop

Dream pop is a subgenre of alternative rock and neo-psychedelia that emphasizes atmosphere and sonic texture as much as pop melody. Common characteristics include breathy vocals, dense productions, and effects processing such as reverb, echo, tremolo and chorus. The genre emerged in the 1980s, pioneered by acts such as the Cocteau Twins and A.R. Kane, and exerted a major influence on the later shoegaze movement.

Characteristics

Dream pop is defined by its emphasis on sonic texture, mood and atmosphere. Vocals are often breathy and submerged in the mix, with lyrics treated as another instrument rather than a narrative focus. Guitar work tends to rely heavily on effects pedals — particularly chorus, delay and reverb — creating shimmering, immersive soundscapes.

History

The term "dream pop" was coined in the late 1980s by Alex Ayuli of A.R. Kane to describe his band's sound. The Cocteau Twins, signed to 4AD Records, became the genre's defining act with albums such as Treasure (1984) and Heaven or Las Vegas (1990). Other key artists include Slowdive, Mazzy Star, Lush, Beach House, and the Jesus and Mary Chain in their mellower moments.

Legacy

Dream pop heavily influenced shoegaze, which pushed its textural approach to greater extremes of volume and distortion. In the 2000s and 2010s, acts such as Beach House, Cigarettes After Sex and Alvvays revived the style for a new generation.
