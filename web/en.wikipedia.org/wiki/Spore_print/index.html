Spore print

The spore print is a powdery deposit formed by the spores of a mushroom falling from its fruiting body onto a surface below. The colour of the print — the spore colour en masse — is a key diagnostic feature used by mycologists and foragers to identify fungi, since spore colour often distinguishes genera whose members otherwise look similar.

Making a spore print

A print is made by cutting off the stem of a mature cap and placing the cap, gills or pores downward, on a sheet of paper, glass or foil, often covered with a bowl or glass to exclude draughts. After several hours to overnight, enough spores accumulate to show their collective colour. Because the colour may be hard to judge on white paper if the spores are pale, prints are often taken on paper that is half black and half white, or on clear glass held against different backgrounds.

Colours

Spore prints range through white, cream, pink, salmon, ochre, olive, brown, purple-brown and black. Groups such as the pink-spored Entoloma and Pluteus, or the dark-spored Agaricus and Psilocybe, are partly characterised by this trait. Spore colour is used in dichotomous keys alongside cap form, gill attachment, odour and microscopy.

Uses

Besides identification, spore prints are used to collect spores for cultivation, and the radial patterns they produce are sometimes made deliberately as a form of natural art.
