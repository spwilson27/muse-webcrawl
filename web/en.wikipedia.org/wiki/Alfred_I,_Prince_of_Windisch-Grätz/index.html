Alfred Candidus Ferdinand, Prince of Windisch-Grätz (11 May 1787 – 21 March 1862) was an Austrian field marshal and one of the leading counter-revolutionary commanders of 1848, responsible for suppressing the uprisings in Prague and Vienna and for the reconquest of Hungary.

Early career

Born in Brussels into the princely Windisch-Grätz family of Styrian origin, he entered Austrian service as a teenager and fought through the Napoleonic Wars, serving at Ulm, Aspern-Essling, Wagram and Leipzig, rising rapidly to senior cavalry command. In 1833 he was promoted field marshal lieutenant and given command in Bohemia. He married Princess Eleonore of Schwarzenberg, whose death in 1848 — shot during the Prague uprising — hardened his reputation for severity.

1848: Prague and Vienna

As military governor of Prague, Windisch-Grätz responded to the June 1848 Slav Congress uprising by bombarding the city into submission — the first of the year's revolutions to be crushed. Summoned to Vienna after the October rising in which the war minister Latour was lynched, he was given supreme command, besieged the capital and retook it by storm (31 October – 1 November 1848). Among the prisoners executed was the Frankfurt Parliament deputy Robert Blum, shot despite his parliamentary immunity, an act that outraged liberals across Germany. His proclamations — "Whoever opposes me, I will smash" in spirit — made him the emblem of reaction.

Hungary and retirement

In December 1848 Windisch-Grätz invaded revolutionary Hungary, occupying Buda and Pest in January 1849. His campaign stalled in the spring against Görgei's counteroffensive, and he was recalled in April 1849, replaced by Welden and then Haynau. He spent his remaining years in honours and semi-retirement, undertaking diplomatic missions, and died in Vienna in 1862.
