Paenungulata

Paenungulata (from Latin paene "almost" + ungulata "having hooves") is a grouping of mammals within the superorder Afrotheria that unites three living orders: the elephants (Proboscidea), the sea cows — manatees and dugongs (Sirenia) — and the hyraxes (Hyracoidea), together with several extinct groups such as the embrithopods and desmostylians. The name reflects the fact that its members were once loosely grouped with the true hoofed mammals (ungulates), though they evolved their hoof-like nails independently.

Characteristics

Despite their very different appearances and lifestyles — elephants are the largest land animals, sirenians are fully aquatic, and hyraxes are small rabbit-sized herbivores — paenungulates share a number of anatomical features, including a distinctive structure of the ankle bone (astragalus), a reduced number or absence of certain teeth, and, in elephants and sirenians, tusks formed from enlarged incisors. Molecular studies strongly support the grouping and place it within Afrotheria, implying an African origin for the common ancestor.

Evolution

The earliest known paenungulates date to the Paleocene and Eocene of Africa. Extinct members include the bizarre horned Arsinoitherium (an embrithopod), the aquatic desmostylians of the North Pacific, and a great diversity of early proboscideans. Together with aardvarks, elephant shrews and tenrecs, paenungulates make up the endemic African radiation of placental mammals.
