The World War II Victory Medal is a service medal of the United States military which was established by an Act of Congress on 6 July 1945 (Public Law 135, 79th Congress) and promulgated by Section 10 of Executive Order 9265. It was awarded to all members of the United States armed forces, including members of the armed forces of the Government of the Philippine Islands, who served on active duty, or as reservists, at any time between 7 December 1941 and 31 December 1946. The corresponding medal from the First World War is the World War I Victory Medal.

Design

The bronze medal was designed by Thomas Hudson Jones. The obverse depicts the figure of Liberation, standing full length with head turned to the dawn of a new day, her right foot resting on a war god's helmet and her hands holding the broken blade of a sword. The word "WORLD WAR II" appears below. On the reverse are inscribed the Four Freedoms — "FREEDOM FROM FEAR AND WANT" and "FREEDOM OF SPEECH AND RELIGION" — separated by a palm branch, encircled by the words "UNITED STATES OF AMERICA 1941 1945". The suspension ribbon is red with white edge stripes and a central rainbow stripe recalling the World War I Victory Medal ribbon.

Criteria and distribution

There was no minimum service duration: any qualifying service within the eligibility window sufficed, making the medal one of the most widely distributed American decorations, with millions awarded. Recipients who also qualified for campaign medals wore the Victory Medal after them in precedence order. The Merchant Marine World War II Victory Medal is a separate, related decoration for members of the United States Merchant Marine.
