MEKO is a family of modular warship designs developed by the German shipbuilder Blohm+Voss, now part of ThyssenKrupp Marine Systems. The name derives from Mehrzweck-Kombination ("multi-purpose combination"). The MEKO concept builds a ship around standardised interfaces so that weapons, sensors and other mission systems can be installed, replaced or upgraded as self-contained modules in container-like units.

Concept

Instead of being designed around a fixed weapons fit, a MEKO hull provides defined module slots with standard connections for power, cooling and data. This allows each export customer to select its preferred radars, missiles and guns, simplifies refits, and lets yards in the customer's own country assemble or build ships under licence. The approach proved commercially successful from the 1980s onwards.

Types

The family includes the MEKO 360 frigates supplied to Argentina and Nigeria, the widely exported MEKO 200 frigate series built for Turkey, Greece, Portugal, Australia and New Zealand (the Anzac class), South Africa's MEKO A-200SAN Valour class, the MEKO 100 patrol vessels and corvettes built for Malaysia and Poland, and the German Navy's own MEKO designs including the F124 Sachsen-class and F125 Baden-Württemberg-class frigates and K130 Braunschweig-class corvettes. The later MEKO A-100 and A-200 designs, and the large MEKO A-300 concept, continue the modular philosophy with reduced radar signatures.

Operators

More than a dozen navies operate MEKO-derived ships, and designs of the family have been built at yards in Germany, Australia, South Africa, Malaysia and elsewhere, making the concept one of the most successful export warship programmes of the post-Cold War era.
