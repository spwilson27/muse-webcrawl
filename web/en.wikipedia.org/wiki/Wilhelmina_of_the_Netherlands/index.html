Wilhelmina, Queen of the Netherlands for nearly 58 years.

Wilhelmina (31 August 1880 – 28 November 1962) was Queen of the Netherlands from 1890 until her abdication in 1948. She was the only child of King William III and reigned for nearly 58 years — longer than any other Dutch monarch — presiding over the country through both world wars.

Early reign

Wilhelmina ascended the throne aged ten; her mother Emma served as regent until her inauguration in 1898. She married Duke Henry of Mecklenburg-Schwerin in 1901, and their only child, Juliana, was born in 1909. During the First World War she maintained Dutch neutrality while keeping the armed forces on a war footing.

Second World War

When Germany invaded in May 1940, Wilhelmina evacuated to London, where she became the symbol of Dutch resistance, broadcasting to her people via Radio Oranje. Winston Churchill called her "the only real man" among the exile governments in London. She returned in 1945 to a devastated country.

Abdication and death

Ill health led Wilhelmina to abdicate on 4 September 1948 in favour of Juliana. She spent her retirement at Het Loo Palace, writing her autobiography Eenzaam maar niet alleen ("Lonely but not alone"), and died in 1962.
