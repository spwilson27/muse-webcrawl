Oktoechos

Oktoechos (Greek: ὁ Ὄκτώηχος, "the eight tones"; Slavonic: Октоихъ, Oktoikh) is the name of the system of eight musical modes used in Byzantine chant and in the chant traditions derived from it, including Russian, Serbian and Georgian chant. The name is also given to the liturgical book that contains the cycle of hymns sung according to the mode of the week.

The eight modes

The eight modes, or echoi, are arranged as four authentic modes and their four plagal counterparts: Mode I, Mode II, Mode III, Mode IV, Plagal of Mode I, Plagal of Mode II, the "Grave" mode (plagal of Mode III), and Plagal of Mode IV. Each mode has its own melodic formulas, intonations and cadential patterns. In the weekly liturgical cycle, the mode changes each Sunday and proceeds through all eight modes in an eight-week cycle, which repeats throughout most of the year.

The liturgical book

The Octoechos book, also called the Parakletike in its fuller form, contains the variable hymns of the daily offices — stichera, troparia, canons and sessional hymns — for each day of the week in each of the eight tones, principally for the resurrectional cycle of services. Its compilation is traditionally associated with St. John of Damascus and the monastery of Mar Saba in the 8th century, though the collection developed over centuries and was reshaped by later hymnographers.

History

The eight-mode system developed in the monasteries of Palestine and Constantinople and was codified in Byzantine theoretical treatises known as the Papadike. Through missions and translation, the system passed into the Slavic churches, where the Oktoikh became one of the most frequently printed liturgical books of early Slavic printing. A distinct Latin counterpart, the eight church modes of Gregorian chant, developed in the West.
