Buzz pollination

Buzz pollination, or sonication, is a technique used by some bees, such as bumblebees and many solitary bees, to release pollen which is more or less firmly held by the anthers of certain flowers. The bee grasps the flower's anther and vibrates its flight muscles without flying, at frequencies typically in the range of a few hundred hertz, shaking the pollen loose from small apical pores (poricidal anthers).

Flowers requiring buzz pollination

About 6–8% of flowering plants have poricidal anthers adapted to buzz pollination, including important crops such as tomatoes, potatoes, peppers and aubergines (family Solanaceae), blueberries and cranberries, and kiwifruit. Because honey bees cannot sonicate, greenhouse tomato production relies on commercially reared bumblebee colonies or mechanical vibration.

Mechanism

The bee curls around the anther cone and contracts its indirect flight muscles, decoupling them from the wings. The resulting vibration dislodges pollen, which falls onto the bee's body and is groomed into pollen baskets; some pollen reaches the stigma, effecting pollination. Flowers reward the bee with pollen alone, offering no nectar.
