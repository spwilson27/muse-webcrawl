Juno (Latin: Iūnō) is an ancient Roman goddess, the protector and special counsellor of the state. She is the queen of the gods, wife and sister of Jupiter, and the goddess of marriage and childbirth. A daughter of Saturn and Ops, she was identified with the Greek goddess Hera.

Role and epithets

Juno presided over every aspect of the lives of women, and over the vital force (iuno) of each woman, counterpart to the male genius. Her many epithets include Juno Regina ("the Queen"), member of the Capitoline Triad with Jupiter and Minerva; Juno Moneta ("the Warner"), whose temple on the Arx housed Rome's mint — the origin of the word "money"; Juno Lucina, who brings children into the light; Juno Sospita, a warlike, goatskin-clad saviour goddess worshipped at Lanuvium; and Juno Pronuba and Iugalis, patrons of marriage.

Festivals

Her principal festival, the Matronalia, was celebrated on 1 March by married women. The Nonae Caprotinae on 7 July honoured Juno Caprotina. The month of June (Latin Iunius) is traditionally said to take its name from her.

Mythology

In Roman epic, most famously Virgil's Aeneid, Juno is the implacable enemy of Aeneas and the Trojans, still resenting the Judgment of Paris and fearing Rome's destined destruction of her beloved Carthage; her wrath drives the poem's opening storms. Ovid's Metamorphoses retells her jealous persecutions of Jupiter's lovers and their offspring, including Io, Callisto, Semele and Hercules.

Legacy

The asteroid 3 Juno and NASA's Juno spacecraft to Jupiter are named for her. She remains a central figure of the Roman pantheon in literature and art.
