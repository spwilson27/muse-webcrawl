Rugby is an unincorporated community in Morgan and Scott counties in Tennessee, United States. Founded in 1880 by the English author Thomas Hughes, author of Tom Brown's School Days, it was planned as a utopian colony where the younger sons of English gentry — who under primogeniture inherited little — could make a living by farming and cooperative enterprise.

History

The colony attracted considerable attention in Britain and America. Public buildings including the Thomas Hughes Free Public Library (1882), which still holds its original collection of some 7,000 Victorian volumes, the Christ Church Episcopal parish, and Kingstone Lisle house were built in a charming board-and-batten style. The experiment faltered within a decade through harsh winters, a typhoid epidemic and the settlers' inexperience, and most colonists drifted away.

Today

About twenty original buildings survive, preserved since the 1960s by Historic Rugby, Inc. The village, set on the Cumberland Plateau near the Big South Fork National River and Recreation Area, hosts festivals, workshops and tours celebrating its Victorian heritage.
