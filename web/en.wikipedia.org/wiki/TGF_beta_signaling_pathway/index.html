TGF beta signaling pathway

TGF-β signalling pathway
LigandsTGF-β1, TGF-β2, TGF-β3 (and superfamily members)
ReceptorsTGFBR2 (type II), TGFBR1/ALK5 (type I)
TransducersSMAD2, SMAD3, SMAD4; inhibited by SMAD6/7
OutputsRegulation of proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, ECM production

The TGF beta signaling pathway is the signal transduction cascade by which transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β) and other members of its superfamily — bone morphogenetic proteins (BMPs), activins, nodal and growth differentiation factors — control gene expression. The pathway is present in all animals and regulates embryonic development, tissue homeostasis, wound repair and immune function.

Canonical (Smad) pathway

Signalling begins when an activated TGF-β dimer binds a constitutively active type II receptor kinase (TGFBR2) on the cell surface. The ligand–receptor complex recruits a type I receptor (TGFBR1, also called ALK5), which the type II receptor phosphorylates and activates. The type I receptor then phosphorylates receptor-regulated Smads (R-Smads): SMAD2 and SMAD3 for TGF-β and activin ligands, or SMAD1, SMAD5 and SMAD8 for BMP ligands.

Phosphorylated R-Smads bind the common-mediator Smad SMAD4, and the complex accumulates in the nucleus, where it associates with DNA-binding partners and co-activators or co-repressors to switch target genes on or off. Targets include the cell-cycle inhibitors p15 and p21, extracellular matrix genes, and feedback regulators of the pathway itself.

Regulation

The pathway is restrained at several levels. Inhibitory Smads — SMAD6 and SMAD7 — block R-Smad phosphorylation and recruit ubiquitin ligases (such as Smurf1/2) that target the receptors for degradation. The co-repressors Ski and SnoN dampen Smad transcriptional activity, extracellular proteins such as follistatin sequester ligands, and receptor complexes are turned over by endocytosis. In the BMP branch, extracellular antagonists including noggin and chordin shape morphogen gradients during development.

Non-canonical pathways

TGF-β receptors can also activate Smad-independent signalling, including the MAP kinase cascades (ERK, JNK, p38), PI3K–Akt, and small GTPases of the Rho family. These branches modulate the epithelial–mesenchymal transition, cell migration and cytoskeletal organisation, and cross-talk extensively with the canonical pathway.

Clinical significance

Disruption of the pathway contributes to cancer (loss of the cytostatic response and later pro-metastatic effects), fibrotic diseases, Marfan syndrome and Loeys–Dietz syndrome, and hereditary haemorrhagic telangiectasia. Small-molecule inhibitors of the type I receptor kinase, ligand traps and antisense drugs against components of the pathway are under clinical investigation.
