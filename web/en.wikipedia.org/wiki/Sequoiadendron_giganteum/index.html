Sequoiadendron giganteum

Giant sequoia
Kingdom: Plantae
Order: Pinales
Family: Cupressaceae
Genus: Sequoiadendron J.Buchholz
Species: S. giganteum
Conservation status: Endangered (IUCN)

Sequoiadendron giganteum, also known as the giant sequoia, giant redwood or Sierra redwood, is a species of coniferous tree, the only living species of the genus Sequoiadendron, and one of three species of conifers known as redwoods. Giant sequoias are among the largest trees in the world by volume; the largest living specimen, the General Sherman Tree, stands about 83.8 metres (275 ft) tall with a trunk volume exceeding 1,487 cubic metres (52,500 cu ft).

Description

The giant sequoia is an evergreen tree with thick, fibrous, reddish-brown bark that can exceed 90 cm (3 ft) in thickness, protecting the tree from fire. Its leaves are awl- or scale-shaped and arranged spirally on the shoots. The cones are ovoid, 4–7 cm long, and may remain on the tree for many years before opening, often requiring the heat of fire to release their seeds. Trees can live for more than 3,000 years.

Distribution

The species occurs naturally only in about 75 groves on the western slopes of the Sierra Nevada of California, at elevations of roughly 1,400–2,150 m (4,600–7,050 ft). Groves are protected within Sequoia, Kings Canyon, and Yosemite national parks and several national monuments and state parks.

Ecology

Giant sequoias are adapted to periodic low-intensity fires, which clear competing vegetation, open cones, and prepare seedbeds. Twentieth-century fire suppression and modern high-severity wildfires, intensified by climate change, have become major threats; severe fires in 2020–2021 killed an estimated 13–19% of all large giant sequoias.

Uses

The wood is highly resistant to decay but brittle, so historic logging, which destroyed many old-growth groves in the late 19th century, yielded lumber used mostly for fence posts, shingles, and matchsticks rather than construction timber. Today the trees are valued chiefly for conservation, tourism, and carbon storage.
