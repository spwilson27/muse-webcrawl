Hatshepsut

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Hatshepsut's terraced mortuary temple at Deir el-Bahari beneath the Theban cliffs]

Hatshepsut (c. 1507–1458 BC) was an ancient Egyptian queen who became one of the few female pharaohs, ruling as co-king — and in effect senior monarch — for some twenty-one years (c. 1479–1458 BC) during the Eighteenth Dynasty. Daughter of Thutmose I, widow of Thutmose II, and regent for her stepson Thutmose III, she assumed full royal titulary and was depicted with the pharaonic false beard. Her prosperous reign is remembered for the trading expedition to Punt and for her magnificent mortuary temple at Deir el-Bahari.

Reign

Hatshepsut's government, administered by her steward Senenmut, emphasised trade and building over conquest: expeditions brought myrrh trees and incense from Punt (in the Horn of Africa), gold from Nubia and timber from Lebanon. She built extensively at Karnak — including two great obelisks and the Red Chapel — and her mortuary temple, designed by Senenmut, terraced into the cliffs of western Thebes, is a masterpiece of Egyptian architecture.

Erasure and recovery

After her death, and late in Thutmose III's reign, her images and names were systematically chiselled out — whether from her stepson's resentment or later political calculation is debated. The damnatio largely succeeded until 19th-century Egyptologists pieced her story back together; she is now recognised as one of the most successful rulers of the New Kingdom.
