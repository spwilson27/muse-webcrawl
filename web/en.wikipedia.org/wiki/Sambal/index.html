Sambal

Sambal
TypeCondiment / relish
Place of originMaritime Southeast Asia (Indonesia, Malaysia)
Main ingredientsChilli peppers, salt; often shrimp paste, garlic, shallots, lime

Sambal is a chilli sauce or paste originating from Maritime Southeast Asia, typically made from a mixture of chilli peppers with secondary ingredients such as shrimp paste, garlic, ginger, shallot, scallion, palm sugar and lime juice. It is an essential condiment in the cuisines of Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Brunei and Sri Lanka, and is also found in the Netherlands and Suriname through colonial ties.

History

Chilli peppers reached Southeast Asia with Portuguese and Spanish traders in the 16th century, after the Columbian exchange; earlier versions of sambal were probably made with native long pepper or ginger. The word sambal is of Javanese origin (sambel). Traditional sambals are freshly ground with a mortar and pestle (cobek and ulekan).

Varieties

Hundreds of regional variants exist. Sambal oelek is a simple raw paste of chillies, salt and vinegar, popular in the West. Sambal terasi (or belacan) includes fermented shrimp paste; sambal matah from Bali is a raw shallot-and-lemongrass relish; sambal ijo from West Sumatra uses green chillies and tomatoes; and sambal dabu-dabu from North Sulawesi is a fresh chunky salsa of chilli, tomato and lime.

Use

Sambal is served as an accompaniment to grilled fish, fried chicken, nasi goreng, soups such as soto, and vegetables, and is used as a cooking base in dishes such as sambal goreng and balado. Commercial bottled sambals are exported worldwide.
