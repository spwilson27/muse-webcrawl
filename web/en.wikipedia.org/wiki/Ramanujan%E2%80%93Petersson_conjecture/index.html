Ramanujan–Petersson conjecture

FieldNumber theory (modular forms)
Conjectured bySrinivasa Ramanujan (1916); Hans Petersson (1930)
Proved byPierre Deligne (1974, cusp forms)

In mathematics, the Ramanujan–Petersson conjecture is a statement about the size of the Fourier coefficients of modular forms. Srinivasa Ramanujan proposed in 1916 that the coefficients τ(n) of the discriminant cusp form satisfy |τ(p)| ≤ 2p11/2 for primes p. Hans Petersson generalised the conjecture in 1930 to cusp forms of arbitrary weight.

Statement

For a cusp form of weight k that is an eigenform for the Hecke operators, the conjecture asserts that the eigenvalues λ(p) satisfy |λ(p)| ≤ 2p(k−1)/2. Equivalently, the local factors of the associated L-function have the size predicted by the local Langlands correspondence for tempered representations.

Proof

Pierre Deligne proved the conjecture for cusp forms in 1974 as a consequence of his proof of the Weil conjectures in algebraic geometry, for which he received the Fields Medal in 1978. The generalisation to automorphic forms on GL(n), known as the Ramanujan conjecture for automorphic forms, remains largely open, though important cases have been proved by methods including those of Laurent Lafforgue and others.
