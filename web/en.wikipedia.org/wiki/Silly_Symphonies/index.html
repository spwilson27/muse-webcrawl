Silly Symphonies (also spelled Silly Symphony) is a series of 75 animated short films produced by Walt Disney Productions from 1929 to 1939. Unlike the studio's Mickey Mouse cartoons, the Silly Symphonies had no recurring stars; each short was a self-contained musical fantasy, letting Disney's animators experiment with story, character animation, music synchronisation and, most importantly, colour. The series won seven Academy Awards for Best Animated Short Film in a row.

History

The series began with The Skeleton Dance (1929), animated largely by Ub Iwerks to music by Carl Stalling, who proposed the concept. Early entries were musical novelties, but the series soon became Disney's research and development lab: Flowers and Trees (1932) was the first cartoon in three-strip Technicolor; Three Little Pigs (1933) was a phenomenon that advanced character personality in animation and gave the Depression-era hit "Who's Afraid of the Big Bad Wolf?"; The Old Mill (1937) debuted the multiplane camera. Donald Duck first appeared in a Silly Symphony, The Wise Little Hen (1934).

Significance

New animators were trained on the series, and techniques proven there fed directly into Disney's feature productions beginning with Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs (1937). The series ended in 1939 as the studio concentrated on features and character shorts. The name was later revived for comic books and reissue compilations.
