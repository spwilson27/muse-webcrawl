The UEFA Euro 2020 was the 16th edition of the UEFA European Championship, the quadrennial international men's football championship of Europe organised by UEFA. It was held in 11 cities in 11 UEFA countries from 11 June to 11 July 2021. The tournament retained its original name despite being postponed by a year because of the COVID-19 pandemic. Italy won the tournament for the second time, defeating England on penalties in the final at Wembley Stadium in London.

Background and format

The tournament was staged as a pan-European event to celebrate the 60th anniversary of the competition, an idea championed by then-UEFA president Michel Platini. Twenty-four teams took part, divided into six groups of four, with the top two in each group and the four best third-placed teams advancing to the knockout stage. There was no single host nation, and no team qualified automatically.

Venues

Matches were played in London (Wembley Stadium, which hosted the semi-finals and final), Saint Petersburg, Munich, Rome, Baku, Bucharest, Amsterdam, Seville, Glasgow, Copenhagen and Budapest. Dublin and Bilbao were originally among the hosts but were replaced after being unable to guarantee spectators amid the pandemic.

Knockout stage and final

The knockout rounds produced several dramatic matches, including Switzerland's penalty shoot-out win over world champions France and England's extra-time semi-final victory over Denmark. The final, played on 11 July 2021, saw England take an early lead through Luke Shaw, before Leonardo Bonucci equalised for Italy. After a 1–1 draw through extra time, Italy won the penalty shoot-out 3–2, with goalkeeper Gianluigi Donnarumma — later named Player of the Tournament — saving decisive kicks from Bukayo Saka and Jadon Sancho, and Marcus Rashford hitting the post.

Statistics and legacy

Cristiano Ronaldo and Patrik Schick finished as joint top scorers with five goals each; Ronaldo was awarded the Golden Boot on the basis of an assist and fewer minutes played. The tournament set a record for own goals, with eleven scored. The event was widely praised for its high-scoring, open football, though the final was marred by disorder outside Wembley and racist abuse directed at England's players online afterwards.
