Le Cid (1637) is a five-act tragicomedy by Pierre Corneille.

The Cid (French: Le Cid) is a five-act tragicomedy written by Pierre Corneille, first performed in December 1636 or January 1637 at the Théâtre du Marais in Paris. It is based on the Spanish play Las Mocedades del Cid (1618) by Guillén de Castro, which dramatizes episodes from the life of the Castilian hero Rodrigo Díaz de Vivar, "El Cid."

Plot

Don Rodrigue and Chimène are in love, but their fathers quarrel: the Comte de Gormas, Chimène's father, slaps Rodrigue's aged father, Don Diègue, after a dispute over a royal appointment. Unable to avenge himself, Don Diègue asks his son to defend the family honor. Rodrigue kills the Comte in a duel, and Chimène, torn between love and duty, demands justice from the King. Rodrigue then defeats a Moorish invasion and earns the name "Le Cid." Chimène's champion, Don Sanche, is defeated by Rodrigue in a final duel, and the King decrees that after a period of mourning and further service, Rodrigue and Chimène may marry.

The Quarrel of Le Cid

The play was an enormous popular success but provoked the famous Querelle du Cid, a pamphlet war over dramatic theory. Critics, encouraged by Cardinal Richelieu, charged Corneille with violating the classical unities and plausibility. The Académie française issued its Sentiments on the play, a landmark in French literary criticism. Despite the controversy, Le Cid is considered foundational to French classical tragedy and contains the famous line "Va, je ne te hais point" ("Go, I do not hate you").
