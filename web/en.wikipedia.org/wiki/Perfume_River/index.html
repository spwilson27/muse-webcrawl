The Perfume River (Vietnamese: Sông Hương or Hương Giang) is a river that crosses the city of Huế in Thừa Thiên Huế Province in central Vietnam. It is renowned for its scenic beauty and its central place in the culture and history of the former imperial capital.

Name

According to tradition, the river takes its name from the fragrant flowers and aromatic plants that fall into its upstream waters in autumn from orchards and forests, perfuming the river as it flows through Huế. Another legend associates the name with incense and aromatic woods once carried down from the mountains.

Course

The river is formed in the Trường Sơn (Annamite) mountains by the confluence of two tributaries, the Tả Trạch and the Hữu Trạch, near the Bằng Lãng fork. It flows northeast past the Imperial City of Huế, the Thiên Mụ Pagoda and the tombs of the Nguyễn emperors, before emptying into the Tam Giang Lagoon and then the South China Sea.

Culture

The river is inseparable from Huế's identity. Dragon-boat rides with traditional ca Huế folk singing are a signature experience, and the riverfront is a centrepiece of the biennial Huế Festival. The river and the imperial monuments along its banks form part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing for the Complex of Huế Monuments. During the 1968 Battle of Huế in the Vietnam War, the river divided the contested city and was the scene of heavy fighting.
