Simla Accord

Simla Accord (Convention of Simla)
Drafted1913–1914, Simla, British India
Signed1914 (initialled; not ratified by China)
PartiesUnited Kingdom (British India), Tibet, Republic of China
Key figuresSir Henry McMahon; Lonchen Shatra; Ivan Chen

The Simla Accord, formally the Convention Between Great Britain, China, and Tibet of 1914, was a treaty negotiated at Simla, India, among representatives of Britain, Tibet, and the Republic of China concerning the status of Tibet and its boundaries with China and British India.

Terms

The draft convention divided Tibet into "Outer Tibet" (under the Dalai Lama's administration, with Chinese suzerainty but autonomous government) and "Inner Tibet" (under greater Chinese influence). Its attached map delineated the boundary between Tibet and British India along the Himalayan crest — the line later known as the McMahon Line. The Chinese plenipotentiary, Ivan Chen, initialled the draft under pressure but Beijing repudiated his action and refused to sign the final text; Britain and Tibet signed a bilateral declaration treating the convention as binding between them.

Legacy

China has never accepted the Simla Accord's validity, and the McMahon Line remains a central issue in the Sino-Indian border dispute, including the 1962 Sino-Indian War. Tibet's legal status in 1914 remains contested by historians and governments.
