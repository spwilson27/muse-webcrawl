Andrés de Urdaneta (1508 – 1568) was a Basque explorer, navigator and Augustinian friar who discovered the practical return route (tornaviaje) from the Philippines to Mexico across the northern Pacific, making the Manila galleon trade possible.

Early voyages

Urdaneta sailed at seventeen with the Loaísa expedition (1525), one of the earliest attempts to reach the Spice Islands by the western route. Stranded in the Moluccas for years and later imprisoned by the Portuguese, he eventually returned to Europe in 1536, completing a circumnavigation and bringing invaluable knowledge of Pacific winds and currents.

The tornaviaje

Having taken Augustinian vows in Mexico, Urdaneta was ordered to accompany the expedition of Miguel López de Legazpi to the Philippines in 1564 as navigator and adviser. Rather than return with Legazpi, Urdaneta set out separately in 1565 and, reasoning from the pattern of the Atlantic volta do mar, sailed north to about 38°N to catch the westerlies and the Kuroshio current, reaching Acapulco after a voyage of some four months. This northern route became the standard return leg of the Manila galleons for 250 years.

Urdaneta died in Mexico City in 1568. He is regarded as one of the great ocean navigators of the age of sail.
