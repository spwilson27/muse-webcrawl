Prince of Orange (Dutch: Prins van Oranje) is a title originally borne by the sovereign rulers of the Principality of Orange, a small feudal state around the town of Orange in the Rhône valley of southern France. Since the 16th century it has been associated above all with the Dutch House of Orange-Nassau, and today it is the title of the heir apparent to the throne of the Netherlands.

History

The principality of Orange passed through several families, including the House of Baux and the House of Châlon. In 1544 the last Châlon prince, René of Châlon, died childless and left his lands and titles to his cousin William of Nassau, who became William I, Prince of Orange — better known as William the Silent. William led the Dutch Revolt against Spain and became the founding figure of the Dutch Republic, whose stadtholders were drawn from his house.

The principality itself, an enclave within France, was annexed by Louis XIV and formally ceded by the Dutch Republic to France under the Treaty of Utrecht in 1713, but the title "Prince of Orange" was retained by the House of Orange-Nassau and its use was recognized in European diplomacy. Members of the house successively became stadtholders of the Dutch provinces, then — after William III also became King of England, Scotland, and Ireland in 1689 — and, from 1815, kings of the Netherlands.

Modern usage

Under the Dutch constitution, the title Prince (or Princess) of Orange is reserved for the heir apparent to the Dutch throne. Willem-Alexander held the title until his accession as king in 2013, when it passed to his eldest daughter, Catharina-Amalia, Princess of Orange — the first woman to hold the title in her own right as heir apparent.

Legacy

The name Orange, from the Dutch royal house, gave rise to the use of orange as the Netherlands' national color, to the Orange Free State and Orange River in South Africa (named by Dutch settlers), to Orange towns and counties in various countries, and to the Orange Order in Northern Ireland, commemorating William III ("King Billy"), Prince of Orange.
