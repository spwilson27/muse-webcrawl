Wadaad writing

Wadaad writing (Somali: Farta Wadaadka), also known as Wadaad's script, is an adaptation of the Arabic script used historically to write the Somali language. It was the principal written form of Somali for centuries before the adoption of the Latin-based Somali alphabet in 1972.

History

Somali scholars and religious teachers — wadaads — adapted Arabic letters with additional marks to represent Somali sounds not present in Arabic. The script was used for religious poetry (qasidas), correspondence, legal documents and personal notes, and appears in inscriptions and manuscripts dating back several centuries.

Characteristics

Wadaad writing follows Arabic conventions of right-to-left direction and cursive joining, with modified letters and vowel signs for Somali phonology. Because usage varied by region and teacher, spelling was not fully standardized.

Decline

In 1972 the Somali government adopted the Latin alphabet devised by Shire Jama Ahmed as the official orthography, and Wadaad writing fell out of general use, though it persists in religious contexts and among older scholars.
