Harvey Weinstein sexual abuse cases

In October 2017, reports in The New York Times and The New Yorker detailed decades of sexual abuse allegations against American film producer Harvey Weinstein, co-founder of Miramax and The Weinstein Company. More than 100 women eventually came forward with allegations ranging from sexual harassment to rape. The revelations precipitated the worldwide Me Too movement and what became known as the "Weinstein effect," in which powerful men in many industries faced accountability for sexual misconduct.

Background

Rumours and informal warnings about Weinstein's behaviour had circulated in Hollywood for decades. An October 5, 2017 report by New York Times journalists Jodi Kantor and Megan Twohey revealed that Weinstein had paid settlements to at least eight women over nearly three decades. Days later, Ronan Farrow's New Yorker investigation reported allegations of rape by three women and described systematic efforts — including the use of private intelligence firms such as Black Cube — to suppress reporting. Weinstein was fired by The Weinstein Company board and expelled from the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences.

Criminal cases

In May 2018 Weinstein surrendered to New York police. In February 2020 a Manhattan jury convicted him of criminal sexual act in the first degree and rape in the third degree; he was sentenced to 23 years in prison. In 2024, however, the New York Court of Appeals overturned the conviction, finding that testimony from women whose allegations were not part of the charges had prejudiced the trial; a retrial followed in 2025. In a separate California case, a Los Angeles jury convicted Weinstein in December 2022 of rape and other counts, and he was sentenced to 16 additional years.

Impact

The case is widely credited with transforming public conversation about workplace sexual harassment. The reporting won the Pulitzer Prize for Public Service. High-profile accusers included Ashley Judd, Rose McGowan, Gwyneth Paltrow, Angelina Jolie, Salma Hayek, and Lupita Nyong'o. The Weinstein Company filed for bankruptcy in 2018, and hundreds of civil claims were channelled through settlements.
