Mike Lazaridis

Mihal "Mike" Lazaridis (born 14 March 1961) is a Canadian businessman and inventor, best known as the founder and co-CEO of Research In Motion (RIM), which created the BlackBerry wireless mobile device. Born in Istanbul, Turkey, to Greek parents, he emigrated to Canada as a child and grew up in Windsor, Ontario.

Career

Lazaridis studied electrical engineering at the University of Waterloo, dropping out shortly before graduation in 1984 to found RIM after winning a contract from General Motors for industrial control displays. RIM became a pioneer of wireless data technology; its two-way pagers and, from 1999, BlackBerry smartphones defined mobile email in the 2000s.

He served as co-CEO alongside Jim Balsillie until January 2012, when the two stepped down as BlackBerry's market share collapsed under competition from the iPhone and Android. Lazaridis later left the board in 2013 and co-founded the investment firm Quantum Valley Investments.

Philanthropy and science

Lazaridis is a major benefactor of theoretical physics and quantum computing in Waterloo. He donated over $100 million to found the Perimeter Institute for Theoretical Physics (1999) and founded the Institute for Quantum Computing at the University of Waterloo (2002), as well as supporting the Quantum-Nano Centre. He is a Fellow of the Royal Society and an Officer of the Order of Canada.
