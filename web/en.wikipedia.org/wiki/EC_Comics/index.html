EC Comics

Covers of EC's horror titles of the early 1950s.

EC Comics, originally Educational Comics and later Entertaining Comics, was an American comic book publisher founded by Maxwell Gaines and run by his son William M. Gaines. It is best remembered for its highly influential horror, crime, science fiction and war comics of the early 1950s — above all Tales from the Crypt, The Vault of Horror and The Haunt of Fear — and for the satirical magazine Mad, its sole surviving publication.

History

Maxwell C. Gaines, a pioneer of the comic book format, founded Educational Comics in 1944, publishing titles such as Picture Stories from the Bible. After his death in a boating accident in 1947, his son William Gaines inherited the company and, with editor Al Feldstein, transformed it. Beginning in 1950, EC's "New Trend" titles — horror (Tales from the Crypt and its companions), crime (Crime SuspenStories), science fiction (Weird Science, Weird Fantasy) and realistic war stories edited by Harvey Kurtzman (Two-Fisted Tales, Frontline Combat) — set new standards for writing and art.

EC's stable of artists included Wally Wood, Jack Davis, Graham Ingels, Johnny Craig, Reed Crandall, Frank Frazetta, Joe Orlando and Al Williamson. Its horror stories were hosted by ghoulish "GhouLunatics" — the Crypt-Keeper, the Vault-Keeper and the Old Witch — and typically ended in ironic, grisly twists; the war and science fiction books often carried anti-war and anti-racist messages unusual for the period.

The comics code and decline

EC became the principal target of the 1950s campaign against comic books, associated with Fredric Wertham's Seduction of the Innocent (1954) and US Senate hearings on juvenile delinquency, at which Gaines testified. The industry responded with the self-censoring Comics Code Authority, whose rules — banning even the words "horror" and "terror" from titles — made EC's core line unpublishable. Gaines cancelled the New Trend titles in 1955 and converted Mad into a magazine format, which placed it outside the Code's remit. The remaining comic book line ("New Direction" titles and the short-lived "Picto-Fiction" magazines) folded by 1956.

Legacy

Mad continued for decades under Gaines and his successors. EC's 1950s comics have been repeatedly reprinted and are regarded as classics: they directly inspired the horror anthology tradition in comics and film, HBO's Tales from the Crypt series, and generations of creators from Stephen King to the underground comix movement. In 2024 the EC brand was revived for new horror and science fiction anthologies published by Oni Press.
