Megaflood

A megaflood is a flood event characterised by an exceptionally large discharge of water — typically with peak flows exceeding 1 million cubic metres per second — capable of dramatically reshaping landscapes on geological timescales. The term is most often applied to the catastrophic outburst floods produced at the end of the last ice age by the sudden failure of ice dams or the breaching of barriers holding back glacial lakes, though it can also describe immense palaeofloods inferred on Earth and on other planets, notably Mars.

Mechanisms

Megafloods are usually generated when a natural dam — commonly an ice sheet margin, a glacier, a landslide deposit or a moraine — fails suddenly, releasing a large proglacial lake in a matter of days or weeks. The resulting discharge can be orders of magnitude greater than that of the largest modern rivers: the Amazon averages roughly 200,000 m³/s, whereas the largest megafloods are estimated at 10–17 million m³/s.

Examples

Missoula floods

The Missoula floods swept across present-day Washington and Oregon repeatedly between about 18,000 and 15,000 years ago, when the Cordilleran ice sheet dammed the Clark Fork River to form Glacial Lake Missoula. Each failure of the ice dam released some 2,000 km³ of water, carving the Channeled Scablands, giant current ripples, and the coulees and cataracts of the Columbia Plateau. Geologist J Harlen Bretz controversially proposed the flood origin of the Scablands in the 1920s.

Altai flood

In the Altai Mountains of Siberia, outbursts from ice-dammed lakes in the Kuray and Chuya basins produced some of the largest known freshwater floods on Earth, with estimated peak discharges rivalling or exceeding those of the Missoula floods, leaving giant gravel bars and boulder deposits tens of metres high.

Other events

Other widely discussed megafloods include the draining of Glacial Lake Agassiz–Ojibway into the Hudson Bay around 8,200 years ago (implicated in the 8.2-kiloyear event), the Bonneville Flood in the western United States, the catastrophic refilling of the Mediterranean in the Zanclean flood, the possible refilling of the Black Sea, and outflow-channel floods on Mars.

Geomorphic evidence

Megafloods leave diagnostic landforms: scoured bedrock channels, anastomosing channel networks, giant current dunes (gravel ripples), pendant and eddy bars, slackwater deposits, and far-travelled erratic boulders. The recognition of such features underpins the field of palaeoflood hydrology.
