Valley of Geysers
Location: Kamchatka Peninsula, Russia
Discovered: 1941
Status: within Kronotsky Nature Reserve

The Valley of Geysers (Russian: Долина гейзеров) is a geyser field on the Kamchatka Peninsula in Russia's Far East, within the Kronotsky Nature Reserve. Stretching roughly six kilometres along the Geysernaya River gorge, it holds the second-largest concentration of geysers in the world — around ninety geysers and numerous hot springs — and is one of the Seven Wonders of Russia.

Discovery and features

The valley was discovered in April 1941 by the geologist Tatyana Ustinova and her guide Anisifor Krupenin, who found it while exploring the reserve by dog sled. Its largest geyser, Velikan ("Giant"), erupts with jets of water tens of metres high, while others such as Bolshoy ("Large One") perform on regular cycles. The basin's mud pots, fumaroles and multicoloured mineral terraces reflect its intense geothermal activity, set among Kamchatka's volcanoes.

The 2007 landslide

On 3 June 2007 a massive mudflow swept down the valley, burying roughly half of its geysers and damming the Geysernaya River to form a new lake. Several major geysers were thought lost, but in the following years the water level fell and a number of buried springs resumed activity; scientists continue to monitor the slow reconfiguration of the field.

Access

As part of a strict nature reserve, the valley can be visited only by helicopter tours from Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky under permit, with visitors confined to boardwalks to protect its fragile bacterial mats and mineral crusts. It forms part of the "Volcanoes of Kamchatka" UNESCO World Heritage Site.
