Derived category

In mathematics, specifically homological algebra, the derived category D(A) of an abelian category A is a construction of category theory introduced in order to give a natural home for the machinery of derived functors. The construction proceeds by first forming the category of chain complexes over A, passing to the homotopy category, and then localizing by inverting the quasi-isomorphisms — morphisms of complexes that induce isomorphisms on all cohomology groups.

Motivation

Derived functors such as Ext and Tor are traditionally computed by choosing injective or projective resolutions of individual objects. In the derived category, an object and any of its resolutions become isomorphic, so a derived functor can be computed uniformly on complexes. Two complexes connected by a quasi-isomorphism are treated as identical, which means that the derived category remembers an object only up to its cohomology and the extensions between its cohomology groups.

Construction

Given an abelian category A:

  • C(A) is the category of cochain complexes over A.
  • K(A), the homotopy category, identifies chain maps that are homotopic.
  • D(A) is obtained from K(A) by formally inverting all quasi-isomorphisms, a localization process due to Verdier.

The derived category is a triangulated category, with translation given by the shift of complexes and distinguished triangles arising from mapping cones.

History and applications

The construction is due to Jean-Louis Verdier, building on the work of Alexander Grothendieck in the early 1960s; Verdier's 1967 thesis developed the theory of triangulated categories for this purpose. Derived categories are now fundamental in algebraic geometry (notably in the study of derived categories of coherent sheaves), representation theory, and mathematical physics, where they appear in homological mirror symmetry.
