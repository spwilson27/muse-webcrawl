Chorazin (Greek: Χοραζίν) was an ancient village in the Galilee, two and a half miles north of Capernaum on a hill above the northern shore of the Sea of Galilee. It is known from the New Testament as one of the towns in which Jesus performed most of his mighty works, and which he rebuked for failing to repent.

Biblical references

Chorazin is mentioned in the gospels of Matthew (11:21) and Luke (10:13): "Woe to you, Chorazin! Woe to you, Bethsaida! For if the mighty works done in you had been done in Tyre and Sidon, they would have repented long ago."

Archaeology

The site, now a national park, preserves the remains of a town built largely of black basalt. The most notable structure is a synagogue of the 3rd–4th century AD, notable for a carved basalt seat known as the "Seat of Moses", with an Aramaic dedicatory inscription. Excavations have also uncovered residential quarters, ritual baths (mikvaot) and olive presses. The town appears to have been inhabited into the Byzantine and early Islamic periods.
