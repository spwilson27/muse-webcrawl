Bonanza Creek

Bonanza Creek is a watercourse in the Yukon Territory of Canada, a tributary of the Klondike River near Dawson City. It is famous as the site of the gold discovery in August 1896 that triggered the Klondike Gold Rush. Originally known as Rabbit Creek, it was renamed Bonanza — Spanish for "prosperity" — after the strike.

The discovery

On 16 August 1896, prospectors George Washington Carmack, his brother-in-law Skookum Jim (Keish), and Tagish Charlie (Dawson Charlie), guided by earlier finds in the region and a tip from Robert Henderson, found gold nuggets in the gravels of Rabbit Creek. Claims staked on Bonanza and the neighbouring Eldorado Creek proved extraordinarily rich; when news reached the outside world in 1897, roughly 100,000 people set out for the Klondike, of whom perhaps 30,000–40,000 arrived.

Mining history

The richest claims on Bonanza Creek were worked first by hand methods and later by large bucket-line dredges operated by companies such as the Yukon Consolidated Gold Corporation, which reworked the creek's gravels for decades. Discovery Claim, the site of the original find, is preserved as part of the Klondike National Historic Sites. Dredge No. 4, a huge wooden-hulled gold dredge that worked Bonanza Creek, is preserved near Dawson City.
