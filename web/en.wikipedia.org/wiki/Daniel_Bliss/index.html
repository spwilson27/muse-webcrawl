Daniel Bliss

Born: August 17, 1823, Georgia, Vermont, U.S.
Died: July 27, 1916 (aged 92), Beirut, Ottoman Empire
Occupation: Missionary, educator
Known for: First president of the American University of Beirut

Daniel Bliss (August 17, 1823 – July 27, 1916) was an American Congregational missionary who founded the Syrian Protestant College in Beirut in 1866 — the institution that became the American University of Beirut (AUB) — and served as its first president.

Life and work

Born in Vermont and educated at Amherst College and Andover Theological Seminary, Bliss sailed for Lebanon in 1855 with the American Board of Commissioners for Foreign Missions. After years of teaching and preaching, he raised funds in the United States and Britain for a college open to students of all religions. The Syrian Protestant College opened with 16 students in 1866; Bliss led it until 1902, overseeing construction of landmarks such as College Hall and the establishment of medical, pharmacy and nursing schools.

Legacy

Bliss insisted the college be non-sectarian in admissions and grounded its charter in the famous principle he voiced that a person may hold whatever belief he pleases so long as he acts rightly. The university's main gate area and Bliss Street in Beirut are named for him, and his son Howard Bliss succeeded him as president in 1902.
