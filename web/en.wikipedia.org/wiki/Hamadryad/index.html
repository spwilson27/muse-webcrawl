Hamadryad

A hamadryad (Greek: Ἁμαδρυάς, Hamadryás) is a Greek mythological being that lives in trees. She is a particular type of dryad, which in turn is a particular type of tree nymph. Hamadryads are born bonded to a particular tree; some believe that hamadryads are the actual tree, while others believe they are creatures living within the tree. If the tree dies, the hamadryad associated with it dies as well, and for that reason, dryads and the Greek gods punished any mortal who harmed trees.

Mythology

Unlike ordinary dryads, who could move freely through the forests, a hamadryad's life was inseparably bound to her tree — usually an oak. When the tree flourished, the hamadryad flourished; when it withered or was felled, she died with it. This bond made hamadryads fierce protectors of their trees.

The hamadryad Atlantia was the mother of the Hamadryades, eight nymphs said to have been born from the union of Oxylus and his sister Hamadryas. Their names included Aigeiros (black poplar), Ampelos (vine), Balanos (oak), Karya (walnut tree), Kraneia (dogwood), Morea (mulberry), Ptelea (elm), and Syke (fig tree) — each name echoing the tree species she embodied.

In later culture

The name has been reused in several fields. The hamadryad is also the common name of the king cobra (Ophiophagus hannah), whose former scientific name was Hamadryas hannah. In biology, Hamadryas is a genus of cracker butterflies found in the Americas.
