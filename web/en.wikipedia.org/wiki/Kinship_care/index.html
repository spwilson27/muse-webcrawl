Kinship care

Kinship care is the full-time care, nurturing and protection of a child by relatives, members of a tribe or clan, godparents, stepparents, or other adults with a close family-like relationship, when parents are unable to care for the child. It includes formal kinship foster care arranged by child welfare services and informal arrangements within families.

Research generally finds children in kinship care experience greater placement stability and maintain cultural and family connections, though kin carers—often grandparents—may receive less financial support and training than unrelated foster carers. Policies vary by country, with growing recognition and support programmes.
