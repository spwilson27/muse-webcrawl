Metroidvania is a subgenre of action-adventure video games focused on non-linear exploration and guided progression through a large, interconnected world map. Access to areas is gated by tools, weapons or abilities the player acquires over time, encouraging backtracking to previously visited regions. The term is a portmanteau of Nintendo's Metroid series and Konami's Castlevania series.

History

Metroid (1986) pioneered open-ended exploration in a side-scrolling world. Super Metroid (1994) refined the formula, while Castlevania: Symphony of the Night (1997), directed by Koji Igarashi, added RPG elements such as experience levels and equipment, cementing the genre template. The Game Boy Advance and Nintendo DS Castlevania titles continued the style.

Characteristics

Typical features include a single large map revealed progressively, save points, ability-gated shortcuts, optional collectibles rewarding thorough exploration, and boss encounters that grant progression items. The design emphasises player mastery of movement and memory of the environment.

Revival

Independent games revived the genre in the 2010s: Cave Story (2004) anticipated the wave, followed by Guacamelee! (2013), Ori and the Blind Forest (2015), Hollow Knight (2017), and Dead Cells (2018) among many others. The genre remains a staple of indie development.
