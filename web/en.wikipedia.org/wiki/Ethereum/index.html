Ethereum is a decentralized blockchain platform that supports smart contracts — programs that run exactly as written on a distributed network of computers. Its native cryptocurrency is ether (ETH). Ethereum was proposed in late 2013 by programmer Vitalik Buterin, funded through an online crowdsale in 2014, and launched on 30 July 2015. Other co-founders included Gavin Wood, who authored the technical Yellow Paper and designed the Solidity programming language, and Joseph Lubin, founder of ConsenSys.

History

A 2016 exploit of a smart-contract fund called The DAO led to a controversial hard fork: the main chain reversed the theft, while a minority continued the original chain as Ethereum Classic. On 15 September 2022, Ethereum completed "The Merge," replacing energy-intensive proof-of-work mining with proof of stake, cutting the network's energy consumption by an estimated 99.9%. Subsequent upgrades, including "Shapella" (2023, enabling staking withdrawals) and "Dencun" (2024, introducing proto-danksharding blobs that lowered layer-2 fees), continued the roadmap toward greater scalability.

Technology

The Ethereum Virtual Machine (EVM) executes smart contracts; computation is metered in "gas," paid in ether. Token standards such as ERC-20 (fungible tokens) and ERC-721 (non-fungible tokens) underpin a large ecosystem of decentralized finance (DeFi) applications, stablecoins, and decentralized autonomous organizations (DAOs). "Layer 2" rollups process transactions off the main chain and post data back to it for security.
