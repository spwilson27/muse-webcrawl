Besa (Albanian culture)

Besa
OriginAlbanian customary law (Kanun)
Meaning"Pledge of honour"; a sworn oath to keep one's word
Related conceptsHospitality (mikpritja), honour, truce in blood feuds

Besa is a central concept in Albanian culture, usually translated as "faith", "pledge", or "word of honour". It embodies the inviolable obligation to keep a promise or oath once given: a person who has given his besa is bound to fulfil it regardless of the cost. Codified in the Kanun, the traditional Albanian customary law attributed to Lekë Dukagjini, besa underpins the famed duties of hospitality and protection of the guest.

In the Kanun

Under the Kanun, besa serves several functions. It is the solemn oath by which agreements are guaranteed; it provides a truce (besa) suspending a blood feud (gjakmarrja) for an agreed period so that families can negotiate or attend to essential business; and it extends absolute protection to a guest — the saying "the house of the Albanian belongs to God and the guest" reflects the principle that a host who has given besa must defend his guest even at the cost of his own life.

Historical examples

The most widely known modern expression of besa was the sheltering of Jews in Albania during the Second World War: Albanian families — Muslim and Christian alike — hid Jewish refugees and local Jews, and Albania was one of the few European countries whose Jewish population was larger at the end of the war than at its beginning; rescuers later honoured by Yad Vashem commonly explained their actions by besa. Besa also figured in the 1990s reconciliation movement in Kosovo, when Anton Çetta's campaign persuaded thousands of families to forgive blood feuds in public ceremonies grounded in the concept.

Cultural significance

To "break besa" (me thye besën) is among the gravest dishonours in traditional Albanian society. The word has passed into modern usage as a byword for trustworthiness and has been adopted as a motto by Albanian institutions and sports teams.
