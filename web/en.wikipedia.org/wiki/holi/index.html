Holi

Holi is a popular and significant Hindu festival celebrated as the Festival of Colours, Love, and Spring. It celebrates the eternal and divine love of the deities Radha and Krishna, and also signifies the triumph of good over evil, commemorating the victory of Vishnu as Narasimha over Hiranyakashipu.

Observed byHindus; also Jains, Sikhs, and others
TypeReligious, spring festival
CelebrationsThrowing coloured powder and water, bonfires, feasting
DatePhalguna Purnima (usually March)

Celebrations

The festival begins on the evening of the full moon with Holika Dahan, a bonfire ritual symbolising the burning of evil. The following day, participants throw coloured powders and water at each other in public festivities, accompanied by music, dance, and traditional sweets such as gujiya.

Significance

Holi marks the arrival of spring and the end of winter, and in agricultural regions it is also a harvest celebration. Distinctive regional forms include Lathmar Holi in Barsana and the grand celebrations of Vrindavan and Mathura.
