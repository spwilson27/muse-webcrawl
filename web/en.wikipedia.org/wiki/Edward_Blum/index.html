Edward Blum

Edward Blum may refer to several people. This article concerns the American conservative legal activist Edward Jay Blum (born 1952), founder of organisations that have brought prominent legal challenges to affirmative action and voting-rights provisions in the United States.

Career and activism

Blum worked in finance before turning to legal activism. He founded the Project on Fair Representation, which supported and financed litigation against race-conscious policies. He was closely involved in cases including Fisher v. University of Texas, in which Abigail Fisher challenged the university's admissions programme, and in organising the plaintiffs and funding behind Students for Fair Admissions v. Harvard and Students for Fair Admissions v. University of North Carolina.

Students for Fair Admissions decisions

On June 29, 2023, the United States Supreme Court ruled in favour of Students for Fair Admissions, the group Blum founded, holding that the race-conscious admissions programmes at Harvard and the University of North Carolina violated the Equal Protection Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment. The decisions effectively ended affirmative action in American college admissions as it had been practised for decades.

Other litigation

Blum's organisations have also challenged provisions of the Voting Rights Act — he backed the plaintiffs in Shelby County v. Holder (2013) — and, through the American Alliance for Equal Rights, have sued law firms and other institutions over diversity fellowship programmes.
