Carménère is a wine grape variety originally planted in the Médoc region of Bordeaux, France, and now most closely associated with Chilean wine. Its name derives from carmin, the French word for crimson, describing the brilliant colour of its autumn foliage.

History

Carménère was one of the six original red grape varieties of Bordeaux, alongside Cabernet Sauvignon, Cabernet Franc, Merlot, Malbec and Petit Verdot. The phylloxera plague of the 1860s devastated European vineyards, and Carménère — difficult to graft and a poor, irregular yielder — was largely abandoned in France and believed extinct. Cuttings had, however, been taken to Chile in the 1850s, where the variety was grown for more than a century in the mistaken belief that it was Merlot. In 1994 the French ampelographer Jean-Michel Boursiquot identified the Chilean "Merlot" as Carménère, and DNA profiling later confirmed the identification and established its parentage.

Viticulture and wine

Carménère is a late-ripening variety that thrives in Chile's long, dry growing season, producing deeply coloured wines with soft tannins, red-fruit and spice flavours and, when under-ripe, a characteristic green, herbaceous note. Chile is by far its largest producer, with the Colchagua and Maipo valleys particularly noted; smaller plantings exist in Italy, China, Argentina and a revived handful in Bordeaux itself.
