Misconception

A misconception is a conclusion that is wrong because it is based on faulty thinking, facts or understanding. Lists of common misconceptions — beliefs widely held but contrary to the evidence — are a popular subject in education and reference works.

In education

Research in science education shows that learners arrive with intuitive preconceptions about physics, biology and other subjects that can resist ordinary instruction. Teaching methods such as conceptual change, prediction-and-demonstration and refutational texts aim to identify and correct these ideas.

Common examples

Frequently cited misconceptions include the beliefs that seasons are caused by Earth's changing distance from the Sun, that humans evolved from chimpanzees rather than sharing a common ancestor with them, and that the Great Wall of China is visible from the Moon with the naked eye.
