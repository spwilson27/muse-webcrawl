Glarus

Glarus (German: [ˈɡlaːrʊs]) is the capital of the canton of Glarus in Switzerland. The town is situated on the Linth River at the foot of the Glärnisch massif in the Glarus Alps, in the north-eastern part of the country. It had a population of roughly 12,000–13,000 following its merger in 2011 with the surrounding municipalities of Ennenda, Netstal and Riedern, which created the new municipality of Glarus as part of a canton-wide reform that reduced the canton's municipalities to three.

History

Glarus is first mentioned in the early 9th century. The valley came under the rule of Säckingen Abbey before passing to the Habsburgs, and joined the Old Swiss Confederacy in 1352. The town was largely destroyed by fire in 1861, after which it was rebuilt on a regular grid plan in the style common in North American cities — one of the few Swiss towns laid out in this way.

Glarus is noted for its Landsgemeinde, the annual open-air assembly of citizens that votes on cantonal matters by show of hands, held on the first Sunday in May. The canton was also an early centre of the Swiss textile industry, particularly cotton printing and embroidery in the 18th and 19th centuries.

Geography

The municipality lies in the Linth valley between high limestone peaks. The Klöntal valley and its lake, the Klöntalersee, lie to the west and are a popular recreation area. The town is a starting point for excursions into the Glarus Alps, including the Sardona Tectonic Arena, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
