French Tarot

French Tarot is played with a 78-card tarot pack.

French Tarot (French: Jeu de tarot) is a trick-taking strategy card game played by three to five players using a traditional 78-card tarot pack. It is one of the oldest games in the tarot family and remains the most popular card game in France after belote; the French Tarot Federation (Fédération Française de Tarot), founded in 1973, organises national championships.

The pack and play

The pack consists of four suits of 14 cards (including a cavalier between jack and queen), 21 numbered trump cards (atouts), and one special card, the Excuse. Three cards are especially important, the "bouts" or "oudlers": the 21 of trumps (le monde), the 1 of trumps (le petit), and the Excuse. One player, the taker (preneur), bids to win against the others with the help of a six-card reserve (the chien, or dog), and must capture a number of points from the bouts that depends on how many bouts they hold at the end — 56 points with none, 51 with one, 41 with two, and 36 with all three.

Scoring

Contracts range from the small (petite) and guard (garde) to garde sans and garde contre, with escalating multipliers; bonuses exist for winning the last trick with the petit and for announced slam (chelem). The four-player partnership version is the standard for tournament play.
