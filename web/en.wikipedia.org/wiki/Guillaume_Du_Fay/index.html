Guillaume Du Fay

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Guillaume Du Fay (also Dufay, Du Fayt; 5 August 1397 – 27 November 1474) was a French composer and music theorist of early Renaissance music, regarded as the central figure of the Burgundian School. Regarded by his contemporaries as the leading composer in Europe, he was celebrated in much the same way as Beethoven was in the 19th century.

Life

Du Fay was born, probably in Beersel near Brussels, the illegitimate son of a priest, and was educated as a choirboy at Cambrai Cathedral. He served Italian patrons including the Malatesta family and the papal chapel in Rome and Bologna, and spent much of the 1440s at the court of Savoy. From about 1450 he was based again at Cambrai, where visiting musicians sought his counsel and where he died in 1474.

Music

Some 200 compositions survive: cyclic masses (including Missa Se la face ay pale and the Missa L'Homme armé, an early setting based on the famous tune), single mass movements, Magnificats, hymns, more than 80 motets — among them the isorhythmic Nuper rosarum flores, written for the consecration of Florence's cathedral dome in 1436 — and scores of French chansons and Italian songs. His music fused English sweetness (the contenance angloise), French technique and Italian melody, and his use of fauxbourdon and paraphrase technique shaped the next century of polyphony.

Legacy

Ockeghem composed a lament on his death, and Tinctoris ranked him at the head of a new art of music. Du Fay's output marks the transition from medieval to Renaissance polyphony.
