Electric vehicle battery

An electric vehicle battery (EVB), also known as a traction battery, is a rechargeable battery used to power the electric motors of a battery electric vehicle (BEV) or hybrid electric vehicle. Modern EV batteries are almost universally lithium-ion based, assembled from thousands of cells grouped into modules and a pack with cooling, power electronics and a battery management system.

Characteristics

Typical passenger-car packs store 40–100 kWh, giving ranges of 250–600 km. Cell chemistries include nickel-manganese-cobalt (NMC) and nickel-cobalt-aluminium (NCA) oxides for high energy density, and lithium iron phosphate (LFP), cheaper and longer-lived, which gained large market share in the 2020s, especially in China. Pack voltages are usually 400 V, with 800 V architectures enabling faster charging.

Charging and lifespan

Batteries charge from AC home chargers over hours or DC fast chargers in tens of minutes. Capacity fades gradually with cycles and calendar age; manufacturers commonly warrant packs for eight years or 160,000 km to 70 percent of original capacity. Degraded EV batteries may be reused for stationary storage ("second life") before recycling recovers lithium, nickel and cobalt.

Cost

Pack prices fell from over $1,000 per kWh in 2010 to under $140 per kWh by the mid-2020s, driven by scale and chemistry shifts, making the battery roughly a third of an EV's cost rather than more than half.
