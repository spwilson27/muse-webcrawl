Earl of Bedford

Earl of Bedford is a title that has been created three times in the Peerage of England and is currently held by members of the Russell family. The current earldom was created in 1551 for John Russell, 1st Earl of Bedford; the family later also received the dukedom of Bedford in 1694.

Earl of Bedford
Creation: 1551 (third creation)
First holder: John Russell, 1st Earl
Family: Russell
Seat: Woburn Abbey

History

An earlier creation in 1138 for Hugh de Beaumont lapsed, and a 1469 creation for George Neville was forfeited. John Russell, a courtier of Henry VIII and Edward VI, received the earldom in 1551.

Dukedom

William Russell, 5th Earl, was created Duke of Bedford in 1694. Since then 'Earl of Bedford' is a subsidiary title, used as a courtesy title by the duke's eldest son, who is styled Marquess of Tavistock in practice through other titles.
