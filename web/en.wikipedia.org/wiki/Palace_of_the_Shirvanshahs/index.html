The Palace of the Shirvanshahs (Azerbaijani: Şirvanşahlar Sarayı) is a 15th-century palace built by the Shirvanshahs, the rulers of Shirvan, in Baku, Azerbaijan. Together with the Maiden Tower, it forms an ensemble of historic monuments in the Walled City of Baku (Icherisheher) inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List. It is described by UNESCO as "one of the pearls of Azerbaijan's architecture".

History

The palace was constructed after the Shirvanshah Ibrahim I moved his capital from Shemakha to Baku following a devastating earthquake. Building took place mainly in the 15th century under Ibrahim I and Khalilullah I. After the Safavid capture of Baku in 1501, the palace was partly repurposed; it later fell under Ottoman and then Russian imperial administration. Restoration work in the twentieth century returned the complex to something close to its original form, and it now functions as a museum.

Complex

The complex occupies the highest point of the Old City and comprises the main residential palace building, the Divankhana—an octagonal pavilion with an ornate carved portal, believed to have served as a court or mausoleum—the Shirvanshahs' burial vault (türbe) dated 1435–1436, built by Khalilullah I for his mother and son, the Shah Mosque (1441), the Mausoleum of Seyid Yahya Bakuvi, and the remains of the Keyqubad Mosque and palace baths (hammam).
