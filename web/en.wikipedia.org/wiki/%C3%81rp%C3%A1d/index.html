Árpád

Árpád (Hungarian pronunciation: [ˈaːrpaːd]; c. 845 – c. 907) was the head of the confederation of the Magyar tribes at the end of the 9th and beginning of the 10th centuries. Tradition credits him with leading the Hungarians during the Honfoglalás ("conquest of the homeland"), the settlement of the Magyars in the Carpathian Basin around 895.

Life

Árpád was the son of Álmos, the first head of the Magyar tribal federation. According to the Gesta Hungarorum, the seven Magyar chieftains elected Árpád their leader before crossing the Carpathian Mountains. He is regarded as the founder of the Árpád dynasty, whose members ruled the Kingdom of Hungary until 1301.

Legacy

Árpád is a national hero of Hungary; statues of him stand in Budapest's Heroes' Square and in towns across the country. The Árpád Bridge and Árpád dynasty bear his name.
