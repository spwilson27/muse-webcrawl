Nuristani languages

The Nuristani languages are a small group of languages spoken by the Nuristani people in Nuristan Province of northeastern Afghanistan and in adjacent valleys of Chitral in Pakistan. They form one of the three principal groups of the Indo-Iranian branch of the Indo-European language family, alongside the Indo-Aryan and Iranian groups — though their precise classification has been debated, with some scholars treating them as an aberrant branch of Indo-Aryan or as an independent third branch.


Distribution of the Nuristani languages in the Hindu Kush.

Languages

The group includes Kati (the largest, with tens of thousands of speakers), Waigali, Ashkun, Prasun (Wasi-wari), Tregami, and Zemiaki. The total number of speakers is on the order of 130,000. The languages are notable for having missed several sound changes that define both Indo-Aryan and Iranian, preserving archaic features that make them important for reconstructing Proto-Indo-Iranian.

History

The Nuristani languages developed in isolation in the high valleys of the Hindu Kush. Their speakers practised a distinctive polytheistic religion until the region — then called Kafiristan, "land of the unbelievers" — was conquered and forcibly converted to Islam by Amir Abdur Rahman Khan in 1895–96, after which it was renamed Nuristan, "land of light."
