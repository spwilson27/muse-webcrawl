Sierra Entertainment

Founded: 1979 (as On-Line Systems)
Founders: Ken and Roberta Williams
Industry: Video games
Defunct: 2008 (brand revived 2014)

Sierra Entertainment, Inc. was an American video game developer and publisher founded in 1979 by Ken and Roberta Williams. Based for much of its life in Oakhurst and later Bellevue, Sierra was a pioneer of the graphic adventure game and one of the most influential companies in early PC gaming.

History

Originally On-Line Systems, the company released Mystery House (1980), the first home computer adventure with graphics, written by Roberta Williams. Sierra's flagship series included King's Quest, Space Quest, Police Quest, Leisure Suit Larry, and Quest for Glory, built on its AGI and SCI engines. In the 1990s the company published titles from other studios, including Valve's Half-Life (1998).

Decline and revival

Sierra was sold to CUC International in 1996 and passed through a series of owners amid accounting scandal and restructuring; its development studios were closed by Vivendi in 2004 and the brand retired in 2008. Activision revived the Sierra name in 2014 as an indie publishing label.
