Teresa Teng (29 January 1953 – 8 May 1995) was a Taiwanese singer, actress, musician, and philanthropist. One of the most celebrated and influential vocalists in the history of Chinese-language popular music, she was famous throughout East and Southeast Asia for her clear, gentle voice and her repertoire of romantic ballads and folk songs sung in Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese, Indonesian, and English. Her best-known songs include "The Moon Represents My Heart" (Yueliang Daibiao Wo de Xin) and "Sweet Honey" (Tian Mi Mi).

Career

Born in Yunlin County, Taiwan, to a family from Hebei, Teng began singing as a child and won talent contests before signing with a record company as a teenager. She achieved stardom in Taiwan and Hong Kong in the early 1970s and then, from 1974, a major career in Japan, where she won the Japan Cable Award for new artists and scored hits such as "Tsugunai" (1984) and "Toki no Nagare ni Mi wo Makase" (1986), winning major prizes three years running.

Although her music was banned for a time in mainland China — where her surname Deng invited the quip that China listened to "Old Deng" (Deng Xiaoping) by day and "Little Deng" (Teng) by night — pirated tapes made her voice ubiquitous there, and she became a potent cultural symbol across the Chinese-speaking world. She performed for Taiwanese troops and at democracy-related events, notably singing in support of the Tiananmen Square protesters in Hong Kong in 1989.

Death and legacy

Teng died on 8 May 1995 in Chiang Mai, Thailand, from a severe asthma attack, aged 42. She was given a state funeral in Taiwan and buried at Chin Pao San cemetery in New Taipei, where her grave remains a site of pilgrimage for fans. She is routinely ranked among the greatest Chinese-language singers of all time, and her songs remain standards across Asia decades after her death.
