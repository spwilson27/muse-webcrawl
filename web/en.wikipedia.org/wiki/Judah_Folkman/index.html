Judah Folkman

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Dr. Judah Folkman]

Moses Judah Folkman (February 24, 1933 – January 14, 2008) was an American medical scientist best known for his research on tumor angiogenesis, the process by which tumors attract new blood vessels to nourish themselves and grow. He founded the field of angiogenesis research and proposed that blocking a tumor's blood supply could be a viable cancer therapy.

Early life and career

Born in Cleveland, Ohio, Folkman graduated from Ohio State University and Harvard Medical School. He served as surgeon-in-chief at Children's Hospital Boston for more than three decades and was a professor at Harvard Medical School.

Angiogenesis research

In a landmark 1971 paper in the New England Journal of Medicine, Folkman hypothesized that all solid tumors are angiogenesis-dependent. Initially met with skepticism, his theory was vindicated by the discovery of angiogenesis inhibitors such as angiostatin and endostatin in his laboratory in the 1990s, and later by anti-angiogenic drugs used clinically.
