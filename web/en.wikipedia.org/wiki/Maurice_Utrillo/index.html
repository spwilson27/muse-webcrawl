Maurice Utrillo
Born: December 26, 1883, Paris, France
Died: November 5, 1955, Dax, France
Known for: Paintings of Paris street scenes, especially Montmartre

Maurice Utrillo (born Maurice Valadon; December 26, 1883 – November 5, 1955) was a French painter of the School of Paris who specialized in cityscapes. Born in the Montmartre quarter of Paris, he was the son of the artist Suzanne Valadon. His paintings of the streets, churches and houses of Montmartre made him one of the most famous and commercially successful French painters of his time.

Life

Utrillo's paternity was uncertain; the Spanish art critic Miguel Utrillo acknowledged him and gave him his name. Troubled by alcoholism from a young age, he took up painting partly as therapy, encouraged by his mother. He was repeatedly institutionalized and spent later years under close supervision, particularly after his marriage to Lucie Pauwels in 1935.

Work

His early "white period" (roughly 1909–1914), characterized by heavily impastoed pale tones sometimes mixed with plaster or sand, is considered his finest. He often painted from postcards rather than en plein air. Later work became more colourful and formulaic as commercial demand grew.

Legacy

Utrillo's scenes of Montmartre — Sacré-Cœur, the Moulin de la Galette, quiet suburban streets — became emblematic images of old Paris, and his works are widely reproduced.
