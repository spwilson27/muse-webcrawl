Silage

Silage is fodder made by preserving green plant material through controlled anaerobic fermentation, a process called ensiling. Whole crops — most commonly maize (corn), grasses, sorghum, alfalfa and other legumes — are chopped, packed tightly to exclude air, and sealed in a silo, bunker, heap or wrapped bale. Lactic acid bacteria ferment the plants' sugars, lowering the pH to around 4 and "pickling" the material so it keeps for months or years as winter or dry-season feed for ruminant livestock. Partly wilted silage of higher dry-matter content is often called haylage or baleage when wrapped in plastic film.

Process

The crop is harvested at the stage balancing yield and digestibility — maize typically at one-third to one-half milk line of the kernel, grasses before or at ear emergence — and chopped to a short length to allow compaction. In the silo, plant respiration and aerobic microbes briefly consume the remaining oxygen; then lactic acid bacteria convert water-soluble carbohydrates mainly to lactic acid. If air penetrates, yeasts and moulds cause spoilage and heating; if the forage is too wet or low in sugars, clostridia may produce butyric acid and a foul, unpalatable product. Inoculants of selected Lactobacillus strains and, occasionally, molasses or acid additives are used to steer fermentation.

Storage

Traditional tower silos of concrete or steel have been largely supplemented by horizontal bunker silos covered with plastic sheeting and tyres, by silage "ag-bags", and by individually wrapped round bales that made silage-making practical on smaller farms. Well-made silage loses on the order of 10–20 per cent of its dry matter in storage and feeding; poor sealing greatly increases losses and the risk of mould toxins and Listeria.

History and use

Preserving fodder in pits is ancient, but modern ensiling spread after the French agriculturalist Auguste Goffart published his influential manual in 1877. Silage is now the principal conserved forage in intensive dairy and beef systems worldwide, allowing farmers to harvest at optimum quality regardless of the weather needed for haymaking. Silage effluent is a potent water pollutant and must be contained; the crop itself is also the main feedstock for anaerobic digestion biogas plants in some countries.
