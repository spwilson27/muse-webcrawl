Krupp

The Krupp family and its firm, Friedrich Krupp AG, formed a prominent 400-year-old German dynasty from Essen, famous for their production of steel, artillery, ammunition and other armaments. The family business, known in America as Krupp, was the premier weapons manufacturer for Germany in both world wars. The company originated in 1811, when Friedrich Krupp founded a cast-steel factory in Essen; under his son Alfred Krupp it grew into one of the largest industrial enterprises in Europe.

History

Alfred Krupp (1812–1887) expanded the firm through railway products, seamless railway tyres and, decisively, steel cannon, which he supplied to armies around the world; the company became synonymous with German heavy industry and its works at Essen employed tens of thousands. His son Friedrich Alfred Krupp continued the expansion; after his death in 1902 the firm passed through his daughter Bertha to her husband Gustav Krupp von Bohlen und Halbach.

During the First World War Krupp produced artillery including the famous "Big Bertha" howitzers, and during the Second World War it was a central part of the Nazi war economy under Gustav and then his son Alfried Krupp von Bohlen und Halbach, employing forced labour on a large scale. Alfried was convicted of crimes against humanity at the Krupp Trial at Nuremberg in 1948 (he was pardoned and his property restored in 1951). After the war the company diversified away from armaments under the leadership of Berthold Beitz.

In 1968 the firm was converted into a corporation and later merged with Hoesch (1992) and, in 1999, with Thyssen to form ThyssenKrupp, one of Germany's largest industrial groups, active in steel, elevators, plant engineering and materials. The Villa Hügel in Essen, the family's former residence, is now a museum.
