Truro (Cornish: Truru) is a cathedral city, county town and civil parish in Cornwall, England. It is Cornwall's only city and the centre for administration, leisure and retail in the county. Truro grew as an ancient tin-mining port and stannary town, and its heritage as a market town and inland port shaped its Georgian and Victorian centre.

History

The earliest settlement at Truro dates to Norman times, when a castle was built by Richard de Luci. By the 14th century Truro was an important port at the head of navigation on the river system formed by the Kenwyn and Allen, and one of Cornwall's stannary towns, where tin was assayed and taxed. Trade in tin and copper, woollen cloth and later china clay sustained the town; when mining declined in the 19th century, Truro reinvented itself as a genteel social and administrative centre — the "metropolis of Cornwall" — with fine Georgian terraces such as Lemon Street.

Cathedral

Truro Cathedral, begun in 1880 after the creation of the Diocese of Truro in 1876, is a Gothic Revival masterpiece designed by John Loughborough Pearson. Built on the site of the parish church of St Mary, it was among the first new Anglican cathedrals erected in Britain since the Reformation; its three spires dominate the city skyline, the central tower rising to 76 metres (250 ft).

Modern Truro

Today Truro is Cornwall's principal retail and cultural centre, home to the Royal Cornwall Museum, the Hall for Cornwall theatre, and the main campus of the county's further education provision. It is served by Truro station on the Cornish Main Line, with a branch line to Falmouth. The city also hosts the county's main hospital at Treliske.
