British humour
Region: United Kingdom
Themes: irony, satire, understatement, wordplay

British humour is the distinctive comedic tradition of the United Kingdom, commonly characterised by irony, sarcasm, understatement, satire, self-deprecation, wordplay and a fondness for the absurd. Its strong themes often include the class system, everyday social awkwardness and the weather.

Characteristics

Deadpan delivery and dry wit are hallmarks, as is the use of comedy to defuse embarrassment or adversity. British humour frequently turns on eccentric characters and situations, from music-hall and radio comedy to Monty Python's surrealism and the cringe comedy of The Office.

Media

Radio programmes such as The Goon Show, television sitcoms including Fawlty Towers and Blackadder, and satirical magazines like Private Eye have all shaped the tradition, which shares much with the humour of Ireland and Commonwealth countries.
