Hussein bin Talal (14 November 1935 – 7 February 1999) was King of Jordan from 1952 until his death in 1999. His 46-year reign, one of the longest in the modern Middle East, spanned the Cold War and several Arab–Israeli conflicts. He was a member of the Hashemite dynasty, which claims descent from the Prophet Muhammad.

Early life and accession

Hussein was educated at Victoria College in Alexandria and at Harrow School in England, and received military training at Sandhurst. He was with his grandfather, King Abdullah I, when the latter was assassinated in Jerusalem in 1951. After his father Talal abdicated on grounds of ill health in August 1952, Hussein was proclaimed king; he assumed full constitutional powers on reaching the age of eighteen (by the lunar calendar) in May 1953.

Reign

Hussein navigated Jordan between Arab nationalism, Western alliances and regional wars. In 1956 he dismissed Glubb Pasha, the British commander of the Arab Legion. Jordan lost the West Bank to Israel in the Six-Day War of 1967. In September 1970 ("Black September") Jordanian forces defeated the PLO in a civil conflict, expelling its fighters to Lebanon. Jordan did not join Egypt and Syria in the 1973 Yom Kippur War in earnest, and in 1988 Hussein formally relinquished Jordan's claim to the West Bank. In 1994 he signed the Israel–Jordan peace treaty, making Jordan the second Arab state to make peace with Israel. Domestically he combined periods of martial law with economic development and gradual political liberalisation, restoring parliamentary elections in 1989.

Death and succession

Hussein died of complications related to non-Hodgkin lymphoma on 7 February 1999. Shortly before his death he replaced his brother Hassan as crown prince with his eldest son, who succeeded him as Abdullah II. His funeral in Amman drew an exceptional gathering of world leaders, including former adversaries.
