Rent regulation

Rent regulation is a system of laws, administered by a court or public authority, that aims to ensure the affordability and quality of housing rentals. Rent regulation typically covers limits on the rent that landlords may charge ("rent control" in the strict sense), restrictions on eviction, and standards for maintenance. Systems vary widely between jurisdictions and over time.

Forms

First-generation rent controls freeze rents at a fixed level, as introduced in many cities during and after the two World Wars. Second-generation systems — often called rent stabilisation, as in New York — allow moderated, regulated increases and usually exempt new construction. Other regulatory tools include security-of-tenure rules, just-cause eviction requirements and minimum habitability standards.

Debate

Economists disagree on the effects of rent regulation. Supporters argue it protects tenants from displacement and preserves mixed-income neighbourhoods; critics argue it reduces the supply and quality of rental housing, discourages maintenance and new construction, and benefits incumbent tenants at the expense of newcomers. Empirical studies find mixed, context-dependent results.
