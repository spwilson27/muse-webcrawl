Diploma

TypeCertificate
Issued byEducational institutions
PurposeCertifying completion of a course of study

A diploma is a certificate or deed issued by an educational institution, such as a college or university, that testifies that the recipient has successfully completed a particular course of study. The word derives from the Greek diploma, meaning a folded paper or letter of recommendation.

History

In ancient Greece and Rome, diplomas were folded documents — originally metal or wooden tablets — granting privileges such as citizenship or travel rights. In education, diplomas came to be written on parchment or vellum (traditionally sheepskin, hence the expression "sheepskin" for a degree certificate) and later on paper.

Types

The term applies at many levels: a high school diploma marks completion of secondary education; college and university diplomas accompany associate, bachelor's, master's and doctoral degrees; and postgraduate and professional diplomas certify specialist training. In some countries, a "Diploma" is also the formal name of a qualification in its own right, such as the German Diplom or vocational diplomas in Australia and the United Kingdom.

Diploma mills

Unaccredited institutions that sell certificates without meaningful study are known as diploma mills; credentials from such sources are widely considered fraudulent.
