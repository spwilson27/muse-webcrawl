Flight training

PurposeAcquisition of knowledge and skills to pilot aircraft
ProvidersFlight schools, flying clubs, universities, military
First licencesStudent pilot, private pilot licence (PPL)

Flight training is the process by which a person acquires the knowledge and practical skills required to pilot an aircraft, progressing from a first instructional flight through solo flight to certification as a private, commercial, and eventually airline transport pilot. Instruction combines ground school (theory: aerodynamics, meteorology, navigation, regulations and human factors) with dual flight instruction and supervised solo practice.

Structure

A typical syllabus covers pre-flight preparation, basic manoeuvres (climbs, descents, turns, slow flight and stalls), takeoffs and landings in the traffic pattern, emergency procedures, cross-country navigation, and night and instrument flying at later stages. Students train in purpose-built light aircraft such as the Cessna 172 or Piper PA-28, and increasingly in flight simulators and flight training devices, which can be credited toward licence requirements. Progress is checked by stage checks and a final practical test (checkride) with an examiner.

Regulatory frameworks

Licensing standards derive from ICAO and are implemented nationally — by the FAA in the United States (Parts 61 and 141) and EASA in Europe. Minimum experience for a private pilot licence is typically 40–45 flight hours, though students commonly take longer. Military flight training follows its own longer, selective pipeline. Ab initio airline cadet programmes, including the multi-crew pilot licence (MPL), compress training toward type-specific airline operations.
