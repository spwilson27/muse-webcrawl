Per-unit system

In electrical power engineering, the per-unit system is a method of expressing system quantities as decimal fractions of a defined base value, rather than in absolute units such as volts, amperes and ohms. It simplifies calculations in power systems containing many transformers and widely differing voltage levels.

Definition

The per-unit value of a quantity is its ratio to a base value: per-unit = actual value ÷ base value. Typically, two base quantities are chosen — usually apparent power (for example 100 MVA) and voltage — and the base current and base impedance are derived from them. In per-unit terms, the impedance of a transformer is roughly the same whether referred to its primary or secondary side, eliminating the need to reflect impedances across each transformer in a network.

Advantages

Per-unit values for similar equipment fall in narrow, predictable ranges, making implausible numbers easy to spot. Manufacturers commonly specify generator and transformer impedances in per-unit on the machine's own rating. The system is standard in load-flow and short-circuit studies; its main drawback is that phase angles and some effects in unbalanced systems require care, and physical intuition in absolute units can be lost.
