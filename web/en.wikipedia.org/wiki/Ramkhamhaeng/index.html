Ramkhamhaeng

Ramkhamhaeng (Thai: รามคำแหง, c. 1237/1247 – 1298), also spelled Ram Khamhaeng, was the third king of the Phra Ruang dynasty, ruling the Sukhothai Kingdom in what is now Thailand from 1279 to 1298, a period regarded as the kingdom's golden age.

Reign

Ramkhamhaeng expanded Sukhothai's influence across much of the Malay Peninsula and into Laos and Burma through diplomacy and warfare. Tradition credits him with creating the Thai alphabet in 1283, adapting earlier scripts, and with promoting Theravada Buddhism as the kingdom's religion.

Ramkhamhaeng Inscription

The famous Ramkhamhaeng Inscription, dated 1292, describes a prosperous, benevolent kingdom where the king hung a bell at his palace gate that any subject could ring to petition for justice. The stele is regarded as the earliest surviving specimen of Thai writing, though its authenticity has been debated by some scholars.

Legacy

Ramkhamhaeng is revered in Thailand as a national hero; Ramkhamhaeng University in Bangkok and monuments at Sukhothai Historical Park bear his name.
