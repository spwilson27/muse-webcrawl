Saint Aloysius de Gonzaga (Italian: Luigi Gonzaga; 9 March 1568 – 21 June 1591) was an Italian aristocrat who became a member of the Society of Jesus (Jesuits). While still a student at the Roman College, he died while caring for the victims of a serious epidemic. He was beatified in 1605, and canonized in 1726. He is a patron saint of youth and of plague victims.

Early life

Aloysius was the eldest of seven children of Ferrante Gonzaga, Marquis of Castiglione, and Marta Tana di Santena. His father served Philip II of Spain and expected his firstborn son to become a soldier like himself. Aloysius received military training from an early age, but as a boy he developed a strong attraction to religious life, influenced by his mother and by the example of Cardinal Charles Borromeo, who gave him his first communion around 1580. He practised severe penances, rose at night for prayer, and took a private vow of chastity at about the age of nine.

Jesuit novitiate

Against his father's initial opposition, Aloysius renounced his inheritance in favour of his younger brother Rodolfo and entered the Jesuit novitiate in Rome in November 1585. He studied philosophy and theology at the Roman College under teachers including Robert Bellarmine, who became his spiritual director and would later request burial near him. Gonzaga's religious life was marked by strict obedience, humility, and an intense prayer life.

Death

In 1591 a plague epidemic swept Rome. The Jesuits opened a hospital for the victims, and Aloysius volunteered to nurse the sick, carrying patients to the hospital and washing and feeding them. He contracted the disease himself and died on 21 June 1591, during the octave of Corpus Christi, at the age of twenty-three. His letters, including devotional writings addressed to his mother, were published soon after his death.

Veneration

Gonzaga was beatified by Pope Paul V on 19 October 1605 and canonized by Pope Benedict XIII on 31 December 1726, together with Stanislaus Kostka. In 1729 Benedict XIII declared him patron of young students; in 1926 Pope Pius XI proclaimed him patron of all Christian youth. His feast day is 21 June. During modern epidemics he has also been invoked as a patron of plague victims and, more recently, of people suffering from AIDS and their caregivers. His relics are enshrined in the church of Sant'Ignazio in Rome, while his skull is preserved at Castiglione delle Stiviere, his birthplace.
