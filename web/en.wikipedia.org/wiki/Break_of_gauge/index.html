Break of gauge

A carriage undergoing bogie exchange at a break of gauge.

A break of gauge occurs when a line of one track gauge meets a line of a different gauge. Trains and rolling stock cannot run through, so freight and passengers must change, or vehicles must be adapted. Breaks of gauge are found where networks of different gauges meet, for example at the borders between the 1,435 mm standard gauge of most of Europe and the 1,520 mm broad gauge of the former Soviet Union, or within Australia, where three gauges historically coexisted.

Solutions

Several techniques reduce the delay at a break of gauge. Freight can be transshipped between wagons, or containers moved by crane. Passenger and freight vehicles may undergo bogie exchange, in which the car body is lifted and its bogies swapped. Variable-gauge axles, used by systems such as Spain's Talgo RD and the Spanish CAF designs, allow wheels to slide to a new spacing as a train passes slowly through a gauge changer. Dual-gauge track, carrying three or four rails, allows both gauges to share a section of line.

Examples

Notable breaks of gauge occur at the Poland–Ukraine and China–Mongolia borders, between France and Spain (largely eliminated for passengers by variable-gauge trains and new standard-gauge lines), and historically at many Australian state borders.
