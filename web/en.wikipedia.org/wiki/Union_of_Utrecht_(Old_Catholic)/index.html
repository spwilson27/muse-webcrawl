The Union of Utrecht of the Old Catholic Churches, most commonly referred to simply as the Union of Utrecht, is a federation of Old Catholic churches, national churches which separated from the Roman Catholic Church over matters of doctrine and authority, principally the dogma of papal infallibility proclaimed at the First Vatican Council in 1870. It should not be confused with the 1579 Union of Utrecht, the political treaty that founded the Dutch Republic.

History

The Union was established on 24 September 1889, when the Old Catholic bishops of the Netherlands, Germany and Switzerland signed the Declaration of Utrecht, together with a Convention and Regulations for their communion. The Dutch Old Catholic Church, centred on the Archbishop of Utrecht, traced its independence to disputes with Rome in the early 18th century, while the German and Swiss churches formed after Vatican I. Churches in Austria, Bohemia (later Czechoslovakia), Poland and elsewhere joined or affiliated during the 20th century.

Organisation and beliefs

Member churches govern themselves synodally with bishops, clergy and laity, while the International Bishops' Conference maintains the communion's unity. The Union accepts the dogmatic decisions of the first seven ecumenical councils, rejects papal infallibility and universal jurisdiction, permits a married priesthood, and in most member churches ordains women. Since the Bonn Agreement of 1931 the Union has been in full communion with the Church of England and the wider Anglican Communion.

Member churches

Members include the Old Catholic Church of the Netherlands, the Catholic Diocese of the Old Catholics in Germany, the Christian Catholic Church of Switzerland, the Old Catholic Church of Austria, the Old Catholic Church of the Czech Republic, and the Polish Catholic Church in Poland. Total membership is estimated at around 115,000.
