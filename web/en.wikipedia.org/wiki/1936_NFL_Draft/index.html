The 1936 NFL Draft was the first draft of college players held by the National Football League, conducted on 8 February 1936 at the Ritz-Carlton Hotel in Philadelphia. Proposed by commissioner Bert Bell to promote competitive balance, it allocated negotiating rights to players in reverse order of the previous season's standings.

First selection

The Philadelphia Eagles used the first overall pick on Jay Berwanger, the University of Chicago halfback who had won the inaugural Downtown Athletic Club Trophy (later the Heisman Trophy). Berwanger demanded a salary the Eagles would not meet, his rights were traded to the Chicago Bears, and he never played professional football, choosing a business career instead.

Legacy

Despite its modest beginnings — teams drafted largely from newspaper lists — the draft became the NFL's principal talent-distribution mechanism and a major sporting event in its own right.
