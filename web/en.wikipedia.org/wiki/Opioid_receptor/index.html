Opioid receptor

Opioid receptors are a group of inhibitory G protein-coupled receptors with opioids as ligands. The endogenous opioids are dynorphins, enkephalins, endorphins, endomorphins and nociceptin. The receptors are widely distributed in the brain, spinal cord, peripheral neurons and digestive tract, and mediate analgesia, euphoria, sedation and respiratory depression.

Mechanism

Activation of opioid receptors inhibits adenylyl cyclase, reducing cyclic AMP, opens inwardly rectifying potassium channels and closes voltage-gated calcium channels. The net effect is reduced neuronal excitability and decreased release of neurotransmitters such as substance P and glutamate in pain pathways.

History

The existence of specific opioid receptors was demonstrated by binding studies in 1973 by groups including Candace Pert and Solomon Snyder, Eric Simon, and Lars Terenius. The discovery prompted the search for the body's own opioids, leading to the identification of the enkephalins by John Hughes and Hans Kosterlitz in 1975. The receptors were cloned in the early 1990s, and crystal structures of all four were reported in 2012.
