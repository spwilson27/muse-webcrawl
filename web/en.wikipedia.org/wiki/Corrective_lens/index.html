A corrective lens is a transmissive optical device that is worn on or in front of the eye (or implanted within it) to correct refractive errors — myopia (short-sightedness), hyperopia (long-sightedness), astigmatism and presbyopia — by bending incoming light so that it focuses precisely on the retina. Corrective lenses are prescribed after refraction by an optometrist or ophthalmologist and are the most common treatment for visual impairment worldwide.

Types of correction

Myopia is corrected with a diverging (minus, concave) lens that moves the focal point back onto the retina; hyperopia with a converging (plus, convex) lens. Astigmatism, caused by an irregularly curved cornea or lens, requires a cylindrical (toric) component with power in one meridian. Presbyopia, the age-related loss of near focus, is corrected with a reading addition in bifocal, trifocal or progressive-addition lenses, which graduate power from distance at the top to near at the bottom.

Forms

Spectacle lenses are made of glass or, increasingly, plastics (CR-39, polycarbonate, high-index resins) that are lighter, thinner and safer; coatings add anti-reflection, scratch resistance, UV blocking and blue-light filtering. Contact lenses sit on the tear film of the cornea — soft hydrogel and silicone-hydrogel lenses dominate, with rigid gas-permeable lenses for high or irregular prescriptions — and require hygiene to avoid infection. Intraocular lenses are implanted surgically, most often replacing the natural lens after cataract extraction; multifocal and toric implants can reduce dependence on spectacles.

Prescriptions

A lens prescription specifies sphere (SPH), cylinder (CYL) and axis for each eye, plus a near addition and pupillary distance where relevant. Lens power is measured in dioptres, the reciprocal of focal length in metres. Common prescription ranges are −10 D to +6 D, though lenses far outside this are made.

History

Reading lenses appeared in Italy in the late 13th century; concave lenses for myopia followed in the 16th. Benjamin Franklin is credited with bifocals in the 1780s, and George Biddell Airy designed cylindrical lenses for astigmatism in the 1820s. The first wearable contact lenses (glass) date to 1888, plastic corneal lenses to the 1940s, and soft lenses, invented by Otto Wichterle, to 1971. Harold Ridley implanted the first intraocular lens in 1949.
