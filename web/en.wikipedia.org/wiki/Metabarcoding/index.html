Metabarcoding

Metabarcoding is the barcoding of DNA/RNA (or eDNA/eRNA) in a manner that allows for the simultaneous identification of many taxa within the same sample. The main difference between barcoding and metabarcoding is that metabarcoding does not focus on one specific organism, but determines species composition within a sample.

Method

A short, standardised gene region (a "barcode", such as 16S rRNA for bacteria or COI for animals) is amplified by PCR from a bulk or environmental sample and sequenced with high-throughput sequencing. Resulting sequences are compared against reference databases to identify the taxa present.

Applications

Metabarcoding is widely used in biodiversity assessment, diet analysis, environmental monitoring, and the detection of invasive or endangered species from soil, water, and faecal samples.
