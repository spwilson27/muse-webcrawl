Zagros Mountains

The Zagros Mountains are a major mountain range in western Asia, stretching about 1,600 km from northwestern Iran through northern Iraq and into southeastern Turkey. The range forms the western and southwestern boundary of the Iranian Plateau.

Zagros Mountains
Countries: Iran, Iraq, Turkey
Length: ~1,600 km
Highest peak: Mount Dena (4,409 m)
Geology: Fold and thrust belt

Geology

The Zagros formed by the collision of the Arabian and Eurasian plates, a process continuing today and causing frequent earthquakes. Its folded sedimentary rocks, including limestones, host major oil fields of Iran and Iraq in structural traps. Salt domes and glaciers also occur.

Ecology and history

Oak and pistachio steppe-forest once covered much of the range, supporting wildlife such as the Persian fallow deer and Zagros leopard. The region is central to early agriculture and goat domestication in the Fertile Crescent; sites like Shanidar Cave in Iraqi Zagros preserve Neanderthal remains.

People

The mountains are home to Kurds, Lurs, Bakhtiari, and other peoples, many traditionally nomadic pastoralists.
