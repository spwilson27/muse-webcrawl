Tibia

Tibia
Latin: tibia (shin bone)
Articulations: Femur (above), fibula, talus (below)
Identifiers: TA98 A02.5.06.001; FMA 24476
Part of: Lower leg

The tibia (plural tibiae or tibias), also known as the shinbone or shankbone, is the larger, stronger, and anterior of the two bones in the leg below the knee in vertebrates — the other being the fibula, which lies behind and to the outside of the tibia — and it connects the knee with the ankle. The tibia is the second-longest bone in the human body after the femur, and it bears most of the weight transmitted through the leg.

The tibia from the front; its sharp medial border forms the shin.

Structure

The upper end of the tibia expands into medial and lateral condyles that articulate with the femur at the knee joint; between them lies the intercondylar eminence, and on the front of the shaft the tibial tuberosity receives the insertion of the patellar ligament. The shaft is triangular in cross-section, with a sharp anterior border palpable beneath the skin as the shin. At the ankle, the tibia's medial malleolus forms the inner prominence of the ankle joint and, with the fibula's lateral malleolus, cradles the talus.

Clinical significance

Tibial fractures are among the most common long-bone fractures, often from high-energy trauma or sports injuries; because much of the bone lies just under the skin, open fractures and complications of healing are frequent. Medial tibial stress syndrome ("shin splints") is a common overuse injury in runners. Stress on the growth plate at the tibial tuberosity causes Osgood–Schlatter disease in adolescents. The tibia is also a common site for bone graft harvest and for the measurement of bone density.

In other animals

In many tetrapods the tibia and fibula are partially fused; in birds they form the tibiotarsus, fused with proximal tarsal bones. The name tibia derives from the Latin word for a flute or pipe, originally made from a hollow shin bone.
