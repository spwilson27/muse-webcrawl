Star Trek is an American science fiction television series created by Gene Roddenberry that follows the adventures of the starship USS Enterprise (NCC-1701) and its crew. It was first broadcast on NBC from September 8, 1966, to June 3, 1969. It is retrospectively known as Star Trek: The Original Series (TOS) to distinguish it from its sequels.

Premise

Set in the 23rd century, the series follows Captain James T. Kirk (William Shatner), the half-Vulcan first officer Spock (Leonard Nimoy), and chief medical officer Leonard "Bones" McCoy (DeForest Kelley) on a five-year mission "to explore strange new worlds, to seek out new life and new civilizations, to boldly go where no man has gone before." Episodes frequently used science fiction premises to comment on contemporary politics, war, and social issues.

History and legacy

Produced by Desilu Productions, the series struggled in the ratings and was cancelled after three seasons despite a fan letter-writing campaign that saved it once. In syndication, however, it became a cultural phenomenon, spawning one of the most successful media franchises in history: an animated series, numerous live-action sequels including The Next Generation, and thirteen feature films beginning with Star Trek: The Motion Picture (1979). Its multicultural cast, optimistic vision, and iconic characters and catchphrases have had a lasting influence on science fiction and popular culture.
