Soft computing

Soft computing is an umbrella term for a collection of computational techniques that tolerate imprecision, uncertainty, partial truth, and approximation in order to achieve tractable, robust, low-cost solutions to complex real-world problems. The term was popularised by Lotfi A. Zadeh, the founder of fuzzy logic, in the early 1990s.

Constituent methodologies

Soft computing is traditionally built from several complementary methodologies:

  • Fuzzy logic — reasoning with degrees of truth rather than strict Boolean values, enabling control systems and decision rules expressed in linguistic terms.
  • Neural networks — models that learn patterns from data, providing approximation and classification capabilities.
  • Evolutionary computation — genetic algorithms, evolution strategies, and genetic programming that search solution spaces by simulated natural selection.
  • Probabilistic reasoning — Bayesian networks and related methods for reasoning under uncertainty.
  • Swarm intelligence and other nature-inspired heuristics such as particle swarm optimisation and ant colony optimisation.

Contrast with hard computing

Conventional ("hard") computing demands precise models and exact solutions; many real problems — controlling a washing machine, recognising handwriting, steering a vehicle in traffic — resist such precision but respond well to approximate methods. Soft computing exploits this tolerance for imprecision, and its methods are often combined into hybrid systems, such as neuro-fuzzy controllers, that are more capable than any single technique alone.

Applications

Applications include consumer appliances (fuzzy rice cookers and washing machines), industrial process control, pattern recognition, data mining, robotics, image processing, and decision support systems.
