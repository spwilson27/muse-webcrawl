The Bishop of Ripon is a senior bishop in the Church of England. The title was first created in 1836, when Ripon became the first new diocese established in England since the Reformation, carved out of the Diocese of York and the Diocese of Chester to serve the growing industrial population of the West Riding of Yorkshire.

History

The Diocese of Ripon was founded in 1836 with Charles Longley as its first bishop; Longley later became Archbishop of Canterbury. The bishop's seat was Ripon Cathedral, a church with origins in the seventh century, when St Wilfrid built a stone church on the site.

In 2014 the dioceses of Ripon and Leeds, Bradford, and Wakefield were dissolved and combined into the new Diocese of Leeds. Within the new diocese, the title Bishop of Ripon was retained for one of the area bishops, responsible for the Ripon episcopal area in the north of the diocese, with a seat at Ripon Cathedral.

Role

As an area bishop in the Diocese of Leeds, the Bishop of Ripon shares in the ministry of the diocesan Bishop of Leeds, overseeing parishes, clergy, and mission in the Ripon area, which includes Ripon, Harrogate, and much of the Yorkshire Dales.
