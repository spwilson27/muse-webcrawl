Music producer (hip hop)

In hip hop, a music producer (also known as a beatmaker) is the person who creates the instrumental backing track — the "beat" — over which rappers perform. Unlike the traditional record producer, who oversees a recording session, the hip hop producer is typically the composer of the track itself, combining sampled recordings, drum machines, synthesisers, and live instrumentation.

A producer crafting a beat on an MPC-style sampler.

History

Hip hop production began with DJs in the Bronx in the 1970s. DJ Kool Herc pioneered the breakbeat technique, using two turntables to extend the percussive breaks of funk records; Grandmaster Flash refined turntable technique, and Afrika Bambaataa expanded the palette to electronic sounds. In the 1980s, producers such as Marley Marl pioneered sampling with drum machines and samplers, and Rick Rubin and Russell Simmons brought a stripped-down rock-influenced sound to Def Jam recordings.

The late 1980s "golden age" saw dense sample collage from producers like Prince Paul and the Bomb Squad (Public Enemy), followed by a legal turning point: the 1991 Grand Upright Music v. Warner Bros. ruling made uncleared sampling legally risky, pushing producers toward fewer, licensed samples and more original instrumentation. Dr. Dre's G-funk sound defined West Coast production in the 1990s, while J Dilla developed a celebrated off-kilter sampling style on the MPC3000.

Techniques

Central techniques include sampling (looping and "chopping" fragments of existing recordings), drum programming, bassline and melody construction, and arrangement. Classic hardware includes the E-mu SP-1200 and Akai MPC series samplers and the Roland TR-808 drum machine, whose booming bass drum became the foundation of trap music. Modern producers work mainly in digital audio workstations such as FL Studio, Ableton Live, and Pro Tools.

Producer as artist

Many producers became stars in their own right — among them Dr. Dre, Timbaland, the Neptunes (Pharrell Williams and Chad Hugo), Kanye West, J Dilla, Madlib, DJ Premier, Pete Rock, Metro Boomin, and Mike Will Made It. Producers often sign the tracks they make with an audio "tag". The role of executive producer and the rise of type beats sold online have further expanded the profession.
