Dietrich Mateschitz

Image: Dietrich Mateschitz

Dietrich Mateschitz (20 May 1944 – 22 October 2022) was an Austrian billionaire businessman. He was the co-founder and 49% owner of Red Bull GmbH, the energy drink company, and built an extensive portfolio of sports teams and media properties around the brand.

Early life

Mateschitz was born in Sankt Marein im Mürztal, Austria, to parents of Croatian and Slovenian ancestry. He studied marketing at the Vienna University of Economics and Business, famously taking ten years to complete his degree, and worked in marketing for Unilever and the toothpaste brand Blendax.

Red Bull

On a business trip to Thailand in 1982, Mateschitz discovered Krating Daeng, a local energy tonic, and found it cured his jet lag. He partnered with its creator, Chaleo Yoovidhya, and in 1984 they founded Red Bull GmbH, adapting the drink's formula and taste for Western markets. Red Bull was launched in Austria in 1987, creating the energy drink category, and grew into a global business selling billions of cans annually. Mateschitz's marketing emphasised extreme sports and aviation, including events such as the Red Bull Air Race and the Red Bull Stratos jump.

Sports and other ventures

Under Mateschitz, Red Bull acquired Formula One teams (Red Bull Racing and the sister team now known as Racing Bulls), football clubs including FC Red Bull Salzburg and RB Leipzig, and an ice hockey team, and established the Hangar-7 exhibition space in Salzburg. His private island, Laucala in Fiji, became a luxury resort.

Death and legacy

Mateschitz died on 22 October 2022, aged 78, after a long illness. At his death he was Austria's richest person, with an estimated net worth of around US$27 billion. His stake in Red Bull passed within the family.
