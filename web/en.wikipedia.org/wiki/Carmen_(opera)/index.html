Carmen (opera)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Carmen in a red dress, hand on hip, defying Don José outside the Seville cigarette factory as soldiers look on]

Carmen is an opera in four acts by the French composer Georges Bizet. The libretto was written by Henri Meilhac and Ludovic Halévy, based on the novella of the same name by Prosper Mérimée. The opera was first performed by the Opéra-Comique in Paris on 3 March 1875, where its breaking of conventions shocked and scandalised its first audiences.

In Seville, the soldier Don José abandons his career and his sweetheart Micaëla for the gypsy Carmen, whose 'Habanera' ('L'amour est un oiseau rebelle') and the toreador Escamillo's couplets are among opera's most famous numbers. When Carmen leaves him for Escamillo, José stabs her outside the bullring. Bizet died three months after the premiere, never knowing Carmen would become one of the most popular operas ever written.
