The Last Waltz

Directed by: Martin Scorsese
Concert: 25 November 1976
Venue: Winterland Ballroom, San Francisco
Released: 1978

The Last Waltz is a 1978 American concert film directed by Martin Scorsese documenting the farewell concert of the rock group The Band, held on Thanksgiving Day, 25 November 1976, at the Winterland Ballroom in San Francisco. It is widely regarded as one of the greatest concert films ever made.

The concert

After sixteen years on the road, The Band — Robbie Robertson, Levon Helm, Garth Hudson, Rick Danko, and Richard Manuel — chose to end their touring career with an elaborate final show. Some five thousand guests were served a Thanksgiving dinner before the performance. The Band was joined on stage by an extraordinary roster of guests, including Bob Dylan, Neil Young, Joni Mitchell, Van Morrison, Eric Clapton, Muddy Waters, Dr. John, Ringo Starr, Ronnie Wood, and Neil Diamond.

The film

Scorsese intercut the concert footage with staged studio performances and interviews in which the musicians reflect on life on the road. Highlights include "The Weight," "Up on Cripple Creek," a rousing "Mystery Train" with Paul Butterfield, and the finale, "I Shall Be Released," with the full company on stage. A triple-album soundtrack was released alongside the film.
