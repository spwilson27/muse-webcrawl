Super-G

Super giant slalom, or super-G, is a racing discipline of alpine skiing. Along with the faster downhill, it is one of the two "speed" events, in contrast to the "technical" events of giant slalom and slalom. Super-G courses have widely spaced gates like downhill but require more turning than downhill, and each skier makes a single run with no course inspection-based practice runs beyond a limited inspection.

History

The discipline was developed in the early 1980s as a compromise between downhill and giant slalom, promoted by figures such as Serge Lang, one of the founders of the World Cup. The first World Cup super-G races were held in December 1982 (men, at Val-d'Isère) and the discipline was added to the World Championships in 1987 and the Winter Olympics at Calgary in 1988, where Franck Piccard and Sigrid Wolf won the first Olympic gold medals.

Course and equipment

Courses must have a vertical drop of 400–600 metres for men and 350–500 metres for women at the World Cup level, with a minimum number of direction changes proportional to the drop. Speeds typically range from 80 to 110 km/h. FIS rules set minimum ski lengths and sidecut radii for super-G (longer and straighter than giant slalom skis, shorter than downhill skis) to balance speed and safety. Racers may inspect the course but cannot ski it beforehand, making super-G a test of improvisation and course memorization.

Successful super-G specialists include Hermann Maier, Aksel Lund Svindal, Kjetil Jansrud, Lindsey Vonn, Lara Gut-Behrami, and Federica Brignone, while Marco Odermatt has dominated the men's event in the 2020s.
