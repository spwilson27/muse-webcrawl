Isaiah (Hebrew: יְשַׁעְיָהוּ, Yəšaʿyāhū, "Yahweh is salvation") was an 8th-century BCE Israelite prophet who lived in the Kingdom of Judah. The Book of Isaiah, bearing his name, is the first of the Major Prophets of the Hebrew Bible and one of the most influential books of the Old Testament. Isaiah is regarded as a prophet in Judaism, Christianity and Islam, and his prophecies — particularly those concerning a coming Messiah and the "Suffering Servant" — occupy a central place in Christian theology.

Life

Isaiah received his call to prophecy in the year of King Uzziah's death (c. 742 BCE), described in Isaiah chapter 6 as a vision of God enthroned in the Temple, attended by seraphim crying "Holy, holy, holy". He prophesied during the reigns of Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah, a period when Judah was threatened first by the Syro-Ephraimite alliance and then by the rising power of Assyria. Isaiah counselled the kings of Judah to trust in God rather than in military alliances — famously advising Ahaz during the crisis of 734 BCE and announcing the sign of Immanuel (Isaiah 7:14).

During the Assyrian siege of Jerusalem under Sennacherib in 701 BCE, Isaiah supported Hezekiah and prophesied the city's deliverance, which the biblical account records. According to later Jewish tradition, Isaiah was martyred by being sawn in half under King Manasseh, an account echoed in the New Testament (Hebrews 11:37).

The book

Most critical scholars hold that the Book of Isaiah is not the work of a single author. Chapters 1–39 ("First Isaiah" or Proto-Isaiah) contain the authentic oracles of the 8th-century prophet; chapters 40–55 ("Deutero-Isaiah"), including the Servant Songs and the prophecy of return from exile under Cyrus, are dated to the Babylonian exile (6th century BCE); and chapters 56–66 ("Trito-Isaiah") to the period after the return. The complete Great Isaiah Scroll found at Qumran (c. 125 BCE) shows the book was already a unity in the Second Temple period.

Themes and influence

Isaiah's themes include the holiness of God, judgement on Judah and the nations, social justice ("learn to do good; seek justice, correct oppression"), the remnant that will be saved, and the hope of a messianic king from the line of David ("For unto us a child is born"). The book is quoted in the New Testament more than any other prophetic book, and Handel's Messiah draws heavily on its texts.
