Elections in Norway are held for the national parliament, the Storting, and for municipal and county councils. Norway is a constitutional monarchy and parliamentary democracy; governments are formed from the Storting majority and typically consist of coalitions or minority cabinets.

Parliamentary elections

The Storting has 169 members elected for fixed four-year terms — uniquely, it cannot be dissolved early. Of these, 150 are constituency seats allocated across Norway's counties and 19 are levelling seats distributed nationally to improve proportionality, with a 4% national threshold for levelling seats. Voters cast ballots for party lists and may alter the ranking of candidates to a limited degree. Elections are held on a Monday in September.

Local and other elections

Municipal and county council elections are held every four years, midway between parliamentary elections. Norway also holds elections to the Sámediggi, the Sami Parliament, representing the Indigenous Sámi people. Turnout is generally high by international standards, usually around 75–80% for parliamentary elections.

History

Norway introduced universal male suffrage in 1898 and full women's suffrage in 1913, among the earliest countries to do so; proportional representation replaced single-member districts in 1919–1921.
