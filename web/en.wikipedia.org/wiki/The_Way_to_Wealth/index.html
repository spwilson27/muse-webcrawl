The Way to Wealth

Author: Benjamin Franklin
Published: 1758 (as preface to Poor Richard's Almanack)
Also known as: Father Abraham's Speech

The Way to Wealth is an essay by Benjamin Franklin first published in 1758 as the preface to that year's edition of Poor Richard's Almanack. It is a collection of proverbs and advice on thrift, industry and frugality drawn from twenty-five years of the Almanack's sayings of "Poor Richard" Saunders, and is one of the most famous pieces of early American literature.

Content

The essay is framed as a speech by an elderly figure, "Father Abraham", who addresses a crowd waiting for an auction to begin. Quoting Poor Richard's aphorisms, he rebukes the people's complaints about hard times and taxes with a chain of maxims on industry ("Early to bed, and early to rise, makes a man healthy, wealthy, and wise"), frugality, debt avoidance ("Rather go to bed supperless than rise in debt") and pride of dress. The crowd approves the doctrine "as commonly sermons are approved" — and then, Franklin ironically notes, immediately opens the auction and buys extravagantly.

Reception

The preface, sometimes reprinted separately as Father Abraham's Speech, became enormously popular: it was translated into many languages and reprinted in scores of editions in America and Europe, shaping an enduring ideal of the self-made man and of Franklin's public image as the apostle of diligence and thrift. Max Weber later discussed "The Way to Wealth", together with Franklin's Advice to a Young Tradesman, in The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism as a classic expression of the spirit of capitalism.
