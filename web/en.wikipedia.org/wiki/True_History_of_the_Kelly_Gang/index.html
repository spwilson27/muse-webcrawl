True History of the Kelly Gang

True History of the Kelly Gang is a novel by Australian author Peter Carey, first published in 2000. Written in the voice of the bushranger Ned Kelly as a series of letters to his unborn daughter, it won the Booker Prize in 2001 and the Commonwealth Writers' Prize, making Carey one of only a few authors to win the Booker twice.

Style and structure

The novel adopts the idiosyncratic, semi-literate voice of Kelly's Jerilderie Letter, written with few commas and frequent colloquial asides, and is divided into thirteen parcels of manuscripts, framed by a brief note from a librarian. Carey's narrative presents Kelly as a victim of corrupt police and Anglo-Irish oppression in colonial Victoria, tracing his life from a childhood of poverty, through the Stringybark Creek killings and the bank robberies at Euroa and Jerilderie, to his capture at Glenrowan wearing his famous suit of armour and his execution in Melbourne in 1880.

Reception

Critics praised the novel's ventriloquism and its sympathetic re-imagining of an Australian folk legend, though some historians objected to its romanticization of Kelly. It is frequently ranked among the finest Australian novels.

Adaptation

The novel was adapted into the 2019 film True History of the Kelly Gang, directed by Justin Kurzel and starring George MacKay as Kelly, which premiered at the Toronto International Film Festival.
