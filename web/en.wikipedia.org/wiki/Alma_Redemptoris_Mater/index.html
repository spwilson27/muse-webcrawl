Alma Redemptoris Mater ("Loving Mother of the Redeemer") is a Marian antiphon, one of the four seasonal antiphons of the Liturgy of the Hours sung in honour of the Virgin Mary. It is appointed for the period from the first Sunday of Advent until the Feast of the Presentation on 2 February, and is traditionally sung at the close of Compline.

Text and origin

The hymn, beginning Alma Redemptoris Mater, quae pervia caeli porta manes ("Loving Mother of the Redeemer, who remains the open gate of heaven"), is of uncertain authorship; it has traditionally been attributed to the 11th-century monk Hermann of Reichenau, to whom the Salve Regina is also ascribed. It is mentioned in Geoffrey Chaucer's Prioress's Tale, where the young clergeon learns to sing the "Alma Redemptoris" — evidence of its familiarity in medieval England.

Musical settings

The antiphon exists in solemn and simple chant tones and has been set polyphonically by many Renaissance composers, including Josquin des Prez, Orlande de Lassus, Tomás Luis de Victoria, and Giovanni Pierluigi da Palestrina, as well as by later composers. Its chant melody remains in regular use in Catholic and some Anglican worship, and the hymn is a fixture of Advent and Christmastide services and recordings of Compline.

The four Marian antiphons

The seasonal cycle of Marian antiphons comprises Alma Redemptoris Mater (Advent to 2 February), Ave Regina Caelorum (Lent), Regina Caeli (Eastertide), and Salve Regina (after Pentecost until Advent).
