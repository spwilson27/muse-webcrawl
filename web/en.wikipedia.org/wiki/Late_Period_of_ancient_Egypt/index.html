Late Period of ancient Egypt

The Late Period of ancient Egypt refers to the last flowering of native Egyptian rule after the Third Intermediate Period, from the 26th Saite Dynasty in 664 BC until the conquest of Egypt by Alexander the Great in 332 BC. It is conventionally divided into the Saite period (26th Dynasty), a period of Persian rule known as the 27th Dynasty (525–404 BC), a final sequence of native dynasties (28th–30th), and a second Persian period (the so-called 31st Dynasty, 343–332 BC).

The Saite period (26th Dynasty)

Psamtik I (664–610 BC), a prince of Sais in the western Delta, reunified Egypt with the help of Greek and Carian mercenaries after the withdrawal of the Assyrians, founding the 26th Dynasty. His reign saw a great cultural renaissance characterised by "archaism" — the deliberate revival of Old and Middle Kingdom art, language, and religious texts. His successor Necho II is credited with commissioning the circumnavigation of Africa by Phoenician sailors and with beginning a canal from the Nile to the Red Sea. Apries and Amasis followed; Amasis (570–526 BC) cultivated close relations with the Greek world, including the trading colony of Naukratis in the Delta.

First Persian period (27th Dynasty)

In 525 BC the Persian king Cambyses II defeated Psamtik III at the Battle of Pelusium and Egypt became a satrapy of the Achaemenid Empire. Persian rule, lasting until 404 BC, was remembered hostilely in later Egyptian tradition, though the Persians administered Egypt through native institutions and Darius I completed the Nile–Red Sea canal and codified Egyptian law.

Last native dynasties (28th–30th)

Amyrtaeus of Sais led a successful revolt in 404 BC, founding the ephemeral 28th Dynasty. The 29th Dynasty, based at Mendes, and the 30th, founded by Nectanebo I of Sebennytos in 380 BC, fought repeated wars to keep the Persians out. Nectanebo II (360–343 BC), the last native pharaoh of Egypt, was a prolific temple builder but was defeated when Artaxerxes III reconquered Egypt in 343 BC.

Second Persian period and Alexander

The second Persian domination (343–332 BC) was brief and unpopular. In 332 BC Alexander the Great entered Egypt without a battle, was recognised as pharaoh, and founded Alexandria, ending the Late Period and beginning the Hellenistic age of Egypt.
