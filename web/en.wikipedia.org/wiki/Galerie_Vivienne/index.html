The Galerie Vivienne is one of the covered passages of Paris, France, located in the 2nd arrondissement. It is 176 metres (577 ft) long and 3 metres (9.8 ft) wide, running between the Rue des Petits-Champs, the Rue de la Banque and the Rue Vivienne, near the Palais-Royal. The gallery has been registered as a historical monument since 7 July 1974.

History

The passage was built in 1823 by Marchoux, president of the Chamber of Notaries, to designs by the architect François-Jean Delannoy. It was inaugurated in 1826 under the name Galerie Marchoux, but quickly renamed after the adjacent Rue Vivienne. Decorated with mosaics, paintings and sculptures celebrating wine and commerce, it initially housed luxury shops — a wine merchant, tailor, cobbler, printsellers and a bookshop — and was one of the most fashionable arcades in Paris, competing with the nearby Galerie Colbert.

The gallery declined with the rise of department stores and Haussmann-era changes, but was revived from the 1960s onward; it now hosts fashion boutiques, tea rooms, wine cellars and the celebrated Legrand wine shop, along with designer showrooms including that of Jean Paul Gaultier, whose first boutique opened there.

Description

Delannoy designed a neoclassical gallery paved with mosaics by the Italian craftsman Giandomenico Facchina, featuring allegories — anchors, cornucopias and the caduceus — and a rotunda crowned by a glass dome. A monumental staircase at No. 13 led to the former home of the adventurer Vidocq, the ex-convict who became chief of police.
