C4 carbon fixation

C4 carbon fixation is a photosynthetic pathway in which CO2 is first incorporated into a four-carbon compound in mesophyll cells and then released around Rubisco in bundle-sheath cells. It is an evolutionary adaptation that concentrates carbon dioxide and suppresses photorespiration.

Mechanism

The enzyme PEP carboxylase fixes CO2 into oxaloacetate, which is converted to malate or aspartate and transported to bundle-sheath cells, where decarboxylation releases CO2 for the Calvin cycle. This gives C4 plants high water- and nitrogen-use efficiency in hot, dry, high-light environments.

Occurrence

C4 photosynthesis has evolved independently dozens of times and occurs in about 3% of plant species, including economically vital crops such as maize, sugarcane and sorghum, yet C4 plants account for a large share of global primary production.
