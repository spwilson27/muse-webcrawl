UNDOF

The United Nations Disengagement Observer Force (UNDOF) is a United Nations peacekeeping force that has maintained the ceasefire between Israel and Syria on the Golan Heights since 1974. It was established by United Nations Security Council Resolution 350 on 31 May 1974, following the Yom Kippur War and the Agreement on Disengagement between Israeli and Syrian forces signed in Geneva that same day.

Mandate

UNDOF supervises the implementation of the disengagement agreement and the ceasefire between Israel and Syria. Its roughly 1,000–1,300 troops patrol an area of separation — a narrow buffer zone running about 75 kilometres along the Golan Heights — and carry out inspections of areas of limitation on both sides where the parties agreed to restrict forces and armaments. The force operates from Camp Faouar on the Syrian side and Camp Ziouani on the Israeli side, with headquarters functions historically also in Damascus.

History

For decades UNDOF operated in relative quiet, its six-monthly mandate renewals by the Security Council passing as routine. The Syrian civil war after 2011 brought fighting into the area of separation; peacekeepers were detained by armed groups on several occasions, and in 2014 the force temporarily abandoned many positions on the Syrian side after the abduction of Fijian peacekeepers and attacks by the al-Nusra Front. Contributing countries have included Austria, Canada, Poland, Fiji, Ireland, Nepal, Uruguay, and India, among others.

Role

Although UNDOF has no authority to prevent violations by force, its presence and reporting are widely credited with keeping the Israel–Syria front the quietest of Israel's borders for half a century. It works alongside the United Nations Truce Supervision Organization (UNTSO), whose military observers staff observation posts in the zone.
