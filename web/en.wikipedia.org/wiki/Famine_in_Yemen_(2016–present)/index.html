A famine has been ongoing in Yemen since 2016, arising from the Yemeni civil war that began in 2014–2015. The United Nations has repeatedly described Yemen as the world's worst humanitarian crisis, with millions of people facing severe food insecurity and the threat of starvation.

Background

Yemen was already the poorest country in the Arab world before the war, importing around 90% of its food. The conflict between the internationally recognised government (backed by a Saudi-led coalition) and the Houthi movement devastated the economy: the currency collapsed, public salaries went unpaid, and fuel and food prices soared. Restrictions on the port of Hudaydah, through which most food imports pass, along with airstrikes on ports, roads, farms, and markets, further choked supply.

Scale of the crisis

The UN estimated that by the late 2010s some 14 million people — around half the population — were at risk of famine conditions, and in 2020–2021 roughly 16 million were food insecure, with about 5 million one step away from famine. The crisis coincided with the largest cholera outbreak ever recorded, with more than two million suspected cases, and the near-collapse of the health system; the COVID-19 pandemic added further strain. The UN Development Programme estimated that by the end of 2021 the war had caused some 377,000 deaths, around 60% of them from indirect causes such as hunger and preventable disease, with children under five accounting for about 70% of those deaths.

Response

Humanitarian agencies including the World Food Programme have mounted enormous aid operations, but funding shortfalls repeatedly forced ration cuts. Save the Children estimated that some 85,000 children under five may have died from extreme hunger between 2015 and 2018. A UN-brokered truce in 2022 reduced fighting, though food insecurity remained severe and aid access precarious.
