MADS-box

The MADS-box is a conserved DNA sequence motif found in a family of transcription factor genes present in almost all eukaryotes. MADS-box proteins bind DNA at CArG-box sequences and regulate major developmental processes, most famously floral organ identity in flowering plants, but also muscle development in animals, mating-type control in fungi and many other pathways. The name is an acronym formed from the initial letters of the first four members discovered: MCM1 (yeast), AGAMOUS (Arabidopsis), DEFICIENS (snapdragon) and SRF (human serum response factor).

Structure and classification

The MADS domain is a highly conserved region of about 58 amino acids that mediates sequence-specific DNA binding, dimerisation and interaction with accessory factors. MADS-box genes fall into two lineages. Type I (SRF-like) genes predominate in animals and fungi, where they include human SRF and the MEF2 family. Type II (MEF2-like) proteins, the MIKC-type, characterise plants: in addition to the MADS (M) domain they carry Intervening (I), Keratin-like (K) and C-terminal (C) domains, the K domain forming a coiled-coil that mediates tetramerisation, allowing the "floral quartet" complexes to bind two DNA sites and loop the intervening DNA.

Function in plants

In flowering plants, MIKC MADS-box genes are the master regulators of flower development. The ABC(E) model explains how overlapping combinations of A, B, C and E class MADS-box genes (such as APETALA1, APETALA3/PISTILLATA, AGAMOUS and the SEPALLATA genes) specify sepals, petals, stamens and carpels. Other family members control flowering time (FLC, SOC1, SVP), vernalisation response, fruit development and ripening (the tomato RIN gene), root and ovule development, and responses to environmental signals. Whole-genome duplications expanded the family dramatically in plants — Arabidopsis thaliana has about 57 MADS-box genes.

Function in animals and fungi

In animals, SRF regulates immediate-early genes such as c-fos and, with myocardin-family coactivators, smooth and cardiac muscle differentiation; MEF2 factors control muscle and neuronal development. In yeast, Mcm1 controls mating type and the cell cycle, and Arg80-family proteins regulate arginine metabolism. Mutations in human MADS-box genes have been implicated in congenital heart defects and other developmental disorders.

Evolutionary significance

The MADS-box family is an ancient toolkit that predates the divergence of plants, animals and fungi. Because MADS-box genes pattern the flower — the defining structure of the angiosperms — their duplication and diversification are central to evolutionary developmental biology ("evo-devo") accounts of floral origins and diversity.
