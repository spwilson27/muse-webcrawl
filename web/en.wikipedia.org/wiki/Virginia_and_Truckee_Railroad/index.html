The Virginia and Truckee Railroad is a privately owned heritage railroad, headquartered in Virginia City, Nevada. Its private and publicly owned route is defined by Nevada Senate Bill 171 as a reconstructed historic route originally completed in 1872 between Reno and Carson City, Nevada. In 1872, the name Virginia & Truckee Railroad was supplanted by the Virginia and Truckee Railroad (V&T), but the company retained the same management as its predecessor.

History

The railroad was incorporated in 1868 to haul ore from the Comstock Lode in Virginia City to mills along the Carson River. The line from Virginia City to Carson City was completed in November 1869, and it was extended to Reno in 1872, connecting with the Central Pacific Railroad. At its peak the road operated frequent trains carrying silver ore, timber, and passengers.

Profits declined as mining output fell in the late nineteenth century. The railroad entered receivership in the 1930s and operated at a loss for years; the Virginia City branch was abandoned in 1939 and the remaining trackage was abandoned in 1950.

Revival

Portions of the line were rebuilt in the 1970s as a tourist railroad operating steam excursions between Virginia City and Gold Hill. The State of Nevada later funded reconstruction of the route from Gold Hill to Carson City, which reopened for passenger excursions in 2009.

Locomotives

The railroad is famous for its fleet of nineteenth-century steam locomotives, including the Reno (No. 11), the Inyo (No. 22), and the Dayton, several of which appeared in Hollywood films.
