Prima ballerina

Prima ballerina (Italian for "first [female] dancer") is the title given to the leading female dancer of a ballet company — the rank equivalent to principal dancer in modern terminology. The corresponding male title is primo ballerino, and the highest honour, granted only rarely, is prima ballerina assoluta.

History

The ranking system descends from the courts and opera houses of Italy and France and was codified in Imperial Russian ballet. Prima ballerinas dance the leading roles of the repertory, such as Odette/Odile in Swan Lake and Aurora in The Sleeping Beauty.

Prima ballerina assoluta

The title assoluta has been conferred on only a handful of dancers, beginning with Pierina Legnani in the 1890s and including Mathilde Kschessinska, Alicia Markova, Galina Ulanova, Margot Fonteyn, and Maya Plisetskaya, in recognition of exceptional artistry of an era-defining kind.
