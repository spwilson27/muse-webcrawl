Yue Lao

DomainMarriage, love, matchmaking
TraditionChinese folk religion, Taoism
SymbolsRed thread (hóngxiàn), book of marriages, staff
Literal name"Old Man of the Moon"

Yue Lao, literally the "Old Man of the Moon", is a deity in Chinese folk religion and Taoism who presides over love and marriage. He is famed for the red thread of fate: he ties an invisible red cord around the ankles (or little fingers) of those destined to marry, however far apart they may be.

Legend

The best-known tale comes from a Tang-dynasty story in which a traveller, Wei Gu, meets an old man reading a book by moonlight and carrying a bag of red cords. Told that his future wife is a poor vegetable-seller's infant daughter and that the thread cannot be severed, Wei tries in anger to have the child killed; years later he marries a young woman bearing a scar from that very attack, confirming the fated match.

Worship

Yue Lao is enshrined in matchmaking temples across China, Taiwan and Southeast Asia — notably at Xiahai City God Temple in Taipei — where single people pray for a spouse and offer thanks after marrying. The red-thread motif remains a widespread metaphor for destined love in Chinese culture.
