Chondroblast

A chondroblast is a cell which originates from a mesenchymal stem cell and forms chondrocytes, commonly known as cartilage cells. Chondroblasts are the immature, actively dividing and secreting cells of cartilage; when a chondroblast becomes surrounded by the extracellular matrix it has produced and its activity declines, it is termed a chondrocyte, residing within a space called a lacuna.

Development

Chondroblasts arise during chondrogenesis, the process by which cartilage is formed from condensed mesenchyme. Transcription factors such as SOX9 drive mesenchymal cells to commit to the chondroblast lineage. The cells secrete the components of cartilage matrix — principally type II collagen and proteoglycans such as aggrecan — which give cartilage its resilient, gel-like properties.

Location and function

In growing cartilage, chondroblasts are found in the inner layer of the perichondrium, the dense connective tissue sheath surrounding most cartilage. Appositional growth occurs as perichondrial cells differentiate into chondroblasts and lay down new matrix at the surface, while interstitial growth results from division of chondrocytes within the matrix. In endochondral ossification, chondroblasts and chondrocytes of the growth plate are progressively replaced by bone.

Clinical relevance

Abnormal proliferation of chondroblasts gives rise to benign cartilage tumours such as chondroblastoma, a rare tumour typically arising in the epiphyses of long bones in adolescents. Chondroblast biology is also important in tissue engineering efforts to repair articular cartilage, which has limited intrinsic healing capacity.
