Sweet chestnut

Scientific nameCastanea sativa
FamilyFagaceae (beech family)
Native rangeSouthern Europe and Asia Minor
Heightup to 35 m

The sweet chestnut (Castanea sativa), also called the Spanish chestnut, is a species of deciduous tree in the beech family, native to southern Europe and Asia Minor and widely cultivated throughout the temperate world for its edible nuts.

Description

Sweet chestnuts are large, long-lived trees, commonly reaching 20–35 metres, with glossy, oblong, toothed leaves. Male flowers appear in long pale catkins in early summer; the nuts ripen in October inside very spiny green cupules, usually three to a husk. Old trees develop a striking spirally fissured bark and can live for many centuries — the Hundred-Horse Chestnut on Mount Etna in Sicily is estimated to be 2,000 to 4,000 years old.

Uses

The nuts have been a staple food since antiquity, eaten roasted or ground into flour in mountainous regions such as Corsica, Tuscany and the Cévennes where grain grows poorly. They feature in marrons glacés, chestnut purée and festive roasts. The durable, tannin-rich timber is used for fencing, joinery and coppice poles. The species must not be confused with the unrelated and toxic horse chestnut (Aesculus hippocastanum).
