The River Lee (Irish: An Laoi) is a river in County Cork, Ireland. It rises in the Shehy Mountains near Gougane Barra and flows eastwards for about 90 km (56 mi) through the city of Cork, entering the sea at Cork Harbour.

Course

From its source in the lakes of Gougane Barra, the Lee flows east through the Gearagh, an ancient submerged oak forest and nature reserve, then through the reservoirs created by the Inniscarra dam, where a hydroelectric power station was built by the ESB in the 1950s, flooding the valley and forming Inniscarra Lake. Below the dam it passes Ballincollig and enters Cork city, where it divides into northern and southern channels that enclose the city centre island on which much of Cork stands, before reuniting and flowing into Lough Mahon and Cork Harbour at Blackrock.

The city river

Cork's identity is bound up with the Lee: the city was founded on marshy islands between its channels, its name derives from corcach, "marsh", and many of its quays and bridges — including St Patrick's Bridge and Brian Boru Bridge — cross the two channels. The river is tidal through the city. Cork suffered severe flooding from the Lee in November 2009, when releases from Inniscarra dam during storms combined with high tides to inundate the city centre; the Lower Lee flood-relief scheme has since been developed to protect the city.

Angling and ecology

The Lee is known for salmon and brown trout fishing above and below the reservoirs, and its estuary around Lough Mahon supports significant birdlife.
