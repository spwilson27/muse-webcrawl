Five Megillot

The Five Megillot or Five Scrolls (Hebrew: Hamesh Megillot) are parts of the Ketuvim ("Writings"), the third major section of the Tanakh (Hebrew Bible). The five scrolls are the Song of Songs, the Book of Ruth, the Book of Lamentations, Ecclesiastes, and the Book of Esther.

The scrolls and their festivals

Each of the five scrolls is traditionally read publicly in the synagogue at a particular point in the Jewish liturgical year:

  • Song of Songs — read on Passover (Pesach), because of its themes of love and spring, interpreted allegorically as God's love for Israel.
  • Book of Ruth — read on Shavuot, the festival of the wheat harvest, matching the book's harvest setting and its theme of accepting the Torah.
  • Book of Lamentations — read on Tisha B'Av, the fast commemorating the destruction of the Temple in Jerusalem.
  • Ecclesiastes — read on Sukkot, the autumn festival.
  • Book of Esther — read on Purim, the festival that the book itself describes and institutes.

Arrangement

In printed Hebrew Bibles the scrolls are grouped together within the Ketuvim, often in the order of the festivals through the year. The Book of Esther is the scroll most commonly meant by the unqualified term "the Megillah". Although all five are called megillot ("scrolls"), in practice only Esther is still regularly produced as a handwritten parchment scroll for liturgical reading in most communities, with the others usually read from printed books.
