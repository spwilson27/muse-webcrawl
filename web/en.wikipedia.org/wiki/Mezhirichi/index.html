Mezhirichi

Mezhirichi (Ukrainian: Межиріч; also spelled Mezhyrich or Mezhirich) is an Upper Palaeolithic archaeological site near the village of the same name in Cherkasy Oblast, central Ukraine, between the Ros and Rosava rivers near Kaniv. Dating to roughly 15,000 years ago, it is famous for its dwellings constructed from the bones of woolly mammoths, among the oldest known substantial shelters built by humans.

Discovery and excavation

The site was discovered in 1965 when a local farmer digging a cellar struck a mammoth jawbone set upright in the soil. Excavations led by the zoologist and palaeontologist Ivan Pidoplichko revealed four dwelling complexes built largely of mammoth bones and tusks.

The dwellings

Each dwelling used the skulls, jaws, long bones and tusks of many mammoths — one structure incorporated bones from an estimated 95 or more animals — stacked to form circular walls that were probably covered with hides, with a hearth inside. Hearths, pits and work areas around the houses yielded flint tools, ornaments of amber and shell, and one of the earliest known maps, engraved on a fragment of mammoth ivory, which appears to depict the settlement itself. A mammoth skull decorated with red ochre patterns was also found. One dwelling has been reconstructed and displayed at the National Museum of Natural History in Kyiv.

Significance

Mezhirichi belongs to the Epigravettian culture of the East European Plain. Comparable mammoth-bone settlements are known from sites such as Mezin and Yudinovo. Whether the bones came primarily from hunting or from scavenging mammoth carcasses in a "bone bed" remains debated, but the scale of construction shows complex, planned architecture among Ice Age hunter-gatherers.
