Pythagorean means


A geometric construction of the three Pythagorean means

In mathematics, the three classical Pythagorean means are the arithmetic mean (AM), the geometric mean (GM), and the harmonic mean (HM). They were studied by the Pythagoreans and later generations of Greek mathematicians, and appear in Euclid's Elements. For positive numbers they satisfy the inequality:

HM ≤ GM ≤ AM

with equality only when all the numbers are equal.

Definitions

  • Arithmetic mean of x1, …, xn: (x1 + … + xn) / n
  • Geometric mean: the n-th root of the product x1 · … · xn
  • Harmonic mean: n / (1/x1 + … + 1/xn), the reciprocal of the arithmetic mean of the reciprocals

Properties

For two numbers a and b, the means are related by GM² = AM × HM. A classical geometric construction using a semicircle on diameter a + b yields all three means as line segments: the radius gives the arithmetic mean, a perpendicular chord gives the geometric mean, and a projection within it gives the harmonic mean.

Uses

The harmonic mean is appropriate for averaging rates and ratios, such as average speed over equal distances or the equivalent resistance of parallel resistors. The geometric mean is used for growth rates and proportional change, as in finance and in the averaging of indices.
