Caroline Herschel

Caroline Lucretia Herschel (16 March 1750 – 9 January 1848) was a German astronomer in England, younger sister and collaborator of William Herschel. She was the first woman to discover a comet, the first woman in Britain to hold a salaried scientific post, and an early fellow recognised by the Astronomical Society.

Life

Born in Hanover, Caroline moved to Bath, England in 1772 to train as a singer and assist her brother William. As his astronomy took over, she became his indispensable assistant, grinding mirrors, recording observations and reducing data.

Discoveries

Using her own small telescopes, she discovered eight comets (1786–1797), including periodic comet 35P/Herschel–Rigollet, and discovered or catalogued many nebulae and star clusters. In 1787 George III granted her a salary of £50 a year as William's assistant — the first salaried scientific position for a woman in Britain. Her catalogue of nebulae underpinned the New General Catalogue (NGC).

Honours

The Astronomical Society awarded her its Gold Medal in 1828 and elected her an honorary member in 1835 (with Mary Somerville, among the first women so honoured). She returned to Hanover after William's death and died aged 97. A lunar crater and asteroid 281 Lucretia bear her name. See William Herschel.
