Jacobellis v. Ohio

Jacobellis v. Ohio, 378 U.S. 184 (1964), was a United States Supreme Court decision concerning obscenity and the First Amendment. The Court reversed the conviction of Nico Jacobellis, an Ohio theatre manager fined for showing the French film Les Amants (The Lovers).

The case

Jacobellis was convicted under an Ohio law banning the possession and exhibition of obscene films. A plurality of the Court held that the film was not obscene under the test of Roth v. United States, which required material to be "utterly without redeeming social importance," and stressed that standards for obscenity must be national rather than local.

"I know it when I see it"

The case is famous for Justice Potter Stewart's concurrence: unable to define hard-core pornography, he wrote, "I know it when I see it, and the motion picture involved in this case is not that." The phrase became the most quoted line in American obscenity law, and the fractured decision left the doctrine unsettled until Miller v. California (1973) adopted a new test.
