William Aspdin (23 September 1815 – 11 April 1864) was an English cement manufacturer, and a pioneer of the Portland cement industry. He was the second son of Joseph Aspdin, who obtained the first patent for Portland cement in 1824. William Aspdin is often credited with producing the first truly modern Portland cement by firing his father's mixture at higher temperatures than the original patent process used.

Early life and career

Aspdin was born in Leeds and joined his father's cement business as a young man. In 1841 he left the family firm after a disagreement and established his own works at Rotherhithe, London. There he experimented with the proportions and firing temperature of the limestone-and-clay mixture. By burning the raw materials to the point of clinkering, he produced a stronger and more reliable cement than the lightly burned product made under his father's 1824 patent.

His material was used in notable mid-Victorian construction, and samples of his cement performed well in comparative tests. Business difficulties, however, followed him throughout his career: he moved between works in London and the Medway area, and later crossed to the continent.

Later years

In his final years Aspdin worked in Germany, managing a cement works at Lägerdorf near Itzehoe in Holstein, where he died in 1864. Historians of cement technology continue to debate the respective contributions of father and son; many conclude that Joseph named and patented "Portland cement", while William's high-temperature clinkering process produced the material recognisably ancestral to the modern product.
