Leonard Peltier (born September 12, 1944) is a Native American activist and enrolled member of the Turtle Mountain Chippewa, of Lakota and Dakota descent. A member of the American Indian Movement (AIM), he was convicted in 1977 of the first-degree murders of two FBI agents, Jack Williams and Ronald Coler, who were killed in a shootout on the Pine Ridge Indian Reservation in South Dakota on 26 June 1975. He served 49 years in federal prison. His conviction became one of the most debated in modern American history: supporters, including Amnesty International, maintained his trial was unfair, while the FBI and the agents' families insisted on his guilt. On 20 January 2025, his last day in office, President Joe Biden commuted Peltier's sentence to home confinement, and Peltier was released from prison in February 2025.

Background and the Pine Ridge shootout

Peltier was raised largely by his grandparents on the Turtle Mountain Reservation in North Dakota and, like many Native children of his generation, was sent to a government boarding school. As an adult in Seattle he co-owned an auto body shop and became involved in Native rights activism before joining AIM. In the mid-1970s, Pine Ridge was in turmoil following the 1973 Wounded Knee occupation, with violent conflict between supporters of tribal chairman Dick Wilson and AIM sympathisers. On 26 June 1975, FBI agents Williams and Coler entered the Jumping Bull compound searching for a robbery suspect. A firefight erupted in which both agents and AIM member Joe Stuntz were killed; the agents were finished off at close range after being wounded. AIM members Darrelle Butler and Robert Robideau were acquitted of the murders on self-defence grounds in a separate trial.

Arrest, trial, and appeals

Peltier fled to Canada, was arrested by the Royal Canadian Mounted Police in February 1976, and was extradited to the United States on the basis of affidavits from Myrtle Poor Bear, who later recanted, saying the FBI had coerced her. Tried in Fargo, North Dakota, in 1977 — unlike Butler and Robideau, he was not permitted to present a self-defence case, and the prosecution argued he was the man who shot the wounded agents — Peltier was convicted and sentenced to two consecutive life terms. Decades of appeals followed. New ballistics evidence and documents obtained under the Freedom of Information Act raised questions about the prosecution's case, but the convictions were upheld, and parole was repeatedly denied. Prominent figures including Nelson Mandela, Archbishop Desmond Tutu, the Dalai Lama, and several former federal officials campaigned for clemency; Pope Francis's representatives also raised the case. The lead prosecutor, former US Attorney James Reynolds, eventually wrote in support of commutation.

Commutation and release

Citing Peltier's age, ill health (including diabetes and an aortic aneurysm), and the near half-century served, President Biden commuted his sentence on 20 January 2025. Peltier was released from the Coleman federal prison in Florida on 18 February 2025 and returned to the Turtle Mountain Reservation to serve the remainder of his sentence in home confinement. The commutation did not pardon him or overturn the conviction, a distinction criticised both by supporters who wanted a full pardon and by FBI representatives who opposed any release.
