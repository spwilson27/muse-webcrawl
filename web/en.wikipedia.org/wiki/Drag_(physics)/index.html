Drag (physics)

In fluid dynamics, drag, sometimes referred to as fluid resistance, is a force acting opposite to the direction of motion of any object moving with respect to a surrounding fluid. This can exist between two fluid layers, two solid surfaces, or between a fluid and a solid surface. Drag is generally caused by the difference in velocity between the object and the fluid; unlike other resistive forces such as dry friction, drag forces depend on velocity.

Drag equation

The drag force on an object moving through a fluid is commonly modelled by the drag equation:

FD = ½ · ρ · v² · CD · A

where FD is the drag force, ρ the fluid density, v the velocity relative to the fluid, CD the drag coefficient, and A a reference area. Drag thus grows with the square of velocity, making it dominant at high speeds — for instance in aerodynamics, where it limits the top speed and fuel economy of aircraft and vehicles.

Types of drag

  • Parasitic drag, comprising skin friction drag (viscous friction at the surface) and form drag (pressure differences from flow separation).
  • Induced drag, generated as a by-product of lift on finite wings.
  • Wave drag, arising from shock waves at transonic and supersonic speeds.
  • Ram drag and interference drag in aerospace usage.

Reynolds number

Drag behaviour depends strongly on the Reynolds number: flow around a sphere changes from laminar to turbulent, producing a sudden drop in drag coefficient known as the drag crisis. Streamlined shapes minimise form drag, which is why teardrop profiles are used for cars, aircraft, and ship hulls.
