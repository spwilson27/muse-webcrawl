Pipeline pigging

An inspection pig at a launcher station

Pigging is the practice in pipeline operations of inserting a device known as a pig into a large-diameter pipeline and travelling it through the line, propelled by the pressure of the product flow, without stopping that flow. Pigs are used to clean pipelines, separate batches of different products, remove liquids, and inspect the condition of the pipe wall.

Types of pigs

Utility or cleaning pigs are made of polyurethane foam or fitted with discs, cups, brushes or scrapers to sweep out wax, scale and debris. Gel pigs are used for special cleaning and drying tasks. Inspection pigs — "smart pigs" — carry sensors using magnetic flux leakage or ultrasound to map corrosion, dents and cracks, and record the data for analysis when the pig is recovered. Pigs are launched and received through oversized barrels (pig launchers and receivers) at stations along the line.

Etymology

The name is commonly explained by the squealing sound early pigs made as they scraped along the pipe; a popular backronym is "pipeline inspection gauge" or "pipeline integrity gadget". Early pipeline pigs were bundles of leather and straw used to clear early oil lines.

Applications

Pigging is routine in oil and gas transmission lines, and is also applied in food, cosmetics, paint and chemical plants, where it pushes product out of process lines, reducing waste and cleaning between batches. Regular "intelligent" inspection runs are required by pipeline safety regulators in many countries.
