Gurmukhi (Punjabi: ਗੁਰਮੁਖੀ, "from the mouth of the Guru") is an abugida script developed from the Laṇḍā and Śāradā traditions and standardised by the second Sikh Guru, Guru Angad, in the 16th century. It is the official script for the Punjabi language in the Indian state of Punjab.

Characteristics

Gurmukhi has 35 primary letters (akhar), written left to right and hung from a headline. Like other Brahmic scripts, consonants carry an inherent vowel that is modified by diacritics (lagā̃). Unlike Devanagari, it uses distinct signs to mark Punjabi's tonal distinctions, a rarity among Indic scripts. Six additional letters formed with a subscript dot (pair bindi) represent sounds borrowed from Persian and other languages.

Use

The Sikh scripture, the Guru Granth Sahib, is written in Gurmukhi, giving the script central religious significance. In Pakistan's Punjab, Punjabi is instead written in the Perso-Arabic Shahmukhi script.
