Re Spectrum Plus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Re Spectrum Plus is a name sometimes informally applied in commentary to the expanded regime of the recurso especial (special appeal, abbreviated REsp) under Brazilian civil procedure, by which the Superior Court of Justice (STJ) decides representative cases whose holdings govern large numbers of identical pending appeals. The regime grew out of Brazil's repetitive-litigation reforms, codified in the 2015 Code of Civil Procedure.

Background

Brazilian courts have long faced mass litigation — hundreds of thousands of appeals raising the same point of law against banks, utilities and public agencies. The 2004 constitutional reform created binding precedents (súmulas vinculantes) and general-repercussion screening at the Supreme Federal Court (STF); the ordinary-law analogue for the STJ was the repetitive special appeals procedure, first enacted in 2008 (Law 11.672) and absorbed into articles 1,036–1,041 of the 2015 Code of Civil Procedure.

Mechanism

When multiple special appeals share an identical question of federal law, the STJ selects one or more as representative (recursos representativos da controvérsia), stays the parallel cases nationwide, and decides the paradigm appeal, frequently in a special repetitive-appeals section or at the Corte Especial. The resulting tema repetitivo (repetitive theme) then binds the stayed cases and guides lower courts. The STJ publishes numbered themes — over a thousand have been catalogued — covering interest rates, health-plan coverage, tax and consumer law.

Effects

Supporters credit the system with sharply reducing the STJ's caseload and increasing legal certainty; critics warn that abbreviated representative treatment can slight individualised review and that stays can delay claimants' cases for years. Comparable mechanisms include the STJ's IRDR (incident for resolution of repetitive demands) at state-court level.
