Sir James Martin CBE FRS (11 September 1893 – 5 January 1981) was an Irish aeronautical engineer. He co-founded the Martin-Baker Aircraft Company with Captain Valentine Baker and pioneered the development of the modern ejection seat, a contribution credited with saving thousands of aircrew lives.

Early life and company

Martin was born in Crossgar, County Down, and largely taught himself engineering while working in Ireland and England. In 1929 he established his own aircraft workshop, and in 1934 he founded the Martin-Baker Aircraft Company at Denham, Buckinghamshire, with test pilot Valentine Baker. The company built several prototype aircraft, including the MB 1, MB 2, MB 3, and MB 5 fighters. Baker was killed in 1942 while flight-testing the MB 3.

Ejection seats

After Baker's death and growing concern over aircrew losses escaping high-speed aircraft, Martin turned the company's focus to escape systems. Beginning in 1944 he developed an explosive-powered ejection seat; the first live test was carried out by Bernard Lynch in July 1946, ejected from a Gloster Meteor at over 300 mph. Martin-Baker seats went on to equip most Western military aircraft, and the company became the world's leading manufacturer of ejection seats, with its seats credited with saving many thousands of lives.

Honours and legacy

Martin was knighted in 1965 and elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1971, a rare honour for a practical engineer of his background. He retained control of Martin-Baker until his death in 1981; the company remains family-run, and aircrew saved by its seats join the "Ejection Tie Club".
