Groupe PSA (formerly PSA Peugeot Citroën from 1991 to 2016) was a French multinational automotive manufacturing company which produced automobiles and motorcycles under the Peugeot, Citroën, DS, Opel and Vauxhall brands. It was the second-largest car manufacturer in Europe, after Volkswagen Group.

History

The group originated in 1976 when Peugeot SA acquired a controlling stake in the struggling Citroën company from Michelin, creating the PSA group (PSA stands for Peugeot Société Anonyme). Citroën had earlier acquired the French sports-car maker Maserati. In 1978 PSA bought Chrysler's European operations, reviving the Talbot brand for the former Rootes and Simca models.

PSA collaborated for decades with other manufacturers through joint ventures, sharing platforms and engines with Fiat, Ford, Toyota, BMW, and Dongfeng, which took a stake in the company during its 2014 restructuring under chief executive Carlos Tavares. In 2017 PSA acquired Opel and Vauxhall from General Motors for €2.2 billion.

Merger into Stellantis

In 2019 PSA and Fiat Chrysler Automobiles announced a merger of equals. The merger was completed on 16 January 2021, creating Stellantis, then the world's fourth-largest automaker by volume, with PSA shareholders and management prominent in the combined group.
