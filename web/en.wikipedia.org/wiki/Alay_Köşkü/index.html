Alay Köşkü

The Alay Köşkü (Alay Pavilion), also known as the Procession Pavilion, is a pavilion in Istanbul, Turkey, located on the outer wall of Topkapı Palace overlooking Gülhane Park. It was built so that Ottoman sultans could watch processions and parades passing below in comfort.

History

A pavilion on the site dates to the 16th century, in the reign of Sultan Murad III, who used it to observe processions. The present structure was rebuilt in the early 19th century under Sultan Mahmud II, in an eclectic style mixing Ottoman and European elements. From its projecting polygonal bay, the sultan could watch military parades, guild processions, and festive corteges on the street below without being seen, behind latticed screens.

Architecture

The two-storey pavilion sits atop the palace's outer walls at the corner facing the Sublime Porte. Its most distinctive feature is a semi-circular, colonnaded projection supported by brackets over the street, enclosed with windows. The interior was furnished as a small viewing salon with a throne. The pavilion forms part of the Topkapı Palace complex, now a museum.

Later use

In the 20th century the pavilion was associated with Turkish literature: it hosted the Ahmet Hamdi Tanpınar Literature Museum Library for a period, named after the novelist Ahmet Hamdi Tanpınar, before the museum library moved. The building remains part of the museum administration of Topkapı Palace.
