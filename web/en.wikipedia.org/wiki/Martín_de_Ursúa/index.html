Martín de Ursúa y Arizmendi, Count of Lizárraga (February 1653 – 4 February 1715), was a Spanish conquistador and colonial administrator in New Spain and the Philippines. He is chiefly remembered for leading the final conquest of the last independent Maya kingdom — the Itza of Tayasal (Nojpetén), on Lake Petén Itzá in present-day Guatemala — in 1697, more than 170 years after Hernán Cortés first passed through the region.

Early career

Ursúa was born in Olóriz, Navarre, and arrived in New Spain as a young man, rising through colonial office. He served in Yucatán, becoming interim governor (gobernador) of the province in the 1690s, during a period when Spanish authorities in Yucatán and Guatemala were pressing to subdue the independent Itza Maya around Lake Petén Itzá and to open a road (camino real) between Yucatán and Guatemala.

Conquest of Tayasal

Building on the embassies of the Itza king Aj Kan Ek' — who had visited Mérida in 1695 and offered submission — and on the road-building advance from both Yucatán and Guatemala, Ursúa mustered an expedition that reached Lake Petén Itzá in early 1697 with a shallow-draft armed vessel (galeota) assembled on the lakeshore. On 13 March 1697 his force attacked and captured the island capital Nojpetén (Tayasal) after a brief battle; the Itza king fled and was later captured. The conquest ended the last unconquered Maya state in Mesoamerica. Ursúa founded a garrison town at the site, modern Flores, and served as the region's first Spanish governor.

Later career and death

For his services Ursúa received the title Count of Lizárraga. He later crossed the Pacific and was appointed Governor-General of the Philippines, serving from 1709 until his death in Manila on 4 February 1715 amid conflict with the archbishop and religious orders over jurisdiction. His kinsman and contemporary, the historian Juan de Villagutierre y Soto-Mayor, chronicled the Itza campaign in the Historia de la conquista de la provincia de el Itza (1701).
