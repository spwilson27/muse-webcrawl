Ogdoad

The Ogdoad of Hermopolis: eight primordial gods in four male-female pairs.

In ancient Egyptian religion, the Ogdoad (from Greek ogdoas, "the Eightfold") was a group of eight primordial deities worshipped principally at Hermopolis (Egyptian: Khemenu, "Eight-Town"). The Ogdoad represented the state of the world before creation, embodied in four male-female pairs of abstract qualities.

The eight deities

The four pairs were: Nu and Naunet, the primeval waters; Heh and Hauhet, infinity or endlessness; Kek and Kauket, darkness; and Amun and Amaunet, hiddenness or invisibility. The male gods were depicted with frog heads and the female gods with serpent heads, animals associated with the primeval waters and mud from which life emerged.

Cosmogony

In the Hermopolitan creation myth, the interaction of the Ogdoad in the primeval waters produced the primordial mound, or an egg, or a lotus, from which the sun god arose and creation began — accounts vary between versions. The myth is among the several competing Egyptian creation systems, alongside those of Heliopolis (the Ennead), Memphis (Ptah), and Thebes (Amun). After creation, the Ogdoad were said to have died and retired to the underworld, from where they caused the Nile to flow and the sun to rise.
