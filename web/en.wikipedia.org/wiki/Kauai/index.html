Kauai (Hawaiian: Kauaʻi) is the oldest and fourth-largest of the main Hawaiian Islands, located in the U.S. state of Hawaii. Nicknamed "the Garden Isle" for its lush emerald landscape, it is the northernmost of the major islands, separated from Oahu by the 72-mile-wide Kauai Channel.

Geography

Formed by the now-dormant shield volcano Waialeale about five million years ago, the island is almost circular, with a rugged, eroded interior. Mount Waialeale, near the centre, receives around 11,700 mm (460 in) of rain a year on average and is among the wettest places on Earth. The island's dramatic scenery includes the fluted cliffs of the Napali Coast, the deep Waimea Canyon — often called the "Grand Canyon of the Pacific" — and the waterfalls of the Wailua River valley. Much of the interior is roadless wilderness.

History and economy

Kauai was the first Hawaiian island visited by Captain James Cook, who landed at Waimea Bay in 1778. It was the last kingdom to join Kamehameha I's unified Hawaii, ceding peacefully in 1810. Sugar plantations dominated the economy in the 19th and 20th centuries; tourism is now the main industry, and the island's landscapes have served as locations for films including South Pacific, Raiders of the Lost Ark, Jurassic Park and The Descendants.

In April 2018 record rainfall — nearly 50 inches in 24 hours at Waimea — caused severe flooding and landslides. Kauai is also a stronghold for conservation of Hawaii's endangered forest birds and is the only major island without established mongooses.
