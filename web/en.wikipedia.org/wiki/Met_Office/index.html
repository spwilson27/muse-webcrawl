The Met Office, until 1990 the Meteorological Office, is the United Kingdom's national weather service. It is an executive agency of the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology and provides weather and climate services to the public, government, businesses, the armed forces, and emergency responders.

Met Office headquarters, Exeter

History

The office was founded in 1854 under Vice-Admiral Robert FitzRoy as a service to mariners, initially supplying meteorological instruments and observations to ships. After the wreck of the Royal Charter in a storm in 1859, FitzRoy developed a storm-warning system, and the first public weather forecasts were published in 1861. The office passed through the Board of Trade, the Air Ministry, and the Ministry of Defence, becoming an executive agency and trading fund in 1996. In 2003 it moved to purpose-built headquarters in Exeter.

Operations

The Met Office runs numerical weather prediction models on some of the world's most powerful weather supercomputers (Cray systems), including the global Unified Model, which is also used for climate modelling at the Met Office Hadley Centre for Climate Science and Services, founded in 1990. It issues the UK's official weather warnings through the National Severe Weather Warning Service, provides the Shipping Forecast on behalf of the Maritime and Coastguard Agency, and since 2016 has named storms affecting the UK and Ireland jointly with Met Éireann (and later the Dutch KNMI). It represents the UK at the World Meteorological Organization.
