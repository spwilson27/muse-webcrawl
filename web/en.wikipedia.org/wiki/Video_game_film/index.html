Video game film

A video game film is a film adaptation of a video game. Video games have inspired a number of films since the late 1980s, with varying degrees of critical and commercial success. Early adaptations such as Super Mario Bros. (1993) and Street Fighter (1994) were critical failures, and for many years the genre carried a reputation for poor quality, sometimes described as a "video game movie curse".

History

The first major adaptation was Super Mario Bros. in 1993, loosely based on Nintendo's platform game series. Through the 1990s and 2000s, adaptations of franchises such as Mortal Kombat, Tomb Raider, Resident Evil, and Silent Hill achieved mixed results; the Resident Evil series became the highest-grossing video game film series of its era despite poor reviews.

Perceptions began to change in the late 2010s with better-received films such as Pokémon Detective Pikachu (2019) and Sonic the Hedgehog (2020). The Super Mario Bros. Movie (2023) grossed over $1.3 billion worldwide, becoming the highest-grossing video game adaptation to that point. A Minecraft Movie (2025) also became a major box-office success.

Reception

Critics have often attributed the genre's struggles to the difficulty of translating interactive experiences into passive storytelling, excessive fidelity or infidelity to source material, and production choices made by filmmakers unfamiliar with games. Recent successes have been credited to involving game creators in production and targeting family audiences.
