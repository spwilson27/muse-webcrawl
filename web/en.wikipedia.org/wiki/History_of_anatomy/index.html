History of anatomy

DisciplineAnatomy
Key figuresHippocrates, Galen, Vesalius, Harvey

The history of anatomy charts the study of the structure of living things from ancient Egyptian and Greek medicine through Galen, the Renaissance anatomists, and the microscope to modern imaging.

Antiquity

Egyptian medical papyri described the heart and vessels. Greek thinkers such as Alcmaeon and the Hippocratic writers dissected animals; in Hellenistic Alexandria, Herophilus and Erasistratus performed the first systematic human dissections. Galen (2nd century CE), working mostly from animal dissection, produced an anatomical system that dominated medicine for 1,400 years.

Renaissance

Public human dissection revived in Italian universities. Leonardo da Vinci made extraordinary anatomical drawings; Andreas Vesalius's De humani corporis fabrica (1543) corrected many Galenic errors and founded modern anatomy. William Harvey demonstrated the circulation of the blood in 1628.

Modern anatomy

The microscope revealed tissues and cells; the 19th century standardised anatomical terminology and clinical anatomy, aided by preservation techniques and, controversially, body-snatching before Anatomy Acts regulated supply. X-rays, CT and MRI now allow anatomy to be studied in the living body.
