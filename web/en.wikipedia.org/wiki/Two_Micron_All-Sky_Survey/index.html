Two Micron All-Sky Survey

The Two Micron All-Sky Survey, or 2MASS, was an astronomical survey of the whole sky in three infrared wavebands, conducted between 1997 and 2001. It produced the most comprehensive catalogue of the near-infrared sky of its era.

Survey and results

Two identical 1.3 m telescopes observed the northern and southern skies. The final data products include images of the entire sky, a Point Source Catalog of some 471 million stars and other objects, and an Extended Source Catalog of about 1.6 million galaxies. 2MASS revealed brown dwarfs, mapped the structure of the Milky Way through interstellar dust, and remains a foundational reference catalogue for astronomy.
