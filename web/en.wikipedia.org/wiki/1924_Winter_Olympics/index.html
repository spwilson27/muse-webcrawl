The 1924 Winter Olympics, officially known as the I Olympic Winter Games (French: Iers Jeux olympiques d'hiver) and commonly known as Chamonix 1924, were a winter multi-sport event held from 25 January to 5 February 1924 in Chamonix, France. Originally held under the title "International Winter Sports Week" in association with the 1924 Summer Olympics in Paris, the event was a success and was retroactively recognised by the International Olympic Committee as the first Olympic Winter Games.

Highlights

Some 258 athletes from 16 nations competed in 16 events, including bobsleigh, curling, ice hockey, figure skating, Nordic skiing, ski jumping, and speed skating. The first gold medal of the Games was won by Charles Jewtraw of the United States in the 500-metre speed skating event. Norway dominated: Clas Thunberg of Finland won five medals in speed skating, and Thorleif Haug of Norway won all three Nordic skiing events plus a (later disputed) ski jumping bronze. Canada's ice hockey team, represented by the Toronto Granites, was utterly dominant, outscoring opponents 110–3 across five games.

The youngest medallist was British figure skater Cecilia Colledge's contemporary — Sonja Henie of Norway, aged 11, competed in ladies' figure skating and finished last, but went on to win gold at the next three Winter Games.

Legacy

The success at Chamonix persuaded the IOC to create a separate cycle of Winter Games; in 1925 it formally established the Olympic Winter Games, with St. Moritz hosting the second edition in 1928. The Chamonix event also saw the first Winter Olympic medal ceremony and the awarding of medals for curling, which the IOC recognised as an official event only decades later.
