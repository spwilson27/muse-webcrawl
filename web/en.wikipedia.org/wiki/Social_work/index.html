Social work

Social work is an academic discipline and practice-based profession concerned with promoting social change and development, social cohesion, and the empowerment and liberation of people. Social workers work with individuals, families, groups and communities facing poverty, disability, illness, discrimination and other disadvantages.

History

Modern social work grew out of 19th-century philanthropy and poor relief: the Charity Organization Societies, which introduced "scientific charity" and casework, and the settlement house movement of Jane Addams and others, which emphasised community and reform. The profession formalised in the 20th century with university training, licensing, and a distinct body of methods: casework, group work and community organisation.

Practice areas

Social workers practise in child protection, hospitals and mental health, schools, substance-use treatment, criminal justice, elder care and disaster response. Clinical social workers are among the largest providers of mental-health therapy in the United States. The profession is guided by codes of ethics emphasising service, social justice, human dignity, and confidentiality.

Education

The standard qualifications are the Bachelor of Social Work and Master of Social Work, followed by state or national licensure for independent or clinical practice.
