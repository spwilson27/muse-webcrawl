The 3 September 1843 Revolution was a largely bloodless uprising by the Greek military and political leadership against King Otto of Greece, demanding the granting of a constitution. It transformed Greece from an absolute monarchy into a constitutional monarchy and gave its name to Syntagma (Constitution) Square in Athens.

Background

Otto, a Bavarian prince installed as king in 1833, ruled through a Bavarian-dominated regency and then personally, without the constitution many Greeks had expected after independence from the Ottoman Empire. Discontent grew over taxation, the exclusion of Greek War of Independence veterans such as Ioannis Makriyannis from office, and the influence of foreign powers.

The uprising

On the night of 3 September 1843, cavalry colonel Dimitrios Kallergis led troops to the royal palace in Athens, joined by crowds demanding a constitution. Otto, advised that resistance was impossible, conceded: he agreed to convene a National Assembly and grant a constitution. The square before the palace was later named Syntagma Square to commemorate the event.

Aftermath

The National Assembly drafted the Constitution of 1844, which established a bicameral legislature and limited royal power, though Otto retained significant authority and repeatedly clashed with parliament until his deposition in 1862.
