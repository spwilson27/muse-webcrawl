Lichen planus

Lichen planus (LP) is a chronic inflammatory and immune-mediated disease that affects the skin, nails, hair and mucous membranes. It is characterised by itchy, flat-topped, polygonal, violaceous (purple) papules and plaques, classically summarised by the "six Ps": pruritic, purple, polygonal, planar, papules and plaques.

Signs and symptoms

Cutaneous lesions favour the wrists, ankles and lower back and may show fine white lines (Wickham striae) and the Koebner phenomenon, appearing along scratches. Oral lichen planus produces lacy white patches on the buccal mucosa and may be erosive and painful. The condition also causes nail dystrophy and a scarring hair loss (lichen planopilaris). The cause is not fully understood but involves T-cell mediated attack on basal keratinocytes; associations include hepatitis C infection and certain drugs, which can trigger lichenoid eruptions.

Treatment

Cutaneous lichen planus often resolves within one to two years, though oral disease tends to persist. Treatment includes potent topical corticosteroids, antihistamines for itch, and for severe cases systemic corticosteroids, retinoids, phototherapy or immunosuppressants.
