Flesch–Kincaid readability tests

The Flesch–Kincaid readability tests are readability tests designed to indicate how difficult a passage in English is to understand. There are two tests: the Flesch Reading Ease and the Flesch–Kincaid Grade Level. Both use average sentence length and average syllables per word as core measures, but weight the factors differently.

Flesch Reading Ease

Developed by Rudolf Flesch, the Reading Ease score is computed as 206.835 − 1.015 × (words/sentences) − 84.6 × (syllables/words). Higher scores indicate text that is easier to read; scores of 60–70 correspond to plain English understood by most adults.

Flesch–Kincaid Grade Level

In 1975, under contract to the United States Navy, J. Peter Kincaid and colleagues reworked the formula to express readability as a U.S. grade level: 0.39 × (words/sentences) + 11.8 × (syllables/words) − 15.59. A score of 8.0 means an eighth grader can understand the text.

Applications

The tests are widely used in education, publishing, and government; many states require consumer documents such as insurance policies to meet minimum Flesch scores. Microsoft Word and other word processors compute the scores automatically.
