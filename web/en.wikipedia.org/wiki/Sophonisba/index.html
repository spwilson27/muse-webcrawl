Sophonisba

Sophonisba (Punic: 𐤎𐤐𐤍𐤁𐤏𐤋, Sapanba'al; died 203 BC) was a Carthaginian noblewoman who lived during the Second Punic War and was the daughter of Hasdrubal Gisco.

Life

Sophonisba was betrothed to the Numidian prince Masinissa but married instead to King Syphax of western Numidia to secure his alliance for Carthage against Rome. After Syphax's defeat and capture in 203 BC, Masinissa married her — partly to protect her from the Romans. Scipio Africanus demanded she be surrendered as a prisoner for his triumph.

Death

To spare her that humiliation, Masinissa sent her a cup of poison, which, according to Livy and other sources, she drank bravely, reproaching him yet accepting the 'wedding gift'. Her story became a popular subject in Renaissance and Baroque art, drama and opera as an exemplum of patriotic self-sacrifice.

In art

Paintings by Rembrandt (attributed subject), Mattia Preti, Giovanni Battista Tiepolo and others depict her death; plays by Corneille and Voltaire retell it.
