Energy in Georgia (country)

Energy in Georgia describes energy production, consumption and policy in the South Caucasus country of Georgia. The country is notable for a power sector dominated by hydropower, supplemented by natural gas imports, and for its role as a transit corridor for Caspian oil and gas bound for Europe.

Electricity

Hydropower provides roughly three-quarters of Georgia's electricity generation, anchored by the Enguri Dam on the Enguri River — one of the world's tallest concrete arch dams (271.5 m) — and the Vardnili cascade. Output is strongly seasonal: spring snowmelt produces surpluses exported to neighbours, while winter low flows and peak heating demand force imports. Thermal generation from imported natural gas (mainly at Gardabani) fills the gap. Wind power began with the small Kartli wind farm (2016), and solar remains modest.

Oil and gas transit

Georgia hosts key transit pipelines from Azerbaijan: the Baku–Tbilisi–Ceyhan oil pipeline, the South Caucasus Pipeline carrying Shah Deniz gas to Turkey, and the Baku–Supsa line to its Black Sea terminal. Transit fees and energy security cooperation with Azerbaijan and Turkey underpin its position between Caspian producers and European markets. Georgia imports nearly all its natural gas, predominantly from Azerbaijan, with a small social-gas allocation from transit arrangements.

Policy

Under its EU Association Agreement and Energy Community membership, Georgia has liberalised its electricity market and adopted renewable-energy and efficiency laws, while large hydropower projects such as Namakhvani have faced strong public protest over environmental and safety concerns.
