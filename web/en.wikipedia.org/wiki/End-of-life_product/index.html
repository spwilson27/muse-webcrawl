End-of-life product

Placeholder image: end-of-life electronics awaiting recycling

An end-of-life product (EOL product) is a product at the end of the product lifecycle, indicating that the product is at the end of its useful life (from the vendor's point of view). At this stage, the vendor may discontinue marketing, selling, or provisioning updates and support for the product, while it may continue—or stop—providing spare parts and repairs.

Lifecycle stages

Vendors typically announce staged milestones: end of sale (last date the product can be ordered), end of support or end of service life (last date for technical support, security patches and spare parts), and final obsolescence. In the software industry, "end of life" commonly means that security updates cease, which can expose users to vulnerabilities; examples include the end of support for Windows XP in 2014 and Windows 7 in 2020. Long-term support (LTS) releases are offered by some vendors to extend the period before end of life.

Customer impact and policy

End-of-life announcements force customers to plan migrations to replacement products. Critics associate aggressive end-of-life policies with planned obsolescence and electronic waste; regulations such as the European Union's directives on waste electrical and electronic equipment (WEEE) and right-to-repair initiatives seek to extend product lifetimes and guarantee availability of spare parts and updates for minimum periods.
