National Indian Gaming Commission

Formed: 1988
Headquarters: Washington, D.C.
Type: Federal regulatory agency
Established by: Indian Gaming Regulatory Act

The National Indian Gaming Commission (NIGC) is a United States federal agency established by the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act of 1988 to regulate gaming conducted by Native American tribes on Indian lands.

Role

The commission oversees Class II gaming (such as bingo and related games), reviews tribal gaming ordinances and management contracts, monitors Class III gaming (casino-style games conducted under tribal-state compacts), and enforces compliance to protect the integrity of Indian gaming and ensure tribes are its primary beneficiaries.

Structure

The NIGC is led by three commissioners appointed by the President and the Secretary of the Interior, and is funded primarily by fees assessed on tribal gaming operations rather than congressional appropriations. It works alongside tribal gaming commissions, which handle day-to-day regulation on the ground.
