Gas burner

A gas burner is a device that offers a controlled flame by mixing a fuel gas such as natural gas, liquefied petroleum gas (propane or butane), coal gas or hydrogen with an oxidiser, usually the oxygen in air, and igniting the mixture. Gas burners range from the small burners of domestic cookers and laboratory Bunsen burners to very large industrial burners used in boilers, furnaces and power stations.

Types

In a premix (aerated) burner, part or all of the air required for combustion is mixed with the gas before the burner ports, as in a Bunsen burner; this produces a hot, non-luminous, relatively short flame. In a diffusion (non-aerated) burner, gas issues from jets and burns as it mixes with surrounding air, giving a longer, luminous yellow flame, a principle used in some decorative appliances and industrial furnaces.

Applications

  • Domestic — gas stoves, ovens, water heaters and central heating boilers.
  • Laboratory — Bunsen and Teclu burners for heating, sterilisation and flame tests.
  • Industrial — kilns, furnaces, boilers and process heaters, where burners are rated in megawatts and fitted with flame-supervision, automatic ignition and emissions controls.

Safety

Gas burners are fitted with safety devices such as thermocouple flame-failure devices that shut off gas if the flame is extinguished, since unburned gas accumulation can cause explosions, and incomplete combustion can produce toxic carbon monoxide.
