Persona non grata

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Persona non grata
Literal meaning"Person not welcome" (Latin)
Legal basisVienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations (1961), Article 9
Applies toDiplomats and other foreign officials
EffectRequired departure from the receiving state

In diplomacy, a persona non grata (plural: personae non gratae) is a foreign diplomat whom the receiving state declares unwelcome. Under Article 9 of the Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations (1961), the receiving state may at any time, and without having to explain its decision, notify the sending state that a member of its diplomatic staff is persona non grata, whereupon the sending state must recall the person or terminate their functions; if it refuses, the receiving state may decline to recognize the person as a member of the mission.

Use

Declarations are used to expel diplomats accused of espionage—normally described officially as "activities incompatible with diplomatic status"—or as a political signal of displeasure short of breaking relations. They frequently trigger reciprocal expulsions. Large-scale episodes include the British expulsion of 105 Soviet officials in 1971 (Operation FOOT) and the coordinated expulsion of more than 150 Russian diplomats by Western countries in 2018 following the poisoning of Sergei Skripal in Salisbury, England. The status can also be declared before a diplomat arrives, effectively vetoing an appointment without public confrontation.

Beyond diplomacy

In ordinary usage, the phrase describes anyone who has fallen out of favor and is unwelcome in a group, organization, or country. States occasionally bar foreign public figures—entertainers, politicians, or activists—from entry in a similar spirit, though the formal term applies to accredited diplomats; a declared person who remains may lose the immunities that would otherwise protect them, much like an unpopular legislator may face a quorum call designed to expose absences, albeit in a different arena.
