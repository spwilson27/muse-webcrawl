Twenty-One (quiz show)

Twenty-One is an American game show originally broadcast on NBC from September 10, 1956, to October 9, 1958. Created by Jack Barry and Dan Enright, the show pitted two contestants in isolation booths answering questions of varying difficulty to accumulate 21 points, in a format modelled on blackjack. It became the most notorious programme of the 1950s quiz show scandals.

Gameplay

Contestants chose categories and point values from 1 to 11, with higher values carrying harder questions. A contestant reaching exactly 21 points won $500 per point of margin over the opponent, and games could continue with escalating stakes. Champions remained until defeated or until they chose to stop, risking their winnings.

Scandal

The show's most famous match-up pitted champion Herb Stempel against Columbia University instructor Charles Van Doren, whose fourteen-week run made him a national celebrity. Stempel later revealed that the show was rigged: contestants were coached, given answers in advance and instructed when to win, lose or tie to maximise drama. Congressional hearings in 1959 exposed the deception, Van Doren confessed before Congress, and quiz shows were disgraced for a generation. The scandal was dramatised in the 1994 film Quiz Show.

Revival

NBC revived Twenty-One in 2000 with host Maury Povich, in a short-lived run. The format has also been adapted internationally.
