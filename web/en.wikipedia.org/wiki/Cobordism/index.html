In mathematics, cobordism is a fundamental equivalence relation on the class of compact manifolds of the same dimension, defined by the existence of a higher-dimensional manifold whose boundary is the disjoint union of the two manifolds. Two manifolds M and N are cobordant if there is a manifold W one dimension higher whose boundary is M together with N (with opposite orientation).

History and theory

The concept was developed by Lev Pontryagin and brought to prominence by René Thom, who in 1954 computed the unoriented cobordism ring and showed that oriented cobordism could be studied through characteristic numbers such as Pontryagin and Stiefel–Whitney numbers; Thom received the Fields Medal in 1958 partly for this work. Cobordism classes of manifolds form a graded ring under disjoint union and Cartesian product, and cobordism theories became the prototype for generalized cohomology theories in algebraic topology.

Applications

Cobordism ideas underlie modern approaches to the classification of manifolds, surgery theory, and topological quantum field theory, where a TQFT is defined as a functor out of a cobordism category whose objects are manifolds and whose morphisms are cobordisms.
