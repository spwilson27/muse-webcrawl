Education in Sierra Leone

Education in Sierra Leone follows a 6-3-3-4 structure: six years of primary school, three years of junior secondary school, three years of senior secondary school and four years of tertiary education. Primary and secondary education is officially free in government schools, and schooling is legally compulsory at the primary and junior secondary levels.

History

Sierra Leone has one of the oldest Western-style education traditions in sub-Saharan Africa: Fourah Bay College, founded in 1827, is among the oldest universities in West Africa, and the country was once called the "Athens of West Africa". The system was severely damaged by the civil war of 1991–2002, during which many schools were destroyed and teachers displaced, and again by the Ebola epidemic of 2014–2016, which closed schools for most of a year.

Recent reforms

In 2018 the government launched the Free Quality School Education programme, abolishing fees in government-assisted schools and providing teaching and learning materials, which sharply increased enrolment. A 2021 policy ended the ban on pregnant girls attending mainstream schools. Examinations include the National Primary School Examination, the BECE at the end of junior secondary, and the WASSCE administered by the West African Examinations Council.

Challenges

The country faces overcrowded classrooms, shortages of trained teachers, low completion rates — especially for girls in rural areas — and an adult literacy rate of roughly half the population. Many schools are run by religious missions with government assistance. Tertiary institutions include the University of Sierra Leone, Njala University and the University of Makeni.
