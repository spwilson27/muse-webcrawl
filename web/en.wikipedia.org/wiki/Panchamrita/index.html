Panchamrita

Panchamrita
TypeRitual offering mixture
TraditionHinduism
IngredientsMilk, curd, honey, sugar, ghee

Panchamrita (Sanskrit: pañcāmṛta, "five nectars") is a mixture of five foods used in Hindu worship and puja, especially for abhisheka, the ritual bathing of a deity's image. The five ingredients are classically milk, curd (yogurt), honey, sugar, and ghee (clarified butter). Regional variations substitute ingredients such as bananas, coconut water, or jaggery.

Use

After the deity is bathed, the panchamrita is distributed to devotees as prasada, believed to carry the deity's blessing. It is prepared for major festivals including Satyanarayana Puja, Janmashtami, and Mahashivaratri, and is also offered at the start of many ceremonies as a symbol of the five elements and of nourishment and immortality — amrita being the nectar of immortality in Hindu mythology.

Symbolism

Each ingredient is sometimes assigned a symbolic meaning: milk for purity, curd for prosperity, honey for sweet and united speech, sugar for sweetness and bliss, and ghee for strength and victory.
