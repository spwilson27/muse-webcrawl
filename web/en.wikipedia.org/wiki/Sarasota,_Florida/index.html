Sarasota is a city in Sarasota County on the southwestern coast of Florida, United States. It is the county seat and a principal city of the North Port–Sarasota–Bradenton metropolitan area. The city is known for its sandy beaches, including Siesta Beach on Siesta Key, its cultural institutions, and its association with the Ringling family and the circus.

History

The area was inhabited by Indigenous peoples for thousands of years before European contact. The modern city developed in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, when Scottish settlers arrived and the railroad reached the area. In the 1920s Florida land boom, Sarasota attracted circus magnate John Ringling, whose winter quarters and art museum became central to the city's identity. Sarasota was incorporated as a town in 1902 and as a city in 1913.

Geography and climate

Sarasota lies on Sarasota Bay, an inlet of the Gulf of Mexico, and includes several barrier islands, or keys, such as Lido Key, Siesta Key, and Longboat Key. The city has a humid subtropical climate with hot, humid summers, frequent afternoon thunderstorms, and mild, drier winters.

Culture

Sarasota is home to the Ringling Museum of Art, the Sarasota Opera, the Sarasota Orchestra, and the Asolo Repertory Theatre. The Sarasota School of Architecture, a regional style of post-war modernism, originated in the area. The city also hosts spring training baseball.
