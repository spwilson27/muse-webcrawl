Poisson process


Sample paths of a Poisson counting process

In probability theory, statistics and related fields, a Poisson point process is a type of random mathematical object that consists of points randomly located on a mathematical space with the key assumption that the points occur independently of one another. The Poisson process in time is the classic counting process in which events occur continuously and independently at a constant average rate: the number of events in any interval of length t follows a Poisson distribution with mean λt, where λ is the rate.

Properties

A homogeneous Poisson process has independent increments and stationary increments: the numbers of events in disjoint intervals are independent, and the distribution of the count in an interval depends only on its length. The waiting time between consecutive events follows an exponential distribution, and the process is memoryless. Named after Siméon Denis Poisson, the process models phenomena such as radioactive decay counts, telephone calls arriving at a switchboard, and requests to a web server.

Variants

The non-homogeneous (inhomogeneous) Poisson process allows the rate λ(t) to vary with time. Spatial Poisson point processes generalize the idea to points scattered in the plane or higher-dimensional spaces, with a constant intensity, and are fundamental models in spatial statistics, telecommunications, and astronomy.
