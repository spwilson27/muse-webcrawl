Dwelling

In law, a dwelling (also a residence or an abode) is a self-contained unit of accommodation used by one or more households as a home or residence, or — more loosely — a place where people live. The concept of a dwelling has legal meaning in many areas, including burglary, landlord and tenant law, welfare entitlements, and census statistics.

Types

Dwellings take many forms across cultures and climates, including:

  • Houses — detached, semi-detached and terraced houses.
  • Apartments (flats) — self-contained units within a larger building.
  • Traditional dwellings — such as yurts, igloos, stilt houses, cave dwellings and earth shelters.
  • Informal dwellings — huts, shanties and other structures in informal settlements.
  • Movable dwellings — caravans, houseboats and residential vehicles.

Legal definition

Many legal systems treat the dwelling as attracting special protection. In English law, burglary of a dwelling is treated more seriously than burglary of other buildings, and the home is protected by Article 8 of the European Convention on Human Rights. In statistics, a dwelling is generally defined as a structure or part of a structure intended for habitation by a household, whether or not it is occupied.

History

Human dwellings have evolved from natural shelters and simple huts to complex structures with services such as water supply, heating, and sanitation. Vernacular architecture reflects local materials, climate and culture, while modern housing is shaped by planning law, building codes and the economics of construction.
