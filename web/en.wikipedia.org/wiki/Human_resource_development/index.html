Human resource development

Human resource development (HRD) is the framework for helping employees develop their personal and organisational skills, knowledge and abilities. It encompasses activities such as training and development, career development, coaching and mentoring, succession planning, performance management and organisation development.

Definition and scope

The term was developed by Leonard Nadler in the 1960s and elaborated by scholars including Richard Swanson, who defined HRD as the process of developing and unleashing human expertise through organisation development and personnel training for the purpose of improving performance. HRD operates at three levels: individual learning, team and process improvement, and organisational change and culture.

Practice

HRD practitioners conduct needs analyses, design and deliver learning programmes (increasingly through digital and blended formats), evaluate their effect, and manage talent pipelines. At the national level, "human resource development" also refers to public policy for improving a country's education, health and skills base, as measured for example by the United Nations' Human Development Index and by national HRD strategies in countries such as South Korea and Singapore. Professional bodies include the Association for Talent Development (ATD) and the Academy of Human Resource Development, which publishes scholarly journals in the field.
