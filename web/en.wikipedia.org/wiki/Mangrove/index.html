Mangrove

[Image: Mangrove forest roots at high tide]
Mangrove forest roots at high tide

Mangroves are trees and shrubs that live in the coastal intertidal zones of tropical and subtropical regions, rooted in saline or brackish water and anaerobic mud. About 70 species of true mangroves are known, with the greatest diversity in Southeast Asia and the largest total area in Indonesia.

Mangrove forests are among the most productive and protective ecosystems on Earth: their tangled roots shelter juvenile fish and crustaceans of great commercial value, their sediments bury ('blue') carbon at exceptional rates, and they shield coasts from storm surges and tsunamis. Despite this, they have been cleared at alarming rates for shrimp ponds, palm oil and development — roughly a third were lost in the late 20th century, though the rate has slowed and restoration is growing.
