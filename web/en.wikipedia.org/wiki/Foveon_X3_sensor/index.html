Foveon X3 sensor

The Foveon X3 sensor is an image sensor for digital cameras developed by Foveon, Inc. and manufactured by Dongbu HiTek. Unlike conventional sensors that use a Bayer filter mosaic—recording only one colour at each pixel location—the X3 captures all three primary colours at every pixel by stacking photodetectors in silicon.

Technology

The sensor exploits the fact that light of different wavelengths penetrates silicon to different depths: blue light is absorbed near the surface, green deeper, and red deepest. Three layers of photodiodes stacked vertically at each pixel therefore record blue, green, and red intensity respectively, much as the layers of colour film do. Because every pixel captures full colour information, X3 images avoid demosaicing artefacts and colour moiré, and produce notably sharp per-pixel detail; manufacturers traditionally quoted the sensor's resolution as the pixel count times three (one value per layer).

Use and reception

The sensor was used principally in Sigma cameras, including the SD series of DSLRs and the DP series of fixed-lens compacts; Sigma acquired Foveon in 2008. Reviewers praised the sensors' resolution and colour rendering at low sensitivities but criticised noise and colour accuracy at high ISO, slow processing, and the special software workflow required. Later "Quattro" generations altered the layer arrangement so that the top layer held higher spatial resolution than the lower layers.
