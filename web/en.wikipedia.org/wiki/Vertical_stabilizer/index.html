Vertical stabilizer

A vertical stabilizer or tail fin is the static part of the vertical tail of an aircraft. The term applies conventionally to the assembly of both this fixed surface and one or more movable rudders hinged to it. The vertical tail functions to provide control, stability and trim in yaw (also known as directional or weathercock stability).

Function

The vertical stabilizer keeps the aircraft's nose pointing into the relative wind: if the aircraft yaws, the resulting aerodynamic force on the fin behind the centre of gravity creates a restoring moment. The hinged rudder at its rear allows the pilot to make deliberate yawing movements, for coordinated turns, crosswind landings and counteracting asymmetric thrust after an engine failure.

Design variations

Most aircraft have a single central fin, but some designs use twin tails or triple tails — the Lockheed Constellation famously had three — or a V-tail in which the fin and horizontal stabilizer are combined into two slanted surfaces, as on the Beechcraft Bonanza. Military aircraft may use all-moving fins or twin fins canted outward for stealth and control. Dorsal and ventral fins are sometimes added for extra stability at high angles of attack.
