Muscle Shoals Sound Studio

Muscle Shoals Sound Studio is a recording studio in Sheffield, Alabama, founded in 1969 by the four session musicians known as the Muscle Shoals Rhythm Section — Barry Beckett, Roger Hawkins, Jimmy Johnson and David Hood — who had been the house band at Rick Hall's FAME Studios across the river in Muscle Shoals, Alabama. The studio's address, 3614 Jackson Highway, became famous as the title of a Cher album recorded there.

Recordings

The studio's house band, nicknamed "the Swampers" (immortalised in Lynyrd Skynyrd's "Sweet Home Alabama"), backed an extraordinary run of hit records blending soul, R&B, country and rock. Artists who recorded at 3614 Jackson Highway include the Rolling Stones — who cut "Brown Sugar" and "Wild Horses" there in December 1969 — Bob Dylan, Paul Simon, Rod Stewart, Bob Seger, Linda Ronstadt, the Staple Singers, Wilson Pickett and Traffic. A distinctive feature of the Muscle Shoals sound was the integration of Black and white musicians in the segregated South.

Later history

The Rhythm Section moved to a larger facility on Alabama Avenue in 1979. The original building later fell into disrepair but was restored and reopened as a working studio and museum in 2017, assisted by the Muscle Shoals Music Foundation and publicised by the 2013 documentary Muscle Shoals. It is listed on the National Register of Historic Places.
