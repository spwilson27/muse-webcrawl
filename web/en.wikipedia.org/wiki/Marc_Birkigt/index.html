Marc Birkigt (8 March 1878 – 15 March 1953) was a Swiss engineer who founded the automobile and aviation company Hispano-Suiza in Barcelona in 1904. He is regarded as one of the pioneers of luxury automobile engineering and of aircraft engine design.

Career

Born in Geneva to German parents, Birkigt studied mechanical engineering before emigrating to Spain in 1899, where he worked for the truck builder La Cuadra. In 1904, together with the Catalan industrialist Damià Mateu, he founded La Hispano-Suiza Fábrica de Automóviles in Barcelona.

As the company's chief engineer, Birkigt designed a series of prestigious luxury cars, including the Alfonso XIII model named after the Spanish king. During the First World War he designed the Hispano-Suiza V8 aero engine, one of the most important Allied aircraft engines; versions powered fighters such as the SPAD S.XIII and the Royal Aircraft Factory S.E.5. The engine's all-aluminium construction and overhead camshafts were advanced for the time.

Later life

Birkigt also designed the Hispano-Suiza H6 and the J12 of the interwar years. He held around 150 patents. He died in Versoix, Switzerland, in 1953.
