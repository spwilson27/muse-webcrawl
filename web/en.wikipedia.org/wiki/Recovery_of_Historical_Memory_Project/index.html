Recovery of Historical Memory Project

The Recovery of Historical Memory Project (Spanish: Proyecto Interdiocesano de Recuperación de la Memoria Histórica, REMHI) was a documentation project of the Human Rights Office of the Archdiocese of Guatemala (ODHAG), led by Bishop Juan José Gerardi. It gathered thousands of testimonies about human rights abuses committed during Guatemala's internal armed conflict (1960–1996).

The report

After several years of work, on 24 April 1998 the project presented its report, Guatemala: Nunca Más ("Guatemala: Never Again"), in Guatemala City Cathedral. Based on some 6,500 testimonies, the report documented roughly 55,000 violations and attributed the great majority — over 90 percent — to the Guatemalan Army and state-aligned paramilitary groups such as the Civil Self-Defense Patrols, with a smaller share attributed to the guerrilla forces of the URNG. It recorded massacres in Maya communities and described the violence as acts of genocide against indigenous groups.

Assassination of Gerardi

Two days after presenting the report, on 26 April 1998, Bishop Gerardi was beaten to death in his garage. The murder shocked Guatemala and the world; several military officers were eventually convicted of involvement. REMHI's findings fed into the work of the UN-sponsored Commission for Historical Clarification, whose 1999 report Memory of Silence reached broadly similar conclusions.
