Art pop

Art pop
Stylistic originsPop music · pop art · avant-garde · art rock · glam rock
Cultural origins1960s, United Kingdom and United States
Derivative formsHyperpop · avant-pop

Art pop is a loosely defined style of pop music influenced by pop art's integration of high and low culture, and which emphasises the manipulation of signs, style and gesture over personal expression. Drawing on art-school ideas, art pop artists commonly incorporate visual art, fashion, performance and conceptual elements into highly stylised pop music and personae.

Characteristics

Where art rock pushed rock towards experimental or classical complexity, art pop works inside the forms of commercial pop — hooks, choruses, glamour — while treating them as artifice to be framed and exaggerated. Critics have associated the approach with artists who consciously construct an image and stage persona, drawing on Pop Art (Warhol), glam theatre and avant-garde performance.

History

Roots lie in the 1960s overlap of art schools, pop art and pop music: the Velvet Underground's collaboration with Andy Warhol, the Beatles' studio experimentation, and Brian Wilson's productions are frequently cited. In the 1970s, Roxy Music and David Bowie epitomised the style; Kate Bush brought it into the late 1970s and 1980s. Later standard-bearers commonly named include Björk, Lady Gaga (whose 2013 album was literally titled Artpop), Lana Del Rey, FKA twigs, St. Vincent, Charli XCX and the avant-pop/hyperpop scenes of the 2010s.
