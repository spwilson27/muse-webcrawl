Leaf warbler

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Leaf warblers
Family: Phylloscopidae
Genus: Phylloscopus
Order: Passeriformes
Species: about 80

Leaf warblers are small insectivorous passerine birds belonging to the genus Phylloscopus. The genus is the only member of the family Phylloscopidae. Leaf warblers were formerly included in the Old World warbler family Sylviidae, but are now considered to belong to their own family.

Description and ecology

Leaf warblers are small, typically 9–13 cm long, with greenish or brown upperparts and whitish or yellowish underparts; many species have distinctive wing bars, supercilia ("eyebrows"), or crown stripes. They are active, constantly moving birds of woodland and scrub that feed by gleaning insects from leaves and twigs, often hovering briefly. Their songs and calls are important for identification, as many species look very similar.

The genus is distributed across Eurasia, with species breeding from western Europe to Japan and south to the Himalayas and Southeast Asia. Most species are strongly migratory, wintering in Africa, southern Asia, or Southeast Asia. Well-known species include the common chiffchaff, willow warbler, wood warbler, and the Arctic warbler.

Taxonomy

The genus Phylloscopus was introduced by the German zoologist Friedrich Boie in 1826. Molecular studies have split off the former subgenus Seicercus and redefined species limits; formerly one species, the greenish warbler, is a classic example of a ring species around the Tibetan Plateau.
