Swabia (German: Schwaben, colloquially Schwabenland) is a cultural, historic, and linguistic region in southwestern Germany. The name ultimately derives from the medieval Duchy of Swabia, one of the stem duchies of the German kingdom, which in turn took its name from the Suebi, a Germanic people of antiquity. Today the region spans much of Baden-Württemberg and the Bavarian administrative region also called Swabia, whose capital is Augsburg.

The Swabian Jura (Schwäbische Alb)

History

The stem duchy emerged in the 10th century and was ruled from 1079 by the Hohenstaufen dynasty, which went on to supply several Holy Roman Emperors. After the dynasty's fall in the 13th century the territory fragmented into numerous counties, free imperial cities, and ecclesiastical states. The modern administrative region of Swabia in Bavaria was assembled after 1803 from former imperial territories ceded to Bavaria.

Culture

Swabians speak Swabian dialects of Alemannic German, often caricatured elsewhere in Germany for their thrift and industry. Regional cuisine includes Spätzle, Maultaschen, and Zwiebelrostbraten. The region is an economic stronghold of the German Mittelstand, with engineering and automotive firms concentrated around Stuttgart and Ulm.
