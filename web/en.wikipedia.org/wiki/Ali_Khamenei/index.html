Ali Hosseini Khamenei (19 April 1939 – 28 February 2026) was an Iranian cleric and politician who served as the second Supreme Leader of Iran from 1989 until his death in 2026, and as President of Iran from 1981 to 1989. As Supreme Leader he was the country's head of state and commander-in-chief, holding ultimate authority over the armed forces, judiciary and major state institutions. He died during the strikes that opened the 2026 conflict between Iran and a US–Israeli coalition, after more than 36 years in office — the longest-serving head of state in the modern Middle East.

Early life and revolutionary activity

Born in Mashhad to an Azeri-Persian clerical family — his father was a mujtahid and the family claims descent from the Prophet Muhammad — Khamenei studied in the seminaries of Mashhad, Najaf and Qom, where he joined the circle of Ruhollah Khomeini. From 1963 he was repeatedly arrested and once exiled to Iranshahr for agitation against the Shah's regime, and he translated works by Sayyid Qutb and other Islamist and anti-colonial authors into Persian.

Presidency

After the 1979 revolution Khamenei served as deputy defence minister, supervisor of the new Revolutionary Guards and Tehran's Friday prayer leader. Following the assassination of President Mohammad-Ali Rajai, he was elected president in October 1981 — the first cleric to hold the office — surviving an assassination attempt in June 1981 that permanently disabled his right arm. His two terms spanned the Iran–Iraq War; he worked alongside Prime Minister Mir-Hossein Mousavi despite deep policy differences.

Supreme Leader

On Khomeini's death in June 1989 the Assembly of Experts elected Khamenei Supreme Leader, after a constitutional revision removed the requirement that the Leader be a marja' (source of emulation); he was promoted to ayatollah and supported in religious standing by the state. Over three and a half decades he consolidated the Leader's office — controlling the IRGC, judiciary, state broadcasting and the Guardian Council, vetting elections, and arbitrating among the regime's factions while keeping formal balance between "principlist" and reformist camps. His tenure saw the 1999 student protests, the disputed 2009 election and the Green Movement, the 2015 nuclear agreement (JCPOA) and the U.S. withdrawal from it in 2018, the 2019 and 2022–23 protest waves — each met with repression — and escalating confrontation with Israel and the United States, including direct missile exchanges in 2024 and the June 2025 war. He issued a widely cited fatwa against the production and use of nuclear weapons while overseeing a uranium enrichment programme that Western governments treated as a threshold capability.

Death

Khamenei was killed on 28 February 2026 in the opening strikes of the 2026 Iran war. His death, confirmed by Iranian state media, left the succession to the Assembly of Experts and triggered a contested transition within the Islamic Republic he had led for more than a third of a century.
