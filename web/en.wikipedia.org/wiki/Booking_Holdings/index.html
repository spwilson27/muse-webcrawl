Booking Holdings

Booking Holdings Inc. is an American travel technology company headquartered in Norwalk, Connecticut, and the world's largest online travel company by gross bookings. It owns and operates several travel fare aggregators and metasearch engines, including Booking.com, Priceline.com, Agoda, Kayak, Rentalcars.com, and the restaurant reservation service OpenTable. The company was founded in 1997 as Priceline.com, incorporating the "Name Your Own Price" travel service launched by Jay S. Walker, and changed its name to Booking Holdings in 2018.

History

Priceline.com launched in 1997 selling airline tickets through a buyer-driven "Name Your Own Price" model, promoted by spokesman William Shatner, and went public in 1999 during the dot-com boom. Although the reverse-auction model faded in importance, the company pivoted under chief executive Jeffery Boyd toward conventional hotel booking, acquiring the Dutch site Bookings.nl and the British Active Hotels to form Booking.com in 2005 — the single most consequential step in its history. It acquired Agoda in 2007, Kayak in 2013, and OpenTable in 2014. Renamed Booking Holdings in 2018, the company is a member of the S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 and has long traded at one of the highest per-share prices of any major listed company, having never split its stock following a 1-for-15 reverse split in 2003.

Business

The group's core is accommodation reservations, in which Booking.com acts as an agency collecting commissions from hotels and other property owners; the group also sells flights, rental cars, and restaurant reservations. It competes principally with Expedia Group, Airbnb, and Google Travel. Its scale has drawn sustained regulatory scrutiny in Europe over rate-parity clauses and its obligations under the EU Digital Markets Act, for which Booking.com was designated a gatekeeper in 2024.
