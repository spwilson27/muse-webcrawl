Hlinka's Slovak People's Party

Hlinka's Slovak People's Party (Slovak: Hlinkova slovenská ľudová strana, HSĽS) was a far-right clerical nationalist political party in Slovakia during the period leading up to and during the Second World War. Named after its founder and long-time leader, the priest Andrej Hlinka, the party advocated Slovak autonomy and later independence from Czechoslovakia.

History

The party's roots go back to 1906, when Slovak national activists broke away from the Hungarian People's Party; it was formally established as the Slovak People's Party in 1913. Under Hlinka it became the strongest Slovak party in interwar Czechoslovakia, campaigning for Slovak autonomy within the republic. After the Munich Agreement of 1938, Slovakia gained autonomy and the party, renamed for Hlinka after his death in August 1938, became the dominant force in the autonomous region.

The Slovak State

In March 1939, under pressure from Nazi Germany, the Slovak Diet declared independence, creating the Slovak Republic (1939–1945), a client state of Germany led by Jozef Tiso. The HSĽS ruled as the sole legal party, and its paramilitary wing, the Hlinka Guard, enforced its policies. The regime deported the majority of Slovakia's Jewish population to Nazi extermination camps in 1942. The party was banned and dissolved after the war, and Tiso was executed for treason in 1947.

Legacy

The party remains a controversial subject in Slovak historiography, associated both with the first experience of Slovak statehood in the modern era and with collaboration and the Holocaust in Slovakia.
