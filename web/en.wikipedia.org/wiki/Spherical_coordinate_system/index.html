Overview

A spherical coordinate system locates a point in three-dimensional space by one distance and two angles: the radius from the origin, the polar angle measured down from a fixed axis, and the azimuthal angle around that axis. It is the natural system for problems with spherical symmetry — gravity, electrostatics, planetary motion.

The geographic coordinate system is essentially the spherical system applied to Earth: latitude is the complement of the polar angle, measured from the equatorial plane rather than the pole, and longitude is the azimuthal angle. On the celestial sphere, declination and right ascension play the same roles.
