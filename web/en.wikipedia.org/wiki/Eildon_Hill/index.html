Eildon Hill

Eildon Hill is a hill in the Scottish Borders, rising to 422 metres (1,385 ft) above the town of Melrose. It is one of the best-known landmarks of the Borders, with three distinctive peaks, and has been a site of human activity since prehistory. The hill is volcanic in origin, a remnant of Carboniferous volcanic activity, and forms part of the Eildon and Leaderfoot National Scenic Area.

History

The North Hill carries one of the largest hillforts in Scotland, enclosing about 16 hectares with ramparts and the remains of several hundred roundhouse platforms, indicating a major settlement of the Iron Age, associated with the tribe known to the Romans as the Selgovae. The Romans established the fort of Trimontium at nearby Newstead, named after the hill's three peaks, with a signal station on the summit.

Folklore

Eildon Hill is rich in legend. It is said to be hollow, and under it sleeps Thomas the Rhymer (Thomas of Erceldoune), the thirteenth-century poet and prophet who, according to the ballad, was carried off by the Queen of Elfland beneath the hill. The split between the peaks was popularly attributed to the wizard Michael Scot. The hill also features in the writings of Sir Walter Scott, who lived nearby at Abbotsford and regarded the view from the slopes as a favourite.
