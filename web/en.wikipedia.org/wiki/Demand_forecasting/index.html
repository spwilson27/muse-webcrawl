Demand forecasting

Demand forecasting is the process of predicting future customer demand for a product or service, using historical data, statistical models and market information. It is a core activity in supply chain management, production planning, inventory control, budgeting and capacity planning, because decisions about purchasing, staffing and manufacturing must be made before actual demand is known.

Methods

Forecasting methods fall into two broad families. Qualitative methods, such as expert judgement, the Delphi method and market surveys, are used when historical data are scarce, for example for new products. Quantitative methods use past data: time-series techniques (moving averages, exponential smoothing, trend and seasonal models such as Holt-Winters, and ARIMA) and causal models such as regression analysis that relate demand to external drivers like price, promotions and economic indicators.

Forecast error

Accuracy is commonly measured with metrics such as mean absolute error (MAE), mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) and root mean square error (RMSE). Forecasts are generally more accurate over shorter horizons and for aggregated groups of products than for individual items. Poor forecasting contributes to the bullwhip effect, in which small demand variations amplify up the supply chain.
