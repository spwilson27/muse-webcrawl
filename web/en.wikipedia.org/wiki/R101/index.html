TypeRigid airship
CountryUnited Kingdom

R101 was one of a pair of British rigid airships built under the Imperial Airship Scheme of the 1920s to link Britain with its dominions. Constructed by the Royal Airship Works at Cardington, it was among the largest flying machines of its era, about 236 metres long after lengthening.

Crash

On its first overseas voyage, bound for Karachi with a stop in Egypt, R101 departed on 4 October 1930 carrying senior officials including the Secretary of State for Air, Lord Thomson. In the early hours of 5 October, in rain and squally winds, it descended and struck a hillside near Beauvais in northern France, bursting into flame. Of 54 people aboard, 48 died — more than in the later Hindenburg disaster.

Aftermath

The inquiry blamed loss of lift from a torn outer cover in bad weather, compounded by the ship's known heaviness. The disaster ended Britain's airship programme; the surviving sister ship R100 was scrapped.
