Lee Myung-bak (Korean: 이명박; born 19 December 1941) is a South Korean businessman and politician who served as the 10th president of South Korea from 2008 to 2013. Before his presidency, he was the CEO of Hyundai Engineering and Construction and served as mayor of Seoul from 2002 to 2006. He is often nicknamed "the Bulldozer" for his reputation as a hard-driving manager.

Early life and business career

Lee was born in Osaka, Japan, to Korean parents and returned to Korea after the Second World War, growing up in poverty in Pohang. He worked his way through Korea University at night while working as a labourer, and joined Hyundai Engineering and Construction in 1965. He rose with extraordinary speed, becoming its CEO at the age of 35 and later chairman of several Hyundai affiliates — a rise celebrated as part of South Korea's rags-to-riches "salaryman legend." His years at Hyundai included major projects in Southeast Asia and the Middle East and, during the Soviet era, early business contacts with North Korea.

Mayor of Seoul

Elected mayor of Seoul in 2002, Lee's signature project was the restoration of the Cheonggyecheon stream, which had been covered by an elevated expressway since the 1970s. The highway was demolished and the stream restored as a linear park through downtown Seoul by 2005 — a project widely praised internationally as a landmark of urban renewal. He also reformed the city's bus system, redesigning routes and introducing dedicated median bus lanes and integrated smart-card fares.

Presidency (2008–2013)

Lee won the December 2007 presidential election for the Grand National Party with about 48.7% of the vote and took office on 25 February 2008, promising a "747" plan of 7% annual growth. His term was shaped by the 2008 global financial crisis, which South Korea weathered comparatively well, and by early mass protests over the resumption of US beef imports amid mad-cow disease fears.

His flagship domestic project was the Four Major Rivers Restoration Project, a large water-management scheme to dam, dredge and develop the Han, Nakdong, Geum and Yeongsan rivers, which remained controversial over cost and environment. In foreign policy he pursued a harder line toward North Korea than his predecessors ("Denuclearization, Opening, 3000"), and relations deteriorated sharply after the sinking of the corvette Cheonan and the shelling of Yeonpyeong Island in 2010. His administration also strengthened the alliance with the United States, ratified the KORUS free trade agreement, hosted the 2010 G20 Seoul Summit and the 2012 Nuclear Security Summit, and promoted "low carbon, green growth" as a national strategy.

Post-presidency and conviction

After leaving office in 2013, Lee became the focus of corruption investigations. In 2018 he was arrested and convicted of bribery, embezzlement and abuse of power — including taking bribes and embezzling funds connected with the auto-parts company DAS — and sentenced to 17 years in prison (finalised in 2020) plus a large fine. In December 2022 he received a special presidential pardon from President Yoon Suk Yeol.
