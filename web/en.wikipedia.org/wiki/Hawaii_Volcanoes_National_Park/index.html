Hawaii Volcanoes National Park (Hawaiian: Pāka Lahui o nā Luapele o Hawaiʻi) is an American national park located on the island of Hawaii in the state of Hawaii. The park encompasses two active volcanoes, Kīlauea and Mauna Loa, among the most active volcanoes on Earth, and protects landscapes ranging from sea level to the 13,681-foot (4,170 m) summit of Mauna Loa.

History

The park was established on August 1, 1916, as part of Hawaii National Park, which also included Haleakalā on Maui; the two were separated in 1961. Kīlauea and its Halemaʻumaʻu caldera were traditionally considered the home of the volcano goddess Pele, and the area holds deep significance in Native Hawaiian culture. The park was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1987.

Geology and eruptions

Kīlauea erupted nearly continuously from 1983 to 2018 along its East Rift Zone, adding new land to the island and destroying the communities of Kalapana and Kapoho in earlier episodes. In 2018 a major summit collapse and lower East Rift Zone eruption transformed the caldera, drained its lava lake, and destroyed hundreds of homes outside the park; much of the park closed for several months. Eruptions resumed within Halemaʻumaʻu in December 2020 and have continued episodically since. Mauna Loa erupted in November–December 2022, its first eruption since 1984.

Features

Visitor highlights include the Kīlauea summit and Crater Rim Drive, the Chain of Craters Road to the coast, the Thurston Lava Tube (Nāhuku), the Jaggar Museum area (closed after 2018 damage), steam vents and sulfur banks, the Puʻuloa petroglyph field, and vast protected habitat for endangered species such as the nēnē (Hawaiian goose) and the Hawaiian petrel.
