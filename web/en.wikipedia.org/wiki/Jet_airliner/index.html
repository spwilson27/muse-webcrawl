Jet airliner

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A twin-aisle jet airliner climbing after takeoff against a blue sky]
A modern twinjet climbing out.

A jet airliner is an airliner powered by jet engines (turbofans), the standard form of commercial air transport since the 1960s. The British de Havilland Comet entered service first (1952), followed by the Soviet Tu-104 (1956) and the American Boeing 707 (1958), whose success — and that of the DC-8 — made jet travel global. Modern jets range from regional types to the A380 superjumbo.

Early jets

The Comet\'s elegant design was undone by metal fatigue at its square window corners, causing fatal breakups in 1954 and a grounding that let Boeing and Douglas capture the market. The 707\'s swept wing, podded engines and size set the template still visible in today\'s airliners.

Turbofan era

High-bypass turbofans (from the 747\'s JT9D, 1970) cut fuel burn and noise; twin-aisle wide-bodies, then efficient twins under ETOPS rules, spread non-stop routes worldwide. Fly-by-wire (Airbus A320, 1988) and glass cockpits followed, and composite airframes (787, A350) cut weight further.

Today

The market is a Boeing–Airbus duopoly challenged by COMAC\'s C919 in China. Orders favour the A320neo and 737 MAX families and long-range twins; production setbacks and safety scrutiny (737 MAX groundings, 2019–20) remain major industry themes.
