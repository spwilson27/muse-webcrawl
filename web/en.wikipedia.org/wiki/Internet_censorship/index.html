Internet censorship

Internet censorship is the legal or technical control or suppression of what can be accessed, published or viewed on the Internet. Censorship is most often conducted by governments, but may also be carried out by private organizations at the behest of government, regulators or on their own initiative.

Methods

Technical methods of Internet censorship include IP address blocking, DNS hijacking and redirection, deep packet inspection, keyword filtering, blocking of specific protocols or ports, and shutting down Internet access entirely. Many nations use sophisticated multi-layered control systems, the best-known being the Great Firewall of China.

Extent and motives

The extent of censorship varies widely. States cite reasons including national security, protection of children, prevention of copyright infringement, suppression of political dissent and preservation of cultural or religious norms. Organizations such as Reporters Without Borders and the OpenNet Initiative have classified countries by the pervasiveness of their Internet filtering, in areas such as political, social, conflict/security and Internet tools.

Circumvention

Users circumvent censorship through tools such as virtual private networks, Tor, proxy servers, alternative DNS services and encryption. The availability and legality of such tools themselves vary by jurisdiction, and censorship systems continually evolve to detect and block circumvention.
