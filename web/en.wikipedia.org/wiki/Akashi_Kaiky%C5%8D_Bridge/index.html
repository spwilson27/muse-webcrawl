Akashi Kaikyō Bridge

The Akashi Kaikyō Bridge spanning the Akashi Strait

The Akashi Kaikyō Bridge (明石海峡大橋, Akashi Kaikyō Ōhashi) is a suspension bridge crossing the Akashi Strait in Japan, linking the city of Kobe on Honshu with Iwaya on Awaji Island. Completed in 1998, it carries the Honshu–Shikoku Expressway and forms part of the Kobe–Awaji–Naruto route. With a main span of 1,991 metres (6,532 feet), it was the longest suspension bridge span in the world for nearly a quarter of a century, until surpassed by Turkey's 1915 Çanakkale Bridge in 2022.

Construction

Calls for a fixed crossing of the dangerous Akashi Strait intensified after a 1955 ferry collision killed 168 people. Construction began in 1988 in challenging conditions: the strait carries heavy shipping traffic and strong currents, and the region is seismically active. During construction, the Great Hanshin earthquake of January 1995 (magnitude 6.9) struck with its epicentre almost directly beneath the bridge; the towers shifted about a metre apart, lengthening the main span, but the structure, then still incomplete, sustained no serious damage and the design was adjusted.

Design

The two steel towers are among the tallest bridge pylons in the world, and the bridge's stiffening truss girder can withstand winds of 286 km/h and earthquakes up to magnitude 8.5. The main cables were spun from high-strength wire; laid end to end, the cable wire would circle the Earth more than seven times. Illumination installed on the cables displays colour patterns that change with seasons, holidays and events, making the bridge a celebrated landmark.
