Hound

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A hound is a type of hunting dog used by hunters to track or chase prey. The word derives from Old English hund, which originally meant any dog, but in modern usage it is restricted to the specialised hunting breeds.

Types

Hounds are conventionally divided by their hunting method. Scent hounds, such as the Bloodhound, Beagle, Basset Hound, and Foxhound, follow prey by smell, often working in packs and "giving tongue" — baying — as they track. Sighthounds (or gazehounds), such as the Greyhound, Whippet, Saluki, and Irish Wolfhound, hunt primarily by sight and speed, coursing game in open country. Some breeds, such as the Rhodesian Ridgeback and various coonhounds, combine scent and sight.

Characteristics

Scent hounds are noted for endurance, powerful noses, and resonant voices suited to being heard over long distances; many have long pendulous ears and loose skin. Sighthounds are typically lean, deep-chested, and extremely fast over short distances. In kennel club classification, hounds form their own group (the Hound Group in the American Kennel Club and The Kennel Club of the UK).

History

Hunting with hounds dates to antiquity: sighthound-like dogs appear in ancient Egyptian art, and scent hounds were developed in medieval Europe for hunting deer, fox, and hare in packs, a practice that shaped much of the terminology of venery.
