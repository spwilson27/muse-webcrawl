Lambert–Eaton myasthenic syndrome

Lambert–Eaton myasthenic syndrome (LEMS) is a rare autoimmune disorder characterised by muscle weakness of the limbs. It results from antibodies against voltage-gated calcium channels on the presynaptic membrane of the neuromuscular junction, reducing acetylcholine release.

Signs and symptoms

Weakness typically begins in the proximal muscles of the legs, and — unlike myasthenia gravis — strength may temporarily improve with repeated effort. Autonomic symptoms such as dry mouth are common, and tendon reflexes are often reduced.

Causes

About half of cases are associated with an underlying malignancy, most often small-cell lung cancer; the remainder are a primary autoimmune disorder. Treatment includes addressing any tumour, amifampridine (3,4-diaminopyridine) to improve neuromuscular transmission, and immunosuppression.
