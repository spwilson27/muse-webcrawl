Yeniseian languages

RegionYenisei River basin, Siberia, Russia
Living memberKet
Extinct membersYugh, Kott, Assan, Arin, Pumpokol
Proposed relativesNa-Dené (Dené–Yeniseian hypothesis)

The Yeniseian languages are a small language family spoken along the middle Yenisei River in central Siberia. The only surviving member is Ket, spoken by a few hundred people; the others — Yugh, Kott, Assan, Arin, and Pumpokol — became extinct between the 18th and 20th centuries.

Classification and history

Early records of the southern Yeniseian languages come from 18th- and 19th-century travellers' wordlists. The family once extended much farther south toward the Sayan Mountains. Ket is known for its complex prefixing verbal morphology and a tonal system unusual for Siberia.

Dené–Yeniseian hypothesis

In 2008 linguist Edward Vajda presented morphological evidence linking Yeniseian with the Na-Dené languages of northwestern North America (such as Navajo and Tlingit), proposing a Dené–Yeniseian family. The hypothesis has been influential and is considered promising by many specialists, though not universally accepted.

Endangerment

Ket is severely endangered, with intergenerational transmission weakened by Russian-language schooling and settlement policies; documentation and revitalisation efforts continue.
