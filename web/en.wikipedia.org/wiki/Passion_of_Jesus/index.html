Passion of Jesus

The Passion of Jesus or Passion of Christ is the short final period in the life of Jesus Christ, from his triumphal entry into Jerusalem to his crucifixion and burial. It includes the Last Supper, the agony in the Garden of Gethsemane, his arrest, trials before the Sanhedrin and Pontius Pilate, his torture, and his execution by crucifixion. The word "Passion" derives from the Latin passio, meaning "suffering".

The Gospel accounts

The Passion narrative occupies a large proportion of each of the four canonical Gospels — Matthew 26–27, Mark 14–15, Luke 22–23, and John 12–19. The accounts agree on the broad sequence of events while differing in details, wording, and emphasis.

Sequence of events

  • Entry into Jerusalem — Jesus enters the city on a donkey, acclaimed by crowds with palm branches.
  • Last Supper — Jesus shares a final meal with his apostles, institutes the Eucharist, and washes their feet in John's Gospel.
  • Agony in the Garden — Jesus prays on the Mount of Olives at Gethsemane and is betrayed by Judas Iscariot with a kiss.
  • Arrest and trials — Jesus is tried before the Jewish council and then before the Roman prefect Pontius Pilate, who sentences him to death.
  • Way of the Cross — Jesus carries his cross to Golgotha, the place of execution.
  • Crucifixion and death — Jesus is crucified between two thieves and dies after several hours.
  • Burial — His body is laid in a tomb provided by Joseph of Arimathea.

Devotion and commemoration

The Passion is commemorated by Christians during Holy Week, especially on Palm Sunday, Maundy Thursday, and Good Friday. Devotional practices include the Stations of the Cross, Passion plays, and the veneration of relics associated with the events. In art and music, the Passion is among the most frequently depicted subjects in Western culture, from medieval altarpieces to Bach's Passions and modern films.
