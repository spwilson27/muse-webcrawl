OPEC

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC) is an organisation of oil-exporting nations — about a dozen members in the mid-2020s, including Saudi Arabia (its de facto leader), Iraq, Iran, Kuwait, the UAE, Nigeria, Libya, Algeria and Venezuela — founded in Baghdad in 1960 to coordinate members' petroleum policies and secure fair and stable prices. Its headquarters are in Vienna, Austria.

History

OPEC rose to prominence in the 1970s: during the 1973 Arab oil embargo (by Arab members through OAPEC) and the 1979 Iranian revolution, prices quadrupled and then rose again, transferring wealth and bargaining power from consuming countries and oil majors to producer states and demonstrating resource nationalism. Since 2016, OPEC has coordinated with non-members including Russia in the "OPEC+" format to manage supply, most dramatically with record cuts during the COVID-19 demand collapse (2020) and in disputes over compliance and market share.

Role and debate

OPEC members hold the large majority of proven crude reserves and a substantial share of production; analysts debate its true market power against US shale, spare capacity politics, and the long-run decline of oil demand under climate policy. Its members' divergent interests — revenue needs, population, relations with Washington — make cohesion a perennial story; several states (Indonesia, Qatar, Angola) have left or suspended membership over the decades.
