Active1891–1918
CountryGerman Empire
RoleColonial defence and internal security

The Schutztruppe (German: "protection force") was the colonial military force of the German Empire, established in 1891 to garrison Germany's overseas colonies. Unlike the Imperial German Army it answered to the colonial administration, and its ranks combined German officers and non-commissioned officers with locally recruited African soldiers, known as askari.

Organisation

Separate Schutztruppe commands were maintained in German East Africa, German South West Africa, and Kamerun, with smaller police-type forces in Togoland and the Pacific colonies. The East African force was the largest and proved the most durable: beginning with Hermann von Wissmann's expeditionary force of the late 1880s, it grew into a professional askari army with German cadres.

Campaigns

The Schutztruppe suppressed numerous colonial revolts, including the Abushiri revolt in East Africa, the Herero and Nama uprisings in South West Africa (1904–1908) — in which the force participated in the genocide of the Herero and Nama peoples — and the Maji Maji rebellion (1905–1907). During the First World War, the East African Schutztruppe under Paul von Lettow-Vorbeck conducted a celebrated guerrilla campaign against far larger British, Belgian, and Portuguese forces, remaining undefeated in the field until the armistice of November 1918.

Dissolution

With the loss of Germany's colonies under the Treaty of Versailles, the Schutztruppe was disbanded. Its askari veterans became a focus of German colonial nostalgia, and Lettow-Vorbeck campaigned for payment of their back pay and pensions, which were eventually honoured by the Federal Republic of Germany in the 1960s.
