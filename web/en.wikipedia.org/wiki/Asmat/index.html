Asmat

Asmat bis poles, among the most celebrated forms of Oceanic art

The Asmat are an ethnic group of New Guinea, residing in the province of South Papua, Indonesia, in a vast lowland of swamps and tidal rivers on the Casuarina Coast. Numbering roughly 110,000 people, they are traditionally hunter-gatherers and fishers whose staple food is sago. The Asmat are world-famous for their woodcarving tradition, and their art is represented in major museum collections.

Culture

Traditional Asmat society was organised around men's houses (jeu), ritual cycles and, historically, headhunting and ritual cannibalism directed at enemy groups, embedded in a cosmology of balance between the worlds of the living and the ancestors. Elaborate carvings — including bis (mambis) poles, ancestor figures, shields and canoes — were made for feasts that fulfilled obligations to the dead. Warfare and headhunting were suppressed under Dutch colonial and missionary pressure in the mid-twentieth century, and many Asmat are now Catholic or Protestant, while ritual feasting and carving continue.

The Asmat came to international attention in 1961 when Michael Rockefeller, son of New York governor Nelson Rockefeller, disappeared while collecting art in the region; his fate — presumed drowned or killed — has never been conclusively established.

Art

Asmat art is among the most renowned of all Melanesian traditions. The bis pole, a towering stack of carved figures erected for a memorial feast, is its most famous form. The Metropolitan Museum of Art's Arts of Africa, Oceania, and the Americas wing holds a major Asmat collection, much of it assembled by Michael Rockefeller. Since 1981 the Asmat Art Festival at Agats, supported by the local diocese, has promoted carving through competitions and auctions.

Environment

The Asmat homeland is one of the world's largest alluvial swamps, threaded by great tidal rivers such as the Betsj, Sirets and Casuarina. The region borders Lorentz National Park, and its forests and fisheries face pressure from logging, transmigration and large-scale development.
