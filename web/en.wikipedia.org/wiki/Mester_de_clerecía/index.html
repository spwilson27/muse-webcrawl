Mester de clerecía

The mester de clerecía ("ministry of the clergy") is a body of medieval Spanish literature composed in the 13th and 14th centuries by learned, clerical authors. It is distinguished from the mester de juglaría — the minstrels' orally transmitted verse — by its written, bookish character, its didactic and religious subject matter, and its regular stanza form.

Form

Works of the mester de clerecía are typically written in the cuaderna vía, monorhymed quatrains of alexandrine lines (fourteen syllables, divided into two hemistichs of seven). The authors, often monks or clerics, presented themselves as craftsmen of a learned "trade" (mester) and drew on Latin sources.

Principal works

The earliest and most famous exponent is Gonzalo de Berceo (c. 1197 – before 1264), the first named poet in the Spanish language, whose Milagros de Nuestra Señora (Miracles of Our Lady) and lives of saints exemplify the school. Other works include the Libro de Alexandre, the Libro de Apolonio, the Poema de Fernán González and, in the 14th century, Juan Ruiz's Libro de buen amor, which loosens the strict form while retaining its clerical framing.
