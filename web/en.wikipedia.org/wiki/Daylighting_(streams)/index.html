Daylighting (streams)

Stream daylighting is the practice of uncovering watercourses that were previously buried in culverts, pipes, or under development, restoring them to open, naturalised channels.


Field: Urban planning, river restoration

Rationale

Buried streams lose ecological function and can cause flooding when culverts fail. Daylighting improves habitat, water quality, flood capacity, groundwater recharge, and urban amenity, and reconnects communities with local waterways. It is part of wider river restoration and green-infrastructure movements.

Examples

Notable projects include Seoul's Cheonggyecheon, which replaced an elevated highway with a restored stream corridor; Zürich's extensive stream-opening programme; Saw Mill River in Yonkers, New York; and numerous smaller schemes in the UK and North America. Projects must manage flood risk, contamination, and cost.
