The Ligurian Sea (Italian: Mar Ligure; French: Mer Ligurienne) is an arm of the Mediterranean Sea between the Italian Riviera (Liguria) and Tuscany to the north and east, and the island of Corsica and the French coast to the west. It is the northwesternmost part of the Mediterranean.

The International Hydrographic Organization defines its limits as running from Cape Ferrat near Nice along the French and Italian coasts to the Tuscan Archipelago, and from there to the northern tip of Corsica. The sea borders the Gulf of Genoa at its northernmost point.

Geography

The coast is mountainous and deeply indented, with the ports of Genoa — one of Italy's busiest — La Spezia, Livorno, Savona and Nice serving shipping between northern Italy, France and the wider Mediterranean. The island of Elba and the smaller islands of the Tuscan Archipelago mark its southeastern edge. The sea reaches depths of roughly 2,850 metres near Corsica.

Hydrology

A cyclonic (counter-clockwise) circulation carries water along the Ligurian coast; in winter, cold dry winds trigger deep-water convection offshore, making the Ligurian Sea one of the sites where Mediterranean deep water forms. The eastward-flowing Ligurian current is the continuation of the current that enters from the Balearic Sea.

Conservation

The Ligurian Sea is exceptionally rich in cetaceans. In 1999 Italy, France and Monaco created the Pelagos Sanctuary for Mediterranean Marine Mammals, covering some 87,500 km² including much of the Ligurian Sea, to protect fin whales, sperm whales, striped dolphins and other species. The Portofino and Cinque Terre marine protected areas lie on its coast.
