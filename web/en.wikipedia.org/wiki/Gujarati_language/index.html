Gujarati language

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Gujarati (ગુજરાતી, Gujarātī) is an Indo-Aryan language native to the Indian state of Gujarat and spoken predominantly by Gujarati people. It is the official language of Gujarat and of the union territory of Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, and is one of the 22 scheduled languages of the Republic of India. With roughly 60 million speakers worldwide, it ranks among the most widely spoken languages in the world.

History

Gujarati evolved from Old Gujarati (c. 1100–1500 CE), the ancestor also of Rajasthani, through a Middle Gujarati stage, acquiring its modern form by about the 19th century. The language has a continuous literary tradition of some 800 years, from Jain narrative poems through the devotional verse of Narsinh Mehta to the modern prose inaugurated by Narmad. It was the mother tongue of Mahatma Gandhi, whose autobiography The Story of My Experiments with Truth was first written in Gujarati.

Script and grammar

Gujarati is written in its own script, derived from Devanagari but lacking the characteristic horizontal headline. The grammar retains three genders (masculine, feminine, neuter), two numbers, three cases in nouns, and a split-ergative verb agreement pattern in the perfective aspect; word order is subject–object–verb.

Geographic distribution

Beyond Gujarat, large Gujarati-speaking communities exist in Mumbai, in East Africa (a legacy of trade and migration), and in the United Kingdom, the United States and Canada, where it is among the most common South Asian languages.
