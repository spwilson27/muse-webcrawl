Poem

A poem is a piece of writing in which the expression of feelings and ideas is given intensity by attention to diction, rhythm, imagery, and the sounds of language. Poems are the characteristic product of poetry, a form of literature that uses the aesthetic and rhythmic qualities of language to evoke meanings in addition to, or in place of, ordinary prosaic meaning. Poems may be written in verse — language arranged in lines, often with metre and rhyme — or in prose, as the prose poem; they range in length from a two-line epigram or a seventeen-syllable haiku to book-length epics such as the Iliad, the Mahabharata, and the Divine Comedy.

Elements

Although no single definition covers all poems, most are marked by some combination of:

  • Line and stanza — the poem's basic units, grouped and broken for rhythm and emphasis rather than by the margin of the page.
  • Metre and rhythm — patterned arrangements of stressed and unstressed syllables, such as iambic pentameter, or freer cadences in free verse.
  • Rhyme and sound patterning — end rhyme, internal rhyme, alliteration, and assonance.
  • Imagery and figurative language — metaphor, simile, personification, and symbol, conveying sense impressions and layered meaning.
  • Voice and tone — a speaker distinct, in lyric poetry, from the poet; dramatic poems may adopt personae.

Forms and genres

Poems are classified by form (fixed forms such as the sonnet, villanelle, sestina, ballad, haiku, and ghazal), by length, and by kind. The broad traditional kinds are lyric (a short poem expressing personal feeling), narrative (telling a story, including the epic and the ballad), and dramatic (speech presented in character, including the dramatic monologue). Other major types include the elegy, ode, pastoral, satire, and didactic poem.

History

Poetry is among the oldest forms of literature: the Sumerian Epic of Gilgamesh, the hymns of the Rigveda, and Egyptian and Chinese lyrics predate most written prose. Early poems were composed for oral performance and preserved through metre and formulaic language. The invention of printing, and later of recording and digital media, changed how poems circulate, but the poem remains a central literary form in virtually every culture, celebrated in readings, slams, and national traditions.
