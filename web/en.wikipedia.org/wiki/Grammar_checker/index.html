Grammar checker

TypeSoftware, natural language processing
FunctionDetect grammatical errors in text

A grammar checker, in computing terms, is a program, or part of a program, that attempts to verify written text for grammatical errors. Grammar checkers may be programmed into word processors or be available as standalone applications and browser extensions that can be used with many different writing programs.

History

Early grammar checkers, such as the Writer's Workbench tools developed at Bell Labs in the late 1970s and the commercial program Grammatik (1981), concentrated on punctuation and style issues. The first program to use the term "grammar checker", in the sense of checking for broader syntactic errors, was Aspell-era contemporary software of the early 1990s. Microsoft Word's grammar checker, a long-standing feature, was initially based on technology licensed from other companies before being developed in-house using natural language parsing.

Implementation

Grammar checking historically relied on rule-based parsing: assigning part-of-speech tags to words and matching structures against patterns of correct and incorrect usage. Modern systems, such as Grammarly and LanguageTool, increasingly incorporate statistical methods and neural machine-learning models, which improves detection of subject–verb agreement errors, article misuse, and misplaced modifiers, though false positives and missed errors remain common.

Criticism

Grammar checkers have been criticized for flagging correct constructions (such as passive voice) as errors, for missing real errors, and for encouraging formulaic writing. Linguists note that automated checkers perform poorly on complex sentences and cannot reliably judge meaning.
