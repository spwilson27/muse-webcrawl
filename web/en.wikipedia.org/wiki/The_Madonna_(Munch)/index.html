The Madonna (Munch)

ArtistEdvard Munch
Year1894–1895
MediumOil on canvas
Dimensions91 cm × 70.5 cm
LocationMunch Museum, Oslo (versions also at the National Gallery, Oslo, and Kunsthalle Hamburg)

Madonna is an expressionistic painting by the Norwegian artist Edvard Munch, first painted in 1894–95. Munch produced several versions of the composition in oil and pastel, as well as a well-known lithograph. The painting depicts a topless woman with long dark hair, her head thrown back and eyes half-closed, against a dark background, with swirling red, blue, and black forms around her.

Title and interpretation

Although the title Madonna is commonly associated with the Virgin Mary, Munch's depiction is of a sensuous, femme fatale-like figure. Munch himself described the image as representing "the moment when life and death join hands"—the moment of conception—writing of the woman's expression capturing both the beauty and sorrow of that instant. A sperm-like border and a small fetus-like figure in the corner of the lithograph version reinforce this theme of life, desire, and death.

Versions

Munch painted five versions of the Madonna between 1894 and 1902. The best-known version is held by the Munch Museum in Oslo; another is in the National Gallery in Oslo, and a third in the Kunsthalle Hamburg. A pastel version sold at auction in 2010. The lithograph, printed in 1895 and hand-colored in some impressions, features a decorative border with spermatozoa and a small crouching figure.

Theft and recovery

On August 22, 2004, the Munch Museum's 1910 version of Madonna was stolen at gunpoint along with Munch's most famous work, The Scream, in a widely publicized daylight robbery. Both paintings were recovered by Oslo police on August 31, 2006, and the thieves were convicted. The recovered Madonna had sustained minor damage, including a tear and water marks, which were subsequently treated.

Legacy

The painting is one of Munch's most iconic images, exemplifying his exploration of love, sexuality, anxiety, and death, and is a key work of the Symbolist and early Expressionist movements.
