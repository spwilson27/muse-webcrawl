War of Chioggia
Date1378–1381
LocationAdriatic and Mediterranean Seas
ResultVenetian victory; Treaty of Turin

The War of Chioggia (1378–1381) was a conflict between the Republic of Venice and the Republic of Genoa, the climax of the long-standing Venetian–Genoese Wars fought over dominance of Mediterranean trade. It is named after the fishing port of Chioggia at the southern entrance to the Venetian Lagoon.

Course

Genoa, allied with Hungary and Padua, won an early victory at Pola in 1379, capturing the Venetian admiral Vettor Pisani's fleet. In August 1379 a Genoese fleet seized Chioggia itself, placing Venice under direct threat of blockade and starvation. Venice responded with an extraordinary mobilisation: the released Pisani and Carlo Zeno's returning fleet trapped the Genoese inside Chioggia by sinking blockships in the channels. After a long siege, the Genoese garrison surrendered in June 1380.

Aftermath

The Treaty of Turin (1381) formally ended the war with concessions on both sides, but Genoa never recovered its former commercial position, while Venice emerged as the dominant maritime power of the eastern Mediterranean for the following century.
