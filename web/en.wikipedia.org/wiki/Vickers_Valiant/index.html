The Vickers Valiant was a British high-altitude, four-engined jet strategic bomber designed by Vickers-Armstrongs in the late 1940s. It was the first of the RAF's "V bombers" to enter service, preceding the Avro Vulcan and Handley Page Victor, and it carried Britain's first operational nuclear weapons.

Development and service

Designed to Air Ministry specification B.35/46, the Valiant had a comparatively conservative crescent-wing design that allowed rapid development: it flew in May 1951 and entered squadron service in 1955. Valiants dropped Britain's first live atomic bomb in tests at Maralinga in 1956 and dropped the first British hydrogen bomb near Christmas Island in 1957 (Operation Grapple). During the Suez Crisis of 1956, Valiants bombed Egyptian airfields from Malta — the type's only conventional bombing operations.

Tanker and reconnaissance roles

Many Valiants were converted to aerial refuelling tankers and to strategic reconnaissance (Valiant B(PR) and B(PR)K.1 variants), and the RAF's tanker fleet was built almost entirely around the type by the early 1960s. From 1962 the V-force was switched to low-level penetration missions.

Retirement

The low-level role exposed a fatal weakness: in August 1964 a Valiant was found to have a cracked wing rear spar caused by metal fatigue, and inspection revealed the problem across the fleet. Repair was judged uneconomic, and all Valiants were withdrawn by January 1965, leaving the tanker role to be filled by converted Victors. Only one complete Valiant survives, XD818 — the aircraft that dropped the first British hydrogen bomb — preserved at the RAF Museum Cosford.
