Brauer group

The Brauer group classifies central simple algebras over a field up to Morita equivalence.

In mathematics, the Brauer group of a field K is an abelian group whose elements are Morita equivalence classes of central simple algebras over K, with addition given by the tensor product of algebras. It was defined by the algebraist Richard Brauer.

Definition

A central simple algebra over K is a finite-dimensional associative algebra that is simple and whose centre is exactly K. Two such algebras A and B are equivalent if they become isomorphic after tensoring with matrix algebras over K. The equivalence classes form a group Br(K) under tensor product; the inverse of a class is represented by the opposite algebra, and every element has finite order, so the Brauer group is a torsion group.

Examples

The Brauer group of an algebraically closed field is trivial, and that of a finite field is also trivial by Wedderburn's little theorem. The Brauer group of the real numbers is cyclic of order two, generated by the quaternions. For a non-archimedean local field, the Brauer group is isomorphic to the group of rationals modulo integers.

Cohomological description

The Brauer group can be identified with the second Galois cohomology group of the algebraic closure of K, and it plays a central role in class field theory and the study of rational points on algebraic varieties.
