Interoperability

Interoperability is a characteristic of a product or system to work with other products or systems. Although the term was initially defined for information technology to permit unrestricted exchange of information, it has broadened to cover organisational, political, and social factors in fields such as healthcare, transport, defence, and public administration.

Types

Syntactic interoperability concerns formats and protocols that let systems exchange data, such as shared communication standards. Semantic interoperability goes further: systems interpret the exchanged information in the same way, which requires common data models and vocabularies. Frameworks such as the European Interoperability Framework distinguish technical, semantic, organisational, and legal layers.

In computing

Interoperability in software is achieved through open standards, published APIs, and common file formats. It contrasts with vendor lock-in, in which proprietary formats and protocols bind users to one supplier. Competition policy has increasingly promoted interoperability — for example, requirements that large messaging platforms interoperate with smaller rivals.

Other domains

In healthcare, interoperability lets electronic health records move between providers. In the military, allied forces require interoperable communications and logistics. Railways pursue interoperability across national signalling and gauge differences.
