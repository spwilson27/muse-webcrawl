The Springfields were a British pop-folk vocal trio, active from 1960 to 1963, composed of Dusty Springfield, her brother Tom Springfield, and Reshad Feild (known as Tim Feild), who was later replaced by Mike Hurst. They were one of the first British groups to achieve success in the United States during the early 1960s, before the British Invasion.

History

Tom Springfield and Tim Feild had performed as the Kensington Squares; when Mary O'Brien joined them in 1960, the trio renamed themselves the Springfields, after a field in Kensington. Signed to Philips Records, they had UK hits with "Dear John" (1961) and "Breakaway" (1961), and their song "Silver Threads and Golden Needles" reached the US top 20 in 1962 — an unusual feat for a British act at the time — and earned them appearances in Nashville and on American television.

The group's album Folk Songs from the Hills (1963) drew on American folk and country material. Dusty Springfield, increasingly drawn to soul and pop, left the trio in late 1963 to begin a solo career; the Springfields disbanded, having their final UK hit with "Island of Dreams". Tom Springfield went on to write hits for the Seekers, including "Georgy Girl".
