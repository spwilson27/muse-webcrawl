Iliacus muscle

Iliacus muscle
OriginIliac fossa and sacrum
InsertionLesser trochanter of the femur
NerveFemoral nerve (L2–L4)
ActionFlexion of the hip

The iliacus is a flat, triangular muscle which fills the iliac fossa, the concave inner surface of the ilium of the pelvis. Together with the psoas major it forms the iliopsoas, the strongest flexor of the hip joint.

Structure

The iliacus arises from the upper two-thirds of the iliac fossa, the inner lip of the iliac crest, and the front of the sacroiliac ligaments and sacrum. Its fibres converge downward and laterally, joining the tendon of the psoas major, and insert into the lesser trochanter of the femur. It is supplied by the femoral nerve.

Function

With the psoas major, the iliacus flexes the thigh at the hip and flexes the trunk when the femur is fixed, as in rising from a lying position. It is active in walking, running, and maintaining posture. Tightness or injury of the iliopsoas complex is implicated in some cases of hip and lower-back pain, and a bursa beneath the muscle may become inflamed (iliopsoas bursitis).
