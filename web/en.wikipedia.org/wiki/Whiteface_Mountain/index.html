Whiteface Mountain

Whiteface Mountain is the fifth-highest mountain in New York State and one of the High Peaks of the Adirondack Mountains, rising to 4,867 ft (1,483 m). Located in the town of Wilmington, near Lake Placid, it is distinctive among the High Peaks for its developed summit, its toll road, and its ski area, which hosted the alpine skiing events of the 1980 Winter Olympics.

Features

The mountain's pale, landslide-scarred face — a great slide of exposed anorthosite rock — gives it its name. The Whiteface Mountain Veterans Memorial Highway, opened in 1935 and dedicated to the veterans of the First World War, climbs nearly to the summit, where a stone castle-like elevator building and a weather observatory stand. The summit offers a 360-degree view reaching, on clear days, to Montreal and Vermont.

Ski area

Whiteface Mountain Ski Area, operated by the New York State Olympic Regional Development Authority (ORDA), boasts the greatest vertical drop in eastern North America at about 3,430 ft. Its steep Olympic runs, including the trails cut for the 1980 downhill, and the glades of "The Slides" give it a reputation as one of the East's most challenging resorts. The mountain also hosted skiing at the 1932 games' era venues nearby and events of the 2023 Winter World University Games.

Climate research

The summit hosts an atmospheric research station of the University at Albany, part of a long-running programme studying acid rain and cloud chemistry that began in the 1960s and helped document the effects of air pollution on northeastern forests.
