The Asian Games, also known as Asiad, is a continental multi-sport event held every four years among athletes from all over Asia. The Games are organised by the Olympic Council of Asia (OCA) and are recognised by the International Olympic Committee. They are the second-largest multi-sport event in the world after the Olympic Games, regularly drawing around 10,000 athletes from 45 National Olympic Committees.

History

The Games grew out of the Far Eastern Championship Games (1913–1934) and the efforts of Indian IOC member Guru Dutt Sondhi, who proposed a pan-Asian competition after independence. The first Asian Games were held in New Delhi in 1951, with 489 athletes from 11 nations competing in six sports. The Asian Games Federation governed the event until 1982, when the Olympic Council of Asia took over.

Programme and hosts

The programme combines Olympic sports with regional disciplines such as kabaddi, sepak takraw, wushu and cricket; esports became a medal event at the Hangzhou Games. Notable editions include Tokyo 1958, Bangkok 1966/1970/1978, Beijing 1990, Guangzhou 2010, Incheon 2014 and Jakarta–Palembang 2018. China, Japan and South Korea have dominated the medal tables; China has topped the table at every Games since 1982. The next editions are scheduled for Aichi–Nagoya (2026) and Doha (2030).

Related events

The OCA also oversees the Asian Winter Games, Asian Indoor and Martial Arts Games, Asian Beach Games and Asian Youth Games.
