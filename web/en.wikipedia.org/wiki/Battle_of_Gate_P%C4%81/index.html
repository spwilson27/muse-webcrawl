The Battle of Gate Pā (Pukehinahina) was fought on 29 April 1864 during the Tauranga campaign of the New Zealand Wars. A British force of some 1,700 men suffered one of its worst defeats of the wars at the hands of about 230 Māori defenders.

The pā

Gate Pā was designed by Pene Taka Tuaia with deep anti-artillery bunkers (rua) covered by timber and earth, connected by trenches and designed to absorb bombardment and ambush an assault.

The battle

After the heaviest bombardment of any battle of the New Zealand Wars, a storming party of some 300 men breached the pā in the afternoon, believing it destroyed. Inside, defenders firing from hidden positions cut them down; British casualties were about 110, including many officers. The defenders slipped away overnight.

Legacy

Gate Pā is remembered for the defenders' code of conduct, drawn up by Hēnare Taratoa, prescribing humane treatment of wounded enemies — water was famously given to the wounded Colonel Booth. The British partially avenged the defeat at Te Ranga in June 1864.
