Glyndŵr Rising

The Glyndŵr Rising (1400–1415) was the last major Welsh rebellion against English rule, led by Owain Glyndŵr, who was proclaimed Prince of Wales in 1400. It is also known as the Welsh Revolt or the Last War of Independence.

Course of the revolt

Beginning as a local dispute with the English lord Reginald Grey of Ruthin, the rising spread rapidly across Wales. Glyndŵr's forces won notable victories at Hyddgen (1401) and Pilleth (1402), captured castles including Conwy, Harlech, and Aberystwyth, and summoned Welsh parliaments at Machynlleth, where Owain was crowned in 1404. The Tripartite Indenture of 1405 planned to divide Britain between Glyndŵr, Henry Percy, and Edmund Mortimer.

Decline

English campaigns under Henry IV and the future Henry V, together with a blockade on supplies and the recapture of the great castles by 1409, gradually crushed the revolt. Glyndŵr disappeared from record around 1415 and became a Welsh national hero and folk legend.
