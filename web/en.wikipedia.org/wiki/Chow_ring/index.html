Chow ring


Placeholder image: intersection of algebraic cycles

In algebraic geometry, the Chow ring of an algebraic variety is an analogue of the cohomology ring of a topological space, built from algebraic cycles modulo rational equivalence. It is named after Wei-Liang Chow, who developed the theory of Chow groups and intersection products in the mid-20th century, building on work of van der Waerden, Severi and others on intersection theory.

Definition

For a smooth variety X of dimension n, the Chow group Ak(X) is the free abelian group on the irreducible subvarieties (cycles) of dimension k, modulo the subgroup generated by divisors of rational functions on subvarieties of dimension k+1 (rational equivalence). The Chow ring A*(X) is the direct sum of the groups Ak(X) of codimension-k cycle classes, equipped with an intersection product defined by moving cycles into general position (the moving lemma) and intersecting them.

Examples

  • For projective space Pn, the Chow ring is Z[h]/(hn+1), where h is the class of a hyperplane; the class of a linear space of codimension k is hk.
  • The Chow ring of a point is Z.
  • For a smooth projective curve, A1 is the Picard group, and intersection numbers recover the degrees of divisors.

Relation to other theories

For smooth complex varieties there is a cycle map from the Chow ring to singular cohomology, and for cellular varieties (such as Grassmannians) it is an isomorphism. Chern classes of vector bundles live in the Chow ring, and Grothendieck's Riemann–Roch theorem relates Chow groups to K-theory. Chow rings are central to enumerative geometry: the famous count of 2,875 lines on a smooth quintic threefold, and its mirror-symmetry extensions, are computations in intersection theory of this kind.
