Hydroxide

Hydroxide is a diatomic anion with the chemical formula OH−, consisting of an oxygen atom and a hydrogen atom joined by a single covalent bond, carrying a net negative charge. It is one of the simplest polyatomic ions and the defining base of aqueous chemistry: solutions containing it are alkaline.

Properties

The hydroxide ion behaves as a strong base, readily accepting a proton to form water, and as a nucleophile in many organic reactions. In water it is heavily solvated and moves partly by the Grotthuss mechanism, giving it anomalously high ionic mobility. Its concentration defines pOH and, through the ion product of water, the pH of a solution.

Compounds

Metal hydroxides include the strongly basic, highly soluble hydroxides of the alkali metals — sodium hydroxide (caustic soda) and potassium hydroxide (caustic potash) — which are major industrial chemicals used in soap-making, paper, and chemical manufacture. Alkaline-earth hydroxides such as calcium hydroxide (slaked lime) are moderately soluble, while most transition-metal hydroxides are insoluble, colored solids precipitated from salt solutions by addition of base. Amphoteric hydroxides such as aluminium hydroxide dissolve in both acids and strong bases.
