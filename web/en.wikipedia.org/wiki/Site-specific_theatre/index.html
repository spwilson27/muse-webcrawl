Site-specific theatre

FieldTheatre and performance
RelatedImmersive theatre, promenade

Site-specific theatre is a theatrical production created for, and inseparable from, a particular non-theatrical location, such as a warehouse, hotel, forest or city street. The site shapes the staging, text and audience experience rather than serving as a neutral backdrop.

Characteristics

Site-specific works often exploit the history and architecture of their location and may involve promenade staging, in which the audience moves through the space. The term overlaps with environmental theatre, immersive theatre and found-space performance.

History

The practice grew with companies such as Brith Gof in Wales, which staged large works in industrial sites, and Punchdrunk, whose masked promenade productions such as Sleep No More brought immersive, site-responsive performance to wide audiences.

Criticism

Scholars debate how strictly 'specific' a work must be to its site: some distinguish site-specific work, which cannot be transferred, from site-generic or site-responsive work adapted to classes of locations.
