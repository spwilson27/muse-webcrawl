Haemophilia C

Haemophilia C, also known as plasma thromboplastin antecedent deficiency or Rosenthal syndrome, is a form of haemophilia caused by deficiency of clotting factor XI. Unlike haemophilia A and B, it is inherited in an autosomal pattern and affects both sexes.

Signs and symptoms

Bleeding is generally milder than in haemophilia A or B and often occurs after surgery, dental extraction, or trauma, particularly at sites of high fibrinolytic activity such as the mouth and urinary tract. Spontaneous joint bleeding is uncommon.

Genetics

The condition is caused by mutations in the F11 gene on chromosome 4. It is more common in Ashkenazi Jews, among whom the carrier frequency is high.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is by prolonged activated partial thromboplastin time and specific factor XI assay. Treatment includes antifibrinolytics such as tranexamic acid, fresh frozen plasma, and, where available, factor XI concentrate.
