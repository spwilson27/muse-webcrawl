Arsenic poisoning (arsenicosis) is a medical condition caused by elevated levels of arsenic in the body. Acute poisoning follows ingestion of a large dose, while chronic poisoning results from long-term exposure, most commonly through naturally contaminated drinking water.


Palmar keratoses in chronic arsenicosis

Signs and symptoms

Acute poisoning begins with severe gastrointestinal symptoms — vomiting, abdominal pain and profuse watery diarrhoea — and can progress to shock, delirium and death. Chronic exposure produces characteristic skin changes (raindrop pigmentation, palmar and plantar keratoses), peripheral neuropathy, diabetes and increased risk of cancers of the skin, lung and bladder, as well as cardiovascular disease. Mees' lines may appear on the nails.

Causes

The largest population at risk is exposed through groundwater drawn from shallow tube wells in arsenic-rich sediments, notably in Bangladesh — described by the World Health Organization as the largest mass poisoning in history — and in parts of India, China, Argentina and the United States. Occupational exposure occurs in smelting and pesticide manufacture, and arsenic has a long history of use in deliberate poisoning.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is by measuring arsenic in urine, hair or nails. Treatment centres on removing the exposure; chelating agents such as dimercaprol (British anti-Lewisite) and succimer increase excretion in acute cases, alongside intensive supportive care.
