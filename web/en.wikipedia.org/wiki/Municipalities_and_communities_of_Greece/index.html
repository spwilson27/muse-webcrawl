The municipalities and communities of Greece are the lowest level of government in the Greek administrative hierarchy. Following the Kallikratis reform of 2010 (Law 3852/2010), which took effect on 1 January 2011, the previous 1,034 municipalities and communities were merged into 325 municipalities (dhímoi, δήμοι). A further reform, the Kleisthenis I programme of 2018, split several municipalities, bringing the total to 332 from the 2019 local elections.

Before Kallikratis, Greek local government distinguished between municipalities, which were urban or larger settlements headed by a mayor (dhímarchos), and communities (koinótites), smaller rural units headed by a president. The 1997 Kapodistrias reform had already reduced the number of local authorities from nearly 6,000 to 1,034.

Structure

Each municipality is governed by a mayor and a municipal council elected for a five-year term. Municipalities are subdivided into municipal units (dhimotikés enótites), corresponding to the former municipalities and communities absorbed in the mergers, and these in turn into local communities. The municipalities belong to Greece's 13 regions (periféreies), with the exception of Mount Athos, an autonomous monastic community.

The Municipality of Athens is the largest by population, while many island and mountain municipalities have only a few hundred residents. The smallest municipalities by population include Agathonisi, Leipsoi and other small Aegean island communities.
