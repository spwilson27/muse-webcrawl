The American League Division Series (ALDS) is the quarterfinal round of the Major League Baseball postseason in the American League. Two best-of-five series are played between four qualifying teams, with the winners advancing to the American League Championship Series.

History

A Division Series was first played in 1981, when a players' strike split the season in two and MLB staged an extra playoff round between the first-half and second-half winners. The round became permanent in 1995 (first contested after the strike-shortened 1994 season was cancelled) when each league realigned into three divisions and a wild card team was added to the playoffs.

Format

Under the format adopted in 2022, the two best division winners receive byes to the ALDS, while the third division winner and the best wild card team emerge from the Wild Card Series to face them. The ALDS is played in a 2-2-1 home-field format, with the higher seed hosting games 1, 2 and 5. Teams from the same division may now meet in the round, a restriction that applied in the earlier format.

Notable series

Memorable ALDS matchups include the 1995 series between the Seattle Mariners and New York Yankees, decided by Edgar Martínez's walk-off double in Game 5, and the 2004 series in which the Boston Red Sox began their run to a World Series title.
