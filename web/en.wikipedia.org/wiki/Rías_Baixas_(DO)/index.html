Rías Baixas is a Spanish Denominación de Origen (DO) for wines located in the Rías Baixas region of Galicia, in northwestern Spain, along the Atlantic coast in the province of Pontevedra and part of A Coruña. The appellation, formally established in 1988, is best known internationally for dry white wines made from the Albariño grape.

Viticulture

The climate is Atlantic: mild and wet, with abundant rainfall. Vines are traditionally trained on granite pergolas (parras) raised above the ground, which keeps the grapes clear of damp soil and allows air to circulate, though modern vineyards also use vertical trellising on decomposed granite and sandy soils. The harvest is still largely done by hand by many small growers; the DO is characterised by an unusually high proportion of women winemakers and growers.

Wines

Wines labelled Rías Baixas must contain a minimum proportion of authorised white grapes. Single-varietal Albariño wines dominate exports; blends may include Loureiro, Treixadura, Caiño blanco, Godello, and Torrontés. The wines are typically pale, aromatic (white flowers, stone fruit, citrus), with racy acidity and a saline mineral note that pairs famously with the region's seafood. Small quantities of red wines from varieties such as Caiño tinto, Espadeiro, and Mencía are also produced.
