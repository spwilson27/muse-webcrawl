Afrika Bambaataa (born Lance Taylor, 17 April 1957) is an American DJ, rapper and producer from the South Bronx, New York City. He is regarded as one of the founding fathers of hip hop culture, credited — along with DJ Kool Herc and Grandmaster Flash — with helping to originate breakbeat DJing, and he coined the term "hip hop" in its cultural sense for many accounts. In the 1970s he founded the Universal Zulu Nation, an organisation promoting hip hop's elements and values of peace, unity, love and having fun.

Career

Raised in the Bronx River Houses, Taylor took the name Afrika Bambaataa after a 19th-century Zulu chief. A former gang member, he turned to organising parties that drew rival gangs together, building a vast record collection spanning funk, rock, reggae and electronic music. With the Soulsonic Force he released "Planet Rock" (1982), which fused Kraftwerk's "Trans-Europe Express" melody with Roland TR-808 beats and helped create electro-funk. Later recordings included "Renegades of Funk" and the anti-apartheid collaboration "Sun City". He also worked with James Brown, John Lydon and Bill Laswell.

Later life and allegations

Bambaataa remained an elder statesman of hip hop for decades and received honours including induction into the Bronx Walk of Fame's hip hop recognitions. In 2016, allegations of child sexual abuse were made against him by several men dating to the 1980s; he denied the allegations, and the Universal Zulu Nation removed him from its leadership and issued an apology. His reputation has since been heavily reassessed.
