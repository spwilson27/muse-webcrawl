Reaction rate


Reaction rates vary with concentration, temperature, and catalysts.

The reaction rate or rate of reaction is the speed at which a chemical reaction takes place, defined as proportional to the increase in the concentration of a product per unit time and to the decrease in the concentration of a reactant per unit time. Reaction rates are usually expressed in mol·L⁻¹·s⁻¹.

Rate law

For a reaction aA + bB → pP + qQ, the rate is often expressed empirically by a rate law, rate = k[A]^m [B]^n, where k is the rate constant and m and n are the partial orders of reaction, determined experimentally. The overall order is m + n.

Factors affecting rate

Factors include the nature of the reactants, concentrations, temperature (described by the Arrhenius equation — rates typically roughly double for each 10 °C rise), surface area of solids, pressure for gases, and the presence of catalysts, which lower the activation energy without being consumed. Light and solvent properties can also matter, notably in photochemistry.

Measurement

Rates are measured by monitoring concentration changes over time, using techniques such as spectroscopy, pressure measurement, titration, or stopped-flow methods for fast reactions. The study of reaction rates is the field of chemical kinetics.
