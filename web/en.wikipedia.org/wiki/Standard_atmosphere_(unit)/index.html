The standard atmosphere (symbol: atm) is a unit of pressure defined as 101,325 Pa. It is approximately equal to Earth's average atmospheric pressure at sea level.

The standard atmosphere was originally defined as the pressure exerted by 760 mm of mercury at 0 °C and standard gravity (gn = 9.80665 m/s2). It was used as a reference pressure for physical and chemical properties, and is related to the International Standard Atmosphere model of pressure and temperature variation with altitude.

History

In 1954 the 10th General Conference on Weights and Measures (CGPM) adopted the standard atmosphere for general use and affirmed its definition of exactly 1,013,250 dynes per square centimetre (101,325 Pa). In chemistry, the original definition of "standard temperature and pressure" used a reference pressure of 1 atm, though the IUPAC later changed its standard pressure to 100 kPa (1 bar) in 1982.

Related units

The technical atmosphere (at) is a different, now-obsolete unit defined as 1 kilogram-force per square centimetre (98,066.5 Pa). The torr is defined as 1/760 of a standard atmosphere.
