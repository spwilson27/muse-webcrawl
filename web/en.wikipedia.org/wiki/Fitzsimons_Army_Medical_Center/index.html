Fitzsimons Army Medical Center (FAMC) was a United States Army military facility located in Aurora, Colorado, a suburb of Denver. Established during the First World War, it grew into one of the Army's largest medical centres, specialising in tuberculosis care, before closing as a military installation in 1999 under the Base Realignment and Closure process. The site was redeveloped as the Anschutz Medical Campus of the University of Colorado.

History

The facility originated in 1918 as Army Hospital 21, built to treat soldiers returning from Europe, many suffering from tuberculosis and the effects of chemical weapons; Colorado's dry, sunny climate was thought beneficial for tuberculosis patients. In 1920 the hospital was renamed for First Lieutenant William T. Fitzsimons, a medical officer regarded as the first American officer killed in the First World War, who died in a bombing raid on a field hospital in France in September 1917.

A large new main hospital building (Building 500), then one of the largest military hospitals in the country, was completed in 1941. During the Second World War Fitzsimons treated large numbers of wounded servicemen. President Dwight D. Eisenhower was a notable patient: while vacationing in Colorado in September 1955 he suffered a heart attack and was treated at Fitzsimons, where a suite served as a temporary “Western White House” during his recovery. The centre was also a major teaching hospital, training physicians, nurses, and medical specialists.

Closure and redevelopment

Fitzsimons was selected for closure in the 1995 Base Realignment and Closure round, and the Army departed in 1999, with functions transferring principally to facilities such as Evans Army Community Hospital at Fort Carson. The 577-acre site was redeveloped by the Fitzsimons Redevelopment Authority into a bioscience and medical district centred on the University of Colorado's Anschutz Medical Campus, including the University of Colorado Hospital and Children's Hospital Colorado. The historic main building was preserved and repurposed, and the campus became one of the largest medical redevelopment projects in the United States.
