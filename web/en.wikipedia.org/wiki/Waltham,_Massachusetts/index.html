Waltham, Massachusetts

Waltham is a city in Middlesex County, Massachusetts, United States, situated on the Charles River about 11 miles (18 km) west of downtown Boston. Its population was 65,218 at the 2020 census. Waltham is historically significant as an early centre of the American Industrial Revolution and as the home of Brandeis University and Bentley University.

History

Settled in 1634 as part of Watertown, Waltham was incorporated as a separate town in 1738. In 1814, the Boston Manufacturing Company built in Waltham the first integrated textile mill in the United States — bringing spinning and weaving under one roof on one site, powered by the Charles River — an innovation that reshaped American manufacturing and gave rise to the "Waltham-Lowell system". Later in the century, the Waltham Watch Company (founded 1850) made the city world-famous for precision watchmaking, producing millions of watches and earning Waltham the nickname "Watch City". The company's methods of mass production with interchangeable parts became known abroad as the "American system of manufacturing".

Modern Waltham

After the decline of its traditional industries, Waltham reinvented itself as a technology and research centre on the Route 128 corridor, hosting offices and research facilities of numerous firms, as well as Brandeis University (founded 1948) and Bentley University. The Charles River Museum of Industry and Innovation preserves the city's industrial heritage in the Boston Manufacturing Company mill building.
