Trāyastriṃśa

Trāyastriṃśa (Sanskrit: त्रायस्त्रिंश, Pali: Tāvatiṃsa) is a heaven in Buddhist cosmology, the second of the six heavens of the desire realm (Kāmadhātu), situated on the summit of Mount Sumeru. Its name means "belonging to the thirty-three," referring to the thirty-three gods (devas) who are said to dwell there, headed by Śakra (Indra), lord of the devas.

Description

Trāyastriṃśa lies above the heaven of the Four Great Kings, which guards the slopes of Sumeru. At its centre is the city of Sudarśana, Śakra's capital, containing his palace Vejayanta and the Sudhammā assembly hall where the gods gather. The heaven is also the home of the Pārijāta wish-fulfilling tree.

According to Buddhist tradition, the Buddha ascended to Trāyastriṃśa during one rainy-season retreat to teach the Abhidharma to his mother, Queen Māyā, who had been reborn there, before descending to earth at Sankassa. The devas of Trāyastriṃśa live for a thousand years of their own reckoning, each day equal to a hundred human years, though they remain subject to death and rebirth within saṃsāra.

The thirty-three

The number thirty-three derives from Vedic tradition, which speaks of thirty-three gods — the Vasus, Rudras, Ādityas and others. Buddhist texts adapt the scheme, listing the inhabitants as the gods of the thirty-three classes over whom Śakra presides.
