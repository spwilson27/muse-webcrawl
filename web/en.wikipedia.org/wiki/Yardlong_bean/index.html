Yardlong bean

The yardlong bean is the common name of Vigna unguiculata subsp. sesquipedalis, a legume cultivated for its edible green pods. Other names include the long-podded cowpea, asparagus bean, snake bean, bora, bodie and Chinese long bean. Despite the common name, the pods are usually only about half a yard (45–50 cm) long; the subspecies name sesquipedalis ("one and a half feet") is closer to the typical length.

Cultivation

The plant is a vigorous climbing annual vine, a subspecies of the cowpea, grown widely in warm climates across South and Southeast Asia. It thrives in hot weather and produces pods rapidly; pods are harvested young and tender, before the seeds swell, and must be picked frequently.

Culinary use

Yardlong beans are used extensively in the cuisines of China, Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia, India and the Philippines. They are cut into short lengths and stir-fried, curried, or served raw in salads such as Thai som tam. Unlike common green beans, they have a denser, chewier texture and hold up well to strong seasonings and high heat.

Nutrition

The pods are a source of protein, vitamin A, vitamin C, folate and minerals, though less substantial than the mature seeds of other cowpeas.
