Jirau Dam

The Jirau Dam is a rock-fill embankment dam with an asphaltic core on the Madeira River in Rondônia, Brazil. Its hydroelectric power station is one of the largest in Brazil, with an installed capacity of about 3,750 megawatts. Construction began in 2008 and the plant was inaugurated in 2016, as part of the Madeira River hydroelectric complex with the Santo Antônio Dam downstream.

Design

The run-of-the-river project uses 50 bulb turbines, designed for low head and high discharge, intended to reduce reservoir size compared with conventional dams. The reservoir covers roughly 258–361 km² depending on level. Power is transmitted over long-distance lines to southeastern Brazil.

Construction and labour unrest

The project was built by Energia Sustentável do Brasil, a consortium led by Engie (formerly GDF Suez), Eletrosul, and others. Construction saw major worker riots and strikes in 2011–2013 over pay and conditions, with fires, arrests, and temporary shutdowns drawing national attention.

Environmental and social impact

Critics raised concerns about fish migration on the Madeira River, mercury, sediment, greenhouse-gas emissions from flooded vegetation, and impacts on Indigenous and riverine communities in Brazil, Bolivia, and Peru. Fish passages and monitoring programmes were required as mitigation, though their effectiveness is debated.
