The Geophysical Institute is a research institute at the University of Alaska Fairbanks, established by an act of the United States Congress in 1946 to study the aurora and other high-latitude geophysical phenomena. It has grown into one of the largest research units of the university.

Research

Research areas include space physics and aeronomy (especially the aurora borealis), atmospheric sciences, snow, ice and permafrost, seismology, volcanology, and remote sensing. The institute operates the Poker Flat Research Range, the world's largest land-based sounding rocket range, and the Alaska Satellite Facility, which downlinks and processes satellite radar data. It also hosts the Alaska Volcano Observatory jointly with state and federal partners and provides earthquake monitoring through the Alaska Earthquake Center.

History

Early auroral research at the institute involved scientists such as Sydney Chapman, who spent extended periods in Fairbanks. The institute's work expanded after the International Geophysical Year of 1957–58 and with the growth of satellite remote sensing.
