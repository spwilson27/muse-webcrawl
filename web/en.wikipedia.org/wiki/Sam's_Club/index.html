Sam's Club (legally Sam's West, Inc.) is an American chain of membership-only warehouse clubs owned by Walmart Inc. Named after Walmart founder Sam Walton of the Walton family, it was founded in 1983 and competes with Costco and BJ's Wholesale Club, selling groceries, general merchandise and bulk goods to members who pay annual fees (Club and Plus tiers).

History

The first Sam's Club opened in Midwest City, Oklahoma, in April 1983, targeting small-business owners. It grew by acquisition (including Pace Membership Warehouse in 1993) and expanded internationally to Mexico (as a Walmart de México venture) and China. In January 2018 Walmart abruptly closed 63 US clubs, converting some to e-commerce fulfilment centres, as part of a portfolio review.

Model

Clubs are large no-frills warehouses (~130,000 sq ft) with a limited assortment of bulk items, the private label Member's Mark, in-store cafés famous for low-priced pizza and hot dogs, and services such as Scan & Go checkout, fuel stations, pharmacies and optical centres. Unlike Walmart supercentres, entry requires membership, and the chain's home office is in Bentonville, Arkansas.
