Stagnation pressure

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A pitot tube senses stagnation (total) pressure at its forward-facing opening.

In fluid dynamics, stagnation pressure (or pitot pressure, or total pressure) is the pressure at a stagnation point in a fluid flow, where the flow is brought to rest isentropically. It is the sum of the static pressure and the dynamic pressure in incompressible flow, and is the quantity measured by a Pitot tube. Stagnation pressure is a key quantity in aerodynamics, since an aircraft's airspeed indicator is essentially a gauge comparing pitot (stagnation) and static pressure.

Incompressible flow

From Bernoulli's equation, along a streamline:

p₀ = p + ½ρv²

where p₀ is the stagnation pressure, p the static pressure, ρ the fluid density, and v the flow speed. The term ½ρv² is the dynamic pressure. At a stagnation point (such as the nose of a body or the leading edge of a wing), v = 0, so the local pressure equals p₀, the maximum pressure on the body in incompressible flow.

Compressible flow

For compressible flow the relation depends on the Mach number M and the ratio of specific heats γ:

p₀/p = (1 + (γ−1)/2 · M²)^(γ/(γ−1))

For air (γ = 1.4) ahead of a normal shock wave at supersonic speed, the stagnation pressure behind the shock — the quantity a pitot tube actually senses — is given by the Rayleigh pitot formula, which accounts for the loss of total pressure across the shock. Stagnation pressure must be distinguished from stagnation temperature, which is constant across a shock in adiabatic flow even though stagnation pressure drops.

Applications

  • Airspeed measurement by Pitot-static systems on aircraft
  • Wind tunnel and turbomachinery instrumentation
  • Compressor and turbine stage performance (total-to-total efficiency)
  • Flow metering and anemometry
