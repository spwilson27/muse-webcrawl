Memory sport

Memory sport is a collection of competitive disciplines in which athletes (mnemonists or memory athletes) memorise large quantities of information — playing cards, random digits, words, names and faces, dates and images — under time pressure, then recall them in order. The sport's premier event is the World Memory Championships, founded in 1991 by Tony Buzan and Raymond Keene.

Events and techniques

Standard events include memorising a shuffled deck of cards as fast as possible (the best times are around 13 seconds), the most binary digits or decimal digits in 5–60 minutes, lists of random words, names paired with faces, historical dates, and abstract images. Competitors rely not on photographic memory but on mnemonic systems: the method of loci (memory palace), in which items are converted to vivid images and placed along a familiar route; the major system and person–action–object (PAO) encodings for numbers and cards; and deliberate chunking.

Notable champions

Dominic O'Brien won the World Memory Championships eight times in the 1990s. Andi Bell, Ben Pridmore, and later Alex Mullen (the first American world champion, with three consecutive titles from 2015) and Johannes Mallow have dominated modern competition. Pridmore famously memorised a deck in under 30 seconds at world level, and current records include thousands of digits memorised in an hour. The sport was popularised for a general audience by Joshua Foer's book Moonwalking with Einstein (2011), written after he won the USA Memory Championship in 2006.
