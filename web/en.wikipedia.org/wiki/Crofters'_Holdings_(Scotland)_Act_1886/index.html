The Crofters' Holdings (Scotland) Act 1886 (49 & 50 Vict. c. 29), commonly called the Crofters' Act, is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that created legal definitions of crofting and crofters and granted small tenants in the Scottish Highlands and Islands security of tenure for the first time.

Background

The Act followed the Highland Clearances and decades of grievance over eviction and rack-renting, and came after the "Crofters' War" of agitation and rent strikes in the 1880s and the Napier Commission's inquiry of 1883–84 into the condition of crofters and cottars. Gladstone's government introduced the legislation amid mounting disorder and the election of crofter-supporting MPs.

Provisions

The Act gave qualifying tenants in the designated crofting counties:

  • Security of tenure — protection from eviction so long as conditions, such as paying rent and not subdividing the holding, were observed.
  • Fair rents — the right to have rent fixed by a new land court, the Crofters Commission, which also reviewed arrears and could cancel them.
  • Compensation for improvements and the right to bequeath the tenancy to a family member.

It did not, however, restore land lost in the Clearances or provide new holdings — a limitation addressed only partly by later legislation such as the Congested Districts (Scotland) Act 1897 and the Small Landholders (Scotland) Act 1911.

Legacy

The Act remains a foundation of Scots land law; crofting tenure it created still governs much land in the Highlands and Islands, and modern crofting law has been reformed by Acts of the Scottish Parliament in 2010 and subsequently. The Act is widely regarded as one of the most significant pieces of social legislation in modern Scottish history.
