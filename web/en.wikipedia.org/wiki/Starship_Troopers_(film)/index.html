Starship Troopers (film)

Directed by: Paul Verhoeven
Screenplay by: Edward Neumeier
Based on: Starship Troopers (1959) by Robert A. Heinlein
Starring: Casper Van Dien, Dina Meyer, Denise Richards, Jake Busey, Neil Patrick Harris, Michael Ironside
Release: 7 November 1997
Budget: about $100–105 million; Box office: about $121 million

Starship Troopers is a 1997 American military science fiction action film directed by Paul Verhoeven and written by Edward Neumeier, loosely based on Robert A. Heinlein's 1959 novel. Set in the 23rd century, it follows Johnny Rico and his fellow members of the Mobile Infantry in humanity's war against the insectoid Arachnids ("Bugs"). The film is a satire of militarism and fascism, presented in the glossy style of wartime propaganda, complete with faux news broadcasts ("Would you like to know more?") from the Federal Network.

Plot

In a future Earth governed by the militaristic Terran Federation — where citizenship, including the right to vote, must be earned through federal service — Buenos Aires school friends Johnny Rico, Carmen Ibanez and Carl Jenkins enlist after graduation. Rico joins the Mobile Infantry, Carmen becomes a starship pilot, and the psychic Carl enters military intelligence. When an Arachnid meteor destroys Buenos Aires, Earth declares war. The invasion of the Bug homeworld Klendathu is a disaster; Rico's unit, the Roughnecks, fights desperate battles on Tango Urilla and Planet P, where the capture of a "Brain Bug" turns the tide. Rico rises to lead the Roughnecks as the war continues.

Satire and reception

Verhoeven, who grew up in the Nazi-occupied Netherlands, and Neumeier intended the film as a critique of fascism: the Federation's uniforms, iconography and propaganda echo Nazi and wartime aesthetics, while the characters remain cheerfully oblivious. Many early critics took the film at face value as a violent action picture, and reviews were mixed; it earned an Academy Award nomination for Best Visual Effects for its pioneering CGI swarms. Its reputation has since risen sharply, and it is now widely regarded as a misunderstood satirical classic. The film spawned direct-to-video sequels, animated films and video games.
