Minutes

Minutes, also known as meeting minutes or protocols, are the written record of a meeting or hearing. They typically describe the event, list the attendees, and record the issues considered and decisions made. Minutes are usually taken by a designated secretary or minute-taker, either during the meeting or compiled afterwards from notes and recordings.

Content

Minutes commonly include the name of the organisation, the date, time, and place of the meeting, the names of those present and of those who sent apologies, confirmation and approval of the previous meeting's minutes, summaries of reports and discussions, the exact wording of motions proposed, seconded, and voted upon, election results, action items with responsible persons and deadlines, and the time of adjournment and date of the next meeting.

Purpose

Minutes serve as an institutional memory, inform absent members, provide evidence of decisions for legal and audit purposes, and drive follow-up actions. In corporate governance, minutes of board meetings are formal legal records; for many companies and public bodies, keeping minutes is a statutory requirement. Under parliamentary procedures such as Robert's Rules of Order, minutes record what was done rather than what was said, omitting the detail of debate except where required.

Approval

Draft minutes are typically circulated to participants and formally approved — sometimes with corrections — at the start of the next meeting, after which they are signed by the chair and archived as the official record.
