The Sultanate of Aussa (also spelled Awsa) was a kingdom that existed in the Afar region of eastern Ethiopia in the 18th to 20th centuries. It was considered to be the leading monarchy of the Afar people, to whom the other Afar sultanates — the sultanates of Girrifo, Dawe, Tadjoura and Rahaito — nominally acknowledged supremacy. Its capital was Asaita (Assayita), near the Awash River.

History

The sultanate was founded in 1734 when the Mudaito dynasty deposed the earlier Imamate of Aussa, which had been established centuries earlier by Afar and Harla peoples. The sultanate controlled the lower Awash valley and the caravan routes between the Ethiopian highlands and the Red Sea coast.

From the 1880s, Aussa negotiated treaties with Italy, and Sultan Mohammed Yayyo concluded agreements with the Italians. In 1936, during the Second Italo-Ethiopian War, the sultanate was absorbed into Italian East Africa, though the sultan was reinstated under British administration in 1943. Sultan Alimirah Hanfare was a prominent figure in the Ethiopian Empire; after the Derg seized power in 1974 he went into exile. The sultanate's traditional authority continues in ceremonial form in the modern Afar Region of Ethiopia.
