Stagecoach Group

Stagecoach Group is a British transport group based in Perth, Scotland. Founded in 1980 by siblings Brian Souter and Ann Gloag with their father, the company grew from a small family coach business into one of the largest bus operators in the United Kingdom, with extensive operations across England, Scotland and Wales. It operates local and regional bus services, intercity coach services under the Megabus brand, and the Scottish Citylink network, and formerly operated rail franchises.

History

The business began in 1980 when the Souter family used their father's redundancy payment to buy two second-hand coaches and began running express services from Dundee to London. Deregulation of express coaches and then of local buses in Great Britain during the 1980s gave the company the opportunity for rapid expansion through the acquisition of municipal and former National Bus Company subsidiaries. Stagecoach floated on the London Stock Exchange in 1993.

The group expanded internationally during the 1990s, at one point operating buses in the United States and Canada through its Coach USA subsidiary, in Hong Kong, and in several other countries; most overseas operations were later sold, and Coach USA was divested in 2019. In the UK the group also ran rail franchises, including South West Trains and East Midlands Trains, and a joint venture (Virgin Rail Group with Virgin) on the West Coast Main Line, before withdrawing from rail franchising in 2019 following a dispute with the Department for Transport over pension risk.

In 2022 the company was acquired by an infrastructure fund managed by DWS and delisted from the stock exchange, after shareholders rejected a rival merger proposal with National Express. Stagecoach remains the largest bus operator in the UK by market share, carrying roughly a billion passengers a year, and it operates the low-cost intercity brand Megabus, launched in 2003 with fares advertised from £1.
