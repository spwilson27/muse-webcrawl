Coins of the Canadian dollar

The coins of the Canadian dollar are produced by the Royal Canadian Mint and denominated in Canadian dollars (CAD) and the subdivision cents. Coins in circulation are 5 cents ("nickel"), 10 cents ("dime"), 25 cents ("quarter"), 50 cents (rarely circulated), $1 ("loonie") and $2 ("toonie"). The 1-cent coin ("penny") was withdrawn from distribution in 2013.

History

Canada's first domestic coinage was issued in 1858 by the Province of Canada; after Confederation in 1867 the decimal dollar coinage continued, initially struck in London and later at the Royal Mint's Ottawa branch, opened in 1908, which became the Royal Canadian Mint in 1931.

Design

The obverse of current coins bears a portrait of the reigning monarch; following the death of Queen Elizabeth II in 2022, coins bearing the effigy of King Charles III began entering circulation in 2023. The gold-coloured one-dollar coin, introduced in 1987, was nicknamed the "loonie" for its loon design, and the bimetallic two-dollar coin of 1996 became the "toonie".

Withdrawal of the penny

The 2012 federal budget announced the elimination of the penny because its production cost exceeded its face value. The Mint stopped distributing pennies in February 2013, and cash transactions are rounded to the nearest five cents.
