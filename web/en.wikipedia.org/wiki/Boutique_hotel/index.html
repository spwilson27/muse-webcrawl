Boutique hotel
Typical size10–100 rooms
Emerged1980s, New York and San Francisco
Key figuresIan Schrager, Bill Kimpton
Defining traitsIndividual design, personal service, urban or resort setting

A boutique hotel is a small hotel which typically has between 10 and 100 rooms in unique settings with upscale accommodations and individualized unique selling points. Distinguished from large chain hotels and motels by their personalised service and distinctive design, boutique hotels are often independently owned, though many major chains have launched boutique-style brands to compete in the segment.

History

The concept emerged in the early 1980s. Bill Kimpton opened the Hotel Bedford in San Francisco in 1981, founding Kimpton Hotels, while Ian Schrager's Morgans Hotel in New York (1984), designed by Andrée Putman, popularised the idea of the hotel as a stylish social destination. The term "boutique" — borrowed from small fashion shops — captured the contrast with the standardised product of the large chains.

Characteristics

Boutique hotels generally emphasise character over uniformity: individually styled rooms, strong interior design or architecture, a sense of place drawn from the neighbourhood, and food and beverage outlets — bars and restaurants — aimed at locals as well as guests. Many occupy converted historic buildings; others are new-build design statements. Sizes are usually small enough for staff to know guests by name.

Industry development

From the 2000s, major groups including Starwood (W Hotels), Marriott (EDITION, Autograph Collection), Hilton (Curio) and Accor (MGallery) developed "soft brands" and boutique lines to capture the segment, blurring the line between independent boutique properties and the chains they originally reacted against.
