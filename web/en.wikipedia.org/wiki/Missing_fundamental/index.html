Missing fundamental

The missing fundamental is a psychoacoustic phenomenon in which the brain perceives the pitch of a sound from its harmonic overtones even when the fundamental frequency itself is absent. A tone made of 200, 300, and 400 Hz components, for example, may be heard as 100 Hz.

Explanation

The effect demonstrates that pitch perception is not simply a matter of detecting the lowest frequency present. Theories involve pattern matching by the auditory system and periodicity analysis in the brain. It is exploited in telephones, which cannot reproduce low bass frequencies, and in small loudspeakers and pipe organs using resultant bass stops.

History

The phenomenon was noted by Giuseppe Tartini and studied by August Seebeck and Jan Schouten, who coined related terms such as residue pitch.
