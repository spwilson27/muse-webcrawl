Payment for ecosystem services

Payments for ecosystem services (PES), also known as payments for environmental services, are incentives offered to farmers or landowners in exchange for managing their land to provide some sort of ecological service. They have been defined as "a voluntary transaction for a well-defined ecosystem service, or a land-use likely to secure that service, by at least one buyer and one provider, conditional on the provision of the service." PES programmes promote the conservation of natural resources in the marketplace, internalising benefits that land stewards otherwise supply for free.

Concept

Ecosystem services — such as water purification by forests, carbon storage, pollination, flood control, and habitat for biodiversity — are essential to human well-being but are often unpriced, giving landowners little economic reason to protect them. PES attempts to convert these positive externalities into financial incentives: downstream water users pay upstream farmers to maintain forest cover; governments pay landowners to set aside habitat; carbon emitters purchase sequestration credits.

Examples

Costa Rica's pioneering Pago por Servicios Ambientales programme, established in 1997 and funded by fuel taxes and water fees, pays landowners for reforestation, forest protection, and sustainable management, and is credited with helping reverse national deforestation. Mexico operates a national hydrological services programme; China has run large "Grain for Green" conversions of cropland to forest; and the city of New York famously invested in watershed protection in the Catskills rather than build a far costlier filtration plant. REDD+ extends the idea internationally, paying for avoided deforestation to mitigate climate change.

Criticism

Evaluations find PES effectiveness mixed: additionality is hard to prove, payments may reward landowners who would have conserved anyway, conditionality is often weakly enforced, and poorly designed schemes can disadvantage poor households lacking formal land titles or crowd out intrinsic conservation motivations.
