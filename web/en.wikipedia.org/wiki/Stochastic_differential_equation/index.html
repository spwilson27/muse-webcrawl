Stochastic differential equation

A stochastic differential equation (SDE) is a differential equation in which one or more of the terms is a stochastic process, resulting in a solution which is also a stochastic process. SDEs are widely used to model systems affected by random noise, including stock prices, physical systems subject to thermal fluctuations, and population dynamics.

Formulation

A typical SDE has the form dXt = μ(Xt, t) dt + σ(Xt, t) dWt, where Wt is a Wiener process (Brownian motion), μ is the drift and σ the diffusion coefficient. Because Brownian paths are nowhere differentiable, the equation is interpreted through its integral form using stochastic calculus — most commonly the Itô integral, defined by Kiyosi Itô in the 1940s, or the Stratonovich integral common in physics.

Solutions

Under Lipschitz and growth conditions on μ and σ, an SDE has a unique strong solution. Some SDEs admit closed-form solutions: geometric Brownian motion (used in the Black–Scholes option pricing model), the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process for mean-reverting quantities, and the Langevin equation. In general, numerical methods such as the Euler–Maruyama and Milstein schemes are used.

Applications

SDEs underpin mathematical finance (option pricing, interest-rate models such as Vasicek and CIR), stochastic control, filtering theory, neuroscience, and epidemiology. The Fokker–Planck equation describes the evolution of the solution's probability density.
