The Eastern Wei (simplified Chinese: 东魏; traditional Chinese: 東魏; pinyin: Dōng Wèi) was a dynasty of northern China that followed the disintegration of the Northern Wei dynasty. It ruled from 534 to 550 and was one of the Northern dynasties during the Northern and Southern dynasties period.

History

In 534, after the warlord Gao Huan installed Emperor Xiaowu on the Northern Wei throne, the emperor fled west to the rival warlord Yuwen Tai. Gao Huan then enthroned a young imperial clansman, Yuan Shanjian, as Emperor Xiaojing and moved the capital east to Ye, while Yuwen Tai's regime in Chang'an became known as Western Wei. Thus Northern Wei split into Eastern and Western halves.

Throughout its existence, Eastern Wei was the stronger eastern state in territory and population, but real power lay with Gao Huan and, after his death in 547, his sons Gao Cheng and Gao Yang. Emperor Xiaojing was a figurehead; when Gao Cheng was assassinated in 549, Gao Yang moved quickly. In 550 he forced the emperor to abdicate and founded the Northern Qi dynasty, ending Eastern Wei. The deposed emperor was later poisoned.

Culture

Eastern Wei is noted for Buddhist art, particularly the sculpture associated with the Xiangtangshan Grottoes, patronised by the Gao family. The dynasty's legal and administrative practices were largely inherited by Northern Qi.
