Yoruba religion

RegionSouthwestern Nigeria, Benin, Togo; diaspora in the Americas
Supreme beingOlodumare (Olorun)
Sacred cityIlé-Ifẹ̀ (Ife)
Divination systemIfá

The Yoruba religion (Yoruba: Ìṣẹ̀ṣe) comprises the traditional religious and spiritual concepts and practices of the Yoruba people of West Africa. It is centred on veneration of a supreme creator, Olodumare, and a large pantheon of intermediary deities known as orishas (òrìṣà), together with reverence for ancestors. Through the Atlantic slave trade, Yoruba religion spread to the Caribbean and the Americas, where it gave rise to traditions such as Cuban Santería (Regla de Ocha), Brazilian Candomblé, and Haitian Vodou influences, and it continues to be practised in Nigeria, Benin, and throughout the Yoruba diaspora.

Beliefs

Olodumare, also called Olorun ("owner of the sky"), is the supreme, remote creator of the universe and source of àṣẹ — the life force and authority that animates all things. Because Olodumare is distant, worshippers approach the divine primarily through the orishas, who govern aspects of nature and human life. Notable orishas include Ṣàngó, deified king of Oyo and god of thunder and lightning; Ògún, god of iron and war; Ọ̀ṣun (Oshun), goddess of rivers, love, and fertility; Yemọja, mother goddess of the sea; Ọbàtálá, creator of human bodies; and Èṣù, the trickster messenger who carries sacrifices and prayers between humans and the divine. Each person is also believed to choose a destiny, governed by their orí (inner head), before birth.

Ifá divination

The Ifá corpus is the tradition's central body of sacred knowledge: an extensive oral literature of poems, myths, and proverbs organised into 256 chapters (odù), consulted by priests called babaláwo ("father of secrets") using palm nuts or a divining chain. Ifá was inscribed by UNESCO on its list of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. Creation in Yoruba cosmology begins at Ilé-Ifẹ̀, the sacred city where, according to myth, the world was created and from which humanity spread.

Practices and festivals

Worship involves prayer, offerings, sacrifice, drumming, song, and possession by orishas during festivals. Ancestor veneration is prominent, celebrated through Egungun festivals in which masked dancers represent returning ancestral spirits. Major festivals include the Ọ̀ṣun-Òṣogbo festival at the Sacred Grove of Osun in Osogbo, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and celebrations of Ṣàngó in Oyo. Yoruba religion coexists — and often syncretises — with Christianity and Islam, which now claim the majority of Yoruba adherents, though traditional practice remains culturally central.
