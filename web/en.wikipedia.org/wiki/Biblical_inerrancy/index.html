Biblical inerrancy
Related conceptsBiblical inspiration, biblical infallibility, literalism

Biblical inerrancy is the belief that the Bible, in its original manuscripts, is without error in all that it affirms, including matters of history and science. It is a doctrine held by many evangelical and fundamentalist Christians, and is distinguished from the related but distinct doctrine of biblical infallibility, which some apply more narrowly to matters of faith and practice.

History

Belief in the truthfulness of Scripture was near-universal in pre-modern Christianity, but the modern doctrine was formulated in response to nineteenth-century historical criticism and Darwinian evolution. Theologians at Princeton Seminary, including Charles Hodge and B. B. Warfield, developed a systematic defence of inerrancy. The doctrine was a defining issue of the fundamentalist–modernist controversy of the 1920s.

Chicago Statement

In 1978, over 200 evangelical leaders signed the Chicago Statement on Biblical Inerrancy, which affirmed that Scripture is without error in its original autographs and that this authority extends to all its teaching.

Criticism

Critics argue that apparent contradictions, anachronisms, and the process of textual transmission make the doctrine untenable, and that it rests on assumptions about the lost autographs. Roman Catholic teaching, expressed in Dei Verbum (1965), holds that Scripture teaches "without error" that which pertains to salvation.
