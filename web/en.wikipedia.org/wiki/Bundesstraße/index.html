Bundesstraße (German for "federal highway"), abbreviated B, is the classification for long-distance federal roads in Germany, ranking below the Autobahn motorways but above state roads (Landesstraßen). Bundesstraßen are identified by a black-on-yellow rectangular sign bearing the letter B and the road's number.

Characteristics

Unlike Autobahns, Bundesstraßen generally pass through towns and villages, have at-grade junctions and traffic lights, and have a default speed limit of 100 km/h outside built-up areas and 50 km/h within them. Some heavily trafficked sections have been upgraded to dual carriageway standard ("gelbe Autobahn", "yellow motorway"), sometimes designated with an "a" suffix or signed as Kraftfahrstraße with motorway-like rules.

History and numbering

The numbering system dates from the Weimar Republic's Fernverkehrsstraßen (1926) and the Reichsstraßen of the 1930s. Numbers were assigned geographically: single-digit roads are the great historic radials (B 1 west–east through Berlin, B 7, etc.), with two- and three-digit numbers filling in by region. After German reunification the network was extended into the new states, and some numbers were reallocated.

Austria

Austria formerly used the same term and signs for its federal roads; since 2002 they have been administered by the states and are called Landesstraßen B, retaining their numbers and yellow signs.
