Ten Lost Tribes

The Ten Lost Tribes were the ten of the Twelve Tribes of Israel that were said to have been deported from the Kingdom of Israel after its conquest by the Neo-Assyrian Empire around 722 BCE. These are the tribes of Reuben, Simeon, Dan, Naphtali, Gad, Asher, Issachar, Zebulun, Manasseh, and Ephraim—all but Judah and Benjamin, who formed the southern Kingdom of Judah (with many of the Levites). Claims of descent from the "lost tribes" have been proposed for various groups, including the Pashtuns of Afghanistan, the Igbo of Nigeria, the Beta Israel of Ethiopia, the Bene Israel of India, and Native American peoples.

Historical background

According to the Hebrew Bible, the united monarchy of Israel split after the death of Solomon into a northern kingdom (Israel) and a southern kingdom (Judah). Assyrian inscriptions of Sargon II claim that 27,290 inhabitants were deported from Samaria after its fall. Assyrian policy relocated conquered populations across the empire, and the deported Israelites were settled in regions such as Halah, Habor (on the Khabur River), and Media, where they largely assimilated into surrounding populations. Many northern Israelites also fled south to Judah, swelling Jerusalem's population.

Later traditions and claims

Jewish tradition, reflected in apocalyptic works such as 2 Esdras, held that the tribes survived beyond the river Sambatyon and would return in the messianic age. In the Middle Ages and early modern period, travellers reported "lost tribe" communities from Central Asia to the Americas; Menasseh ben Israel's 1650 work The Hope of Israel argued that Native Americans descended from the tribes. In the 19th century, the theory of British Israelism claimed that the peoples of Britain and northwestern Europe were descended from them. Modern genetic studies have generally not supported direct descent claims, though some groups, such as the Lemba of southern Africa, show evidence of partial Middle Eastern paternal ancestry.
