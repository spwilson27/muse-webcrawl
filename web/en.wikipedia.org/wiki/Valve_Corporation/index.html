Valve Corporation, also known as Valve Software, is an American video game developer, publisher and digital distribution company headquartered in Bellevue, Washington. Founded in 1996 by former Microsoft employees Gabe Newell and Mike Harrington, Valve achieved fame with its debut game Half-Life (1998) and went on to operate Steam, the dominant digital distribution platform for PC gaming, launched in 2003.

History

Half-Life, built on a heavily modified Quake engine, won widespread acclaim for its seamless storytelling and became a landmark first-person shooter. Valve fostered a modding community whose creations — Counter-Strike, Team Fortress and later Dota — it acquired and developed into major franchises. Half-Life 2 (2004) debuted alongside Steam and the Source engine; Steam initially met resistance as a mandatory activation system but grew into the largest PC game storefront, with tens of millions of users and a vast third-party catalogue.

Products and culture

Subsequent titles include the Portal series, Left 4 Dead, Dota 2 and the VR flagship Half-Life: Alyx (2020). Valve has also developed hardware: the Steam Machine initiative, the Valve Index virtual-reality headset, and the commercially successful handheld Steam Deck (2022), which runs the Linux-based SteamOS. The company is famous for its flat organisational structure, documented in a leaked employee handbook: employees choose their own projects, and there are (formally) no managers or job titles. Valve remains privately held and is considered among the most profitable companies per employee in the industry.
