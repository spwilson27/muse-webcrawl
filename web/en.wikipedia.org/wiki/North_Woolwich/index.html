Overview

North Woolwich is a district in the London Borough of Newham, in east London. It lies on the north bank of the River Thames, opposite Woolwich, to which it is connected by the Woolwich Ferry and the Woolwich foot tunnel. Historically part of the parish of Woolwich in Kent — an anomaly explained by medieval landholdings — it was transferred to London administration in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries.

History

North Woolwich developed in the Victorian era with the arrival of the railway in 1847 and the opening of pleasure gardens intended to rival those south of the river. The Royal Victoria Dock brought industry and housing, and the area was heavily bombed during the Second World War. The King George V Dock and, later, London City Airport reshaped the district.

Transport

The Woolwich Ferry has carried vehicles and pedestrians across the river since 1889. North Woolwich station closed in 2006, and the former station building houses a small museum; the area is now served by King George V station on the Docklands Light Railway.
