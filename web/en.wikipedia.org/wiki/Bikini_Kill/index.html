Bikini Kill is an American punk rock band formed in Olympia, Washington, in October 1990. The group consists of singer and songwriter Kathleen Hanna, guitarist Billy Karren, bassist Kathi Wilcox, and drummer Tobi Vail. The band pioneered the riot grrrl movement, with feminist lyrics and fiery performances that encouraged women to take a front-and-centre role in punk culture.

History

Hanna, Vail, and Wilcox met as students at The Evergreen State College. They began publishing a feminist zine, also called Bikini Kill, before forming the band with Karren. Their self-released demo tape Revolution Girl Style Now! appeared in 1991, followed by a split LP with the British band Huggy Bear in 1993.

The band released two full-length studio albums, Pussy Whipped (1993) and Reject All American (1996), on the independent label Kill Rock Stars. Their best-known song, "Rebel Girl", became an anthem of the riot grrrl movement. Bikini Kill actively encouraged female audience participation, famously asking "girls to the front" at concerts, and distributed lyric sheets so that the words would not be lost in performance.

Breakup and reunion

Bikini Kill disbanded in 1997. Hanna went on to front Le Tigre and The Julie Ruin; Vail, Wilcox and Karren performed in other projects including The Frumpies. The band reunited for a one-off performance in 2017 and undertook reunion tours beginning in 2019.

Legacy

The band is widely credited with making feminism a central, confrontational presence in 1990s underground music, and its archives were acquired by the Fales Library at New York University as part of the Riot Grrrl Collection.
