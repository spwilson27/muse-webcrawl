Steinway & Sons

Steinway & Sons, also known as Steinway, is a German-American piano company founded in New York City in 1853 by the German immigrant Heinrich Engelhard Steinweg (later Henry E. Steinway) and his sons. It is widely regarded as one of the finest piano makers in the world, and its grand pianos are the instrument of choice of most concert pianists.

History

The company grew rapidly from its Manhattan workshop, opening a factory in Queens, New York, and later a second factory in Hamburg, Germany (1880), which supplies the world outside the Americas. Steinway developed many foundational piano innovations, including the overstrung scale and the cast-iron frame designs that became industry standards, accumulating numerous patents.

Products

Steinway builds grand and upright pianos largely by hand; a grand piano takes nearly a year to complete. The flagship is the Model D concert grand. The company also markets the Boston and Essex lines, designed by Steinway but built by other manufacturers, and the Spirio self-playing piano system.
