Local area network

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Diagram of a small LAN: several computers and a printer connected through a switch and router]

A local area network (LAN) is a computer network that interconnects computers within a limited area such as a residence, school, laboratory, university campus or office building. By contrast, a wide area network (WAN) covers a much larger area and typically leases telecommunications circuits.

The two dominant LAN technologies are wired Ethernet and wireless Wi-Fi (WLAN). Within the LAN, switches forward traffic between devices, printers and servers are shared, and a router usually provides the single connection outward to the WAN and the Internet. Devices on the same LAN are typically given private IP addresses and are reachable to each other directly, while outsiders see only the router's public address.

LANs emerged in the late 1970s with technologies such as ARCNET and Ethernet, replacing terminal-to-mainframe wiring with peer networking. LAN parties, office file sharing and home networks followed; today the distinction between a home LAN and the cloud services it reaches is increasingly blurred, but the local network remains the unit of trust and administration inside a building.
