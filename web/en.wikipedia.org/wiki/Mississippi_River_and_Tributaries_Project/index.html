Mississippi River and Tributaries Project

The Mississippi River and Tributaries Project (MR&T) is a flood-control and navigation project of the United States Army Corps of Engineers for the lower Mississippi River valley, authorized by the Flood Control Act of 1928 following the catastrophic Great Mississippi Flood of 1927.

Components

The project combines some 3,500 miles of levees, floodways such as the Birds Point–New Madrid and Morganza floodways, channel improvements and bank stabilization, drainage structures, and tributary reservoirs and pumping plants across the Yazoo, St. Francis, White, Arkansas and Red River basins. The Old River Control Structure, completed in 1963, regulates the distribution of flow between the Mississippi and the Atchafalaya River.

Purpose and performance

The MR&T is designed to protect the alluvial valley from the "project flood", larger than the 1927 flood, while maintaining a navigation channel. The system was severely tested in the floods of 1973, 2011 and 2019, when floodways were operated to relieve pressure on the levees protecting cities such as New Orleans and Baton Rouge.
