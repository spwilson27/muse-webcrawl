The octocontrabass clarinet is the largest, longest, and lowest member of the clarinet family. It is in B♭, pitched three octaves below the standard soprano clarinet. Only a single experimental instrument is known to have been built.

The one octocontrabass clarinet was constructed by the Belgian instrument maker Charles Houvenaghel for the Paris firm Leblanc in the 1930s, as part of a complete family of clarinets extending the range of the family downward. The instrument stands roughly 2.4 metres tall and its lowest notes descend to frequencies so low that individual vibrations become perceptible rather than a clear pitch.

Context

Leblanc also produced the smaller octocontralto clarinet in E♭. The giant clarinets were conceived in the era when makers extended wind families to extremes, parallel to instruments such as the octobass in the string family. The unique octocontrabass instrument is held in museum collections and has been demonstrated and recorded on rare occasions.
