History of mathematical notation

The history of mathematical notation describes the evolution of the symbols used to express mathematics, from tally marks and ancient number systems to the compact notation of modern algebra and calculus.

Ancient systems

Egyptians used hieroglyphic numerals, Babylonians a sexagesimal positional system, and Greeks alphabetic numerals. Hindu–Arabic numerals, including zero, developed in India and spread through the Islamic world to Europe by the late Middle Ages.

Symbolic algebra

In the 16th and 17th centuries, François Viète introduced letters for unknowns and parameters; Robert Recorde invented the equals sign (1557); and Descartes standardised exponents and the x, y, z convention. Leibniz and Newton developed rival notations for calculus — Leibniz's ∫ and d/dx prevailed.

Modern notation

The 19th and 20th centuries added set theory symbols (Cantor, Peano), logic notation, vector and matrix notation, and Einstein summation, producing the dense symbolic language of contemporary mathematics. See also Mathematical notation.
