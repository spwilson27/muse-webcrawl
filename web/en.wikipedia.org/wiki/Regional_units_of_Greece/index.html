Regional units of Greece

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
The 74 regional units of Greece

The regional units (Greek: περιφερειακές ενότητες, perifereiakés enótites; singular περιφερειακή ενότητα, perifereiakí enótita) are the second-level administrative subdivisions of Greece. There are 74 regional units, comprising the country's 13 regions (peripheries). They were established by the Kallikratis Programme, which entered into force on 1 January 2011, largely replacing the former prefectures (nomoi).

Administration

Unlike the prefectures they replaced, the regional units have no elected council or prefect of their own. They are administered as subdivisions of their region: each regional unit is headed by a deputy regional governor (antiperifereiarchis), who is a member of the winning ticket in regional elections and is assigned to the unit by the elected regional governor. The regional units retain geographical and administrative functions, such as the organisation of state services and electoral districts, within the authority of the region.

Geography

Most regional units correspond closely to the former prefectures. In Attica, the former Athens and Piraeus prefectures were divided into eight smaller regional units (e.g. Central Athens, North Athens, South Athens, West Athens, Piraeus and Islands). Other prefectures were split, such as Magnesia and the Sporades, and Achaea and Elis arrangements in the Peloponnese, to reflect geography and population.

The regional units range greatly in population: the Regional Unit of Central Athens has over one million residents, while some island units, such as Ithaca or Ikaria, have only a few thousand. Each regional unit is in turn divided into municipalities (demoi), the first level of local government.
