Sour beer

Sour beer is beer which has an intentionally acidic, tart or sour taste. In Belgium, sour beers are a long-established tradition, produced by allowing wild yeast and bacteria — notably Brettanomyces yeast and Lactobacillus and Pediococcus bacteria — to enter the brew, either through spontaneous fermentation in open coolships or through ageing in wooden vessels that harbour the microbes. In modern craft brewing, sour flavours are also produced more quickly by "kettle souring", in which lactobacillus is added to the wort before boiling.

Styles

  • Lambic — spontaneously fermented wheat beer from Belgium's Pajottenland, including straight lambic, fruit lambic (kriek, framboise) and blended gueuze.
  • Flanders red ale and Oud bruin — oak-aged ales with lactic and acetic character.
  • Berliner Weisse — a low-alcohol, sharply lactic German wheat beer, traditionally served with flavoured syrups.
  • Gose — a Leipziger style soured with lactobacillus and flavoured with coriander and salt.
  • American wild ale — a broad modern category of barrel-aged and mixed-culture beers.

Production and flavour

The souring organisms produce lactic acid (a clean, yoghurty tartness) and, with oxygen exposure, acetic acid (vinegar notes). Brettanomyces contributes complex "funky" aromas and slowly ferments sugars other yeasts cannot. Traditional examples can take years to mature; kettle-soured "quick sours" reach the market in days and dominate the contemporary fruited sour and "pastry sour" segments of craft beer.
