Jangle pop is a subgenre of pop rock and alternative rock that emphasizes bright, chiming guitar sounds — the "jangle" — typically produced by un-effected or lightly processed 12-string electric guitars, especially the Rickenbacker, in emulation of the 1960s sound of the Byrds. It flourished in the 1980s college-rock scene.

Characteristics

Jangle pop songs favor arpeggiated, ringing guitar lines over distortion, upbeat tempos, and melodic hooks, often combined with introspective or literate lyrics inherited from post-punk. The term "jangle" derives from Bob Dylan's "Mr. Tambourine Man" ("in the jingle jangle morning"), famously covered by the Byrds.

History

The genre crystallized around 1980–1983 with Athens, Georgia bands such as R.E.M. and the dB's, British groups like the Smiths (whose guitarist Johnny Marr built his style on layered, chiming guitars), and Australia's the Go-Betweens. Jangle pop was a core ingredient of college rock and the British C86 scene, and its sound fed directly into indie pop and later indie rock revivalists.
