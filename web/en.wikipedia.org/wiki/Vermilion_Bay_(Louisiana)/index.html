Vermilion Bay is a bay in south-central Louisiana in the United States, on the northern shore of the Gulf of Mexico. It lies along the coasts of Vermilion and Iberia parishes, west of Atchafalaya Bay and east of Cameron Parish, and is connected to the Gulf through Southwest Pass. Marsh Island separates Vermilion Bay from the open Gulf waters to the south.

Geography

The bay is a shallow estuarine system fed by rivers and bayous of the Acadiana region, including the Vermilion River. Its waters range from fresh to brackish to saline depending on season and river discharge. East Cote Blanche Bay and West Cote Blanche Bay lie to the east, and the system forms part of the wider Chenier Plain and deltaic wetlands of coastal Louisiana. The Intracoastal Waterway crosses the area, and the port of Intracoastal City sits on the bay's western reaches.

Ecology and economy

Vermilion Bay's marshes and oyster reefs support important fisheries for shrimp, blue crab, oysters and finfish such as red drum and speckled trout. The surrounding wetlands provide wintering habitat for migratory waterfowl on the Mississippi Flyway. Like much of coastal Louisiana, the bay's shoreline suffers from land loss caused by subsidence, sea-level rise, canal dredging and reduced sediment delivery from the leveed Mississippi River. The bay area is also active in oil and gas production, with rigs and service ports scattered through the marsh.
