Tangent bundle

In differential geometry, the tangent bundle of a differentiable manifold M is the disjoint union of the tangent spaces at every point of M, equipped with a natural topology and smooth structure making it itself a manifold of twice the dimension. Denoted TM, it comes with a projection π : TM → M sending each tangent vector to its base point. The tangent bundle is the prototypical example of a vector bundle: each fibre π−1(p) = TpM is a vector space.

Structure

If M has local coordinates (x1, …, xn), tangent vectors have coordinates (x1, …, xn, v1, …, vn) giving TM the structure of a 2n-dimensional smooth manifold. Smooth sections of the tangent bundle are vector fields. The tangent bundle is trivial (a product M × Rn) only for parallelizable manifolds such as Lie groups and the spheres S1, S3, S7; the hairy ball theorem, for instance, says the tangent bundle of S2 has no nowhere-vanishing section.

Uses

In Lagrangian mechanics, configurations and velocities together form points of the tangent bundle, on which the Lagrangian is defined; the dual cotangent bundle T*M is the phase space of Hamiltonian mechanics. The total space TM carries canonical structures (such as the vertical and horizontal subbundles defined by a connection) used throughout geometric mechanics and gauge theory. Associated bundles — tensor bundles, the frame bundle — are built from the tangent bundle, and characteristic classes such as the Euler and Pontryagin classes measure its twisting.
