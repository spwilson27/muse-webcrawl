Persuasive definition

Persuasive definition
Concept byCharles Stevenson (1938)
FieldPhilosophy of language, argumentation theory
MechanismAltering a term's descriptive meaning while retaining its emotive meaning

A persuasive definition is a form of stipulative definition which purports to state the "true" or commonly accepted meaning of a term, while in reality it reshapes that meaning to advance the definer's argument. The concept was introduced by philosopher Charles Stevenson in his 1938 article "Persuasive Definitions" and developed in his book Ethics and Language (1944).

Mechanism

Stevenson observed that many words have both a descriptive meaning and an emotive meaning — a tendency to express or evoke attitudes. A persuasive definition changes the descriptive content attached to a term while keeping its emotive charge intact, so that approval or disapproval transfers to a new referent. Defining "true courage" as, say, only actions performed for one particular cause keeps the positive glow of "courage" while quietly excluding rival cases.

Examples

Political and moral vocabulary is the classic territory: rival definitions of "democracy", "freedom", "justice", "art" or "torture" in public debate often work persuasively rather than descriptively. The related rhetorical pattern "No true Scotsman" is a familiar fallacious form of the move. Persuasive definitions are not always fallacious — reformers may consciously propose redefinitions — but they are deceptive when presented as mere reports of established usage.
