Algor mortis

Algor mortis
From Latin algor (coldness) + mortis (of death)
Stage: Second stage of death
Related: Pallor mortis, livor mortis, rigor mortis, decomposition

Algor mortis (from Latin algor 'coldness' and mortis 'of death'), the second stage of death, is the change in body temperature post mortem, until the ambient temperature is matched. The decrease in temperature follows a somewhat linear progression: about 1.5 °F (0.83 °C) per hour in typical conditions, though the rate varies greatly.

Process

After circulation stops, the body no longer generates or distributes metabolic heat effectively, and it cools toward environmental temperature, generally following Newton's law of cooling with an initial plateau. Rectal temperature is traditionally measured at a scene; in modern forensic practice ocular and other methods are also studied.

Forensic use

Because cooling is roughly predictable, algor mortis is used by forensic pathologists to estimate the post-mortem interval (time since death), most usefully within the first 24 hours. Accuracy is limited by variables: body size and clothing, ambient temperature and wind, immersion in water, fever or hypothermia at death, and cause of death.

Related signs

Other early post-mortem changes include pallor mortis (paling), livor mortis (pooling of blood) and rigor mortis (stiffening), followed by decomposition (putrefaction and decay).
