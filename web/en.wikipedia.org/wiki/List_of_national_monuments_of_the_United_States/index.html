List of national monuments of the United States

In the United States, a national monument is a protected area that may be created from any land owned or controlled by the federal government by proclamation of the President of the United States under the Antiquities Act of 1906, or by an act of Congress. National monuments protect a wide variety of natural and historic resources, including objects of historic and scientific interest.

The first national monument, Devils Tower in Wyoming, was proclaimed by President Theodore Roosevelt in 1906. There are more than 130 national monuments in the United States, managed variously by the National Park Service, the Bureau of Land Management, the U.S. Forest Service, the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, and other agencies.

History and legal debates

The Antiquities Act gives presidents broad discretion to reserve federal lands. Several designations have proved controversial, particularly very large monuments in the West. President Donald Trump reduced the size of Bears Ears and Grand Staircase–Escalante in 2017; President Joe Biden restored their boundaries in 2021. Questions about whether a president may substantially reduce a monument remain legally unsettled.
