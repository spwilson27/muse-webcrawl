The Kalahari Desert is a large semi-arid sandy savanna in Southern Africa extending about 900,000 square kilometres, covering much of Botswana, parts of Namibia, and regions of South Africa. Unlike a true desert, it receives 110–200 millimetres of rain annually in most areas and supports a great variety of plants and animals.

Geography

The Kalahari basin is a vast interior plateau covered by deep sand sheets with parallel dunes in its drier south-west. The Okavango River empties into the Okavango Delta at its north-west, a seasonal wetland of global ecological importance, while the Makgadikgadi salt pans lie to the north-east.

People and wildlife

The San (Bushmen) have lived in the Kalahari for tens of thousands of years as hunter-gatherers. Wildlife includes lions, meerkats, gemsbok, springbok, and the black-maned Kalahari lions; protected areas include the Central Kalahari Game Reserve and the Kgalagadi Transfrontier Park.
