Saint Moluag

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[hero image: Saint Moluag]
Lismore, Moluag's island see

Saint Moluag (c. 530 – 25 June 592), also written Mo Luóc and known in Latin as Moluocus, was a Scottish missionary and contemporary of Saint Columba, whom legend portrays as a friendly rival. He founded the great monastery on the Isle of Lismore in Argyll, which later became the seat of the medieval Bishop of Argyll, and is credited with founding churches at Mortlach and Rosemarkie in northern Scotland. His feast day is 25 June.

Life

Moluag was an Irish noble of the Dál nAraidi, educated at Bangor under Saint Comgall, who also taught Columba. According to tradition, Moluag and Columba raced across the sea to claim the Isle of Lismore; seeing Columba's boat ahead, Moluag cut off his own finger and threw it ashore so that he might claim the island by "blood and flesh" before his rival landed. He evangelised among the Picts of the Great Glen and founded some hundred churches, with outposts attributed at Mortlach in Banffshire and Rosemarkie in Ross, as well as Alyth in Perthshire. He died on 25 June 592 at Rosemarkie, according to the Annals of Tigernach.

Legacy

Lismore Cathedral (St Moluag's Cathedral) preserves part of the medieval church of the Bishops of Argyll. The saint's pastoral staff, the Bachuil Mòr ("Great Staff"), survives in the keeping of hereditary custodians, the Livingstone family of Lismore — a rare survival of an early Scottish saint's relic. Churches dedicated to Moluag include those at Clatt, Mortlach, and Tarland in Aberdeenshire, and the parish of Alyth. His name survives in Scottish place names and in the modern Gaelic form Mo-Luag.
