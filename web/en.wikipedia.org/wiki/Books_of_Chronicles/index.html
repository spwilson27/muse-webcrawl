The Books of Chronicles are the two books of the Hebrew Bible and Christian Old Testament known as 1 Chronicles and 2 Chronicles. In the Hebrew Bible they form a single work, Divrei ha-Yamim ("The Words of the Days"), placed at the end of the Ketuvim (Writings); the division into two books was made in the Greek Septuagint, where they were called Paralipomena, "the things left out" (of Samuel and Kings) — a title St Jerome considered misleading and replaced with "Chronicle".

Structure

1 Chronicles

Chapters 1–9 consist of genealogies from Adam to the post-exilic community, giving special prominence to Judah, Levi and Benjamin. Chapters 10–29 narrate the death of Saul and the reign of David: his anointing, wars, census, and above all his extensive preparations for building the Temple — amassing materials, organising the priestly and Levitical courses, and charging Solomon with the work.

2 Chronicles

Chapters 1–9 cover Solomon's reign, the construction and dedication of the Temple in Jerusalem. Chapters 10–36 trace the kings of Judah from Rehoboam to Zedekiah, concentrating on rulers who upheld Temple worship — Asa, Jehoshaphat, Hezekiah and Josiah receive extended, largely favourable treatment — and end with the fall of Jerusalem and Cyrus's edict of restoration.

Authorship and date

Jewish tradition ascribed the work to Ezra. Modern scholarship places its composition in the late fourth or third century BC, in the Persian- or early Hellenistic-era province of Yehud, and treats it as the product of Temple circles. Chronicles once was thought to form a single history with Ezra–Nehemiah by the same author ("the Chronicler"); many scholars now distinguish them, though the question remains debated.

Relation to Samuel–Kings

Chronicles covers the same ground as Samuel and Kings but recasts it: northern Israel virtually disappears after the schism; David's sins are omitted while his Temple arrangements are expanded; and a doctrine of immediate retribution replaces the long accumulative judgement of Kings. Its final verses overlap the opening of the Book of Ezra.
