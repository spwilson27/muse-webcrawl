55P/Tempel–Tuttle

Designation55P/Tempel–Tuttle
Orbital period33.2 years
DiscoverersWilhelm Tempel (1865); Horace Tuttle (1866, independent)
Parent body ofLeonid meteor shower

55P/Tempel–Tuttle is a periodic comet with an orbital period of about 33 years, best known as the parent body of the Leonid meteor shower. It was discovered independently by Wilhelm Tempel in December 1865 and Horace Parnell Tuttle in January 1866, and was later identified with comets observed in 1366 and 1699.

Orbit

The comet travels in a retrograde orbit inclined about 162 degrees to the ecliptic, reaching perihelion just inside Earth's orbit. Its 33-year period is near a resonance with Jupiter's, which helps concentrate the dust trails that produce Leonid storms.

Leonid meteor storms

When Earth passes through dense debris trails left by the comet's recent returns, the Leonids can erupt into storms of thousands of meteors per hour, as occurred in 1833, 1866, 1966, and 2001–2002. The comet last reached perihelion in 1998 and is due to return in the early 2030s.
