55P/Tempel–Tuttle is a retrograde periodic Halley-type comet with an orbital period of 33 years. It was discovered independently by Ernst Wilhelm Leberecht Tempel on December 19, 1865, and by Horace Parnell Tuttle on January 6, 1866. It is the parent body of the Leonids meteor shower.

Orbit and observations

The comet travels in a retrograde orbit inclined about 163° to the ecliptic, with perihelion just inside Earth's orbit at about 0.977 AU and aphelion near 19.7 AU, roughly the orbit of Uranus. It was observed in 1366 and 1699 in earlier apparitions identified retrospectively, but was lost after 1866 because its period had been miscalculated; it was recovered in 1965 using refined orbital computations. Its most recent perihelion passage occurred on February 28, 1998.

Leonid meteor storms

Debris shed by the comet along its orbit produces the annual Leonid meteor shower each November. When Earth passes near fresh dust trails shortly after the comet's perihelion, activity can escalate into spectacular meteor storms, as occurred in 1833, 1866, 1966, and 1999–2002, with zenithal hourly rates reaching many thousands.
