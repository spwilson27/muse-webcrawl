55P/Tempel–Tuttle is a periodic Halley-type comet with an orbital period of roughly 33 years. It was discovered independently by Ernst Wilhelm Leberecht Tempel on 19 December 1865 and by Horace Parnell Tuttle on 5 January 1866. The comet is best known as the parent body of the Leonid meteor shower, which produces notable meteor storms when Earth passes through dense, recently shed debris trails near the comet's perihelion returns.

Orbit and observations

The comet follows a retrograde orbit with a period of about 33.2 years. Historical observations have been traced to earlier apparitions, including returns in 1366 and 1699, and the comet was recovered in 1997 ahead of its 25 February 1998 perihelion passage. During the 1998 return the comet brightened sufficiently to be observed with binoculars.

The Leonids

Dust ejected by 55P/Tempel–Tuttle along its orbit forms the stream responsible for the Leonid meteor shower, which peaks each year around 17–18 November. In years near the comet's perihelion, Earth may cross young, dense dust trails, producing meteor storms with thousands of meteors per hour; spectacular storms were recorded in 1833, 1866, 1966, and around 1999–2002.
