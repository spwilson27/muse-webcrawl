Zürich Airport (IATA: ZRH, ICAO: LSZH), also known as Kloten Airport, is the largest international airport in Switzerland and the principal hub of Swiss International Air Lines. It lies in the municipality of Kloten, about 13 kilometres north of central Zürich, and serves as Switzerland's main gateway for long-haul air travel.

History

The airport opened in 1948, replacing the Dübendorf airfield as Zürich's civil airport, and its first intercontinental flights began in the early 1950s with Swissair. Successive expansions added new runways and terminals, including Dock B (1975), Dock A (1985), and the midfield Dock E (2003), connected to the main complex by the Skymetro underground people mover. After Swissair's collapse in 2001–2002, the airport's traffic recovered under its successor airline, Swiss, part of the Lufthansa Group.

Facilities

The airport has three runways and a central Airside Center linking Docks A, B, and E. The Circle, a large commercial and service complex, opened in 2020 adjacent to the terminals. Zürich Airport is consistently ranked among Europe's best airports for passenger service, in part for its efficiency and its observation decks.

Ground transport

Zürich Flughafen railway station, beneath the terminal complex, connects the airport to Zürich Hauptbahnhof in about ten minutes and to cities throughout Switzerland; trams and buses link it to the surrounding region via Glattalbahn lines.
