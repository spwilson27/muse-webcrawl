Les Liaisons dangereuses

Les Liaisons dangereuses (Dangerous Liaisons) is a French epistolary novel by Pierre Choderlos de Laclos, first published in 1782. Told in 175 letters, it recounts the intrigues of two aristocratic rivals and former lovers, the Marquise de Merteuil and the Vicomte de Valmont, who amuse themselves by manipulating and ruining others through seduction.

Plot

The Marquise de Merteuil asks Valmont to seduce the convent-educated Cécile de Volanges, whose marriage to Merteuil's former lover would thereby be tainted; Valmont at first prefers the challenge of seducing the virtuous Madame de Tourvel. As letters criss-cross, Valmont succeeds with both women, Cécile is drawn into the games of the libertines, and the young Chevalier Danceny becomes her lover and later Valmont's duelling opponent. The schemes unravel in catastrophe: Tourvel dies of grief, Valmont is killed by Danceny, and Merteuil, her letters exposed, is disgraced, disfigured by smallpox, and flees to Holland.

Reception and adaptations

The novel was a sensation and a scandal; its author, an artillery officer, claimed a moral purpose in depicting the corruption of the pre-Revolutionary aristocracy. It is now regarded as a masterpiece of psychological fiction. Famous adaptations include Roger Vadim's 1959 film, Stephen Frears's Dangerous Liaisons (1988) from Christopher Hampton's stage play, Miloš Forman's Valmont (1989), and the teen reworking Cruel Intentions (1999).
