Makhachkala

Makhachkala is the capital and largest city of Dagestan, a republic of Russia, located on the western shore of the Caspian Sea. With a population of over 600,000, it is the largest city in the North Caucasus region and an important port and industrial centre.

History

The city was founded in 1844 as the fortress of Petrovskoye, named for Peter the Great, who had campaigned in the region in 1722. It developed into the port of Petrovsk-Port and was renamed Makhachkala in 1921 in honour of the Bolshevik revolutionary Makhach Dakhadaev. Under the Soviet Union it became the capital of the Dagestan ASSR and grew as an oil, fishing, and manufacturing centre.

Geography and demographics

Makhachkala lies between the Caspian Sea and the foothills of the Caucasus, near Mount Tarki-Tau. It is one of Russia's most ethnically diverse cities, home to Avars, Dargins, Kumyks, Lezgins, Laks, Russians, and many other groups. The city is known for the football club Anzhi Makhachkala and for the Grand Mosque of Makhachkala, one of the largest in Russia.
