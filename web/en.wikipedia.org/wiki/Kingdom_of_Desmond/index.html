The Kingdom of Desmond (Irish: Deasmhumhain, meaning "South Munster") was a historic kingdom in southwestern Ireland. It was founded in 1118 when the Kingdom of Munster was partitioned between the north (Thomond) and the south (Desmond), and it survived, under the MacCarthy dynasty, through the Norman invasion until its last king's submission and the kingdom's absorption into the Tudor Kingdom of Ireland in the late 16th century.

Origins

Following the death of the high king Muirchertach Ua Briain, the Treaty of Glanmire (1118) divided Munster between Tadhg Mac Cárthaigh, who took Desmond, and the O'Briens, who took Thomond. The MacCarthys, descendants of the Eóganacht kings of Munster, ruled from shifting seats and resisted both Thomond and, after 1169, the Anglo-Norman invaders, retaining much of the rugged southwest.

Relations with the Normans

Norman families such as the FitzGeralds (the earls of Desmond) and the Roches and Barrys carved out lordships in eastern Desmond, while the MacCarthys held the west around Cork and Kerry, at times acknowledging English overlordship and at times fighting it. Cormac Mac Cárthaigh built the famous Blarney Castle in the 15th century. The MacCarthy Mór, as chief king, exacted tribute from subordinate lords and maintained brehon law and Irish custom longer than most of Ireland.

Decline and end

The Tudor reconquest of Ireland brought Desmond under pressure. The Desmond Rebellions (1569–73 and 1579–83)—waged by the FitzGerald earls, not the MacCarthys—devastated Munster. In 1596 Domhnall Mac Cárthaigh, the last king, surrendered and regranted his lands under English law; on his death in 1596 the kingship effectively ended, and the territory was incorporated into the province of Munster and later planted with English settlers.
