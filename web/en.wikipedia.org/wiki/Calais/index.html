Overview

Calais is a port city in northern France, in the Pas-de-Calais department, the closest point on the French coast to England — the Strait of Dover is about 34 km wide here, and the White Cliffs of Dover are visible on clear days. It is France's busiest passenger port and the terminus of the Channel Tunnel at nearby Coquelles.

History

England held Calais from 1347 to 1558 — its last possession on the French mainland — and its loss was said to have been engraved on Queen Mary I's heart. The town hall and belfry are UNESCO-listed. Rodin's sculpture The Burghers of Calais (1889) commemorates the six citizens who offered themselves to Edward III in 1347. In recent years Calais was the site of the migrant encampment known as the "Jungle", cleared in 2016.
