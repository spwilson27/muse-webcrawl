Groundcover

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Low groundcover planted to suppress weeds and prevent erosion

Groundcover or ground cover is any plant that grows over an area of ground. In a garden context, groundcover usually refers to low-growing, spreading plants used to cover bare soil, suppress weeds, and reduce erosion, as an alternative or complement to lawn grasses or mulch. In ecology, the term describes the lowest layer of vegetation in a plant community.

Uses

Groundcovers are planted where mowing is difficult (steep banks, under trees), where grass grows poorly (deep shade, dry or poor soils), or where a low-maintenance carpet of foliage is desired. A mature groundcover shades the soil, limiting the germination of weed seeds, conserving moisture, and protecting slopes from erosion by rain splash and runoff.

Common groundcover plants

  • English ivy (Hedera helix)
  • Lesser periwinkle (Vinca minor)
  • Pachysandra (Pachysandra terminalis)
  • Creeping juniper (Juniperus horizontalis)
  • Creeping thyme (Thymus species)
  • Clover (Trifolium species)
  • Stonecrop (Sedum species)

Some widely planted groundcovers, such as English ivy and periwinkle, are considered invasive in parts of North America and elsewhere, escaping gardens to displace native woodland flora; gardeners are increasingly advised to choose regionally native alternatives.

Ecology

In forest ecology, ground cover provides habitat and forage for invertebrates and small animals, contributes leaf litter to soil formation, and its percentage cover is a standard metric in vegetation surveys.
