Piccadilly Circus is a road junction and public space of London's West End in the City of Westminster. It was built in 1819 to connect Regent Street with Piccadilly. In this context, a circus, from the Latin word meaning "circle", is a round open space at a street junction.


Piccadilly Circus, looking towards Shaftesbury Avenue, with its famous illuminated signs.

The Circus now connects Piccadilly, Regent Street, Shaftesbury Avenue, the Haymarket, Coventry Street (onwards to Leicester Square) and Glasshouse Street. It is close to major shopping and entertainment areas in the West End. Its status as a major traffic junction has made Piccadilly Circus a busy meeting place and a tourist attraction in its own right.

The Circus is particularly known for its video display and neon signs mounted on the corner building on the northern side, as well as the Shaftesbury Memorial Fountain and the statue of Anteros (often popularly mistaken for Eros). It is surrounded by several notable buildings, including the London Pavilion and Criterion Theatre.

History

Piccadilly was named in the 17th century after the piccadills — stiff collars with scalloped edges — sold by tailor Robert Baker, whose house stood nearby. John Nash's Regent Street scheme created the circus in 1819; the circular form was largely lost when Shaftesbury Avenue was cut through in 1886. Electric advertising signs appeared from 1908 (Perrier was the first brand advertised), and the junction became famous for its illuminated billboards, now consolidated into a single large curved digital screen.

Shaftesbury Memorial Fountain and "Eros"

The Shaftesbury Memorial Fountain, designed by Alfred Gilbert and unveiled in 1893, commemorates the philanthropist Lord Shaftesbury. Its winged aluminium figure represents Anteros, the Greek god of selfless love, but is almost universally known as Eros and has become an emblem of London.

In popular culture

The saying "like Piccadilly Circus" denotes a very busy place. The junction appears in countless films, photographs and musical works as shorthand for central London nightlife, and it is a traditional focal point for celebrations and gatherings, including New Year's Eve.
