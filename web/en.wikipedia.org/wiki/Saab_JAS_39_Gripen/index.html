Saab JAS 39 Gripen

The Saab JAS 39 Gripen is a light single-engine supersonic multirole fighter aircraft manufactured by Swedish aerospace company Saab AB. Designed to replace the Saab 35 Draken and 37 Viggen, it first flew in 1988 and entered Swedish Air Force service in 1996.

Design

JAS stands for jakt, attack, spaning (fighter, attack, reconnaissance). The Gripen has a delta wing and canard configuration, fly-by-wire controls, and is designed for short take-offs from dispersed road bases — a Swedish Cold War requirement. The name Gripen means "griffin".

Variants

Early A/B (single/two-seat) and improved C/D export versions were followed by the larger Gripen E/F, with a more powerful General Electric F414 engine, greater fuel capacity, AESA radar and advanced electronic warfare suite. Brazil selected the E/F as the F-39, with technology transfer and local assembly.

Operators and sales

Operators include Sweden, South Africa, Hungary, the Czech Republic, Thailand and Brazil. The type has competed — sometimes controversially — in fighter competitions worldwide, marketed on lower operating costs than rivals such as the F-35.
