State banquet

A state banquet is an official banquet hosted by a head of state in their official residence for another head of state, or sometimes head of government, on the occasion of a state visit. It is the most formal type of banquet and typically forms the centrepiece of the ceremonial programme of a state visit.

Protocol

State banquets are characterised by strict protocol, including prescribed seating plans, dress codes (commonly white tie or black tie), speeches and toasts by the host and guest of honour, and musical entertainment. Guests often number in the hundreds and may include members of royal families, government ministers, diplomats, and prominent figures from business, the arts, and academia.

United Kingdom

In the United Kingdom, state banquets are held at Buckingham Palace in London or occasionally at Windsor Castle. Around 170 guests are seated at a single horseshoe-shaped table in the Ballroom; preparations for the table, using the Grand Service of silver-gilt tableware, can take household staff several days.

United States

In the United States, the equivalent event is the state dinner, held in the State Dining Room of the White House. State dinners have been hosted by presidents since the 19th century to honour visiting heads of state.
