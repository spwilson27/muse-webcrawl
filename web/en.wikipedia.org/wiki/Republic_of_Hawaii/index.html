The Republic of Hawaii (Hawaiian: Lepupalika o Hawaiʻi) was a short-lived one-party state in Hawaii between July 4, 1894, when the Provisional Government of Hawaii ended, and August 12, 1898, when it became annexed by the United States as the Territory of Hawaii.

History

The republic was proclaimed by the same group that had overthrown Queen Liliʻuokalani in 1893, after U.S. President Grover Cleveland refused to annex the islands and sought to restore the monarchy. Sanford B. Dole served as its only president. In 1895 royalist supporters attempted a counter-revolution; Liliʻuokalani was arrested and formally abdicated.

Annexation

During the Spanish–American War, Hawaii's strategic value rose, and the United States annexed the islands by the Newlands Resolution, signed on July 7, 1898. Sovereignty was formally transferred on August 12, 1898, and the republic's government became the territorial government, with Dole as first territorial governor.
