Morne Trois Pitons National Park

Morne Trois Pitons National Park is a national park in Dominica established in July 1975, the first to be legally established in the country. Covering some 6,857 hectares, it is named after its highest mountain, Morne Trois Pitons (1,342 m), meaning "mountain of three peaks". The park was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1997 in recognition of its rugged volcanic landscape and rich biodiversity.

Features

The park contains a striking concentration of volcanic features: five volcanoes, fumaroles, hot springs, three freshwater crater lakes — Boeri Lake, Freshwater Lake and Middleham Falls lake — and the famous Boiling Lake, a flooded fumarole about 63 metres wide whose grey-blue water is kept at near-boiling temperatures by volcanic gases, the second-largest such lake in the world after Frying Pan Lake in New Zealand.

Other attractions include the Valley of Desolation, a steaming volcanic valley of hot springs and mud pots; Titou Gorge; and the Emerald Pool. The park's forests range from lush tropical rainforest to montane and elfin cloud forest, providing habitat for many species, including Dominica's endemic parrots, the sisserou (imperial amazon) and the red-necked amazon.

World Heritage status

UNESCO described the park as combining "features of volcanic activity, which are of scientific interest, with species-rich areas of tropical vegetation, including many endemic plants". It remains the principal protected area of Dominica's mountainous interior and a centre for hiking and ecotourism.
