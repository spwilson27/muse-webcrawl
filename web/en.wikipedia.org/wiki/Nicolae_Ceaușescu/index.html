Nicolae Ceaușescu

Nicolae Ceaușescu (26 January 1918 – 25 December 1989) was a Romanian communist politician who led Romania as General Secretary of the Romanian Communist Party from 1965 and as head of state from 1967 until his overthrow and execution during the Romanian Revolution of December 1989.

Nicolae Ceaușescu

Born: 26 January 1918, Scornicești, Romania

Died: 25 December 1989, Târgoviște, Romania

Office: General Secretary of the Romanian Communist Party (1965–1989); President of Romania (1974–1989)

Spouse: Elena Petrescu

Rise to power

Born into a peasant family in Scornicești, Ceaușescu moved to Bucharest as a youth and joined the then-banned communist movement, spending periods in prison during the 1930s and 1940s. After the communists took power he rose through the party ranks under Gheorghe Gheorghiu-Dej, whom he succeeded in 1965.

Rule

Ceaușescu initially pursued a relatively independent foreign policy within the Warsaw Pact, condemning the 1968 invasion of Czechoslovakia and maintaining relations with Western states. Domestically, however, his rule became increasingly authoritarian and marked by a pervasive personality cult. His Decree 770 restricted abortion and contraception, and his drive to repay Romania's foreign debt in the 1980s produced severe austerity and shortages. The Securitate secret police maintained extensive surveillance of the population.

Fall and execution

In December 1989 protests in Timișoara spread to Bucharest, and Ceaușescu and his wife Elena fled the capital on 22 December. They were captured, subjected to a summary military trial, and executed by firing squad on 25 December 1989 in Târgoviște. He was the last leader of a Warsaw Pact state to be removed during the revolutions of 1989, and Romania's transition was the only violent one.
