The mazurka (Polish: mazurek) is a Polish musical form based on stylised folk dances in triple meter, usually at a lively tempo, with the accent characteristically falling on the second or third beat. It takes its name from the Mazur people of Mazovia in north-central Poland. The folk dance is danced by couples, combining sliding steps, running steps and heel clicks with improvised figures.

The folk dance

The mazurka family includes the fast, whirling oberek, the moderate mazur, and the slower, lyrical kujawiak. From Poland the dance spread to ballrooms throughout Europe in the early 19th century, becoming — alongside the waltz and polka — one of the most fashionable social dances of the era, and travelled as far as the Philippines and Latin America.

In classical music

Frédéric Chopin composed at least 69 mazurkas for solo piano, transforming the folk form into miniature masterpieces full of chromatic harmony, modal inflections and rhythmic flexibility (tempo rubato). Other composers of mazurkas include Karol Szymanowski (whose 20 Mazurkas, Op. 50, fuse the form with Polish highland music), Alexander Scriabin, and composers of orchestral and operatic mazurkas such as Glinka, Tchaikovsky and Stanisław Moniuszko.
