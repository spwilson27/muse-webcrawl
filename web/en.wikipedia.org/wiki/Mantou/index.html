Mantou

Mantou
TypeSteamed bread
Place of originChina
Main ingredientsWheat flour, water, leavening agent

Mantou (simplified Chinese: 馒头; traditional Chinese: 饅頭) are plain steamed buns or bread made from fermented wheat flour dough, a staple food especially in northern China, where wheat rather than rice predominates. Soft, white and mildly flavoured, they are eaten plain or with savoury dishes in place of rice.

History and folklore

Steamed leavened breads have been made in China since at least the Han dynasty. A famous legend attributes the mantou to the strategist Zhuge Liang during his southern campaign in the Three Kingdoms period: unwilling to sacrifice men to appease river spirits demanding heads, he substituted dough balls shaped like heads — hence one folk etymology, "barbarian heads" (mán tóu), later softened to the present characters.

Varieties

Mantou may be small individual buns or large loaves sliced after steaming. Unlike baozi, mantou are unfilled. Fried mantou (zhá mántou) are crisped in oil, and a sweetened golden-and-silver version is served with condensed milk in Cantonese restaurants.
