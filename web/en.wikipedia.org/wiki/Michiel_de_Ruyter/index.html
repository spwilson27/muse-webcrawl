Michiel de Ruyter

De Ruyter, the most celebrated admiral of the Dutch Republic

Michiel Adriaenszoon de Ruyter (1607–1676) was a Dutch admiral, widely regarded as the greatest naval commander of the Dutch Republic. From humble origins as a rope-maker's apprentice who went to sea at eleven, he rose through the merchant service to command the Dutch fleet in the Anglo-Dutch Wars.

Career

De Ruyter first distinguished himself against the Dunkirk privateers and in Mediterranean service against Barbary corsairs. In the First Anglo-Dutch War he served under Maarten Tromp. During the Second War he won the Four Days' Battle (1666) and led the audacious Raid on the Medway (1667), sailing up the Thames estuary to burn the English fleet at Chatham — the most humiliating defeat in Royal Navy history.

In the Third Anglo-Dutch War his defensive victories at Solebay, Schooneveld, and Texel (1672–73) saved the Republic from Franco-English invasion. Sent to aid Spain against France, he was mortally wounded at the Battle of Augusta off Sicily in April 1676 and died at Syracuse.

Legacy

De Ruyter is a Dutch national hero; his tomb monument stands in Amsterdam's Nieuwe Kerk, and numerous Dutch warships have borne his name. His flagship De Zeven Provinciën is emblematic of the Dutch Golden Age at sea.
