The World Rugby Sevens Series, known since 2023–24 as HSBC SVNS, is an annual series of international rugby sevens tournaments run by World Rugby featuring national teams. The men's series was launched in 1999–2000, growing out of established invitational events such as the Hong Kong Sevens, and a women's series followed in 2012–13. Teams earn points at each tournament according to their finishing position, and the series champion is the team with the most points at the end of the season.

Format

Each tournament is played over two or three days with pool stages followed by knockout rounds. Historically, sixteen men's teams contested each event, with fifteen core teams holding places for the whole season and one invited team. From 2023–24 the series was remodelled as SVNS with twelve men's and twelve women's teams competing in a smaller number of combined events, concluding with a grand final tournament; a promotion and relegation play-off with teams from the second-tier Challenger Series was introduced.

History and champions

New Zealand won the first six men's series and remains the most successful team, while Fiji, South Africa, Samoa and Australia have also won titles; Fiji's back-to-back Olympic gold medals in 2016 and 2020 built on their sevens series pedigree. In the women's series, New Zealand and Australia have dominated, with Canada, the United States and France among the other leading teams. Long-standing events include the Hong Kong, Dubai, Singapore, Vancouver, Los Angeles, London, Paris and Sydney sevens.
