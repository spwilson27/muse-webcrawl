Body piercing

Body piercing, a form of body modification, is the practice of puncturing or cutting a part of the human body, creating an opening in which jewellery may be worn. The word "piercing" can refer to the act or practice of body piercing, or to an opening in the body created by this act or practice. Ear piercing is the most common form worldwide; other popular sites include the nostril, eyebrow, navel, tongue, lip, and various parts of the ear cartilage.

History

Piercing has ancient roots: mummified remains over 5,000 years old bear earrings; nose rings are mentioned in the Book of Genesis; lip and tongue piercing featured in Aztec and Maya ritual; and ear stretching was practiced by pharaonic Egyptians and many indigenous peoples. In the West, ear piercing for women was standard for centuries, while broader piercing culture grew out of the 1970s punk and "modern primitive" movements—figures such as Fakir Musafar—and was mainstreamed in the 1990s.

Practice and risks

Professional piercing is normally performed with a single-use hollow needle rather than a piercing gun (considered unsuitable for cartilage). Healing times vary from weeks (earlobe) to many months (navel, cartilage). Risks include infection, allergic reaction to nickel-bearing jewellery, keloid scarring, migration and rejection of the jewellery, and—for oral piercings—damage to teeth and gums. Implant-grade steel, titanium, niobium, and gold are recommended materials.
