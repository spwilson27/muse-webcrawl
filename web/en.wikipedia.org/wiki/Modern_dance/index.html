Modern dance

OriginUnited States and Germany, early 20th century
PioneersIsadora Duncan, Martha Graham, Doris Humphrey, Mary Wigman, Ruth St. Denis
CharacteristicsUse of gravity, floor work, contraction and release, rejection of ballet conventions

Modern dance is a broad genre of western concert dance that emerged in the early 20th century in the United States and Germany as a deliberate break from the strict conventions of classical ballet. Although the term has come to describe many diverse styles, modern dance in general treats movement as a vehicle for personal and emotional expression, embraces the weight of the body and the floor as a partner, and freely rejects ballet's turnout, pointed feet and codified vocabulary.

Origins

Precursors include François Delsarte's system of expressive gesture and the "free dance" of Loie Fuller and Isadora Duncan, who danced barefoot in flowing tunics inspired by ancient Greece. Ruth St. Denis and Ted Shawn, whose Denishawn school (founded 1915) trained many of the next generation, drew on Eastern and exotic themes. In Germany, Ausdruckstanz ("expressive dance") developed through Rudolf Laban and Mary Wigman.

The first generation

From Denishawn came the dancers who founded the classic American techniques. Martha Graham built a technique on "contraction and release", rooted in the breath and the pelvis, and created psychologically intense works such as Appalachian Spring (1944). Doris Humphrey developed "fall and recovery", exploring the body's negotiation with gravity. Charles Weidman brought humour and Americana; Hanya Holm carried Wigman's German approach into American studios. Lester Horton, on the West Coast, built a multicultural company and a fortification-based technique.

Postmodern dance and beyond

In the 1940s–50s, Graham's dancers Erick Hawkins, Merce Cunningham and Paul Taylor developed divergent paths; Cunningham, working with composer John Cage, introduced chance procedures and severed dance from narrative and musical dependence. From the Judson Dance Theater (1962–64) in New York emerged postmodern dance — Yvonne Rainer, Trisha Brown, Steve Paxton (founder of contact improvisation) — which questioned virtuosity itself and used everyday movement. Later developments include Tanztheater in Germany under Pina Bausch, Japanese butoh, and contemporary dance, which blends modern, ballet and street forms. Major companies today include the Martha Graham Dance Company, Alvin Ailey American Dance Theater, Paul Taylor American Modern Dance and Nederlands Dans Theater.

Technique

Modern dance training commonly includes floor work, falls and recovery, spirals, tilts, improvisation and partner work. Unlike ballet, it is usually performed barefoot, and the torso is used expressively rather than held in a fixed vertical alignment. Many schools teach named techniques — Graham, Limón (after José Limón), Horton, Cunningham, release technique — each with its own class structure and philosophy.
