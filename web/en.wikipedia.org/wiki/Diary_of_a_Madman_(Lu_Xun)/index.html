"Diary of a Madman" (Chinese: 狂人日記) is a short story by the Chinese writer Lu Xun, published in 1918 in the journal New Youth. It was the first significant work of modern Chinese literature written in vernacular Chinese (baihua) and is considered a foundational text of the May Fourth Movement and of modern Chinese fiction.

Synopsis

The story is framed as the diary of a man suffering from a persecution complex who becomes convinced that the people around him — his neighbours, his doctor, and even his elder brother — are cannibals who intend to eat him. Reading a Confucian history book by night, he finds the words "eat people" written between the lines of the virtues of benevolence and righteousness. At the end, despairing that even the children have been taught to eat others, he issues the famous plea: "Save the children..." A short preface in classical Chinese notes that the diarist has since recovered and taken up an official post — an irony underscoring the story's critique.

Themes and influence

The "madman's" vision is a metaphor for the oppressive, "man-eating" character of traditional Confucian society and its family system. Lu Xun took his title and diary format in part from Nikolai Gogol's story of the same name. The work's blend of vernacular narration and classical preface, and its attack on tradition, made it an emblem of the New Culture Movement.
