Ashtanga vinyasa yoga

A Mysore-style Ashtanga class

Ashtanga vinyasa yoga is a modern style of yoga as exercise, codified and popularised by K. Pattabhi Jois (1915–2009) in Mysore, India. It consists of a fixed sequence of postures (asanas) linked by flowing movements (vinyasa) synchronised with a deep, audible breathing technique (ujjayi), combined with internal energy locks (bandha) and fixed gazing points (dristi).

Origins

Jois studied yoga under Tirumalai Krishnamacharya in Mysore from 1927 and began teaching in 1948. He attributed the sequences to an ancient text called the Yoga Korunta, said to have been transmitted to Krishnamacharya by his teacher Rama Mohan Brahmachari; the text itself has never been produced. Jois's 1960 book Yoga Mala set out the primary series, and Western students began arriving in Mysore in the 1970s, spreading the practice worldwide.

Practice

Practice begins with five repetitions each of Surya Namaskara A and B (sun salutations), followed by a standing sequence, one of six series of seated and finishing postures, and a closing sequence. The best-known is the Primary Series (Yoga Chikitsa, "yoga therapy"). Students traditionally practise in "Mysore style", working through the sequence at their own pace under a teacher's supervision, learning new postures only when the preceding ones are mastered. Practice is traditionally six days a week, resting on moon days.

Influence

Ashtanga was one of the principal influences on the development of power yoga and modern vinyasa flow classes in the West. Pattabhi Jois's son Manju and grandson Sharath continued the lineage, and the practice is now taught by certified teachers around the world.
