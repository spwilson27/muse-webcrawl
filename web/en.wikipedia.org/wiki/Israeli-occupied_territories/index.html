Israeli-occupied territories

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Israeli-occupied territories are territories captured by Israel from Egypt, Jordan and Syria during the Six-Day War of June 1967: the West Bank (including East Jerusalem), the Gaza Strip, the Sinai Peninsula and the Golan Heights. The Sinai was returned to Egypt under the 1979 peace treaty, and Israel formally annexed East Jerusalem (1980) and the Golan Heights (1981), annexations not recognised by most of the international community. The West Bank and Gaza remain the focus of the Israeli–Palestinian conflict.

Legal status

The United Nations Security Council, the International Court of Justice and the International Committee of the Red Cross regard the West Bank (including East Jerusalem) and Gaza as occupied territory subject to the Fourth Geneva Convention, and Israeli settlements there as illegal under international law. Israel disputes this characterisation, preferring the term "disputed territories" for the West Bank, while accepting that the law of belligerent occupation applies de facto in some respects. Israel's 2005 disengagement removed its settlements and permanent ground presence from Gaza, though its control of Gaza's borders, airspace and maritime access — tightened after Hamas took power in 2007 — leads many bodies to continue to regard Gaza as occupied.

Administration

Under the Oslo Accords (1993–1995), the Palestinian Authority administers parts of the West Bank (Areas A and B), while Israel retains full control of Area C, about 60 per cent of the territory, where most Israeli settlements — housing over 500,000 settlers in the West Bank and East Jerusalem combined — are located. The Golan Heights, home to Israeli settlements and a Druze population, is administered as part of Israel's Northern District.
