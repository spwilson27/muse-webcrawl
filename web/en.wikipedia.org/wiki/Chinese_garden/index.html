Chinese garden

A Suzhou scholar's garden pavilion

The Chinese garden (Chinese: 中國園林; pinyin: Zhōngguó yuánlín) is a landscape garden style that has evolved over more than three thousand years. Inheriting classical and Taoist ideals, the Chinese garden seeks to recreate natural scenery in miniature — as a microcosm of the universe — combining rocks, water, plants, pavilions and walls into an idealized landscape for contemplation, poetry and painting.

Elements

Classical Chinese gardens balance complementary forces: rocks symbolize permanence and mountains; water stands for impermanence and life. Winding paths deny the visitor a complete view, so the garden unfolds scene by scene — the principle of "borrowed scenery" (jiejing) frames distant pagodas or hills as part of the composition. Structures — pavilions, moon gates, covered corridors, miniature bridges — punctuate the landscape, while plantings carry symbolic weight: pine and bamboo stand for resilience and virtue, plum blossom for renewal, lotus for purity. Walls create the garden's secluded world; inscriptions and couplets on buildings pair architecture with poetry.

History and types

Early imperial parks served as hunting reserves and symbolic microcosms of the cosmos under the Han dynasty. The mature forms developed through the Tang, Song and Ming dynasties: the grand northern imperial gardens, such as the Summer Palace in Beijing, and the refined private scholar gardens of the south, above all the gardens of Suzhou — the Humble Administrator's Garden, the Lingering Garden, the Garden of the Master of the Nets and the Lion Grove Garden — eight of which are UNESCO World Heritage sites. Ming and Qing treatises such as Ji Cheng's Yuanye (The Craft of Gardens, 1631) codified the design principles.

Influence

The Chinese garden profoundly influenced the Japanese garden, and in the 18th century Chinese-inspired scenery contributed to the English landscape garden movement and to European chinoiserie. Chinese gardens have since been built in many countries as cultural gifts and botanical attractions.
