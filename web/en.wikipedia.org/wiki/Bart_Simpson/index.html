Bart Simpson

Bartholomew Jojo "Bart" Simpson is a fictional character in the animated television series The Simpsons, voiced by Nancy Cartwright. He is the eldest child and only son of Homer and Marge Simpson, and the mischievous, rebellious, underachieving brother of Lisa and Maggie. A ten-year-old fourth-grader at Springfield Elementary, Bart is known for his pranks, his skateboard, his alter ego "El Barto", and catchphrases including "Eat my shorts", "¡Ay, caramba!" and "Don't have a cow, man".

Creation and role

Cartoonist Matt Groening created the Simpson family while waiting to pitch a series of shorts to James L. Brooks; Bart's name is an anagram of "brat", and his character draws partly on Groening's older brother Mark. Bart debuted in the short "Good Night" in 1987 and in the half-hour series in 1989. In the early seasons Bart was the show's breakout star — the "Bartmania" phenomenon — before the emphasis shifted toward Homer.

Cultural impact

Bart's unruly image sparked controversy: T-shirts bearing slogans such as "I'm Bart Simpson. Who the hell are you?" were banned in some schools, and in 1990 he was denounced in public debate as a poor role model — even as Time named him one of the 100 most influential people of the 20th century. "Do the Bartman", co-written with Michael Jackson, topped charts in several countries. Running gags include Bart's prank calls to Moe's Tavern, his chalkboard punishment lines in the opening sequence, and his treehouse alter ego Bartman from the comics.
