Battery Park City

Battery Park City is a mainly residential 92-acre (37 ha) planned community and neighborhood on the west side of the southern tip of the island of Manhattan in New York City. It is bounded by the Hudson River on the west, the Hudson River shoreline on the north and south, and the West Side Highway on the east. The neighborhood is named for adjoining Battery Park.

History

The land was created by landfill, largely using earth and rock excavated during construction of the World Trade Center in the late 1960s and early 1970s. The Battery Park City Authority, a New York State public-benefit corporation created in 1968, oversees development under a master plan that reserves roughly a third of the site as open space. A revised 1979 master plan by Cooper, Eckstut & Associates restored the traditional Manhattan street grid and required buildings to follow the city's architectural vocabulary, shaping the neighborhood's distinctive mix of residential towers and parks.

The first residential building opened in 1980, and development continued in phases through the 1980s and 1990s, including the World Financial Center (now Brookfield Place). The neighborhood was heavily affected by the September 11 attacks, which damaged buildings and forced a months-long evacuation, but it recovered and was fully rebuilt. Its esplanade and parks — including Robert F. Wagner Jr. Park, Teardrop Park, and the Irish Hunger Memorial — are notable features.

Features

  • Brookfield Place (formerly the World Financial Center), a major office and retail complex
  • The Hudson River Esplanade, running the length of the neighborhood
  • Stuyvesant High School and other public schools
  • The Museum of Jewish Heritage and the Skyscraper Museum
  • North Cove Marina and the Irish Hunger Memorial

Governance

Residents do not own the land outright; buildings sit on ground leases from the Battery Park City Authority, which collects payments in lieu of taxes that support the authority's operations and contribute revenue to New York City.
