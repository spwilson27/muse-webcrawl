The common crane (Grus grus), also known as the Eurasian crane, is a large bird of the crane family, Gruidae. It is a long-distance migrant breeding across northern Europe and Asia and wintering in southern Europe, North Africa, and southern Asia.

Description

Adults are slate-grey with a black-and-white neck pattern, a red crown patch, and long inner wing feathers that form a drooping, bustle-like tail. Their loud, trumpeting calls are produced by an elongated, coiled trachea and can carry for several kilometres.

Migration and habitat

Common cranes breed in wetlands and boggy forests, building nests of vegetation in shallow water. On migration, huge flocks gather at traditional staging sites such as Lake Hornborga in Sweden and the Laguna de Gallocanta in Spain. The species became extinct as a British breeding bird in the seventeenth century but recolonised Norfolk in 1979 and has since been reintroduced to the Somerset Levels.
