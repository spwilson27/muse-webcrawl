The Azure Dragon (Chinese: 青龍; pinyin: Qīnglóng) is one of the Dragon Gods who represent the mount or chthonic forces of the Five Regions' Highest Deities in Chinese religion, and one of the Four Symbols of the Chinese constellations. It represents the east and the spring season, and is the essence of the dragon in Chinese mythology — a benevolent, imperial creature associated with water, rain and the emperor's authority. Its Japanese name is Seiryū, Korean Cheongnyong, and Vietnamese Thanh Long.

Astronomy

In Chinese uranography, the Azure Dragon governs the eastern palace of the sky, comprising seven lunar mansions (xiu): Horn, Neck, Root, Room, Heart, Tail and Winnowing Basket. These correspond roughly to parts of the Western constellations Virgo, Libra, Scorpius and Sagittarius. As the mansions rise in sequence through the year, the dragon's body was imagined to emerge in spring, matching its seasonal association.

Symbolism and worship

The Azure Dragon is associated with the colour qing — a blue-green ranging from azure to deep green — and with the element wood. It appears on flags, tomb murals, roof tiles and temple art throughout East Asia, guarding the eastern side of sacred spaces. In feng shui, the "green dragon" side of a site (the left, viewed from within) should ideally be higher than the "white tiger" side. The Azure Dragon of the East, along with the Vermilion Bird of the South, the White Tiger of the West and the Black Tortoise of the North, was depicted on military banners from at least the Han dynasty onward.
