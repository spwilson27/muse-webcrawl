Roubaix (French pronunciation: [ʁu.bɛ]; Dutch: Robaais) is a city in northern France, in the department of Nord in the Hauts-de-France region, close to the Belgian border. Part of the Lille metropolitan area together with Tourcoing and Villeneuve-d'Ascq, it is one of the most populous communes of the region and was once a world centre of the textile industry.

History

Roubaix is first mentioned in the 9th century as a small settlement. Its destiny was transformed in the 19th century when mechanised spinning and weaving, powered by coal from the nearby Nord-Pas-de-Calais basin, made it a boom town of the Industrial Revolution — the "Manchester of France" — and its population soared past 120,000. Mill dynasties such as the Motte family built factories, workers' housing, and churches, while immigrant labour arrived from Belgium and later from North Africa and elsewhere.

The collapse of the French textile industry in the 1970s hit Roubaix hard, leaving it one of the poorest large towns in France, with high unemployment. Since the 1990s the city has pursued regeneration through culture, retail, and education. Retailers founded in the city include the mail-order pioneer La Redoute and the supermarket group Auchan (founded in Roubaix in 1961 by Gérard Mulliez).

Culture and landmarks

The city's best-known museum is La Piscine, the Musée d'Art et d'Industrie André Diligent, opened in 2000 in the converted Art Deco municipal swimming pool of 1932, displaying fashion collections — including archives of Chanel and other houses — alongside paintings and sculpture set around the original pool. Roubaix is also famous in world sport as the finish of the Paris–Roubaix cycling classic, "the Hell of the North", whose riders complete their cobbled ordeal with a lap of the Roubaix velodrome (Vélodrome André-Pétrieux).
