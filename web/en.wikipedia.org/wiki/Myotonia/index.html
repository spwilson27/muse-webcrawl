Myotonia

Myotonia is a medical sign and symptom defined as delayed relaxation of skeletal muscle after voluntary contraction, percussion, or electrical stimulation. It results from repetitive firing of muscle fibre action potentials due to abnormal membrane excitability, and is characteristic of the myotonic disorders, including myotonic dystrophy and the non-dystrophic myotonias (channelopathies of muscle).

Clinical features

Patients describe stiffness and difficulty releasing a grip (grip myotonia), trouble starting to walk after rest, or eyelids that close slowly after sneezing. Percussion of a muscle — classically the thenar eminence or the tongue — produces a sustained local contraction that relaxes slowly (percussion myotonia). Symptoms of the congenital channelopathies typically improve with repeated activity (the "warm-up" phenomenon), whereas in paramyotonia congenita stiffness paradoxically worsens with exercise and cold. Myotonia characteristically lessens with age in myotonic dystrophy, in which weakness becomes dominant.

Causes

  • Myotonic dystrophy type 1 — CTG repeat expansion in the DMPK gene; multisystem disease with weakness, cataracts, cardiac conduction defects, and endocrine problems.
  • Myotonic dystrophy type 2 — CCTG expansion in CNBP; generally milder, proximal-predominant.
  • Myotonia congenita — loss-of-function mutations in the chloride channel gene CLCN1 (Thomsen disease, dominant; Becker disease, recessive); the basis of the "fainting goat" phenotype in goats.
  • Sodium channel myotonias — gain-of-function mutations in SCN4A, including paramyotonia congenita and potassium-aggravated myotonia.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on the clinical examination, electromyography (which reveals characteristic myotonic discharges that wax and wane, sounding like a dive-bomber or revving motorcycle), and genetic testing. Mexiletine is the best-supported antimyotonic drug; other options include lamotrigine, carbamazepine, and ranolazine. Patients may need anaesthetic precautions because depolarizing muscle relaxants can provoke severe myotonic spasm.
