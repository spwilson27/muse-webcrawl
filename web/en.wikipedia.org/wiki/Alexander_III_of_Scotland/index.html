Alexander III (Medieval Gaelic: Alaxandair mac Alaxandair; 4 September 1241 – 19 March 1286) was King of Alba (Scotland) from 1249 until his death. He concluded the Treaty of Perth, by which Scotland acquired the Western Isles and the Isle of Man from Norway, and his reign was remembered as a period of peace and prosperity.

Reign

Alexander succeeded his father, Alexander II, at the age of seven. His minority was marked by rivalry between noble factions for control of the government. In 1263 he faced the expedition of Haakon IV of Norway, which ended inconclusively after the Battle of Largs; the subsequent Treaty of Perth (1266) transferred the Hebrides and Man to Scotland in return for payment.

Death and succession

Alexander's children predeceased him, and after the death of his first wife Margaret of England he married Yolande of Dreux in 1285. On the night of 18–19 March 1286, riding in the dark to visit his queen at Kinghorn, he fell from his horse and was found dead at the foot of a cliff with a broken neck. His death left his infant granddaughter, Margaret, Maid of Norway, as heir and triggered the succession crisis that led to the Wars of Scottish Independence.
