Juniperus communis

Scientific classification
Kingdom: Plantae
Division: Pinophyta
Class: Pinopsida
Order: Cupressales
Family: Cupressaceae
Genus: Juniperus
Species: J. communis

Juniperus communis, the common juniper, is a species of small tree or shrub in the cypress family Cupressaceae. It has one of the widest natural ranges of any woody plant, extending across the cool temperate Northern Hemisphere from the Arctic south to mountain regions at about 30°N latitude in North America, Europe and Asia.

Description

The common juniper varies from a low, spreading, mat-forming shrub to a small conical tree of up to about 10 metres. Its needle-like leaves are borne in whorls of three, sharply pointed, with a single white band on the inner surface. The species is dioecious, with separate male and female plants; the female seed cones are berry-like, ripening from green to blue-black over about 18 months.

Uses

The ripe berries are the principal flavouring of gin and are used as a spice in Northern European cooking, in marinades for game and in sauerkraut. Juniper wood and berries also have traditional medicinal and craft uses, and numerous cultivars are grown as ornamental ground cover.
