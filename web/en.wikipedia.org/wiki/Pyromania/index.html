Pyromania

Pyromania is an impulse control disorder in which individuals repeatedly fail to resist impulses to deliberately start fires, in order to relieve tension or for gratification. The term comes from the Greek pŷr (fire) and mania (madness).

Diagnosis

According to the DSM-5, diagnosis requires deliberate and purposeful fire-setting on more than one occasion; tension or affective arousal before the act; fascination with fire and its situational contexts; and pleasure, gratification or relief when setting or witnessing fires. The fire-setting must not be done for monetary gain, as an expression of sociopolitical ideology, to conceal criminal activity, to express anger or vengeance, in response to a delusion or hallucination, or as a result of impaired judgment.

Causes and treatment

Pyromania is rare, and most arsonists do not meet its criteria. In children, fire-setting is more often associated with conduct disorder, ADHD or adjustment difficulties. Treatment typically involves cognitive behavioural therapy; in children, family-based interventions and fire-safety education are used.
