Motorcycle

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A rider on a classic motorcycle leaning through a mountain road bend]
Motorcycling on a mountain road.

A motorcycle is a two-wheeled (sometimes three-wheeled) motor vehicle steered by handlebars, with the rider seated astride. The first production motorcycle is usually credited to Hildebrand & Wolfmüller (1894), after Daimler\'s 1885 Reitwagen experiment. Motorcycles range from 50 cc mopeds and scooters — the transport backbone of much of Asia — to litre-class superbikes and heavy touring machines from Harley-Davidson and BMW; Honda\'s Super Cub (1958) is the best-selling motor vehicle in history, with over 100 million built.

Types

Categories include standards, cruisers, sport bikes, tourers, dual-sports and off-road machines, plus step-through scooters (Vespa, Honda Super Cub) that dominate fleets in Vietnam, Indonesia, India — the world\'s largest motorcycle market — and China. Electric models are growing fastest in the small classes.

Riding and safety

Motorcycles lean to steer and expose the rider, so protective gear and helmets (mandatory in most countries) are crucial; motorcycling carries markedly higher risk per kilometre than driving. Training and licensing are tiered by engine size in the EU and Japan.

Culture and sport

Motorcycling spans commuter utility, outlaw-club mythology, and café-racer and adventure-touring subcultures. Motorcycle racing includes MotoGP, the Isle of Man TT (among the most dangerous events in sport), motocross and the Dakar Rally.
