Troika Laundromat

The scheme was uncovered from leaked bank records

The Troika Laundromat was a network of offshore companies and bank accounts used to move billions of dollars out of Russia, revealed in 2019 by the Organized Crime and Corruption Reporting Project (OCCRP) and partner media organisations, based on a leak of banking transactions largely from the Lithuanian bank Ūkio Bankas.

The scheme

Investigators reported that between roughly 2006 and 2013, some US$4.6 billion passed through the network, which centred on companies linked to Troika Dialog, once one of Russia's largest investment banks. Money flowed through shell companies with nominee directors, some of whom were unaware their signatures were being used, and was spent on yachts, art, property, private school fees and other purchases in the West.

Reactions and consequences

Troika Dialog had been sold to Sberbank in 2012. Its former head, Ruben Vardanyan, denied wrongdoing, saying the bank operated lawfully. The revelations prompted investigations and regulatory scrutiny of several European banks that had processed the funds, and Ūkio Bankas had already been shut down by Lithuanian regulators in 2013. The reporting formed part of a series of "Laundromat" investigations into money-laundering channels from the former Soviet Union.
