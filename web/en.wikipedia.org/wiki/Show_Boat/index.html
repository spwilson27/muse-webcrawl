Show Boat is a musical with music by Jerome Kern and book and lyrics by Oscar Hammerstein II. Based on Edna Ferber's best-selling 1926 novel of the same name, it premiered on Broadway in 1927, produced by Florenz Ziegfeld. It is widely considered a landmark in American musical theatre — the first musical to integrate songs, dance, and dialogue into a serious dramatic narrative.

Synopsis

The story follows the lives of performers aboard the Cotton Blossom, a Mississippi River show boat, from 1887 to 1927. Magnolia Hawks, daughter of the boat's captain, falls in love with the charming gambler Gaylord Ravenal; their marriage collapses under his gambling debts and is later repaired. A parallel plot follows Julie, a singer of mixed race passing as white, who is exposed and forced off the boat under miscegenation laws — giving the show its most famous song, "Ol' Man River", sung by the stevedore Joe.

Songs

The score includes "Ol' Man River", "Make Believe", "Can't Help Lovin' Dat Man", "You Are Love", "Why Do I Love You?", "Bill" (with lyrics partly by P. G. Wodehouse from an earlier Kern song), and "Life Upon the Wicked Stage". Paul Robeson, for whom the role of Joe was written, became indelibly associated with "Ol' Man River".

Significance and adaptations

Unlike the lightweight revues and operettas of its day, Show Boat confronted racial prejudice and marital hardship, and its plot-driven integration of music and story set the template later perfected by Rodgers and Hammerstein. It was filmed three times (1929, 1936, and 1951), frequently revived, and the 1994 Broadway revival directed by Harold Prince won the Tony Award for Best Revival of a Musical.
