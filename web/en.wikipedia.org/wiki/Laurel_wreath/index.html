Laurel wreath

A laurel wreath is a round wreath made of connected branches and leaves of the bay laurel (Laurus nobilis), an aromatic broadleaf evergreen. It is a symbol of triumph and achievement originating in ancient Greek and Roman culture.

Origins

In Greek myth the laurel was sacred to Apollo; the nymph Daphne was transformed into a laurel tree to escape him, and Apollo adopted the wreath. Victors at the Pythian Games, held in Apollo's honour at Delphi, received laurel wreaths, while Olympic victors received olive wreaths. Rome adopted the laurel for triumphant generals and emperors; the corona laurea appears on imperial coinage from Augustus onward.

Later use

The wreath became the emblem of poets — hence "poet laureate" — and of academic distinction; Italian university graduates still wear laurel. It appears in heraldry, on medals and diplomas, in the emblem of the United Nations (as an olive wreath variant), and in the idiom "resting on one's laurels".
