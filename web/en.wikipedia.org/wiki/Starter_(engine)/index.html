Starter (engine)

A starter (also self-starter, cranking motor, or starter motor) is a device used to rotate an internal combustion engine so as to initiate the engine's operation under its own power. Starters can be electric, pneumatic, or hydraulic. The starter can also be another internal combustion engine in the case of very large engines, or a human hand in small engines.

History

Before the electric starter, engines were started by hand cranking, which was laborious and could be dangerous due to kickback. The electric starter was invented by Charles Kettering and colleagues at Dayton Engineering Laboratories (Delco) and first appeared as standard equipment on the 1912 Cadillac. Its adoption, which also enabled the use of higher-compression engines, rapidly spread through the automobile industry.

Electric starter

The modern electric starter is a series-wound DC motor fitted with a solenoid-operated pinion that engages the engine's flywheel ring gear when the ignition switch is turned. A Bendix drive or pre-engaged mechanism moves the pinion into mesh during cranking and disengages it once the engine starts, protecting the starter motor from being spun at excessive speed.
