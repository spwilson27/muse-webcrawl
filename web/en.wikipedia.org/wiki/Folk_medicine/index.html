Folk medicine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Folk medicine consists of the healing practices, beliefs and remedies transmitted informally within a culture, usually by word of mouth across generations, outside the framework of institutional, scientifically based medicine. It encompasses herbal remedies, dietary practices, manual therapies, rituals and the work of traditional healers such as herbalists, midwives and bonesetters.

Characteristics

Folk medicine is embedded in the worldview of the community that practises it. Explanations of illness may invoke natural causes, imbalance of bodily humours or elements, spiritual forces, or social relationships. Treatments are typically inexpensive, locally available and administered by family members or recognised local practitioners. Knowledge is experiential: remedies persist because they are believed to have worked, and are adapted over time.

Relationship to scientific medicine

Some folk remedies have been validated and absorbed into modern pharmacology — willow bark led to aspirin, foxglove to digoxin, and the Chinese herbal remedy qinghao to the antimalarial artemisinin. Others are ineffective or harmful, and folk treatment can delay effective care. The World Health Organization uses the broader term traditional medicine and encourages study of traditional practices, many of which remain the main source of healthcare for large parts of the world's population.

Examples

Examples include European herbalism and "kitchen medicine", the doctrine of signatures, bloodletting and cupping, sweat lodges, curanderismo in Latin America, and countless local practices such as using honey for coughs or chicken soup for colds. Folk medicine persists in industrialised societies alongside biomedicine, often for chronic conditions, minor ailments and cultural or spiritual needs.

Study

Folklorists, medical anthropologists and ethnobotanists document folk medical systems both as cultural heritage and as a possible source of new treatments, while physicians caution about interactions between folk remedies and prescribed drugs.
