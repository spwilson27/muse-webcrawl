White rhinoceros

Scientific classification
FamilyRhinocerotidae
GenusCeratotherium
SpeciesC. simum
StatusNear Threatened (southern); Critically Endangered (northern)

The white rhinoceros (Ceratotherium simum) is the largest of the five extant species of rhinoceros and, after the elephants, among the largest land mammals. Despite its name it is not white; its hide is grey, and the name is widely thought to derive from a mistranslation of the Dutch wijd ("wide"), referring to its wide, squared lips, adapted for grazing. It is distinguished from the black rhinoceros by this square lip, a longer head, and a more social temperament.

Subspecies

Two subspecies are recognised. The southern white rhinoceros (C. s. simum), recovered from about 50 animals in the early 20th century to roughly 16,000–18,000, is one of conservation's great success stories, centred on South Africa's Hluhluwe–Imfolozi Park. The northern white rhinoceros (C. s. cottoni) is functionally extinct: only two females survive, under armed guard at Ol Pejeta Conservancy in Kenya, following the death of the last male, Sudan, in 2018. Scientists are attempting to restore the subspecies using in vitro fertilisation with preserved genetic material.

Behaviour and threats

White rhinos are grazers of open grassland and live in groups called crashes. Their principal threat is poaching for their horns, which are trafficked for use in traditional medicine and as status symbols, despite being composed of keratin with no proven medicinal value.
