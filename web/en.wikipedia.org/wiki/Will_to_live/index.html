Will to live

The will to live (German: Wille zum Leben) is a concept in psychology, medicine and philosophy referring to a person's drive toward self-preservation and continued existence, even in the face of pain, illness or adversity. It is closely related to resilience and the instinct of survival.

In psychology and medicine

Clinicians have long observed that a patient's will to live can influence recovery and longevity; loss of the will to live is associated with depression, apathy and, in terminal illness, a wish to hasten death. Viktor Frankl's logotherapy held that finding meaning in life sustains the will to live even under extreme suffering.

In philosophy

Arthur Schopenhauer placed the "will to life" at the centre of his metaphysics, regarding it as a blind striving that manifests in all living beings. Friedrich Nietzsche reinterpreted the instinct as the will to power.
