STM32

STM32 is a family of 32-bit microcontroller integrated circuits by STMicroelectronics. The STM32 chips are grouped into related series that are based around the same 32-bit ARM Cortex-M processor core, such as the Cortex-M33F, Cortex-M7F, Cortex-M4F, Cortex-M3, Cortex-M0+, or Cortex-M0. Each STM32 microcontroller integrates a processor core, static RAM, flash memory for program and data storage, debugging interfaces, and various peripherals.

History

ST announced the first STM32 product, based on the ARM Cortex-M3 core, in June 2007. It was the first ARM Cortex-M based microcontroller from a major semiconductor manufacturer. The family expanded rapidly: the lower-cost STM32F0 series (Cortex-M0) arrived in 2012, followed by high-performance parts based on the Cortex-M4 with floating-point and DSP instructions, and later the Cortex-M7-based STM32H7 series, which reached clock speeds of several hundred megahertz.

Series

The family is organised into mainstream series (such as the F0, F1, F2, F3, F4 and G0, G4 lines), ultra-low-power series (L0, L1, L4, L5, U5), high-performance series (F7, H7), and wireless series (WB, WL) that integrate radios for Bluetooth Low Energy or long-range sub-GHz communication. Parts are named with a scheme that encodes the series, core, flash size and package; for example, the STM32F103C8 is an F1-series part with 64 KB of flash in a 48-pin package.

Development ecosystem

STMicroelectronics supplies the STM32Cube software platform, including the STM32CubeMX graphical configurator, hardware abstraction libraries, and the free STM32CubeIDE integrated development environment based on Eclipse and GCC. Low-cost Nucleo development boards and Discovery kits, together with the ST-LINK debug probe, made the family popular with hobbyists as well as professional embedded engineers. Third-party support includes the Arduino framework, MicroPython, and the Zephyr real-time operating system.
