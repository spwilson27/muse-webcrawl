Ruth Shady

Ruth Martha Shady Solís (born 29 December 1946, in Callao, Peru) is a Peruvian anthropologist and archaeologist. She is best known as the discoverer and principal investigator of Caral, in the Supe River valley of the north-central coast of Peru—one of the oldest centres of civilisation in the Americas, dating to about 3000 BCE. Her work established the Caral-Supe (Norte Chico) civilisation as contemporary with ancient Egypt and Mesopotamia and as the oldest known civilisation in the Americas.

Career

Shady studied anthropology and archaeology at the National University of San Marcos in Lima, later earning her doctorate there. She worked at the National Museum of Archaeology, Anthropology and History of Peru and held academic posts at San Marcos. Earlier fieldwork included research on the Late Archaic sites of the Supe valley and the site of Áspero.

From 1994 she began full-scale excavations at Caral, a large inland settlement of monumental platform mounds, circular sunken plazas and residential complexes spreading over about 66 hectares. Radiocarbon dates published in Science in 2001 (with Jonathan Haas and Winifred Creamer) demonstrated occupation as early as the 27th century BCE, showing that complex society in Peru emerged a millennium earlier than previously believed and without ceramics or (apparently) warfare. Shady has since directed the Caral Archaeological Project and promoted research at related sites such as Vichama and Peñico. She led Peru's Special Archaeological Project Caral-Supe and has campaigned for the protection of the sites; Caral was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2009.

Recognition and threats

Shady has received numerous national and international honours, including being named to the BBC's 100 Women list in 2020. Her conservation work has come at personal cost: she has reported receiving threats from land traffickers and illegal occupants of archaeological land in the Supe valley, and in 2021 her dog was killed and she received death threats linked to her efforts to protect the sites—events widely reported in Peruvian and international media.
