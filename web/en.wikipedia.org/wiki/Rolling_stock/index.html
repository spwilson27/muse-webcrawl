Rolling stock

Rolling stock in the rail transport industry refers to railway vehicles, including both powered and unpowered vehicles: for example, locomotives, freight and passenger cars (or carriages), and multiple units. The term derives from the stock of vehicles held by a railway company and the fact that the vehicles roll on rails.

Types

Powered rolling stock includes steam, diesel, and electric locomotives and self-propelled multiple units; unpowered stock includes passenger coaches, freight wagons or cars, and specialised vehicles such as brake vans, inspection cars, and maintenance equipment. Vehicles are connected by couplings and run on bogies (trucks) or fixed axles.

Management

Rolling stock represents one of the largest capital costs of a railway. In many countries, trains are owned by dedicated rolling stock leasing companies (such as the British ROSCOs) and leased to train operating companies. Classification and numbering schemes, such as Britain's TOPS and the UIC classification of locomotive axle arrangements, organise fleets for operation and maintenance.
