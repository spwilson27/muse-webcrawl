Fin de siècle

Fin de siècle (French for "end of the century") refers to the close of the 19th century and the cultural mood associated with it — a mixture of decadence, aestheticism, symbolism, pessimism about civilisation and anticipation of a new era. The term is closely associated with France and the wider European culture of the 1880s and 1890s.

Periodc. 1880–1900
MovementsSymbolism, Decadence, Art Nouveau, Aestheticism
Key figuresBaudelaire (precursor), Huysmans, Wilde, Beardsley

Cultural characteristics

Fin de siècle culture combined world-weariness and fascination with decay, artificiality and altered states, expressed in Joris-Karl Huysmans's À rebours (1884), the poetry of the Symbolists, Oscar Wilde's works, Aubrey Beardsley's drawings and Art Nouveau design. Themes included the femme fatale, degeneration (as popularised by Max Nordau's 1892 critique), and spiritual searching outside organised religion.

Historical context

The period followed the Belle Époque's prosperity yet carried anxieties about urbanisation, science, empire and approaching war. Related moods appeared in Vienna 1900, the British 1890s and Russia's Silver Age.

Legacy

The phrase is now applied retrospectively to any century's end, notably the 1990s, and fin de siècle aesthetics influenced modernism, cinema and fashion.
