Frenet–Serret formulas

In differential geometry, the Frenet–Serret formulas describe the kinematic properties of a particle moving along a differentiable curve in three-dimensional Euclidean space, or equivalently the geometric properties of the curve itself. Specifically, the formulas describe the derivatives of the tangent, normal, and binormal unit vectors in terms of one another, using the curve's curvature κ and torsion τ.

The formulas

Let T be the unit tangent vector, N the unit normal vector, and B = T × N the binormal vector. Taken together, T, N, and B form the Frenet–Serret frame, an orthonormal basis that moves along the curve. With s denoting arc length, the formulas state:

dT/ds = κN
dN/ds = −κT + τB
dB/ds = −τN

The curvature measures how sharply the curve bends away from a straight line, while the torsion measures how sharply it twists out of its osculating plane.

History and applications

The formulas are named after French mathematicians Jean Frédéric Frenet, who presented them in his 1847 doctoral thesis, and Joseph Alfred Serret, who published them in 1851. They are fundamental to the theory of space curves and find applications in physics, including the motion of particles, the geometry of relativistic world lines, and computer graphics.
