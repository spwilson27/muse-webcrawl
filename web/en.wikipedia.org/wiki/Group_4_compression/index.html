Group 4 compression

StandardITU-T T.6
Also known asMMR (Modified Modified READ)
Data typeBilevel (black-and-white) images
UsesGroup 4 fax, TIFF compression option

Group 4 compression, defined in ITU-T Recommendation T.6 and also called MMR (Modified Modified READ), is a lossless method of image compression used in Group 4 facsimile machines to compress scans of black-and-white documents. It achieves higher compression than the earlier Group 3 (T.4) schemes by exploiting correlation between consecutive scan lines.

Algorithm

MMR is a two-dimensional line-by-line coding scheme. Each line is encoded relative to the line above it, using pass, vertical, and horizontal modes inherited from the two-dimensional READ code, but without the periodic one-dimensional lines and error-correction overhead used in fax transmission over telephone lines. Because Group 4 fax assumed error-free digital networks (ISDN), a single bit error could corrupt the remainder of a page, so the scheme is mainly used in controlled environments.

Applications

Beyond Group 4 fax, T.6 compression is a standard option in the TIFF image file format (compression scheme 4) and is widely used for scanned documents, engineering drawings, and archival imaging where crisp bilevel reproduction is needed.

History

The CCITT standardised Group 4 facsimile in 1984 as part of its telematic services specifications, alongside the Group 3 standard that became dominant on the public telephone network.
