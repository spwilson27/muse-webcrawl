Newport (Welsh: Casnewydd) is a city and county borough in Wales, situated on the River Usk close to its confluence with the Severn Estuary, about 12 miles (19 km) northeast of Cardiff. Newport has been an important port since medieval times, when the first Newport Castle was built, and grew dramatically in the nineteenth century as a coal-exporting port serving the South Wales Valleys. It was granted city status in 2002.

History

The Roman legionary fortress of Caerleon lies just northeast of the modern city. Medieval Newport grew around its castle and bridge on the Usk. In 1839 the town was the scene of the Newport Rising, the last large-scale armed rebellion in Britain, when Chartists led by John Frost marched on the Westgate Hotel; Frost and other leaders were sentenced to death, later commuted to transportation. The docks expanded through the nineteenth and twentieth centuries — Alexandra Docks opened in 1875 — and Newport handled large volumes of coal, iron and steel. The Transporter Bridge, opened in 1906, is one of only a handful of its kind still operating.

Economy and culture

Steel (notably the Llanwern steelworks), engineering, electronics and public administration have been major employers; more recently the city has developed financial-services, data-centre and semiconductor activity, including a compound semiconductor cluster. Newport hosted the 2010 Ryder Cup golf tournament at the Celtic Manor Resort and the 2014 NATO summit. The University of South Wales has a campus in the city centre.

Transport

The M4 motorway passes through Newport, including the Brynglas Tunnels, and Newport railway station lies on the South Wales Main Line with services to Cardiff, Bristol, London and Manchester.
