The Caves of Steel is a science fiction novel by American writer Isaac Asimov, first published as a serial in Galaxy magazine in 1953 and as a book by Doubleday in 1954. It is the first of Asimov's Robot novels and a landmark fusion of the science fiction and detective genres, which Asimov undertook partly to prove that a science fiction mystery could obey the fair-play rules of classic whodunits.

Plot

Some three millennia in the future, humanity is divided between the Spacers — descendants of colonists on fifty Outer Worlds, long-lived and dependent on robot labor — and the eight billion people of Earth, crowded into vast enclosed megacities, the "caves of steel," and fearful of robots that take their jobs. When Dr. Han Fastolfe's colleague Roj Nemmenuh Sarton is murdered in Spacetown, the Spacer enclave near New York City, plainclothesman Elijah Baley is assigned to investigate, and is given a Spacer robot partner, R. Daneel Olivaw, who is indistinguishable from a human. Baley must overcome his own prejudices, confront the Medievalist movement that wants to return Earth to the soil, and solve a murder that threatens the fragile peace between Earth and the Outer Worlds.

Themes

The novel explores overpopulation, technological unemployment, agoraphobia bred by urban enclosure, and the tension between stagnant civilizations — Earth's overcrowded cities and the Spacers' decadent, robot-dependent longevity. Its resolution points toward eventual colonization of new worlds by Earth humans, a thread Asimov later wove into the combined Robot–Empire–Foundation universe, much as long-running fictional universes are unified across separate series.

Adaptations

The novel was adapted for BBC television in 1964 (lost, apart from fragments) and dramatized for BBC Radio 4 in 1989. Sequels The Naked Sun (1957) and The Robots of Dawn (1983) continue the Baley–Olivaw partnership.
