The Great Limpopo Transfrontier Park is a transfrontier conservation area linking national parks in South Africa, Mozambique, and Zimbabwe, forming the core of the wider Great Limpopo Transfrontier Conservation Area. It joins South Africa's Kruger National Park, Mozambique's Limpopo National Park, and Zimbabwe's Gonarezhou National Park, together with adjoining reserves and communal lands, into a protected landscape of about 37,500 square kilometres.

History

The idea of a cross-border "peace park" in the region was promoted from the 1990s by the Peace Parks Foundation, founded in 1997 with Nelson Mandela, Prince Bernhard of the Netherlands, and Anton Rupert among its patrons. A trilateral treaty signed on 9 December 2002 formally established the park. Sections of the border fence between Kruger and Limpopo National Park were removed to reopen historical wildlife migration routes, and from 2001 elephants, zebra, and other game were translocated from Kruger's overpopulated herds into Mozambique.

Wildlife and tourism

The combined ecosystem supports the full complement of southern African savanna wildlife, including lion, leopard, elephant, white and black rhinoceros, buffalo, giraffe, and African wild dog. Border posts at Giriyondo and Pafuri allow tourists to cross between Kruger and the Mozambican park. Development of the Mozambican side has been slower than planned, and the project has had to address rhino poaching crossing from Mozambique into Kruger, human–wildlife conflict, and the resettlement of communities living inside Limpopo National Park.
