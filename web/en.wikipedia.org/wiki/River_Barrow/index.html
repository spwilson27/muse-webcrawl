The River Barrow (Irish: An Bhearú) is a river in Ireland. It is one of The Three Sisters; the other two being the River Suir and the River Nore. The Barrow is the longest of the three rivers, at 192 km, and the second-longest river in Ireland, after the River Shannon.

Course

The Barrow rises in the Slieve Bloom Mountains in County Laois and flows south through Portarlington, Monasterevin, Athy, Carlow, Leighlinbridge, Bagenalstown, Goresbridge, Graiguenamanagh, and St Mullin's. It meets the River Nore near New Ross, and the combined waters join the River Suir at Waterford Harbour, flowing into the Celtic Sea between Dunmore East and Hook Head.

The river's catchment drains parts of counties Laois, Kildare, Carlow, Kilkenny, Wexford, and Waterford. Its principal tributaries include the Burren, the Blackwater, the Douglas, and the Lerr.

Navigation

The Barrow Navigation, completed in the late 18th and early 19th centuries, links the river with the Grand Canal at Athy, making it navigable for much of its length. The towpath along the navigation, the Barrow Way, is a popular long-distance walking trail of about 100 km. The river is noted for its coarse fishing, boating, and the historic quays and mills along its banks.

History

The river's name derives from an early Irish word possibly meaning "the boiling one", a reference to its sometimes turbulent flow. In classical geography, Ptolemy referred to the river as the Birgus. The Barrow formed a historic boundary of the Pale and features prominently in the medieval history of south-east Ireland.
