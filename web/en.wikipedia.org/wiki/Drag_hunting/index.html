Drag hunting

Drag hunting is an equestrian sport in which mounted riders follow a pack of hounds that track an artificial scent laid over a predetermined course. Unlike fox hunting, no live animal is pursued: the scent — traditionally aniseed or another strong-smelling substance — is dragged along the ground by a runner or rider ahead of the field.

History

Drag hunting developed in Britain in the nineteenth century, with early drag hunts established at military academies and universities, where it provided fast cross-country riding and training over fences. Because the route is planned in advance, a drag hunt can include a line of jumps and gallops without the unpredictability of a live quarry.

The sport

A "runner" lays the drag, typically starting shortly before the hunt sets off, so the scent is fresh. The field follows the hounds over several "lines" separated by checks. The pace is usually faster than in fox hunting, and the day is shorter and more predictable, which makes the sport popular with riders who want the jumping and galloping without a full day's hunting. Since bans on hunting wild mammals with dogs in Britain, interest in drag hunting and trail hunting has grown.
