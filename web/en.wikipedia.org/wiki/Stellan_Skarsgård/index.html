Stellan John Skarsgård (born 13 June 1951) is a Swedish actor. A leading figure of Scandinavian cinema who became one of Europe's most prolific character actors in English-language film, he is known for his collaborations with Lars von Trier, his roles as Bootstrap Bill Turner in the Pirates of the Caribbean films, Dr Erik Selvig in the Marvel Cinematic Universe, Boris Shcherbina in Chernobyl (2019), for which he won a Golden Globe Award, and Baron Vladimir Harkonnen in Dune (2021) and Dune: Part Two (2024).

Early life and Swedish career

Skarsgård was born in Gothenburg and raised in a freethinking, humanist household that moved around Sweden. He became known nationally as a teenager in the television series Bombi Bitt och jag (1968) and worked extensively at the Royal Dramatic Theatre in Stockholm. His film breakthrough came with The Simple-Minded Murderer (1982), winning the Silver Bear for Best Actor at the Berlin International Film Festival.

International career

Skarsgård's work with Lars von Trier began with Breaking the Waves (1996) and continued with Dancer in the Dark (2000), Dogville (2003), Melancholia (2011) and Nymphomaniac (2013). Hollywood roles include The Hunt for Red October (1990), Good Will Hunting (1997, as Professor Lambeau), Ronin (1998), The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo (2011), Mamma Mia! (2008) and its sequel, Thor (2011) and The Avengers (2012). He played the villain Luthen Rael in the Star Wars series Andor (2022–2025). His portrayal of Soviet deputy premier Boris Shcherbina in HBO's Chernobyl won him the Golden Globe for Best Supporting Actor in a series.

Personal life

Skarsgård has eight children, six with his first wife, physician My Skarsgård, including actors Alexander, Gustaf, Bill and Valter Skarsgård, and two with his wife Megan Everett. He is an outspoken atheist and critic of religious schooling.
