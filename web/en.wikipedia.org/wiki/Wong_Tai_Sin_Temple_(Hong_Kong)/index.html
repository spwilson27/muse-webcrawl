Wong Tai Sin Temple (Hong Kong)

LocationWong Tai Sin, Kowloon, Hong Kong
Established1921
DeityWong Tai Sin (Huang Daxian)
Site areaapprox. 18,000 m²

Sik Sik Yuen Wong Tai Sin Temple (Chinese: 嗇色園黃大仙祠) is a well-known shrine in Hong Kong, located in the Wong Tai Sin area of Kowloon, which takes its name from the temple. The temple is dedicated to Wong Tai Sin, the "Great Immortal Wong", a Taoist deity renowned for the power of healing and for answering the prayers of worshippers — in Cantonese tradition, he is said to "grant whatever is requested" (有求必應).

History

According to legend, Wong Tai Sin was a shepherd boy from Jinhua, Zhejiang, who was led by an immortal to practise Taoism and later became a god worshipped for curing the sick. In 1915, Taoist priest Leung Ren-yan (梁仁菴) brought a portrait of the deity from Guangzhou to Hong Kong. After first worshipping at a small temple in Wan Chai, the portrait was moved in 1921 to the present site beneath Lion Rock, where followers had been advised by fortune-telling that a temple should be built.

The temple is administered by Sik Sik Yuen, a charitable organisation founded in 1921 that also runs schools, clinics and elderly care services.

The temple complex

The main hall, the Great Hall, shelters altars to Wong Tai Sin as well as to the Buddhist figure Guanyin and other deities; the temple unusually combines elements of Taoism, Buddhism and Confucianism. Other features include the Three Saints Hall, the Good Wish Garden (從心苑) with its pavilions and artificial lake, a Nine Dragon Wall modelled on the one in Beijing, and the Yuk Yik Fountain.

The temple is famous for kau chim, the practice of shaking a cylinder of numbered bamboo sticks until one falls out, whose number corresponds to a fortune poem interpreted by temple soothsayers. The temple is especially crowded on Wong Tai Sin's birthday (the 23rd day of the eighth lunar month) and during the Chinese New Year period, when worshippers compete to be the first to offer incense at midnight.

Transport

The temple is served by Wong Tai Sin station on the MTR Kwun Tong line, whose name likewise derives from the temple.
