Daraa

Daraa (Arabic: درعا) is a city in southwestern Syria, located about 13 kilometres (8.1 mi) north of the border with Jordan and some 90 kilometres south of Damascus. It is the capital of the Daraa Governorate and an important agricultural and trading centre of the Hauran plain.

History

Daraa is an ancient city, identified with the biblical Edrei and mentioned in Egyptian inscriptions. It flourished in the Roman, Byzantine and early Islamic periods and later became a station on the Hejaz railway. The city acquired a reputation as a hub for grain trade and smuggling owing to its position near the Jordanian frontier.

Syrian civil war

Daraa gained worldwide attention in March 2011 as the cradle of the Syrian uprising: the arrest and reported torture of teenage boys who had painted anti-government graffiti sparked protests that spread across the country and escalated into the Syrian civil war. Government forces retook the city in 2018 under a Russian-brokered reconciliation agreement, though unrest and clashes there continued in later years.
