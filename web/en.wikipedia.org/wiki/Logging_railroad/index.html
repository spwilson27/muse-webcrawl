Logging railroad
Type: Industrial railway
Era: 19th-20th centuries

A logging railroad is a railway built to transport felled timber from forests to sawmills. Especially common in North America, they used steep grades, temporary track, and specialised locomotives such as the Shay, Climax and Heisler geared engines.

History

Logging railroads expanded with steam power in the late 19th century, replacing river drives and ox teams, and declined with trucks and road building after World War II.

Equipment

Geared steam locomotives traded speed for tractive effort on rough, steep track, hauling log cars deep into mountainous timber country.
