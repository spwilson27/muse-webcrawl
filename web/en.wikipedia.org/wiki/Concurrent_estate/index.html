Concurrent estate

A concurrent estate, or co-tenancy, is a concept in real-property law under which two or more persons own the same interest in the same parcel of land at the same time. Common-law systems recognise several forms of concurrent ownership, which differ in how the owners' shares are held and what happens when one owner dies.

Forms

Joint tenancy requires the four unities of time, title, interest, and possession. Its defining feature is the right of survivorship: when one joint tenant dies, their interest passes automatically to the surviving joint tenants rather than to their estate. A joint tenant can sever the tenancy, converting it to a tenancy in common.

In a tenancy in common, each co-owner holds an undivided fractional share which may be unequal, and there is no right of survivorship: a deceased owner's share passes under their will or by intestacy.

A tenancy by the entirety, available in some jurisdictions only to married couples, treats the spouses as a single owner and generally protects the property from the individual creditors of one spouse.

Rights and duties

Co-tenants generally each have the right to possess the whole property, and may owe one another contributions toward necessary expenses such as taxes and repairs. A co-owner who wishes to end the co-ownership may bring an action for partition, asking a court to divide the land physically or to order its sale and divide the proceeds.
