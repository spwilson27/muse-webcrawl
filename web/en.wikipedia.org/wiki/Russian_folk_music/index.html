Russian folk music

Russian folk music encompasses the traditional vocal and instrumental music of the Russian people, developed over centuries in village life, ritual, work, and celebration. It is characterised regionally varied singing styles, distinctive instruments, and a repertoire of epic, lyrical, dance, and ritual songs.

Songs and singing

Vocal music predominates: epic narrative songs (byliny), sung by northern storytellers; drawn-out lyrical songs (protyazhnye) with elaborate melismatic ornamentation and improvised under-voices (podgoloski), creating a distinctive polyphony; calendar and wedding ritual songs; lullabies; and the short, witty chastushka quatrains that flourished from the late nineteenth century. Well-known songs include "The Red Sarafan," "Volga Boatmen's Song," and "Korobeiniki."

Instruments

Traditional instruments include the triangular balalaika, the psaltery-like gusli, the domra, wind instruments such as the zhaleika and svirel, the rozhok horn, wooden spoons (lozhki), and later the accordion (garmon) and bayan. In the late nineteenth century Vasily Andreyev organised the first balalaika orchestra, launching folk-instrument orchestras that became a fixture of Soviet-era performance.

Legacy

Russian composers from Glinka onward drew heavily on folk material, and in the Soviet period state folk ensembles professionalised the tradition while collectors and ethnographers recorded village singers, preserving a repertoire that continues to be revived today.
