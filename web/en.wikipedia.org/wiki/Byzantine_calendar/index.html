Byzantine calendar

The Byzantine calendar, also called the Roman calendar by its users, the Creation Era of Constantinople, or the Era of the World (Ancient Greek: Ἔτος Κόσμου, Étos Kósmou), was the calendar used by the Eastern Orthodox Church from c. 691 to 1728 in the Ecumenical Patriarchate. It was also the official calendar of the Byzantine Empire from 988 to 1453 and of Kievan Rus' and Russia from c. 988 to 1700.

Structure

The calendar was based on the Julian calendar, except that the year started on 1 September and the year number was reckoned from the date of the creation of the world, calculated in the Septuagint tradition as 1 September 5509 BC. Thus the Byzantine year 1 ran from 1 September 5509 BC to 31 August 5508 BC. Years were also identified by their place in the 15-year indiction cycle, originally a Roman fiscal period.

Conversion

To convert a Byzantine year (AM, Anno Mundi) to an AD date: for dates from 1 September to 31 December, subtract 5509; for dates from 1 January to 31 August, subtract 5508. For example, the fall of Constantinople on 29 May 1453 fell in the Byzantine year 6961.

History and replacement

The era was fixed in its final form by the 7th century and was adopted officially under Basil I's successors; it was used in Russia until Peter the Great replaced it with the Anno Domini system on 1 January 1700. The Eastern Orthodox Church retained the September New Year as the start of the liturgical year, a practice still observed.
