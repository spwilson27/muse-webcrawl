Langues d'oïl

The langues d'oïl (French for "oïl languages") are a dialect continuum of Romance languages and dialects spoken in northern France, southern Belgium, and the Channel Islands. They include standard French as well as Picard, Norman, Walloon, Gallo, Champenois, Poitevin-Saintongeais, and others.

Name and history

The name derives from the Old French word oïl (modern oui) for "yes", contrasting with the langues d'oc of southern France, named for their word òc. The oïl dialects developed from Vulgar Latin spoken in northern Gaul. Over the centuries the Francien dialect of Paris rose to dominance and became standard French, while the other oïl varieties declined and are now mostly endangered regional languages.
