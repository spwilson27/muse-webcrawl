Chuckwagon

A chuckwagon serving cowboys on the trail

A chuckwagon is a horse-drawn wagon used for storing food and cooking equipment on a cattle drive. Part of a cowboy's work was to take cattle from a ranch to a market, and the chuckwagon carried the provisions needed to feed the men along the way. The wagon was also the social centre of the outfit, where cowboys ate their meals and gathered.

History

The chuckwagon is credited to Texas rancher Charles Goodnight, who in 1866 designed a durable field kitchen from a surplus Army wagon for use on cattle drives. He added a slanted wooden box, the "chuck box", at the rear, fitted with compartments and drawers for utensils, spices, and dry goods, and a hinged lid that folded down into a flat work surface for the cook.

The name derives from "chuck", a slang term for food. The cook, often called "Cookie", was one of the most important members of the crew, and the chuckwagon also carried bedrolls, tools, and medical supplies.

Legacy

Chuckwagons remain symbols of the American West. Chuckwagon racing is a popular event at rodeos such as the Calgary Stampede, in which teams race covered wagons pulled by thoroughbred horses. Chuckwagon-style cooking is also celebrated at heritage festivals and cook-offs across the United States and Canada.
