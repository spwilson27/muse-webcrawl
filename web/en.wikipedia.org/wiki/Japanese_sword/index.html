Japanese sword

A Japanese sword (nihontō) is a traditionally made sword from Japan. The best-known type is the katana, the long sword worn by samurai; others include the shorter wakizashi, the tantō dagger, the earlier tachi, and the great ōdachi.

Manufacture

Blades are forged from tamahagane steel smelted in a tatara furnace, repeatedly folded and forge-welded, then differentially hardened by coating the blade with clay before quenching, producing a hard edge with a visible temper line (hamon) and a softer, shock-absorbing spine.

History

Straight swords based on Chinese models were made in ancient Japan; the curved single-edged blade emerged in the Heian period. The Kamakura period is regarded as a golden age of swordsmithing, with masters such as Masamune. After the Meiji Restoration's ban on wearing swords, production declined, reviving as an art craft; surviving antique blades are prized, and some are designated National Treasures.

Classification

Swords are classified by length and period: kotō (old swords, before about 1596), shintō, shinshintō, gendaitō (modern) and shinsakutō (newly made).
