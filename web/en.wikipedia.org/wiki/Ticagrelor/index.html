Ticagrelor, sold under the brand names Brilinta and Brilique, is an antiplatelet medication used to reduce the risk of cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction, and stroke in people with acute coronary syndrome, and in some patients with coronary artery disease or after ischaemic stroke. It was developed by AstraZeneca and approved in the European Union in 2010 and by the US Food and Drug Administration in 2011.

Mechanism of action

Ticagrelor is a cyclopentyltriazolopyrimidine that reversibly binds to the P2Y12 subtype of the ADP receptor on platelet cell membranes, preventing ADP-mediated platelet activation and aggregation. Unlike the thienopyridines clopidogrel and prasugrel, it is not a prodrug and does not require metabolic activation; it also has an active metabolite of roughly equal potency. Its reversible binding gives it a faster onset and faster offset of action than clopidogrel.

Clinical use

The pivotal PLATO trial (2009), involving more than 18,000 patients with acute coronary syndrome, found ticagrelor (with aspirin) superior to clopidogrel in reducing the composite of cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction, and stroke, including a reduction in all-cause mortality. Ticagrelor is usually given as a 180 mg loading dose followed by 90 mg twice daily, in combination with low-dose aspirin, typically for up to twelve months after an acute coronary event or stent placement. A 60 mg twice-daily dose is used for extended prevention in some high-risk patients.

Adverse effects

The most important adverse effect is bleeding, including an increased risk of non-surgical major bleeding. Distinctive side effects include dyspnoea (shortness of breath, reported in roughly one in seven patients in PLATO) and occasional ventricular pauses, both attributed to ticagrelor's inhibition of adenosine uptake. It should not be combined with strong CYP3A inhibitors or inducers, and aspirin doses above 100 mg daily may reduce its effectiveness.
