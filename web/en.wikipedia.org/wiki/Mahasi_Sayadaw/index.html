Mahasi Sayadaw

Mahasi Sayadaw U Sobhana (29 July 1904 – 14 August 1982) was a Burmese Theravada Buddhist monk and meditation master who had a significant impact on the teaching of vipassanā (insight) meditation in the West and throughout Asia. He developed the "Mahasi method", a systematic approach to insight meditation based on mindful noting, which he taught at the Mahasi Meditation Centre in Rangoon (Yangon) and which spread through a network of centres in Burma, Thailand, Sri Lanka, and later Europe and North America.

Life

U Sobhana was born in Seikkhun village in Upper Burma in 1904. He became a novice monk at twelve and was fully ordained at twenty. He excelled in the study of the Pali scriptures, passing the highest examinations, and later practised meditation under Mingun Jetavan Sayadaw. During the Second World War he refined his teaching method at a monastery in Thaton, and in 1947 the Burmese prime minister U Nu invited him to lead a new meditation centre in Rangoon, Sasana Yeiktha, which became known as the Mahasi Meditation Centre.

Teaching

The Mahasi method centres on moment-to-moment mindfulness of bodily and mental phenomena using mental noting — labelling experiences such as "rising" and "falling" of the abdomen in sitting meditation, and noting sensations, thoughts and intentions in walking meditation and daily activities. By sustained noting, the meditator is said to perceive the three marks of existence — impermanence, suffering and non-self — directly, progressing through the stages of insight described in the commentaries. Mahasi Sayadaw played a key role in the Sixth Buddhist Council (1954–56) and authored numerous works on Abhidhamma and meditation.

Legacy

His students and grand-students — including Sayadaw U Pandita, and through them teachers in the West — carried the method worldwide, making it one of the most influential forms of vipassanā practice in the modern world.
