Fort Bridger

Fort Bridger was originally a 19th-century fur trading post established in 1843 by mountain man Jim Bridger and his partner Louis Vasquez on Blacks Fork of the Green River, in what is now Uinta County, Wyoming. It became one of the most important supply points for emigrants on the Oregon, California, and Mormon trails.

History

Bridger and Vasquez built a small fort with a blacksmith shop and stocked trade goods for passing emigrant companies. Its location west of South Pass made it a natural junction: from Fort Bridger, trails branched toward the Great Salt Lake (the Hastings Cutoff and later the Mormon Trail) and toward Fort Hall. In 1847 Brigham Young's pioneer company passed through, and Mormon settlers under tension with Bridger operated nearby Fort Supply.

In 1853 Mormon militia arrested Bridger on charges of selling arms and alcohol to Native Americans; he fled, and the Mormons purchased or seized the post. During the Utah War of 1857–58, Mormon forces burned the fort rather than let it fall to the advancing U.S. Army under Albert Sidney Johnston. The Army rebuilt and garrisoned Fort Bridger from 1858, keeping it active until 1890, with interruptions, and a detachment of the Pony Express and the transcontinental telegraph used the site.

Today

The grounds are preserved as the Fort Bridger State Historic Site, with reconstructed buildings, a museum, and an annual mountain man rendezvous held each Labor Day weekend.
