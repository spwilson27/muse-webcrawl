Team 10

Formation1953 (as CIAM critics); first meeting under the name, 1960
Dissolved1981 (death of Jaap Bakema)
FieldArchitecture and urban planning
Key membersAlison and Peter Smithson, Aldo van Eyck, Jaap Bakema, Giancarlo De Carlo, Georges Candilis, Shadrach Woods

Team 10, also known as Team X, was a group of architects and other invited participants who assembled from July 1953 at the 9th Congress of CIAM and created a schism within CIAM by challenging its doctrinaire approach to urbanism. The group criticised the rigid functional zoning of the Athens Charter and sought a humanist architecture organised around community, identity and patterns of human association.

Origins

The younger CIAM members charged with preparing the 10th congress in Dubrovnik (1956)—among them Alison and Peter Smithson, Aldo van Eyck, Jaap Bakema, Georges Candilis, Shadrach Woods and John Voelcker—became known as Team 10. Their critique contributed to the dissolution of CIAM in 1959 at the Otterlo meeting. From 1960 the group met informally, without formal membership or manifesto, publishing the Team 10 Primer (edited by Alison Smithson, 1968).

Ideas

Team 10 replaced the four abstract functions of the Charter—dwelling, work, recreation, transport—with scales of association: house, street, district and city. Concepts such as "cluster", "mobility", "stem" and "web" informed projects like the Smithsons' Golden Lane housing, Candilis-Josic-Woods' Toulouse-Le Mirail and the Free University of Berlin, and van Eyck's Amsterdam Orphanage, a key work of structuralism. The group's thinking influenced New Brutalism, Dutch structuralism and later participatory design.

Legacy

Meetings continued until Bakema's death in 1981. Although Team 10 produced few buildings collectively, its members' writings and projects reshaped postwar architectural discourse, moving modernism toward context, user participation and the "in-between" realm van Eyck championed.
