Shiing-Shen Chern

Shiing-Shen Chern (Chinese: 陳省身; 26 October 1911 – 3 December 2004) was a Chinese-American mathematician and poet. He is regarded as the father of global differential geometry, and one of the most influential geometers of the twentieth century.

Career

Born in Jiaxing, Zhejiang, Chern studied at Nankai and Tsinghua universities, then in Hamburg under Wilhelm Blaschke and in Paris with Élie Cartan. During the Second Sino-Japanese War he worked at the Academia Sinica's Institute of Mathematics in China, and later at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton, the University of Chicago, and the University of California, Berkeley, where he co-founded the Mathematical Sciences Research Institute (MSRI) in 1982.

Work

Chern's contributions include the Chern classes in algebraic topology, the Chern–Gauss–Bonnet theorem (an intrinsic proof of the generalised Gauss–Bonnet formula), Chern–Weil theory, and the Chern–Simons form, which found applications in theoretical physics. He received the US National Medal of Science (1975) and the Wolf Prize (1983/84).
