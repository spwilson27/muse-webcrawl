Frustrated Lewis pair

A frustrated Lewis pair (FLP) is a compound or mixture containing a Lewis acid and a Lewis base that, because of steric hindrance, cannot combine to form a classical adduct. The unquenched reactivity of the pair can instead be directed at other molecules: famously, such pairs can heterolytically split dihydrogen (H2), enabling metal-free hydrogenation catalysis.

History

The concept was articulated by Douglas Stephan and co-workers in 2006, who showed that a bulky phosphine and the strong Lewis acid tris(pentafluorophenyl)borane could activate H2. The term "frustrated" evokes the pair's inability to satisfy the usual acid–base interaction. The idea has roots in earlier observations, including work by H. C. Brown on steric effects in Lewis adducts and by Tochtermann on unquenched acid/base pairs.

Applications

FLPs activate a range of small molecules including CO2, alkenes, alkynes, and N2O, and catalyse hydrogenation of imines, enamines and some alkenes without transition metals. Intramolecular FLPs, in which acid and base are linked in one molecule, are also known. The field has expanded toward cheaper, earth-abundant components and asymmetric variants for enantioselective synthesis.
