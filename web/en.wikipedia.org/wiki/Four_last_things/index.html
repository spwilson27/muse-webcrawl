Four last things

In Christian eschatology, the Four Last Things — Latin: quattuor novissima — are Death, Judgment, Heaven, and Hell: the final realities awaiting every human soul. Meditation on the Four Last Things has been a central theme of Christian preaching, devotion, and art, intended to remind believers of the transience of earthly life and the eternal consequences of their choices.

The four

  • Death — the separation of body and soul, and the end of the time of merit and choice.
  • Judgment — the particular judgment of each soul at death, and the general (Last) Judgment at the end of time, when Christ returns.
  • Heaven — the state of eternal, perfect happiness in the vision of God, promised to the saved.
  • Hell — the state of eternal separation from God, the destiny of the damned.

In devotion and art

The Four Last Things were a favourite subject of sermons, treatises, and spiritual exercises from the medieval period through the Counter-Reformation; works such as the Imitation of Christ and later Jesuit meditations structured prayer around them. They appear in countless altarpieces, frescoes and Books of Hours, often alongside the related theme of the memento mori. Catholic tradition sometimes treats purgatory as a related, though temporary, post-mortem state.
