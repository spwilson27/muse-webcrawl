Ryan Coogler
Born: Ryan Kyle Coogler, May 23, 1986, Oakland, California, U.S.
Occupation: Film director, screenwriter, producer
Years active: 2009–present

Ryan Kyle Coogler (born May 23, 1986) is an American film director, producer, and screenwriter. He is a recipient of four NAACP Image Awards and four Black Reel Awards, and has been nominated for two Academy Awards, a Golden Globe Award, and a Grammy Award.

Coogler first gained recognition for writing and directing the independent drama Fruitvale Station (2013), based on the fatal police shooting of Oscar Grant, which won the Grand Jury Prize and Audience Award at the Sundance Film Festival. He then directed and co-wrote the Rocky spin-off sequel Creed (2015) and the Marvel Studios superhero film Black Panther (2018), the latter of which became the highest-grossing film of all time by an African American director and was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Picture. He returned to direct Black Panther: Wakanda Forever (2022) and the horror film Sinners (2025).

Early life

Coogler was born in Oakland, California, to a community organizer mother and a juvenile hall probation counselor father. He attended Saint Mary's College of California on a football scholarship, where he played wide receiver and studied chemistry, before the football program was cancelled; he then transferred to Sacramento State University, where he graduated with a degree in finance. He went on to earn a Master of Fine Arts from the USC School of Cinematic Arts.

Career

Coogler frequently collaborates with actor Michael B. Jordan, who has appeared in all of his feature films, as well as with composer Ludwig Göransson and production designer Hannah Beachler. In 2016 he co-founded the production company Proximity Media with his wife, Zinzi Coogler, and Sev Ohanian. His films are noted for their grounding in African American communities and their blend of personal storytelling with genre filmmaking.
