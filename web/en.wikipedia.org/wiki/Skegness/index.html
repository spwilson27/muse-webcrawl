Skegness

Skegness
County: Lincolnshire, England
Population: ~21,000
Known for: Seaside resort

Skegness is a seaside town and civil parish in the East Lindsey district of Lincolnshire, England, on the Lincolnshire coast of the North Sea. It is one of Britain's best-known traditional seaside resorts, famed for its long sandy beach, pier, and the slogan "Skegness is SO bracing", popularised by the 1908 "Jolly Fisherman" poster designed by John Hassall for the Great Northern Railway.

History

The original settlement, a small fishing village, was washed away by the sea in the 1520s, and the town was rebuilt further inland. Skegness developed as a resort after the railway arrived in 1875, promoted by the Earl of Scarbrough, whose estate developed the town with wide streets, a pier (opened 1881), and gardens. The town motto derives from the bracing east-coast air said to benefit health.

Attractions

In 1936 Billy Butlin opened his first holiday camp just outside the town at Ingoldmells, pioneering the British holiday camp; the resort still operates today. Other attractions include the Skegness Pier, the Embassy Theatre, the Natureland Seal Sanctuary, and the nearby Gibraltar Point nature reserve to the south. The town's clock tower, unveiled in 1899 to commemorate Queen Victoria's Diamond Jubilee, is a local landmark.

Geography

Skegness sits on low-lying land protected by sea defences; the area is within the floodplain defended after the devastating North Sea flood of 1953. The town is the terminus of the Poacher Line railway from Grantham and Nottingham.
