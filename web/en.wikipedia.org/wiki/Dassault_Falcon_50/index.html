The Falcon 50 was the first business jet with the range to cross the Atlantic non-stop.

The Dassault Falcon 50 is a French super-midsize, long-range business jet, featuring a trijet layout with an S-duct air intake for the central engine. It was the first business jet in the world with intercontinental range, capable of crossing the Atlantic Ocean non-stop. The Falcon 50 was developed from the earlier twin-engine Falcon 20.

Development and design

Dassault began design in 1974 as a development of the Falcon 20 with greater range, retaining the fuselage cross-section but adding a third engine on the tail, a new supercritical wing and increased fuel capacity. The prototype first flew on 7 November 1976 and certification followed in 1979. Its three Garrett TFE731 turbofans gave it the redundancy and performance to operate over long over-water routes under extended-range rules of the era, a capability that made it popular with governments and corporations.

Variants

The improved Falcon 50EX, introduced in 1996, featured more efficient engines, longer range, improved avionics and reduced cabin noise. The type was succeeded in the Dassault line by the Falcon 900, which used a larger fuselage.

Operators

Falcon 50s have served as VIP transports with several air forces, including those of France, Italy, Spain, Morocco and South Africa, as well as with corporate and charter operators worldwide. The French Navy operated a maritime-surveillance variant, the Falcon 50 Surmar.

Specifications

  • Crew: 2; capacity: 8–9 passengers
  • Maximum speed: Mach 0.84
  • Range: about 5,700 km (3,100 nmi) for the 50EX
  • Service ceiling: 14,935 m (49,000 ft)
