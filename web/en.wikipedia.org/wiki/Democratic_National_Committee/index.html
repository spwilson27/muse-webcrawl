The Democratic National Committee (DNC) is the principal executive leadership board of the United States Democratic Party. Established at the 1848 Democratic National Convention, it is responsible for promoting the Democratic platform, coordinating fundraising and election strategy, and organising the Democratic National Convention held every four years to nominate the party's candidate for President.

Role and structure

The DNC is composed of members elected by state parties and Democratic officeholders; its chairperson is elected by the committee. Between conventions the DNC sets party rules for delegate selection, maintains voter data and research operations, and raises funds. It does not directly control party candidates or officeholders — a contrast with party organisations in many parliamentary democracies — but works through state and local party committees and affiliated bodies such as the Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee and the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee.

History

The committee's headquarters on South Capitol Street in Washington, D.C. entered history in 1972, when operatives linked to President Richard Nixon's re-election campaign were caught burglarising the DNC offices in the Watergate complex — the crime that ignited the Watergate scandal and led to Nixon's resignation. The break-in was run from the Committee to Re-elect the President.

In 2016 the DNC was again the target of a break-in, this time a cyber-attack: internal emails stolen by hackers and published by WikiLeaks during the presidential campaign revealed apparent bias toward Hillary Clinton over Bernie Sanders in the primaries, prompting the resignation of chair Debbie Wasserman Schultz on the eve of the convention.

Chairs

Recent chairs include Howard Dean (2005–2009), Tim Kaine (2009–2011), Debbie Wasserman Schultz (2011–2016), Tom Perez (2017–2021), Jaime Harrison (2021–2025) and Ken Martin, elected on 1 February 2025.
