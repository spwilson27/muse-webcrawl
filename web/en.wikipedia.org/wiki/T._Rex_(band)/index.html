T. Rex (band)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

T. Rex were an English rock band formed in London in 1967 by singer-songwriter and guitarist Marc Bolan, who remained the band's only constant member. Initially performing as the psychedelic folk duo Tyrannosaurus Rex with percussionist Steve Peregrin Took, the band shortened their name and adopted an electric sound in 1970, becoming pioneers of the glam rock movement.

History

The acoustic Tyrannosaurus Rex released four albums of whimsical, Tolkien-influenced folk between 1968 and 1970, championed by DJ John Peel. Bolan's transition to electric music brought breakthrough success: the single "Ride a White Swan" (1970) reached No. 2 in the UK, and the albums Electric Warrior (1971) and The Slider (1972) defined the glam era, producing hits including "Hot Love", "Get It On" (released as "Bang a Gong (Get It On)" in the US), "Telegram Sam", "Metal Guru", "Children of the Revolution", "Solid Gold Easy Action" and "20th Century Boy".

The phenomenon dubbed "T. Rextasy" rivalled Beatlemania in the British press of 1971–72. After 1973 the band's commercial fortunes faded amid Bolan's personal difficulties and changing musical fashion, though albums such as Tanx (1973) and Dandy in the Underworld (1977) won later critical reappraisal. Bolan was killed in a car crash in Barnes, London, on 16 September 1977, two weeks before his 30th birthday, effectively ending the band.

Legacy

T. Rex's fusion of rock and roll, glitter imagery and Bolan's distinctive vibrato-laden vocals influenced glam, punk, Britpop and alternative rock; artists from David Bowie to the Smiths and Oasis acknowledged their impact. The band was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2020.
