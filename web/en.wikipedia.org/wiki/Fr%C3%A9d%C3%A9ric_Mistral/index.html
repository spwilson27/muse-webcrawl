Frédéric Mistral (8 September 1830 – 25 March 1914) was a French writer of Occitan literature and lexicographer of the Provençal form of the language. He received the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1904, shared with José Echegaray, "in recognition of the fresh originality and true inspiration of his poetic production."

Life and work

Born in Maillane in the Bouches-du-Rhône, Mistral in 1854 co-founded the Félibrige, a movement devoted to reviving Provençal language and culture. His epic poem Mirèio (1859), written in Provençal and telling of the love of a farmer's daughter for a basket-weaver, won wide acclaim and remains his masterpiece. He also compiled the monumental Provençal dictionary Lou Tresor dóu Felibrige (1878–1886).

Legacy

Mistral used his Nobel prize money to found the Museon Arlaten, an ethnographic museum in Arles. The Chilean poet Gabriela Mistral took her pen name in his honor. He is remembered as the central figure of the nineteenth-century Occitan revival.
