The Greco-Turkish War of 1919–1922 was waged between Greece and the Turkish National Movement during the partitioning of the Ottoman Empire in the aftermath of World War I. In Greece it is known as the Asia Minor Campaign and its outcome as the Asia Minor Catastrophe; in Turkey it forms the western front of the Turkish War of Independence. Greek ambitions to realise the Megali Idea — uniting Greek-inhabited Ottoman lands — under Prime Minister Eleftherios Venizelos drove the Greek landing at Smyrna on 15 May 1919, authorised by the Allies.

Course

The Treaty of Sèvres (1920) awarded Greece Smyrna and Eastern Thrace, but was rejected by Mustafa Kemal's nationalist government in Ankara. Greek offensives in 1920–21 took Eskişehir and Kütahya and advanced towards Ankara, but were checked at the Battle of the Sakarya (August–September 1921). After a year of stalemate, the Turkish Great Offensive of August 1922 shattered the Greek front at Dumlupınar; Turkish forces re-entered Smyrna (İzmir) on 9 September 1922. Days later the Great Fire of Smyrna destroyed the Greek and Armenian quarters of the city, and hundreds of thousands of Greek and Armenian civilians fled or were evacuated.

Aftermath

The Armistice of Mudanya (11 October 1922) ended the fighting, and the Treaty of Lausanne (24 July 1923) superseded Sèvres, recognising the Republic of Turkey within essentially its modern borders. Lausanne legitimised a compulsory population exchange: about 1.2–1.5 million Orthodox Christians left Turkey for Greece, and some 400,000 Muslims left Greece for Turkey — following the far larger wartime flight and the deaths of hundreds of thousands of Ottoman Greeks and Armenians. In Greece the disaster brought down the government and monarchy's prestige: six royalist leaders were executed after the Trial of the Six, Venizelos negotiated Lausanne, and the refugee influx transformed Greek society for generations.
