Lives of the Most Excellent Painters, Sculptors, and Architects

Lives of the Most Excellent Painters, Sculptors, and Architects (Italian: Le Vite de' più eccellenti pittori, scultori, e architettori), often simply called the Lives (Le Vite), is a series of biographies of artists written by the Italian painter and architect Giorgio Vasari. First published in Florence in 1550, with a greatly expanded second edition in 1568, it is regarded as the founding work of art history and one of the most influential books ever written about the visual arts.

Overview

Vasari's work is structured as a narrative of the rebirth (rinascita) of Italian art after a period of decline following antiquity. He divided the history of Renaissance art into three ages of progressive improvement, culminating in the perfection of his contemporary and hero, Michelangelo Buonarroti. The biographies proceed chronologically from Cimabue and Giotto in the late thirteenth century through masters such as Leonardo da Vinci, Raphael, and Titian.

Editions

The first edition was printed in Florence by Lorenzo Torrentino in 1550 and dedicated to Cosimo I de' Medici. The second edition, published by the Giunti press in 1568, was substantially revised and expanded: Vasari added biographies of living artists, included woodcut portraits of many of the artists (some of them fanciful), and updated the text following his travels around Italy to verify details.

Influence and criticism

The Lives established the biographical model of art writing that dominated the field for centuries and shaped the canon of Renaissance artists. Modern scholars caution that Vasari was not a neutral historian: he was biased toward artists of his native Florence and Tuscany, promoted the idea of a single line of artistic progress, and included anecdotes that were sometimes inaccurate or invented. Nevertheless, the work remains an indispensable primary source for the art of the Italian Renaissance.
