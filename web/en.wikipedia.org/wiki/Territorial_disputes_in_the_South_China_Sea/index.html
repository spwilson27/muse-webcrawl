Territorial disputes in the South China Sea

The territorial disputes in the South China Sea involve competing claims by several sovereign states over islands, reefs, and maritime zones in the South China Sea. The claimants include the People's Republic of China, the Republic of China (Taiwan), Vietnam, the Philippines, Malaysia, and Brunei. The disputes concern sovereignty over the Spratly Islands, the Paracel Islands, Scarborough Shoal, and other features, as well as the delimitation of exclusive economic zones and continental shelves.

Background

The South China Sea is a major shipping route through which trillions of dollars of trade pass each year, and its waters are believed to hold significant oil and natural gas reserves. China bases its claims largely on the "nine-dash line," a demarcation that first appeared on Chinese maps in the late 1940s and encompasses most of the sea. Other claimants base their claims on geography, historical occupation, and the provisions of the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS).

Key disputes

Spratly Islands

The Spratly Islands are claimed in whole or in part by China, Taiwan, Vietnam, the Philippines, Malaysia, and Brunei. Several claimants maintain garrisons and structures on features they occupy. Since about 2013, China has undertaken large-scale land reclamation and construction on seven features in the Spratlys, including Fiery Cross Reef, Subi Reef, and Mischief Reef.

Paracel Islands

The Paracel Islands are claimed by China, Taiwan, and Vietnam but have been controlled by China since the Battle of the Paracel Islands in 1974, when Chinese forces seized the islands from South Vietnam.

Scarborough Shoal

Scarborough Shoal is claimed by China, Taiwan, and the Philippines. Following a standoff in 2012, China gained effective control of the shoal, though Filipino fishermen have since been permitted varying degrees of access to its vicinity.

2016 arbitral ruling

In 2013 the Philippines initiated arbitration against China under UNCLOS. On 12 July 2016, the arbitral tribunal in Philippines v. China ruled that China's claims to historic rights within the nine-dash line were without lawful effect insofar as they exceed the entitlements provided by UNCLOS, and that certain Chinese activities had violated the Philippines' sovereign rights. China rejected the ruling, declaring it null and void, and has not complied with it.
