Sarin

Sarin (NATO designation GB) is an extremely toxic organophosphorus compound, an organophosphate nerve agent. It is a colourless, odourless liquid that inhibits acetylcholinesterase, causing overstimulation of nerves and muscles. Its production and stockpiling are outlawed under the Chemical Weapons Convention of 1993.

Sarin
Formula: C4H10FO2P
Type: Nerve agent, organophosphate
NATO designation: GB
Status: Banned by CWC (1993)

History

Sarin was discovered in 1938 in Germany by scientists at IG Farben developing pesticides; its name derives from its discoverers (Schrader, Ambros, Rüdiger, van der Linde). Nazi Germany produced it but did not use it in World War II.

Effects and treatment

Exposure causes miosis (pinpoint pupils), salivation, convulsions and death by asphyxiation from paralysis of respiratory muscles. Antidotes include atropine and pralidoxime.

Use in attacks

Sarin was used by Iraq in the Iran–Iraq War, in the 1995 Tokyo subway attack by Aum Shinrikyo (13 deaths), and in attacks during the Syrian civil war, notably Ghouta (2013) and Khan Shaykhun (2017).
