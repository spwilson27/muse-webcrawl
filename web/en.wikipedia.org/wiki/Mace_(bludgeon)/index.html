Mace (bludgeon)

A mace is a blunt weapon, a type of club that uses a heavy head on the end of a handle to deliver powerful blows. Stone and copper maceheads are among the earliest weapons known, appearing in prehistoric Egypt and Mesopotamia, and the weapon remained in military and ceremonial use for millennia.

History

By the Middle Ages, metal maces with flanged or knobbed heads were widely used by infantry and cavalry because they could injure an opponent armoured in mail or plate, against which swords were less effective. The flanged mace was particularly associated with Eastern Europe and the steppe.

Ceremonial use

The mace survives today chiefly as a symbol of authority: ceremonial maces are carried in parliaments and universities, and the House of Commons of the United Kingdom and the United States House of Representatives both conduct business in the presence of a mace.
