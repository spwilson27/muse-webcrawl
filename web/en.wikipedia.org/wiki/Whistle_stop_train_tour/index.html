Whistle stop train tour

A whistle stop train tour is a style of political campaigning in which a politician travels by train through small towns, making a series of brief appearances or speeches from the rear platform of the train. The term derives from "whistle stop", a small community at which trains stop only on signal.

History

Train campaigning became common in the United States in the late 19th century, when rail was the fastest way to reach many communities. William Jennings Bryan was a famous practitioner of relentless rail tours. In 1948 President Harry S. Truman made the whistle stop tour legendary, travelling some 31,000 miles and delivering hundreds of rear-platform speeches during his come-from-behind victory in that year's presidential election.

Decline and revival

Air travel and television reduced the practicality of train tours after the 1950s, but candidates have periodically revived them for their nostalgic value and media coverage, including Ronald Reagan in 1984, and Barack Obama and Joe Biden, who used Amtrak trains on campaign trips in 2008 and 2020 respectively.
