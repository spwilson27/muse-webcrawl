The T-90 is a third-generation Russian main battle tank developed by Uralvagonzavod from the T-72 design. It entered service with the Russian Army in 1992 and became the most widely exported tank of its era, forming the backbone of several armies.

Development

The T-90 combines the hull and chassis of the T-72 with the turret systems, fire control, and protection features of the T-80U, including Kontakt-5 explosive reactive armour and Shtora-1 infrared countermeasures. The export T-90S was built under licence in India as the Bhishma. The modernised T-90M "Proryv" features a new turret with Relikt ERA, improved autoloader protection, a remote-controlled weapon station, and a new fire-control system.

Combat use

T-90s have been used in Dagestan and Chechnya, by the Syrian Army in the Syrian Civil War, and extensively by Russia in the Russo-Ukrainian War, where substantial numbers, including T-90Ms, were lost — the type's first large-scale high-intensity combat.
