Cervarix
Type: HPV vaccine (bivalent)
Targets: HPV types 16 and 18
Manufacturer: GSK
Approval: EU 2007; US 2009
Cervarix, a bivalent vaccine against human papillomavirus.

Cervarix is a vaccine against certain types of cancer-causing human papillomavirus (HPV), developed by the pharmaceutical company GSK (GlaxoSmithKline). It is a bivalent vaccine, protecting against HPV types 16 and 18, which together cause about 70% of cervical cancers worldwide.

Composition and mechanism

Cervarix contains virus-like particles (VLPs) assembled from the L1 capsid protein of HPV types 16 and 18, produced in insect cells using a baculovirus expression system. The vaccine uses GSK's AS04 adjuvant system, combining aluminium hydroxide with monophosphoryl lipid A (MPL), which is intended to enhance and prolong the immune response. It contains no live virus and cannot cause HPV infection.

Approval and use

Cervarix was approved in the European Union in 2007 and by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration in October 2009, for the prevention of cervical cancer and precancerous lesions in females. It is administered by intramuscular injection, typically in a two- or three-dose schedule depending on age. In 2016 GSK voluntarily withdrew Cervarix from the United States market, citing low demand as the competing vaccine Gardasil (which covers additional HPV types) dominated; Cervarix continued to be sold in many other countries and used in national immunisation programmes, including school-based vaccination of girls.

Efficacy

Large clinical trials (including the PATRICIA trial) demonstrated high efficacy against persistent infection and high-grade cervical intraepithelial neoplasia associated with HPV 16 and 18, with evidence of cross-protection against some related types such as 31 and 45.
