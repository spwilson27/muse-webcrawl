Fundamental frequency

The fundamental frequency, often referred to simply as the fundamental, is defined as the lowest frequency of a periodic waveform. In music, the fundamental is the musical pitch of a note that is perceived as the lowest partial present. It is denoted f0 (or FF), and in terms of a superposition of sinusoids it is the greatest common divisor of the frequencies of all the harmonic components.

Explanation

All sinusoidal and many non-sinusoidal waveforms repeat exactly over time — they are periodic. The period is the duration of one cycle, and the fundamental frequency is its reciprocal:

f0 = 1 / T

For a vibrating string or an air column, the fundamental is the mode of vibration in which the whole medium vibrates as a single segment; higher modes, the harmonics, vibrate at integer multiples of the fundamental. For a string fixed at both ends, the fundamental wavelength is twice the string length, so f0 = v / 2L, where v is the wave speed on the string.

Perception

The human auditory system usually perceives the pitch of a complex tone at its fundamental, even when that component is weak or absent — the phenomenon of the missing fundamental, exploited in small loudspeakers and telephone systems. In speech analysis, f0 is the main acoustic correlate of intonation and voice pitch, typically around 85–155 Hz for adult men and 165–255 Hz for adult women.
