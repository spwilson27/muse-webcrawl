Poitín

A bottle of Irish poitín

Poitín (anglicised as poteen) is a traditional Irish distilled beverage. It was traditionally distilled in a small pot still, from which it takes its name — poitín is the diminutive of the Irish word pota, "pot". It is typically a very strong spirit, historically produced illicitly, and is often compared to moonshine in other countries.

History

Poitín has been made in Ireland for centuries, originally from malted barley, and later also from ingredients such as potatoes, sugar beet, and molasses. In 1661 the English Crown made private distillation illegal in Ireland without a licence, driving production underground and making poitín one of the world's oldest illicit spirits. Illicit producers, sometimes called moonshiners, distilled in remote areas to avoid excise officers.

Legal status

In 1997 Irish law changed to allow poitín to be produced and sold legally under licence, and several distilleries now market it openly. In 2008, poitín was granted Geographical Indicative status by the European Union. Traditional poitín ranged from about 40% to as much as 90% alcohol by volume; modern legal versions are usually bottled at strengths comparable to other spirits.
