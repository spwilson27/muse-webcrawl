Sarah Waters

Sarah Waters (born 21 July 1966) is a Welsh novelist best known for historical fiction with lesbian protagonists, especially set in Victorian and mid-20th-century Britain.

Works

Her debut, Tipping the Velvet (1998), a picaresque novel about a music-hall performer, was adapted by the BBC. Affinity (1999) and Fingersmith (2002) followed; Fingersmith was shortlisted for the Booker Prize and adapted for television and, loosely, as the Korean film The Handmaiden. Later novels — The Night Watch (2006), The Little Stranger (2009), a ghost story shortlisted for the Booker Prize, and The Paying Guests (2014) — moved to the 1920s–1940s.

Background

Waters was born in Neyland, Wales, and wrote a doctoral thesis on lesbian historical fiction before turning to fiction herself. She has won the Betty Trask Award and been named among Granta's Best of Young British Novelists.
