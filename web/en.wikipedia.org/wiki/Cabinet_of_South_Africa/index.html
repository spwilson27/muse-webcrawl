Cabinet of South Africa

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Cabinet of South Africa is the executive branch of the government of South Africa. It consists of the President, the Deputy President, and the Ministers appointed by the President. Ministers head the national government departments and are accountable, collectively and individually, to Parliament.

Constitutional basis

Under Chapter 5 of the Constitution of South Africa, executive authority is vested in the President, who exercises it together with the Cabinet. The President appoints the Deputy President and Ministers, assigns their powers and functions, and may dismiss them. All but at most two Ministers must be selected from the members of the National Assembly. Deputy Ministers, who are not members of the Cabinet, assist the relevant Ministers.

Meetings and work

The Cabinet meets regularly, chaired by the President, and much of its preparatory work is done through cabinet committees and the cluster system, which groups departments into areas such as economic sectors, governance, and justice and security. Decisions are generally taken by consensus.

Recent cabinets

After the 2024 general election, in which the African National Congress lost its parliamentary majority for the first time since 1994, the Cabinet formed under President Cyril Ramaphosa became a multi-party Government of National Unity, including ministers from the ANC, the Democratic Alliance, the Inkatha Freedom Party, and several smaller parties.
