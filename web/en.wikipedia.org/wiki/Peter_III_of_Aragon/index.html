Peter III (c. 1240 – 1285), called the Great, was King of Aragon, Valencia, and Sicily and Count of Barcelona in the late thirteenth century. His conquest of Sicily after the Sicilian Vespers made Aragon a great Mediterranean power and embroiled him in war with France, the papacy, and the Angevins of Naples.

Reign

The son of James I the Conqueror, Peter succeeded in 1276 and subdued rebellious nobles and Muslim revolts in Valencia. Through his wife Constance, daughter of Manfred of Sicily, he claimed the Hohenstaufen inheritance in southern Italy. When the Sicilians rose against Charles of Anjou in the Vespers of Easter 1282, they offered Peter the crown; he landed at Trapani in August 1282 and was crowned at Palermo.

Aragonese Crusade

Pope Martin IV excommunicated Peter, declared his kingdoms forfeit, and proclaimed a crusade — the Aragonese Crusade — granting Aragon to Charles of Valois. A French army invaded Catalonia in 1285 but was destroyed by disease, supply failure, and the campaigns of Peter and his admiral Roger of Lauria, who repeatedly defeated French and Angevin fleets. Peter died in November 1285, shortly after the French retreat, and was succeeded in Aragon by Alfonso III and in Sicily by James II. Dante placed him among the rulers in the Valley of the Princes in Purgatorio.
