National statistics

National statistics (official statistics) are quantitative data produced by a country's government statistical system — typically a national statistical office such as the U.S. Census Bureau, the Office for National Statistics in the United Kingdom, or Statistics Canada — together with statistical units in ministries and central banks. They include censuses of population, national accounts (such as GDP), price indices, labour force surveys, and vital statistics.

Principles

The United Nations Fundamental Principles of Official Statistics (1994, endorsed by the UN General Assembly in 2014) require official statistics to be impartial, professionally produced, transparent about methods, and protected from political interference. Most systems also operate under statistical legislation guaranteeing the confidentiality of individual data.

Coordination

International comparability is promoted by bodies including the UN Statistics Division, Eurostat, the OECD, and the International Monetary Fund, which maintain standards such as the System of National Accounts and classification systems for industries, occupations, and diseases.
