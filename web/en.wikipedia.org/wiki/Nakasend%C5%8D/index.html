Nakasendō

Country: Japan
Length: About 534 km
Route: Edo (Tokyo) to Kyoto through the interior mountains

The Nakasendō (中山道, "central mountain route") was one of the five routes of the Edo period connecting Edo (modern Tokyo) with Kyoto. Unlike the coastal Tōkaidō, it ran inland through the mountains of central Honshu, passing through modern Saitama, Gunma, Nagano, Gifu, and Shiga prefectures.

Post towns

The route had 69 post stations (shukuba), the most famous being Narai-juku, Tsumago-juku, and Magome-juku, whose restored Edo-period streetscapes attract walkers today. The famous woodblock artist Hiroshige depicted the route in the series Sixty-nine Stations of the Kiso Kaidō.

Today

Sections of the old road, especially between Magome and Tsumago, survive as popular hiking trails through cedar forest and preserved villages.
