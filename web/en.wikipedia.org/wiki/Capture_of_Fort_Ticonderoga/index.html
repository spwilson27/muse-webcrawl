Capture of Fort Ticonderoga

Date10 May 1775
LocationFort Ticonderoga, New York
BelligerentsGreen Mountain Boys and militia vs. Great Britain
CommandersEthan Allen, Benedict Arnold; Capt. William Delaplace
ResultAmerican victory; fort and artillery captured

The capture of Fort Ticonderoga occurred on 10 May 1775, during the American Revolutionary War, when a small force of Green Mountain Boys and militia led by Ethan Allen and Benedict Arnold surprised and captured the fort's British garrison. It was the first offensive victory for American forces in the war. The fort's cannon and mortars were later hauled by Henry Knox to Boston, where their emplacement on Dorchester Heights forced the British evacuation of the city in March 1776.

Background

Fort Ticonderoga, on Lake Champlain, had been a strategic prize in the French and Indian War but by 1775 was dilapidated and held by only about 50 men of the 26th Regiment of Foot under Captain William Delaplace. Connecticut and Massachusetts patriots recognised the value of its artillery, and two rival expeditions — Allen's Green Mountain Boys and Arnold's Massachusetts commission — converged on the fort, with Arnold and Allen agreeing uneasily to joint command.

The capture

Before dawn on 10 May, about 83 men crossed the lake and entered the fort through the wicket gate while the rest followed. Allen confronted Delaplace at his quarters and demanded surrender — in Allen's later account, "in the name of the Great Jehovah and the Continental Congress", a quotation historians regard as embellished. The garrison surrendered without a shot, and no one was killed in the operation.

Aftermath

The Americans also seized Crown Point and, days later, raided Fort Saint-Jean. Control of the Lake Champlain corridor gave the colonies the northern invasion route into Canada used later in 1775. The fort changed hands again in 1777 during the Saratoga campaign.
