George Ogden Abell (1 March 1927 – 7 October 1983) was an American astronomer at the University of California, Los Angeles, known for his catalogue of galaxy clusters and his work in science education and scepticism.

Work

Abell earned his doctorate at Caltech and worked on the Palomar Observatory Sky Survey. From its plates he compiled the Abell catalogue (1958, expanded posthumously in 1989 with Harold Corwin and Ronald Olowin), listing more than 4,000 rich clusters of galaxies, which remains fundamental to studies of large-scale structure. He also catalogued 86 planetary nebulae that bear his name, and discovered the comet C/1953 T1 (Abell).

Education and scepticism

Abell taught at UCLA for over two decades and chaired its astronomy department. He was a prominent critic of pseudoscience, writing about astrology and claims of UFOs, and helped found the Southern California Skeptics. The asteroid 3449 Abell is named in his honour.
