Ferdinand Georg Frobenius

Ferdinand Georg Frobenius
Born: 26 October 1849, Charlottenburg, Berlin
Died: 3 August 1917, Berlin
Fields: Group theory, representation theory, number theory

Ferdinand Georg Frobenius (1849–1917) was a German mathematician best known for founding the representation theory of finite groups, and for contributions to differential equations, number theory and linear algebra.

Life

Frobenius studied at the University of Berlin under Weierstrass and Kronecker, taking his doctorate in 1870. After teaching at the Joachimsthalsches Gymnasium and the ETH in Zürich (from 1875), he returned to Berlin in 1892 to succeed Kronecker. He was a dominant, sometimes combative figure in Berlin mathematics and a member of the Prussian Academy of Sciences.

Work

Beginning in 1896, responding to correspondence with Richard Dedekind about factorising group determinants, Frobenius created the theory of group characters and representations of finite groups — work that became fundamental to mathematics and later to quantum mechanics. His name attaches to many concepts: the Frobenius endomorphism in characteristic-p algebra (and the Frobenius theorem on division algebras, proving the reals, complexes and quaternions are the only finite-dimensional associative division algebras over the reals), the Frobenius method for power-series solutions of differential equations, the Frobenius norm and Perron–Frobenius theorem on positive matrices, the Frobenius integrability theorem for differential systems, Frobenius groups and the Frobenius coin problem.

With Issai Schur and William Burnside he shaped early representation theory; his students included Edmund Landau and Schur.
