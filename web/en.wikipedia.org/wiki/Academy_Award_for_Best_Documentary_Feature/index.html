Academy Award for Best Documentary Feature

The Academy Award for Best Documentary Feature is an award presented annually by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences (AMPAS) to the best documentary film of the year. A documentary category was first presented at the 15th Academy Awards in 1943, initially covering both feature-length and short documentaries and honoring wartime films such as The Battle of Midway and Prelude to War. A separate feature category was soon established.

History and rules

Eligibility rules have changed repeatedly. Documentaries must generally receive a qualifying theatrical run, and since rule changes in the 2010s and early 2020s, members of the Academy's Documentary Branch vote on a shortlist (now 15 films) and the five nominees, with the final winner chosen by the wider membership. In 2022 the category was formally titled Best Documentary Feature Film to parallel the short documentary category.

For many years, Academy rules prevented notable works from competing or winning; Michael Moore's Bowling for Columbine (2002) and Fahrenheit 9/11 (2004) revived mainstream attention to the category. Winners are awarded to the film's credited directors and, in recent practice, producers.

Selected winners

  • 1942: The Battle of Midway, Prelude to War and others (shared war-documentary honors)
  • 1961: Sky Above and Mud Beneath
  • 1974: Hearts and Minds
  • 1985: Broken Rainbow
  • 1999: One Day in September
  • 2002: Bowling for Columbine
  • 2006: An Inconvenient Truth
  • 2009: The Cove
  • 2012: Searching for Sugar Man
  • 2015: Amy
  • 2018: Free Solo
  • 2020: My Octopus Teacher
  • 2021: Summer of Soul
  • 2022: Navalny
  • 2023: 20 Days in Mariupol
