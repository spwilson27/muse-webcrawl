Papists Act 1778

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Papists Act 1778 (18 Geo. 3. c. 60), also known as the First Catholic Relief Act, was an Act of the Parliament of Great Britain that began the process of Catholic emancipation by repealing some of the penal laws against Roman Catholics in England and Wales. It was the first major legislative relief granted to Catholics since the Reformation-era penal code.

Provisions

The Act repealed the statute 11 & 12 William 3. c. 4 (the Popery Act 1698), under which Catholic priests could be imprisoned for life for celebrating Mass, Catholic schoolmasters were liable to imprisonment, and Catholics were barred from inheriting or purchasing land. Under the 1778 Act, Catholics who took a prescribed oath of allegiance renouncing the temporal jurisdiction of the Pope and the Jacobite claim were permitted to inherit and purchase land, and the penalties for priests and schoolmasters were lifted.

Background

The measure, promoted by Sir George Savile with government support, was intended in part to encourage Catholic recruitment into the armed forces during the American War of Independence. A parallel and more extensive act was passed for Scotland, though it was abandoned after rioting in Edinburgh and Glasgow.

Gordon Riots

The Act provoked a fierce Protestant backlash. The Protestant Association, led by Lord George Gordon, campaigned for its repeal, and in June 1780 a mass petition and march on Parliament escalated into the Gordon Riots, a week of anti-Catholic violence in London in which chapels, houses, prisons, and the Bank of England were attacked and several hundred people were killed. The riots were suppressed by the army. The Act itself was not repealed.

Subsequent relief

Further relief followed with the Roman Catholic Relief Act 1791, which legalized Catholic worship and schools under conditions, the Roman Catholic Relief Act 1829, which admitted Catholics to Parliament and most public offices, and the Roman Catholic Relief Act 1926, which removed most remaining restrictions.
