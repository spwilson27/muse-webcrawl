Nereids

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In Greek mythology, the Nereids (Greek: Νηρηΐδες, Nērēḯdes; singular Νηρηΐς, Nērēḯs) are sea nymphs (female spirits of sea waters), the 50 daughters of the "Old Man of the Sea" Nereus and the Oceanid Doris. They often accompany Poseidon, the god of the sea, and can be friendly toward sailors whom they aid in storms, notably the Argonauts in their search for the Golden Fleece.

The Nereids symbolised everything that is beautiful and kind about the sea. Their melodious voices sang as they danced around their father. They are represented as very beautiful girls, crowned with branches of red coral and dressed in white silk robes trimmed with gold, but who went barefoot. They were associated with the Aegean Sea, where they lived with their father in the depths within a silvery cave. The most notable of them are Thetis, wife of Peleus and mother of Achilles; Amphitrite, wife of Poseidon and queen of the sea; Galatea, beloved of the Cyclops Polyphemus; and Psamathe.

Names

This list is correlated from four sources: Hesiod's Theogony, Homer's Iliad, and the Bibliotheca of Apollodorus and the Fabulae of Hyginus. Because of this, the total number of names listed exceeds fifty, though the tradition consistently speaks of fifty Nereids. Hesiod names, among others, Ploto, Eucrante, Sao, Amphitrite, Eudora, Thetis, Galene, Glauce, Cymothoe, Speio, Thoe, Halie, Pasithea, Erato, Eunice, Melite, Eulimene, Agaue, Doto, Proto, Pherusa, Dynamene, Nesaea, Actaea, Protomedeia, Doris, Panope, Galatea, Hippothoe, Hipponoe, Cymodoce, Cymatolege, Amphitrite, Cymo, Eione, Alimede, Glauconome, Pontoporeia, Leucothoe, Euagore, Laomedeia, Polynoe, Autonoe, Lysianassa, Euarne, Psamathe, Menippe, Neso, Eupompe, Themisto, Pronoe, and Nemertes.

Mythology

In Homer's Iliad XVIII, when Thetis cries out in sympathy for the grief of Achilles over Patroclus, her thirty-three sisters appear to share her lament. In the Odyssey and in Apollonius's Argonautica, the Nereids guide the Argo safely between the Wandering Rocks and past Scylla and Charybdis. When Cassiopeia, queen of Aethiopia, boasted that she (or her daughter Andromeda) surpassed the Nereids in beauty, the nymphs appealed to Poseidon, who sent a flood and the sea monster Cetus against the land.

Worship and art

In ancient art the Nereids are depicted riding dolphins, hippocamps and other sea creatures; they escort Thetis bringing the arms of Achilles, and adorn the famous 4th-century BC Nereid Monument from Xanthos in Lycia, now in the British Museum. Along the coasts of the Greek world they received cult as protectors of sailors and fishermen.
