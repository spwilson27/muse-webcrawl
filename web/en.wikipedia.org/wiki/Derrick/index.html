Derrick

A derrick is a lifting device composed of at least one guyed mast, as in a gin pole, which can be articulated over a load by adjusting its guys. The name derives from Thomas Derrick, an English executioner of the late 16th and early 17th centuries, whose name was applied first to a type of gallows and then to cranes resembling them.

Types

Derricks take several forms. The guy derrick uses a mast held by guy ropes with a boom whose angle is controlled by tackle; the stiffleg derrick replaces the guys with rigid struts (stifflegs); the A-frame derrick uses two legs; and the gin pole is a simple single guyed mast used for small lifts. Derricks are used aboard ships for handling cargo, in construction, and in forestry and quarrying.

Oil drilling derricks

The term is most widely recognised from the oil industry, where a derrick is the tall tower-like structure of a drilling rig that supports the drill string and houses the crown block and travelling equipment used to raise and lower pipe into the borehole. Classic wooden and steel lattice derricks were built as permanent structures over wells; modern rigs typically use a mast that can be raised and lowered as a unit and transported between sites. The worker who attends the top of the derrick during tripping operations is known as the derrickhand.
