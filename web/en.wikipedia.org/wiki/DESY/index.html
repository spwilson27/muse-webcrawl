DESY (German: Deutsches Elektronen-Synchrotron, "German Electron Synchrotron") is a national research centre in Germany that operates particle accelerators used to investigate the structure of matter. Founded in 1959 in Hamburg, with a second site at Zeuthen near Berlin, DESY is a member of the Helmholtz Association and is one of the world's leading accelerator laboratories.

History

DESY's first electron synchrotron began operation in 1964. Its succession of colliders — DORIS, PETRA and HERA — made major contributions to particle physics: PETRA provided the first direct evidence of the gluon in 1979, and HERA (1992–2007), a 6.3 km ring beneath Hamburg, was the world's only electron–proton collider, probing the internal structure of the proton at the experiments H1 and ZEUS.

Photon science

After HERA's shutdown, DESY reinvented itself as a leading centre for photon science. Its facilities include PETRA III, one of the brightest synchrotron X-ray sources in the world; the FLASH free-electron laser, the first to operate in the soft X-ray range; and the European XFEL, a 3.4 km X-ray free-electron laser extending from the DESY campus to Schenefeld, which began operation in 2017 and is run as an international facility. These light sources serve thousands of users in physics, chemistry, biology, materials and medical research.

Other activities

DESY works in astroparticle physics (including the IceCube neutrino observatory at the South Pole, coordinated partly from Zeuthen), in accelerator research, and in theory. The centre employs around 2,500 staff and hosts several thousand guest scientists annually.
