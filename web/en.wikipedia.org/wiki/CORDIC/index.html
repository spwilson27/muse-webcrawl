CORDIC

CORDIC (coordinate rotation digital computer), also known as Volder's algorithm, is a simple and efficient algorithm to calculate trigonometric functions, hyperbolic functions, multiplication, division, square roots, and conversion between Cartesian and polar coordinates. It requires only addition, subtraction, bitshift, and table lookup operations, which makes it attractive for hardware implementation where multipliers are expensive.

History

CORDIC was developed by Jack Volder at Convair in 1956–1959 while designing a digital replacement for the analog resolver in the B-58 Hustler bomber navigation computer. John Meggitt at IBM proposed a similar method (pseudo-multiplication/pseudo-division) in 1961. Hewlett-Packard adopted CORDIC in its HP-35 (1972), the first handheld scientific calculator, because it implemented the transcendental functions with a small amount of logic; Intel's x87 floating-point coprocessors (8087 onward) also used CORDIC-style methods for transcendental instructions.

The algorithm

CORDIC works by rotating a vector through a sequence of progressively smaller, predetermined angles whose tangents are powers of two (arctan 2−i), so that each microrotation can be performed with shifts and adds rather than multiplications. In rotation mode, the angle accumulator is driven to zero, computing sine and cosine of the input angle; in vectoring mode, the y coordinate is driven to zero, yielding the magnitude and angle (arctangent) of the input vector. Each run introduces a known gain factor (the product of cosines, approaching about 0.6073 in circular mode), which is corrected by a constant multiplication or by pre-scaling.

By choosing different microrotation angle sets, the same iterative core computes hyperbolic functions (hyperbolic mode), and in linear mode it reduces to multiplication and division.

Applications

CORDIC remains widely used in FPGAs, DSPs, calculators, robotics, communications (digital down-converters, FFT butterflies, NCOs), and embedded systems where area and power are limited.
