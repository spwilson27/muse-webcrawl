Single-camera setup

FieldFilm and television production
ContrastMultiple-camera setup

The single-camera setup, or single-camera mode of production, is a method of filmmaking and television production in which scenes are shot with one camera at a time, with each shot and angle recorded separately and assembled in editing. It contrasts with the multiple-camera setup common in sitcoms and live programming.

Television

Single-camera production is typical of drama series and many modern comedies, which are shot on location or sound stages without a studio audience. Because lighting and camera positions are optimised for each shot, the style tends to look more cinematic than multi-camera work.

Filmmaking

Nearly all feature films use the single-camera approach, repeating performances for close-ups, wide shots and reaction shots. Action sequences and stunts are exceptions, often using several cameras simultaneously to capture unrepeatable events.

Advantages and costs

The method offers greater creative control over composition and lighting but is slower and usually more expensive per finished minute than multi-camera production.
