Bone disease

A bone disease is any disorder of bone — its formation, growth, mineralisation, strength or internal structure. Bone is living tissue, continuously remodelled by osteoblasts that build it and osteoclasts that resorb it, so diseases may arise from imbalance in remodelling, nutritional deficiency, genetic defects, infection, tumours or hormonal disturbance. Bone diseases can cause pain, deformity and fractures, and some are systemic with effects far beyond the skeleton.

Metabolic bone diseases

  • Osteoporosis — loss of bone mass and microarchitectural deterioration, greatly increasing fracture risk, especially in postmenopausal women and older men.
  • Rickets and osteomalacia — defective mineralisation from vitamin D, calcium or phosphate deficiency; rickets affects growing children, causing bowed legs and deformity.
  • Paget's disease of bone — focal disorder of chaotic remodelling producing enlarged, weak bones.
  • Renal osteodystrophy — bone changes arising in chronic kidney disease, including secondary hyperparathyroidism.

Genetic and developmental disorders

Osteogenesis imperfecta ("brittle bone disease") results from defective type I collagen and causes recurrent fractures from minor trauma. Others include achondroplasia, the commonest form of dwarfism, fibrous dysplasia, and the skeletal dysplasias as a class. Osteopetrosis produces abnormally dense but fragile bones due to failed osteoclast function.

Infection and tumours

Osteomyelitis is infection of bone, usually bacterial, which may follow trauma, surgery or bloodstream spread. Primary bone cancers include osteosarcoma, chondrosarcoma and Ewing sarcoma; far more common in adults is metastatic spread to bone from breast, prostate, lung and other cancers, as well as multiple myeloma, a cancer of plasma cells in the marrow.

Diagnosis and treatment

Assessment uses imaging (X-ray, CT, MRI), bone densitometry (DXA) for osteoporosis, and laboratory tests of calcium, phosphate, alkaline phosphatase and vitamin D. Treatment ranges from calcium and vitamin D supplementation and antiresorptive or anabolic drugs, to surgery for deformity, fracture and tumours.
