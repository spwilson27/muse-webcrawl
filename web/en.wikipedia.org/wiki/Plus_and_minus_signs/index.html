Plus and minus signs

The + and − signs

The plus sign (+) and the minus sign (−) are mathematical symbols used to represent the notions of positive and negative, respectively, and the operations of addition and subtraction. Their use has been extended to many other meanings, more or less analogous.

History

The signs appear in print at the end of the 15th century: Johannes Widmann used + and − in his arithmetic of 1489, initially to denote surplus and deficit in warehousing rather than operations. The symbols probably derive from abbreviations – + from a ligature of Latin et ("and"), − from a shortened, barred form of m for minus. They displaced earlier notations (such as p̄ and m̄) during the 16th century, appearing in the works of Robert Recorde and others.

Uses

Besides addition and subtraction, + and − denote the sign of numbers, positive and negative charges and poles in physics and chemistry, degrees of comparison in some notations, blood types (Rh factor), and grades. The minus sign (U+2212) is typographically distinct from the hyphen (U+002D) and en dash, being longer and aligned to the height of the plus sign. In computing, the ASCII hyphen-minus serves both purposes.

Related symbols

The combined sign ± (plus–minus) indicates a value that may be either, as in measurement uncertainty or the two solutions of a quadratic equation; ∓ (minus-or-plus) is its counterpart. In Jewish tradition, a "plus" for addition is sometimes written ﬩ to avoid the resemblance of + to a cross.
