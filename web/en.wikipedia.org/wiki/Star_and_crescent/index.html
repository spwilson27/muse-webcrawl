Star and crescent

The star and crescent is an iconographic symbol used in various historical contexts. It is most widely associated today with the Ottoman Empire and with Islam, appearing on the national flags of several countries including Turkey, Algeria, Azerbaijan, Malaysia, Pakistan, and Tunisia.

History

The symbol predates Islam. Crescent and star motifs appeared in the ancient Near East, in Hellenistic coinage, and in the city of Byzantium, where the crescent was associated with the goddess Artemis. Under the Ottomans the crescent and star became state emblems, and after the empire's adoption of a standardized flag in 1793 and 1844 the pairing spread across the Muslim-majority world. The five-pointed star is often interpreted as representing the five pillars of Islam, though this reading is retrospective.

Use

Beyond national flags, the star and crescent appears on military insignia, coinage, and as a general symbol of Islam, sometimes coloured green. Its meaning varies by context and is not universally accepted as a religious emblem by all Muslims.
