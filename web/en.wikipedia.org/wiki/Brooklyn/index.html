Brooklyn

The Brooklyn Bridge connects Brooklyn to Manhattan across the East River.

Brooklyn is the most populous of the five boroughs of New York City, coextensive with Kings County in the U.S. state of New York. Located at the western end of Long Island, it had a population of about 2.6 million in the 2020 census, making it the most populous county in New York State and the second-most densely populated county in the United States after Manhattan (New York County).

History

The area was inhabited by the Lenape people before Dutch colonists founded the town of Breuckelen in the 17th century, named after the town of Breukelen in the Netherlands. Brooklyn grew through the 19th century as a manufacturing and port city, absorbing surrounding towns until it became a single city in 1896. In 1898 it was consolidated into the City of New York together with Manhattan, the Bronx, Queens and Staten Island.

The Brooklyn Bridge, completed in 1883, was the first fixed crossing of the East River and a landmark of 19th-century engineering. Brooklyn's waterfront, navy yard and industries drew generations of immigrants, shaping the borough's famously diverse neighbourhoods.

Geography and neighbourhoods

Brooklyn occupies roughly 71 square miles of land. Well-known neighbourhoods include Williamsburg, Brooklyn Heights, Park Slope, Bedford-Stuyvesant, Flatbush, Bay Ridge, Bushwick, Greenpoint and Coney Island, the last famous for its boardwalk, amusement parks and the annual Nathan's hot dog eating contest.

Culture

The borough is home to institutions such as the Brooklyn Museum, the Brooklyn Academy of Music and Prospect Park, designed by Frederick Law Olmsted and Calvert Vaux. The Brooklyn Dodgers baseball team played at Ebbets Field until moving to Los Angeles in 1957; professional sports returned in 2012 with the Brooklyn Nets at the Barclays Center. Brooklyn has also been a centre of literature, hip hop and independent music.
