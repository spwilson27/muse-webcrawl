Settlement of the Americas

The settlement of the Americas refers to the migration of anatomically modern humans from Asia into North and South America during the last Ice Age, and their subsequent dispersal across both continents. Genetic and archaeological evidence indicates the ancestors of Indigenous peoples of the Americas diverged from Siberian populations and crossed Beringia.

Models

The long-dominant "Clovis first" model placed entry around 13,000 years ago through an ice-free corridor. Sites such as Monte Verde in Chile (c. 14,500 years ago) and evidence for a Pacific coastal route have pushed accepted dates earlier; contested claims, including footprints at White Sands (c. 21,000–23,000 years ago), suggest still earlier presence.

Evidence

Genetics links Indigenous Americans to ancient Siberians and East Asians, with a period of isolation in Beringia. Later migrations brought Na-Dene speakers and Inuit ancestors.
