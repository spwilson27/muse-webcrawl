Omikuji

Japaneseおみくじ / 御神籤
Meaning"Sacred lot"
Found atShinto shrines and Buddhist temples in Japan

Omikuji (おみくじ, 御神籤・御御籤・お神籤) are random fortunes written on strips of paper at Shinto shrines and Buddhist temples in Japan. Literally meaning "sacred lot", these fortunes are usually received by making a small offering (generally a five-yen coin, as it is considered lucky) and randomly choosing one from a box, hoping for the resulting fortune to be good.

Fortune grades

The omikuji predicts the person's chances of their hopes coming true, of finding a good match, and generally matters of health, fortune, life and love, graded on a scale from great blessing (dai-kichi, 大吉) through middle blessing, small blessing and blessing, down to curse (kyō, 凶) and great curse (dai-kyō, 大凶). The slip also contains verses and specific predictions for categories such as travel, business, study, marriage and lost things.

Custom

When the prediction is bad, it is customary not to keep the slip; instead the supplicant ties it to a pine tree or a wall of metal wires alongside other bad fortunes, so that the bad luck can wait by the tree rather than follow the bearer. A pun reinforces the practice: the word for pine, matsu, also means "to wait". If the fortune is good, the bearer may also tie it up in hope the fortune will have greater effect, or keep it in a wallet.
