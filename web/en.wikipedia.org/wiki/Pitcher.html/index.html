Pitcher


A pitcher delivering a pitch

In baseball, the pitcher is the player who throws ("pitches") the baseball from the pitcher's mound toward the catcher to begin each play, with the goal of retiring a batter, who attempts to either make contact with the pitched ball or draw a walk. In the numbering system used to record defensive plays, the pitcher is assigned the number 1. The pitcher is often considered the most important player on the defensive side of the game, and as such is situated at the right end of the defensive spectrum.

Overview

A starting pitcher begins the game, while relief pitchers enter later; specialized relief roles include the closer, who finishes close games, and the setup man. Pitchers throw a variety of pitches—fastballs, curveballs, sliders, changeups, and others—with different speeds, movements, and release points designed to deceive the batter.

In most leagues, including the American League and (since 2022) the National League of Major League Baseball, the pitcher does not bat; a designated hitter bats in his place. Pitchers are typically rested on a rotation of four or five starters, and their workloads are closely monitored because of the injury risk to the throwing arm, including ulnar collateral ligament damage requiring "Tommy John surgery".

Pitching mechanics and strategy

The pitcher delivers the ball from a raised mound 60 feet 6 inches from home plate, using a windup or the stretch position. Strategy involves varying pitch type, speed, and location, and holding runners on base to prevent stolen bases. Left-handed pitchers are especially valued against left-handed batters and for their pickoff advantage with runners on first base.
