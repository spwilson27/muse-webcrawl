Armenian cuisine

Armenian cuisine reflects the history and geography of the Armenian Highlands, drawing on grains, legumes, dairy products, herbs and grilled meats. Its techniques and dishes spread widely through the Caucasus, the Levant and the Armenian diaspora.

Staples

Lavash, a thin flatbread baked in a clay tonir oven, is central to the cuisine; UNESCO recognised lavash-making as Armenian Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2014. Other staples include bulgur, rice pilafs, yogurt (matsun) and cheeses such as lori and chanakh.

Notable dishes

Signature dishes include khorovats (barbecued meat), dolma (stuffed vegetables and vine leaves), harisa (wheat and meat porridge), khash (boiled cow's feet, eaten communally in winter), and lahmajun. Desserts include gata, a sweet pastry, and paklava.
