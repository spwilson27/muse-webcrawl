Mahadji Scindia

Mahadji Scindia (3 December 1730 – 12 February 1794), also spelled Mahadji Shinde, was a Maratha statesman and ruler of Gwalior in central India. He was the youngest son of Ranoji Rao Scindia, founder of the Scindia dynasty, and became the most powerful ruler in northern India in the last quarter of the 18th century.

Early life and survival of Panipat

Mahadji fought at the Third Battle of Panipat (1761), where he was wounded in the leg and left lame for life; according to tradition a water-carrier rescued him from the field. He inherited the family territories after the deaths of his elder brothers and spent the following decade rebuilding Scindia power in Malwa.

Restorer of Maratha power in the north

In 1771 Mahadji escorted the Mughal emperor Shah Alam II back to Delhi, restoring Maratha influence over the imperial capital. He modernized his army with European-style infantry and artillery under the Savoyard officer Benoît de Boigne, whose disciplined brigades made Scindia's forces the most formidable in India. He defeated Rajput states and rival Maratha houses, imposed settlements at the Treaty of Salbai (1782) with the British East India Company, and after victory at the Battle of Patan (1790) stood as the de facto arbiter of Hindustan, receiving the title Vakil-ul-Mutlaq (regent plenipotentiary) from the emperor.

Death and succession

Mahadji died at Wanawadi near Pune in 1794, at the height of his power. Having no son, he was succeeded by his grand-nephew Daulat Rao Scindia. His military reforms and the state he built made Gwalior a central actor in Indian politics into the next century.
