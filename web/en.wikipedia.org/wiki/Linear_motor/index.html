Linear motor

A linear motor is an electric motor that produces motion in a straight line rather than rotational motion. It can be imagined as a conventional rotary motor that has been cut open and unrolled flat: instead of a rotating shaft, it produces a linear force along its length.

Types

Linear motors are divided into categories such as linear induction motors, linear synchronous motors, linear stepper motors, and piezoelectric or voice-coil actuators. In a linear induction motor, a travelling magnetic field in the primary induces currents in a conductive secondary (often a simple aluminium plate), generating thrust.

Applications

Linear motors drive maglev trains such as the Shanghai Transrapid and airport people movers, and are used in precision machine tools, semiconductor manufacturing stages, sliding doors, roller-coaster launches, and coilgun and railgun research. Their advantages include direct drive without mechanical transmission, high acceleration, and low maintenance; disadvantages include lower efficiency over long strokes and the cost of magnet track.
