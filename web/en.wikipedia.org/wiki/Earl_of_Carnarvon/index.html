Earl of Carnarvon

Earl of Carnarvon is a title that has been created three times in British history. The current holder is George Herbert, 8th Earl of Carnarvon, who succeeded in 2001. The family seat is Highclere Castle in Hampshire, famous worldwide as the filming location for the television series Downton Abbey.

History

The first creation came in the Peerage of England in 1628 for Robert Dormer, 2nd Baron Dormer. The title became extinct on the death of the fourth Earl in 1709. The second creation came in the Peerage of Great Britain in 1761 for James Brydges, 1st Duke of Chandos; it became extinct on the death of the third Duke in 1789.

The third creation came in the Peerage of Great Britain in 1793 for Henry Herbert, 1st Baron Porchester. The Herbert family descends from Henry Herbert of Highclere. The most famous holder was George Herbert, 5th Earl of Carnarvon (1866–1923), the aristocrat who financed the excavation of the tomb of Tutankhamun in Egypt's Valley of the Kings, carried out by Howard Carter. The 5th Earl died in Cairo in 1923, shortly after the tomb's discovery, fuelling legends of a "pharaoh's curse".

Subsidiary titles

The Earl holds the subsidiary title Baron Porchester, of Highclere in the County of Southampton, created in 1780. The heir apparent to the earldom is usually styled Lord Porchester.
