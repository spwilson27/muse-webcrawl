Battle of Sobraon

The Battle of Sobraon was fought on 10 February 1846 between the British East India Company and the Sikh Khalsa Army of the Sikh Empire. It was the last and decisive battle of the First Anglo-Sikh War, ending in a crushing British victory that broke Sikh military power.

Background

The Sikh army had crossed the Sutlej and entrenched a bridgehead on the British side of the river around Sobraon, connected to the far bank by a bridge of boats. Governor-General Sir Henry Hardinge and commander-in-chief Sir Hugh Gough waited for heavy guns and reinforcements before attacking the position in February 1846.

The battle

After a heavy artillery bombardment — hampered by British ammunition shortages — Gough launched infantry assaults against the Sikh works. The right of the Sikh line, held by troops under Sham Singh Attariwala, resisted fiercely; Sham Singh, who had sworn to die in the battle, was killed fighting. On the Sikh left, the commander Tej Singh fled across the bridge of boats early in the action, and the bridge was broken — whether by damage or design — trapping the Khalsa army against the river. Thousands of Sikh soldiers were killed or drowned in the Sutlej.

Aftermath

Sikh losses were estimated at 8,000–10,000 men and 67 guns; British casualties were over 2,300, among the heaviest of the war. With its army destroyed, the Sikh Empire sued for peace. The Treaty of Lahore (March 1846) ceded the Jullundur Doab, reduced the Khalsa army, imposed a large indemnity, and placed a British resident at Lahore — steps that led within three years to the annexation of the Punjab after the Second Anglo-Sikh War.
