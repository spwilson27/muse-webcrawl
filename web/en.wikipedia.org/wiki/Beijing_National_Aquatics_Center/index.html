Beijing National Aquatics Center

The Beijing National Aquatics Center, officially the National Aquatics Centre and commonly known as the Water Cube, is an aquatics venue in the Olympic Green in Beijing, China. Built for the swimming, diving and synchronised swimming competitions of the 2008 Summer Olympics, it became one of the Games' most recognisable buildings because of its bubble-like facade of blue ETFE panels. For the 2022 Winter Olympics it was converted to host curling, earning a second nickname, the "Ice Cube."

Design and construction

The centre was designed by a consortium including the Australian firm PTW Architects, the China State Construction Engineering Corporation, and Arup. Its structure is based on the Weaire–Phelan structure, a geometry of foam bubbles, rendered as a steel space frame clad in translucent ETFE cushions that glow blue at night and admit daylight while reducing solar heat gain. Ground was broken in December 2003 and the building was completed in 2007.

The 2008 Olympics

During the 2008 Games the venue seated about 17,000 spectators and saw an exceptional run of records: 25 world records were broken in its pool, and American swimmer Michael Phelps won eight gold medals there, the most by any athlete at a single Olympics. Swimmers praised the pool's depth and design as contributing to fast times.

Later use

After 2008 the centre was remodelled, with part of its interior converted into a public water park that opened in 2010; permanent seating was reduced to about 6,000. For the 2022 Winter Olympics, the competition hall was fitted with a convertible ice rink over the pool, making it the first Olympic venue repurposed from swimming to curling.
