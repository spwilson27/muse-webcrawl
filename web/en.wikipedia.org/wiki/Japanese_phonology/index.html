Japanese phonology

Image placeholder: Japanese phonology

This article deals with the phonology of the Japanese language. The standard dialect is the one spoken and taught in Japan, generally based on the pronunciation of educated speakers of the Tokyo region (Yamanote dialect).

Phonemes

Japanese has five vowel phonemes: /a/, /i/, /u/, /e/, and /o/. Vowels can be short or long; long vowels are often analyzed as sequences of two identical short vowels occupying two morae. The consonant inventory includes voiceless and voiced stops /p b t d k ɡ/, fricatives /s z h/, affricates /ts tɕ dʑ/, nasals /m n/, the flap /ɾ/, and approximants /j w/.

The mora

Japanese is a mora-timed language. The mora is a unit of phonological weight: a syllable may contain one or two morae, but not more. Special morae include the moraic nasal /N/, the first half of a geminate consonant (the sokuon), and the second half of a long vowel.

Pitch accent

Standard Japanese employs a lexical pitch accent system: words are distinguished by the position at which the pitch drops from high to low (the accent nucleus), or by the absence of a drop. Dialects differ widely in their accent systems; some, such as those of Kansai, use a tonal register distinction, while others lack pitch accent altogether.

Phonological processes

Notable processes include devoicing of high vowels /i/ and /u/ between voiceless consonants, sequential voicing (rendaku) in compounds, and palatalization of coronals before /i/ and /j/.
