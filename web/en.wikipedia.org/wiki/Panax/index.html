Panax

FamilyAraliaceae
GenusPanax L.
Type speciesPanax ginseng
Common nameGinseng

The genus Panax, commonly known as ginseng, is a group of flowering plants in the ivy family, Araliaceae. It comprises roughly a dozen species of slow-growing perennial plants with fleshy roots, native to cool regions of the Northern Hemisphere — eastern Asia, especially Korea, northeastern China and far eastern Siberia, and eastern North America. Ginseng is characterised by the presence of ginsenosides and gintonin, and its roots have been used in traditional medicine for millennia.

Etymology

The name Panax derives from the Greek pan ("all") and akos ("cure"), a reference to its traditional reputation as a cure-all; Linnaeus, who named the genus, was aware of its wide use in Chinese medicine. The word "ginseng" comes from the Chinese rénshēn (人蔘), "man-root", after the root's sometimes human-like forked shape.

Species

Notable species include:

  • Panax ginseng — Asian (Korean/Chinese) ginseng
  • Panax quinquefolius — American ginseng
  • Panax notoginseng — sanqi, used in Chinese medicine
  • Panax japonicus — Japanese ginseng
  • Panax vietnamensis — Vietnamese ginseng
  • Panax trifolius — dwarf ginseng
  • Panax pseudoginseng — Himalayan ginseng

Cultivation and use

Wild ginseng has been heavily over-harvested, and most commercial root comes from cultivated plants grown under shade for four to six years, chiefly in South Korea, China, Canada and the United States. Roots are sold fresh, dried ("white ginseng"), or steamed and dried ("red ginseng"), and are consumed as teas, extracts and supplements. Ginseng species are listed in CITES Appendix II, and international trade in wild roots is regulated. Siberian ginseng (Eleutherococcus senticosus) is a different plant and not a true Panax.
