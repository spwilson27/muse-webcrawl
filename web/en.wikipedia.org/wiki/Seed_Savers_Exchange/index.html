Seed Savers Exchange (SSE) is a non-profit organisation based near Decorah, Iowa, United States, dedicated to preserving the genetic diversity of heirloom and open-pollinated plants. Founded in 1975, it is one of the largest non-governmental seed banks in North America and operates one of the largest member seed exchanges in the world, through which gardeners share seeds of thousands of vegetable, fruit, herb, flower and grain varieties that are not available commercially.

History

Seed Savers Exchange began when Diane Ott Whealy inherited seeds of two plants brought from Bavaria by her grandfather — a tomato ('German Pink') and a morning glory — and she and her husband Kent Whealy recognised that countless family heirloom varieties were being lost as their elderly custodians died. In 1975 they organised a small exchange among gardeners who had saved such seeds; the network grew into an annual yearbook listing thousands of varieties offered by member seed savers. In 1986 the organisation acquired Heritage Farm, an 890-acre property near Decorah, which became its headquarters, with display gardens, preservation collections, education programmes and seed storage facilities.

Work

SSE maintains a seed bank of more than 20,000 varieties at Heritage Farm, growing out accessions on rotation to regenerate fresh seed, and it sells a selection of heirloom varieties through its catalogue and website to support its preservation work. It also keeps heritage livestock breeds and an orchard of historic apple varieties. Backup samples of many accessions are deposited in long-term storage, including the Svalbard Global Seed Vault in Norway. The organisation's annual conference and campout, seed-saving education, and publications have made it a central institution of the heirloom-seed and seed-sovereignty movements.
