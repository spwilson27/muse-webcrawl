The U.S. Green Building Council (USGBC) is an American nonprofit organization that promotes sustainability in building design, construction, and operation. USGBC is best known for its development of the Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design (LEED) green building rating systems and its annual Greenbuild International Conference and Expo, the world's largest conference dedicated to green building.

History

USGBC was founded in 1993 by Rick Fedrizzi, David Gottfried, and Mike Italiano. The council's first meeting was held in April 1993, bringing together representatives from firms across the building industry. The founding firms included companies from architecture, engineering, construction, and product manufacturing.

LEED

The LEED (Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design) rating system, developed by USGBC beginning in 1994, is the most widely used green building rating system in the world. Buildings are certified at four levels—Certified, Silver, Gold, and Platinum—based on points earned across categories including sustainable sites, water efficiency, energy and atmosphere, materials and resources, and indoor environmental quality. Certification is administered by the Green Business Certification Inc. (GBCI).

Activities

Beyond LEED, USGBC supports education for building professionals, credentials such as the LEED Green Associate and LEED AP, advocacy for green building policy, and research through programs addressing healthier buildings, resilient design, and net-zero carbon and energy goals.
