Elliptic filter

An elliptic filter (also known as a Cauer filter, named for Wilhelm Cauer, or as a Zolotarev filter, after Yegor Zolotarev) is a signal processing filter with equalized ripple (equiripple) behavior in both the passband and the stopband. The amount of ripple in each band is independently adjustable, and no other filter of equal order can have a faster transition in gain between the passband and the stopband, for the given values of ripple (whether the ripple is equalized or not).

Properties

The magnitude response of an elliptic filter exhibits ripples of equal amplitude in the passband and stopband. The roll-off is the steepest possible for a given filter order, but this comes at the cost of ripple in both bands and nonlinear phase response.

Comparison with other filters

Unlike the Butterworth filter, which is maximally flat, and the Chebyshev filter, which ripples in only one band, the elliptic filter allows ripple in both bands in exchange for the sharpest cutoff transition. Elliptic filters are used where very sharp selectivity is needed, such as in telecommunications channel filtering.

Mathematics

The magnitude-squared response involves a rational elliptic function, which is where the filter gets its name. The design uses elliptic integrals and Jacobian elliptic functions to compute the transfer function.
