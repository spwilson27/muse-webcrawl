Eighteenth Dynasty of Egypt

Eighteenth Dynasty

Period: c. 1550 – c. 1292 BC

Era: New Kingdom

Capital: Thebes; Amarna (under Akhenaten)

Notable rulers: Ahmose I, Hatshepsut, Thutmose III, Akhenaten, Tutankhamun, Horemheb

The Eighteenth Dynasty of ancient Egypt (notated Dynasty XVIII) is the first dynasty of the New Kingdom, lasting from about 1550 to 1292 BC. It is the best known of the ancient Egyptian dynasties, including many of Egypt's most famous pharaohs, and saw Egypt become an empire dominating the Near East from Nubia to the Euphrates.

Founding and expansion

The dynasty was founded by Ahmose I, who completed the expulsion of the Hyksos from the Nile Delta begun by Seqenenre Tao and Kamose, reunifying Egypt. His successors campaigned deep into Nubia and the Levant. Queen Hatshepsut ruled as pharaoh for over two decades, promoting trade — including the famous expedition to Punt — and monumental building. Her successor Thutmose III, sometimes called the "Napoleon of Egypt", created the largest empire Egypt had ever controlled, campaigning as far as the Euphrates and winning the Battle of Megiddo.

Amarna period

Under Amenhotep IV, who renamed himself Akhenaten, Egypt underwent a religious revolution centred on the sun-disc Aten, with a new capital at Amarna. After his death the traditional cults were restored under Tutankhamun, whose intact tomb, discovered in 1922, made him the dynasty's most famous king to modern audiences. The dynasty closed with the reigns of Ay and the general Horemheb, who had no heir and passed the throne to his vizier, founding the Nineteenth Dynasty.

List of pharaohs
