In mathematics, an affine Lie algebra is an infinite-dimensional Lie algebra constructed from a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra in a canonical way. It is the simplest class of Kac–Moody algebra beyond the finite-dimensional ones, and its representation theory underlies much of two-dimensional conformal field theory and string theory.

Construction

Starting from a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra g, one first forms the loop algebra of polynomial maps from a circle to g — equivalently, the tensor product of g with the ring of Laurent polynomials in a variable t. The affine Lie algebra is (up to isomorphism) the unique one-dimensional central extension of this loop algebra: it adds a central element c whose bracket terms are given by a residue formula involving the invariant bilinear form on g. Adjoining a derivation d that counts the power of t gives the extended algebra used in practice.

Structure

Affine Lie algebras are classified by affine Dynkin diagrams — the ordinary Dynkin diagram of g with one extra node — and are described by generalised Cartan matrices that are positive semidefinite with a one-dimensional kernel. Their root systems consist of the finite roots shifted by integer multiples of the imaginary root δ, and their Weyl group is the affine Weyl group, a semidirect product of the finite Weyl group with a lattice of translations.

Representations

Integrable highest-weight representations are indexed by a non-negative integer called the level (the eigenvalue of the central element); at each level there are only finitely many. Their characters are given by the Weyl–Kac character formula and transform as modular forms, which is the algebraic core of the Wess–Zumino–Witten models in physics. The Sugawara construction builds Virasoro algebra elements from affine currents, linking the theory to the representation theory of the Virasoro algebra.
