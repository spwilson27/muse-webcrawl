Finite field

In mathematics, a finite field or Galois field (so-named in honour of Évariste Galois) is a field that contains a finite number of elements. As with any field, a finite field is a set on which multiplication, addition, subtraction and division are defined and satisfy the basic rules of arithmetic. The number of elements of a finite field is called its order or its size.

Existence and uniqueness

A finite field of order q exists if and only if q is a prime power pn. For prime order p, the field is simply the integers modulo p. For n > 1, the field is constructed as polynomials over GF(p) modulo an irreducible polynomial of degree n. Any two finite fields of the same order are isomorphic, justifying the notation GF(q).

Structure

The nonzero elements of a finite field form a cyclic group under multiplication. The characteristic of a finite field is its prime p; the field contains a copy of GF(p) as its prime subfield. The Frobenius map x ↦ xp is an automorphism, and its powers generate the Galois group over the prime field.

Applications

Finite fields are fundamental in number theory, algebraic geometry, combinatorics and finite geometry. In applied mathematics and computer science they underlie error-correcting codes (such as Reed–Solomon codes), the AES encryption standard (which uses GF(28)), elliptic-curve cryptography and stream ciphers.
