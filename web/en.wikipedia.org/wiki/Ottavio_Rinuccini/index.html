Ottavio Rinuccini

Ottavio Rinuccini (1562–1621)

Born: 20 January 1562, Florence, Italy

Died: 28 March 1621, Florence

Occupation: Poet, librettist

Known for: Libretti of the first operas

Ottavio Rinuccini (20 January 1562 – 28 March 1621) was an Italian poet, courtier, and librettist at the court of Ferdinand I de' Medici, Grand Duke of Tuscany. He is best known as the librettist of the earliest surviving operas, works created in collaboration with the Florentine Camerata circle and composers Jacopo Peri and Giulio Caccini.

Rinuccini wrote the libretto for Dafne (1598), generally regarded as the first opera, set to music by Peri (with contributions from Caccini), of which only fragments of the score survive. He was also the librettist of Euridice (1600), performed for the wedding celebrations of Maria de' Medici and Henry IV of France, with music by Peri and Caccini — the earliest opera whose score survives complete. He went on to write the libretto for Claudio Monteverdi's L'Arianna (1608), of which only the famous "Lamento d'Arianna" survives, and for Monteverdi's ballo Il ballo delle ingrate (1608).

Rinuccini accompanied Maria de' Medici to France, where Henry IV made him a gentilhomme de la chambre. In his poetry, written in imitation of Chiabrera and the French Pléiade poets, he experimented with free verse forms that helped shape the flexible poetic style of early opera.
