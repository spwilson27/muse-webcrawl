International Business Machines Corporation (IBM), nicknamed Big Blue, is an American multinational technology corporation headquartered in Armonk, New York. Founded in 1911 as the Computing-Tabulating-Recording Company and renamed IBM in 1924, it grew under Thomas J. Watson Sr. into the dominant maker of tabulating equipment and then of computers.

History

IBM's punched-card machines were used in censuses and business accounting worldwide. Under Thomas J. Watson Jr., the company bet heavily on electronic computers, launching the System/360 family in 1964 — a $5 billion gamble that redefined the industry. IBM mainframes became the backbone of large-enterprise computing, and the System/370 and its descendants (today IBM Z) continue that line.

Modern era

After a near-collapse in the early 1990s, IBM pivoted toward services and software, later selling its PC business to Lenovo (2005) and acquiring Red Hat for $34 billion (2019), its largest acquisition. Its current strategy centers on hybrid cloud and artificial intelligence, including the watsonx platform, while remaining a major research organization and patent holder.
