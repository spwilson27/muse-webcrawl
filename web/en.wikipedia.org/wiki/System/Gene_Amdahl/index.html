Gene Myron Amdahl (November 16, 1922 – November 10, 2015) was an American computer architect and high-tech entrepreneur, chiefly known for his work at IBM, where he was the chief architect of the System/360 mainframe family, and for founding Amdahl Corporation, which sold plug-compatible mainframes competing with IBM.

Career

Amdahl earned his PhD in theoretical physics from the University of Wisconsin–Madison in 1952, where he designed the WISC, one of the first university computers. At IBM he worked on the IBM 704 and 709 and led the architecture of System/360 (announced 1964), the first family of compatible computers spanning a wide performance range. He formulated Amdahl's law in 1967, which bounds the speedup obtainable from parallelizing part of a computation.

Amdahl Corporation

Leaving IBM in 1970, he founded Amdahl Corporation with Fujitsu backing; its 470V/6 (1975) outperformed IBM's comparable machines at lower cost, pioneering the plug-compatible mainframe market. He later founded Trilogy Systems and other ventures. He died in 2015 of pneumonia, having also suffered from Alzheimer's disease.
