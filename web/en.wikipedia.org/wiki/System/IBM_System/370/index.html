The IBM System/370 (S/370) is a family of mainframe computers announced by IBM on June 30, 1970, as the successor to the System/360. The family maintained backward compatibility with the 360 while introducing improved technology, and its architecture — extended over decades through System/390, zSeries, and IBM Z — remains in use today.

Features

Initial models lacked virtual memory, but the 1972 "Advanced Function" announcement added dynamic address translation to most of the line, along with virtual-machine support through VM/370. The 370 replaced core memory with semiconductor memory, used monolithic integrated circuits, and added features such as multiprocessor support and more precise floating-point and time-of-day facilities.

Models and legacy

The range ran from the small Model 115 to the high-end Model 195. Plug-compatible clones from Amdahl, Hitachi, and others competed directly. Later evolutions included the 370-XA extended architecture (31-bit addressing, 1983) and ESA/370, before the line was rebranded System/390 in 1990.
