System

A system is a group of interacting or interrelated elements that act according to a set of rules to form a unified whole. A system, surrounded and influenced by its environment, is described by its boundaries, structure, and purpose, and is expressed in its functioning. Systems are the subjects of study of systems theory and other systems sciences.

The word derives from the Greek systēma, meaning a "whole compounded of several parts". Systems are found in nature (the solar system, ecosystems, the cardiovascular system), in technology (computer systems, control systems), and in society (economic, legal, and political systems). A defining idea is emergence: a system can exhibit properties that none of its parts possess alone.

Concepts

  • Components and relations: the elements of a system and the connections between them.
  • Boundary and environment: the line between what belongs to the system and its surroundings; open systems exchange matter, energy, or information with the environment, while closed and isolated systems do not.
  • Inputs and outputs: flows that enter and leave the system.
  • Feedback: loops in which outputs influence future inputs — negative feedback stabilizes, positive feedback amplifies.
  • Hierarchy: systems are typically composed of subsystems and are themselves parts of larger suprasystems.

Systems theory

General systems theory, founded by biologist Ludwig von Bertalanffy in the mid-20th century, sought principles common to systems in all fields. Related disciplines include cybernetics, system dynamics, complexity science, and systems engineering, which applies systems thinking to the design and management of engineered systems. Systems concepts have been influential in fields from biology and ecology to psychology, management, and computer science.
