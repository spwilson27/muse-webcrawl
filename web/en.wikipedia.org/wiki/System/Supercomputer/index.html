System/Supercomputer

System/Supercomputer is a naming convention associated with pages and topics dealing with IBM's large-scale computing systems and supercomputers — the lineage of IBM mainframes and high-performance computing machines marketed under the "System" brand, from System/360 and System/370 to later System z mainframes, and IBM supercomputers such as the Blue Gene series, the POWER-based machines, Summit, and Sierra.

Background

IBM introduced the System/360 family in 1964, a landmark line of mainframes sharing a common architecture across a range of performance levels — a concept that defined enterprise computing. The "System" branding continued through System/370, System/390, and System z. In parallel, IBM became a leading builder of supercomputers: Deep Blue famously defeated Garry Kasparov at chess in 1997; Blue Gene/L led the TOP500 rankings in the mid-2000s; and Summit, installed at Oak Ridge National Laboratory in 2018, was for a time the world's fastest supercomputer, exceeding 100 petaflops.

Scope

Articles gathered under this topic cover the hardware architectures, operating systems (such as z/OS), and notable installations of IBM's largest machines, as well as their role in scientific research, weather modelling, nuclear simulation, and artificial intelligence research.
