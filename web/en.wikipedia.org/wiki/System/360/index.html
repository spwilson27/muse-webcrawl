IBM System/360

Manufacturer: IBM

Released: April 7, 1964

Predecessor: IBM 7000 series

Successor: System/370

The IBM System/360 (S/360) is a family of mainframe computer systems announced by IBM on April 7, 1964. It was the first family of computers designed to cover both commercial and scientific applications and to cover a complete range of sizes, all with a single instruction set architecture. The name "360" referred to the 360 degrees of a compass, suggesting a system for all directions of computing.

Architecture

The System/360 introduced the 8-bit byte as the basic unit of addressing, 32-bit words, hexadecimal floating point, and standardized peripheral interfaces. Because all models — from the small Model 30 to the large Model 91 — implemented the same architecture, customers could upgrade hardware without rewriting software, an idea that revolutionized the industry and influenced all later computer families. The chief architect was Gene Amdahl, and the project was led by Fred Brooks, who later wrote about its famously troubled software effort, the OS/360 operating system, in The Mythical Man-Month.

Impact

The development program cost IBM more than $5 billion, a gamble often described as "betting the company". It succeeded overwhelmingly: System/360 established IBM's dominance of mainframe computing, and its architecture survives, through System/370, System/390 and z/Architecture, in IBM mainframes sold today.
