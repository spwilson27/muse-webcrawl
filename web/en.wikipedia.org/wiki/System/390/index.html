IBM System/390

The IBM System/390 is a discontinued mainframe product family, the fifth generation of the System/360 instruction set architecture. Announced in September 1990, the ES/9000 series introduced the ESCON fiber-optic channel architecture, Sysplex clustering, and significant CMOS-based successors (the S/390 G-series) that replaced bipolar processor technology.

Features

System/390 ran operating systems including MVS/ESA (later OS/390), VM/ESA, and VSE/ESA. The Parallel Sysplex allowed multiple systems to share data with a coupling facility, improving availability and scalability. Later CMOS models (9672 series) dramatically reduced power, cooling, and floor-space requirements compared with earlier water-cooled machines.

Legacy

In 2000 IBM replaced the S/390 line with the 64-bit zSeries (beginning with the z900), moving the architecture to z/Architecture while retaining backward compatibility with System/390 and even System/360 application binaries — a lineage of compatibility stretching back to 1964. Server consolidation on S/390 also popularized running many Linux images under VM, foreshadowing modern virtualization.
