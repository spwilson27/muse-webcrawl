Ralph Waldo Emerson (1803–1882) was an American essayist, lecturer, philosopher and poet who led the transcendentalist movement of the mid-19th century. His essays on individualism, self-reliance and the spiritual significance of nature made him the central figure of the American Renaissance.

Life and ideas

Educated at Harvard, Emerson served briefly as a Unitarian minister before resigning in 1832 over doctrinal doubts. His anonymous book Nature (1836) became the movement's manifesto, arguing that divinity is experienced directly in nature and the soul rather than through tradition. His addresses "The American Scholar" (1837) and the Divinity School Address (1838), and essays such as "Self-Reliance", "The Over-Soul" and "Experience", urged Americans to trust their own intuition: "Trust thyself: every heart vibrates to that iron string."

Influence

Emerson was a mentor to Henry David Thoreau, an influence on Walt Whitman and later on pragmatist philosophy. His home in Concord, Massachusetts, became a gathering place for the transcendentalist circle, and he lectured tirelessly across the country for four decades.
