Diffie–Hellman key exchange

Diffie–Hellman key exchange
Designers: Whitfield Diffie, Martin Hellman, Ralph Merkle
Published: 1976
Type: Key-agreement protocol
Based on: Discrete logarithm problem

Diffie–Hellman (DH) key exchange is a mathematical method of securely exchanging cryptographic keys over a public channel and was one of the first public-key protocols as conceived by Ralph Merkle and named after Whitfield Diffie and Martin Hellman. DH is one of the earliest practical examples of public key exchange implemented within the field of cryptography.

Protocol

The two parties publicly agree on a large prime p and a generator g. Each party chooses a secret exponent and sends the other the corresponding power of g modulo p. Both can then compute the same shared secret by raising the value they received to their own secret exponent. An eavesdropper who sees the exchanged values must solve the discrete logarithm problem to recover the secret.

Security

The protocol provides no authentication by itself and is vulnerable to man-in-the-middle attacks unless combined with signatures or certificates. Elliptic-curve Diffie–Hellman (ECDH) is a widely used variant, and ephemeral DH provides forward secrecy in protocols such as TLS.
