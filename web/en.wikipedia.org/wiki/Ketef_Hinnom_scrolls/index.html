Ketef Hinnom scrolls

Placeholder image: the Ketef Hinnom silver scrolls.

The Ketef Hinnom scrolls, also known as the Ketef Hinnom amulets, are two tiny silver scrolls inscribed with ancient Hebrew texts, discovered in 1979 in a burial cave at Ketef Hinnom, southwest of the Old City of Jerusalem. Dated to the late 7th or early 6th century BC, they contain the oldest surviving texts closely paralleling passages of the Hebrew Bible.

Discovery and decipherment

Archaeologist Gabriel Barkay excavated the First Temple–period burial caves in 1979; the rolled silver strips were found in a repository of grave goods. Because the metal was fragile and corroded, unrolling and photographing the inscriptions took years of delicate conservation work. Improved imaging in the early 2000s enabled a fuller reading and republication of the texts.

Contents

The longer scroll (KH1) and shorter scroll (KH2) bear lines invoking YHWH and a blessing closely matching the Priestly Blessing of Numbers 6:24–26: "May YHWH bless you and keep you; may YHWH make his face shine upon you…" The inscriptions suggest the scrolls were worn as amulets for protection.

Significance

The scrolls are frequently cited in debates over the dating of biblical texts, as they provide material evidence that the Priestly Blessing circulated in Judah before the Babylonian exile. They are now held by the Israel Museum.
