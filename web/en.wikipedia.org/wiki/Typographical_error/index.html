Typographical error

A typographical error (often shortened to typo), also called a misprint, is a mistake made in the typing of printed or electronic material. Historically, the term referred to errors introduced by mechanical typesetting; most typos today are the result of haste or mis-keying by authors rather than mechanical faults.

Types and consequences

Common forms include transposed letters, omitted or duplicated words, and "atomic typos" in which a wrong but correctly spelled word (such as "from" for "form") evades spell checkers. Usually typos are inconsequential, but some have been costly or famous: the so-called Wicked Bible of 1631 omitted the word "not" from the commandment against adultery, and an extra "s" in a wire-service report was blamed for a stock-market dip. Intentional misprints called trap streets and copyright traps are sometimes inserted to detect copying.
