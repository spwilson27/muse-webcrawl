International observance

An international observance, also known as an international dedication or international anniversary, is a period of time to observe some issue of international interest or concern. Many such observances are established by the United Nations General Assembly, the Economic and Social Council, or specialised agencies such as UNESCO, UNICEF, and the World Health Organization.

Purpose

International days, weeks, years, and decades are used to raise public awareness, mobilise political will, and coordinate action on issues such as health, human rights, the environment, and cultural heritage. The United Nations designates specific days and other observances to mark particular aspects of life or history; member states are invited, but not obliged, to mark them.

Examples

Well-known observances include World Health Day (7 April), International Women's Day (8 March), Earth Day (22 April), World AIDS Day (1 December), Human Rights Day (10 December), and the International Day of Peace (21 September). Longer designations include the International Year of Astronomy (2009) and the United Nations Decade on Ecosystem Restoration (2021–2030).

Establishment

Most UN observances are proposed by member states and adopted by resolution of the General Assembly. The economic impact and proliferation of new days have occasionally prompted debate about how many observances the calendar can usefully support.
