Neon
Symbol: Ne

Neon is the chemical element with the symbol Ne and atomic number 10. It is a colourless, inert noble gas that glows a brilliant orange-red when an electric discharge passes through it — the light of classic neon signs.

Neon makes up only about 18 parts per million of air and is obtained by fractional distillation of liquid air. Besides signage and advertising tubes, it is used in high-voltage indicator lamps, lasers (helium–neon) and cryogenics.

[Image: A neon sign glowing orange-red in the dark]

History

William Ramsay and Morris Travers discovered neon in London in 1898, shortly after krypton and just before xenon, by fractionating liquid air; Ramsay's son suggested the name from the Greek neos, new. Georges Muse commercialised neon lighting in Paris from 1910.

Inertness

Neon is among the least reactive of all elements — no true neutral compounds of neon are known under normal conditions — because its electron shell is completely filled.
