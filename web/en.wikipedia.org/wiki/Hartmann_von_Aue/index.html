Hartmann von Aue (c. 1160–1170 – c. 1210–1220) was a German poet of the Middle High German period, regarded with Wolfram von Eschenbach and Gottfried von Strassburg as one of the three great narrative poets of the German courtly flowering around 1200. A ministerialis (service nobleman) of the lords of Aue, he introduced the Arthurian romance into German literature with his adaptations of Chrétien de Troyes.

Works

Hartmann's surviving oeuvre comprises two Arthurian romances, two religious or exemplary tales, a didactic debate poem and a group of lyric songs (Minnesang), including crusading songs suggesting he took part in a crusade, probably that of 1189 or 1197.

  • Erec (c. 1185) — the first Arthurian romance in German, adapted from Chrétien's Érec et Énide, on a knight who must balance love and chivalric duty.
  • Gregorius (c. 1190) — a legend of a man born of incest who, after unwittingly repeating the sin, does seventeen years of penance chained to a rock and becomes pope.
  • Der arme Heinrich ("Poor Heinrich", c. 1195) — a short tale of a leprous knight healed by a peasant girl's willingness to sacrifice her life for him; later adapted by Longfellow and Gerhart Hauptmann.
  • Iwein (c. 1200) — from Chrétien's Yvain, the story of a knight who breaks faith with his wife, goes mad, and wins her back through deeds performed with a lion.
  • Das Klagebüchlein ("Little Book of Lament") — a debate between body and heart on love.

Style and influence

Hartmann was praised by contemporaries — Gottfried von Strassburg singled out his "crystal-clear words" — for smooth, lucid verse and for reshaping his French sources toward ethical reflection on guilt, penance and the reconciliation of minne (love) and êre (honour). His romances fixed the German Arthurian tradition and were imitated for two centuries.
