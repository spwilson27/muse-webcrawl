Aggressor squadron

An aggressor squadron or adversary squadron is a unit in the United States military (and the forces of allied nations) that flies aircraft as an opposing force ("red air") during training exercises, simulating the tactics, techniques, and aircraft of potential adversaries. Aggressor pilots study enemy doctrine and replicate it so that operational ("blue air") crews can practice against realistic opposition.

History

The concept arose from the lessons of the Vietnam War, in which U.S. aircrews suffered unexpectedly high losses in air-to-air combat. Studies such as the Navy's Ault Report led to the creation of dedicated dissimilar air combat training (DACT) programs, most famously the U.S. Navy's TOPGUN school in 1969 and the U.S. Air Force's aggressor squadrons beginning in the early 1970s, closely associated with the large-force Red Flag exercises at Nellis Air Force Base.

Aircraft and organisation

Aggressor units have flown aircraft such as the A-4 Skyhawk, F-5 Tiger II, F-21 Kfir, F-16 Fighting Falcon, and F/A-18 Hornet, often painted in distinctive camouflage schemes imitating foreign air forces. In recent years, budget pressures have expanded the role of private contractor companies providing adversary support (ADAIR), flying retired military jets. The U.S. Air Force's aggressor fleet has also begun incorporating the F-35 to replicate fifth-generation threats.
