Imidacloprid

Imidacloprid
Formula: C9H10ClN5O2
Molar mass: 255.66 g/mol
Class: Neonicotinoid

Imidacloprid is a systemic insecticide that acts as an insect neurotoxin and belongs to a class of chemicals called the neonicotinoids, which act on the central nervous system of insects. The chemical works by interfering with the transmission of stimuli in the insect nervous system: it binds irreversibly to specific insect nicotinic acetylcholine receptors, causing paralysis and death. Because it binds much more strongly to insect receptors than to those of mammals, imidacloprid is selectively more toxic to insects than to mammals.

Imidacloprid was patented and developed by Bayer and was first registered for use in the 1990s. It became the most widely used insecticide in the world, applied as seed treatments, soil treatments, foliar sprays, and in veterinary products for flea control on domestic animals.

Uses

Imidacloprid is effective against sucking insects such as aphids, leafhoppers and whiteflies, as well as termites, soil insects, and some beetles. It is used on cereals, rice, cotton, potatoes, maize, sugar beets, vegetables, and fruit. As a systemic insecticide it is taken up by the plant and distributed throughout its tissues, protecting the whole plant, including new growth.

Environmental effects

Imidacloprid is highly toxic to many insects, including beneficial species such as bees. Sub-lethal exposure has been linked to impaired navigation, learning and foraging in honey bees, and neonicotinoids have been implicated—though the extent of their role is debated—in pollinator declines and colony collapse disorder. The chemical is persistent in soil, water-soluble, and can leach into waterways, where it is toxic to aquatic invertebrates.

Concerns over pollinators led the European Union to restrict imidacloprid and two other neonicotinoids in 2013, and in 2018 to ban all outdoor uses of the three substances. Other jurisdictions have introduced labelling requirements and targeted restrictions.

Toxicology

Imidacloprid is moderately toxic to mammals if ingested, and is classified by the World Health Organization as a Class II (moderately hazardous) pesticide. It is of low toxicity by dermal and inhalation routes in typical formulations, but it is highly toxic to birds in some exposure scenarios and extremely toxic to aquatic invertebrates.
