Die grinder

A die grinder or rotary tool is a handheld power tool used for grinding, sanding, honing, polishing, and machining. The tool is driven by compressed air or electricity and spins a collet-mounted bit — such as a carbide burr, mounted grinding stone, or cut-off wheel — at very high speed, typically 20,000 to 35,000 revolutions per minute.

Uses

Die grinders owe their name to their use in tool and die making, where they shape and finish dies and molds. They are also used for deburring machined parts, porting engine cylinder heads, removing rust and weld material, and detailed craft work. Straight and right-angle (90-degree) configurations exist; the smaller, multi-purpose rotary tools popularized by brands such as Dremel are close relatives.

Safety

Because of their high rotational speeds, bits must be rated for the tool's maximum rpm; a failed grinding stone can shatter explosively. Eye protection is essential, and workpieces should be clamped rather than hand-held where possible.
