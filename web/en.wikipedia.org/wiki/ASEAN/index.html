ASEAN

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) is a regional grouping of ten Southeast Asian states — Brunei, Cambodia, Indonesia, Laos, Malaysia, Myanmar, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand and Vietnam — promoting intergovernmental cooperation and economic, political and cultural integration. Founded on 8 August 1967 in Bangkok by Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore and Thailand, it expanded after the Cold War to encompass the whole region; Timor-Leste, admitted in principle in 2022, has been completing its accession roadmap.

The "ASEAN Way"

ASEAN's working style — consensus, non-interference in internal affairs, informality and quiet diplomacy (the "ASEAN Way") — underpins both its longevity and its limitations. The ASEAN Charter (2008) gave it legal personality and human-rights and community-building commitments organised in political-security, economic and socio-cultural communities (AEC launched 2015). The bloc, with a combined population of about 680 million and one of the world's largest economies, sits at the centre of wider Asian architecture — the East Asia Summit, ASEAN+3 and the RCEP trade agreement (2020) — while managing tensions over the South China Sea, Myanmar's post-2021-coup civil war (where its Five-Point Consensus has had limited effect) and US–China rivalry.
