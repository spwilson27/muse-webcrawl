Centroid

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics and physics, the centroid, also known as the geometric center or center of figure, of a plane figure or solid figure is the arithmetic mean position of all the points in the figure. The same definition extends to any object in n-dimensional Euclidean space. Informally, it is the point at which a cutout of the shape (with uniformly distributed mass) could be perfectly balanced on the tip of a pin.

Properties

The centroid of a convex object always lies within the object; the centroid of a non-convex object can lie outside it — for example, the centroid of a ring or a crescent lies outside the material. If a figure possesses a line of symmetry, its centroid lies on that line; if it has a centre of symmetry, the centroid is that centre. The centroid of a composite figure can be found by dividing it into simpler figures, computing their centroids and areas, and taking the area-weighted average.

Of a triangle

The centroid of a triangle is the point where its three medians intersect. It divides each median in the ratio 2:1, and it coincides with the centre of mass of a uniform triangular plate, as well as the average of the three vertices' coordinates. It is one of the triangle's classical centres, known as X(2) in the Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers.

Computation

For a planar region bounded by a closed curve, the centroid coordinates can be computed by line or area integrals; for polygons, simple formulas based on the vertices (the shoelace-like formulae) give the area and centroid exactly. In data analysis, the centroid of a set of points — their coordinate-wise mean — is used in clustering algorithms such as k-means, where each cluster is represented by its centroid.

Centroid and centre of mass

For an object of uniform density in a uniform gravitational field, the centroid coincides with the centre of mass. In a non-uniform gravitational field — such as that experienced by a very tall structure — the centre of gravity can differ slightly from the centre of mass and hence from the centroid.
