Planning permission

Planning permission or developmental approval refers to the approval needed for construction or expansion (including significant renovation) of buildings and other development, in addition to the work of the building control body such as building permit. In the United Kingdom, planning permission is governed primarily by the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 and is administered by local planning authorities.

United Kingdom

In England and Wales, planning permission is required for most "development", defined as building operations and material changes of use of land or buildings. Certain minor works fall within "permitted development" rights and do not need an express application. Applications are decided in accordance with the local development plan unless material considerations indicate otherwise. Decisions can be appealed to the Planning Inspectorate.

Types of permission

Permissions may be full (detailed), outline (establishing the principle of development with details reserved), or subject to conditions. Conservation area consent and listed building consent are separate regimes protecting heritage assets.

Other jurisdictions

Analogous systems exist in many countries, such as development consent in Australia and land-use permits in the United States, where zoning ordinances regulate what may be built. The term is also used for consents required for changes of use, advertisements, and works to protected trees.
