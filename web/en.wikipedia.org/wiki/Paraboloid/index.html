Paraboloid

A paraboloid is a quadric surface in three-dimensional geometry whose cross-sections along one axis are parabolas. There are two kinds: the elliptic paraboloid, which has the shape of an oval cup, and the hyperbolic paraboloid, which has the shape of a saddle.

Equations

In a Cartesian coordinate system, the elliptic paraboloid is given by the equation z = x²/a² + y²/b². When a = b, the surface is a paraboloid of revolution (circular paraboloid), obtained by rotating a parabola about its axis. The hyperbolic paraboloid is given by z = x²/a² − y²/b²; its sections parallel to the coordinate planes are parabolas in one direction and hyperbolas in another, and it is a doubly ruled surface, a property exploited in thin-shell roof construction.

Properties and applications

The paraboloid of revolution has the famous reflective property: rays entering parallel to its axis are reflected through its focal point, and conversely a source at the focus produces a parallel beam. This makes it the ideal shape for satellite dishes, radio telescope reflectors, parabolic microphones, solar concentrators, and headlamp reflectors.

Hyperbolic paraboloid ("hypar") shells are used in architecture for roofs that are both structurally efficient and visually striking; because the surface contains straight lines, it can be formed from straight boards or beams. A popular everyday illustration is the hyperbolic paraboloid shape of certain potato crisps.
