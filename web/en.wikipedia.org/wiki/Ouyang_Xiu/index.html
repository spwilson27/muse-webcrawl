Ouyang Xiu

Ouyang Xiu (1007–1072), courtesy name Yongshu, was a Chinese historian, essayist, calligrapher, poet and politician of the Song dynasty. One of the most versatile and influential literary figures of his age, he was a central leader of the literary reform movement that championed clear, plain prose modelled on Han dynasty writers over the ornate parallel prose then in fashion.

Life

Born in Mianzhou (in modern Sichuan) and raised in Jizhou, Jiangxi, Ouyang lost his father at age four and was educated by his mother, who famously taught him to write with a reed on sand. He passed the imperial examinations with the jinshi degree in 1030. His career alternated between high office in the capital Kaifeng and postings in the provinces, including Chuzhou, where he wrote the celebrated Record of the Pavilion of the Drunken Old Man (Zuiweng Ting Ji), taking his pen name from that work.

Works

Ouyang compiled the New History of the Tang Dynasty (Xin Tang Shu) and authored the Historical Records of the Five Dynasties, the only privately written work among the Twenty-Four Histories. His essays, poetry and ci lyrics were widely admired, and he mentored younger writers including Su Shi, Su Zhe and Zeng Gong — together counted among the Eight Masters of the Tang and Song.

Legacy

His advocacy of guwen (ancient prose) reshaped Chinese literary style for centuries, and his historical scholarship set standards for official historiography.
