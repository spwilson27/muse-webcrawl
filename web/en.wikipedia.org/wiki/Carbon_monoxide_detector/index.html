Carbon monoxide detector

A typical domestic carbon monoxide alarm with digital display. (Image placeholder)

A carbon monoxide detector or CO detector is a device that detects the presence of carbon monoxide (CO) gas in order to prevent carbon monoxide poisoning. Carbon monoxide is a colourless, odourless and tasteless gas produced by the incomplete combustion of carbon-containing fuels in appliances such as furnaces, boilers, gas stoves, fireplaces and vehicle engines; because it cannot be sensed by humans, an electronic detector is the only reliable warning.

How it works

Domestic detectors use one of three sensor types:

  • Electrochemical sensors — the most common in homes; CO reacts in a chemical cell, generating a small electric current proportional to the gas concentration.
  • Biomimetic sensors — a gel changes colour as it absorbs CO, triggering an alarm; they are cheap but slower and less precise.
  • Metal oxide semiconductor sensors — heated tin dioxide whose conductivity changes in the presence of CO; more common in industrial settings.

Alarm thresholds

Because CO harms through cumulative exposure, standards such as UL 2034 in the United States and EN 50291 in Europe require alarms to sound after defined concentration–time combinations: for example, an alarm within 60–240 minutes at 70 parts per million, within 10–50 minutes at 150 ppm, and within a few minutes at 400 ppm, while ignoring low levels (below about 30 ppm) that might arise from transient sources. Many devices also display peak or current readings.

Installation and regulation

Detectors should be installed on each floor of a home and near sleeping areas and fuel-burning appliances. Many jurisdictions now mandate CO alarms in homes with fuel-burning appliances or attached garages, and in rental properties. Sensors have a limited life — typically five to ten years — after which the unit must be replaced; alarms signal end-of-life automatically. Detectors may be battery powered, mains powered with battery backup, or combined with smoke alarms.
