Super Bowl XII

The first Super Bowl played indoors, at the Louisiana Superdome

Super Bowl XII was an American football game between the NFC champion Dallas Cowboys and the AFC champion Denver Broncos to decide the NFL champion for the 1977 season. The Cowboys defeated the Broncos 27–10 on 15 January 1978 at the Louisiana Superdome in New Orleans — the first Super Bowl played in a domed stadium and the first broadcast in prime time. Dallas's defensive linemen Harvey Martin and Randy White were named co-MVPs, the only time the award has been shared.

The subplot of the game was Broncos quarterback Craig Morton, who had started Super Bowl V for Dallas and lost; facing his old team, he was intercepted four times in the first half and eventually benched for Norris Weese. The Cowboys' "Doomsday Defense" forced eight turnovers in total. Roger Staubach threw a touchdown to Butch Johnson, and fullback Robert Newhouse threw a famous 29-yard touchdown pass to Golden Richards on a trick play to seal the win.

Significance

It was the second Super Bowl championship for coach Tom Landry's Cowboys. Denver's "Orange Crush" defence had carried the Broncos to their first Super Bowl, but the offence's collapse against Dallas became the game's enduring story. The shared MVP award for Martin and White recognised a defensive performance that ranks among the most dominant in Super Bowl history.
