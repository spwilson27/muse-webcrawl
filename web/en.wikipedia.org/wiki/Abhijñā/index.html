Abhijñā

Abhijñā (Sanskrit: अभिज्ञा; Pali: abhiññā) is a Buddhist term meaning "direct knowledge" or "higher knowledge". In the Pali Canon it refers to the supernormal powers and insights gained through deep meditation (dhyāna), classically enumerated as six.

The six abhijñās

#Power
1Iddhi — psychokinetic powers such as walking on water or multiplying the body
2Divine ear — hearing sounds at any distance, human and divine
3Knowledge of others' minds (telepathy)
4Recollection of one's former lives
5Divine eye — seeing beings die and be reborn according to their karma
6Extinction of the mental intoxicants (āsava), the knowledge constituting liberation

Significance

The first five abhijñās are considered mundane attainments available to skilled meditators, including non-Buddhists, and the texts warn against attachment to them. Only the sixth — the destruction of the āsavas — is supramundane and identical with arahantship. The Buddha is described as realising the "three knowledges" (tevijjā) on the night of his awakening.
