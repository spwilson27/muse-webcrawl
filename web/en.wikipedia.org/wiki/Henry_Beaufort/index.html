Henry Beaufort (c. 1375 – 11 April 1447) was an English Catholic prelate and statesman, Bishop of Winchester, three times Lord Chancellor of England, and a cardinal. The second son of John of Gaunt by his mistress and later wife Katherine Swynford, he was one of the richest and most powerful men in England during the reigns of Henry IV, Henry V and Henry VI.

Early career

The Beaufort children were legitimised by Richard II and later by Henry IV, though with a contested bar on inheriting the throne. Ordained and educated at Oxford and Aachen, Henry became Bishop of Lincoln in 1398 and, in 1404, was translated to the wealthy see of Winchester, which he held for the rest of his life. He served as Chancellor under Henry IV and again under Henry V, supporting the crown's finances and the war in France.

Cardinal and power broker

In 1426 Pope Martin V made him a cardinal and papal legate — to the fury of his nephew Humphrey, Duke of Gloucester, with whom Beaufort feuded bitterly over the legality of an English bishop holding a cardinal's hat. During the minority of Henry VI, Beaufort was the effective head of the peace party at court, favouring negotiation with France, and he repeatedly lent enormous sums to the crown from his fortune, effectively bankrolling the war effort even as he sought to end it. He crowned Henry VI at Paris in 1431 and presided over aspects of the trial of Joan of Arc in his capacity as a senior churchman in English-held France — tradition even assigning him a role at her execution, though the extent of his involvement is debated.

Death and reputation

Beaufort died at Wolvesey Palace, Winchester, on 11 April 1447 and was buried in Winchester Cathedral, where his chantry chapel survives. Shakespeare dramatised him in the Henry VI plays as the proud, scheming "Cardinal of Winchester", fixing his popular image as an ambitious prince of the church.
