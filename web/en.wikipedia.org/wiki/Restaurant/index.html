Restaurant

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: The dining room of a restaurant with set tables]

A restaurant is a business that prepares and serves food and drinks to customers, generally for eating on the premises, though many also offer take-out and delivery. Restaurants vary greatly in appearance and offerings, from fast-food counters to luxury establishments serving Haute cuisine.

The modern restaurant emerged in Paris in the 1760s–80s — the word derives from restaurant, a restorative broth — and flourished after the French Revolution. Earlier eating houses, inns, teahouses and coffeehouses existed in Song-dynasty China, the Islamic world and Europe, but the Parisian model of individual tables, menus and à la carte choice became the global template.

Types

Restaurants are classed by service style (table service, counter, buffet), cuisine, price and formality. Fast food chains industrialised the model in the 20th century with standardised menus and franchising; at the other extreme, tasting-menu restaurants compete for Michelin Guide stars. Street stalls and food trucks form a parallel sector worldwide.

Industry

The industry is a major employer and famously demanding: kitchens are organised on Escoffier's brigade system, margins are thin, and most new restaurants fail within a few years. Tipping customs, licensing and hygiene inspection vary by country.
