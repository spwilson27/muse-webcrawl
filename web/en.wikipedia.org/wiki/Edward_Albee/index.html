Edward Franklin Albee III (March 12, 1928 – September 16, 2016) was an American playwright known for works such as The Zoo Story (1958), The Sandbox (1959), Who's Afraid of Virginia Woolf? (1962), A Delicate Balance (1966) and Three Tall Women (1994).

Career

Adopted as an infant by a wealthy theatre-owning family, Albee left home as a young man and became a central figure of the Off-Broadway movement. Who's Afraid of Virginia Woolf? made him internationally famous and was adapted into the acclaimed 1966 film starring Elizabeth Taylor and Richard Burton.

Awards

Albee won three Pulitzer Prizes for Drama—for A Delicate Balance, Seascape and Three Tall Women—and multiple Tony Awards, and is regarded as one of the foremost American dramatists of the twentieth century.
