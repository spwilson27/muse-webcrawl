São Paulo is the most populous city in Brazil and the capital of the state of São Paulo. Located in southeastern Brazil on a plateau inland from Santos, it is the country's financial and economic centre and one of the largest cities in the world by population.

History

The city was founded in 1554 by Jesuit missionaries, including Manuel da Nóbrega and José de Anchieta, at a site between the Tamanduateí and Anhangabaú rivers. It grew slowly as a base for bandeirantes, then rapidly in the late 19th century with the coffee economy and mass immigration from Italy, Portugal, Spain, Japan, Lebanon and elsewhere.

Economy and culture

São Paulo hosts the B3 stock exchange, headquarters of major banks and corporations, and a large service sector. Cultural landmarks include the São Paulo Museum of Art (MASP), Ibirapuera Park, Avenida Paulista, and major events such as the São Paulo Biennial and the Brazilian Grand Prix at Interlagos.

Geography

The city sits about 760 metres above sea level, giving it a subtropical highland climate. Its sprawling metropolitan region faces persistent challenges with traffic, flooding and inequality.
