Porta Nigra

Porta Nigra

Location: Trier, Germany
Type: City gate
Built: c. 170 AD
Status: UNESCO World Heritage Site

The Porta Nigra (Latin for "black gate") is a large Roman city gate in Trier, Germany. It is today the largest Roman city gate north of the Alps and forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Roman Monuments, Cathedral of St Peter and Church of Our Lady in Trier".

History

The gate was built in grey sandstone around 170 AD as part of the city wall of Augusta Treverorum. The name Porta Nigra originated in the Middle Ages, a reference to the darkened colour of its stone after centuries of weathering. The original Roman name has not been preserved.

In the early Middle Ages the gate was converted into a church. The hermit Simeon settled in the eastern tower in 1028, and after his death and canonisation the gate was transformed into a double church dedicated to St Simeon, with an added apse and additional floors. This religious use protected the structure from being quarried for stone, unlike most other Roman monuments in Trier.

In 1804, during the Napoleonic occupation, the church additions were ordered demolished and the gate was restored toward its Roman appearance, though the eastern apse was retained. Further restoration took place in the 19th and 20th centuries.

Architecture

The gate is about 36 metres wide, 21.5 metres high and 21.5 metres deep. It consists of two four-storey towers projecting outward, flanking a double-arched gateway. Remarkably, its huge sandstone blocks — some weighing up to six tons — were held together by iron clamps rather than mortar; the clamps were largely stolen in the Middle Ages, leaving the characteristic holes visible today.
