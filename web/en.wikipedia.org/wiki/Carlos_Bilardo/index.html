Carlos Salvador Bilardo (born 16 March 1938), nicknamed El Narigón ("the big-nosed one"), is an Argentine former football player and manager, and a trained physician. He coached the Argentina national team to victory at the 1986 FIFA World Cup and to the final in 1990.

Playing career

A midfielder, Bilardo played for San Lorenzo and Deportivo Español before joining Osvaldo Zubeldía's Estudiantes de La Plata, where he was part of the side that won three consecutive Copa Libertadores titles (1968–1970) and the 1968 Intercontinental Cup against Manchester United. He retired in 1970 after completing his medical degree at the University of Buenos Aires.

Managerial career

Bilardo won the 1982 Argentine championship with Estudiantes before taking charge of Argentina in 1983. He built the national team around Diego Maradona, popularised the 3–5–2 formation, and led Argentina to World Cup glory in Mexico in 1986. Four years later his injury-hit side reached the 1990 final, losing to West Germany. His meticulous, results-first philosophy — known in Argentina as bilardismo — stood in contrast to the more romantic menottismo of his predecessor César Luis Menotti. He later managed Sevilla (briefly reuniting with Maradona) and Boca Juniors, and served as Argentina's general manager during Maradona's tenure as coach.
