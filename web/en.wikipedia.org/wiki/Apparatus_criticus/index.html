Apparatus criticus

Apparatus criticus
Field: Textual criticism
Function: Records variant readings
Location: Foot of the page in critical editions

An apparatus criticus (Latin for "critical apparatus") is the system of annotations and abbreviations printed, usually at the foot of each page, in a critical edition of a text. It records the variant readings found in the surviving manuscripts and earlier editions, the editor's reasons for choosing among them, and conjectural emendations, allowing readers to reconstruct the evidence behind the edited text.

Format

A typical entry begins with the line number or a quoted word from the text (the lemma), followed by the reading adopted, then the sigla — short codes — of the witnesses supporting it, and finally the alternative readings with their witnesses. Sigla conventionally use capital letters or numbers for major manuscripts and are defined in a list (conspectus siglorum) near the front of the edition. Latin abbreviations such as om. (omitted), add. (added), and del. (deleted) compress the information into little space.

Purpose

The apparatus makes textual scholarship verifiable: rather than trusting the editor's judgement silently, readers can see exactly where the manuscripts disagree and judge the decisions themselves. In editions of the Bible, classical Greek and Latin authors, and medieval texts, the apparatus is often as important to scholars as the text it supports.
