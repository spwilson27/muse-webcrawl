Musta'li Ismailism is a branch of Isma'ili Shia Islam that upheld the imamate of al-Musta'li, the younger son of the Fatimid caliph al-Mustansir Billah, following the succession dispute of 1094 that split the Ismaili movement from the Nizari branch.

History

After the death of the imam al-Amir in 1130, the Musta'li community divided over the succession of his infant son al-Tayyib, who is believed by Tayyibi Musta'lis to have entered concealment. In his absence the community is led by a Da'i al-Mutlaq ("absolute missionary").

Communities

The largest Musta'li group today is the Dawoodi Bohra community, centred in India and led by the Syedna, alongside the Sulaymani and Alavi Bohras and the Hebtiahs. Musta'li doctrine preserves much of the Fatimid-era theological and legal tradition.
