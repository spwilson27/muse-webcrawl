Othello (character)

Othello is the title character and protagonist of William Shakespeare's tragedy Othello, the Moor of Venice (c. 1603). A Moorish general in the service of Venice, he is a respected and eloquent soldier who has risen through merit, and has secretly married Desdemona, the daughter of the Venetian senator Brabantio.

Sent to Cyprus to defend the island against the Turks, Othello is manipulated by his ensign Iago, who plants the false suspicion that Desdemona has been unfaithful with the lieutenant Cassio. Consumed by jealousy, Othello smothers Desdemona, and on learning the truth from Iago's wife Emilia, kills himself. His speeches — including "Put out the light, and then put out the light" — are among Shakespeare's most famous.

Othello has been analysed as a study of jealousy, race, outsiderhood and self-dramatisation. The role has been played by many celebrated actors; historically performed in blackface, it is now generally played by Black actors. Shakespeare's source was a tale by the Italian writer Cinthio.
