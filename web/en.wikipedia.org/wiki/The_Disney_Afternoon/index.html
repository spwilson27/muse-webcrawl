The Disney Afternoon

The Disney Afternoon was a two-hour American syndicated television programming block of animated series produced by Walt Disney Television Animation, which aired on weekdays from September 10, 1990, to August 29, 1997. Created to consolidate Disney's afternoon cartoon line-up, the block became a defining fixture of after-school television for a generation of viewers.

Programming

The original 1990 line-up combined the established hits DuckTales and Chip 'n Dale: Rescue Rangers with the new series TaleSpin and Darkwing Duck. Over the following seasons the block introduced series including Goof Troop, Bonkers, Aladdin, Gargoyles, The Shnookums & Meat Funny Cartoon Show, Timon & Pumbaa and Quack Pack. Series rotated through the block's four half-hour slots, with older shows typically retired or moved to Saturday mornings.

Production and legacy

The block grew out of Disney's success in syndication under executive Michael Eisner and syndication chief Bob Jacquemin; a 1989 pilot block tested the concept. Its shows were largely produced overseas with animation by studios in Japan, Korea, France and Australia. The Disney Afternoon is fondly remembered in nostalgia culture, and many of its series were revived or rebooted, notably DuckTales in 2017.
