Monitor lizards are lizards in the genus Varanus, the only genus in the family Varanidae. They are native to Africa, Asia and Oceania, and one species, the Nile monitor, is also found as an invasive species in the southern United States. About 80 species are recognised. Monitor lizards have long necks, powerful tails and claws, well-developed limbs, and a forked tongue used to sample scent chemicals, like snakes. Most species are terrestrial, but some are arboreal or semi-aquatic; most are carnivorous, although a few Philippine species eat fruit.

The Komodo dragon (Varanus komodoensis), the largest living lizard.

Description and biology

Monitors range from the 20 cm short-tailed pygmy monitor of Australia to the Komodo dragon, which exceeds 3 m and 70 kg. They are active, intelligent foragers with relatively high metabolic rates for reptiles, an aerobic scope aided by a heart that functions in an almost four-chambered manner, and in some species a unidirectional airflow through the lungs. They possess large brains for lizards and show complex behaviour, including play and, in captivity, the ability to count small numbers of prey items.

Venom

In 2005 and subsequent studies, researchers led by Bryan Fry showed that monitor lizards possess venom glands in the lower jaw; the Komodo dragon's venom contains anticoagulant and hypotensive compounds that promote shock and blood loss in prey. Combined with serrated teeth that inflict deep wounds, this overturned the older belief that Komodo prey died mainly from bacterial infection of bites.

Relationship with humans

Monitor lizards are hunted for their skins (used in leather goods), meat and fat used in traditional medicine, and are widely kept in the pet trade; several species are threatened, and international trade is regulated under CITES Appendix I or II. Large species feature prominently in the folklore of their range countries, and the name "monitor" is said to derive from a mistaken translation of the Arabic waran ("warner") into German Warneidechse.

Famous species include the Komodo dragon, Nile monitor, Asian water monitor, savannah monitor, perentie and lace monitor.
