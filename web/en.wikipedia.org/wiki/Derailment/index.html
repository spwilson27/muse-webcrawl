Derailment

A derailment is an accident on a railway in which a train runs off its rails. Although relatively rare, derailments can have serious consequences, particularly when they occur at speed, on bridges or embankments, or when derailed vehicles obstruct adjacent lines and are struck by other trains.

Causes

Derailments fall into broad categories identified by accident investigators: mechanical failure of track components such as broken rails or failed sleepers; mechanical failure of rolling stock, including wheel, axle and bearing failures; geometric faults in track design or maintenance; operational errors such as excessive speed on curves or passing signals at danger; and external causes including collisions with obstacles, landslides, washouts and level-crossing accidents.

A frequent mechanism is a wheel climbing the rail head under lateral force, for instance when a wagon with a twisted frame or uneven loading negotiates a curve. Excessive speed on a curve can overturn vehicles outright, as in the 2015 Philadelphia derailment of Amtrak Northeast Regional train 188, which was travelling at more than twice the permitted speed.

Prevention and mitigation

Safety measures include regular ultrasonic inspection of rails, acoustic bearing monitoring, speed restrictions, automatic train protection systems, and containment kerbs on bridges. Derailers — devices that deliberately derail runaway stock — are installed on sidings and catch points to protect main lines. When a derailment occurs at low speed in a yard, damage is usually minor and vehicles can often be re-railed with jacks or cranes.
