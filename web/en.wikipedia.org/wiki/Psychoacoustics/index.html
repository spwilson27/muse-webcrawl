Psychoacoustics

Psychoacoustics is the branch of psychophysics involving the scientific study of sound perception and audiology — how humans perceive various sounds. More specifically, it studies the psychological responses associated with sound, including noise, speech and music, and the limits and peculiarities of human hearing.

Phenomena

Key psychoacoustic phenomena include the equal-loudness contours, which show that sensitivity to loudness varies with frequency (most sensitive around 2–5 kHz); the threshold of hearing; critical bands and frequency masking, in which a louder sound renders a nearby quieter frequency inaudible; the missing fundamental, whereby pitch is perceived even when the fundamental frequency is absent; and binaural cues — interaural time and level differences — used to localize sounds.

Applications

Psychoacoustic models underpin perceptual audio coding: MP3, AAC and Opus compression discard information the ear is predicted not to hear, such as tones masked by louder neighbours. The field also informs hearing-aid and cochlear-implant design, room acoustics, noise control, and the evaluation of sound quality in products and environments.
