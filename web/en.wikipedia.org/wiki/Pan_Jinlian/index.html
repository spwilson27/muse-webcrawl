Pan Jinlian (Chinese: 潘金蓮; pinyin: Pān Jīnlián) is a fictional character in the Chinese novels Water Margin and Jin Ping Mei (The Plum in the Golden Vase), and one of the most notorious femme fatales in Chinese literature. Her name has become a byword in Chinese culture for a promiscuous and murderous wife.

In Water Margin

Pan Jinlian is sold as a maid in childhood and, after resisting her master's advances, is married off in spite to Wu Dalang, a short, ugly seller of sesame cakes — the elder brother of the hero Wu Song. Living above a street in Yanggu County, she first attempts to seduce Wu Song, who sternly rejects her. She then begins an affair with the wealthy libertine Ximen Qing. When Wu Dalang discovers the adultery, the lovers poison him. On his return, Wu Song uncovers the murder, kills Pan Jinlian and Ximen Qing, and offers their heads at his brother's grave before fleeing into outlawry.

In Jin Ping Mei

The Ming-dynasty novel Jin Ping Mei expands Pan Jinlian's story from the same opening chapters of Water Margin into a detailed domestic chronicle of Ximen Qing's household, in which she is his fifth wife and a central figure in its intrigues, rivalries and decline. Her psychology, wit and cruelty are developed at far greater length than in the earlier novel.

Cultural legacy

Pan Jinlian remains a stock character in Chinese opera, storytelling and film, and her name is still invoked — controversially, in modern feminist readings that re-examine her as a victim of a patriarchal society — in discussions of female desire and morality in traditional China.
