In the United Kingdom, a scheduled monument is a nationally important archaeological or historic site given legal protection by being placed on a list (the "Schedule") maintained under the Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Areas Act 1979. Scheduling is the oldest form of heritage protection in Britain, beginning with the Ancient Monuments Protection Act 1882.

Protection

Once a monument is scheduled, it is an offence to carry out works that demolish, damage, alter, or flood it, or to use a metal detector on the site, without Scheduled Monument Consent from the Secretary of State, administered in England by Historic England. Unlike listed building consent, there is no deemed consent and the system gives no automatic right to compensation.

What is scheduled

Scheduled monuments range from prehistoric standing stones, burial mounds, and hillforts to Roman forts, medieval castles, deserted villages, moated sites, industrial remains, and even twentieth-century structures such as Cold War installations. Buildings in active use — especially places of worship and occupied structures — are generally protected by listing instead, and occupied dwellings are excluded from scheduling.

Administration

Each UK nation administers its own schedule: Historic England, Historic Environment Scotland, Cadw in Wales, and the Department for Communities in Northern Ireland. Inclusion on the Schedule is based on criteria of national importance including rarity, survival, documentation, and group value.
