The history of science fiction traces the development of the literary genre of science fiction, in which speculative elements drawn from science and technology shape the narrative. Though fantastic voyages appear in ancient literature, the genre proper emerged in the 19th century.

Precursors and the 19th century

Mary Shelley's Frankenstein (1818) is often cited as the first science fiction novel. Jules Verne's "Voyages extraordinaires" and H. G. Wells's scientific romances, such as The War of the Worlds (1898), established central themes: space travel, time travel, and encounters with alien life.

The pulp era and Golden Age

Hugo Gernsback founded Amazing Stories in 1926 and coined the term "science fiction." Under editor John W. Campbell, Astounding launched the so-called Golden Age (c. 1938–1946), featuring Isaac Asimov, Robert A. Heinlein, and Arthur C. Clarke.

New Wave and beyond

The 1960s New Wave, associated with Michael Moorcock's New Worlds and writers such as J. G. Ballard and Ursula K. Le Guin, brought literary experimentation and social themes. The 1980s saw the rise of cyberpunk, led by William Gibson's Neuromancer (1984). Science fiction has since become a dominant global popular genre across literature, film, and television.
