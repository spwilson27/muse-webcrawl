Zangezur corridor
LocationSyunik Province, southern Armenia
PurposeProposed transport link between mainland Azerbaijan and the Nakhchivan exclave
StatusDisputed proposal

The Zangezur corridor is a proposed transport route through Armenia's southern Syunik Province (historic Zangezur) that would connect mainland Azerbaijan with its exclave of Nakhchivan, and onward to Turkey. The concept rose to prominence after the 2020 Nagorno-Karabakh war and the Russian-brokered ceasefire agreement of 9 November 2020, which provided for the unblocking of regional transport communications.

Background and dispute

Azerbaijan has promoted the corridor as an "extraterritorial" route over which Armenia would exercise no customs or border control. Armenia rejects any extraterritorial arrangement as a violation of its sovereignty, but has offered to open transport links under full Armenian jurisdiction as part of its "Crossroads of Peace" initiative. Iran, which borders Syunik, has repeatedly stated that changes to its border with Armenia are a red line.

Significance

A functioning route would form part of a wider east–west "Middle Corridor" linking Central Asia and Turkey while bypassing Russia and Iran. Negotiations over its status have been a central issue in the Armenia–Azerbaijan peace process.
