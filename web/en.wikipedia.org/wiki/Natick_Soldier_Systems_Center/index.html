The Natick Soldier Systems Center (NSSC) is a United States Army installation in Natick, Massachusetts, that serves as the Army's principal centre for the research, development, and engineering of soldier systems — everything a soldier wears, carries, consumes, and uses for shelter. The campus sits on approximately 78 acres along the shore of Lake Cochituate.

History

The centre traces its origins to 1949, when the Army established the Quartermaster Research Facility at Natick to study environmental protection for soldiers. Construction of the main complex was completed in the 1950s, and the site grew into the Natick Research and Development Center. It was later known as the U.S. Army Soldier Systems Command and the Natick Soldier Center before receiving its current name. A notable early facility was the Climatic Research Laboratory, which could simulate environments from tropical heat to arctic cold for testing clothing and equipment.

Mission and work

Research at Natick covers individual equipment (helmets, body armour, and load-bearing gear), combat rations, aerial delivery and parachute systems, expeditionary shelter, and textile science. Its best-known product line is the Meal, Ready-to-Eat (MRE), developed at Natick to replace earlier canned field rations. The centre's food scientists have also contributed technologies that reached civilian markets, including freeze-drying techniques and high-energy snack bars. Natick researchers developed the energy bar and worked on the irradiation preservation of food, and the installation has supported the development of improved combat helmets and chemical-protective clothing.

Facilities

  • Doriot Climatic Chambers — large environmental chambers capable of simulating temperatures from −57 °C to 74 °C (−70 °F to 165 °F), wind, rain, and snow for human and equipment testing.
  • Combat feeding facilities — test kitchens and sensory evaluation laboratories for ration development.
  • Aerial delivery laboratories — for parachute and cargo-drop system testing.
