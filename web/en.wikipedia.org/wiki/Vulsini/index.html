Vulsini

Vulsini, also known as the Volsini volcanic district, is a volcanic complex in northern Lazio, Italy, centred on Lake Bolsena, the largest volcanic lake in Europe. Activity in the district began roughly 600,000 years ago and produced one of the largest caldera systems of the Italian peninsula.

Geology

The Vulsini complex belongs to the Roman magmatic province, characterised by potassium-rich magmas. Its evolution included ignimbrite eruptions, the formation of the Bolsena caldera, and later hydromagmatic activity where magma interacted with groundwater. The Latera caldera, on the western side of the district, was the site of the most recent major eruptive phase, ending roughly 160,000 years ago.

Legacy

Volcanic deposits such as tuff and peperino have been quarried as building stone since Etruscan and Roman times; the Etruscan city of Velzna (near modern Orvieto) stood on a plateau of Vulsini tuff. The district's geothermal resources have also been explored for power generation.
