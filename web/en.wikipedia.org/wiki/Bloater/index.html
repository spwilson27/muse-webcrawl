Bloater

Bloater may refer to several things. The best-known uses are a type of cured herring and a freshwater fish of the Great Lakes. This article covers the principal meanings.

FoodWhole smoked herring, lightly cured and ungutted

Bloater (food)

A bloater is a whole herring that is salted and lightly smoked without being gutted, giving it a fuller, slightly gamey flavour compared with a kipper, which is split, gutted and cold-smoked. The fish swells or "bloats" during the process, hence the name. Bloaters were famously associated with Great Yarmouth in Norfolk, and also produced at other east-coast ports. They are traditionally grilled or fried and eaten whole. Yarmouth bloaters appear in literature, including a mention in W. W. Jacobs' story collections about fictional Watchman of Wapping.

Bloater (fish)

The bloater (Coregonus hoyi) is a freshwater whitefish of the Great Lakes of North America, named for the swollen appearance of specimens brought up from deep water, whose swim bladders expand. Once one of the most important commercial fishes of the lakes as part of the "chub" fishery, its populations collapsed in the mid-twentieth century through overfishing and predation by invasive sea lampreys and alewives; it has been the subject of reintroduction efforts in Lake Ontario.
