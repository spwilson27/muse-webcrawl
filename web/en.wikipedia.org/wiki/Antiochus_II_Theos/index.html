Antiochus II Theos

Antiochus II Theos (Greek: Ἀντίοχος Β΄ ὁ Θεός, "the God"; 286 – July 246 BC) was a king of the Hellenistic Seleucid Empire who reigned from 261 BC until his death. He was the son and successor of Antiochus I Soter.

Reign: 261 – 246 BC
Predecessor: Antiochus I Soter
Successor: Seleucus II Callinicus
Born: 286 BC
Died: July 246 BC, Ephesus

Reign

Antiochus II fought the Second Syrian War (260–253 BC) against Ptolemy II of Egypt, recovering much of the coast of Asia Minor and the Levant. To seal the peace in 253 BC he married Ptolemy's daughter Berenice, setting aside his first wife Laodice and her children — a decision that proved disastrous. He is credited in Babylonian records with restoring temples, and the cities of Asia Minor gave him the epithet Theos after he freed Miletus from its tyrant.

Death and succession crisis

Antiochus died at Ephesus in 246 BC, possibly poisoned, after returning to Laodice. The rival claims of Laodice's son Seleucus II and Berenice's infant son triggered the Laodicean (Third Syrian) War with Egypt, plunging the empire into conflict.
