Germania Superior ("Upper Germania") was an imperial province of the Roman Empire on the upper Rhine, comprising territory in modern-day western Switzerland, the French Jura and Alsace, and southwestern Germany. Its capital was Mogontiacum (modern Mainz). It was the more southerly of the two militarised frontier provinces of Roman Germany, the other being Germania Inferior on the lower Rhine.

History

The area was conquered during Augustus's campaigns and long administered as military districts attached to Gallia Belgica. After campaigns beyond the Rhine under Domitian, including the annexation of the Agri Decumates between the Rhine and Danube, the district was raised to a full province around AD 90. The province was defended by legions stationed at Mogontiacum, Argentorate (Strasbourg) and other fortresses, and its eastern frontier was protected by the Limes Germanicus.

From the mid-third century, pressure from the Alamanni led to the abandonment of the Agri Decumates (c. 260) and the frontier fell back to the Rhine. Under Diocletian's reforms the province was reorganised as Germania Prima in the Diocese of Gaul.

Geography

Germania Superior included important towns such as Argentorate, Aquae (Baden-Baden), Augusta Raurica near modern Basel and Noviomagus (Speyer). The province guarded the Rhine crossing points and the routes over the Vosges and into the Black Forest region.
