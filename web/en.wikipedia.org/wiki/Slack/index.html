Slack is a cloud-based team-messaging and collaboration platform developed by Slack Technologies, now part of Salesforce. It organises workplace communication into channels, supports direct messages, file sharing, voice and video "huddles", and integrations with thousands of third-party applications.

History

Slack originated as an internal tool at Stewart Butterfield's company Tiny Speck during development of the online game Glitch. After Glitch shut down in 2012, the tool was launched publicly in August 2013 and grew explosively, becoming one of the fastest-growing business applications ever. The name is said to be an acronym for "Searchable Log of All Conversation and Knowledge". Slack Technologies listed on the New York Stock Exchange via a direct listing in 2019 under the ticker WORK.

Acquisition by Salesforce

In December 2020 Salesforce, led by Marc Benioff, announced it would acquire Slack for approximately US$27.7 billion, its largest acquisition ever; the deal closed in July 2021 and Slack became a core part of Salesforce's Customer 360 platform. Slack competes principally with Microsoft Teams, whose bundling with Office 365 prompted Slack to file an EU antitrust complaint in 2020.

Features

Workspaces are divided into public and private channels, with threaded replies, emoji reactions, search across message history, workflow automation, and AI features added in the 2020s. A free tier with limited message history is offered alongside paid plans.
