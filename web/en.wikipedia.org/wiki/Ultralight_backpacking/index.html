Ultralight backpacking

Ultralight backpacking is a style of wilderness travel that emphasizes carrying the lightest and simplest gear possible, enabling hikers to travel farther and faster with less strain. Enthusiasts commonly aim for a "base weight" — everything except food, water and fuel — of under 10 pounds (4.5 kg).

Philosophy and techniques

The approach popularized by Ray Jardine's 1992 book PCT Hiker's Handbook involves weighing every item, choosing multi-use gear, substituting lightweight materials such as Dyneema composite fabrics and titanium, and often making gear at home. Typical techniques include using a tarp or single-wall shelter instead of a tent, a frameless pack, a quilt instead of a sleeping bag, and trail-running shoes instead of boots.

Criticism

Critics note that ultralight choices can reduce safety margins and durability if taken to extremes, and experienced practitioners stress that skills and judgment matter more than equipment weight alone.
