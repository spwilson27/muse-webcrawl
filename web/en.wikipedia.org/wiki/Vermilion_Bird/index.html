Vermilion Bird

The Vermilion Bird (Chinese: 朱雀; pinyin: Zhūquè) is one of the Four Symbols of the Chinese constellations. According to Wu Xing, the Taoist five-element system, it represents the fire element, the direction south, and the season of summer. In Chinese astronomy, the Vermilion Bird presides over the southern quadrant of the sky, which is divided into seven mansions (xiu) forming its body: Well, Ghosts, Willow, Star, Extended Net, Wings and Chariot Crossbar.

Names and counterparts

The creature is known as Zhujak (주작) in Korean, Suzaku in Japanese, and Chu Tước in Vietnamese; as Suzaku it gives its name to one of the guardian deities of Kyoto and appears widely in Japanese popular culture. The Vermilion Bird is often depicted as a majestic bird resembling a golden pheasant or peacock with flame-coloured plumage. It should not be confused with the fenghuang, the "Chinese phoenix", although the two are sometimes conflated in modern media.

The Four Symbols

Together with the Azure Dragon of the east, the White Tiger of the west and the Black Tortoise of the north (with the Yellow Dragon or Qilin at the centre in some schemes), the Vermilion Bird is a cornerstone of Chinese cosmology, feng shui and funerary art: the four symbols were painted on the walls of tombs from the Han dynasty onward to guard the corresponding directions.
