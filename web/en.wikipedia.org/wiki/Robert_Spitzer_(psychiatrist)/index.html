Robert Spitzer (psychiatrist)


Born: May 22, 1932, White Plains, New York, U.S.
Died: December 25, 2015, Seattle, Washington
Occupation: Psychiatrist
Known for: DSM-III; removing homosexuality from the DSM

Robert Leopold Spitzer (May 22, 1932 – December 25, 2015) was an American psychiatrist and professor at Columbia University who played a central role in modern psychiatric classification.

Work

In 1973, Spitzer brokered the compromise by which the American Psychiatric Association removed homosexuality from its list of mental disorders in the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual (DSM-II), a landmark in gay civil rights. He then chaired the task force that produced the DSM-III (1980), which revolutionised psychiatry by replacing psychoanalytic, theory-based descriptions with explicit, criterion-based diagnoses — reshaping research, insurance and clinical practice worldwide.

Spitzer developed widely used diagnostic interview tools and later, in retirement, publicly apologised for his 2003 study purporting to show that some gay people could change their sexual orientation through therapy; he repudiated the paper in 2012, calling the apology the most significant professional act of his career alongside the 1973 decision.
