Storting

The Storting (Norwegian: Stortinget, lit. "the great thing" or "the great assembly") is the supreme legislature of Norway, located in Oslo. The unicameral parliament has 169 members and is elected every four years by the Norwegian people through a system of proportional representation in multi-seat constituencies.

History

The Storting was established by the Constitution of Norway in 1814. From 1884, parliamentarism has been a feature of Norwegian politics: the government is responsible to the Storting and can be dismissed by a vote of no confidence. Until 2009 the Storting divided itself into two chambers, the Odelsting and the Lagting, for legislative purposes; this division was abolished and the Storting has since functioned as a fully unicameral body.

Presidium and committees

The Storting is led by a presidium headed by the President of the Storting, assisted by five vice-presidents. Much of the parliament's detailed work is conducted in standing committees covering areas such as finance, foreign affairs and defence, justice, and education. Elections to the Storting are held every four years on a fixed date in September, and members may not be recalled during their term.
