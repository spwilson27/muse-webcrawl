Kingdom of Galicia and Lodomeria

The Kingdom of Galicia and Lodomeria (Polish: Królestwo Galicji i Lodomerii; Ukrainian: Королівство Галичини і Володимирії) was a crown land of the Habsburg monarchy, from 1804 of the Austrian Empire and from 1867 of Cisleithanian Austria-Hungary, existing from 1772 to 1918. Its capital was Lemberg (Polish: Lwów; Ukrainian: Lviv).

History

The kingdom was created from territory annexed by Austria in the First Partition of Poland in 1772. Its name revived the medieval principalities of Halych (Galicia) and Volodymyr (Lodomeria) to justify the Habsburg claim. The territory was enlarged in the Third Partition (1795, "New Galicia") and with the addition of Kraków in 1846 after the Kraków uprising. Bukovina was administered with Galicia before becoming a separate crown land.

Society

Galicia was one of the empire's poorest and most populous crown lands, inhabited by Poles, Ukrainians (Ruthenians), Jews, Germans and others. After the Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867 it enjoyed autonomy dominated by the Polish aristocracy, with Polish as the language of administration and education. Severe rural poverty drove mass emigration to the Americas. The province's oil fields around Borysław were among the world's earliest centres of petroleum production.

Dissolution

The kingdom collapsed at the end of the First World War in 1918; its territory was contested between Poland and the West Ukrainian People's Republic and ultimately divided between Poland, and later the Soviet Union and Ukraine.
