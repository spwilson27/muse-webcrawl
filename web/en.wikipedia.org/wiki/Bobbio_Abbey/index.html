Bobbio Abbey (Italian: Abbazia di San Colombano) is a monastery founded by the Irish missionary Saint Columbanus in 614 in the Apennines near the Trebbia river in northern Italy. It became one of the great centres of learning of early medieval Europe.

History

Columbanus received the site from the Lombard king Agilulf and his queen Theodelinda. After his death in 615 he was buried in the abbey church. Bobbio's scriptorium produced illuminated manuscripts in Insular-influenced styles, and its library — catalogued in the 10th century at roughly 700 volumes — was one of the largest in the Christian world, preserving classical and patristic texts, including palimpsests of Cicero and works associated with the Muratorian fragment.

Legacy

Manuscripts from Bobbio are now scattered among libraries in Milan, Turin, the Vatican and elsewhere. The town of Bobbio grew up around the abbey, and the tomb of Columbanus remains a place of pilgrimage.
