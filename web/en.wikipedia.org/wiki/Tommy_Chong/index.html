Thomas B. Kin Chong (born May 24, 1938) is a Canadian comedian, actor, musician and cannabis rights activist. He is best known as one half of the comedy duo Cheech & Chong, alongside Cheech Marin, whose cannabis-themed comedy albums and films, beginning with Up in Smoke (1978), made them icons of 1970s counterculture.

Early life

Chong was born in Edmonton, Alberta, to a Chinese immigrant father and a mother of Scots-Irish and French descent. He grew up in Calgary, where he began playing guitar in R&B bands as a teenager. In the 1960s he played with the band The Shades and later co-founded the soul group Bobby Taylor & the Vancouvers, which signed to Motown's Gordy label and had a hit with "Does Your Mama Know About Me" in 1968.

Career

After meeting Cheech Marin in Vancouver, where the pair performed improvisational comedy, they moved to Los Angeles and developed their act. Their comedy albums won wide popularity, and Los Cochinos (1973) won the Grammy Award for Best Comedy Album. Their films include Up in Smoke, Cheech and Chong's Next Movie and Nice Dreams. Chong later played the recurring character Leo on the television sitcom That '70s Show.

Legal issues and activism

In 2003 Chong was prosecuted in the United States in connection with his son's company, which sold glass pipes; he pleaded guilty and served nine months in federal prison. He has since been a prominent advocate for cannabis legalisation and founded a cannabis products brand.
