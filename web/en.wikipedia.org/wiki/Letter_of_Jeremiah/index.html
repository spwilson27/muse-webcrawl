Letter of Jeremiah

TypeDeuterocanonical text
LanguageGreek (probably from a Hebrew/Aramaic original)
Datec. 300 BC (debated)
PlacementChapter 6 of Baruch (Vulgate); separate book in some canons
ThemePolemic against idolatry

The Letter of Jeremiah, also known as the Epistle of Jeremiah, is a deuterocanonical text of the Old Testament, presented as a letter sent by the prophet Jeremiah to the Jews being taken captive to Babylon. It is a vigorous sermon against idolatry rather than a true letter, consisting of a single chapter of 72 or 73 verses.

Content

The text warns the exiles not to be seduced by Babylonian religion, describing at length — and with deliberate ridicule — how idols of wood, silver and gold are made by craftsmen, carried on shoulders, dressed, guarded against thieves and dusted by priests, yet can neither speak, see, save themselves nor reward their worshippers. The refrain that the idols are "not gods" and are not to be feared recurs throughout.

Canonicity and textual history

In the Septuagint the Letter stands as a separate work following Lamentations; in the Latin Vulgate it was appended as chapter 6 of the Book of Baruch, and it appears in Catholic and Orthodox Bibles as part of Baruch, while Protestant traditions class it among the Apocrypha. The Greek text is generally thought to translate a lost Semitic original, and scholars date its composition to the Hellenistic period, perhaps around 300 BC, drawing on Jeremiah 10 and earlier anti-idol polemic such as Isaiah 44.
