National parks of China

Zhangjiajie, one of China's earliest designated scenic areas

The national parks of China form a protected-area system that was formally launched in 2021, when the People's Republic of China officially established its first group of national parks. The system complements the older categories of nature reserves, scenic areas, forest parks and geoparks, which had together covered a large but fragmented network of protected sites.

History

China designated its first national scenic areas in 1982, including sites such as Zhangjiajie, Mount Huang (Huangshan), and the Jiuzhaigou valley. In 2015 the government announced a pilot programme to build a unified national park system, and ten pilot parks were trialled, including Sanjiangyuan (the source of the Yangtze, Yellow and Lancang rivers) on the Qinghai–Tibet Plateau and the Giant Panda habitat parks spanning Sichuan, Shaanxi and Gansu.

First national parks (2021)

  • Sanjiangyuan National Park (Qinghai)
  • Giant Panda National Park (Sichuan, Shaanxi, Gansu)
  • Northeast China Tiger and Leopard National Park (Jilin, Heilongjiang)
  • Hainan Tropical Rainforest National Park
  • Wuyishan National Park (Fujian, Jiangxi)

Together these five parks protect about 230,000 square kilometres. A spatial layout plan published in 2022 envisages around 49 national parks in total, which would make China's system the largest national park system in the world by area.
