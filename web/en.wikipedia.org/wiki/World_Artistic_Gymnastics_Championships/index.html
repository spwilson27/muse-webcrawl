The World Artistic Gymnastics Championships are the world championships for artistic gymnastics governed by the Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique (FIG). They were first held in 1903 in Antwerp, Belgium, initially as a men's-only competition held every two years. Women's events were added in 1934. The championships were held every four years from the 1950s through the 1980s, and have been held annually since the early 1990s, except in Olympic years during some cycles.

Events

Men compete on six apparatus: floor exercise, pommel horse, still rings, vault, parallel bars and horizontal bar. Women compete on four: vault, uneven bars, balance beam and floor exercise. The competition includes team all-around, individual all-around and apparatus finals. In the year before the Olympic Games, the championships serve as a major qualification route for the Olympic team competition.

History

The early championships were dominated by European gymnasts, and for many years only a small number of nations participated. After the Second World War, the Soviet Union became a dominant force in both men's and women's competition, and Japan emerged as the leading men's nation in the 1960s and 1970s. Since the 1990s, China and the United States have been the most successful nations, with Romania also prominent in women's gymnastics. Japan's Kōhei Uchimura won a record six consecutive men's all-around titles between 2009 and 2015, and Simone Biles of the United States became the most decorated gymnast in championship history, surpassing the overall medal record of Vitaly Scherbo.

Editions

Recent editions have been held in Montreal (2017), Doha (2018), Stuttgart (2019), Kitakyushu (2021), Liverpool (2022) and Antwerp (2023), the latter marking a return to the city that hosted the first championships 120 years earlier.
