William Wyler

William Wyler (born Willi Wyler; July 1, 1902 – July 27, 1981) was a German-born American film director and producer. He is regarded as one of the most accomplished directors of the Golden Age of Hollywood, winning three Academy Awards for Best Director.

William Wyler (born Willi Wyler; July 1, 1902 – July 27, 1981) was a German-born American film director and producer. He won the Academy Award for Best Director three times: for Mrs. Miniver (1942), The Best Years of Our Lives (1946), and Ben-Hur (1959). His 12 Best Director Oscar nominations remain a record, and his films earned 36 Academy Award acting nominations for his performers.

Early life and career

Wyler was born in Mülhausen, Alsace (then German Empire, now Mulhouse, France), to a Swiss father and German mother. A distant cousin of Universal Pictures founder Carl Laemmle, he emigrated to the United States in 1920 and worked his way up at Universal from errand boy to director of two-reel Westerns by 1925.

Major films

Moving to major features, Wyler directed Dodsworth (1936), Jezebel (1938), Wuthering Heights (1939), The Letter (1940), The Little Foxes (1941) and the wartime morale drama Mrs. Miniver (1942). During the Second World War he served in the U.S. Army Air Forces and filmed the documentaries The Memphis Belle (1944) and Thunderbolt (1947) in combat zones, losing most of his hearing in one ear.

The Best Years of Our Lives (1946), about returning veterans, won seven Academy Awards. Later successes included The Heiress (1949), Roman Holiday (1953), Friendly Persuasion (1956), Ben-Hur (1959) — which won a record 11 Oscars — The Collector (1965), and Funny Girl (1968). Renowned as a perfectionist who demanded many takes, he was celebrated for deep-focus staging developed with cinematographer Gregg Toland.

Death

Wyler died of a heart attack in Los Angeles on 27 July 1981. He received the AFI Life Achievement Award in 1976.
