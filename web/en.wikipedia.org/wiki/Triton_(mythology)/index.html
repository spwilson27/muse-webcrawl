Triton (mythology)

Triton (Greek: Τρίτων) is a Greek god of the sea, the son and herald of Poseidon and Amphitrite. He is usually depicted as a merman — with the upper body of a human and the tail of a fish — blowing a twisted conch shell like a trumpet, with which he could raise or calm the waves.

Mythology

According to Hesiod's Theogony, Triton dwelt with his parents in a golden palace beneath the sea. In later myth he was associated with Lake Tritonis in Libya, where he aided the Argonauts when their ship was stranded, guiding them back to the Mediterranean. Virgil's Aeneid tells how Triton drowned the trumpeter Misenus, who had challenged the gods to a musical contest. Over time "tritons" became a whole class of lesser sea beings, companions of the Nereids and a common motif in Hellenistic and Roman art, fountains, and heraldry.

Legacy

Triton gives his name to Neptune's largest moon, to a genus of sea snails whose shells were used as trumpets, and to the largest genus of newts in older classifications. The interval of the tritone in music is unrelated etymologically, deriving from Greek words for "three tones".
