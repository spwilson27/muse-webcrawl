Ezra Cornell

Ezra Cornell (January 11, 1807 – December 9, 1874) was an American businessman, politician and philanthropist, a founder of Western Union and co-founder, with Andrew Dickson White, of Cornell University in Ithaca, New York.

Ezra Cornell
Born: 1807, Westchester County, New York
Died: 1874, Ithaca, New York
Known for: Western Union, Cornell University

Life and career

A carpenter turned salesman of plows, Cornell partnered with Samuel Morse to build telegraph lines, developed a plow for burying cable, and amassed a fortune consolidating telegraph companies into Western Union. He served in the New York State Assembly and Senate.

Cornell University

Using New York's Morrill Land-Grant funds, Cornell endowed the university chartered in 1865 with $500,000 and land, under the motto of instruction in "any study". He profited the endowment further by skilful sale of western land scrip.
