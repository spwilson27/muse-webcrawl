Dysgerminoma

A dysgerminoma is a type of germ cell tumour of the ovary. It is the most common malignant ovarian germ cell tumour, accounting for roughly 1–2% of all ovarian malignancies, and corresponds to the seminoma of the testis. Dysgerminomas occur most often in adolescents and young women, and a notable proportion arise in abnormal gonads, for example in women with gonadal dysgenesis.

Presentation

Patients commonly present with abdominal pain, a pelvic mass, or abdominal distension. The tumours are usually unilateral and can grow large. Blood tests may show elevated lactate dehydrogenase (LDH); a minority produce hCG or AFP, the latter suggesting mixed germ cell elements.

Pathology

On microscopy, dysgerminoma consists of sheets of large, uniform polygonal cells with clear cytoplasm and central nuclei, separated by fibrous septa infiltrated by lymphocytes. This appearance mirrors seminoma closely. Immunohistochemistry typically shows positivity for markers such as OCT3/4 and PLAP.

Treatment and prognosis

Dysgerminoma is highly sensitive to chemotherapy and radiotherapy. Standard management is fertility-sparing surgery (unilateral salpingo-oophorectomy) with staging, followed by platinum-based chemotherapy (commonly BEP: bleomycin, etoposide, cisplatin) for advanced disease. Prognosis is excellent, with survival rates above 90% even in many cases of advanced-stage disease.
