The Arno is a river in the Tuscany region of Italy. At 241 kilometres it is the most important river of central Italy after the Tiber. It flows through Florence and Pisa before emptying into the Ligurian Sea near Marina di Pisa.

Course

The river rises on Monte Falterona in the Casentino area of the Apennines, flows south through the Casentino valley, then turns west past Arezzo and through the Valdarno. It passes beneath the bridges of Florence, including the Ponte Vecchio, continues through Empoli and Pontedera, crosses Pisa, and reaches the sea.

Floods

The Arno is notorious for flooding. The flood of 4 November 1966 devastated Florence, damaging priceless art and manuscripts, and earlier catastrophic floods struck in 1333 and 1844. Flood-control works and upstream dams have since reduced, but not eliminated, the risk.
