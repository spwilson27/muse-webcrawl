The nth taxicab number, denoted Ta(n), is defined as the smallest integer that can be expressed as a sum of two positive cubes in n distinct ways (up to the order of the summands). The concept is famous because of an anecdote about G. H. Hardy and Srinivasa Ramanujan.

The 1729 anecdote

Hardy recalled visiting Ramanujan in hospital at Putney:

"I remember once going to see him when he was ill at Putney. I had ridden in taxi cab number 1729 and remarked that the number seemed to me rather a dull one, and that I hoped it was not an unfavourable omen. 'No,' he replied, 'it is a very interesting number; it is the smallest number expressible as the sum of two cubes in two different ways.'"

Indeed, 1729 = 1³ + 12³ = 9³ + 10³. Because of the story, 1729 is also called the Hardy–Ramanujan number.

Known values

Ta(1) = 2 and Ta(2) = 1729. Ta(3) = 87,539,319 = 167³ + 436³ = 228³ + 423³ = 255³ + 414³ was found by John Leech in 1957. Ta(4), Ta(5) and Ta(6) were found by computer search in the late twentieth and early twenty-first centuries; Ta(6) = 24,153,319,581,254,312,065,344 was confirmed by Uwe Hollerbach in 2008. Upper bounds are known for Ta(7) through Ta(12), but the exact values remain open, and it is conjectured that taxicab numbers exist for every n.

Variations

Allowing negative cubes gives the "cabtaxi numbers"; the smallest number expressible as a sum of two cubes in three ways with signs permitted is 15170835645. Fermat's Last Theorem implies no cube is a sum of two positive cubes in even one way, so the two-cube sums here involve distinct bases both strictly smaller than the cube root of the total.
