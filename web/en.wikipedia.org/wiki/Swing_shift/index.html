Swing shift

The swing shift is the work shift that runs between the day shift and the night (graveyard) shift — typically from mid-afternoon to around midnight, such as 4 p.m. to 12 a.m. It is a standard component of three-shift systems used in manufacturing, healthcare, emergency services, hospitality and other round-the-clock operations.

Characteristics

Swing shift hours suit workers who prefer to sleep late and keep mornings free, and the shift often carries a pay differential. The term became widespread in American industry during the Second World War, when war production plants ran three shifts around the clock and the middle shift "swung" between day and night crews. Musicians of the era made the phrase famous through songs about wartime workers, most famously "On the Swing Shift" from the 1942 film Star Spangled Rhythm.

Health and social effects

Compared with the graveyard shift, the swing shift is less disruptive to circadian rhythms, but it isolates workers from family and social life, which is concentrated in the evenings. Shift-work research groups the swing shift with other irregular schedules studied for effects on sleep, metabolism and accident risk.
