Tottenham Court Road

Tottenham Court Road is a major road in central London, running from St Giles Circus in the south (near Oxford Street) northwards to Euston Road. It forms the boundary between the City of Westminster to the west and the London Borough of Camden to the east for much of its length, passing through the district of Fitzrovia.

History

The road takes its name from Tottenham Court, a manor house that stood near its northern end; the manor is mentioned in the Domesday Book as belonging to the Dean and Chapter of St Paul's Cathedral. The area developed in the 18th century, and the road became known for furniture shops — Heal's, founded in 1810, is a famous survivor — and later for electronics and computer retailers concentrated around its southern end.

Transport

Tottenham Court Road station, at the junction with Oxford Street, is served by the Central and Northern lines and, since 2022, the Elizabeth line, whose new station entrance transformed St Giles Circus. Goodge Street and Warren Street stations also serve the road. The southern end was converted from one-way working back to two-way traffic as part of the West End Project in the early 2020s.

Landmarks

Landmarks include the Dominion Theatre, the American International Church, the former Tower Records building, and the BT Tower visible nearby in Fitzrovia. The road was also the site of the 1980s "Tottenham Court Road" fairground of computer shops that defined early British home computing retail.
