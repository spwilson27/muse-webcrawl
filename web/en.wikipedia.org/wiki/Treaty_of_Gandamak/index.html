The Treaty of Gandamak was signed on 26 May 1879 between the British government of India and Mohammad Yaqub Khan, Emir of Afghanistan, bringing the first phase of the Second Anglo-Afghan War to a close. It was negotiated for the British by Sir Louis Cavagnari.

Terms

Under the treaty the Amir accepted a permanent British resident mission in Kabul, surrendered control of Afghanistan's foreign relations to Britain, and ceded frontier districts including the Khyber and Michni passes and the territories of Kurram, Pishin and Sibi. In return Britain guaranteed the Amir against foreign aggression and paid him an annual subsidy of six lakh rupees.

Aftermath

The settlement collapsed within months. Cavagnari and his mission were massacred in Kabul on 3 September 1879, reopening the war; British forces under General Roberts reoccupied Kabul, Yaqub Khan abdicated and was exiled to India. His cousin Abdur Rahman Khan was installed as emir in 1880, accepting the treaty's external settlement — British control of Afghan foreign policy — without a resident mission in Kabul, an arrangement that lasted until 1919.
