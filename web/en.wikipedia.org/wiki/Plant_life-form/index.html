Plant life-form

Plant life-form schemes classify plants by their overall growth form and, especially, by the position of their perennating (survival) buds relative to the ground during the unfavourable season. The best-known scheme is the Raunkiær system, developed by the Danish botanist Christen C. Raunkiær in the early 20th century.

Raunkiær categories

The principal categories are: phanerophytes, trees and shrubs whose buds are held well above ground on aerial shoots; chamaephytes, with buds close to the ground (below about 25 cm); hemicryptophytes, with buds at the soil surface; cryptophytes (including geophytes), with buds buried underground or underwater; and therophytes, annuals that survive the bad season only as seeds. Raunkiær also recognised epiphytes and succulents in some versions of the system.

Use

The proportions of life-forms in a flora — its biological spectrum — reflect climate: phanerophytes dominate in warm humid tropics, hemicryptophytes in cool temperate regions, and therophytes in deserts. Comparisons of spectra remain a tool in biogeography and vegetation science.
