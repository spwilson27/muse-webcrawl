Cause marketing

Cause marketing is marketing done by a for-profit business that seeks both to increase profits and to better society in accordance with corporate social responsibility, such as by including activist messages in advertising. A common form is cause-related marketing, in which a company promises to donate a portion of revenue from the sale of a product to a charitable cause.

History

One of the first large-scale cause-related marketing campaigns was run by American Express in 1983, which donated one cent toward the restoration of the Statue of Liberty for each use of its card, raising about $1.7 million and increasing card usage substantially. The practice expanded widely during the 1990s and 2000s, with prominent examples including Product Red, launched in 2006 to support the Global Fund to fight AIDS, tuberculosis and malaria.

Forms

Cause marketing can take the form of transaction-based promotions, point-of-sale donations, licensing of a charity's logo, employee volunteering campaigns, and digital advocacy campaigns. It is distinguished from ordinary corporate philanthropy because the donation is tied to consumer activity and is promoted as part of the marketing message.

Criticism

Critics raise concerns about transparency (consumers may not know how much is actually donated), "pinkwashing" and other forms of cause-washing, and campaigns in which the donation is capped at a small maximum while advertising spending dwarfs the charitable contribution.
