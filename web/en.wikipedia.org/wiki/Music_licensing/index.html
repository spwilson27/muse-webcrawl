Music licensing

Music licensing is the licensed use of copyrighted music. It ensures that owners of copyrights on musical works are compensated for uses of their work. A music licence is normally negotiated between the copyright holder (such as a music publisher or record label) and the person or organisation wishing to use the work. In most jurisdictions, a musical recording embodies two distinct copyrights: the musical composition (song), administered by publishers, and the sound recording (master), administered by record labels.

Types of licence

Performance licensing

Public performances of compositions — on radio, television, in venues and via streaming — are licensed collectively in most countries by performing rights organisations (PROs) such as ASCAP, BMI and SESAC in the United States, which issue blanket licences and distribute royalties to songwriters and publishers.

Mechanical licensing

A mechanical licence permits the reproduction of a composition in audio recordings. In the United States, once a song has been commercially released, anyone may record a cover version under a compulsory mechanical licence at a statutory rate, administered in part by the Harry Fox Agency and, for streaming, the Mechanical Licensing Collective created by the Music Modernization Act of 2018.

Synchronisation licensing

A synchronisation ("sync") licence is required to place a composition or recording in timed relation with visual media such as film, television, advertising or video games; it must be negotiated separately for both the composition and the master recording.

Master licensing

A master licence (or master use licence) grants permission to use a specific sound recording, and is issued by the owner of the recording, usually a record label.

Broadcasting and public performance

Restaurants, shops, broadcasters and other businesses that play music publicly generally require licences from the relevant PROs. Blanket licensing has repeatedly been the subject of antitrust scrutiny, and in the US ASCAP and BMI operate under long-standing consent decrees.
