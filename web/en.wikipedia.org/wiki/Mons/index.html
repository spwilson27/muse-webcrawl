Mons (French: Mons; Dutch: Bergen; Picard: Mont) is a city and municipality of Wallonia, and the capital of the province of Hainaut, Belgium. It lies about 50 km southwest of Brussels, near the French border. The municipality has a population of around 95,000.

History

The town developed around a fortified settlement on a hill, associated in tradition with Saint Waltrude (Waudru), who founded a chapter of canonesses in the 7th century. Mons grew as an administrative centre of the County of Hainaut and was fortified repeatedly; its fortifications were dismantled in the 19th century and replaced by boulevards.

In August 1914, Mons was the site of the Battle of Mons, the first major action of the British Expeditionary Force in the First World War — a battle around which the legend of the "Angels of Mons" later formed. The city was occupied by Germany in both world wars; Canadian troops liberated it on 11 November 1918, and it was liberated again in September 1944.

Culture and landmarks

The collegiate church of Sainte-Waudru is a major Brabantine Gothic building housing relics of the city's patron saint. The Baroque belfry of Mons (1672), standing 87 m tall on the castle hill, is inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as part of the Belfries of Belgium and France. The annual Ducasse de Mons, known as the "Doudou", features a procession and a staged combat between Saint George and a dragon; it is recognised by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage.

Mons was a European Capital of Culture in 2015, jointly with Plzeň. The city hosts the University of Mons and several museums, including the Mons Memorial Museum and the Belfry museum. The headquarters of NATO's Supreme Headquarters Allied Powers Europe (SHAPE) is located at Casteau, just north of the city.
