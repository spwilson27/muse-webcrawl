William Gardner Pfann

Born: 27 October 1917, Brooklyn, New York
Died: 22 October 1982
Known for: Zone melting; zone refining
Employer: Bell Telephone Laboratories

William Gardner Pfann (October 27, 1917 – October 22, 1982) was an American materials scientist at Bell Telephone Laboratories who invented zone melting and zone refining, techniques fundamental to producing the ultra-pure semiconductor crystals on which the transistor and modern electronics depend.

Career

Pfann joined Bell Labs in 1935 as a chemical technician and worked his way up while studying at night, receiving a degree in chemical engineering from Cooper Union in 1944. Assigned to support transistor research, he confronted the problem that germanium and silicon had to be purified far beyond what existing methods allowed.

Zone refining

In 1951 Pfann devised zone melting: a narrow molten zone is passed repeatedly along a solid bar, sweeping impurities toward one end because they preferentially dissolve in the liquid. Repeating the passes — zone refining — reduced impurity levels to parts per billion. He also developed zone leveling for uniform doping. The method underpinned the first reliable transistors and remains a standard purification technique in materials science.

Recognition

Pfann held numerous patents and received many honours for work that made large-scale semiconductor manufacture practical.
