Laurasia

Laurasia was the northern of the two large landmasses that formed part of the Pangaea supercontinent from around 335 to 175 million years ago. It comprised the ancient cores of modern North America, Europe, and Asia (excluding the Indian subcontinent and Arabia). The name is a portmanteau of Laurentia (the craton of North America) and Eurasia.

Laurasia (north) and Gondwana (south) after the breakup of Pangaea.

Formation and breakup

Laurasia formed as Pangaea began to rift apart during the Jurassic period, separating from the southern supercontinent Gondwana as the Tethys Sea opened between them. Laurasia itself later split into North America and Eurasia as the North Atlantic Ocean opened, a process beginning in the Jurassic and continuing through the Cenozoic.

Biota

The shared Laurasian landmass explains many similarities between the fossil floras and faunas of North America, Europe, and Asia, including the distribution of early mammals and many dinosaur groups during the Mesozoic era.
