Extinct dog breeds

Extinct dog breeds are breeds, varieties and landraces of domestic dog that are no longer recognised as distinct populations, usually because the work they were bred for disappeared, because they were absorbed into other breeds, or because their populations collapsed. Unlike extinct wild species, a dog breed becomes extinct when breeding ceases and no purebred stock remains.

The turnspit dog, bred to run in a wheel turning roasting meat, vanished with mechanised kitchens.

Causes of extinction

Most dog breeds were created for specific tasks. When the task disappeared — through mechanisation, legal change (such as bans on bull-baiting or dog fighting), or shifting fashion — the breed often followed. Others were deliberately merged into newer breeds during the formation of kennel-club standards in the late 19th century, or were lost in the disruption of the two world wars.

Recreation

Some extinct breeds have been "recreated" by crossbreeding modern breeds to approximate the historical type — a process that yields a look-alike rather than a genetic continuation, as with various old-bulldog and ancient-war-dog recreations.
