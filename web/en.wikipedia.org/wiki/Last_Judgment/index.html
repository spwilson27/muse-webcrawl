Last Judgment

A medieval depiction of the Last Judgment: Christ in Majesty above, the saved rising to the left and the damned cast down to the right.

The Last Judgment, also called the Final Judgment or Day of Judgment, is the idea in Abrahamic religions that, at the end of time, God will pass sentence on every human being, assigning souls to their eternal fate in Heaven or Hell. It forms a central element of Christian eschatology, drawing on prophecies in Daniel, the Synoptic Gospels, and the Book of Revelation.

In Christianity

Christian teaching holds that after the Second Coming of Christ, the dead will be resurrected and judged according to their deeds (Matthew 25 describes the separation of the "sheep" from the "goats"). Roman Catholic theology distinguishes this general judgment from the particular judgment each soul undergoes at death. Christ is depicted as judge, often flanked by Mary and John the Baptist (the Deesis), with the Archangel Michael weighing souls.

In Islam

In Islam, Yawm al-Qiyāmah (the Day of Resurrection) is the day on which Allah judges all humanity; deeds are weighed in the balance (mizan), and each person crosses the bridge of Sirat to paradise or falls to hellfire.

In art

The Last Judgment is one of the most common subjects of medieval church decoration, carved on cathedral portals such as Autun and painted most famously by Michelangelo on the altar wall of the Sistine Chapel (1536–1541).
