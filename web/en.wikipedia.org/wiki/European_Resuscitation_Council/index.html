The European Resuscitation Council (ERC) is an interdisciplinary European organisation for resuscitation medicine and emergency medical care. Founded in 1989, it brings together national resuscitation councils from across Europe and publishes the European Resuscitation Guidelines, which set the standard for basic and advanced life support training and practice in most European countries. Its journal is Resuscitation.

Guidelines

The ERC updates its guidelines roughly every five years (2000, 2005, 2010, 2015, 2021), drawing on the evidence evaluations of the International Liaison Committee on Resuscitation (ILCOR), of which it is a founding member. The 2021 guidelines cover basic life support, advanced life support, paediatric and newborn life support, resuscitation in special circumstances, post-resuscitation care, first aid, and the ethics of resuscitation, with emphasis on the "chain of survival", systems that save lives, high-quality chest compressions, early defibrillation and recognition of conditions such as ventricular tachycardia and other shockable rhythms.

Activities

The ERC standardises courses including Basic Life Support/AED, Immediate Life Support, Advanced Life Support (ALS), European Paediatric Advanced Life Support and generic instructor courses, certifying providers and instructors through national councils. It coordinates the European Registry of Cardiac Arrest (EuReCa) studies, which compare out-of-hospital cardiac arrest incidence and outcomes, and organises the annual European Restart a Heart initiative (linked to World Restart a Heart Day, October 16), promoting bystander CPR training. The modern practice the ERC teaches descends from the work of pioneers such as Peter Safar, who combined chest compression with rescue breathing into CPR.
