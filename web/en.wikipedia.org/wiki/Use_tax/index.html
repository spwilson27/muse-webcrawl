Use tax

Many states include a use tax line on income tax returns

A use tax is a tax on the storage, use, or consumption of tangible personal property purchased without payment of the applicable sales tax — typically goods bought from an out-of-state vendor and brought into the taxing state. Levied by most US states that impose a sales tax, it is assessed at the same rate as the sales tax and serves to prevent residents from avoiding tax by purchasing in lower-tax jurisdictions and to protect in-state retailers from unfair competition.

Compliance

Individual compliance with use tax is historically low, since buyers must self-report purchases (for example, on out-of-state mail-order and online purchases) on their annual state tax returns. States have pursued greater collection through agreements with large remote sellers and reporting requirements. The US Supreme Court's decision in South Dakota v. Wayfair (2018), permitting states to require remote sellers to collect sales tax, reduced — but did not eliminate — reliance on consumer use tax.
