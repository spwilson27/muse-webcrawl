Sizdah Bedar

Sizdah Bedar سیزده‌بدر
Observed byIran and the Persian diaspora
Date13th day of Farvardin (usually 1 or 2 April)
SignificanceLast day of Nowruz festivities; "Nature Day" in Iran

Sizdah Bedar (Persian: سیزده‌بدر, Sizdahbedar or Sizdah be Dar), also known as Nature's Day (روز طبیعت, Ruz-e Tabi'at), is an Iranian festival held annually on the thirteenth day of Farvardin, the first month of the Iranian calendar, falling on 1 or 2 April. It is the final day of the Nowruz celebrations, traditionally spent entirely outdoors picnicking with family and friends, because staying at home on the thirteenth day is believed to bring bad luck.

Customs

Families carry the sabzeh — the sprouted wheat or lentils grown for the Haft-Seen table — to parks, plains, and riverbanks, and release it into running water, symbolically casting away sickness, misfortune, and negative energy accumulated during the New Year period. Young people knot blades of grass while making a wish, most often for a spouse in the coming year; undoing another's knots is said to grant the wish. Traditional foods include ash reshteh (noodle soup) and lettuce with sekanjebin (a sweet mint-vinegar syrup). Joking and playful falsehoods, called Dorugh-e Sizdah ("the lie of the thirteenth"), resemble April Fools' Day pranks.

Origins

Like Nowruz itself, the festival has roots in ancient Iranian and Zoroastrian tradition, honouring the divinity of rain, Tishtrya, and the renewal of creation in spring.
