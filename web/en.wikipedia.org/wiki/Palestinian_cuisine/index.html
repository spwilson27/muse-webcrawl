Palestinian cuisine

Palestinian cuisine consists of foods from or commonly eaten by Palestinians, in Palestine, Israel, Jordan, and the Palestinian diaspora. It is part of the wider Levantine cuisine, sharing much with Lebanese, Syrian, and Jordanian cooking, while retaining distinctive local dishes tied to regional ingredients—olive oil, olives, sage, sumac, thyme (za'atar), wheat, chickpeas, lentils, and seasonal vegetables.

Notable dishes

Musakhan, often considered the national dish, consists of roasted chicken with sumac, onions, pine nuts, and olive oil served over taboon flatbread. Maqluba ("upside down") is a layered rice dish with fried vegetables and meat, inverted onto a platter for serving. Other staples include musakhan variants, maftoul (large couscous-like pearls), shishbarak, and knafeh, the cheese-and-syrup dessert strongly associated with Nablus, which is also famous for its olive-oil soap and white brined cheese. In Gaza, cuisine features seafood, hot peppers, and dishes such as sumaghiyyeh, a sumac-flavoured stew, and dagga, a spicy tomato-herb salad.

Ingredients and traditions

Olive oil from Palestine's ancient groves is central to the cuisine, used for cooking and dressing alike. Meals are structured around seasonal produce, preserved foods such as dried okra and jameed (dried fermented yogurt), and communal platters eaten with bread. Coffee, tea with sage or mint, and hospitality rituals figure prominently in social life. Dishes vary between the Galilee, the West Bank hills and cities such as Jerusalem, Hebron, and Nablus, the coastal plain, and Gaza.
