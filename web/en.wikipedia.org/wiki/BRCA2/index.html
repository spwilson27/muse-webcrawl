BRCA2

BRCA2 (breast cancer type 2 susceptibility protein) is a protein that in humans is encoded by the BRCA2 gene, located on chromosome 13. BRCA2 is a tumour suppressor gene: its protein product is responsible for repairing damaged DNA, principally through the process of homologous recombination. Orthologs of BRCA2 are common in most mammals and many other organisms.

Function

The BRCA2 protein helps repair damaged DNA by regulating the activity of RAD51, a recombinase that binds single-stranded DNA at double-strand breaks and searches for a homologous template so the break can be repaired accurately. If the BRCA2 gene itself is damaged by mutation, broken DNA is not properly repaired, increasing the risk of cancer.

Clinical significance

Certain inherited variants of BRCA2, as with BRCA1, increase the risk for breast cancer and ovarian cancer, and have been associated with other cancers including prostate cancer, pancreatic cancer, and Fanconi anaemia. Women who inherit a harmful BRCA2 mutation face a substantially elevated lifetime risk of breast cancer, though risk varies between families and variants. Genetic testing, enhanced screening, risk-reducing surgery and PARP inhibitor therapies are all used in the management of carriers.
