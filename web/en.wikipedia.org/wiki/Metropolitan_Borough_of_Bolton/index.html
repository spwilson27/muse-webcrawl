The Metropolitan Borough of Bolton is a metropolitan borough of Greater Manchester in North West England, United Kingdom. It takes its name from Bolton, its main town, and also covers the towns of Farnworth, Horwich, Westhoughton and Blackrod, along with smaller villages and suburbs.

History

The borough was created on 1 April 1974 under the Local Government Act 1972, by merging the former county borough of Bolton with several urban districts from Lancashire, including Farnworth, Horwich, Westhoughton, Blackrod, Kearsley and Little Lever, plus part of Turton Urban District. It is governed by Bolton Council, a unitary-style metropolitan borough council providing services including education, housing, waste and planning.

Geography and population

The borough occupies roughly 140 km² on the edge of the West Pennine Moors, with moorland rising to the north and west. Its population is around 295,000, making it one of the larger boroughs of Greater Manchester. The borough is known for its textile heritage — Bolton was a major cotton mill town — and for Bolton Wanderers Football Club, based at the University of Bolton Stadium in Horwich.
