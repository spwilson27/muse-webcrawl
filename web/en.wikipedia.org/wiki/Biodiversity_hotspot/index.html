Biodiversity hotspot

A biodiversity hotspot is a biogeographic region with significant levels of biodiversity that is threatened by human habitation. The concept was developed by Norman Myers and Conservation International.

Biodiversity hotspots
Criteria: ≥1,500 endemic vascular plant species; ≥70% original habitat lost
Number recognised: 36
Key proponent: Conservation International

Criteria

To qualify, a region must have at least 1,500 endemic vascular plant species (irreplaceability) and have lost at least 70% of its original natural vegetation (vulnerability).

Distribution

The 36 hotspots cover about 2.5% of Earth's land surface but support more than half of the world's endemic plant species and a large share of endemic vertebrates. Examples include Madagascar and the Indian Ocean Islands, the Tropical Andes, Sundaland, the Mediterranean Basin, and the California Floristic Province.

Conservation

The hotspot framework guides funding by organisations such as the Critical Ecosystem Partnership Fund, prioritising places where exceptional concentrations of endemic species face exceptional loss.
