Curriculum for Excellence


The four capacities: successful learners, confident individuals, responsible citizens, effective contributors

Curriculum for Excellence (CfE) is the national curriculum of Scotland, used in state and independent schools for learners aged 3 to 18. It was developed following a national debate on education in 2002 and the 2004 report A Curriculum for Excellence, and was rolled out in schools from 2010, replacing the previous 5–14 guidelines and associated qualifications framework. It is overseen by Education Scotland and the Scottish Qualifications Authority (SQA).

Aims and structure

CfE aims to develop four capacities in every learner: successful learners, confident individuals, responsible citizens, and effective contributors. Learning is organised into eight curriculum areas — expressive arts, health and wellbeing, languages, mathematics, religious and moral education, sciences, social studies, and technologies — together with literacy, numeracy and health and wellbeing as responsibilities of all teachers.

The curriculum is structured in levels (Early, First, Second, Third and Fourth) within a Broad General Education up to the end of S3, followed by a Senior Phase (S4–S6) in which pupils take National 4, National 5, Higher and Advanced Higher qualifications, which replaced Standard Grades and Intermediates from 2013–14.

Assessment and criticism

Supporters credit CfE with encouraging interdisciplinary learning, skills development, and teacher professional judgement. It has also faced sustained criticism over the clarity of its guidance, assessment burden, subject choice narrowing in the senior phase, and Scotland's performance trends in international surveys; an OECD review in 2021 recommended reforming the qualification and curriculum agencies, prompting a programme of education reform in Scotland.
