Norfolk

[Image: Location of Norfolk in England]

Norfolk is a ceremonial county in the East of England and East Anglia. It borders Lincolnshire and the Wash to the north-west, the North Sea to the north and east, Cambridgeshire to the west and south-west, and Suffolk to the south. The county town is the city of Norwich. With an area of 2,074 square miles (5,370 km2) and a population of about 925,000, Norfolk is largely rural and low-lying.

Geography

The county includes the Norfolk Broads, a network of navigable rivers and lakes protected as part of the Broads national park, and a long North Sea coastline with resorts such as Cromer, Great Yarmouth, and Hunstanton. The Fens extend into the west of the county, and Thetford Forest lies in the south-west Breckland.

History

Norfolk formed part of the territory of the Iceni tribe, associated with the revolt of Boudica against Roman rule in AD 60–61. In the Anglo-Saxon period it belonged to the kingdom of East Anglia; the name derives from the "north folk" of that kingdom. Norwich became one of England's largest cities in the medieval period on the strength of the wool and weaving trades, later boosted by Flemish "Strangers". Kett's Rebellion of 1549 was centred in the county.

Economy and transport

Agriculture — especially arable farming, sugar beet, and poultry — remains important, alongside tourism, food processing, and energy from offshore North Sea gas fields and wind farms. The A11 and A47 link Norwich to Cambridge, London, and the Midlands; rail services run from Norwich and King's Lynn.

Settlements

  • King's Lynn
  • Great Yarmouth
  • Thetford, Dereham, Cromer, Wymondham
