Jeremy Bentham (1748–1832) was an English philosopher and legal reformer, the founder of modern Utilitarianism. Opening his Principles of Morals and Legislation (1789) with the claim that nature has placed mankind under two sovereign masters, pain and pleasure, he proposed that laws and actions be judged solely by their tendency to maximise happiness — 'the greatest happiness of the greatest number'.

Reforms and legacy

Bentham attacked the chaos of English common law, designed the Panopticon prison, and campaigned — far ahead of his time — for universal (male) suffrage, decriminalisation of homosexuality, animal welfare ('The question is not, Can they reason? ... but, Can they suffer?') and abolition of the death penalty for most offences. His disciple John Stuart Mill both spread and revised his philosophy. Bentham's preserved, clothed skeleton — the 'auto-icon' — is displayed at University College London, whose founding he inspired.
