The Mathematical Association of America (MAA) is a professional society of college and university teachers of mathematics, as well as high school teachers and graduate and undergraduate students. Founded in 1915, it is the largest community of mathematicians, students and enthusiasts oriented toward accessible, undergraduate-level mathematics. Its headquarters are in Washington, D.C.

History

The association grew out of Benjamin Finkel's journal The American Mathematical Monthly, founded in 1894, whose readership felt the American Mathematical Society did not serve their pedagogical interests. The MAA was formally established in December 1915 at Ohio State University, with its first president being E. R. Hedrick. The Monthly became the association's official journal.

Publications

The MAA publishes several journals, including The American Mathematical Monthly, Mathematics Magazine, The College Mathematics Journal, Math Horizons and the online journal MAA Reviews. Its book programme issues textbooks and expository works, many through its MAA Press imprint.

Competitions

Through its American Mathematics Competitions programme, the MAA administers the AMC 8, AMC 10 and AMC 12 examinations, the American Invitational Mathematics Examination (AIME), and the United States of America Mathematical Olympiad (USAMO). It also runs the William Lowell Putnam Mathematical Competition for undergraduates in the United States and Canada.

Meetings

The association's summer meeting, MAA MathFest, and its participation in the Joint Mathematics Meetings each January bring together thousands of mathematicians for talks, workshops and professional development.
