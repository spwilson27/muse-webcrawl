Diagnosis of exclusion

A diagnosis of exclusion is a medical diagnosis reached by a process of elimination, used when a condition cannot be confirmed by a definitive test and all other plausible causes have been ruled out.

Process

The clinician constructs a differential diagnosis — a list of conditions that could explain the patient's symptoms — and progressively excludes each possibility through history, examination and investigation. What remains, however improbable, is accepted as the working diagnosis.

Examples

Conditions commonly described as diagnoses of exclusion include irritable bowel syndrome, fibromyalgia, chronic fatigue syndrome, and sudden infant death syndrome. Psychiatric diagnoses are also largely made by exclusion of organic causes.

Criticism

The approach is criticised for its dependence on the completeness of the differential and the accuracy of negative tests; a diagnosis of exclusion may be revised when new evidence or tests emerge.
