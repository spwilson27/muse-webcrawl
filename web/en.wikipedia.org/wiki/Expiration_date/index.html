Expiration date

An expiration date or expiry date is a previously determined date after which something should no longer be used, either by operation of law or by exceeding the anticipated shelf life for perishable goods. Expiration dates are applied to selected food products, pharmaceutical drugs, chemicals, and other substances that may spoil or become unsafe or ineffective over time.

Food labelling

Terms such as "best before," "use by," and "sell by" carry different meanings. In most jurisdictions, "use by" dates relate to safety, while "best before" dates relate to quality. In the United States, except for infant formula, federal law generally does not require date labels, and wording is inconsistently regulated by states, contributing to consumer confusion and food waste.

Pharmaceuticals

For medicines, the expiration date is the date through which the manufacturer guarantees full potency and safety when stored as directed. Studies under the US Shelf Life Extension Program have found that many stockpiled drugs retain potency well beyond labelled dates under controlled storage, though patients are advised not to use expired medicines.

Other uses

Expiration dates also appear on payment cards, coupons, contracts, and options, where the date marks the end of validity rather than degradation.
