Uppsala Cathedral (Swedish: Uppsala domkyrka) is a cathedral located in the city of Uppsala, Sweden, between the Uppsala University Main Building and the Fyris river. Built of brick in the French Gothic style, it is the tallest church in the Nordic countries at 118.7 metres (389 ft) and is the seat of the Archbishop of Uppsala, primate of the Church of Sweden.

History

Construction began in 1272, after the archbishopric was moved from Old Uppsala, and continued for roughly 165 years; the cathedral was consecrated in 1435. It was heavily restored in the 19th century by architect Helgo Zettervall, who added the present French-style spires. The cathedral contains the tombs of King Gustav Vasa, Carl Linnaeus, Emanuel Swedenborg, and Saint Eric (Eric IX), patron saint of Sweden, as well as the relics of Saint Bridget nearby in its chapel.
