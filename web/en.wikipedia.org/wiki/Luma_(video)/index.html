Luma (video)

In video, luma represents the brightness in an image — the achromatic portion of the signal. Luma is typically paired with chrominance, which encodes the colour information. Separating brightness from colour allows chroma subsampling, exploiting the human visual system's greater sensitivity to luminance detail than to colour detail, and provides backward compatibility between colour and monochrome television.

Luma versus luminance

Luma is the gamma-compressed weighted sum of red, green, and blue components, usually written Y′ (Y-prime), where the prime denotes gamma correction. This differs from luminance (Y), the linear-light weighted sum defined colorimetrically. In the ITU-R BT.601 standard for standard-definition video, luma is computed as:

Y′ = 0.299 R′ + 0.587 G′ + 0.114 B′

High-definition standards (BT.709) use different coefficients reflecting their primaries:

Y′ = 0.2126 R′ + 0.7152 G′ + 0.0722 B′

BT.2020 wide-gamut video uses 0.2627, 0.6780, and 0.0593 respectively.

Use in compression

Colour formats such as Y′CbCr store luma at full resolution while subsampling the chroma channels (e.g. 4:2:0), which substantially reduces bandwidth with little perceived loss. JPEG, MPEG, H.264/AVC, and successor codecs all operate on luma/chroma separations of this kind, and most image-processing operations such as sharpening and noise reduction are applied primarily to the luma channel.
