Silicosis
SpecialtyPulmonology, occupational medicine
CauseInhalation of crystalline silica dust
SymptomsCough, breathlessness, chest pain, fatigue
PreventionDust control, respirators, wet methods

Silicosis is a form of occupational lung disease — a pneumoconiosis — caused by inhaling respirable crystalline silica dust, usually over many years of work in mining, quarrying, tunnelling, sandblasting, stonecutting, foundries or construction. The silica particles provoke inflammation and progressive scarring (fibrosis) of the lungs, which stiffens them and impairs breathing. Silicosis is irreversible but preventable, and it increases susceptibility to tuberculosis and other diseases.

Forms and symptoms

Chronic silicosis develops after 10 or more years of lower-level exposure and may cause few symptoms at first, later producing cough, shortness of breath and disability; in advanced disease the fibrotic nodules merge into progressive massive fibrosis. Accelerated silicosis appears within 5–10 years of heavy exposure. Acute silicosis (silicoproteinosis) can develop within months of very high exposure — as in uncontrolled sandblasting or cutting engineered stone — and may be rapidly fatal. Diagnosis rests on exposure history, chest imaging showing characteristic upper-lung nodules, and lung function tests.

History and notable outbreaks

The disease has been recognised since antiquity among stonecutters and miners; Agricola and Ramazzini described it centuries ago. The Hawks Nest Tunnel disaster in West Virginia (1930–31), in which hundreds of mostly Black workers died after drilling through nearly pure silica with little protection, became a notorious symbol of industrial negligence. In recent decades an epidemic of accelerated silicosis among workers fabricating artificial (engineered) stone benchtops has prompted bans and stricter rules, including Australia's 2024 prohibition of engineered stone.

Treatment and prevention

There is no cure; management is supportive — stopping exposure, treating infections and, in end-stage disease, lung transplantation. Prevention relies on engineering controls (ventilation, water suppression, isolation of dusty processes), exposure limits set by regulators, monitoring, and respiratory protection.
