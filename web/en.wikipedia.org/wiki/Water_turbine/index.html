Water turbine

TypeRotary engine
Energy sourceKinetic and potential energy of water
ApplicationsHydroelectric power generation

A water turbine is a rotary machine that converts kinetic energy and potential energy of water into mechanical work. Water turbines were developed in the 19th century and are now widely used for hydroelectric power generation in dams and run-of-river plants.

Types

Turbines are classified as impulse or reaction machines. In an impulse turbine, such as the Pelton wheel, a high-velocity jet strikes cups on the runner in open air; these suit high-head, low-flow sites. In a reaction turbine, such as the Francis and Kaplan turbines, the runner is fully immersed and pressure changes across its blades; these suit lower heads and larger flows. The Turgo and cross-flow (Banki–Michell) turbines occupy intermediate niches.

Theory of operation

The power available depends on the head and flow rate. Euler's turbine equation relates the change in angular momentum of the water to the work done on the runner. Modern turbines can achieve efficiencies above 95 percent at their design point.
