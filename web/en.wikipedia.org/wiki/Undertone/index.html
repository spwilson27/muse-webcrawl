Undertone

An undertone is a subtle colouration underlying a surface colour, most often discussed in relation to paint, cosmetics and human skin. Whereas the surface or "overtone" is the colour immediately perceived — for example red, beige or grey — the undertone is the underlying hue that shows through and affects how the top colour appears, especially in thin layers or varying light.

In colour and paint

In painting and interior design, pigments have both a masstone (their appearance in thick application) and an undertone (their appearance when spread thinly or mixed with white). Two paints that look identical in the tin may behave differently because of divergent undertones — a neutral grey, for instance, may carry a blue, green or violet undertone that becomes apparent on the wall and determines which other colours harmonise with it. Colour theorists describe undertones on a warm–cool axis: warm undertones lean towards yellow, gold and red; cool undertones towards blue, pink and grey.

In cosmetics

In cosmetics, matching foundation to skin undertone — conventionally classified as warm, cool or neutral, with olive sometimes added — is considered more important than matching surface tone alone. Common tests include the colour of the veins at the wrist (greenish suggesting warm, bluish suggesting cool), whether gold or silver jewellery flatters the wearer, and how the skin reacts to sun exposure. "Colour analysis" systems for clothing, popularised in the 1980s, likewise assign individuals to seasonal palettes based on undertone.

Figurative use

By extension, "undertone" in language means an underlying or implied quality, feeling or meaning beneath what is said explicitly — a conversation may carry an undertone of menace or affection. The musical sense of a low, subdued sound is related; the "undertone series" in acoustics is a theoretical mirror of the overtone series below a fundamental.
