Hassler Whitney

Born: March 23, 1907, New York City, U.S.

Died: May 10, 1989 (aged 82), Princeton, New Jersey, U.S.

Alma mater: Yale University; Harvard University

Known for: Whitney embedding theorem; Whitney immersion theorem; matroid theory

Awards: National Medal of Science (1976); Wolf Prize (1982); Steele Prize (1985)

Hassler Whitney (March 23, 1907 – May 10, 1989) was an American mathematician. He was one of the founders of singularity theory and did foundational work in manifolds, embeddings, immersions, characteristic classes, and geometric integration theory.

Biography

Whitney was born in New York City, the son of the jurist Edward Baldwin Whitney and the painter and writer Aline Roomet Whitney; his grandfather William Dwight Whitney was a noted Sanskrit scholar. He studied physics and mathematics at Yale University, receiving degrees in 1928 and 1929, and earned his PhD in mathematics at Harvard University in 1932 under George David Birkhoff, with a dissertation on the coloring of graphs.

He spent most of his career at Harvard, becoming professor of mathematics, and in 1952 moved to the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton. He was a member of the National Academy of Sciences and a keen mountaineer; two Swiss Alpine peaks were climbed by him for the first time with colleagues.

Work

Whitney's early work was in graph theory, including contributions to the four-colour problem and the theory of planar graphs. In 1935 he introduced the concept of a matroid, founding an important branch of combinatorics and algebra. His work in differential topology produced the Whitney embedding theorem, which shows that every smooth manifold can be embedded in Euclidean space of twice its dimension, and the corresponding immersion theorem. He introduced concepts such as characteristic classes (the Stiefel–Whitney classes are named partly for him), the Whitney umbrella singularity, and foundational ideas in the theory of differentiable manifolds and geometric measure theory. Late in his career he took a strong interest in mathematics education.
