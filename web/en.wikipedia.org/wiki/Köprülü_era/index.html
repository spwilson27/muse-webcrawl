The Köprülü era (Turkish: Köprülüler Devri) was a period of Ottoman history, from 1656 to 1703, in which the empire was ruled largely by a succession of grand viziers drawn from the Köprülü family and their household. Through ruthless administrative reform and successful warfare, the Köprülü viziers are credited with temporarily reversing the Ottoman Empire's mid-17th-century decline.

Köprülü Mehmed Pasha

In 1656, amid the Cretan War with Venice, military revolts, and fiscal crisis, Sultan Mehmed IV appointed the elderly Albanian-born Köprülü Mehmed Pasha as grand vizier — on Mehmed's own terms: the sultan granted him near-absolute authority over appointments and policy. Köprülü purged corrupt officials, suppressed revolts in Anatolia with great severity, recaptured the Aegean islands of Tenedos and Lemnos from Venice, and restored the treasury through austerity and confiscations.

Fazıl Ahmed Pasha and expansion

His son Fazıl Ahmed Pasha (vizier 1661–1676) succeeded him — a near-unprecedented hereditary transfer of the office. He concluded the long Cretan War by taking Candia (Heraklion) in 1669, completing the conquest of Crete, and campaigned in Hungary and Podolia. His brother-in-law Kara Mustafa Pasha then led the empire's last great offensive, ending in the failed siege of Vienna in 1683, for which he was executed.

Decline and end

The ensuing War of the Holy League (1683–1699) stripped the Ottomans of Hungary and Transylvania, confirmed by the Treaty of Karlowitz (1699). Further Köprülü viziers — Fazıl Mustafa Pasha, killed at Slankamen in 1691, and Amcazade Hüseyin Pasha (1697–1702) — attempted reform, but the era effectively closed with the Edirne Incident of 1703, a revolt that deposed Sultan Mustafa II.
