Fort Lee, New Jersey

Fort Lee is a borough in Bergen County, New Jersey, situated atop the Hudson Palisades on the western bank of the Hudson River, across from Manhattan. It is the western terminus of the George Washington Bridge. The population was about 40,000 at the 2020 census.

History

The borough is named for Fort Lee, an American Revolutionary War encampment built in 1776 under General Charles Lee on the Palisades to contest British control of the Hudson. In November 1776 the fort was evacuated in haste as British forces advanced, shortly after the fall of Fort Washington across the river — an episode later fictionalized in the musical Hamilton.

In the early 20th century, Fort Lee was a center of the young American film industry: studios including Universal Pictures (founded in part from Fort Lee operations) shot many early movies on the cliffs and streets before the industry migrated to Hollywood. The opening of the George Washington Bridge in 1931 transformed the borough into a commuter suburb.

Community

Fort Lee has a large Korean American population and a dense high-rise corridor along the Palisades. In 2013 the borough drew national attention in the "Bridgegate" scandal, when access lanes to the bridge in Fort Lee were closed, allegedly as political retaliation against its mayor.
