Ranks and insignia of NATO

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The ranks and insignia of NATO are the combined military insignia used by the member countries of the North Atlantic Treaty Organization. To allow service members of different nations to work together, NATO standardises the equivalence of ranks through a system of codes defined in the standardisation agreement STANAG 2116.

The NATO rank code system

Officer ranks are graded from OF-1 (junior officers such as lieutenants) to OF-10 (the highest rank, equivalent to a field marshal or five-star general, reserved for wartime or honorary use in most nations). Other ranks — non-commissioned officers and enlisted personnel — are graded OR-1 to OR-9. The codes do not replace national rank titles and insignia, which remain determined by each member state, but they provide a common reference so that, for example, a British lance corporal, a German Gefreiter-level soldier and an American private can be placed at equivalent grades.

Insignia

Insignia styles vary widely: many European armies use shoulder-strap or sleeve devices with stars, bars, crowns or national emblems, while the United States and some others use metal pin-on devices. NATO comparison charts are published showing the officer and enlisted insignia of each member's army, navy and air force side by side by code.
