Nonlinear system

In mathematics and science, a nonlinear system (or nonlinearity) is a system in which the change of the output is not proportional to the change of the input. Nonlinear problems are of interest to engineers, biologists, physicists, and mathematicians because most real systems are inherently nonlinear in nature.

Definition

A system is linear if it satisfies the superposition principle: the response to a sum of inputs equals the sum of the responses, and scaling the input scales the output. Nonlinear systems violate one or both properties. Their governing equations — nonlinear algebraic equations or nonlinear differential equations — are usually much harder to solve than linear ones, and closed-form solutions often do not exist.

Characteristic behaviour

Nonlinear dynamical systems can display behaviour absent from linear systems: multiple equilibria, limit cycles, bifurcations, and chaos — deterministic but highly sensitive dependence on initial conditions, exemplified by the Lorenz equations for atmospheric convection. The double pendulum, the logistic map, and the Navier–Stokes equations of fluid flow are classic examples.

Solution methods

Because exact methods are limited, nonlinear systems are studied by numerical simulation, perturbation methods, and linearization about equilibrium points, which gives local insight into stability. Solitons — self-reinforcing solitary waves in nonlinear media, governed for example by the Korteweg–de Vries equation — are a notable exception where exact nonlinear solutions exist.
