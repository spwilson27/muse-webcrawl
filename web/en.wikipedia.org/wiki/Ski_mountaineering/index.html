Ski mountaineering

Ski mountaineering (abbreviated to skimo) is a skiing discipline that involves climbing mountains either on skis or carrying them, depending on the steepness of the ascent, and then descending on skis. It combines elements of ski touring, Telemark skiing, backcountry skiing, and mountaineering, and is practised both as a recreational activity and as a competitive sport.

History

Ski mountaineering developed in the European Alps, where skiers needed to traverse high passes and glaciers in winter long before the invention of ski lifts. Early practitioners included Wilhelm von Arlt and Georg Bilgeri in Austria in the 1890s. The discipline grew through military patrol races in the early twentieth century; a military patrol event was contested at the 1924 Winter Olympics in Chamonix.

Competitive ski mountaineering emerged as an organized sport in the 1970s and 1980s, particularly in Italy, France, Switzerland, and Spain. The International Ski Mountaineering Federation (ISMF) was founded in 2008 to govern the sport internationally. Ski mountaineering made its Olympic debut at the 2026 Winter Olympics in Milan and Cortina d'Ampezzo, with sprint and mixed relay events.

Equipment

Ski mountaineers use specialized lightweight equipment: short, light alpine touring skis with bindings that release the heel for climbing and lock it down for descending; removable climbing skins applied to the ski bases for grip on ascents; ski crampons for icy traverses; and, in steep terrain, ice axes, boot crampons, harnesses, and ropes. Avalanche safety equipment — a transceiver, shovel, and probe — is standard.

Competitive formats

Races include individual events over long alpine courses with multiple ascents and descents, team races, vertical races (ascent only), sprint races over short courses, and mixed relays. Racers must carry mandatory safety equipment and may face mandatory boot-packing and fixed-rope sections.
