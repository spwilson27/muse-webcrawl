Coalbed methane

Coalbed methane (CBM) is natural gas extracted from coal seams, where methane is adsorbed onto the surface of the coal. It became a significant source of natural gas in the United States, Canada, Australia and China from the 1980s onward.

Extraction

To release the gas, water is pumped from the coal seam to reduce pressure, allowing methane to desorb and flow to the well. Produced water can be saline and its disposal is a major environmental consideration. Unlike conventional reservoirs, coalbed gas content generally increases with depth and coal rank.

History

Methane in coal mines was long a hazard (firedamp). Deliberate production began at scale in the San Juan Basin of New Mexico and Colorado and the Black Warrior Basin of Alabama, aided by US tax incentives in the 1980s.
