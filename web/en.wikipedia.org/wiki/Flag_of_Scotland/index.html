The flag of Scotland (Scottish Gaelic: bratach na h-Alba) is the national flag of Scotland, which consists of a white saltire — the diagonal cross of the patron saint of Scotland, Saint Andrew — on a blue field. The Saltire is one of the oldest flags in the world still in use, with its origins traced by legend to the 9th century, and it forms part of the Union Flag of the United Kingdom.


The Saltire

Legend and history

According to legend, in 832 AD King Óengus II led the Picts and Scots against the Angles near Athelstaneford in East Lothian. On the night before the battle, Saint Andrew appeared to the king in a dream promising victory; the next day a white diagonal cross appeared against the blue sky, and the Scots and Picts were victorious. The saltire was in documented use as a Scottish emblem by the 14th century and appeared on seals and banners through the medieval period. In 1385 the Scottish Parliament ordered that Scottish soldiers wear the white cross of St Andrew as an identifying badge.

Colour and use

The shade of blue varied historically from dark navy to sky blue; in 2003 the Scottish Parliament standardised it as Pantone 300. The flag is flown from Scottish Government buildings daily, alongside the Union Flag, and is widely flown by the public, especially on St Andrew's Day (30 November). When flown together, protocol questions about the precedence of the Saltire and the Union Flag remain a mild source of controversy in Scotland.
