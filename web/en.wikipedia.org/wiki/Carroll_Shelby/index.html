Carroll Hall Shelby (11 January 1923 – 10 May 2012) was an American racing driver and automotive designer best known for the Shelby Cobra and for leading Ford's GT40 programme that defeated Ferrari at Le Mans. He won the 24 Hours of Le Mans as a driver in 1959.

Racing career

After serving as a flight instructor in the Second World War, Shelby raced sports cars in the 1950s, driving for Aston Martin, Maserati and Ferrari. With Roy Salvadori he won Le Mans in 1959 in an Aston Martin DBR1, wearing distinctive striped farmer's overalls. A heart condition (angina, later requiring a transplant) forced his retirement from driving in 1960.

Shelby American

In 1962 Shelby founded Shelby American in California, inserting a Ford V8 engine into the British AC Ace chassis to create the Cobra. The big-block Cobra 427 followed, and Ford hired Shelby to develop the GT40, which won Le Mans four consecutive times from 1966 — a story dramatised in the film Ford v Ferrari (2019). Shelby also produced high-performance Shelby Mustang GT350 and GT500 models, later collaborated with Chrysler in the 1980s, and developed the Shelby Series 1. He received a heart transplant in 1990 and remained active in the car world until his death in 2012.
