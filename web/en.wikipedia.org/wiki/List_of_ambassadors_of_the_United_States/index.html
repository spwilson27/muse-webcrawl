List of ambassadors of the United States

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ambassadors of the United States are persons nominated by the President of the United States and confirmed by the Senate to serve as the country's chief representatives to foreign nations and international organizations. Roughly 70 percent of ambassadors are career members of the Foreign Service; the remainder are political appointees, a practice that has drawn periodic criticism. There are over 190 ambassador-level posts, and the full lists are maintained in separate articles by country (for example, "List of ambassadors of the United States to France").

Notable ambassadors in history

  • Benjamin Franklin — Minister to France (1778–1785), secured the French alliance during the Revolutionary War.
  • John Adams and Thomas Jefferson — early envoys to Britain and France respectively, both later presidents.
  • George F. Kennan — ambassador to the Soviet Union (1952), author of the "Long Telegram" and the containment doctrine.
  • Clare Boothe Luce — ambassador to Italy (1953–1956), one of the first prominent American women ambassadors.
  • Shirley Temple Black — ambassador to Ghana (1974–1976) and Czechoslovakia (1989–1992).
  • Pamela Harriman — ambassador to France (1993–1997).
  • Richard Holbrooke — ambassador to the United Nations (1999–2001), architect of the Dayton Accords.
  • J. Christopher Stevens — ambassador to Libya, killed in the 2012 Benghazi attack; one of eight U.S. ambassadors killed in the line of duty.
  • Marie Yovanovitch — career ambassador to Ukraine (2016–2019), a central figure in the first impeachment inquiry of Donald Trump.
