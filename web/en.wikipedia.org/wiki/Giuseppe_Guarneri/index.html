Giuseppe Guarneri

Giuseppe Antonio Guarneri (21 August 1687 – 17 October 1744), known as Giuseppe Guarneri del Gesù, was an Italian luthier from Cremona, the most celebrated member of the Guarneri family of violin makers. His instruments — of which roughly 150–200 survive — are, with those of Antonio Stradivari, the most prized violins in the world; the nickname del Gesù derives from the "I·H·S" Christogram that appears on his labels.

Life and work

Guarneri was the grandson of Andrea Guarneri, founder of the family's workshop, and son of Giuseppe Giovanni Battista Guarneri (filius Andreae). Trained in the family shop, he developed a distinctive, increasingly bold style in the 1730s and 1740s: broad arching, strong f-holes, rich varnish and a powerful, dark tone. His late work, produced in difficult financial circumstances (he was at times imprisoned for debt, according to persistent tradition), has an almost rough spontaneity that modern players cherish.

Famous instruments

Celebrated del Gesù violins include the Il Cannone of 1743, the favourite instrument of Niccolò Paganini, now preserved in Genoa; the Lord Wilton (1742), played by Yehudi Menuhin; and the Vieuxtemps (1741), among the most expensive violins ever sold. Soloists noted for preferring Guarneri del Gesù over Stradivari include Jascha Heifetz, Isaac Stern, Pinchas Zukerman and Anne-Sophie Mutter.
