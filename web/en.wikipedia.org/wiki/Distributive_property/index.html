Distributive property

Multiplication distributing over addition, illustrated with areas.

The distributive property is a property of binary operations in mathematics. In elementary arithmetic it is the rule that multiplication distributes over addition:

a × (b + c) = (a × b) + (a × c)

More generally, a binary operation ∗ distributes over another binary operation + if, for all elements a, b and c, a ∗ (b + c) = (a ∗ b) + (a ∗ c), and similarly on the right. The distributive property is one of the defining axioms of rings, fields and other algebraic structures.

Examples

Multiplication of real numbers is distributive over addition and subtraction, though division is not distributive over addition on the right in general. In set theory, union distributes over intersection and vice versa. In propositional logic, conjunction distributes over disjunction and vice versa. Matrix multiplication distributes over matrix addition.

Applications

The distributive property underlies mental arithmetic strategies (for example, computing 7 × 13 as 7 × 10 + 7 × 3), the expansion of algebraic expressions, and the factorisation of polynomials. It is closely related to the associative property and the commutative property, which together describe the basic arithmetic operations.
