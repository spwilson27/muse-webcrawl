Geirangerfjord

The Geirangerfjord, a branch of the Storfjord in western Norway.

The Geirangerfjord (Norwegian: Geirangerfjorden) is a fjord in the Sunnmøre region of Møre og Romsdal county, Norway. It is located entirely in Stranda Municipality. The fjord is a 15-kilometre (9.3 mi) long branch off the Sunnylvsfjorden, which is itself a branch off the Storfjorden (Great Fjord). The small village of Geiranger is located at the end of the fjord, where the Geirangelva river flows into it.

Geography

The fjord is one of Norway's most visited tourist sites. In 2005, it was listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site, jointly with the Nærøyfjord, as the "West Norwegian Fjords". Steep mountains rise almost vertically from the water, and several waterfalls descend the cliffs, including the Seven Sisters (De syv søstrene), the Suitor (Friaren), and the Bridal Veil (Brudesløret).

Fjord farms

Abandoned farms cling to ledges along the fjord, including Skageflå, Knivsflå, and Blomberg. Some have been restored and are accessible by ferry or on foot. The fjord is also under threat from landslides from the mountain Åkerneset, which could generate a large tsunami; this risk is continuously monitored.

Tourism

The Geirangerfjord is visited by hundreds of cruise ships and hundreds of thousands of tourists each year. The car ferry between Geiranger and Hellesylt carries passengers along the full length of the fjord, and sightseeing boats operate during the summer season.
