Jannah (Arabic: جنّة, janna, plural jannat, "paradise, garden") is the Islamic concept of Paradise, the final abode of the righteous in the afterlife. According to the Quran, after death people pass through the Barzakh and await resurrection and judgment; those admitted to Jannah dwell in gardens beneath which rivers flow, in eternal bliss in the presence of God's pleasure.

Quranic description

The Quran describes Jannah as gardens of perpetual residence (often identified with the Garden of Eden, Jannat 'Adn) with rivers of water, milk, honey, and wine that does not intoxicate; fruit of every kind; spouses and companions; garments of silk and ornaments of gold; and freedom from fatigue, grief, and death. Its highest level is often identified as al-Firdaws (the Paradise par excellence), lying beneath the Throne of God. The vision of God — seeing the face of Allah — is regarded in Sunni tradition as the greatest of Paradise's delights.

Levels and inhabitants

Hadith literature describes many levels or grades of Paradise — traditionally up to one hundred — assigned according to faith and deeds. Inhabitants include the prophets, martyrs, and the righteous; Islamic theologians also debated the fate of righteous non-Muslims and children. Entry is ultimately attributed to God's mercy rather than deeds alone, a point emphasized in a well-known hadith in which Muhammad states that none enter Paradise by deeds alone, "not even I, unless God envelops me in His mercy."

Related concepts

Jannah's imagery influenced Islamic garden design — the charbagh, a four-part garden divided by water channels, is widely read as an earthly reflection of the Quranic paradise. Its counterpart is Jahannam, the fire of punishment.
