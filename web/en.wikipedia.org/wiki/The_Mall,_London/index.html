The Mall (rhyming with "pal") is a ceremonial route in the City of Westminster, central London. It runs from the Victoria Memorial in front of Buckingham Palace eastward to Admiralty Arch and Trafalgar Square, flanked by St James's Park to the south and Green Park and St James's Palace to the north. Its red-coloured surface gives the impression of a red carpet leading to the palace.

History

The name derives from pall-mall, a ball game played on the site in the seventeenth century; the tree-lined avenue became a fashionable promenade after the Restoration. The Mall was transformed into a grand ceremonial approach in the early twentieth century to designs associated with Sir Aston Webb, as part of the memorial scheme to Queen Victoria, completed around 1911.

Ceremonial use

The Mall is closed to traffic on Sundays and ceremonial occasions. It forms the route for royal processions, state visits (when it is hung with Union Flags and the flags of visiting nations), Trooping the Colour, and royal weddings and jubilees. It is also the finishing straight of the London Marathon. Buildings along it include St James's Palace, Clarence House, Lancaster House, and Marlborough House.
