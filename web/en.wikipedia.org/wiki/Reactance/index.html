Reactance

Reactance is the opposition presented to alternating current by the inductance or capacitance of a circuit element. Together with resistance it forms the impedance of the element. Unlike resistance, reactance does not dissipate energy; instead energy is stored in magnetic or electric fields and returned to the circuit each cycle. Reactance is measured in ohms, symbol Ω.

Inductive reactance

An inductor opposes changes in current. Its reactance is XL = 2πfL = ωL, increasing linearly with frequency f and inductance L. In an ideal inductor the voltage leads the current by 90°.

Capacitive reactance

A capacitor opposes changes in voltage. Its reactance is XC = 1/(2πfC) = 1/(ωC), decreasing as frequency or capacitance increases. In an ideal capacitor the current leads the voltage by 90°.

Total reactance

With the sign convention that inductive reactance is positive and capacitive reactance negative, the total reactance of a series combination is X = XL − XC, and impedance is written Z = R + jX. When the two cancel, the circuit is at resonance. The reciprocal of reactance is susceptance, measured in siemens.
