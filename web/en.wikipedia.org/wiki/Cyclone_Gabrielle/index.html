Cyclone Gabrielle

Formed: 6 February 2023
Dissipated: 16 February 2023
Peak intensity: Category 3 severe tropical cyclone
Areas affected: New Zealand (North Island), Norfolk Island
Damage: over NZ$13 billion (estimated)

Severe Tropical Cyclone Gabrielle was a severe tropical cyclone that devastated parts of the North Island of New Zealand in February 2023. It is, to date, the costliest tropical cyclone on record in the Southern Hemisphere, with damage estimated at over NZ$13.5 billion, and one of the worst storms to hit New Zealand in decades. Eleven people died in the event and thousands were displaced.

Meteorological history

Gabrielle developed over the Coral Sea in early February 2023 and tracked southeast, reaching Category 3 intensity on the Australian scale as it passed near Norfolk Island. It then transitioned into a powerful subtropical low and struck the upper North Island on 12–14 February, bringing extreme rainfall to Hawke's Bay, Gisborne/Tairāwhiti, Northland, Auckland, and the Coromandel.

Impact

Rivers burst their banks, bridges were swept away, and landslides cut off communities; the Esk Valley and other parts of Hawke's Bay were inundated, with floodwaters carrying huge quantities of silt and forestry debris. Around 10,000 people were displaced, and large areas lost power, water, and telecommunications for days. A national state of emergency was declared on 14 February — only the third in New Zealand's history.

Aftermath

The cyclone prompted a major government recovery and buyout programme for homes in high-risk areas, and reviews of land use — particularly forestry slash (woody debris) management, stopbank funding, and warning systems. Gabrielle, arriving weeks after Auckland's January 2023 floods, intensified debate in New Zealand about climate change adaptation and managed retreat.
