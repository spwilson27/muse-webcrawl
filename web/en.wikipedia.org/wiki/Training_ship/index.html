Training ship

A training ship is a ship used to train students as sailors, particularly by navies, coast guards and merchant marine academies. The term covers both purpose-built vessels and older ships modified for the role, from large square-rigged sailing barques crewed by naval cadets to modern powered ships used by maritime universities to provide cadets with the sea time required for licensing.

Naval training ships

Many navies maintain sail training ships to teach seamanship, teamwork and leadership under demanding conditions. Famous examples include the United States Coast Guard's barque Eagle, the German Navy's Gorch Fock, Italy's Amerigo Vespucci and Chile's Esmeralda. These ships frequently serve as goodwill ambassadors, visiting foreign ports and taking part in tall ships races.

Merchant and stationary ships

State maritime academies in the United States operate large training ships — several converted from cargo vessels — on which cadets make summer training cruises. In the 19th and early 20th centuries, stationary training ships (often retired warships moored in port, such as HMS Conway and HMS Worcester on the Thames) housed schools for officer cadets and for boys entering the sea services.

Modern role

Although simulators now cover much technical training, regulators still require actual sea service for officer certification, and sail-training organisations operate civilian training ships offering character-building voyages to young people.
