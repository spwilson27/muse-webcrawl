K. Pattabhi Jois

Sri Krishna Pattabhi Jois (26 July 1915 – 18 May 2009) was an Indian yoga teacher from Mysore who developed and popularized Ashtanga vinyasa yoga, a flowing style of yoga as exercise linking breath with a progressive series of postures. His students carried the method worldwide, making it one of the most influential forms of modern yoga.

Early life and studies

Jois was born in Kowshika village near Hassan, Karnataka, into a Brahmin family; his father was an astrologer and priest. In 1927, at the age of twelve, he attended a yoga demonstration and lecture by Tirumalai Krishnamacharya in Hassan and became his student the next day. He studied with Krishnamacharya for about two years in his home village and, after moving to Mysore in 1930, renewed the study, continuing until 1953. During this time he also studied Sanskrit and the yoga texts at the Maharaja's Sanskrit College, where he later taught yoga and eventually became a professor.

Teaching career

From 1937 Jois taught yoga at the Maharaja of Mysore's palace and at the Sanskrit College, later founding the Ashtanga Yoga Research Institute in Lakshmipuram, Mysore, in 1948. His book Yoga Mala, written in Kannada in the 1950s and published in 1962 (English edition 1999), sets out his method. Western students began arriving in the 1960s and 1970s — beginning with the Belgian André Van Lysbeth's pupils and Americans such as Norman Allen and David Williams — and Jois first taught in the United States in 1975. The "Mysore style" of self-paced practice under supervision, and the fixed Primary, Intermediate, and Advanced series, became hallmarks of his lineage, continued by his family, including his grandson Sharath Jois.

Legacy and criticism

Jois's energetic style underlies much of modern "power yoga." His work has also been examined critically: scholars question the claimed ancient provenance of the sequences, and since 2019 former students and journalists have documented allegations of sexual misconduct in adjustments during his lifetime, prompting public apologies from his family and changes within the Ashtanga community.
