Disease

A disease is a particular abnormal condition that adversely affects the structure or function of all or part of an organism and is not immediately due to any external injury. Diseases are often known to be medical conditions associated with specific signs and symptoms. A disease may be caused by external factors such as pathogens or by internal dysfunctions.

In humans, disease is often used more broadly to refer to any condition that causes pain, dysfunction, distress, social problems, or death to the person affected, or similar problems for those in contact with the person. The study of disease is called pathology, which includes the study of etiology, or cause.

Classification

Diseases can be classified by cause (infectious, deficiency, hereditary, physiological), by pathogenesis, or by the organ system involved. The World Health Organization's International Classification of Diseases (ICD) is the standard diagnostic tool for epidemiology and health management. Infectious diseases are caused by pathogens such as bacteria, viruses, fungi, and parasites, and may be communicable between individuals.

Causes

Only some diseases, such as influenza, are contagious and commonly believed to be infectious. Microorganisms that cause such diseases are known as pathogens, and the diseases they cause are called infectious diseases. Other diseases arise from genetic defects, environmental exposures, lifestyle factors, or the ageing process.

Treatment and prevention

Treatments range from medication and surgery to lifestyle change and palliative care. Prevention includes vaccination, sanitation, nutrition, screening, and public health measures aimed at risk factors.
