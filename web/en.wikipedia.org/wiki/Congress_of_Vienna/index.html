Congress of Vienna

The Congress of Vienna (November 1814 – June 1815) was a conference of ambassadors and rulers of European states chaired by the Austrian statesman Klemens von Metternich. It met to settle the issues arising from the French Revolution and Napoleonic Wars and to reshape Europe's political map after Napoleon's first abdication. Its settlement, designed around legitimacy and a balance of power, was remarkably durable: no general European war occurred for nearly four decades, and no war engulfed the whole continent for a century.

Deliberations

Although hundreds of delegations attended — and Vienna famously danced as much as it negotiated — the real work was done by the great powers: Austria, Britain, Prussia and Russia, joined after skilful manoeuvring by Talleyrand representing defeated France. The Polish–Saxon crisis nearly caused war among the victors themselves in January 1815, before a compromise partitioned Saxony and gave most of the former Duchy of Warsaw to Russia as "Congress Poland". Napoleon's escape from Elba and the Hundred Days concentrated minds, and the Final Act was signed on 9 June 1815, nine days before the Battle of Waterloo.

Terms

France was reduced roughly to its 1790 frontiers and the Bourbons restored. Austria gave up the distant Austrian Netherlands (Belgium) but gained Lombardy–Venetia and influence in Italy and the 39-state German Confederation. Prussia gained the Rhineland and part of Saxony, positioning it facing France. Russia kept Finland and Bessarabia and gained Congress Poland. Britain kept its colonial and maritime gains, including Malta, the Cape Colony and Ceylon. Switzerland's perpetual neutrality was guaranteed, and the slave trade was formally condemned.

Legacy

The "Concert of Europe" that followed managed great-power disputes by conference diplomacy, but the settlement's disregard for nationalism and liberalism stored up the revolutions of 1830 and 1848. Historians still debate whether Vienna created a century of relative peace or merely froze a map that events would eventually shatter.
