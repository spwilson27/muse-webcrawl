Special pleading

Special pleading is an informal fallacy in which a person applies rules or standards to others while exempting themselves (or something they favour) from those same standards without providing a relevant justification for the exemption. It is a form of double standard.

Description

The fallacy occurs when an arguer insists that a general principle applies in all cases but claims that their own case is an exception for reasons that do not hold up — for example, demanding rigorous evidence for an opponent's claim while asserting their own claim needs no evidence, or asserting that a rule is universal while granting an ad-hoc exception. If a genuinely relevant difference between the cases exists, differential treatment is not fallacious; special pleading lies in asserting the exception without such a justification.

Legal sense

Historically, "special pleading" also had a technical meaning in common-law pleading: pleading that introduced new matter rather than merely denying the opponent's allegations. The term's modern rhetorical sense — pleading one's own special case — derives from this usage.
