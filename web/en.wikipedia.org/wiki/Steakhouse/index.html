Steakhouse

A steakhouse, or chophouse, is a restaurant that specialises in steaks and chops, usually grilled beef served with side dishes such as potatoes, vegetables and sauces. Modern steakhouses may also serve seafood, poultry and elaborate starters and desserts.

Steakhouse
Main dish: Beef steak
Notable examples: Peter Luger, Keens, Hawksmoor
Related: Churrascaria, Asado

History

Chophouses arose in London in the 18th and 19th centuries serving individual portions of meat to businessmen. In the United States, New York institutions such as Delmonico's and later Peter Luger (founded 1887) defined the classic steakhouse: dry-aged beef, dark wood interiors, and à la carte sides like creamed spinach and hash browns.

Menus and cuts

Common cuts include ribeye, striploin (New York strip), filet mignon, porterhouse and T-bone, often USDA Prime and dry-aged in-house. Cooking is to order by doneness, from rare to well done; Japanese steakhouses may feature wagyu, and Brazilian churrascarias serve rodizio-style grilled meats.

Chains

Major chains include Ruth's Chris, Morton's, The Capital Grille, Outback Steakhouse and Texas Roadhouse.
