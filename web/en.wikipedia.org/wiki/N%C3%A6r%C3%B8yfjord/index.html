The Nærøyfjord (Norwegian: Nærøyfjorden) is a fjord in Aurland Municipality in Vestland county, Norway. It is a narrow branch of the Aurlandsfjord, itself a branch of the great Sognefjord, and is about 18 kilometres long. At its narrowest the fjord is only about 250 metres wide, flanked by steep mountains rising up to 1,800 metres.

Heritage and tourism

In 2005 the Nærøyfjord, together with the Geirangerfjord, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as the "West Norwegian Fjords", recognised as a classic and largely unspoiled example of a fjord landscape. The fjord has been named among the world's foremost natural heritage sites in evaluations by National Geographic.

The small village of Gudvangen lies at the head of the fjord, and Bakka and Dyrdal are traditional hamlets along its shores, reachable in part only by boat. Passenger ferries and sightseeing boats run between Gudvangen and Flåm, and the area is popular for kayaking and hiking. The historic Undredal stave church stands on the neighbouring Aurlandsfjord.
