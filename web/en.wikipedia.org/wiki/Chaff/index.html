Chaff

Chaff is the dry, scaly protective casings of the seeds of cereal grain, or similar fine, dry, scaly plant material, separated from the seed during threshing and winnowing. It is generally inedible to humans but is sometimes used as livestock fodder or incorporated into compost or soil.

Process

In traditional agriculture, grain was threshed to loosen the seeds and then winnowed – tossed into the air so the wind blew away the lighter chaff while the heavier grains fell back. Modern combine harvesters perform both operations.

Uses and idiom

Chaff has been used as fuel, thatch, and animal bedding. The phrase "separate the wheat from the chaff" derives from this process, meaning to distinguish valuable from worthless. In radar countermeasures, strips of metal foil are also called chaff.
