Ahmad ibn Fadlan was a 10th-century Arab Muslim traveller and writer from Baghdad, best known for his account (risala) of an embassy sent by the Abbasid caliph al-Muqtadir to the king of the Volga Bulgars in 921–922. His manuscript is one of the most important primary sources on the peoples of Eurasia in the early Middle Ages.

The journey

The embassy left Baghdad in June 921, travelling via Bukhara and Khwarazm, across the steppe among the Oghuz Turks and Pechenegs, and reached the Volga Bulgar ruler Almish in May 922. The mission's purposes included delivering funds for a fortress and mosque and instructing the Bulgars, recent converts to Islam, in religious law.

The account

Ibn Fadlan described the customs of the peoples he encountered, most famously the Rus' — Scandinavian traders on the Volga — including a detailed eyewitness description of a Rus ship burial in which a slave girl was sacrificed to accompany her master. His Risala survives only partially, in a manuscript discovered in Mashhad, Iran, in 1923. The account inspired Michael Crichton's novel Eaters of the Dead (1976) and the film The 13th Warrior (1999).
