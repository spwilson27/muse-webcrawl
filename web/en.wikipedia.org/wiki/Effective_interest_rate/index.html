Effective interest rate

Compounding raises the effective rate above the nominal rate

The effective interest rate (EIR), or effective annual rate, is the interest rate on a loan or financial product restated from the nominal interest rate so as to express the true annual interest earned or paid when compound interest is taken into account. It allows different products with different compounding periods to be compared on an equal footing.

Calculation

Where a nominal annual rate r is compounded n times per year, the effective annual rate is:

EIR = (1 + r/n)n − 1

For example, a nominal rate of 12% compounded monthly gives (1 + 0.12/12)12 − 1 ≈ 12.68%. As the compounding frequency increases, the effective rate rises toward the limit given by continuous compounding, er − 1.

Uses

Consumer-credit regulation in many jurisdictions requires lenders to disclose an effective or annual percentage rate (see annual percentage rate) so borrowers can compare loans. In accounting, the effective interest method amortizes a bond's premium or discount using the market rate at issuance, producing a constant periodic interest expense as a proportion of the carrying amount.
