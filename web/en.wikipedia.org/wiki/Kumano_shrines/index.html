Kumano shrines

A Kumano shrine (熊野神社, Kumano-jinja) is a type of Shinto shrine which enshrines the three Kumano mountains: Hongū, Shingū and Nachi (the Kumano Gongen). There are roughly 3,000 Kumano shrines throughout Japan.

The Kumano Sanzan

The head shrines are the Kumano Sanzan, the "Three Grand Shrines of Kumano": Kumano Hongū Taisha, Kumano Hayatama Taisha (Shingū) and Kumano Nachi Taisha, located in the mountainous Kii Peninsula in Wakayama Prefecture. Pilgrimage to the Kumano shrines flourished from the Heian period, when retired emperors such as Shirakawa and Go-Shirakawa made repeated journeys along the Kumano Kodō pilgrimage routes.

Worship and spread

Kumano worship reflects the syncretic blending of Shinto and Buddhism (shinbutsu-shūgō); the Kumano deities were identified with Buddhist figures such as Amida Buddha. Shugendō mountain ascetics helped spread the cult, and branch shrines were founded across Japan. The Kumano Sanzan and the Kumano Kodō routes were inscribed as part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Sacred Sites and Pilgrimage Routes in the Kii Mountain Range" in 2004.
