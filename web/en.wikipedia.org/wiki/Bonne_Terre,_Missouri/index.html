Bonne Terre (French for "good earth") is a city in St. Francois County, Missouri, United States. The population was 6,903 at the 2020 census. It originated as a lead-mining settlement in Missouri's Old Lead Belt and is famous today for the flooded Bonne Terre Mine, operated as an underground diving and boat-tour attraction.

History

Lead was mined in the district from the French colonial era, but Bonne Terre itself was founded in 1864 when the St. Joseph Lead Company began deep mining there; the company town was first known as St. Joe and took the name Bonne Terre ("good soil/earth") from the French-speaking miners. St. Joseph Lead became the largest lead producer in the United States, and Bonne Terre's mines were among the most productive in the Old Lead Belt. The mines closed in 1961 as operations shifted west to the Viburnum Trend. The city is also home to the Eastern Reception, Diagnostic and Correctional Center, a state prison that houses Missouri's execution chamber; the state's executions are carried out there.

Bonne Terre Mine

The abandoned mine's lower levels filled with clear groundwater after pumping stopped, creating an enormous underground lake. Since 1981 it has operated as "Bonne Terre Mine — the world's largest man-made underground lake", offering guided boat tours and scuba diving along illuminated, air-filled upper levels and flooded shafts with submerged mining artefacts. National Geographic named it one of America's top adventure dives. Above ground, the related Missouri Mines State Historic Site at nearby Park Hills preserves the district's mining heritage.

Geography and demographics

According to the United States Census Bureau, the city has a total area of 4.10 square miles (10.62 km²), of which 4.04 square miles is land. Bonne Terre lies on U.S. Route 67 about 60 miles (97 km) southwest of St. Louis. The 2020 census recorded 6,903 residents; the population figure includes inmates of the correctional centre. The city is served by the North St. Francois County R-I School District.
