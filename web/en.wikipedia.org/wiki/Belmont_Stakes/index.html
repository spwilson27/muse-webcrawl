Belmont Stakes

The Belmont Stakes is an American Grade I Thoroughbred horse race held annually in early June at Belmont Park in Elmont, New York. It is the third and final leg of the American Triple Crown, following the Kentucky Derby and the Preakness Stakes. Run over 1½ miles (12 furlongs, 2.4 km) on dirt — the longest of the three races — it is known as "The Test of the Champion". The race was first run in 1867, making it the oldest of the Triple Crown events.

History

The race is named for August Belmont Sr., who financed the first running at Jerome Park in the Bronx. It moved to Morris Park and then to Belmont Park in 1905. The winner is draped with a blanket of white carnations, and the song "New York, New York" is traditionally sung. Because of its length, the Belmont has thwarted many Triple Crown bids: horses attempting the sweep must win three races in five weeks, culminating in the longest race most will ever run.

Memorable runnings

Secretariat's 31-length victory in 1973, completing the first Triple Crown in 25 years, is regarded as one of the greatest performances in racing history. Other famous editions include Affirmed's duel with Alydar in 1978, and American Pharoah (2015) and Justify (2018) ending long Triple Crown droughts. In the 2020s the race was temporarily relocated to Saratoga Race Course at a shorter distance while Belmont Park was rebuilt.
