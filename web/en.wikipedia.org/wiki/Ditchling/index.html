Ditchling is a village and civil parish in the Lewes District of East Sussex, England. It is located at the foot of the South Downs escarpment, around 7 miles (11 km) north of Brighton, overlooking the Weald. The village lies in the South Downs National Park. Nearby Ditchling Beacon is, at 248 metres (814 ft), one of the highest points on the South Downs and the third-highest point in Sussex.

History

Ditchling appears in the Domesday Book of 1086 as Dicelinges, held by William de Warenne. It was a royal manor in Saxon times and the site of an early minster church; the present St Margaret's Church dates mainly from the 13th century. Historically the village's chief industry was agriculture, with a busy market and trades supporting the surrounding downland farms.

In the early 20th century Ditchling became known for an influential community of artists and craftspeople. The sculptor and letter-cutter Eric Gill moved to the village in 1907, and in 1920 he co-founded the Guild of St Joseph and St Dominic with Hilary Pepler and Desmond Chute — a Catholic community of craftsmen combining worship with workshops in stonecutting, weaving, printing, and silversmithing. The calligrapher Edward Johnston, creator of the London Underground typeface, also lived in Ditchling. The village's artistic heritage is preserved by the Ditchling Museum of Art + Craft, which reopened in 2013.

Governance and amenities

Ditchling is represented on Lewes District Council and East Sussex County Council, and forms part of the Lewes parliamentary constituency. The village has a primary school, a museum, village hall, pubs, and shops, and is crossed by the South Downs Way's nearby route and long-distance paths including the Sussex Border Path.
