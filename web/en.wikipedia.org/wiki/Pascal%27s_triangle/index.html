Pascal's triangle

In mathematics, Pascal's triangle is a triangular array of the binomial coefficients. It is named after the French mathematician Blaise Pascal, who studied it in his Traité du triangle arithmétique (1654), although the triangle had been known for centuries in China, India, and Persia, where it is associated with mathematicians such as Yang Hui and Omar Khayyam.

Construction

The triangle is constructed by starting with 1 at the top; each subsequent row begins and ends with 1, and every interior entry is the sum of the two numbers directly above it. The first rows are:

          1
        1   1
      1   2   1
    1   3   3   1
  1   4   6   4   1
1   5  10  10   5   1

Properties

The entries in row n are the binomial coefficients C(n, k), the coefficients in the expansion of (a + b)n. The sum of the entries in row n is 2n. The triangle contains many patterns: its diagonals give the natural numbers, triangular numbers, and tetrahedral numbers; shallow diagonals sum to the Fibonacci numbers; and coloring the odd entries reveals a fractal pattern known as the Sierpiński triangle. Pascal's triangle also underlies the binomial distribution in probability and appears in combinatorics throughout mathematics.
