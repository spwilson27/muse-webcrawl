Fur

Fur is a thick growth of hair that covers the skin of almost all mammals. It consists of a combination of oily guard hair on top and underfur on bottom. The guard hair keeps moisture and dirt from reaching the underfur, while the underfur acts as an insulating blanket that keeps the animal warm.

Composition

Fur is composed of keratin, the same material as hair and nails. Fur-bearing mammals possess two distinct layers of hair: the underfur, or undercoat, which is short, dense and fine, and the guard hairs, which are longer and coarser and provide protection from abrasion and water.

Uses

Fur has been used by humans for clothing and ornamentation since prehistoric times. The fur trade was a major economic activity in North America and Russia during the colonial era, with pelts of beaver, mink, sable and fox highly valued. Fur farming became widespread in the 20th century, and the use of real fur in fashion has been controversial because of animal welfare concerns, leading many designers to use faux fur.

Fur in language and culture

The word "fur" also describes a coat made from the pelt of an animal with the hair left on, and fur clothing generally. Animals prized for their fur include the mink, fox, chinchilla and sable.
