Kuratowski's theorem

Kuratowski's theorem is a mathematical result in graph theory published in 1930 by the Polish mathematician Kazimierz Kuratowski. It characterizes planar graphs in terms of two forbidden subgraphs.

Statement

A finite graph is planar if and only if it does not contain a subgraph that is a subdivision of K5 (the complete graph on five vertices) or of K3,3 (the complete bipartite graph on six vertices, three of which connect to each of the other three).

Related results

Wagner's theorem gives the equivalent characterization using graph minors rather than subdivisions. Together, these results form the foundations of the theory of graph minors, later extended by Robertson and Seymour.
