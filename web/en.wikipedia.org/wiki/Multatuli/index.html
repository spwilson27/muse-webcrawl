Multatuli

Eduard Douwes Dekker
Pen name: Multatuli
Born: 2 March 1820, Amsterdam
Died: 19 February 1887, Nieder-Ingelheim, Germany
Notable work: Max Havelaar (1860)

Multatuli was the pen name of Eduard Douwes Dekker (1820–1887), a Dutch writer best known for the novel Max Havelaar (1860), which denounced the abuses of Dutch colonial rule in the Netherlands East Indies (now Indonesia). His pen name is Latin for "I have suffered much" (multa tuli).

Life

Dekker served as a colonial civil servant in the East Indies for nearly two decades. As assistant resident of Lebak in Java, he protested against the exploitation of the Javanese population by local regents under the Cultivation System and against the indifference of his superiors; his complaints led nowhere, and he resigned in 1856. Returning to Europe in poverty, he wrote Max Havelaar in a matter of weeks in Brussels.

Max Havelaar and influence

The novel — part autobiographical narrative, part polemic, framed by the voice of a complacent Amsterdam coffee broker — exposed the coffee auctions and forced cultivation that enriched the Netherlands at Javanese expense. It caused a sensation, is a classic of Dutch literature, and is often credited with inspiring the later "Ethical Policy" toward the colonies; its influence extended to Indonesian nationalist writers.

Later works

Multatuli's later writings include Love Letters (1861) and the seven volumes of Ideas (1862–1877), mixing essays, aphorisms, and plays. He spent his final years in Germany, dying at Nieder-Ingelheim in 1887. The Multatuli Museum stands at his birthplace in Amsterdam.
