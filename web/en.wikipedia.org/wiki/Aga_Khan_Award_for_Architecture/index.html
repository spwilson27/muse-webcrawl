Aga Khan Award for Architecture

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A past winning project of the Aga Khan Award — a mud-brick school in Burkina Faso]
A past winning project of the Aga Khan Award — a mud-brick school in Burkina Faso

The Aga Khan Award for Architecture is an architectural prize established by the Aga Khan IV in 1977 to identify and encourage building concepts that address the needs of societies in which Muslims have a significant presence. Given every three years to multiple projects and sharing US$1 million, it is among the largest architecture prizes in the world, distinguished by its focus on social impact, sustainability and cultural continuity rather than iconic form alone.

Scope and process

Projects range from slum upgrading and schools to skyscrapers and landscape restoration — past recipients include the Walled City of Lahore's conservation, Hassan Fathy-influenced village housing, the Petronas Towers, and modest rural schools. A master jury selects winners after on-site reviews by technical experts; the Award's documentation archive (Archnet) is a major open resource for architecture in the Muslim world.

Contrast with other prizes

Where the Pritzker Prize honours individual oeuvres, the Aga Khan Award honours projects and their communities — clients, builders and users share recognition — and it has repeatedly surfaced architects and regions outside the Western star system, from Balkrishna Doshi's early work to contemporary practices across Africa and Central Asia.
