The 7 July 2005 London bombings, often referred to as 7/7, were a series of four coordinated suicide attacks carried out by Islamist terrorists in London that targeted commuters travelling on the city's public transport system during the morning rush hour.

The attacks

At 08:50, three bombs exploded within fifty seconds of each other aboard London Underground trains: on the Circle line near Aldgate, on the Circle line at Edgware Road, and on the Piccadilly line between King's Cross and Russell Square. Nearly an hour later, at 09:47, a fourth bomb exploded on a double-decker bus in Tavistock Square. Fifty-two people were killed, in addition to the four bombers, and more than 700 were injured.

Perpetrators

The bombers were Mohammad Sidique Khan, Shehzad Tanweer, Germaine Lindsay and Hasib Hussain — British citizens, three of Pakistani descent and one a convert to Islam born in Jamaica. The attacks were claimed in the name of al-Qaeda, and the bombs were homemade organic-peroxide devices carried in rucksacks.

Aftermath

The bombings were the deadliest terrorist attacks in the United Kingdom since the 1988 bombing of Pan Am Flight 103. Two weeks later, on 21 July, a second series of attempted bombings failed when the devices did not detonate. Official inquests concluded in 2011, and the attacks prompted lasting changes to British counter-terrorism policing and transport security.
