Medupi Power Station

Location: Lephalale, Limpopo, South Africa
Operator: Eskom
Capacity: 4,764 MW (6 × 794 MW)

The Medupi Power Station is a dry-cooled coal-fired power plant operated by Eskom near Lephalale in Limpopo province, South Africa. With six units of 794 MW each, giving a total installed capacity of 4,764 MW, it is among the largest coal-fired stations in the world and the largest dry-cooled plant.

Construction

Construction began in 2007 with completion originally planned for 2015, but labour disputes, technical defects and disputes with contractors delayed the project for years and greatly increased its cost; the final unit entered commercial operation in 2021. Medupi uses supercritical boilers and is designed to be retrofitted with flue-gas desulphurisation. In October 2021 an explosion during hydrogen purging seriously damaged Unit 4.

Context

Together with its sister station Kusile, Medupi was intended to end South Africa's electricity shortages, though delays and breakdowns meant load-shedding continued. Its emissions have made it a focus of environmental criticism.
