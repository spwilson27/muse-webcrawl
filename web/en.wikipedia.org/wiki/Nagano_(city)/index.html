Nagano (Japanese: 長野市) is the capital and largest city of Nagano Prefecture, located in the Nagano Basin in the northern part of the prefecture, in the Chūbu region of Japan. The city grew up around Zenkō-ji, a seventh-century Buddhist temple that remains one of Japan's most important pilgrimage sites. Nagano is internationally known as the host city of the 1998 Winter Olympics.

History

The temple of Zenkō-ji was founded in 642 and houses what is traditionally regarded as the first Buddhist statue brought to Japan. The settlement that developed around the temple prospered as a post town and religious centre. During the Sengoku period the region was contested in the famous Battles of Kawanakajima between Takeda Shingen and Uesugi Kenshin. The modern city was incorporated in 1897 and expanded through mergers with surrounding towns and villages in the twentieth and twenty-first centuries.

1998 Winter Olympics

Nagano hosted the Winter Olympic Games in February 1998, the most recent Winter Games held in Asia before Pyeongchang 2018. Venues built for the Games included the M-Wave speed-skating oval and the White Ring arena. The Hokuriku Shinkansen was extended to Nagano in time for the event, placing the city roughly 80 minutes from Tokyo by bullet train.

Geography and economy

The city lies at the confluence of the Chikuma River — Japan's longest — and the Sai River, surrounded by mountains popular for skiing, including the Togakushi and Iizuna highlands. The region is a major producer of apples, grapes, and other fruit, and local specialties include oyaki dumplings and soba noodles. Tourism, precision manufacturing, and food processing anchor the economy.
