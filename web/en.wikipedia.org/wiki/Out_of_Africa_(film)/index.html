Out of Africa (film)

Directed by: Sydney Pollack

Starring: Meryl Streep, Robert Redford, Klaus Maria Brandauer

Released: 1985

Based on: Out of Africa by Isak Dinesen (Karen Blixen)

Awards: 7 Academy Awards, incl. Best Picture and Best Director

Out of Africa is a 1985 American epic romantic drama film directed by Sydney Pollack and starring Meryl Streep, Robert Redford and Klaus Maria Brandauer. Loosely based on the 1937 memoir Out of Africa by Isak Dinesen (the pen name of Karen Blixen), it tells of Blixen's years running a coffee plantation in British East Africa (Kenya) and her love affair with the hunter Denys Finch Hatton.

Plot

Danish aristocrat Karen Blixen marries her friend Baron Bror Blixen, partly for convenience, and moves to a farm at the foot of the Ngong Hills. The marriage falters amid Bror's infidelities, which leave Karen with syphilis, while she finds companionship and then love with the free-spirited big-game hunter Denys Finch Hatton. Drought, falling coffee prices and a warehouse fire destroy the farm, and Finch Hatton's death in a plane crash ends the African chapter of her life; she returns to Denmark to become a writer.

Production

The film was shot on location in Kenya and England, with David Watkin's cinematography and John Barry's Oscar-winning score — which incorporates Mozart's Clarinet Concerto — widely praised. Streep's Danish-accented performance earned her an Academy Award nomination.

Reception and awards

The film was a major commercial success, grossing about $227 million worldwide, and won seven Academy Awards from eleven nominations, including Best Picture, Best Director, Best Adapted Screenplay, Best Cinematography and Best Original Score. Critics praised its sweep and performances while some faulted its romanticised view of colonial Kenya.
