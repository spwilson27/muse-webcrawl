History of Dutch

History of Dutch
Stages: Old Dutch (c. 500–1150); Middle Dutch (1150–1500); Early Modern and Modern Dutch (1500– )
Family: Indo-European → Germanic → West Germanic

The history of Dutch traces the West Germanic language of the Low Countries from its origins in the Frankish dialects of the early Middle Ages to modern standard Dutch, spoken in the Netherlands, Belgium (Flanders) and Suriname.

Old and Middle Dutch

Old Dutch emerged around 500 CE from Old Low Franconian; the oldest known sentence, "Hebban olla vogala…", dates to about 1100. Middle Dutch was a cluster of regional written varieties — Flemish, Brabantine, Hollandic and Limburgish — used in a rich medieval literature including the Reynard the Fox epic Van den vos Reynaerde.

Standardisation

In the 16th–17th centuries, the Dutch Revolt, the printing press and the Statenvertaling Bible translation (1637) drove standardisation on a Hollandic-Brabantine base. Spelling was progressively regulated in the 19th and 20th centuries, and the Dutch Language Union (1980) now coordinates policy between the Netherlands, Belgium and Suriname. Afrikaans developed from 17th-century Dutch at the Cape.
