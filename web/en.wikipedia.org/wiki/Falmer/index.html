Falmer is a small village and civil parish in the Lewes District of East Sussex, England, situated on the South Downs about 5 miles (8 km) northeast of Brighton. The village lies in a dry valley and is best known today as the home of the University of Sussex campus and the Falmer Stadium, home ground of Brighton & Hove Albion Football Club.

History

The name Falmer derives from Old English, generally interpreted as "fallow (dark) pool" or "fallow mere," referring to the village pond, which remains a central feature. The parish church of St Laurence is a small flint church of Norman origin. Historically an agricultural village on the Lewes–Brighton road, Falmer was a centre of local controversy and celebration alike; its pond was the site of traditional ducking and village gatherings. The A27 Brighton bypass and the railway line between Brighton and Lewes pass close to the village, which has its own station.

University of Sussex

Much of the parish's land was given over in the 1960s to the new University of Sussex, designed by Sir Basil Spence with distinctive modernist buildings around Fulton Court and Falmer House, the latter Grade I listed. The university, founded in 1961, grew to serve well over 15,000 students and is the village's dominant institution, together with the adjacent campus of the University of Brighton at Falmer (home to its education and health faculties).

Falmer Stadium

After years of campaigning and planning inquiries, Brighton & Hove Albion built their new stadium at Falmer on land north of the village. The Falmer Stadium — known for sponsorship reasons as the American Express Community Stadium (the Amex) — opened in 2011 with an initial capacity of about 30,750, ending the club's nomadic period since leaving the Goldstone Ground in 1997. The stadium hosted matches at the 2015 Rugby World Cup and group matches of UEFA Women's Euro 2022.

Geography

The parish lies within the South Downs National Park. The village is split by the busy A27 road, and local governance has long dealt with balancing the small resident population (fewer than 300 people) against the tens of thousands of students and football supporters who arrive in the parish each week.
