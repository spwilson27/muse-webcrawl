Market trend

A market trend is the tendency of a financial market to move in a particular direction over time. A bull market is a prolonged period in which investment prices rise faster than their historical average, encouraging buying; a bear market is a period of falling prices and pessimism, conventionally defined as a decline of 20% or more from recent highs. A sideways or flat market shows little net movement.

Primary, secondary and minor trends

Following Dow Theory, analysts distinguish primary trends lasting a year or more, secondary trends — corrections lasting weeks or months within the primary trend — and minor day-to-day fluctuations. Trends are identified with tools of technical analysis such as moving averages, trend lines and momentum indicators, and trend-following is a major systematic trading strategy.

Causes

Trends arise from economic fundamentals, monetary policy, investor sentiment and herd behaviour. Behavioural finance explains persistent trends partly through cognitive biases such as conservatism and representativeness, and positive-feedback trading in which rising prices attract further buyers. Conversely, efficient-market theorists argue that apparent trends are largely unpredictable and cannot be reliably exploited after costs.

Secular trends

Long "secular" bull and bear markets can last a decade or more, such as the bull market from 1982 to 2000 or the long post-2009 expansion. Distinguishing a genuine trend from noise, and a trend reversal from a correction, is a central difficulty of investment analysis. Understanding trends helps traders interpret the prices produced by ordinary market orders and the total market value of listed companies.
