The Anxious Generation
Author: Jonathan Haidt
Published: March 26, 2024
Publisher: Penguin Press
Subtitle: How the Great Rewiring of Childhood Is Causing an Epidemic of Mental Illness

The Anxious Generation: How the Great Rewiring of Childhood Is Causing an Epidemic of Mental Illness is a 2024 book by American social psychologist Jonathan Haidt. It argues that the rapid shift in the early 2010s from a "play-based childhood" to a "phone-based childhood" — the near-universal adoption of smartphones and social media by adolescents — is the principal cause of a steep rise in depression, anxiety, self-harm and suicide among teenagers in the United States and other Western countries.

Argument

Haidt contends that two trends converged: the overprotection of children in the real world, which curtailed free play and independence from the 1990s onward, and their under-protection online, where social media platforms expose them to social comparison, cyberbullying, sleep deprivation and addiction-like engagement. Girls, he argues, are harmed chiefly by social media's effects on relationships and self-image; boys by disengagement into video games, pornography and online worlds. He dates the sharp inflection in adolescent mental-health statistics to roughly 2012, when smartphone ownership crossed 50 per cent among teens and front-facing cameras and apps such as Instagram reshaped social life.

Recommendations

The book closes with four proposed reforms: no smartphones before high school, no social media before age 16, phone-free schools, and far more unsupervised play and childhood independence.

Reception

The book debuted at number one on the New York Times nonfiction bestseller list and influenced legislative debates in several countries, including Australia's 2024 law setting 16 as the minimum age for social media accounts. Critics, including psychologist Candice Odgers in a prominent Nature review, argued that the evidence is largely correlational, that effect sizes are small, and that the book overstates a causal claim; Haidt and his collaborators responded that the timing and cross-national consistency of the trends support his thesis.
