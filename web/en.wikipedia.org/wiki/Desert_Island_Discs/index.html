Desert Island Discs

Desert Island Discs is a radio programme broadcast on BBC Radio 4. Guests, called "castaways", are invited to imagine themselves cast away on a desert island and to choose eight pieces of music, a book, and a luxury item to take with them. The programme was created by Roy Plomley and first broadcast on 29 January 1942, making it one of the longest-running radio programmes in the world.

Each guest is also given a copy of the Bible and the Complete Works of Shakespeare. At the end of the interview, the castaway must choose one of the eight recordings to save above the others, together with their book and luxury. Presenters have included Plomley himself (1942–1985), Michael Parkinson, Sue Lawley, Kirsty Young (2006–2018), and Lauren Laverne.

Notable castaways have ranged from prime ministers and royalty to scientists and entertainers; the most requested piece of music over the programme's history has frequently been reported as Beethoven's "Ode to Joy".
