Battle of Messines (1917)

The Battle of Messines (7–14 June 1917) was a battle of the First World War, fought by the British Second Army under Herbert Plumer against the German Fourth Army on the Western Front near the village of Messines (Mesen) in West Flanders, Belgium. The battle opened with the detonation of 19 huge mines beneath the German positions on the Messines–Wytschaete ridge, one of the largest non-nuclear explosions in history.

Background

The German-held ridge south of Ypres overlooked the British lines in the salient. Plumer's methodical "bite and hold" plan called for taking the ridge with overwhelming artillery support, preceded by the explosion of mines dug by tunnelling companies — some galleries having been started as early as 1915. Twenty-one mines were laid, charged with nearly a million pounds of explosive; nineteen were fired.

The battle

At 03:10 on 7 June the mines detonated, reportedly killing up to 10,000 German soldiers and leaving craters up to 80 feet deep; the shock was felt in London and may have been heard in Dublin. Nine infantry divisions advanced behind a creeping barrage, taking the ridge by mid-afternoon, and the remaining objectives fell by 14 June.

Aftermath

Messines was a model limited-objective victory and a morale boost after the disappointments of the Nivelle Offensive. It served as a preliminary to the Third Battle of Ypres (Passchendaele) later that summer. The largest surviving crater, the Spanbroekmolen crater, is preserved today as the "Pool of Peace".
