Tengwar

The Tengwar (Quenya for "letters") is a writing system created by J. R. R. Tolkien for the languages of his legendarium, principally the Elvish languages Quenya and Sindarin. Within the fiction, the script was invented by the Elf Fëanor in the First Age, developing the earlier Sarati of Rúmil. In the real world, Tolkien devised the Tengwar in the late 1920s and 1930s, and described them in Appendix E of The Lord of the Rings (1955).

Structure

The primary tengwar letters consist of a stem (telco) and a bow (lúva). Letters are organized into a systematic grid of four series (témar) and six grades (tyeller), where modifications of the basic shape correspond regularly to changes in pronunciation: doubling the bow denotes voicing, raising the stem denotes a fricative, and so on. Vowels are usually written as diacritic marks called tehtar placed over a preceding consonant or on a vowel carrier.

Modes

Because the letters map to sounds rather than to a fixed alphabet, the Tengwar can be adapted to many languages through different "modes" specifying which letter stands for which phoneme: there are modes for Quenya, Sindarin (both the tehta mode and the Mode of Beleriand, which uses full letters for vowels), and several modes for English, all devised or attested by Tolkien himself. Samples appear in The Hobbit and The Lord of the Rings — most famously the inscription on the One Ring, written in the Black Speech using Tengwar.

Use today

Fans use the Tengwar for calligraphy, tattoos, inscriptions, and typesetting of Tolkien's languages; several fonts and keyboard layouts exist, though the script is not encoded in Unicode and is handled by the volunteer ConScript Unicode Registry in the Private Use Area.
