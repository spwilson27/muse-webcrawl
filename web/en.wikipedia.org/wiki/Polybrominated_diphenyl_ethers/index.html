Polybrominated diphenyl ethers

Polybrominated diphenyl ethers (PBDEs) are a class of organobromine compounds formerly used widely as flame retardants in plastics, foams, textiles, and electronic equipment. There are 209 possible congeners, differing in the number and position of bromine atoms.

Environmental and health concerns

PBDEs are persistent organic pollutants: they resist degradation, accumulate in fat tissue, and have been detected in wildlife and human blood and breast milk worldwide. Several commercial mixtures (pentaBDE and octaBDE) were banned under the Stockholm Convention, and decaBDE was later added as well.

Regulation

The European Union restricted PBDEs in electronics under the RoHS directive, and manufacturers phased them out during the 2000s, replacing them with other flame retardants.
