Jim Henson

James Maury Henson (1936–1990) was an American puppeteer, animator, and filmmaker, best known as the creator of the Muppets. He founded the Jim Henson Company and directed films including The Dark Crystal and Labyrinth, and was a central creative force behind Sesame Street.

Early life and career

Henson was born in Greenville, Mississippi, and raised in Maryland. While a student at the University of Maryland he created the five-minute puppet show Sam and Friends for local television, which featured an early version of Kermit the Frog.

The Muppets

The Muppet Show (1976–1981) made Henson's characters international stars, followed by feature films such as The Muppet Movie (1979). His workshop pioneered new puppetry and animatronic techniques. Henson died suddenly in 1990 of a bacterial infection, aged 53.
