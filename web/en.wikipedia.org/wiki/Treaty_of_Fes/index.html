Treaty of Fes

The Treaty of Fes (also spelled Fez) was signed on 30 March 1912 between Sultan Abdelhafid of Morocco and the French government, establishing the French protectorate in Morocco. It followed the Agadir Crisis of 1911, in which France and Germany had contested influence over Morocco and settled their differences by the Treaty of Fez's Franco-German companion agreement of November 1911.

Terms

The Sultan accepted French "reforms" and the presence of a French Resident-General who would effectively direct foreign affairs, finance, and the military, while the Sultan retained nominal sovereignty and religious authority. Spain was separately assigned northern and southern zones of influence, formalized later in 1912, and Tangier was later made an international zone.

Consequences

News of the treaty provoked riots in Fes in April 1912, suppressed by French forces. The first Resident-General, Hubert Lyautey, built the protectorate's administration over the following years amid armed "pacification" campaigns in the interior. The protectorate lasted until Moroccan independence in 1956, when the treaty was abrogated.
