Nut allergy

A nut allergy is a hypersensitivity to dietary substances from nuts that causes an overreaction of the immune system. The term commonly covers allergy to tree nuts—such as almond, walnut, cashew, pistachio, hazelnut, pecan and Brazil nut—and is often discussed alongside peanut allergy, although the peanut is botanically a legume rather than a true nut. Nut allergies are among the most common food allergies and are a leading cause of fatal food-induced anaphylaxis.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms usually appear within minutes to a few hours after ingestion and range from mild oral itching, hives and swelling to severe reactions including wheezing, vomiting, a drop in blood pressure and anaphylaxis. Because trace contamination can trigger reactions, strict avoidance and careful reading of food labels are required.

Diagnosis and management

Diagnosis is based on clinical history supported by skin-prick testing, specific IgE blood tests and, where necessary, supervised oral food challenges. There is no cure for most tree nut allergies, though research into oral immunotherapy is ongoing. Management centres on avoidance and carrying epinephrine auto-injectors for emergency treatment of anaphylaxis. Unlike some childhood food allergies, tree nut allergy is usually lifelong, though a minority of children outgrow it.
