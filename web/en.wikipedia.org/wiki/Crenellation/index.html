Crenellation

Crenellation is the pattern of openings — called crenels or embrasures — along the top edge of a defensive wall, forming the characteristic tooth-like silhouette of a castle's battlement. The solid upright blocks between the openings are known as merlons.

Function

Crenellations allowed defenders to shoot arrows or other missiles through the gaps while taking cover behind the merlons. The design appears in fortifications from antiquity through the medieval period, and in many cultures, from European castles to the walls of ancient Mesopotamia and China.

Licence to crenellate

In medieval England, a royal licence to crenellate was required to fortify a manor house or castle. Historians debate whether such licences were strict military controls or largely matters of status and display.

Decorative use

After their military value faded, crenellations continued as a decorative architectural motif, notably in Gothic Revival buildings, where they evoke the romantic image of the medieval castle.
