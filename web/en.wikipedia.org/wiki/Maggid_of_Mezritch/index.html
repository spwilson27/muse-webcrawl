Maggid of Mezritch

Dov Ber ben Avraham of Mezeritch (c. 1704–1772), known as the Maggid of Mezritch ("Preacher of Mezritch"), was the disciple of the Baal Shem Tov, the founder of Hasidic Judaism, and succeeded him as leader of the movement. More than any other figure, he systematized Hasidic thought and spread it across Eastern Europe.

Life

Born in Volhynia, Dov Ber was a learned Talmudist and student of Kabbalah who, after meeting the Baal Shem Tov, became his devoted follower. Following the Baal Shem Tov's death in 1760, Dov Ber established his court at Mezritch (Mezhirichi) and later at Rovno, where he attracted a circle of brilliant disciples.

Legacy

The Maggid's students — including Elimelech of Lizhensk, Levi Yitzchok of Berditchev, Shneur Zalman of Liadi (founder of Chabad), and Zusha of Hanipol — carried Hasidism throughout Poland, Ukraine, and Russia, founding dynasties that shaped Eastern European Jewish life. His teachings were collected posthumously in works such as Maggid Devarav le-Ya'akov, emphasizing devekut (cleaving to God), the divine presence in all things, and the spiritual elevation of everyday life.
