Despicable Me is a 2010 American animated comedy film produced by Illumination Entertainment and distributed by Universal Pictures. Directed by Pierre Coffin and Chris Renaud, it was Illumination's debut feature. The film stars Steve Carell as Gru, a supervillain who adopts three orphaned girls — Margo, Edith and Agnes — as part of a scheme to steal a shrink ray and shrink the Moon, only to find himself changed by fatherhood.

Plot

Outdone by his rival Vector, who stole the Great Pyramid of Giza, Gru plans to shrink and steal the Moon, but the Bank of Evil refuses him a loan until he obtains a shrink ray. After Vector steals the ray from him, Gru adopts three orphan girls who sell cookies, using them to infiltrate Vector's fortress with cookie robots. The heist succeeds and Gru, funded at last, shrinks the Moon — but on the same day he has promised to attend the girls' ballet recital. When the Moon begins to return to normal size, Gru rescues the girls (kidnapped by Vector) and gives up the Moon, choosing to become their father in earnest.

Reception and legacy

The film received positive reviews and grossed $543 million worldwide on a $69 million budget. Its gibberish-speaking yellow sidekicks, the Minions, became a pop-culture phenomenon. The film launched one of the most successful animated franchises of all time, followed by Despicable Me 2 (2013), Despicable Me 3 (2017) and Despicable Me 4 (2024), plus the prequel spin-offs Minions (2015) and Minions: The Rise of Gru (2022).
