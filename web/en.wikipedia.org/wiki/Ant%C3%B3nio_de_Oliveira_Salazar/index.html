António de Oliveira Salazar (1889–1970) was a Portuguese statesman, economist and academic who served as Prime Minister of Portugal from 1932 to 1968. He founded and led the Estado Novo ("New State"), an authoritarian corporatist regime that governed Portugal until 1974.

Rise to power

An economics professor at the University of Coimbra, Salazar was appointed Minister of Finance in 1928 after the military coup of 1926, stabilising Portugal's finances. As prime minister he promulgated the constitution of 1933 that formalised the Estado Novo.

Rule

Salazar kept Portugal neutral in the Second World War and was a founding member of NATO, but his regime suppressed political opposition through censorship and the PIDE political police, and fought costly colonial wars in Africa from 1961. Incapacitated by an accident in 1968, he was replaced by Marcelo Caetano; the regime fell in the Carnation Revolution of 1974.
