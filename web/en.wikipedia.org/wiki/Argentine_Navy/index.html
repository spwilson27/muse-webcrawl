The Argentine Navy (Spanish: Armada de la República Argentina) is the naval service branch of the Armed Forces of Argentina. It traces its origins to the naval forces organized during the May Revolution of 1810 and is traditionally associated with Admiral William Brown, an Irish-born officer regarded as the father of the navy.

History

Argentine naval forces fought during the war of independence and the Cisplatine War against Brazil in the 1820s. In the late 19th and early 20th centuries Argentina built a substantial fleet, including dreadnought battleships, entering a naval arms race with Chile and Brazil. The navy expanded its naval aviation and submarine arms over the 20th century.

Falklands War

During the 1982 Falklands War against the United Kingdom, the Argentine Navy deployed its carrier ARA Veinticinco de Mayo and the cruiser ARA General Belgrano, which was sunk by the British submarine HMS Conqueror with the loss of 323 lives — the largest single loss of life in the war. Argentine naval aircraft, including Super Étendards firing Exocet missiles, sank HMS Sheffield and the container ship Atlantic Conveyor.

Modern force

The modern navy operates destroyers, frigates, corvettes, patrol vessels, submarines, and naval aviation, headquartered principally at Puerto Belgrano near Bahía Blanca. In November 2017 the submarine ARA San Juan was lost with all 44 crew in the South Atlantic, the navy's worst peacetime disaster.
