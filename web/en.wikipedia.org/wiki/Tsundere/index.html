Etymologytsun tsun (aloof, blunt) + dere dere (lovestruck, affectionate)
OriginJapanese otaku slang, popularised c. 2000s

Tsundere (Japanese: ツンデレ; pronounced [tsɯndeɾe]) is a Japanese term for a character development process that depicts a character with an initially harsh, blunt, cold, or hostile personality who gradually reveals a warmer, friendlier side over time. The word is derived from the terms tsun tsun (ツンツン), meaning "to turn away in disgust" or "aloof", and dere dere (デレデレ), meaning "to become lovestruck" or "affectionate".

Usage

The term emerged from the community around visual novels and dating simulation games in the early 2000s and spread through anime, manga and light-novel fandom. It is commonly associated with romantic comedy storylines in which the character's gruff exterior conceals embarrassment about their feelings. Common traits include verbal defensiveness ("It's not like I like you or anything"), blushing, and tsundere characters are often given twin-tail hairstyles in visual media. Related archetype terms include yandere, kuudere and dandere.

Criticism and discussion

Commentators within and outside Japan have debated whether the archetype glamourises poor communication in relationships; others treat it as a harmless stock-character convention. The word has been included in Japanese dictionaries and is occasionally borrowed into other languages' fandom slang.
