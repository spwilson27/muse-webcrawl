List of federally recognized tribes in the United States

The List of federally recognized tribes in the United States enumerates the Native American tribes, nations, bands, pueblos, and Alaska Native villages that maintain a formal government-to-government relationship with the United States. As of the mid-2020s there are 574 federally recognized tribal entities, published periodically by the Bureau of Indian Affairs in the Federal Register.

Background

Federal recognition confers tribal sovereignty, eligibility for federal services, and certain immunities. Tribes may be recognized by treaty, statute, executive order, or through the federal acknowledgment process administered since 1978. The largest by enrollment include the Navajo Nation, the Cherokee Nation, and the Choctaw Nation of Oklahoma; the Navajo Nation also holds the largest reservation, spanning parts of Arizona, Utah, and New Mexico.

Alaska and regions

More than 200 of the recognized entities are Alaska Native villages. Others are organised historically by region — the Eastern Woodlands, Plains, Southwest, Great Basin, Plateau, Northwest Coast, and California. Separate lists exist for state-recognized tribes and for tribes whose recognition was terminated during the mid-twentieth-century termination era.
