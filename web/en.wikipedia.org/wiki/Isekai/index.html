Isekai

Meaning"Different world" (Japanese: 異世界)
MediumsLight novels, manga, anime, games
SubgenresReincarnation, summoning, trapped in a game

Isekai (Japanese: 異世界, "different world") is a genre of Japanese fantasy in which a protagonist from Earth is transported to, reborn in, or trapped in a parallel fantasy world, often one resembling a role-playing game. It became one of the dominant genres of light novels, manga and anime in the 2010s.

Characteristics

Common devices include death and reincarnation (sometimes jokingly via a truck, "Truck-kun"), summoning as a hero, virtual-reality games that become real, and "cheat" abilities that make the hero overpowered. Many stories feature game-like status screens, levels and guilds. Variants include reverse isekai (fantasy beings arriving in modern Earth) and slower "slice of life" farming or cooking stories.

History

Antecedents include folk tales such as Urashima Tarō and Western portal fantasies like Alice's Adventures in Wonderland and Narnia. Modern foundations were laid by works such as Aura Battler Dunbine (1983), El-Hazard and 1990s series, then by .hack and Sword Art Online (trapped-in-a-game). Web novel sites like Shōsetsuka ni Narō fuelled a boom with Mushoku Tensei, Re:Zero, That Time I Got Reincarnated as a Slime, Overlord and KonoSuba.

Criticism

Critics note formulaic plots and wish-fulfilment, while defenders point to inventive worldbuilding and its reflection of escapist desires in contemporary Japan. The genre's saturation led some contests to ban isekai submissions.
