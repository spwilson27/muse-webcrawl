Achatinidae

Achatinidae is a family of medium to large tropical land snails, terrestrial pulmonate gastropod molluscs in the superfamily Achatinoidea. The family is native mainly to Africa south of the Sahara and includes the giant African land snails — some of the largest land snails in the world — in genera such as Achatina, Lissachatina and Archachatina.

Description

Achatinids have elongated, often conical shells and can reach exceptional sizes: the giant African snail Lissachatina fulica commonly grows to about 20 cm shell length, and Achatina achatina is among the largest of all living land snails. They are herbivorous and detritivorous, feeding on living and decaying plant matter, and are simultaneous hermaphrodites that lay clutches of eggs in soil.

Distribution and invasiveness

Although centred on Africa, several species have been transported around the tropics as food animals, pets or by accident. Lissachatina fulica in particular is listed among the world's worst invasive species: it damages crops and native snail faunas, competes with local species and can carry parasites such as the rat lungworm. Subfamilies recognised within Achatinidae include Achatininae and others, with about 200 species in roughly 20 genera.

Human uses

In West Africa, giant achatinids are gathered and farmed as an important source of protein ("Congo meat"), and they are popular, if controversial, terrarium pets in Europe and elsewhere. In many countries their import is restricted because of invasion and disease risk.
