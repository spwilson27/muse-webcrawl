Albedo (from Latin albedo, "whiteness") is the fraction of sunlight that a surface or body reflects back into space. It is a dimensionless quantity between 0 and 1 and a key concept in climatology, astronomy and remote sensing. Surfaces with high albedo, such as fresh snow and ice, reflect most incoming radiation; dark surfaces such as oceans and forests absorb most of it.

Types

The Bond albedo measures the total fraction of electromagnetic radiation of all wavelengths reflected by an astronomical body, weighted by the solar spectrum; geometric albedo compares brightness at zero phase angle with an idealised flat disc. For Earth, clouds, aerosols and surface cover combine to give an average Bond albedo of about 0.30.

Climate role

Albedo is central to the Earth's energy budget and to feedbacks such as the ice–albedo feedback, in which warming melts reflective ice, exposing darker ocean and accelerating further warming. Changes in land use, soot deposition, clouds and aerosols all alter planetary albedo, and proposals for solar geoengineering include increasing it deliberately.
