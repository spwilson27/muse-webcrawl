Canary Wharf is a London Underground station at Canary Wharf in the London Borough of Tower Hamlets. It is on the Jubilee line, between Heron Quays-adjacent Canada Water and North Greenwich stations, and is in Travelcard Zone 2. It is distinct from the nearby Docklands Light Railway stations of the same and similar names; the Elizabeth line station (Canary Wharf railway station) is a separate station nearby.

Design

The station was designed by Sir Norman Foster's practice, Foster and Partners, and constructed in a drained arm of the North Dock of the West India Docks. It is one of the largest stations on the Underground network, built to handle the crowds of the expanding Docklands financial district: the ticket hall and platforms lie in a vast concrete box some 24 metres deep, covered by a landscaped park (Jubilee Park) with curved glass entrance canopies that flood the interior with daylight.

History

Canary Wharf station opened on 17 September 1999 as part of the Jubilee Line Extension from Green Park to Stratford. It quickly became one of the busiest stations outside central London, and in the early 2020s it vied with stations such as Stratford and London Bridge among the most-used on the network, with entries and exits in the tens of millions per year.
