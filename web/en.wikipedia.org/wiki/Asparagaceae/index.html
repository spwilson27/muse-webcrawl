Asparagaceae

Kingdom: Plantae
Order: Asparagales
Family: Asparagaceae Juss.
Genera: approx. 150; species approx. 2,500

The family includes both asparagus and agave plants.

Asparagaceae, known as the asparagus family, is a family of flowering plants in the order Asparagales. Under the APG III and APG IV classification systems the family is circumscribed broadly, absorbing many formerly separate families such as the Agavaceae, Hyacinthaceae and Ruscaceae; it now includes roughly 150 genera and about 2,500 species distributed worldwide, with particular diversity in Mexico, the Mediterranean and southern Africa.

Subfamilies

The APG system recognises seven subfamilies:

  • Agavoideae — agaves, yuccas, hostas and tuberoses.
  • Aphyllanthoideae — the single species Aphyllanthes monspeliensis.
  • Asparagoideae — asparagus and the genus Hemiphylacus.
  • Brodiaeoideae — brodiaeas and relatives, formerly Themidaceae.
  • Lomandroideae — lomandras and cordylines.
  • Nolinoideae — lily-of-the-valley, dracaena, sansevieria and butcher's broom.
  • Scilloideae — hyacinths, bluebells, scillas and ornithogalums.

Characteristics

Members are mostly perennial herbs, shrubs or climbers, often with rhizomes, bulbs or tuberous roots. Flowers typically have six undifferentiated tepals, six stamens and a superior or inferior ovary, and the fruit is a berry or capsule. Many species have reduced leaves, with photosynthesis carried out by flattened stems called cladodes, as in asparagus.

Economic importance

The family's best-known crop is garden asparagus (Asparagus officinalis). Agaves furnish sisal fibre, tequila and mezcal; other members yield the ornamental hostas, hyacinths, dracaenas and snake plants, and saponin-rich species have traditional medicinal uses.
