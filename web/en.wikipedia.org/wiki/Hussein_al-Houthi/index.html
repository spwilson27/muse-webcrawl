Hussein Badreddin al-Houthi (Arabic: حسين بدر الدين الحوثي; 20 August 1959 – 10 September 2004) was a Yemeni Zaydi religious and political leader who founded the movement that became known as the Houthis (Ansar Allah). A former member of Yemen's parliament, he led an uprising against the Yemeni government in 2004 and was killed by Yemeni forces in September of that year. The Houthi movement takes its name from his family.

Early life and career

Al-Houthi was born in 1959 in the northern governorate of Saada, the heartland of Yemen's Zaydi Shia community, into a prominent scholarly family; his father, Badreddin al-Houthi, was a leading Zaydi cleric. He received a traditional religious education and in the 1990s became involved with the "Believing Youth" (al-Shabab al-Mu'min) movement, which sought to revive Zaydi religious teaching in the north.

From 1993 to 1997 al-Houthi served in the Yemeni House of Representatives as a member of the al-Haqq party. He reportedly studied in Sudan and Iran during this period and grew increasingly critical of the Yemeni government of President Ali Abdullah Saleh, accusing it of corruption and of subservience to the United States and Israel. His lectures popularized the movement's slogan — the sarkha — "God is great, death to America, death to Israel, curse on the Jews, victory to Islam."

2004 uprising and death

In June 2004, after the government moved to arrest him and banned the chanting of his slogan, al-Houthi's supporters took up arms in Saada, beginning the conflict known as the Houthi insurgency or Saada Wars. After several months of fighting, Yemeni government forces killed al-Houthi on 10 September 2004 in the Marran mountains. His body was later returned to his family and reburied in 2013.

Leadership of the movement passed to his father and then to his younger brother, Abdul-Malik al-Houthi, under whom the Houthis expanded their power, seizing the capital Sanaa in 2014–15 and becoming a principal party in the Yemeni civil war.
