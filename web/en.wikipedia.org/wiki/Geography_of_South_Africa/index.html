The geography of South Africa covers the country at the southern tip of Africa, bordered by Namibia, Botswana, and Zimbabwe to the north, Mozambique and Eswatini to the northeast, and by the Atlantic and Indian Oceans to the west and east. South Africa completely surrounds the enclaved kingdom of Lesotho.

Topography

Much of the interior is a high, rolling plateau — the Highveld — at elevations between 1,000 and 2,100 metres, ringed by the Great Escarpment, whose best-known section is the Drakensberg in the east. The Kalahari Desert occupies the northwest, while the Cape Floristic Region in the southwest is famed for its fynbos vegetation. Table Mountain overlooks Cape Town at the southwestern tip.

Climate and rivers

Climates range from Mediterranean in the southwest to subtropical in the northeast and arid in the northwest. The longest river is the Orange River, which forms the northern border with Namibia; other major rivers include the Limpopo and the Vaal.
