Symptom

A symptom (from Greek σύμπτωμα, "accident, misfortune, that which befalls") is a departure from normal function or feeling which is noticed by a patient, indicating the presence of disease or abnormality. Symptoms are subjective, observed by the patient, and cannot be measured directly, in contrast to signs, which are objectively observable by others, such as a clinician.

Types

Symptoms may be chronic, relapsing, or remitting. They may be specific, pointing strongly toward a particular disease, or non-specific, such as fatigue or nausea, which occur in many conditions. Common examples include pain, fever as felt by the patient, cough, itching, and dizziness. A condition that produces no noticeable symptoms is called asymptomatic.

Diagnosis

Taking a history of the presenting symptoms is a central part of medical diagnosis. Clinicians characterize symptoms by their onset, duration, location, severity, and associated features, and combine this history with physical examination findings (signs) and investigations to form a differential diagnosis.

Terminology

The term is sometimes applied more loosely to physical findings discovered by examination. A cardinal symptom is one that is especially important in reaching a diagnosis. Symptomatology is the branch of medicine concerned with symptoms.
