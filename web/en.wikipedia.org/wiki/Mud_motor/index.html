Mud motor

A mud motor (or drilling motor) is a progressive cavity positive displacement pump placed in the drill string to provide additional power to the bit while drilling. The mud motor uses drilling fluid (commonly called mud) to create eccentric motion in the power section of the motor which is transferred as concentric power to the drill bit. Mud motors use different rotor and stator configurations to provide optimum performance for the desired drilling operation, typically increasing the number of lobes and length of power section for greater horsepower.

Because the bit can rotate without the entire drill string rotating, mud motors are essential for directional drilling: with a slightly bent housing, the driller can "slide" the bottom-hole assembly to build angle while the motor alone turns the bit. In rotary mode, the string is also turned from surface, and the motor then adds bit speed and torque.

The Moineau-style power section consists of a helically shaped steel rotor turning inside a rubber-lined stator with one more lobe than the rotor; pumped mud forces the rotor to nutate and turn. Motors are standard in horizontal and extended-reach wells, and are often combined with measurement-while-drilling (MWD) tools in the bottom-hole assembly.
