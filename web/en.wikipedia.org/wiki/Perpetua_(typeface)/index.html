Perpetua (typeface)

Perpetua
Category: Serif
Designer: Eric Gill
Foundry: Monotype
Date released: 1929

Perpetua is a serif typeface designed by the English sculptor and letter-cutter Eric Gill for the British Monotype Corporation. Commissioned in the mid-1920s and first released in 1929, it was designed as a book typeface with letterforms based on classical Roman inscriptional capitals.

Design

Perpetua has a delicate, carved quality, with small serifs, a relatively low x-height and elegant italics that are more cursive than sloped roman. The design reflects Gill's background as a stonecutter; its capitals derive from the inscriptional letters he cut in stone. The typeface was first used for a privately printed book, The Passion of Perpetua and Felicity, from which it took its name.

Usage and related designs

Perpetua has been widely used for fine book printing, titles and certificates. Its companion titling face, Perpetua Titling, provides lighter inscriptional capitals. Gill Sans, designed by Gill in the same period, shares some of its proportions. Digital versions of Perpetua have been issued by Monotype and shipped with Microsoft software, giving the face renewed popularity.
