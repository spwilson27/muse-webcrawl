Jung District (Korean: 중구, Jung-gu) is one of the 25 districts (gu) of Seoul, South Korea. Located in the historic centre of the city on the southern bank of the Han River area north of Namsan, it is the smallest district in Seoul by population but contains many of the capital's most famous landmarks, shopping districts, and financial institutions.

Geography

Jung District lies at the heart of Seoul, bordered by Jongno District to the north, Dongdaemun and Seongdong to the east, Yongsan to the west, and Mapo to the southwest. Mount Namsan and the N Seoul Tower dominate its southern edge, while the Cheonggyecheon stream runs along its northern boundary.

Landmarks

The district includes Myeong-dong, one of South Korea's best-known shopping and tourism areas; the historic Deoksugung palace; Seoul City Hall and Seoul Plaza; Namdaemun Market, the country's oldest and largest traditional market; Dongdaemun Market; and the Bank of Korea headquarters. Myeongdong Cathedral, completed in 1898, is a landmark of Korean Catholicism.

Economy

Jung District is a major centre of finance and commerce, hosting the headquarters of many banks and corporations, along with large department stores such as Lotte and Shinsegae, whose main stores stand near Namdaemun and Myeong-dong.

Transport

Seoul Station, the city's principal railway terminal, lies on the district's western edge. Numerous subway lines — including Lines 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 — cross the district, making it one of the most connected areas of the capital.
