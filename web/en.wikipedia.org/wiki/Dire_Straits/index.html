Dire Straits were a British rock band formed in London in 1977 by Mark Knopfler (lead vocals, lead guitar), his brother David Knopfler (rhythm guitar), John Illsley (bass) and Pick Withers (drums). They became one of the best-selling music acts of all time, with worldwide sales of more than 100 million records.

Career

The band's self-titled debut album (1978) contained the hit single "Sultans of Swing", showcasing Mark Knopfler's distinctive fingerstyle guitar playing and Dylan-influenced songwriting. Follow-up albums Communiqué (1979), Making Movies (1980) — with "Romeo and Juliet" — and Love over Gold (1982) established them as a major act, and the live album Alchemy (1984) captured their stadium-era sound.

Their fifth album, Brothers in Arms (1985), was an international blockbuster: it topped charts worldwide, won Grammy Awards, and was one of the first albums aimed at the new compact disc market, becoming one of the best-selling albums in history. Its singles included "Money for Nothing" (whose video was a landmark of early MTV), "Walk of Life" and the title track. The final studio album, On Every Street (1991), was followed by a gruelling world tour, after which Knopfler quietly dissolved the band in 1995. Dire Straits were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2018.
