Circle group

The circle group, denoted T (or 𝕋), is a mathematical group consisting of all complex numbers with absolute value 1, under the operation of multiplication. It is the group of rotations of a circle and is a fundamental example of a Lie group and an abelian group.

Definition and properties

As a set, T = { z ∈ ℂ : |z| = 1 }. The map θ ↦ eiθ identifies the circle group with the quotient group ℝ/ℤ of the real numbers by the integers. The circle group is isomorphic to the special orthogonal group SO(2), the group of rotations in the plane.

The circle group plays a central role in harmonic analysis: its continuous homomorphisms into itself are the maps z ↦ zn for integers n, and Fourier series can be understood as the representation theory of T. Its finite subgroups are the cyclic groups of roots of unity, and its closed subgroups are either finite cyclic groups or T itself.
