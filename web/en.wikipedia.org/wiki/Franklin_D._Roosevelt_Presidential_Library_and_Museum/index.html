Franklin D. Roosevelt Presidential Library and Museum

The Franklin D. Roosevelt Presidential Library and Museum is the presidential library of Franklin D. Roosevelt, the 32nd president of the United States. Located on the grounds of Springwood, the Roosevelt family estate in Hyde Park, New York, it was the first presidential library in the United States and set the model for the presidential library system that followed.

History

Roosevelt conceived the library himself: he designed the Dutch Colonial Revival fieldstone building, donated 16 acres of the family estate, and raised private funds for its construction. Built in 1939–40, it was dedicated on June 30, 1941, with Roosevelt in attendance — the only presidential library used by a sitting president, who kept an office there during his later terms.

Roosevelt wished to gather all his papers in one place and make them available to scholars, breaking with the tradition under which presidents' papers were personal property, often dispersed or lost. The precedent led Congress to pass the Presidential Libraries Act of 1955.

Collections and museum

The library holds some 17 million pages of documents, including Roosevelt's White House files, personal and family papers, and the records of his pre-presidential career, administered by the National Archives and Records Administration. The museum displays Roosevelt's 1936 Ford Phaeton with hand controls, his Oval Office desk, a recreation of his White House map room, and exhibits on the Great Depression, the New Deal and the Second World War. A major renovation of the permanent museum exhibits was completed in 2013.

Grounds

The library stands beside Springwood, Roosevelt's birthplace and lifelong home, which is preserved as the Home of Franklin D. Roosevelt National Historic Site. Roosevelt and his wife Eleanor are buried in the rose garden between the house and the library.
