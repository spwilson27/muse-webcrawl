Cleopatra (1963 film)

Cleopatra is a 1963 American epic historical drama film directed by Joseph L. Mankiewicz and produced by Walter Wanger for 20th Century Fox. It stars Elizabeth Taylor as Cleopatra VII of Egypt, Richard Burton as Mark Antony, and Rex Harrison as Julius Caesar. The film chronicles Cleopatra's relationships with Caesar and Antony and her struggle to resist the political expansion of Rome.

Production

The production became notorious as one of the most troubled and expensive in cinema history. Filming began in England in 1960 under director Rouben Mamoulian, but Taylor's illnesses, bad weather, and the loss of footage led to the shoot being shut down and restarted in Rome under Mankiewicz in 1961, with the script rewritten daily. The final cost reached approximately $31 million (with additional marketing and overhead costs often cited around $44 million), making it the most expensive film ever made at the time and nearly bankrupting Fox, despite becoming the highest-grossing film of 1963. The studio survived in part by selling its backlot — the land that became Century City — and through the success of The Sound of Music (1965).

The highly publicised love affair between Taylor and Burton, both married to others at the time, turned the production into a global media sensation and is often cited as the beginning of modern paparazzi celebrity culture.

Reception and legacy

The film premiered on 12 June 1963 in New York, running over four hours in its roadshow version before being cut. Reviews were mixed, praising the spectacle and Harrison's performance while criticising the script and pacing. At the 36th Academy Awards, Cleopatra was nominated for nine awards, including Best Picture, and won four: Best Art Direction (Color), Best Cinematography (Color), Best Costume Design (Color), and Best Visual Effects. Mankiewicz's much longer intended cut was later partially reconstructed, and the film's reputation has grown as a landmark of the Hollywood epic era.
