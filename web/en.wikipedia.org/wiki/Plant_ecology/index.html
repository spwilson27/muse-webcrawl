Plant ecology

Plant ecology is a subdiscipline of ecology that studies the distribution and abundance of plants, the effects of environmental factors upon the abundance of plants, and the interactions among plants and between plants and other organisms. Related fields include vegetation science, plant geography, and physiological ecology.

Overview

Plants are sessile organisms, so their ecology is strongly shaped by local conditions: light, water, nutrients, temperature, and soil. Plant ecologists study topics such as succession, competition, herbivory, pollination, seed dispersal, and the structure of plant communities and biomes.

History

The discipline emerged in the late nineteenth century, influenced by plant geographers such as Alexander von Humboldt and by the work of Eugenius Warming, whose 1895 book on plant communities is often considered the founding text of ecology. Frederic Clements developed theories of succession and climax communities, while Henry Gleason advanced the individualistic concept of species distributions.

Applications

Plant ecology informs conservation biology, restoration ecology, forestry, rangeland management, and predictions of how vegetation will respond to climate change and invasive species.
