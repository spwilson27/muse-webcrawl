The MITRE Corporation (stylized as MITRE) is an American not-for-profit organization with dual headquarters in Bedford, Massachusetts, and McLean, Virginia. It manages federally funded research and development centers (FFRDCs) supporting various U.S. government agencies in the aviation, defense, healthcare, homeland security, judiciary and cybersecurity fields, among others.

History

MITRE was founded in 1958 as a spin-off of the computer systems division of the MIT Lincoln Laboratory, which had developed the SAGE air-defense system. Its charter deliberately modeled it as a systems-engineering organization working solely in the public interest, forbidden from manufacturing products or competing with industry, so that it could advise the government without conflicts of interest. Early work included the joint surveillance system for air traffic control and support to military command-and-control programs.

Work

MITRE operates several FFRDCs, including centers for the Department of Defense, the Federal Aviation Administration (the Center for Advanced Aviation System Development), the Internal Revenue Service and Treasury, the Department of Homeland Security, the federal judiciary, and the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (the CMS Alliance to Modernize Healthcare). It conducts independent research and operates MITRE Labs to apply its expertise to problems across sponsors.

The organization is widely known in cybersecurity for maintaining the CVE (Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures) list of publicly known security vulnerabilities, launched in 1999, and for the MITRE ATT&CK framework, a knowledge base of adversary tactics and techniques first published in 2015 that has become a standard reference for threat-informed defense. MITRE also maintains CWE, CAPEC, and the Engage framework, and runs the Center for Threat-Informed Defense.

Organization

As a nonprofit, MITRE is governed by a board of trustees and is chartered to operate in the public interest. It employs roughly 9,000 scientists, engineers and support staff at sites across the United States and abroad. Because its FFRDC status bars it from commercial work, its revenue derives almost entirely from its federal sponsors, amounting to around $2 billion annually in recent years.
