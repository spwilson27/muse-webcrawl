Ash'ari

Founder: Abu al-Hasan al-Ash'ari (873–935/6)

Region: Iraq; later the wider Sunni world

Type: School of Islamic theology (kalam)

Ash'arism (Arabic: الأشعرية, al-Ashʿariyya) is a school of Islamic theology founded by the Arab Muslim scholar, reformer and theologian Abu al-Hasan al-Ash'ari in the 10th century. It became, together with Maturidism, one of the two principal orthodox schools of Sunni kalam, and remains influential across the Muslim world, especially within the Shafi'i and Maliki legal traditions.

History

Al-Ash'ari was trained in the rationalist Mu'tazilite school under his stepfather al-Jubba'i, whose doctrine he publicly renounced around 912/913, reportedly after a dream of the Prophet Muhammad. He spent the rest of his life defending traditional Sunni doctrine using the rational methods of kalam. His successors — notably al-Baqillani, al-Juwayni and above all Abu Hamid al-Ghazali — systematised and spread the school, and Ash'arism became the dominant theology of the Sunni heartlands.

Doctrines

Characteristic Ash'ari positions include: the affirmation of God's attributes as real and eternal yet "without asking how" (bila kayf), between Mu'tazilite negation and literalist anthropomorphism; the uncreatedness of the Qur'an; occasionalism — the doctrine that God is the direct creator of every event, so created things have no intrinsic causal power; and the theory of kasb ("acquisition"), by which human acts are created by God but acquired by the human agent, preserving moral responsibility.

People

Major Ash'ari figures include al-Baqillani, Ibn Furak, al-Juwayni, al-Ghazali, al-Shahrastani, Fakhr al-Din al-Razi and later scholars such as al-Taftazani and al-Sanusi.
