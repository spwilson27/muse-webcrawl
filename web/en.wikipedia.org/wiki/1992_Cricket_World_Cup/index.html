The 1992 Cricket World Cup (officially the Benson & Hedges World Cup 1992) was the fifth edition of the Cricket World Cup, jointly hosted by Australia and New Zealand from 22 February to 25 March 1992. It was won by Pakistan, captained by Imran Khan, who defeated England by 22 runs in the final at the Melbourne Cricket Ground.

Imran Khan's "cornered tigers" lifted the trophy at the Melbourne Cricket Ground.

Innovations

The tournament introduced many features that became standard in one-day cricket: coloured clothing, white balls, day/night matches under lights, and fielding restrictions in the early overs. It also featured the controversial rain rule — a precursor of the Duckworth–Lewis method — which was widely blamed for South Africa's semi-final exit, when their target against England was recalculated to require 22 runs from a single ball after a rain delay.

Tournament summary

For the first time, all nine teams played each other once in a single round-robin group, with the top four advancing to the semi-finals. South Africa competed in their first World Cup following the end of apartheid-era isolation. Pakistan, having nearly been eliminated in the group stage and saved by a rained-out match against England, surged late in the tournament behind the batting of Inzamam-ul-Haq and Javed Miandad and the bowling of Wasim Akram and Mushtaq Ahmed.

Final

Pakistan made 249 for 6, with Imran Khan scoring 72 and Inzamam 42. England's chase faltered when Wasim Akram dismissed Allan Lamb and Chris Lewis with consecutive deliveries; England were bowled out for 227. Wasim was named player of the match, and Imran Khan famously compared his team to "cornered tigers" in his victory speech.
