Western Norway (Norwegian: Vestlandet) is the region along the Atlantic coast of southern Norway, comprising the counties of Rogaland, Vestland and Møre og Romsdal. It is famous for its fjords — including the Sognefjord, the Hardangerfjord and the UNESCO-listed Geirangerfjord — which cut deep into the mountainous coastline.

Geography and economy

The region's cities include Bergen, historically a Hanseatic trading port, and Stavanger, the centre of Norway's offshore petroleum industry. Fishing, shipping, maritime industries, hydroelectric power and tourism are all economically important, and the rainy coastal climate is among the wettest in Europe.

Language

Western Norway is the heartland of Nynorsk, one of Norway's two written standards, created by Ivar Aasen in the 19th century from western rural dialects.
