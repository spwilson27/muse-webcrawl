Catiline

Lucius Sergius Catilina (c. 108 BC – January 62 BC), known in English as Catiline, was a Roman politician and soldier best known for the Catilinarian conspiracy, an attempt to overthrow the Roman Republic in 63 BC.

Catiline
Born: c. 108 BC, Rome
Died: January 62 BC, near Pistoria
Known for: Catilinarian conspiracy
Opponent: Cicero

Career and conspiracy

Catiline served under Sulla and held offices including praetor and governor of Africa. Twice defeated in consular elections, and heavily in debt, he conspired in 63 BC to seize power, recruiting disaffected aristocrats and veterans and raising an army in Etruria.

Exposure and death

Consul Cicero exposed the plot in his Catilinarian Orations, beginning "How long, O Catiline, will you abuse our patience?" Five conspirators in Rome were executed; Catiline fled and died fighting at the Battle of Pistoria in 62 BC. Sallust's Bellum Catilinae is the main surviving account, portraying him as emblematic of late-republican corruption.
