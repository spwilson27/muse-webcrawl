Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality (also Cauchy–Bunyakovsky–Schwarz inequality) is a fundamental inequality in linear algebra and analysis, stating that for vectors in an inner product space, the absolute value of their inner product is at most the product of their norms. This article is at the hyphenated title; the result is identical to that stated at the en-dash title.

|⟨x, y⟩| ≤ ‖x‖ ‖y‖, with equality iff x and y are linearly dependent.

Special cases

For sums: (Σ aibi)2 ≤ (Σ ai2)(Σ bi2). For integrals, the analogous form holds for square-integrable functions. Named for Augustin-Louis Cauchy (1821), Viktor Bunyakovsky (1859), and Hermann Schwarz (1888).

Applications

The inequality underlies the triangle inequality for norms, proofs in probability (bounding covariance by standard deviations), quantum mechanics, and signal processing.
