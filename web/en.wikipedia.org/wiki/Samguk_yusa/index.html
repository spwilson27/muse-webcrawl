The Samguk yusa (Korean: 삼국유사; Hanja: 三國遺事), translated as Memorabilia of the Three Kingdoms, is a collection of legends, folktales, and historical accounts of the Three Kingdoms of Korea (Goguryeo, Baekje, and Silla), as well as earlier and later periods. It was compiled in Classical Chinese by the Buddhist monk Ilyeon (1206–1289) in the late 13th century, during the Goryeo dynasty, and is one of the two principal surviving sources for early Korean history, alongside the more official and Confucian Samguk sagi (1145).

Contents

The work is arranged in five volumes and nine sections covering royal chronologies, "wonders" and foundation myths, the rise of Buddhism in each kingdom, biographies of eminent monks, pagodas and images, hymns (including hyangga songs preserved in phonetic transcription), and tales of filial piety. Where the Samguk sagi largely omitted myth in the interest of sober historiography, the Samguk yusa deliberately preserved material its compiler thought the earlier history had lost — hence its title, "memorabilia" or "things left behind."

Significance

The Samguk yusa is the earliest extant source for the myth of Dangun, the legendary founder of Gojoseon, in which the bear-woman Ungnyeo gives birth to the progenitor of the Korean people; the Dangun story, marginal in earlier eras, became central to Korean national identity in the 20th century. The text also preserves fourteen hyangga poems, a major source for the study of Old Korean, and much of what is known about Korean Buddhism, shamanic practice, and folklore of the first millennium. The oldest extant printed edition dates to 1512 (the Jeongdeok edition). The work is designated a treasure of Korean cultural heritage and remains foundational for the study of ancient Korea.
