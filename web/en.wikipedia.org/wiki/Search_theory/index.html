Search theory

Search theory
FieldMicroeconomics, operations research
Key figuresGeorge Stigler, Peter Diamond, Dale Mortensen, Christopher Pissarides

Search theory is the study of transactional frictions: how buyers and sellers, or workers and employers, find each other when information is costly and offers arrive over time. It explains why identical goods can sell at different prices and why unemployment and vacancies coexist.

Origins

George Stigler's 1961 paper on the economics of information founded price-search theory: consumers sample prices until the expected gain from further search falls below its cost, yielding a reservation price. John McCall developed sequential job search models.

Matching models

The Diamond–Mortensen–Pissarides (DMP) model treats hiring through a matching function of unemployed workers and vacancies, with wages set by Nash bargaining. Its creators shared the 2010 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economics. The framework underlies modern labour-market policy analysis, including unemployment insurance effects.

Applications

Search theory applies to housing, consumer markets, marriage markets, money, and online platforms. See Second-language acquisition for a different field using optimal-stopping analogies only loosely; the core link is to labour economics and information economics.
