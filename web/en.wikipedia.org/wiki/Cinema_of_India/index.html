Cinema of India

Cinema of India
First film: Raja Harishchandra (1913)
Major industries: Hindi (Bollywood), Telugu (Tollywood), Tamil (Kollywood), Malayalam, Kannada, Bengali
Output: Among the world's largest, often 1,500+ films/year

The cinema of India comprises films produced across India in many languages and regional industries, of which Hindi-language Bollywood (Mumbai) is the best known internationally. India is among the world's largest producers of films by number.

History

Dadasaheb Phalke's Raja Harishchandra (1913) was India's first full-length feature. Sound arrived with Alam Ara (1931). The 1950s saw a golden age: Satyajit Ray's Pather Panchali (1955) won international acclaim, alongside Guru Dutt and Raj Kapoor. Parallel (art) cinema developed alongside popular song-and-dance 'masala' films.

Regional industries and recent era

South Indian industries — Telugu, Tamil, Malayalam and Kannada — have grown in national reach, with pan-Indian successes such as Baahubali, RRR (whose song 'Naatu Naatu' won an Oscar in 2023), and KGF. Music, dance and melodrama remain central popular conventions.
