Omar al-Mukhtar

Omar al-Mukhtar
Born: 20 August 1858, Zanzur, Ottoman Libya
Died: 16 September 1931, Suluq, Italian Libya
Nickname: Shaykh ash-Shuhadāʾ ("Chief of the Martyrs"), "Lion of the Desert"

Omar al-Mukhtar (20 August 1858 – 16 September 1931) was a Libyan resistance leader who fought against the Italian colonization of Libya for nearly twenty years. A member of the Senussi religious order, he became the chief organizer of the resistance in Cyrenaica after Italy invaded Libya in 1911.

Resistance

Mukhtar, a Quran teacher by training, proved a gifted guerrilla commander. Using intimate knowledge of the desert terrain, his small mobile bands harassed Italian forces, ambushing columns and supply lines while avoiding pitched battle. The Italian campaign under General Rodolfo Graziani responded with brutal measures, including concentration camps into which much of the Cyrenaican population was forced.

Capture and death

Wounded and captured in September 1931 near Slonta, Mukhtar was tried by a military court in Benghazi and publicly hanged at Suluq on 16 September 1931, before thousands of camp prisoners. He remains a national hero of Libya; his face appears on the Libyan ten-dinar note, and his life was dramatized in the 1981 film Lion of the Desert.
