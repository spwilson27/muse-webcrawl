Hohenzollern Redoubt

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Hohenzollern Redoubt (German: Hohenzollernwerk) was a strongpoint of the German front line near Auchy-les-Mines in northern France during the First World War. A network of trenches and strongpoints built around a spoil heap position east of the Loos battlefield, it was named after the German imperial house of Hohenzollern.

Battle of Loos, 1915

The redoubt was a principal objective of the British attack on 25 September 1915, the first day of the Battle of Loos. Troops of the 9th (Scottish) Division captured much of the position on the first day, but German counterattacks, aided by the redoubt's maze of communication trenches, retook most of it by 28 September. Renewed British attacks in October — the "Actions of the Hohenzollern Redoubt," 13–19 October 1915 — gained little ground at heavy cost, and historian's estimates put total British casualties around the redoubt in the thousands.

1916 and legacy

Fighting continued in the sector in 1916, including mine warfare in the craters before the redoubt. The area is commemorated by Commonwealth War Graves Commission cemeteries and the Loos Memorial at Dud Corner Cemetery, which records the names of over 20,000 soldiers with no known grave. The redoubt site itself lies on farmland between Hulluch and Auchy-les-Mines.
