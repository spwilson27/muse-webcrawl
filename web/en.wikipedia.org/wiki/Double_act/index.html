Double act

A double act (also known as a comedy duo) is a form of comedy originating in music hall and vaudeville in which two performers — typically a "straight man" (or straight woman) and a "funny man" — work together, the humour arising from their contrasting personalities and interactions.

Double act
Type: Comedy format
Roles: Straight man, funny man (foil)
Origins: Music hall, vaudeville

Structure

The straight man feeds lines, maintains a facade of rationality and often exasperation, while the funny man delivers eccentric, illogical or childlike behaviour. Roles may blur or swap. Timing, cross-talk and repetition are characteristic techniques.

Famous examples

Laurel and Hardy, Abbott and Costello, Morecambe and Wise, Peter Cook and Dudley Moore, French and Saunders, Fry and Laurie, Mitchell and Webb, and in Japan the manzai tradition of boke and tsukkomi. Double acts have thrived across film, radio, television and stand-up.
