Original six frigates of the United States Navy

The original six frigates of the United States Navy were authorised by the Naval Act of 1794 and were the first ships of the newly formed United States Navy. Designed by Joshua Humphreys, they were larger and more heavily built than contemporary European frigates, combining the firepower of a ship of the line with the speed to escape one. The six ships were Constitution, President, United States, Congress, Constellation and Chesapeake.

USS Constitution, the most famous of the six frigates, still in commission

Design

Humphreys designed the frigates with long keels, heavy scantlings and dense southern live oak framing, and mounted a main battery of 24-pounder long guns where most frigates carried 18-pounders. Three of the class were built to a larger 44-gun plan and three to a smaller 36-gun plan. Their diagonal riders and massive construction gave them exceptional strength; Constitution earned the nickname "Old Ironsides" when British shot was seen to rebound from her hull in 1812.

Service history

The frigates served in the Quasi-War with France, the First Barbary War — including Stephen Decatur's raid to burn the captured Philadelphia in Tripoli harbour — and the War of 1812, where single-ship victories by Constitution over HMS Guerriere and HMS Java had an outsized effect on morale. Not all fared well: Chesapeake was captured by HMS Shannon in 1813, and President was taken by a British squadron in 1815.

Survivors

Only Constitution survives, preserved in Boston as a museum ship and still commissioned in the US Navy; she sails periodically and is the world's oldest commissioned naval vessel afloat. The name Constellation is borne by a preserved sloop-of-war in Baltimore long misidentified as the frigate.
