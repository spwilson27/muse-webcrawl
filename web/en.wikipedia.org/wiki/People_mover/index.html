People mover

A people mover, or automated people mover (APM), is a small-scale automated guideway transit system. The term is generally used only to describe systems serving relatively small areas such as airports, downtown districts, or theme parks. Such systems are fully automated, driverless, and typically run on elevated guideways or in tunnels over short distances.

History

The concept grew out of studies in the 1960s and was showcased at expositions and by Walt Disney, whose company developed the WEDway PeopleMover at Disneyland and later the system at Walt Disney World. Early airport installations, such as Tampa International Airport's shuttles (1971), established the model.

Applications

Notable systems include airport inter-terminal shuttles at Hartsfield–Jackson Atlanta, Singapore Changi, and London Stansted; the Detroit People Mover and Miami Metromover downtown circulators; and systems by manufacturers such as Bombardier (Innovia), Mitsubishi (Crystal Mover), and cable-drawn systems by Doppelmayr/POMA. Technologies range from rubber-tired vehicles on concrete guideways to steel-wheel and cable-propelled designs.
