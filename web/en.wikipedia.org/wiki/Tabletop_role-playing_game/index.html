Tabletop role-playing game

A tabletop role-playing game (abbreviated as TRPG or TTRPG), also known as a pen-and-paper role-playing game, is a form of role-playing game (RPG) in which the participants describe their characters' actions through speech. Participants determine the actions of their characters based on their characterisation, and the actions succeed or fail according to a set formal system of rules and guidelines, usually involving randomisers such as dice. Within the rules, players have the freedom to improvise; their choices shape the direction and outcome of the game.

Gameplay

Most games are run by a game master (GM), who describes the setting and situations and plays the non-player characters, while each other participant plays a single player character defined by a character sheet recording attributes, skills and equipment. Dice rolls or other randomisers resolve uncertain actions; in dice-based games such as Dungeons & Dragons, different polyhedral dice are used for different purposes. Sessions may last a few hours, and continuing games, called campaigns, may run for years.

History

The hobby began in the early 1970s in the wargaming community of the American Midwest. Dungeons & Dragons, designed by Gary Gygax and Dave Arneson and published in 1974 by TSR, was the first commercially successful role-playing game, growing out of the miniatures wargame Chainmail and Arneson's Braunstein-style scenarios. It was followed by games such as Traveller (1977), RuneQuest (1978), Call of Cthulhu (1981) and GURPS (1986). A "golden age" of indie design followed in the 2000s, and streaming actual-play shows in the 2010s brought the hobby to a much wider audience.

Systems and settings

Games span fantasy, science fiction, horror, superhero and historical settings. Some systems, such as GURPS and Fate, are generic and can be adapted to many genres, while others are tied to a specific fictional world. Rules range from rules-light systems emphasising narration to highly detailed simulations.
