Classical guitar

The classical guitar is a member of the guitar family of instruments, used in classical music and other styles. It is an acoustic wooden string instrument with strings made of nylon or gut, as opposed to the metal strings of the acoustic and electric steel-string guitars. Its design was standardized in 19th-century Spain, largely through the work of the luthier Antonio Torres Jurado.

Description

A typical modern classical guitar has six nylon strings, a wide flat neck, and a hollow wooden body typically with spruce or cedar top and rosewood back and sides. It is played with the fingertips or fingernails, using fingerstyle technique, usually with the instrument resting on the left leg supported by a footstool or guitar support.

History

The modern classical guitar developed from earlier instruments such as the vihuela, the Baroque guitar, and the early Romantic guitar. In the 19th century, Torres enlarged the body and developed fan bracing, giving the instrument its modern sound. In the 20th century, Andrés Segovia popularized the guitar as a concert instrument and commissioned much new repertoire.

Repertoire

The repertoire includes works by composers such as Francisco Tárrega, Heitor Villa-Lobos, Joaquín Rodrigo (whose Concierto de Aranjuez is among the most famous guitar concertos), and many transcriptions of works originally for lute, piano, or other instruments.
