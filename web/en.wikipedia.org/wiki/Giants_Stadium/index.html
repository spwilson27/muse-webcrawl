Giants Stadium was a stadium in the Meadowlands Sports Complex in East Rutherford, New Jersey, United States. Opened in 1976, it was the home of the NFL's New York Giants for its entire existence and of the New York Jets from 1984. It closed in 2010 and was demolished the same year, replaced by MetLife Stadium built nearby.

History

The stadium was built after the Giants agreed to leave Yankee Stadium for a purpose-built home in New Jersey. Its first event was a Giants game against the Dallas Cowboys on 10 October 1976. Beyond American football it hosted the New York Cosmos of the NASL — including Pelé's farewell match in 1977 — and later the New York/New Jersey MetroStars (Red Bulls) of MLS. Giants Stadium was a venue for the 1994 FIFA World Cup, including a semi-final, and hosted numerous major concerts and a 1995 Mass celebrated by Pope John Paul II.

Legacy

The stadium is also famous in popular culture through the persistent (and unproven) legend that the remains of labour leader Jimmy Hoffa, who disappeared in 1975, were buried beneath it — a claim dramatised in film and television. The final event was a Jets game in January 2010; demolition was completed by the summer of that year.
