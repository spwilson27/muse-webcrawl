Enterprise agreement

An enterprise agreement is a collective agreement made at the level of a single enterprise or workplace between an employer and its employees (often represented by a trade union), setting out wages and conditions of employment. In Australian industrial relations, enterprise agreements are a central instrument of the federal system under the Fair Work Act 2009, having largely displaced industry-wide awards as the primary source of negotiated conditions.

Australian system

Under the Fair Work Act, an enterprise agreement must be genuinely agreed to by a majority of employees who cast a valid vote, and must pass the "better off overall test" (BOOT), which requires that each employee be better off under the agreement than under the applicable modern award. Agreements are assessed and approved by the Fair Work Commission, have a maximum nominal expiry of four years, and continue to operate until terminated or replaced.

Types

Single-enterprise agreements cover one employer (or related employers operating as a single enterprise); multi-enterprise agreements cover two or more employers; and greenfields agreements are made for a new enterprise before employees are engaged, negotiated with a union. Certain low-paid and supported bargaining streams were expanded by amendments in 2022.

Enterprise bargaining

The process of negotiating an agreement is called enterprise bargaining, a system introduced federally in the early 1990s to shift wage-fixing from centralised arbitration towards workplace-level negotiation. Protected industrial action (such as strikes) may lawfully be taken only in pursuit of a proposed enterprise agreement and after a secret ballot of employees.
