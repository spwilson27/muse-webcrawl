Gaius Marius

Gaius Marius (c. 157 – 13 January 86 BC) was a Roman general and statesman, a "new man" from Arpinum who was elected consul an unprecedented seven times. His military reforms transformed the Roman army, but his rivalry with Sulla helped ignite the Republic's civil wars.


Consul: 107, 104–100, 86 BC
Wars: Jugurthine War, Cimbrian War
Rival: Lucius Cornelius Sulla

Rise

Marius won fame under Scipio Aemilianus at Numantia, then took command against Jugurtha in Numidia, and defeated the migrating Cimbri and Teutones at Aquae Sextiae (102 BC) and Vercellae (101 BC).

Marian reforms

Traditionally credited to Marius are the opening of recruitment to landless citizens, state-supplied equipment, the cohort as the main tactical unit, and soldiers carrying their own gear — "Marius's mules".

Rivalry with Sulla

Dispute over the Mithridatic command led Sulla to march on Rome in 88 BC; Marius fled, returned in 87 BC with Cinna amid bloody reprisals, and died weeks into his seventh consulship. See Gaius Gracchus.
