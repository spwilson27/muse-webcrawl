The Prime Minister of Ethiopia is the head of government of the Federal Democratic Republic of Ethiopia. The prime minister is the chief executive, chairs the Council of Ministers and is commander-in-chief of the Ethiopian National Defense Force. The officeholder is chosen from members of the House of Peoples' Representatives belonging to the ruling party or coalition, and is formally appointed by the President, who is head of state.

History

Under the Ethiopian Empire, chief ministers and prime ministers served at the pleasure of the Emperor; notable holders include Makonnen Wolde Mikael and, in the final imperial years, Aklilu Habte-Wold and Endelkachew Makonnen. The office was suspended during much of the Derg military regime, and restored under the People's Democratic Republic in 1987 with Fikre Selassie Wogderess as prime minister. After the fall of the Derg in 1991, Tamirat Layne led the transitional government. Under the 1995 constitution, executive power was firmly vested in the prime minister, an office held by Meles Zenawi from 1995 until his death in 2012 — the dominant figure of the EPRDF era.

Recent officeholders

Meles was succeeded by Hailemariam Desalegn (2012–2018), who resigned amid widespread protests. Abiy Ahmed became prime minister in April 2018, launched a programme of political and economic reform and received the Nobel Peace Prize in 2019 for resolving the border conflict with Eritrea; his tenure also saw the Tigray War of 2020–2022 and continuing internal conflict. Abiy formed the Prosperity Party in 2019, replacing the EPRDF coalition, and won the 2021 general election.

Residence and office

The prime minister's official residence and office is the National Palace (Menelik Palace) compound in Addis Ababa, which Abiy renovated as the Unity Park project.
