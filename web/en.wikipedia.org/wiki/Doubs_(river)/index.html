Doubs (river)

SourceMouthe, Jura Mountains, France
MouthSaône at Verdun-sur-le-Doubs
Length453 km (281 mi)
Basin size7,710 km²
CountriesFrance, Switzerland

The Doubs (French pronunciation: [du]) is a 453-kilometre (281 mi) river in eastern France and western Switzerland. A left tributary of the Saône, it rises near Mouthe in the Jura Mountains, flows northeast along the Franco-Swiss border — looping dramatically around the citadel city of Besançon — and eventually turns southwest to join the Saône at Verdun-sur-le-Doubs. It gives its name to the French department of Doubs.

At Besançon the Doubs forms an almost complete loop around the old town, overlooked by Vauban's citadel.

Course

From its source at Mouthe, the highest and coldest commune of France, the Doubs flows to Lake Saint-Point and past Pontarlier before forming the border between France and the Swiss canton of Neuchâtel. There it plunges over the Saut du Doubs waterfall and through deep limestone gorges. It then enters France fully, passes Montbéliard, and meanders around Besançon in its famous loop (la Boucle). In its lower course it is followed by the Rhône–Rhine Canal, with which it shares stretches of channel, before meeting the Saône.

Features

The river is known for the Saut du Doubs, a 27-metre waterfall on the border; for Lac des Brenets above it; and for fishing, kayaking, and hydroelectric installations. The apron, a rare fish once found in the Doubs, has become a symbol of the river's fragile ecology.
