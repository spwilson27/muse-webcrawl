Wade–Giles

Wade–Giles is a system of romanization for Mandarin Chinese, developed from a system created by Thomas Francis Wade in the mid-19th century and completed by Herbert A. Giles in his Chinese–English Dictionary of 1892.

Features

The system distinguishes aspirated and unaspirated consonants with an apostrophe (e.g., p vs. p'), which pinyin instead marks as b vs. p. Familiar examples include Peking (Beijing), Mao Tse-tung (Mao Zedong), Tao (Dao), kung fu (gongfu), and Ch'ing (Qing).

History and use

Wade–Giles was the dominant romanization in the English-speaking world for most of the 20th century and remains common in older scholarship and in some names in Taiwan. It has been largely superseded by Hanyu Pinyin, adopted by the People's Republic of China in the 1950s and by ISO in 1982, though Wade–Giles persists in library cataloguing traditions and established spellings.
