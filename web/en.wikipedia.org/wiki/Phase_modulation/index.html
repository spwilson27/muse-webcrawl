Phase modulation

Phase modulation (PM) is a modulation pattern in which the phase of a carrier wave is varied in proportion to the instantaneous amplitude of the message signal. It is one of the two main forms of angle modulation, the other being frequency modulation (FM), and the two are closely related: changing the phase of a wave also changes its instantaneous frequency.

Theory

For a carrier of amplitude Ac and frequency fc, the phase-modulated signal can be written as y(t) = Ac sin(2πfct + ΔΦ(t)), where the phase deviation ΔΦ(t) is proportional to the modulating signal. The size of the phase shift is characterised by the modulation index. Because phase is the integral of frequency, PM with an integrated message signal is equivalent to FM, and FM with a differentiated message is equivalent to PM — a fact exploited in the design of transmitters and receivers.

Applications

Phase modulation underlies digital phase-shift keying (PSK), used in Wi-Fi, satellite links and many other radio systems. In analogue form, PM was used in some early radio and television sound systems, and it is a standard technique in electronic music synthesis: the "FM synthesis" popularised by the Yamaha DX7 actually implements phase modulation. PM signals are typically demodulated with a phase-locked loop or a coherent detector.

Compared with amplitude modulation, angle modulation offers greater immunity to noise at the cost of wider bandwidth.
