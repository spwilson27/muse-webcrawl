La Raza Unida Party

The Partido Nacional de La Raza Unida, commonly called La Raza Unida Party, was a Chicano political party founded in Texas in 1970. Emerging from the Chicano Movement, it sought political self-determination for Mexican Americans, particularly in the heavily Mexican-American counties of South Texas, and became the most significant third-party effort of the movement.

Founding

The party grew out of the Mexican American Youth Organization (MAYO) and student walkouts protesting discrimination in Texas schools. It was formally organized in Crystal City, Zavala County, where in April 1970 a La Raza Unida slate swept city council and school board elections — an event often called the Crystal City revolt. José Ángel Gutiérrez, a leading founder, was elected county judge of Zavala County in 1974.

Statewide campaigns

In 1972 the party ran Ramsey Muñiz, a former Baylor football player, for Governor of Texas; he won about 6 percent of the vote, and the party fielded candidates across the state. A national convention in El Paso in 1972 proclaimed El Partido Nacional de La Raza Unida, though state organizations in California, Colorado, and elsewhere pursued differing strategies. Muñiz ran again in 1974 with a reduced vote share.

Decline and legacy

Internal disputes, pressure from the Democratic Party, and the legal troubles of its candidates weakened the party by the late 1970s, and it faded as an electoral force by the early 1980s. La Raza Unida nevertheless trained a generation of Latino officeholders and demonstrated the electoral potential of Mexican-American voters in the Southwest.
