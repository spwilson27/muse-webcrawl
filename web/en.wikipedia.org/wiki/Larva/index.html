Larva

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A larva is the juvenile form of an animal that undergoes metamorphosis before reaching its adult form. Larvae often look completely different from adults, live in different habitats and eat different foods — caterpillars become butterflies, tadpoles become frogs, maggots become flies, and countless marine invertebrates begin as drifting planktonic larvae.

Roles

The larval stage serves growth and dispersal: many bottom-dwelling sea animals (oysters, barnacles, corals) spread via swimming larvae carried by currents, and the feeding larva accumulates the resources that fuel the non-feeding transformation in the pupa or equivalent stage. Larvae are vital food for other animals — fish stocks depend on planktonic larvae, including their own.

Study and use

Parasitic worms pass through larval stages in intermediate hosts, central to their control. Some animals retain larval features as adults (neoteny — the axolotl keeps its gills). Eating larvae (entomophagy) is traditional in many cultures, and fly larvae are used medically to clean wounds (maggot therapy).
