Nolan Bushnell

Nolan Kay Bushnell (born February 5, 1943) is an American businessman and electrical engineer. He established Atari, Inc. and the Chuck E. Cheese pizza restaurant chain. He is widely regarded as one of the founding fathers of the video game industry.

Atari

Inspired by the mainframe game Spacewar!, Bushnell and Ted Dabney built Computer Space (1971), the first commercially sold coin-operated video game. In 1972 they founded Atari, whose Pong became the first commercially successful video game. Atari's home console, the Video Computer System (2600), launched the home video game market. Bushnell sold Atari to Warner Communications in 1976 and left the company in 1979, having also hired a young Steve Jobs and Steve Wozniak, who worked at Atari before founding Apple.

Later ventures

Bushnell founded Chuck E. Cheese's Pizza Time Theatre in 1977, combining pizza, animatronic entertainment and arcade games. He went on to found or fund numerous other companies in technology, robotics and education, and wrote the business book Finding the Next Steve Jobs (2013). He was inducted into the Video Game Hall of Fame and received a BAFTA Fellowship for his contribution to the industry.
