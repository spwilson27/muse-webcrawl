Dose (biochemistry)

A dose is a measured quantity of a medicine, nutrient, or pathogen which is delivered as a unit. Dosage is often expressed in pharmacology as the amount of an active pharmaceutical ingredient per unit body weight or body surface area, and over a given period of time. The study of how doses relate to biological responses is central to pharmacology, toxicology and nutrition.

Key concepts

Important dose-related quantities include:

  • Effective dose (ED50) — the dose producing a specified effect in 50% of a population.
  • Median lethal dose (LD50) — the dose lethal to 50% of test subjects, used in toxicology.
  • Therapeutic dose — the amount needed to produce the desired clinical effect.
  • Loading dose and maintenance dose — an initial higher dose followed by smaller regular doses to sustain a target concentration.
  • Absorbed dose — in radiation biology, the energy deposited per unit mass, measured in grays.

Dose–response relationship

The relationship between dose and the magnitude of response is typically described by a sigmoidal dose–response curve. The difference between effective and toxic doses defines a drug's therapeutic index, a measure of its safety margin.

Units

Doses may be measured in mass units (milligrams, micrograms), in moles, in international units for biological activity, or per kilogram of body weight (mg/kg), the last being common in toxicology and paediatric prescribing.
