Texel

Texel (pronounced [ˈtɛsəl]) is a municipality and an island in the Netherlands, in the province of North Holland. It is the largest and southernmost of the West Frisian (Wadden) Islands in the Wadden Sea, separated from the mainland by the Marsdiep strait. The island is a popular tourist destination known for its beaches, dune landscapes, birdlife and sheep farming.

Geography

Texel consists of the former islands of Texel and Eierland, joined in the 17th century by the construction of dikes and sand drift dykes. The western coast is a long sandy beach backed by the Dunes of Texel National Park, while the east coast borders the mudflats of the Wadden Sea, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The island is about 20 km long and 8 km wide.

History

In the Dutch Golden Age, Texel Roads was a major anchorage where ships awaited favourable winds. At the end of the Second World War, in April–May 1945, the island was the scene of the Georgian uprising, in which Georgian soldiers of the German army revolted — sometimes called the last battle of the war in Europe.

Economy

Tourism employs a large part of the population, and the island is also famed for the Texel sheep breed and its lamb. A ferry service connects Den Helder on the mainland with 't Horntje.
