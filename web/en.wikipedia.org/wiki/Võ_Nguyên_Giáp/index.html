Võ Nguyên Giáp (25 August 1911 – 4 October 2013) was a Vietnamese general, politician, and military theoretician who served as commander-in-chief of the People's Army of Vietnam (PAVN) and Minister of Defence of North Vietnam. One of the most celebrated military leaders of the twentieth century, he led Vietnamese forces to victory over France at the Battle of Dien Bien Phu (1954) and directed North Vietnam's war effort during much of the Vietnam War against South Vietnam and the United States.

Early life and revolutionary activity

Born in Quảng Bình Province to a family of scholar-gentry, Giáp was educated at the lycée in Huế and studied law and political economy at university in Hanoi, working as a teacher and journalist. He joined the Indochinese Communist Party in the 1930s and fled to China in 1940 after a French crackdown in which his wife and sister-in-law were executed. With Hồ Chí Minh he helped found the Việt Minh front in 1941 and built its first armed units in the mountains of northern Vietnam.

First Indochina War

After the August Revolution of 1945, Giáp became commander of the Viet Minh forces fighting France. Learning from early defeats, he developed a doctrine of protracted people's war combining guerrilla operations with conventional mobile warfare. His campaigns at Hòa Bình and along Route Coloniale 4 eroded French control of the north, and his victory at Dien Bien Phu in May 1954 — achieved by hauling artillery through jungle tracks to surround the French garrison — ended the war on Viet Minh terms at Geneva.

Vietnam War

During the Vietnam War, Giáp oversaw the PAVN's strategy, including the build-up along the Hồ Chí Minh Trail and the planning behind major operations such as the 1968 Tet Offensive and the 1972 Easter Offensive. His influence waned as Lê Duẩn and Văn Tiến Dũng took leading roles, and Dũng commanded the final 1975 Spring Offensive that captured Saigon. Giáp remained Defence Minister until 1980 and a deputy prime minister until 1991.

Legacy

Giáp wrote extensively on military doctrine, notably People's War, People's Army (1961), one of the classic texts of revolutionary warfare. When he died in Hanoi in 2013 at the age of 102, hundreds of thousands of Vietnamese queued to pay their respects during a state funeral; he was buried in his home province of Quảng Bình.
