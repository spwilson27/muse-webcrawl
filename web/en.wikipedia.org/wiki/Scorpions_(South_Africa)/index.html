Scorpions (South Africa)

The Scorpions was the popular name of the Directorate of Special Operations (DSO), an elite multidisciplinary unit of South Africa's National Prosecuting Authority, established in 1999 and formally launched in 2001 to investigate and prosecute organised crime, corruption and racketeering.

Work

Modelled in part on the American FBI, the unit combined prosecutors, police investigators, forensic accountants and intelligence analysts in a "troika" approach, and achieved a high conviction rate. It investigated several politically sensitive cases, including the arms deal investigation that led to the prosecution of Jacob Zuma's financial adviser Schabir Shaik, and charges against national police commissioner Jackie Selebi.

Disbandment

Following resolutions of the African National Congress, the DSO was disbanded in 2009 and replaced within the police by the Directorate for Priority Crime Investigation, known as the Hawks, a move widely criticised by opposition parties and civil society as weakening the fight against corruption. In 2011 the Constitutional Court ruled in the Glenister case that the replacement legislation did not meet the state's obligation to maintain an adequately independent anti-corruption body, prompting amendments.
