Debye length

In plasmas and electrolytes, the Debye length (also called the Debye radius), denoted λD or κ−1, is the measure of a charge carrier's net electrostatic effect in a solution and how far its electrostatic effect persists. With each Debye length, charges are increasingly electrically screened. A Debye sphere is a volume whose radius is the Debye length, and within which there are enough charge carriers to screen the field of a charge placed at its centre.

The concept is named after the Dutch-American physicist and chemist Peter Debye, who developed the theory of charge screening in electrolyte solutions with Erich Hückel in 1923, in what became known as Debye–Hückel theory.

Definition

For a plasma or electrolyte containing species with number densities ni and charges qi = zie, the Debye length is

λD = √( ε0 εr kB T / Σi ni zi2 e2 )

where ε0 is the permittivity of free space, εr the relative permittivity (dielectric constant) of the medium, kB the Boltzmann constant, T the absolute temperature, and e the elementary charge. For a single-component plasma of electrons, this reduces to λD = √( ε0 kB Te / ne e2 ).

Physical significance

The Debye length sets the characteristic scale of electrostatic screening. At distances much larger than λD from a charge, the potential is strongly suppressed, decaying approximately exponentially as e−r/λD/r (the Yukawa form). It also determines the thickness of the electrical double layer at charged surfaces and interfaces, and it plays a central role in colloid science, where the DLVO theory of colloidal stability compares double-layer repulsion over the Debye length with van der Waals attraction.

In electrolyte solutions, the Debye length decreases with increasing ionic strength: in a 1:1 electrolyte in water at 25 °C, λD is roughly 0.304 nm divided by the square root of the ionic strength in mol/L, so it is about 10 nm in a 1 mM solution and about 1 nm in a 100 mM solution.

Debye length in plasmas

In plasma physics, a necessary condition for a quasineutral ionized gas to behave collectively as a plasma is that the Debye length be much smaller than the size of the system, and that the number of particles in a Debye sphere be much greater than one. The Debye length also enters the plasma parameter and is related to the electron plasma frequency.
