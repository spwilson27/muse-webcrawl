Kama

TraditionHinduism; also Buddhism and Jainism

Kāma (Sanskrit: काम) is a concept in Indian religions denoting desire, longing and sensual pleasure, especially aesthetic and sexual pleasure. In Hindu thought kama is one of the four purusharthas, the proper goals of human life, alongside dharma (duty), artha (prosperity) and moksha (liberation). Pursued in harmony with dharma and artha, kama is affirmed as a legitimate and even noble aim; pursued alone or against dharma, it is considered a source of bondage.

In Hinduism

The personification of kama is the god Kamadeva, who shoots arrows of flowers and is said to have been burned to ash by Shiva's third eye when he disturbed the god's meditation, thereafter existing bodiless (Ananga). The most famous classical treatise on kama is Vatsyayana's Kama Sutra, a wide-ranging work on love, courtship, household life and the arts, of which sexual technique is only one part.

In Buddhism

In Buddhist usage, kāma more often denotes sensual desire as an obstacle to awakening; the realm of desire is called the kāmadhātu, and craving is analysed among the defilements to be abandoned on the path.
