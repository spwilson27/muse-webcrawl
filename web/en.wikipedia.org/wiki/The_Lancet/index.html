The Lancet is a weekly peer-reviewed general medical journal and one of the world's best known, oldest, and most highly respected medical journals. It was founded in 1823 by the English surgeon Thomas Wakley, who named it after the lancet, a surgical instrument, and also after the architectural term for a lancet window, suggesting that the journal would "let in light" on the medical profession.

History

Wakley founded the journal as a campaigning publication, using it to attack corruption, nepotism, and incompetence in London's hospitals and medical schools, and to publish verbatim accounts of lectures by prominent surgeons that were otherwise available only to fee-paying students. The journal quickly became influential and sometimes controversial: Wakley was repeatedly sued for libel, and the journal's radical stance helped drive reforms in medical education and governance, including the Medical Act 1858.

Through the nineteenth and twentieth centuries the journal published many landmark papers. In 1867 Joseph Lister published his pioneering work on antiseptic surgery in its pages. The journal was acquired by Elsevier in 1991 and remains edited from London, with offices in New York and Beijing.

Content and reputation

The Lancet publishes original research, review articles, editorials, book reviews, correspondence, and news features across all areas of medicine. Its acceptance rate for research papers is low, and its impact factor has consistently ranked it alongside the New England Journal of Medicine as one of the two most influential general medical journals in the world.

The journal has also been at the centre of notable controversies, most famously the 1998 paper by Andrew Wakefield and colleagues suggesting a link between the MMR vaccine and autism. The paper was partially retracted in 2004 and fully retracted in 2010 after investigations found the research fraudulent; Wakefield was struck off the UK medical register.

Family of journals

The Lancet has spawned a large family of specialty journals, including The Lancet Neurology, The Lancet Oncology, The Lancet Infectious Diseases, The Lancet Psychiatry, and The Lancet Global Health. It also publishes a series of Commissions — major reports by international expert groups on topics such as climate change and health, dementia, and global surgery.
