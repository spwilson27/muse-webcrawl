Spock is a fictional character in the Star Trek media franchise. The son of the Vulcan ambassador Sarek and the human Amanda Grayson, he serves as science officer and first officer aboard the starship Enterprise under Captain James T. Kirk. Renowned for his commitment to logic and his suppression of emotion, Spock is also identified by his pointed ears, the Vulcan salute, and the phrase "Live long and prosper."

Character history

Created by Gene Roddenberry, Spock first appeared in Star Trek: The Original Series (1966–1969), played by Leonard Nimoy, who continued in the role through films and guest appearances for nearly five decades. Spock's internal conflict between his Vulcan logic and human emotion became a central theme of the franchise. He dies saving the Enterprise in Star Trek II: The Wrath of Khan (1982) and is resurrected in the following film.

Zachary Quinto portrayed a younger Spock in the Kelvin-timeline films beginning with Star Trek (2009), and Ethan Peck plays the character in Star Trek: Discovery and Star Trek: Strange New Worlds.

Cultural impact

Spock is among the most recognisable characters in science fiction. His outsider's perspective and divided heritage have been read as commentaries on identity and belonging, and the Vulcan nerve pinch, mind meld, and salute have entered popular culture.
