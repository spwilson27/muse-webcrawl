Random House is an American book publisher and the flagship imprint of Penguin Random House, the world's largest trade book publisher. It was founded in 1927 by Bennett Cerf and Donald Klopfer, who had acquired the Modern Library imprint two years earlier and decided to publish a small number of books "at random" alongside it — the origin of the company's name.

History

Random House grew into one of the most prestigious American publishers, issuing works by authors such as William Faulkner, James Joyce, Truman Capote and Toni Morrison. It acquired many notable imprints, including Alfred A. Knopf, Pantheon Books and Crown Publishing. In 1998 it was acquired by the German media group Bertelsmann, and in 2013 it merged with Penguin Group to form Penguin Random House.

Imprints

Its numerous imprints and divisions include Ballantine Books, Bantam Books, Crown, Del Rey, Doubleday (historically), Knopf, Pantheon, Spiegel & Grau and Random House Children's Books, publisher of Dr. Seuss's works in many editions.
