Condominium

A high-rise condominium building

A condominium (or condo for short) is a type of living space similar to an apartment but independently sellable and therefore regarded as real estate. The condominium building structure is divided into several units that are each separately owned, surrounded by common areas that are jointly owned and managed by the community of unit owners through an association, typically called a homeowners association (HOA) or condominium association.

The word derives from the Latin con- ("together") and dominium ("ownership, dominion"). Alongside standalone houses and townhouses, condominiums constitute one of the main forms of housing tenure in North America and are common worldwide under related legal forms such as strata title in Australia and British Columbia, commonhold in England and Wales, and sectional title in South Africa.

Legal concept

A condominium regime is created by recording a declaration that divides a parcel of real property into individual units and common elements. Each unit owner holds title to the interior of their unit and an undivided percentage interest in the common elements — the land, structure, hallways, roofs, amenities and systems serving the whole property. Owners pay periodic assessments ("condo fees" or HOA dues) that fund maintenance, insurance of the building, reserves, and shared services. Bylaws and rules govern use of units and common areas, and the association may enforce them and place liens for unpaid assessments.

History

Divided ownership of buildings has ancient precedents, but modern condominium statutes date from the mid-20th century. Puerto Rico enacted a Horizontal Property Act in 1958, and the United States followed with state statutes in the early 1960s, encouraged by Section 234 of the National Housing Act of 1961, which allowed federal mortgage insurance for condominium units. The resulting boom made condos a mainstream tenure in the 1970s; conversions of rental apartment buildings ("condo conversions") were especially common.

Types

Condominium units may be high-rise apartments, townhouse-style residences, detached houses within a gated community, or non-residential units in mixed-use, commercial or hotel developments ("condotels"). "Site condominiums" consist of detached single-family homes with condominium-style ownership of common areas. Timeshares typically use a related but distinct form of fractional ownership.

By country

In Canada, condominiums are regulated provincially and are especially prevalent in Toronto and Vancouver. In the United States, condominiums are heavily concentrated in metropolitan and resort areas such as South Florida, New York City, Chicago and Hawaii. Singapore and Thailand permit foreigners to own condominium units under quota rules that do not apply to landed property.
