Flan (food)

This article is about the caramel custard dessert. For the pastry, see Flan.

Alternative namesCrème caramel, caramel custard, leche flan
TypeCustard
CourseDessert
Place of originEurope (Spain/France)
Serving temperatureChilled or room temperature
Main ingredientsMilk, eggs, sugar, caramel

Flan, in the sense used throughout Spain, Latin America, and the Philippines, is a dessert of baked custard topped with a layer of clear caramel sauce. It is essentially the same dish as the French crème caramel and the Italian budino. The custard is made by gently baking a mixture of milk or cream, eggs, sugar and vanilla in a caramel-lined mould, usually in a bain-marie, then chilling and unmoulding it so the caramel runs down over the set custard.

Preparation

Sugar is heated until it melts and turns amber, then poured into the mould and allowed to harden. The custard mixture is poured over it and baked at a low temperature until just set — it should still tremble slightly in the centre. After chilling for several hours in the mould, the flan is inverted onto a serving dish; the caramel, re-liquefied by the custard's moisture, forms a glossy sauce.

Regional variants

Philippines

Leche flan is a heavier, richer version made with condensed milk and extra egg yolks, traditionally steamed in oval tin moulds called llaneras. It appears at nearly every festive table, often as a topping for halo-halo.

Latin America

Mexican flan is commonly made with condensed and evaporated milk; the flan napolitano adds cream cheese for a denser texture. In Argentina and Uruguay it is classically served with dulce de leche and whipped cream ("flan mixto"). Cuban flan, Puerto Rican flancocho (with a cake layer), and Venezuelan quesillo are further variations.

Japan

Introduced through European trade, purin is a ubiquitous convenience-store dessert, typically firmer than its European ancestor.
