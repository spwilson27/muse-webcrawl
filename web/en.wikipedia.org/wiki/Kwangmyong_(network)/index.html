Kwangmyong (network)

Kwangmyong (Korean for "bright star" or "bright light") is a North Korean national intranet service, opened in the early 2000s. It is a closed, domestic "walled garden" network that is not connected to the global Internet, providing web browsing, email, news and library services to approved institutions and users inside North Korea.

Kwangmyong Type: National intranet
Country: North Korea
Launched: early 2000s
Access: Institutions, some homes
Content: State-approved websites

Features and access

Access to Kwangmyong is provided mainly through universities, government offices, research institutes, libraries and some larger workplaces, with limited household access for elites. Users can browse a few thousand domestic websites, search the collection of the Grand People's Study House, read domestic news, exchange email within the network, and use bulletin boards and chat services. The system uses private IP addresses in the 10.0.0.0/8 range, which North Korea obtained by allocation in the 2010s.

Content

Content is produced and censored by state bodies such as the Korea Computer Center. Sites include the Korean Central News Agency, party newspapers, scientific databases, technical reference works, translated foreign texts and e-book libraries. In the mid-2010s, reports described tablet computers and a "national network" service expanding access to educational and scientific material.

Relation to the Internet

Only a tiny number of North Koreans have permission to access the global Internet. Kwangmyong serves as a substitute, offering some network services while keeping users isolated from outside information. Telecommunications in the country also include the domestic Koryolink mobile network.
