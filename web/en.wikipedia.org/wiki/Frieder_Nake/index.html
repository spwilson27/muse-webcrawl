Frieder Nake (born 16 December 1938 in Stuttgart) is a German mathematician, computer scientist and artist. He is regarded as one of the pioneers of computer art, having produced algorithmic drawings with computers and pen plotters beginning in 1963.

Career

Nake studied mathematics at the University of Stuttgart, where he encountered the computer pioneer Max Bense and worked with the Zuse Z64 plotter. In 1965 he took part, together with Georg Nees and A. Michael Noll, in some of the earliest exhibitions of computer-generated art; his show with Nees at the Galerie Niedlich in Stuttgart in November 1965 is considered one of the first exhibitions of computer art. His works are generated by algorithms using pseudo-random processes, and he has written extensively on the theory of algorithmic and interactive art.

Nake became a professor of computer science at the University of Bremen, where he co-founded the graphics programme and worked on computer graphics, digital media and semiotics. His writings, including Ästhetik als Informationsverarbeitung (1974), helped establish the theoretical foundations of computer aesthetics.
