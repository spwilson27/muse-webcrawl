Touraine
Country: France
Capital (historic): Tours
Modern dept: Indre-et-Loire (mainly)
Nickname: Garden of France

Touraine is a historic province in west-central France, centred on the city of Tours and broadly corresponding to the modern department of Indre-et-Loire. It takes its name from the Gallic tribe of the Turones.

Geography and history

Crossed by the Loire and its tributaries the Cher, Indre and Vienne, Touraine is famed for fertile gardens, vineyards (Vouvray, Chinon, Bourgueil) and an exceptionally pure form of French. Contested between the counts of Blois and Anjou in the Middle Ages, it passed to the French crown under Philip II. In the 15th–16th centuries French kings and courtiers built or rebuilt the great Loire châteaux — Amboise, Chenonceau, Azay-le-Rideau, Villandry and Loches.

Culture

Touraine was home to Rabelais (born near Chinon) and Balzac, and Descartes spent his youth at La Haye. Ronsard called it the 'Garden of France'. See Tours and Loire Valley.
