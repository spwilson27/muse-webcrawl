Yelu Deguang (耶律德光; 902–947), posthumously known as Emperor Taizong of Liao, was the second emperor of the Khitan-led Liao dynasty of China. The second son of the dynasty's founder Abaoji, he succeeded to the throne in 927 and greatly expanded Khitan power southward into the North China Plain.

Accession

Although Abaoji's eldest son, Yelu Bei, was the designated heir, the influence of Abaoji's widow, Empress Shulü Ping, secured the throne for Deguang, who had distinguished himself as a military commander during his father's campaigns against the Bohai kingdom. Yelu Bei eventually fled to the Later Tang court in China proper.

Expansion into China

In 936 Deguang backed the rebel general Shi Jingtang against the Later Tang, receiving in return the Sixteen Prefectures — a strategically vital strip of northern China including the area of modern Beijing — and an annual payment of silk. Shi founded the Later Jin dynasty as a Liao client. When Shi's successor defied the Khitan, Deguang invaded in 946, destroyed the Later Jin, and in early 947 briefly proclaimed himself emperor of China in Kaifeng, formally adopting the dynastic name "Great Liao." Widespread resistance, looting by his troops, and logistical strain forced a withdrawal within months.

Death

Deguang died of illness in May 947 while retreating north. According to a famous legend, his attendants preserved his body with salt for transport back to the Liao homeland — the origin of the Chinese expression comparing the preserved corpse to dried meat. He was succeeded by Yelu Ruan, the son of Yelu Bei.
