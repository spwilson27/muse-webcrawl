Palm Islands

The Palm Islands are three artificial islands — Palm Jumeirah, Palm Jebel Ali, and Palm Deira (now Deira Islands) — off the coast of Dubai, United Arab Emirates. Created by land reclamation by the developer Nakheel, each island is shaped like a palm tree surrounded by a crescent breakwater, and together they were intended to add hundreds of kilometres of new beachfront and host luxury residences, hotels, and entertainment venues.

Palm Jumeirah

Construction of Palm Jumeirah began in 2001 and the first residences were handed over in 2006. The island consists of a trunk, a crown of seventeen fronds, and a surrounding crescent, connected to the mainland by a monorail — the first in the Middle East. It is home to hotels including Atlantis, The Palm, which opened in 2008.

Palm Jebel Ali and Deira

Palm Jebel Ali, about fifty percent larger than Jumeirah, was begun in 2002 but its reclamation and development were put on hold following the 2008 financial crisis; work has since resumed on parts of the project. Palm Deira, the largest of the three, was scaled back and rebranded as Deira Islands, a waterfront development of a different layout.

Environmental concerns

The islands have been criticised for altering currents and sediment transport along the coast, increasing water turbidity, and burying marine habitats under dredged sand and rock. Studies have documented changes to local wildlife and shoreline erosion in parts of Dubai.
