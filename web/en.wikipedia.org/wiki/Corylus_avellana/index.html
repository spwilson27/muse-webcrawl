Corylus avellana

Corylus avellana, the common hazel, is a species of flowering plant in the birch family Betulaceae, native to Europe and Western Asia, from the British Isles south to Iberia, Italy, Greece, Turkey and the Caucasus, and north to central Scandinavia. It is an important component of the hedgerows that were the traditional field boundaries in lowland England, and is cultivated for its nuts, the hazelnuts (cobnuts).

Scientific classification

Kingdom: Plantae

Order: Fagales

Family: Betulaceae

Genus: Corylus

Species: C. avellana L.

Description

Common hazel is a deciduous shrub or small tree growing to 3–8 m tall, usually with multiple stems. Its male catkins appear in late winter before the leaves, and the nuts ripen in autumn, each held in a leafy involucre. Hazel has a long history of coppice management, providing poles, hurdles and thatching material.

Cultivation and uses

Hazelnuts are eaten raw or roasted and are used in confectionery and spreads. Commercial production centres on related large-fruited selections, with Turkey the world's largest producer. The purple-leaved cultivar 'Purpurea' and the contorted hazel are grown as ornamentals.
