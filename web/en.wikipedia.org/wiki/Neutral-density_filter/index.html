Neutral-density filter

In photography and optics, a neutral-density filter, or ND filter, is a filter that reduces or modifies the intensity of all wavelengths, or colours, of light equally, giving no change in hue of colour rendition. It can be a colourless (clear) or grey filter, and is denoted by the Wratten number 96. The purpose of a standard photographic neutral-density filter is to reduce the amount of light entering the lens, allowing the photographer to use a larger aperture or a longer exposure than would otherwise be possible.

Uses

  • Long exposures in bright light — blurring moving water, clouds, or crowds, and rendering moving objects invisible.
  • Wide apertures in bright light — using a large aperture for shallow depth of field without overexposing, especially with fast lenses or high-ISO/film-speed constraints.
  • Motion blur for artistic effect — panning and waterfall photography.
  • Cinematography — maintaining the conventional 180-degree shutter angle under bright conditions.
  • Observing bright sources — strong ND filters (and specialised solar filters) are used for viewing intense light sources; improper filters are dangerous for viewing the Sun.

Types

Fixed and variable

Fixed filters have a single density. Variable ND filters use two polarising filters rotated against each other to vary attenuation over a range, at the cost of possible colour casts and cross-pattern ("X-cross") artefacts at high densities.

Graduated

A graduated neutral-density filter transitions from clear to dark across its surface and is used to balance a bright sky against a darker foreground in landscape photography. Transitions may be "hard edge" or "soft edge", and "reverse graduated" filters are darkest in the middle for sunrises and sunsets.

Materials

ND filters are made by coating glass, by dyeing resin or gelatin, or using absorptive glass. Cheap filters may introduce colour casts (often magenta or green) and may not block infrared, causing IR contamination visible as reddish casts in long exposures; better filters include IR-cut coatings.
