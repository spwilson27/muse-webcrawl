The South Oil Company (SOC) is an Iraqi state-owned oil company headquartered in Basra, responsible for oil and gas operations in southern Iraq under the Ministry of Oil. Renamed the Basra Oil Company (BOC) in 2016, it oversees some of the world's largest crude oil fields, concentrated in Basra Governorate and neighbouring provinces.

Operations

The company is responsible for the supergiant fields of southern Iraq, including Rumaila, West Qurna, Zubair and Majnoon, which together account for the majority of Iraq's crude production and exports. Most southern production is exported through offshore terminals in the northern Gulf, principally the Basra Oil Terminal (formerly Mina al-Bakr) and the newer al-Amaya terminal, along with single-point moorings.

Under Iraq's post-2003 licensing rounds, the South Oil Company/Basra Oil Company partnered with international oil companies under technical service contracts: BP and CNPC at Rumaila, ExxonMobil (later succeeded as operator) at West Qurna-1, Eni at Zubair, and Shell at Majnoon. The state company retains ownership of the fields and the produced crude.

History

The company originated in the nationalisation of Iraq's oil industry, completed in 1972, when the assets of the foreign-owned Iraq Petroleum Company were taken over by the state. Regional operating companies including the South Oil Company were established to manage production outside the northern fields administered from Kirkuk. In April 2016 the Ministry of Oil renamed the company the Basra Oil Company to reflect its main area of operations.
