Electronics manufacturing services


Surface-mount assembly in an EMS factory

Electronics manufacturing services (EMS) is a term used for companies that design, manufacture, test, distribute, and provide return/repair services for electronic components and assemblies for original equipment manufacturers (OEMs). The concept is also referred to as electronics contract manufacturing (ECM).

Many consumer electronics are built by EMS providers that operate large factories, particularly in Asia, purchasing components at scale and assembling printed circuit boards with surface-mount technology lines. Customers range from startups that outsource their entire production to large brands that use EMS partners to supplement their own plants.

History

The industry began in the 1970s with firms assembling circuit boards for computer makers. It expanded rapidly in the 1980s and 1990s as personal-computer production was outsourced, and grew further with mobile phones and consumer electronics in the 2000s. Major providers include Hon Hai Precision Industry (Foxconn), Flex, Jabil, Pegatron, and Wistron.

Services

Beyond PCB assembly, EMS companies may offer design-for-manufacturability analysis, component procurement, system-level "box build" assembly, functional testing, configuration and direct shipment to end customers, and after-sales repair and refurbishment.

Market tiers

The industry is often divided into tiers by annual revenue, with Tier 1 firms exceeding several billion dollars and smaller tiers serving low-volume, high-mix production such as medical devices, aerospace, and industrial equipment, where certification and traceability requirements are stricter.
