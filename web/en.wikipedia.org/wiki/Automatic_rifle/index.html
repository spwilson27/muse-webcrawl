Automatic rifle

An automatic rifle is a type of magazine-fed rifle that is capable of fully automatic fire, chambering a full-power rifle cartridge. Automatic rifles are generally selective-fire weapons capable of both semi-automatic and automatic fire.

The term is often applied to early- and mid-20th-century designs such as the American M1918 Browning Automatic Rifle, the Russian Fedorov Avtomat and the German FG 42, which bridged the gap between rifles and light machine guns. Most modern assault rifles fire intermediate cartridges and are usually classed separately.

History

The first practical automatic rifles appeared around the First World War. The French Chauchat and the Lewis gun were light machine guns rather than rifles, but designs such as the Italian Cei-Rigotti of 1900 had already demonstrated a gas-operated selective-fire shoulder arm. The Fedorov Avtomat, issued in Russia from 1916, used the smaller 6.5 mm Arisaka cartridge to tame recoil and is sometimes described as an early assault rifle.

Full-power cartridges such as .30-06 Springfield or 7.62×54mmR generate strong recoil in automatic fire, making automatic rifles hard to control from the shoulder; many were intended to be fired from a bipod in short bursts in the walking-fire or light machine gun role. This difficulty helped drive the development of intermediate cartridges and the modern assault rifle after the Second World War.
