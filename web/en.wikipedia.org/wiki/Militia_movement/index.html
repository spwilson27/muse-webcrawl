Militia movement

The militia movement is a political movement in the United States that emerged in the 1990s among right-wing activists who organized themselves into paramilitary groups. Its adherents generally believe that the federal government has become, or is becoming, tyrannical, and that armed citizens must be prepared to resist it. The movement is closely related to the older Patriot movement and to sovereign citizen and tax protester ideologies.

History

Although armed citizens' militias have deep roots in American history, the modern militia movement took shape in the early 1990s. Events such as the standoff at Ruby Ridge in 1992 and the federal siege at Waco in 1993 were widely cited by activists as evidence of government overreach. The passage of the Brady Handgun Violence Prevention Act and the Federal Assault Weapons Ban further energized recruitment, and groups such as the Militia of Montana and the Michigan Militia became nationally prominent.

The movement was thrust into national attention after the Oklahoma City bombing of April 1995, carried out by Timothy McVeigh, who had associated with figures on the movement's fringes. In the following years, increased law-enforcement scrutiny and public backlash caused many groups to disband or go underground, and activity declined through the early 2000s.

Resurgence

Monitoring organizations such as the Southern Poverty Law Center reported a resurgence of militia activity beginning around 2008–2009, coinciding with the election of Barack Obama and the Great Recession. Groups including the Oath Keepers and the Three Percenters gained prominence. Militia members participated in armed standoffs with federal authorities, such as the 2014 Bundy standoff in Nevada and the 2016 occupation of the Malheur National Wildlife Refuge in Oregon.

Beliefs

Adherents commonly hold that the Second Amendment guarantees an individual right to keep and bear arms as a check on tyranny, that the federal government has exceeded its constitutional authority, and that a "New World Order" conspiracy threatens American sovereignty. Many groups conduct paramilitary training and stockpile supplies in preparation for perceived crises.
