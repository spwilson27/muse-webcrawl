Thornton is a home rule municipality in Adams and Weld counties in the U.S. state of Colorado, forming part of the Denver metropolitan area. With a population of 141,867 at the 2020 census, it is one of the largest cities in Colorado and among the fastest-growing suburbs of Denver.

History

Thornton began in 1953 as a planned subdivision developed by Sam Hoffman on farmland northeast of Denver. The city was named after Daniel I. J. Thornton, a former governor of Colorado, and was incorporated in 1956 with about 6,000 residents. Growth accelerated with the expansion of the Denver suburbs along the Interstate 25 corridor.

Geography and government

The city lies on the high plains east of the Front Range and is crossed by the South Platte River along its western edge. Thornton operates under a council–manager government and is served by RTD bus routes and the N Line commuter rail, which opened in 2020.
