Westminster Kennel Club Dog Show

The Westminster Kennel Club Dog Show is an annual all-breed conformation show held in New York City by the Westminster Kennel Club. First held in 1877, it is the second-longest continuously held sporting event in the United States, behind only the Kentucky Derby, and predates the invention of the automobile, the light bulb and American professional football. Often called simply "Westminster" or "the Garden" for its long association with Madison Square Garden, the show is the most prestigious dog show in the United States.

History

The show takes its name from the Westminster Hotel, where a group of sporting gentlemen who hunted and owned pointing dogs gathered in the 1870s. They formed a kennel club and held the first show in 1877 at Gilmore's Garden in Manhattan, drawing more than 1,200 dogs. The event proved so popular that it was extended from three days to four. The Westminster Kennel Club is one of the oldest member clubs of the American Kennel Club, which it predates.

For most of its history the show was held at Madison Square Garden in February. It has run uninterrupted through world wars, depressions and blizzards. Recent editions moved to new venues, including the Lyndhurst estate in Tarrytown during the COVID-19 pandemic, and the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center in Queens, before returning to Madison Square Garden.

Competition format

Entry is limited to champion dogs — those that have already earned their championship titles. Judging proceeds through three levels. First, dogs of each breed compete for Best of Breed, judged against the written breed standard. Breed winners then compete within one of seven groups:

  • Sporting — pointers, retrievers, setters and spaniels
  • Hound — scenthounds and sighthounds
  • Working — guard, sled and rescue breeds
  • Terrier — breeds developed to hunt vermin
  • Toy — small companion breeds
  • Non-Sporting — a varied group of breeds that fit no other category
  • Herding — breeds developed to move livestock

The seven group winners meet in the final competition for Best in Show, the show's highest honour, judged by a single judge whose identity is kept secret until the event. Wire Fox Terriers have won Best in Show more often than any other breed, and famous winners include the German Shorthaired Pointer CJ, the Pekingese Wasabi and the Whippet Whiskey.

Cultural significance

Televised nationally for decades — long on the USA Network and later on Fox Sports — Westminster is a fixture of American popular culture, and its winner traditionally receives a media tour including the ceremonial steak dinner at Sardi's restaurant. The show also hosts agility and obedience championships open to mixed-breed dogs, alongside the conformation competition reserved for purebred champions.
