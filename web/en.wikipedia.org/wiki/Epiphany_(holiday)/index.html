Epiphany (holiday)

Epiphany
Date6 January (or nearest Sunday)
Also calledTheophany, Three Kings' Day, Twelfth Night (eve)
ObservancesChurch services, blessing of water, king cake, chalking the door
Related toChristmas, Baptism of Jesus

Epiphany (from Greek epiphaneia, "manifestation") is a Christian feast day, celebrated on 6 January, that commemorates the revelation of God incarnate as Jesus Christ. In Western Christianity it principally marks the visit of the Magi to the Christ child; in Eastern Christianity it commemorates the baptism of Jesus in the Jordan, known as Theophany.

Customs

Traditions include eating king cake (such as the French galette des rois or Spanish roscón de reyes), chalking the door with the year's initials of the Magi (e.g. 20+C+M+B+26), blessing homes and waters, and, in many countries, gift-giving on Epiphany rather than Christmas Day. The eve, Twelfth Night, traditionally ends the Christmas season.

Observance

Epiphany is a public holiday in many countries including Spain, Italy, Greece, Poland and Sweden. Eastern Orthodox churches following the Julian calendar celebrate Theophany on 19 January Gregorian, with the Great Blessing of Waters, sometimes performed by casting a cross into icy water for swimmers to retrieve.
