Marvin Gaye

Marvin Gaye (born Marvin Pentz Gay Jr.; April 2, 1939 – April 1, 1984) was an American soul and R&B singer, songwriter, and producer. One of Motown's signature artists, he earned the nicknames "Prince of Motown" and "Prince of Soul". His landmark albums What's Going On (1971) and Let's Get It On (1973) reshaped popular music, and singles such as "I Heard It Through the Grapevine", "Ain't No Mountain High Enough" (with Tammi Terrell), and "Sexual Healing" remain standards.

Early life

Gaye was born in Washington, D.C., the son of a Pentecostal minister, Marvin Gay Sr., whose strict and often abusive discipline dominated his childhood. He sang in his father's church and taught himself piano and drums, but was expelled from the house as a teenager. After service in the U.S. Air Force he sang with doo-wop groups, including the Marquees, before moving to Detroit, where he worked as a session drummer and singer.

Motown career

Signed to Motown's Tamla label in 1961 (adding the "e" to his surname), Gaye scored hits with "Stubborn Kind of Fellow", "Pride and Joy", "How Sweet It Is (To Be Loved by You)", and duets with Mary Wells, Kim Weston and Tammi Terrell. "I Heard It Through the Grapevine" (1968) became Motown's best-selling single to that date.

Frustrated with Motown's control, Gaye fought to release What's Going On, a song cycle on war, ecology and faith inspired by his brother Frankie's Vietnam service; Berry Gordy reluctantly issued it in 1971, and it became a critical and commercial triumph. Later albums — Trouble Man (1972), Let's Get It On (1973), I Want You (1976) and Here, My Dear (1978) — explored sensuality, spirituality and his divorce from Anna Gordy.

Later years and death

Battling tax debts and cocaine addiction, Gaye lived in Europe before returning to the United States and signing with Columbia Records. His comeback single "Sexual Healing" (1982), from the album Midnight Love, won two Grammy Awards. On April 1, 1984, the day before his 45th birthday, Gaye was shot and killed by his father during an argument at the family home in Los Angeles. He was posthumously inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1987, and What's Going On was ranked the greatest album of all time in Rolling Stone's 2020 list.
