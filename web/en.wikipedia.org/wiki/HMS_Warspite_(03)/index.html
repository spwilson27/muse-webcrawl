HMS Warspite (03)

HMS Warspite was a Queen Elizabeth-class battleship of the Royal Navy, launched in 1913 and commissioned in 1915. Nicknamed the "Grand Old Lady", she fought in both world wars, was the most decorated British battleship of the Second World War, and was credited with one of the longest-range naval gunnery hits in history at the Battle of Calabria in 1940.

Commissioned8 March 1915

FateWrecked under tow 1947; scrapped

First World War

Warspite fought at the Battle of Jutland in 1916, where a jammed rudder caused her to circle twice under heavy German fire while her crew fought to regain control; she survived fifteen heavy-shell hits and her manoeuvre inadvertently drew fire away from the stricken cruiser Warrior.

Second World War

Extensively modernised in the 1930s, Warspite destroyed German destroyers at the Second Battle of Narvik (1940), hit the Italian battleship Giulio Cesare at extreme range at Calabria, led the Mediterranean Fleet's victory at Cape Matapan (1941), and served in the Indian Ocean and at Salerno — where a German Fritz X guided bomb put her out of action for months. Partially repaired, she bombarded German defences on D-Day and later at Brest and Walcheren. Refused preservation, she broke loose under tow to the breakers in 1947 and ran aground in Mount's Bay, Cornwall, where she was scrapped in situ.
