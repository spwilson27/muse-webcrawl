Andrew Barton "Banjo" Paterson (17 February 1864 – 5 February 1941) was an Australian bush poet, journalist, and author. He is regarded, with Henry Lawson, as one of the two defining voices of Australian literature, and his ballads of the outback — horsemen, drovers, swagmen, and shearers — did much to create the mythology of the Australian bush.

Life

Paterson was born at Narrambla, near Orange in New South Wales, and raised on properties in the Yass district and at Buckinbah station near Yeoval. Educated at Sydney Grammar School, he qualified as a solicitor and practised law in Sydney. Writing under the pen name "The Banjo" — taken, he said, from a racehorse owned by his family — he began publishing verse in The Bulletin in 1885.

Works

His first collection, The Man from Snowy River and Other Verses (1895), sold out within weeks and outsold every Australian book of verse before it; it contains the title poem, "Clancy of the Overflow," and "The Man from Ironbark." In 1895, at Winton in Queensland, he wrote the words of "Waltzing Matilda," set to the tune "Craigielee" played by Christina Macpherson; the song became Australia's best-known folk song and an unofficial national anthem. Paterson served as a war correspondent in the Boer War and the First World War, edited the Sydney Evening News, and wrote prose including the novel An Outback Marriage.

Legacy

Paterson's portrait appears on the Australian ten-dollar note, alongside lines from "The Man from Snowy River." His ballads remain staples of Australian school curricula and folk festivals, and the 1982 film The Man from Snowy River carried his most famous narrative to a worldwide audience.
