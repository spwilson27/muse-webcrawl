Romani (also Romany, Roma; Romani: rromani ćhib) is an Indo-Aryan macrolanguage of the Romani people. It originated in northern India and developed in the Byzantine Empire and Europe, absorbing Greek, Slavic, Romanian, and other loanwords.

History and dialects

Linguistic evidence traces Romani migration from India westward about 1,000 years ago. Major dialect groups — Balkan, Vlax, Carpathian, Baltic, and Sinti — are not all mutually intelligible. Para-Romani varieties mix Romani vocabulary with local grammar, e.g. Angloromani.

Status

Romani is recognised as a minority language in many European countries and is taught, broadcast, and published in several standardised forms, though it has no single universal standard. It uses Latin or Cyrillic script depending on region.
