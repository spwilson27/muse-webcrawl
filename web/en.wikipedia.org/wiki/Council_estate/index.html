Council estate

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A council estate is a form of British public housing built by local authorities. A mixture of council houses and flats, estates were typically built between 1919 and 1980 to provide decent, affordable rented homes, initially for working-class families and later as part of slum clearance and post-war reconstruction programmes.

History

Large-scale council building began with the Housing Act 1919 (the "Addison Act"), prompted by promises of "homes fit for heroes" after the First World War. Construction peaked after 1945 under both Labour and Conservative governments. The Housing Act 1980 introduced the Right to Buy, allowing tenants to purchase their homes at a discount; millions of dwellings were sold and the stock of council housing declined sharply.

Design and legacy

Early estates favoured low-rise cottage-style housing; the 1950s and 1960s saw high-rise tower blocks and deck-access developments, some of which later became notorious for construction faults and social problems. Many estates nevertheless remain settled communities, and council housing continues to be a significant tenure in the United Kingdom.
