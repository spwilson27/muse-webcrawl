Suijin (水神, "water god") is the Shinto god of water in Japan. The term refers to the heavenly and earthly manifestations of the benevolent Shinto divinity of water, and serves as an umbrella name for many water kami worshipped at shrines near rivers, lakes, springs, wells, and irrigation channels.

Worship

Suijin are venerated by farmers praying for rain and good rice harvests, by fishermen, and by people seeking protection from drowning and water-borne disease. Small stone shrines and markers inscribed with the characters for "Suijin" are a common sight along riverbanks and paddy field channels. Offerings traditionally included cucumbers, also associated with the kappa, a water creature sometimes treated as a servant or avatar of the water god.

Related deities

Water deities in the Shinto pantheon include Mizuha-no-me, created in the myth of Izanami, and various dragon deities such as Ryūjin, the dragon king of the sea. Suijin worship blends these native traditions with influences from Chinese water gods and Buddhist serpent cults (nāga).
