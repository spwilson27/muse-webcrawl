Eudaimonia

Eudaimonia
FieldAncient Greek ethics
Translation'Flourishing', 'well-being', literally 'good spirit'
Key thinkersAristotle (Nicomachean Ethics); the Stoics; Epicurus
[Image: A Greek laurel wreath and olive branch on a marble plinth]

Eudaimonia is the ancient Greek concept — usually translated 'flourishing' or 'well-being' — of living and doing well, the highest human good in classical ethics. In Aristotle's Nicomachean Ethics it is activity of the soul in accordance with virtue over a complete life: not a feeling but an achievement, combining excellent reasoning, character virtues learned by habit, friendship and enough external goods not to be crushed by fortune.

Schools and revival

Stoics radicalised eudaimonia into virtue alone sufficing even on the rack; Epicurus identified it with untroubled pleasure. Modern virtue ethics and positive psychology (flourishing measures, PERMA) consciously revive the concept, distinguishing it from hedonic happiness as mere pleasant feeling.
