Local government financing vehicle

A local government financing vehicle (LGFV) is an investment company established by a local government, most notably in China, to raise funds for infrastructure and development projects outside the government's own budget. LGFVs borrow from banks, issue bonds, and raise capital using land and other local assets as collateral.

Background

Chinese local governments were long prohibited from borrowing directly or running budget deficits, yet they bore responsibility for funding most public infrastructure. From the 1990s, and especially after the 2008 stimulus programme, municipalities set up thousands of LGFVs — often called "chengtou" (urban development investment) companies — to channel bank loans and bond proceeds into roads, rail, utilities, and property development.

Risks and reform

Because LGFV debt was implicitly backed by local governments, it grew to a very large share of China's overall "hidden" local debt, drawing concern from regulators, auditors, and credit rating agencies. Reforms since the mid-2010s, including the revised Budget Law allowing limited direct provincial bond issuance and debt-swap programmes, have sought to bring this borrowing onto official balance sheets and to harden budget constraints.
