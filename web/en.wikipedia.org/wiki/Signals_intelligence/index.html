Signals intelligence (SIGINT) is intelligence-gathering by interception of signals, whether communications between people (communications intelligence, COMINT) or electronic signals not directly used in communication (electronic intelligence, ELINT). Because sensitive information is often encrypted, SIGINT is closely intertwined with cryptanalysis.

History

Interception of telegraph and radio traffic became systematic in the First World War; Britain's Room 40 decrypted the Zimmermann Telegram in 1917, helping bring the United States into the war. In the Second World War, Allied breaking of the German Enigma machine (Ultra) and the Japanese Purple cipher (Magic) provided decisive intelligence. During the Cold War, vast listening stations, spy aircraft, and satellites collected signals worldwide.

Modern practice

Today SIGINT agencies such as the US National Security Agency and Britain's GCHQ intercept satellite, microwave, cellular, and internet traffic. The global surveillance disclosures of 2013 revealed programmes for bulk collection of metadata and content, prompting worldwide debate about privacy, encryption policy, and oversight of intelligence agencies.

Related disciplines

SIGINT is one of several intelligence collection disciplines, alongside HUMINT (human intelligence), IMINT (imagery intelligence), MASINT (measurement and signature intelligence), and OSINT (open-source intelligence).
