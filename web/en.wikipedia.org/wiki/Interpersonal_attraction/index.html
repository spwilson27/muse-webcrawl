Interpersonal attraction

Interpersonal attraction is the attraction between people that leads to the development of friendships and romantic relationships. It is a major topic in social psychology.

Determinants

Research identifies several main factors: physical attractiveness, proximity, familiarity, similarity in attitudes and background, and reciprocity — the tendency to like those who like us. Repeated exposure to a person generally increases liking, a phenomenon known as the mere-exposure effect.

Physical attractiveness

Studies such as the classic "what is beautiful is good" effect show that physically attractive people are often assumed to have other positive traits. Cultural and individual variation is substantial.

Matching

The matching hypothesis proposes that people tend to form relationships with partners of similar overall social desirability, including attractiveness.
