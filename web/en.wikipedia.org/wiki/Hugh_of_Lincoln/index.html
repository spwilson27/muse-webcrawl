Hugh of Lincoln (c. 1140 – 16 November 1200), also known as Saint Hugh of Lincoln, was a French Benedictine and Carthusian monk who became Bishop of Lincoln, and the first prior of the first Carthusian house in England. He is the best documented of English medieval saints, thanks to the detailed Magna Vita Sancti Hugonis written by his chaplain Adam of Eynsham.

Life

Born at Avalon in Burgundy, Hugh became a monk at the Grande Chartreuse, where he served as procurator, before Henry II brought him to England about 1175 to head the new charterhouse at Witham in Somerset. In 1186 he was elected Bishop of Lincoln, then the largest diocese in England. Hugh proved an energetic and independent bishop: he rebuilt Lincoln Cathedral in the new Gothic style after an earthquake (beginning the work, with its innovative vaulting, in 1192), defended the rights of his church against Henry II and Richard I — famously refusing to fund Richard's foreign wars and to provide a benefice for a courtier — and protected Jews from violence at a time of rising persecution, intervening against mobs at Lincoln and elsewhere. He was famed for his pastoral visiting, his austerity, and his pet swan, which accompanied him at his manor of Stow and mourned famously at his death.

Veneration

Hugh was canonised in 1220, only twenty years after his death. His shrine at Lincoln drew pilgrims until its destruction in the Reformation. He is depicted in art with his swan and a chalice. He should not be confused with Little Saint Hugh of Lincoln, a child around whose alleged death in 1255 a blood libel was directed against the Jews of Lincoln.
