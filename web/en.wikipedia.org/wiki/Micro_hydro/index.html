Micro hydro is a type of hydroelectric power that typically produces up to 100 kW of electricity using the natural flow of water. These installations can provide power to an isolated home or small community and are sometimes connected to electric power networks. Micro hydro is a form of small hydro, below mini hydro and above pico hydro in the usual classification of run-of-river systems.

Characteristics

Micro hydro systems are usually run-of-river schemes: water is diverted from a stream through an intake and penstock to a small powerhouse, where a turbine (common types include Pelton, Turgo and crossflow designs) drives a generator, after which the water returns to the stream. Because little or no reservoir is needed, environmental impact is modest, and civil works can often be built with local labour and materials. Output depends on the flow of the stream and the vertical drop (head) available; higher-head sites require less flow for the same power.

Applications

Micro hydro has been widely used for off-grid rural electrification in mountainous regions of Nepal, Peru, China, Vietnam and sub-Saharan Africa, where community-owned schemes supply lighting, communications and small agro-processing loads. In grid-connected countries it is also installed by individual households, farms and estates with suitable streams, and by water utilities harvesting energy from municipal water flows. Because generation continues day and night whenever water flows, micro hydro complements intermittent sources such as solar power.

Advantages and limitations

Advantages include low running costs, long equipment life, high reliability compared with other microgeneration technologies, and a small environmental footprint. Limitations include dependence on a suitable water resource close to the point of use, seasonal variation in flow, potential conflicts over water rights, and the cost of connecting remote sites to loads.
