Calligram

A calligram is a poem (or other text) in which the words are arranged in a visual design that reflects or illustrates the subject of the text. The typeface, calligraphy or handwriting is arranged so that the text itself forms a picture related to its content.

History

Shaped verse is ancient: Greek poets of the Hellenistic era, such as Simias of Rhodes, wrote pattern poems in the outlines of wings, an egg and an axe, and medieval scribes produced similar figures. Islamic calligraphy developed the art of forming images from sacred text, including zoomorphic calligrams.

Apollinaire

The modern calligram is associated above all with Guillaume Apollinaire, whose collection Calligrammes (1918) — with poems shaped as rain, a mandolin, the Eiffel Tower and a dove stabbed with a fountain — made the form famous and influenced concrete poetry and typographic art in the twentieth century.

Related forms

Calligrams overlap with concrete poetry, micrography and ambigrams; in digital culture their descendants include ASCII art and typographic posters.
