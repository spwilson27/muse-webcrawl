Johan Galtung

Born: 24 October 1930, Oslo, Norway

Died: 17 February 2024 (aged 93), Bærum, Norway

Known for: Founder of peace and conflict studies; structural violence; positive peace

Awards: Right Livelihood Award (1987)

Johan Vincent Galtung (24 October 1930 – 17 February 2024) was a Norwegian sociologist and mathematician regarded as the founder of the academic discipline of peace and conflict studies.

Work

In 1959 Galtung founded the Peace Research Institute Oslo (PRIO), the world's first peace research institute, and in 1964 he founded the Journal of Peace Research. His most influential conceptual contributions are the distinction between negative peace (the absence of direct violence) and positive peace (the absence of structural violence and presence of social justice), and the theory of cultural violence.

Mediation and later life

Galtung founded the Transcend network for peace and development and worked as a mediator in numerous international conflicts. He received the Right Livelihood Award in 1987. His prolific output — over 150 books and 1,500 articles — also included controversial predictions about the decline of American power, which drew both attention and criticism.
