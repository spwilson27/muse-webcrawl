San Gabriel Valley

The San Gabriel Valley is one of the principal valleys of Southern California, located in eastern Los Angeles County. It lies just east of the city of Los Angeles and is bordered by the San Gabriel Mountains to the north, the San Rafael Hills to the west, the Puente Hills to the south, and the San Jose Hills to the east. The valley takes its name from the Spanish Mission San Gabriel Arcángel, founded in 1771, and the San Gabriel River that flows through it.

Communities

The valley includes the cities of Pasadena, Alhambra, Arcadia, San Gabriel, Rosemead, Monterey Park, San Marino, Temple City, El Monte, West Covina, Covina, Azusa, Glendora, Pomona (sometimes included), and dozens of other communities, with a combined population of roughly two million residents.

Demographics and culture

The San Gabriel Valley is noted for its large Asian American population; cities such as Monterey Park — the first suburban Chinatown in the United States — along with San Gabriel, Rowland Heights, and Arcadia, form one of the largest concentrations of Chinese and other Asian communities in the country. The valley is renowned for its Chinese and broader Asian cuisine and is sometimes called the "626" after its area code.

History

Before European settlement, the valley was home to the Tongva (Gabrieleño) people. After the mission era, the land passed through Mexican ranchos and then American ownership, becoming a centre of citrus and walnut agriculture before rapid suburbanisation after the Second World War. Pasadena, at the valley's northwestern end, hosts the Rose Parade and Rose Bowl Game each New Year.
