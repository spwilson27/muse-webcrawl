History of the Czech Republic

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The history of the Czech Republic spans the territory of Bohemia, Moravia, and Czech Silesia from early Slavic settlement through the Great Moravian Empire, the medieval Kingdom of Bohemia, centuries of Habsburg rule, the creation of Czechoslovakia in 1918, Nazi occupation and communist rule, and the peaceful Velvet Divorce of 1993 that produced the modern Czech Republic (Czechia).

Early history and Bohemia

Slavic tribes settled the region in the 6th century. The Great Moravian Empire flourished in the 9th century under rulers such as Mojmír I and Svatopluk I, and the missionaries Cyril and Methodius brought the Glagolitic script. The Duchy of Bohemia emerged under the Přemyslid dynasty; St. Wenceslaus became its patron saint. Bohemia became a kingdom in 1198, and under Charles IV (r. 1346–1378) Prague became an imperial capital, home to Charles University (1348), the first university in Central Europe.

Hussites and Habsburgs

The execution of reformer Jan Hus in 1415 sparked the Hussite Wars. After 1526 the Habsburgs ruled Bohemia; the Battle of White Mountain (1620) crushed the Bohemian estates' revolt and ushered in forced re-Catholicization and Germanization during the Thirty Years' War. A Czech national revival in the 18th and 19th centuries revived the Czech language and culture.

Czechoslovakia and the 20th century

Czechoslovakia was proclaimed in 1918 under Tomáš Garrigue Masaryk. The Munich Agreement of 1938 handed the Sudetenland to Nazi Germany, and Bohemia and Moravia became a German protectorate in 1939. After liberation in 1945, the Communist Party seized power in 1948. The Prague Spring reform movement of 1968 was crushed by a Warsaw Pact invasion. Dissident Václav Havel and the Charter 77 movement sustained opposition until the Velvet Revolution of 1989 restored democracy.

The Czech Republic

On 1 January 1993 Czechoslovakia split peacefully into the Czech Republic and Slovakia. The Czech Republic joined NATO in 1999 and the European Union in 2004. Václav Havel served as its first president, followed by Václav Klaus, Miloš Zeman, and Petr Pavel.
