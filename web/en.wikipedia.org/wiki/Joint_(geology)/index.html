Joint (geology)

Columnar jointing in basalt
FieldStructural geology
DefinitionA fracture in rock with no appreciable displacement

A joint is a break (fracture) in rock along which there has been no visible or measurable movement parallel to the surface (plane) of the fracture. Joints differ from faults, in which displacement has occurred along the break.

Formation

Joints form when rock is subjected to tensional or shear stresses, cooling contraction, or unloading as overlying material is eroded. Systematic joints occur in parallel sets with regular spacing, and intersection of joint sets divides rock into blocks. Columnar joints form in cooling lava flows, producing the hexagonal columns seen at places such as the Giant's Causeway.

Significance

Joints influence the flow of groundwater and hydrocarbons through rock, control patterns of weathering and erosion, and are important in engineering geology for quarrying, tunnelling, and slope stability.
