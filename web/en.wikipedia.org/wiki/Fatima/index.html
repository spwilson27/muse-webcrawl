Fatima bint Muhammad (Arabic: فَاطِمَة بِنْت مُحَمَّد, c. 605/615–632), commonly known as Fatima al-Zahra ("Fatima the Radiant"), was the daughter of the Islamic prophet Muhammad and his first wife Khadija. She was the wife of Ali ibn Abi Talib, the fourth Rashidun caliph and the first imam in Shia Islam, and the mother of Hasan and Husayn. She is one of the most revered women in Islam and the only member of Muhammad's immediate family through whom his descendants — the sayyids — trace their lineage.

Life

Fatima was born in Mecca and endured the early persecution of the Muslims there, caring for her father after her mother's death. She migrated to Medina in 622 and married Ali shortly afterwards; their household was famously austere. Fatima's relations with the first caliph, Abu Bakr, were strained over her claim to her father's inheritance — notably the property of Fadak — a dispute of lasting political and theological significance, especially in Shia tradition. She died in 632, only a few months after Muhammad, and the circumstances of her death and the location of her grave remain subjects of disagreement between Sunni and Shia sources.

Veneration

Fatima is honoured as a member of the Ahl al-Bayt and one of the four "perfect women" in Islamic tradition. In Shia Islam she is counted among the Fourteen Infallibles, and her martyrdom is commemorated during the days of Fatimiyya. In Sunni Islam she is likewise revered as the beloved daughter of the Prophet and a model of piety. Her kunya Umm Abiha ("mother of her father") reflects the closeness of her bond with Muhammad, who is reported to have said that "Fatima is a part of me."
