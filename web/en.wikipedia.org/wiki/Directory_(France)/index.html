Directory (France)

The Directory (French: Directoire) was the governing five-member committee in the French First Republic from 26 October 1795 (4 Brumaire an IV) until 10 November 1799, when it was overthrown by Napoleon Bonaparte in the Coup of 18 Brumaire and replaced by the Consulate. It was the last government of the French Revolution proper and inherited the War of the First Coalition, severe economic instability, and persistent internal unrest including royalist revolts and the aftermath of the Reign of Terror.

Structure

Under the Constitution of the Year III, executive power was vested in five Directors chosen by the legislature, one replaced each year by lot. The legislature consisted of two houses: the Council of Five Hundred, which proposed laws, and the Council of Ancients, which approved or rejected them. Men of property voted in primary assemblies, restricting the franchise compared with the Convention era. Prominent Directors included Paul Barras, the only man to serve for the regime's entire duration, Jean-François Reubell, Louis-Marie de La Révellière-Lépeaux, Lazare Carnot, and Emmanuel Joseph Sieyès.

Policies and crises

The Directory continued the revolutionary wars, creating satellite "sister republics" in the Netherlands, Switzerland, and Italy, where the young general Napoleon Bonaparte won fame in the Italian campaigns of 1796–97. Domestically the regime lurched between royalist and Jacobin threats, repeatedly annulling elections and staging coups against its own legislature — Fructidor (1797), Floréal (1798), and Prairial (1799). Financial collapse forced a partial bankruptcy in 1797, and conscription under the Jourdan Law provoked resistance. Gracchus Babeuf's Conspiracy of Equals was suppressed in 1796.

Fall

Discredited by corruption, military reverses in 1799, and constitutional deadlock, the Directory was overthrown when Napoleon, newly returned from Egypt, dissolved the councils with military support on 18–19 Brumaire (9–10 November 1799), inaugurating the Consulate and ending the revolutionary decade.
