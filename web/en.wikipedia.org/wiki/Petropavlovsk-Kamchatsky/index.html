Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky

Country: Russia

Federal subject: Kamchatka Krai (capital)

Founded: 1740

Population: approx. 180,000

Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky (Russian: Петропавловск-Камчатский) is a city and the administrative centre of Kamchatka Krai, Russia, situated on Avacha Bay on the southeast coast of the Kamchatka Peninsula. It is one of the oldest Russian settlements in the Far East and has no road connection to the rest of Russia — it is reachable only by air or sea.

History

The anchorage was visited by Vitus Bering's Second Kamchatka Expedition, and the town was founded in 1740; its name honours Bering's two ships, the packet boats Saint Peter and Saint Paul. During the Crimean War, in August–September 1854, the small garrison repelled an Anglo-French naval assault in the Siege of Petropavlovsk, a rare Russian success of the war.

In the Soviet era the city became a major fishing port and naval base; the nearby Vilyuchinsk base hosts Pacific Fleet ballistic-missile submarines, and Kamchatka remained closed to foreigners until 1990.

Geography and economy

The city looks across Avacha Bay — one of the world's largest natural harbours — toward the "home volcanoes" Koryaksky, Avachinsky and Kozelsky. The economy rests on fishing and fish processing, shipping, the military and a growing tourism trade drawn by Kamchatka's volcanoes, bears and wilderness. Yelizovo Airport serves as the gateway, with flights to Vladivostok, Moscow and Khabarovsk.
