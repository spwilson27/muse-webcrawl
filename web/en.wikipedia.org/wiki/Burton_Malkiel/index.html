Burton Malkiel

Burton Gordon Malkiel (born August 28, 1932) is an American economist and writer, most famous for his classic finance book A Random Walk Down Wall Street (1973). He is the Chemical Bank Chairman's Professor of Economics Emeritus at Princeton University, where he also served as dean of the Yale School of Management and as a member of the Council of Economic Advisers.

Career

Malkiel graduated from Harvard College in 1953 and, after service as an infantry officer in Korea, earned an MBA from Harvard Business School and a PhD from Princeton in 1964. He joined Princeton's economics department, which he later chaired, and served on President Gerald Ford's Council of Economic Advisers (1975–1977). From 1981 to 1988 he was dean of the Yale School of Management. He has also served as a director of The Vanguard Group and other companies, and as chief investment officer of the robo-advisor Wealthfront.

A Random Walk Down Wall Street

First published in 1973 and repeatedly updated — a thirteenth edition appeared in 2023 — A Random Walk Down Wall Street popularised the efficient-market hypothesis and the random walk theory of stock prices for a general audience. Malkiel argues that investors generally cannot beat the market through stock picking or market timing, and that a portfolio of low-cost, broadly diversified index funds is the most reliable route to long-term returns. The book is credited with helping launch the index fund industry and remains one of the best-selling investment guides ever written.

Other work

Malkiel has written on the Chinese economy, co-authoring From Wall Street to the Great Wall (2008), and has argued in later editions of his book for the continued durability of indexing against challenges ranging from smart beta to behavioural finance.
