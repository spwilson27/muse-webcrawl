A film producer is a person who oversees the making of a film. Producers either are employed by a production company or work independently, and they are involved throughout all phases of the filmmaking process — from development and financing through production and post-production to distribution. The producer typically initiates the project, secures the rights to source material, hires the screenwriter, director, and key crew, and supervises the creative and financial progress of the film.

Responsibilities

During development, the producer assembles the creative package — script, director, principal cast — used to attract finance. In pre-production they approve the budget and schedule and hire department heads; during shooting they monitor progress and solve problems; in post-production they oversee editing, music, and delivery to distributors. The producer is often the person ultimately accountable for completing the film on time and on budget.

Types of producer

  • Executive producer — typically a financier or rights holder who supervises at a distance and supplies or arranges major funding.
  • Producer — the lead creative and organisational figure, credited with the Producers Mark (p.g.a.) when certified by the Producers Guild of America.
  • Co-producer — shares substantial producing duties, or holds a partial financial/creative stake.
  • Associate producer — performs a limited subset of producing tasks, often in development or post-production.
  • Line producer — manages the day-to-day physical production, budget, and logistics, usually hired after financing is in place.
  • Unit production manager — oversees budgetary and scheduling matters on behalf of the studio.

History

In the classical Hollywood studio system, producers such as Irving Thalberg at MGM exercised near-total creative control over their units. The post-studio era elevated independent producers and hyphenates — actor-producers and director-producers such as Robert Evans, Kathleen Kennedy, and Jerry Bruckheimer — whose names became brands in themselves. The Academy Award for Best Picture is presented to the film's producers.

The "producer" credit

Credits have proliferated as they became currency in negotiations, prompting the Producers Guild of America to campaign against vanity credits and to certify genuine lead producers with the Producers Mark. The Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences likewise limits the number of producers eligible for the Best Picture Oscar.
