Union for Democracy and Social Progress (Democratic Republic of the Congo)

The Union for Democracy and Social Progress (French: Union pour la Démocratie et le Progrès Social, UDPS) is a major political party in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Founded in 1982, it was for decades the principal nonviolent opposition movement under Mobutu Sese Seko and his successors.

History

The UDPS was founded on 15 February 1982 by Étienne Tshisekedi and other parliamentarians who broke with Mobutu's ruling MPR, at a time when one-party rule made its existence illegal. Its members suffered imprisonment, exile and torture, and Tshisekedi was repeatedly detained.

In power

After years in opposition, the UDPS won the presidency when Félix Tshisekedi, son of the founder, won the December 2018 general election and took office in January 2019 — the country's first peaceful transfer of power. He was re-elected in December 2023. The party's traditional strongholds are in the Kasai region.
