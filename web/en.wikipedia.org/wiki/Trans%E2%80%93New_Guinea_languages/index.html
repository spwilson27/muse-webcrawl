Trans–New Guinea languages

Geographic distributionNew Guinea and neighbouring islands
Number of languagesAround 480 (including proposed members)
Notable membersEnga, Melpa, Kuman, Dani, Ekari

The Trans–New Guinea languages are a proposed major language family of the island of New Guinea and nearby islands, spoken by roughly three and a half million people. If valid, it would be one of the world's largest language families by number of languages, though the grouping's membership remains disputed among linguists.

Proposal and evidence

The family was first outlined by Joseph Greenberg and developed extensively by Stephen Wurm and Malcolm Ross. Evidence includes shared vocabulary in basic items (such as words for "louse" and "ear"), characteristic pronouns reconstructed for the proto-language, and widespread verb-final (SOV) word order with clause-chaining structures. The family is generally associated with the spread of agriculture in the New Guinea highlands several thousand years ago.

Subgroups

Core members include the Chimbu–Wahgi, Enga–Kewa–Huli, Dani, Paniai Lakes, and Finisterre–Huon groups. Many proposed peripheral members, especially in the Bird's Head and southern lowlands, are contested, and alternative classifications treat parts of the family separately.
