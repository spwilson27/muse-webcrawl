The London Borough of Hackney is a London borough in Inner London, England. The historical and administrative heart of Hackney is Mare Street, which lies 5 kilometres (3 miles) northeast of Charing Cross. The borough includes areas such as Hackney Central, Dalston, Shoreditch, Hoxton, Stoke Newington, Stamford Hill, Clissold Park and Hackney Wick.

History

The borough was formed in 1965 by the merger of the former Metropolitan Boroughs of Hackney, Shoreditch and Stoke Newington. The name Hackney derives from an Old English form recorded as Haconeia in the 13th century. In Tudor times Hackney was a rural retreat for courtiers; Henry VIII had a palace at Lea Bridge Road. The area industrialised in the 19th century, then declined in the late 20th century before becoming one of London's most rapidly gentrifying districts in the 21st.

Governance

Hackney London Borough Council is the local authority, and since 2002 the borough has been led by a directly elected mayor. The council offices are at Hackney Town Hall on Mare Street.

Demographics and culture

Hackney is one of the most ethnically diverse boroughs in London, with large Black, Turkish and Kurdish, South Asian and Orthodox Jewish communities — Stamford Hill is home to the largest Hasidic Jewish community in Europe. The borough has a strong arts and nightlife scene, particularly around Shoreditch and Dalston, and includes the Hackney Empire theatre and the Hackney Museum. Part of the Queen Elizabeth Olympic Park lies within the borough at Hackney Wick.
