François Viète

Born1540, Fontenay-le-Comte, France
Died23 February 1603, Paris, France
Known forSymbolic algebra, Viète's formulas

François Viète, Seigneur de la Bigotière (Latin: Franciscus Vieta; 1540 – 23 February 1603) was a French mathematician whose work on new algebra was an important step towards modern algebra, due to his innovative use of letters as parameters in equations.

Mathematics

In his In artem analyticem isagoge (1591), Viète systematically used letters — vowels for unknowns and consonants for known quantities — laying the foundations of symbolic algebra. He gave Viète's formulas relating the coefficients of a polynomial to its roots, discovered an infinite product converging to π, and advanced trigonometry and geometry.

Codebreaking

Serving Henry III and Henry IV of France, Viète famously deciphered Spanish diplomatic ciphers during the war with Spain, a feat so complete that Spain complained to the Pope that France must be using sorcery.
