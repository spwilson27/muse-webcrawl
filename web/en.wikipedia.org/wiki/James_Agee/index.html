James Rufus Agee (27 November 1909 – 16 May 1955) was an American novelist, journalist, poet, screenwriter, and film critic. He is chiefly remembered for Let Us Now Praise Famous Men (1941), his collaboration with photographer Walker Evans, and for the posthumous novel A Death in the Family, which won the Pulitzer Prize.

Life

Agee was born in Knoxville, Tennessee; his father's death in a car accident in 1916 marked his childhood and later inspired A Death in the Family. Educated at Phillips Exeter and Harvard, where he edited the Harvard Advocate, he went to work for Fortune magazine in the 1930s.

Let Us Now Praise Famous Men

In 1936 Fortune sent Agee and Evans to document the lives of impoverished white tenant farmers in Alabama during the Great Depression. The resulting book (1941) — pairing Evans's stark photographs with Agee's anguished, lyrical prose — sold poorly on publication but came to be regarded as a masterpiece of documentary literature.

Film work

As film critic for Time and The Nation in the 1940s, Agee wrote criticism still admired for its wit and passion. He turned to screenwriting, co-writing The African Queen (1951) with John Huston and writing The Night of the Hunter (1955). Heavy drinking contributed to his death from a heart attack at age 45.
