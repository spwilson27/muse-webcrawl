The White Ship (French: la Blanche-Nef) was a vessel conveying the heir to the English throne, William Adelin, and many leading Anglo-Norman nobles, which sank in the English Channel near Barfleur, Normandy, on 25 November 1120. Only one person survived. The death of William Adelin, the only legitimate son of King Henry I of England, threw the succession into crisis and ultimately led, after Henry's death, to the civil war known as the Anarchy.

The disaster

The ship, captained by Thomas FitzStephen, whose father had steered William the Conqueror's ship in 1066, departed Barfleur for England in darkness after extensive drinking by passengers and crew. It struck a submerged rock (identified with the Quillebœuf rock) and foundered. William Adelin initially escaped in a small boat but returned on hearing his half-sister's cries; the boat was swamped by others trying to board and all were drowned. The sole survivor was Berold, a butcher from Rouen, who clung to the rock overnight.

Consequences

Among the dead were two of Henry I's children, his nephew, and much of the young Anglo-Norman court. Henry's only remaining legitimate child was the Empress Matilda; the barons' refusal to accept her led Stephen of Blois to seize the throne in 1135, beginning the Anarchy (1135–1153).
