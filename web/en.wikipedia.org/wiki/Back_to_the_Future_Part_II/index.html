Back to the Future Part II is a 1989 American science fiction film directed by Robert Zemeckis and written by Bob Gale. It is the sequel to the 1985 film Back to the Future and the second instalment in the franchise. The film stars Michael J. Fox, Christopher Lloyd, Lea Thompson and Thomas F. Wilson.

Plot

Picking up where the first film ended, Marty McFly is whisked by Dr. Emmett "Doc" Brown from 1985 to October 21, 2015, to prevent his future son from making a disastrous mistake. In the future Hill Valley — with flying cars, hoverboards and self-lacing shoes — Marty buys a sports almanac containing fifty years of results; the elderly Biff Tannen steals the time machine, gives the almanac to his younger self in 1955, and thereby creates a dystopian alternate 1985 in which Biff is rich and powerful. Marty and Doc return to 1955 to recover the almanac, carefully avoiding their earlier selves from the first film's events. The film ends on a cliffhanger: Doc and the DeLorean are struck by lightning and vanish to 1885, setting up Back to the Future Part III (1990).

Production

Zemeckis and Gale had not planned a sequel during the first film's production; its closing scene was intended as a joke. After the original's success, Universal commissioned sequels, and Parts II and III were filmed back-to-back. Crispin Glover, who played George McFly in the first film, did not return after a salary dispute, and the role was reduced and recast; his likeness was used in ways that led to a lawsuit and to new Screen Actors Guild rules on the use of actors' images. Elisabeth Shue replaced Claudia Wells as Jennifer Parker.

Reception and legacy

The film grossed over $330 million worldwide, the third-highest-grossing film of 1989. Reviews were more mixed than for the first instalment, with criticism of its complex plot, though its reputation has grown over time. Its vision of 2015 became a cultural touchstone: the date October 21, 2015 was widely celebrated as "Back to the Future Day", and its predictions — video calling, wearable electronics, biometric locks, and, less accurately, hoverboards and flying cars — were extensively revisited. Nike released limited self-lacing shoes inspired by Marty's footwear.
