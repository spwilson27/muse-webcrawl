Charles X Gustav

Charles X Gustav, also known as Charles Gustav (Swedish: Karl X Gustav; 8 November 1622 – 13 February 1660), was King of Sweden from 1654 until his death. A member of the House of Palatinate-Zweibrücken, he succeeded his cousin Queen Christina and is remembered as a warrior king whose campaigns in Poland and Denmark brought the Swedish Empire close to its greatest territorial extent.

Reign and wars

Charles Gustav gained military experience in the final years of the Thirty Years' War. Crowned in 1654 after Christina's abdication, he launched the Second Northern War in 1655, invading the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth during the period known in Poland as the Deluge. Swedish forces occupied Warsaw and Kraków, though resistance under Stefan Czarniecki and the defence of Jasna Góra eventually forced a retreat.

When Denmark declared war in 1657, Charles marched his army across the frozen Danish straits — the famous March Across the Belts in the winter of 1658 — compelling Denmark to sign the Treaty of Roskilde, which ceded Scania, Halland, Blekinge and Bohuslän to Sweden. A second campaign against Denmark failed to take Copenhagen. Charles died suddenly at Gothenburg in 1660 while preparing further campaigns; the Treaties of Oliva and Copenhagen later that year ended the war.
