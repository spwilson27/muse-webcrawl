Gilles Fauconnier

Gilles Fauconnier (19 August 1944 – 3 February 2021) was a French linguist, researcher in cognitive science, and author, then a professor at the University of California, San Diego in the Department of Cognitive Science. His work with mental spaces and conceptual blending was foundational for cognitive linguistics.

Born: 19 August 1944, France
Died: 3 February 2021
Known for: Mental spaces, conceptual blending
Fields: Linguistics, cognitive science

Mental spaces

Fauconnier's book Mental Spaces: Aspects of Meaning Construction in Natural Language (1985; expanded edition 1994) proposed that natural language expressions do not refer directly to the world but instead prompt the construction of "mental spaces" — partial cognitive structures built up as discourse unfolds, connected by mappings that account for reference, tense, counterfactuality, and pragmatic inference.

Conceptual blending

With Mark Turner, Fauconnier developed conceptual integration theory ("blending"), set out in The Way We Think (2002). Blending describes how elements from two or more input mental spaces are selectively projected into a blended space with emergent structure, a process proposed to underlie metaphor, analogy, counterfactual reasoning, and much of human creativity.
