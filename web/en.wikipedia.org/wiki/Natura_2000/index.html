Natura 2000

Established: 1992 (Habitats Directive)
Coverage: About 18% of EU land area; some 27,000 sites
Legal basis: Birds Directive (1979/2009), Habitats Directive (1992)

Natura 2000 is a network of protected areas covering Europe's most valuable and threatened species and habitats. It is the largest coordinated network of protected areas in the world, and the centrepiece of the European Union's nature conservation policy.

Structure

The network consists of Special Protection Areas (SPAs), designated under the Birds Directive for rare and migratory birds, and Special Areas of Conservation (SACs), designated under the Habitats Directive for listed habitat types and species other than birds. Sites are proposed by member states on purely scientific criteria and, once designated, must be managed to maintain or restore their habitats and species at "favourable conservation status".

Management

Natura 2000 is not a system of strict nature reserves: most sites are privately owned and farmed, fished or forested, and human activity continues where it is compatible with the conservation objectives. Any plan or project likely to significantly affect a site must undergo an "appropriate assessment", and may proceed despite negative effects only for imperative reasons of overriding public interest, with compensation. The network underpins much European environmental law litigation and reporting.
