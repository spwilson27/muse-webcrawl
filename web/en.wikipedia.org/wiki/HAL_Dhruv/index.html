The HAL Dhruv is a utility helicopter designed and developed by Hindustan Aeronautics Limited (HAL) in the Advanced Light Helicopter (ALH) programme. "Dhruv" is a Sanskrit name meaning 'unshakeable' or 'firm'. Development began in 1984 (with design assistance from Messerschmitt-Bölkow-Blohm of Germany), the type first flew on 20 August 1992, and it entered service with the Indian armed forces in 2002 following a prolonged development period caused in part by changing requirements and by sanctions after India's 1998 nuclear tests.

Design

The Dhruv is a twin-engine, multi-role helicopter in the 5.5-tonne class, seating up to 14 in transport configuration. Early aircraft use two Turbomeca TM 333 engines; most later aircraft use the HAL–Turbomeca Shakti (Safran Ardiden 1H1) engine, improving high-altitude performance in the Himalayas. The aircraft has a hingeless main rotor, glass cockpit and wheeled or skid landing gear options. Variants include the weaponised HAL Rudra and the Light Combat Helicopter derived from the platform.

Operational history

The Dhruv serves with all branches of the Indian military and with civil and export operators including Nepal, Mauritius, the Maldives and Israel. The type has been used extensively for high-altitude logistics and rescue, including at the Siachen Glacier, and in disaster relief. The Indian Air Force's Sarang display team flies the Dhruv. Export sales were set back by the grounding and return of Ecuador's Dhruvs after several crashes; the type has also suffered attrition accidents in Indian service, leading to fleet-wide safety checks and modifications.

Variants

  • Dhruv Mk I–IV — progressive avionics and engine upgrades.
  • HAL Rudra — armed Weapon System Integrated (WSI) version.
  • Dhruv (NG) — civil variant offered for offshore and utility roles.
