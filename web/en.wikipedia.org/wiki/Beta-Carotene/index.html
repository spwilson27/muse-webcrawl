β-Carotene (beta-carotene) is an organic, strongly coloured red-orange pigment abundant in fungi, plants, and fruits. It is a member of the carotenes, a class of terpenoids, and is the most familiar of the carotenoids. In the human body, β-carotene is cleaved by an enzyme in the intestinal mucosa to form retinol (vitamin A), and it is therefore classified as a provitamin A carotenoid.

Sources

Rich dietary sources of β-carotene include carrots, sweet potatoes, pumpkins, spinach, kale, cantaloupe, and mangoes. The compound's name derives from the Latin for carrot, carota. It is also produced commercially — by chemical synthesis, by fermentation, or from the alga Dunaliella salina — for use as a food colouring (E160a) and dietary supplement.

Health

Vitamin A deficiency remains a major cause of preventable blindness in children in developing countries, and β-carotene-rich foods and biofortified crops such as Golden Rice are used to combat it. Large clinical trials, however, found that high-dose β-carotene supplements increased the risk of lung cancer in smokers and asbestos workers, so supplements are not recommended for that population.
