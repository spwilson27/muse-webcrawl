Rope access

Rope access or industrial climbing is a form of work positioning, initially developed from techniques used in climbing and caving, which applies practical ropework to allow workers to access difficult-to-reach locations without the use of scaffolding, cradles, or aerial work platforms.

Technique and safety

Rope access technicians descend, ascend, and traverse ropes while suspended in a harness, using a two-rope system: a working line for positioning and an independent safety (backup) line. The discipline has a strong safety record attributed to rigorous training and certification, notably through bodies such as IRATA International (the Industrial Rope Access Trade Association, founded in the UK in the 1980s for work on offshore oil and gas installations) and SPRAT in North America.

Applications

Common applications include inspection, surveying, maintenance, and repair of bridges, dams, wind turbines, offshore platforms, high-rise buildings (including window cleaning), and geological work on cliffs and rock faces. Advantages over scaffolding include rapid setup, minimal footprint, and lower cost for short-duration tasks.
