Pattern language

A pattern language is an organized and coherent set of patterns, each of which describes a problem and a proven solution, together with the links between them, that can be used to design complex structures. The term was coined by architect Christopher Alexander and popularised in his books A Pattern Language (1977) and The Timeless Way of Building (1979).

Alexander's pattern language

A Pattern Language, written with Sara Ishikawa, Murray Silverstein and others at the Center for Environmental Structure in Berkeley, presents 253 patterns descending in scale from regions and towns to neighbourhoods, buildings, rooms and construction details. Each pattern states a context, a problem arising from competing forces, and a solution, and connects upward to larger patterns and downward to smaller ones it needs to complete it. Alexander argued that such languages let ordinary people design their own environments and generate a "quality without a name" in built form.

Influence

The concept profoundly influenced software engineering, inspiring the design patterns movement — notably the 1994 book Design Patterns by the "Gang of Four" — as well as object-oriented programming culture, agile methods, and wiki software: Ward Cunningham created the wiki partly to support pattern sharing. Pattern languages have since been applied to education, organisation design and human–computer interaction.
