The Uruk-hai are a fictional race of Orc-kind in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium, appearing principally in The Lord of the Rings. They are stronger, taller and more disciplined than ordinary Orcs, and can move swiftly in daylight. In the Third Age they served both Sauron from Mordor and the wizard Saruman at Isengard, whose Uruk-hai bore the emblem of the White Hand.

In Tolkien's writings

Uruk-hai is Black Speech for "Orc-folk": uruk (orc, especially a great soldier-orc) plus hai ("folk"). Great Uruks had served Mordor since the fall of Barad-dûr's first age of power, but Saruman's breeding of his own Uruk-hai in the years before the War of the Ring produced the force that dominates the Isengard strand of the story. Treebeard and Aragorn speculate that Saruman had cross-bred Orcs and Men — a suggestion Tolkien's drafts support — giving his Uruk-hai their stature, endurance and contemptuous discipline.

Role in the narrative

A troop of Saruman's Uruk-hai under Uglúk, clashing with Mordor Orcs under Grishnákh, ambushes the Fellowship at Amon Hen, kills Boromir and captures Merry and Pippin; the band is destroyed by Éomer's riders in Rohan after the hobbits escape into Fangorn. Ten thousand Uruk-hai form the main strength of Saruman's army at the Battle of Helm's Deep, where they are annihilated after the Huorns close in. Individual Uruk-hai such as Shagrat and the tracker Orcs of Mordor also appear around Cirith Ungol.

Adaptations

In Peter Jackson's film trilogy the Uruk-hai are heavily built prosthetic creations, led by Lurtz, an original character, whose birth scene — dug from a membrane in the pits of Isengard — visualises Saruman's breeding programme. Their design, with white handprints on faces and shields, became one of the films' most recognisable images.
