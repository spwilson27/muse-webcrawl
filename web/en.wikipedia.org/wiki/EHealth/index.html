eHealth

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

eHealth (also written e-health or EHealth) describes healthcare practice supported by electronic processes and communication, including the internet, mobile devices and related digital technologies. The term emerged in the late 1990s alongside "e-commerce" and has been defined in dozens of ways; a widely cited early definition by Gunther Eysenbach framed it as an emerging field at the intersection of medical informatics, public health and business.

Scope

eHealth encompasses electronic health records, telemedicine and telehealth, health information exchange, patient portals, online health information and peer support, remote monitoring, and mobile health (mHealth) delivered through phones and wearables. Related but distinct terms include digital health, which is often used more broadly to include consumer wellness technologies and data-driven services.

Benefits and challenges

Advocates point to improved access for rural and mobility-limited patients, more efficient administration, better data for research and public health, and support for self-management of chronic conditions. Use of telehealth expanded dramatically during the COVID-19 pandemic. Challenges include interoperability between systems, privacy and security of sensitive data, the digital divide that can exclude poorer or older patients, variable evidence for the effectiveness of health apps, and regulatory questions over licensing, reimbursement and liability across jurisdictions.

Policy

The World Health Organization's Global Observatory for eHealth surveyed member states' adoption and published a global strategy on digital health. Many countries have national eHealth strategies covering electronic prescriptions, shared care records and telehealth standards.
