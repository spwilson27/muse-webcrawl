United Bible Societies

The United Bible Societies (UBS) is a global fellowship of national Bible Societies working to translate, publish and distribute the Bible. Founded in 1946 at a conference at Elfinsward, Haywards Heath, England, it brings together around 150 Bible Societies operating in more than 200 countries and territories. The fellowship's stated mission is to make the Bible available to everyone in a language and format they can understand and afford.

History

The Bible Society movement began with the British and Foreign Bible Society in 1804 and the American Bible Society in 1816. After the Second World War, national societies sought closer coordination, forming the UBS in 1946 with delegates initially from 13 countries. The fellowship grew rapidly during decolonisation, supporting the creation of indigenous Bible Societies across Africa, Asia and Latin America.

Translation work

UBS is known for its scholarly translation programme. Its translation consultants support hundreds of local projects, and its Greek New Testament and Hebrew Bible editions — produced with the German Bible Society and the Institute for New Testament Textual Research — are standard scholarly texts. Landmark UBS-supported translations include the Good News Bible (Today's English Version), first published in full in 1976, and many first-time translations into minority languages. The fellowship set a goal of completing translation work in hundreds of languages under its "Bible Translation Roadmap" towards 2038.

Distribution and engagement

Member societies distribute tens of millions of Bibles and Scripture portions annually, in print, audio and digital formats, often working with churches of all denominations. UBS also runs literacy programmes and trauma-healing initiatives based on Bible engagement. The fellowship is governed by a Global Council and a Global Mission Team, and as an ecumenical body it cooperates with the Roman Catholic Church — under guidelines agreed with the Vatican in 1968 for interconfessional translation — as well as Orthodox and Protestant churches.
