The Barrow-downs, or Tyrn Gorthad in Sindarin, are a fictional tract of rolling hills and ancient burial mounds in J. R. R. Tolkien's Middle-earth legendarium, appearing principally in The Lord of the Rings. They lie east of the Old Forest, within sight of the village of Bree, and consist of great grass-covered barrows raised over the tombs of the Men of the First Age and later of the Dúnedain of Cardolan.

Description

The downs are a treeless country of low hills crowned by burial mounds, some marked with rings of standing stones. The oldest graves date from the First Age, when the Edain buried their dead there before crossing into Beleriand. In the Third Age the kingdom of Cardolan used the downs as a royal necropolis, and after Cardolan fell to Angmar, the Witch-king sent evil spirits — the Barrow-wights — to inhabit the tombs, making the downs a place of dread avoided by the peoples of Eriador.

In The Lord of the Rings

In "Fog on the Barrow-downs", the eighth chapter of The Fellowship of the Ring, Frodo Baggins and his companions are trapped by a Barrow-wight after losing their way in fog, and are rescued by Tom Bombadil, who routs the wight and distributes the treasure of the barrow. From it Merry, Pippin and Sam take ancient blades of Westernesse, forged for the wars against Angmar — the sword with which Merry later wounds the Witch-king at the Battle of the Pelennor Fields. The episode was adapted from Tolkien's 1934 poem "The Adventures of Tom Bombadil" and was omitted from Peter Jackson's film trilogy.

Inspiration

The Barrow-downs draw on the real barrow cemeteries of southern England, such as those on the Berkshire and Wiltshire downs near Tolkien's Oxford, and on the undead draugar of Norse saga.
