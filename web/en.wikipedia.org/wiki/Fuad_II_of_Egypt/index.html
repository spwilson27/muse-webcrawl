Fuad II of Egypt

Fuad II (born 16 January 1952), born Prince Ahmad Fuad, was the last King of Egypt and the Sudan. He ascended the throne on 26 July 1952, aged six months, upon the abdication of his father, Farouk I, following the Egyptian revolution of 1952. He reigned as an infant under a regency council for less than a year, until the monarchy was abolished and Egypt declared a republic on 18 June 1953.

Reign and exile

Fuad was the son of King Farouk and his second wife, Narriman Sadek. After the Free Officers' coup of July 1952, Farouk abdicated in favour of his son and sailed into exile aboard the royal yacht. The infant king remained briefly in Egypt under a Council of Regency headed by Prince Muhammad Abdel Moneim, while real power rested with the Revolutionary Command Council. When the republic was proclaimed in 1953, Fuad left Egypt to join his father in Italy.

Later life

Fuad II was educated in Switzerland, at the Institut Le Rosey, and studied at the University of Geneva. He worked in finance and has lived mainly in Switzerland and France. In 1976 he married Dominique-France Loeb-Picard in Paris, in a wedding attended by the Shah of Iran; the couple, who had three children — including Muhammad Ali, Prince of the Sa'id, his heir — later divorced. Fuad II has occasionally visited Egypt, where he has been received with public interest, and he has supported charitable and cultural causes relating to Egyptian heritage.
