The Estonian Soviet Socialist Republic (Estonian: Eesti Nõukogude Sotsialistlik Vabariik, abbreviated Estonian SSR or ESSR) was a first-level administrative unit and a union republic of the Soviet Union from 1940 to 1991. It was established in the territory of the previously independent Republic of Estonia following its occupation by Soviet forces in June 1940, and its annexation was never recognised as legal by most Western democracies.

History

Under the secret protocols of the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact, Estonia fell into the Soviet sphere. After an ultimatum and military occupation in June 1940, a staged election produced a parliament that proclaimed the Estonian SSR on 21 July 1940 and petitioned for admission to the USSR, granted on 6 August. Mass deportations and repression followed; in 1941 Estonia was occupied by Nazi Germany, then reoccupied by the Red Army in 1944, restoring Soviet rule for nearly five decades. The March deportation of 1949 removed more than 20,000 people to Siberia, and armed resistance by the Forest Brothers continued into the 1950s.

The republic's economy was integrated into the Soviet system, with industrialisation and immigration from other parts of the USSR reducing the ethnic Estonian share of the population from about 88% in 1934 to about 61% by 1989.

Restoration of independence

During the Singing Revolution, the Estonian Supreme Soviet declared Soviet rule illegal in November 1988 and renamed the republic the Republic of Estonia on 8 May 1990. On 20 August 1991, during the attempted coup in Moscow, Estonia declared the restoration of its independence, which the USSR recognised on 6 September 1991.
