Rubella

SynonymsGerman measles, three-day measles
CauseRubella virus (Rubivirus)
TransmissionRespiratory droplets
SymptomsRash, low-grade fever, swollen lymph nodes
PreventionMMR vaccine

Rubella, also known as German measles or three-day measles, is an infection caused by the rubella virus. It is usually a mild illness characterised by a pink rash, low-grade fever and swollen lymph nodes, and many infections pass without symptoms.

Signs and symptoms

The rash typically begins on the face and spreads to the trunk and limbs, lasting about three days. Joint pain is common in adults, especially women. Complications are rare in childhood but can include encephalitis and bleeding problems.

Congenital rubella syndrome

Rubella is most dangerous in pregnancy: infection in the first trimester can cause miscarriage or congenital rubella syndrome, with deafness, cataracts, heart defects and intellectual disability in the child. This risk is the main reason for immunisation programmes.

Prevention and elimination

The rubella vaccine, usually given as part of the MMR vaccine, is highly effective. Rubella was declared eliminated from the Americas in 2015, and many other regions have interrupted endemic transmission, though outbreaks still occur where vaccination coverage is low.
