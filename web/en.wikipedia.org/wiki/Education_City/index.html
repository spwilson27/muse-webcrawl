Education City

LocationAl Rayyan, near Doha, Qatar
Areaapprox. 14 km² (5.4 sq mi)
DeveloperQatar Foundation

Education City (Arabic: المدينة التعليمية, al-Madīna at-Taʿlīmiyya) is a large campus development on the outskirts of Doha, Qatar, created by the Qatar Foundation for Education, Science and Community Development. Covering some 14 square kilometres, it hosts branch campuses of several foreign universities, schools, research institutes, the Qatar National Library, and cultural and sporting facilities, including the Education City Stadium built for the 2022 FIFA World Cup.

Branch campuses

Education City is best known for hosting branch campuses of American and European universities that award the same degrees as their home institutions, with admission open to students from Qatar, the region and worldwide:

  • Weill Cornell Medicine – Qatar (medical school of Cornell University)
  • Carnegie Mellon University in Qatar (computer science, business, information systems)
  • Georgetown University in Qatar (School of Foreign Service)
  • Northwestern University in Qatar (journalism and communication)
  • Texas A&M University at Qatar (engineering)
  • Virginia Commonwealth University School of the Arts in Qatar (design)
  • HEC Paris in Qatar (executive education)
  • University College London Qatar (museum studies and related programmes; closed 2020)

Students can cross-register for courses at the different campuses. The first branch campus, Virginia Commonwealth's, opened in 1998; Virginia Commonwealth and the others followed through the 2000s.

Other institutions

The campus also includes Hamad Bin Khalifa University (a homegrown graduate institution), Qatar Academy schools, the Qatar Science and Technology Park, the Sidra Medicine hospital, the Qatar National Library (opened 2018, designed by Rem Koolhaas's OMA), Mathaf: Arab Museum of Modern Art, and the Qatar Philharmonic Orchestra's home venues. A dedicated tram system links the campuses.

Criticism and context

The project is a flagship of Qatar's strategy to diversify beyond hydrocarbons into a knowledge economy. It has drawn debate abroad about academic freedom at branch campuses in an absolute monarchy, and about the labour conditions of the migrant workers who built the facilities — issues also raised around the 2022 World Cup, for which Education City Stadium hosted matches.
