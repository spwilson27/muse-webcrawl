Dauer larva

Dauer (German for "duration" / "enduring") is an alternative developmental stage of nematode worms, particularly rhabditids such as Caenorhabditis elegans, in which the larva enters a type of stasis, survives harsh conditions, and does not feed. It is analogous to hibernation or diapause in other animals.

Dauer larva
Organisms: Nematodes, esp. C. elegans
Stage: Alternative third larval stage (L3)
Triggers: Crowding pheromone, low food, high temperature
Key pathways: Insulin/IGF-1 (daf-2), TGF-β (daf-7)

Formation

Under favourable conditions C. elegans develops through four larval stages (L1–L4) to adulthood. When L1/L2 larvae sense crowding (ascaroside pheromones, including daumone), scarce food and heat, they enter the dauer stage instead of L3. Dauer larvae are thin, radially constricted, have a sealed mouth, thickened cuticle with alae, remodelled metabolism relying on fat stores, and stress-resistant physiology; they can survive months without feeding and resume development when conditions improve, moulting to L4.

Genetics and ageing research

Dauer decision is controlled by insulin-like signalling (daf-2 / age-1), TGF-β-like daf-7 signalling and steroid hormone dafachronic acid acting on DAF-12. Dauer biology underpins research on longevity, because daf-2 mutants form dauers and live much longer as adults—work foundational to ageing biology recognized in studies of insulin signalling.

Ecology

Dauers disperse by nictation—standing on their tails and waving to attach to passing invertebrate carriers (phoresis)—and homologous stages occur in parasitic nematodes as infective larvae.
