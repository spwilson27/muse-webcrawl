The alkali metals are the elements of group 1 of the periodic table: lithium (Li), sodium (Na), potassium (K), rubidium (Rb), caesium (Cs) and francium (Fr). They are soft, silvery metals with low melting points and one electron in their outer shell, which they lose eagerly — making them the most reactive metals.

All react with water to give an alkaline solution of the metal hydroxide plus hydrogen gas, hence their name. Reactivity increases down the group: lithium fizzes steadily, sodium melts and darts about, potassium ignites with a lilac flame, and caesium explodes on contact.

[Image: Alkali metals stored under oil in glass jars, with flame-test colours]

Properties and storage

Alkali metals are so reactive that they are stored under oil to exclude air and water, and they tarnish within seconds of being cut. They are excellent conductors and their compounds give characteristic flame colours used in fireworks and flame tests: lithium crimson, sodium yellow, potassium lilac.

Uses

Sodium and potassium compounds are industrially and biologically essential; lithium powers rechargeable batteries and treats bipolar disorder; caesium's atomic vibrations define the second. Francium is vanishingly rare and highly radioactive.
