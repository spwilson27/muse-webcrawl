Ibrox disaster

The term Ibrox disaster refers to two catastrophic crowd incidents at Ibrox Stadium in Glasgow, Scotland, the home of Rangers Football Club. The first occurred in 1902, when a section of wooden terracing collapsed during a Scotland v England international, killing 25 people. The second, and deadlier, occurred on 2 January 1971, when a crush on a stairway at the end of an Old Firm match against Celtic killed 66 people and injured more than 200.

1971 disaster

On 2 January 1971, Rangers played Celtic at Ibrox before a crowd of around 80,000. With Celtic leading 1–0 late in the game, many Rangers supporters began leaving via Stairway 13 at the eastern end of the ground. In the 89th minute Colin Stein scored an equalizer for Rangers. The disaster occurred on the stairway shortly afterward, when barriers gave way under pressure and supporters were crushed in a pile that in places reached six feet deep. Sixty-six people died, most from compressive asphyxia; the victims ranged in age from 13 to 49, and included 31 teenagers.

For many years it was popularly believed that the crush was caused by fans rushing back up the stairs after Stein's goal, but the official inquiry found no evidence for this: the victims were moving in the same direction, down the stairway, when the crush happened.

Aftermath

The 1971 disaster was the worst in British football history at the time and led directly to the Safety of Sports Grounds Act 1975 and the licensing and modernization of stadiums. Ibrox was comprehensively redeveloped in the following decade into one of Britain's first modern all-seater stadiums. A memorial and statue of John Greig, the Rangers captain on the day, stand at the ground commemorating the victims; smaller incidents on the same stairway in 1961, 1967, and 1969 had preceded the disaster.
