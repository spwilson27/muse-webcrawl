First Australian Imperial Force

The First Australian Imperial Force (1st AIF) was the main expeditionary force of the Australian Army during the First World War. It was formed as an all-volunteer force on 15 August 1914, following Britain's declaration of war on Germany, initially with a strength of one infantry division and a light horse brigade. Over the course of the war the AIF eventually expanded to five infantry divisions plus supporting units, and was noted for its effectiveness, particularly in 1918 under General Sir John Monash.

Formation and Gallipoli

Because the Defence Act restricted the peacetime militia to service in Australia, a separate volunteer force was raised for overseas service. The AIF's first contingent sailed in November 1914, originally bound for England but diverted to Egypt, where it trained and helped defend the Suez Canal. On 25 April 1915 AIF troops, alongside New Zealanders as the Australian and New Zealand Army Corps (ANZAC), landed at Gallipoli in the Dardanelles campaign. After eight months of stalemate and heavy casualties, the force was evacuated in December 1915. The Gallipoli landing became a defining event in Australian national memory, commemorated annually on Anzac Day.

Western Front and the Middle East

After Gallipoli the AIF was reorganised: infantry divisions went to the Western Front in France and Belgium, while the light horse remained in the Middle East, fighting in the Sinai and Palestine campaign. On the Western Front the Australians fought at Fromelles, Pozières, Bullecourt, Messines, Passchendaele, and in the German spring offensive and Hundred Days of 1918, including the Battle of Amiens (8 August 1918) and the breaking of the Hindenburg Line at Montbrehain. Meanwhile mounted troops took part in the charge of the Australian Light Horse at Beersheba (31 October 1917).

Numbers and legacy

In total, 331,781 Australians served in the 1st AIF overseas, of whom more than 60,000 were killed and 156,000 wounded, gassed, or taken prisoner — a casualty rate among the highest of the belligerents, reflecting that Australia (uniquely among the major dominions) relied entirely on volunteers after two conscription referendums failed in 1916 and 1917. The force was disbanded progressively after the Armistice, and a new, separate Second Australian Imperial Force was raised for the Second World War.
