Tennessee Valley Authority v. Hill, 437 U.S. 153 (1978), was a landmark United States Supreme Court case interpreting the Endangered Species Act of 1973 (ESA). The Court held that the plain language of the ESA required halting construction of the nearly completed Tellico Dam on the Little Tennessee River because it would destroy the habitat of the snail darter, a small fish listed as an endangered species.

Background

The Tennessee Valley Authority (TVA) had begun construction of the Tellico Dam in 1967. In 1973, ichthyologist David Etnier discovered the snail darter in the Little Tennessee River, and the fish was listed as endangered in 1975. Citizens, led by Hiram G. Hill, sued to stop the dam, arguing that impounding the river would violate the Endangered Species Act by destroying the species' critical habitat.

Decision

Chief Justice Warren Burger, writing for the majority, concluded that Section 7 of the ESA plainly required federal agencies to ensure their actions did not jeopardize listed species or destroy their critical habitat, and that no exception existed for projects already under construction or nearly complete. The Court affirmed the injunction despite the dam being almost finished and more than $100 million having been spent.

Aftermath

Congress responded by amending the ESA to create an exemption process (the "God Committee"), but ultimately exempted the Tellico Dam through a separate appropriations rider in 1979, and the dam was completed. The snail darter survived, was later found in other streams, and was eventually downlisted; the case remains a central precedent on statutory interpretation and the force of environmental law.
