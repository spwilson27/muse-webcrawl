Volva (mycology)

In mycology, a volva is a cup-like structure at the base of a mushroom that is the remnant of the universal veil, a membrane that encloses the entire immature fruiting body of a gasteroid fungus. As the mushroom grows, the veil ruptures; in some species the volva remains as a distinct cup or sac around the base of the stipe, while in others it breaks into warts or patches on the cap.

Identification

The presence, absence, and form of the volva are important identification features, particularly in the genus Amanita, which includes both choice edibles and dangerously poisonous species such as the death cap (Amanita phalloides) and destroying angels. Because the volva may be buried underground, careful excavation of the base of the stipe is necessary when identifying amanitas. Volvae also occur in genera such as Volvariella (whose name derives from "volva"), Volvopluteus, and stinkhorns, where the volva is the gelatinous "egg" from which the spongy stalk emerges.

Etymology

The word comes from Latin volva (or vulva), meaning a wrapper or womb.
