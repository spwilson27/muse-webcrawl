Philippines

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Philippines
Capital: Manila
Population: about 115 million
Geography: Archipelago of about 7,641 islands

The Philippines is an archipelagic country in Southeast Asia, in the western Pacific Ocean, comprising about 7,641 islands, of which Luzon and Mindanao are the largest. Its capital, Manila, anchors one of the world's largest metropolitan areas. The country is one of Asia's two predominantly Christian nations, a legacy of more than three centuries of Spanish rule.

History

Austronesian peoples settled the islands over millennia, trading with China, India and the Islamic world. Magellan's expedition reached the islands in 1521 (Magellan died there, at Mactan); Spanish colonisation from 1565 made the Philippines a hub of the Manila–Acapulco galleon trade. The Philippine Revolution and the Spanish–American War transferred the colony to the United States in 1898; after the Commonwealth period, Japanese occupation (1942–45) and liberation, independence was recognised on 4 July 1946.

Modern Philippines

The republic's history since has swung between democracy and authoritarianism: Ferdinand Marcos's martial-law dictatorship (1972–86) ended in the People Power Revolution that restored democracy under Corazon Aquino. Today the Philippines is a lower-middle-income economy powered by services, electronics and the remittances of some ten million overseas Filipino workers. It faces typhoons of increasing intensity, rapid population growth, and a maritime dispute with China in the South China Sea (the "West Philippine Sea"). Basketball is a national passion, English is an official language, and Filipino culture blends Malay, Spanish, American and Chinese influences.
