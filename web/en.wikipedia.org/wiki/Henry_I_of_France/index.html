Henry I (French: Henri Ier; 4 May 1008 – 4 August 1060) was king of the Franks from 1031 to 1060. He was the third son of Robert II and Constance of Arles and a member of the Capetian dynasty. His reign, like that of his predecessors, was marked by feudal struggles against powerful vassals and the small size of the royal domain, centred on the Île-de-France and Orléanais.

Reign

Crowned co-king in 1027, Henry succeeded his father in 1031 against the opposition of his mother Constance, who favoured his younger brother Robert; with the help of Robert I, duke of Normandy, Henry prevailed and rewarded his brother with the duchy of Burgundy. Henry allied with the Normans against rebellious magnates, and in 1047 helped the young William of Normandy secure his duchy at the battle of Val-ès-Dunes. Relations later soured, and Henry invaded Normandy twice (1054 and 1058), suffering defeats at Mortemer and Varaville.

Marriages and succession

After the death of his first wife Matilda of Franconia, Henry married Anne of Kiev, daughter of Yaroslav the Wise, in 1051 — a marriage notable for bringing the name Philip into the French royal line. Henry had his son Philip crowned co-king in 1059 to secure the succession, and died the following year at Vitry-aux-Loges. Philip I succeeded as a minor under the regency of Anne and Baldwin V of Flanders.
