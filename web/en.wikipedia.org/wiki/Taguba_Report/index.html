Taguba Report

The Taguba Report is the official United States Army report of the investigation, conducted in early 2004 by Major General Antonio M. Taguba, into prisoner abuse at Abu Ghraib prison in Iraq. It documented "systemic and illegal abuse of detainees" by military police personnel of the 372nd Military Police Company and members of the American intelligence community.

Background

The investigation was ordered in January 2004 after a soldier, Specialist Joseph Darby, passed photographs of abuse to investigators. Taguba's team reviewed records, photographs, and testimony relating to the period from October to December 2003, when detainees at the prison's hard site were subjected to physical and sexual humiliation, beatings, and other mistreatment.

Findings

The report found that numerous incidents of "sadistic, blatant, and wanton criminal abuses" were inflicted on detainees, and it criticized failures of leadership and training. It recommended disciplinary action and administrative measures against several officers and soldiers. Parts of the report leaked to the press in May 2004 alongside the publication of the Abu Ghraib photographs, prompting worldwide controversy and later courts-martial.
