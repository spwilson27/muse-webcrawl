Jet Propulsion Laboratory

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

RoleRobotic deep-space missions
JPL in the Arroyo Seco.

The Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) is a federally funded research centre managed by Caltech for NASA, and the world's leading builder of robotic deep-space missions. From its campus in the Arroyo Seco near Pasadena, JPL has flown America's interplanetary fleet: the Voyagers, Galileo, Cassini–Huygens, every successful Mars rover from Sojourner to Perseverance, and the Europa Clipper.

Founded by Caltech rocket experimenters — including the charismatic and tragic Jack Parsons and the Chinese émigré Qian Xuesen — JPL built America's first satellite (Explorer 1, with the Army) in 1958 and the Ranger and Surveyor lunar scouts that preceded Apollo.

Culture

JPL's 'Dare Mighty Things' ethos (its unofficial motto, from Theodore Roosevelt) and traditions — mission-control peanuts for luck since Ranger days — mark a culture distinct from the human-spaceflight centres: small teams, one-off spacecraft, decade-long missions run from the Space Flight Operations Facility. Its Deep Space Network of antennas in California, Spain and Australia keeps contact with the fleet.
