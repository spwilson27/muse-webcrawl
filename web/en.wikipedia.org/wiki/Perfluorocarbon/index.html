Perfluorocarbon

Perfluorocarbons (PFCs) are compounds composed only of carbon and fluorine, derived from hydrocarbons in which all hydrogen atoms have been replaced by fluorine. The strong carbon–fluorine bond makes them exceptionally stable, chemically inert and hydrophobic, and able to dissolve large volumes of gases such as oxygen and carbon dioxide.

Properties and uses

Fully fluorinated liquids such as perfluorodecalin and perflubron can carry so much dissolved oxygen that they have been investigated for liquid breathing and as blood substitutes (emulsions such as Fluosol). PFCs are also used as tracers, in ophthalmic surgery, in medical imaging as ultrasound contrast agents, and historically as insulating and cooling fluids in electronics.

Environmental concerns

Gaseous perfluorocarbons such as tetrafluoromethane (CF₄) and hexafluoroethane (C₂F₆), emitted in aluminium smelting and semiconductor manufacture, are extremely potent and long-lived greenhouse gases, with atmospheric lifetimes of tens of thousands of years; they are covered by international climate agreements. Perfluorocarbons should not be confused with PFAS (per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances) more broadly, though the families overlap in their persistence.
