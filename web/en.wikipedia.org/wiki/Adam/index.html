Adam is the name given in the Book of Genesis (chapters 1–5) to the first human. Adam is the first human being aware of God, and features as such in various belief systems, including Judaism, Gnosticism, Christianity, Islam, and the Baháʼí Faith. In the biblical narrative, Adam and his wife Eve are expelled from the Garden of Eden after eating the forbidden fruit.

Etymology

The Hebrew word adam is connected to adamah, "ground" or "earth", reflecting the Genesis account in which God forms Adam from the dust of the ground. In Genesis the word functions both as a proper name and as a generic term for humankind.

In Genesis

Genesis presents two creation accounts. In the first (Genesis 1), God creates male and female together in the divine image and charges them to multiply and have dominion over the earth. In the second (Genesis 2–3), God forms Adam from dust, places him in the Garden of Eden, and creates Eve from his side as a companion. After the serpent tempts Eve to eat from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, Adam also eats; both are banished from Eden, and Adam is condemned to toil for food and to return to dust. Genesis 5 gives Adam's lifespan as 930 years and lists his descendants through Seth.

In Judaism

In Jewish tradition Adam is the archetypal human being, created in the image of God. Rabbinic literature elaborates extensively on his creation, his stature before the Fall, and his repentance. The medieval philosopher Maimonides interpreted the Eden narrative allegorically.

In Christianity

Christian theology contrasts Adam, through whom sin and death entered the world (see original sin), with Christ, sometimes called the "Last Adam" or "Second Adam" (1 Corinthians 15), through whom redemption comes. Adam and Eve are commemorated as saints in some traditions, with a feast day on 24 December.

In Islam

Adam (Arabic: Ādam) is regarded in Islam as the first prophet (nabi) and the first Muslim. The Quran relates that God created Adam from clay, taught him the names of all things, and commanded the angels to bow before him; Iblis refused and was cast out. Adam and his wife resided in Paradise, ate from the forbidden tree, repented, and were forgiven and sent down to earth.

Historicity

The story of Adam and Eve is generally regarded by modern scholarship as mythological rather than historical. Population genetics indicates that the human population never fell to a single couple; geneticists nevertheless use the labels "Y-chromosomal Adam" and "Mitochondrial Eve" for the most recent common patrilineal and matrilineal ancestors of living humans, who lived at different times.
