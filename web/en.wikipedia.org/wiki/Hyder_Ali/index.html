Hyder Ali

Hyder Ali (c. 1720 – 7 December 1782) was the de facto ruler of the Kingdom of Mysore in southern India. Born into a family of military adventurers of Arab descent, he rose through the ranks of the Mysore army to become its commander-in-chief and, by 1761, the effective sovereign of the kingdom, although the Wodeyar maharajas remained nominal heads of state.

Rise to power

Hyder distinguished himself in the Carnatic Wars, where he observed European military methods and employed French-trained troops. His skill in siegecraft and cavalry warfare, and his role in relieving Trichinopoly, won him the title Fateh Hyder Bahadur and control of Bangalore. By the early 1760s he had displaced the powerful minister Nanjaraja and assumed full control of Mysore's government and revenues.

Anglo-Mysore Wars

Hyder Ali fought the British East India Company in the First Anglo-Mysore War (1767–1769), which ended with the Treaty of Madras after he threatened Madras itself, and in the Second Anglo-Mysore War (1780–1784). In alliance with the Marathas and the Nizam at various times, he inflicted notable defeats on Company forces, including at Pollilur in 1780 — among the worst British defeats in India. He also pioneered the military use of iron-cased Mysorean rockets.

Death and legacy

Hyder died of cancer in December 1782 while the second war was still in progress, and was succeeded by his son Tipu Sultan. An energetic administrator, he expanded Mysore's territory, encouraged trade and industry, and is remembered as one of the most formidable Indian opponents of the East India Company.
