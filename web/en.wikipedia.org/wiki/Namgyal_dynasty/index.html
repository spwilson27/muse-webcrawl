Namgyal dynasty
Country: Kingdom of Sikkim
Founded: 1642
Founder: Phuntsog Namgyal
Final ruler: Palden Thondup Namgyal
Deposed: 1975

The Namgyal dynasty was the ruling dynasty of the Kingdom of Sikkim from 1642 to 1975. Its monarchs, called Chogyals ("dharma kings"), traced their descent to Guru Tashi, a 13th-century prince from the Kham region of Tibet. The dynasty was founded when Phuntsog Namgyal was consecrated as the first Chogyal at Yuksom in 1642 by three lamas who had come from the north, south and west, fulfilling a prophecy attributed to Guru Rinpoche.

History

The Namgyal rulers consolidated a Buddhist kingdom in the eastern Himalaya, at times ruling territory extending into present-day Nepal, Bhutan and the Darjeeling region. Conflicts with the Gorkhas of Nepal and Bhutan reduced Sikkim's territory in the 18th and early 19th centuries. After the Anglo-Nepalese War and the Treaty of Titalia (1817), Sikkim became increasingly tied to British India, becoming a protectorate under the Treaty of Tumlong (1861).

The last Chogyal, Palden Thondup Namgyal, reigned from 1963. Following political unrest and a referendum, Sikkim merged with India as its 22nd state in 1975, and the monarchy was abolished. The Namgyal family retained cultural and religious influence in Sikkim thereafter.
