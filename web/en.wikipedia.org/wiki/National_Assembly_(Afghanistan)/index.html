The National Assembly (Pashto: ملی شورا Melay Shura; Dari: شورای ملی Shura-ye Melli) was the bicameral legislature of the Islamic Republic of Afghanistan, established under the Constitution of 2004. It consisted of two houses: the Wolesi Jirga (House of the People), the lower house, and the Meshrano Jirga (House of Elders), the upper house. The assembly ceased functioning after the Taliban takeover of Kabul in August 2021.

Composition

The Wolesi Jirga had 250 seats: 249 members elected from provincial constituencies, plus reserved representation, using the single non-transferable vote system. At least 68 seats were reserved for women (two per province), and ten seats were reserved for the Kuchi nomadic community. Members served five-year terms.

The Meshrano Jirga had 102 members: 34 elected by provincial councils, 34 by district councils, and 34 appointed by the President, half of the appointees being women. Meshrano Jirga members served varying terms depending on their route of selection.

Powers

The National Assembly approved legislation, the national budget, and international treaties; it could question and impeach ministers and, in the Wolesi Jirga's case, reject ministerial nominees of the President. A special assembly, the Loya Jirga, could be convened for decisions on sovereignty, constitutional amendment, and prosecution of the President.

Elections

The first post-Taliban parliamentary elections were held on 18 September 2005, followed by elections in 2010 and, after long delay, in October 2018. Elections were organised by the Independent Election Commission and were frequently marred by insecurity, fraud allegations, and low turnout.

Parliament building

A new parliament complex in Kabul's Darul Aman area, constructed with Indian assistance at a cost of about $220 million, was inaugurated in December 2015 by Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi and President Ashraf Ghani.

History

Afghanistan's first legislatures date to the reign of Amanullah Khan in the 1920s; a bicameral parliament existed under the 1964 Constitution of the Kingdom. The 2004 constitutional order revived the National Assembly after the 2001 Bonn Agreement. Following the collapse of the Republic on 15 August 2021, the Taliban did not retain the elected assembly, and the parliament building was repurposed; the legislature has not been reconstituted under the Islamic Emirate.
