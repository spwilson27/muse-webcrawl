Pneumonoultramicroscopicsilicovolcanoconiosis

Pneumonoultramicroscopicsilicovolcanoconiosis is a 45-letter word that is often cited as the longest word in the English language appearing in major dictionaries. It was coined by Everett M. Smith, president of the National Puzzlers' League, in 1935, and popularised when the League used it as a subject of discussion; it was subsequently included in the Oxford English Dictionary and other reference works.

The word was coined to denote a lung disease caused by inhaling very fine silica or volcanic dust.

Meaning and construction

The word purports to name a lung disease contracted from the inhalation of very fine silica particles, specifically from a volcano — a condition closely related to silicosis, a real occupational disease of miners and stonecutters. It is built from Greek and Latin elements that can be unpacked as follows:

  • pneumono- — relating to the lungs (Greek pneumōn)
  • ultra- — beyond, extremely
  • microscopic — visible only under a microscope, i.e. extremely fine
  • silico- — silica, silicon dioxide dust
  • volcano — of volcanic origin
  • coni- — dust (Greek konis)
  • -osis — a diseased condition

Put together, it can be rendered as "a lung disease caused by inhaling extremely fine silica dust, specifically from a volcano".

Status as a word

Linguists and lexicographers commonly note that the word was deliberately invented as a curiosity rather than arising from genuine medical usage; doctors refer to the actual condition as silicosis or pneumoconiosis. Nonetheless, because it has been entered into major dictionaries, it is routinely reported in popular lists as "the longest word in English", competing with other very long coinages and with naturally occurring long words such as antidisestablishmentarianism (28 letters) and the chemical name of the protein titin, which runs to tens of thousands of letters when written in full but is generally excluded as a formula rather than a word.
