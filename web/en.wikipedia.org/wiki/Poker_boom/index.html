Poker boom

The poker boom was a period of greatly increased popularity of poker worldwide, generally dated from 2003 to 2006, during which poker — especially no-limit Texas hold 'em — surged in television exposure, online participation, and tournament prize pools.

Causes

Two developments are most often credited. First, the introduction of the hole-card camera ("lipstick camera") in televised poker, pioneered by the British programme Late Night Poker (1999) and adopted by the World Poker Tour (2002), let viewers see players' hidden cards, transforming poker into compelling television. Second, in May 2003 amateur Chris Moneymaker won the World Series of Poker Main Event after qualifying through an online satellite tournament costing only tens of dollars — the "Moneymaker effect" convinced millions of recreational players that anyone could win. His victory coincided with rapid growth in online poker sites such as PokerStars and PartyPoker.

Effects

The WSOP Main Event field grew from 839 entrants in 2003 to 8,773 in 2006, with the prize pool reaching nearly $83 million. Televised poker proliferated on cable networks, poker strategy books and training sites flourished, and a generation of young online professionals emerged. Celebrity players such as Doyle Brunson, Phil Hellmuth, and Daniel Negreanu became mainstream figures.

Decline

The passage of the Unlawful Internet Gambling Enforcement Act in the United States in October 2006 sharply restricted online poker, forcing publicly traded operators out of the US market. The boom period is usually considered to have ended, though poker retained a much larger permanent audience than before 2003. Further damage came on "Black Friday," 15 April 2011, when US authorities seized the domains of the three largest sites serving American players.
