Mirza Muhammad Siraj-ud-Daulah (1733 – 2 July 1757), commonly known as Siraj-ud-Daulah or Siraj ud-Daula, was the last independent Nawab of Bengal. His reign began in 1756, succeeding his maternal grandfather Alivardi Khan, and ended the following year when he was defeated at the Battle of Plassey by the British East India Company under Robert Clive — an event widely regarded as the beginning of British rule in India.

Conflict with the East India Company

Siraj resented the Company's fortification of Fort William in Calcutta without permission and its abuse of trading privileges. In June 1756 he captured Calcutta; after the surrender, British prisoners were held overnight in a small guardroom in the episode known as the Black Hole of Calcutta, in which many died. Clive retook Calcutta in January 1757, and the Nawab signed the Treaty of Alinagar.

Battle of Plassey and death

Clive conspired with the Nawab's commander Mir Jafar and the Jagat Seth bankers. At Plassey on 23 June 1757, much of Siraj's army stood aside, and his forces were routed. Siraj fled Murshidabad but was captured and executed on 2 July 1757 on Mir Jafar's orders. Mir Jafar was installed as Nawab under Company control.

Legacy

Siraj ud-Daulah is remembered in Bengal as a symbol of resistance to foreign domination, and is a central character in Bengali literature and theatre. Historians continue to debate his character and the scale of the Black Hole incident.
