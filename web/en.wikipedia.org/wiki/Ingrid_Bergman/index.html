Ingrid Bergman

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Ingrid Bergman as Ilsa Lund in Casablanca, in soft focus beneath a wide-brimmed hat]
Ingrid Bergman as Ilsa Lund in Casablanca, in soft focus beneath a wide-brimmed hat

Ingrid Bergman (29 August 1915 – 29 August 1982) was a Swedish actress who starred in a variety of European and American films, television movies and plays. Considered one of the most influential actresses of the twentieth century, she won three Academy Awards — for Gaslight (1944), Anastasia (1956) and Murder on the Orient Express (1974) — and is immortal as Ilsa in Casablanca (1942). Her natural, unglamorous screen presence changed Hollywood acting.

Career

Already a star in Sweden, Bergman was brought to Hollywood by David O. Selznick for Intermezzo (1939). Refusing the studio makeover, she triumphed in Casablanca, For Whom the Bell Tolls (1943), Gaslight, Alfred Hitchcock's Spellbound (1945) and Notorious (1946), and Joan of Arc (1948).

Scandal and comeback

Her affair with director Roberto Rossellini during Stromboli (1950) — both married to others — provoked a US scandal in which she was denounced on the Senate floor as 'a powerful influence for evil' and exiled from Hollywood. In Italy she made a series of neorealist films with Rossellini now valued as masterpieces. Hollywood forgave her with Anastasia (1956) and a second Oscar; the Senate formally apologised in later years.

Later years

Bergman worked in European and American film and theatre into her seventies, winning her third Oscar for Murder on the Orient Express and an Emmy for A Woman Called Golda (1982). She died in London on her 67th birthday, of breast cancer, after completing Ingmar Bergman's Autumn Sonata (1978) — a searing mother–daughter drama reckoned among her greatest performances.
