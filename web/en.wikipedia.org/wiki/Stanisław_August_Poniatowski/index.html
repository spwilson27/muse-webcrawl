Stanisław August Poniatowski

Reign: 7 September 1764 – 25 November 1795
Born: 17 January 1732, Wołczyn
Died: 12 February 1798, Saint Petersburg
House: Poniatowski

Stanisław II August, known in English by his regnal name Stanisław August Poniatowski (17 January 1732 – 12 February 1798), was the last king of Poland and grand duke of Lithuania, reigning from 1764 until his abdication in 1795 amid the Third Partition of Poland.

Early life and accession

Born at Wołczyn into the powerful Poniatowski family, he travelled widely in Western Europe and served as a diplomat in Saint Petersburg, where he became a lover of the future Empress Catherine II of Russia. With Russian support and troops, he was elected king by the Polish Sejm in 1764 after the death of Augustus III.

Reign

Stanisław August was an enlightened patron of the arts and sciences who founded schools, the Corps of Cadets, the National Theatre in Warsaw, and promoted the first Polish-language newspaper and educational reforms. His reign was dominated by the struggle between reform and the veto-wielding magnate class backed by Russia, Prussia and Austria. The First Partition of 1772 stripped the Commonwealth of a third of its territory. The reforming Great Sejm (1788–1792) produced the Constitution of 3 May 1791, the second modern written constitution in the world, but Russia invaded in 1792, the Targowica Confederation reversed the reforms, and the Second Partition followed in 1793. The Kościuszko Uprising of 1794 failed, Warsaw was taken, and in 1795 the Third Partition erased Poland from the map of Europe. The king abdicated on 25 November 1795, lived thereafter at Grodno and then Saint Petersburg, and died there in 1798.

Legacy

Historians remain divided: he is blamed for weakness toward Russia and personal dependence on Catherine, but also credited with a cultural golden age and serious attempts at reform in an impossible geopolitical position.
