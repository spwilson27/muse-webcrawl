Private investigator

A private investigator (often abbreviated to PI and informally called a private eye), a private detective, or inquiry agent is a person who can be hired by individuals, groups or NGOs to undertake investigatory law services. Private investigators typically work for attorneys in civil and criminal cases.


The private investigator is a staple of crime fiction and film noir.

History

In 1833, Eugène François Vidocq, a French soldier, criminal and privateer, founded the first known private detective agency, Le Bureau des Renseignements ("Office of Intelligence"), in Paris. In the United States, Allan Pinkerton founded the Pinkerton National Detective Agency in 1850, which became famous for foiling a plot to assassinate president-elect Abraham Lincoln and for its role in labour disputes.

Work

Private investigators undertake a wide variety of work, including finding missing persons, conducting surveillance, performing background checks, investigating insurance fraud, locating witnesses, and gathering evidence for litigation. Many jurisdictions require private investigators to be licensed, and their powers are generally no greater than those of any other citizen: they cannot make arrests or impersonate law enforcement officers.

In popular culture

The private eye became an iconic figure of detective fiction, exemplified by characters such as Sam Spade, Philip Marlowe, and Sherlock Holmes (a "consulting detective"). The hardboiled detective archetype, developed by writers such as Dashiell Hammett and Raymond Chandler, remains influential in literature and film.
