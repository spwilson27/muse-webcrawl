Chord (aeronautics)

In aeronautics, the chord is the imaginary straight line joining the leading edge and trailing edge of an aerofoil. The chord length is the distance between the trailing edge and the point where the chord intersects the leading edge. The chord is a fundamental reference dimension in aerodynamics: wing area, aspect ratio and aerodynamic coefficients are all defined with reference to it.

Definitions

For a wing of non-rectangular planform the chord varies along the span. The standard mean chord (SMC) is the wing area divided by the span. The mean aerodynamic chord (MAC) is a weighted average that represents the chord of an equivalent rectangular wing with the same pitching-moment behaviour; the position of an aircraft's centre of gravity is conventionally expressed as a percentage of the MAC, for example "25% MAC".

Use in aerodynamics

The chord line forms the datum for measuring angle of attack and the position of the aerodynamic centre (approximately the quarter-chord point for subsonic aerofoils). The Reynolds number used to characterise flow over a wing is based on chord length. Control surfaces are often described by their chord relative to the parent surface, and devices such as flaps and slats effectively change camber and chord.
