Magnox

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Magnox is a type of nuclear power and reprocessing plant designed in the United Kingdom, and also the name of the magnesium alloy used for the fuel cladding. Magnox reactors are pressurised, carbon dioxide–cooled, graphite-moderated reactors using natural uranium metal fuel with magnox alloy cladding. The name comes from "magnesium non-oxidising", the alloy being mainly magnesium with small amounts of aluminium and other metals.

History

The world's first commercial nuclear power station, Calder Hall in Cumbria, opened in 1956 with Magnox reactors, dual-purpose machines producing plutonium for weapons as well as electricity. Eleven Magnox stations were built in the UK, and single stations were exported to Italy (Latina) and Japan (Tōkai). The design was succeeded by the Advanced Gas-cooled Reactor (AGR).

Decommissioning

The last Magnox reactor, at Wylfa in Anglesey, shut down in December 2015. The stations are being decommissioned by Magnox Ltd, a subsidiary of the Nuclear Decommissioning Authority; spent magnox fuel was reprocessed at Sellafield, whose Magnox reprocessing plant closed in 2022.
