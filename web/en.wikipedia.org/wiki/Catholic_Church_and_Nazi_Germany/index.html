Catholic Church and Nazi Germany

The relationship between the Catholic Church and Nazi Germany involved both accommodation and conflict. In 1933 the Holy See signed the Reichskonkordat with the German government, seeking legal protection for Catholic institutions; the regime soon violated its terms, persecuting Catholic organisations, schools, and clergy.

Key episodes

The Centre Party dissolved in 1933 amid pressure. Pope Pius XI's 1937 encyclical Mit brennender Sorge, smuggled into Germany and read from pulpits, condemned Nazi racial ideology and breaches of the concordat. Under Pius XII during the Second World War, the Vatican maintained diplomatic neutrality while elements of the Church both aided victims — including hiding Jews in some places — and, in some cases, collaborated; the wartime record remains intensely debated by historians.

Persecution

Thousands of priests were sent to concentration camps, notably at Dachau's priests' barracks. Bishops such as Clemens August Graf von Galen publicly denounced the Aktion T4 euthanasia programme in 1941.
