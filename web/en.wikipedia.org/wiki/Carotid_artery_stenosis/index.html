Carotid artery stenosis

Carotid artery stenosis
SpecialtyCardiology, neurology, vascular surgery
CauseAtherosclerosis

Carotid artery stenosis is a narrowing of the lumen of the carotid arteries, usually caused by atherosclerosis. The plaque typically forms at the carotid bifurcation, where the common carotid divides into the internal and external carotid arteries. Carotid stenosis is an important risk factor for ischemic stroke, which may occur through embolisation of plaque fragments or thrombus to the brain, or less commonly through critically reduced blood flow.

Signs and symptoms

Many people with carotid stenosis have no symptoms. Others present with a transient ischemic attack (TIA) or stroke in the territory of the affected internal carotid artery, or with transient monocular blindness (amaurosis fugax) from retinal emboli. A carotid bruit may be heard on auscultation of the neck.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is usually by duplex ultrasound, with CT or MR angiography used for confirmation and surgical planning. Management includes antiplatelet therapy, statins, blood-pressure and diabetes control, and smoking cessation. For suitable patients with severe symptomatic stenosis, revascularisation by carotid endarterectomy or carotid stenting reduces the risk of subsequent stroke.
