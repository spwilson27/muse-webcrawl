Virginity

Virginity is the state of a person who has never engaged in sexual intercourse. The term originally referred only to sexually inexperienced women, but has evolved to encompass men and a range of definitions based on sexual orientation and the acts considered to constitute intercourse. Virginity has held social, moral, and religious significance in many cultures, particularly the historical emphasis on female virginity before marriage.

Definitions and terminology

Definitions vary: most focus on penile–vaginal intercourse, while others include oral or anal sex or any partnered sexual activity. Because the concept is socially constructed, medical authorities note there is no physical test for virginity; the World Health Organization has condemned so-called "virginity testing" as unscientific and a violation of human rights.

Cultural and religious views

Many world religions have prized virginity as a spiritual ideal — exemplified by consecrated virgins and figures such as the Virgin Mary — while marriage systems in numerous societies historically attached economic and familial value to a bride's virginity, attitudes that remain influential but contested. Modern phenomena include abstinence pledges and purity balls in some communities, and, conversely, feminist and public-health critiques of virginity norms.

Loss of virginity

The first act of intercourse is commonly termed the "loss of virginity" or deflowering. Research in many Western countries places typical ages in the mid-to-late teens, and sociologists study the transition's perceived significance, which ranges from milestone to stigma.
