King County is a county located in the U.S. state of Washington. The population was 2,269,675 in the 2020 census, making it the most populous county in Washington and the 13th-most populous in the United States. Its county seat is Seattle, which is also its largest city and the state's largest.

History

The county was formed on December 22, 1852, out of territory within Thurston County, and named for William R. King, a U.S. senator from Alabama and the newly elected vice president under Franklin Pierce. European-American settlement centred on the logging and coal industries and on the port of Seattle, founded in 1851.

In 1986 the King County Council voted to rename the county in honour of civil rights leader Martin Luther King Jr., noting that the original namesake had been a slaveholder; the change was formally ratified by the state in 2005, and the county logo was replaced with an image of King.

Geography

King County covers 2,307 square miles, of which about 2,116 square miles are land. It stretches from Puget Sound in the west to the crest of the Cascade Range in the east, and includes Vashon Island. Major cities besides Seattle include Bellevue, Kent, Renton, Federal Way, Kirkland, Redmond, and Auburn. The highest point is Mount Daniel at 7,960 feet.

Economy and government

The county is the economic centre of the Pacific Northwest, home to Amazon, Microsoft (in neighbouring Redmond), Boeing facilities, Starbucks, and a large technology and biotechnology sector. It is governed by an elected county executive and a nine-member Metropolitan King County Council, and operates Metro transit and a large public-health and regional services system.
