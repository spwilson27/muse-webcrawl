Information ethics

Information ethics is the branch of ethics that studies the moral problems arising from the creation, collection, organisation, dissemination, and use of information. It overlaps with computer ethics, media ethics, and library and information science, and addresses issues such as privacy, intellectual property, censorship, access to information, and the ethical responsibilities of information professionals.

History

The field emerged in the mid-20th century as computers transformed how information was stored and shared. Norbert Wiener raised ethical questions about computing in The Human Use of Human Beings (1950), and scholars such as Rafael Capurro, who coined "information ethics" in its current usage, and Luciano Floridi, whose "information ethics" framework treats information itself as a moral environment (the "infosphere"), shaped the discipline.

Key issues

Core topics include information privacy and surveillance; the digital divide and equitable access; ownership and copyright in digital goods; misinformation and the responsibilities of platforms; data protection and consent; and the ethical treatment of the "information poor" and marginalised groups. Professional codes from bodies such as the American Library Association articulate principles of intellectual freedom and equitable service.
