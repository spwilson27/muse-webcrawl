Citadel of Lille

The Citadel of Lille (French: Citadelle de Lille) is a military fortress built between 1667 and 1670 at Lille in northern France, designed by the military engineer Sébastien Le Prestre de Vauban. It was constructed shortly after Louis XIV captured the city from Spain in 1667, and Vauban regarded it as the "Queen of Citadels" (la reine des citadelles).

Description

The citadel is a pentagonal star fort surrounded by moats fed by the Deûle canal, with five bastions named for aspects of the French monarchy. It was built of brick and stone by some 2,000 workers and could house a garrison of several thousand men, complete with barracks, a chapel, stores, and workshops arranged around a central parade ground. It remains an active military installation, hosting the headquarters of France's Rapid Reaction Corps, while the surrounding grounds form a public park popular with residents.

History

The fortress was besieged and captured during the War of the Spanish Succession in 1708 after a celebrated siege. It forms part of the pré carré, Vauban's double line of fortified towns defending France's northern frontier, several of which were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2008 as the Fortifications of Vauban.
