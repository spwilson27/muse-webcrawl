Functional group

In organic chemistry, a functional group is a specific group of atoms or bonds within a compound that is responsible for the characteristic chemical reactions of that compound. The same functional group undergoes the same or similar chemical reactions regardless of the size or rest of the molecule in which it occurs. This allows the systematic prediction of chemical reactions and the classification of organic compounds into families such as alcohols, carboxylic acids and amines.

Concept

The concept of functional groups is central to organic chemistry, both as a classification scheme and as a tool in retrosynthetic analysis, where target molecules are conceptually broken apart at functional groups into simpler precursors (synthons). Atoms or groups that are not part of a recognised functional group are often treated collectively as a hydrocarbon residue, symbolised by the letter R.

Nomenclature

IUPAC nomenclature of organic chemistry is built around functional groups: the principal characteristic group of a molecule determines the suffix of its systematic name (for example -ol for alcohols, -oic acid for carboxylic acids) and the numbering of the carbon chain, while other groups are cited as prefixes.
