The San Bruno pipeline explosion occurred on 9 September 2010 in the Crestmoor neighbourhood of San Bruno, California, when a 30-inch natural gas transmission pipeline operated by Pacific Gas and Electric Company (Line 132) ruptured and ignited.

Disaster

The explosion, at about 6:11 p.m., sent a fireball hundreds of feet into the air and started a blaze that destroyed 38 homes and damaged many more. Eight people were killed and 58 injured. Residents initially feared an aircraft crash from nearby San Francisco International Airport.

Investigation and aftermath

The National Transportation Safety Board found the rupture originated in a defective weld on a pipe segment whose records PG&E had mischaracterised, and faulted the utility's pipeline integrity management and state oversight. PG&E paid over a billion dollars in penalties and settlements, and in 2016 was convicted in federal court of obstructing the investigation and violating pipeline safety law.
