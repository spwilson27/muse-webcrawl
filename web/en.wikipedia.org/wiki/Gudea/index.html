Gudea

Gudea (Sumerian: 𒅗𒌤𒀀, Gu-de-a) was a ruler (ensi) of the state of Lagash in southern Mesopotamia, reigning approximately 2144–2124 BCE during the Neo-Sumerian period. He is famous for the remarkable series of diorite statues depicting him — over two dozen survive — usually shown seated or standing with hands clasped in prayer, wearing a turban-like cap.

Gudea devoted much of his reign to rebuilding temples, above all the Eninnu temple of the god Ningirsu in Girsu. His building projects and the dream in which the god ordered the temple's construction are recorded in the two great Gudea cylinders, among the longest known compositions in Sumerian cuneiform.

Inscriptions boast of trade reaching from the Mediterranean and Magan (Oman) to Meluhha (the Indus valley), bringing cedar, gold and diorite to Lagash. Gudea's pious, peaceful image contrasts with the warlike propaganda of Akkadian kings and makes his reign a hallmark of Sumerian cultural revival.
