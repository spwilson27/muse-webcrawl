Tewfik Pasha

Mohamed Tewfik Pasha

Born: 15 November 1852, Cairo

Died: 7 January 1892, Helwan

Reign: 26 June 1879 – 7 January 1892

Predecessor: Isma'il Pasha

Dynasty: Muhammad Ali dynasty

Mohamed Tewfik Pasha (Arabic: محمد توفيق باشا; 15 November 1852 – 7 January 1892), also known as Tawfiq of Egypt, was the sixth ruler of Egypt from the Muhammad Ali dynasty. He was Khedive of Egypt and the Sudan from 1879 to 1892.

Early life

Tewfik was the eldest son of Khedive Isma'il Pasha and was born in Cairo. Unlike many of his contemporaries he was educated in Egypt rather than sent to Europe, attending the Manial School founded by his grandfather. He served in administrative posts under his father and briefly as president of the council (prime minister) in 1879.

Accession

On 26 June 1879, Isma'il was deposed by the Ottoman Sultan Abdülhamid II under pressure from Britain and France, who blamed him for Egypt's financial collapse. Tewfik succeeded him as khedive. He inherited a country whose finances were controlled by the Anglo-French Dual Control and whose army was increasingly restless.

Reign

Tewfik's reign was dominated by the 'Urabi revolt (1879–1882), led by the Egyptian officer Ahmed 'Urabi, which sought to end foreign domination and limit the khedive's autocracy. When riots broke out in Alexandria in June 1882, Britain intervened militarily: the Royal Navy bombarded Alexandria and British forces defeated 'Urabi at the Battle of Tel el-Kebir in September 1882. Tewfik was restored to power, but Egypt passed under effective British occupation, administered through the British consul-general (notably Evelyn Baring, later Lord Cromer) for the remainder of his reign and beyond.

During the same period the Mahdist War in the Sudan led to the loss of Egyptian control over the Sudan, the death of General Gordon at Khartoum in 1885, and the evacuation of Egyptian garrisons.

Death

Tewfik died on 7 January 1892 at his palace in Helwan, south of Cairo, and was succeeded by his eldest son, Abbas II.
