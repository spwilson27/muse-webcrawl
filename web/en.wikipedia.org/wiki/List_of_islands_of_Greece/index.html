List of islands of Greece

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Greece has a vast number of islands — estimates range from 1,200 to 6,000 depending on the minimum size counted — of which about 227 are inhabited. They are grouped into clusters: the Argo-Saronic Islands, the Cyclades, the North Aegean Islands, the Dodecanese, the Sporades, the Ionian Islands, and Crete with its surrounding islets. The largest island is Crete, followed by Euboea, Lesbos and Rhodes.

Cyclades

The principal Cyclades are Naxos, Andros, Paros, Tinos, Milos, Kea, Amorgos, Ios, Mykonos, Delos (uninhabited, a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the most important archaeological sites in Greece), Syros (whose capital, Ermoupoli, is the administrative centre of the South Aegean region), Thira (Santorini), Serifos, Sifnos, Sikinos, Anafi, Kimolos, Antiparos, Folegandros and Kythnos. Santorini's caldera was formed by the Minoan eruption of about 1600 BC, one of the largest volcanic eruptions in recorded history.

Dodecanese

Rhodes, Kos, Karpathos, Kalymnos, Leros, Patmos (where tradition holds John wrote the Book of Revelation), Astypalaia, Symi, Tilos, Nisyros, Chalki, Kastellorizo (Megisti) and Lipsi. The group was under Italian rule from 1912 to 1947.

Ionian Islands

Corfu, Cephalonia, Zakynthos, Lefkada, Ithaca (reputed home of Odysseus), Paxos, Kythira and their satellites. The Ionian islands were a British protectorate (the United States of the Ionian Islands) from 1815 until their union with Greece in 1864.

North Aegean, Sporades and Saronic islands

  • North Aegean: Lesbos, Chios, Samos, Lemnos, Thasos, Ikaria, Samothrace (site of the Winged Victory), Agios Efstratios, Fournoi.
  • Sporades: Skiathos, Skopelos (filming location of Mamma Mia!), Alonnisos (centre of a marine national park), Skyros.
  • Argo-Saronic: Salamis (site of the 480 BC naval battle), Aegina, Poros, Hydra (car-free), Spetses, Angistri.
  • Near Athens: Euboea is separated from the mainland by the Euripus Strait, where the tidal current famously reverses direction.
