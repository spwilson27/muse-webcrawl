Environmental noise

Environmental noise is the accumulation of all noise present in a specified environment. The predominant sources of environmental noise worldwide are surface motor vehicles including automobiles, trucks and buses, aircraft, rail transport, industrial activity, and construction. Excessive noise exposure, often called noise pollution, is associated with adverse effects on human health and wildlife.

Sources

Road traffic is usually the largest contributor to environmental noise in urban areas. Other major sources include aircraft operations near airports, railway lines, factories, building sites, and amplified music from entertainment venues. Noise levels are typically measured in A-weighted decibels (dB(A)) and summarised with indicators such as Lden (day-evening-night level) in the European Union.

Health effects

Chronic exposure to environmental noise has been linked to sleep disturbance, cardiovascular disease, cognitive impairment in children, annoyance, and hearing damage. The World Health Organization has estimated that at least one million healthy life years are lost annually in western Europe from traffic-related noise alone.

Regulation

Many jurisdictions regulate environmental noise through land-use planning, noise mapping, building insulation standards, and limits on vehicle and aircraft emissions. The European Union's Environmental Noise Directive (2002/49/EC) requires member states to produce strategic noise maps and action plans for large agglomerations and major transport infrastructure.
