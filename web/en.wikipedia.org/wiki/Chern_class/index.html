Chern class

In mathematics, in particular in algebraic topology, differential geometry and algebraic geometry, the Chern classes are characteristic classes associated to complex vector bundles. They were introduced by Shiing-Shen Chern in the 1940s and are fundamental invariants used throughout modern geometry.

A complex vector bundle, to which Chern classes are associated

Definition

Given a complex vector bundle E of rank n over a topological space X, the total Chern class is an element of the cohomology ring of X:

c(E) = 1 + c1(E) + c2(E) + ... + cn(E),

where the i-th Chern class ci(E) lies in the cohomology group H2i(X; Z). The classes are characterised by a set of axioms: naturality under pullback, the Whitney sum formula c(E ⊕ F) = c(E)·c(F), and a normalisation condition for the tautological line bundle over complex projective space.

Geometric definition via curvature

In differential geometry, Chern classes can be computed from the curvature of a connection. If ∇ is a connection on a Hermitian vector bundle E with curvature form Ω, then the Chern forms are given by the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial

det(I + (i/2π) Ω t) = Σ ck(E) tk,

and the resulting differential forms represent the Chern classes in de Rham cohomology. This theory, developed by Chern and André Weil, is the prototype of the Chern–Weil theory of characteristic classes.

Properties

  • The first Chern class c1(L) classifies complex line bundles: the map L ↦ c1(L) is an isomorphism from the Picard group of line bundles to H2(X; Z).
  • The top Chern class of the tangent bundle of a compact complex manifold, evaluated on its fundamental class, equals the Euler characteristic.
  • Chern classes satisfy the splitting principle: calculations may be performed as if the bundle split as a sum of line bundles with Chern roots.

Applications

Chern classes appear in the Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem and its generalisation, the Atiyah–Singer index theorem. Combinations of Chern classes give rise to the Chern character, the Todd class and Chern numbers, which are important cobordism invariants. In physics, the first Chern class of a line bundle over a torus is related to quantised Hall conductance in the quantum Hall effect, and Chern classes are central to the theory of topological insulators.
