Battle of Blood River

The Battle of Blood River (Afrikaans: Slag van Bloedrivier) was fought on 16 December 1838 between about 470 Voortrekkers led by Andries Pretorius and an estimated 10,000–15,000 Zulu warriors of King Dingane, on the banks of the Ncome River in what is now KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa. The Voortrekkers won a decisive victory, losing no men killed, while some 3,000 Zulu died.

Background

Earlier in 1838 Dingane's forces massacred the trek party of Piet Retief and attacked Voortrekker encampments at Bloukrans and Weenen. Pretorius arrived with a commando of 64 wagons and took a vow, attributed to Sarel Cilliers, promising to build a church and commemorate the day if God granted victory — the origin of the Day of the Vow.

The battle

Pretorius formed a wagon laager in a strong position anchored by the river and a deep donga. Zulu impis attacked in waves through the morning and were shattered by musket and cannon fire from the laager, including two small artillery pieces. A mounted sortie completed the rout; Zulu casualties were around 3,000, and the river was said to have run red, giving the battle its name.

Legacy

The victory opened Zululand to the Voortrekkers and led to the Natalia Republic. In apartheid-era South Africa 16 December was celebrated as Dingaan's Day and the Day of the Vow, central to Afrikaner nationalist mythology; since 1994 it has been observed as the Day of Reconciliation, and the Ncome Museum on the Zulu side of the river presents the battle from a Zulu perspective.
