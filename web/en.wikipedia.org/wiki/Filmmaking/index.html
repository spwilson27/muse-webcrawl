Filmmaking

Filmmaking is the process by which a motion picture is produced. It involves a number of distinct stages during which an initial story, idea, or commission is developed, scripted, cast, shot, sound-recorded and edited, and the product is screened to an audience. Filmmaking takes place in many places around the world in a range of economic, social and political contexts.

Stages of production

Development — the project is conceived; a screenplay is written and financing arranged. Pre-production — cast and crew are hired, locations scouted, sets and schedules planned. Production (principal photography) — the film is shot, with the director working with actors, cinematographer and crew. Post-production — picture editing, sound design and mixing, music, visual effects, colour grading and titles are completed. Distribution — the finished film is marketed and exhibited in cinemas, on broadcast media, or via streaming platforms.

Crew roles

Key roles include the producer, who oversees the production as a whole; the director, responsible for the creative vision; the screenwriter; the cinematographer (director of photography); the production designer; the editor; and the composer. On large productions, crews number in the hundreds.

Independent and digital filmmaking

Independent film is produced outside the major studio system, often on smaller budgets. The arrival of inexpensive digital cameras and desktop editing in the 1990s and 2000s greatly lowered the cost of entry, expanding who can make and distribute films.
