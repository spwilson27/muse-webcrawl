Turn and slip indicator

Image placeholder: turn and slip indicator

The turn and slip indicator, sometimes called the turn and bank indicator, is a flight instrument that shows the rate of turn of an aircraft together with an inclinometer indicating whether the turn is coordinated — that is, whether the aircraft is slipping or skidding. It combines two displays in a single case: a curved glass tube containing a ball (the slip indicator) and a gyroscopically driven needle or miniature aircraft symbol showing turn rate.

Turn indicator

The turn portion uses a gyroscope mounted so that precession causes a needle to deflect in proportion to the rate of yaw when the aircraft turns. Markings on the dial indicate a standard rate turn — commonly a "two-minute turn" of 3 degrees per second, in which the aircraft completes 360 degrees in two minutes. In many light aircraft the turn indicator is electrically powered, providing a backup attitude reference if the vacuum-driven attitude indicator fails.

Slip indicator

The slip portion, or inclinometer, is simply a heavy ball in a curved, liquid-filled glass tube. Under gravity and centrifugal effects the ball rests at the lowest point of the tube when the turn is coordinated. If the aircraft slips toward the inside of the turn or skids toward the outside, the ball displaces to one side. The pilot corrects by "stepping on the ball" — applying rudder on the side toward which the ball has moved.

Turn coordinator

A later development, the turn coordinator, mounts its gyro at an angle so that it responds to roll as well as yaw, giving a quicker and more informative indication when entering and leaving turns. Turn coordinators display a miniature aircraft whose wings bank in the direction of turn, and are standard in most modern light aircraft, while the classic needle-and-ball turn and slip indicator remains common in gliders and older types.
