An assault rifle is a selective-fire rifle that uses an intermediate cartridge and a detachable magazine. "Selective fire" means the shooter can choose between semi-automatic, fully automatic, or sometimes burst fire. Assault rifles are the standard service rifles of most modern armies, representing a compromise between the long-range power of full-power battle rifles and the controllability of submachine guns in close combat.

Development

The concept emerged in the Second World War: most infantry combat occurred within 300 metres, making full-power rifle cartridges unnecessarily powerful and hard to control in automatic fire. Germany fielded the Sturmgewehr 44 (StG 44, "assault rifle 44") chambered for the intermediate 7.92×33mm Kurz cartridge. After the war the Soviet Union adopted the AK-47 in 7.62×39mm, which became the most widely produced firearm design in history, while the United States eventually standardised the M16 in 5.56×45mm NATO, a smaller, high-velocity cartridge; the USSR followed with the AK-74 in 5.45×39mm.

Characteristics and terminology

Defining features are an intermediate cartridge, detachable magazine (commonly 30 rounds), effective range of roughly 300–600 metres, and selective fire. The military term should not be confused with "assault weapon", a legal and political term used in some jurisdictions (notably the United States) for certain semi-automatic firearms defined largely by external features; nor is it related to the legal offence of assault.
