Common Application

Founded: 1975
Type: Nonprofit organization
Members: More than 1,000 colleges and universities
Website: commonapp.org

The Common Application (informally known as the Common App) is an undergraduate college admission application that applicants may use to apply to any of more than 1,000 member colleges and universities in all 50 U.S. states and the District of Columbia, as well as in Canada, China, Japan, and many European countries.

History

The Common Application was established in 1975, when 15 private colleges began using a shared paper application form to reduce duplicated effort for applicants. It moved online in the late 1990s and grew steadily; by the 2020s more than a million applicants a year submitted applications through the platform.

The application

Applicants complete a single core application — personal information, family, education, activities, and a personal essay chosen from a set of prompts — and submit it to multiple member institutions, which may add their own supplementary questions, essays, and fees. The system also routes school reports, transcripts, and teacher recommendations.

The launch of the fourth-generation platform (CA4) in August 2013 was marred by technical glitches that forced some colleges to push back deadlines. The organization has since introduced transfer and adult-learner applications and tools aimed at first-generation and low-income applicants, including fee waivers and guidance resources.

Similar systems

Related services include the Coalition Application, the Universal College Application, and public systems such as ApplyTexas and the University of California application.
