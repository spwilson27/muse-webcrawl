Henry Wadsworth Longfellow (27 February 1807 – 24 March 1882) was an American poet and educator. His original works include the poems "Paul Revere's Ride", The Song of Hiawatha, and Evangeline. He was the first American to completely translate Dante Alighieri's Divine Comedy and was one of the fireside poets from New England.

Life

Born in Portland, Maine (then part of Massachusetts), Longfellow graduated from Bowdoin College, where Nathaniel Hawthorne was a classmate. After travelling in Europe to prepare for a teaching post, he became a professor at Bowdoin and then at Harvard College. He retired from teaching in 1854 to focus on writing, living for the rest of his life in the Craigie House in Cambridge, Massachusetts, once George Washington's headquarters.

His first wife, Mary Potter, died in 1835 after a miscarriage. His second wife, Frances Appleton, died in 1861 after sustaining severe burns when her dress caught fire. Longfellow, burned trying to save her, grew the beard that became his trademark. He had difficulty writing after her death and turned to translation, including his celebrated Dante.

Works and fame

Longfellow's narrative poems — Evangeline (1847), about the Acadian expulsion; The Song of Hiawatha (1855), written in trochaic tetrameter adapted from the Finnish Kalevala; and The Courtship of Miles Standish (1858) — made him the most popular American poet of his century, beloved on both sides of the Atlantic. "Paul Revere's Ride" (1860) fixed the midnight-ride legend in American memory. A bust of Longfellow was placed in Poets' Corner in Westminster Abbey in 1884, the first American poet so honoured.
