The Spanish Tragedy

Written by: Thomas Kyd
Date: c. 1587
Full title: The Spanish Tragedy, or Hieronimo is Mad Again

The Spanish Tragedy, or Hieronimo is Mad Again is an Elizabethan revenge tragedy by Thomas Kyd, written between 1582 and 1592 and first performed around 1587. Hugely popular in its day, it established the revenge play as a dominant genre of the English Renaissance stage and strongly influenced Shakespeare's Hamlet.

Plot

Framed by the ghost of Don Andrea and the personified figure of Revenge, the play tells of Hieronimo, Knight Marshal of Spain, whose son Horatio is murdered by Lorenzo and Balthazar to clear Balthazar's path to Horatio's beloved Bel-imperia. Discovering the killers' identities but unable to obtain justice through the law, Hieronimo feigns madness, stages a play-within-the-play in which the murders are re-enacted and made real, and kills Lorenzo and Balthazar before taking his own life.

Influence

The play introduced or popularized the ghost demanding vengeance, the feigned-mad avenger, soliloquies of deliberation, and the play within a play — machinery that shaped Hamlet a decade later. References to "Hieronimo" were ubiquitous in the period, and additions to the text were printed in 1602, some attributed to Ben Jonson.
