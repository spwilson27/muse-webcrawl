Hall's marriage theorem

In mathematics, Hall's marriage theorem, proved by Philip Hall in 1935, gives a necessary and sufficient condition for selecting distinct representatives from a collection of finite sets. It takes its name from an application to "marriages": given a group of men and women, where each woman knows some of the men, when can each woman marry a man she knows?

Statement

Let S₁, …, Sₙ be finite sets. There exists a system of distinct representatives — distinct elements xᵢ ∈ Sᵢ — if and only if for every subset of the indices, the union of the corresponding sets contains at least as many elements as there are sets in the subset. This condition is called Hall's condition.

Graph-theoretic formulation

Equivalently, in a bipartite graph, there is a matching that saturates one part X if and only if every subset S of X has at least |S| neighbours. The theorem has applications to Latin squares, transversal theory, scheduling, and flows in networks, and is closely related to Kőnig's theorem and the max-flow min-cut theorem.
