Ediacaran

Ediacaran Period
Time: c. 635–541 million years ago
Preceded by: Cryogenian
Followed by: Cambrian

The Ediacaran Period is a geological period spanning 94 million years from the end of the Cryogenian Period 635 million years ago to the beginning of the Cambrian Period 538.8 million years ago. It is the last period of the Proterozoic Eon and was ratified in 2004, the first new period added in over a century.

Life

The period saw the appearance of the Ediacara biota — large, complex, mostly soft-bodied multicellular organisms such as Dickinsonia, Charnia and Spriggina, among the earliest evidence of complex life. Their affinities — animals, fungi, or an extinct experiment — remain debated. Early bilaterians, burrowing and possibly the first skeletons (Cloudina) appear near its end.

Context

The Ediacaran followed Snowball Earth glaciations and coincided with rising oxygen. It ends at the Cambrian explosion. Type fossils come from the Ediacara Hills of South Australia. See also Cambrian explosion.
