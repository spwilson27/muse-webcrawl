Daniel O'Connell

Daniel O'Connell (Irish: Dónall Ó Conaill; 6 August 1775 – 15 May 1847), often referred to as the Liberator or the Emancipator, was an Irish political leader in the first half of the 19th century. He campaigned for Catholic emancipation — the right for Catholics to sit in the Westminster Parliament — and for repeal of the Acts of Union between Ireland and Great Britain.

Early life

O'Connell was born near Cahirciveen, County Kerry, into a wealthy Catholic landowning family. Educated at St Omer and Douai in France, he fled the French Revolution in 1793 and trained as a barrister at Lincoln's Inn, London, and King's Inns, Dublin. Called to the Irish bar in 1798, he became one of Ireland's most successful lawyers.

Catholic emancipation

In 1823 O'Connell founded the Catholic Association, financed by the "Catholic rent" of a penny a month, which mobilised the Irish Catholic peasantry into the first mass political movement of its kind in Europe. In 1828 he won a by-election in County Clare but, as a Catholic, could not take his seat. Faced with the threat of mass unrest, the Duke of Wellington's government conceded the Roman Catholic Relief Act 1829, admitting Catholics to Parliament. O'Connell entered the House of Commons and dominated Irish politics for a decade.

Repeal movement

From 1840 O'Connell led the campaign to repeal the Union and restore an Irish parliament, organising "monster meetings" of hundreds of thousands. In 1843 the government banned a planned meeting at Clontarf; O'Connell cancelled it rather than risk bloodshed, and his movement lost momentum. He was prosecuted for conspiracy and briefly imprisoned in 1844 before the verdict was reversed on appeal.

Death and legacy

His health broken, O'Connell travelled toward Rome in 1847 and died at Genoa on 15 May 1847, on the eve of the Great Famine's worst years. His heart was buried in Rome and his body in Glasnevin Cemetery, Dublin. O'Connell Street in Dublin is named for him, and he remains a foundational figure of Irish constitutional nationalism and peaceful mass politics.
