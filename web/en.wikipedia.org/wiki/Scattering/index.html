Scattering

Scattering is a general physical process in which moving particles or radiation, such as light or sound, are forced to deviate from a straight trajectory by localized non-uniformities (including particles and radiation) in the medium through which they pass. In conventional use, this also includes deviation of reflected radiation from the angle predicted by the law of reflection.

Types

Elastic scattering occurs when the kinetic energy of the scattered particle is conserved, as in Rayleigh scattering of light by molecules much smaller than its wavelength — the effect responsible for the blue colour of the sky. Inelastic scattering involves a change in energy, as in Raman scattering and Compton scattering. Mie scattering describes scattering by particles comparable to or larger than the wavelength, such as water droplets in clouds.

Single and multiple scattering

In single scattering, particles interact with only one scattering centre, as in Rutherford scattering of alpha particles by atomic nuclei — the experiment that revealed the atomic nucleus. In multiple scattering, particles are deflected many times, leading to diffusion-like spreading, as with light in fog or electrons in solids.

Applications

Scattering phenomena are exploited in radar, medical imaging, materials characterization (neutron and X-ray scattering), atmospheric science, and particle physics, where scattering experiments probe the structure of matter.
