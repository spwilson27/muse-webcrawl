South Tyneside is a metropolitan borough in Tyne and Wear, England, on the south bank of the River Tyne near its mouth. Its principal towns are South Shields (the administrative centre), Jarrow, and Hebburn, together with the villages of Boldon, Cleadon, and Whitburn. The borough was formed in 1974 from parts of County Durham.

History

The area has a distinguished industrial and religious heritage. The Venerable Bede lived at the twin monasteries of Wearmouth and Jarrow in the 7th and 8th centuries. Jarrow was the starting point of the 1936 Jarrow March, when 200 unemployed men marched to London to protest mass unemployment after the closure of Palmer's shipyard — an emblematic event of the Great Depression in Britain. Shipbuilding and coal mining have since largely vanished, and the borough today is largely residential, served by the Tyne and Wear Metro.
