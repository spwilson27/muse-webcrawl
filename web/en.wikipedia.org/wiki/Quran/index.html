The Quran (also romanised Qur'an or Koran) is the central religious text of Islam, believed by Muslims to be a revelation from God (Allah) to the Prophet Muhammad through the angel Jibreel (Gabriel), over about 23 years (610–632 CE). Written in Arabic, it consists of 114 chapters (suras) comprising about 6,236 verses (ayat, count varies slightly by tradition), arranged roughly longest-first after the opening Al-Fatiha.

The Quran's themes include monotheism, prophecy, ethics, law and the Last Judgment; it retells narratives also found in the Bible (Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, Jesus — honoured as a prophet) from an Islamic perspective. Muslims regard the Arabic original as the literal word of God — translations are considered interpretations — and memorisation of the whole text (hafiz) is a revered attainment. Compilation into a standard text is associated with the caliph Uthman within a generation of Muhammad's death.
