CONCACAF Champions Cup

Founded: 1962 (as the CONCACAF Champions' Cup)
Region: North America, Central America and the Caribbean (CONCACAF)
Teams: 27 (2024 onwards)
Most successful club: Club América / Cruz Azul (7 titles each era-inclusive tallies vary)

The CONCACAF Champions Cup is an annual continental club football competition organised by CONCACAF, the governing body for North America, Central America and the Caribbean. It is the region's premier club tournament, and its winner qualifies for the FIFA Club World Cup (and its expanded successor, the FIFA Intercontinental Cup/Champions qualification pathway).

History

The competition was founded in 1962 as the CONCACAF Champions' Cup and was contested, with several interruptions, until 2008, when it was rebranded and expanded as the CONCACAF Champions League with a group stage. In 2024 the tournament returned to the Champions Cup name and an expanded 27-team knockout format, with qualification through domestic leagues and cups and through the regional Leagues Cup, Central American Cup and Caribbean Cup competitions.

Dominance and records

Mexican clubs have dominated the competition, with Liga MX sides winning every edition of the Champions League era from 2009 until 2022, when the Seattle Sounders became the first Major League Soccer club to win the modern tournament. Cruz Azul, Club América, Pachuca and Monterrey are among the most successful clubs overall. Pachuca won the 2024 edition, defeating Columbus Crew in the final.

Format

Under the current format, 27 clubs compete in a five-round knockout tournament played over two legs (except the one-off final), with some top-ranked clubs receiving byes to the round of 16.
