Mathura

Mathura is a city in the Indian state of Uttar Pradesh, situated on the banks of the Yamuna River, about 57 kilometres north of Agra and 145 kilometres south-east of Delhi. It is one of the Sapta Puri, the seven cities regarded as holy in Hinduism, and is revered as the birthplace of the deity Krishna. The region around the city, known as Braj, is central to Krishna devotion and pilgrimage.

History

Mathura has been inhabited since ancient times and was an important centre of trade at the junction of caravan routes. In the first centuries BCE and CE it flourished under the Kushan Empire, whose emperor Kanishka made it one of his capitals. The city became a renowned centre of art: the Mathura school of sculpture, working in red sandstone, produced some of the earliest images of the Buddha, alongside Gandhara art, as well as Jain and Hindu images. The city was later ruled by the Guptas, sacked by Mahmud of Ghazni in 1018, and incorporated into the Delhi Sultanate and the Mughal Empire. The great Keshav Dev temple was destroyed under Aurangzeb in 1670 and the Shahi Idgah mosque built on part of the site, a history that remains politically sensitive.

Religious significance

The Shri Krishna Janmasthan Temple Complex marks the traditional site of Krishna's birth. Nearby Vrindavan, Govardhan, Gokul, and Barsana form a pilgrimage circuit visited by millions, especially during Janmashtami and Holi. Mathura is also sacred to Jains — it is associated with the seventh Tirthankara, Suparshvanatha — and its stupas and monasteries were described by the Chinese pilgrims Faxian and Xuanzang.

Economy and transport

Today Mathura is known for its dairy products and sweets such as peda, for oil refining (the Mathura Refinery of Indian Oil), and for religious tourism. It is a major rail junction on the Delhi–Agra line and lies on National Highway 19.
