Circular sector

A minor circular sector (green) and the corresponding major sector

A circular sector, also known as a circle sector or disk sector, is the portion of a disk or circle enclosed by two radii and an arc: the smaller area is known as the minor sector and the larger as the major sector. In the diagram, θ is the central angle, r is the radius of the circle, and L is the arc length of the minor sector. A sector with a central angle of 180° is a semicircle, and one with a central angle of 90° is a quadrant.

Area

The total area of a circle is πr². The area A of a sector with central angle θ (measured in radians) is obtained by multiplying the circle's area by the ratio of the angle to 2π:

A = (θ / 2π) · πr² = ½ r² θ.

If θ is measured in degrees, then A = (θ / 360°) · πr². The sector area can also be expressed in terms of its arc length: A = ½ r L.

Perimeter

The perimeter of a sector is the sum of the lengths of the arc and the two radii: P = L + 2r = θr + 2r = r(θ + 2), where θ is in radians.

Centre of mass

The centre of mass of a uniform sector lies along its axis of symmetry, at a distance from the centre of the circle equal to (2 r sin(θ/2)) / (3 · (θ/2)) — i.e. two thirds of the corresponding distance for the circular segment's arc centroid — a formula often written as d = (4 r sin³(θ/2)) / (3(θ − sin θ)) measured from the circle's centre along the bisector.

Special sectors

A sector is frequently compared to a slice of pie or pizza. The area between the sector and its chord is the circular segment. In polar coordinates, the sector is the natural domain for evaluating integrals involving circular symmetry.
