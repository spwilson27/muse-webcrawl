Grant Park is a large urban park in the Loop community area of Chicago, Illinois, often called "Chicago's front yard". Located between the downtown skyline and Lake Michigan, it contains the Art Institute of Chicago, Buckingham Fountain, Millennium Park, and the Museum Campus, and hosts the city's largest festivals.

History

The land originated as a strip of public ground along the lake set aside in the 1830s–40s, protected by the famous legal restriction that it remain "public ground — forever to remain vacant of buildings", defended in court by retailer Aaron Montgomery Ward. Much of the park is landfill created after the Great Chicago Fire of 1871. The park took its modern formal design from the 1909 Plan of Chicago of Daniel Burnham and Edward H. Bennett. It was renamed in 1901 for President Ulysses S. Grant.

Events

Grant Park was the scene of clashes during the 1968 Democratic National Convention and of Barack Obama's 2008 election-night victory speech before an estimated 240,000 people. Recurring events include the Taste of Chicago, the Chicago Marathon start/finish, Lollapalooza, and the Chicago Blues and Jazz festivals.

Features

Notable features include Buckingham Fountain (1927), the Petrillo Music Shell, the Art Institute of Chicago, and, as adjacent additions since 1998–2004, the Museum Campus (Field Museum, Shedd Aquarium, Adler Planetarium) and Millennium Park with its Cloud Gate sculpture.
