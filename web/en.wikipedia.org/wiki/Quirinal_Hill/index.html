The Quirinal Hill (Latin: Collis Quirinalis; Italian: Colle Quirinale) is one of the Seven Hills of Rome, at the north-east of the city centre. It is the location of the official residence of the President of the Italian Republic, the Quirinal Palace; by metonymy, "the Quirinale" is often used to refer to the Italian presidency itself.

History

The hill was Sabi​ne territory in Rome's earliest history, and its name is traditionally derived from the god Quirinus, who had an ancient temple and altar there; the Sabine settlers from Cures were said to have become Roman citizens known as Quirites.

In the Republican era the hill was a fashionable residential district of temples, gardens, and aristocratic houses; Julius Caesar and the poet Martial lived there. In Imperial times it held the enormous Baths of Constantine and the temples of Serapis and Quirinus.

From the late 16th century the popes built the Quirinal Palace as a summer residence, more salubrious than the Vatican. After 1870 the palace passed to the kings of Italy, and in 1947 it became the residence of the Presidents of the Republic.

Notable buildings

  • Quirinal Palace (Palazzo del Quirinale) — official residence of the President of Italy.
  • Palazzo Barberini — Baroque palace, now a gallery of ancient art.
  • San Carlo alle Quattro Fontane — Borromini's Baroque church.
  • Sant'Andrea al Quirinale — Bernini's oval church.
  • Piazza del Quirinale — with its ancient Dioscuri (horse-tamers) statues and obelisk.
  • Palazzo della Consulta — seat of the Constitutional Court of Italy.
