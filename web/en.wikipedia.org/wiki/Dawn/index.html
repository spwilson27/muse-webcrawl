Dawn is the time of morning twilight when the Sun is just below the horizon but its light begins to illuminate the sky, before sunrise. It marks the transition from night to day. Dawn occurs in stages corresponding to the Sun's depression below the horizon: astronomical dawn (Sun 18° below the horizon), nautical dawn (12° below), and civil dawn (6° below), after which the Sun rises.

Phases of dawn

Astronomical dawn begins when the Sun is 18 degrees below the horizon; the faintest stars begin to disappear from view. Nautical dawn begins at 12 degrees below the horizon, when the horizon at sea becomes visible enough for navigation by stars. Civil dawn begins at 6 degrees below the horizon, when there is generally enough light for ordinary outdoor activities without artificial illumination. The period ends at sunrise, when the Sun's upper limb appears above the horizon.

Duration

The length of dawn varies with latitude and season. Near the equator it is brief—about twenty minutes—because the Sun's path crosses the horizon steeply. At high latitudes in summer, twilight can last all night, producing "white nights," and beyond the polar circles there are periods when the Sun never gets far enough below the horizon for full darkness.

Cultural and religious significance

Dawn has rich symbolism in mythology and religion as a time of renewal and beginnings. The Greek goddess Eos and the Roman Aurora personify the dawn. Many traditions schedule prayers or rituals at dawn, including the Islamic Fajr prayer and monastic hours such as Lauds. The "dawn chorus" of birdsong is a familiar natural feature of the period in spring and summer.
