Espalier

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Espalier (from French espalier, originally meaning a trellis or frame) is the horticultural practice of pruning and training a tree or shrub so that it grows flat against a surface such as a wall, fence or trellis, usually in formal, symmetrical patterns. The technique was refined in European gardens from the 17th century onward, notably at Versailles, and became a hallmark of the French potager.

Purpose

Espalier serves both ornamental and practical ends. Against a wall, a tree benefits from reflected and stored heat, allowing tender fruit varieties — such as peaches, nectarines and figs — to ripen in cooler climates. Training a tree flat also saves space in small gardens, creates a living fence or screen, and exposes fruit evenly to sunlight, improving quality and ease of picking.

Forms

Common espalier patterns include the horizontal cordon, in which a central trunk bears tiers of horizontal arms; the fan, whose branches radiate from a short trunk; the candelabra or multiple vertical cordon; and the Belgian fence, a lattice formed by planting trees at an angle and grafting crossing branches. Informal espalier relaxes geometric precision but keeps the plant essentially two-dimensional.

Technique

Young, flexible trees — most often apple and pear, which fruit on long-lived spurs — are planted beside a support of wires or a wall. New shoots are tied in as they grow, unwanted growth is pruned in summer to encourage fruit buds, and structural pruning is done in winter. Suitable species also include quince, loquat, citrus, olive, and ornamentals such as pyracantha and magnolia. The practice is related to topiary and to the training used in bonsai.
