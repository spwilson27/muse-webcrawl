Battle of Stirling Bridge

The Battle of Stirling Bridge was fought on 11 September 1297 during the First War of Scottish Independence. The Scottish forces of William Wallace and Andrew Moray defeated a larger English army under John de Warenne, Earl of Surrey, and Hugh de Cressingham, at the wooden bridge over the River Forth near Stirling. It was Wallace's first great victory and established his reputation.

The battle

The English army had to cross the Forth by a narrow wooden bridge only wide enough for a few horsemen abreast. Wallace and Moray waited until a substantial portion — but not the whole — of the English force had crossed, then attacked, seizing the bridgehead and preventing reinforcements. The English on the north bank were cut off and destroyed; Cressingham was killed, and legend holds his skin was flayed. Warenne escaped south with the remainder of the army.

Aftermath

Moray died of his wounds within weeks. Wallace was made a Guardian of Scotland and raided into northern England. Edward I responded in person the following year, defeating Wallace at the Battle of Falkirk. A modern bridge stands near the site; the medieval bridge's remains lie slightly upstream.
