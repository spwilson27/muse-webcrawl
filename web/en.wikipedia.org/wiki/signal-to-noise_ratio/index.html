Signal-to-noise ratio

TypeMeasure in science and engineering
AbbreviationSNR, S/N
UnitsDecibels (dB)

Signal-to-noise ratio (SNR or S/N) is a measure used in science and engineering that compares the level of a desired signal to the level of background noise. SNR is defined as the ratio of signal power to noise power, often expressed in decibels.

Definition

SNR = P_signal / P_noise; in decibels, 10·log10 of that ratio, or 20·log10 of the amplitude ratio. A ratio higher than 1:1 (greater than 0 dB) indicates more signal than noise.

Applications

In audio, high SNR means clean reproduction — hi-fi equipment often exceeds 90 dB. In imaging, SNR affects image quality in cameras, telescopes, and MRI scanners. In communications, SNR limits channel capacity through the Shannon–Hartley theorem, and receiver sensitivity is specified relative to noise floors. Noise may arise from thermal (Johnson–Nyquist) noise, shot noise, or interference.

Improvement

Techniques include amplification before lossy stages, filtering, averaging repeated measurements, shielding, and error-correcting codes.
