Michael

This article is about the given name. For the archangel, see Michael (archangel).

Michael is a masculine given name derived from the Hebrew phrase Mi-ka-El (מִיכָאֵל), meaning "Who is like God?" — a rhetorical question implying that no one is like God. The name is borne in the Bible by the archangel Michael, and it has been in continuous use in Christian, Jewish and Islamic cultures for centuries.

Popularity

Michael was among the most popular boys' names in the English-speaking world for much of the 20th century; in the United States it held the number-one spot for boys for most years from 1954 to 1998, an unusually long run of popularity. Variants exist in nearly every European language, including Michel (French), Miguel (Spanish and Portuguese), Michele (Italian), Mikhail (Russian), Mikael (Scandinavian), Michał (Polish) and Mícheál (Irish).

Religious significance

The archangel Michael is venerated in Judaism, Christianity and Islam (as Mika'il). He is regarded as the leader of the heavenly host, a protector of Israel, and, in Catholic tradition, a psychopomp who weighs souls. Numerous churches, towns (such as St Michael's Mount) and institutions bear his name.

Notable bearers

The name has been borne by many emperors of Byzantium, kings, and modern figures in sport and entertainment, including basketball player Michael Jordan, singer Michael Jackson and racing driver Michael Schumacher.
