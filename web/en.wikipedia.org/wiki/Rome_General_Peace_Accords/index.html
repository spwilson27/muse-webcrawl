The Rome General Peace Accords (Portuguese: Acordo Geral de Paz) were a peace treaty signed between the government of Mozambique and the rebel group RENAMO in Rome on 4 October 1992, which formally ended the Mozambican Civil War. The war, fought between the ruling FRELIMO party and the anti-communist RENAMO insurgency since 1977, had killed an estimated one million people and displaced several million more.

Background

Mozambique gained independence from Portugal in 1975 under FRELIMO, led by Samora Machel. The Rhodesian government, and later apartheid South Africa, sponsored RENAMO as a destabilising force, and by the mid-1980s the war and drought had produced a humanitarian catastrophe. The 1984 Nkomati Accord with South Africa failed to end the fighting. After Machel's death in a plane crash in 1986, his successor Joaquim Chissano pursued economic liberalisation and, as Cold War patronage dried up, a negotiated settlement.

Negotiations

Peace talks began in July 1990 in Rome, hosted and mediated by the Community of Sant'Egidio, a Catholic lay organisation, with the support of the Italian government. The principal mediators included Andrea Riccardi, founder of Sant'Egidio, and Matteo Zuppi, later a cardinal. The Mozambican government delegation faced RENAMO leader Afonso Dhlakama's representatives across twelve rounds of talks over twenty-seven months. The process was unusual for relying on a non-governmental religious community as mediator, supported quietly by Italy, the United States, France, Portugal and the United Nations.

The negotiations produced seven protocols covering a ceasefire, political party law, electoral arrangements, military matters — including the formation of a unified armed force of 30,000 drawn from both sides — guarantees for the opposition, and a conference of donor countries to fund implementation.

Implementation

The ceasefire held from October 1992. The United Nations Operation in Mozambique (ONUMOZ), authorised by Security Council Resolution 797, deployed some 6,500 troops and thousands of civilian staff to monitor the ceasefire, demobilise combatants and organise elections. Under Special Representative Aldo Ajello, ONUMOZ overcame repeated delays and mistrust. Mozambique held its first multiparty elections in October 1994, which FRELIMO and Chissano won; RENAMO accepted the results and became the parliamentary opposition. The mission is generally regarded as one of the UN's successful peacekeeping operations of the era.

Legacy

The accords ended one of Africa's most destructive civil wars and ushered in a period of sustained economic growth in Mozambique. The "spirit of Rome" and the Sant'Egidio model of track-two diplomacy influenced later mediation efforts elsewhere in Africa. Peace between FRELIMO and RENAMO remained imperfect: low-level clashes resumed between 2013 and 2016 before a new peace agreement was signed in 2019, following Dhlakama's death in 2018.
