Luna 3

Luna 3 was a Soviet spacecraft launched on 4 October 1959 as part of the Luna programme. It was the first mission to photograph the far side of the Moon, which is never visible from Earth, returning the first images of that hemisphere on 7 October 1959.

Mission

The cylindrical probe carried a camera system that exposed conventional film, developed and fixed it automatically on board, and then scanned the negatives and transmitted the images to Earth by a facsimile-like radio system as the craft returned toward Earth. Its trajectory used lunar gravity to swing the spacecraft around the Moon and back over the northern hemisphere, where Soviet ground stations could receive the signals.

Results

Luna 3 returned 29 photographs covering about 70 percent of the far side. The pictures, though crude, revealed a surface strikingly different from the near side: mountainous highlands with very few of the dark maria familiar from Earth. Two maria that were identified were named Mare Moscoviense and Mare Desiderii (later reclassified), and features were given names including the Tsiolkovsky crater. The images were published in an atlas of the far side in 1960.

Contact with the spacecraft was lost in April 1960, and it is believed to have burned up in Earth's atmosphere. Luna 3 demonstrated television imaging from deep space and greatly embarrassed the United States in the early Space Race.
