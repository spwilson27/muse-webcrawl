Moisei Ginzburg

Moisei Yakovlevich Ginzburg (Russian: Моисей Яковлевич Гинзбург; 4 June 1892 – 7 January 1946) was a Soviet constructivist architect, best known for his 1929 Narkomfin Building in Moscow. He was a leading theorist of constructivist architecture and founder of the OSA Group (Organisation of Contemporary Architects) in 1925.

Life and career

Ginzburg was born in Minsk, then in the Russian Empire, into a Jewish family of architects. He studied in Milan and at the Imperial Academy of Arts in St Petersburg, graduating in 1917. After the Russian Revolution he taught at VKhUTEMAS in Moscow and became a central figure of the Soviet avant-garde.

Work and theory

With Alexander Vesnin he founded OSA and edited its journal SA (Contemporary Architecture). His book Style and Epoch (1924) was the first theoretical statement of constructivism in architecture, arguing that new social conditions required a new architectural method based on function and industrial technology. His best-known built work, the Narkomfin Building (1928–1932, with Ignaty Milinis), was a "social condenser" — a communal housing block designed to transition residents toward collective living, with duplex units, a communal block containing a kitchen, crèche and library, and a roof garden. Other works include the Gosstrakh housing in Moscow and sanatoria in Kislovodsk. Ginzburg travelled to Central Asia in the 1920s and wrote on its vernacular architecture.

Later years

As socialist realism became official doctrine in the 1930s, Ginzburg's constructivist work was denounced as formalism. He continued to practise and teach in a more restrained idiom until his death in Moscow in 1946. The Narkomfin Building, long dilapidated, was restored in 2017–2020 and is regarded as an icon of the European avant-garde.
