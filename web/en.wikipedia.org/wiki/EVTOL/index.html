EVTOL

An electric vertical take-off and landing (eVTOL) aircraft is a variety of VTOL (vertical take-off and landing) aircraft that uses electric power to hover, take off, and land vertically. This technology came about from major advances in electric propulsion (motors, batteries, and electronic speed controllers) and the growing need for new vehicles for urban air mobility (air taxi) services.

Design types

  • Multirotor designs rely on many small rotors for lift in all phases of flight (e.g., Volocopter, EHang).
  • Lift + cruise designs use separate rotors for vertical lift and a propeller for forward flight.
  • Tilt-rotor / tilt-wing / tilt-duct designs rotate their propulsors or wings to transition between hover and wing-borne flight (e.g., Joby Aviation, Lilium).
  • Vectored thrust designs use a smaller number of movable propulsors.

Development and regulation

NASA's research on distributed electric propulsion in the 2010s, followed by Uber's Elevate initiative (2016), spurred hundreds of startups and established aerospace firms to develop eVTOL aircraft. Certification frameworks have been developed by the FAA (under the "powered-lift" category) and EASA (SC-VTOL). Companies including Joby Aviation, Archer Aviation, Beta Technologies, Volocopter, Lilium, Vertical Aerospace, and EHang have flown full-scale prototypes; EHang received a type certificate in China in 2023, and several Western manufacturers target commercial air-taxi entry into service in the mid-to-late 2020s.

Advantages and challenges

Electric propulsion offers quieter operation, lower operating and maintenance costs, zero local emissions, and distributed redundancy. Key challenges include battery energy density (which limits range and payload), certification of novel configurations, charging infrastructure and vertiports, air traffic integration, and public acceptance.
