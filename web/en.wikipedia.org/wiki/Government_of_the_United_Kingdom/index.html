The Government of the United Kingdom, officially His Majesty's Government, is the central government of the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland. It is headed by the Prime Minister, who selects the other ministers. The Government is responsible to Parliament, from which its members are drawn; by convention the Prime Minister and most senior ministers sit in the House of Commons.

Structure

Executive power is exercised in the name of the monarch by the Government. Ministers lead government departments — such as the Treasury, the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office, and the Home Office — staffed by politically impartial civil servants. The Cabinet, a committee of the most senior ministers, is the supreme decision-making body. Devolved governments in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland exercise authority over matters not reserved to Westminster.

The Prime Minister and ministers

The Prime Minister is appointed by the monarch, by convention the leader of the party able to command the confidence of the House of Commons. The Prime Minister chairs the Cabinet, appoints and dismisses ministers, and is responsible for the overall organisation of the Government. Ministers are bound by collective ministerial responsibility and the Ministerial Code.

Accountability

The Government must retain the confidence of the Commons; it answers questions in Parliament, is scrutinised by select committees, and its spending is authorised by statute. General elections are held at most every five years under the Dissolution and Calling of Parliament Act 2022.
