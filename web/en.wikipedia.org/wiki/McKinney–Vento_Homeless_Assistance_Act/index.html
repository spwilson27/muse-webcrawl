McKinney–Vento Homeless Assistance Act

McKinney–Vento Act
Full title: Stewart B. McKinney Homeless Assistance Act
Enacted: 22 July 1987 (signed by Ronald Reagan)
Codified: 42 U.S.C. § 11301 et seq.
Named for: Rep. Stewart McKinney; Sen. Pete Vento (added 2000)

The McKinney–Vento Homeless Assistance Act of 1987 is a United States federal law that provides federal money for homeless shelter programs. It was the first significant federal legislative response to modern homelessness and remains the principal federal statute addressing it.

History

Originally titled the Homeless Persons' Survival Act, the bill was introduced in 1986 and renamed in honour of its chief Republican sponsor, Representative Stewart B. McKinney of Connecticut, who died in May 1987, months before President Ronald Reagan signed the act into law on 22 July 1987. In 2000 the law was renamed the McKinney–Vento Act to honour Democratic Representative Bruce Vento of Minnesota, another leading advocate, who died that year.

Provisions

The act created a patchwork of programs administered chiefly by the Department of Housing and Urban Development, including the Emergency Shelter Grants program, the Supportive Housing program, Shelter Plus Care, and the Continuum of Care framework under which local coalitions plan and apply for funding. It also established the federal Interagency Council on Homelessness to coordinate the response across agencies.

Education subtitle

The act's Education for Homeless Children and Youth program guarantees homeless children the right to enrol in school immediately, to remain in their school of origin where feasible, and to receive transportation to it. School districts must designate a homeless liaison, and the law's definition of homelessness — children lacking a fixed, regular and adequate nighttime residence, including those "doubled up" with others — governs educational services and statistics nationwide.

HEARTH Act

The Homeless Emergency Assistance and Rapid Transition to Housing (HEARTH) Act of 2009 amended and reauthorised the McKinney–Vento Act, consolidating competitive grant programs, emphasising homelessness prevention and rapid rehousing, and broadening the statutory definition of homelessness.
