Layla and Other Assorted Love Songs

Layla and Other Assorted Love Songs is the debut studio album by the English–American rock band Derek and the Dominos, released on 9 November 1970 by Polydor and Atco Records. The album is best known for its title track, "Layla", written by Eric Clapton and Jim Gordon, and is considered one of the greatest albums of all time.

Background

Derek and the Dominos formed in 1970 with Clapton joined by Bobby Whitlock, Carl Radle, and Jim Gordon. Guitarist Duane Allman of the Allman Brothers Band contributed to most of the album's tracks. Much of the material was inspired by Clapton's unrequited love for Pattie Boyd, the wife of his friend George Harrison; the title song drew on the Persian tale of Layla and Majnun.

Music and reception

The double album blends blues rock standards such as "Nobody Knows You When You're Down and Out" and "Key to the Highway" with originals including "Bell Bottom Blues", "Why Does Love Got to Be So Sad?", and "Layla", whose second half features Gordon's celebrated piano coda. Initially a modest commercial success, the album's reputation grew steadily; "Layla" became a signature Clapton song, and the album was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame.

Track listing

Highlights include "I Looked Away", "Bell Bottom Blues", "Keep on Growing", "Anyday", "Tell the Truth", "Little Wing" (a Jimi Hendrix cover), "It's Too Late", and "Layla".
