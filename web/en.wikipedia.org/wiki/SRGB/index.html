sRGB is a standard RGB (red, green, blue) colour space that HP and Microsoft created cooperatively in 1996 for use on monitors, printers and the web. It was standardised by the International Electrotechnical Commission as IEC 61966-2-1:1999 and is the default colour space of the World Wide Web, HTML, CSS and most consumer imaging.

Specification

sRGB uses the chromaticities of the ITU-R BT.709 primaries, a D65 reference white, and a viewing environment based on typical CRT monitors of the era. Its nonlinear transfer function is a piecewise curve closely approximating a gamma of 2.2, with a short linear segment near black to avoid an infinite slope. The gamut covers roughly 35% of the visible colours specified by the CIE, which is sufficient for most display applications but smaller than wide-gamut spaces such as Adobe RGB or Display P3.

Use

Because sRGB was built into operating systems, browsers and image formats as the assumed default, images without an embedded ICC profile are conventionally treated as sRGB. It remains the baseline colour space for web graphics, video games, and office and consumer photography, while wider-gamut workflows in professional photography and digital cinema use other spaces.
