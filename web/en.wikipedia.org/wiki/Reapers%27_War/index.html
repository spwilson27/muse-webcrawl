Reapers' War

The Reapers' War (Catalan: Guerra dels Segadors; 1640–1659) was a revolt of the Principality of Catalonia against the Spanish monarchy of Philip IV, fought within the wider Franco-Spanish War. Triggered by the burdens of billeting Castilian troops fighting France and by the centralising policies of the Count-Duke of Olivares, it began with the Corpus Christi riots of June 1640 — the Corpus de Sang — in which reapers entering Barcelona killed the viceroy.

Course

Led by Pau Claris, the Catalan institutions proclaimed a republic in January 1641 and then accepted Louis XIII of France as Count of Barcelona, bringing French armies into the principality. War devastated Catalonia for over a decade, compounded by plague in 1650–1654. Barcelona fell to Spanish forces in 1652, though fighting continued along the Pyrenean frontier.

Settlement and legacy

The Treaty of the Pyrenees (1659) ended the Franco-Spanish War: Spain recovered most of Catalonia but ceded Roussillon and part of Cerdanya to France — a partition of the Catalan lands that persists in the modern border. The revolt's anthem, the hymn Els Segadors ("The Reapers"), recalls the events of 1640 and is today the national anthem of Catalonia.
