C. R. Rao

Born: 10 September 1920, Hoovina Hadagali, British India
Died: 22 August 2023, Buffalo, New York, United States
Known for: Cramér–Rao bound, Rao–Blackwell theorem
Fields: Statistics, mathematics

Calyampudi Radhakrishna Rao (10 September 1920 – 22 August 2023), commonly known as C. R. Rao, was an Indian-American mathematician and statistician, widely regarded as one of the most influential statisticians of the twentieth century. His name is attached to the Cramér–Rao bound on the variance of estimators and to the Rao–Blackwell theorem on improving estimators by conditioning.

Life and career

Rao was born in Hadagali in present-day Karnataka. He earned a master's degree in mathematics from Andhra University and in statistics from the University of Calcutta, and completed his PhD at King's College, Cambridge, in 1948 under R. A. Fisher, followed by a ScD in 1965. He joined the Indian Statistical Institute in Kolkata in 1944 and helped shape its research and teaching; he later served as its director. In 1979 he moved to the United States, holding chairs at the University of Pittsburgh and then Pennsylvania State University, where he became Eberly Professor Emeritus of Statistics. He died in Buffalo, New York, in 2023, a few weeks short of his 103rd birthday.

Contributions

Rao's 1945 paper in the Bulletin of the Calcutta Mathematical Society introduced the Cramér–Rao lower bound, the Rao–Blackwell theorem and the Rao distance used in information geometry — three results that each founded lines of research. His textbook Linear Statistical Inference and Its Applications (1965) became a standard reference. He also made major contributions to multivariate analysis, design of experiments, orthogonal arrays, and statistical genetics, and supervised more than fifty doctoral students.

Honours

Rao received the Padma Vibhushan (2001) and Padma Bhushan (1968) from the Government of India, the U.S. National Medal of Science (2001), the India Science Award (2010) and the International Prize in Statistics (2023). He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1967. The C. R. Rao Advanced Institute of Mathematics, Statistics and Computer Science at the University of Hyderabad is named in his honour.
