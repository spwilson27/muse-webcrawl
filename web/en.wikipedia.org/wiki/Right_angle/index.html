Right angle

In geometry and trigonometry, a right angle is an angle of exactly 90 degrees or π/2 radians, corresponding to a quarter turn. If a ray is placed so that its endpoint is on a line and the adjacent angles are equal, then they are right angles.

Definition and notation

The term is a calque of the Latin angulus rectus. A right angle is commonly denoted by a small square drawn at the vertex of the angle. Lines that meet at a right angle are said to be perpendicular to one another. The concept is fundamental to Euclidean geometry: in Euclid's Elements, right angles are the basis of the fourth postulate, which states that all right angles are congruent.

Units

A right angle may be expressed in different units: 90°, π/2 radians, 100 gradians, 8 points (on a 32-point compass rose), or 6 hours of right ascension. It is one quarter of a full turn or of a circle.

Applications

Right angles are ubiquitous in construction, carpentry, and engineering, where squareness determines whether structures are plumb and true. The Pythagorean theorem applies to triangles containing a right angle, and trigonometric ratios such as sine and cosine are defined with reference to right triangles. The 3-4-5 triangle has long been used as a practical way of laying out a right angle.
