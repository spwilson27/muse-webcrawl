Brimstone (missile)

TypeAir-to-surface missile
ManufacturerMBDA UK
In service2005–present
GuidanceMillimetric-wave radar; dual-mode adds laser
MassAbout 50 kg

Brimstone is a ground-attack and anti-armour missile developed by MBDA UK for the Royal Air Force. Originally conceived as a fire-and-forget weapon for destroying massed Warsaw Pact tank formations, it uses a millimetric-wave radar seeker to autonomously find and track armoured targets, and can be carried in large numbers — three missiles per launcher station — on fast jets and other platforms.

Development

Brimstone evolved from the AGM-114 Hellfire airframe, replacing its seeker and warhead arrangement for RAF requirements. It entered service in 2005. In response to rules of engagement in Afghanistan demanding minimal collateral damage, the Dual-Mode Brimstone added semi-active laser guidance, allowing a human operator to designate a precise target; this variant proved highly successful from 2008 onwards. The improved Brimstone 2, with a longer-range motor and insensitive-munition warhead, followed, and the Spear 3 programme builds on the design.

Operational use

Brimstone has been used in combat over Afghanistan, Libya (2011) and Iraq and Syria against Islamic State. It has been launched from Tornado GR4 and Typhoon aircraft and tested from helicopters, remotely piloted aircraft and surface launchers; in 2022 the United Kingdom supplied Brimstone missiles to Ukraine, where ground-launched versions were used against Russian armour.
