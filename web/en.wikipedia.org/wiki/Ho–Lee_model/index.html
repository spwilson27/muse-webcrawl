Ho–Lee model

The Ho–Lee model is a short-rate model used in mathematical finance to describe the evolution of interest rates. Published in 1986 by Thomas Ho and Sang-Bin Lee, it was the first no-arbitrage model of the term structure: it is calibrated to fit the observed initial term structure of interest rates exactly, so that it prices the underlying bonds consistently with the market.

The model

In its original form the model uses a binomial lattice for the short rate. In continuous time, the short rate under the risk-neutral measure follows a stochastic differential equation of the form

dr(t) = θ(t) dt + σ dW(t)

where θ(t) is a time-dependent drift chosen to reproduce the initial term structure, σ is a constant volatility, and W(t) is a Brownian motion. Because the diffusion is normally distributed, the model permits negative interest rates.

Significance and extensions

Ho and Lee's innovation was to make the whole term structure the input to the model rather than an output, ensuring no arbitrage relative to observed bond prices. The model is a special case of the broader Hull–White model, which adds mean reversion, and it inspired a generation of arbitrage-free term-structure models and lattice-based pricing methods for interest-rate derivatives such as bond options, caps, and swaptions.
