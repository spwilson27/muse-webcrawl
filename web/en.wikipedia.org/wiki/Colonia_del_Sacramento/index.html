Colonia del Sacramento

Colonia del Sacramento (Spanish pronunciation: [koˈlonja ðel sakɾaˈmento]) is a city in southwestern Uruguay, by the Río de la Plata, facing Buenos Aires, Argentina. It is one of the oldest towns in Uruguay and the capital of the Colonia Department. It has a population of about 27,000. Its historic quarter (Barrio Histórico) was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1995.

History

The town was founded in 1680 by Manuel Lobo, the Portuguese governor of Rio de Janeiro, as a fortified outpost — Nova Colônia do Santíssimo Sacramento — on the eastern bank of the Río de la Plata, directly opposite Spanish Buenos Aires. The settlement became a centre for smuggling into Spanish territory and changed hands repeatedly between Portugal and Spain through the 18th century, being finally ceded to Spain by the Treaty of San Ildefonso (1777). It became part of independent Uruguay in the 1820s.

Historic quarter

The old town preserves an irregular, Portuguese-influenced street plan on a small peninsula, including the drawbridge gate (Portón de Campo), fragments of the city walls, the lighthouse built into the ruins of the San Francisco convent, the basilica of the Blessed Sacrament, among the oldest churches in Uruguay, and the cobbled Calle de los Suspiros ("Street of Sighs").

Tourism and transport

Colonia is a popular day trip from Buenos Aires, reached by fast ferries across the Río de la Plata in about an hour, and draws visitors for its museums, Portuguese and Spanish colonial architecture and riverside setting.
