Muawiya I


Gold dinar of the Umayyad Caliphate.

Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan (c. 597, 603 or 605 – April 680), commonly known as Muawiya I, was the founder and first caliph of the Umayyad Caliphate, ruling from 661 until his death. He had previously served as governor of Syria under the caliphs Umar and Uthman.

A member of the Meccan Quraysh clan of Banu Umayya and a late convert to Islam, Mu'awiya became a scribe of Muhammad and took part in the Muslim conquest of Syria, after which he governed the province for some twenty years, building a loyal power base and the first Muslim navy.

Caliphate

After the assassination of Uthman in 656, Mu'awiya refused to recognise Ali as caliph until Uthman's killers were punished, leading to the First Fitna and the inconclusive Battle of Siffin (657). Following Ali's assassination in 661, Mu'awiya gained recognition as caliph and made Damascus his capital, ending the civil war.

As caliph, Mu'awiya consolidated the empire, resumed expansion against the Byzantine Empire — including naval raids and an unsuccessful siege of Constantinople — and established administrative practices drawing on Byzantine and Sasanian precedents. By designating his son Yazid as heir, he transformed the caliphate into a hereditary monarchy, a move controversial in Sunni historiography and condemned in Shia tradition, where he is reviled for his conflict with Ali and the events leading to Karbala.
