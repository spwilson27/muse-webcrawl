Clash of civilizations

The clash of civilizations is a thesis in political science proposed by Samuel P. Huntington, first in a 1993 article in Foreign Affairs and then in his 1996 book The Clash of Civilizations and the Remaking of World Order. Huntington argued that after the Cold War, the dominant source of conflict would no longer be ideology or economics but cultural and religious identity — that the world would divide along the fault lines between large "civilizations".

The thesis

Huntington identified roughly eight major civilizations: Western, Orthodox, Islamic, Sinic (Chinese), Hindu, Japanese, Latin American and (possibly) African. He predicted conflicts along their borders — for example between the West and Islam, and in the Balkans, Ukraine and the South China Sea — while arguing that the West should preserve its own cohesion and avoid intervening in other civilizations' core affairs.

Criticism

The thesis has been widely criticised. Scholars such as Amartya Sen and Edward Said — who wrote a famous rebuttal, "The Clash of Ignorance" — argued that it essentialises cultures, ignores diversity and conflict within civilizations, and risks becoming a self-fulfilling prophecy that licenses hostility toward immigrants and minorities. Empirical studies have found little evidence that civilizational differences predict war better than older explanations. Nevertheless, the phrase "clash of civilizations" became a common framework in commentary after the September 11 attacks.
