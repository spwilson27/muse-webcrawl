Twenty-eight mansions

The twenty-eight mansions (Chinese: 二十八宿, èrshíbā xiù) are the divisions of the ecliptic in traditional Chinese astronomy, corresponding to the positions occupied by the Moon on successive nights of its sidereal cycle. The term "mansion" (宿, xiù, also translated "lodge" or "lunar station") reflects the idea of the Moon lodging each night in a different region of the sky.

This article is written with a lowercase title in this form of the name; see also the capitalised entry Twenty-eight Mansions.

Overview

The 28 mansions run from the Horn mansion (角宿, near Spica in Virgo) westward through the constellations of the zodiac and beyond, each identified by a principal star or small asterism. They are grouped into four "palaces" of seven mansions each, governed by the Azure Dragon (east), Black Tortoise (north), White Tiger (west), and Vermilion Bird (south). Unlike the equal 30-degree signs of the Western zodiac, the mansions vary greatly in width, from under two degrees to over thirty, because they are tied to actual star positions along the Moon's path rather than to an abstract division of the circle.

Origins

The system is very old: the names of all 28 mansions are inscribed on a lacquer clothes chest from the tomb of Marquis Yi of Zeng, sealed in 433 BC, arranged around a diagram of the Big Dipper. Earlier still, oracle bone inscriptions of the Shang dynasty (c. 1200 BC) may record individual mansions such as the Fire star (Antares). Scholars have long compared the mansions with the Indian nakshatra system, which likewise counts 27 or 28 lunar stations, and debated which tradition influenced the other or whether both descend from a common source.

In astrology and daily life

In Chinese almanacs (tong shu), each day is under the influence of one mansion in rotation, with associated lucky and unlucky activities, and an animal sign (such as the Horn Mansion's dragon or the Emptiness Mansion's rat). The mansions also figure in feng shui compass schools and in esoteric Daoism, where they are embodied as 28 generals or spirits. In Japan the system was adopted as the shukuyō astrology of the lunar mansions.
