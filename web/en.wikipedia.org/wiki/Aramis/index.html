Aramis

René d'Herblay, alias Aramis, is a fictional character in the novels The Three Musketeers (1844), Twenty Years After (1845), and The Vicomte de Bragelonne: Ten Years Later (1847–1850) by Alexandre Dumas, père. He and the other two musketeers, Athos and Porthos, are friends of the novel's protagonist, d'Artagnan.

The fictional Aramis is loosely based on the historical musketeer Henri d'Aramitz.

Character

Aramis is depicted as an elegant, vain, and ambitious young man who, despite his career as a musketeer, continually studies theology and dreams of entering the Church. Throughout the trilogy he is torn between his worldly life — duels, intrigues, and love affairs — and his religious vocation. He has a deep fondness for Latin verses, fine clothing, and the company of noble ladies, whose patronage advances his schemes.

Of the four friends, Aramis is the most secretive and politically ambitious. In Twenty Years After he has become a Jesuit and a member of the Fronde conspiracy; by the third novel he has risen to Bishop of Vannes and, ultimately, Superior General of the Jesuits, secretly plotting to replace King Louis XIV with his imprisoned twin brother — the plot that drives the famous "Man in the Iron Mask" storyline.

Historical basis

Henri d'Aramitz (c. 1620 – c. 1655 or 1674) was a Gascon abbé and musketeer in the company of the King's Musketeers. He was a nephew of the company's captain, the Comte de Troisville, and a cousin of the real-life Athos and Porthos. Dumas drew on the pseudo-memoirs of Gatien de Courtilz de Sandras for his characters.

In adaptations

Aramis has been portrayed on screen by many actors, including Robert Warwick, John Gallaudet, Miles Mander, Jacques Toja, Richard Chamberlain, Charlie Sheen in the 1993 film, Jeremy Irons in The Man in the Iron Mask (1998), Luke Evans in the 2011 film, and Santiago Cabrera in the BBC series The Musketeers (2014–2016).
