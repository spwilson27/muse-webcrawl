Badr al-Jamali (Arabic: بدر الجمالي; c. 1015 – 1094) was an Armenian-born Fatimid statesman and military commander who served as vizier of the Fatimid Caliphate from 1074 until his death. Summoned from Acre to rescue the caliphate from civil strife and famine, he restored order in Egypt and became the effective ruler of the state, founding a line of military viziers.

Career

Originally an Armenian slave soldier, Badr rose in Fatimid service in Syria, twice governing Damascus and later Acre. When the Caliph al-Mustansir called him to Egypt in 1073–1074, he arrived with his own troops, eliminated rival military factions, and was appointed vizier with plenary powers as amir al-juyush (commander of the armies).

Rule

Badr suppressed Bedouin and military revolts, restored the irrigation and tax systems after the years of famine known as the great calamity (al-shidda al-uzma), and reasserted Fatimid authority in the Hejaz and parts of Syria. He rebuilt the walls of Cairo, erecting the surviving gates Bab al-Futuh, Bab al-Nasr and Bab Zuweila, and founded the Juyushi Mosque on the Muqattam hills.

Legacy

On his death in 1094 he was succeeded by his son al-Afdal Shahanshah, inaugurating a half-century in which the vizierate, held by military strongmen, eclipsed the caliph in power.
