Anatoly Yevgenyevich Karpov (Russian: Анатолий Евгеньевич Карпов; born 23 May 1951) is a Russian chess grandmaster, former World Chess Champion, and politician. He was the 12th World Chess Champion from 1975 to 1985, a three-time FIDE World Champion (1993, 1996, 1998), and twice World Champion as a member of the Soviet team in the Chess Olympiads.

Career

Karpov learned chess at age four and progressed rapidly, becoming a master at fifteen and winning the World Junior Championship in 1969. He won the 1973 Leningrad Interzonal and the Candidates matches, defeating Lev Polugaevsky, Boris Spassky, and Viktor Korchnoi. When Bobby Fischer declined to defend his title in 1975 under the conditions set, Karpov was declared World Champion by default. He defended the title against Korchnoi in 1978 and 1981, and dominated tournament chess for a decade, amassing one of the largest collections of tournament victories in history.

In 1984–85 Karpov's title match against Garry Kasparov was halted after 48 games without a result; Kasparov won the rematch in 1985. The two contested five world championship matches in one of the greatest rivalries in chess. After the 1993 split in world chess, Karpov regained the FIDE world title and held it until 1999. His playing style is positional and prophylactic, often described as boa-constrictor-like, gradually squeezing opponents without taking risks.

Later life

Karpov has served as a deputy in the Russian State Duma and has been involved in chess promotion and charitable work. His tournament successes, numbering well over 160 first-place finishes, rank him among the most successful players of all time.
