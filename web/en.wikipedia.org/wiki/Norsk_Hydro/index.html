Norsk Hydro ASA is a Norwegian aluminium and renewable energy company headquartered in Oslo. Founded in 1905 by the engineer and industrialist Sam Eyde, the company began by harnessing Norwegian waterfalls to produce artificial fertiliser using the Birkeland–Eyde process for nitrogen fixation. Today Hydro is one of the world's largest aluminium companies, with operations in some 50 countries, and remains a major producer of hydroelectric power.

History

Hydro grew around its plants at Notodden and Rjukan in Telemark, where cheap hydropower supported first fertiliser and later chemical production. The Rjukan plant at Vemork became famous during the Second World War as the site of the German heavy water programme and the Norwegian resistance sabotage operations against it. After the war Hydro expanded into petrochemicals, oil and gas (its petroleum division merged with Statoil in 2007 to form StatoilHydro, now Equinor) and aluminium, acquiring the German company VAW in 2002 and the Brazilian assets of Vale in 2011. Its fertiliser business was demerged in 2004 as Yara International.

Operations

Hydro's business areas cover bauxite and alumina (notably the Alunorte refinery in Brazil, among the world's largest), primary aluminium smelters in Norway and elsewhere, metal recycling, extruded products, and energy — it is Norway's second-largest producer of hydroelectric power, which supplies its energy-intensive smelters.
