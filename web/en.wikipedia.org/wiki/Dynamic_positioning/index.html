Dynamic positioning

Dynamic positioning (DP) is a computer-controlled system to automatically maintain a vessel's position and heading by using its own propellers and thrusters. Position reference sensors, combined with wind sensors, motion sensors and gyrocompasses, provide information to the computer pertaining to the vessel's position and the magnitude and direction of environmental forces affecting its position.

Applications

Examples of vessel types that employ DP include offshore drilling vessels and drillships, floating production storage and offloading (FPSO) units, diving support vessels, pipe-laying and cable-laying vessels, dredgers, shuttle tankers, and some cruise ships and mega yachts.

Classes

The International Maritime Organization publishes equipment class requirements: DP1 (no redundancy), DP2 (redundancy such that no single fault causes loss of position), and DP3 (redundancy plus physical separation, including fire and flood protection). Position references include DGPS, acoustic systems such as USBL and LBL, taut wire, and laser- and radar-based systems such as Fanbeam and Artemis.
