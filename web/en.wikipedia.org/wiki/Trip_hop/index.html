Trip hop

Trip hop is a genre of electronic music that emerged in the early 1990s in Bristol, England — hence its early nickname, the "Bristol sound". It blends hip hop's slow breakbeats and sampling with elements of dub, soul, jazz, ambient, and psychedelic rock, typically producing a slow, atmospheric, and melancholic mood.

History

The genre coalesced around Bristol's Wild Bunch sound system. Massive Attack's Blue Lines (1991) is widely considered its founding album. Portishead's Dummy (1994), built on samples of film scores and torch songs sung by Beth Gibbons, won the Mercury Prize and brought the genre international attention; Tricky's Maxinquaye (1995) added a darker, more fractured sensibility. The term "trip hop" itself, popularised by the music press (notably Mixmag), was disliked by many of the artists involved.

Characteristics and influence

Typical trip hop tracks use downtempo beats (around 80–100 bpm), vinyl crackle and sampled loops, deep bass, and sparse, often female, vocals. Its influence spread widely in the 1990s and beyond, touching artists such as Björk, Radiohead, Gorillaz, UNKLE, and Morcheeba, and leaving a lasting mark on downtempo, chill-out, and film and television scoring.
