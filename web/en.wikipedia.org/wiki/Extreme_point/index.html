Extreme point

Extreme point
FieldConvex analysis, functional analysis
Related conceptsConvex set, convex combination, Krein–Milman theorem

In mathematics, an extreme point of a convex set S in a real or complex vector space is a point in S that does not lie in any open line segment joining two points of S. Equivalently, a point x in S is extreme if it cannot be written as a proper convex combination x = ty + (1 − t)z with y, z in S, y ≠ z, and 0 < t < 1. Intuitively, the extreme points of a convex body are its "corners": the vertices of a polygon or polyhedron are extreme points, and every point of the boundary of a disk is an extreme point of the disk.

Properties

The set of extreme points of a convex set need not itself be convex, and it may be empty for unbounded sets without suitable compactness. The fundamental result of the theory is the Krein–Milman theorem, which states that a compact convex set in a locally convex topological vector space is the closed convex hull of its extreme points; in particular, every nonempty compact convex set has at least one extreme point. A finite-dimensional counterpart is Minkowski's theorem: a compact convex set in finite dimensions is the convex hull of its extreme points.

Applications

Extreme points play a central role in optimization: a linear functional on a compact convex set attains its maximum at an extreme point, which is the geometric basis of the simplex method in linear programming, where optima occur at vertices of the feasible polytope. In Choquet theory, points of a compact convex set are represented as averages (barycenters) of probability measures concentrated on the extreme points, generalizing the Krein–Milman picture.
