Salutation

A salutation is a gesture or utterance used to greet another person or to acknowledge their presence or departure. Salutations vary widely across cultures and situations and may be verbal (such as "hello", "good morning", or "shalom"), physical (such as a handshake, bow, wave, hug or salute), or written (such as the opening line of a letter, "Dear Sir").

Verbal salutations

Many languages have conventional greeting formulas that often invoke health, peace or divine blessing: Latin salve ("be well!", from which "salutation" derives), Hebrew shalom and Arabic as-salāmu ʿalaykum ("peace be upon you"), Hindi namaste, Japanese konnichiwa, and French bonjour. Greetings may change with the time of day and the relative status of the speakers, and leave-takings ("goodbye", from "God be with ye") serve as closing salutations.

Gestures

Common physical salutations include the handshake in much of the Western world; bowing in East Asia, where depth conveys respect; the placing of palms together in South and Southeast Asia; cheek kissing in parts of Europe and Latin America; and the military salute, a formal gesture of respect in armed forces. Waves, nods and raised hands are informal everyday greetings. During the COVID-19 pandemic, elbow bumps and other contact-free greetings gained popularity in place of handshakes.

In writing

In correspondence, the salutation opens the letter ("Dear Ms Smith,") and is matched by a complimentary close ("Yours sincerely", "Yours faithfully", "Regards"). Valedictorians at graduations deliver a salutatory address — in U.S. schools, the student ranked second in the class is the salutatorian, traditionally charged with greeting the audience.

Religious salutations

Salutations feature in religious practice: the Angelic Salutation is the greeting of the Angel Gabriel to Mary ("Hail, full of grace"), forming the first part of the Hail Mary prayer; the phrase "salutation" in the epistles of the New Testament refers to greetings sent between early Christian communities.
