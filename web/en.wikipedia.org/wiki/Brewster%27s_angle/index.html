Brewster's angle

Brewster's angle (also known as the polarisation angle) is the angle of incidence at which light with a particular polarisation is perfectly transmitted through a transparent dielectric surface, with no reflection. Light of the perpendicular polarisation is partially reflected, so reflected light at this angle is fully polarised parallel to the surface.

Relation

For light passing from a medium of refractive index n₁ to one of n₂, Brewster's angle θ_B satisfies tan θ_B = n₂ / n₁. It is named after the Scottish physicist Sir David Brewster, who described it in 1815.

Applications

Brewster-angle windows are used in gas lasers to minimise reflection losses, and the effect underlies polarising filters in photography.
