Rösti or rööschti (Swiss German: [ˈrøːʃti]) is a Swiss dish consisting mainly of potatoes, originally a farmers' breakfast from the canton of Bern, but now much more widely consumed all over Switzerland and around the world. Many Swiss people consider rösti a national dish. Rather than considering it a complete breakfast, lunch or dinner, it is more commonly served to accompany other dishes such as Spinat und Spiegelei (spinach and fried eggs, sunny side up), cervelas or Fleischkäse. It is also a standard accompaniment to the dish Zürcher Geschnetzeltes.

Preparation

Rösti dishes are made with coarsely grated potato, either parboiled or raw. Depending on regional or personal preference, the grated potatoes are mixed with salt, and the mixture is fried in a hot pan with a liberal quantity of butter, oil or other fat. It is often formed into a round cake the size of the frying pan. Although the basic rösti consists of nothing but potato, a number of additional ingredients are sometimes added, such as bacon, onions, cheese, apples or fresh herbs. This is usually considered a regional touch.

Cultural impact

The name derives from the German word rösten, meaning "to roast". The cultural boundary between German-speaking and French-speaking Switzerland is sometimes jokingly referred to as the Röstigraben ("rösti ditch"), a play on the supposed differences in mentality between the two language regions.

Related dishes

Similar dishes include the Jewish latke, the American hash brown and the French pommes soufflées. Cooked leftover potatoes may also be used in dishes such as Gschwellti.
