Salpingostomy

Salpingostomy
Type: Surgical procedure (gynaecology)
Site: Fallopian tube
Indications: Ectopic pregnancy; tubal blockage (hydrosalpinx)

Salpingostomy is a surgical procedure in which an opening is created in a fallopian tube. It is performed either to treat an ectopic pregnancy located in the tube — removing the products of conception through a linear incision while preserving the tube (sometimes termed salpingotomy in this context) — or to relieve a blockage at the fimbrial end of the tube, as in a hydrosalpinx, in an attempt to restore fertility.

Comparison with salpingectomy

Unlike salpingectomy, which removes the affected tube, salpingostomy preserves it, keeping open the possibility of future natural conception. However, it carries a risk of persistent trophoblast after ectopic pregnancy and of the tube re-occluding, so follow-up with serial hCG measurement is recommended after treatment of ectopic pregnancy.

Technique

The procedure is usually performed laparoscopically. For distal tubal blockage, the closed end of the tube is opened and the edges everted (neosalpingostomy) to form a new ostium.
