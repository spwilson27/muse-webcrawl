Saltoro Kangri

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[hero image: Saltoro Kangri]
The highest peak of the Saltoro Mountains

Saltoro Kangri (also known as K10) is the highest peak of the Saltoro Mountains, a subrange of the Karakoram in the Siachen region near the India–Pakistan Line of Control. With an elevation of 7,742 metres (25,400 ft) and a prominence of about 2,160 metres, it ranks among the highest mountains on Earth, standing southeast of Gasherbrum and overlooking the Siachen Glacier.

Climbing history

The mountain was reconnoitred in 1911–12 by the explorer Filippo De Filippi's party and the Bullock Workman expedition. The first ascent was made on 24 July 1962 by a Japanese–Pakistani expedition led by Tsunahiko Shidei; the summit team of Atsuo Saito, Yasuo Takamura, and the Pakistani climber Rai Karim climbed the southeast ridge. Later ascents were hampered by the militarisation of the Siachen conflict from 1984, which made the area effectively closed to civilian climbers.

Geography and politics

Saltoro Kangri lies west of the Siachen Glacier, along the Actual Ground Position Line separating Indian- and Pakistani-controlled territory in the disputed region of Kashmir. The Saltoro ridge gives its name to the military standoff over Siachen, in which the peak and its neighbouring heights such as Bilafond La and Sia La are of strategic significance. Because of the dispute, sovereignty over the mountain is contested between India and Pakistan.
