Libertarian Party (United States)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Libertarian Party (LP) is a political party in the United States that promotes civil liberties, non-interventionism, Laissez-faire capitalism and limiting the size and scope of government. Founded on 11 December 1971 in Colorado Springs, Colorado, it is generally the third-largest political party in the United States by membership and ballot access, and has elected candidates to state legislatures and local offices though never to Congress.

The party's first presidential ticket, John Hospers and Tonie Nathan (1972), won an electoral vote from a faithless Republican elector in Virginia — making Nathan the first woman to receive an electoral vote for vice president. Its best-known presidential candidates include Ron Paul (1988), the former Republican congressman, and Gary Johnson (2012, 2016), the former governor of New Mexico, whose 2016 campaign won about 3.3 per cent of the national vote, the party's strongest showing. The party's platform applies the Non-aggression principle across policy: free markets, drug decriminalisation, gun rights, open immigration in most formulations, and a non-interventionist foreign policy.

Organisation and factions

The LP is organised by state affiliates with a national committee and biennial conventions; its internal life features recurring contests between pragmatist and radical (“abolitionist”) wings over how uncompromisingly to state libertarian principle. A founding peculiarity is the membership pledge, requiring signatories to certify that they do not believe in or advocate the initiation of force to achieve political or social goals.
