Whitewater rafting on the River Tryweryn

Canolfan Tryweryn is the National White Water Centre for Wales, located on the River Tryweryn near Bala in Gwynedd, north-west Wales. It is one of the United Kingdom's premier venues for whitewater rafting, kayaking and canoeing, and the home of Canoe Wales' slalom and rafting programmes.

The river

The River Tryweryn is a dam-controlled river: water is released from the Llyn Celyn reservoir, a short distance upstream, providing reliable whitewater conditions even during dry summer periods when natural rivers run low. The upper section used by the centre offers roughly 2 km of continuous grade III–IV rapids with features such as the Graveyard, Ski Jump and Chapel Falls.

Activities

The centre offers guided rafting trips, kayak and canoe coaching, safety and rescue courses, and hosts national competitions. Releases are scheduled in coordination with the reservoir operator, and the published release calendar draws paddlers from across the UK.

Setting

The Tryweryn valley carries historical significance: the flooding of the valley's village of Capel Celyn in 1965 to create Llyn Celyn reservoir for Liverpool's water supply became a landmark event in Welsh nationalism, remembered by the slogan Cofiwch Dryweryn ("Remember Tryweryn").
