Dame Elizabeth Rosemond Taylor (February 27, 1932 – March 23, 2011) was a British and American actress. She began her career as a child actress in the early 1940s and was one of the most popular stars of classical Hollywood cinema in the 1950s. She then became the world's highest-paid movie star in the 1960s, remaining a well-known public figure for the rest of her life. In 1999, the American Film Institute named her the seventh-greatest female screen legend of classic Hollywood cinema.

Career

Born in London to socially prominent American parents, Taylor moved with her family to Los Angeles in 1939. She made her acting debut with a minor role in the comedy There's One Born Every Minute (1942), but her first success was playing a young woman who trains her horse for the Grand National in the film National Velvet (1944). As an adult actress, she starred in Father of the Bride (1950), A Place in the Sun (1951), and Giant (1956). She won Academy Awards for Best Actress for BUtterfield 8 (1960) and for playing Martha in Who's Afraid of Virginia Woolf? (1966). For Cleopatra (1963) she became the first movie star to be paid $1 million for a single role.

Personal life and philanthropy

Taylor's private life — eight marriages to seven men, including two to Richard Burton — attracted intense press coverage throughout her life. After the death of her friend Rock Hudson, she became a pioneering AIDS activist, co-founding the American Foundation for AIDS Research (amfAR) in 1985 and the Elizabeth Taylor AIDS Foundation in 1991. She also built a successful business empire with fragrance brands including White Diamonds.

Death

Taylor died of congestive heart failure on March 23, 2011, at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center in Los Angeles, aged 79.
