Social and emotional learning (SEL) is the process through which children and adults acquire and apply the knowledge, skills, and attitudes needed to understand and manage emotions, set and achieve positive goals, feel and show empathy for others, establish and maintain positive relationships, and make responsible decisions. The term was coined in the 1990s and popularised by the Collaborative for Academic, Social, and Emotional Learning (CASEL), founded in 1994.

Framework

CASEL's framework organises SEL into five competency clusters: self-awareness (recognising one's emotions and values), self-management (regulating emotions and behaviours), social awareness (empathy and perspective-taking), relationship skills (communication, cooperation, and conflict resolution), and responsible decision-making. Programmes are delivered in classrooms, school-wide climates, families, and communities, and are intended to be embedded in everyday teaching rather than taught only as stand-alone lessons.

Evidence

A widely cited 2011 meta-analysis by Durlak and colleagues, covering 213 school-based programmes and more than 270,000 students, found that SEL participants showed improved social-emotional skills, attitudes, and behaviour, and an 11-percentile-point gain in academic achievement compared with controls. Later meta-analyses have reported smaller and more variable effects, prompting debate about programme quality, implementation fidelity, and the danger of overclaiming.

History and policy

SEL grew out of work on emotional intelligence (popularised by Daniel Goleman's 1995 book) and prevention science. In the United States, several states have adopted free-standing SEL standards or competencies for K–12 education, and SEL language appears in the federal Every Student Succeeds Act (2015). Internationally, SEL-like frameworks are promoted by UNESCO and the OECD.

Criticism

Critics argue that SEL can be vaguely defined, that assessment of students' emotions raises privacy concerns, and that classroom time might be better spent on academic instruction. Since about 2021, SEL has also become politically contested in parts of the United States, where some opponents associate it with critical race theory or view it as values instruction that belongs to families rather than schools; supporters respond that the competencies are non-partisan life skills.
