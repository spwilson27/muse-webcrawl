Plot (narrative)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In a literary work, film, or other narrative, the plot is the sequence of events in which each event affects the next one through the principle of cause-and-effect. The causal events of a plot can be thought of simply as a series of events linked by the connector "and so". Plots can vary from the simple structure of a fairy tale — a sequence such as "the king died and then the queen died" — to complex interwoven structures. The term was introduced by Aristotle in his Poetics (c. 335 BCE), where plot (mythos) is ranked the most important element of tragedy.

The novelist E. M. Forster famously distinguished plot from story: a story is "a narrative of events arranged in their time-sequence", whereas a plot adds causality — "the king died and then the queen died" is a story; "the king died, and then the queen died of grief" is a plot.

Structure

Freytag's pyramid

The German playwright and novelist Gustav Freytag, in Die Technik des Dramas (1863), proposed a five-part structure sometimes drawn as a pyramid: exposition, rising action, climax, falling action, and dénouement (resolution/catastrophe). The exposition introduces background and characters; rising action builds complications toward the climax, the turning point; falling action unwinds the consequences; and the dénouement resolves the conflict.

Three-act structure

Screenwriting commonly divides plot into three acts: the setup, the confrontation, and the resolution, separated by plot points that spin the action in a new direction. Related frameworks include the hero's journey (monomyth) popularized by Joseph Campbell and Christopher Vogler, and the seven basic plots catalogued by Christopher Booker.

Elements

Plot is conventionally analysed in terms of conflict (protagonist versus antagonist, society, nature, or self), inciting incident, complications, climax, and resolution. Devices that manipulate the order or revelation of plot events include flashbacks (analepsis), flashforwards (prolepsis), foreshadowing, in medias res openings, plot twists, cliffhangers, and the deus ex machina. A subplot is a secondary strand of events that supports or contrasts with the main plot.

Plot and other narrative elements

Plot is one element of narrative alongside setting, character, theme, and style. In Aristotle's account, plot takes priority over character because drama is an imitation of action. Later critics, particularly modernists and proponents of character-driven fiction, have disputed this hierarchy, but the causal arrangement of events remains the standard definition of plot in narratology.
