Vichy (French pronunciation: [viʃi]) is a commune in the Allier department of central France, on the river Allier. A celebrated spa town since Roman times, it became known worldwide as the seat of the French government of Marshal Philippe Pétain during the Second World War — "Vichy France" — and its historic spa quarter is now part of a UNESCO World Heritage listing of the Great Spa Towns of Europe.

The spa town

Vichy's alkaline mineral waters were known to the Romans, and the town flowered in the 19th century under Napoleon III, who visited repeatedly and endowed it with parks, a casino, an opera house and grand villas. Visitors came to "take the waters" for liver and digestive complaints, and bottled Vichy water and the town's pastilles became famous exports. The Hall des Sources, with its celebrated drinking fountain under a glass canopy, remains emblematic of the belle époque spa.

Vichy France

After the armistice of June 1940, the French government moved to Vichy in the unoccupied zone, and in July 1940 the National Assembly voted full powers to Marshal Pétain, ending the Third Republic. The "French State" headquartered at Vichy collaborated with Nazi Germany, enacted antisemitic statutes, and after the German occupation of the southern zone in November 1942 became progressively a German client. The government fled in 1944 as France was liberated. The town's name remains synonymous in French political vocabulary with collaboration — a legacy the modern spa town has had to reckon with.

Today

Vichy is a centre for thermal medicine, cosmetics (the Vichy Laboratoires brand derives its name from the town) and sport, hosting major triathlon and equestrian events. In 2021 its spa architecture joined the transnational UNESCO listing "The Great Spa Towns of Europe".
