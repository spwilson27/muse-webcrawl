Underground home

An underground home is a house built partially or entirely below ground level, or covered ("bermed") with earth on some sides. Earth-sheltered housing is an ancient practice, exemplified by cave dwellings, pit houses, and the underground villages of Cappadocia, and it enjoyed a revival of interest during and after the 1970s energy crisis as a form of energy-efficient housing.

Design

Common designs include earth-bermed walls with one exposed glazed facade, atrium or courtyard plans that admit light and air to below-grade rooms, and fully underground structures lit by skylights. The surrounding earth acts as insulation and thermal mass, damping daily and seasonal temperature swings, protecting the structure from storms, and reducing noise.

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages claimed for underground homes include lower heating and cooling costs, durability, reduced maintenance of exterior surfaces, and minimal visual impact on the landscape. Disadvantages include high construction cost, the need for careful waterproofing and drainage, risks of condensation and poor air quality without mechanical ventilation, limited natural light, and problems of resale, financing, and building-code compliance in some jurisdictions. Notable examples include Bill Gates' earth-sheltered mansion on Lake Washington and the hobbit-like earth houses built in Switzerland by Peter Vetsch.
