Joan Benoit Samuelson (born May 16, 1957) is an American marathon runner who won the gold medal at the 1984 Summer Olympics in Los Angeles, the first Olympic Women's Marathon. She also won the Boston Marathon twice, in 1979 and 1983, setting course and world records.

Benoit, from Cape Elizabeth, Maine, began running to recover from a skiing injury. While a student at Bowdoin College she became one of the leading American distance runners. Her 1979 Boston victory, run while relatively unknown, announced her arrival; in 1983 she set a world marathon record of 2:22:43 in Boston.

1984 Olympics

At the Los Angeles Games, in the first marathon held for women at an Olympics, Benoit broke away early and won in 2:24:52, defeating a field that included Grete Waitz and Ingrid Kristiansen. Her victory is regarded as a landmark moment in women's sport.

Later career

Benoit set an American marathon record of 2:21:21 in Chicago in 1985, a mark that stood for 18 years. She continued competing at an elite masters level for decades, and founded the Beach to Beacon 10K race in Maine.
