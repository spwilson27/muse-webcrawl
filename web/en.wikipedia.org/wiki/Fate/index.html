Fate

ConceptPredetermined course of events
Related ideasDestiny, providence, determinism, karma

Fate is the idea that the course of events is predetermined by a supernatural power or an impersonal cosmic order, beyond the control of the individuals it affects. Belief in fate appears in many religions and philosophies, where it is often contrasted with free will.

In ancient thought

In Greek mythology, fate was governed by the three Moirai — Clotho, who spun the thread of life; Lachesis, who measured it; and Atropos, who cut it. Even the gods were in some accounts subject to fate. The Romans adopted the trio as the Parcae and personified fate as Fatum. Norse mythology had the Norns weaving destinies at the well of Urd, and Anglo-Saxon tradition knew fate as wyrd. Stoic philosophy identified fate with a rational, providential ordering of the universe.

In religion

Various traditions grapple with fate and divine foreknowledge: Islamic theology debates qadar (divine decree) against human responsibility; Hindu and Buddhist thought link one's circumstances to karma, the accumulated consequences of past action; Calvinist Christianity teaches predestination. Chinese thought speaks of the Mandate of Heaven and ming, the allotted lot of a life.

Fate and destiny

The words "fate" and "destiny" are often used interchangeably, though some writers distinguish fate as an unavoidable, often negative outcome and destiny as a course that can be shaped by choices. The tension between fate and free will remains a central theme in literature from Oedipus Rex to modern fantasy.
