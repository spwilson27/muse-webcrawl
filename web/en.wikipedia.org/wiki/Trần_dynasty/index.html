Trần dynasty

The Trần dynasty ruled Vietnam (Đại Việt) from 1225 to 1400. It succeeded the Lý dynasty when the child empress Lý Chiêu Hoàng was induced to abdicate in favour of her husband Trần Cảnh (Emperor Trần Thái Tông), in an arrangement engineered by the courtier Trần Thủ Độ.

Mongol invasions

The dynasty is most famous for repelling three Mongol invasions (1258, 1285, and 1287–88) by the Yuan dynasty. Under the strategic leadership of Prince Trần Hưng Đạo, Vietnamese forces used scorched-earth tactics, guerrilla warfare, and finally a naval victory at the Battle of Bạch Đằng (1288), where stakes embedded in the river bed destroyed the Yuan fleet at low tide — a feat celebrated as one of Vietnam's greatest military triumphs.

Culture and decline

The Trần era saw flourishing Buddhism, the development of chữ Nôm scholarship, and major historical writing such as the Đại Việt sử ký. From the late 14th century the dynasty weakened through court intrigues and conflict with Champa; in 1400 Hồ Quý Ly usurped the throne, ending the dynasty and briefly founding the Hồ dynasty before Ming China's invasion of 1407.
