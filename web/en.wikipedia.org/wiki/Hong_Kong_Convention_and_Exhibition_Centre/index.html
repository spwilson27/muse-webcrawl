Hong Kong Convention and Exhibition Centre

Hong Kong Convention and Exhibition Centre
Location: Wan Chai, Hong Kong Island
Opened: November 1988
Expansion: 1997
Operator: HKCEC (Management) Ltd.
Owner: Hong Kong Trade Development Council / Hong Kong SAR Government

The Hong Kong Convention and Exhibition Centre (HKCEC) is one of the two major convention and exhibition venues in Hong Kong, along with AsiaWorld-Expo. Located in Wan Chai North on Victoria Harbour, its distinctive curved, wing-like roof is a landmark of the Hong Kong skyline. It was built on reclaimed land and opened in 1988; a major extension onto an artificial island was completed in 1997.

History

The original building was constructed on the site of the old Gloucester Road reclamation and opened in November 1988. The HK$4.8 billion extension, designed with a dramatic curved roof resembling a seabird in flight (nicknamed the "turtle" locally), was completed in time for the handover of Hong Kong on 30 June–1 July 1997: the handover ceremony itself took place in the new wing's Grand Hall. The Golden Bauhinia statue, a gift from the central government, stands in the square outside. A further expansion completed in 2009 added exhibition space by linking the two phases with an atrium over the harbour channel.

Events

The HKCEC hosts trade fairs organised by the Hong Kong Trade Development Council, international conferences (including the 2005 WTO Ministerial Conference, held largely here), concerts, and exhibitions such as Art Basel Hong Kong and the Hong Kong Book Fair.
