Vicente Fernández

Born17 February 1940, Huentitán El Alto, Jalisco, Mexico
Died12 December 2021, Guadalajara, Jalisco, Mexico
GenresRanchera, mariachi
OccupationsSinger, actor, film producer
Nicknames"Chente", "El Ídolo del Pueblo", "El Charro de Huentitán"

Vicente Fernández Gómez (17 February 1940 – 12 December 2021) was a Mexican singer, actor and film producer. Known as the foremost living exponent of ranchera music for much of his career, he was nicknamed "Chente" and "El Ídolo del Pueblo" ("The Idol of the People") and is regarded as a cultural icon in Mexico.

Career

Fernández grew up in poverty in Jalisco and began singing in restaurants and at weddings in Guadalajara and Mexico City. After signing with CBS Mexico in 1966, he built a recording career that eventually spanned more than 100 albums and some 40 films, mostly ranchera-themed melodramas. His signature songs include "Volver, Volver", "El Rey", "Por Tu Maldito Amor" and "Estos Celos". He received three Grammy Awards and eight Latin Grammy Awards, and sold tens of millions of records worldwide.

Later life and legacy

Fernández retired from live performance in 2016 with a farewell concert at the Estadio Azteca, though he continued recording. His son Alejandro Fernández is also a leading ranchera and pop singer. Vicente Fernández died in December 2021, aged 81, from complications after a fall, and his death prompted national mourning in Mexico.
