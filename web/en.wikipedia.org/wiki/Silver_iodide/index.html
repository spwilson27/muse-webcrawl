Silver iodide

Chemical formulaAgI
Molar mass234.77 g/mol
AppearanceYellow crystalline solid
Density5.68 g/cm³
Melting point558 °C

Silver iodide is an inorganic compound with the formula AgI. The compound is a bright yellow solid, but samples almost always contain impurities of metallic silver that give a grey colouration. It is practically insoluble in water and is highly photosensitive, darkening on exposure to light.

Structure

The structure adopted by silver iodide depends on temperature. Below 147 °C it exists primarily in a hexagonal wurtzite-like form known as β-AgI. Above this temperature it converts to the α phase, in which the iodide ions form a body-centred cubic lattice while the silver ions are disordered and highly mobile, making α-AgI a superionic conductor of silver ions. This property has made silver iodide a model system in the study of fast ion conductors and a component of solid-state batteries and sensors.

Preparation and reactions

Silver iodide can be prepared by reaction of an iodide solution with silver ions, for example by mixing potassium iodide with silver nitrate; the yellow precipitate of AgI forms immediately. Unlike silver chloride and silver bromide, silver iodide does not dissolve appreciably in concentrated ammonia solution, a distinction used in qualitative analysis of halides.

Uses

The most widespread application of silver iodide is in cloud seeding for weather modification. The hexagonal crystal structure of β-AgI closely resembles that of ice, so particles of silver iodide act as effective ice nuclei in supercooled clouds, promoting the formation of ice crystals and, under suitable conditions, rain or snow. Silver iodide is dispersed from aircraft or ground-based generators.

Silver iodide has also been used in photography (as a light-sensitive halide in early processes), as an antiseptic in medicine, and in electrochemical devices exploiting its ionic conductivity.
