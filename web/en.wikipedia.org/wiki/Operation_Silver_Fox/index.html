Operation Silver Fox (German: Unternehmen Silberfuchs) was a German–Finnish military operation on the northern flank of the Eastern Front in 1941, forming part of the Continuation War. Its goal was to capture the Soviet port of Murmansk, the only ice-free northern port receiving Allied supplies.

Plan and course

The operation comprised subordinate offensives — Platinum Fox toward Murmansk by the Mountain Corps Norway, and Arctic Fox toward Kandalaksha by the German XXXVI Corps and Finnish III Corps — launched from late June 1941. German forces, ill-equipped for roadless tundra and supplied over enormous distances, advanced slowly against determined Soviet defence and were halted well short of the Murmansk railway.

Outcome

By November 1941 the offensives were abandoned. Murmansk remained in Soviet hands throughout the war, and Arctic convoys continued to deliver Lend-Lease supplies to the Soviet Union, making Silver Fox one of Germany's clearest strategic failures of the 1941 campaign.
