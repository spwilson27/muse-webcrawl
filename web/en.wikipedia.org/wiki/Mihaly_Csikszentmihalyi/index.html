Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi

Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi (Hungarian: Csíkszentmihályi Mihály; 29 September 1934 – 20 October 2021) was a Hungarian-American psychologist best known as the architect of the concept of flow — the highly focused mental state of complete absorption in an activity. He was a founder of positive psychology and taught for many years at the University of Chicago and later at Claremont Graduate University.

Early life

Born in Fiume to a Hungarian diplomatic family, Csikszentmihalyi spent part of his childhood in wartime Italy, an experience he credited with sparking his interest in how people find meaning amid chaos. He emigrated to the United States at 22 and studied psychology at the University of Chicago, earning his PhD in 1965.

Flow

Through interviews with artists, athletes, chess players, and surgeons — later supplemented by the experience sampling method, in which subjects recorded their feelings at random beeper prompts — Csikszentmihalyi identified a common state of optimal experience occurring when a person's skill is balanced against a high level of challenge. Flow (1990) presented this research to a general audience and became a bestseller; Csikszentmihalyi went on to explore flow in creativity (Creativity, 1996), work, and adolescence.

Positive psychology

Together with Martin Seligman, Csikszentmihalyi helped launch positive psychology as a formal movement — Seligman's 1998 APA presidency and their joint 2000 article in American Psychologist marked its arrival — shifting attention from mental illness toward the study of happiness, strengths, and flourishing. He co-founded the Quality of Life Research Center at Claremont and was a noted adviser on education.
