Samuel Taylor Coleridge

Samuel Taylor Coleridge (21 October 1772 – 25 July 1834) was an English poet, literary critic, philosopher and theologian who, with his friend William Wordsworth, was a founder of the Romantic Movement in England and a member of the Lake Poets.

Samuel Taylor Coleridge
Born: 21 October 1772, Ottery St Mary, Devon
Died: 25 July 1834, Highgate, London
Occupation: Poet, critic, philosopher
Notable works: The Rime of the Ancient Mariner, Kubla Khan, Christabel

Life

Born in Devon, the son of a vicar, Coleridge studied at Jesus College, Cambridge. He planned with Robert Southey a utopian community, Pantisocracy, in America, which was never realised. In 1795 he married Sara Fricker.

Poetry

With Wordsworth he published Lyrical Ballads (1798), which helped launch English Romanticism. It includes The Rime of the Ancient Mariner. His "conversation poems" include Frost at Midnight and This Lime-Tree Bower My Prison. Kubla Khan (written 1797, published 1816) is a famous fragment, composed, he claimed, after an opium-influenced dream.

Criticism and prose

Biographia Literaria (1817) is his major prose work, containing his theory of the imagination — primary and secondary imagination, and fancy — and influential Shakespeare criticism. He coined many phrases, including "suspension of disbelief".

Later years

Coleridge struggled for years with ill health and opium addiction. He spent his final years at Highgate under the care of Dr James Gillman, writing and dictating.
