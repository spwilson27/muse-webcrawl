The Second Bulgarian Empire was a medieval Bulgarian state that existed between 1185 and 1396. It was established by the uprising of the brothers Peter and Asen against the Byzantine Empire, restoring Bulgarian statehood after nearly two centuries of Byzantine rule.

History

Tsar Kaloyan secured papal recognition and defeated the Latin Crusaders at Adrianople (1205), capturing Emperor Baldwin I. Under Ivan Asen II, victory over Epirus at Klokotnitza (1230) made Bulgaria the dominant Balkan power, stretching between the Black, Aegean, and Adriatic seas. Tarnovo became a major cultural and Orthodox centre, and the Bulgarian Patriarchate was recognized in 1235.

Decline

After Ivan Asen II's death, Mongol pressure, civil wars, and Serbian and Byzantine rivalry weakened the empire. It fragmented among rival tsars in Tarnovo, Vidin, and Dobruja. The Ottomans captured Tarnovo in 1393 and Vidin in 1396, ending the empire.

Culture

The Tarnovo Literary School and distinctive art and architecture flourished in the 14th century under Ivan Alexander, including illuminated manuscripts such as the Tetraevangelia of Ivan Alexander.
