IEEE 802.3

Working group and standards collection defining Ethernet

Ethernet cabling in a network installation

IEEE 802.3 is a working group and the collection of standards it produces that define the physical layer and data link layer's media access control (MAC) for wired Ethernet. First published in 1983 as IEEE 802.3, based on the Ethernet technology developed at Xerox PARC by Robert Metcalfe and colleagues and promoted by the DIX consortium (DEC, Intel, Xerox), it has been amended dozens of times as speeds have risen.

Evolution

The standard began at 10 Mbit/s over coaxial cable (10BASE5, 10BASE2) and twisted pair (10BASE-T), and progressed through Fast Ethernet (100 Mbit/s), Gigabit Ethernet, and 10, 25, 40, 100, 200 and 400 Gbit/s variants over copper and optical fibre. Power over Ethernet (PoE, 802.3af/at/bt) is also part of the family. Although amendments once had their own identities, they are periodically rolled into consolidated editions of the base standard.

Legacy

Ethernet defined by 802.3 became the dominant wired local area network technology worldwide, displacing rivals such as Token Ring.
