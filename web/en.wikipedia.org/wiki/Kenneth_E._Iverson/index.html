Kenneth E. Iverson

Kenneth Eugene Iverson (17 December 1920 – 19 October 2004) was a Canadian computer scientist noted for the development of the programming language APL. In 1979 he received the Turing Award for his pioneering effort in programming languages and mathematical notation, and for his contributions to interactive systems and APL implementations.

Career

Iverson earned a PhD in applied mathematics from Harvard University in 1954 and taught there before joining IBM in 1960. At IBM he developed his notation into the APL programming language, described in his 1962 book A Programming Language. APL's array-oriented style and compact symbol set influenced later array languages.

J language

In later life, working with Roger Hui, Iverson developed the J programming language, an ASCII-only successor to APL that retained its array-processing power without requiring a special character set.
