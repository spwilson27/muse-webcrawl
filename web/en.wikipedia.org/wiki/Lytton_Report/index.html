SubjectMukden Incident and the Japanese invasion of Manchuria

The Lytton Report was the report of a commission of inquiry appointed by the League of Nations to investigate the Mukden Incident of September 1931 and Japan's subsequent occupation of Manchuria. The commission was chaired by Victor Bulwer-Lytton, 2nd Earl of Lytton, and included members from France, Germany, Italy and the United States.

Findings

Published on 2 October 1932, the report concluded that the Japanese military operations of 18–19 September 1931 could not be regarded as legitimate self-defence, and that the new state of Manchukuo, proclaimed by Japan, was not the product of a genuine independence movement but had been created by the Japanese army and officials. The commission nevertheless recognised Japan's special interests in the region and recommended that Manchuria become autonomous under Chinese sovereignty, with an international advisory role.

Consequences

The League Assembly adopted the report in February 1933 and refused to recognise Manchukuo. Japan responded by announcing its withdrawal from the League of Nations, which took effect in 1935. The episode exposed the League's inability to restrain aggression by a major power and is often cited as a key step on the road to the Second World War.
