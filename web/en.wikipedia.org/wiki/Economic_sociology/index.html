Economic sociology

Economic sociology is the study of the social causes and effects of economic phenomena. It examines how economic exchange is embedded in social relations, institutions, culture, and power.

Classical foundations

Karl Marx, Max Weber, Émile Durkheim, Georg Simmel, and Karl Polanyi laid foundations. Weber's The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism and Polanyi's The Great Transformation (1944) with its concept of embeddedness are central.

New economic sociology

Revived in the 1980s, notably by Mark Granovetter's 1985 paper on embeddedness and the strength of weak ties, the field studies markets as networks, organizations, money's social meanings (Viviana Zelizer), and cultural shaping of economies (performativity).

Topics

Research covers firms, labor markets, finance, consumption, economic development, and varieties of capitalism.
