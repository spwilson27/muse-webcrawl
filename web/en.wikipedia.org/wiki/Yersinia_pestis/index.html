Yersinia pestis

Scientific classification
Domain: Bacteria
Phylum: Pseudomonadota
Class: Gammaproteobacteria
Order: Enterobacterales
Family: Yersiniaceae
Genus: Yersinia
Species: Y. pestis

Yersinia pestis is a gram-negative, non-motile, coccobacillus bacterium in the family Yersiniaceae. It is the infectious agent responsible for plague, a disease that has caused several of the deadliest pandemics in recorded history, including the Black Death of the 14th century, the Plague of Justinian in the 6th century, and the Third Plague Pandemic that began in China in the 19th century.

Discovery

The bacterium was discovered in 1894 during a plague outbreak in Hong Kong by Alexandre Yersin, a Swiss-French physician and bacteriologist working for the Pasteur Institute. It was initially named Pasteurella pestis and later renamed in Yersin's honour. The Japanese bacteriologist Kitasato Shibasaburō independently identified a bacterium during the same outbreak, though Yersin's description was ultimately credited.

Transmission and disease

Y. pestis is primarily a pathogen of rodents and is transmitted to humans most commonly through the bite of infected fleas, especially the oriental rat flea (Xenopsylla cheopis). Depending on the route of infection, plague manifests in three principal forms: bubonic plague, characterised by swollen lymph nodes called buboes; septicemic plague, in which the bacteria multiply in the bloodstream; and pneumonic plague, a lung infection that can spread directly between people through respiratory droplets.

Virulence

The bacterium carries several plasmids encoding virulence factors, including a type III secretion system (the Ysc injectisome) that delivers Yop effector proteins into host immune cells, disabling phagocytosis and inflammatory responses. Other factors include the F1 capsular antigen and the plasminogen activator Pla. Untreated bubonic plague has a high fatality rate, but the disease responds well to antibiotics such as streptomycin, gentamicin, and doxycycline when treated promptly.

Genomics and evolution

The genome of Y. pestis is about 4.65 million base pairs. Genetic studies indicate that it evolved from the gastrointestinal pathogen Yersinia pseudotuberculosis within the last several thousand years, acquiring plasmids that enabled flea-borne transmission. Ancient DNA recovered from Bronze Age and Neolithic remains has confirmed that plague lineages circulated in Eurasia far earlier than the first recorded pandemics.

Biological warfare

Because of its lethality and potential for aerosol transmission, Y. pestis is classified as a select agent and a potential bioweapon. Historical instances of its deliberate use include alleged catapulting of plague corpses during the 1347 siege of Caffa and experiments by Unit 731 during the Second World War.
