The Rutherford Appleton Laboratory (RAL) is one of the national scientific research laboratories in the United Kingdom operated by the Science and Technology Facilities Council (STFC). Situated on the Harwell Campus in Oxfordshire, it employs about 1,200 staff and supports more than 10,000 scientists and engineers, mainly from the university research community.

History

The laboratory was formed in 1957 as the Rutherford High Energy Laboratory, named after the physicist Ernest Rutherford, to house the Nimrod proton synchrotron. In 1975 it merged with the nearby Atlas Computer Laboratory and the Appleton Laboratory, the latter named after Edward Appleton, taking its present name. The laboratory became part of STFC when the council was formed in 2007.

Facilities

RAL hosts several major national and international facilities, including:

  • ISIS Neutron and Muon Source — a pulsed spallation neutron source used for materials and condensed-matter research.
  • Diamond Light Source — the UK's national synchrotron light source, operated as a joint venture and located on the same campus.
  • Central Laser Facility — home to the Vulcan and Gemini high-power lasers.
  • RAL Space — a space science and technology department involved in more than 200 space missions.
  • Scientific Computing Department — providing large-scale computing, including the JASMIN facility for environmental science data.

Research

Research at the laboratory spans particle physics, neutron and synchrotron science, laser science, space instrumentation, energy, and scientific computing. RAL staff played roles in landmark experiments including work connected with the Higgs boson research programme, and the laboratory's computing centres process data for the Large Hadron Collider at CERN.
