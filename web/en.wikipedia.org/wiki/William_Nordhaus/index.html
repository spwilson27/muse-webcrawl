William Dawbney Nordhaus (born May 31, 1941) is an American economist. He is the Sterling Professor of Economics at Yale University and is best known for his work in economic modelling and climate change. In 2018 he was co-recipient (with Paul Romer) of the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences "for integrating climate change into long-run macroeconomic analysis".

Early life and education

Nordhaus was born in Albuquerque, New Mexico. He received his B.A. from Yale University in 1963 and his PhD in economics from MIT in 1967. Apart from a period as a Member of the President's Council of Economic Advisers (1977–1979) under President Jimmy Carter, he has spent his career at Yale.

Climate economics

Nordhaus pioneered the integrated assessment modelling of climate policy. His DICE model (Dynamic Integrated model of Climate and the Economy), first developed in the early 1990s, links emissions, atmospheric carbon concentrations, temperature change, economic damage, and the costs of abatement within a single framework. The related RICE model treats the world as multiple regions. These models have been widely used to estimate the social cost of carbon and to evaluate carbon taxes and emissions-trading schemes. His books on the subject include Managing the Global Commons (1994) and The Climate Casino (2013).

Other work

With Paul Samuelson, Nordhaus co-authored later editions of the classic textbook Economics. He has also written on the economics of invention, productivity measurement, and the "New Economy", and early in his career on the theory of political business cycles.

Honours

He is a member of the National Academy of Sciences and the American Academy of Arts and Sciences, and served as president of the American Economic Association in 2014.
