Hardwood

Hardwood
SourceAngiosperm (flowering) trees
ExamplesOak, maple, mahogany, teak, walnut, balsa
UsesFurniture, flooring, construction, tools

Hardwood is wood from angiosperm trees. These are usually found in broad-leaved temperate and tropical forests. In temperate and boreal latitudes they are mostly deciduous, but in the tropics and subtropics they are mostly evergreen. Hardwood contrasts with softwood, which comes from gymnosperm trees.

Characteristics

Hardwoods are produced by angiosperm trees that reproduce by flowers and have broad leaves. Many species are deciduous. Those of temperate regions lose their leaves every autumn as temperatures fall and are dormant in winter, but those of tropical regions may shed their leaves in response to seasonal or sporadic periods of drought. Hardwood from deciduous species, such as oak, normally shows annual growth rings, which may be less obvious in some tropical species.

Hardwoods have a more complex structure than softwoods and are often much slower growing. The dominant feature separating hardwoods from softwoods is the presence of pores, or vessels, in the wood structure.

Applications

Hardwoods are employed in a large range of applications, including fuel, tools, construction, boat building, furniture, musical instruments, flooring, cooking, barrels, and manufacture of charcoal. Solid hardwood joinery tends to be expensive compared to softwood, although cheaper hardwoods such as poplar and pine are widely available.

Hardwood versus softwood

The terms hardwood and softwood do not refer directly to the actual hardness of the wood. Some hardwoods, such as balsa, are much softer than most softwoods, while the yew, a softwood, is harder than many hardwoods. In general, however, hardwoods tend to be denser and more durable.
