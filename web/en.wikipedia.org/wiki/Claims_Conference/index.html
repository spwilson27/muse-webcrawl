Claims Conference

The Conference on Jewish Material Claims Against Germany, or Claims Conference, represents the world's Jews in negotiating for compensation and restitution for victims of Nazi persecution and their heirs.

Claims Conference
Founded: 1951
Full name: Conference on Jewish Material Claims Against Germany
Headquarters: New York City
Purpose: Compensation and restitution for Holocaust victims

History

Founded in 1951 by Nahum Goldmann and 23 Jewish organisations, it negotiated the 1952 Luxembourg Agreements with West Germany, under which Germany paid reparations to Israel and to survivors. It later negotiated with the reunified Germany and administered funds including the Hardship Fund, Article 2 Fund and Central and Eastern European Fund.

Programmes

The Conference secures pensions and one-time payments, funds home care and social services for elderly survivors worldwide, recovers heirless Jewish property in former East Germany to finance its work, and supports Holocaust education and documentation.

Criticism and oversight

It has faced criticism over executive pay, allocation priorities, and a major fraud case uncovered around 2010 in which employees diverted funds with false claims; reforms and audits followed.
