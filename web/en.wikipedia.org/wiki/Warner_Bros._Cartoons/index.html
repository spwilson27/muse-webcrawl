Warner Bros. Cartoons, Inc. was the in-house animation studio of Warner Bros. Pictures during the golden age of American animation. Founded in 1933 as Leon Schlesinger Productions and sold to Warner Bros. in 1944, the studio produced the Looney Tunes and Merrie Melodies theatrical short subjects.

The Termite Terrace era

Working in a shabby building nicknamed "Termite Terrace", directors Tex Avery, Bob Clampett, Chuck Jones, Friz Freleng and Robert McKimson created enduring characters including Bugs Bunny, Daffy Duck, Porky Pig, Elmer Fudd, Yosemite Sam, Sylvester, Tweety, the Road Runner and Wile E. Coyote, with voices largely by Mel Blanc and music by Carl Stalling. The studio's fast, anarchic humour distinguished its cartoons from Disney's more polished style.

Later years

After the original theatrical shorts ended in the early 1960s, production continued under DePatie–Freleng and other arrangements until Warner Bros. closed the studio in 1969. Its legacy endures through constant television syndication and the canonisation of shorts such as What's Opera, Doc? and Duck Amuck.
