2024 French legislative election

Legislative elections were held in France on 30 June and 7 July 2024 to elect all 577 members of the National Assembly. President Emmanuel Macron called the snap election on 9 June after his centrist alliance was heavily defeated by Marine Le Pen's far-right National Rally (RN) in the European Parliament elections. The result produced a hung parliament of three blocs, with the left-wing New Popular Front (NFP) winning the most seats but falling far short of a majority.

Campaign

The far-right National Rally led by Jordan Bardella won the first round with about 33% of the vote, ahead of the hastily assembled NFP (~28%) and Macron's Ensemble alliance (~20%). Between rounds, over 200 third-placed candidates withdrew under a "republican front" strategy to concentrate the anti-RN vote, which succeeded in limiting the RN and its allies to third place in seats despite their leading the popular vote.

No bloc could govern alone. After weeks of stalemate, Macron appointed the conservative Michel Barnier as prime minister in September 2024 at the head of a minority government reliant on RN tolerance; Barnier's government fell to a no-confidence vote in December 2024, and François Bayrou succeeded him.
