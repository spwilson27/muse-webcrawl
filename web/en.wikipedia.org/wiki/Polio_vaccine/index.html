Polio vaccine

Polio vaccine
Type: Vaccine
Target: Poliomyelitis
Routes: Injection (IPV), oral (OPV)

Polio vaccines are vaccines used to prevent poliomyelitis (polio). Two types are used: an inactivated poliovirus vaccine (IPV) given by injection, developed by Jonas Salk and first tested in 1952, and a weakened live vaccine given by mouth, developed by Albert Sabin and coming into commercial use in 1961.

History

Salk's inactivated vaccine was announced as safe and effective on 12 April 1955 after a massive field trial. Sabin's oral vaccine, cheaper and easier to administer, became the mainstay of global eradication campaigns. The Global Polio Eradication Initiative, launched in 1988, has reduced polio cases by over 99 percent.

Types

IPV contains inactivated virus of all three poliovirus types. OPV originally contained live attenuated virus of all three types (trivalent); after wild type 2 was declared eradicated in 2015, most programmes switched to a bivalent OPV covering types 1 and 3.
