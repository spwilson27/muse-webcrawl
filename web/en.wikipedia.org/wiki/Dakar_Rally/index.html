The Dakar Rally (formerly the Paris–Dakar Rally) is an annual rally raid organised by the Amaury Sport Organisation. It is regarded as the world's most demanding off-road endurance event, covering thousands of kilometres of desert and rough terrain over roughly two weeks each January. Most of its competitive ("special") stages are off-road, crossing dunes, mud, camel grass, rocks and other difficult terrain, and competitors navigate by roadbook.

History

French motorcyclist Thierry Sabine founded the event after getting lost in the Libyan desert during the 1977 Abidjan–Nice rally. The first Paris–Dakar ran from December 1978 into January 1979, starting in Paris and finishing at Dakar, Senegal, and the race became an annual fixture crossing the Sahara. Sabine was killed in a helicopter crash during the 1986 rally. Security concerns forced the cancellation of the 2008 event after terrorist threats in Mauritania; from 2009 to 2019 the rally was held in South America, and since 2020 it has taken place in Saudi Arabia.

Format and vehicles

Competitors race in timed special stages linked by untimed liaison sections. Classes include motorcycles, quads, cars (T1), lightweight vehicles/SSVs, and large racing trucks. Stages may exceed 800 km in a day, and a "marathon" stage may prohibit outside mechanical assistance. The event is open to professionals and amateurs, the latter making up the majority of entrants.

Notable records and dangers

French rider and driver Stéphane Peterhansel is the most successful competitor, with 14 victories across motorcycles and cars. The rally is notoriously dangerous: dozens of competitors and others have died over its history, including its founder, prompting continuing debate over safety and over the event's environmental and cultural impact on host regions.
