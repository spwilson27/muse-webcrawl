Literary society

A literary society is a group of people interested in literature. In the widest sense, such a society is devoted to books and writing, but the name typically refers to more specific groups: learned societies for scholars, university student societies dedicated to debate and letters, and local clubs for reading and discussing literature.

History

Literary societies flourished in eighteenth- and nineteenth-century Europe and America. The Lunar Society of Birmingham, the Literary and Philosophical Societies of British industrial cities, and American college literary societies — often the centre of campus intellectual life, maintaining libraries and sponsoring oratory contests — are notable examples.

Modern societies

Today the term includes author-specific appreciation societies (such as the Jane Austen Society), national academies of letters, and professional bodies like PEN International. Activities include lectures, publications, prizes, and advocacy for literature and freedom of expression.
