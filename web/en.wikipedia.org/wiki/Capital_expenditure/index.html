Capital expenditure

AbbreviationCapEx, CAPEX
FieldAccounting, corporate finance
ContrastOperating expense (OpEx)

Capital expenditure or capital expense (abbreviated CapEx or CAPEX) is the money an organization or corporate entity spends to buy, maintain, or improve its fixed assets, such as buildings, vehicles, equipment, or land. It is considered a capital expenditure when the asset is newly purchased, or when money is used towards extending the useful life of an existing asset, such as repairing a roof.

Accounting treatment

Unlike operating expenses, which are fully deducted in the period incurred, capital expenditure is capitalised on the balance sheet as an asset and then expensed gradually through depreciation (for tangible assets) or amortisation (for intangible assets) over the asset's useful life. For tax purposes, deductions for capital expenditure are typically given through capital allowance or depreciation regimes rather than immediately.

Cash flow

In a cash flow statement, capital expenditure appears in the investing activities section. Analysts commonly compute free cash flow by subtracting capital expenditure from operating cash flow. A distinction is often drawn between maintenance capex, needed to sustain existing operations, and growth capex, invested to expand capacity.

Usage

Capital expenditures are a key indicator of a company's investment in its future operations. Capital-intensive industries such as telecommunications, utilities, oil and gas, and manufacturing devote a large share of revenue to capex, and the ratio of capital expenditure to depreciation is used as a rough gauge of whether a business is expanding or contracting its asset base.
