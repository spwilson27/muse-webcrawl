Design thinking

Design thinking is a process for creative problem solving. It refers to the set of cognitive, strategic and practical procedures used by designers in the process of designing, and to an approach to resolving problems outside professional design practice, such as in business and social contexts. Design thinking is a form of solution-focused thinking, starting with a goal and exploring multiple possible solutions in parallel.

Stages

Popular formulations describe five stages, popularised by the Hasso Plattner Institute of Design at Stanford (the d.school):

  1. Empathize — research the users' needs.
  2. Define — state the users' needs and problems.
  3. Ideate — challenge assumptions and create ideas.
  4. Prototype — start to create solutions.
  5. Test — try out the solutions with users.

The stages are often run in parallel and repeated iteratively rather than followed once in strict sequence.

History

The term grew out of design-methods research of the 1960s and was developed by David Kelley and the consultancy IDEO, which applied the designer's toolkit to business and management problems. Herbert A. Simon's The Sciences of the Artificial (1969) is frequently cited as a foundational text.

Criticism

Design thinking has been criticised as a management fad, for packaging common-sense practices in jargon, and for claims of effectiveness that outrun the empirical evidence.
