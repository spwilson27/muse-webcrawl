Assertive community treatment

Abbreviation: ACT

Field: Psychiatry, community mental health

Developed: 1970s, Madison, Wisconsin

Assertive community treatment (ACT) is an intensive and highly integrated approach for community mental health service delivery. ACT teams provide comprehensive, community-based psychiatric treatment, rehabilitation and support to persons with serious and persistent mental illness, such as schizophrenia and bipolar disorder, particularly those who do not engage with conventional services.

Model

ACT was developed in the 1970s in Madison, Wisconsin, by Arnold Marx, Leonard Stein and Mary Ann Test, as an alternative to repeated hospitalisation. Its defining features include a multidisciplinary team (psychiatrist, nurses, social workers, vocational and substance-use specialists) sharing a small caseload (about 10 clients per staff member), services delivered in the community rather than the clinic, 24-hour availability, assertive outreach and engagement, medication management, and support with housing, finances and daily living — provided on a time-unlimited basis.

Evidence

Numerous randomised trials and systematic reviews show that high-fidelity ACT reduces hospital admissions and homelessness and improves housing stability and client satisfaction, though effects on symptoms and social functioning are more modest. Fidelity is measured with scales such as DACTS and TMACT. Variants include forensic ACT and "flexible ACT" (FACT) in the Netherlands.
