Haakon IV of Norway

Haakon IV Haakonsson (c. March/April 1204 – 16 December 1263), sometimes called Haakon the Old in contrast to his namesake son, was King of Norway from 1217 to 1263. His reign lasted 46 years, longer than any Norwegian king before him. Haakon is regarded as a prominent leader of the "golden age" of medieval Norway, which reached its apex under his rule.

Early life and accession

Haakon was the posthumous son of King Haakon III, and was born at Folkenborg amid the civil war era. His claim to the throne was contested by rival factions; as a child he was famously carried to safety across the mountains by the Birkebeiner warriors, an episode commemorated in Norway by the Birkebeinerrennet ski race. Following the death of King Inge II in 1217, Haakon was elected king, defeating the claim of Inge's half-brother Skule Bårdsson, who was made earl and later rebelled, being killed in 1240.

Reign

Haakon's reign ended Norway's long period of civil wars. He consolidated royal power, developed the administration and law codes, and pursued an active foreign policy: Iceland and Greenland accepted Norwegian overlordship in the early 1260s. In 1263 he sailed to defend Norwegian claims in Scotland, fought the inconclusive Battle of Largs, and died that winter at Kirkwall in Orkney, where he was buried before his remains were returned to Bergen.
