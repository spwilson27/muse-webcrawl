Malcolm John MacDonald OM (17 August 1901 – 11 January 1981) was a British politician and diplomat. He was the son of Ramsay MacDonald, Britain's first Labour prime minister, and himself served in the National Government of the 1930s before a long second career in diplomacy and colonial administration.

Political career

Elected to Parliament for Bassetlaw in 1929, MacDonald followed his father into the National Government in 1931. He served as Under-Secretary for Dominion Affairs, then as Secretary of State for the Colonies (1935, and again 1938–1940) and Secretary of State for Dominion Affairs, and as Minister of Health (1940–1941). As Colonial Secretary he issued the 1939 White Paper on Palestine, which limited Jewish immigration and land purchase — a document long controversial. He lost his seat in 1945.

Diplomatic career

MacDonald then served as Governor-General of the Malayan Union and British Borneo territories, Commissioner-General for South-East Asia during the early Cold War, High Commissioner to Canada (1941–1946, before his Asian postings sequence in government service), High Commissioner to India, and finally Governor (later Governor-General) of Kenya in 1963–1964, overseeing the transition to independence under Jomo Kenyatta. He was a co-chairman of the 1961–62 Laos conference in Geneva and later Chancellor of the University of Durham.

Personal life

MacDonald was a keen ornithologist and photographer, publishing books on birds, and collected Chinese ceramics. He died in 1981 at his home in Kent.
