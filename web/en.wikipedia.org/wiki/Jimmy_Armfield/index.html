Jimmy Armfield

James Christopher Armfield
Born: 21 September 1935, Denton, Lancashire, England
Died: 22 January 2018 (aged 82), Blackpool
Position: Right-back
Clubs: Blackpool (1954–1971); England (43 caps)

James Christopher Armfield CBE (21 September 1935 – 22 January 2018) was an English professional footballer, manager and broadcaster. A one-club man, he played 627 games as a right-back for Blackpool between 1954 and 1971, and won 43 caps for England, captaining his country 15 times.

Career

Armfield was part of the England squad that won the 1966 World Cup, though injury kept him out of the tournament itself and he received a winners' medal only in 2009 after a campaign for non-playing squad members. Widely regarded as one of the finest right-backs of his generation, he was noted for overlapping attacking runs that foreshadowed the modern full-back. After retiring he managed Bolton Wanderers, winning the Third Division title in 1973, and Leeds United, whom he led to the 1975 European Cup Final.

Later life

Armfield became a beloved radio summariser for BBC Radio 5 Live over more than 35 years, served as an FA consultant and adviser, and remained a central figure in Blackpool life: the South Stand at Bloomfield Road is named after him and bears his statue. He was appointed CBE in 2010 for services to football and the community in Lancashire.
