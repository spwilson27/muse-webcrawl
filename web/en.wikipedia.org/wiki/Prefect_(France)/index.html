Prefect (France)

A prefect (French: préfet) in France is the representative of the central government in a department or region. Appointed by the President of the Republic on the advice of the Minister of the Interior, prefects are members of the prefectoral corps of senior civil servants and are charged with maintaining public order, supervising the legality of local government acts, coordinating state services, and implementing national policy locally. Those who serve a region in addition to its capital department are titled préfet de région.

The prefect sits in the prefecture of the department's capital city; the post has existed since 1800.

History

The office was created by Napoleon Bonaparte as First Consul by the law of 28 Pluviôse Year VIII (17 February 1800), replacing the revolutionary administrations with a uniform hierarchy: a prefect in each department, subprefects in the arrondissements, and appointed mayors. The system epitomised French administrative centralisation, and prefects became powerful local figures — "emperors in their departments". The decentralisation laws of 1982–83 (the Defferre laws) transferred the executive power of departments and regions to elected councils, leaving the prefect as guardian of legality and representative of the state rather than local ruler. In 1982–88 the office was briefly renamed commissaire de la République before the traditional title was restored.

Duties

  • Public order and security: directing police and gendarmerie operations in the department, crisis management, and powers to ban demonstrations or impose curfews.
  • Legality control (contrôle de légalité): reviewing acts of communes, departments and regions after the fact.
  • Issuing identity documents, residence permits and driving licences through the prefecture; organising elections.
  • Regional prefects coordinate economic development and EU funding and chair defence-zone administrations.

Special cases include the Prefect of Police of Paris, who heads the Paris police, and the maritime prefects responsible for state action at sea. Prefects wear a distinctive uniform with oak- and olive-leaf embroidery on official occasions and are expected to be politically neutral in public while serving the government of the day.
