TitleBlack Madonna of Częstochowa (Our Lady of Częstochowa)
TypeIcon — Virgin and Child (Hodegetria)

LocationJasna Góra Monastery, Częstochowa, Poland

The Black Madonna of Częstochowa (Polish: Czarna Madonna), also known as Our Lady of Częstochowa, is a venerated icon of the Virgin Mary housed at the Jasna Góra Monastery in Częstochowa, Poland. It is one of the most important religious symbols in Poland, where Mary is honoured as "Queen of Poland", and the sanctuary is a major pilgrimage destination attracting millions of visitors each year.

History

Tradition ascribes the painting of the original image to Saint Luke the Evangelist on a cedar table from the Holy Family's house. The icon was brought to Jasna Góra in 1384 by Prince Władysław of Opole, who installed it in the newly founded Pauline monastery. In 1430 Hussite raiders looted the monastery and damaged the icon; the two cuts visible on the Virgin's right cheek are traditionally attributed to a robber's sword strokes, and attempted restorations have preserved them as part of the image.

The icon's fame grew dramatically after the successful defence of Jasna Góra against the Swedish army during the Deluge in 1655, which King John II Casimir attributed to the Virgin's protection; in 1656 he proclaimed Mary Queen of Poland in Lwów Cathedral. The icon received a canonical coronation with papal crowns from Clement XI in 1717, the first such coronation of an image outside Rome.

Description

The painting, executed in tempera on a lime-wood panel, depicts the Virgin in the Hodegetria type: Mary gestures toward the Christ Child, who raises one hand in blessing and holds a book in the other. The dark, olive complexion of the figures — the source of the name "Black Madonna" — results from age, varnish and candle soot as much as from the original pigments. The icon is normally covered by a decorative metal dress (sukienka) studded with jewels; famous examples include the amber-and-silver dress and the coral dress.
