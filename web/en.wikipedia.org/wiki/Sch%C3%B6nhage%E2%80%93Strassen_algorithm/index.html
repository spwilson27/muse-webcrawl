Schönhage–Strassen algorithm

The Schönhage–Strassen algorithm is an asymptotically fast multiplication algorithm for large integers. It was developed by Arnold Schönhage and Volker Strassen in 1971 and became the standard for multiplying very large numbers until it was outperformed by the Harvey–van der Hoeven algorithm published in 2019.

Method

The algorithm uses fast Fourier transforms over rings of the form Z/(22n + 1) (Fermat-number rings), where roots of unity of large power-of-two order are simply powers of 2, so multiplications by them reduce to cheap shifts. The two input integers are split into many small pieces, convolved via the transform, and reassembled; recursion applies the same method to the coefficient arithmetic.

Complexity

Multiplying two n-bit numbers takes O(n log n log log n) bit operations, far faster than the O(n2) of schoolbook multiplication or the O(nlog2 3) of the Karatsuba algorithm. Schönhage and Strassen conjectured that an O(n log n) bound should be achievable, which the 2019 algorithm of David Harvey and Joris van der Hoeven finally established.

Practical use

The algorithm is used for numbers with tens of thousands of digits or more, for example in computations of π and searches for large Mersenne primes. Libraries such as GMP switch to an FFT-based method in this size range.
