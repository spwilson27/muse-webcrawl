Charles Portis

Charles McColl Portis (December 28, 1933 – February 17, 2020) was an American author best known for his novels Norwood (1966) and the classic Western True Grit (1968). Described by Roald Dahl as "totally unique," Portis developed a devoted following among writers while remaining largely indifferent to publicity; his work blends deadpan comedy, regional speech, first-person narration and eccentric drifting characters.

Life

Portis was born in El Dorado, Arkansas, and grew up in various towns in the state. He served in the U.S. Marine Corps during the Korean War, reaching the rank of sergeant, then studied journalism at the University of Arkansas, graduating in 1958. He worked as a reporter for the Commercial Appeal in Memphis and the Arkansas Gazette, and then for the New York Herald Tribune, where he became its London bureau chief. In 1964 he resigned to return to Arkansas and write fiction.

Novels

His first novel, Norwood (1966), follows a young ex-Marine travelling from Texas to New York. True Grit (1968), narrated by elderly spinster Mattie Ross, tells of her 1870s quest, with drunken U.S. Marshal Rooster Cogburn, to avenge her father's murder. It was adapted into a 1969 film starring John Wayne, who won his only Academy Award for the role, and again by the Coen brothers in 2010, to wide acclaim.

His later novels are The Dog of the South (1979), in which a memorably unreliable narrator chases his runaway wife to Belize; Masters of Atlantis (1985), a satire of a fraudulent Gnostic cult founded on a forged text; and Gringos (1991), set among American expatriates and treasure hunters in Mexico's Yucatán. A collection of his shorter writing, Escape Velocity: A Charles Portis Miscellany, appeared in 2012.

Style and reputation

Portis's prose is marked by precise, deadpan narration through distinctive first-person voices, mixing period detail with absurdist humour. Critics and fellow writers — including Roy Blount Jr., Nora Ephron and the Coen brothers — praised him as one of the most under-recognised American novelists of his generation. He lived most of his later life in Little Rock, Arkansas, where he died in 2020.
