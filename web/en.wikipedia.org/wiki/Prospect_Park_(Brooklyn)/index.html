Prospect Park (Brooklyn)

Prospect Park is a 526-acre (213 ha) urban park in the borough of Brooklyn in New York City. Designed by Frederick Law Olmsted and Calvert Vaux after their work on Central Park, it opened in 1867 and is widely considered their masterpiece. The park is operated by the Prospect Park Alliance in partnership with the New York City Department of Parks and Recreation.

Features

The park contains the Long Meadow, a 90-acre stretch of rolling grassland; a 60-acre lake; and the Ravine, Brooklyn's last remaining old-growth forest. Other features include the Prospect Park Zoo, the Boathouse, the Bandshell, the LeFrak Center at Lakeside, sports fields, and the Grand Army Plaza entrance with its Soldiers' and Sailors' Arch. The park adjoins the Brooklyn Botanic Garden and the Brooklyn Museum.

History

The site includes part of the ground fought over in the Battle of Long Island (1776) during the American Revolutionary War; Battle Pass in the park's northeast marks a key defensive position. Olmsted and Vaux's 1866 design organised the park as a sequence of pastoral, picturesque and recreational landscapes. During the twentieth century the park fell into decline, but restoration from the 1990s onward — led in part by the Prospect Park Alliance, founded in 1987 — revitalised its landscapes and facilities.

Use

Prospect Park hosts concerts, farmers' markets, running and cycling races, and the start of numerous parades. Its perimeter loop is a major commuting and exercise route, and the park is the principal green space for the surrounding neighbourhoods of Park Slope, Prospect Heights, Windsor Terrace and Flatbush.
