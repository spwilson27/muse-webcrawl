Interceptor aircraft

An interceptor aircraft is a type of fighter aircraft designed specifically to intercept and destroy enemy aircraft, particularly bombers and reconnaissance planes, before they reach their targets. Interceptors emphasise high speed, rapid climb and powerful radar and missile armament at the expense of manoeuvrability and range. They typically operate as part of an integrated air defence system, directed to their targets by ground-controlled interception (GCI) or airborne early warning aircraft.

History

The role emerged during the First World War in the defence of London against German bombers and airships. In the Second World War, radar-directed fighters such as those of RAF Fighter Command proved decisive in the Battle of Britain, and specialised night fighters and rocket-powered point-defence interceptors like the Messerschmitt Me 163 appeared late in the war. During the Cold War, the threat of nuclear-armed bombers drove development of ever faster interceptors: the Soviet Union fielded the MiG-21, the very fast MiG-25 and MiG-31, while the United States developed the "Century Series" and the Convair F-106 Delta Dart, and Britain the English Electric Lightning. Dedicated interceptors were expected to climb rapidly to altitude and engage bombers with air-to-air missiles, sometimes including nuclear-tipped rockets such as the AIR-2 Genie.

Decline of the dedicated type

As intercontinental ballistic missiles displaced bombers as the main nuclear threat from the 1960s, and as multirole fighters gained comparable speed, radar and missile capability, the dedicated interceptor largely disappeared. Modern air defence is usually performed by multirole or air-superiority fighters such as the F-15 and Su-27 families. A notable surviving specialist is Russia's MiG-31, used for long-range patrol and interception over the Arctic and, in a modified version, as a carrier of the Kinzhal hypersonic missile. "Point-defence interceptors" traded range for climb performance, while "area-defence interceptors" carried fuel and sensors for long patrols.
