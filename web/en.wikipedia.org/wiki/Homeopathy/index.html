Homeopathy

FounderSamuel Hahnemann (1796)
Principles"Like cures like"; potentization by dilution and succussion
Scientific statusPseudoscience; no evidence of efficacy beyond placebo
ClaimsTreatment of acute and chronic conditions

Homeopathy is a system of alternative medicine created in 1796 by the German physician Samuel Hahnemann. It is based on the doctrine of "like cures like" (similia similibus curentur) and on remedies prepared by serial dilution and vigorous shaking (succussion), a process called potentization.

Principles and practice

Homeopaths select remedies that, in undiluted form, produce symptoms similar to those of the patient's illness. Remedies are diluted, often to the point where no molecule of the original substance is likely to remain—potencies such as 30C exceed the Avogadro limit. Practitioners claim the solvent retains a "memory" or energetic imprint of the substance.

Scientific assessment

The central claims of homeopathy conflict with established chemistry and physics, and systematic reviews by bodies such as Australia's National Health and Medical Research Council and the European Academies' Science Advisory Council have found no reliable evidence that homeopathic remedies are effective for any condition beyond placebo effects. Homeopathy is accordingly classified as pseudoscience by the scientific community.

Regulation and use

Despite the scientific consensus, homeopathy remains popular in several countries, notably India, Germany, and France, and is regulated to varying degrees; some health systems have withdrawn public funding on efficacy grounds. Critics warn against substituting homeopathy for effective treatment, especially for serious diseases.
