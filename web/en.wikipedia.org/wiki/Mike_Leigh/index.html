Mike Leigh

Mike Leigh (born 20 February 1943) is an English film and theatre director, screenwriter and playwright. He is renowned for a distinctive method of developing scripts through extensive improvisation with actors, and for films portraying ordinary British working- and middle-class life, including Naked (1993), Secrets & Lies (1996) and Vera Drake (2004).

Career

Leigh trained at RADA and worked in theatre and television before making his feature debut with Bleak Moments (1971). During the 1970s and 1980s he made a celebrated series of films for British television, among them Abigail's Party (1977) and Meantime (1983). Returning to cinema with High Hopes (1988) and Life Is Sweet (1990), he gained international prominence in the 1990s.

Secrets & Lies won the Palme d'Or at Cannes, and Vera Drake won the Golden Lion at Venice; Leigh has been nominated for seven Academy Awards for directing and writing. His later films include the Gilbert and Sullivan biographical drama Topsy-Turvy (1999), Happy-Go-Lucky (2008), Another Year (2010), Mr. Turner (2014) and Peterloo (2018).

Method

Leigh builds each film over months of improvisation: actors develop characters from real-life models, and scenes are shaped in rehearsal before any conventional script exists. The resulting dialogue is then fixed for filming. The method produces performances widely praised for their naturalism and emotional detail.
