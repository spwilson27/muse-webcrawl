Kaymakli underground city

Kaymakli underground city (Turkish: Kaymaklı Yeraltı Şehri) is an ancient multi-level underground city in the town of Kaymaklı in Nevşehir Province, in the Central Anatolia region of Turkey. Extending to a depth of approximately 60 metres (197 ft), it is large enough to have sheltered as many as 3,500 people.

History

The city was probably first built by the Phrygians in the 8th–7th centuries BC and greatly expanded during the Roman and Byzantine periods, when Christians used it as a refuge from persecution and Arab invasions. Its tunnels, storage rooms, stables, cellars, and kitchens could sustain inhabitants for extended periods.

Description

Kaymakli consists of eight underground levels, of which the top four are open to visitors. The rooms are organised around ventilation shafts, and massive circular stone doors could be rolled across corridors to seal off sections in case of attack. It is connected to the larger Derinkuyu underground city by a tunnel several kilometres long.
