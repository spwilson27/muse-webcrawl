Star Trek: Picard

Star Trek: Picard is an American science fiction television series created by Akiva Goldsman, Michael Chabon, Kirsten Beyer, and Alex Kurtzman for the streaming service CBS All Access (later Paramount+). It premiered on January 23, 2020, and ran for three seasons, concluding on April 20, 2023. The series stars Patrick Stewart, reprising his role as Jean-Luc Picard from Star Trek: The Next Generation, two decades after the events of the film Star Trek: Nemesis (2002).

Premise

Retired Admiral Jean-Luc Picard has withdrawn to his family's vineyard in France, haunted by the death of Data and by Starfleet's abandonment of the Romulan evacuation after the supernova of the Romulan sun and a ban on synthetic lifeforms following an attack on Mars. In season one, a young woman, Dahj, seeks his help, drawing him into a conspiracy involving her twin Soji, the Romulan secret police (the Zhat Vash), and the fate of synthetic beings; Picard assembles a new crew aboard Cristóbal Rios's ship La Sirena. Season two sees the entity Q transport the crew to a dystopian alternate timeline, forcing a trip to 21st-century Earth. Season three reunites Picard with the entire principal cast of The Next Generation — including William Riker, Deanna Troi, Worf, Geordi La Forge, Beverly Crusher, and a reconstructed Data — against a conspiracy involving the Changelings and the Borg.

Cast and reception

Stewart returned on the condition that the series not simply repeat The Next Generation; supporting regulars included Alison Pill, Isa Briones, Michelle Hurd, Santiago Cabrera, Evan Evagora, and Jeri Ryan as Seven of Nine (from Star Trek: Voyager). The series received generally positive reviews and won a Primetime Emmy Award for its prosthetic makeup; Stewart and the series received Saturn Awards. The third season, showrun by Terry Matalas, was particularly praised by critics and fans as a satisfying conclusion to the Next Generation era.
