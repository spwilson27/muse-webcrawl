Bolinus brandaris

Bolinus brandaris (originally called Murex brandaris by Linnaeus), commonly known as the purple dye murex or the spiny dye-murex, is a species of medium-sized predatory sea snail, a marine gastropod mollusk in the family Muricidae, the murex snails or rock snails. Along with the related Hexaplex trunculus, it was the principal source of Tyrian purple dye in the ancient world.

Description and distribution

The shell reaches about 60–90 mm in length and bears prominent spines and a long, straight siphonal canal. The species lives on sandy and muddy bottoms in the Mediterranean Sea and adjacent Atlantic coasts of Spain, Portugal and northwest Africa, feeding on other mollusks.

Dye production

Phoenician and Roman dyers extracted a secretion from the snail's hypobranchial gland that, after exposure to sunlight, becomes the famous purple dye. Thousands of snails were needed for a single gram of dye; vast shell middens remain at Tyre, Sidon and other ancient dye-works.
