Robert Lopez


Born: February 23, 1975, New York City, U.S.
Occupation: Songwriter
Known for: The Book of Mormon, Frozen, Coco

Robert Lopez (born February 23, 1975) is an American songwriter for musical theatre and film. He is the youngest person to achieve EGOT status — winning Emmy, Grammy, Academy and Tony Awards — and the only person to have won all four twice.

Career

Lopez co-created the puppet musical Avenue Q (2003), which won the Tony Award for Best Musical. With Trey Parker and Matt Stone he wrote The Book of Mormon (2011), a satirical hit that won nine Tony Awards including Best Original Score. With his wife, Kristen Anderson-Lopez, he wrote the songs for Disney's Frozen (2013) — including "Let It Go", which won the Academy Award for Best Original Song — and Coco (2017), whose "Remember Me" won him a second Oscar. The couple also wrote songs for Frozen II and the Marvel series WandaVision, including the Emmy-winning "Agatha All Along".

Lopez became the youngest EGOT winner in 2014 at age 39, and with his second Oscar in 2018 became the first double EGOT in history.
