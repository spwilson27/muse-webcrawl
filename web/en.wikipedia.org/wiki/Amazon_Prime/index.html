Amazon Prime is a paid subscription programme of Amazon that bundles expedited shipping with a growing suite of digital services. Launched in the United States in 2005 with unlimited two-day shipping for an annual fee, Prime is widely credited with transforming consumer expectations of delivery speed and with anchoring Amazon's retail ecosystem: members order more frequently and spend substantially more than non-members.

History

Prime debuted in February 2005 at $79 per year, offering free two-day shipping on most items regardless of order size. It expanded internationally from 2007, added streaming video in 2011 (the seed of Prime Video), same-day and one-day delivery in major metros, and Prime Day — a members-only sales event launched in 2015 that grew into one of the largest retail events of the year. In 2018 Amazon reported passing 100 million members; by 2021 it reported over 200 million. US pricing rose in steps to $139 per year in 2022, and in 2024 ads were introduced into Prime Video with an ad-free tier sold as an add-on.

Benefits

  • Delivery: free one-day or same-day shipping on eligible items in covered areas, plus release-date delivery.
  • Prime Video: streaming of films and series, including originals such as The Marvelous Mrs. Maisel, The Boys, and The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power, plus live sports including NFL Thursday Night Football.
  • Prime Music and Amazon Music tiers; Prime Reading and Kindle lending offerings; Prime Gaming (formerly Twitch Prime) with monthly games and a Twitch subscription.
  • Shopping perks: Prime Day and early-access deals, Prime-exclusive prices, grocery benefits via Amazon Fresh and Whole Foods Market discounts, and household sharing.

Business significance and criticism

Prime underpins Amazon's flywheel strategy: the membership fee subsidises a vast logistics network and locks in purchasing, while content spending retains members between orders. Analysts estimate Prime members account for a majority of Amazon retail spending. The programme has also drawn criticism and regulatory scrutiny — over difficult cancellation flows ("dark patterns") that prompted an FTC lawsuit in 2023 and EU commitments in 2022, and over the environmental footprint of fast delivery. Discount tiers exist for students (Prime Student/Young Adults) and for recipients of government assistance.
