Drill sergeant

A drill sergeant is a non-commissioned officer in the United States Army charged with the training of recruits during initial entry training (basic combat training). Drill sergeants are distinguished by the distinctive campaign hat they wear and are responsible for transforming civilians into soldiers, instilling discipline, physical fitness, military bearing and army values.

Role

Drill sergeants lead nearly every aspect of a recruit's early army life: drill and ceremony, physical training, marksmanship, field exercises and barracks inspections. The role is demanding and selective; soldiers volunteer or are selected to attend the United States Army Drill Sergeant Academy at Fort Jackson, South Carolina.

Other services and countries

Equivalent roles exist in other branches — the Marine Corps drill instructor, the Navy's recruit division commander and the Air Force military training instructor — and in many other armed forces, where drill instructors are central figures of basic training. In popular culture the drill sergeant is often portrayed as a fearsome, shouting figure, a stereotype reinforced by films such as Full Metal Jacket.
