MeToo movement

The Me Too movement, often styled #MeToo, is a social movement and awareness campaign against sexual abuse, sexual harassment, and rape culture, in which women publicize their experiences of sexual abuse and harassment. The phrase "Me Too" was first used in this context on social media in 2006 by activist and survivor Tarana Burke, and the movement was popularized in October 2017 when actress Alyssa Milano encouraged women to use the hashtag amid the Harvey Weinstein sexual abuse allegations.

Origins

Tarana Burke, an activist from the Bronx, founded the "Me Too" campaign in 2006 while working with Just Be Inc., a nonprofit supporting young women of color who had experienced sexual abuse. Burke said the phrase was meant to convey empathy and solidarity: telling survivors "you're not alone."

2017 viral spread

On October 5, 2017, The New York Times published an investigation by Jodi Kantor and Megan Twohey documenting decades of sexual harassment allegations against film producer Harvey Weinstein; a New Yorker investigation by Ronan Farrow followed. On October 15, 2017, Milano tweeted: "If you've been sexually harassed or assaulted write 'me too' as a reply to this tweet." The hashtag was used millions of times within days across dozens of countries, adapted into local forms such as #YoTambién in Spanish, #MoiAussi in French, and #BalanceTonPorc.

Impact

The movement led to the exposure and downfall of numerous powerful figures in entertainment, media, politics, and business, and prompted widespread public debate about workplace conduct, power, and accountability. It fueled the creation of the Time's Up legal defense fund (2018) and legislative reforms, including U.S. state laws limiting nondisclosure agreements in harassment cases and the federal Ending Forced Arbitration of Sexual Assault and Sexual Harassment Act (2022). Time magazine named "The Silence Breakers" its Person of the Year for 2017.

Criticism and debate

Commentators have debated the movement's scope and fairness, including questions about due process, the conflation of offenses of different severity, racial and class inclusivity, backlash and defamation suits, and whether high-profile reckonings have translated into change for low-wage and vulnerable workers. The movement has also faced organized backlash and, in some countries, official suppression.
