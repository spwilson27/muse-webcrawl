Project Cheetah

Project Cheetah is a wildlife reintroduction program of the Government of India to re-establish cheetahs in the country, where the Asiatic cheetah had been declared extinct in 1952. Under the project, African cheetahs from Namibia and South Africa were translocated to Kuno National Park in Madhya Pradesh beginning in September 2022 — the first intercontinental transfer of a large wild carnivore for reintroduction.

Background

The Asiatic cheetah was hunted to extinction in India; the last three were shot in 1947, and the subspecies survives only in Iran. Efforts to reintroduce cheetahs were discussed for decades and gained momentum after a 2009 expert consultation and a Supreme Court of India ruling in 2020 permitting the introduction of African cheetahs on an experimental basis.

Translocation

On 17 September 2022, eight cheetahs from Namibia were released at Kuno by Prime Minister Narendra Modi. In February 2023, twelve more arrived from South Africa. The animals were initially kept in quarantine and hunting enclosures before release into the wild. The program has seen births of cubs in India as well as a number of deaths from causes including infection and mating injuries, prompting debate about habitat size, prey base, and management. Plans envisaged eventual metapopulation management across several sites in India.

Objectives

The project's stated goals are to establish a viable cheetah metapopulation in India, restore open-forest and grassland ecosystems, and use the cheetah as a flagship species for the conservation of neglected savanna habitats.
