Roman military

The Roman military was the armed forces of ancient Rome, from the militia of the early kingdom through the professional army of the Republic and Empire to the transformed forces of late antiquity. Over more than a thousand years it conquered and defended one of the largest empires of the ancient world, and it is regarded as one of the most effective military organisations in history.

Structure

Under the Republic, citizen legions of heavy infantry were raised for campaigns; reforms traditionally attributed to Gaius Marius opened service to the propertyless and made soldiering a career. Augustus regularised a standing army of legions (each about 5,000 citizen infantry) supported by auxiliary units of non-citizens, with fixed terms of service, pay and retirement grants. The navy, Praetorian Guard and later field armies (comitatenses) and frontier troops (limitanei) completed the establishment.

Equipment and tactics

The classical legionary carried the gladius short sword, pilum javelins, a large curved shield (scutum) and wore mail, scale or segmented plate armour. Roman strengths included engineering — roads, bridges, siege works and fortified camps built nightly — logistics, discipline and the ability to absorb defeats and raise new armies. The army was also a vehicle of Romanisation, spreading citizenship, language and urban life across the provinces.
