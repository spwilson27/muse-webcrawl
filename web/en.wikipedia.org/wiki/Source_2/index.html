Source 2

Developer: Valve Corporation

Initial release: June 2015 (Dota 2 Reborn)

Predecessor: Source

Platforms: Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, iOS

License: Proprietary (free for Steam releases)

Source 2 is a video game engine developed by Valve Corporation as the successor to the original Source engine, which had powered games from Half-Life 2 (2004) onward. Announced at the Game Developers Conference in March 2015, Source 2 was designed for modern 64-bit, multicore hardware, with a new physically based renderer, Vulkan and Direct3D 11 support, improved physics (the in-house Rubikon system, replacing the licensed Havok), unified lighting and improved audio and networking.

History and games

The engine debuted publicly in June 2015 when Dota 2 was ported to it as "Dota 2 Reborn", making Dota 2 the first full Source 2 game. Valve subsequently released The Lab (2016), the card game Artifact (2018), Dota Underlords (2019) and the VR flagship Half-Life: Alyx (2020), widely praised for its visual fidelity and interaction design. Counter-Strike 2 (2023) replaced Counter-Strike: Global Offensive on the Source 2 engine.

Tools and licensing

Valve made the Source 2 tools available to developers free of charge for games released through Steam, including the Hammer level editor and the Steam Audio spatial-audio system. Community creation tools ship with titles such as Dota 2 and Half-Life: Alyx, supporting an extensive modding ecosystem.
