Katanga Province

Katanga was a province of the Democratic Republic of the Congo, and until 2015 the country's second-largest province by area, occupying the south-eastern part of the nation. Its capital was Lubumbashi. In 2015, under the territorial reorganization mandated by the 2006 constitution, Katanga was divided into four new provinces: Tanganyika, Haut-Lomami, Lualaba and Haut-Katanga. Administrative history

The province originated as the Katanga district of the Congo Free State and became a vice-government of the Belgian Congo. At independence in 1960, Katanga under Moise Tshombe seceded as the State of Katanga, a breakaway recognized by no country but backed by Belgian mining interests, until UN and Congolese forces reintegrated it in 1963. Mobutu Sese Seko renamed the province Shaba in 1972 as part of his authenticité campaign; the name Katanga was restored in 1997 after his overthrow.

Geography

Katanga covered about one-fifth of the Congo's territory, bordering Angola and Zambia to the south and Tanzania across Lake Tanganyika to the east. Its southern districts form part of the Central African Copperbelt, and the north includes savanna and the Upemba depression with its lakes and national park.

Economy

The province's economy was dominated by mining: copper and cobalt around Kolwezi and Likasi, processed under the state company Gécamines (successor to the colonial Union Minière), as well as uranium formerly mined at Shinkolobwe, zinc, manganese and coal. The provincial capital Lubumbashi, founded as Élisabethville in 1910, is the DRC's second city and its mining capital.

Division in 2015

The 2006 constitution prescribed 26 provinces in place of 11. Implemented in 2015, this divided Katanga into Tanganyika (capital Kalemie), Haut-Lomami (Kamina), Lualaba (Kolwezi) and Haut-Katanga (Lubumbashi). Debate over the split, opposed by the provincial governor Moïse Katumbi, was a major issue in Congolese politics in the 2010s.
