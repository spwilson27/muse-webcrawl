Causes of World War II

The causes of World War II, the global war of 1939–1945, are debated by historians but are generally traced to the unresolved settlement of the First World War, the rise of fascism and Nazism, the Great Depression, and the failure of the League of Nations and the policy of appeasement to restrain aggression by Germany, Italy, and Japan.

Legacy of the First World War

The Treaty of Versailles (1919) imposed territorial losses, disarmament, and reparations on Germany, fostering resentment that extremists exploited through the "stab-in-the-back" myth. The treaty's settlement left revisionist grievances in Germany, Italy (which felt cheated of promised territory), and Hungary, while the League of Nations lacked enforcement power and members such as the United States never joined.

Rise of fascism and the Depression

Mussolini came to power in Italy in 1922; the Wall Street Crash of 1929 and the ensuing Great Depression devastated the German economy, and Adolf Hitler's Nazi Party rose to power in 1933 on promises to overturn Versailles, rearm, and reclaim "living space" (Lebensraum) in the east. Japan, driven by militarism and resource scarcity, invaded Manchuria in 1931; Italy invaded Ethiopia in 1935; and Germany remilitarised the Rhineland (1936), annexed Austria (1938), and dismembered Czechoslovakia after the Munich Agreement (1938) — each step met by League weakness or Anglo-French appeasement.

Immediate causes

The Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact of August 1939 freed Hitler from fear of a two-front war and secretly partitioned Eastern Europe. Germany invaded Poland on 1 September 1939; Britain and France declared war on 3 September. In Asia, Japan's full-scale war against China, begun in 1937, merged into the world war after Pearl Harbor in December 1941.
