Autoregressive–moving-average model

In the statistical analysis of time series, autoregressive–moving-average (ARMA) models provide a parsimonious description of a (weakly) stationary stochastic process in terms of two polynomials, one for the autoregression (AR) and the second for the moving average (MA).

The general ARMA model was described in the 1951 thesis of Peter Whittle and popularised by George E. P. Box and Gwilym Jenkins in their 1970 book, giving rise to the Box–Jenkins approach to time-series modelling and forecasting.

Definition

An ARMA(p, q) model expresses the current value of the series Xt as a linear combination of the p previous values, the present and q previous white-noise error terms, and a constant. The AR part involves regressing the variable on its own lagged values; the MA part models the error term as a linear combination of error terms occurring contemporaneously and at various times in the past.

Extensions and use

ARMA family

Parametersp (AR order), q (MA order)
IntroducedWhittle (1951); Box & Jenkins (1970)
ExtensionsARIMA, SARIMA, ARMAX, GARCH
UsesForecasting, econometrics, signal processing

If the series must be differenced to become stationary, the model becomes an ARIMA (autoregressive integrated moving average) model; seasonal versions are called SARIMA. ARMA-type models are widely used in economics, finance, hydrology and engineering for short-term forecasting, with model orders typically chosen by information criteria such as the AIC or by inspecting autocorrelation and partial autocorrelation functions.
