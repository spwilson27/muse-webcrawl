Buffalo nickel

The Buffalo nickel or Indian Head nickel is a copper–nickel five-cent piece struck by the United States Mint from 1913 to 1938. It was designed by sculptor James Earle Fraser, whose initials appear below the date on the obverse.

Design

The obverse bears a right-facing portrait of a Native American man, a composite Fraser said was based on several models, including Iron Tail, Big Tree and Two Moons. The reverse shows an American bison standing on a mound (in the first 1913 issue) and, in the revised issue, on a flat line. The bison model is often said to have been Black Diamond, a resident of the Bronx Zoo. Fraser, who had grown up in the Dakota Territory, intended the coin to be distinctly American in character.

History

The nickel replaced Charles E. Barber's Liberty Head nickel under Treasury Secretary Franklin MacVeagh, part of the early-20th-century renaissance of American coin design. Production began in February 1913 at Philadelphia, Denver and San Francisco. Early feedback showed the reverse mound design wore quickly, so Chief Engraver Charles Barber modified the reverse to place the denomination on a recessed line; even so, the date on the obverse was notoriously prone to wear, and "dateless" Buffalo nickels remain common.

Key dates and varieties

Notable issues include the 1913-S Type 2, the 1918/7-D overdate, the famous 1937-D "three-legged" variety (caused by over-polishing of a die), and the low-mintage 1916 doubled die obverse. The series was replaced in 1938 by the Jefferson nickel, designed by Felix Schlag.

Composition

Like other US nickels, the coin is 75% copper and 25% nickel, weighs 5.000 grams and measures 21.2 mm in diameter.
