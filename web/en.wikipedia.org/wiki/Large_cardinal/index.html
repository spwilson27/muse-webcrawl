Large cardinal

In the mathematical field of set theory, a large cardinal property is a property of cardinal numbers that implies their existence cannot be proved in standard set theory (ZFC), assuming ZFC is consistent. Cardinals with such properties are called large cardinals. They are so large that their existence implies the consistency of ZFC itself, so by Gödel's second incompleteness theorem ZFC cannot prove that they exist.

Examples and hierarchy

Large cardinal notions form a roughly linear hierarchy of increasing consistency strength: inaccessible, Mahlo, weakly compact, measurable, strong, Woodin, supercompact, huge and rank-into-rank cardinals, among many others. A measurable cardinal, for instance, carries a two-valued measure defined on all its subsets, and its existence implies that the constructible universe is far from exhausting the set-theoretic universe.

Role in set theory

Large cardinal axioms are studied as strong extensions of ZFC. They settle many questions left independent by ZFC: for example, sufficient large cardinals imply that all projective sets of real numbers have regularity properties such as Lebesgue measurability (projective determinacy), and they calibrate the consistency strength of other mathematical statements.
