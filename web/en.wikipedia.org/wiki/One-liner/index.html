One-liner

A one-liner is a joke that is delivered in a single line. A good one-liner is said to be pithy and concise. Comedians and actors use the style frequently, and it is a staple of stand-up comedy routines.

Style

One-liners typically rely on compression, wordplay, and a sudden twist of meaning in the final words. Comedians famous for the form include Henny Youngman ("Take my wife — please!"), Rodney Dangerfield ("I don't get no respect"), Steven Wright, Mitch Hedberg, and Milton Berle. In British comedy, Tim Vine and Stewart Francis are noted exponents, delivering rapid sequences of short jokes.

Film and drama

The term also describes a single witty line of dialogue in film or theatre, often a retort or punchline delivered by a character; memorable cinematic one-liners are a recognized element of action cinema. In writing, a one-liner can also refer to a one-sentence summary of a story or pitch.

Computing

In computing, a "one-liner" is a complete command or short program typed in a single line, common in shell scripting and languages such as Perl and AWK.
