The Marinid Sultanate was a polity ruled by the Marinid dynasty, a Zenata Berber tribe, which controlled present-day Morocco—and at its height parts of Algeria, Tunisia and Iberia—from the mid-13th to the 15th century.

Rise

The Marinids gradually displaced the Almohad Caliphate, capturing Fes in 1248 and Marrakesh in 1269 under Abu Yusuf Yaqub. Under Abu al-Hasan (r. 1331–1348) they briefly united the Maghreb, taking Tlemcen and Tunis, though these conquests were soon lost after the defeat at Kairouan and the Black Death.

Culture

The Marinids made Fes a great centre of learning, founding celebrated madrasas such as the Bou Inania and Al-Attarine, masterpieces of Maghrebi architecture with carved cedar, stucco and zellij tilework. They built the fortified necropolis of Chellah in Rabat.

Decline

Weakened by internal strife, Portuguese expansion (Ceuta fell in 1415) and revolts, the dynasty was overthrown in the Moroccan revolution of 1465 and replaced by the Wattasids.
