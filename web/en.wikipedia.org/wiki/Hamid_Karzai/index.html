Hamid Karzai

Hamid Karzai (Pashto: حامد کرزی, Ḥāmid Karzay; born 24 December 1957) is an Afghan politician who served as the fourth president of Afghanistan from 2001 to 2014, including as interim president after the fall of the Taliban regime in 2001 and as the country's first democratically elected head of state from 2004. A Pashtun of the Popalzai tribe, he became the dominant figure of the post-Taliban order for over a decade.

Early life

Karzai was born in the village of Karz near Kandahar, son of Abdul Ahad Karzai, a Popalzai tribal elder and deputy speaker of parliament. He studied political science at Himachal Pradesh University in India and worked in Pakistan during the Soviet–Afghan War supporting the mujahideen. He served as deputy foreign minister in the post-communist government in 1992, but fled as the Taliban rose; his father was assassinated in Quetta in 1999, after which Karzai became chief of the Popalzai.

Presidency

After the September 11 attacks, Karzai worked with the United States to rally Pashtun resistance to the Taliban and was selected at the Bonn Conference in December 2001 to chair Afghanistan's interim administration. He won the 2004 presidential election outright and was re-elected in 2009 in a vote marred by fraud allegations. His governments were supported by a large international military presence but criticized for corruption and weak governance. Relations with Washington deteriorated over civilian casualties and his refusal to sign a bilateral security agreement; he left office in 2014 in Afghanistan's first democratic transfer of power, succeeded by Ashraf Ghani.

After the presidency

Karzai remained a prominent political elder in Kabul. When the Taliban returned to power in August 2021, he stayed in the capital, participated in talks on a transitional arrangement, and has since periodically met foreign delegations and commented on issues such as girls' education.
