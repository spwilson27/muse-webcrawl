Southern Victory

The Southern Victory series is a series of alternate history novels written by Harry Turtledove, set in a world in which the Confederate States of America won the American Civil War and remained independent. The series spans eleven novels, beginning with How Few Remain (1997) and concluding with the Settling Accounts trilogy, which ends in 1945. It is sometimes called the Timeline-191 series, after the order number of the Confederate general Robert E. Lee's campaign orders whose loss, in real history, led to the Union victory at the Battle of Antietam.

Premise

The point of divergence occurs in September 1862, when a Confederate courier does not lose the copy of Lee's Special Orders 191 that, in real history, Union troops found wrapped around three cigars. Lee's Army of Northern Virginia wins the Battle of Antietam, and British and French intervention forces the United States to recognise Confederate independence. The Confederacy thereafter stands as a slaveholding republic increasingly surrounded by hostile powers, while the United States, humiliated, drifts toward alliance with Germany.

Books

The series comprises the standalone novel How Few Remain (1997), set during a second war between the USA and CSA in 1881–82; the Great War trilogy (1998–2000), covering a First World War in which the USA and Germany defeat the CSA, Britain, France and Russia; the American Empire trilogy (2001–2003), set between the wars as the Confederacy turns to the fascist-like Freedom Party under Jake Featherston; and the Settling Accounts tetralogy (2004–2007), depicting a Second World War ending with the destruction of the Confederacy and its annexation by the United States.

Themes

Turtledove uses the series to explore how slavery's survival reshapes the twentieth century: the Confederacy's Black population becomes the target of a genocide during the Second Great War, in an extended parallel to the Holocaust. The novels are told from many viewpoints, including historical figures such as Featherston (a fictional analogue of Hitler) and real people in altered circumstances.
