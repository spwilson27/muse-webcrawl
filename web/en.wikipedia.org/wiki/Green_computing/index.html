Green computing

Green computing (also called green IT or sustainable computing) is the environmentally responsible design, manufacture, use, and disposal of computers, servers, and associated subsystems. Its goals include reducing energy consumption and carbon emissions, minimising the use of hazardous materials, and maximising the recyclability and working life of equipment.

Origins

The movement is commonly dated to the launch of the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency's Energy Star programme in 1992, which promoted energy-efficient monitors and computers and popularised "sleep" power states. Subsequent initiatives include the EU's RoHS and WEEE directives restricting hazardous substances and mandating electronics recycling, the EPEAT product registry, and the 80 Plus certification for efficient power supplies.

Approaches

Green computing practice spans several levels:

  • Energy-efficient hardware and data centres — efficient processors, solid-state storage, free-air and liquid cooling, and measuring performance by metrics such as power usage effectiveness (PUE).
  • Software and workload management — virtualisation and server consolidation, power-aware scheduling, and algorithms optimised for energy as well as speed.
  • Lifecycle management — extending equipment life, refurbishing and remarketing machines, and responsible e-waste recycling to recover metals and prevent toxic dumping.
  • Behavioural measures — remote work, paperless workflows, and procurement policies favouring certified products.

Challenges

Computing's total footprint continues to grow with demand for cloud services, streaming, and artificial intelligence, raising "rebound effect" concerns: efficiency gains can be outpaced by increased use. Accurate measurement of embodied carbon in hardware manufacture remains an active area of research.
