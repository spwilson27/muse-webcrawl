Cycle sport

Cycle sport is competitive physical activity using bicycles. There are several categories of bicycle racing including road bicycle racing, cyclo-cross, mountain bike racing, track cycling, BMX, and cycle speedway. Non-racing cycling sports include artistic cycling, cycle polo, freestyle BMX, mountain bike trials, and cycle ball.

The world governing body is the Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI), founded in 1900 and headquartered in Aigle, Switzerland.

History

The first documented bicycle race was held on 31 May 1868 at the Parc de Saint-Cloud in Paris. The first organisation to govern the sport, the International Cycling Association, was founded in 1892; the UCI succeeded it in 1900. The Tour de France, first run in 1903, became the most famous road race in the world. Cycling has been part of the Olympic Games since the first modern Games in 1896.

Road racing

Road bicycle racing takes place on paved roads and includes one-day races and stage races lasting up to three weeks. The three Grand Tours — the Tour de France, Giro d'Italia, and Vuelta a España — are the sport's most prestigious stage races. Team tactics, drafting in the peloton, and specialist roles (climbers, sprinters, domestiques) define the discipline.

Other disciplines

Track cycling is held on banked velodromes and includes sprint, pursuit, and points races. Cyclo-cross combines road and off-road racing over short laps with obstacles requiring riders to dismount. Mountain bike racing covers cross-country and downhill formats. BMX includes racing over dirt tracks and freestyle disciplines. Time trials, held against the clock either individually or by team, appear across road and track competition.

Issues

The sport has repeatedly confronted doping, most notoriously the scandals involving Lance Armstrong and the Festina affair, leading to extensive anti-doping programmes such as the biological passport. Safety, particularly in road races sharing space with traffic and spectators, is an ongoing concern.
