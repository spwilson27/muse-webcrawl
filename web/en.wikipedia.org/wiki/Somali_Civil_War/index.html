Somali Civil War

The Somali Civil War is an ongoing conflict in Somalia that began in 1991 with the overthrow of the military regime of Siad Barre and the collapse of the Somali state. In the decades since, the country has passed through phases of warlord fragmentation, an Islamist insurgency, and an internationally backed effort to rebuild a federal government, while the self-declared Republic of Somaliland in the northwest has maintained relative stability without international recognition. Hundreds of thousands of people have died from fighting, famine, and disease, and millions have been displaced.

Mogadishu, the Somali capital, was devastated by factional fighting in the 1990s.

Background and collapse

Siad Barre seized power in 1969 and ruled increasingly repressively. Defeat in the Ogaden War with Ethiopia (1977–78) and clan-based repression spawned armed opposition movements; government atrocities against the Isaaq in the north in 1988 are regarded as an opening atrocity of the civil war. In January 1991 rebels of the United Somali Congress took Mogadishu and Barre fled. The victors immediately fell out, and no successor authority emerged: the country fragmented among clan-based factions and warlords.

International intervention and after

War combined with drought to produce a famine that killed roughly 300,000 people in 1991–92. A US-led Unified Task Force (Operation Restore Hope, December 1992) secured food deliveries, followed by the UN mission UNOSOM II. In October 1993 the Battle of Mogadishu — the "Black Hawk Down" incident — killed 18 US soldiers, and foreign forces withdrew by 1995. Numerous reconciliation conferences failed to restore a central government until the Transitional National Government (2000) and then the Transitional Federal Government (2004) were formed abroad with limited reach inside the country.

Islamic Courts, Ethiopia, and al-Shabaab

In 2006 the Islamic Courts Union seized Mogadishu, bringing a measure of order before Ethiopian forces, backed by the United States, invaded and installed the transitional government. A radical youth militia of the Courts, al-Shabaab, emerged as the core of an insurgency that eventually controlled much of south-central Somalia. African Union troops (AMISOM, later ATMIS/AUSSOM) and Somali offensives from 2011 pushed al-Shabaab from Mogadishu and major towns, but the group remains a potent guerrilla and terrorist force, carrying out major attacks including the October 2017 Mogadishu truck bombing that killed more than 500 people. A Federal Government of Somalia was established in 2012, and federal member states have gradually been formed, though elections, security, and relations between Mogadishu and the regions remain contested.
