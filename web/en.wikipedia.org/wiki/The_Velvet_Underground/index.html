The Velvet Underground was an American rock band formed in New York City in 1964 by singer-guitarist Lou Reed and Welsh multi-instrumentalist John Cale. Managed for a time by pop artist Andy Warhol, the band achieved little commercial success during its career but is now regarded as one of the most influential groups in the history of rock music, a foundational influence on punk, alternative, and indie rock.

History

Reed and Cale, joined by guitarist Sterling Morrison and drummer Angus MacLise (soon replaced by Maureen Tucker), formed the core of the band, named after a 1963 pulp paperback about the American sexual underground. Warhol incorporated them into his Exploding Plastic Inevitable multimedia shows and produced their debut album, The Velvet Underground & Nico (1967), featuring German singer Nico and Warhol's famous banana cover art. The album's stark songs about drugs, street life, and transgression sold poorly on release but became one of the most acclaimed albums of all time. After breaking with Warhol and losing Cale (replaced by Doug Yule), the band released White Light/White Heat (1968), The Velvet Underground (1969), and Loaded (1970), which included "Sweet Jane" and "Rock & Roll". Reed quit in 1970 and the band dissolved by 1973.

Legacy

Brian Eno famously remarked that while the first Velvet Underground album sold only about 30,000 copies, "everyone who bought one of those 30,000 copies started a band." The group was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1996.
