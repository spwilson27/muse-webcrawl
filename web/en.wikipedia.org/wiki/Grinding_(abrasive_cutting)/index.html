Grinding (abrasive cutting)

Grinding is a machining process that uses an abrasive wheel as the cutting tool. Each grain of abrasive on the wheel's surface cuts a small chip of material from the workpiece through shear deformation. Grinding is used to finish workpieces to close tolerances and fine surface finishes, and to machine very hard materials that are difficult to cut with conventional tooling.

Process

The grinding wheel consists of abrasive grains — commonly aluminium oxide, silicon carbide, cubic boron nitride or diamond — held together by a bond. As the wheel rotates at high speed against the workpiece, the sharp edges of the grains remove material; dull grains fracture or pull out, exposing fresh cutting edges, a behaviour known as self-sharpening. Coolant is usually applied to control heat, since grinding generates significant friction and can burn the work surface if uncontrolled.

Types of grinding

Common grinding operations include surface grinding on flat workpieces, cylindrical grinding (external and internal) on round workpieces, centreless grinding, creep-feed grinding for deep cuts, and tool and cutter grinding for sharpening cutting tools. Belt grinding and bench grinding are used for deburring and rough shaping. Tolerances of a few micrometres and surface finishes of 0.1 micrometres Ra or better are achievable.

Applications

Grinding is essential in bearing, automotive, aerospace and toolmaking manufacture, wherever hardened steels or ceramics require precision finishing. It is also used for sharpening blades and tools, and in construction for cutting stone and concrete with diamond wheels.
