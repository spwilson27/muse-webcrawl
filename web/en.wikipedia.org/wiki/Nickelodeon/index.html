Nickelodeon

Launched December 1, 1977 (as Pinwheel); April 1, 1979 (as Nickelodeon)

Owner Paramount Media Networks (Paramount Global)

Headquarters New York City, New York, U.S.

Nickelodeon (often shortened to Nick) is an American pay television channel and entertainment brand owned by Paramount Global through its Paramount Media Networks division. Launched on April 1, 1979, as the first cable channel for children, it primarily broadcasts programming aimed at children and adolescents aged 2 to 17, along with some family-oriented fare.

History

The channel's roots go back to Pinwheel, a children's program launched in 1977 on the QUBE interactive cable system in Columbus, Ohio. On April 1, 1979, the service was relaunched nationally as Nickelodeon. Early programming included Pinwheel and imported series; the channel initially carried no advertising. In 1984 the channel was restructured under president Geraldine Laybourne, adopting an advertising-supported model and the famous orange "splat" logo, becoming a ratings and cultural force for children.

Programming

Nickelodeon built its success on original series known as "Nicktoons," beginning in 1991 with Doug, Rugrats, and The Ren & Stimpy Show. Live-action successes included sketch comedy All That and series such as Clarissa Explains It All and Drake & Josh. The network's longest-running and most profitable franchise is SpongeBob SquarePants (premiered 1999). Other notable Nicktoons include Hey Arnold!, The Fairly OddParents, Avatar: The Last Airbender, and The Loud House. The annual Kids' Choice Awards, famous for "sliming" celebrities with green slime, is a signature event.

Related brands

Sister channels include Nick Jr. (for preschoolers), Nicktoons, TeenNick, and NickMusic. Nickelodeon Movies releases family films, and the brand has extended to theme-park attractions, hotels, and consumer products worldwide.
