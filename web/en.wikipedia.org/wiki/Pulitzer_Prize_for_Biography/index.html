Pulitzer Prize for Biography

The Pulitzer Prize for Biography is one of the fourteen American Pulitzer Prizes awarded annually for letters and drama. It honours a distinguished and appropriately documented biography by an American author published in the United States during the preceding calendar year. The category was established in 1917 as the Pulitzer Prize for Biography or Autobiography; autobiographies and memoirs later received their own category in 2023, and the prize was renamed accordingly.

History

The first winner, in 1917, was Laura E. Richards and Maud Howe Elliott, assisted by Florence Howe Hall, for Julia Ward Howe, 1819–1910, a biography of their mother. Distinguished later winners have included The Education of Henry Adams (autobiography, 1919), David McCullough's Truman (1993) and John Adams (2002), Ron Chernow's Washington: A Life (2011), and biographies of figures from Benjamin Franklin to Lyndon B. Johnson — Robert Caro's The Years of Lyndon Johnson volumes won in other categories and decades-long spans. In 2020 the prize went to Benjamin Moser for Sontag: Her Life and Work, and in 2023 Imani Perry's South to America was among the last winners under the combined Biography or Autobiography designation.

Selection

As with other Pulitzer categories, entries are reviewed by a jury of specialists, which forwards three finalists to the Pulitzer Prize Board, which selects the winner. The prize carries a cash award of $15,000.
