Spanish missions in the Sonoran Desert

The Spanish missions in the Sonoran Desert are a chain of Jesuit and later Franciscan mission churches founded from the late 17th century among the Indigenous peoples of the Pimería Alta — present-day northern Sonora, Mexico, and southern Arizona, United States. Many were established under the direction of the Italian Jesuit missionary Eusebio Francisco Kino.

History

Beginning in 1687, Kino founded more than twenty missions and visitas, introducing cattle, wheat and new crops alongside Catholic teaching, and mapping the region (proving Baja California was a peninsula). After the expulsion of the Jesuits in 1767, Franciscans took over the missions. Notable churches include Mission San Xavier del Bac near Tucson — the "White Dove of the Desert", completed in 1797 and still serving the Tohono O'odham community — San José de Tumacácori, now a U.S. National Historical Park, and, in Sonora, missions at Caborca, Magdalena de Kino (where Kino is buried) and San Ignacio.

Architecture

Built of adobe, fired brick and stone with lime plaster, the churches combine Spanish colonial forms with local materials and labour, featuring bell towers, domes and richly painted interiors adapted to the desert light and climate.
