Retainer (orthodontics)

An orthodontic retainer is a custom-made appliance, usually of wires and acrylic or clear plastic, that holds teeth in position after orthodontic treatment with braces or aligners has finished. Because the periodontal ligament and surrounding soft tissues take months to reorganise, and because teeth continue to shift throughout life, retention is regarded as an essential final phase of orthodontic treatment; without it, teeth tend to relapse toward their original positions.

Types

Removable retainers include the Hawley retainer, a wire-and-acrylic plate invented by Charles A. Hawley in the early twentieth century, and vacuum-formed clear retainers (such as the Essix type), which cover the whole arch and resemble clear aligners. Fixed (bonded) retainers consist of a wire bonded to the tongue-side surfaces of the front teeth and can remain in place for many years; they are often used where relapse risk is high. Patients are usually instructed to wear removable retainers full-time initially, then at night only, often indefinitely ("night-time for a lifetime" is a common recommendation).

Care

Retainers require regular cleaning and periodic review; lost or broken removable retainers should be replaced quickly to prevent tooth movement, and the wire of a bonded retainer can debond unnoticed, allowing individual teeth to drift.
