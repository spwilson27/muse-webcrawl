Match play

Match play is a scoring system for golf in which a player, or team, earns a point for each hole in which they have bested their opponents; as opposed to stroke play, in which the total number of strokes is counted over one or more rounds. In match play the winner is the player or side that wins more holes than the margin left to play.

Scoring

Each hole is won, lost, or halved (tied). The state of the match is expressed relative to holes won: a side is "2 up" or "3 down", or the match is "all square". A match ends before 18 holes when one side leads by more holes than remain to be played; the result is recorded as, for example, "5 and 4" — five holes up with four to play. When a side is only as many holes ahead as remain, it is called "dormie" and cannot lose the match outright. If the match is tied after the final hole, competitions usually continue to extra holes (sudden death) or remain halved for team points.

Concessions

Uniquely in match play, a player may concede the opponent's next stroke (commonly short putts, "gimmes"), concede a hole, or concede the entire match at any time. A concession cannot be declined or withdrawn.

Rules differences

Match play penalties differ from stroke play: many breaches result in loss of hole rather than penalty strokes, and the order of play ("the honour") matters strategically. Opponents may also agree to halve a hole in advance of its completion only within specific limits, and under the Rules of Golf players in a match are expected to protect their own rights, since an opponent may overlook a breach.

Strategy

Because only the outcome of each hole matters, not the margin, match play encourages risk-taking: a disastrous hole costs only that hole, while conservative play may yield many halved holes. Skilled players adjust tactics to their opponent's position — playing aggressively when behind on the hole, safely when the opponent is in trouble.

Major match play events

The Ryder Cup, Solheim Cup and Presidents Cup use match play formats (foursomes, fourballs and singles) between teams. Professional events include the WGC Match Play and the World Match Play Championship (first held in 1964). Match play was the standard competitive format of early golf and of the first Amateur and Open-era competitions, and remains the format of most club knockout tournaments and the U.S. Amateur.
