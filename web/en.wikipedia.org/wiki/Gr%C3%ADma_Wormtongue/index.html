Gríma, known as Wormtongue, is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's The Lord of the Rings. He is the chief counsellor of Théoden, King of Rohan, and secretly an agent of the wizard Saruman, whose corrupting advice he feeds to the king in order to weaken Rohan.

Role in the narrative

By the time Gandalf and his companions arrive at Edoras in The Two Towers, Théoden has sunk into premature age and debility under Gríma's influence. Gandalf frees the king from this influence, and Gríma is exposed; Théoden offers him a chance to prove his loyalty in battle, but Gríma instead flees to Saruman at Isengard. In The Return of the King, Gríma accompanies Saruman to the Shire, where, after years of abuse, he finally turns on his master and kills him during the Scouring of the Shire, before being shot by Hobbit archers.

Name and characterisation

The name Gríma derives from Old English for "mask" or "spectre", and "Wormtongue" evokes serpentine, poisonous speech. Scholars have compared his insinuating counsel to the whisperers and slanderers of Old Norse and Old English literature. In Peter Jackson's film adaptations he is portrayed by Brad Dourif.
