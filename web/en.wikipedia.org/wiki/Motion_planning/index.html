Motion planning

Motion planning computes a collision-free path for a robot

Motion planning, also called the piano mover's problem, is a computational problem to find a sequence of valid configurations that moves an object from a source to a destination. The term is used in computational geometry, computer animation, robotics, and computer games.

Concepts

The basic motion planning problem is to compute a continuous path in the object's configuration space (C-space) that connects a start configuration to a goal configuration while avoiding collision with known obstacles. The set of configurations that avoid collision is called the free space. A motion planner is said to be complete if it always finds a solution when one exists, and sampling-based planners such as the probabilistic roadmap (PRM) and the rapidly exploring random tree (RRT) are probabilistically complete.

Algorithms

Grid-based methods overlay a regular grid on configuration space and search it with algorithms such as A*. Sampling-based methods instead probe the configuration space with random samples, avoiding explicit construction of the obstacle geometry, and have proved effective for robots with many degrees of freedom. Other approaches include potential fields, cell decomposition, and trajectory optimisation such as CHOMP and TrajOpt.

Applications

Motion planning is central to autonomous vehicles, robotic manipulation, surgical robotics, digital prototyping and assembly analysis, protein folding studies, and the movement of non-player characters and cameras in games and animated films.
