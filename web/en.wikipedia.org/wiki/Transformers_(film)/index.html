Transformers is a 2007 American science fiction action film directed by Michael Bay, based on the Transformers toy line and media franchise. It is the first instalment of the live-action Transformers film series. The film stars Shia LaBeouf as Sam Witwicky, a teenager who becomes caught in the war between the heroic Autobots and the villainous Decepticons, two factions of alien robots that can disguise themselves as ordinary vehicles and machinery.

Plot

Thousands of years after a civil war destroyed the planet Cybertron, the life-giving AllSpark cube has been lost on Earth. Both the Autobots, led by Optimus Prime, and the Decepticons, led by the temporarily frozen Megatron, converge on Earth to recover it. Teenager Sam Witwicky unknowingly holds the key to its location — the coordinates etched on his great-great-grandfather's glasses — and his battered Chevrolet Camaro turns out to be the Autobot scout Bumblebee. As the US military, a secret agency called Sector 7, and the robots battle over the AllSpark, the conflict culminates in a destructive fight in Mission City, where Sam uses the cube to destroy Megatron.

Production

Producers Don Murphy and Tom DeSanto developed the project from 2003, with Steven Spielberg as executive producer and Roberto Orci and Alex Kurtzman writing the screenplay. Michael Bay, initially reluctant, joined after studying the franchise. The robot designs were substantially more complex and mechanical than the original toys, and were realised by Industrial Light & Magic with extensive digital effects — the transformations alone involved thousands of moving parts. The US armed forces provided substantial support, lending aircraft, vehicles and personnel.

Reception

The film was a major commercial success, grossing about $709.7 million worldwide against a budget of around $150 million and becoming one of the highest-grossing films of 2007. Reviews were mixed: critics praised the visual effects and action spectacle while criticising the thin plot, human characterisation and running time. It was nominated for three Academy Awards, including Best Visual Effects. The film launched a franchise of sequels — Revenge of the Fallen (2009), Dark of the Moon (2011), Age of Extinction (2014) and The Last Knight (2017) — and the spin-off/prequel Bumblebee (2018).
