Pulmonary trunk

The pulmonary trunk (also called the pulmonary artery or main pulmonary artery) is the large artery that carries deoxygenated blood from the right ventricle of the heart to the lungs. It is the only artery in the adult body that carries deoxygenated blood.

Structure

The pulmonary trunk arises from the conus arteriosus of the right ventricle, guarded at its origin by the pulmonary valve. It ascends and passes backward for about 5 cm before dividing, at the level of the fifth thoracic vertebra, into the right and left pulmonary arteries, which supply the respective lungs. The bifurcation is connected to the aortic arch by the ligamentum arteriosum, the remnant of the fetal ductus arteriosus.

Development and clinical significance

In the fetus the ductus arteriosus shunts most blood past the unventilated lungs into the aorta. Failure of the ductus to close after birth causes a patent ductus arteriosus. Enlargement of the pulmonary trunk may be a sign of pulmonary hypertension, and obstruction by clot constitutes a pulmonary embolism.
