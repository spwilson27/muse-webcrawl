Santander, Spain

Country: Spain
Autonomous community: Cantabria (capital)
Population: about 172,000

Santander (Spanish: [santanˈdeɾ]) is the capital of the autonomous community of Cantabria, Spain. It has a population of about 172,000. It is a port city located in the northern coast of the Iberian Peninsula, facing the Cantabrian Sea.

History

The settlement traces origins to the Roman port Portus Victoriae Iuliobrigensium. The name Santander is thought to derive from Saint Emeterio (Santemter, Santero, Santander). The city became an important trading port in the Middle Ages and, in the 19th and early 20th centuries, a fashionable summer resort favoured by the Spanish royal family, for whom the Palacio de la Magdalena was built (1909–1911) on the Magdalena Peninsula. A devastating fire in February 1941 destroyed much of the historic centre, which was subsequently rebuilt.

Sights and culture

Landmarks include the Gothic Santander Cathedral, the Palacio de la Magdalena, the Centro Botín arts centre designed by Renzo Piano (opened 2017), the El Sardinero beaches, and the lighthouse at Cabo Mayor. Banco Santander, one of the world's largest banks, was founded in the city in 1857. The city is also known for the nearby prehistoric cave art of the Altamira region and the Maritime Museum of Cantabria.
