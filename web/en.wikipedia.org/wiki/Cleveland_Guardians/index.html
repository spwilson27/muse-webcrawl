The Cleveland Guardians are an American professional baseball team based in Cleveland, Ohio. They compete in Major League Baseball (MLB) as a member club of the American League Central Division. The team has played its home games at Progressive Field since 1994.

History

The franchise traces its roots to 1894 and joined the American League as one of its charter franchises in 1901, playing under names including the Blues, Bronchos and Naps (after star player Nap Lajoie). In 1915 it adopted the name "Indians", under which it won the World Series in 1920 — led by player-manager Tris Speaker — and in 1948, with Lou Boudreau, Bob Feller, Satchel Paige and Larry Doby, the first African American player in the American League.

The 1948 championship was followed by the longest title drought in the American League. The club returned to the World Series in 1995, 1997 and 2016, losing the last in seven games to the Chicago Cubs. The 2017 team won 22 consecutive games, an American League record.

Name change

After decades of controversy over the "Indians" name and the Chief Wahoo logo, the club announced in July 2021 that it would become the Guardians, taking the name from the "Guardians of Traffic" statues on Cleveland's Hope Memorial Bridge. The new name took effect after the 2021 season.
