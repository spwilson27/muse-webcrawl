Addison's disease

Other namesPrimary adrenal insufficiency
SymptomsFatigue, weight loss, abdominal pain, darkening of the skin
Usual causeAutoimmune destruction of the adrenal glands
TreatmentHormone replacement (hydrocortisone, fludrocortisone)

Addison's disease, also known as primary adrenal insufficiency, is a rare long-term endocrine disorder in which the adrenal glands do not produce enough steroid hormones — principally cortisol and often aldosterone. It is named after Thomas Addison, who described the condition in 1855.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms generally develop slowly and may include fatigue, muscle weakness, weight loss, low blood pressure, salt craving and hyperpigmentation of the skin. An acute deterioration, an adrenal crisis, can be life-threatening and is a medical emergency.

Causes and treatment

In developed countries the most common cause is autoimmune adrenalitis; worldwide, tuberculosis remains an important cause. Diagnosis is typically confirmed with blood tests and an ACTH stimulation test. Treatment involves lifelong replacement of the missing hormones, usually with oral hydrocortisone and fludrocortisone.
