Izates II

Izates II (c. AD 1 – c. 55) was king of Adiabene, a Parthian vassal kingdom in northern Mesopotamia, from about AD 34 until his death. He is best known, from the account of the historian Josephus, for his conversion to Judaism, following his mother Queen Helena of Adiabene.

Conversion

Advised by the Jewish merchant Ananias and later by another Jew, Eleazar, Izates adopted Jewish practices and eventually accepted circumcision, despite fears that a Jewish king would be unacceptable to his subjects. His mother Helena became a benefactor of Jerusalem, famously sending aid during a famine in the reign of Claudius and building palaces and a monumental family tomb north of the city.

Reign

Izates navigated the rivalries of the Parthian court, surviving plots by nobles opposed to his religion and intervening in Parthian succession disputes. His many sons were sent to Jerusalem to be educated in Jewish language and law. He was succeeded by his elder brother Monobazus II.
