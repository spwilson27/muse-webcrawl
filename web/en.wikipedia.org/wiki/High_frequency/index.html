High frequency

Frequency range3–30 MHz
Wavelength range100–10 m
Also calledShortwave band; decameter band

High frequency (HF) is the ITU designation for the range of radio frequencies between 3 and 30 megahertz (MHz). It is also called the decameter band or decameter wave, because its wavelengths range from one to ten decameters (ten to one hundred metres). Frequencies immediately below HF are called medium frequency (MF), while the next band is known as very high frequency (VHF).

Propagation

The HF band is the principal part of the shortwave spectrum. Its distinctive feature is skywave propagation: HF waves are refracted by the ionosphere back toward Earth, allowing communication over intercontinental distances, with the usable frequencies varying by time of day, season, and the solar cycle. Groundwave propagation along Earth's surface is also used over short ranges.

Uses

Applications of HF include international shortwave broadcasting, amateur radio, military and diplomatic communications, aviation and maritime mobile services, over-the-horizon radar, automatic link establishment systems, and time signals such as WWV. Citizens band radio sits at about 27 MHz, near the top of the band.
