The APG III system of flowering plant classification is the third version of a modern, mostly molecular-based, system of plant taxonomy published by the Angiosperm Phylogeny Group (APG) in October 2009 in the Botanical Journal of the Linnean Society. It replaced the APG II system of 2003 and was in turn superseded by the APG IV system in 2016.

Content

APG III recognized 59 orders and 415 families, abandoning the optional "bracketed" families allowed in APG II in favour of single, larger, monophyletic families (for example, merging Agapanthaceae and Alliaceae into Amaryllidaceae). Fourteen new orders were formally described relative to earlier systems, including Petrosaviales, and a number of previously unplaced families and genera were assigned to orders. The system retained the informal major groupings: magnoliids, monocots (with commelinids), and eudicots (with core eudicots divided into rosids and asterids).

Philosophy

The APG systems classify plants strictly by monophyly inferred from DNA sequence data, favouring stability of names where the evidence permits and avoiding formal ranks above order. APG III continued the policy of naming only well-supported clades and leaving poorly resolved parts of the tree as lists of incertae sedis taxa rather than forcing uncertain placements.
