Sclerostin

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sclerostin

Gene: SOST (human)

Location: Chromosome 17q21.31

Produced by: Osteocytes

Function: Inhibitor of Wnt signalling and bone formation

Sclerostin is a glycoprotein secreted principally by osteocytes — the mature bone cells embedded in mineralised matrix — that acts as a potent inhibitor of bone formation. It is encoded by the SOST gene in humans. Sclerostin binds to the LRP5 and LRP6 co-receptors on osteoblasts and thereby blocks the canonical Wnt signalling pathway, restraining the production of new bone. It functions as a key local brake in the control of bone mass and as a messenger by which osteocytes respond to mechanical loading: loading suppresses SOST expression, permitting bone formation, whereas unloading raises it.

Discovery and genetics

The protein came to attention through two rare inherited disorders of excessive bone growth. Sclerosteosis, in which patients develop massively thickened bones, nerve compression and sometimes early death, is caused by loss-of-function mutations in SOST. Van Buchem disease, a milder related condition, results from a deletion of a downstream regulatory element of SOST in a Dutch population. These findings showed that sclerostin normally limits bone formation, and that its partial absence produces high bone mass without major detriment — making it an attractive drug target.

Clinical significance

The monoclonal antibody romosozumab, which binds and neutralises sclerostin, is an anabolic treatment for osteoporosis: by releasing the Wnt pathway it both stimulates bone formation and reduces resorption. Approved in 2019 in the United States, Europe and elsewhere for patients at high risk of fracture, it is given as monthly injections for one year. Because sclerostin also participates in vascular and other Wnt-dependent processes, trials monitored cardiovascular safety, and the drug carries warnings for patients with recent heart attack or stroke.

Sclerostin biology also informs research into other skeletal diseases, including bone loss in chronic kidney disease, in which sclerostin levels rise early, and the mechanobiology of how exercise builds bone.
