Cost–volume–profit analysis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cost–volume–profit (CVP) analysis is a method of cost accounting that looks at the impact that varying levels of costs and volume have on operating profit. It is used by managers to make short-term economic decisions, and is also commonly known as break-even analysis.

Overview

CVP analysis rests on a classification of costs into fixed costs, which do not change with the level of activity, and variable costs, which change in proportion to output. From these, the contribution margin — sales revenue minus variable costs — can be calculated. The break-even point is the level of sales at which total revenue equals total cost and profit is zero:

Break-even units = Fixed costs ÷ (Price per unit − Variable cost per unit)

Assumptions

The basic model assumes that selling price per unit, variable cost per unit, and total fixed costs are constant; that everything produced is sold; and that costs can be cleanly divided into fixed and variable components. Although these assumptions are simplifications, the model remains a widely used planning tool.

Applications

Managers use CVP analysis to determine the sales volume needed to reach a target profit, to evaluate the effects of changes in price or cost structure, and to assess the margin of safety — the extent to which sales can fall before losses begin.
