Story within a story

Story within a story
Also calledFrame story, nested narrative
Classic examplesOne Thousand and One Nights, The Canterbury Tales, Heart of Darkness

A story within a story, also known as a frame narrative or nested narrative, is a literary device in which a character within a story narrates another story. The outer (frame) narrative sets the scene for the inner tale, which may itself contain further nested stories, a structure sometimes compared to a set of Russian nesting dolls.

History

The device is ancient: the Indian Panchatantra and the Mahabharata employ nested tales, and the frame of Scheherazade telling stories to postpone her execution structures One Thousand and One Nights. In medieval Europe, Geoffrey Chaucer's The Canterbury Tales and Boccaccio's Decameron used pilgrimage and flight from plague as frames for collections of tales.

Functions

Frame narratives can establish the reliability or unreliability of a narrator, comment thematically on the inner story, organise collections of otherwise independent tales, and create parallels or contrasts between the levels of narration. In film and television, the device appears in works such as The Princess Bride and Forrest Gump.

Examples

  • Heart of Darkness — Marlow's tale within an unnamed narrator's account
  • Wuthering Heights — Nelly Dean's narration within Lockwood's
  • Frankenstein — Victor's tale within Walton's letters
  • The Name of the Rose and Cloud Atlas — deeply nested structures
