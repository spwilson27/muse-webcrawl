Grok

Grok is a neologism coined by American writer Robert A. Heinlein for his 1961 science fiction novel Stranger in a Strange Land. In Heinlein's invented Martian language it literally means "to drink" and metaphorically means to understand something so thoroughly that it becomes part of oneself — to merge, blend, and lose identity in group experience.

Meaning in the novel

The protagonist Valentine Michael Smith, a human raised by Martians, uses "grok" for a mode of comprehension deeper than intellectual analysis: to grok something is to take it into oneself completely, as water is taken in by a drinker. The word's exact definition is deliberately elusive, and Smith's struggle to explain it drives much of the novel's philosophy.

Usage

"Grok" passed into countercultural and then hacker slang in the 1960s–1980s, meaning to understand intuitively or profoundly, especially computer systems; it appears in the Jargon File and in Unix culture (the grok pattern tool, later a Logstash filter, took its name from it). The word's popularity led to its inclusion in major English dictionaries. More recently the name has been adopted for commercial products, including an AI chatbot launched in 2023, trading on the sense of deep comprehension.
