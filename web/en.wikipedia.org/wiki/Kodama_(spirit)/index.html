Kodama (spirit)

OriginJapanese folklore (Shinto)
HabitatOld trees, forests
Japanese木霊, 木魂 (kodama)

Kodama (木霊 or 木魂) are spirits in Japanese folklore that inhabit trees, particularly ancient ones. The term is also used to denote the tree in which such a spirit resides, and by extension an echo — the spirit was traditionally held responsible for the reverberation of sound in mountains and valleys.

Beliefs

In Shinto belief, kodama dwell in certain trees, and cutting down such a tree is said to bring misfortune or a curse upon the village. Old and remarkable trees are often marked with a sacred rope (shimenawa) to indicate the spirit's presence and protect the tree. Folklore holds that kodama take human or ghostly forms when they appear, and that when a tree harbouring a kodama is felled, it may bleed or cry out.

In popular culture

Kodama are familiar to modern audiences from the 1997 Studio Ghibli film Princess Mononoke, in which they are depicted as small, pale, bobble-headed forest spirits whose presence indicates a healthy forest.
