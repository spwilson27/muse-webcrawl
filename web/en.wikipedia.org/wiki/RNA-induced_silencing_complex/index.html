RNA-induced silencing complex

The RNA-induced silencing complex, or RISC, is a multiprotein complex, specifically a ribonucleoprotein, which functions in gene silencing via a variety of pathways at the transcriptional and translational levels. Using a small RNA guide—either small interfering RNA (siRNA) or microRNA (miRNA)—RISC finds complementary messenger RNA (mRNA) targets and either cleaves them or represses their translation, thereby downregulating gene expression. RISC is the effector machinery of RNA interference (RNAi), a process first described in the nematode Caenorhabditis elegans by Andrew Fire and Craig Mello.

Composition and loading

The catalytic heart of RISC is an Argonaute protein (in humans, Ago2 is the main "slicer"). The loading pathway begins when the RNase III enzyme Dicer processes long double-stranded RNA or hairpin precursors into short duplexes of about 21–23 nucleotides. With the help of double-stranded RNA-binding partners (TRBP in humans), one strand—the guide strand—is loaded into Argonaute while the passenger strand is discarded or cleaved. In mammals, additional proteins including the fragile X protein family and GW182 proteins associate with the mature complex, the latter mediating deadenylation and decay of targets in miRNA pathways.

Mechanism of action

  • Cleavage: when the guide RNA is fully complementary to a target mRNA (typical of siRNAs), Ago2 slices the target opposite nucleotides 10–11 of the guide, and the fragments are degraded.
  • Translational repression: partial pairing (typical of miRNAs, centred on the "seed" region, nucleotides 2–8) recruits machinery that blocks translation and accelerates removal of the poly(A) tail, leading to decapping and decay.

Biological roles and applications

RISC-mediated silencing regulates development and cell differentiation through endogenous miRNAs, defends against viruses and transposons, and guides related silencing systems such as piRNA pathways in the germline. Deliberately designed siRNAs loaded into RISC are used as research tools and as therapeutics: the drug patisiran (approved in 2018) was the first RNAi therapy to reach the market, treating hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis.
