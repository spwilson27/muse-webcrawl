Polypodiopsida

A leptosporangiate fern with sori on the underside of its fronds.

Polypodiopsida is a class of vascular plants, commonly known as the leptosporangiate ferns. Under the Pteridophyte Phylogeny Group classification of 2016 (PPG I), it is the largest class of ferns, containing the great majority of living fern species — roughly 10,000 or more — in the single order Polypodiales and several smaller orders.

Characteristics

Members of the class are defined by their leptosporangia: sporangia that develop from a single initial cell and typically have a wall one cell thick and a distinctive annulus of thickened cells that helps fling the spores free. This contrasts with the eusporangiate ferns (classes Psilotopsida and Marattiopsida), whose sporangia arise from several cells.

Diversity and ecology

Polypodiopsida includes tree ferns (Cyatheales), the aquatic ferns (Salviniales), and the vast order Polypodiales, which alone accounts for most fern diversity. Ferns in this class occur on every continent except Antarctica and in habitats from tropical rainforests to deserts; many are epiphytes, growing on other plants.

Classification

In PPG I the class contains the orders Osmundales, Hymenophyllales, Gleicheniales, Schizaeales, Salviniales, Cyatheales and Polypodiales. The last of these includes the familiar families Polypodiaceae, Dryopteridaceae, Pteridaceae and Aspleniaceae.
