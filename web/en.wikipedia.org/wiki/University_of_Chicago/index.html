University of Chicago

The University of Chicago (UChicago) is a private research university in Chicago, Illinois, founded in 1890 with funding from John D. Rockefeller and the American Baptist Education Society. Located in Hyde Park, it is known for rigorous academics and its influence across economics, law, and the sciences.

History

Under founding president William Rainey Harper, the university adopted a research-intensive model, admitted women and minorities early, and pioneered the quarter system and liberal-arts Core curriculum. In 1942 Enrico Fermi's team achieved the first self-sustaining nuclear chain reaction beneath Stagg Field.

Academics

Its schools include the undergraduate College, Booth School of Business, Law School, Pritzker School of Medicine, and Harris School of Public Policy. The Chicago school of economics and the law-and-economics movement originated here; affiliates have won around 100 Nobel Prizes.

Campus

The Gothic-revival campus is a designated botanical garden, with landmarks including Rockefeller Chapel and the Robie House nearby.
