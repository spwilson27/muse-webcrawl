Leonardo Helicopters

Leonardo Helicopters is the helicopter manufacturing division of the Italian aerospace and defence group Leonardo S.p.A. It traces its heritage to Agusta of Italy and the British Westland Helicopters, which merged in 2001 as AgustaWestland; the business was absorbed into Finmeccanica (renamed Leonardo in 2017) and now operates under the Leonardo Helicopters brand. It is one of the world's leading producers of civil and military rotorcraft.

History

Agusta, founded by Count Giovanni Agusta, began building helicopters under licence in the 1950s before developing its own designs, including the successful A109 light twin (1971) and the A129 Mangusta attack helicopter. Westland, based at Yeovil in England, had its own long line of designs, including the Lynx. GKN and Finmeccanica merged the two businesses as AgustaWestland in 2001; Finmeccanica took full control in 2004. Major programme wins included the VH-71 presidential helicopter (later cancelled), the AW101 Merlin, and the AW139, which became one of the best-selling medium helicopters worldwide. In 2016 the group adopted a "One Company" model, and AgustaWestland's activities continued as Leonardo's helicopter division.

Products

The company's range spans light single- and twin-engine helicopters through to heavy-lift and tiltrotor aircraft, including:

  • AW09 (light single)
  • AW109 and AW119
  • AW139 (medium twin)
  • AW149 and AW189
  • AW101 (heavy, three-engine)
  • AW249 attack helicopter and AW609 tiltrotor (in development and certification)
  • Uncrewed and optionally piloted rotorcraft systems

Operations

Principal sites include Cascina Costa and Vergiate in Italy and Yeovil in the United Kingdom, with additional production and support facilities in Poland (through PZL-Świdnik) and the United States, and a global customer support network.
