Archilochus

Archilochus (Ancient Greek: Ἀρχίλοχος Arkhílokhos; c. 680 – c. 645 BC) was a Greek lyric poet of the Archaic period from the island of Paros. He is celebrated for his versatile and innovative use of poetic meters and as the earliest Greek author to compose almost entirely on the theme of his own emotions and experiences. The ancient Greeks ranked him with Homer and Hesiod, and Alexandrian scholars placed him at the head of the iambic poets.

Life

According to tradition, Archilochus was the son of Telesicles, a leading citizen of Paros, and a slave woman named Enipo, a background that marked him as an outsider. He took part in the Parian colonization of Thasos in the northern Aegean and fought against Thracian tribes on the mainland opposite. His poems bitterly attack a certain Lycambes, who allegedly broke a promise of marriage to his daughter Neobule; legend held that the invective was so savage that Lycambes and his daughters hanged themselves. He was said to have been killed in battle against the Naxians.

Poetry

Archilochus wrote elegies, iambic trimeters, trochaic tetrameters, and epodes, much of it in a blunt, vivid, first-person voice. His famous fragment boasting of abandoning his shield in battle — "some Saian exults in my shield... I saved my life; I'll get another just as good" — mocked heroic conventions and shocked ancient moralists. Other fragments praise wine, comradeship, and endurance: the celebrated "fox and hedgehog" line, "The fox knows many things, but the hedgehog knows one big thing," survives because later authors quoted it.

Reception

Only fragments survive, preserved in quotations and on papyri from Oxyrhynchus, including the long Cologne Epode discovered in 1974. Pindar, Aristophanes, Horace, and Quintilian all acknowledged his force; Horace claimed to have brought Archilochus's meters into Latin. His shrine, the Archilocheion on Paros, treated him as a hero, and inscriptions there recorded his life and miracles.
