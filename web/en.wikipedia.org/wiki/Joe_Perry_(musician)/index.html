Anthony Joseph Pereira (born September 10, 1950), better known by his stage name Joe Perry, is an American musician best known as the founding member, lead guitarist, backing and occasional lead vocalist of the rock band Aerosmith.

Perry formed Aerosmith in New Hampshire in 1970 with Steven Tyler, with whom — as the "Toxic Twins" — he co-wrote much of the band's catalogue, including "Dream On" era material, "Walk This Way", "Back in the Saddle" and "Sweet Emotion". He left the band in 1979, formed the Joe Perry Project, and returned in 1984 ahead of Aerosmith's commercial comeback.

Recognition

Perry was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as a member of Aerosmith in 2001 and into the Songwriters Hall of Fame with Tyler in 2013. He has also released several solo albums and performed with the supergroup Hollywood Vampires. He is known for his blues-based playing and extensive guitar collection, and has released a signature line of hot sauces.
