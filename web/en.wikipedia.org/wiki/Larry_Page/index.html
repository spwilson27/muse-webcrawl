Larry Page

Born: Lawrence Edward Page, March 26, 1973, East Lansing, Michigan, U.S.

Occupation: Computer scientist, internet entrepreneur

Known for: Co-founding Google and Alphabet Inc.; PageRank

Lawrence Edward Page (born March 26, 1973) is an American businessman, computer scientist, and internet entrepreneur best known for co-founding Google with Sergey Brin. Page was the chief executive officer of Google from 1997 until August 2001, then from April 2011 until July 2015, when he moved to become CEO of Alphabet Inc., a role he held until December 2019.

Early life and education

Page was born in East Lansing, Michigan, to Carl and Gloria Page, both computer science professors at Michigan State University. He attended East Lansing High School and graduated from the University of Michigan with a Bachelor of Science in computer engineering. He later enrolled in a computer science PhD program at Stanford University, where he met Sergey Brin.

Founding of Google

At Stanford, Page's research project explored the mathematical properties of the World Wide Web via its link structure. With Brin, he developed the PageRank algorithm, which ranked web pages by their links, and built a prototype search engine called BackRub. In 1998 the two founded Google Inc. Page invented PageRank, the foundational algorithm of Google Search, and received the Marconi Prize in 2004 for it.

Later career

As Alphabet's CEO, Page oversaw the company's "Other Bets" in areas such as life sciences, self-driving cars, and artificial intelligence. In December 2019, Page and Brin stepped down from their executive roles at Alphabet, remaining board members and controlling shareholders. Page has also invested in flying-car ventures, including Kitty Hawk and Opener, and is one of the wealthiest people in the world.
