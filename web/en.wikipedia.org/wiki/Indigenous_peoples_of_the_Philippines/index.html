Indigenous peoples of the Philippines

The Indigenous peoples of the Philippines are the ethnolinguistic groups that retained traditional cultures distinct from the Christianised lowland majority shaped by Spanish and American colonisation. They comprise an estimated 10–20% of the country's population — some 14 to 17 million people in over 100 groups.

Major groups

In the northern highlands of Luzon, the Igorot peoples — including the Kankanaey, Ibaloi, Ifugao, Bontoc and Kalinga — are famed for the rice terraces of the Cordillera. Mangyan peoples live on Mindoro; the Lumad groups of Mindanao include the Manobo, T'boli and Bagobo; and Negrito groups such as the Aeta and Ati are among the archipelago's earliest inhabitants. The Moro peoples — Muslim Filipinos such as the Tausug, Maranao and Maguindanaon — are sometimes counted separately.

Rights

The Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act of 1997 (IPRA) recognised ancestral domain rights and created the National Commission on Indigenous Peoples; certificates of ancestral domain title have since been issued over millions of hectares. Ongoing issues include land encroachment, mining, displacement by development projects and the protection of cultural heritage.
