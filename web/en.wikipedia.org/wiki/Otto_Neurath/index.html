Otto Neurath (10 December 1882 – 22 December 1945) was an Austrian philosopher of science, sociologist and political economist. A leading member of the Vienna Circle of logical positivists, he was the movement's chief organiser and publicist, an advocate of physicalism and the unity of science, and the inventor of the ISOTYPE system of pictorial statistics.

Life

Neurath studied economics and sociology in Vienna and Berlin. After the First World War he directed socialisation planning in Munich, for which he was briefly imprisoned after the fall of the Bavarian Soviet Republic. In "Red Vienna" he directed the Social and Economic Museum (Gesellschafts- und Wirtschaftsmuseum), where with the artist Gerd Arntz and Marie Reidemeister he developed ISOTYPE, a method of conveying statistics through easily read pictograms, used in exhibitions and popular publications throughout the world.

Philosophy

Neurath championed the unity of science movement and edited its International Encyclopedia of Unified Science. His physicalism held that all scientific statements should be formulated in a single spatio-temporal language. In the protocol-sentence debate he argued against any incorrigible foundation for knowledge, comparing science to sailors who must rebuild their ship on the open sea—"Neurath's boat", a famous metaphor for anti-foundationalism. He fled Austria in 1934 for the Netherlands and in 1940 for England, where he died in Oxford in 1945.
