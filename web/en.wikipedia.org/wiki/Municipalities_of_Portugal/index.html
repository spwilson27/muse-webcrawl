Municipalities of Portugal

A municipality (Portuguese: município or concelho) is the second-level administrative subdivision of Portugal, as defined by the 1976 Constitution. There are 308 municipalities in Portugal: 278 in mainland Portugal, 19 in the autonomous region of the Azores and 11 in the autonomous region of Madeira. Municipalities are typically larger than the civil parishes (freguesias) into which they are subdivided, and are governed by a municipal chamber (câmara municipal, the executive) and a municipal assembly (assembleia municipal, the deliberative body), both elected every four years.

History

The municipality has been the most stable subdivision of Portugal since the foundation of the country in the 12th century. Medieval municipalities received royal charters (forais) granting them self-government. The modern system took shape through reforms in the 19th century, notably under Mouzinho da Silveira in 1832 and the administrative codes that followed. The number of municipalities has changed little since the mid-20th century; the most recent creation was Vizela in 1998.

Structure

Each municipality is governed by two bodies. The câmara municipal is the executive, headed by a president (mayor) and a number of councillors (vereadores) proportional to the electorate. The assembleia municipal is the legislative body, composed of directly elected members together with the presidents of the parish councils within the municipality. Municipal responsibilities include urban planning, water supply, sanitation, local roads, primary education facilities, culture and social welfare.

Largest and smallest

The most populous municipality is Lisbon, the capital, followed by Sintra, Vila Nova de Gaia, Porto and Cascais. The largest by area is Odemira in the Alentejo, one of the largest municipalities in the European Union by surface area. The least populous is Corvo in the Azores, with only a few hundred inhabitants. Portuguese municipalities vary enormously in size: metropolitan municipalities can have several hundred thousand residents, while rural interior municipalities may have only a few thousand.

Subdivisions

Municipalities are subdivided into civil parishes (freguesias). A major administrative reform in 2013 reduced the number of parishes from 4,259 to 3,091 by merging smaller ones. Municipalities are in turn grouped into districts on the mainland, intermunicipal communities, and the metropolitan areas of Lisbon and Porto for certain purposes.
