Arts in Australia

The Sydney Opera House, opened in 1973

The arts in Australia encompass the visual arts, literature, music, theatre, dance, film and Indigenous cultural practices of Australia. The oldest continuous artistic traditions belong to Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples, whose rock art, bark painting, carving and songlines date back tens of thousands of years.

Visual arts

Colonial art began with natural-history illustration and landscape painting. In the late 19th century the Heidelberg School — including Tom Roberts, Arthur Streeton and Frederick McCubbin — created a national landscape style in the plein air tradition. Twentieth-century modernists such as Sidney Nolan, Albert Tucker and Margaret Preston reshaped Australian painting, while the Papunya Tula movement from 1971 brought Aboriginal acrylic painting to international attention.

Performing arts and film

Major institutions include the Australian Ballet, Opera Australia, the Sydney Theatre Company and Bangarra Dance Theatre. Australian cinema, from The Story of the Kelly Gang (1906) through the 1970s New Wave to directors such as Peter Weir and Jane Campion, has an international profile, while literature counts Patrick White, Australia's only Nobel laureate in Literature (1973), among its figures.

Funding

The principal federal funding body is Creative Australia (formerly the Australia Council for the Arts), complemented by state and territory agencies and a large cultural sector centred on institutions such as the National Gallery of Australia and the Sydney Opera House.
