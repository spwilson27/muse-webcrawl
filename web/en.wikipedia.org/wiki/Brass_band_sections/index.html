Brass band sections

A British-style brass band is a musical ensemble made up of a standard complement of brass instruments plus a percussion section. Because players move between bands and compete in contests, the instrumentation has been standardized since the 19th century, most famously following the format promoted by the Cyfarthfa Band and the contest movement in northern England. A full contesting band fields 25 brass players and up to three percussionists.

Sections

The instruments are organized by voice and function, almost all of them conical-bore instruments in B♭ or E♭, which gives the ensemble its characteristic mellow blend:

Roles

Within the cornet section, the principal (or leader) cornet is the band's concertmaster figure. The soprano cornet plays the highest parts, often above the solo cornets. The repiano cornet acts as a floating soloist, filling gaps across the band. The flugelhorn bridges the cornet and horn sections, while the tenor horns carry the inner harmonies historically supplied by saxhorns. Euphoniums, with their rich tenor voice, are frequently given virtuoso solo passages, and the basses provide the foundation of the ensemble.

Scoring

Brass band music is written in transposing parts, nearly all in the treble clef — even the tubas read treble clef on the British system, except for the bass trombone, which reads bass clef. This uniform system allows players to change instruments relatively easily, a practical inheritance from the amateur working-class bands of the industrial north and midlands of England where the movement originated.
