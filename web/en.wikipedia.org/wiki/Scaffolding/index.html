Scaffolding

A tube and fitting scaffold providing temporary access to a building under renovation.

Scaffolding, also called scaffold or staging, is a temporary structure used to support a work crew and materials to aid in the construction, maintenance, and repair of buildings, bridges, and other structures. Scaffolds are widely used on site to give access to heights and areas that would otherwise be hard to reach; unsafe scaffolding has the potential to result in death or serious injury, and it is among the most cited construction safety hazards.

History

Sockets in the walls around Stonehenge suggest scaffolding was used in its construction, and sockets found in the walls of the Lascaux caves suggest a scaffold system was used to paint its ceilings more than 17,000 years ago. In ancient Egypt, Greece, and China, wooden scaffolds supported builders of temples and walls; bamboo scaffolding remains common in parts of Asia today. Leonardo da Vinci sketched scaffold designs, and in 1913 the steel tube-and-coupler system was patented, enabling the standardized modular scaffolds used worldwide.

Types

Tube and coupler (fitting) scaffolds are assembled from steel or aluminium tubes joined by right-angle and swivel couplers, offering maximum adaptability. Prefabricated frame scaffolds use welded H-frames for speed on regular facades. Suspended scaffolds hang from roofs by ropes or chains and can be raised or lowered. Bamboo scaffolding, lashed with straps, is still ubiquitous in Hong Kong and southern China. Other forms include mobile towers, cantilever scaffolds, and system scaffolds with proprietary rosette or wedge connections.

Components and safety

A typical scaffold comprises standards (vertical tubes), ledgers (horizontal tubes), transoms, braced bays, base plates or castors, and working platforms of boards or decks, with guardrails and toe boards at open edges. Regulations such as OSHA standards in the United States require scaffolds to support at least four times the maximum intended load, to be erected and inspected by competent persons, and to provide fall protection above specified heights.
