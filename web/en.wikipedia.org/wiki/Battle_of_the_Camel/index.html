Battle of the Camel

The Battle of the Camel (Arabic: معركة الجمل), also known as the Battle of Basra, was fought in December 656 CE near Basra, Iraq, between the forces of the caliph Ali ibn Abi Talib and a rebel coalition led by Talha ibn Ubayd Allah, Zubayr ibn al-Awwam and Aisha, widow of the Prophet Muhammad. It was the first battle of the First Fitna and the first major battle fought between Muslims.

Background

After the murder of the caliph Uthman, Talha, Zubayr and Aisha demanded vengeance for his blood and rejected Ali's caliphate, marching from Mecca to Basra to gather support. Ali followed them to Iraq, hoping for negotiation; attempts at reconciliation nearly succeeded before fighting broke out, in circumstances the sources describe confusedly.

The battle

The fiercest fighting raged around the camel carrying Aisha in her litter (howdah), from which the battle takes its name: successive standard-bearers and camel-handlers were cut down defending it. Talha was killed by an arrow; Zubayr left the field and was killed shortly afterwards while at prayer. When Ali ordered the camel hamstrung and the litter lowered, resistance ended. Casualty figures in the sources range from several thousand to over ten thousand.

Aftermath

Ali treated the defeated honourably, pardoning the rebels and escorting Aisha back to Medina, where she withdrew from politics. The victory secured Iraq for Ali, who moved his capital to Kufa, but the bloodshed between companions of the Prophet scandalised the community and opened the way to Ali's greater confrontation with Mu'awiya at Siffin the following year.
