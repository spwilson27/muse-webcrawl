Cyperus papyrus, commonly known as papyrus, papyrus sedge, paper reed, or Nile grass, is a species of aquatic flowering plant belonging to the sedge family Cyperaceae. It is a tender herbaceous perennial, native to Africa, that forms tall stands of reed-like swamp vegetation in shallow water.

Description

This tall, robust aquatic plant can grow 4 to 5 metres (13 to 16 feet) high. It forms a grass-like clump of triangular green stems that rise from thick, woody rhizomes. Each stem is topped by a dense cluster of thin, bright green, thread-like rays around 10 to 30 cm (4 to 12 in) in length, resembling a feather duster when the plant is young. Greenish-brown flower clusters eventually appear at the ends of the rays, giving way to brown, nut-like fruits.

Distribution

Papyrus is native to wetland areas of Africa, including the Sudd wetlands of South Sudan, the Okavango Delta, and historically the banks and marshes of the Nile. It was once abundant in the Nile Delta of Egypt but is now much reduced there in the wild.

History and uses

The plant is famous as the source of papyrus paper, one of the earliest writing materials, manufactured in ancient Egypt from the pith of the stem. The Ancient Egyptians also used the plant for boats, mats, ropes, sandals, and many other purposes. The explorer Thor Heyerdahl built the reed boat Ra from papyrus to demonstrate that early transoceanic voyages were possible.

Today papyrus is widely grown as an ornamental plant for water gardens and ponds, and it plays an ecological role as natural filtration vegetation in constructed wetlands.
