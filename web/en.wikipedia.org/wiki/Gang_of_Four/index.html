Gang of Four

The Gang of Four (simplified Chinese: 四人帮) was a Maoist political faction composed of four Chinese Communist Party officials — Jiang Qing, Zhang Chunqiao, Yao Wenyuan and Wang Hongwen — who rose to prominence during the Cultural Revolution (1966–1976) and controlled the power organs of the Party and state in its later years. They were arrested in October 1976, shortly after Mao Zedong's death, and were later tried and blamed for the worst excesses of the Cultural Revolution.

Rise to power

Jiang Qing, Mao's wife, together with Shanghai radicals Zhang Chunqiao and Yao Wenyuan and the young worker leader Wang Hongwen, formed the core of the radical faction that directed cultural and ideological policy during the Cultural Revolution. By the early 1970s they dominated propaganda, education and culture and struggled against more pragmatic leaders such as Zhou Enlai and Deng Xiaoping.

Fall and trial

After Mao's death on September 9, 1976, his successor Hua Guofeng, with Marshal Ye Jianying and Wang Dongxing, ordered the Gang's arrest on October 6, 1976, ending the Cultural Revolution. In the televised trial of 1980–81, Jiang Qing and Zhang Chunqiao received death sentences with reprieve (later commuted to life imprisonment), Wang Hongwen life imprisonment, and Yao Wenyuan a 20-year sentence.
