Khadi

Khadi (Hindi: खादी), also called khaddar, is a hand-spun and hand-woven natural fibre cloth originating in eastern regions of the Indian subcontinent. It is made from cotton, and may also include silk or wool, spun on a spinning wheel called a charkha.

History and the freedom movement

Mahatma Gandhi promoted khadi from 1918 onward as a symbol of self-reliance and resistance to British colonial rule: boycotting imported Lancashire cloth and spinning one's own yarn became central acts of the Swadeshi movement and the Indian independence struggle. Gandhi himself spun daily on the charkha, which became an emblem on Congress flags.

Characteristics

Khadi is valued for being cool in summer and warm in winter. Its slightly rough texture softens with washing. Because yarn is hand-spun, the fabric has a distinctive unevenness prized by weavers and designers.

Modern era

In independent India, the Khadi and Village Industries Commission (est. 1956) promotes production and sale; khadi remains both a political symbol and a fashion fabric.
