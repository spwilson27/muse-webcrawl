Japan–Korea Treaty of 1910

Signed22 August 1910
Effective29 August 1910
PartiesEmpire of Japan; Korean Empire
EffectAnnexation of Korea by Japan

The Japan–Korea Treaty of 1910, also known as the Japan–Korea Annexation Treaty, was signed on 22 August 1910 by Korean Prime Minister Yi Wan-yong and Japanese Resident-General Terauchi Masatake. It formally annexed the Korean Empire to the Empire of Japan, ending Korea's existence as an independent state and beginning 35 years of Japanese colonial rule.

Background

Following Japan's victory in the Russo-Japanese War, Korea had become a Japanese protectorate under the Eulsa Treaty of 1905, which deprived it of diplomatic sovereignty; Emperor Gojong was forced to abdicate in 1907 after secretly appealing to the Hague Peace Conference. The 1910 treaty, proclaimed on 29 August 1910, transferred "completely and forever" the sovereignty of Korea to the Emperor of Japan.

Legality and legacy

The treaty's validity has long been disputed in Korea, where it is regarded as having been coerced and never properly ratified by the Korean emperor. In the 1965 Treaty on Basic Relations between Japan and South Korea, both governments agreed that all treaties concluded on or before 22 August 1910 were "already null and void". Japanese rule ended with Japan's defeat in 1945.
