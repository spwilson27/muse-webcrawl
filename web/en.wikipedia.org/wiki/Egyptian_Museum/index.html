The Museum of Egyptian Antiquities, commonly known as the Egyptian Museum or the Cairo Museum, in Cairo, Egypt, houses the world's largest collection of pharaonic antiquities. Located on Tahrir Square, the museum holds some 120,000 items, with a representative selection on display.

History

An earlier museum was founded under Auguste Mariette at Bulaq in 1858, moving to Giza in 1891. The present purpose-built building, designed by French architect Marcel Dourgnon in a neoclassical style, opened in 1902. Its distinctive pink facade became a landmark of downtown Cairo.

Collection

Highlights include the treasures of Tutankhamun, including his gold funerary mask; the Royal Mummy Room; statues of Khufu, Khafre and Menkaure; the Narmer Palette; and jewellery, papyri and coffins spanning predynastic to Greco-Roman Egypt.

Grand Egyptian Museum

Many masterpieces, including the full Tutankhamun collection, are being transferred to the new Grand Egyptian Museum near the Giza pyramids, though the Tahrir museum remains open and important. In 2021 the royal mummies were moved in the celebrated Pharaohs' Golden Parade to the National Museum of Egyptian Civilization.
