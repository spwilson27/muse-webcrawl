Knot (mathematics)

In mathematics, a knot is an embedding of a circle in three-dimensional Euclidean space, considered up to continuous deformations (ambient isotopy). Knots differ from ordinary loops in rope in having no free ends; two knots are equivalent if one can be deformed into the other without cutting.

Knot theory

Knot theory, a branch of topology, studies such embeddings and their invariants — quantities unchanged under deformation, used to tell knots apart. Knots are classified by crossing number; the simplest nontrivial knot is the trefoil (3 crossings), followed by the figure-eight knot (4 crossings). Important invariants include the Alexander polynomial, the Jones polynomial (1984), knot groups, and tricolourability. Prime knots are those that cannot be decomposed as a connected sum of two nontrivial knots.

Applications

Knot theory has found applications in molecular biology, where DNA and enzymes act by knotting and unknotting, in chemistry, and in statistical mechanics and quantum field theory.
