Monclova is a city and seat of the municipality of the same name in the northern Mexican state of Coahuila. Located in the state's central region at an elevation of about 600 metres, it is one of the most important industrial cities in northern Mexico, historically centred on the steel industry.

History

Monclova was founded on 25 July 1577 by Spanish conquistadors and is considered the first settlement of Coahuila; it was re-founded several times during the colonial period and for a time served as capital of the province of Coahuila y Tejas. The city's modern growth came with the founding in 1942 of the steel company Altos Hornos de México (AHMSA), long one of the largest steel producers in Mexico, whose furnaces dominated the city skyline and economy. AHMSA's financial collapse in the 2020s brought severe economic difficulties to the city.

Economy and culture

Besides steel, Monclova's industries include metal products, rail cars, and food processing. The city is home to the Acereros de Monclova baseball team of the Mexican League, and landmarks include the Santiago Apóstol parish church, the Coahuila y Tejas museum, and the Xochipilli park complex.
