Washita River

The Washita River is a river in the south-central United States, flowing through the Texas Panhandle and Oklahoma. It runs generally eastward for about 295 miles (475 km) before joining the Red River, forming part of Lake Texoma at its mouth region. Its name derives from a Native American expression meaning "good hunting grounds".

Geography

The river rises in Roberts County in the Texas Panhandle and flows east through western Oklahoma, passing near Cheyenne and Clinton, then south and southeast past Anadarko and Chickasha, and through the Arbuckle Mountains region before emptying into the Red River basin near Tishomingo. Fossil Reservoir (Foss Reservoir) was impounded on the river in Washita County.

History

The river valley was home to Plains Indian peoples including the Cheyenne and Arapaho. It is notable as the site of the Battle of Washita River in November 1868, when Lt. Col. George Armstrong Custer's 7th Cavalry attacked the Southern Cheyenne village of Chief Black Kettle, an event commemorated at the Washita Battlefield National Historic Site. The river also figures in the campaigns that followed, including the Red River War of 1874–75.
