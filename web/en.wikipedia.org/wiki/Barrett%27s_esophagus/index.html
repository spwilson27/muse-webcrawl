Barrett's esophagus

Barrett's esophagus is a condition in which the normal squamous lining of the lower esophagus is replaced by columnar epithelium resembling that of the intestine (intestinal metaplasia), usually as a result of chronic gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD). It is named after the British surgeon Norman Barrett and is considered a premalignant condition because it increases the risk of esophageal adenocarcinoma.

Causes and risk factors

Long-standing reflux of stomach acid and bile damages the esophageal lining, which heals with acid-resistant intestinal-type cells. Risk factors include male sex, age over 50, central obesity, smoking, and a family history of the condition.

Diagnosis and surveillance

Diagnosis requires upper endoscopy showing salmon-coloured mucosa extending above the gastroesophageal junction, confirmed by biopsy showing intestinal metaplasia. Patients typically undergo periodic surveillance endoscopies to detect dysplasia.

Treatment

Management centres on acid suppression with proton-pump inhibitors. Dysplastic Barrett's may be treated with endoscopic mucosal resection or radiofrequency ablation to remove the abnormal lining and reduce cancer risk.
