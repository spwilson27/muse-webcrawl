The Château de Chenonceau is a French château spanning the River Cher, near the village of Chenonceaux in the Indre-et-Loire department of the Loire Valley. It is one of the best-known châteaux of the Loire and is nicknamed the "Château des Dames" (the Ladies' Château) for the succession of women who built, owned and preserved it.

History

The original château was built in 1513–1515 for Katherine Briçonnet. It was later acquired by King Henry II for his mistress Diane de Poitiers, who added the arched bridge across the Cher in 1556–1559. After Henry's death, his widow Catherine de' Medici forced Diane to exchange Chenonceau for Chaumont and added the famous two-storey gallery on the bridge (1576–1577), making the château the setting of splendid court festivals. In the 18th century Louise Dupin preserved it through the French Revolution. During the First World War the gallery served as a hospital ward, and in the Second World War the château straddled the demarcation line, aiding escapes from occupied France.

Today

Owned by the Menier family since 1913, Chenonceau is open to the public and is among the most visited châteaux in France, known for its gardens — Diane's garden and Catherine's garden flank the keep — and its floral displays. It forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing of the Loire Valley.
