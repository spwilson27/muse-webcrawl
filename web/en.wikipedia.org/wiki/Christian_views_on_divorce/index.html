Christian views on divorce

Christian views on divorce vary considerably between denominations, reflecting different interpretations of the teachings of Jesus and Paul in the New Testament. Almost all Christian traditions regard marriage as sacred and lifelong, but they differ on whether, and on what grounds, a marriage may be dissolved and a divorced person may remarry.

Biblical basis

In the Gospels, Jesus teaches that "what God has joined together, let no one separate" (Mark 10:9). Matthew's Gospel includes an exception clause permitting divorce in cases of porneia (sexual immorality). Paul, in 1 Corinthians 7, addresses mixed marriages and allows separation where an unbelieving spouse departs — the basis of the "Pauline privilege".

By tradition

The Roman Catholic Church teaches that a ratified and consummated sacramental marriage cannot be dissolved by any human power, though a declaration of nullity (annulment) may establish that no valid marriage existed. The Eastern Orthodox Church, while upholding the ideal of indissolubility, permits ecclesiastical divorce and remarriage (up to a third marriage) as an act of pastoral economy (oikonomia). Most Protestant churches permit divorce and remarriage on biblical grounds such as adultery or desertion, and many have become more permissive in practice over the twentieth century; some conservative evangelical bodies continue to restrict remarriage while the former spouse lives.
