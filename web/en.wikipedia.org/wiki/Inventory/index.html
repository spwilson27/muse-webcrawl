Inventory (American English) or stock (British English) refers to the goods and materials that a business holds for the purpose of resale, production or utilisation. Inventory management is a discipline primarily about specifying the shape and placement of stocked goods. It is required at different locations within a facility or within many locations of a supply network to precede the regular and planned course of production and stock of materials.

The concept of inventory, stock or work in process has been extended from manufacturing systems to service businesses and projects, by generalising the definition to be "all work within the process of production — all work that is or has occurred prior to the completion of production."

Types of inventory

Manufacturers typically classify inventory into three categories. Raw materials are materials and components scheduled for use in making a product. Work in process (WIP) consists of materials and components that have begun their transformation into finished goods but are not yet complete. Finished goods are completed products ready for sale. A fourth category, maintenance, repair and operations (MRO) supplies, covers items used in production that do not become part of the finished product.

Inventory management

Businesses hold inventory to buffer against uncertain demand, to smooth production, to exploit quantity discounts, and to hedge against price increases or supply disruptions. Holding inventory incurs costs — storage, handling, insurance, spoilage and the opportunity cost of tied-up capital — commonly estimated at 20–30 percent of inventory value per year. Models such as the economic order quantity (EOQ) balance ordering costs against holding costs to determine optimal order sizes. Approaches such as just-in-time (JIT) production, popularised by Toyota, seek to minimise stock by synchronising deliveries with production.

Accounting

In accounting, inventory is a current asset on the balance sheet. Because inventory valuation affects cost of goods sold and therefore profit, accounting standards permit methods such as FIFO (first in, first out), LIFO (last in, first out, permitted under US GAAP but not IFRS), and weighted-average cost. Inventory turnover, calculated as cost of goods sold divided by average inventory, measures how quickly stock is sold and replaced.
