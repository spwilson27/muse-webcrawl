Kōkeʻe State Park is a state park located in northwestern Kauaʻi in the Hawaiian Islands, covering an area of 4,345 acres (17.6 km²) on a plateau between roughly 3,200 and 4,200 feet (975 and 1,280 m) elevation. The park lies at the end of State Highway 550, adjacent to Waimea Canyon State Park, and offers views into the Kalalau Valley and toward the Nā Pali Coast.

Features

The park contains roughly 45 miles (72 km) of hiking trails, including routes to waterfalls, through native forest, and along the rim of Waimea Canyon and the Kalalau Valley lookouts. The Kōkeʻe Natural History Museum, operated by a nonprofit organization, interprets the park's natural and cultural history. The park also provides campgrounds, cabins, and picnic areas amid koa and ʻōhiʻa forest.

Flora and fauna

Because of its elevation, Kōkeʻe has a cooler, wetter climate than coastal Kauaʻi. Its forests are home to many of Hawaiʻi's endemic forest birds, including the ʻapapane, ʻiʻiwi, and Kauaʻi ʻamakihi, and native plants such as the mokihana berry, Kauaʻi's official flower and lei material. The region is also known for wild chickens and for plum trees introduced in the 19th century that fruit in summer.
