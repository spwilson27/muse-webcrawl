Margery Kempe

Margery Kempe (c. 1373 – after 1438) was an English Christian mystic, known for dictating The Book of Margery Kempe, a work considered by some to be the first autobiography in the English language.

Born Margery Brunham in Bishop's Lynn (now King's Lynn), Norfolk, the daughter of a prosperous merchant and mayor, she married John Kempe and bore fourteen children. After a period of mental crisis following her first childbirth, she reported a vision of Christ that began her religious life. She negotiated a chaste marriage, undertook ambitious business ventures (a brewery and a horse mill) that failed, and embarked on pilgrimages to Jerusalem, Rome, Santiago de Compostela, and sites in northern Europe, including a visit to Julian of Norwich, whose counsel she sought around 1413.

The Book

Kempe was noted for her loud, public weeping at the Passion and was repeatedly examined by church authorities, including Archbishop Thomas Arundel, on suspicion of Lollardy, but was never convicted. Illiterate herself, she dictated her Book to scribes in the 1430s. The sole surviving manuscript, rediscovered in 1934, records her visions, travels, trials, and spiritual struggles; the work is now a major source for late medieval religious life and women's writing.
