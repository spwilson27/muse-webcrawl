Shark Island concentration camp

The Shark Island concentration camp (German: Konzentrationslager Haifischinsel) was a camp operated by the German Empire in German South West Africa, on Shark Island off the coastal town of Lüderitz (in present-day Namibia). Between 1905 and 1907 it held Herero and Nama prisoners captured during the Herero Wars, and it is regarded by historians as a central site of the Herero and Namaqua genocide.

History

After the Battle of Waterberg (1904) and General Lothar von Trotha's extermination order, thousands of Herero were driven into the Omaheke desert or captured. Prisoners were deported to camps, of which Shark Island — cold, windswept and exposed to the Atlantic — proved the deadliest. Prisoners, including women and children, were subjected to forced labour on harbour and railway construction, starvation rations, disease and abuse. Mortality was extremely high: by some estimates more than half, and possibly around 3,000, of the prisoners died. Nama prisoners, including followers of Hendrik Witbooi, were sent there from late 1905. The camp closed in April 1907 under public pressure in Germany, and survivors were transferred elsewhere.

Legacy

Prisoners' skulls were collected and sent to Germany for racial research; many human remains were repatriated to Namibia in ceremonies beginning in 2011. In 2021 the German government formally recognized the atrocities as genocide and pledged €1.1 billion in development support to Namibia. A memorial stands on the island today, though much of the site is now occupied by a campsite, a fact that has drawn criticism.
