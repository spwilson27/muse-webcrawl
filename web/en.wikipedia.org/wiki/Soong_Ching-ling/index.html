Soong Ching-ling
Born: 27 January 1893, Shanghai, Qing China
Died: 29 May 1981, Beijing, China
Spouse: Sun Yat-sen (m. 1915; d. 1925)
Offices: Vice Chairperson of the PRC; Honorary President of the PRC (1981)

Soong Ching-ling (27 January 1893 – 29 May 1981) was a Chinese political figure, the wife of Sun Yat-sen, founder of the Republic of China, and one of the famous Soong sisters. She held high office in the People's Republic of China and was named Honorary President shortly before her death.

Early life and marriage

Born in Shanghai to the wealthy businessman and missionary Charlie Soong, she was educated at Wesleyan College in Georgia, United States. In 1915 she married Sun Yat-sen, despite her family's objections, and worked as his secretary and political partner until his death in 1925.

Political career

After Sun's death she broke with Chiang Kai-shek's Kuomintang over its purge of communists, and spent periods in exile in Moscow and Europe. During the Second Sino-Japanese War she founded the China Defence League to raise relief funds. In 1949 she sided with the communists, becoming a vice chairperson of the Central People's Government. Revered as the "mother of modern China" and a link to Sun Yat-sen's legacy, she survived the Cultural Revolution with Zhou Enlai's protection and in May 1981 was admitted to the Chinese Communist Party and made Honorary President of the People's Republic.
