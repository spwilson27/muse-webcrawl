Rear naked choke

The rear naked choke (RNC) is a chokehold in martial arts applied from an opponent's back. It is often called hadaka-jime ("naked strangle") in judo and mata-leão ("lion killer") in Brazilian jiu-jitsu. The word "naked" refers to the fact that the hold does not require gripping the opponent's clothing (gi), unlike collar chokes.

Technique

From back control — typically secured with hooks or a body triangle — the attacker slides one arm under the opponent's chin so that the crook of the elbow aligns with the chin and the forearm and biceps compress the carotid arteries on both sides of the neck. The other hand is placed behind the opponent's head to reinforce the choke and the arms squeeze together. As a "blood choke," it restricts cerebral blood flow and can render an opponent unconscious within seconds if they do not submit (tap out).

Use in competition

The rear naked choke is among the most common finishing submissions in mixed martial arts and submission grappling, because the back mount is considered the most dominant position and the choke works regardless of size disparity. It has been the finishing technique in numerous UFC championship bouts.

Safety

Because it can cause unconsciousness quickly, the technique is trained under supervision with immediate release when a partner taps or loses consciousness.
