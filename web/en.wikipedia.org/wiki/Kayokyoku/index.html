Kayōkyoku

Kayōkyoku (Japanese: 歌謡曲, "popular song") is a genre of Japanese popular music that formed the basis of modern J-pop. The term originally described Western-influenced popular songs of the pre-war era, in contrast to traditional Japanese music.

History

Kayōkyoku emerged in the late 1920s with the spread of records and radio, blending Western harmonies and instrumentation with Japanese pentatonic melodies. After the Second World War it dominated the Japanese charts, encompassing both the mood kayō ballad style and enka, its more traditional offshoot.

Legacy

As Western-style pop and rock took hold in the 1980s and 1990s, kayōkyoku gave way to J-pop as the mainstream label, but its songwriting conventions — strong melody, sentimental lyrics — remained influential.
