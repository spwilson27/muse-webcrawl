Human mitochondrial DNA haplogroup

In human genetics, a human mitochondrial DNA haplogroup is a haplogroup defined by differences in human mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA). Haplogroups are used to represent the major branch points on the mitochondrial phylogenetic tree. Understanding the evolutionary path of female ancestry has helped population geneticists trace the matrilineal inheritance of modern humans back to human origins in Africa and the subsequent spread across the globe.

mtDNA haplogroups
RootMitochondrial Eve (haplogroup L)
InheritanceMatrilineal (mother to offspring)
Macro-haplogroupsL, M, N (and descendants incl. R)

Phylogeny

The deepest branches of the mtDNA tree are African: haplogroup L and its subgroups (L0–L6). All non-African lineages descend from two daughter groups of L3: macro-haplogroups M and N, which left Africa roughly 60,000–70,000 years ago. From N descends R, ancestral to most European and many Asian lineages, including haplogroups H, V, J, T, U and K common in Europe.

Major haplogroups

  • Africa: L0, L1, L2, L3
  • Europe: H, V, U, K, J, T, X, I, W
  • Asia: A, B, C, D, F, G, M (subgroups), Z
  • Americas (Indigenous): A, B, C, D, X
  • Oceania: P, Q, B

Uses

mtDNA haplogroups are used in genetic genealogy, forensic identification, studies of ancient DNA, and research into migration history. Because mtDNA is inherited only from the mother and does not recombine, it provides a clear record of maternal ancestry, though it represents only one lineage of a person's many ancestors.
