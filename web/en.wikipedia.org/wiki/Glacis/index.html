Glacis

A glacis (from French glacis, "slope") in military engineering is an artificial slope of earth, typically in front of a fortification, sloping gently downward away from the defences so that attackers are exposed to the defenders' fire for as long as possible while being unable to return effective fire. The term is also used in geomorphology and in armoured vehicle design.

Fortification

In early modern bastion fortification, the glacis formed the outermost element of the fortress's defences, beyond the ditch and the covered way. Its gentle, cleared slope denied besiegers any cover as they approached, exposed them to grazing fire from the ramparts, and made it difficult to bring siege artillery to bear on the walls themselves. The slope was commonly kept clear of buildings and vegetation; in many fortress towns the glacis survived as a ring of open ground or parkland after the walls were demolished.

Other uses

In geomorphology, a glacis is a broad, gently sloping erosional or depositional surface, particularly at the foot of mountains in arid regions, broadly comparable to a pediment. In tank design, glacis plate refers to the sloped front armour plate of a hull, whose angle increases the effective thickness of armour against incoming projectiles — an example of sloped armour.
