Reasons and Persons

Author: Derek Parfit
Published: 1984
Publisher: Oxford University Press
Subject: Moral philosophy, personal identity

Reasons and Persons is a 1984 book by the British philosopher Derek Parfit, widely regarded as one of the most important works of moral philosophy of the twentieth century. The book is divided into four parts, covering theories of rationality and morality, personal identity, and our obligations to future generations.

Contents

The first part examines self-interest theory and consequentialism, arguing that commonly held moral beliefs may be collectively self-defeating. The second part develops Parfit's reductionist view of personal identity: what matters in survival is not identity itself but "Relation R" — psychological connectedness and continuity with any cause. This view has radical implications for how we should think about rationality, morality, and the separateness of persons.

The third part explores the non-identity problem: the difficulty of saying that choices which determine who will exist can be wrong even when they make the people who exist worse off than they might otherwise have been. The final part considers population ethics, presenting the "repugnant conclusion" — the implication that for any population of people with very high quality of life, there is a better population consisting of a vastly larger number of people whose lives are barely worth living.

Reception

The book has been highly influential in ethics, influencing fields from population axiology to climate ethics. Parfit continued his work in the three-volume On What Matters (2011–2017).
