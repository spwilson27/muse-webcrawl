Retinotopy

Retinotopy is the mapping of visual input from the retina to neurons, particularly those neurons within the visual stream. In the visual system, the spatial arrangement of light striking the retina is preserved (with distortions) in the arrangement of neurons in the lateral geniculate nucleus (LGN) and the primary visual cortex (V1). Many areas of the brain, including V1, V2, V3, the LGN, and parts of the parietal and temporal lobes, are organised retinotopically.

Properties

The retinotopic map in V1 is not a uniform projection: the cortical magnification factor means that a disproportionately large area of cortex is devoted to the fovea, where visual acuity is highest, while the periphery is represented by much less cortical tissue. Adjacent points in the visual field generally map to adjacent points in V1, though the map contains discontinuities, notably at the representations of the vertical meridian.

Study

Retinotopic maps were first inferred from lesion studies — notably observations by Tatsuji Inouye and Gordon Holmes of visual field defects in soldiers with occipital gunshot wounds — and later mapped directly with single-unit electrophysiology and functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) using phase-encoded visual stimuli.
