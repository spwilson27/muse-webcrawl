Dogra–Tibetan War

The Dogra–Tibetan War, also known as the Sino-Sikh War, was fought between the Dogra Raja Gulab Singh of Jammu, under Sikh Empire suzerainty, and Tibet under the Qing dynasty of China, in 1841–1842.

Course of the war

General Zorawar Singh Kahluria led a Dogra army across the Himalayas, conquering Ladakh's dependencies and defeating Tibetan forces at Taklakot. However, the onset of the harsh Himalayan winter, overextended supply lines, and a Tibetan-Qing counteroffensive led to Zorawar Singh's death at the Battle of Toyo in December 1841. In 1842 the Dogras won the Battle of Chushul, and the war ended with the Treaty of Chushul (September 1842), which restored the status quo ante bellum and confirmed the traditional Ladakh–Tibet boundary.
