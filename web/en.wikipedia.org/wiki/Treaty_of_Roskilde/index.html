Treaty of Roskilde

Signed26 February 1658 (O.S.)
LocationRoskilde, Denmark
PartiesSweden; Denmark–Norway
ContextDano-Swedish War (1657–58)

Territories Denmark–Norway ceded to Sweden under the Treaty of Roskilde.

The Treaty of Roskilde was concluded on 26 February 1658 during the Second Northern War between Frederick III of Denmark–Norway and Charles X Gustav of Sweden. It was one of the most consequential treaties in Scandinavian history, transferring extensive Danish territory to Sweden.

Background

In the winter of 1657–58, Charles X Gustav led his army across the frozen Danish straits — the famous March Across the Belts — first crossing the Little Belt to Funen and then the Great Belt to Zealand, appearing before Copenhagen. With the capital threatened, Denmark was compelled to negotiate.

Terms

Denmark ceded the provinces of Scania, Halland (which had been under Swedish occupation since 1645), Blekinge and the island of Bornholm in the east, and the Norwegian provinces of Bohuslän and Trøndelag in the north. Denmark also agreed to prevent hostile fleets from passing through the Danish straits into the Baltic, to renounce its anti-Swedish alliances, and to provide troops and ships to Sweden.

Aftermath

Charles X Gustav soon broke the peace and invaded again in August 1658, besieging Copenhagen. His death in 1660 led to the Treaty of Copenhagen, which moderated Roskilde: Bornholm and Trøndelag were returned to Denmark–Norway, but Scania, Halland and Blekinge remained Swedish — giving Sweden approximately its modern southern borders. The cession triggered a long process of "Swedification" in the Scanian provinces and armed resistance by the snapphane guerrillas during the Scanian War.
