Publius Helvius Pertinax (1 August 126 – 28 March 193) was Roman emperor for the first three months of 193, the "Year of the Five Emperors". The son of a freedman, he rose through a distinguished military and civil career to become emperor after the assassination of Commodus, but was murdered by the Praetorian Guard after only 87 days.

Career

Born at Alba Pompeia in Liguria, Pertinax began as a teacher of grammar before obtaining a commission in the army. He distinguished himself in the Parthian War and on the Danube frontier, served as governor of several provinces including Moesia, Dacia and Syria, suppressed unrest in Britain as its governor, and was proconsul of Africa and twice prefect of Rome. He was urban prefect when Commodus was assassinated on 31 December 192, and the Senate acclaimed him emperor the same night.

Reign and death

Pertinax set about restoring discipline and the public finances: he sold Commodus's luxuries, tightened court spending, and tried to curb the privileges of the Praetorians, paying them only part of the donative they expected. The guards, resentful of his discipline, stormed the palace on 28 March 193 and killed him when he confronted them. The throne was then notoriously auctioned to Didius Julianus. Septimius Severus later avenged Pertinax, deified him, and took his name.

Assessment

Ancient historians such as Cassius Dio, who knew him, praised his integrity and competence while judging that he attempted reform too hastily given the corruption of the times; he is remembered as a capable emperor destroyed by the military anarchy that followed Commodus.
