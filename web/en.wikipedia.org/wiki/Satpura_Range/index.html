Satpura Range

The Satpura Range is a range of hills in central India, running roughly 900 km east–west across Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Chhattisgarh and Gujarat, roughly parallel to and south of the Vindhya Range, with the Narmada River flowing in the rift valley between them and the Tapti River to the south. Its highest point is Dhupgarh (1,350 m) near Pachmarhi, the range's best-known hill station.

Geology and geography

The Satpuras are a horst range of ancient, largely basaltic and granite rocks, much of it capped by Deccan Trap lavas. The name "Satpura" derives from Sanskrit for "seven folds". Major sub-ranges include the Rajpipla hills in the west, the Mahadeo hills in the centre and the Maikal hills in the east, which meet the Eastern Ghats.

Ecology

The teak and sal forests of the Satpuras are among central India's most important wildlife habitats. Protected areas include Satpura, Kanha, Pench, Melghat, Bori and Pachmarhi reserves, which together form a major tiger landscape; the Satpura Tiger Reserve is noted for tigers, leopards, sloth bears, gaur and dholes. The region is also home to Gond and other Adivasi communities.

Places

Pachmarhi, a colonial-era hill station and cantonment, is the principal resort. The pilgrimage town of Amarkantak near the Maikal hills is the source of the Narmada and Son rivers.
