Don't Stop Believin'

"Don't Stop Believin'" is a song by American rock band Journey, released in 1981 as the second single from their seventh studio album, Escape. Written by Steve Perry, Neal Schon, and Jonathan Cain, it reached number nine on the Billboard Hot 100 on its original release and later became one of the best-selling digital tracks of the 20th century.

Composition

The song is notable for its structure: the title and chorus are withheld until the final minute, with the verses telling of a "small-town girl" and a "city boy" "born and raised in South Detroit" (a lyrical licence, as no area of Detroit is generally called that) taking the "midnight train goin' anywhere". It opens with Jonathan Cain's distinctive piano riff over Neal Schon's guitar and a driving rhythm section.

Cultural resurgence

Although only a modest hit in 1981, the song became ubiquitous in the 2000s through its prominent use in the final scene of the television series The Sopranos (2007), its cover by the cast of Glee (2009), and adoption as an anthem by sports teams and fans. It became the most downloaded 20th-century song in the iTunes Store and one of the most recognisable rock songs of its era.
