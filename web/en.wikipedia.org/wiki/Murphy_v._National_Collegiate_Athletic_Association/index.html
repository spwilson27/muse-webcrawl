Murphy v. National Collegiate Athletic Association

Murphy v. NCAA, 584 U.S. ___ (2018)
Court: Supreme Court of the United States
Decided: May 14, 2018
Holding: PASPA violates the anticommandeering doctrine

Murphy v. National Collegiate Athletic Association, 584 U.S. ___ (2018), was a landmark United States Supreme Court case that struck down the Professional and Amateur Sports Protection Act of 1992 (PASPA), the federal law that had effectively banned state-authorized sports betting outside a few grandfathered states.

Background

New Jersey voters and legislators repeatedly sought to legalize sports wagering at the state's casinos and racetracks; the NCAA and the major professional leagues sued under PASPA to stop them. The case — first captioned Christie v. NCAA for Governor Chris Christie, and re-captioned for his successor Phil Murphy — reached the Supreme Court after the Third Circuit ruled for the leagues.

Decision

In a 6–3 opinion by Justice Samuel Alito, the Court held that PASPA's ban on states "authorizing" sports gambling commandeered state legislatures in violation of the Tenth Amendment: Congress may not issue direct orders to state governments. Because the statute's provisions were not severable, the whole act fell. Within weeks, New Jersey and other states legalized sports betting, triggering a rapid nationwide expansion of the industry.
