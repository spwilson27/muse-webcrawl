The Pánuco River (Spanish: Río Pánuco) is a river of east-central Mexico that drains one of the largest river basins in the country into the Gulf of Mexico. It is formed by the confluence of the Moctezuma and Tampaón rivers in the Huasteca region and flows roughly 120 kilometres from that junction — some 510 kilometres measured from its farthest headwaters — to its mouth at the port city of Tampico, on the border of the states of Tamaulipas and Veracruz.

Course

The Pánuco's headwaters rise on the Mexican Plateau around Mexico City and the state of Hidalgo, where the Tula River and its continuation, the Moctezuma River, cut through the Sierra Madre Oriental in deep canyons. Below the confluence with the Tampaón, the river crosses the flat Huasteca coastal plain, meandering through wetlands and lagoons before reaching the Gulf of Mexico.

Basin and tributaries

The Pánuco basin covers roughly 84,000 square kilometres across parts of the states of Mexico, Hidalgo, Querétaro, San Luis Potosí, Guanajuato, Veracruz, Tamaulipas, and others. By mean discharge it is among the largest rivers of Mexico — by some measures the fourth-largest by volume of flow. Notably, the upper basin receives much of the drainage (and treated and untreated wastewater) of the Mexico City metropolitan area via the Tula River, making pollution a major concern along its course.

History and economy

The river's lower course near Tampico was an early site of Spanish exploration and of the Huastec (Wastek) civilization in pre-Columbian times; the name Pánuco derives from the Huastec language. Tampico became one of Mexico's most important oil ports in the early 20th century, and the lower river remains navigable for ocean-going vessels serving the port and the industrial corridor of Altamira.

Flooding

The lower Pánuco is notoriously prone to flooding during the summer and autumn rainy season, when tropical storms and hurricanes strike the Gulf coast; major floods, such as those of 1955, 1993, and 2007, have inundated large areas of the Huasteca lowlands.
