Schiedam (Dutch pronunciation: [sxiˈdɑm]) is a city and municipality in the province of South Holland in the Netherlands. It lies on the river Schie, immediately west of Rotterdam, within the Rotterdam–The Hague metropolitan area. Schiedam is famous for its historic centre with canals, its jenever (Dutch gin) industry, and its windmills — among the tallest traditional windmills in the world.

History

Schiedam grew around a dam in the Schie built around 1230, and received city rights in 1275 from Aleida of Holland. The town's prosperity came first from herring fishing and trade, but from the 17th century onward it was dominated by distilling: malt wine and jenever production filled the town with distilleries, malt houses, and the great tower mills that ground the grain. The blackened warehouses of the "burners' town" (brandersstad) characterise the old centre to this day.

In 1856 the last town windmill of the classic fleet was joined by later restorations. The mill De Noord (1803), at 33.5 metres, was long considered the tallest windmill in the world. Six historic mills survive, plus the reconstructed De Kameel.

Jenever

Schiedam jenever — brands such as Ketel One (Nolet Distillery, founded 1691) — is internationally known. The Jenever Museum on the Lange Haven preserves the distilling heritage, with a working traditional distillery producing "Old Schiedam" malt wine gin.

Landmarks

  • The six great tower windmills, including De Noord, De Vrijheid, and De Walvisch
  • The Grote or Sint Janskerk, a Gothic church dating from the 14th–15th century
  • The ruins of Huis te Riviere (Mathenesse Castle), one of the oldest castle remains in Holland
  • The Stedelijk Museum Schiedam, known for its collection of CoBrA and modern Dutch art
  • The Appelmarkt and Lange Haven canals with distillers' warehouses

Transport

Schiedam Centrum station connects the city by rail and metro to Rotterdam, Delft, The Hague, and Hoek van Holland. The A4 and A20 motorways pass nearby.
