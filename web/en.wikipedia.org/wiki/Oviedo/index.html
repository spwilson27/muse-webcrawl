Oviedo

Oviedo (Asturian: Uviéu)
Capital of Asturias, Spain
Founded: 8th century (761 AD traditionally)
Population (2023): ~217,000
Elevation: 232 m

Oviedo (Asturian: Uviéu) is the capital city of the Principality of Asturias in northern Spain and the administrative and commercial centre of the region. Founded in the 8th century as a monastery settlement that became the seat of the Asturian monarchy, it is noted for its remarkably preserved pre-Romanesque churches, its university founded in 1608, and for hosting the Princess of Asturias Awards.

History

Tradition dates the founding to 761, when the monks Máximo and Fromestano established a monastery dedicated to San Vicente. King Fruela I built a palace and church nearby, and Alfonso II "the Chaste" made Oviedo the capital of the Kingdom of Asturias, building the Cámara Santa of the cathedral, the church of San Julián de los Prados and the Foncalada fountain. The pre-Romanesque monuments of Oviedo and the Kingdom of the Asturias are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Culture

Oviedo is associated with Leopoldo Alas ("Clarín"), whose novel La Regenta is set in the fictional "Vetusta", a thinly veiled Oviedo, and with the Sidrerías of Gascona street, where Asturian cider is poured from a height. The city famously inspired Woody Allen, who called it a fairy-tale city and received the Prince of Asturias Award in 2002.
