The Hunger Games is a 2012 American dystopian action film directed by Gary Ross and based on Suzanne Collins's 2008 novel of the same name. It is the first instalment in The Hunger Games film series. The film stars Jennifer Lawrence as Katniss Everdeen, a teenage girl from the impoverished District 12 of the nation of Panem who volunteers to take her younger sister's place in the Hunger Games, a televised fight to the death staged annually by the ruling Capitol.

Plot

In post-apocalyptic Panem, the Capitol punishes its twelve districts for a past rebellion by selecting two "tributes" from each to compete in the Hunger Games. When her sister Prim is chosen, Katniss Everdeen volunteers in her place, joining baker's son Peeta Mellark. Mentored by the alcoholic former victor Haymitch Abernathy and styled for Capitol audiences, Katniss survives the arena through her hunting skills and a staged romance with Peeta. When the Gamemakers revoke a rule change that would have allowed two victors, Katniss and Peeta threaten suicide with poisonous berries, forcing the Capitol to declare them joint winners — an act of defiance that President Snow views as a spark for rebellion.

Production

Lionsgate acquired the rights in 2009, with Collins adapting her own novel alongside Ross and Billy Ray. Filming took place in North Carolina in 2011. The film's soundtrack included "Safe & Sound" by Taylor Swift and the Civil Wars, which won a Grammy Award.

Reception

The film grossed $694 million worldwide, at the time the ninth-highest-grossing film of 2012. Critics praised Lawrence's performance and the film's themes, though some criticised the shaky-cam depiction of violence. It launched a major franchise and is credited with popularising the young-adult dystopian film boom of the 2010s. It was followed by The Hunger Games: Catching Fire (2013).
