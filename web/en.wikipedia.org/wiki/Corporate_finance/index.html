Corporate finance

Corporate finance is the area of finance that deals with the sources of funding, the capital structure of corporations, the actions that managers take to increase the value of the firm to the shareholders, and the tools and analysis used to allocate financial resources. The primary goal of corporate finance is to maximise or increase shareholder value.

Overview

Main areas: Capital budgeting, capital structure, working capital management

Primary goal: Maximising shareholder value

Capital budgeting

Capital budgeting concerns identifying and evaluating investment projects whose value to the firm exceeds their cost, using techniques such as net present value (NPV), internal rate of return (IRR) and discounted cash flow analysis. Projects are selected subject to the firm's available capital.

Capital structure

The financing decision concerns the mix of debt and equity used to fund the firm's assets. The Modigliani–Miller theorem provides the theoretical starting point, while in practice taxes, bankruptcy costs, agency costs and information asymmetries shape the optimal structure. Related decisions include dividend policy and the management of working capital.
