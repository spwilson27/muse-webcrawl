Sigd

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sigd (Ge'ez: ስግድ, "prostration") is a holiday of the Beta Israel (Ethiopian Jewish) community, celebrated on the 29th of the Hebrew month of Cheshvan, fifty days after Yom Kippur. It symbolises the community's acceptance of the Torah and its longing for Jerusalem, and since 2008 it has been an official national holiday in Israel.

Observance in Ethiopia

In Ethiopia, the community would fast and gather on a high mountaintop, where the qessoch (priests) carried the Orit (Torah) in procession and read from Exodus and Nehemiah — including the covenant renewal ceremony of Ezra and Nehemiah — in Ge'ez and Amharic. The ceremony combined prayer for the return to Zion, communal self-examination, and reconciliation, and ended with a festive meal after midday.

Observance in Israel

In Israel the central ceremony is held at the Sherover Promenade in Jerusalem, overlooking the Old City, attended by thousands of Ethiopian Israelis and public officials. In 2008 the Knesset passed the Sigd Law, making it a state holiday and a day of recognition for the Ethiopian Jewish community and its heritage.
