"Civil War" is a 2006–07 Marvel Comics crossover storyline built around a seven-issue limited series written by Mark Millar and penciled by Steve McNiven. The story centers on the Superhuman Registration Act, a United States law requiring all superpowered individuals to register with and work for the government, which splits the superhero community into opposing factions led by Iron Man (pro-registration) and Captain America (anti-registration).

Plot

After the young New Warriors' televised fight with the villain Nitro destroys a Stamford, Connecticut school and kills hundreds, public opinion turns against superheroes. Congress passes the Superhuman Registration Act. Iron Man supports registration; Captain America refuses and goes underground with a resistance of heroes. Battles between the factions escalate, involving a cyborg clone of Thor whose killing of the hero Goliath shocks both sides, and Spider-Man publicly unmasking before switching allegiance. In the final battle in New York, Captain America surrenders when he sees the destruction the fighting has caused, and is arrested; he is later assassinated on the courthouse steps in his own title.

Reception and legacy

Civil War was one of Marvel's best-selling events of the decade and reshaped the Marvel Universe, leading into storylines such as "The Initiative", World War Hulk, and Secret Invasion. Tony Stark became director of S.H.I.E.L.D., while a sequel, Civil War II, appeared in 2016. The storyline was loosely adapted in the film Captain America: Civil War (2016).
