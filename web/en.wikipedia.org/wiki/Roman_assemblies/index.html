Roman assemblies


Types: Comitia Curiata, Comitia Centuriata, Comitia Tributa, Concilium Plebis
Functions: Elections, legislation, trials, declarations of war

The Roman assemblies were institutions in ancient Rome that functioned as the machinery of approval of the legislative proposals of magistrates. The assemblies operated under a principle of direct democracy: citizens did not elect representatives but voted themselves, in organised groups, on proposals placed before them by presiding magistrates. An assembly could only meet when summoned, could only vote yes or no on a proposal as presented (without debate or amendment on the floor), and voted by units — the majority within each unit determined that unit's single vote.

The assemblies

  • Comitia Curiata — the oldest assembly, organised by the thirty curiae of the regal period. By the late republic it survived mainly to confirm the imperium of magistrates and witness adoptions and wills, represented by thirty lictors.
  • Comitia Centuriata — the centuriate assembly, organised by wealth and age into 193 centuries, originally the Roman army in civilian form. It elected consuls, praetors, and censors, declared war, and heard capital appeals. Its structure weighted the votes of the wealthy, who voted first and held a majority of centuries.
  • Comitia Tributa — the tribal assembly of all citizens organised by the 35 tribes, electing quaestors and aediles and passing legislation.
  • Concilium Plebis — the plebeian council, organised by tribes but excluding patricians, electing tribunes of the plebs and plebeian aediles. Its resolutions (plebiscita) became binding on all citizens after the Lex Hortensia of 287 BC.

Procedure

A magistrate with the right of summoning (ius agendi cum populo) published a proposal in advance, took the auspices, and addressed a preliminary meeting (contio) at which speeches could be made. Voting was originally oral, then by secret ballot after laws of the second century BC. Citizens filed across raised gangways (pontes) dropping tablets marked V (uti rogas, "as you propose") or A (antiquo, "I reject") into urns.

Decline

Under the principate the assemblies lost their functions: elections were transferred to the Senate under Tiberius, and legislation by assembly faded as imperial enactments took its place, though the forms survived as ceremony into the later empire.
