Cellular repeater

A cellular repeater (also known as a cell phone signal booster, amplifier, or cell signal extender) is a type of bi-directional amplifier used to improve cell phone reception in areas where the signal is weak. A typical system consists of an external donor antenna, a signal amplifier, and an internal rebroadcast antenna.

Operation

The donor antenna, usually mounted on a roof or exterior wall, receives the downlink signal from a nearby cell tower. The amplifier boosts both the downlink and uplink signals, and the rebroadcast antenna distributes the improved signal within a building or vehicle. Repeaters are commonly used in rural areas, large buildings, and underground spaces.

Regulation

In many countries, including the United States, signal boosters must be certified by regulators such as the Federal Communications Commission and used with carrier consent, since poorly designed or malfunctioning amplifiers can interfere with cellular networks.
