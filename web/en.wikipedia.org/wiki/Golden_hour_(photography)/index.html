Golden hour (photography)

In photography, the golden hour is the period of daytime shortly after sunrise or before sunset, during which daylight is redder and softer than when the sun is higher in the sky. The golden hour is also sometimes called the "magic hour", especially by cinematographers and photographers.

Golden hour
OccursShortly after sunrise; shortly before sunset
Sun positionBetween about −4° and +6° elevation
Preceded/followed byBlue hour

Characteristics

When the sun is low, its light travels through more of the atmosphere, scattering blue wavelengths (Rayleigh scattering) and leaving warmer red and orange tones. The low angle produces long shadows, softer contrast, and textured modelling of landscapes and subjects. Because the sun is near the horizon, conditions change quickly, and the duration of the golden hour varies with latitude and season — lasting only minutes near the equator and much longer at high latitudes.

Use in filmmaking

Cinematographers prize the golden hour for romantic and dramatic scenes; the film Days of Heaven (1978) was famously shot largely during this brief daily window. The period just after sunset, when the sky remains lit but the sun has set, is called the blue hour.
