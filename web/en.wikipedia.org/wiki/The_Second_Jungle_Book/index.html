The Second Jungle Book

Author: Rudyard Kipling
Published: 1895

The Second Jungle Book is a collection of short stories and poems by Rudyard Kipling, published in 1895 as the sequel to The Jungle Book (1894). Most of its tales continue the story of Mowgli, the boy raised by wolves in the Seeonee jungle of India, concluding his arc as he returns to live among men.

Contents

  • "How Fear Came" — the myth of how the tiger got his stripes and fear entered the jungle
  • "The Miracle of Purun Bhagat" — a Brahmin minister who renounces the world
  • "Letting in the Jungle" — Mowgli saves his adoptive human parents from hostile villagers
  • "The Undertakers" — a jackal, an adjutant stork, and a crocodile converse
  • "The King's Ankus" — Mowgli and Kaa explore a ruined city's treasure
  • "Quiquern" — an Inuit boy and his dog team face starvation
  • "Red Dog" — the dholes invade the Seeonee, and Akela dies in the defence
  • "The Spring Running" — Mowgli's final farewell to the jungle

Themes

The Mowgli stories here are darker than in the first book, tracing his growth to adolescence and his inevitable departure from the jungle law he loves. The framing poems and the Law of the Jungle verses remain among Kipling's best-known lines for younger readers.
