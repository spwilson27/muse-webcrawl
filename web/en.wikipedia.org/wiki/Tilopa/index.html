Tilopa (Prakrit: Tillipāda; Tibetan: ཏི་ལོ་པ་; c. 988 – c. 1069) was an Indian mahasiddha and Buddhist tantric practitioner who is regarded as the human founder of the Kagyu lineage of Tibetan Buddhism. He is said to have received the Mahamudra teachings directly from the primordial Buddha Vajradhara, and he transmitted them to his disciple Naropa, from whom they passed to Marpa and into Tibet.

Life

Traditional accounts describe Tilopa as born in Bengal — some say a Brahmin, others a prince — who became a monk and scholar before abandoning conventional monastic life under the guidance of the dakinis. He worked by day grinding sesame oil (from which his name, from tila, "sesame", derives) and by night in the service of a courtesan, practising tantra in secret. His biography is dominated by his relationship with Naropa, the famed abbot of Nalanda, whom Tilopa subjected to twelve years of ordeals — the "twelve great hardships" — before granting him the full transmission.

Teachings

Tilopa's most famous instruction is the Ganges Mahamudra (Mahāmudrā-upadeśa), a brief, celebrated set of verses given to Naropa on the banks of the Ganges, summarising the path of Mahamudra: resting the mind in its natural state without fabrication, "like a sheaf of reeds unbound". He is also credited with the Six Words of Advice ("Do not recall, do not imagine, do not think, do not examine, do not control, rest") and with assembling the "four special transmissions" that became the core of the Kagyu lineage: inner heat (tummo), illusory body, dream yoga, luminosity, and the practices of bardo and phowa associated with the Six Yogas of Naropa.
