Skee-Ball

Skee-Ball is an arcade game and one of the first redemption games, invented in 1908 by Joseph Fournier in Philadelphia and popularised from 1909 onward. It remains a staple of arcades, boardwalks and amusement parks.

Inventor: Joseph Fournier
Year: 1908
Type: Arcade redemption game

Gameplay

The player rolls balls up an inclined lane over a hump so they launch toward a series of concentric target rings. Each ring is worth a different number of points — traditionally 10 to 50, with small side pockets worth 100 — and scores are exchanged for prize tickets at redemption arcades.

History

Fournier patented the game in 1908 and the Skee-Ball Alley Company began selling alleys in 1909. The first national tournament was held in Atlantic City in 1932. Modern versions include electronic scoring, and competitive leagues such as Brewskee-Ball have been organised in American cities since the 2000s.

In popular culture

The game is closely associated with seaside boardwalks in New Jersey and with arcade chains such as Chuck E. Cheese.
