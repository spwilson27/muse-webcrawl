Canadian Maple Leaf coins

The Canadian Maple Leaf coins are a series of bullion coins issued by the Royal Canadian Mint in gold, silver, platinum, and palladium. The gold Maple Leaf, introduced in 1979, was the first bullion coin struck in 24-karat (99.99% pure) gold, and the series remains among the world's most widely traded investment coins.

Gold Maple Leaf

The Gold Maple Leaf was launched in 1979, initially struck in .999 fine gold; from November 1982 the mint raised the purity to .9999, making it the first 24-karat bullion coin. It is issued in denominations of 1 oz and in fractional sizes of 1/2, 1/4, 1/10, and 1/20 oz, with face values from $50 down to $1. In 2007 the mint produced a promotional 100 kg, .99999 fine gold coin with a face value of $1 million, then the largest gold coin ever made.

Silver Maple Leaf

The Silver Maple Leaf debuted in 1988 and contains one troy ounce of .9999 fine silver with a face value of $5, the highest face value of any comparable silver bullion coin. It frequently carries privy marks and special finishes, and in 2014 the mint added radial-line security engraving and, later, MintShield surface protection to reduce milk spotting.

Other metals

Platinum Maple Leafs were issued from 1988 to 2002, resumed in 2009, and palladium Maple Leafs were struck from 2005 to 2007 and again in later years. All share the maple leaf reverse designed by the mint's engravers and an obverse portrait of the reigning monarch — for most of the series' life, Queen Elizabeth II, succeeded by Charles III beginning in 2024.

Security features

From 2015, gold and silver Maple Leafs incorporate a micro-engraved privy mark bearing the last two digits of the year of issue, verifiable under magnification. The bullion DNA program allows registered dealers to authenticate coins against the mint's database.
