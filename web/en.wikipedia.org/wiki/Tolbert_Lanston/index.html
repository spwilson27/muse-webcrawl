Tolbert Lanston

Tolbert Lanston (1844–1913) was an American inventor, founder of the Lanston Monotype Machine Company, and inventor of the Monotype typesetting system — a mechanical typesetting device that, together with Ottmar Mergenthaler's Linotype, transformed printing in the late 19th century.

Life and work

Born in Troy, Ohio, Lanston served in the Union Army during the Civil War and later worked as a clerk in the US Pension Office in Washington, D.C. Frustrated with slow record-keeping, he turned to invention, patenting a type-casting and composing system from the 1880s. Unlike the Linotype, which cast whole lines, the Monotype cast individual letters from a paper-tape-driven keyboard and caster, allowing easier corrections and fine typography — qualities that made it favoured for book work.

Lanston's company, reorganised commercially in the 1890s with investment organised by banker J. Maury Dove, introduced the Monotype to Britain in 1897, where the Lanston Monotype Corporation became central to fine printing. Lanston died in Washington in 1913.
