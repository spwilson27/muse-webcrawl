Melnikov House

The Melnikov House (Russian: Дом Мельникова) is a one-family house in Krivoarbatsky Lane, in the Arbat district of Moscow, built in 1927–1929 by the Soviet avant-garde architect Konstantin Melnikov for himself and his family. It is widely regarded as an icon of Constructivist architecture and of the Soviet architectural avant-garde, and is recognisable by its two intersecting cylindrical towers, hexagonal windows, and a striking fully glazed street façade.

Design

The plan consists of two interlocking cylinders of different heights, a form Melnikov argued economised on materials while giving unusual spatial effects. The rear cylinder is pierced by about sixty hexagonal windows arranged in a honeycomb pattern, which distribute light and reduce the need for internal load-bearing walls. Inside, the house contained Melnikov's double-height studio, living quarters, and a famously minimalist bedroom with built-in plaster beds. Unusually for the Soviet Union of the period, the house remained private property, largely because of Melnikov's prominence and a special dispensation.

History and preservation

The house fell into disrepair late in the Soviet period and its preservation became a cause célèbre of international conservation. After the architect's son, the artist Viktor Melnikov, died in 2006, disputes over ownership and access followed. In 2014 the house became a branch of the Shchusev State Museum of Architecture as the Konstantin and Viktor Melnikov Museum, with restoration work proceeding to preserve it as a monument of world significance; it has been on UNESCO's Tentative World Heritage List (in a serial nomination of Melnikov's work) and on watch lists of endangered heritage.
