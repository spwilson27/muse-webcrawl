Limit (category theory)

Limit
FieldCategory theory

In category theory, a branch of mathematics, the abstract notion of a limit captures the essential properties of universal constructions such as products, inverse limits and pullbacks. The dual notion is a colimit, which captures constructions such as disjoint unions, direct sums and pushouts.

Formally, let F : J → C be a diagram of shape J in a category C. A cone to F is an object N together with a family of morphisms from N to each object of the diagram, such that all triangles formed with the morphisms of the diagram commute. A limit of F is a cone that is universal: every other cone factors uniquely through it. Limits, when they exist, are unique up to unique isomorphism.

Definition

A limit of the diagram F : J → C is a cone (L, φ) from an object L to F such that for any other cone (N, ψ) there exists a unique morphism u : N → L with φX ∘ u = ψX for all objects X of J. The object L is written lim← F. A category has all limits of a given shape if every diagram of that shape has a limit; a category with all small limits is called complete.

Examples

The limit of a diagram with no morphisms is a product; of a parallel pair of morphisms, an equalizer; of a cospan, a pullback; of the empty diagram, a terminal object; and of a diagram indexed by a directed set with maps running "backwards", an inverse limit — for example the ring of p-adic integers as an inverse limit of the rings Z/pn.

Properties

Functors that preserve limits are central to the theory: the hom-functor preserves all limits (limits commute with representables), right adjoint functors preserve limits, and monadic functors create them. Many standard categories — sets, groups, rings, topological spaces — are complete and cocomplete, with limits computed essentially as in sets with the appropriate structure.

Limits in specific categories

In Set, a limit is the set of compatible families ("threads") of elements of the diagram. Weighted limits generalise the notion by replacing cones with a profunctor weight; ordinary limits are recovered with a constant weight.
