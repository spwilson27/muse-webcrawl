Octamethylcyclotetrasiloxane

Octamethylcyclotetrasiloxane, commonly called D4, is an organosilicon compound with the formula [(CH3)2SiO]4 — a ring of four repeating dimethylsiloxane units. It is a colourless, viscous, volatile liquid, and a member of the cyclic siloxanes (cyclomethicones), together with D5 (decamethylcyclopentasiloxane) and D6.

Production and uses

D4 is produced on a large scale by hydrolysis of dimethyldichlorosilane, which yields a mixture of cyclic siloxanes and linear polymers; the cyclics are separated by distillation. Most D4 is used as a chemical intermediate — ring-opening polymerisation converts it into polydimethylsiloxane silicone polymers — and the remainder has historically been used directly in personal-care products such as antiperspirants, skin creams, and hair products, where it acts as an emollient that evaporates cleanly, and in some medical and industrial applications.

Safety and regulation

D4 has attracted regulatory attention because it is persistent and bioaccumulative in the environment and is suspected of affecting fertility. The European Chemicals Agency classified it as a substance of very high concern, and the European Union has restricted D4 and D5 in wash-off cosmetic products and progressively widened restrictions; regulators in the United States and elsewhere have assessed its presence in wastewater and surface waters. Its environmental behaviour — volatility to the atmosphere combined with persistence in sediments — has made it a model compound in the study of siloxane fate and transport.
