Paradise Papers

The Paradise Papers are a set of over 13.4 million confidential electronic documents concerning offshore investments, leaked to the German newspaper Süddeutsche Zeitung and published by the International Consortium of Investigative Journalists (ICIJ) beginning on 5 November 2017. The bulk of the documents came from the offshore law firm Appleby, together with corporate registries from 19 tax jurisdictions.

Background

The leak came about a year and a half after the Panama Papers. As with that investigation, Süddeutsche Zeitung obtained the data from an anonymous source and shared it with the ICIJ, which organized reporting by some 380 journalists from 96 media partners in 67 countries. Appleby stated that it had been the victim of a cyberattack in 2016 and denied wrongdoing.

Contents

The documents exposed the financial affairs of more than 120,000 people and companies, including politicians, heads of state, corporate giants, and celebrities. Among those named were Queen Elizabeth II (whose Duchy of Lancaster estate had invested in offshore funds), U.S. Commerce Secretary Wilbur Ross (whose shipping interests did business with a firm linked to associates of Vladimir Putin), and major multinational corporations such as Apple, Nike, and Glencore. The papers also traced how the social media companies Twitter and Facebook had received investments via Russian state-linked institutions.

Reactions and consequences

Unlike the Panama Papers, much of the activity revealed was lawful tax avoidance rather than crime, but the disclosures intensified political pressure: the European Union accelerated work on its blacklist of tax havens, tax authorities in several countries opened inquiries, and the leak reignited debate over the fairness of aggressive corporate tax planning and the use of secrecy jurisdictions. In 2018 the ICIJ and partners won awards for the investigation, and reporting continued to emerge from the documents for years afterward.
