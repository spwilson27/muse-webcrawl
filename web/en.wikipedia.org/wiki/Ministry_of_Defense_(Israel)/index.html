The Ministry of Defense (Hebrew: מִשְׂרַד הַבִּטָּחוֹן, Misrad HaBitahon) is the governmental department responsible for defending the State of Israel from internal and external military threats. Established in 1948 upon the founding of the state, its political head is the Defense Minister of Israel, and its headquarters are in the Kirya district of Tel Aviv. The ministry exercises civilian authority over the Israel Defense Forces (IDF), whose Chief of the General Staff is subordinate to the Defense Minister.

The ministry's headquarters in the Kirya, Tel Aviv.

Role and structure

The ministry is responsible for defense policy, the defense budget, weapons development and procurement, military exports, and the welfare of soldiers and veterans. It comprises civilian directorates alongside the IDF, and supervises or coordinates with defense industries such as Israel Aerospace Industries, Rafael Advanced Defense Systems and Elbit Systems, as well as bodies including the Administration for the Development of Weapons and Technological Infrastructure (Maf'at). Attached units include the Coordinator of Government Activities in the Territories (COGAT) and the military censorship.

History

David Ben-Gurion, Israel's first prime minister, held the defense portfolio himself for most of 1948–1953 and again from 1955 to 1963, shaping the ministry's central role in national life. Subsequent ministers have included Moshe Dayan, Shimon Peres, Yitzhak Rabin, Ariel Sharon, Ehud Barak, Moshe Ya'alon, Avigdor Lieberman, Naftali Bennett, Benny Gantz and Yoav Gallant. The ministry's budget is consistently among the largest items of Israeli government expenditure.
