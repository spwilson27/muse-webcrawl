Bronisław Malinowski

Bronisław Kasper Malinowski (7 April 1884 – 16 May 1942) was a Polish-British anthropologist whose writings on ethnography, social theory, and field research established him as one of the most important anthropologists of the 20th century. He is widely considered a founder of social anthropology and of the functionalist school.

Life and career

Born in Kraków, Malinowski studied at the Jagiellonian University and the London School of Economics. His fieldwork in the Trobriand Islands (1915–1918), off New Guinea, produced Argonauts of the Western Pacific (1922), a landmark study of the Kula exchange system. He later taught at the LSE, where his seminar trained a generation of anthropologists, and spent his final years at Yale University.

Method and theory

Malinowski championed participant observation — living among the people studied and learning their language — and argued that cultural practices should be understood by the function they serve in meeting human needs. His posthumously published field diary provoked debate about the private attitudes of ethnographers.
