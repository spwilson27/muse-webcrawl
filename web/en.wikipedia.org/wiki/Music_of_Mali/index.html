Music of Mali

The music of Mali is, like that of most West African nations, ethnically diverse, but one influence predominates: that of the ancient Mali Empire of the Mandinka (from c. 1230 to c. 1670). Mande people (Bambara, Maninka, Soninke) make up the majority of the population, and their musical traditions — carried for centuries by hereditary praise-singers called griots (in Mandinka, jali) — dominate the country's national culture.

A jali (griot) performing on the kora.

The griot tradition

Griots are hereditary musicians, historians, and praise-singers attached to noble families. Their instruments include the kora, a 21-string bridge-harp built from a calabash; the balafon (wooden xylophone); and the ngoni, a small lute considered an ancestor of the banjo. The legendary kora player Toumani Diabaté came from a line of griots said to stretch back 70 generations.

Modern popular music

In the independence era, state-sponsored dance bands flourished; the Rail Band, formed in Bamako in 1970 at the Buffet Bar of the railway station, launched the careers of Salif Keïta and Mory Kanté. Keïta, an albino descendant of Sundiata Keita who defied caste expectations to sing, became known as the "Golden Voice of Africa".

Ali Farka Touré of Niafunké fused traditional Songhai music with American blues, famously describing the blues as having Malian roots; his album Talking Timbuktu (1994), recorded with Ry Cooder, won a Grammy Award. Other internationally known artists include the duo Amadou & Mariam, singer Oumou Sangaré — a star of Wassoulou music, known for songs addressing women's lives — Rokia Traoré, Vieux Farka Touré, and the Tuareg "desert blues" band Tinariwen, formed in refugee camps in the Sahara.

Disruption and resilience

In 2012, armed groups occupying northern Mali banned music in areas under their control, driving musicians into exile — a crisis documented in the film They Will Have to Kill Us First. The famous Festival au Désert, held near Timbuktu, was suspended, but Malian music continued through exile festivals and international touring.
