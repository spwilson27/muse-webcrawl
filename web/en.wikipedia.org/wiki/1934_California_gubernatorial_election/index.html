1934 California gubernatorial election

Election dateNovember 6, 1934
WinnerFrank F. Merriam
PartyRepublican
Principal opponentUpton Sinclair
ContextGreat Depression in California

The 1934 California gubernatorial election was held on November 6, 1934. Republican incumbent Frank F. Merriam, who had succeeded to the governorship after the death of James Rolph, defeated Democratic nominee Upton Sinclair and a field of minor candidates. The contest became nationally famous because Sinclair, a socialist writer, ran on the End Poverty in California program, usually shortened to EPIC.

Background

California entered the election amid mass unemployment, farm distress, bank failures and conflict over relief policy. Sinclair had changed his registration from Socialist to Democratic and won the Democratic primary by promising production-for-use cooperatives, public works, pensions and emergency economic planning. His campaign alarmed business groups, newspaper publishers and many regular Democrats, who feared a socialist takeover of the state government.

Campaign

Merriam presented himself as the defender of constitutional government, private property and orderly relief. The anti-Sinclair campaign used newspaper attacks, staged newsreel material and warnings that an EPIC victory would bring confiscation and chaos. Sinclair answered with rallies, pamphlets and a network of EPIC clubs. The race drew Hollywood, organized labor and national political figures into California politics.

Results and legacy

Merriam won with a clear plurality in a multi-candidate field. Sinclair ran strongly for a writer identified with socialism, but he did not carry the state. The election is remembered as a landmark in modern campaign media, negative advertising and the politics of the Great Depression. It also anticipated later California contests in which celebrity, mass media and ideological movements shaped statewide elections. Related topics include Upton Sinclair, Frank Merriam and End Poverty in California.
