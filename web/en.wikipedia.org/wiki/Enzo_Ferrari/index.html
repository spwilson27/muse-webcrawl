Enzo Ferrari

Enzo Ferrari (1898–1988), founder of Ferrari

Enzo Anselmo Giuseppe Maria Ferrari (18 February 1898 – 14 August 1988) was an Italian racing driver and entrepreneur, the founder of the Scuderia Ferrari Grand Prix motor racing team, and subsequently of the Ferrari automobile marque. Under his leadership, Ferrari became one of the most successful racing teams in history, winning numerous Formula One world championships.

Early life and racing career

Ferrari was born in Modena, Italy. After his family's carpentry business collapsed and the deaths of his father and brother during the First World War, he worked at a small car workshop. He became a test driver and then a racing driver for Alfa Romeo in the 1920s, winning races such as the Coppa Acerbo, though he never ranked among the very top drivers of his era. In 1929 he founded Scuderia Ferrari in Modena, initially as the racing division and sponsor of Alfa Romeo's competition cars.

Ferrari marque

After leaving Alfa Romeo in 1939, Ferrari founded Auto Avio Costruzioni; the first car to bear his own name, the 125 S, appeared in 1947. Based at Maranello, the company built racing cars and, to fund them, road-going sports cars renowned for their performance and exclusivity. Ferrari's Formula One team, the only team to have competed in every season of the World Championship since 1950, won a record number of constructors' and drivers' titles. Enzo Ferrari sold a 50% stake in the company to Fiat in 1969 but retained control of the racing division until his death.

Management style and legacy

Ferrari was famous for his exacting, sometimes ruthless management, for playing drivers against one another, and for his maxim that "what's behind you doesn't matter." His animosity with Ferruccio Lamborghini is credited with prompting the latter to build rival sports cars. He died in Modena in 1988, shortly after the launch of the F40, the last road car he personally approved. The Museo Casa Enzo Ferrari and the marque's enduring racing programme commemorate his legacy.
