Cheech Marin

Richard Anthony "Cheech" Marin (born July 13, 1946) is an American comedian, actor, musician and writer who gained fame as part of the comedy duo Cheech & Chong during the 1970s and 1980s. He later built a varied solo career in film and television and became a prominent collector and promoter of Chicano art.

Early life

Marin was born in South Los Angeles to parents of Mexican descent and raised in Granada Hills in the San Fernando Valley. His nickname comes from "chicharrón", fried pork rind. He studied English at California State University, Northridge, and moved to Vancouver, British Columbia, in the late 1960s, where he met Tommy Chong.

Cheech & Chong

With Chong, Marin released a string of best-selling comedy albums — including Cheech and Chong (1971), Big Bambu (1972) and Los Cochinos (1973), which won the Grammy Award for Best Comedy Album — built around countercultural, cannabis-centred sketches. Their first film, Up in Smoke (1978), was a major hit and made them icons of stoner comedy. The duo split in the mid-1980s and reunited for tours in 2008.

Solo career

Marin wrote, directed and starred in Born in East L.A. (1987), a comedy inspired by his parody song of the same name about a Mexican-American deported to Mexico by mistake. He played Inspector Joe Dominguez opposite Don Johnson in the television series Nash Bridges (1996–2001), appeared in films including Desperado, From Dusk till Dawn, Spy Kids and Machete, and voiced Ramone in Pixar's Cars films and Banzai the hyena in The Lion King. He is also remembered by quiz fans for winning the first Celebrity Jeopardy! tournament in 1992, defeating a former US senator among others.

Chicano art

Marin assembled one of the largest private collections of Chicano painting in the United States. His touring exhibition Chicano Visions: American Painters on the Verge opened in 2001. In 2022 the Cheech Marin Center for Chicano Art & Culture opened in Riverside, California, housing his donated collection of some 700 works.
