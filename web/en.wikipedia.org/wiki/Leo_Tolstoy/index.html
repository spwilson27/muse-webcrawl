Leo Tolstoy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Leo Tolstoy in old age at Yasnaya Polyana: white beard, peasant blouse, penetrating eyes]

Count Lev Nikolayevich Tolstoy (9 September 1828 – 20 November 1910), usually referred to in English as Leo Tolstoy, was a Russian writer regarded as one of the greatest authors of all time. He received nominations for the Nobel Prize in Literature every year from 1902 to 1906 but never won. Best known for War and Peace and Anna Karenina, he is the master of the panoramic, psychologically deep novel.

Born at Yasnaya Polyana, Tolstoy served as an artillery officer in the Crimean War (his Sevastopol Sketches made his reputation). War and Peace (1865–69) follows five families through the Napoleonic Wars; Anna Karenina (1875–77) opens with perhaps the most famous first line in fiction, 'All happy families are alike; each unhappy family is unhappy in its own way.'

Later life and beliefs

After a spiritual crisis recorded in A Confession (1882), Tolstoy became a radical Christian anarchist and pacifist, advocating nonviolent resistance — ideas that influenced Gandhi and Martin Luther King Jr. He was excommunicated by the Russian Orthodox Church in 1901 and died at Astapovo railway station after fleeing home at 82.
