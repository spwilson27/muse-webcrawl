Independence Park

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Independence Park is a sports and cultural complex in Kingston, Jamaica. Built for the 1962 Central American and Caribbean Games — held the same year Jamaica became independent from Britain, hence the name — the complex centres on the National Stadium, which remains the country's principal venue for football and athletics.

Facilities

The National Stadium has a capacity of roughly 35,000 and hosts the Jamaican national football team's home matches, school athletics championships (notably the ISSA/GraceKennedy Boys and Girls' Championships, "Champs"), and major concerts and national celebrations. The wider complex includes the National Arena, an Olympic-size swimming and diving complex, and facilities for basketball, netball, and cycling. Statues of Jamaican sporting heroes, including sprinters who rose to world fame, stand at the stadium entrance.

Significance

Independence Park was among the first monumental projects of the independent Jamaican state, and the stadium hosted the 1966 British Empire and Commonwealth Games. It remains the symbolic home of Jamaican track and field, the sport in which the island has achieved outsized international success.

Independence Park may also refer to parks of the same name in other cities, including Independence Park (HaKirya/הקריה area) in Jerusalem and public squares in several Latin American and African capitals.
