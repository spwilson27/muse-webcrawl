Stanley Smith Stevens

DiedJanuary 18, 1973 (aged 66), Cambridge, Massachusetts, U.S.

Stanley Smith Stevens (November 4, 1906 – January 18, 1973) was an American psychologist who founded Harvard's Psycho-Acoustic Laboratory, studying the effects of noise on military personnel during World War II, and is credited with the introduction of Stevens's power law. Stevens authored a milestone textbook, the 1,400-page Handbook of Experimental Psychology (1951). He was also the founding editor of the journal Perception & Psychophysics.

Levels of measurement

Stevens is best known outside psychophysics for his 1946 paper "On the Theory of Scales of Measurement", which proposed the influential classification of measurement into four levels: nominal, ordinal, interval, and ratio. The typology became widely used in statistics and the social sciences, though it also attracted debate about which statistical operations are permissible for each level.

Psychophysics

Building on the work of Gustav Fechner and Ernst Weber, Stevens developed direct magnitude estimation methods, asking observers to assign numbers proportional to perceived intensity. From these experiments he formulated Stevens's power law, which states that the perceived intensity of a stimulus grows as a power function of its physical magnitude, with an exponent that varies by sensory modality.
