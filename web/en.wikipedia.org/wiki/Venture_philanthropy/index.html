Venture philanthropy

Venture philanthropy is a type of impact investment that applies the techniques of venture capital — long-term, high-engagement funding with managerial support and measurable outcomes — to charitable and social-purpose organisations.

Characteristics

Venture philanthropists typically provide multi-year core funding rather than project grants, take an active role in governance and capacity building, tailor financing (grants, loans or equity) to the organisation's stage, and plan an exit once the organisation is sustainable. Performance is measured against social, not only financial, returns.

History

The term gained currency in the late 1990s, drawing on earlier traditions of engaged grantmaking associated with foundations such as the Rockefeller Foundation. Organisations and networks such as the European Venture Philanthropy Association (EVPA, founded 2004) and the Asian Venture Philanthropy Network spread the model internationally.

Criticism

Critics argue that venture philanthropy can import commercial metrics ill-suited to social change, skew nonprofits toward measurable short-term outputs, and give wealthy donors disproportionate influence over public priorities.
