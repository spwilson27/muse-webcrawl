Great Northern Expedition

The Great Northern Expedition (Russian: Великая Северная экспедиция), also known as the Second Kamchatka Expedition (1733–1743), was one of the largest exploration enterprises in history, sponsored by the Russian Empire to map the Arctic and Pacific coasts of Siberia, investigate the strait between Asia and America, and explore the northwest coast of North America. Commanded by the Danish-born navigator Vitus Bering, it employed over 3,000 people in several detachments.

Results

The expedition charted much of Siberia's Arctic coast from the White Sea to the Kolyma, described the natural history and peoples of Kamchatka through scientists such as Georg Wilhelm Steller and Stepan Krasheninnikov, and in 1741 Bering's ships reached Alaska, discovering the Aleutian Islands and Commander Islands. Bering died on the island now named after him during the return voyage, but the reports of abundant sea otters launched Russia's fur trade in the North Pacific and its colonization of Russian America.
