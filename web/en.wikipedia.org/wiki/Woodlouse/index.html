Woodlouse

A woodlouse (plural woodlice) is a terrestrial isopod crustacean belonging to the suborder Oniscidea. Woodlice are not insects but are related to shrimp and crabs; they are among the few crustaceans adapted to life entirely on land. There are over 4,000 described species worldwide. Common names include pillbug, roly-poly, sow bug and slater.

Description

Woodlice have a segmented, armoured exoskeleton, seven pairs of legs, and two pairs of antennae, one pair much reduced. They range from a few millimetres to about 18 mm in common species. Some, like Armadillidium, can roll into a perfect ball (conglobation) when threatened.

Ecology

Woodlice feed mainly on decaying plant material and are important decomposers in gardens and woodlands. They require damp conditions because their gill-like respiratory structures lose water readily, and they shelter under stones, bark and leaf litter by day. Females carry their eggs in a brood pouch (marsupium) until they hatch as tiny white young.

Relationship with humans

Woodlice are harmless to humans and beneficial to soil, though large numbers may occasionally nibble seedlings. They are popular in the vivarium hobby as "clean-up crew" and as feeder animals.
