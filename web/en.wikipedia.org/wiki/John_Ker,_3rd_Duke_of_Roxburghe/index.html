John Ker, 3rd Duke of Roxburghe

John Ker, 3rd Duke of Roxburghe
Born: 23 April 1740, London, England
Died: 19 March 1804 (aged 63), London, England
Known for: Book collecting; the Roxburghe sale and Roxburghe Club

John Ker, 3rd Duke of Roxburghe (23 April 1740 – 19 March 1804) was a Scottish peer and one of the most famous book collectors of the eighteenth century. His library, auctioned after his death, became legendary in the history of bibliophily.

Life

Ker was born in Hanover Square, London, the son of Robert Ker, 2nd Duke of Roxburghe, whom he succeeded as duke in 1755 while still in his teens. He was educated at Eton and undertook the Grand Tour of Europe. At court he served as a Lord of the Bedchamber to George III and was made a Knight of the Thistle and later a Knight of the Garter. According to a well-known story, a youthful attachment to Princess Christina of Mecklenburg-Strelitz, younger sister of Queen Charlotte, was thwarted by royal protocol; the Duke never married, a circumstance often linked to the intensity of his later collecting.

The library and the Roxburghe sale

Over decades Roxburghe assembled a library of some 30,000 volumes, especially strong in early printed books, Shakespeare, drama, and the works of Caxton. After his death the collection was sold at auction in London in 1812 in a sale lasting more than forty days, which became one of the most celebrated book sales in history. Its highlight was a copy of Boccaccio's Decameron printed by Valdarfer in 1471, which fetched £2,260 — then the highest price ever paid for a book — purchased for the Marquis of Blandford after fierce bidding.

To commemorate the sale, a group of collectors founded the Roxburghe Club in 1812, the first of the great bibliophile societies, which still exists and is named in his honour.
