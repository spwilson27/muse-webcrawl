Pharmacy

Pharmacy is the science and practice of discovering, producing, preparing, dispensing, reviewing and monitoring medications, aiming to ensure the safe, effective, and affordable use of medicines. It is a miscellaneous science as it links health sciences with pharmacology and natural sciences. The professional practice is becoming more clinically oriented, and the term also denotes the premises — a community pharmacy or drugstore — where most pharmacists practise.

History

The preparation of remedies dates to antiquity, with apothecaries compounding plant and mineral medicines. Pharmacy emerged as a distinct profession in the Islamic Golden Age, where the first drug stores opened in Baghdad in the 8th century, and later in medieval Europe. In 1546, the Nuremberg pharmacopoeia set early standards; the 19th and 20th centuries saw the industrial manufacture of medicines shift much compounding from pharmacies to factories, while the discovery of antibiotics and synthetic drugs transformed practice.

Branches and settings

Pharmacy practice includes community pharmacy, hospital pharmacy, clinical pharmacy, industrial pharmacy, compounding pharmacy, consultant pharmacy, ambulatory care pharmacy, regulatory pharmacy, and informatics. Academic pharmacy encompasses disciplines such as pharmaceutics, medicinal chemistry, pharmacognosy, pharmacology, toxicology, and pharmacy practice.

Regulation

Pharmacies and pharmacists are licensed and regulated by governments in most countries. Regulations govern the storage and sale of controlled substances, record-keeping, and the premises and staffing required for dispensing. Scope of practice has expanded to include vaccination, medication therapy reviews, and, in some jurisdictions, pharmacist prescribing.
