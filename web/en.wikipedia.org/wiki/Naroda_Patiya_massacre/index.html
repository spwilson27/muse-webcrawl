The Naroda Patiya massacre took place on 28 February 2002 at Naroda Patiya, a locality in Ahmedabad, Gujarat, India, during the 2002 Gujarat violence. A mob killed 97 Muslims, making it the deadliest single incident of the riots, which erupted after the burning of a train at Godhra the previous day killed 59 Hindu pilgrims and activists.

The massacre

During a statewide strike (bandh) called by the Vishwa Hindu Parishad, a crowd estimated in the thousands attacked the Muslim neighbourhood of Naroda Patiya over the course of the day. Victims were hacked, beaten, and burned alive; homes and businesses were looted and set alight, and survivors later described sexual violence and the failure of police to intervene effectively. Many of the dead were women and children. The events, alongside the simultaneous Gulbarg Society massacre in the same city, became emblematic of the 2002 violence.

Investigation and trial

The case was investigated by a Special Investigation Team appointed by the Supreme Court of India. In August 2012 a special court convicted 32 people, including Maya Kodnani, a former state minister and sitting legislator, who was described by the court as a principal conspirator and sentenced to 28 years in prison, and Bajrang Dal leader Babu Bajrangi, sentenced to life imprisonment. In 2018 the Gujarat High Court acquitted Kodnani, giving her the benefit of the doubt, while upholding the convictions of several others including Bajrangi.

Legacy

The massacre and its long legal aftermath remain central to debate in India about communal violence, state complicity, and the adequacy of justice for victims of the 2002 riots.
