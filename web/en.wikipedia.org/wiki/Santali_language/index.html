Santali (Ol Chiki: ᱥᱟᱱᱛᱟᱲᱤ, santāḷi) is a language of the Munda branch of the Austroasiatic language family, spoken by about 7.6 million people, mostly the Santal people, in the eastern Indian states of Jharkhand, West Bengal, Odisha, Assam, Bihar and Chhattisgarh, and in northern Bangladesh and eastern Nepal. It is the most widely spoken of all Munda languages.

Status and script

Santali was added to the Eighth Schedule of the Indian Constitution by the 92nd Amendment in 2003, making it one of India's 22 scheduled (officially recognised) languages. Historically written in whichever regional script predominated — Bengali, Devanagari, Odia or Latin — Santali acquired its own purpose-built alphabet in 1925 when Pandit Raghunath Murmu created Ol Chiki, an alphabetic script designed to capture Santali phonology, including its checked consonants and nasalised vowels. Ol Chiki is now the most politically and culturally significant script for the language.

Features

Santali is an agglutinative language with subject–object–verb word order. It has a dual number in addition to singular and plural, distinguishes inclusive and exclusive "we", and employs animate/inanimate gender. Its phonology is unusual among Indian languages for its series of checked (glottalised) final consonants and for nasalised vowels. Although surrounded by Indo-Aryan languages for millennia, Santali has retained a distinctly Munda grammar while absorbing substantial vocabulary from Bengali, Hindi and Odia.

Santali has a growing literature of poetry, novels, drama and journalism, and a Wikipedia edition written in Ol Chiki was launched in 2018, the first for an Indian tribal language. Language activists continue to campaign for Santali-medium education and fuller official use in Jharkhand and West Bengal.
