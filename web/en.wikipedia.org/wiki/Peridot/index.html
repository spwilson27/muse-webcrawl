Peridot

Peridot

Category: Nesosilicate (olivine group)

Formula: (Mg,Fe)₂SiO₄

Colour: Yellow-green to olive green

Mohs hardness: 6.5–7

Birthstone: August

Peridot is the gem-quality variety of the mineral olivine, composed of magnesium iron silicate. Its distinctive green colour comes from iron within the crystal structure, and unlike many gems it occurs in only one colour family, ranging from yellow-green to deep olive. It is the birthstone for August.

Occurrence

Peridot forms deep in the Earth's mantle and is brought to the surface by volcanic activity and tectonic uplift. Historic sources include Zabargad Island in the Red Sea, mined since ancient Egyptian times; today major deposits are found in Arizona (the San Carlos Apache Reservation is a leading source), Pakistan, Myanmar, China and Vietnam. Peridot crystals have also been recovered from pallasite meteorites, making it one of few gems known to occur extraterrestrially.

History

Peridot has been prized since antiquity — the Egyptians called it the "gem of the sun". Some historians believe the "emeralds" worn by Cleopatra were in fact peridots. Fine stones are faceted for jewellery, though the gem is somewhat soft and sensitive to acids and rapid temperature change.
