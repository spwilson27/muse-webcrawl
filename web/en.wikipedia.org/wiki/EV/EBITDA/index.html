EV/EBITDA

EV/EBITDA (also known as the enterprise multiple or EV multiple) is a ratio used to determine the value of a company. It compares a company's enterprise value (EV) to its earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization (EBITDA). The enterprise multiple is considered more comprehensive than the price–earnings ratio (P/E) because it accounts for the company's debt and cash position as well as its earnings.

Enterprise multiple = EV / EBITDA

Components

Enterprise value is calculated as market capitalization plus total debt, minority interest and preferred shares, minus total cash and cash equivalents. It represents the theoretical takeover price of a company.

EBITDA is earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization. It is often used as a proxy for a company's operating cash flow, although it excludes capital expenditure requirements and changes in working capital.

Interpretation

A lower EV/EBITDA multiple may indicate that a company is undervalued relative to its earnings power, while a higher multiple may suggest overvaluation or expectations of high future growth. Typical values vary considerably by industry: capital-intensive industries such as telecommunications and energy often trade at lower multiples, while technology companies frequently trade at higher ones.

Investors commonly compare a company's EV/EBITDA to those of comparable companies in the same industry and to the company's own historical multiple.

Reciprocal

The reciprocal of EV/EBITDA, EBITDA/EV, is sometimes referred to as the enterprise value yield and is used analogously to the earnings yield derived from the price–earnings ratio.

Advantages and limitations

Because enterprise value includes debt, the multiple is capital-structure neutral and allows comparisons between companies with different levels of leverage and across different tax jurisdictions. However, EBITDA ignores capital expenditures, which can be substantial for capital-intensive businesses, and the ratio does not account for differences in earnings quality or growth prospects.
