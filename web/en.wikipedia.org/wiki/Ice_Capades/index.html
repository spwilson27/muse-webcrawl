Ice Capades

The Ice Capades were a touring ice skating show of theatrical productions featuring elaborate costumes, choreography, and champion figure skaters. Founded in 1940, the Ice Capades entertained audiences across North America for more than five decades and helped popularize ice shows as a form of mass entertainment, alongside rivals such as the Ice Follies and Holiday on Ice.

History

The Ice Capades were organized in 1940 by a group of arena managers led by John H. Harris of Pittsburgh, who sought a touring production that could play the arenas they operated. The first performances took place in Hershey, Pennsylvania, in February 1940, and the show proved an immediate success. The term "icecapades" — a portmanteau of "ice" and "escapades" — entered popular usage as a generic name for any ice revue.

The shows combined classical figure skating with comedy acts, chorus lines of precision skaters, and segments themed to popular films, musicals, and cartoon characters. Olympic and world champion skaters who headlined or appeared with the Ice Capades included Dorothy Hamill, Peggy Fleming, and Scott Hamilton in guest appearances. Television specials brought the production to a national audience, and in 1941 Republic Pictures released the film Ice-Capades, featuring skating star Vera Hruba Ralston.

Decline and revival attempts

Changing tastes, rising production costs, and competition from other entertainment eroded the audience for touring ice shows in the 1980s and early 1990s. The company changed ownership several times; it was purchased in the 1980s by International Broadcasting Corporation and later came under the control of evangelist Pat Robertson's companies before filing for bankruptcy in the early 1990s. Dorothy Hamill bought the company's assets in 1993 and attempted a revival, but the original Ice Capades ceased touring in 1995. The name was later licensed for occasional productions.

Legacy

The Ice Capades professionalized the careers of competitive skaters, offering one of the few ways champion skaters could earn a living performing after their amateur careers, and their lavish production style influenced later touring shows such as Disney on Ice and Stars on Ice.
