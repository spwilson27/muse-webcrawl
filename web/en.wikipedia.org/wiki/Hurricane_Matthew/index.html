Hurricane Matthew

Hurricane Matthew was a powerful and destructive Category 5 Atlantic hurricane in October 2016, the first Category 5 Atlantic hurricane since Felix in 2007. It caused catastrophic damage in Haiti and significant flooding and beach erosion along the southeastern coast of the United States, with total damage estimated at about $16.5 billion.

Meteorological history

Matthew developed from a tropical wave on 28 September 2016 near the Windward Islands and rapidly intensified in the Caribbean, reaching peak winds of 165 mph (270 km/h) on 1 October south of Jamaica. It made landfall in Haiti's Tiburon Peninsula on 4 October as a Category 4 hurricane, then struck eastern Cuba, the Bahamas, and finally made landfall near McClellanville, South Carolina, on 8 October as a Category 1 storm.

Impact

Haiti suffered the worst effects, with estimates of at least 674 deaths (some sources higher), massive destruction in the Sud and Grand'Anse departments, and a worsened cholera outbreak. In the United States, at least 49 people died, with severe flooding in North and South Carolina. The name Matthew was retired after the season.
