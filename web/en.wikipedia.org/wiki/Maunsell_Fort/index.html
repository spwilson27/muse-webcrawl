Maunsell Forts

The Maunsell Forts are towers built in the Thames and Mersey estuaries during the Second World War to help defend the United Kingdom against German air raids and naval minelaying. Designed by the civil engineer Guy Maunsell, they were operated as Royal Navy sea forts and British Army anti-aircraft forts, and were decommissioned in the late 1950s.

Design and construction

Maunsell designed two types. The navy forts — Rough Sands, Sunk Head, Tongue Sands, and Knock John — were large structures resembling small oil platforms: a pontoon barge base was sunk onto the seabed, supporting two hollow concrete legs topped by a deck carrying guns, radar, and accommodation for over 100 men. The army forts in the Thames Estuary (Nore, Red Sands, and Shivering Sands) and Liverpool Bay consisted of clusters of seven interconnected steel towers on legs, housing Bofors and 3.7-inch anti-aircraft guns, searchlights, and radar. The forts were prefabricated in dockyards, floated out, and sunk into position in 1942–43.

Wartime service and later use

The estuary forts protected London and the Thames shipping lanes, and are credited with downing aircraft, destroying flying bombs, and deterring minelaying by E-boats. After abandonment, several forts were occupied in the 1960s by pirate radio stations such as Radio Caroline and Radio City. Rough Sands was seized in 1967 by Roy Bates and declared the independent micronation of the Principality of Sealand. Red Sands and Shivering Sands survive as decaying landmarks, and preservation groups have proposed restoring one group as a museum.
