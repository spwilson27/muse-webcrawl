Bragança (in the local Mirandese language Bergáncia) is a city and municipality in north-eastern Portugal, capital of the district of the same name in the traditional province of Trás-os-Montes, close to the border with Spain. One of Portugal's most remote and historically frontier cities, it is the seat of the district's administration and home to the Polytechnic Institute of Bragança.

History

The area was inhabited in Roman times, and the medieval town grew around a castle founded in the 12th century. King Sancho I granted Bragança a charter in 1187 and rebuilt its walls. In 1442 Afonso V created the Duchy of Braganza for his uncle Afonso, Count of Barcelos; the House of Braganza became Portugal's richest and most powerful noble family and ascended the throne in 1640 when the Duke of Braganza was acclaimed King John IV, restoring Portuguese independence from Spain. The dynasty reigned until the republic of 1910, and the Brazilian imperial family also descended from it.

Landmarks

The walled citadel preserves the 12th-century castle with its tall keep (Torre de Menagem), the five-sided Romanesque church of Santa Maria, and the Domus Municipalis — a rare Romanesque civic building, traditionally dated to the 12th or 13th century and unique in Iberia. The Montesinho Natural Park, one of Portugal's largest protected areas, lies just north of the city.
