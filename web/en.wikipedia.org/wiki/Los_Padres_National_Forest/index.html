Los Padres National Forest

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Los Padres National Forest Location: Central Coast and southern California, U.S.
Area: about 7,700 km² (1.9 million acres)
Established: 1936 (as a national forest)

Los Padres National Forest is a United States national forest in Southern and Central California, administered by the U.S. Forest Service. Covering approximately 1.9 million acres (7,700 km²), it extends across the Coast Ranges from the Big Sur coastline in Monterey County southward through San Luis Obispo, Santa Barbara and Ventura counties to the edge of Los Angeles County.

Geography and ecology

The forest encompasses rugged mountains, chaparral, oak woodland, grassland and stands of conifers including the Santa Lucia fir, one of the rarest firs in North America. Elevations range from sea level along the dramatic Big Sur coast to 8,831 feet (2,692 m) at Mount Pinos. Ten federally designated wilderness areas lie wholly or partly within the forest, including the Ventana and Silver Peak wildernesses.

Wildlife

Los Padres is a core release site for the California condor recovery programme; condors released by the Ventana Wildlife Society and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service soar over its Big Sur and San Rafael units. The forest also supports mountain lions, black bears, mule deer and the endangered arroyo toad and California red-legged frog.

Recreation and fire

Popular recreation areas include the trails of Big Sur, the hot springs of the Sespe Wilderness and the Figueroa Mountain area near Santa Barbara. Because of its chaparral vegetation and Mediterranean climate, the forest is highly fire-prone, and large wildfires — such as the Zaca Fire (2007) and the Thomas Fire (2017), which began in the forest — have burned extensive areas.
