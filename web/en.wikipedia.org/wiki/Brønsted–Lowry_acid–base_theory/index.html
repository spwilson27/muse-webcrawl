Brønsted–Lowry acid–base theory

Proposed1923
ProponentsJohannes Nicolaus Brønsted; Thomas Martin Lowry
DefinitionAcid = proton donor; base = proton acceptor

The Brønsted–Lowry theory is an acid–base reaction theory proposed independently in 1923 by Danish chemist Johannes Nicolaus Brønsted and English chemist Thomas Martin Lowry. It defines an acid as a substance that can donate a proton (H+) and a base as a substance that can accept a proton.

Concepts

Every acid–base reaction involves a conjugate pair: when an acid donates its proton it becomes its conjugate base, and when a base accepts a proton it becomes its conjugate acid. Water reacting with hydrogen chloride, for example, acts as a base, forming hydronium and chloride ions. Substances that can act as either acid or base, such as water and bicarbonate, are called amphiprotic. Relative acid strength is measured by the acid dissociation constant, pKa.

Comparison with other theories

The theory broadens the older Arrhenius definition, which required hydrogen-containing acids and hydroxide bases in water, but it is itself narrower than the Lewis theory, which defines acids and bases by electron-pair transfer and covers reactions with no protons at all.
