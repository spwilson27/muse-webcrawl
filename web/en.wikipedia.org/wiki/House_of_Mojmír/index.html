House of Mojmír

The House of Mojmír (Slovak: Mojmírovci) was the ruling dynasty of Great Moravia, the first Slavic state in Central Europe, during the 9th and early 10th centuries. The dynasty is named after its first known member, Mojmír I, who appears in Frankish sources from the 820s.

Rulers

Mojmír I united the Moravian and Nitrian principalities around 833, expelling Prince Pribina of Nitra and founding the Great Moravian state. His nephew and successor Rastislav (846–870) sought to free Moravia from East Frankish dominance and invited the Byzantine missionaries Cyril and Methodius in 863, who created the Glagolitic script and translated liturgy into Old Church Slavonic. Rastislav was betrayed by his nephew Svatopluk I (871–894), under whom Great Moravia reached its greatest territorial extent, encompassing Bohemia, parts of Poland, Hungary and Serbia at various times.

Decline

After Svatopluk's death, his sons Mojmír II and Svatopluk II fought a civil war that fatally weakened the state. Pressured by the Bavarians and the newly arrived Magyars, Great Moravia collapsed in the first decade of the 10th century, conventionally dated to 906/907, ending the rule of the Mojmír dynasty.
