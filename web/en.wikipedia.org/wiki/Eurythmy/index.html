Eurythmy

Eurythmy is an expressive movement art originated by Rudolf Steiner in conjunction with his wife Marie Steiner-von Sivers in the early twentieth century. Primarily a performance art, it is also used in education, especially in Waldorf schools, and — as a component of anthroposophic medicine — for therapeutic purposes.

History

Steiner began developing eurythmy in 1911, initially working with Lory Maier-Smits, the young daughter of one of his followers. The first public performance took place in 1919 in Zurich. Steiner described the art as "visible speech" and "visible singing": performers make gestures corresponding to the sounds of spoken language and the tones and intervals of music.

Practice

In speech eurythmy, each phoneme is assigned a characteristic gesture, and performers move in choreographed spatial forms that correspond to the structure of the poem or text being interpreted. In tone eurythmy, movements correspond to musical elements. Practitioners wear flowing silk veils and dresses intended to extend and colour the movement. Eurythmy is taught as a core subject throughout Waldorf education, and professional training schools exist in several countries.
