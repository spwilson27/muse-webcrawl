Council of Vienne

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Council of Vienne was the fifteenth ecumenical council of the Catholic Church, convened by Pope Clement V and held from October 1311 to May 1312 at Vienne, in the Dauphiné (then part of the Holy Roman Empire, now France). Some 300 bishops and prelates attended.

Suppression of the Templars

The council's most famous act was the issuance of the bull Vox in excelso (March 1312), by which Clement V dissolved the Order of the Knights Templar under pressure from King Philip IV of France, who had arrested the French Templars in 1307 on charges of heresy and other crimes. A second bull, Ad providam, transferred most Templar property to the Knights Hospitaller.

Other decrees

The council also condemned doctrines attributed to the Beguines and Beghards and to Peter John Olivi, legislated on the reform of mendicant orders, and decreed the founding of chairs of Hebrew, Arabic, and Chaldean at the universities of Paris, Oxford, Bologna, and Salamanca to train missionaries.
