Specular reflection

Specular reflection, or regular reflection, is the mirror-like reflection of waves, such as light, from a surface. In this process, each incident ray is reflected at the same angle to the surface normal as the incident ray, on the opposite side of the normal, in the plane formed by the incident ray and the normal. This behaviour is described by the law of reflection: the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, θi = θr.

Contrast with diffuse reflection

Specular reflection occurs when the reflecting surface is smooth on the scale of the wavelength of the radiation — a polished metal, a calm water surface, or a mirror. When a surface is rough at that scale, rays striking different microscopic facets are reflected in many directions, producing diffuse reflection, which is how most objects are visible from all directions. Most real surfaces exhibit a mixture of the two; polished surfaces with a coating of roughness are modelled in computer graphics by combining specular and diffuse components, for example in the Phong reflection model and microfacet BRDF models such as Cook–Torrance.

Applications

  • Mirrors rely on specular reflection from a metallic coating or total internal reflection in prisms.
  • Gloss and highlights: the perceived shininess of a material, and the bright highlights on objects, are specular effects.
  • Radar and acoustics: smooth surfaces reflect radio waves and sound specularly, which matters for stealth design (faceted surfaces direct reflections away from the source) and concert-hall acoustics.
  • Retroreflection is the special case in which light is returned directly toward its source.

Mathematical description

If d is a unit vector in the direction of the incident ray and n the unit surface normal, the reflected direction is r = d − 2(d·n)n. For electromagnetic waves, the fraction of power reflected specularly at an interface between two media is given by the Fresnel equations, depending on polarisation and the angle of incidence; beyond the critical angle, total internal reflection occurs.
