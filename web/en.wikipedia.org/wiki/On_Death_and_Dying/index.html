On Death and Dying

On Death and Dying is a book written by the Swiss-American psychiatrist Elisabeth Kübler-Ross and first published in 1969. Based on interviews with terminally ill patients at the University of Chicago's Billings Hospital, the book introduced the model of the "five stages of grief" and helped launch the modern hospice movement by bringing discussion of death and the needs of dying patients into mainstream medicine.

The five stages

Kübler-Ross proposed that terminally ill patients commonly pass through five stages in coping with the knowledge that they are dying: denial, anger, bargaining, depression, and acceptance (often abbreviated DABDA). The model was later popularly applied to grief and loss more generally. Kübler-Ross herself cautioned that the stages are not a rigid linear sequence and not every person experiences all of them.

Reception and influence

The book became a bestseller and transformed attitudes toward terminal care, encouraging doctors to listen to dying patients and contributing to the growth of hospice and palliative care. Critics have noted the limited, non-systematic evidence base of the stage model, and later bereavement research has questioned fixed stages, though the book's humanising influence on end-of-life care is widely acknowledged.
