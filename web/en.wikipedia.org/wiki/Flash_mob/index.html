Flash mob

A flash mob is a group of people who assemble suddenly in a public place, perform for a brief time, then quickly disperse, often for the purposes of entertainment, satire and artistic expression. Flash mobs are typically organized via telecommunications, social media or viral emails.

History

The first flash mobs were created in New York City in 2003 by Bill Wasik, senior editor of Harper's Magazine, who organised gatherings by email; the first attempt was thwarted when the target store was tipped off, but a subsequent event, in which dozens of people converged on a rug department in Macy's claiming to shop communally, succeeded. The term "flash mob" was added to major dictionaries in 2004.

Notable forms

Popular variants include "freeze" mobs, in which participants stand motionless for several minutes (as in the 2008 Grand Central Terminal freeze), mass choreographed dances in railway stations and shopping centres, and the annual No Pants Subway Ride organised by Improv Everywhere. Flash mobs have also been used for advertising, political protest and marriage proposals, and, in some reported cases, as a cover for theft ("flash robs"), which organisers of genuine flash mobs condemn.

Legal issues

Some jurisdictions have moved to restrict or require notification of flash mobs after incidents of disruption or violence associated with spontaneous youth gatherings arranged on social media.
