A Model of Christian Charity

"A Model of Christian Charity" is a sermon by Puritan leader John Winthrop, written in 1630 and probably preached aboard the ship Arbella or shortly before the Massachusetts Bay colonists' departure from England. It is famous for its image of the new colony as "a city upon a hill", an expression drawn from Jesus's Sermon on the Mount which has become a defining metaphor of American identity and American exceptionalism.

Content

Winthrop, the first governor of the Massachusetts Bay Colony, argues that God has ordained differences in human station — rich and poor, high and low — so that people may exercise mutual love and dependence. The colonists, he says, have entered a covenant with God for their enterprise; to succeed they must practise charity, "rejoice together, mourn together, labour and suffer together", and hold their goods accountable to the community. If they keep the covenant, God will prosper them and they shall be "as a city upon a hill", watched by the world; if they break it, they will become a byword.

Legacy

Although little known for centuries, the sermon was revived in the twentieth century and quoted by presidents including John F. Kennedy and Ronald Reagan, who popularised the "shining city upon a hill" as a symbol of the United States.
