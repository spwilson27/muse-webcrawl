The Office québécois de la langue française (OQLF) is a public organisation of the Government of Quebec, Canada, responsible for the standardisation and promotion of the French language in the province. It enforces the Charter of the French Language and provides linguistic guidance to the public and businesses.

History

The office was created in 1961 as the Office de la langue française, during Quebec's Quiet Revolution, amid growing concern that French was being displaced by English in Montreal's economy. Its mandate expanded under the Charter of the French Language (Bill 101) of 1977, which made French the official language of Quebec. In 2002 the office received its present name following a merger with other language bodies, and its powers were strengthened again by Bill 96 in 2022.

Functions

The OQLF standardises French terminology through its Grand dictionnaire terminologique, investigates complaints about violations of the Charter — such as signage that does not give French sufficient prominence — and certifies businesses' francisation programmes. Its inspectors' interventions, sometimes reported in Canadian media as the work of "language police", have periodically provoked controversy, as in the 2013 "pastagate" affair, when a restaurant was criticised over an Italian word on its menu and the office's head resigned.
