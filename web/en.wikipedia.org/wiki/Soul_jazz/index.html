Soul jazz

Soul jazz
Stylistic origins: Hard bop, blues, gospel, rhythm and blues
Peak period: Late 1950s–1960s
Key instruments: Hammond organ, tenor saxophone, guitar, drums
Notable artists: Jimmy Smith, Horace Silver, Cannonball Adderley, Ramsey Lewis, Les McCann

Soul jazz is a subgenre of jazz that developed in the late 1950s, blending hard bop with strong influences from blues, gospel and rhythm and blues. It favours earthy, groove-based playing, singable melodies and a down-home feel over harmonic complexity.

Characteristics and history

Soul jazz is strongly associated with the organ trio format — Hammond B-3 organ, guitar and drums — popularised by Jimmy Smith, and with small combos led by pianists such as Horace Silver and Ramsey Lewis and alto saxophonist Cannonball Adderley. Many recordings were made live in clubs for labels like Blue Note and Prestige. Hits crossing over to the pop charts included Adderley's "Mercy, Mercy, Mercy" (1966), Lewis's "The 'In' Crowd" (1965) and Lee Morgan's "The Sidewinder" (1964).

Legacy

Soul jazz fed into jazz-funk and was heavily sampled by hip hop producers; its accessibility also underlies the later acid jazz movement. The term is sometimes confused with "soulful" hard bop generally, but its core repertoire of bluesy organ combos defines the style.
