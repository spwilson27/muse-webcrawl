Container orchestration

Container orchestration is the automation of the deployment, management, scaling, networking and availability of containers (such as those managed by Docker) across clusters of host machines in cloud and data-center environments.

Functions

  • Scheduling — placing containers on nodes based on resource requests, constraints and affinity.
  • Self-healing — restarting failed containers and replacing failed nodes.
  • Scaling — horizontal autoscaling with load.
  • Service discovery and load balancing — stable networking names and traffic distribution.
  • Rollouts and rollbacks — declarative, versioned deployments.

Platforms

The dominant platform is Kubernetes (open-sourced by Google in 2014), governed by the Cloud Native Computing Foundation. Alternatives include Docker Swarm, Apache Mesos/Marathon, HashiCorp Nomad, and managed services such as Amazon ECS/EKS, Azure AKS and Google GKE.

History

Orchestration grew out of internal systems — Google's Borg and Omega — and the rapid adoption of Docker after 2013, which standardized the container image format and left scheduling to a new layer of tooling.
