The acre is a unit of land area used in the British imperial and United States customary systems. It is defined as the area of one chain by one furlong (66 by 660 feet), which is exactly 4,840 square yards, or 1/640 of a square mile. In metric terms an acre equals 4,046.8564224 square metres, or about 0.4047 hectares.

History

The word derives from Old English æcer, originally meaning open field. In the Middle Ages an acre was conceived as the amount of land a yoke of oxen could plough in a single day, and its dimensions were standardised as a strip one furlong long and one chain wide — a shape suited to ploughing long furrows. Statutes under Edward I and later monarchs fixed its size, and it became the standard unit for agricultural land, taxation and conveyancing across England and, later, the British Empire and the United States.

Usage

The acre remains in everyday use for land measurement in the United States and, informally, in the United Kingdom and several Commonwealth countries, although the hectare is the official unit in most. Related historical units include the Scottish acre, the Irish (plantation) acre and the rood (a quarter of an acre). A commercial acre, used in some American real-estate contexts, deducts space for roads and pavements. For comparison, an American football field including end zones covers about 1.32 acres, and a typical association football pitch is roughly 1.7 acres.

Survey and legal definitions

The international acre is based on the international yard of exactly 0.9144 metres. The United States survey acre, based on the survey foot, differed by about two parts per million and was deprecated along with the US survey foot at the end of 2022.
