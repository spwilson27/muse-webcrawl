Sulfide mineral

The sulfide minerals are a class of minerals containing sulfide (S2−) or disulfide (S22−) as the major anion. Some sulfide minerals are economically important as metal ores. The sulfide class also includes the selenides, tellurides, arsenides, antimonides, bismuthinides, sulfarsenides and sulfosalts, in which arsenic, antimony or related elements partly substitute for sulfur.

Properties

Most sulfides are opaque with distinct colours and coloured streaks; many have a metallic lustre and are relatively soft and dense compared with silicate minerals. Bonding is more covalent than in oxides, which accounts for their semiconductor behaviour and their tendency to occur in veins and massive ore deposits. Sulfides weather readily at the surface, oxidising to sulfates and oxides and generating sulfuric acid — the basis of acid mine drainage.

Common sulfide minerals

  • Pyrite FeS2 — "fool's gold", the most common sulfide; used for sulfur and sulfuric acid.
  • Chalcopyrite CuFeS2 — the principal ore of copper.
  • Galena PbS — the principal ore of lead and an important source of silver.
  • Sphalerite ZnS — the principal ore of zinc.
  • Pyrrhotite Fe1−xS — magnetic iron sulfide associated with nickel deposits.
  • Cinnabar HgS — the principal ore of mercury.
  • Molybdenite MoS2 — the principal ore of molybdenum; a layered lubricant.
  • Pentlandite (Fe,Ni)9S8 — the principal ore of nickel.
  • Bornite Cu5FeS4 and chalcocite Cu2S — copper ores.
  • Arsenopyrite FeAsS, realgar As4S4 and orpiment As2S3 — arsenic sulfides.

Occurrence

Sulfides form in a variety of geological settings: magmatic segregation deposits (such as Sudbury and Norilsk, rich in nickel and platinum-group elements), porphyry copper systems, hydrothermal veins, volcanogenic massive sulfide deposits on the seafloor (including modern "black smoker" chimneys), and sedimentary exhalative and Mississippi Valley-type lead-zinc deposits. Because most base-metal ores are sulfides, the class dominates the mining of copper, lead, zinc, nickel and molybdenum.

Classification

In the Strunz classification, sulfides form class 2, subdivided by metal-to-sulfur ratio and by structural type. Dana's system similarly groups sulfides, sulfosalts and related compounds by composition and structure.
