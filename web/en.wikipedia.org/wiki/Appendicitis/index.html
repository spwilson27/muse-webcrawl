Appendicitis

Appendicitis is inflammation of the appendix, a small pouch attached to the caecum. It is one of the most common causes of emergency abdominal surgery worldwide. Appendicitis typically results from obstruction of the appendiceal lumen, for example by a faecalith, followed by bacterial overgrowth, swelling and impaired blood flow.

Symptoms and diagnosis

The classic presentation is abdominal pain that begins vaguely around the navel and migrates to the right lower quadrant, with loss of appetite, nausea and low-grade fever. Tenderness at McBurney's point is a well-known sign. Diagnosis combines history, examination, blood tests and imaging such as ultrasound or CT; scoring systems including the Alvarado score may assist. Presentations in children, pregnant women and older adults can be atypical.

Treatment

The standard treatment is appendectomy, usually laparoscopic, together with fluids and antibiotics. Selected uncomplicated cases may be managed with antibiotics alone, with a risk of recurrence. Delay can lead to perforation, abscess or generalised peritonitis, which carry greater risk.
