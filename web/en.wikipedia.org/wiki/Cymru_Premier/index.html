Cymru Premier

The Cymru Premier, known as the JD Cymru Premier for sponsorship reasons, is the national football league of Wales. It has both professional and semi-professional status clubs and is at the top of the Welsh football league system. Prior to 2002, the league was known as the League of Wales, but changed its name as part of a sponsorship deal to the Welsh Premier League. The league was rebranded as the Cymru Premier for the 2019–20 season.

Formation

The league was formed in October 1991 by Alun Evans, then secretary-general of the Football Association of Wales (FAW), amid fears that the Welsh national team might be abolished by FIFA if Wales continued without its own national league. At the time, Wales was unusual in having no national league of its own; its biggest clubs played in the English football league system, and many Welsh clubs in the Welsh pyramid played in English leagues. The FAW therefore established a national league, and clubs that refused to join were forced to play their "home" matches outside Wales for several seasons.

Format

The league has 12 clubs. The season is split: in phase one each team plays the others twice (home and away); in phase two the league splits into a championship conference (top six) and a play-off conference (bottom six). The champions qualify for the UEFA Champions League qualifying rounds, and other European places are decided through runners-up spots and end-of-season play-offs for the UEFA Conference League.

Dominant clubs

The New Saints (TNS), based in Oswestry, have dominated the league, winning a record number of titles, including a long run of consecutive championships during the 2010s and 2020s. Other title winners include Barry Town United, Rhyl, Bangor City and Connah's Quay Nomads.

European competition

Welsh Premier clubs historically struggled in Europe, but The New Saints achieved a milestone in 2024 by becoming the first club from Wales's domestic league system to reach the league phase of a major UEFA competition (the UEFA Conference League).
