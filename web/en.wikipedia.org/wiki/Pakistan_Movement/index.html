Pakistan Movement

The Pakistan Movement was a political movement in British India in the first half of the 20th century that aimed for the creation of an independent Muslim state in the north-western and eastern Muslim-majority regions of the subcontinent. Led by the All-India Muslim League under Muhammad Ali Jinnah, and intellectually inspired by Muhammad Iqbal, the movement culminated in the Partition of India and the creation of the Dominion of Pakistan on 14 August 1947.

Origins

The movement's roots lay in the 19th-century reform efforts of Sir Syed Ahmad Khan and the Aligarh Movement, which sought to reconcile Indian Muslims with modern education and British rule while preserving a distinct Muslim political identity. The founding of the Muslim League in 1906 and the grant of separate electorates for Muslims in 1909 institutionalised that identity. Iqbal's presidential address at Allahabad in 1930 articulated the idea of a consolidated Muslim state in the north-west; the name "Pakistan" was coined by Choudhry Rahmat Ali in his 1933 pamphlet Now or Never.

The road to partition

After the League's poor showing in the 1937 provincial elections, Jinnah reorganised it into a mass movement. In March 1940 the League adopted the Lahore Resolution, demanding that Muslim-majority areas be grouped into "independent states". The failure of negotiations with the Indian National Congress — including the collapse of the Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946 — and mounting communal violence, including Direct Action Day in August 1946, made partition increasingly inevitable. Under the Mountbatten Plan of June 1947, British India was divided, and Pakistan emerged as a dominion comprising West Pakistan (including today's Pakistan) and East Bengal (later Bangladesh).
