Color charge

Color charge is a property of quarks and gluons that is related to the particles' strong interactions in the theory of quantum chromodynamics (QCD). It has nothing to do with visual color; the name was chosen because the three values — red, green and blue — combine to a neutral "white" much as in additive color mixing.

Colors and anticolors

Quarks carry one of three color charges (red, green, blue) and antiquarks carry anticolors. Gluons, the carriers of the strong force, carry a color–anticolor pair, giving eight types. Observable particles must be color-neutral: baryons combine one quark of each color, and mesons combine a color with its anticolor.

Confinement

Unlike electric charge, color charge is never observed in isolation: quarks and gluons are confined within hadrons, a phenomenon known as color confinement. The strong coupling grows at large distances, so attempting to separate quarks produces new quark–antiquark pairs instead.
