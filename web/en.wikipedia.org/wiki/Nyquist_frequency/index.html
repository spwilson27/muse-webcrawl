Nyquist frequency

The Nyquist frequency (or folding frequency), named after electronic engineer Harry Nyquist, is a device or system property. When a continuous function or signal is sampled, it equals half the sampling rate. In other words, it is the highest frequency that can be unambiguously represented by the sampled data: signal components above the Nyquist frequency are "folded" back into the representable range, producing the distortion known as aliasing.


A signal above half the sampling rate folds back and aliases into the baseband.

Relation to the sampling theorem

According to the Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem, a band-limited signal can be perfectly reconstructed from its samples provided the sampling rate exceeds twice the signal's highest frequency component; that minimum required rate is called the Nyquist rate. The Nyquist frequency should not be confused with the Nyquist rate: the rate is a property of the signal (twice its bandwidth), whereas the Nyquist frequency is a property of the sampler (half its sampling rate). For compact disc audio, sampled at 44,100 Hz, the Nyquist frequency is 22,050 Hz, just above the upper limit of human hearing.

Aliasing and anti-aliasing

If a signal contains energy above the Nyquist frequency, the sampled version contains spurious low-frequency components. Practical systems therefore apply an anti-aliasing filter before sampling to attenuate frequencies above the Nyquist frequency, and rely on the transition band of real filters by sampling somewhat faster than the theoretical minimum.
