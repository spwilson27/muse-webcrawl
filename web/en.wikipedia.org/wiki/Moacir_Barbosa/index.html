Moacir Barbosa Nascimento (27 March 1921 – 7 April 2000) was a Brazilian professional footballer who played as a goalkeeper. He is remembered primarily as the goalkeeper of the Brazil national team in the decisive match of the 1950 FIFA World Cup, the famous Maracanaço, in which Uruguay defeated Brazil 2–1 at the Maracanã Stadium and denied the hosts the trophy.

Career

Barbosa began his career in São Paulo before joining Vasco da Gama in 1945, where he became one of the most celebrated goalkeepers in Brazilian football. With Vasco he won several Campeonato Carioca titles and was part of the celebrated "Expresso da Vitória" side. He was known for his agility, courage in coming off his line, and his habit of playing without gloves.

For Brazil, Barbosa won the 1949 South American Championship and kept goal throughout the 1950 World Cup on home soil. Brazil needed only a draw in the final match against Uruguay to be crowned champions. After Brazil took the lead through Friaça, Uruguay equalised and then, in the 79th minute, Alcides Ghiggia beat Barbosa at his near post to score the winning goal.

Legacy and later life

The defeat was treated in Brazil as a national tragedy, and Barbosa — one of the few Black players in the team — was widely scapegoated for the rest of his life. He later recalled being pointed out in public decades afterwards as "the man who made Brazil cry." In a much-quoted remark, he observed that under Brazilian law the maximum prison sentence was thirty years, yet he had been punished for more than forty.

In 1993, during preparations for the 1994 World Cup, Barbosa was reportedly turned away from a Brazil training session so as not to bring bad luck. He died in 2000 in Praia Grande, São Paulo. In later years his reputation has been substantially rehabilitated, and he is now frequently ranked among the greatest Brazilian goalkeepers of his era.
