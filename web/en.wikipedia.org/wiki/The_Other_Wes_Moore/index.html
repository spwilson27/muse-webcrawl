The Other Wes Moore

The Other Wes Moore: One Name, Two Fates is a 2010 non-fiction book by Wes Moore, a Rhodes Scholar, decorated Army combat veteran, and later the 63rd governor of Maryland. The book contrasts the author's life with that of another Baltimore man with the same name, a few years older, who is serving a life sentence for his role in an armed robbery in which a police officer was killed.

Synopsis

Moore learned of his namesake from a Baltimore Sun article while studying at Oxford and began a correspondence and series of prison visits. The book alternates chapters between the two men's lives: both grew up fatherless in similar Baltimore neighbourhoods amid the crack epidemic, but their paths diverged — the author through a mother's intervention, military school, mentors, and education; the other Moore through drug dealing, dropping out, and ultimately a fatal jewellery-store robbery in 2000. Moore resists simple explanations, emphasising instead the accumulation of small decisions, expectations, and support systems.

Reception

The book became a New York Times bestseller and a widespread choice for first-year and common-reading programs at colleges and schools. A young-adult adaptation, Discovering Wes Moore, followed in 2012.
