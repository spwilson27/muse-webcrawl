St James's

St James's is a district in the City of Westminster, London, forming part of the West End. It is bounded to the north by Piccadilly, to the west by Green Park, to the south by The Mall and St James's Park, and to the east by Haymarket. The area takes its name from St James's Palace, the most senior royal palace in London, which was built by Henry VIII on the site of a leper hospital dedicated to St James the Less.

Character

St James's has long been associated with the British aristocracy and royalty, and the district is famous for its gentlemen's clubs concentrated along Pall Mall and St James's Street, including White's, Boodle's, and Brooks's. Jermyn Street is renowned for its traditional shirtmakers, tailors, perfumers, and other luxury retailers, many trading since the 18th and 19th centuries. Notable institutions in the area include the auction house Christie's, the wine merchants Berry Bros. & Rudd, and Fortnum & Mason on Piccadilly.

Notable sites

Besides St James's Palace, landmarks include Clarence House, the official London residence of the King before his accession and now of the Queen Mother historically, Spencer House, the London Library in St James's Square, and the church of St James's, Piccadilly, designed by Christopher Wren. The diplomatic phrase "Court of St James's" is still used as the formal name of the British royal court, and ambassadors are accredited to it.
