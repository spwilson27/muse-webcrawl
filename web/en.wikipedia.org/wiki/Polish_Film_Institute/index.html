The Polish Film Institute (Polish: Polski Instytut Sztuki Filmowej, PISF) is a state legal entity established under the Polish Cinematography Act of 30 June 2005 to support the development of Polish cinema. Modelled in part on film-funding bodies elsewhere in Europe, the Institute co-finances film production, supports film education and festivals, promotes Polish film abroad, and finances the digitisation and maintenance of cinema infrastructure.

Funding

The Institute is financed largely by a levy — typically 1.5% of revenue — on broadcasters, cable and satellite operators, digital platforms, distributors and cinema exhibitors, supplemented by state budget subsidies. This mechanism replaced the earlier system of direct ministerial grants and gives the Institute an annual budget in the range of 150–200 million złoty, making it the principal public funder of Polish feature films.

Activities and supported films

PISF co-financed many of the internationally recognised Polish films of the 21st century, including Ida (2013) by Paweł Pawlikowski, which won the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film; Cold War (2018); Corpus Christi (2019) by Jan Komasa; Agnieszka Holland's Spoor (2017); and EO (2022) by Jerzy Skolimowski. The Institute also runs the Polish Film Festival support programmes, co-organises Poland's Oscar submission selection, funds film schools and education projects, and supports the network of arthouse ("studio") cinemas across Poland.

Governance

The Institute is headed by a director appointed by the Minister of Culture. Its first director was Agnieszka Odorowicz (2005–2015), followed by Magdalena Sroka (2015–2017, dismissed amid controversy), Radosław Śmigulski (from 2017), and Karolina Rozwód (appointed 2024). A council of film-industry representatives advises on funding priorities.
