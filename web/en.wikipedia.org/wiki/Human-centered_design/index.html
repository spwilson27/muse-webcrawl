Human-centered design

Human-centered design (HCD), also called human-centred design, is an approach to problem-solving and design that develops solutions by involving the human perspective in all steps of the process — observing the problem in context, brainstorming, prototyping, and testing with the people who will use the result.

Process

The international standard ISO 9241-210 describes human-centred design for interactive systems through activities including understanding the context of use, specifying user requirements, producing design solutions, and evaluating them against the requirements. Design firms such as IDEO popularised a three-phase framing — inspiration, ideation, and implementation — and the related concept of design thinking.

Principles

Key principles include an explicit understanding of users and their environments, involvement of users throughout design, iterative refinement driven by user-centred evaluation, attention to the whole user experience, and multidisciplinary design teams. Cognitive scientist Don Norman is a leading advocate, popularising the term in The Design of Everyday Things.

Applications

HCD is applied in product and software design, public services, healthcare, and international development, where it is used to design interventions that fit local practices and constraints.
