White Star Line

The White Star Line was a British shipping line. Founded in Liverpool in 1845 as a packet line to Australia, it was refounded in 1869 by Thomas Ismay as the Oceanic Steam Navigation Company and became famous for its transatlantic passenger liners, most notably RMS Titanic.

History

Under Ismay and his son J. Bruce Ismay, White Star emphasised comfort and size over speed in rivalry with Cunard Line. Ships were built by Harland & Wolff in Belfast, including the Oceanic class and the great Olympic-class liners Olympic, Titanic and Britannic, all lost or famous through disaster: Titanic sank in 1912 and Britannic in 1916.

In 1902 White Star joined J.P. Morgan's International Mercantile Marine combine. Financial difficulties during the Great Depression led to its merger with Cunard in 1934, forming Cunard-White Star Line; the White Star name was dropped in 1950.

Legacy

The last surviving White Star vessel is SS Nomadic (1911), a tender preserved in Belfast. White Star ships are recognisable by their buff funnels with black tops and names ending in -ic.
