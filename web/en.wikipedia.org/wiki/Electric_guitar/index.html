Electric guitar

An electric guitar is a guitar that requires external amplification to be heard at typical performance volumes. It uses electromagnetic pickups to convert the vibration of its steel strings into electrical signals, which are sent to an amplifier and loudspeaker. The electric guitar was pivotal in the development of blues, rock and roll, heavy metal, jazz fusion and many other genres.

History

The first commercially successful electric guitar was the lap-steel "Frying Pan" made by Rickenbacker in 1931–32. Archtop electrics such as the Gibson ES-150 followed. In 1950–51 Leo Fender introduced the mass-produced solid-body Telecaster, followed by the Stratocaster in 1954; Gibson responded with the Les Paul in 1952. Solid bodies reduced feedback and sustain problems, enabling high-volume playing.

Design

Electric guitars may be solid-body, semi-hollow or hollow-body. Pickups are single-coil (bright, prone to hum) or humbuckers (two coils cancelling hum, with a thicker sound). Controls typically include volume and tone knobs and a pickup selector. Effects such as distortion, overdrive and reverb shaped the instrument's sound in popular music.

Cultural impact

Players such as Chuck Berry, Jimi Hendrix, Eric Clapton, Jimmy Page and Eddie Van Halen made the electric guitar the emblematic instrument of rock music, and iconic models have become highly collectible.
