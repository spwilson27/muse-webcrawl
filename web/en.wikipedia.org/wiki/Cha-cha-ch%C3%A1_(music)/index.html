Cha-cha-chá (music)

Cha-cha-chá
OriginCuba, early 1950s
CreatorEnrique Jorrín
Derived fromDanzón, danzonete
Time signature4/4

Cha-cha-chá is a genre of Cuban dance music that emerged in the early 1950s, developed by composer and violinist Enrique Jorrín while playing with Orquesta América. It evolved from the danzón through Jorrín's new rhythmic style, the nuevo ritmo.

Origin of the name

According to Jorrín, the name is onomatopoeic: it imitates the sound of dancers' feet shuffling on the floor during the characteristic triple step, as heard in early recordings such as "La engañadora" (1953), widely considered the first cha-cha-chá.

Musical structure

Cha-cha-chá is written in 4/4 time at a moderate tempo. Its defining feature is a syncopated rhythm in which the beat is split — "two, three, cha-cha-chá" — created by the güiro and the melody instruments playing in unison. The classic charanga francesa instrumentation combines European strings and French flute with an Afro-Cuban rhythm section.

Popularity and dance

The genre became an international craze in the 1950s, spread by orchestras such as Orquesta Aragón and José Fajardo's band, and it remains a standard category in Latin ballroom dancing competitions, where it is danced as "cha-cha".
