Sutra

In Indian literary traditions, sutra (Sanskrit: सूत्र, romanized: sūtra, lit. "string, thread") is an aphorism or a collection of aphorisms in the form of a manual or, more broadly, a condensed manual or text. Sutras are a genre of ancient and medieval Indian texts found in Hinduism, Buddhism and Jainism.

In Hinduism, sutras are a distinct type of literary composition, based on short aphoristic statements, generally using various technical terms. This literary form was designed for concision, as the texts were intended to be memorized by students in some of the formal methods of scriptural and scientific study. Since each line is highly condensed, many commentaries (bhāṣya) were written to explain and expand them.

Buddhist sutras

In Buddhism, sutras (Pali: sutta) are canonical scriptures, many of which are regarded as records of the oral teachings of Gautama Buddha. They form part of the Sutta Pitaka of the Pali Canon and include collections such as the Digha Nikaya, the Majjhima Nikaya and the Samyutta Nikaya. In Mahayana Buddhism, later compositions such as the Heart Sutra, the Diamond Sutra and the Lotus Sutra hold a central place.

Jain sutras

In Jainism, sutras form part of the canonical literature (Agamas), with works such as the Tattvartha Sutra of Umaswati accepted by both the Digambara and Svetambara traditions.

Examples in Hindu tradition

Well-known Hindu sutra texts include the Brahma Sutras, the Yoga Sutras of Patanjali, the Nyaya Sutras, the Kama Sutra, and the Dharma Sutras dealing with ritual and social duty.
