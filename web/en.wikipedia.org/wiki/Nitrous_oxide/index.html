Nitrous oxide

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The N2O molecule

Nitrous oxide (dinitrogen oxide, N2O), commonly known as laughing gas, is a colourless, non-flammable gas with a slightly sweet odour and taste. It is used in surgery and dentistry for its anaesthetic and analgesic effects, as an oxidiser in rocketry and motor racing, and as a propellant in food aerosols such as whipped cream. It is also a potent greenhouse gas and an ozone-depleting substance.

History

The gas was first synthesised by Joseph Priestley in 1772. Humphry Davy investigated its effects in the 1790s, coined the name "laughing gas" for the euphoria it produced, and suggested it might relieve surgical pain. Its anaesthetic use was established in the 1840s, when dentist Horace Wells demonstrated painless tooth extraction under the gas.

Medical use

A 50/50 mixture of nitrous oxide and oxygen (Entonox) is widely used for analgesia in childbirth, emergency medicine, and minor procedures. Nitrous oxide is a weak general anaesthetic, so it is usually combined with more potent agents for surgery. It acts principally as an NMDA receptor antagonist. Chronic or recreational exposure can inactivate vitamin B12, causing neurological damage, and the gas is subject to legal controls in several countries because of recreational misuse.

Environmental impact

Nitrous oxide has a global warming potential about 270 times that of carbon dioxide over 100 years and an atmospheric lifetime of over a century. Agriculture — chiefly nitrogen fertilisers and manure — is the largest human source. It is also now the most significant ozone-depleting emission not controlled by the Montreal Protocol.
