Visa policy of Vietnam

The visa policy of Vietnam describes the requirements foreign nationals must meet to enter the Socialist Republic of Vietnam. Most visitors to Vietnam must obtain a visa in advance from a Vietnamese diplomatic mission, through an online e-visa system, or through a licensed travel agency that arranges a visa approval letter for pickup on arrival. Certain nationalities are exempt from visa requirements for short stays, and Vietnam has progressively expanded its electronic visa programme as part of efforts to promote tourism and trade.

Visa exemption

Citizens of a number of countries may enter Vietnam without a visa for stays of up to 14, 21, 30, 45 or 90 days depending on nationality. Nationals of ASEAN member states generally enjoy visa-free access for short stays under bilateral and regional arrangements. Citizens of several European countries, Japan, South Korea and others have at various times benefited from unilateral visa exemptions for tourism, typically for stays of up to 45 days following reforms that took effect in August 2023.

Visitors to the island of Phú Quốc may enter visa-free for up to 30 days under a special economic zone policy, provided they arrive directly at Phú Quốc International Airport or transit through another Vietnamese gateway without entering the mainland.

Electronic visa (e-visa)

Vietnam launched its electronic visa system in February 2017, initially as a pilot for citizens of 40 countries. In August 2023, the National Assembly's amendments to the Law on Entry, Exit, Transit and Residence of Foreigners extended e-visa validity from 30 to 90 days, allowed multiple entries, and opened the programme to citizens of all countries and territories. E-visas are issued through the official immigration portal and are valid at a list of designated international border gates, airports and seaports.

Visa on arrival

Visa on arrival is available at Vietnam's international airports for travellers who obtain an approval letter in advance through a Vietnamese travel agency or visa service. The traveller presents the letter, a completed entry-exit form, photographs and a stamping fee in US dollars or Vietnamese đồng at the landing visa counter. This method is not available at land border crossings.

History

Before the Đổi Mới reforms, entry to Vietnam was tightly restricted. As the country opened to tourism in the 1990s and 2000s, visa procedures were gradually simplified. The 2014 Law on Entry, Exit, Transit and Residence of Foreigners unified the visa categories, replacing earlier ordinances. Subsequent amendments lengthened stays and expanded digital processing, reflecting the government's aim to make tourism a spearhead economic sector.

Visa categories

Vietnamese visas are classified by purpose, including tourist (DL), business (DN), labour (LĐ), investment (ĐT), student (DH), diplomatic (NG) and family visit (TT) categories, each with its own maximum validity and conditions for extension or conversion to a temporary residence card.
