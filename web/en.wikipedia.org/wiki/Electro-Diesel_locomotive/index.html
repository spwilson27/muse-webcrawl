Electro-diesel locomotive

An electro-diesel locomotive (also called a dual-mode or bi-mode locomotive) is a railway locomotive that can be powered either from an external electrical supply, such as overhead lines or a third rail, or from an on-board diesel engine driving a generator. This allows through running over routes that are only partly electrified.

Operation

On electrified sections the locomotive draws current from the line supply to power its traction motors; where electrification ends, the diesel engine is started and provides power instead. The diesel engine is usually smaller and less powerful than that of a pure diesel locomotive of similar size, since full performance is expected mainly under electric operation.

Examples

Early examples were built for operation into terminals where steam and later diesel exhaust was restricted, such as the New York Central and New Haven locomotives serving Grand Central Terminal, which used third rail in tunnels and diesel power elsewhere. In Britain, the Southern Region's Class 73 electro-diesels used the 750 V DC third rail with an auxiliary diesel engine for yards and non-electrified branches, and the later Class 88 works from 25 kV AC overhead lines. Modern bi-mode multiple units, such as Britain's Class 800, apply the same principle to passenger trains, and dual-mode freight locomotives operate in North America for services into New York Penn Station.
