The Chūbu region (中部地方, Chūbu-chihō) is the central region of Honshu, Japan's main island. "Chūbu" means "central part", and the region spans the middle of the country between Kantō in the east and Kansai in the west. It is conventionally divided into three subregions: Hokuriku on the Sea of Japan coast, Kōshin'etsu in the mountainous interior, and Tōkai on the Pacific side.

Geography

Chūbu contains the Japanese Alps — the Hida, Kiso, and Akaishi mountain ranges — including many of Japan's highest peaks and the country's longest river, the Shinano. The wide Nōbi Plain around Nagoya opens to the Pacific, while heavy snowfall (the yukiguni, or snow country) characterises the Sea of Japan side in winter.

Economy and cities

The region's largest city is Nagoya, centre of the Chūkyō metropolitan area and one of Japan's great industrial regions, home to Toyota Motor Corporation's headquarters in the nearby city of Toyota and a dense cluster of automotive and aerospace manufacturing. Other major cities include Niigata, Shizuoka, Kanazawa, and Gifu. Mount Fuji, on the border of Shizuoka and Yamanashi prefectures, is the region's most famous landmark.
