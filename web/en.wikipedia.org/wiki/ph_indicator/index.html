pH indicator

TypeAcid–base indicator
ExamplesLitmus, phenolphthalein, methyl orange, bromothymol blue
UseEstimating pH; titration endpoints

A pH indicator is a halochromic chemical compound added in small amounts to a solution so the pH (acidity or basicity) of the solution can be determined visually or spectroscopically by changes in absorption or emission properties.

Principle

Indicators are typically weak acids or bases whose protonated and deprotonated forms have different colors. As the pH passes through the indicator's transition range (around its pKa), the equilibrium shifts and the color changes.

Common indicators

Litmus, derived from lichens, turns red below pH 4.5 and blue above 8.3. Phenolphthalein is colorless in acid and pink above about 8.2. Methyl orange is red in strong acid and yellow above 4.4. Universal indicator is a mixture giving a continuous rainbow of colors across the pH scale, and is impregnated into pH test paper.

Applications

Indicators mark the endpoint of acid–base titrations, test swimming-pool water and aquarium water, and are used in soil testing and education.
