seL4

seL4 is a high-assurance microkernel operating system, a member of the L4 family, developed principally by the Trustworthy Systems group at CSIRO's Data61 in Australia. It is famous as the first operating-system kernel whose implementation was formally verified — that is, mathematically proved to conform to its specification and to be free of whole classes of bugs such as buffer overflows, null-pointer errors and memory leaks.

Design

seL4 provides minimal mechanisms in the kernel — address spaces, threads, inter-process communication and capability-based access control — and leaves services such as device drivers and file systems to user-level programs, so faults in them cannot compromise the kernel. The formal proof, completed in 2009 and covering about 10,000 lines of C code, was later extended to security properties (integrity and confidentiality) and to binary-level correctness.

Adoption

seL4 was released as open source in 2014 and is stewarded by the seL4 Foundation. It has been used in defence and security research — including DARPA's HACMS programme, which rebuilt a military helicopter's software on seL4 and demonstrated resistance to red-team attacks — and in autonomous vehicles, avionics and critical infrastructure.
