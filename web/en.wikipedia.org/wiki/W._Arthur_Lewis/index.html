Sir William Arthur Lewis (23 January 1915 – 15 June 1991) was a Saint Lucian economist and the James Madison Professor of Political Economy at Princeton University. A founder of modern development economics, he is best known for the dual-sector model of economic development. In 1979 he shared the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences with Theodore Schultz, becoming the first Black person to win a Nobel Prize in a category other than peace or literature.

Early life and career

Lewis was born in Castries, Saint Lucia, then part of the British West Indies, to parents who had emigrated from Antigua. Barred as a Black man from an engineering scholarship by racial restrictions, he instead won a scholarship to the London School of Economics, where he earned a BSc (1937) and PhD (1940) and joined the faculty at 22 — its first Black lecturer. He later held chairs at the University of Manchester (1948–1958), where his famous 1954 paper appeared, served as principal of the University College of the West Indies, and in 1963 moved to Princeton.

The dual-sector model

In "Economic Development with Unlimited Supplies of Labour" (1954), often called the most influential article in development economics, Lewis modelled a developing economy as two sectors: a large traditional subsistence sector with surplus labour whose marginal productivity is near zero, and a small modern capitalist sector. Labour migrates from the subsistence sector to the capitalist sector at a wage slightly above subsistence, generating profits that are reinvested, expanding the modern sector until the surplus labour is exhausted — the "Lewis turning point", after which wages begin to rise.

Advisory work and later life

Lewis advised governments across the developing world, including Ghana under Kwame Nkrumah, and served as president of the Caribbean Development Bank from 1970 to 1973. His books include The Theory of Economic Growth (1955) and Growth and Fluctuations 1870–1913 (1978). Knighted in 1963, he died in Barbados in 1991 and was buried at the University of the West Indies at Cave Hill.
