Associated Legendre polynomials

In mathematics, the associated Legendre polynomials are the canonical solutions of the general Legendre equation

(1 − x²) d²y/dx² − 2x dy/dx + [ ℓ(ℓ + 1) − m²/(1 − x²) ] y = 0,

or equivalently

d/dx [ (1 − x²) dy/dx ] + [ ℓ(ℓ + 1) − m²/(1 − x²) ] y = 0,

where the indices ℓ and m (which are integers) are referred to as the degree and order of the associated Legendre polynomial respectively. This equation has nonzero solutions that are nonsingular on [−1, 1] only if ℓ and m are integers with 0 ≤ m ≤ ℓ, or with trivially equivalent negative values.

When in addition m is even, the function is a polynomial. When m is zero and ℓ integer, these functions are identical to the Legendre polynomials. In general, when ℓ and m are integers, the regular solutions are sometimes called "associated Legendre polynomials", even though they are not polynomials when m is odd.

Definition

These functions are denoted Pℓm(x), where the superscript indicates the order, not a power of P. Their most straightforward definition is in terms of derivatives of ordinary Legendre polynomials (m ≥ 0):

Pℓm(x) = (−1)m (1 − x²)m/2 dm/dxm Pℓ(x).

The factor (−1)m in this formula is known as the Condon–Shortley phase. Some authors omit it, defining the associated Legendre polynomials differently.

Orthogonality

Assuming 0 ≤ m ≤ ℓ, the associated Legendre functions satisfy the orthogonality condition for fixed m:

∫−11 Pkm(x) Pℓm(x) dx = 2/(2ℓ + 1) · (ℓ + m)!/(ℓ − m)! · δk,ℓ,

where δk,ℓ is the Kronecker delta function.

Negative m and negative degree

The associated Legendre functions for negative order are proportional to those for the corresponding positive order. Negative-degree solutions are proportional to positive-degree ones as well, which is why the definitions can be restricted to degrees ℓ ≥ 0.

Applications

These functions play an important role in the solution of the Schrödinger equation for a central potential, such as the hydrogen atom, and in the construction of spherical harmonics, which are frequently encountered in physics and engineering problems with spherical symmetry.
