Fulminic acid (HCNO) is a chemical compound, an acid and an isomer of isocyanic acid (HNCO). It is a gas at room temperature and is notable principally through its salts, the fulminates, which are extremely sensitive explosives. Its free form is unstable and polymerises readily, and it has a disagreeable odour reminiscent of hydrocyanic acid.

History

Fulminates came to attention around 1800, when the English chemist Edward Charles Howard prepared mercury(II) fulminate ("fulminating mercury") by dissolving mercury in nitric acid and adding ethanol. Fulminates were used for more than a century as primary explosives in percussion caps and detonators. The isomer relationship between fulminic acid and cyanic acid was an early demonstration of isomerism: in 1825, Justus von Liebig and Friedrich Wöhler showed that silver fulminate and silver cyanate have the same composition but different properties.

Structure and properties

Fulminic acid has the structure H–C≡N⁺–O⁻, a linear molecule. It is a weak acid and is highly reactive: it trimerises to cyanuric acid derivatives and polymerises spontaneously. Its salts are the more important compounds. The fulminate anion, CNO⁻, is resonance-stabilised but readily detonates on shock, friction or heating, a sensitivity exploited in primers but also a major handling hazard.

Uses and safety

Mercury fulminate was the standard detonator explosive until gradually replaced by lead azide and lead styphnate in the 20th century. Fulminates still appear in toy cap-gun caps (as silver fulminate) and in some specialty detonators. Pure fulminic acid and its salts should never be prepared or stored outside a controlled laboratory: even milligram quantities can detonate.
