La Grande Soufrière

La Grande Soufrière, or simply La Soufrière, is an active stratovolcano on the French Caribbean island of Basse-Terre in Guadeloupe. At 1,467 metres (4,813 ft), it is the highest peak in the Lesser Antilles. Its name means "the great sulphur outlet," referring to the sulfur-spewing fumaroles near its summit.

Geology and activity

La Soufrière is an andesitic volcano formed within the caldera of the older Grande Découverte volcano. The present cone is surmounted by a lava dome extruded during the 1530 eruption, its last magmatic event. The volcano lies within Guadeloupe National Park and is monitored by the Volcanological and Seismological Observatory of Guadeloupe.

1976 crisis

In 1976, a phreatic eruption and escalating seismic activity led authorities, advised by volcanologist Claude Allègre, to evacuate some 73,000 people from the zone around the volcano, including the town of Saint-Claude and parts of Basse-Terre. The feared major eruption never occurred, and the evacuation became a famous controversy — volcanologist Haroun Tazieff publicly insisted the volcano posed no great danger, a dispute dramatized in Werner Herzog's short film La Soufrière (1977). The episode remains a landmark case study in volcanic risk communication.
