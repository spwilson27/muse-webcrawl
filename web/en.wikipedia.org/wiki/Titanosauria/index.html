Titanosauria

Titanosauria is a diverse group of sauropod dinosaurs, and the last surviving lineage of long-necked sauropods, flourishing through the Cretaceous period until the end-Cretaceous extinction 66 million years ago. Named after the Titans of Greek mythology, the group includes the largest land animals known.

Characteristics

Titanosaurs had relatively small heads, wide ("wide-gauge") stances, and procoelous tail vertebrae; some, such as Saltasaurus, bore bony armour (osteoderms). Giants like Argentinosaurus and Patagotitan may have exceeded 30 metres in length and 60 tonnes in mass.

Distribution

Fossils are known from every continent except (until recent finds) Antarctica, with particular diversity in South America, India and Madagascar — a largely Gondwanan distribution — though they also ranged across Laurasia. Over 80 genera have been named, and their trackways, nests and eggs (such as the Auca Mahuevo nesting site in Argentina) are well documented.
