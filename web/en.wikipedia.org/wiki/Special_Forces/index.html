Special forces

Special forces or special operations forces (SOF) are military units trained to conduct special operations. NATO has defined special operations as "military activities conducted by specially designated, organized, selected, trained, and equipped forces using unconventional techniques and modes of employment". Such units typically operate in small teams, are selected through rigorous assessment processes, and receive extensive training in skills such as reconnaissance, direct action, unconventional warfare, counter-terrorism, hostage rescue, and foreign internal defense.

History

Although elite and irregular units have existed throughout military history, modern special forces trace their origins to the Second World War. Units such as the British Special Air Service (SAS), founded in 1941 by David Stirling for raids behind Axis lines in North Africa, and the Special Boat Service, the Commandos, the Chindits in Burma, and various American units including the OSS detachments, the Marine Raiders, and the First Special Service Force, pioneered the tactics and organisational models still used today.

After 1945, special forces proliferated during the Cold War as instruments of counter-insurgency and proxy warfare. The United States Army Special Forces — the "Green Berets" — were established in 1952 with a primary mission of training and advising indigenous forces. The US Navy SEALs were formed in 1962. The growth of international terrorism in the 1970s led to dedicated counter-terrorist units such as Germany's GSG 9, France's GIGN, and the US Army's Delta Force (1st SFOD-D), founded in 1977.

Capabilities and missions

  • Special reconnaissance — gathering intelligence in hostile or denied territory
  • Direct action — short-duration strikes, raids, and ambushes against high-value targets
  • Unconventional warfare — supporting resistance movements and insurgencies
  • Foreign internal defense — training and advising allied and partner forces
  • Counter-terrorism — including hostage rescue and recovery of personnel
  • Military assistance and civil affairs — supporting stabilisation and humanitarian operations

Notable units

Well-known special forces units include the British SAS and SBS, the US Army Special Forces, Delta Force, Navy SEALs (including DEVGRU) and Army Rangers, Russia's Spetsnaz forces, Israel's Sayeret Matkal, France's 1er RPIMa, India's MARCOS and Para SF, Australia's SASR, and Canada's JTF 2. In the United States, most special operations forces are administered by the United States Special Operations Command (USSOCOM), established in 1987.

Selection and training

Selection for special forces is deliberately arduous, testing physical endurance, navigation, teamwork, and psychological resilience; attrition rates of 50–90 per cent are common. Successful candidates then undergo lengthy qualification training in weapons, demolitions, communications, medicine, languages, and survival, evasion, resistance and escape (SERE).
