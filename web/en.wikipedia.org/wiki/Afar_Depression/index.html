Afar Depression

The Afar Depression — also called the Afar Triangle or Danakil Depression — is a geological depression in the Horn of Africa where the borders of Ethiopia, Eritrea, and Djibouti meet. It is the junction of three tectonic plates pulling apart: the African (Nubian), Somali, and Arabian plates, and is one of the most active continental rift zones on Earth.

Geology

The depression contains the lowest point in Africa, Lake Assal in Djibouti at about 155 metres below sea level. The rift is slowly widening; in 2005 a 60-km-long fissure opened in the Ethiopian desert within days, an event widely reported as the birth of a future ocean basin. The region features lava lakes (such as Erta Ale), sulphur springs at Dallol, vast salt flats worked by Afar salt miners, and some of the hottest average temperatures of any inhabited place on Earth.

Palaeontology

The Afar is one of the world's richest sources of hominin fossils. The Hadar locality yielded "Lucy" (Australopithecus afarensis, discovered in 1974), and the Middle Awash and Dikika sites have produced fossils including Ardipithecus and the infant "Selam", making the region central to the study of human evolution.
