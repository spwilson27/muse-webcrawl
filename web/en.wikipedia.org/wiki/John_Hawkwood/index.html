John Hawkwood

Sir John Hawkwood (known in Italian as Giovanni Acuto; c. 1323 – 1394) was an English soldier and mercenary captain (condottiero) who was the foremost mercenary commander in 14th-century Italy, leading the famous White Company.

Career

The son of an Essex tanner, Hawkwood fought in France during the Hundred Years' War, probably at Crécy and Poitiers. When peace released thousands of soldiers, he joined the free companies that crossed into Italy, eventually commanding the White Company in the service — successively and sometimes simultaneously against — Pisa, Milan, the Papal States, and finally Florence.

Feared and courted in turn, Hawkwood was notorious for episodes such as the massacre at Cesena (1377) while in papal service, yet Florence retained him as captain-general in its wars against Milan in his final years, granting him citizenship and a pension.

Legacy

Florence honoured Hawkwood with a monumental equestrian fresco by Paolo Uccello (1436) in Florence Cathedral, painted in simulated bronze, which still survives. His name, Italianised as "Acuto" ("the sharp one"), became a byword for the professional mercenary captain.
