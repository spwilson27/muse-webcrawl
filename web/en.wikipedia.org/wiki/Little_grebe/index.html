The little grebe (Tachybaptus ruficollis), also known as the dabchick, is a member of the grebe family of water birds. It is the smallest European member of the family and one of the smallest grebes in the world. The genus name Tachybaptus means "fast sinking," referring to the bird's habit of vanishing beneath the surface when alarmed.

Description

At 23–29 cm in length, the little grebe is a small, dumpy waterbird with a stubby bill and no tail. In breeding plumage it is rich chestnut on the neck, cheeks, and flanks, with a dark brown back and a conspicuous pale yellow-green patch at the gape. In winter it becomes duller and paler, mainly buff-brown. Sexes are similar. Its call is a distinctive whinnying trill.

Distribution and habitat

The species breeds across Europe, much of Asia, and Africa, in small ponds, lakes, slow rivers, and marshes with dense emergent vegetation. Northern populations are migratory, while many others are resident or move only locally to larger, ice-free waters.

Behaviour

An excellent swimmer and diver, the little grebe pursues fish, aquatic invertebrates, mollusks, and amphibian larvae underwater. It builds a floating nest of plant material anchored to reeds, in which it lays 4–6 white eggs that stain brown from the vegetation; the striped chicks often ride on a parent's back. Like other grebes, it eats its own feathers, which may protect the stomach from fish bones.
