Battle of Qarqar

The Battle of Qarqar was fought in 853 BC near the town of Qarqar in northwestern Syria, between the Neo-Assyrian Empire under Shalmaneser III and an alliance of twelve Levantine kings, including Hadadezer of Damascus and Ahab of Israel. It is one of the earliest battles for which reasonably detailed numbers survive, recorded on Shalmaneser's Kurkh Monolith. Although Assyrian inscriptions claim a victory, the coalition appears to have checked the Assyrian advance into Syria for several years.

The coalition

The Kurkh Monolith lists the allies' contingents: Hadadezer of Damascus fielded 1,200 chariots and 20,000 infantry; Ahab of Israel, notably, contributed 2,000 chariots and 10,000 infantry — the largest chariot force in the alliance. Other members included Hamath, Que, Byblos, Arvad, and an Arab contingent under Gindibu, whose 1,000 camels are the first recorded use of camel troops in battle.

Outcome

Shalmaneser claimed to have slaughtered 14,000 enemy troops, but he did not capture Damascus and had to campaign again in 849, 848, and 845 BC against substantially the same coalition. Only after the alliance broke up — following the deaths of Ahab and Hadadezer and the usurpation of Hazael — did Assyria gain the upper hand in Syria.
