Resettlement of the Jews in England

The resettlement of the Jews in England refers to the gradual return of Jewish communities to England during the 1650s, under Oliver Cromwell, nearly four centuries after the Edict of Expulsion of 1290 had banished Jews from the kingdom.

Background

In 1655 the Amsterdam rabbi Menasseh ben Israel came to London to petition for the readmission of Jews, prompted by religious arguments (that Jewish dispersion had to be complete before redemption) and by the Protectorate's commercial interests. The Whitehall Conference of December 1655 adjourned without a formal verdict, but the judges concluded there was no law actually forbidding Jews to reside in England.

Resettlement

From 1656, London Sephardic merchants, including Antonio Robles and the community around the Creechurch Lane synagogue (1657), lived openly as Jews; the Bevis Marks Synagogue opened in 1701. Readmission was informal and never enacted by statute, but the community endured and grew.
