Polywell

The polywell is a proposed design for a fusion reactor using an electric field to heat ions to fusion conditions. It is closely related to the fusor, the lattice confinement reactor, and is one of many devices classed as inertial electrostatic confinement (IEC) reactors. The polywell was developed by physicist Robert W. Bussard and his company Energy/Matter Conversion Corporation (EMC2).

Design

The device consists of a set of electromagnets arranged in a polyhedral configuration (typically six coils forming a cube) inside a vacuum chamber. The coils create a magnetic field with cusps that traps electrons in the centre, forming a "virtual cathode" — a region of negative potential (the "well") that accelerates and confines positive ions, which oscillate through the centre and may fuse. Bussard proposed that the magnetic cusps of the polywell would confine electrons far better than the grids of a conventional fusor, avoiding grid losses.

History and status

Bussard presented the concept in the 1980s–2000s and received funding from the United States Navy for a series of experimental machines (the WB series, up to WB-8). Following Bussard's death in 2007, EMC2 continued the work. Critics have questioned whether electron losses through the cusps and other effects would prevent the device from reaching net energy gain; no polywell device has demonstrated net fusion power.
