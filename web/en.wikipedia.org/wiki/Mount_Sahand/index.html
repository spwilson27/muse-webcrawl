Sahand (Persian: سهند) is a massive, heavily eroded stratovolcano in north-western Iran, south of the city of Tabriz in East Azerbaijan province. Its highest peak, Jam Daqi, reaches 3,707 metres (12,162 ft), making it one of the highest mountains in Iranian Azerbaijan.

Sahand is believed to be the ancient Mount Zoroaster of some traditions, and the region around it is rich in sites associated with early Iranian religion. The volcano's activity is thought to have ended in the Holocene, and its cone has been deeply dissected by erosion, leaving a ring of peaks around a broad central depression.

Geography and climate

The mountain's slopes are an important source of water for the surrounding agricultural plains; springs and qanats fed by Sahand's snowmelt supply orchards and fields famous for fruit and for the mineral waters of the region. The village of Kandovan, carved into volcanic rock formations on the north-western foothills, is noted for its troglodyte dwellings, still inhabited today.

In winter, Sahand's northern slopes host the Sahand ski resort, one of the principal ski areas of north-western Iran, served from Tabriz and Maragheh.
