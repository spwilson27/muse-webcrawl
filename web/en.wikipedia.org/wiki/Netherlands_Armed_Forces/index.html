Netherlands Armed Forces

Netherlands Armed Forces (Nederlandse krijgsmacht)
Service branches: Royal Netherlands Army; Royal Netherlands Navy; Royal Netherlands Air and Space Force; Royal Marechaussee
Active personnel: approx. 42,000 (plus reserves)
Chief of Defence: senior serving officer

The Netherlands Armed Forces are the military forces of the Kingdom of the Netherlands. They consist of the Royal Netherlands Army (Koninklijke Landmacht), the Royal Netherlands Navy (Koninklijke Marine), the Royal Netherlands Air and Space Force (Koninklijke Luchtmacht), and the Royal Marechaussee (Koninklijke Marechaussee), a gendarmerie force performing military police and border duties. The armed forces fall under the Ministry of Defence, and the government holds supreme command under the constitution.

History and role

The Netherlands has been a founding member of NATO since 1949 and contributes to allied and UN missions. Dutch forces served in Korea, Lebanon, Bosnia, Iraq, Afghanistan (notably Uruzgan) and Mali. Conscription is formally retained but suspended in peacetime since 1997, making the forces an all-volunteer professional military.

Organization

The Chief of Defence is the highest-ranking officer and principal military adviser to the minister. Joint support and special operations commands coordinate across the branches, and the Caribbean coastguard and defence forces protect the Dutch Caribbean territories.
