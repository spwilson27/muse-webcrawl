Sunday

Sunday is the day of the week between Saturday and Monday. In most Western countries, Sunday is a day of rest and part of the weekend, while in most Islamic countries Sunday is a working day. The international standard ISO 8601 counts Sunday as the seventh and final day of the week, though in many countries — including the United States, Canada, and Japan — it is traditionally regarded as the first day.

History

The English name derives from Old English Sunnandæg, "sun's day," a translation of Latin dies Solis, itself reflecting the Greco-Roman custom of naming days for celestial bodies; cognates survive in other Germanic languages, while Romance languages use forms of dies Dominica, "the Lord's day." In 321 CE the Roman emperor Constantine I decreed Sunday a day of rest in honour of the Sun god, and Christians, who worshipped on the first day of the week to commemorate the Resurrection of Jesus, adopted it as their principal holy day.

Religious observance

Most Christian denominations hold worship services on Sunday morning. The Eastern Orthodox and many Protestant traditions observe it as a feast day, while Seventh-day Adventists and some other groups instead keep Saturday as the Sabbath. In countries with a Christian heritage, "Sunday trading" and "blue laws" historically restricted commerce and alcohol sales, and such restrictions survive in diluted form in many places.

Position in the week

ISO 8601 assigns Sunday the number 7. Countries whose calendars begin the week on Monday treat Sunday as the close of the weekend; the United States, where weeks are printed beginning with Sunday, nonetheless treats it socially as part of the weekend. In the liturgical calendars of most churches the week begins on Sunday.
