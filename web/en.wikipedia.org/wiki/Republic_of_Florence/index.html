Republic of Florence

The Republic of Florence (Italian: Repubblica di Firenze) was a city-state centred on Florence in Tuscany, Italy, which existed, with interruptions, from 1115 to 1569. Ruled formally by a guild-based republican constitution headed by the Signoria, it became one of the wealthiest and most culturally influential cities of Europe and the cradle of the Renaissance — though from 1434 it was dominated in practice by the Medici family.

Government

Supreme executive authority rested with the Signoria: eight priors chosen by lot from guild members plus the Gonfaloniere of Justice, serving two-month terms from the Palazzo della Signoria (Palazzo Vecchio). Councils of the people and the commune legislated, and extraordinary balìe were repeatedly used by dominant factions — above all the Medici — to manipulate elections while preserving republican forms.

Economy

Florence grew rich on the woollen cloth trade and on banking: Florentine houses financed kings and popes across Europe, and the gold florin, first minted in 1252, became a standard trade coin. Banking failures of the 1340s (Bardi and Peruzzi) and the Black Death, which may have halved the population, were severe blows, but the city recovered to bankroll the artistic flowering of the fifteenth century.

Medici ascendancy and fall

Cosimo de' Medici returned from exile in 1434 and ruled through clients and electoral control; his grandson Lorenzo the Magnificent presided over the city's cultural zenith. The Medici were expelled in 1494, and a restored republic — briefly dominated by the friar Savonarola and later advised by Niccolò Machiavelli — survived until Spanish and papal arms restored the family in 1512 and again, definitively, in 1530 after the siege of Florence. Alessandro de' Medici became duke in 1532, and in 1569 Cosimo I was made Grand Duke of Tuscany, ending the republic in form as well as fact.
