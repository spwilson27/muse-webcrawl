Sisera

Sisera (Hebrew: סִיסְרָא Sîsərāʾ) was the commander of the Canaanite army of King Jabin of Hazor, according to the Book of Judges in the Hebrew Bible. He is best known for being defeated by the Israelite forces under Deborah and Barak and subsequently killed by Yael (Jael), the wife of Heber the Kenite.

Biblical account

Judges 4 relates that Jabin oppressed Israel for twenty years with an army equipped with nine hundred iron chariots commanded by Sisera, who lived at Harosheth Haggoyim. At the prompting of the prophetess Deborah, Barak son of Abinoam mustered ten thousand men from the tribes of Naphtali and Zebulun at Mount Tabor. In the ensuing battle near the Kishon River, Sisera's chariots were thrown into confusion — the Song of Deborah in Judges 5 attributes the rout in part to a storm that flooded the river and bogged down the chariots. Sisera fled on foot to the tent of Yael, whose family was at peace with Jabin. Yael gave him milk, covered him, and, while he slept from exhaustion, drove a tent peg through his temple with a hammer, killing him. She then showed the body to the pursuing Barak.

The Song of Deborah

Judges 5, one of the oldest passages in the Bible on linguistic grounds, retells the story in verse, ending with a haunting scene of Sisera's mother waiting at the window, imagining her son delayed by dividing the spoils — unaware that he is dead.

In art and music

The killing of Sisera became a popular subject in Renaissance and Baroque painting, treated by artists including Artemisia Gentileschi, Luca Giordano, and Gregorio Lazzarini, who often paired Yael and Sisera with other biblical heroines. Handel's oratorio Deborah (1733) also dramatises the story.
