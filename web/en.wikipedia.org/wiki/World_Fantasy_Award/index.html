World Fantasy Award

The World Fantasy Awards are a set of awards given each year for the best fantasy fiction published during the previous calendar year. Organized and overseen by the World Fantasy Convention, the awards were first given in 1975 and are considered among the most prestigious in speculative fiction, alongside the Hugo and Nebula Awards. Winners are selected by a panel of judges, typically five professionals chosen by the convention.

Categories

Current categories include Best Novel, Best Novella, Best Short Fiction, Best Anthology, Best Collection, Best Artist, and special awards for professional and non-professional achievement, as well as a Life Achievement award. The Best Novel award has gone to writers including Patricia A. McKillip, Gene Wolfe, Tim Powers, Neil Gaiman, Susanna Clarke, and N. K. Jemisin.

The trophy

Winners originally received a bust of horror writer H. P. Lovecraft designed by cartoonist Gahan Wilson, nicknamed the "Howard". Following controversy over Lovecraft's racist views, the award was redesigned in 2017 as a statuette of a tree in moonlight, designed by Vincent Villafranca.
