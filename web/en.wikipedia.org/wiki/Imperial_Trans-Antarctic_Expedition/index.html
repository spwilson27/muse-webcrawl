Imperial Trans-Antarctic Expedition

Dates1914–1917
LeaderErnest Shackleton
ShipsEndurance (Weddell Sea party); Aurora (Ross Sea party)
ObjectiveFirst crossing of Antarctica via the South Pole
OutcomeCrossing abandoned; all Weddell Sea party survived

The Imperial Trans-Antarctic Expedition of 1914–1917 was an attempt, led by Sir Ernest Shackleton, to make the first land crossing of the Antarctic continent. The expedition is instead remembered as one of the greatest survival stories in exploration history: after the ship Endurance was trapped and crushed by ice in the Weddell Sea, Shackleton brought every member of his party home alive, while the supporting Ross Sea party, which laid depots for a crossing that never came, lost three men.

Frank Hurley's photograph of Endurance beset in the Weddell Sea ice, 1915.

The Weddell Sea party

Endurance sailed from South Georgia in December 1914 and became beset in pack ice in January 1915, drifting for ten months before being crushed and sinking on 21 November 1915. The 28 men camped on the ice — at "Ocean Camp" and "Patience Camp" — until the floes broke up in April 1916, then sailed in three open lifeboats to Elephant Island. Shackleton and five companions then made the celebrated 1,300 km (800 mi) voyage in the 22-foot James Caird to South Georgia, crossed its unmapped interior on foot, and reached the whaling station at Stromness. After several attempts, the Chilean tug Yelcho rescued the remaining 22 men from Elephant Island on 30 August 1916.

The Ross Sea party

The expedition's second ship, Aurora, was to land a party at McMurdo Sound to lay supply depots across the Ross Ice Shelf toward the Beardmore Glacier for Shackleton's crossing party. Blown to sea in a blizzard in May 1915, Aurora left ten men stranded; despite scurvy, frostbite, and the deaths of Arnold Spencer-Smith, Aeneas Mackintosh, and Victor Hayward, they completed the depot line. The survivors were rescued by Shackleton aboard the repaired Aurora in January 1917.

Legacy

Coming at the close of the Heroic Age of Antarctic Exploration and overshadowed by the First World War, the expedition achieved none of its geographic goals yet became the defining exemplar of expedition leadership; Frank Hurley's photographs and film remain iconic. The wreck of Endurance was located in March 2022 by the Endurance22 expedition, remarkably preserved some 3,000 metres beneath the Weddell Sea.
