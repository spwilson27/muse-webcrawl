Pure Land Buddhism

Pure Land Buddhism is a broad tradition of Mahayana Buddhism focused on rebirth in the Pure Land (Sukhavati) of the Buddha Amitabha, through faith, devotion and recitation of his name. It is the dominant form of Buddhism in East Asia, practised in China, Japan, Korea, Taiwan and Vietnam.

Pure Land Buddhism
Central figure: Amitabha (Amida)
Key practice: Nianfo / Nembutsu
Texts: Longer and Shorter Sukhavativyuha Sutras, Amitayus Sutra
Schools: Jodo-shu, Jodo Shinshu, Chinese Pure Land

Doctrine

According to the Longer Sukhavativyuha Sutra, the bodhisattva Dharmakara made 48 vows, the 18th promising that beings who sincerely call his name even ten times will be born in his Pure Land, where enlightenment is assured. Practice centres on nianfo (Chinese) / nembutsu (Japanese): "Namo Amituofo" / "Namu Amida Butsu".

History

Pure Land ideas arose in India and flourished in China through Huiyuan, Tanluan, Daochuo and Shandao. In Japan, Honen founded Jodo-shu and his disciple Shinran founded Jodo Shinshu, emphasising "other-power" (tariki) over self-power.

Practice today

Pure Land is often combined with Chan/Zen in China and Vietnam. It remains the largest Buddhist tradition in Japan and one of the most widespread in the Chinese-speaking world.
