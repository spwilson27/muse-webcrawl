Judith R. Faulkner (born 1943) is an American software executive and billionaire, the founder and chief executive officer of Epic Systems, the largest provider of electronic health record (EHR) software in the United States. Epic's systems hold the medical records of well over 250 million patients worldwide.

Career

Faulkner earned a bachelor's degree in mathematics from Dickinson College and a master's in computer science from the University of Wisconsin–Madison. While working at the University of Wisconsin Hospitals, she helped develop a system for tracking patient data. In 1979, with an investment of about $70,000 and a small team working in a basement in Madison, she founded Human Services Computing, which became Epic Systems.

Epic grew steadily without venture capital or acquisitions and remains privately held — Faulkner has repeatedly refused to take it public. Its sprawling, whimsically themed campus in Verona, Wisconsin, employs more than ten thousand people. The company supplies software to most of the largest American hospital systems, including Kaiser Permanente, the Mayo Clinic and Cleveland Clinic.

Views and philanthropy

Faulkner has been a prominent voice — and sometimes controversial figure — in health-information policy, including debates over interoperability and data sharing between rival EHR systems. She has signed The Giving Pledge, committing to donate the great majority of her wealth, and her philanthropy has focused on health, education and organizations addressing root causes of poverty.
