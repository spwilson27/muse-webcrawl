Scone Abbey was a house of Augustinian canons at Scone in Perthshire, Scotland, founded in the early 12th century. Scone was the ancient crowning place of the kings of Scots and the original home of the Stone of Scone (Stone of Destiny), giving the abbey a central place in Scottish royal ritual.

History

Scone was an early centre of the Scottish church and of royal inauguration; Kenneth MacAlpin is traditionally associated with bringing the Stone of Destiny there in the 9th century. Alexander I refounded the religious house for Augustinian canons between 1114 and 1114–20, possibly colonised from Nostell Priory in Yorkshire. The abbey church was the site of coronations including those of Robert II and Robert III, and parliaments met at Scone.

The Stone of Destiny

The kings of Scots were enthroned on the Stone of Scone until Edward I of England seized it in 1296 and took it to Westminster Abbey. It remained in the Coronation Chair in London — apart from its theft by Scottish students in 1950 and return — until it was formally returned to Scotland in 1996, and is now kept at Perth Museum.

Decline

The abbey was burned by a mob from Dundee in 1559 during the Scottish Reformation and was secularised in 1600 as a lordship for the Ruthven family, later passing to the Murrays, earls of Mansfield. The present Scone Palace (1808) stands near the abbey site; only fragments and the Moot Hill coronation mound survive above ground.
