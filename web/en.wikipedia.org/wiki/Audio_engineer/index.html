Audio engineer

An audio engineer (also known as a sound engineer or recording engineer) is a person who works with the technical aspects of sound and music: recording, editing, mixing and reproduction, using microphones, mixing consoles, signal processors and digital audio workstations. Audio engineers work in music recording, film and television, live performance, broadcasting, theatre and game audio. The role is distinct from that of the acoustician, though the fields overlap.

Roles

Recording engineer

The recording engineer captures performances in the studio, choosing and placing microphones, setting levels and operating the recording system. In large sessions the engineer may be assisted by second engineers and tape operators.

Mixing engineer

The mixing engineer balances the recorded tracks — adjusting level, equalisation, dynamics, stereo placement and effects — to produce the final stereo or surround mix. See audio mixing (recorded music).

Live sound engineer

Live sound engineers mix performances in real time: the front-of-house engineer balances the sound for the audience, while the monitor engineer provides mixes for the performers' stage monitors or in-ear systems.

Other roles

Specialisations include mastering engineers, who prepare final recordings for distribution; broadcast engineers; post-production engineers for film and television; and system engineers who design and tune large sound-reinforcement installations.

Training

Audio engineers come from varied backgrounds, including degree programmes in audio engineering, music production and electrical engineering, as well as apprenticeship and self-directed routes. Professional development is supported by bodies such as the Audio Engineering Society, founded in 1948.
