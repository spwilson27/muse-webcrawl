Christopher Hitchens

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Christopher Eric Hitchens (13 April 1949 – 15 December 2011) was a British-American author, journalist, orator and literary critic. He wrote or edited more than thirty books on politics, culture and religion, and was a prominent public intellectual and polemicist noted for his contrarian positions and rhetorical skill.

Career

Educated at Balliol College, Oxford, Hitchens began his career in British journalism, writing for the New Statesman, before moving to the United States in 1981. He was a long-time columnist for The Nation and later for Vanity Fair, Slate and The Atlantic. Once a socialist, he broke with much of the left over his support for the Iraq War and his opposition to Islamism.

Works

His best-known books include God Is Not Great: How Religion Poisons Everything (2007), which made him a leading figure of the "New Atheism" alongside Richard Dawkins, Sam Harris and Daniel Dennett; The Missionary Position, a critique of Mother Teresa; No One Left to Lie To, on Bill Clinton; Letters to a Young Contrarian; Hitch-22, his memoir; and Mortality, essays on his illness published posthumously. He was also a frequent debater in public debates on religion and foreign policy.

Death

Hitchens was diagnosed with esophageal cancer in 2010 and died in Houston, Texas, in December 2011, donating his body to medical research.
