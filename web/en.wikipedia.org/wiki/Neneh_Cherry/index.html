Neneh Mariann Karlsson (born 10 March 1964), better known by her stepfather's surname as Neneh Cherry, is a Swedish singer-songwriter, rapper and DJ. Blending hip hop with dance music and pop, she achieved international success with her debut album Raw Like Sushi (1989) and has remained an influential, genre-crossing artist across five decades.

Early life

Cherry was born in Stockholm to a Sierra Leonean father, the musician Ahmadu Jah, and a Swedish mother, the painter and textile artist Monika "Moki" Karlsson. After her parents separated she was raised largely by her mother and stepfather, the American jazz trumpeter Don Cherry, whose surname she took. The family lived between Sweden and New York, and Cherry grew up around avant-garde jazz circles. Leaving school as a teenager, she moved to London in the early 1980s, where she sang with post-punk and new wave bands including the Slits (as a backing singer), Rip Rig + Panic, and Float Up CP.

Breakthrough

Working with DJ Tim Simenon (Bomb the Bass) and producer Cameron McVey (whom she later married), Cherry released "Buffalo Stance" in 1988, which reached the top five in the UK, the US and many other countries and became an anthem of the era. Her debut album Raw Like Sushi (1989) also yielded the hits "Manchild" and "Kisses on the Wind". The follow-ups Homebrew (1992) and Man (1996) included her duet with Youssou N'Dour, "7 Seconds" (1994), a major international hit. She also co-wrote songs and appeared with Massive Attack, whose sound her circle helped shape in Bristol.

Later work

After a long recording hiatus devoted partly to family and to the project cirKus, Cherry returned with Blank Project (2014), a stark electronic album produced by Four Tet, and Broken Politics (2018). In 2022 she released The Versions, an album of her songs re-recorded by artists including Robyn, Sia and her daughters Mabel and Tyson. Her daughter Mabel is a successful pop singer in her own right.
