Enol

In organic chemistry, enols (also known as alkenols) are a type of reactive structure or intermediate in organic chemistry represented by an alkene (olefin) with a hydroxy group attached to one of the alkene carbon atoms (that is, a carbon–carbon double bond adjacent to an alcohol). The terms enol and alkenol are portmanteaus deriving from "-ene"/"alkene" and the "-ol" suffix indicating an alcohol.

The generation of enols often involves removal of a hydrogen atom adjacent (α-) to the carbonyl group — that is, deprotonation at the α-position. Enols are related to ketones and aldehydes by tautomerism: the keto–enol tautomerisation moves a proton from the α-carbon to the carbonyl oxygen and shifts the double bond. For simple carbonyl compounds the keto form is strongly favoured at equilibrium — the enol content of acetone, for example, is only about one part in ten million — but the tiny amount of enol present is crucial to the reactivity of carbonyl compounds.

Keto–enol tautomerism

Tautomerisation is catalysed by both acids and bases, and proceeds through the enol (or, under basic conditions, the enolate anion). The equilibrium shifts toward the enol when conjugation or intramolecular hydrogen bonding stabilises it: 1,3-diketones such as pentane-2,4-dione (acetylacetone) exist predominantly as enols, especially in non-polar solvents, and phenols can be regarded as fully enolised cyclic systems stabilised by aromaticity. Vinyl alcohol, the simplest enol, tautomerises rapidly to acetaldehyde and cannot be isolated in pure form.

Reactivity

Because the α-carbon of an enol is nucleophilic, enols and enolates are central intermediates in carbonyl chemistry: they undergo α-halogenation (as in the haloform reaction), alkylation, and condensation reactions such as the aldol reaction. Enols also explain the racemisation of chiral α-centres adjacent to carbonyl groups, since passage through the planar enol destroys the stereocentre. Stable analogues used synthetically include enol ethers and silyl enol ethers, which act as enolate equivalents in reactions such as the Mukaiyama aldol reaction.
