Chichen Itza

Chichen Itza was a large pre-Columbian city built by the Maya people of the Terminal Classic period. The archaeological site is located in Tinum Municipality, Yucatán State, Mexico.

History

Chichen Itza rose to regional prominence in the late Classic period and became one of the largest Maya cities, with a diverse population and architectural styles reflecting influences from central Mexico, including those attributed to the Toltec. Major structures include El Castillo (the Temple of Kukulcan), a step pyramid famous for the equinox shadow effect resembling a descending serpent; the Great Ball Court, the largest and best-preserved ball court in Mesoamerica; the Temple of the Warriors; and the Sacred Cenote, a natural sinkhole used for offerings.

Tourism

Chichen Itza is one of Mexico's most visited archaeological sites and was named one of the New7Wonders of the World in 2007. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
