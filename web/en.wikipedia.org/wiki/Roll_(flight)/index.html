Roll (flight)


Axis: Longitudinal (roll axis)
Primary control: Ailerons
Related rotations: Pitch, yaw

Roll is the rotation of an aircraft about its longitudinal axis, the imaginary line running from nose to tail. Together with pitch (rotation about the lateral axis) and yaw (rotation about the vertical axis), roll defines the attitude of the aircraft in three-dimensional space. The angle of roll is called the bank angle, and rolling is the primary means by which an aircraft begins a turn.

Control

Roll is controlled principally by the ailerons, hinged surfaces near the wingtips that deflect in opposite directions: the aileron on the rising wing deflects downward to increase lift, while the aileron on the descending wing deflects upward to reduce it. On many aircraft, spoilers assist the ailerons, and fly-by-wire systems coordinate roll with rudder input to counter adverse yaw — the tendency of the nose to yaw away from the direction of roll caused by the increased drag of the downward-deflected aileron.

Roll in turning flight

When an aircraft banks, the lift vector tilts with it. The vertical component continues to oppose weight while the horizontal component pulls the aircraft into a turn. To maintain altitude in a turn, the pilot increases the angle of attack (and hence total lift) as bank increases; in a steep turn of 60 degrees of bank, the load factor reaches 2 g.

Rolling manoeuvres

  • Aileron roll — a one-turn roll performed largely with aileron input.
  • Barrel roll — a combined roll and loop tracing a helical path.
  • Snap roll — a rapid autorotative roll induced by stalling one wing.
  • Slow roll — a precisely controlled roll at a constant slow rate.
  • Dutch roll — an undesirable coupled oscillation of roll and yaw found in some aircraft, damped by a yaw damper.
