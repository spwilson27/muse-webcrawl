The Central Kalahari Game Reserve (CKGR) is an extensive national game reserve in the Kalahari Desert of central Botswana. Established in 1961 and covering 52,800 square kilometres — roughly the size of Denmark and about five percent of the country — it is one of the largest protected areas in the world, characterised by vast open grasslands, fossil river valleys, salt pans and scattered acacia woodland.

Wildlife

After the summer rains the northern valleys, notably Deception Valley (an ancient riverbed), fill with nutritious grasses and attract large herds of springbok and gemsbok (oryx), followed by predators including the reserve's famous black-maned Kalahari lions, cheetahs, leopards and brown hyenas. Other wildlife includes giraffe, wildebeest, hartebeest, kudu, ostrich, honey badger and black-backed jackal. Because of its remoteness and lack of permanent surface water, the reserve offers a distinctly wild, low-density tourism experience, with a handful of lodges and public campsites reachable only by 4×4.

San people and relocation controversy

The reserve was originally created to protect the hunting grounds of the San (Bushmen) — G/wi, G//ana and other groups — as well as wildlife. From the 1990s the Botswana government relocated most San residents to settlements outside the reserve, a policy the government justified by conservation and service provision but which critics and the San's supporters contested. In 2006 Botswana's High Court ruled the removals unlawful and unconstitutional, and many San subsequently returned. The area also drew attention because of diamond exploration at Gope (the Ghaghoo mine, opened 2014 and later mothballed).
