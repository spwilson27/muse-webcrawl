Alodia

Alodia, also known as Alwa, was a medieval Christian kingdom in what is now central and southern Sudan. It was the southernmost of the three Nubian kingdoms, the others being Nobatia to the north and Makuria between them. Its capital was Soba, on the Blue Nile near modern Khartoum.

History

Alodia emerged after the fall of the Kingdom of Kush and the city of Meroë. According to tradition, it was converted to Christianity in the 6th century, when missionaries from the Byzantine world, including Longinus, evangelised Nubia; Alodia adopted the Monophysite (Miaphysite) Christianity associated with Alexandria. At its height it may have controlled the Gezira plain and trade routes between the Nile valley, Ethiopia, and the Red Sea.

Less is known of Alodia than of Makuria because few of its written records survive and its heartland has been less excavated. Arab geographers such as al-Aswani described Soba as a wealthy city of fine houses and churches. The kingdom declined from around the 13th century under pressure from Arab nomad migration and internal fragmentation, and Soba fell to the Funj around 1504, after which the Funj Sultanate of Sennar rose in its place.

Archaeology

Excavations at Soba have uncovered churches, a possible palace, and burials, but much of the site has been lost to agriculture, brick-robbing and the growth of modern settlement. Alodian pottery, inscriptions in Greek and Old Nubian, and church remains attest to a literate Christian society.
