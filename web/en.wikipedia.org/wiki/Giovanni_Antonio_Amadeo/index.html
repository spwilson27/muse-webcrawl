Giovanni Antonio Amadeo

Died27/28 August 1522, Milan
Known forSculpture, architecture
MovementItalian Renaissance (Lombard)

Giovanni Antonio Amadeo (c. 1447 – 1522) was an Italian Renaissance sculptor and architect, the leading figure of Lombard sculpture in the later 15th century. He worked extensively for the Sforza dukes of Milan and on the great building sites of Lombardy.

Career

Amadeo was trained in Milan, where from about 1466 he worked on the Certosa di Pavia, executing rich decorative sculpture. His masterpiece is the façade and funeral monument of the Colleoni Chapel in Bergamo (1470–1476), built for the condottiero Bartolomeo Colleoni, notable for its coloured marble inlay and reliefs of the Labours of Hercules. He also sculpted the tomb of Medea Colleoni and much of the Arca of St. Lanfranc at Pavia.

As an architect, Amadeo became engineer of Milan Cathedral in 1490 and, with Bramante, designed the crossing dome (tiburio) of the cathedral; he also worked on the church of Santa Maria presso San Celso in Milan and the cathedral of Pavia, and advised on the dome of the sanctuary of Santa Maria di Canepanova. His mature work shows the transition from exuberant Lombard Gothic ornament to the classical vocabulary of the High Renaissance.
