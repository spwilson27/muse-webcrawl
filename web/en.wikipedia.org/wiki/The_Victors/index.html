The Victors is a 1963 war film written, produced, and directed by Carl Foreman, adapted from Alexander Baron's autobiographical short-story collection The Human Kind. An ensemble anti-war film, it follows a squad of American infantrymen through Italy, France, and Germany in the final years of the Second World War.

Plot

Rather than a single campaign narrative, the film is a series of episodes showing how war corrupts victors as well as victims: soldiers exploit desperate civilians, a deserter is executed to Christmas music, an officer steals from the men under him, and in the final sequence—set in occupied Berlin—an American soldier and a Russian soldier stab each other to death in a senseless drunken brawl, the film's bleak concluding comment on "victory".

Production and release

Foreman, blacklisted in Hollywood during the McCarthy era, made the film in Britain. Its unflinching tone and several controversial scenes led to cuts, particularly in the United States, where roughly twenty minutes were removed. Though a commercial disappointment on release, the film has been reassessed as one of the most uncompromising anti-war films of its era, and it earned a BAFTA nomination.
