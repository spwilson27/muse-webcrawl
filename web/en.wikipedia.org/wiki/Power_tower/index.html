Power tower

The term power tower may refer to several distinct concepts:

  • A solar power tower, a type of solar furnace and concentrated solar power plant using a tower to receive focused sunlight from an array of mirrors (heliostats).
  • In mathematics, a power tower is an iterated exponential, also called tetration: an expression of the form a^(a^(a^...)) with a finite or infinite number of terms.
  • A cooling tower or other tower structure associated with a power station.

Solar power tower

A solar power tower is a system of flat, moveable mirrors called heliostats that aim sunlight onto a receiver at the top of a tall tower. The concentrated light heats a working fluid — historically water/steam, and in newer designs molten salt — which is then used to generate electricity through a conventional turbine. Because molten salt retains heat, tower plants can store thermal energy and generate power after sunset. Examples include the Gemasolar plant in Spain, the Ivanpah Solar Power Facility in California, and the Crescent Dunes project in Nevada.

In mathematics

In mathematics, a power tower of height n is formed by iterated exponentiation, evaluated from the top down (right-associative). Power towers grow extremely rapidly: even a tower of a few small numbers quickly exceeds any practical means of writing the result. Infinite power towers converge for a certain range of bases, a result connected with work of Leonhard Euler; for the base x, the infinite tower x^(x^(x^...)) converges when e^(−e) ≤ x ≤ e^(1/e).
