Cowpox

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cowpox is a viral disease of cattle caused by the cowpox virus, a member of the orthopoxvirus genus related to smallpox (variola) and vaccinia. In humans it usually causes a mild, localised skin infection — historically contracted by milkmaids from cows' udders — with lesions mainly on the hands.

Cowpox is famous for its role in the birth of vaccination: Edward Jenner noticed that milkmaids who had caught cowpox appeared protected against smallpox, and in 1796 used cowpox matter to inoculate James Phipps, demonstrating immunity. The vaccine virus used for two centuries afterwards, vaccinia, is related but not identical to cowpox — its exact origin is still debated by historians of medicine.

Today cowpox is rare and mostly an occupational or pet-associated infection in Europe (cats and rodents are common reservoirs), occasionally serious in immunocompromised people. It remains a standard example of cross-protective immunity between related viruses (see vaccine).
