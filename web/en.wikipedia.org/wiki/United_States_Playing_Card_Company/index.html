United States Playing Card Company

The United States Playing Card Company (USPCC) is the world's largest manufacturer of playing cards. Founded in Cincinnati, Ohio, in 1867 as Russell, Morgan & Co., it produces the famous Bicycle brand (introduced in 1885), along with Bee, Tally-Ho, Aviator, Hoyle and many custom and casino decks. The company is headquartered with its factory in Erlanger, Kentucky, and since 2019 has been a subsidiary of the Belgian cardmaker Cartamundi.

History

The firm began as the printing business of A. O. Russell and Robert J. Morgan, adding playing cards in 1867. Its "Tigers" (No. 101) was the first brand; the Bicycle brand followed in 1885 and, with its distinctive cupid-on-a-bicycle back design, became the best-known American card. In 1894 the company took the name United States Playing Card Company after acquiring several rivals, becoming the dominant US manufacturer. It absorbed competitors including the Standard Playing Card Company and the New York Consolidated Card Company (maker of Bee).

Operations and culture

USPCC's Cincinnati plant in Norwood operated for more than a century before production moved in 2009 to a modern facility in Erlanger, Kentucky. The company prints hundreds of millions of decks annually, supplies custom cards to casinos, magicians and designers, and is a fixture of crowdfunding culture through custom Kickstarter decks. During the Second World War it famously produced "map decks" for Allied prisoners of war, in cooperation with British intelligence: cards that, when soaked, revealed escape maps inside.

Bicycle cards remain the default deck for cardistry, magic and home poker in North America, and the company's brands are licensed for countless novelty and commemorative editions.
