Human Development Index

Human Development Index (HDI)
Publisher: United Nations Development Programme
Created: 1990, by Mahbub ul Haq and Amartya Sen
Components: Life expectancy, education, GNI per capita (PPP)
Scale: 0 to 1; ≥0.800 = very high

The Human Development Index (HDI) is a statistical composite index of life expectancy, education (mean and expected years of schooling), and per capita income indicators, used to rank countries into tiers of human development. It was developed by Pakistani economist Mahbub ul Haq, drawing on the capability approach of Amartya Sen, and is published by the United Nations Development Programme in its annual Human Development Report.

Method

Since 2010 the HDI has been the geometric mean of normalised indices for health (life expectancy at birth), education, and standard of living (GNI per capita, PPP, log-transformed). Countries are grouped as very high (≥0.800), high (0.700–0.799), medium (0.550–0.699), and low (<0.550). Switzerland, Norway, and Iceland have frequently topped recent rankings.

Criticism and variants

Critics note the HDI omits inequality, environment, and political freedom. Related measures include the inequality-adjusted HDI (IHDI), the Gender Development Index, and the planetary-pressures-adjusted HDI.
