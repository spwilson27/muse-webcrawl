SN 1987A

SN 1987A was a type II supernova in the Large Magellanic Cloud, about 168,000 light-years from Earth. Discovered on 24 February 1987, it was the closest observed supernova since SN 1604 and the first visible to the naked eye since 1604, offering astronomers an unprecedented close-up of a stellar explosion.

DateFebruary 24, 1987
ConstellationDorado
Distance~168,000 light-years
ProgenitorSanduleak −69 202, a blue supergiant

Discovery

Ian Shelton at Las Campanas Observatory in Chile first noticed it photographically; neutrinos were detected hours earlier by Kamiokande II and other detectors, confirming core collapse and marking the birth of neutrino astronomy beyond the Solar System.

Progenitor and remnant

Unexpectedly, the progenitor was a blue supergiant, Sanduleak −69 202, not a red supergiant as theory then expected. The explosion produced a triple-ring system — material shed thousands of years earlier — lit up as the shock wave reached it. Observations by Hubble, Chandra and ALMA have tracked the expanding remnant and dust formation; evidence for the leftover neutron star has grown, including a James Webb Space Telescope detection of ionised argon in 2024 consistent with a young neutron star.
