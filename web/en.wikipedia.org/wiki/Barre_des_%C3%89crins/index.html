The Barre des Écrins is a mountain in the French Alps, the highest peak of the Massif des Écrins in the Dauphiné Alps, reaching 4,102 metres (13,458 ft). Until the annexation of Savoy in 1860 it was the highest mountain in France (excluding the disputed summit of Mont Blanc). It stands within the Écrins National Park, on the border of the Hautes-Alpes and Isère departments, near the village of La Bérarde and the resort-accessible Glacier Blanc.

Geography

The summit has two closely spaced tops: the Barre proper (4,102 m) and the neighbouring Dôme des Écrins (4,015 m), a broad snow dome that is one of the more accessible 4,000-metre summits in the Alps. The mountain rises between the Glacier Blanc to the north-east and the Glacier Noir to the south, and its dramatic north face is a major alpine wall.

Climbing history

The first ascent was made on 25 June 1864 by the English climbers Adolphus Warburton Moore, Horace Walker and Edward Whymper, guided by Michel Croz and Christian Almer (father and son). The north face was first climbed in 1944. The usual route today ascends via the Écrins Hut (Refuge des Écrins) on the Glacier Blanc to the Dôme, and continues along the ridge to the Barre for the slightly higher summit.
