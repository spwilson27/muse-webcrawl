Ol Chiki script

The Ol Chiki (ᱚᱞ ᱪᱤᱠᱤ) script, also known as Ol Cemet', Ol script and Ol Chemet', is the official writing system for the Santali language, an Austroasiatic language spoken by about 7.6 million people in India, Bangladesh, Nepal and Bhutan. It was invented by Pandit Raghunath Murmu in 1925.

Design

Unlike most Indic scripts, Ol Chiki is a true alphabet rather than an abugida: it has distinct letters for vowels and consonants and is written left to right. The script contains 30 letters, whose shapes are said to be derived from natural forms — for example, shapes evoking a plough, a hand, or a tree. The script also has its own set of digits and diacritics used to mark nasalisation and other features of Santali phonology, including checked consonants.

History and use

Before the creation of Ol Chiki, Santali was written, when written at all, in borrowed scripts such as Bengali, Odia, Devanagari and Latin. Murmu devised Ol Chiki to give the Santal people a script of their own suited to the sounds of their language. The script is taught in schools in Santali-speaking regions, is used in publications and examinations, and received a boost when Santali was added to the Eighth Schedule of the Indian Constitution in 2003, recognising it as a scheduled language.

Unicode

Ol Chiki was added to the Unicode Standard in 2008 (version 5.1), in its own block (U+1C50–U+1C7F).
