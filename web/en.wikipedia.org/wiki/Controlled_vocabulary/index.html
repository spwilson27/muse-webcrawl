Controlled vocabulary

A controlled vocabulary is a carefully selected list of words and phrases used to tag units of information (such as documents or database records) so that they may be retrieved by search. Controlled vocabularies are used in subject indexing schemes, subject headings, thesauri, taxonomies and other knowledge organization systems.

Purpose

Natural language is ambiguous: the same concept can be expressed by many synonyms, and the same word can denote several concepts. A controlled vocabulary mandates a single preferred term for each concept and cross-references non-preferred synonyms to it, improving both recall (all documents on a topic are found under one term) and precision (documents on different topics are not conflated). Examples include the Library of Congress Subject Headings, MeSH (Medical Subject Headings) used to index PubMed, and the Art & Architecture Thesaurus.

Types

Controlled vocabularies range from simple term lists and authority files through taxonomies with hierarchical structure to rich thesauri expressing equivalence, hierarchical and associative relationships, and full ontologies.
