Sony α (Alpha) is a series of digital cameras produced by Sony. The brand was introduced in 2006 after Sony acquired the camera business of Konica Minolta, and initially covered digital single-lens reflex cameras using the A-mount inherited from Minolta. The line later expanded to mirrorless interchangeable-lens cameras using the E-mount, which became the company's main photographic system.

History

Sony announced its entry into the DSLR market in 2005 through a partnership with Konica Minolta and acquired that company's camera assets in 2006. The first product, the α100, appeared that year. Sony subsequently released a full range of A-mount bodies, from entry-level models to the full-frame α900.

E-mount mirrorless cameras

In 2010 Sony launched the NEX series of compact mirrorless cameras using the new E-mount, later rebranded under the α name. The full-frame α7 series, launched in 2013, popularised full-frame mirrorless photography, and high-end models such as the α9 and α1 targeted professional sports and flagship stills/video use. The range also includes APS-C bodies (α6000 series), cinema-oriented models, and the compact ZV series for video creators.

Lenses

The system is supported by Sony's own E-mount lenses, including the premium G Master line, and by third-party manufacturers. A-mount lenses can be used on E-mount bodies with adapters, some of which preserve autofocus.
