Early warning system
Purpose: Advance alert of hazards
Applies to: Natural, health, military, financial hazards

An early warning system is a system of sensors, forecasting, communication and response procedures designed to detect an impending hazard and warn people in time to reduce harm. The term is used for natural hazards such as earthquakes, tsunamis, floods and storms, as well as for missile attack, epidemics and financial crises.

Components

Effective systems are usually described as having four elements: risk knowledge, monitoring and forecasting, dissemination of understandable warnings, and response capability in the community. International frameworks such as the UN's Early Warnings for All initiative aim to ensure every person on Earth is protected by such a system.

Examples

Examples include Japan's earthquake early warning, which uses seismic networks to give seconds of notice before strong shaking; Pacific and Indian Ocean tsunami warning centres established after the 2004 tsunami; and wireless emergency alerts sent to mobile phones in many countries.
