Airbus A330

Airbus A330
Role: Wide-body airliner
Manufacturer: Airbus
First flight: 2 November 1992
Introduction: 17 January 1994 (Air Inter)
Status: In production and service
Variants: A330-200, A330-300, A330neo (-800/-900), A330 MRTT, Beluga XL derived

The Airbus A330 is a wide-body, twin-engine airliner developed and produced by Airbus. Developed alongside the four-engine A340, it first flew in 1992 and entered service in 1994. Versions include the A330-200, the longer A330-300, freighter and tanker (MRTT) variants, and the re-engined A330neo family.

Development

Airbus sought a twinjet to compete with the Boeing 767 and 777, sharing the A340's fuselage cross-section and a glass cockpit derived from the A320 family. Extended-range twin-engine operations (ETOPS) certification let the type fly long overwater routes.

Design

The A330 seats typically 250–290 passengers (up to over 400 in high-density layouts) and has a range of roughly 11,750 km (A330-300) to 13,450 km (A330-200), powered by Rolls-Royce Trent 700, GE CF6, or Pratt & Whitney PW4000 engines; the neo uses Trent 7000s with larger wings and Sharklet winglets for improved fuel burn.

Operators and service

Over 1,500 A330s have been delivered to airlines worldwide, and the type also serves as a military tanker/transport (A330 MRTT) and as the basis of the Beluga XL transporter. Notable accidents include Air France Flight 447 (an A330-200 lost over the Atlantic in 2009), which prompted changes in pitot-tube maintenance and crew training.
