The Pyrenean chamois (Rupicapra pyrenaica), known as the isard in Catalan and sarrio in Aragonese, is a species of goat-antelope native to the Pyrenees, the Cantabrian Mountains, and the Apennines of Italy. It is one of two species in the genus Rupicapra, the other being the Alpine chamois (R. rupicapra).

Description

The Pyrenean chamois stands about 80 cm at the shoulder and weighs 25–50 kg. Both sexes bear short, black, hook-shaped horns curving backwards at the tip. The summer coat is short and reddish-brown; in winter it grows longer and darker. A bold dark stripe runs along the back, and a characteristic dark mask crosses the eyes against a pale face—markings more contrasting than in the Alpine chamois.

Distribution and subspecies

Three subspecies are recognised: R. p. pyrenaica of the Pyrenees, R. p. parva of the Cantabrian Mountains, and R. p. ornata of the central Apennines (the Apennine chamois). Animals inhabit steep rocky terrain between about 400 and 2,800 metres, moving to lower forested slopes in winter. They feed on grasses, herbs, lichens and shoots, and live in herds of females and young, with adult males mostly solitary outside the autumn rut.

Conservation

Once reduced by hunting, populations have recovered under protection; the Pyrenean and Cantabrian subspecies together number in the tens of thousands and are classified as Least Concern. The Apennine subspecies, however, remains small and vulnerable, confined mainly to the Abruzzo, Lazio and Molise National Park area, and is the subject of reintroduction efforts in other Apennine parks.
