Urban air mobility

Urban air mobility (UAM), increasingly discussed under the broader term advanced air mobility (AAM), refers to proposed systems of safe, sustainable air transport within and around metropolitan areas, typically using small, highly automated electric vertical take-off and landing aircraft (eVTOLs) to carry passengers or cargo on short routes. The concept aims to relieve surface congestion by using low-altitude airspace.

Vehicles and infrastructure

Most UAM vehicles in development are eVTOL designs with distributed electric propulsion — multiple small rotors or tilting propellers driven by electric motors — intended to be quieter, cheaper to maintain, and emission-free at point of use compared with helicopters. Companies developing such aircraft include Joby Aviation, Archer Aviation, Lilium, Volocopter, and Vertical Aerospace. Ground infrastructure takes the form of "vertiports": compact terminals with landing pads, charging facilities, and passenger processing, often sited on existing heliports, airport land, or rooftop structures.

Regulation and challenges

Regulators such as the US Federal Aviation Administration and the European Union Aviation Safety Agency have been developing certification frameworks for eVTOL aircraft and their pilots, with early commercial air-taxi services targeted for the mid-2020s. Key challenges include battery energy density, noise, air traffic management for high-density low-altitude operations (sometimes termed U-space or UTM), public acceptance, and the economics of competing with ground transport. NASA's Advanced Air Mobility National Campaign has worked with industry to test operational concepts.
