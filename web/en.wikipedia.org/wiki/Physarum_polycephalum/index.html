Physarum polycephalum is an acellular slime mould, or myxomycete, belonging to the class Myxogastria. It is commonly found in shady, cool, moist areas, such as on decaying leaves and logs. The specific epithet polycephalum means "many-headed", referring to the multinucleate nature of its vegetative stage.

Life cycle

The main vegetative phase of P. polycephalum is the plasmodium — a bright yellow, multinucleate mass of cytoplasm that can grow to cover many square centimetres and contains many diploid nuclei within a single cell. The plasmodium creeps over surfaces, engulfing bacteria, spores and decaying matter by phagocytosis, and exhibits a rhythmic shuttle streaming of cytoplasm. Under stress or starvation it can form tough dormant structures called sclerotia, or produce sporangia that release haploid spores, which germinate into swimming or amoeboid cells that fuse to begin the cycle again.

Behaviour and research

Although it has no brain or nervous system, P. polycephalum displays behaviours reminiscent of learning and problem-solving. Experiments have shown that the plasmodium can find the shortest path through a maze to food sources, form efficient transport networks resembling the Tokyo rail system when food is placed at "city" positions, anticipate periodically recurring stimuli, and habituate to repellents. It has become a popular model organism for studying decentralised biological computation and is also famous as "the blob", kept in laboratories and science museums around the world.
