Gustav Lindenthal

Gustav Lindenthal (21 May 1850 – 31 July 1935) was an Austrian civil engineer and bridge designer who emigrated to the United States in 1874. He designed some of the most significant bridges in New York City, most famously the Hell Gate Bridge (1916), a steel arch bridge over the East River, and the Queensboro Bridge (1909).

Lindenthal served as commissioner of bridges for New York City from 1902 to 1903, and spent decades advocating for a rail bridge across the Hudson River at 57th Street, a project never built. His work also included the Lindenthal Medal's namesake designs in Pittsburgh, where his Smithfield Street Bridge (1883) used his lenticular truss design. He was known for monumental, aesthetically ambitious engineering.
