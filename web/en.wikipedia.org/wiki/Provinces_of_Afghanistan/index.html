Provinces of Afghanistan

The provinces of Afghanistan (Pashto: ولایت, wilayat; Dari: ولایت) are the primary administrative divisions of Afghanistan. The country is divided into 34 provinces, each governed by a provincial governor appointed by the central government in Kabul. Each province is further subdivided into districts.

Administration

Provincial governors are appointed by the president (and, since 2021, confirmed under the Taliban administration). Provinces vary widely in population and area: Kabul Province is by far the most populous, while Nimruz and other southern and western provinces are large but sparsely populated. Provincial councils were elected bodies under the former republic's constitution.

History

The modern system of provinces developed during the reign of Abdur Rahman Khan in the late 19th century and was repeatedly reorganized in the 20th century. The most recent province, Daykundi, was created in 2004 from parts of Uruzgan.
