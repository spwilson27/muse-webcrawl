East Asian studies

RegionEast Asia: China, Japan, Korea, Mongolia, Taiwan
DisciplinesHistory, languages, literature, politics, economics, art, religion
Related fieldsSinology, Japanology, Korean studies, Asian studies

East Asian studies is an interdisciplinary academic field devoted to the study of East Asia — principally China, Japan, Korea, Taiwan and Mongolia — encompassing their languages, histories, literatures, religions, politics, economies and cultures. It is a form of area studies offered by many universities as undergraduate and graduate degree programmes, usually combining language training with coursework in the humanities and social sciences.

Scope and methods

Programmes typically require proficiency in at least one regional language — Mandarin Chinese, Japanese or Korean — and draw on methods from history, literary studies, anthropology, political science, economics, art history and religious studies. Students may concentrate on a single country (as in Sinology for China or Japanology for Japan) or take a comparative regional approach covering topics such as Confucian heritage, modernisation, and regional security.

History

Western study of East Asia grew out of missionary and diplomatic activity and 19th-century philology. In the United States the field expanded rapidly after the Second World War, with government funding under the National Defense Education Act of 1958 and support from foundations such as the Ford Foundation, producing major centres at Harvard, Columbia, the University of Michigan and elsewhere. Scholars such as John King Fairbank shaped the study of modern China. The Association for Asian Studies, founded in 1941, is the field's main professional organisation, and the Journal of Asian Studies a leading publication.

Criticism

East Asian studies, like other area studies, has been criticised for its origins in Cold War policy needs, for treating the region through Western theoretical frameworks (a critique associated with Edward Said's Orientalism), and for funding dependence on governments and on the states it studies.
