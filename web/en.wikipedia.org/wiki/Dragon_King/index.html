The Dragon King, also translated as the Dragon God (Chinese: 龍王, Lóngwáng), is a Chinese water and weather god in Chinese folk religion and mythology. He is depicted as the zoomorphic incarnation of the lóng (Chinese dragon) and rules the seas from a crystal palace beneath the water, guarded by shrimp soldiers and crab generals. As dispenser of rain, he was prayed to by farmers during drought and placated by coastal communities fearing storms and floods.

The Four Dragon Kings

There are four principal Dragon Kings, each ruling one of the Four Seas corresponding to the cardinal directions: Ao Guang of the East Sea (the eldest and most prominent), Ao Qin of the South Sea, Ao Run of the West Sea and Ao Shun of the North Sea. In imperial China the Dragon Kings received official cult status, with temples, dragon dances and rain-making rituals performed in their honour under the supervision of local officials.

In literature

The Dragon Kings feature prominently in classic Chinese fiction. In Journey to the West, Sun Wukong obtains his magic staff, the Ruyi Jingu Bang, from Ao Guang's treasury. In Investiture of the Gods (Fengshen Yanyi), Ao Guang's son Ao Bing is killed by the child hero Nezha, provoking the Dragon Kings' wrath and floods. The Dragon Kings also appear in Japanese mythology as Ryūō/Ryūjin, adapted from the Indian nāga kings through Buddhism.
