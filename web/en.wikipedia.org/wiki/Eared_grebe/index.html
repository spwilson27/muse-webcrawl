The eared grebe (Podiceps nigricollis), also known as the black-necked grebe in Europe, is a member of the grebe family of water birds. It was described in 1831 by Christian Ludwig Brehm. There are currently three subspecies recognised.

Description

The eared grebe is 28–34 centimetres (11–13 in) long with a wingspan of 41–65 cm. In breeding plumage it has a distinctive black head with golden fan-shaped tufts of feathers extending behind the eye ("ears"), a black neck, and chestnut flanks. The eyes are bright red. In non-breeding plumage it is much duller, grey-black above and white below, lacking the golden tufts.

Distribution and habitat

This species breeds on vegetated areas of freshwater lakes across Europe, Asia, Africa, northern South America and the southwest and western United States. The North American population is the largest in the world; huge staging flocks gather on hypersaline lakes such as the Great Salt Lake in Utah and Mono Lake in California, where the birds feed on brine shrimp and alkali flies and may double their weight before migrating. Most northern populations are migratory, wintering on coasts and ice-free lakes.

Behaviour

Like all grebes, the eared grebe is an excellent swimmer and diver, pursuing aquatic invertebrates and small fish underwater. It is a reluctant flier and typically escapes danger by diving. During the staging period on saline lakes, the birds undergo a simultaneous wing moult and become flightless for several weeks.

The species breeds colonially, building a floating nest of plant material anchored to emergent vegetation. Both parents incubate the eggs and carry the striped young on their backs.

Subspecies

  • P. n. nigricollis — western Eurasia
  • P. n. gurneyi — southern Africa
  • P. n. californicus — North America
