San Francisco Tape Music Center

Founded1962, San Francisco, California
FoundersRamon Sender, Morton Subotnick
FocusElectronic and tape music
SuccessorCenter for Contemporary Music, Mills College (1966)

The San Francisco Tape Music Center was a nonprofit studio and performance organisation for electronic music founded in 1962 by composers Ramon Sender and Morton Subotnick, with early associates including Pauline Oliveros, Terry Riley and engineer Tony Martin. Housed on Divisadero Street, it became the West Coast hub of experimental music in the 1960s.

Activities

The Center presented concerts of tape music, live electronics and multimedia works, hosting premieres by its founders and guests. Terry Riley's In C (1964), a founding work of musical minimalism, was premiered in its orbit, and Steve Reich's tape piece It's Gonna Rain was composed there in 1965. The Center also pioneered light-show visuals and improvisation as concert practice.

The Buchla synthesizer

In 1963 the Center commissioned inventor Don Buchla to build an electronic music instrument with a grant from the Rockefeller Foundation. The result, the Buchla 100 Series modular synthesizer, emphasised touch plates and unconventional control rather than a piano keyboard — the origin of the so-called "West Coast" approach to synthesis, in contrast to Moog's East Coast instruments.

Legacy

In 1966 the Center merged with Mills College in Oakland to become the Center for Contemporary Music, transferring its equipment and mission. Its alumni and ideas shaped minimalist composition, live electronic performance and the development of the voltage-controlled synthesizer.
