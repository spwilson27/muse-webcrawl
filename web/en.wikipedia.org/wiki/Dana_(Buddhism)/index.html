Dana (Buddhism)

Offering food to monks is a classic expression of dāna.

Dāna (Sanskrit: दान; Pali: dāna) is the practice of cultivating generosity or giving in Buddhism. It is the first of the pāramitās (perfections) in both Theravāda and Mahāyāna traditions, and is regarded as the foundational virtue from which the other perfections grow.

Practice

In Buddhist communities, lay followers traditionally offer food, robes, shelter and medicine to the monastic Sangha, sustaining monks and nuns who by rule do not handle money or prepare their own meals. Such giving generates merit (puñña) and weakens attachment and greed, one of the three poisons at the root of suffering.

Forms of giving

Classical texts distinguish three kinds of giving: the giving of material things (āmisadāna), the giving of fearlessness or safety (abhayadāna), and the giving of the Dhamma (dhammadāna) — teaching or sharing the Buddha's doctrine — which the Buddha described as the highest gift.

Intention

The value of an act of dāna depends chiefly on the giver's intention before, during and after the act: giving with a mind free of expectation, and dedicating the merit to the welfare of others, is considered the most fruitful form of generosity.
