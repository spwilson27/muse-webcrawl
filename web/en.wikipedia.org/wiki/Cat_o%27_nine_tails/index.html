Cat o' nine tails

The cat o' nine tails, commonly shortened to "the cat", is a type of multi-tailed whip that originated as an implement of severe physical punishment, notably in the Royal Navy and British Army. It consists of nine knotted cords of cotton or leather attached to a handle, designed to lacerate the skin and inflict intense pain.

Naval use

In the age of sail, flogging with the cat was the standard punishment for serious offences aboard Royal Navy ships. The cat was traditionally made by the boatswain's mate, kept in a red baize bag (the source of phrases such as "let the cat out of the bag"), and a dozen lashes was a common sentence, with courts martial able to award hundreds. The victim was tied to a grating and the crew assembled to witness punishment.

Abolition and legacy

Flogging was progressively restricted in the 19th century, suspended in the Royal Navy in 1879 and abolished in the British Army in 1881; judicial corporal punishment with the cat survived in some prisons and colonies into the 20th century. The instrument survives in idioms such as "not enough room to swing a cat" and remains a symbol of brutal naval discipline.
