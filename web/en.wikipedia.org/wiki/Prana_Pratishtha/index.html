Prana Pratishtha

Prana Pratishtha (Sanskrit: prāṇa pratiṣṭhā, "establishment of the life force") is the Hindu rite of consecration by which a deity is invoked into a murti (sacred image or statue), transforming it from a mere representation into an embodiment of the divine presence that can receive worship. The ceremony is performed when a new image is installed in a temple or home shrine, and forms the culminating act of temple consecration.

The rite

The ceremony unfolds over several stages, often across one or more days, guided by Agama and Tantric texts. Preparatory rites purify the image and the site; the murti may be ritually bathed (snana), laid to rest (shayana), and awakened. Through mantra recitation, nyasa (the ritual placing of mantras on the image and on the priest's own body), and meditation, the priest invites the deity's prana — vital breath — into the image. The climactic moment is frequently the opening of the eyes (netronmilana), when the priest ceremonially paints or uncovers the pupils of the deity, traditionally while a mirror first receives the gaze.

Theology

Within Hindu theology the rite rests on the doctrine that the deity, though omnipresent, graciously condescends to be specially present in the consecrated image (archa, "worshipful descent"), making worship and darshan (seeing and being seen by the deity) possible. After consecration, the murti is treated as a living royal personage — awakened, bathed, dressed, fed, and put to rest daily.

Notable ceremonies

Large-scale Prana Pratishtha ceremonies draw wide public attention in India; the consecration of the Ram Lalla murti at the Ram Mandir in Ayodhya on 22 January 2024, attended by the Prime Minister and broadcast nationally, is a prominent recent example.
