Cape Breton fiddling

Cape Breton fiddling is a regional style of violin music from Cape Breton Island, Nova Scotia, Canada. It descends from the Scottish fiddle traditions brought by Highland emigrants in the late 18th and early 19th centuries, and is considered one of the most faithful survivals of 18th-century Scottish playing, preserved in relative isolation. The music accompanies step dancing and square dances at community ceilidhs.

Style

The Cape Breton style is characterised by driving rhythm, heavy use of ornamentation such as cuts, crans and triplets, and a repertoire of strathspeys, reels, jigs, marches and slow airs drawn heavily from Scottish collections such as those of the Gow family and J. Scott Skinner. Fiddlers are traditionally accompanied by piano playing a distinctive syncopated "boom-chuck" vamping pattern, and sometimes by guitar.

Notable performers

Prominent Cape Breton fiddlers include Buddy MacMaster, Natalie MacMaster, Ashley MacIsaac, Jerry Holland, Winston "Scotty" Fitzgerald, and Dan Hughie MacEachern. The annual Celtic Colours International Festival, held each October, draws visitors from around the world to concerts across the island.

Preservation

Because the tradition was transmitted largely by ear, local teachers and organisations have worked to document tunes, and a resurgence of interest since the 1970s — aided by recordings and the work of players such as Buddy MacMaster — has carried the style to international audiences.
