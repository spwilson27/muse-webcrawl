The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) is a physical sciences laboratory and non-regulatory agency of the United States Department of Commerce. Its mission is to promote American innovation and industrial competitiveness by advancing measurement science, standards, and technology. From 1901 to 1988 it was known as the National Bureau of Standards (NBS).

Work

NIST operates laboratories in Gaithersburg, Maryland, and Boulder, Colorado. It maintains the primary standards for the United States' customary and metric units, operates atomic clocks that contribute to Coordinated Universal Time, and develops reference materials and data used throughout industry and science. Its computer security standards — including the Federal Information Processing Standards (FIPS) and the NIST Cybersecurity Framework — are widely adopted, and its open competitions selected the AES encryption standard and the SHA-3 hash function.

Organisation

NIST's laboratory units cover engineering, information technology, material measurement, physical measurement, neutron research, and nanoscale science and technology. It also runs outreach programmes such as the Hollings Manufacturing Extension Partnership and the Baldrige Performance Excellence Program. The institute's investigations have included the official federal studies of the collapse of the World Trade Center towers after the September 11 attacks.
