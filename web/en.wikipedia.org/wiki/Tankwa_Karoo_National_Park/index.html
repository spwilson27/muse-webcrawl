The arid Tankwa Karoo landscape

Tankwa Karoo National Park is a national park in South Africa, situated in one of the most arid regions of the country, on the border of the Northern Cape and Western Cape provinces. The park lies in the Tankwa Karoo, a sparsely populated basin between the Roggeveld Mountains to the east, the Cederberg to the west, and the Klein Roggeveld Mountains to the south.

History

The park was proclaimed on 19 May 1986, initially covering a much smaller area. Through subsequent land purchases and land reform transfers, it has expanded to roughly 143,600 hectares, protecting a representative portion of the Succulent Karoo biome, one of the world's recognised biodiversity hotspots.

Flora and fauna

Despite annual rainfall of only about 70 to 160 millimetres, the Tankwa Karoo supports a remarkable diversity of succulent plants, bulbs and annual wildflowers that carpet the veld after good winter rains. The park is an important area for endemic plant species and is regarded by botanists as one of the richest arid floras on Earth.

Mammals in the park include springbok, gemsbok (oryx), red hartebeest, kudu, aardvark, and smaller species such as the bat-eared fox and African wildcat; leopards occur in the mountain fringes. The birdlife is notable for Karoo endemics such as the Karoo korhaan, Ludwig's bustard, black-eared sparrow-lark and the near-endemic cinnamon-breasted warbler, making the park one of South Africa's recognised Important Bird Areas.

Tourism

The park offers remote cottages, restored farmhouses, wilderness camps and 4x4 routes. Its extreme isolation, dark skies and stark scenery attract stargazers and travellers seeking solitude. Access is by gravel road from towns such as Ceres, Calvinia and Sutherland, and high-clearance vehicles are recommended.
