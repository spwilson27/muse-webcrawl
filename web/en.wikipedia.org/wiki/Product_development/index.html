Product development

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

FieldBusiness, marketing, engineering, industrial design
Also calledNew product development (NPD)

Product development, also called new product management, is a series of steps that includes the conceptualization, design, development and marketing of newly created or newly rebranded goods or services. Product development is the creation of products with new or different characteristics that offer new or additional benefits to the customer. It may involve modification of an existing product or its presentation, or formulation of an entirely new product that satisfies a newly defined customer want or market niche.

Process

The classic stage-gate model of new product development (NPD), popularised by Booz, Allen & Hamilton and refined by Robert G. Cooper, includes:

  1. Idea generation
  2. Idea screening
  3. Concept development and testing
  4. Marketing strategy development
  5. Business analysis
  6. Product development (prototyping and engineering)
  7. Test marketing
  8. Commercialization

The "fuzzy front end" refers to the early, uncertain stages before formal development begins. Modern approaches include agile product development, lean startup methods with minimum viable products, design thinking, and concurrent engineering, in which design, manufacturing, and marketing proceed in parallel rather than sequentially.

Considerations

Successful product development requires understanding customer needs, competitive positioning, technical feasibility, cost targets, and time-to-market. Failure rates for new products are high; estimates commonly cited in marketing literature suggest most new products fail to reach their commercial objectives.
