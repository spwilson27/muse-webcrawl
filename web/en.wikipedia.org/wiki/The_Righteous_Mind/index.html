The Righteous Mind

AuthorJonathan Haidt
SubjectMoral psychology, political psychology
PublisherPantheon Books
Published2012

The Righteous Mind: Why Good People Are Divided by Politics and Religion is a 2012 book by social psychologist Jonathan Haidt. In it, Haidt argues that moral judgment is driven primarily by intuition, with reasoning serving largely as a post-hoc justification, and that this explains the persistent divisions of politics and religion.

Main ideas

The book presents three principles. First, "intuitions come first, strategic reasoning second," illustrated by the metaphor of a rider (reason) on an elephant (intuition): the rider thinks he steers, but mostly serves as the elephant's lawyer. Second, "there's more to morality than harm and fairness": Haidt's moral foundations theory proposes six foundations — care/harm, fairness/cheating, loyalty/betrayal, authority/subversion, sanctity/degradation, and liberty/oppression — of which political liberals rely mainly on care and fairness while conservatives use all six more evenly. Third, "morality binds and blinds": humans are, in Haidt's phrase, "90 percent chimp, 10 percent bee," groupish creatures whose moral systems unite them into teams that then cannot see opposing views.

Reception

The book became a New York Times bestseller and one of the most discussed works of popular psychology of its decade. Scholars have debated whether the social intuitionist model understates deliberation and whether the foundations are the right taxonomy, while political writers of many persuasions have drawn on its account of polarization. Haidt later extended its concerns to campus culture in The Coddling of the American Mind (2018, with Greg Lukianoff) and to adolescent mental health in The Anxious Generation (2024).
