Slope soaring

Slope soaring is a technique of flying unpowered aircraft — gliders, hang gliders, paragliders and radio-controlled models — using the rising air produced when wind is deflected upward by a hill, ridge, cliff or dune. The upward component of the airflow in front of the slope can sustain flight for as long as the wind blows.

Slope soaring is one of the basic forms of soaring, along with thermal, wave and convergence lift. At suitable coastal and inland sites, pilots can remain airborne for hours, flying back and forth along the ridge. A related advanced technique, dynamic soaring on the lee side of a ridge, allows radio-controlled gliders to reach speeds of several hundred kilometres per hour.

Conditions

Ideal conditions are a steady wind blowing roughly perpendicular to a smooth slope. Hazards include rotor turbulence behind the ridge and sudden wind changes.
