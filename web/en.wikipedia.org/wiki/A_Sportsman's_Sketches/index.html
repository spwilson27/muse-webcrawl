A Sportsman's Sketches (Russian: Записки охотника, "Notes of a Hunter"), also translated as Sketches from a Hunter's Album, is a cycle of short stories by the Russian writer Ivan Turgenev, published as a book in 1852. Narrated by a landowner travelling with his rifle and dog through the Oryol region, the sketches portray peasants and serfs as full human beings and are credited with influencing opinion in favour of the emancipation of the serfs in 1861.

Contents

The collection comprises 25 sketches. Among the best known are "Khor and Kalinych", contrasting two serfs; "Bezhin Lea" (Bezhin Meadow), in which peasant boys tell folk tales around a night fire; "The Singers", set in a village tavern; and "Living Relic", the portrait of a paralysed peasant woman of extraordinary patience. Ernest Hemingway praised the book highly, and "Bezhin Meadow" was the subject of Sergei Eisenstein's suppressed film of the same name (1937).

Significance

Turgenev's dignified, observant depictions of serf life — published when serfdom was still the foundation of the Russian rural order — were read as an indictment of the system. Legend holds that Tsar Alexander II kept the book in mind when emancipating the serfs. The work also established Turgenev's reputation in Europe and remains a landmark of Russian realism and of the sketch as a literary form.
