ACT UP, the AIDS Coalition to Unleash Power, is an international, grassroots political group working to end the AIDS pandemic. Founded in New York City in March 1987 by playwright Larry Kramer and fellow activists, it became the most influential activist organisation of the AIDS crisis, famous for confrontational direct action under the slogan "Silence = Death".

History and tactics

ACT UP chapters spread to dozens of cities in the United States and abroad. The group staged "die-ins", shut down the Food and Drug Administration headquarters in 1988 to demand faster approval of AIDS drugs, disrupted trading on the New York Stock Exchange to protest the price of AZT, and demonstrated inside St. Patrick's Cathedral in 1989 against the Catholic Church's positions on condoms and abortion. Its Treatment and Data Committee mastered the science of clinical trials and won seats at regulatory tables, helping reshape drug-approval processes, including expanded access and parallel-track trials.

Achievements and legacy

ACT UP is credited with accelerating antiretroviral drug development, forcing price reductions (Burroughs Wellcome cut AZT's price after the Stock Exchange action), and institutionalising patient representation in research. Its art collectives — Gran Fury and the creators of the SILENCE = DEATH poster — defined the visual language of AIDS activism. The organisation declined in intensity in the 1990s as combination therapy transformed HIV into a manageable condition, but chapters remain active in campaigns on drug pricing, PrEP access and global treatment. The 2012 documentary How to Survive a Plague chronicled its history.
