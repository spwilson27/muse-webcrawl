VDSL

Very high-speed digital subscriber line (VDSL) and VDSL2 are digital subscriber line (DSL) technologies providing data transmission faster than asymmetric digital subscriber line (ADSL). VDSL offers speeds of up to 52 Mbit/s downstream and 16 Mbit/s upstream over a single flat untwisted pair of copper wires using frequencies up to 12 MHz.

Standards

VDSL was standardized as ITU G.993.1 in 2001. Its successor, VDSL2 (ITU G.993.2, 2005), uses frequencies up to 30 MHz and can deliver aggregate speeds approaching 200 Mbit/s very close to the exchange or cabinet, falling with loop length. Later enhancements include vectoring (G.993.5), which cancels crosstalk between lines in a cable bundle, and bonding of pairs.

Deployment

VDSL is typically used in fibre-to-the-cabinet (FTTC) architectures, where optical fibre runs to a street cabinet and VDSL covers the final short copper segment to the customer premises.
