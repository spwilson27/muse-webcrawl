Thunderbolt (interface)

ConnectorMini DisplayPort (TB1–2); USB-C (TB3+)
Bandwidth10–120 Gbit/s depending on version
ProtocolsPCI Express, DisplayPort, USB

Thunderbolt is a hardware interface for connecting peripheral devices to a computer, developed by Intel in collaboration with Apple. It combines PCI Express and DisplayPort signals into a single serial signal, along with DC power, in one cable. Thunderbolt controllers multiplex data from connected PCIe and DisplayPort devices for transmission over the cable and demultiplex it at the receiving end.

History

The technology was developed at Intel Labs under the codename "Light Peak", originally conceived as an optical interconnect. The first commercial version, using copper wiring, shipped in Apple's MacBook Pro in February 2011. Thunderbolt 2 (2013) doubled aggregate bandwidth to 20 Gbit/s. Thunderbolt 3 (2015) moved to the USB-C connector, raised bandwidth to 40 Gbit/s, and incorporated USB, DisplayPort and power delivery, becoming a one-cable docking solution. Thunderbolt 4 (2020) tightened minimum requirements, and Thunderbolt 5 (announced 2023) raised bandwidth to 80 Gbit/s symmetric and up to 120 Gbit/s with Bandwidth Boost.

Characteristics

Thunderbolt supports daisy-chaining of up to six devices, peer-to-peer networking over cables, external graphics enclosures, and high-speed storage. From Thunderbolt 3 onward it is built on and compatible with the USB4 specification, which Intel contributed to the USB Implementers Forum along with the royalty-free Thunderbolt protocol.
