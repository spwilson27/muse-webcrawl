Console (architecture)

A console in architecture is an ornamental bracket of stone, wood or metal that projects from a wall to support a cornice, balcony, shelf or sculpture. Characteristically, a console is taller than it projects, and it commonly takes the form of a scroll or volute, often curling in an S-shape reminiscent of an acanthus scroll, sometimes paired with a smaller counter-scroll at its base.

Characteristics

Consoles are especially associated with classical and classically derived architecture — Renaissance, Baroque and Neoclassical buildings — where they appear beneath balconies and window sills, under cornices, framing doorways, and supporting statues, busts or urns. In French architecture the console is a prominent feature of façades in the Louis XV and Régence styles, often richly carved with shells, foliage and keystones.

Related elements

The console is distinguished from the corbel, a plain structural block stepped out from a wall, and from the modillion, a smaller, repeated bracket under a Corinthian cornice. A console table — a small table designed to stand against a wall, sometimes with only two front legs and a bracket fixing it to the wall — takes its name from the architectural console.
