Marin Mersenne (8 September 1588 – 1 September 1648) was a French Minim friar, mathematician, music theorist and polymath. He is best remembered for the Mersenne primes — prime numbers of the form 2n − 1 — named after him, and for his vast correspondence with the leading scholars of Europe, which made him a central clearing-house of the seventeenth-century Republic of Letters and earned him the nickname "the post-office of Europe".

Life

Mersenne was educated at the Jesuit college at La Flèche (where René Descartes was a slightly younger contemporary) and joined the Minim order in 1611. Settled at the Minim convent in Paris from 1619, he devoted himself to scholarship and to defending orthodox science and religion against occultism and scepticism, notably in Quaestiones celeberrimae in Genesim (1623) and La vérité des sciences (1625). His cell hosted regular meetings of scholars — among them Descartes, Pascal, Fermat, Roberval, Gassendi and Hobbes — a circle regarded as a forerunner of the French Academy of Sciences.

Work

In mathematics, Mersenne studied prime numbers and published in Cogitata physico-mathematica (1644) his claims about which numbers 2p − 1 are prime for p up to 257; his list contained errors but stimulated research that continues in the modern Great Internet Mersenne Prime Search (GIMPS). In music, his Harmonie universelle (1636) is a foundational work of acoustics, stating Mersenne's laws for the frequency of vibrating strings in terms of length, tension and mass, and giving the first satisfactory measurement estimates of the speed of sound. He also translated and promoted Galileo's mechanics in France (Les nouvelles pensées de Galilée, 1639) and corresponded with Torricelli about the barometer experiments that led, after his death, to Pascal's famous Puy-de-Dôme experiment.

Death and legacy

Mersenne died in Paris on 1 September 1648 following complications from surgery for a lung abscess. His name is attached to Mersenne primes, Mersenne's laws, the Mersenne twister pseudorandom number generator (anachronistically), and the lunar crater Mersenne.
