The Pont Alexandre III is a deck arch bridge that spans the Seine in Paris. It connects the Champs-Élysées quarter with those of the Invalides and Eiffel Tower. The bridge is widely regarded as the most ornate, extravagant bridge in the city, and has been classified as a French monument historique since 1975.

History

The bridge, named after Tsar Alexander III of Russia, was built as a symbol of the Franco-Russian Alliance concluded in 1892. The foundation stone was laid by Alexander's son, Tsar Nicholas II, in October 1896. Construction, directed by engineers Jean Résal and Amédée d'Alby, was completed in time for the Exposition Universelle of 1900, for which the bridge formed a grand entrance together with the Grand Palais and Petit Palais.

Design and decoration

The bridge is a single steel arch 107 metres long, designed so as not to obstruct views of the Invalides and the Champs-Élysées. It is lavishly decorated in the Beaux-Arts style with Art Nouveau lamps, cherubs, nymphs and winged horses. At each end stand 17-metre pylons crowned with gilded statues of Pegasus held by Fames. The nymph reliefs at the centre commemorate the Franco-Russian Alliance: the Nymphs of the Seine bear the arms of Paris, and the Nymphs of the Neva those of Russia.

In popular culture

The bridge has appeared in numerous films, including Anastasia (1997) and Midnight in Paris (2011), and in music videos and advertising campaigns as an emblem of Parisian elegance.
