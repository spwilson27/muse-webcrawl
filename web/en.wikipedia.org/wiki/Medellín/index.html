Medellín (Spanish pronunciation: [meðeˈʝin]), officially the Special District of Science, Technology and Innovation of Medellín, is the second-largest city in Colombia, after Bogotá, and the capital of the department of Antioquia. It is located in the Aburrá Valley, a central region of the Andes Mountains, and has a pleasant temperate climate that gives it the nickname "City of the Eternal Spring" (Ciudad de la Eterna Primavera).

History

Founded as a Spanish colonial town in 1675, Medellín grew into a coffee, textile, and manufacturing center in the 19th and 20th centuries. In the 1980s and early 1990s the city became notorious for violence associated with Pablo Escobar's Medellín Cartel. Since the 2000s, however, urban interventions — including the Metrocable gondola lines connecting hillside informal settlements, escalators in Comuna 13, libraries, and public spaces — have been widely cited as a model of urban transformation, and in 2013 the Urban Land Institute named Medellín the world's most innovative city.

Transport and culture

Medellín operates Colombia's only metro system, integrated with cable cars, a tram, and bus rapid transit. The city is known for the annual Flower Festival (Feria de las Flores), the botero sculptures of Fernando Botero at Plaza Botero, and the Museo de Antioquia. It is also a center of the Colombian textile and fashion industry.
