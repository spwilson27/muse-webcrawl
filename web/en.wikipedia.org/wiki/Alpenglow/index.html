Alpenglow

Alpenglow (from German Alpenglühen, "Alp glow") is an optical phenomenon in which mountain peaks take on a rosy, reddish or golden glow around sunrise and sunset. It is most striking when the peaks are snow-covered and the surrounding lower slopes and valleys are already in shadow.

Mechanism

Strictly defined, alpenglow occurs when the Sun is just below the horizon: its direct light reaches the high peaks at a grazing angle after passing through a long path of atmosphere, which scatters away short (blue) wavelengths and leaves long red and orange wavelengths. A brighter, direct illumination shortly before sunset or after sunrise is sometimes called "false alpenglow". Fine particles in the air — dust, ice crystals, or volcanic aerosols — can intensify the colours, and a related glow may appear on the sky opposite the Sun, where the pink Band of the Belt of Venus sits above the Earth's shadow.

In culture

The phenomenon is celebrated in Alpine, Himalayan and Rocky Mountain regions and is a prized subject of mountain photography; the Italian term enrosadira is used for the reddening of the Dolomites at dusk, woven into local Ladin legends.
