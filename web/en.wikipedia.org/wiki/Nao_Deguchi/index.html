Nao Deguchi (1837–1918) was a Japanese religious leader, the foundress of Oomoto, one of Japan's "new religions". An illiterate peasant woman from Fukuchiyama, she began in 1892 to receive what she experienced as divine possession by the god Ushitora no Konjin, and the automatic writings she produced while in trance became the sacred scripture of the new faith.

Life

Born into poverty, Nao was widowed young and raised eight children in hardship, experiences that shaped the apocalyptic and egalitarian tone of her revelations. Beginning on New Year's Day 1892, she entered trance states, prophesied the destruction and renewal of the world, and denounced the corruption of the powerful. Her writings, recorded in phonetic script because she could not read kanji, were gathered as the Ofudesaki ("Tip of the Divine Brush"). In 1898 she met the younger spiritual seeker Kisaburo Ueda, who organised her following, married her daughter, and took the name Onisaburo Deguchi, becoming the movement's co-founder and its public leader.

Legacy

Nao's austere, otherworldly message sometimes sat uneasily beside Onisaburo's more worldly expansion of the movement, and tensions between them marked Oomoto's early history. She died in 1918 and is venerated in Oomoto as its foundress; her Ofudesaki remains the movement's primary scripture, while the movement she founded survived later government suppression and continues today, headquartered at Ayabe and Kameoka.
