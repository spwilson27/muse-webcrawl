Stephen Báthory

Stephen Báthory (Hungarian: Báthory István; Polish: Stefan Batory; 27 September 1533 – 12 December 1586) was Prince of Transylvania from 1571 and, from 1576, King of Poland and Grand Duke of Lithuania. One of the most successful rulers of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, he is remembered for his military reforms and his victories over Ivan the Terrible of Russia in the Livonian War.

Prince of Transylvania

Born at Szilágysomlyó into the powerful Báthory family, Stephen was elected voivode, then prince, of Transylvania in 1571 under Ottoman suzerainty. He defeated his rival Gáspár Bekes, who enjoyed Habsburg backing, at the Battle of Kerelőszentpál in 1575, consolidating his rule.

King of Poland

After King Henry of Valois fled Poland in 1574, the Polish nobility split between the Habsburg candidate Maximilian II and Báthory, who was proclaimed king on condition of marrying the Jagiellon heiress Anna. Crowned in Kraków in 1576, he first subdued the defiant city of Gdańsk, then turned east. Relying on his able chancellor and hetman Jan Zamoyski, Báthory reformed the army, expanding the famed winged hussars and registering Cossacks for royal service.

Livonian War

In a series of campaigns (1579–1581) Báthory retook Polotsk and Velikiye Luki and besieged Pskov, forcing Ivan IV to conclude the Truce of Yam-Zapolsky (1582), by which Russia surrendered its claims to Livonia and Polotsk. The victories made Poland–Lithuania the dominant power in north-eastern Europe.

Death and legacy

Báthory died suddenly at Grodno in December 1586 while planning further campaigns, possibly against the Ottomans. He founded the University of Vilnius (1579, entrusted to the Jesuits) and strengthened royal authority against the magnates. In Polish historical memory he ranks among the Commonwealth's greatest kings, despite — or partly because of — his Hungarian origins.
