Liquidation preference

A liquidation preference is a clause in a venture capital investment contract specifying how much a holder of preferred stock is entitled to receive before common stockholders in a "liquidity event" — such as the sale, merger, liquidation, or dissolution of the company.

Types

The preference is usually expressed as a multiple of the original investment (for example, 1x). In a non-participating preference, the investor chooses either the preference amount or conversion into common stock, whichever is greater. In a participating preference, the investor receives the preference amount and then also shares pro rata in the remaining proceeds with common holders — sometimes subject to a cap.

Seniority

Where several rounds of preferred stock exist, proceeds may be distributed either in order of seniority (later rounds paid first) or pari passu, according to the company's charter. Liquidation preferences strongly influence the distribution of returns in modest exits, where common stock may receive little or nothing.
