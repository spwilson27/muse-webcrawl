Chebyshev's inequality

Chebyshev's inequality
FieldProbability theory, statistics
Named afterPafnuty Chebyshev
First published1867 (proved by Chebyshev; stated by Bienaymé, 1853)

In probability theory, Chebyshev's inequality guarantees that, for a wide class of probability distributions, no more than a certain fraction of values can be more than a certain distance from the mean. It has great generality because it makes no assumptions about the shape of the distribution beyond a finite mean and variance.

Statement

Let X be a random variable with expected value μ and finite, non-zero variance σ². Then for any real number k > 0: Pr(|X − μ| ≥ kσ) ≤ 1/k². Equivalently, at least 1 − 1/k² of the distribution's values lie within k standard deviations of the mean. For example, at least 75% of values lie within two standard deviations, and at least 88.9% within three.

Proof and generalisations

The inequality follows directly from Markov's inequality applied to the non-negative random variable (X − μ)². One-sided variants, such as Cantelli's inequality, give sharper bounds for deviations in a single direction. Related forms hold for higher moments.

Applications

Chebyshev's inequality is a standard tool for proving the weak law of large numbers and for deriving distribution-free confidence bounds in statistics and measurement science, where it is prized for its robustness despite being conservative for well-behaved distributions.
