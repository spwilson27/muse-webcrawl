Custer State Park is a South Dakota State Park and wildlife reserve in the Black Hills of the United States. The park is South Dakota's largest and first state park, named in honor of Lt. Colonel George Armstrong Custer. The park covers an area of 71,000 acres (287 km²) of varied terrain including rolling prairie grasslands and rugged mountains.

Wildlife

The park is home to a herd of roughly 1,300–1,500 free-roaming American bison, one of the largest publicly owned herds in the world. Other wildlife includes elk, mule deer, white-tailed deer, mountain goats, bighorn sheep, pronghorn, mountain lions, and feral burros known locally as the "begging burros." An annual Buffalo Roundup, held each autumn since 1965, gathers the herd for health checks and auctions surplus animals to control the population.

Scenic drives

The park is known for its scenic drives, including the Needles Highway (SD-87), with its narrow tunnels and granite spires; the Iron Mountain Road (US-16A), famous for its pigtail bridges and tunnels framing Mount Rushmore; and the 18-mile Wildlife Loop Road through the park's grasslands.

History and lodging

Established in 1919, the park grew under the advocacy of Governor Peter Norbeck. President Calvin Coolidge spent the summer of 1927 at the State Game Lodge, his "Summer White House." Lodges and resorts within the park include the Sylvan Lake Lodge, near Sylvan Lake and the trailhead for Black Elk Peak, the highest point in South Dakota.
