Oceania

[Image: Map of Oceania and the Pacific island nations]
Map of Oceania and the Pacific island nations

Oceania is a geographical region that includes Australasia, Melanesia, Micronesia and Polynesia, spanning the eastern and western hemispheres. Oceania covers a vast swathe of the Pacific Ocean; its total land area is about 8.5 million square kilometres, dominated by Australia, and its population is more than 44 million. Strictly used, Oceania's land area would be the smallest of the continents.

The region stretches from Southeast Asia in the west to Easter Island in the east, and from Hawaii in the north to New Zealand in the south. Unlike the other continents, Oceania is a region defined more by the ocean that connects its islands than by a continuous landmass.

Subregions

Australasia includes Australia, New Zealand, New Guinea and neighbouring islands. Melanesia, northeast of Australia, includes Papua New Guinea, Fiji, the Solomon Islands and Vanuatu. Micronesia, in the north-central Pacific, includes the Federated States of Micronesia, Palau, the Marshall Islands, Kiribati and Nauru. Polynesia forms a vast triangle defined by Hawaii, Easter Island and New Zealand, including Samoa, Tonga and French Polynesia.

History

Oceania was settled by Austronesian-speaking peoples in one of the greatest maritime expansions in human history, reaching remote islands such as Hawaii, Easter Island and New Zealand by around 1250 CE. European exploration began in the 16th century, and most of the region was colonised in the 18th and 19th centuries. Many Pacific island nations gained independence in the decades after the Second World War.
