Moombahton

Stylistic originsDutch house, reggaeton, dancehall, electro house
Cultural origins2009, Washington, D.C., United States
Derivative formsMoombahcore, moombahsoul

Moombahton (/ˈmuːmbətɒn/) is an electronic dance music genre that fuses Dutch house music with the tempo and dembow rhythm of reggaeton. It was created in 2009 by the American DJ and producer Dave Nada, and its name is a portmanteau of "Moombah" — the Chuckie remix of Silvio Ecomo and Chuckie's track "Moombah", central to the sound — and "reggaeton".

History

In autumn 2009, Nada was DJing a party for his younger cousin in Washington, D.C., where the crowd preferred reggaeton and Latin music to the house records he had brought. To bridge the gap, he slowed Afrojack's remix of "Moombah" from 128 BPM down to 108 BPM, the tempo of reggaeton, and played other Dutch house tracks at the same speed over dembow-style beats. The experiment, circulated online the following year, became the template for the genre.

Moombahton spread rapidly in 2010–2012 through producers and DJs such as Dillon Francis, Munchi (who coined the harder "moombahcore" style), Diplo and his Mad Decent label, and Nadastrom. Skrillex's 2011 track "Reptile's Theme" and the broader EDM boom brought the sound to festival audiences, and its influence fed into later pop and dancehall-inflected EDM.

Characteristics

Moombahton tracks typically run at 100–110 BPM (most often 108), combining the soaring synth leads and build-ups of Dutch house with the 3+3+2 dembow percussion pattern of reggaeton, chopped vocal samples, and heavy bass. Sub-styles include moombahcore (with dubstep-influenced bass at the same tempo), moombahsoul, and Latin-influenced variants.
