Bungakuza (Japanese: 文学座, "Literature Theatre") is a Japanese theatre company. It is one of the three major troupes of modern shingeki theatre in Japan, alongside Haiyuza and Mingei. Bungakuza stages classic and contemporary plays, including works by Japanese playwrights and translated Western drama, and is noted for its emphasis on literary quality and the playwright's text.

History

The company was founded in 1937 by a group of writers, playwrights and actors including Kunio Kishida, Mantaro Kubota and Bunroku Shishi, at a time when government pressure on left-leaning theatre was growing; its founders chose the name "Literature Theatre" in part to present the troupe as a literary rather than political enterprise. Unlike more overtly ideological companies, Bungakuza avoided political identification, which helped it survive the wartime years.

Postwar development

After World War II the company became a leading force in Japanese modern theatre, staging Shakespeare, Chekhov, Ibsen and contemporary Japanese works, and training generations of prominent actors. In 1963 a number of members left to form the Mingei-associated splinter group that became the theatre company NL T; nevertheless Bungakuza continued as a central pillar of shingeki. The company operates from Shinanomachi in Tokyo, where its Atelier has been a venue since 1950, and maintains an associated research institute for actor training.
