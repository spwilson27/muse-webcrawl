Okinoshima (Fukuoka)

Okinoshima (Japanese: 沖ノ島) is an island off the coast of Munakata, Fukuoka, Japan. It is considered a sacred island in Shinto, administered by Munakata Taisha shrine, and is famous for the large number of ritual offerings deposited there between the 4th and 9th centuries. In 2017 it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as part of the "Sacred Island of Okinoshima and Associated Sites in the Munakata Region".

Ritual sites

Archaeological excavations have uncovered some 80,000 artefacts deposited at more than twenty ritual sites on the island, including bronze mirrors, gold rings, weapons and horse trappings, many of them imports from the Korean Peninsula. These offerings are believed to have been made for the safety of ships crossing the sea routes between Japan, Korea and China, and for the protection of the nation. All of the excavated artefacts are designated National Treasures of Japan and are kept at the shrine's treasure hall on Kyushu.

Access restrictions

The island is governed by strict taboos administered by Munakata Taisha. Women are traditionally prohibited from setting foot on the island, and access for men is highly restricted: only a limited number of visitors are permitted on one day each year, after a purification ritual, and no one may remove anything from the island or speak of what they saw there. The island is normally inhabited only by a single shrine priest who rotates in service.

Geography

Okinoshima lies in the Genkai Sea, about 60 kilometres off the coast of Kyushu and midway between Kyushu and Tsushima Island. The small, steep island is covered by an ancient primeval forest that is protected as a natural monument.
