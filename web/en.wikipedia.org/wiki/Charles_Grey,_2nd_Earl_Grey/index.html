Charles Grey, 2nd Earl Grey (13 March 1764 – 17 November 1845) was a British Whig politician who served as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom from 1830 to 1834. He is best remembered for the Reform Act 1832, which reformed the electoral system of England and Wales, and for the Slavery Abolition Act 1833.

Career

Grey entered Parliament in 1786 and became a leading associate of Charles James Fox. He was First Lord of the Admiralty and then Foreign Secretary in the Ministry of All the Talents (1806–07), succeeding to the earldom in 1807 and spending over two decades in opposition in the Lords.

Premiership

Becoming prime minister in 1830, Grey pledged parliamentary reform. After the Lords rejected the Reform Bill, the "Days of May" crisis of 1832 — mass unrest and a run on gold — ended only when William IV agreed to create new peers, and the bill passed. His government also abolished slavery throughout most of the British Empire (1833) and passed the Factory Act 1833. Grey resigned in 1834 over Irish church policy. Earl Grey tea is popularly said to have been named or blended for him.
