Electronics

Electronics is the study and application of electrical circuits involving active components — such as vacuum tubes, transistors, diodes and integrated circuits — and associated passive components and interconnection technologies. It deals with circuits that process information and control energy, in contrast to classical electrical engineering's emphasis on power generation and distribution.

History

Electronics began with Edison's observation of thermionic emission and the invention of the vacuum-tube diode by Fleming (1904) and the triode by Lee De Forest (1906), enabling radio, telephony and early computers. The invention of the transistor at Bell Labs in 1947 and the integrated circuit in 1958–59 made devices progressively smaller, cheaper and more reliable, leading to the microprocessor (1971) and the modern digital age.

Branches

Electronics divides broadly into analogue electronics, concerned with continuously varying signals (amplifiers, radio receivers), and digital electronics, using discrete signal levels and logic circuits, which underlies computers and embedded systems. Power electronics handles efficient conversion and control of electrical energy. Electronic devices are assembled on printed circuit boards using components from resistors and capacitors to complex microchips designed by electronic design automation.

Impact

Electronics underpins telecommunications, computing, consumer electronics, medicine, transport and industry; the global electronics industry is among the largest sectors of the world economy.
