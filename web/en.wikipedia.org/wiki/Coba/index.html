Coba is the conventional unaccented rendering of the name of a large ruined city of the pre-Columbian Maya civilization, located in the state of Quintana Roo, Mexico, near the Caribbean coast of the Yucatán Peninsula. The site is written Cobá with an accented final vowel in Spanish orthography.

Overview

Coba was a major Maya city between about AD 600 and 900, with an estimated population of 50,000 at its peak, spread over some 80 square kilometres between several lakes. It is notable for its network of raised stone causeways (sacbeob), including one running about 100 km west to Yaxuná, and for its many carved stelae. Its tallest structure, the Nohoch Mul pyramid, rises about 42 metres. Coba's power waned after about AD 1000 as Chichén Itzá rose, though occupation continued into the Postclassic period.
