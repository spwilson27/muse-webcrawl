Lisbon Lions

The Lisbon Lions is the nickname given to the Celtic team that won the European Cup at the Estádio Nacional in Lisbon, Portugal on 25 May 1967, defeating Inter Milan 2–1. All fifteen members of the Celtic squad were born within 30 miles of Celtic Park in Glasgow, and the style with which Celtic won the cup was described as being like "lions" by the winning manager Jock Stein.

The final

Celtic went into the final as underdogs against Helenio Herrera's Inter Milan, winners of the trophy in 1964 and 1965 and masters of the defensive catenaccio system. Inter took an early lead through a Sandro Mazzola penalty after Jim Craig was adjudged to have fouled Renato Cappellini. Celtic responded with relentless attacking football, striking the crossbar through Bobby Lennox and Bertie Auld. Tommy Gemmell equalised from the penalty spot in the 62nd minute after Bobby Murdoch's shot was handled, and with seven minutes remaining Stevie Chalmers diverted a Murdoch shot past Giuliano Sarti to win the cup.

The team

The starting eleven were: Ronnie Simpson; Jim Craig, Billy McNeill (captain), John Clark, Tommy Gemmell; Bobby Murdoch, Bertie Auld; Jimmy Johnstone, Willie Wallace, Stevie Chalmers, Bobby Lennox. The substitutes and squad members included John Hughes, Charlie Gallagher and Joe McBride. As no substitutes were permitted, the eleven who started also finished the match.

Legacy

Celtic became the first British club, and the first from northern Europe, to win the European Cup. The team formed the core of the side that won nine Scottish league championships in a row. In 2017, on the fiftieth anniversary of the victory, the surviving Lions were honoured, and a stand at Celtic Park bears the name "The Lisbon Lions Stand". The achievement is widely regarded as one of the greatest in Scottish sporting history, achieved with an entirely home-grown squad playing attacking football.
