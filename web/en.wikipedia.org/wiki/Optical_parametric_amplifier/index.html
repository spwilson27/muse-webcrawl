Optical parametric amplifier

An optical parametric amplifier, abbreviated OPA, is a laser light source that emits light of variable wavelengths by an optical parametric amplification process. It is essentially the same as an optical parametric oscillator, but without the optical cavity (i.e., the light beams pass through the apparatus just once or twice, rather than many times).

Operation

In an OPA, a high-intensity pump beam and a weaker signal beam pass through a nonlinear optical crystal, such as beta barium borate (BBO) or lithium niobate. Energy is transferred from the pump to the signal, and a third beam, the idler, is generated, satisfying the energy conservation relation ωpump = ωsignal + ωidler and the phase-matching conditions. By adjusting the crystal angle or temperature, the output wavelength can be tuned over a wide range.

Types and applications

Variants include the optical parametric generator (OPG), which starts from quantum noise rather than a seed signal, and the optical parametric chirped-pulse amplifier (OPCPA), used to amplify ultrashort laser pulses to very high peak powers. OPAs are widely used in spectroscopy and ultrafast science as tunable sources of femtosecond pulses.
