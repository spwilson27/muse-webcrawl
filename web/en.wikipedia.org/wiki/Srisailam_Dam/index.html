Country:India
Location:Nandyal district, Andhra Pradesh / Nagarkurnool district, Telangana
River:Krishna River
Height:145.1 m (476 ft)

The dam across the Krishna River in the Nallamala Hills.

The Srisailam Dam is constructed across the Krishna River in Nandyal district, Andhra Pradesh, and Nagarkurnool district, Telangana, near the temple town of Srisailam, and is the second largest capacity working hydroelectric station in India. The dam was built in a deep gorge in the Nallamala Hills, as part of the Srisailam project originally conceived for power generation and irrigation.

History

Construction began in 1960 and the dam was completed in 1980, with the power station commissioned in stages thereafter. The project, initially called the Neelam Sanjeeva Reddy Sagar, supplies irrigation water to hundreds of thousands of hectares in the Rayalaseema and Telangana regions and feeds major lift and canal schemes, while its reservoir serves as the forebay for downstream projects such as the Nagarjuna Sagar Dam.

Power houses

Srisailam has two power houses on opposite banks: the right-bank station in Andhra Pradesh with seven 110 MW units, and the left-bank underground station in Telangana with six 150 MW reversible units, giving a total installed capacity of 1,670 MW. The left-bank station can also operate in pumping mode, one of the few such facilities in southern India.

2009 floods

In October 2009, unprecedented inflows from the Krishna during severe flooding in the basin raised the reservoir well above its full level, submerging the right-bank powerhouse area and the town approaches and severely testing the dam, which survived the event. The flood prompted reviews of discharge capacity and dam-safety procedures across the basin.
