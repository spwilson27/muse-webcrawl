The Matenadaran, officially the Mesrop Mashtots Institute of Ancient Manuscripts, is a museum, repository and research institute of ancient manuscripts in Yerevan, Armenia. It holds one of the world's richest collections of medieval manuscripts and books, spanning Armenian history and culture as well as works in many other languages. The word matenadaran means "repository of manuscripts" in Armenian.

History

The collection traces its origins to the scriptorium of Etchmiadzin, where manuscripts had been kept since the creation of the Armenian alphabet by Mesrop Mashtots in the 5th century AD; the earliest surviving reference to a manuscript repository in Armenia dates to that period. Over the centuries the collection survived invasions, relocations and the Armenian genocide, during which many manuscripts were lost or dispersed. In 1939 the surviving collection was moved from Etchmiadzin to Yerevan, and in 1959 it was housed in the present monumental basalt building on Mashtots Avenue, designed by architect Mark Grigorian in a style evoking medieval Armenian architecture, with statues of Mashtots and his student Koryun in front.

Collection

The Matenadaran holds around 23,000 manuscripts and manuscript fragments, in Armenian, Greek, Latin, Syriac, Hebrew, Arabic, Persian, Georgian, Russian, Amharic and other languages. Its treasures include the Lazarian Gospel (887), the Etchmiadzin Gospel (989), with its late-antique ivory binding, the huge Homiliarium of Mush (1200–1202), the largest surviving Armenian manuscript, and the small "Calendar" prayer book, the smallest. Holdings cover theology, history, philosophy, medicine, cosmography, literature and miniature painting, and include manuscripts that preserve the only surviving texts of some ancient and medieval authors. Collections grew through donations from the Armenian diaspora and acquisitions of manuscripts rescued from Western Armenia.

The manuscripts of the Matenadaran were inscribed on UNESCO's Memory of the World Register in 1997. The institute conducts research, restoration, digitisation and publication, and its exhibition halls are a major cultural attraction in Yerevan.
