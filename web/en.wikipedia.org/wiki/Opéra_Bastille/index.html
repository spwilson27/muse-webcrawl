The Opéra Bastille is a modern opera house in Paris, France, located on the Place de la Bastille in the 12th arrondissement. It is the home of the Paris Opera, sharing productions with the older Palais Garnier. Inaugurated on 13 July 1989, on the eve of the bicentenary of the storming of the Bastille, it was one of President François Mitterrand's Grands Projets.

History and design

The decision to build a large, modern, "popular" opera house was announced in 1982. An international competition was won, controversially, by the Uruguayan-Canadian architect Carlos Ott; the design features a curved glass façade and interiors in granite, oak and pear wood. The building incorporates extensive backstage facilities, workshops and rehearsal rooms, and includes the grand salle with about 2,745 seats, a smaller amphitheatre and a studio theatre.

Use

The Opéra Bastille opened with the concert La Nuit avant le jour conducted by Georges Prêtre on 13 July 1989; its first staged opera season followed in 1990. The Paris Opera now stages the bulk of its opera productions at the Bastille, with ballet concentrated at the Palais Garnier, although both companies perform in both houses. The building's architecture has received mixed critical assessments, and its façade has required major renovation work, but its size and technical facilities made it one of the world's largest opera venues.
