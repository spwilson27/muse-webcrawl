The M14 rifle, officially the United States Rifle, Caliber 7.62 mm, M14, is an American battle rifle chambered for the 7.62×51mm NATO cartridge. It became the standard-issue rifle of the U.S. military in 1959, replacing the M1 Garand and a family of other weapons.

History

The M14 was the last American battle rifle issued in quantity to be fitted with a wooden stock and to fire a full-power rifle cartridge. It served as the standard infantry rifle during the early years of the Vietnam War before being replaced by the lighter M16, which fired the intermediate 5.56×45mm cartridge.

Although its period as the primary service rifle was brief, the M14's accuracy and range kept it in use in specialised roles. Modified variants have served as designated marksman and sniper rifles, and ceremonial units continue to carry it.

Design

The M14 is a direct development of the M1 Garand, using a similar gas system and rotating bolt but feeding from a detachable 20-round box magazine and chambered for the shorter 7.62×51mm NATO round. It was select-fire, though in practice most rifles were issued with the automatic option locked out because the full-power cartridge made the light rifle difficult to control in automatic fire.

Variants and successors

Derivatives include the M21 and M25 sniper rifles and the modernised Mk 14 Enhanced Battle Rifle. Civilian semi-automatic versions, such as the Springfield Armory M1A, remain popular with target shooters.
