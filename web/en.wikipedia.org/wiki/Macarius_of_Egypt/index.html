Macarius of Egypt

Macarius of Egypt (c. 300 – 391), also known as Macarius the Great, was an Egyptian Christian monk and hermit, one of the most renowned of the Desert Fathers. He spent some sixty years in the desert of Scetis (Wadi El Natrun), first as a hermit and later as a priest and leader of the monastic community there.

Life

According to tradition, Macarius worked as a camel driver trading in natron before withdrawing to the desert, where he became a disciple of Anthony the Great. He was ordained a priest around 340 and became known for humility, gentleness toward erring brethren, and gifts of healing and discernment. During the Arian persecution under Valens he was briefly exiled to an island in the Nile. The Monastery of Saint Macarius the Great in Wadi El Natrun bears his name, and his relics are preserved there.

Writings

Fifty Spiritual Homilies were traditionally attributed to him and influenced Eastern Orthodox spirituality deeply, including the hesychast tradition; modern scholars generally attribute them to an anonymous author, "Pseudo-Macarius", likely writing in late fourth-century Syria or Asia Minor. Sayings of Macarius also appear in the Apophthegmata Patrum (Sayings of the Desert Fathers). He is venerated as a saint in the Oriental Orthodox, Eastern Orthodox, and Roman Catholic churches.
