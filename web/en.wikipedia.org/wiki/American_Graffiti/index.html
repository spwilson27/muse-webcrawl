American Graffiti

American Graffiti is a 1973 American coming-of-age comedy-drama film directed by George Lucas and produced by Francis Ford Coppola. Set over one late-summer night in Modesto, California, in 1962, it follows several recent high-school graduates as they cruise, flirt, race cars and face decisions about college, work and leaving home.

Production and style

The film interweaves multiple storylines accompanied almost continuously by period rock-and-roll and doo-wop songs, framed by disc jockey Wolfman Jack. Its cast included Richard Dreyfuss, Ron Howard, Paul Le Mat, Charles Martin Smith, Cindy Williams, Candy Clark, Mackenzie Phillips and Harrison Ford. Made cheaply, it became one of the most profitable films of its era relative to cost.

Legacy

American Graffiti helped define cinematic nostalgia for the early 1960s and influenced later ensemble coming-of-age films and television. It was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Picture, selected for preservation in the National Film Registry, and followed by the less celebrated More American Graffiti in 1979.
