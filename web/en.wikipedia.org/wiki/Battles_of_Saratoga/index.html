Battles of Saratoga

The Battles of Saratoga (19 September and 7 October 1777) were the climax of the Saratoga campaign of the American Revolutionary War, ending with the surrender of British General John Burgoyne's army at Saratoga, New York. Widely regarded as a turning point of the war, the American victory persuaded France to enter the war openly as an ally of the United States.

Background

Burgoyne's plan was to march south from Canada along the Hudson to isolate New England, but expected support from New York City largely failed to materialise, his supply lines lengthened, and raids for provisions (such as the Battle of Bennington) cost him men. Facing him, the American Northern Army under Horatio Gates, with field commanders including Benedict Arnold and Daniel Morgan's riflemen, fortified Bemis Heights, designed by Tadeusz Kościuszko.

The battles

At Freeman's Farm (19 September), Burgoyne's advance was fought to a costly standstill. On 7 October, at Bemis Heights, a British reconnaissance in force was routed — Benedict Arnold, though relieved of command, famously rode into the fight and was wounded storming the Breymann redoubt. Surrounded, short of food, and with relief from New York defeated, Burgoyne surrendered his army of about 6,000 men on 17 October under the Convention of Saratoga.

Significance

News of the surrender reached Paris in December 1777 and led directly to the Franco-American treaties of February 1778, transforming a colonial rebellion into a global war. The battlefield is preserved as Saratoga National Historical Park.
