Reform War


Date: 1858–1861
Location: Mexico
Result: Liberal victory

The Reform War (Spanish: Guerra de Reforma), also known as the Three Years' War (Guerra de los Tres Años), was a civil war in Mexico fought from 1858 to 1861 between Liberals and Conservatives over the liberal reforms of La Reforma.

Background

After the Plan of Ayutla (1854) brought liberals to power, the governments of Juan Álvarez and Ignacio Comonfort enacted the Reform Laws—the Ley Juárez abolishing special courts for clergy and military, the Ley Lerdo disentailing corporate landholdings, and the Ley Iglesias regulating parish fees. The liberal Constitution of 1857 incorporated these measures, provoking a conservative backlash backed by the Church hierarchy.

Course of the war

In December 1857 General Félix Zuloaga proclaimed the Plan of Tacubaya, repudiating the constitution. Comonfort vacillated and resigned; Benito Juárez, as Chief Justice, succeeded constitutionally and established a liberal government in Guanajuato and then Veracruz. From Veracruz Juárez issued the more radical Reform Laws of 1859, nationalising Church property, separating church and state, and instituting civil marriage and registries. The war was exceptionally bloody, marked by executions of prisoners on both sides. Liberal forces under Jesús González Ortega finally defeated the conservatives at the Battle of Calpulalpan in December 1860, and Juárez entered Mexico City in January 1861.

Aftermath

The liberal victory was short-lived in practice: a bankrupt Mexico suspended foreign debt payments, prompting the French intervention of 1862 and the Second Mexican Empire. The Reform laws, however, permanently reshaped Mexican church–state relations.
