Bede (c. 673 – 26 May 735), also known as the Venerable Bede, was an English Benedictine monk at the monastery of St Peter and St Paul at Wearmouth-Jarrow in the Kingdom of Northumbria. He is best known as the author of the Ecclesiastical History of the English People, which earned him the title "The Father of English History".

Life

Given to the monastery at the age of seven, Bede spent almost his entire life there in study, teaching and writing. He was ordained deacon at nineteen and priest at thirty. He wrote in Latin on history, chronology, grammar, biblical commentary and computus (the calculation of calendar dates, notably Easter).

Works

The Ecclesiastical History, completed about 731, tells the story of the conversion of the Anglo-Saxon kingdoms to Christianity and remains a principal source for early medieval England. In De temporum ratione (The Reckoning of Time, 725) Bede popularised dating years from the birth of Christ — the Anno Domini system of Dionysius Exiguus. His account of the deathbed singing of the poet Cædmon preserves the earliest English poem.

Legacy

Bede was declared a Doctor of the Church in 1899 and is venerated as a saint in both Catholic and Anglican traditions; his feast day is 25 May. His tomb is in Durham Cathedral.
