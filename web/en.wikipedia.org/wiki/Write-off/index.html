Write-off

A write-off is a reduction of the recognized value of something. In accounting terminology, it refers to recognition of the reduced or zero value of an asset. In income tax terminology, a write-off is a reduction in taxable income, such as a deduction for a bad debt, a donation, or a depreciation charge.

Accounting write-offs

In financial accounting, a write-off occurs when an asset previously recorded on the balance sheet can no longer be expected to generate future economic benefits, or when its recoverable amount falls below its carrying amount. Common examples include writing off uncollectible accounts receivable (bad debts), obsolete inventory, and impaired goodwill. When an asset is written off, its book value is reduced, often to zero, and the loss is recognized in the income statement for the period.

Under the allowance method, companies estimate uncollectible accounts in advance and maintain an allowance for doubtful accounts; when a specific receivable is identified as uncollectible, it is written off against the allowance. Under the direct write-off method, bad debts are expensed only when they are actually determined to be worthless, a method generally not accepted under generally accepted accounting principles for material amounts.

Tax write-offs

In taxation, "write-off" is used colloquially for any deduction that reduces taxable income. Businesses may deduct ordinary and necessary expenses, depreciation of equipment, and losses on worthless securities or bad debts. Individuals may claim deductions such as those for charitable contributions or mortgage interest, depending on the jurisdiction. A tax write-off does not mean an item is free; it means its cost reduces the income on which tax is calculated.

Vehicle write-offs

In the insurance industry, a vehicle is described as a "write-off" (or total loss) when the cost of repairing it after damage exceeds its market value, or a specified proportion of it. Insurers classify damaged vehicles into categories according to the severity of damage and whether the vehicle may legally or safely be returned to the road after repair.
