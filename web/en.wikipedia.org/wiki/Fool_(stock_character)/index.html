Fool (stock character)

TypeStock character
FormsCourt jester, rustic fool, wise fool

The fool is a stock character found in literature, drama and folklore across many cultures. Traditionally licensed to speak freely where others must be deferential, the fool combines apparent simplicity or madness with pointed wit, and often functions as the unlikely voice of truth within a story.

Types

Scholars commonly distinguish the rustic fool, a simpleton whose comedy arises from ignorance and misunderstanding (the "natural fool"), from the wise fool or court jester, a professional entertainer whose folly is a performance concealing intelligence and insight. The jester's motley costume, cap and bells, and sceptre (bauble) are the classic attributes of the role. A third figure, the holy fool, appears in religious traditions — notably the yurodivy of Russia — where eccentric or scandalous behaviour cloaks spiritual devotion.

History

Court fools were employed by rulers from ancient Egypt, Rome and China to the royal courts of medieval and Renaissance Europe. Because their words could be dismissed as nonsense, fools enjoyed a unique licence to criticise the powerful. The tradition shaped the professional clown, from commedia dell'arte figures such as the zanni through to Shakespeare's clowns.

In Shakespeare

William Shakespeare gave the fool some of his most memorable dramatic roles: Touchstone in As You Like It, Feste in Twelfth Night, and the Fool in King Lear, whose riddling commentary exposes the king's folly and whose disappearance from the play mirrors Lear's descent into madness. Robert Armin, the company's specialist clown actor, is associated with creating several of these roles.

Symbolism

In tarot, The Fool is the first (or unnumbered) trump card, depicting a carefree traveller stepping toward a cliff — symbolising beginnings, spontaneity and risk. More broadly, the fool remains a symbol of inversion: the lowly figure who turns the wisdom of the world upside down.
