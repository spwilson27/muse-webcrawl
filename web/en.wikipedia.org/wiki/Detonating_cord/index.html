Detonating cord

Detonating cord (also called detonation cord, detcord, primer cord, or Cordeau detonat) is a thin, flexible tube with a core of high explosive, usually PETN (pentaerythritol tetranitrate), sometimes RDX. When initiated by a blasting cap or booster, the core detonates along the cord's length at about 7,000 metres per second, so a cord network can fire many charges essentially simultaneously.

Construction

The explosive core is wrapped in textile or plastic yarns and given a waterproof plastic coating, often brightly coloured. Cords are manufactured with core loads from a few grams per metre up to around 100 g/m; standard blasting cord is often near 10 g/m. Low-energy variants and lead-sheathed cord (historically "Cordeau") exist for specialised work.

Uses

  • Mining, quarrying, and demolition — trunklines and downlines that initiate many borehole charges at once, and in pre-splitting.
  • Military engineering — ring mains and branch lines for demolitions, and the explosive train of line charges such as Bangalore torpedoes.
  • Ejection systems and aerospace — mild detonating cord shatters canopies (as in the Harrier and other jets), severs stages, and drives explosive bolts.
  • Oil and gas — perforating guns for well completion.

Unlike safety fuse, which burns slowly and gives a time delay, detcord propagates a detonation nearly instantaneously, so it is chosen to synchronise multiple charges; delays must be inserted as separate delay elements.
