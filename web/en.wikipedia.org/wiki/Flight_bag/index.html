Flight bag

A flight bag is a bag carried by pilots and other aircrew containing the documents and equipment needed for a flight: charts, approach plates, the aircraft flight manual, operations manuals, weather information, a logbook, calculator or E6B flight computer, headset, flashlight, and personal items. Traditionally a bulky leather or nylon satchel holding kilograms of paper publications, the flight bag has largely been replaced in modern airline operations by the electronic flight bag (EFB).

Traditional flight bag

Because aeronautical charts and manuals are revised on regular cycles, pilots were required to carry current editions, and airlines employed staff to keep manuals updated with amendment pages. A full set of company manuals, Jeppesen charts for all destinations and alternates, and performance data could weigh 15–20 kg or more. Pilots' flight bags became a familiar part of the uniformed image of the profession.

Electronic flight bag

An electronic flight bag is a portable electronic device — typically a tablet such as an iPad, or an installed cockpit display — that hosts digital charts, manuals, performance calculations, and company communications. EFBs reduce weight and fuel burn (fleet-wide savings can be substantial), simplify updates, and enable functions impossible on paper, such as own-ship position display on airport diagrams and real-time weather. Aviation regulators classify EFB hardware and software by type and require approval for operational use.
