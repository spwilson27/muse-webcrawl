One Hundred and One Dalmatians

One Hundred and One Dalmatians is a 1961 American animated comedy adventure film produced by Walt Disney Productions and based on the 1956 novel The Hundred and One Dalmatians by Dodie Smith. It was the 17th Disney animated feature film. The film tells the story of the Dalmatians Pongo and Perdita, whose fifteen puppies are stolen by the villainous Cruella de Vil, who wants to make them into fur coats; with the help of animals across England, they rescue their puppies along with 84 others—101 Dalmatians in all.

Production

The film was notable for its pioneering use of Xerox photocopying in animation, which allowed animators' drawings to be transferred directly to cels. This both cut costs at a time when the animation department's future was in doubt and gave the film its distinctive sketchy line style, well suited to Bill Peet's storyboards and the film's contemporary London setting. The voice cast included Rod Taylor as Pongo and Betty Lou Gerson as Cruella de Vil.

Reception and legacy

The film was a critical and commercial success and is regarded as one of Disney's finest animated features; Cruella de Vil is frequently ranked among the greatest animated villains. It spawned a live-action remake, 101 Dalmatians (1996) starring Glenn Close, an animated series, a direct-to-video sequel, and the live-action prequel Cruella (2021).
