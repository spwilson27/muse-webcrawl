Beach resort

A beach resort is a seaside resort town or hotel complex developed for tourism and recreation centred on a beach. Beach resorts range from modest coastal towns serving domestic holidaymakers to large, self-contained developments with hotels, marinas, golf courses and entertainment aimed at international visitors.

History

Seaside resorts first developed in eighteenth-century Britain, where sea bathing was promoted as a health treatment; Scarborough had been a spa town since the seventeenth century, and Brighton grew rapidly after the Prince Regent built the Royal Pavilion there. The coming of the railways in the nineteenth century opened resorts such as Blackpool to mass working-class tourism. On the continent, resorts grew along the French Riviera, the Italian Adriatic and the Baltic coast.

In the twentieth century, air travel and the package holiday transformed beach tourism: from the 1950s the Spanish Mediterranean coast (the Costa del Sol and Costa Blanca) and later the Balearic and Canary Islands, Greece, Turkey, the Caribbean, Florida, and Southeast Asian destinations such as Bali and Phuket built large resort sectors. Purpose-built resort regions, such as Cancún in Mexico — developed from 1970 on a government plan — were created expressly for beach tourism.

Types

Common forms include the resort town (a settlement whose economy is dominated by visitors), the resort hotel (a large hotel offering accommodation, food, pools and beach access in one property), and the all-inclusive resort, where a single price covers lodging, meals, drinks and activities. Integrated resorts add casinos, convention centres and shopping.

Impacts

Beach resorts bring employment and foreign exchange but are criticised for seasonal low-wage work, water and land consumption, coastal erosion from construction, damage to dunes and coral reefs, and cultural change in host communities. Ecotourism and sustainable tourism movements have pressed resorts to reduce environmental footprints, while sea-level rise threatens low-lying resort areas.
