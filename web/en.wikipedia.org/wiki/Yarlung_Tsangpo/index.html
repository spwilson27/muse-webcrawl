The Yarlung Tsangpo (Tibetan: ཡར་ཀླུངས་གཙང་པོ་) is the upper course of the Brahmaputra River, flowing across the Tibetan Plateau in China. It is the highest major river in the world, running at an average elevation of about 4,000 metres, and one of the longest rivers in Asia when its full course through India and Bangladesh is included.

Course

The river rises from the Angsi Glacier in western Tibet, southeast of Mount Kailash and Lake Manasarovar, and flows eastward for over 1,100 kilometres through southern Tibet, passing the Yarlung Valley — cradle of Tibetan civilisation and seat of its early kings — and south of Lhasa. Near the town of Pe it turns sharply around the peak Namcha Barwa, carving the Yarlung Tsangpo Grand Canyon, among the deepest gorges on Earth, before crossing into India, where as the Siang (Dihang) it descends to Assam and becomes the Brahmaputra.

Hydropower and significance

The great bend of the river concentrates an enormous drop in elevation and is the site of major Chinese hydropower development, including the Zangmu Dam and the much larger Motuo project approved in 2024, which have raised environmental and downstream concerns in India and Bangladesh. The river sustains agriculture in Tibet's relatively mild southern valleys and holds deep religious significance in Tibetan Buddhism and Hinduism.
