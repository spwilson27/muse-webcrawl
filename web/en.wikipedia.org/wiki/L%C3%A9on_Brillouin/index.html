Léon Brillouin

Born7 August 1889, Sèvres, France
Died4 October 1969, New York, U.S.
FieldsPhysics

Léon Nicolas Brillouin (1889–1969) was a French physicist known for his work in quantum mechanics and solid-state physics. Son of physicist Marcel Brillouin, he studied in Paris under Jean Perrin and attended lectures in Munich and Copenhagen.

Contributions

Brillouin introduced the concept of Brillouin zones, fundamental to band theory; proposed the WKB (Wentzel–Kramers–Brillouin) approximation in quantum mechanics; and predicted Brillouin scattering of light by acoustic waves. During the Second World War he moved to the United States, working on radar; he later made contributions linking information theory and thermodynamics (negentropy). He was a professor at the Collège de France and later Columbia University.
