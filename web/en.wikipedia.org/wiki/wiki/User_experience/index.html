User experience

User experience (UX) is how a user interacts with and experiences a product, system or service. It includes a person's perceptions of utility, ease of use, and efficiency. Improving user experience is important to most companies, designers, and creators when creating and refining products because negative user experience can diminish the use of the product and, therefore, any desired positive impacts; conversely, designing toward profitability often conflicts with ethical user experience objectives and can even cause harm.

User experience is a central component of human–computer interaction and overlaps with user interface design, interaction design, usability, information architecture and accessibility. The term was popularized by Don Norman while at Apple in the 1990s; Norman later co-founded the Nielsen Norman Group, which has been influential in defining UX research and practice.

Elements

UX design considers many facets: usability, accessibility, performance, design and aesthetics, utility, ergonomics, and overall human interaction with the product. Jesse James Garrett's model of the "elements of user experience" describes five planes — strategy, scope, structure, skeleton and surface — that build from abstract decisions about user needs and business objectives to concrete decisions about visual design.

Methods

Common UX methods include user research, personas, user journey mapping, wireframing, prototyping, usability testing and A/B testing. UX practitioners seek to understand users' behaviours, needs and motivations through observation techniques, task analysis and other feedback methodologies.
