Plain language

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Plain language is communication that is clear, concise, well-organized, and appropriate to its audience, so that readers can find what they need, understand it, and use it. Plain language avoids unnecessary jargon, inflated vocabulary, and convoluted sentence structure, while still being accurate and complete. It is sometimes called plain writing or, in legal contexts, plain English.

Principles

Common guidelines include: write for the audience; organize content logically with informative headings; use short sentences and everyday words; prefer the active voice and address the reader directly ("you"); use lists and tables where they help; and test documents with real users. Readability formulas are sometimes used as rough checks but are not substitutes for user testing.

Government adoption

Many governments have mandated or promoted plain language. In the United States, the Plain Writing Act of 2010 requires federal agencies to use clear communication in public documents, and the Federal Plain Language Guidelines provide standards. The Securities and Exchange Commission promoted plain English in financial disclosure from the 1990s. Similar movements exist in the United Kingdom (the Plain English Campaign, founded 1979), Australia, New Zealand, Canada, and the European Union, and an international plain language standard (ISO 24495-1) was published in 2023.

Benefits

Studies and government reports associate plain language with fewer errors, fewer calls for help, faster completion of forms, and higher public trust. In law and medicine, it supports informed consent and access to justice.
