Textbook

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A textbook is a book containing a comprehensive compilation of content in a branch of study with the intention of explaining it. Textbooks are produced to meet the needs of educators, usually at educational institutions, and are designed for use in the classroom, typically alongside lectures, exercises, and assessments. Schoolbooks are textbooks and other books used in schools.

History

Standardized instructional texts date to antiquity — Donatus's grammar in Roman education and Confucian classics in imperial China are examples. Comenius's Orbis Pictus (1658) is often considered the first illustrated textbook for children. Mass schooling in the 19th century created a large publishing market, and in the 20th century textbooks became central curricular artifacts, sometimes controversial for their content choices in history and science.

Production and adoption

In many countries and in large US states such as Texas and California, textbooks for public schools are adopted through state or district review processes, which shapes publishers' content nationally. Textbooks are often revised in multi-year edition cycles, written by teams of academics and teachers, and supplemented with workbooks, teacher editions, and digital resources.

Costs and open textbooks

College textbook prices rose much faster than general inflation in the late 20th and early 21st centuries, prompting criticism, rental and used markets, and the growth of open educational resources — openly licensed textbooks from projects such as OpenStax, which distribute free digital editions funded by philanthropy.
