Usability

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Usability refers to the quality of a user's experience when interacting with products or systems, including websites, software, devices, or applications. The international standard ISO 9241-11 defines usability as the extent to which a product can be used by specified users to achieve specified goals with effectiveness, efficiency, and satisfaction in a specified context of use.

Components

Jakob Nielsen's influential formulation decomposes usability into five quality components: learnability (how easy first-time tasks are), efficiency (speed once learned), memorability (ease of returning after time away), errors (frequency, severity, and recoverability), and satisfaction. Nielsen's ten usability heuristics are widely used for expert "heuristic evaluation" of interfaces.

Methods

  • Usability testing — observing representative users performing tasks, often thinking aloud.
  • Heuristic evaluation and cognitive walkthroughs — expert inspection methods.
  • Surveys and metrics — standardized questionnaires such as the System Usability Scale (SUS), task success rates, and time-on-task.
  • A/B testing and analytics — behavioural evidence at scale in deployed products.

Relationship to design fields

Usability is a core concern of human–computer interaction and user experience (UX) design; UX is the broader concept encompassing usefulness, desirability, accessibility, and credibility. Usability is distinct from, but related to, accessibility — designing for users with disabilities — which is required by law for many public digital services.
