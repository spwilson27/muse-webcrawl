Literacy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Literacy is the ability to read and write. In modern usage the term has been extended to include the ability to use language, numbers, images, computers, and other means to understand, communicate, and gain useful knowledge within a culture or society. UNESCO defines literacy as the ability to identify, understand, interpret, create, communicate, and compute using printed and written materials associated with varying contexts.

History

Writing emerged independently in Mesopotamia, Egypt, China, and Mesoamerica, but for most of history literacy was confined to small elites of scribes, clergy, and officials. The spread of printing in the 15th century, Protestant emphasis on Bible reading, and state-sponsored mass schooling in the 19th and 20th centuries drove literacy toward universality in industrialized countries.

Global rates

According to UNESCO estimates, the global adult literacy rate reached roughly 86–87% in the early 2020s, up from about 12% in 1820. Youth literacy is higher (around 92%), and rates are lowest in sub-Saharan Africa and parts of South Asia. A persistent gender gap remains: women account for about two-thirds of the world's illiterate adults.

Forms of literacy

  • Functional literacy — reading and writing sufficient for everyday tasks and employment.
  • Digital literacy — using computers, the internet, and digital media effectively and critically.
  • Media and information literacy — evaluating sources and media messages.
  • Health, financial, and scientific literacy — domain-specific competencies built on basic literacy.

Effects

Literacy is strongly associated with higher earnings, better health outcomes, lower fertility rates, and greater civic participation, which is why it is a core target of UN Sustainable Development Goal 4 (quality education).
