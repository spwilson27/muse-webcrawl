Domestic robot

A domestic robot is a type of service robot, an autonomous machine primarily used for household chores, but may also be used for entertainment, education or companionship. Though most domestic robots are simple, some are connected to home automation networks or act as mobile telepresence devices.

Types

The most widespread domestic robots are robotic vacuum cleaners, beginning with Electrolux's Trilobite (1996) and iRobot's Roomba (2002), followed by robotic mops, lawn mowers, pool cleaners and window cleaners. Social and companion robots — such as Sony's Aibo robot dog, SoftBank's Pepper and various elder-care robots — provide interaction rather than physical labour. Telepresence robots and smart speakers with displays blur the boundary with other home electronics.

Technology

Domestic robots navigate with bump sensors, infrared, cameras, lidar and simultaneous localisation and mapping (SLAM), returning to charging docks autonomously. Increasingly they integrate with voice assistants and smart-home platforms, raising discussion about privacy, since mapping and camera data describe the inside of users' homes.
