N,N-Dimethyltryptamine

N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (DMT or N,N-DMT) is a substituted tryptamine that occurs in many plants and animals and is a derivative and structural analogue of tryptamine. It is used as a psychedelic drug and prepared by various cultures for ritual purposes.

DMT produces a short but intense psychedelic experience when smoked or injected, and is orally active with a monoamine oxidase inhibitor, as in ayahuasca brews of Amazonian tradition. It is also produced endogenously in trace amounts in mammals.

History

First synthesised in 1931 by Richard Manske, its psychoactive effects were recognised in the 1950s through the work of Stephen Szára. It is a controlled substance in most countries.
