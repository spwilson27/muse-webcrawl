Messier 74 (also known as NGC 628 and the Phantom Galaxy) is a large spiral galaxy in the constellation Pisces, about 32 million light-years from Earth. It was discovered by Pierre Méchain in 1780 and communicated to Charles Messier, who listed it in his catalogue.

The galaxy contains two clearly defined spiral arms, making it a prototypical example of a grand design spiral galaxy. Its low surface brightness, however, makes it one of the most difficult Messier objects for amateur astronomers to observe — the source of its "phantom" nickname.

Structure and group

M74 is oriented almost perfectly face-on to Earth, providing an excellent view of spiral structure and star-forming regions traced in H-alpha emission. It is the largest member of the M74 Group, a small group of galaxies that includes the peculiar galaxy NGC 660 and several smaller irregulars. The galaxy's disk is about 95,000 light-years across, comparable to the Milky Way, and contains an estimated 100 billion stars.

Supernovae and notable features

Several supernovae have been observed in M74, including SN 2002ap (a rare hypernova/peculiar Type Ic event), SN 2003gd, and SN 2013ej. In 2022 the James Webb Space Telescope imaged M74 in the mid-infrared, revealing delicate filaments of gas and dust in its spiral arms, and Hubble and Webb images of the galaxy are frequently cited as iconic views of a face-on spiral.
