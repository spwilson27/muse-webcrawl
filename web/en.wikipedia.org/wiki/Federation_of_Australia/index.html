Federation of Australia

The Federation of Australia was the process by which the six separate British self-governing colonies of New South Wales, Victoria, Queensland, South Australia, Tasmania and Western Australia united on 1 January 1901 to form the Commonwealth of Australia, a federal nation under the Australian Constitution.

Movement

Trade barriers between colonies, defence concerns, immigration restriction and national sentiment drove federation debates from the 1880s. Henry Parkes's Tenterfield Oration (1889) spurred constitutional conventions in 1891 and 1897–98, where delegates including Edmund Barton, Alfred Deakin and Samuel Griffith drafted a constitution.

Referendums and inauguration

Colonies approved the draft in referendums in 1898–1900 (Western Australia joining late), the British Parliament enacted it in 1900, and the Commonwealth was proclaimed at Centennial Park, Sydney, on 1 January 1901. The first federal Parliament opened in Melbourne in May 1901; Canberra was later chosen as capital.

Legacy

Federation created a federal system dividing powers between Commonwealth and states, a High Court, and a constitution changeable only by referendum. It also entrenched exclusions of Indigenous Australians, addressed only partly by the 1967 referendum.
