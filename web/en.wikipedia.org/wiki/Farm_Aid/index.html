Farm Aid is an American nonprofit organisation whose mission is to keep family farmers on the land. It was founded by musicians Willie Nelson, John Mellencamp and Neil Young, who staged the first Farm Aid concert on 22 September 1985 in Champaign, Illinois, in response to the 1980s farm crisis, in which falling land values and high interest rates drove hundreds of thousands of family farms into foreclosure.

History

Nelson, performing at Live Aid earlier in 1985, remarked that he wished some of the effort would help American farmers, and set about organising a counterpart benefit. The first concert drew about 78,000 people and raised over $7 million, featuring performers including Bob Dylan, Tom Petty, Johnny Cash and Loretta Lynn. Farm Aid has since become an annual festival that moves to a different US location each year, and the organisation has raised well over $70 million in total.

Work

Beyond the festival, Farm Aid operates a farmer hotline, makes grants to rural organisations, advocates for fair farm policy in Washington, and promotes family-farm food. Its board has long included its three founders, joined by Dave Matthews in 2001 and later by Margo Price. The organisation campaigns on issues including farm bankruptcy protections, antitrust enforcement in agriculture, climate resilience and access to credit.
