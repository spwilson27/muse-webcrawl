Pontcysyllte Aqueduct

The Pontcysyllte Aqueduct (Welsh: Traphont Pontcysyllte) is a navigable aqueduct that carries the Llangollen Canal across the valley of the River Dee in north-east Wales. Completed in 1805 to the design of Thomas Telford and William Jessop, its cast-iron trough is carried 38 metres (126 ft) above the river on slender masonry piers over a length of 336 yards (307 m), making it the longest canal aqueduct in Great Britain and the highest canal aqueduct in the world. It remains in use by pleasure boats and is the centrepiece of a UNESCO World Heritage Site inscribed in 2009.

Construction

The aqueduct was built for the Ellesmere Canal, whose engineers Jessop and Telford needed to cross the Dee valley to link the coal and iron districts around Ruabon with the canal network. Earlier aqueducts had used massive masonry filled with puddled clay; Telford instead adopted a trough of flanged cast-iron plates, bolted together and sealed with Welsh flannel dipped in boiling sugar — later replaced by tarred flannel and lead — set on iron arch ribs and tall, hollow masonry piers that saved material and reduced weight. The mortar used lime mixed with ox blood, said to improve its strength. Work began in 1795 and took ten years; remarkably few deaths were recorded for so daring a project. The structure cost about £47,000.

Design and use

The trough is 11 ft 10 in (3.6 m) wide and 5 ft 3 in (1.6 m) deep; the towpath is cantilevered over the water on the eastern side, so the waterway fills nearly the full width of the trough and boats displace water back past themselves. There is a handrail on the towpath side but only a low iron lip on the other, a vertiginous arrangement that has made crossing by boat a celebrated experience. The canal beyond Trevor was never completed as a through route, but the feeder from Llangollen — which supplies water to the Shropshire Union system — kept the aqueduct in use after commercial traffic declined.

Heritage

Now managed by the Canal & River Trust, the aqueduct carries thousands of hire boats and trip boats each year and is lit at night. In June 2009 UNESCO inscribed "Pontcysyllte Aqueduct and Canal" as a World Heritage Site, covering the aqueduct and 11 miles of canal to the Horseshoe Falls at Llantysilio, recognising it as a masterpiece of civil engineering from the first Industrial Revolution.
