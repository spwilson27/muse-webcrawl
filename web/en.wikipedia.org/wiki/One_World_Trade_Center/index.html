One World Trade Center (also known as One WTC and formerly as the Freedom Tower) is the main building of the rebuilt World Trade Center complex in Lower Manhattan, New York City. It is the tallest building in the United States, the tallest in the Western Hemisphere, and among the tallest in the world. The supertall skyscraper stands on the northwest corner of the 16-acre World Trade Center site, on the site of the original 6 World Trade Center. Its total height of 1,776 feet (541.3 m) is a deliberate reference to the year of American independence, 1776.

History

The original World Trade Center's Twin Towers were destroyed in the September 11 attacks of 2001. After a lengthy and contentious planning process, a master plan by Daniel Libeskind was selected for the site. The tower's design, by David Childs of Skidmore, Owings & Merrill, evolved from an earlier Libeskind concept; a cornerstone was laid on 4 July 2004, but construction of the final design began on 27 April 2006. The building topped out on 10 May 2013, when its spire was installed, and it opened on 3 November 2014 with Condé Nast as anchor tenant. The One World Observatory, on floors 100–102, opened in May 2015.

Architecture and design

The tower rises from a square base whose edges taper as it ascends, forming eight elongated isosceles triangular facets; at mid-height the plan is a regular octagon, and it resolves into a square at the top, rotated 45 degrees from the base. The glass curtain wall is clad in prismatic glass panels. The building contains about 3.5 million square feet of space, mostly offices, and was built with extensive safety features exceeding New York building codes: a massive reinforced concrete core, protected and pressurised stairwells, and redundant structural systems. It achieved LEED Gold certification, incorporating rainwater recycling and energy-efficient systems.

Memorial context

One WTC stands immediately northwest of the National September 11 Memorial & Museum, whose reflecting pools occupy the footprints of the original Twin Towers.
