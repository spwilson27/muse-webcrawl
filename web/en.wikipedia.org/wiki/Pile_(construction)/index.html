Pile (construction)

A pile is a vertical structural element of a deep foundation, driven or drilled deep into the ground at a building site. Piles transfer the loads of a structure to deeper, stronger soil or rock layers when the soil near the surface is too weak or compressible to support the structure on a shallow foundation.

Function

Piles carry load in two principal ways:

  • End bearing — the pile acts like a column, resting on a firm stratum of rock or dense soil at depth.
  • Skin friction — the pile transfers load to the surrounding soil through friction along its shaft; such piles are called friction piles.

Most piles combine both mechanisms. Groups of piles are usually connected at the top by a pile cap, which supports columns or walls and distributes the load among the piles.

Types and materials

Piles may be made of timber, steel (H-sections or tubes), precast or cast-in-place reinforced concrete, or composite materials. Installation methods include impact driving with a pile driver, vibration, augering (bored piles), and jetting. Micropiles are small-diameter drilled piles used for underpinning and in restricted-access sites.

Uses

Pile foundations are used for bridges, high-rise buildings, offshore structures, and buildings on reclaimed or waterside land. Venice, for example, rests on millions of timber piles driven into the lagoon bed. Piles are also used as retaining elements in sheet-pile walls and to resist uplift and lateral loads.

Testing

Installed piles are verified by static load tests, dynamic (pile-driving analyzer) tests, and integrity tests that detect defects along the shaft.
