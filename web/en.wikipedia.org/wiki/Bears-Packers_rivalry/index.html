The Bears–Packers rivalry is a National Football League (NFL) rivalry between the Chicago Bears and the Green Bay Packers. It is the oldest rivalry in the NFL, dating to 1921, when the Bears were known as the Chicago Staleys. The two teams have met more than 200 times, making it also the league's most-played rivalry.

History

The rivalry began in 1921, the Packers' first season in the league. It intensified under legendary coaches George Halas of Chicago and Curly Lambeau and later Vince Lombardi of Green Bay. Memorable episodes include the 1985 Bears' championship season, the "Instant Replay Game" of 1989, and the 2010 NFC Championship Game, in which Green Bay defeated Chicago at Soldier Field on its way to winning Super Bowl XLV.

Significance

The two franchises are among the most successful in NFL history, having combined for numerous league championships. The proximity of Chicago and Green Bay — separated by roughly 200 miles along Lake Michigan — and the deep civic identification with each team have sustained the rivalry for over a century. Both teams compete in the NFC North division, guaranteeing two meetings per regular season.
