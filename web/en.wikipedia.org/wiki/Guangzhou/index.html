Guangzhou

Guangzhou (Chinese: 广州; Cantonese: Gwóngjàu), historically known as Canton, is the capital of Guangdong province in southern China, a megacity on the Pearl River about 120 km northwest of Hong Kong, with a population of about 19 million in the city and over 65 million in the Pearl River Delta metropolitan area.

History

Founded as Panyu over 2,200 years ago, Guangzhou was a Silk Road seaport and, under the Qing Canton System (1757–1842), the sole Chinese port for Western trade — the era of the Thirteen Factories. It was a centre of the First Opium War, Sun Yat-sen's revolutionary governments, and post-1978 economic reform.

Economy and culture

The Canton Fair (since 1957) is China's largest trade fair. Guangzhou is a manufacturing, logistics and tech hub, home of Cantonese language, dim sum cuisine, Canton Tower (600 m), and the 2010 Asian Games. See Guangdong and Pearl River Delta.
