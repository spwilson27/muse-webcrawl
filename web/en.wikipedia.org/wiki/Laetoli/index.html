Laetoli

Laetoli
Location: Ngorongoro Conservation Area, Tanzania
Age of footprints: c. 3.66 million years
Discovered by: Mary Leakey's team (1976–1978)

Laetoli is a palaeoanthropological site in northern Tanzania, within the Ngorongoro Conservation Area, famous for fossilised hominin footprints preserved in volcanic ash about 3.66 million years old.

Footprints

In 1976–78, Mary Leakey's expedition excavated a 27-metre trail of footprints made by three individuals — likely Australopithecus afarensis — walking bipedally across wet ash from the Sadiman volcano, which then hardened and was buried. The prints show a fully upright, humanlike gait with an arched foot and non-divergent big toe, proving bipedalism long predated large brains or stone tools.

Other finds

Laetoli also yielded jaws and teeth used to define A. afarensis (the type specimen LH 4) and abundant animal trackways. Additional footprint sites reported in 2016 suggest notable size variation among individuals. See Mary Leakey and Lucy.
