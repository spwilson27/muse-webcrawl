Silverstone Circuit

Location: Silverstone, Northamptonshire/Buckinghamshire, England
Length: 5.891 km (3.660 mi)
Turns: 18
Opened: 1948

The Silverstone Circuit is a motor racing circuit in England, near the Northamptonshire villages of Towcester, Silverstone, and Whittlebury (the circuit straddles the Northamptonshire and Buckinghamshire border). It is the home of the British Grand Prix, which it first hosted as the 1948 British Grand Prix held in the circuit's first year of operation. On 13 May 1950, Silverstone hosted the first-ever Formula One World Championship race, the 1950 British Grand Prix, won by Giuseppe Farina.

History

The circuit was built on the site of RAF Silverstone, a Royal Air Force bomber station constructed during the Second World War, whose three runways lay within the present track. Racing began on the perimeter roads and runways in 1948. Over the decades the layout was repeatedly revised, notably in 1991, 2010 (the "Arena" layout extending the lap to 5.891 km), and with the construction of the Silverstone Wing pit and paddock complex, opened in 2011.

Famous corners include Copse, the Maggotts–Becketts–Chapel sequence, Stowe, Club, and Abbey. The circuit is owned by the British Racing Drivers' Club (BRDC).

Events

Besides the Formula One British Grand Prix, Silverstone hosts the British round of MotoGP, the World Endurance Championship (historically), British Touring Cars, and numerous club and historic events, including the Silverstone Festival (formerly Silverstone Classic).
