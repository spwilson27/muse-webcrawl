Ko-fi is a British crowdfunding and creator-support platform that allows creators, artists, writers, streamers, and developers to receive donations, memberships, and commissions from their audience. The name is a play on "coffee": supporters are invited to "buy a coffee" for a creator, typically a small payment of a few dollars.

History and model

Ko-fi was founded in 2012 in the United Kingdom. It grew popular among online artists and fan creators as a lightweight alternative to subscription platforms: a creator creates a page, links it from social media, and receives payments via PayPal or Stripe. Distinctively, Ko-fi charges no platform fee on one-off donations, taking its revenue instead from optional "Ko-fi Gold" subscriptions and fees on shop and commission features. The platform later added recurring membership tiers, a shop for digital and physical goods, and commission listings. It is frequently compared with Patreon and Buy Me a Coffee, and it expanded substantially during the growth of the online creator economy in the late 2010s and early 2020s.

Features

Creators can set fundraising goals, post supporter-only content, stream alerts for donations received while live-streaming, and integrate Ko-fi buttons and widgets into their own websites. The service emphasises direct payment — funds from donations go directly to the creator's own payment account rather than being held by the platform.
