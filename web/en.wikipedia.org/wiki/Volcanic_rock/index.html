Volcanic rock

Volcanic rock (often shortened to volcanics in scientific contexts) is a rock formed from lava erupted from a volcano. In other words, it is an extrusive igneous rock, distinguished from intrusive (plutonic) rocks by its origin above the surface and its resulting fine-grained or glassy texture, caused by rapid cooling that leaves little time for crystal growth.

Basalt (left) and pumice (right), two common volcanic rocks.

Types

Volcanic rocks are classified by chemical composition and texture. Basalt, the most common volcanic rock, is mafic and forms most oceanic crust. Andesite typifies subduction-zone volcanoes, rhyolite is the volcanic equivalent of granite, and obsidian is a natural volcanic glass. Pumice, riddled with gas bubbles, can float on water.

Distribution

Volcanic rocks cover large continental provinces — the Deccan Traps of India and the Columbia River Basalts of North America among them — emplaced by flood-basalt eruptions that are among the largest volcanic events in Earth's history.
