Helsingør (Danish pronunciation: [helseŋˈøɐ̯ˀ]; Swedish: Helsingör), classically known in English as Elsinore, is a city in eastern Denmark. It is situated on the north-eastern coast of the island of Zealand at the narrowest part of the Øresund, opposite the Swedish city of Helsingborg, with which it is connected by frequent ferry services.

History

Helsingør grew wealthy in the Middle Ages from the Sound Dues, tolls levied on ships passing through the Øresund between 1429 and 1857. King Eric of Pomerania built the castle of Krogen, later rebuilt as Kronborg, to enforce the toll. Kronborg is famous as the setting of William Shakespeare's play Hamlet.

Sights

Besides Kronborg Castle, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, the city is home to St Olaf's Church, St Mary's Church and the Carmelite Priory, the M/S Maritime Museum of Denmark (opened in 2013 in a former dry dock), and the Culture Yard (Kulturværftet), a cultural centre in the former shipyard.
