The Sorcerer's Apprentice (French: L'apprenti sorcier) is a symphonic poem by the French composer Paul Dukas, completed in 1897. Subtitled "Scherzo after a ballad by Goethe", the piece is based on Johann Wolfgang von Goethe's 1797 poem Der Zauberlehrling.

Music and story

The music tells the story of an apprentice who, left alone in his master's workshop, enchants a broom to do his chores for him. The broom fetches water uncontrollably; the apprentice, unable to stop it, splits it in two with an axe, whereupon each half becomes a whole broom and the workshop floods. Only the return of the sorcerer restores order. Dukas's score is celebrated for its brilliant orchestration, its memorable bassoon theme for the broom, and its depiction of the mounting aquatic chaos.

In popular culture

The piece achieved worldwide fame through Walt Disney's 1940 animated film Fantasia, in which the sequence was played by Mickey Mouse as the apprentice. The music was also used in the Fantasia 2000 sequel and has become Dukas's best-known work, overshadowing the rest of his small but distinguished output.
