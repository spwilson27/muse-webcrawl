ClassPolychaeta

Polychaetes are a class (Polychaeta) of annelid worms, generally marine, commonly known as bristle worms. Each body segment bears a pair of fleshy lobes called parapodia carrying many bristles, or chaetae, from which the class takes its name. More than 10,000 species have been described.

A polychaete worm showing the bristle-bearing parapodia.

Description

Polychaetes range in size from microscopic planktonic forms to the three-metre bobbit worm. The head is well developed in most species, with eyes, antennae, and sensory palps. Many are active predators with eversible jaws; others graze, scavenge, or feed on deposits. Sedentary tube-dwelling forms, such as fan worms (Sabellida), extend feathery radioles to filter food from the water.

Lifestyles

Traditionally the class was split into the mobile "Errantia" and the sedentary "Sedentaria." Polychaetes inhabit virtually every marine environment, from intertidal flats to hydrothermal vents, where the Pompeii worm tolerates extreme temperatures. A small number live in fresh water or on land.

Reproduction

Many polychaetes reproduce through epitoky, transforming into a swimming reproductive stage that swarms at the surface to spawn — spectacularly in the palolo worms of the South Pacific, harvested as a seasonal delicacy. Fertilisation is usually external, producing a trochophore larva.
