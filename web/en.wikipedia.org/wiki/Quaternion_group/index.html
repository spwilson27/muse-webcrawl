Quaternion group

In group theory, the quaternion group Q8 (sometimes just denoted by Q) is a non-abelian group of order eight, isomorphic to the eight-element subset {1, i, j, k, −1, −i, −j, −k} of the quaternions under multiplication. It is given by the group presentation

Q8 = ⟨−1, i, j, k | (−1)2 = 1, i2 = j2 = k2 = ijk = −1⟩.

Cayley graph of Q8

Properties

The quaternion group has the unusual property of being Hamiltonian: every subgroup is a normal subgroup, yet the group is non-abelian. Every Hamiltonian group contains a copy of Q8. It has five irreducible representations over the complex numbers and a center of order two, {1, −1}. The quotient Q8/{1, −1} is isomorphic to the Klein four-group.

Compared with the dihedral group of order eight, D4, the quaternion group has only one element of order two (−1), whereas D4 has five. Q8 can be realized as a subgroup of the general linear group GL(2, C), and as a group of 2×2 complex matrices.

Generalized quaternion group

Q8 is the smallest member of the family of generalized quaternion groups Q4n, which are dicyclic groups of order a power of two. These groups arise naturally in the classification of groups with cyclic Sylow subgroups.
