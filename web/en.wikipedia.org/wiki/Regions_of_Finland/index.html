Regions of Finland

Number: 19 regions (maakunta)
Largest by population: Uusimaa
Special status: Åland (autonomous)
Governed by: Regional councils

Finland is divided into 19 regions (Finnish: maakunta; Swedish: landskap), the principal tier of regional administration in the country. Each region is governed by a regional council, which is a joint municipal authority formed by its member municipalities, and is responsible for regional planning, development and coordination of central government functions.

List and roles

The regions include Lapland, North Ostrobothnia, Kainuu, North Karelia, North Savo, South Savo, South Karelia, Central Finland, South Ostrobothnia, Ostrobothnia, Central Ostrobothnia, Pirkanmaa, Satakunta, Southwest Finland, Kanta-Häme, Päijät-Häme, Kymenlaakso, Uusimaa and Åland. Uusimaa, containing the capital Helsinki, is by far the most populous, while Åland is an autonomous, Swedish-speaking archipelago province with its own parliament.

History

The modern regions were established in the 1990s, partly following historical provinces whose roots lie in the period of Swedish rule. A major reform in 2023 transferred responsibility for health and social services from municipalities to new wellbeing services counties, whose boundaries largely follow the regions.
