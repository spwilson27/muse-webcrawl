Krzysztof Kieślowski (1941–1996) was a Polish film director and screenwriter, widely regarded as one of the most influential European filmmakers of his generation.

Career in Poland

Kieślowski began with documentaries and features reflecting everyday life under communism, including Camera Buff (1979). His television series Dekalog (1988), ten films loosely based on the Ten Commandments, brought him international acclaim; two episodes were expanded into A Short Film About Killing and A Short Film About Love.

International films

Working in France with co-writer Krzysztof Piesiewicz, he made The Double Life of Véronique (1991) and the Three Colours trilogy — Blue (1993), White (1994) and Red (1994), the last nominated for Academy Awards including Best Director. He died in 1996 at the age of 54.
