Allegany Indian Reservation

The Allegany Indian Reservation (Seneca: Ohí:yo') is a territory of the federally recognized Seneca Nation of Indians, located in western New York State, United States. The reservation lies primarily in Cattaraugus County, along the Allegheny River, with a small portion in Erie County. The city of Salamanca, which lies within the reservation, is notable as a U.S. city located almost entirely on Native American land under lease.

Geography

The reservation covers approximately 30,469 acres (about 123 km²). It runs along the Allegheny River and the Allegheny Reservoir, formed by the Kinzua Dam (completed 1965), whose construction controversially flooded Seneca land — including the Cornplanter Tract in Pennsylvania — despite guarantees in the 1794 Treaty of Canandaigua. The reservoir occupies a significant part of the reservation's riverfront.

History

The reservation was established under the Treaty of Big Tree in 1797, in which the Seneca ceded most of their lands in western New York while reserving several parcels, including Allegany. The Seneca Nation adopted a constitutional government in 1848, with its capital rotating between Allegany and Cattaraugus; today the administrative capital is at Jimersontown, a community founded in the 1960s after the Kinzua flooding displaced families from older settlements.

Communities and economy

Communities include Jimersontown, Salamanca (under lease agreements with the Seneca Nation), and the villages near Steamburg and Kill Buck. The Seneca Allegany Casino in Salamanca is a major economic enterprise of the Nation. According to recent census figures, the population of the reservation is roughly 1,000 people, although many tribal members live nearby off-reservation.
