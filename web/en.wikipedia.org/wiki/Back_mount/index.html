Back mount

Back mount is a scuba diving equipment configuration in which the breathing gas cylinders are carried on the diver's back, as opposed to sidemount, in which cylinders are clipped alongside the torso. It is the traditional configuration taught to recreational divers worldwide and the standard arrangement for technical diving with twinsets and stage cylinders.

Configuration

In a typical back-mount setup, one or two cylinders are attached to a buoyancy compensator or to a metal backplate with a harness, and the first-stage regulators sit behind the diver's head between the cylinder valves. A long-hose primary regulator is commonly donated to an out-of-gas diver, a convention popularised by technical and cave diving agencies.

Advantages and disadvantages

Back mount gives a streamlined, stable profile and leaves the chest uncluttered for cameras, reels and other equipment. Its main disadvantages are that the valves and first stages are hard to reach and inspect underwater, gas shutdowns require reaching behind the head, and the cylinders are awkward to handle on shore or in small boats. Sidemount was developed in part to address these issues in cave and wreck penetration diving.
