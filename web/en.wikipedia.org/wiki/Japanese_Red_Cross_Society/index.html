The Japanese Red Cross Society (日本赤十字社, Nippon Sekijūjisha) is the national Red Cross society of Japan, a member of the International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement. Founded in 1877, it is one of the largest national societies in the world, operating hospitals, blood transfusion services, nursing colleges and nationwide disaster-relief networks.

History

The society began as the Hakuaisha ("Philanthropic Society"), founded by Count Tsunetami Sano and others during the Satsuma Rebellion of 1877 to care for wounded soldiers of both sides, inspired by the Geneva Convention — which Japan joined in 1886. In 1887 the organisation was renamed the Japanese Red Cross Society. With the patronage of the Imperial family, it grew rapidly, providing nurses in the First Sino-Japanese War, the Russo-Japanese War and both World Wars. Empress Shōken was a notable benefactor; the Empress Shōken Fund, established in 1912, still supports Red Cross work worldwide.

Activities

The JRCS runs about 90 hospitals and clinics across Japan, coordinates the national blood donation and transfusion programme, and trains nurses at its universities and colleges. In disasters — earthquakes, typhoons and the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami — it deploys medical and relief teams and distributes donations on a large scale. Internationally it supports sister societies with funding, personnel and disaster response, particularly in the Asia-Pacific region.

Organisation

The society is headquartered in Shiba, Minato Ward, Tokyo, with chapters in all 47 prefectures and thousands of local volunteer corps. The Emperor has traditionally served as honorary patron and the Crown Princess/Empress as honorary president; regular presidents are drawn from senior public life. Its legal status is that of a special corporation authorised under Japanese law, acting as an auxiliary to the public authorities in the humanitarian field.
