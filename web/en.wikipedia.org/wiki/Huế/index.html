Huế is a city in central Vietnam and the former imperial capital of the country under the Nguyễn dynasty (1802–1945). Situated on the banks of the Perfume River a few kilometres from the South China Sea, it is renowned for its citadel, royal tombs, and pagodas, recognized by UNESCO as the Complex of Huế Monuments World Heritage Site. Effective 1 January 2025, the former Thừa Thiên Huế province was reconstituted as the centrally governed municipality of Huế, making it Vietnam's sixth municipality.

History

The area was the southern frontier of Đại Việt before the Nguyễn lords made Phú Xuân (Huế) their seat in the 17th century. Emperor Gia Long, founder of the Nguyễn dynasty, chose it as his capital in 1802 and built the great Citadel modelled on Beijing's Forbidden City. Huế remained the seat of the dynasty—though under French protectorate from the 1880s—until Bảo Đại's abdication in 1945.

Vietnam War

During the 1968 Tet Offensive, communist forces seized much of the city, and the month-long Battle of Huế became one of the longest and bloodiest engagements of the war. Much of the Citadel was destroyed in the fighting, and the battle was accompanied by the massacre of civilians by retreating communist forces, an event still politically sensitive in Vietnam.

Attractions

The Imperial City within the Citadel, the royal tombs of emperors Tự Đức, Minh Mạng, and Khải Định along the Perfume River, and the seven-storey Thien Mu Pagoda are the city's principal monuments. Huế is also known for its court cuisine, its royal court music (nhã nhạc, recognized by UNESCO as intangible heritage), and the biennial Huế Festival.
