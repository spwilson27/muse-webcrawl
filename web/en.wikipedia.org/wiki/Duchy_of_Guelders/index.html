Duchy of Guelders

The Duchy of Guelders (Dutch: Hertogdom Gelre; German: Herzogtum Geldern) was a historical duchy of the Holy Roman Empire, straddling the lower Rhine, Meuse, and IJssel rivers in what is now the Dutch province of Gelderland and part of the German district of Kleve. Named after the town of Geldern, it grew from an 11th-century county into one of the most powerful principalities of the Low Countries.

History

The counts of Guelders expanded from Wassenberg holdings, acquiring Zutphen and the Veluwe and the loyalty of cities such as Nijmegen, Arnhem, Roermond, and Zutphen. Guelders was raised to a duchy in 1339 under Reginald II. The duchy long resisted the expansion of Burgundy and then the Habsburgs; under Charles of Egmond (duke 1492–1538) it waged decades of war against Habsburg Netherlands with French support, its armies led by the notorious commander Maarten van Rossum.

By the Treaty of Venlo (1543), after the Guelders Wars, William of Jülich-Cleves-Berg surrendered Guelders and Zutphen to Emperor Charles V, and the duchy became one of the Seventeen Provinces of the Habsburg Netherlands. In the Dutch Revolt the duchy split: the northern quarters joined the Dutch Republic (forming the modern province of Gelderland), while the Overkwartier around Roermond remained Spanish and later Austrian, and the original town of Geldern passed eventually to Prussia.
