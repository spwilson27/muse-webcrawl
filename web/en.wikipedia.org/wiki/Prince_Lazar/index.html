Lazar Hrebeljanović (c. 1329 – 15 June 1389), known as Prince Lazar and in Serbian tradition as Tsar Lazar, was a medieval Serbian ruler who created the largest and most powerful state on the territory of the disintegrated Serbian Empire. He died leading the Serbian army at the Battle of Kosovo against the Ottoman Empire in 1389, and is venerated as a saint and martyr by the Serbian Orthodox Church.

Rise to power

Lazar served as a courtier (stavilac) at the court of Emperor Stefan Dušan and his son Stefan Uroš V. After Uroš's death in 1371 ended the Nemanjić dynasty, Lazar emerged from his base at Prilepac and Kruševac as the most powerful of the regional lords, styling himself "Lord of the Serbs and Podunavlje". He secured legitimacy by marrying Milica, a descendant of the Nemanjić line, and by close alliance with the Serbian Patriarchate, with which he healed the schism with Constantinople in 1375.

Rule

Moravian Serbia, as his state is known, was a prosperous land of mines, trade and church building; Lazar founded the monasteries of Ravanica, where he was buried, and Ljubostinja, his wife's foundation. He fought Ottoman raiders at Dubravnica (1381) and Pločnik (1386) before confronting Sultan Murad I in the great battle on Kosovo Field on Vidovdan, 15 June 1389. Both rulers were killed; Serbian epic tradition, in the Kosovo cycle of poems, remembers Lazar choosing a "heavenly kingdom" over an earthly one — the foundation of the Kosovo myth.

Legacy

Lazar was canonised soon after his death; his relics rest at Ravanica Monastery. He is commemorated on Vidovdan (28 June, Gregorian), a central date in Serbian national memory.
