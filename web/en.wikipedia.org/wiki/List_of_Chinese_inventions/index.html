List of Chinese inventions

China has been the source of many significant inventions. The so-called Four Great Inventions — the compass, gunpowder, papermaking and printing — are celebrated in Chinese culture for their contribution to civilisation worldwide. This article lists inventions originating in China across its long history.

The Four Great Inventions

Papermaking: Cai Lun is traditionally credited with improving the paper-making process in 105 AD, using bark, hemp, rags and fishnets. Printing: woodblock printing was developed by the Tang dynasty (the Diamond Sutra of 868 AD is the earliest dated printed book), and Bi Sheng invented movable type using earthenware pieces around 1040 AD during the Song dynasty. Gunpowder: Taoist alchemists seeking an elixir of life discovered the explosive mixture by the 9th century; it was used in fire lances, bombs and later guns. Compass: lodestone compasses were used for geomancy from the Han dynasty, and by the Song dynasty Shen Kuo described the magnetic needle compass used in navigation.

Other notable inventions

China also gave the world the seismometer (Zhang Heng, 132 AD), cast iron production, the blast furnace, porcelain, silk weaving, tea cultivation, acupuncture, the umbrella, matches, the toothbrush with bristles, paper money (Song dynasty), the canal pound lock, chain pumps, the wheelbarrow, stirrups in their earliest known form, crossbow technology, kites, parachutes in early form, deep drilling for brine and natural gas, the cardan suspension, negative numbers in mathematics, the decimal system in use, inoculation against smallpox (variolation), fireworks and rockets, noodles and tofu, the stern-post rudder, watertight bulkhead compartments in ships, and the folding fan.

Modern inventions

In the modern era, Chinese researchers contributed artemisinin (Tu Youyou, Nobel Prize 2015) for malaria treatment, hybrid rice cultivation (Yuan Longping), and the synthesis of bovine insulin (1965).
