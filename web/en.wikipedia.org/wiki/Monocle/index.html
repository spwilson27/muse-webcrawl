Monocle

A monocle is a type of corrective lens used to correct or enhance the vision in only one eye. It consists of a circular lens, generally with a wire ring around the circumference that can be attached to a string or wire, and sits in the eye socket held in place by the pressure of the orbicularis oculi muscle and the brow ridge.

History

The monocle first came into fashion in the early 19th century, evolving from earlier single-lens reading aids such as the quizzing glass. It reached the height of its popularity in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, when it was strongly associated with wealthy and military men of the period — particularly German and British officers. Famous wearers in popular culture include the fictional Colonel Klink, Mr. Peanut, and the Penguin from Batman comics.

Styles

Three styles are generally recognised. The first consists of a simple metal ring with a handle, held up to the eye. The second, the most common, is held in the eye socket itself and often fitted with a gallery — a raised rim to keep the lens away from the eyelashes. The third style, popularised by manufacturers in the early 20th century, was custom-fitted by opticians for maximum comfort and security.

Decline

The monocle declined after the First and Second World Wars as spectacles became cheaper and more socially acceptable, and the monocle's association with the German officer class fell out of favour. It survives today largely as a fashion or novelty item, and in formal wear for a small number of enthusiasts.
