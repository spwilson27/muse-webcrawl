Danish phonology

The phonology of Danish is similar in many ways to that of the other Scandinavian languages, but it also has several features that set it apart, including a rich vowel inventory, the phenomenon known as stød, and a strong tendency towards the lenition and vocalisation of consonants.

Vowels

Danish has one of the largest vowel inventories of any European language. Depending on the analysis, the language distinguishes around 20 or more vowel phonemes in stressed syllables, including contrasts of vowel quality, length, and the presence or absence of stød. Front vowels may be rounded or unrounded, and Danish makes extensive use of vowel lowering before /r/.

Stød

The stød is a prosodic feature, often described as a creaky-voiced or glottal constriction, that distinguishes word pairs such as hun "she" (without stød) and hund "dog" (with stød). It occurs only in stressed syllables with a particular syllable structure and is historically related to the pitch accents of Norwegian and Swedish.

Consonants

Danish consonants are notable for the lenition of the plosives /p t k/ and /b d ɡ/ in post-vocalic position; post-vocalic /d/ is realised as a soft approximant [ð̞ˠ], the famous "soft d". The uvular or velar approximant realisation of /r/ also colours adjacent vowels.
