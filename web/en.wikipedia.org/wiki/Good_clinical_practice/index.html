Good clinical practice

Good clinical practice (GCP) is an international quality standard for designing, conducting, recording, and reporting trials that involve the participation of human subjects. Compliance with GCP assures that the rights, safety, and well-being of trial subjects are protected and that the clinical trial data are credible. The most widely adopted version is the guideline ICH E6 of the International Council for Harmonisation of Technical Requirements for Pharmaceuticals for Human Use.

Principles

The ICH GCP guideline sets out principles including: trials must conform to the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki; foreseeable risks must be weighed against anticipated benefits; the rights and safety of subjects prevail over science and society; trials must follow a scientifically sound, ethics-committee-approved protocol; informed consent must be freely given; and trial information must be recorded and stored so as to allow accurate reporting, interpretation, and verification.

Roles and oversight

GCP defines responsibilities of sponsors, investigators, monitors, and institutional review boards / independent ethics committees. Regulators such as the U.S. Food and Drug Administration and the European Medicines Agency require GCP compliance for trial data submitted in support of marketing approvals, and inspect trial sites for adherence.
