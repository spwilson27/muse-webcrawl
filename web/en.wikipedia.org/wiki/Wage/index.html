Wage

[Image: a worker receiving a payslip at the end of the week]

A wage is the payment made by an employer to an employee in exchange for their labour, usually calculated per hour, week or piece, as distinct from a salary (fixed per period regardless of hours) and from commission. Wages are the main income source for most households and the main cost for most firms — the Price formed in labour markets and studied in Labour economics.

Determination

Neoclassical theory ties wages to the marginal productivity of labour; real-world wages also reflect bargaining power, unions, minimum-wage law, efficiency-wage considerations, firm-specific rents and discrimination. Concepts include the real wage (adjusted for Inflation), the wage share of national income, wage rigidity — a reason unemployment persists in macroeconomic models — and international wage differences driving both trade in labour-intensive goods and migration. Wage growth lagging productivity growth since the 1970s in the US and elsewhere ('the decoupling') is a central fact in debates about inequality.
