Canadian Film Centre

The Canadian Film Centre (CFC) is a charitable cultural organization in Toronto, Ontario, that trains and supports Canadian talent in film, television, and digital media. Founded in 1988 by filmmaker Norman Jewison, it is one of Canada's leading advanced training institutions for screen-based industries.

History

Norman Jewison established the centre in 1988 after concluding that Canada lacked a graduate-level institution comparable to the American Film Institute or Britain's National Film and Television School. The centre is housed at Windfields, the former estate of businessman E. P. Taylor in Toronto's North York district, centred on a Georgian Revival mansion built in the 1930s.

The grounds include a restored windmill and stables that have been adapted as production and teaching facilities. In 2013 the Northern Dancer Pavilion, a new building for classrooms and events, opened on the site.

Programs

The CFC offers residency-style programs for producers, directors, writers, actors, editors, and composers. Its flagship offerings have included the Cineplex Entertainment Film Program, the CBC Actors Conservatory, and programs in television writing and producing. The CFC Media Lab, founded in the 1990s, supports creators working in interactive and digital media, and the centre has run initiatives for documentary and short-form creators.

Productions developed at the centre include short films showcased at festivals and feature projects developed through its feature-film and television streams.

Alumni and influence

Alumni of the centre include filmmakers and showrunners who have gone on to prominent careers in Canadian and international screen industries. The centre works in partnership with broadcasters, studios, and funding agencies, and hosts industry events including its annual barbecue fundraiser held during the Toronto International Film Festival.
