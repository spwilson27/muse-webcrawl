John Winthrop (January 12, 1588 – March 26, 1649) was an English Puritan lawyer and one of the leading founders of the Massachusetts Bay Colony, the first major English settlement in New England. He led the largest group of Puritan migrants in the "Great Migration" of 1630 and served as the colony's governor for twelve of its first twenty years. His sermon or lay exhortation "A Model of Christian Charity" contains the famous phrase "a city upon a hill", central to later ideas of American exceptionalism.

Early life

Winthrop was born at Edwardstone, Suffolk, son of Adam Winthrop, lord of the manor of Groton. He studied briefly at Trinity College, Cambridge, practised law in London, inherited Groton Manor in 1617, and lost a court post in 1629 amid Charles I's campaign against Puritan officeholders. With other Puritan gentlemen he took up the Massachusetts Bay Company's charter — uniquely carried to America with the settlers — and was elected governor in October 1629.

Migration and governorship

Winthrop sailed in the flagship Arbella in 1630, at the head of a fleet of eleven ships carrying about 700 settlers — the first wave of some 20,000 migrants of the Great Migration. Tradition holds that he delivered "A Model of Christian Charity" during the voyage or shortly before departure, urging the colonists to be "as a city upon a hill" because "the eyes of all people are upon us". He founded Boston and governed — sometimes as governor, sometimes as deputy or assistant, in a politics that alternated him with Thomas Dudley and John Haynes — until his death.

Winthrop's rule balanced his vision of a godly commonwealth against pressures for broader consent: he resisted demands for a written legal code before reluctantly accepting the Massachusetts Body of Liberties (1641), presided over the banishments of Roger Williams and Anne Hutchinson during the Antinomian Controversy, and chaired the New England Confederation of 1643. His Journal, kept from 1630 to 1649, is the principal narrative source for the colony's first two decades.

Death and legacy

Winthrop died in Boston in March 1649. His phrase "city upon a hill" was revived by John F. Kennedy in 1961 and by Ronald Reagan, and remains a touchstone of American political rhetoric. His son John Winthrop the Younger governed Connecticut Colony, and his descendants include the Winthrops of Massachusetts, among them Secretary of War Robert Charles Winthrop.
