The president of the Philippines (Filipino: Pangulo ng Pilipinas) is the head of state, head of government, and chief executive of the Philippines, and the commander-in-chief of the Armed Forces of the Philippines.

Election and term

The president is elected by direct popular vote for a single six-year term and may not seek re-election. The vice president is elected separately and may succeed to the presidency. Ferdinand "Bongbong" Marcos Jr., son of the former dictator Ferdinand Marcos, won the 2022 election and is the incumbent.

Powers

The president appoints cabinet secretaries and other officials, exercises control over the executive departments, may declare martial law under constitutional limits, grants pardons and reprieves, and conducts foreign policy. The seat of government is Malacañang Palace in Manila.

History

The presidency traces to Emilio Aguinaldo's First Philippine Republic of 1899; the modern office was shaped by the Commonwealth under Manuel L. Quezon and the constitutions of 1973 and 1987.
