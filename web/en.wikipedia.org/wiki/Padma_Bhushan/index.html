Padma Bhushan

The Padma Bhushan is the third-highest civilian award in the Republic of India, preceded by the Bharat Ratna and the Padma Vibhushan and followed by the Padma Shri. Instituted on 2 January 1954, the award is given for "distinguished service of a high order" in any field.

TypeCivilian award
CountryIndia
Presented byPresident of India

PrecedenceNext higher: Padma Vibhushan; next lower: Padma Shri

History

The award was established together with the other Padma awards in 1954. It was briefly suspended from 1977 to 1980 and again in 1992–1995 during litigation, which concluded with the Supreme Court upholding the awards' validity. Recipients are announced annually on the eve of Republic Day, 26 January.

Design

The decoration is a circular bronze medallion bearing a lotus flower in the centre with the word "Padma" above and "Bhushan" below in Devanagari script. It is worn suspended from a pink ribbon with a white stripe.

Selection

Nominations are received from state governments, ministries, previous awardees and the public, and are considered by a committee constituted by the Prime Minister; final approval rests with the President of India. The award may be conferred posthumously and on foreign nationals.
