Colony of New South Wales

The Colony of New South Wales was a British colony from 1788 to 1901, when it became a state of the Commonwealth of Australia. Established as a penal settlement at Sydney Cove, it originally encompassed the whole eastern half of the Australian mainland claimed by Britain, together with Van Diemen's Land and Norfolk Island, and its territory was progressively reduced as new colonies were separated from it.

Foundation

The First Fleet under Captain Arthur Phillip arrived at Botany Bay in January 1788 and founded the settlement at Sydney Cove in Port Jackson on 26 January 1788. The colony initially depended on convict labour, government farms and supplies from Britain, and suffered near-starvation in its first years. Phillip was succeeded by a series of governors, including Lachlan Macquarie (1810–1821), who expanded settlement and public works.

Development

Free immigration grew from the 1820s, and pastoral expansion spread inland across the Blue Mountains after 1813. Gold rushes beginning in 1851 transformed the economy and population. Responsible government was granted in 1856 with an elected legislative assembly. Over the 19th century separate colonies were carved out of New South Wales territory: Van Diemen's Land (Tasmania, 1825), South Australia (1836), New Zealand (1841), Victoria (1851) and Queensland (1859), while the Australian Capital Territory and Jervis Bay Territory were surrendered to the Commonwealth in the 20th century.

Federation

New South Wales played a leading role in the federation movement and became a founding state of the Commonwealth of Australia on 1 January 1901, with Sydney as its capital.
