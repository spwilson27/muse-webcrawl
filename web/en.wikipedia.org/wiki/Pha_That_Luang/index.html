Overview

Pha That Luang is a large gold-covered Buddhist stupa in Vientiane, the capital of Laos, and the country's most important national monument, appearing on the national emblem and banknotes. Tradition holds that a stupa on the site enshrines a relic of the Buddha brought by Indian missionaries in the 3rd century BC.

History

The present structure was built in 1566 by King Setthathirath when he moved the capital to Vientiane. It was repeatedly damaged by Burmese, Siamese and Chinese invasions and by looting in 1828, then restored by the French in the 1930s (initially in a redesigned form, later rebuilt to the Lao original). The annual That Luang festival in November is Laos's largest religious gathering.
