Reservation in India

Reservation is a system of affirmative action in India that provides historically disadvantaged groups representation in education, employment, services, and politics. Based on provisions in the Indian Constitution, it allows the Union and State Governments to set reserved quotas or seats, at particular percentages in admissions into public sector and educational institutions, government jobs, and political representation.

Categories

CategoryCentral quota (typical)
Scheduled Castes (SC)15%
Scheduled Tribes (ST)7.5%
Other Backward Classes (OBC)27%
Economically Weaker Sections (EWS)10%

The system has its origins in the caste-based reservations introduced in princely states and British India in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The Constitution of 1950 empowered the state to make special provisions for socially and educationally backward classes, Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes. The Mandal Commission (1979–80) recommended extending reservation to Other Backward Classes, implemented in central government jobs in 1990 amid large protests, and upheld — with a "creamy layer" exclusion and an overall 50% ceiling — in the Supreme Court's Indra Sawhney judgment of 1992. In 2019 the 103rd Amendment introduced a 10% quota for Economically Weaker Sections, upheld by the Supreme Court in 2022.

Debates

Supporters see reservation as necessary to correct centuries of caste discrimination and to ensure representation; critics argue it compromises merit, entrenches caste identity, and benefits relatively advantaged members of listed groups. Reservation remains one of the most contested issues in Indian politics.
