Podgórze

Podgórze (roughly, "the foothills district") is a district of Kraków, Poland, situated on the right (southern) bank of the Vistula River, at the foot of Lasota Hill. It was founded in 1784 as a separate, rival city by the Austrian emperor Joseph II, and was known in German as Josephstadt. Podgórze remained a separate town until 1915, when it was incorporated into Kraków. Since 1990 greater Podgórze forms one of Kraków's eighteen administrative districts (District XIII).

History

Settlement south of the Vistula is ancient: the Krakus Mound, a prehistoric tumulus on Lasota Hill traditionally identified as the grave of the legendary King Krakus, watches over the district. In 1784, after the First Partition of Poland placed Kraków's southern suburbs under Austrian rule, Joseph II chartered Podgórze as a free royal city to draw trade away from Kraków across the river. It received a regular market square, churches including the neo-Gothic St Joseph's Church, and a town hall, and it grew with industry attracted by tax privileges.

Under German occupation during the Second World War, Podgórze became the site of the Kraków Ghetto, established in March 1941, into which the city's Jewish population was forced. The ghetto was liquidated in March 1943; its inhabitants were murdered on the spot, sent to the Płaszów concentration camp nearby, or deported to Bełżec and Auschwitz. The main square of the ghetto, Plac Zgody (now Plac Bohaterów Getta, "Ghetto Heroes Square"), is marked by a memorial of oversized empty chairs. Oskar Schindler's enamel factory across the river in Zabłocie, and Tadeusz Pankiewicz's pharmacy "Under the Eagle" on the square, are now museums. The Płaszów camp site, in the south of Podgórze, is preserved as a memorial landscape.
