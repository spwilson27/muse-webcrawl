The AGM-88 HARM (High-speed Anti-Radiation Missile) is a tactical, supersonic, air-to-surface anti-radiation missile designed to home in on and destroy enemy air-defence radars. It is the principal suppression of enemy air defences (SEAD) weapon of the United States armed forces and numerous allies, carried by aircraft including the F-16CJ, EA-18G Growler, and Tornado ECR.

Design and operation

The HARM's seeker detects radar emissions from ground-based air defences and guides the missile onto the emitter; even if the radar is switched off after launch, earlier variants could lose the target, a weakness addressed by later versions. The AGM-88E Advanced Anti-Radiation Guided Missile (AARGM) adds GPS/INS guidance and a millimetre-wave terminal seeker, allowing it to strike radars that have shut down or moved, while the extended-range AARGM-ER has been developed for internal carriage on stealth aircraft. The missile carries a blast-fragmentation warhead optimised to shred radar antennas.

Operational history

The HARM was first used in combat against Libyan air defences in 1986 and was used extensively in the 1991 Gulf War, where thousands were fired to suppress Iraqi radars. It has since been employed in conflicts in the Balkans, Iraq, and Libya, and was supplied to Ukraine following the Russian invasion of 2022, integrated onto Ukrainian aircraft to attack Russian air-defence radars.
