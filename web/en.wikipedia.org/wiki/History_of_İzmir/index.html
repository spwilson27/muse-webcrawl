History of İzmir

The history of İzmir stretches back some 8,500 years, making the city — known in the West for centuries as Smyrna — one of the oldest continuously inhabited settlements of the Mediterranean world. Archaeological excavations at Yeşilova Höyük have revealed Neolithic occupation dating to around 6500 BC.

Ancient Smyrna

The first major settlement, at Bayraklı (Old Smyrna), was founded by Aeolian Greeks around 1000 BC and later taken over by Ionians. The city was destroyed by the Lydian king Alyattes around 600 BC. According to tradition, Alexander the Great refounded Smyrna on the slopes of Mount Pagos (Kadifekale); under Hellenistic and then Roman rule it grew into a wealthy port city, famed for its temples, its library, and as one of the Seven Churches of Asia addressed in the Book of Revelation. The Christian bishop and martyr Polycarp was executed in its stadium around AD 155.

Byzantine and Ottoman periods

After the division of the Roman Empire, Smyrna remained Byzantine despite Persian and Arab attacks, and later passed among Genoese, Turkish emirate, and Ottoman hands. The Knights Hospitaller held its sea castle until Timur's forces destroyed it in 1402. Under Ottoman rule, especially from the seventeenth century, Smyrna became a great cosmopolitan entrepôt — sometimes called "Infidel Smyrna" for its large Greek, Armenian, Jewish, and Levantine populations — exporting raisins, figs, cotton, and carpets.

Twentieth century

After the First World War, Greek forces occupied Smyrna in May 1919 under the terms of the armistice, and the Treaty of Sèvres assigned the region to Greece. Turkish nationalist forces under Mustafa Kemal retook the city on 9 September 1922. Days later, the Great Fire of Smyrna destroyed much of the Greek and Armenian quarters; the Greek and Armenian populations fled or were expelled, and the 1923 Treaty of Lausanne population exchange formalised the city's transformation. Renamed İzmir, the city was rebuilt as a modern Turkish port and hosts the annual İzmir International Fair.
