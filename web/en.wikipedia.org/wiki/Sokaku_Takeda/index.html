Sokaku Takeda


Born: 10 October 1859, Aizu, Japan
Died: 25 April 1943 (aged 83)
Style: Daitō-ryū Aiki-jūjutsu
Notable students: Morihei Ueshiba, Yukiyoshi Sagawa

Sokaku Takeda (武田 惣角, Takeda Sōkaku, 1859–1943) was a Japanese martial artist from a samurai family of Aizu. He is the founder of Daitō-ryū Aiki-jūjutsu, a classical jujutsu school whose teachings profoundly influenced modern aikido, hapkido, and other arts. He spent much of his life travelling throughout Japan teaching martial arts to military officers, police, and officials.

Life

Takeda grew up during the turbulent end of the Tokugawa shogunate and the Boshin War, in which Aizu was defeated. He trained in kenjutsu, staff and spear arts, sumo, and the jujutsu traditions of his family, studying under masters including Sakakibara Kenkichi and, according to tradition, Saigō Tanomo, from whom he is said to have received the Daitō-ryū transmission. From the 1890s he taught full-time, holding seminars across Japan and Hokkaido.

Legacy

His most famous student was Morihei Ueshiba, founder of aikido, who incorporated Daitō-ryū techniques and principles into his own art. Another student, the Korean Choi Yong-sool, carried related teachings back to Korea where they contributed to hapkido. Takeda kept meticulous enrolment registers (eimeiroku) recording tens of thousands of students, which remain key documents of his teaching career.
