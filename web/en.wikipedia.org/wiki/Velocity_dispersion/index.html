Velocity dispersion

In astronomy, velocity dispersion is the statistical dispersion of velocities about the mean velocity for a group of astronomical objects, such as stars in a galaxy or galaxies in a cluster. It is usually denoted by the Greek letter sigma (σ) and measured in kilometres per second.

Velocity dispersion is measured from the broadening of spectral lines by the Doppler effect. Together with a system's size, it allows mass estimates through the virial theorem. The high velocity dispersions of galaxy clusters were among the first evidence for dark matter, noted by Fritz Zwicky in the 1930s.

Scaling relations

For elliptical galaxies, central velocity dispersion correlates with luminosity in the Faber–Jackson relation and with central black hole mass in the M–sigma relation, making dispersion a key indicator of a galaxy's mass and evolutionary history.
