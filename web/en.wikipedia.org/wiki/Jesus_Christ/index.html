Jesus Christ

Jesus Christ is the central figure of Christianity, the world's largest religion. Christians believe him to be the incarnation of God the Son and the awaited Messiah (the Christ) prophesied in the Old Testament. Virtually all scholars of antiquity agree that Jesus existed historically.

Life and teachings

According to the canonical gospels, Jesus was born in Bethlehem, grew up in Nazareth, and was baptised by John the Baptist before beginning a ministry of preaching, parables, and miracles in Galilee and Judea, proclaiming the coming of the Kingdom of God. He gathered twelve apostles, and his teachings emphasised love of God and neighbour, mercy, and humility.

Crucifixion and resurrection

Around AD 30 or 33 Jesus was arrested in Jerusalem and crucified by order of the Roman prefect Pontius Pilate. Christians believe he rose from the dead three days later and ascended into heaven; the resurrection is the foundation of Christian faith.

In other traditions

In Islam, Jesus (Isa) is revered as a prophet and the Messiah, born of the Virgin Mary, though not as the Son of God. See also Jesus and Christianity.
