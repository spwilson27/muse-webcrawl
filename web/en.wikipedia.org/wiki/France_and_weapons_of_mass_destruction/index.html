France is one of the five nuclear-weapon states recognized under the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons (NPT). It developed its own nuclear deterrent, the force de frappe, beginning in the 1950s, and conducted 210 nuclear tests between 1960 and 1996 in the Algerian Sahara and in French Polynesia. France possesses the world's third-largest declared nuclear stockpile, delivered by submarine-launched ballistic missiles and air-launched cruise missiles. France has renounced biological and chemical weapons and is a party to the Biological Weapons Convention and the Chemical Weapons Convention.

Nuclear weapons program

Following the Suez Crisis of 1956 and doubts about the reliability of allies, the government of Pierre Mendès France and then the Fifth Republic under Charles de Gaulle pressed ahead with an independent deterrent. France exploded its first atomic bomb, codenamed Gerboise bleue, on 13 February 1960 at Reggane in the Algerian Sahara, with a yield of about 65 kilotons. Testing moved to the atolls of Moruroa and Fangataufa in French Polynesia in 1966, including France's first thermonuclear device in 1968. After a final series of tests in 1995–1996 under President Jacques Chirac — which drew international protest — France signed the Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty and dismantled its Pacific test site.

Delivery systems

The French deterrent rests on two legs. The oceanic component comprises four Triomphant-class nuclear-powered ballistic missile submarines, each armed with M51 submarine-launched ballistic missiles carrying multiple independently targetable warheads, maintaining a continuous at-sea deterrent. The airborne component consists of Rafale fighter aircraft carrying the ASMP-A supersonic cruise missile with a nuclear warhead. France retired its land-based ballistic missiles on the Albion Plateau in 1996, ending the former nuclear triad.

Biological and chemical weapons

France maintained chemical weapons stocks during the Cold War but ratified the Geneva Protocol in 1926, the Biological Weapons Convention in 1984, and the Chemical Weapons Convention in 1993, and has destroyed its declared chemical arsenals in accordance with those treaties.
