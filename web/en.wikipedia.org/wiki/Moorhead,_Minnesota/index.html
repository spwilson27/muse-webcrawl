Moorhead, Minnesota

Moorhead is a city in and the county seat of Clay County, Minnesota, United States, on the eastern bank of the Red River of the North, directly across from Fargo, North Dakota. It is the largest city in northwestern Minnesota, with a population of 44,505 at the 2020 census, and forms part of the Fargo–Moorhead metropolitan area.

History

Moorhead was founded in 1871 as the Northern Pacific Railway's crossing of the Red River and named for William G. Moorhead, a director of the railroad. Settlement in the flat Red River Valley centred on bonanza wheat farms and the river trade. The city has repeatedly contended with Red River flooding — most severely in 1997 and 2009 — and has since built extensive permanent flood protection.

Education and culture

Moorhead is a college city: it hosts Concordia College, Minnesota State University Moorhead, and Minnesota State Community and Technical College. Cultural landmarks include the Hjemkomst Center, which houses a full-scale replica of the Norwegian Hopperstad stave church and the Hjemkomst Viking ship replica that sailed to Norway in 1982, and the Rourke Art Museum.
