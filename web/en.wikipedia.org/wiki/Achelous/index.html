Achelous

AbodeThe river Achelous, western Greece
ParentsOceanus and Tethys (or Gaia)
OffspringThe Sirens (in some accounts), various nymphs

In Greek mythology, Achelous (Greek: Ἀχελώϊος) was the god of the Achelous River, the largest river in Greece, and by extension a god of fresh water generally. He was the eldest of the three thousand Potamoi, the river-god sons of Oceanus and Tethys.

Wrestling with Heracles

Achelous competed with Heracles for the hand of Deianira, daughter of Oeneus of Calydon. In their wrestling match Achelous transformed first into a serpent and then into a bull; Heracles tore off one of his horns, which the Naiads filled with fruit and flowers, creating the cornucopia, the horn of plenty. Defeated, Achelous withdrew, and Heracles married Deianira.

Other myths

Achelous was said to be the father of the Sirens in some traditions, and of various fountain nymphs. His contest with Heracles symbolised the taming of the river's flooding for agriculture. The river god was widely depicted in Greek art as a bull with a man's bearded face, a form echoed in man-faced bulls on coins of Greek cities in Sicily and Italy.
