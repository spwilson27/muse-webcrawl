Leônidas da Silva (6 September 1913 – 24 January 2004) was a Brazilian footballer who played as a forward. Nicknamed the "Black Diamond" (Diamante Negro) and the "Rubber Man" for his extraordinary agility, he is credited with popularising — and is often said to have invented — the bicycle kick (bicicleta).

Career

Leônidas played for São Cristóvão, Sírio e Libanês, Bonsucesso, Peñarol (Uruguay), Vasco da Gama, Botafogo, Flamengo and São Paulo. With Flamengo he won the Campeonato Carioca in 1939, and with São Paulo he won five Campeonato Paulista titles in the 1940s.

For Brazil he starred at two World Cups. At the 1934 tournament he scored Brazil's only goal, and at the 1938 World Cup in France he finished as top scorer with seven goals, including a hat-trick in the famous 6–5 extra-time victory over Poland, during part of which — according to celebrated legend — he played in bare feet after his boot fell apart in the mud. He scored 21 goals in 19 appearances for the national team.

Legacy

Leônidas was one of the first great black stars of Brazilian football and a national celebrity in the radio era, lending his name to products such as the Diamante Negro chocolate bar, which is still sold in Brazil. After retiring he became a radio commentator. He died in 2004 after many years suffering from Alzheimer's disease.
