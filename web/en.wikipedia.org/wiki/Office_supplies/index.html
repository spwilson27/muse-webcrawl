Office supplies

Office supplies are the consumables and small equipment used in offices and other workplaces for writing, record-keeping, filing and communication. Typical examples include paper, pens, pencils, notebooks, envelopes, folders, paper clips, staplers and staples, adhesive tape, sticky notes, printer ink and toner, and correction fluid.

History

The category grew with the expansion of clerical work in the 19th and 20th centuries, as businesses adopted typewriters, filing systems and mass-produced stationery. Many now-standard items date from this period: the paper clip in its familiar Gem form from the 1890s, the stapler's widespread office adoption in the early 20th century, and the ballpoint pen after the Second World War. The sticky note, developed at 3M and launched in 1980, became another office staple.

Industry

Office supplies are sold by specialist retailers and superstores — historically chains such as Staples and Office Depot — as well as increasingly through online marketplaces and business-to-business contracts. The shift to digital documents and remote work has reduced demand for paper-based consumables while increasing sales of home-office equipment and electronics accessories.

Environmental aspects

Paper products account for a large share of office waste, prompting recycling programmes, recycled-content paper, and refillable writing instruments. Ink and toner cartridges are widely collected for remanufacturing.
