Carlo Buonaparte

Carlo Maria Buonaparte (27 March 1746 – 24 February 1785) was a Corsican lawyer and diplomat, best known as the father of Napoleon Bonaparte and grandfather of Napoleon III.

Life

Carlo Buonaparte was born in Ajaccio, Corsica, into a family of minor Tuscan nobility that had settled on the island in the 16th century. He studied law at the University of Pisa. In 1764 he married the 14-year-old Letizia Ramolino, with whom he had thirteen children, eight of whom survived infancy.

During the Corsican struggle for independence, Buonaparte served as secretary and aide to Pasquale Paoli and fought against the French purchase of the island, taking part in the Battle of Ponte Novu in 1769. After the French victory he pragmatically accommodated himself to the new regime, was recognised as a member of the French nobility, and was appointed assessor of the royal jurisdiction of Ajaccio. In 1778 he travelled to Versailles as one of Corsica's deputies to the court of Louis XVI, and he used his connections to obtain places for his sons at French military schools — including a scholarship for the young Napoleon at Brienne.

Death and legacy

Carlo died in Montpellier in 1785, probably of stomach cancer, the disease that would also kill his famous son. He never lived to see the rise of his family: four of his sons became kings or heads of state, and his descendants sat on thrones across Europe.
