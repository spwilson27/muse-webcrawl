North West England is one of the nine official regions of England and comprises the counties of Cheshire, Cumbria, Greater Manchester, Lancashire and Merseyside. It is the third most populous region of the United Kingdom, with around 7.4 million inhabitants.

Geography

The region stretches from the Scottish border in the north to Cheshire in the south, and from the Irish Sea coast in the west to the Pennine hills in the east. Its landscapes include the mountains and lakes of Cumbria's Lake District (a UNESCO World Heritage Site and home to Scafell Pike, England's highest mountain), the Pennines, the Cheshire Plain and the coastal lowlands around Morecambe Bay and the Wirral.

Major settlements

The two great conurbations are Greater Manchester and Liverpool on Merseyside; other significant towns and cities include Chester, Lancaster, Carlisle, Preston, Blackpool, Warrington, Stockport, Bolton, Blackburn and Barrow-in-Furness. Manchester and Liverpool — linked in 1830 by the world's first inter-city passenger railway — remain the region's economic and cultural anchors.

Economy and history

The North West was the cradle of the Industrial Revolution: its cotton mills, powered first by Pennine water and then by steam, made "Cottonopolis" (Manchester) the centre of the world textile trade, while Liverpool became one of the busiest ports of the British Empire. Later industries included shipbuilding (Barrow, Birkenhead), chemicals (Widnes, Runcorn), engineering and nuclear power (Sellafield). Today the region's economy centres on services, media (MediaCityUK at Salford Quays), advanced manufacturing, life sciences and two major universities in Manchester and Liverpool.

Culture

The region is famous for its football clubs (Manchester United, Manchester City, Liverpool, Everton), its musical heritage (the Merseybeat of the Beatles; the "Madchester" scene; the Northern Soul movement), and its contributions to comedy and television. The regional dialects — Scouse, Mancunian, Lancastrian and Cumbrian — reflect its distinct identity within England.
