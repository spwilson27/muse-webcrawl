Lieutenant Colonel Sir Arthur Henry McMahon (28 November 1862 – 29 December 1949) was a British Indian Army officer and diplomat who served as an administrator in British India and as the first High Commissioner of Egypt. His name is attached to two legacies that remain controversial: the McMahon–Hussein Correspondence and the McMahon Line.

Career in India

McMahon was commissioned into the Indian Army and served on the northwest frontier, becoming an expert on Baluchistan and Afghanistan; he was political agent in Baluchistan and served on commissions demarcating Persia's and Afghanistan's borders. He became Foreign Secretary to the Government of India in 1911.

Simla Convention and the McMahon Line

In 1913–14 McMahon chaired the Simla Conference with Britain, Tibet, and China. The resulting notes defined the McMahon Line, the boundary between Tibet and British India running along the Himalayan crest in what is now Arunachal Pradesh. China refused to ratify the convention, and the line remains at the heart of the Sino-Indian border dispute, a cause of the 1962 Sino-Indian War.

Egypt and the Hussein correspondence

As High Commissioner in Egypt during the First World War, McMahon conducted the famous correspondence of 1915–16 with Hussein bin Ali, Sharif of Mecca. His letters promised British support for an independent Arab kingdom in return for an Arab revolt against the Ottoman Empire. Arabs later regarded the promise as betrayed by the Sykes–Picot Agreement and the Balfour Declaration, a grievance that shaped Middle Eastern politics for a century. McMahon was replaced in 1917 by Sir Reginald Wingate and retired from public life.
