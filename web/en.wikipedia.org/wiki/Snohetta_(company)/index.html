Snøhetta (company)

Snøhetta (Norwegian for "snow-hood," named after the mountain in Dovrefjell) is an international architecture, landscape architecture, interior design, and brand design office based in Oslo, Norway, with additional studios in New York, San Francisco, Innsbruck, Paris, Adelaide, Hong Kong, and Stockholm. Founded in 1989, the practice is known for buildings that integrate architecture with landscape and public space.

History and approach

Snøhetta was founded by Kjetil Trædal Thorsen, Christoph Kapeller, and Craig Dykers, and gained early prominence by winning the competition for the Bibliotheca Alexandrina in Egypt (completed 2001), the great library built near the site of the ancient Library of Alexandria. The firm works as a transdisciplinary collective, with architects, landscape architects, and designers collaborating from the earliest concept stages, and it emphasizes "contextual" design in which buildings emerge from their cultural and physical setting.

Major works

Snøhetta's best-known project is the Oslo Opera House (2008), whose sloping marble roof rises directly from Oslofjord and invites the public to walk on top of the building; it won the European Union Prize for Contemporary Architecture (Mies van der Rohe Award) in 2009. Other notable works include the National September 11 Memorial Museum pavilion in New York; the San Francisco Museum of Modern Art expansion (2016); the Ryerson University Student Learning Centre in Toronto; the Lascaux IV cave-art museum in France; Under (2019), an underwater restaurant off Norway's southern coast — the first of its kind in Europe; and the Shanghai Grand Opera House. The practice has also designed the graphics and banknotes for Norway's currency series.
