Vajradhara

Vajradhara
Sanskrit: वज्रधर, "Holder of the Vajra"
Tradition: Vajrayana Buddhism (esp. Tibetan)
Role: Primordial Buddha (Adi-Buddha); source of the tantras
Attributes: Vajra and ghanta (bell), dark blue body

Vajradhara (Sanskrit: वज्रधर, "Bearer of the Diamond/Thunderbolt"; Tibetan: རྡོ་རྗེ་འཆང་, Dorje Chang) is the primordial Buddha — the Adi-Buddha — in several schools of Vajrayana Buddhism, particularly in the Kagyu and Gelug traditions of Tibet. He represents the dharmakaya, the ultimate, formless nature of Buddhahood itself, from which all other Buddhas and teachings emanate.

Iconography

Vajradhara is depicted as a Buddha of deep dark-blue colour, seated in vajra posture, wearing the silk and jewel ornaments of a sambhogakaya Buddha. In each hand he holds one of the two supreme symbols of tantra — a vajra (diamond sceptre) in the right hand and a ghanta (ritual bell) in the left — with his arms crossed at his chest in the vajra-humkara mudra, signifying the union of method (vajra) and wisdom (bell). In some traditions he appears in union (yab-yum) with his consort, representing the inseparability of bliss and emptiness.

Role in Tibetan Buddhism

According to Kagyu tradition, the historical Buddha Shakyamuni taught the tantras in the form of Vajradhara. The Indian mahasiddha Tilopa is said to have received the transmission directly from Vajradhara, beginning the lineage that passed through Naropa, Marpa and Milarepa into Tibet. In the Gelug school, Vajradhara is likewise regarded as the ultimate source of the tantric teachings received by Tsongkhapa. Many Tibetan lineages also identify Vajradhara with Vajrasattva or regard Vajrasattva as his sambhogakaya emanation.

As the embodiment of the primordial ground of enlightenment, Vajradhara occupies the apex of the refuge trees and lineage diagrams of the schools that revere him, and guru yoga practices direct devotion to the teacher as inseparable from Vajradhara.
