Nuyorican

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
El Barrio (Spanish Harlem), a centre of Nuyorican life.

Nuyorican is a portmanteau of "New York" and "Puerto Rican" and refers to Puerto Ricans who live in or near New York City, as well as to their culture, language and artistic movements. The term also describes members of the Puerto Rican diaspora elsewhere in the mainland United States. Originally used by island Puerto Ricans — sometimes pejoratively, to question the authenticity of mainland-raised Puerto Ricans — the word was reclaimed in the 1960s and 1970s by artists and activists as a badge of identity.

Migration

Mass Puerto Rican migration to New York followed the Second World War, driven by cheap air travel, industrial recruitment and economic change on the island under Operation Bootstrap. Puerto Ricans, US citizens since the Jones Act of 1917, settled first in East Harlem (El Barrio), the South Bronx and the Lower East Side (Loisaida). At its height, New York held the largest Puerto Rican population of any city in the world; the diaspora has since spread widely across the mainland, with large communities in Florida, New Jersey, Pennsylvania and Chicago.

Nuyorican movement

The Nuyorican literary and cultural movement emerged in the late 1960s and 1970s, giving voice to the experience of poverty, racism and bicultural identity in the city. Its landmark institution is the Nuyorican Poets Cafe, founded on the Lower East Side in 1973 by Miguel Algarín, Miguel Piñero and others. Piñero's play Short Eyes (1974) won an Obie Award; writers such as Piri Thomas (Down These Mean Streets, 1967), Pedro Pietri (Puerto Rican Obituary), Sandra María Esteves, Tato Laviera and Giannina Braschi shaped a literature that mixes English, Spanish and Spanglish. Nuyorican communities also played foundational roles in salsa music and in the birth of hip hop in the South Bronx.
