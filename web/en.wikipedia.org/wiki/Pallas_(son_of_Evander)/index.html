Pallas (son of Evander)

In Roman mythology, Pallas (Ancient Greek: Πάλλας) was the son of King Evander of Pallantium, the Arcadian settlement on the future site of Rome. He is a major character in Virgil's Aeneid, in which he becomes the ally, protégé, and close companion of Aeneas during the war in Latium.

In the Aeneid

When Aeneas seeks allies against Turnus and the Rutulians, Evander entrusts his young son and an Arcadian cavalry force to him (Book 8). Pallas fights bravely in the battle at the Trojan camp (Book 10), killing a string of opponents including Lagus, Hisbo, and the brothers Larides and Thymber, before confronting Turnus himself. Turnus slays Pallas and strips from his body the ornate sword-belt (balteus), which he wears as a trophy—a fatal act of hubris. Aeneas, enraged at the news, captures eight youths alive to sacrifice at Pallas's funeral pyre; the funeral itself occupies much of Book 11.

Death of Turnus

In the poem's final scene (Book 12), Aeneas has defeated Turnus, who pleads for mercy. Aeneas hesitates—then sees the belt of Pallas on Turnus's shoulder, and in a burst of fury kills him, dedicating the death to Pallas: "It is Pallas who strikes, Pallas who makes sacrifice of you." The death of Pallas thus drives the controversial ending of the epic.
