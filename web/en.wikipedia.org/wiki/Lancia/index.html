Lancia (pronounced [ˈlantʃa]) is an Italian automobile manufacturer founded in Turin in 1906 by racing driver Vincenzo Lancia and Claudio Fogolin. Long celebrated for engineering innovation and motorsport success, it became part of the Fiat group in 1969 and is today a premium brand of Stellantis, sold mainly in Italy with the Ypsilon hatchback, with a relaunch across Europe planned around new electric models.

Innovations

Lancia pioneered many features now taken for granted: the Lambda (1922) was the first production car with a load-bearing unitary body and independent front suspension, and used a narrow-angle V4; the Aprilia (1937) was aerodynamically advanced; the Aurelia (1950) introduced the production V6 engine and rear transaxle; and the company was early with five-speed gearboxes, inboard brakes and, in the Flaminia and others, sophisticated all-independent suspension. Lancia models were traditionally named with Greek letters — a practice echoed in its badge and revived for concepts.

Rallying

Lancia is the most successful marque in the history of the World Rally Championship, winning eleven manufacturers' titles. The Fulvia HF won the International Championship for Manufacturers in 1972; the mid-engined Stratos dominated in 1974–76; the 037 won in 1983 as the last rear-wheel-drive car to take the title; the fearsome Group B Delta S4 followed; and the Delta HF Integrale won six consecutive manufacturers' championships (1987–1992) with drivers including Juha Kankkunen and Miki Biasion. Lancia also won the Mille Miglia and Targa Florio and competed in Formula One in 1954–55, its D50 cars and assets passing to Ferrari, whose Alberto Ascari had driven for the team.

Decline and revival

Quality problems, notably rust on the Beta in the 1970s, damaged Lancia's reputation in export markets; the brand withdrew from right-hand-drive markets in 1994 and shrank to a single Italy-only model by the late 2010s. Under Stellantis, a ten-year relaunch plan from 2024 introduced a new electric Ypsilon and announced the revival of Gamma and Delta nameplates as electrified flagships.
