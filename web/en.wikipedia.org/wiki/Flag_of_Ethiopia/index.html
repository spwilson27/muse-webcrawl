Flag of Ethiopia

The flag of Ethiopia (Amharic: ye-Ītyōṗṗyā bandīrā) consists of a green, yellow, and red tricolour — the Pan-African colours — with the national emblem centred on the middle stripe. The current flag was adopted on 11 October 1997, with the emblem added in 1996 and modified in 2009. The emblem, a radiant star with rays on a blue disc, symbolizes unity and diversity of the peoples of Ethiopia.

History

Ethiopia's green, yellow, and red pennants date back centuries; the rectangular tricolour was formally adopted in 1897 under Emperor Menelik II, a year after Ethiopia defeated Italy at the Battle of Adwa. Because Ethiopia remained independent during the Scramble for Africa, its colours were adopted by many African nations upon independence and by the pan-African movement. Earlier versions bore the Lion of Judah emblem under the monarchy; plain tricolours and versions with socialist emblems were used under the Derg.

Symbolism

Green traditionally represents the land and fertility, yellow peace and hope, and red the blood of those who defended the country. The central emblem represents the diversity and unity of Ethiopia's peoples, its bright future, and equality of nations, nationalities, and religions.
