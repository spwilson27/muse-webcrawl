Valinor

Valinor is a fictional place in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium, the home of the immortal Valar on the continent of Aman, far to the west of Middle-earth. It is also called the Undying Lands, although the name properly applies to Aman as a whole. Valinor features prominently in The Silmarillion and is the destination of the Ring-bearers at the end of The Lord of the Rings.

Geography

Valinor lies on the eastern coast of Aman, sheltered to the east by the Pelóri, the highest mountains in Arda, raised by the Valar as a defence against Melkor. The land is lit originally by the Two Trees, Laurelin and Telperion, which stand on the green mound of Ezellohar before the western gates of Valmar, the city of the Valar with its golden roofs and many bells.

The chief Elven cities are Tirion upon Túna, built by the Noldor and Vanyar in a cleft in the Pelóri, and Alqualondë, the Swanhaven of the Teleri on the shore, famed for its pearl-fisheries and swan ships. The island of Tol Eressëa lies offshore, home to many of the Teleri.

History

After the destruction of their first dwelling on the Isle of Almaren in Middle-earth by Melkor, the Valar withdrew to Aman and founded Valinor. The realm was hidden and guarded after the Elves awoke; many Elves journeyed there in the Great March. The peace of Valinor was broken when Melkor, with Ungoliant, destroyed the Two Trees and stole the Silmarils, leading to the Flight of the Noldor. After the War of Wrath, Aman remained the home of most Elves; after the Fall of Númenor, the Undying Lands were removed from the circles of the world, reachable only along the Straight Road.

Significance

Valinor stands as the earthly paradise of Tolkien's world: a place of bliss and memory, yet its bliss contributed to the Valar's detachment from Middle-earth. Mortals may not dwell there without dying the sooner, for the land does not grant immortality; rather, as the Elves explain, mortals who came there would wither quickly, like moths in a light too strong for them.
