The Battle of Guadalete was fought in July 711 between the Visigothic kingdom of King Roderic and an invading army of the Umayyad Caliphate commanded by Tariq ibn Ziyad, composed largely of Berber troops from North Africa. The Visigothic army was destroyed and Roderic killed, opening the way to the Muslim conquest of most of the Iberian Peninsula within a few years.

Background

Roderic's contested accession had left the Visigothic kingdom divided; tradition holds that supporters of the rival claimant invited Musa ibn Nusayr, the Umayyad governor of Ifriqiya, to intervene. Tariq crossed the strait that now bears his name (Gibraltar, from Jabal Tariq) in the spring of 711 and advanced inland, while Roderic hurried south from a campaign against the Basques.

Battle

The armies met in the far south, traditionally near the Guadalete River, though many historians favour the vicinity of Lake La Janda. The fighting may have lasted several days. According to later chronicles, the wings of the Visigothic army commanded by Roderic's rivals abandoned or betrayed him; Tariq's cavalry then overwhelmed the centre, and Roderic perished — his riderless horse and a boot were reportedly found by a riverbank.

Aftermath

With the king and much of the Gothic nobility dead, organized resistance collapsed. Tariq and Musa conquered Toledo, Córdoba and most of Hispania by 718, beginning nearly eight centuries of Muslim rule in al-Andalus.
