Shemya is a small island in the Semichi Islands group of the Near Islands chain in the Aleutian Islands, Alaska, United States. It lies near the western end of the Aleutian archipelago, roughly 1,200 miles (1,900 km) southwest of Anchorage.

The island is best known as the site of Eareckson Air Station, a United States Air Force facility that has served as a radar and surveillance station, an emergency diversion airfield, and a refuelling stop on trans-Pacific air routes.

History

Shemya was uninhabited when Russian explorers reached the Aleutians in the 18th century; its Russian name was recorded as Semichi. During the Second World War the United States military built an airfield on the island as part of the Aleutian Islands campaign infrastructure, and the flat terrain made it one of the few western Aleutian islands suitable for a long runway.

Cold War and later use

During the Cold War, Shemya hosted radar installations used to monitor Soviet missile tests and air activity over the North Pacific. The large Cobra Dane radar became a prominent feature of the station. The island's airfield also served as an important emergency landing site for civilian aircraft crossing the Pacific, and Northwest Orient Airlines historically used Shemya as a refuelling stop. A major earthquake struck near the island in 1975, damaging facilities.

Geography and climate

Shemya is low and relatively flat, with tundra vegetation. Its maritime climate is persistently windy, foggy and cool, with frequent storms sweeping in from the North Pacific and the Bering Sea.
