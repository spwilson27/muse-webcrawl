Naming rights

Naming rights are a financial transaction in which a corporation or other entity purchases the right to name a facility, event or organisation, typically for a defined period of years, in exchange for payment. The practice is most visible in sports stadiums and arenas.

History

Early examples include Wrigley Field in Chicago, named for the chewing-gum company of the team's owner. Corporate naming deals grew rapidly from the 1970s and 1980s, and top stadium deals can now be worth tens of millions of dollars per year.

Criticism

Fans and preservationists have criticised the practice for erasing historic names and over-commercialising public spaces; naming-rights transactions are also studied in research on personal identity and the right to one's own name.
