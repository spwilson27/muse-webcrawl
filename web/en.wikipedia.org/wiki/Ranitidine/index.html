Ranitidine

Ranitidine, sold under the brand name Zantac among others, is an H2 histamine receptor antagonist that decreases stomach acid production. Developed by Glaxo (now GSK) and introduced in 1981, it was used worldwide to treat peptic ulcer disease, gastroesophageal reflux disease, and Zollinger–Ellison syndrome, becoming one of the best-selling prescription drugs of the 1980s.

Withdrawal

In 2019, testing found that ranitidine products could contain or form the probable carcinogen N-nitrosodimethylamine (NDMA), with levels increasing during storage, especially at higher temperatures. Regulators progressively restricted and then removed the drug: the US Food and Drug Administration requested withdrawal of all ranitidine products from the US market in April 2020, and the European Union suspended them. The Zantac brand was later relaunched in some markets with famotidine as the active ingredient instead.
