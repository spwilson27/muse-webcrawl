Roxy Music are an English rock band formed in 1970 by Bryan Ferry — who became their lead vocalist and principal songwriter — and bassist Graham Simpson. The classic line-up included Ferry, Brian Eno (synthesiser and tapes), Andy Mackay (saxophone and oboe), Phil Manzanera (guitar) and Paul Thompson (drums). One of the most influential British bands of their era, Roxy Music pioneered a sophisticated blend of glam, art rock and pop, with striking visual style.

History

Their debut album Roxy Music (1972) and For Your Pleasure (1973) combined rock instrumentation with Eno's experimental electronic treatments. After Eno's departure in 1973 (replaced by Eddie Jobson), albums such as Stranded (1973), Country Life (1974) and Siren (1975) — featuring the hit "Love Is the Drug" — brought commercial success in Britain and Europe. The band split in 1976, reformed in 1978 for Manifesto, Flesh + Blood (1980) and the chart-topping Avalon (1982), whose lush, atmospheric singles "More Than This" and "Avalon" defined their late sound. Roxy hit number one in the UK in 1981 with a cover of John Lennon's "Jealous Guy".

Legacy

The band's fusion of retro glamour, modernist art and musical eclecticism influenced punk, new wave, synth-pop and alternative rock acts including the Sex Pistols, Siouxsie and the Banshees, Duran Duran and Franz Ferdinand. Roxy Music were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2019, and undertook a 50th-anniversary reunion tour in 2022.
