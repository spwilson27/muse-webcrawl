Torino Football Club (Italian pronunciation: [toˈriːno]), colloquially referred to as Toro, is an Italian professional football club based in Turin, Piedmont. It currently plays in Serie A. Founded as Foot-Ball Club Torino in 1906, Torino are among the most successful clubs in Italy domestically, with seven league titles and five Coppa Italia trophies.

History

The club was founded in 1906 by dissidents from Juventus together with members of older Turin clubs; the rivalry persists as the Derby della Mole. Torino's greatest era was the 1940s, when the team known as Grande Torino won five consecutive Serie A titles (1943 and 1946–1949) and supplied most of the Italian national team.

On 4 May 1949, tragedy struck: the aeroplane carrying the entire Grande Torino squad crashed into the retaining wall of the Basilica of Superga on the hills above Turin, killing all 31 people on board — players, coaches, journalists and crew. The disaster, known as the Tragedia di Superga, devastated Italian football. Torino have commemorated the team ever since; the hill of Superga remains a place of pilgrimage for supporters.

Torino won their seventh and most recent Scudetto in 1975–76 under Luigi Radice, edging out Juventus. They won the Coppa Italia for the fifth time in 1993 and reached the UEFA Cup final in 1992, losing to Ajax on away goals. Financial troubles led to bankruptcy in 2005; the club was refounded and purchased by publisher Urbano Cairo, returning to Serie A and stabilizing as a mid-table/top-half side.

Stadium

Torino play at the Stadio Olimpico Grande Torino (the former Stadio Comunale), home since 2006; historically the club's home was the beloved Stadio Filadelfia, partially rebuilt and reopened as a training ground and museum site in 2017.

Honours

  • Serie A: 7 titles (1927–28, 1942–43, 1945–46, 1946–47, 1947–48, 1948–49, 1975–76)
  • Coppa Italia: 5 (1935–36, 1942–43, 1967–68, 1970–71, 1992–93)
  • UEFA Cup: runners-up 1991–92
