Impactite

Impactite is rock created or modified by the impact of a meteorite. The term covers impact breccias, impact melt rocks and suevite, and more loosely shocked target rocks and ejecta found in and around impact craters.

Formation

When a large extraterrestrial body strikes the Earth, shock pressures of tens of gigapascals fracture, melt and sometimes vaporise the target rock in seconds. Diagnostic features include shatter cones, shocked quartz with planar deformation features, and high-pressure minerals such as coesite and stishovite.

Examples

Well-known impactites include the suevites of the Ries crater in Germany, the melt rocks of Sudbury in Canada and the tektites — natural glasses — strewn far from some craters.
