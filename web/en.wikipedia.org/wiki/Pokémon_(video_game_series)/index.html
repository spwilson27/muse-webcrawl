Pokémon (video game series)

Pokémon
Genre: Role-playing
Developer: Game Freak
Publisher: Nintendo / The Pokémon Company
First release: 1996 (Pokémon Red and Green)

Pokémon is a Japanese role-playing video game series developed by Game Freak and published by Nintendo and The Pokémon Company. First released in 1996 for the Game Boy with Pokémon Red and Green in Japan, it became the highest-grossing media franchise of all time and one of the best-selling video game series ever, with the core games having sold over 480 million units.

Gameplay

Players take the role of a Pokémon Trainer who catches wild Pokémon — creatures with elemental types and special abilities — and trains them to battle other Trainers. Core mechanics include turn-based battles based on type matchups, trading between game versions (the signature "two-version" releases that encouraged use of the Game Boy Link Cable), completing the Pokédex by catching or seeing every species, and challenging eight Gym Leaders and the Elite Four to become regional Champion. The series' slogan is "Gotta catch 'em all!"

Generations

The series is divided into generations, each introducing a new region, new Pokémon, and usually new hardware: Generation I (Kanto, Game Boy, 1996); II (Johto, Gold and Silver, 1999); III (Hoenn, Ruby and Sapphire, 2002); IV (Sinnoh, Diamond and Pearl, 2006); V (Unova, Black and White, 2010); VI (Kalos, X and Y, 2013) saw the move to full 3D; VII (Alola, Sun and Moon, 2016); VIII (Galar, Sword and Shield, 2019) moved the series to home-console hardware with the Nintendo Switch; IX (Paldea, Scarlet and Violet, 2022) adopted an open-world structure. Spin-offs include Pokémon Snap, the Mystery Dungeon games, and the mobile hit Pokémon Go (2016).

Development and reception

The franchise was created by Satoshi Tajiri, inspired by his childhood hobby of insect collecting, and Ken Sugimori provided the original creature designs. The games are credited with popularising handheld gaming and monster-collecting RPGs, and competitive play is organised officially through the Video Game Championships (VGC) circuit.
