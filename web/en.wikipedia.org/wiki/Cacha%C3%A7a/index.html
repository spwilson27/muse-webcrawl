Cachaça

TypeDistilled spirit
OriginBrazil
Alcohol content38–48% ABV
Base ingredientSugarcane juice

Cachaça is a distilled spirit made from fermented sugarcane juice, and the most popular distilled alcoholic beverage in Brazil. Unlike most rum, which is made from molasses, cachaça is produced from fresh sugarcane juice, giving it a distinctive grassy, earthy flavour.

Brazil produces roughly 800 million litres annually, most of it consumed domestically; it is the essential ingredient of the caipirinha cocktail.

Production

Harvested sugarcane is pressed, and the juice is fermented — traditionally with natural yeasts, sometimes aided by cornmeal or rice — then distilled in copper pot stills (alambiques) or column stills. Industrial cachaça is column-distilled and unaged; artisanal cachaça may be rested or aged in Brazilian woods such as amburana, jequitibá, and balsam.

Classification

Cachaça is classed as branca (white/unaged) or amarela (yellow, lightly aged), with premium aged expressions labelled envelhecida. Brazilian law reserves the name 'cachaça' for Brazilian production, and it is recognised abroad as a distinctive Brazilian product.

History

Distillation of sugarcane spirits in Brazil dates to the 16th century on colonial engenhos (sugar mills), where cachaça was first a drink of enslaved workers before spreading through all levels of society.
