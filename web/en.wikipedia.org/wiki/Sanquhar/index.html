Sanquhar (/ˈsæŋkər/; Scottish Gaelic: Seanchair, "old fort") is a town on the River Nith in Dumfries and Galloway, Scotland. A former royal burgh, it sits on the A76 between Dumfries and Cumnock and is notable for its post office, opened in 1712 and recognised as the oldest working post office in the world, and for the distinctive two-colour "Sanquhar pattern" of knitted gloves.

History

Sanquhar grew around a castle held by the Crichton family; the ruined Sanquhar Castle stands south of the town. In the 17th century the town was associated with the Covenanters: Richard Cameron's followers issued the Sanquhar Declaration of 1680, renouncing allegiance to Charles II, an early statement of resistance to royal absolutism. Coal mining in the surrounding hills supported the town in the 19th and 20th centuries.

Landmarks and culture

  • Sanquhar Post Office (1712) — continuously operating; local campaigns secured its future after closure threats.
  • Tolbooth Museum — housed in the 1735 tolbooth designed in the orbit of William Adam, covering the knitting tradition, mining, and Covenanter history.
  • Sanquhar knitting — geometric black-and-white patterned gloves and knitwear produced since the 18th century.
  • The annual Riding of the Marches festival each August.
