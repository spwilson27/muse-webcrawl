Sir Rudolf Ernst Peierls (5 June 1907 – 19 September 1995) was a German-born British physicist who played a major role in nuclear physics and the Manhattan Project.

Early life

Peierls studied under Sommerfeld, Heisenberg, and Pauli. Of Jewish descent, he left Germany in 1933 and settled in Britain, working at Birmingham and later Oxford as Wykeham Professor of Physics.

War work

With Otto Robert Frisch he wrote the Frisch–Peierls memorandum (1940), the first technical exposition showing an atomic bomb was feasible with a small critical mass of uranium-235, spurring the British Tube Alloys project and the Manhattan Project, in which Peierls worked at Los Alamos on implosion.

Later work

Peierls contributed to solid-state theory (Peierls distortion, Peierls stress), mentored many physicists including Klaus Fuchs — later exposed as a Soviet spy — and was active in the Pugwash movement for nuclear disarmament. See Robert Woodrow Wilson.
