Finnish noun cases

Finnish noun cases Number of cases: 15 (commonly listed)
Language: Finnish (Uralic, Finnic)
Features: agglutinative suffixes, consonant gradation, vowel harmony
Example noun: talo ("house")

Finnish nouns inflect for fifteen cases, expressed by suffixes added to the noun stem, often accompanied by consonant gradation. The case system is a hallmark of Finnish grammar and largely replaces the prepositions used in languages such as English; possessive suffixes and clitics may follow the case ending.

Grammatical cases

The nominative is the dictionary form and the usual subject case. The accusative marks definite direct objects and is formally identical to the nominative or genitive in most contexts. The genitive (-n) indicates possession and is also used with many postpositions. The partitive expresses partiality, indefiniteness, and ongoing action, and is used after numerals (other than one) and many verbs.

Locative cases

Six locative cases form two series: the internal cases inessive (-ssa/-ssä, "in"), elative (-sta/-stä, "out of"), and illative ("into"); and the external cases adessive (-lla/-llä, "on/at"), ablative (-lta/-ltä, "from"), and allative (-lle, "to/onto").

Other cases

The essive (-na/-nä) denotes a state or role, the translative (-ksi) a change of state, the abessive (-tta/-ttä) absence, the comitative (-ine-) accompaniment, and the rare instructive (-n) means or manner, mostly in fixed expressions. Marginal cases such as the prolative are sometimes also recognised.
