Roc-A-Fella Records was an American hip hop record label and multimedia company founded in 1995 by Jay-Z, Damon Dash, and Kareem "Biggs" Burke. Named in a play on the surname of oil magnate John D. Rockefeller, the label grew from an independent imprint distributing Jay-Z's debut album Reasonable Doubt (1996) into one of the dominant forces in hip hop.

The Roc-A-Fella "diamond" logo.

History

After major labels declined to sign Jay-Z, the three founders created Roc-A-Fella to release his music independently, initially through a distribution arrangement with Priority Records and later with Def Jam Recordings. Jay-Z's commercial breakthrough with Vol. 2... Hard Knock Life (1998) expanded the label's roster and influence; Def Jam acquired a stake in the label, and in 2004 bought the founders' remaining shares.

Following the sale, Jay-Z became president of Def Jam, and the Roc-A-Fella roster included artists such as Kanye West, Cam'ron and the Diplomats, Beanie Sigel, Freeway, Memphis Bleek, and later Rihanna and J. Cole-affiliated acts through its later reactivation. The label's original partnership dissolved amid the well-publicised split between Jay-Z and Dash, and activities wound down in the 2010s after a final period under Def Jam.

Legacy

Roc-A-Fella's artist-led business model, its Rocawear clothing line, and its roster's run of landmark albums — including The Blueprint and The College Dropout — made it one of the defining hip hop labels of the late 1990s and 2000s.
