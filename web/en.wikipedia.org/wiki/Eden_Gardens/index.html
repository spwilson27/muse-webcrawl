Eden Gardens is an international cricket stadium in Kolkata, India. Established in 1864, it is the oldest and one of the most famous cricket grounds in India, often described as cricket's answer to the Colosseum. It is the home ground of the Kolkata Knight Riders of the Indian Premier League and is administered by the Cricket Association of Bengal.

History

The ground takes its name from the Eden Gardens park laid out in 1841 beside the Hooghly River, named after Emily and Fanny Eden, sisters of the Governor-General, Lord Auckland. Cricket developed on the Maidan, and the ground hosted its first Test match in 1934, between India and Douglas Jardine's England. For much of the twentieth century Eden Gardens could hold around 100,000 spectators, and its vast, passionate crowds became legendary — as did occasional outbreaks of crowd trouble, notably when rioting ended the 1996 World Cup semi-final against Sri Lanka, which was awarded to the visitors.

Famous matches

Eden Gardens hosted the tied 1987 World Cup final (Australia's first World Cup win, over England) and the 2016 World Twenty20 finals. In 2001 it staged one of Test cricket's greatest matches, when India, following on against Australia, won through V. V. S. Laxman's 281 and Rahul Dravid's 180. Rohit Sharma scored 264 here in 2014, the highest individual score in one-day internationals. Redevelopment for the 2011 World Cup reduced capacity to about 66,000 but modernised the stadium, which remains one of the sport's most atmospheric venues.
