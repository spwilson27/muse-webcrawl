Boom bap

Boom bap is a subgenre and style of hip hop production that rose to prominence on the East Coast of the United States during the golden age of hip hop, from the late 1980s into the 1990s. The term is an onomatopoeia for the sounds of the prominent kick ("boom") and snare ("bap") drum beats that define the style.

Characteristics

Boom bap production typically features hard-hitting drum breaks sampled from funk and soul records, a prominent snare on the two and four, deep kick drums, and chopped melodic samples from jazz, soul, and R&B sources, usually sequenced on machines such as the E-mu SP-1200 and the Akai MPC. The aesthetic emphasizes gritty, loop-based beats and lyrically dense rapping.

Artists

Producers closely associated with boom bap include DJ Premier, Pete Rock, Large Professor, Diamond D, Q-Tip, Marley Marl, and the RZA, with landmark releases by acts such as Gang Starr, A Tribe Called Quest, Wu-Tang Clan, Nas, and The Notorious B.I.G. The style experienced a revival in the 2010s among underground and independent artists.
