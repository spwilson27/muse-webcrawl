The Tao Te Ching (also Daodejing) is a Chinese classic and the foundational text of Taoism, traditionally credited to the sage Laozi. Its 81 short chapters of verse and paradox — opening 'The Tao that can be told is not the eternal Tao' — offer guidance on ruling, living and yielding that has made it one of the most translated works in world literature.

Content and transmission

The text's first part treats the Tao — ineffable source of all things — and the second Te (virtue/power), praising water-like softness, the usefulness of emptiness (a wheel's hub, a room's space), and government by non-action so that people 'transform themselves'. Silk manuscripts from Mawangdui (buried 168 BC) and bamboo slips from Guodian (c. 300 BC) are the earliest witnesses and differ in arrangement from the received Wang Bi edition. Commentaries by Heshang Gong and Wang Bi shaped its two-thousand-year reception.
