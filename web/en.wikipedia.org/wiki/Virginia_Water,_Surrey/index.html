Virginia Water, Surrey

Virginia Water is a wealthy village in the borough of Runnymede in Surrey, England, known for its large, gated mansions, the Wentworth Club golf courses and the ornamental lake of the same name in Windsor Great Park. It lies about 23 miles west of central London, near Egham and the Berkshire border, and is served by a station on the Waterloo to Reading line.

The lake and park

Virginia Water Lake was created in the 18th century under the Duke of Cumberland, ranger of Windsor Great Park; work began in 1746 and the dam was rebuilt after a flood destroyed the first version in 1768. Fed by streams and lined with woodland, the lake is decorated with features including a five-arch bridge, a cascade, a grotto, and the "Roman ruins" — columns and fragments genuinely imported from Leptis Magna in Libya and erected here in the 1820s after first being displayed in London. A 100-foot totem pole, carved by Kwakiutl artists and given by British Columbia in 1958, stands near the lake. The nearby Valley Gardens and Savill Garden are celebrated for their azaleas, rhododendrons and magnolias.

The village

The settlement grew up in the 19th and 20th centuries around the station and the Wentworth estate, developed from the 1920s with large detached houses behind hedges and security gates; the Wentworth Club hosted the first Ryder Cup matches and long hosted the PGA Championship. Virginia Water consistently records some of the highest house prices in England outside London, and has been home to many prominent sportspeople, business figures and entertainers.
