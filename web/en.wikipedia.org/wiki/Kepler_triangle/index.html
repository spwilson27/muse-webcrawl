Kepler triangle

A Kepler triangle is a right triangle formed by three squares whose areas are in geometric progression according to the golden ratio. Its side lengths are therefore in the ratio 1 : √φ : φ, where φ ≈ 1.618 is the golden ratio. It is named after the mathematician and astronomer Johannes Kepler.

History

Kepler first described the triangle in a 1597 letter, noting the golden ratio's special division property. The triangle has been discussed in connection with the geometry of the Great Pyramid of Giza, though claims that its proportions were used intentionally there remain speculative.

Construction

A Kepler triangle can be constructed by drawing a line segment, forming a perpendicular at its midpoint based on the golden section, and connecting the endpoints; its hypotenuse and longer leg then stand in the golden ratio, while the hypotenuse and shorter leg stand in the ratio √φ.
