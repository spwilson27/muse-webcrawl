Nā Pali Coast State Park

Nā Pali Coast State Park
Location: Kauai, Hawaiʻi, United States
Area: 6,175 acres (2,499 ha)
Governing body: Hawaiʻi Division of State Parks

Nā Pali Coast State Park is a 6,175-acre (2,499 ha) state park in the U.S. state of Hawaiʻi, lying on the northwestern shore of the island of Kauai. It encompasses the famed Nā Pali Coast, a rugged stretch of coastline where steep, fluted sea cliffs — pali in Hawaiian — rise as much as 4,000 feet (1,200 m) above the Pacific Ocean, cut by narrow hanging valleys and waterfalls.

Geography and features

The park runs along roughly 15 miles (24 km) of coastline from Keʻe Beach in the northeast to Polihale State Park in the southwest. Its valleys — including Kalalau, Hanakoa, Hanakāpīʻai, Miloliʻi, and Nuʻalolo — shelter streams, waterfalls such as Hanakāpīʻai Falls and Hanakoa Falls, and archaeological remains of ancient Hawaiian settlements, including terraced taro fields, house platforms, and heiau (temples). There are no roads into the park; access is by foot, boat, kayak, or air.

Kalalau Trail

The Kalalau Trail, originally built in the late 1800s, traverses the coast for 11 miles (18 km) from Keʻe Beach to Kalalau Beach. It is widely regarded as one of the most beautiful and most demanding coastal hikes in the United States, crossing five valleys and steep eroded sections before descending to Kalalau. Permits are required for hiking beyond Hanakāpīʻai Valley and for camping, and visitor numbers are limited to protect the fragile environment.

History and ecology

Hawaiians settled the coast's valleys for centuries, farming taro and fishing its waters, before most communities departed by the early 20th century. The area was designated a state park to protect its natural and cultural resources. Native plants and seabirds inhabit the cliffs, although introduced species such as feral goats and pigs have caused erosion and damage that management programs seek to control.

In popular culture

The dramatic scenery has served as a filming location for movies including King Kong (1976), Jurassic Park, The Hunger Games: Catching Fire, and Pirates of the Caribbean: On Stranger Tides, and is a celebrated subject for aerial and boat tours.
