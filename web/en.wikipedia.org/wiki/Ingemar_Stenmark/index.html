Ingemar Stenmark

Born: 18 June 1956, Joesjö, Sweden
Disciplines: Slalom, giant slalom
World Cup wins: 86
Olympic medals: 2 gold (1980)

Jan Ingemar Stenmark (born 18 June 1956) is a Swedish former World Cup alpine ski racer. He is regarded as one of the most prominent Swedish athletes ever, and as the greatest slalom and giant slalom specialist of all time. He competed for Tärna IK Fjällvinden.

Career

Born in Joesjö, Storuman Municipality, in Swedish Lapland, Stenmark began skiing at the age of five. He moved with his family to Tärnaby when he was four, and became neighbours of fellow future skier Stig Strand.

During the years 1976 to 1978, Stenmark won the overall World Cup title three times consecutively. He won a record 86 World Cup races: 46 in giant slalom and 40 in slalom, a record total that still stands. His total of 86 victories was more than double that of the next male skier at the time of his retirement.

At the 1980 Winter Olympics in Lake Placid, Stenmark won gold medals in both the slalom and the giant slalom. He was unable to compete at the 1984 Winter Olympics in Sarajevo because of his professional status, and finished fifth in the giant slalom and out of the medals at the 1988 Winter Olympics in Calgary, his final Olympic appearance.

Legacy

Stenmark was awarded the Svenska Dagbladet Gold Medal in 1975 and 1978, and the Holmenkollen Medal in 1979. In Sweden his dominance made alpine skiing a national spectator sport, and he remains one of the country's most beloved sporting figures. His record of 86 World Cup wins stood as the all-time mark for both men and women for more than three decades.
