Cysticercosis


Neuroimaging in neurocysticercosis (placeholder image)

Specialty: Infectious disease
Cause: Larvae of Taenia solium
Transmission: Ingestion of tapeworm eggs (fecal–oral)

Cysticercosis is a parasitic tissue infection caused by the larval stage (cysticerci) of the pork tapeworm Taenia solium. People develop cysticercosis by ingesting tapeworm eggs shed in the feces of a human tapeworm carrier — not by eating undercooked pork, which instead causes adult tapeworm infection (taeniasis).

Clinical features

Larval cysts lodge in muscle, skin, eyes and the central nervous system. Cysts in the brain — neurocysticercosis — are a leading cause of acquired epilepsy in endemic regions of Latin America, sub-Saharan Africa and Asia; symptoms such as seizures and headaches often appear as cysts degenerate and provoke inflammation. Diagnosis rests on neuroimaging (CT or MRI) and serology.

Treatment and prevention

Treatment may involve antiparasitic drugs (albendazole, praziquantel) given with corticosteroids to control inflammation, antiepileptic medication, and surgery for ocular or ventricular disease; antiparasitics are avoided in eye involvement because killing cysts there can cause blindness. Prevention centers on sanitation, hand hygiene, pig husbandry and meat inspection. The World Health Organization lists cysticercosis among the neglected tropical diseases.
