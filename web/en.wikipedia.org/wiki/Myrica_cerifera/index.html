Myrica cerifera

FamilyMyricaceae
GenusMyrica
Common namesSouthern wax myrtle, southern bayberry
Native rangeSoutheastern North America, Central America, Caribbean

Myrica cerifera is an evergreen tree or large shrub in the family Myricaceae, native to North and Central America and the Caribbean. Common names include southern wax myrtle, southern bayberry, candleberry, and tallow shrub. It is sometimes classified in the genus Morella as Morella cerifera.

Description

The plant grows up to about 14 metres tall, often with multiple slender trunks. Its aromatic, glossy leaves are lance-shaped with serrated edges and dotted with resin glands. Small blue-grey fruits are coated with a waxy substance that historically was boiled off to make bayberry candles and soap — the source of the specific epithet cerifera, "wax-bearing".

Ecology and uses

Myrica cerifera grows in wetlands, pine flatwoods, and coastal scrub, tolerating salt spray and poor soils. Its roots host nitrogen-fixing actinobacteria, improving the soil. The fruits are eaten by many birds, including the yellow-rumped warbler, once called the "myrtle warbler" for its reliance on them. The plant is widely used in landscaping as a fast-growing screen, and the root bark has been used in traditional herbal medicine.
