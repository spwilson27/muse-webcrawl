The Return of the King

Author: J. R. R. Tolkien
Published: 20 October 1955
Series: The Lord of the Rings, volume 3

The Return of the King is the third and final volume of J. R. R. Tolkien's epic fantasy novel The Lord of the Rings, first published in 1955. It contains Books V and VI, together with extensive appendices chronicling the history, languages, and genealogies of Middle-earth.

Plot

Book V follows the defense of Minas Tirith: Gandalf and Pippin reach Gondor, Aragorn comes by the Paths of the Dead with the Army of the Dead, and the Battle of the Pelennor Fields sees the fall of the Witch-king at the hands of Éowyn and Merry. Aragorn then leads the Armies of the West to the Black Gate as a diversion. Book VI returns to Frodo and Sam: Sam rescues Frodo from Cirith Ungol, and at Mount Doom the Ring is destroyed when Gollum bites it from Frodo's hand and falls into the fire. The volume closes with Aragorn's coronation, the hobbits' return and the Scouring of the Shire, and Frodo's departure from the Grey Havens.

Appendices

The appendices — especially Appendix A (including the Tale of Aragorn and Arwen) and Appendix B, the Tale of Years — provide the historical framework of Tolkien's legendarium.

Adaptations

The volume has been adapted in the 1980 animated film (see The Return of the King (1980 film)) and as the concluding part of Peter Jackson's 2003 film, which won eleven Academy Awards.
