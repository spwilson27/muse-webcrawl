Human Rights Watch

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Human Rights Watch (HRW) is an international non-governmental organisation, headquartered in New York City, founded in 1978 as Helsinki Watch to monitor Soviet-bloc compliance with the Helsinki Accords. Renamed in 1988 as regional "Watch" committees merged, it conducts research and advocacy on human rights in about 100 countries.

Methods and influence

HRW researchers document abuses by governments and armed groups, publish detailed reports timed for media and policy impact, and press for prosecutions and policy change ("naming and shaming" professionalised). It played prominent roles in campaigns for the landmine ban treaty (sharing the 1997 Nobel Peace Prize as a coalition member), the creation of the International Criminal Court, and documentation of conflicts from Rwanda and the Balkans to Syria, Myanmar and Ukraine. It accepts no government funding, relying on private donations.

Criticism

Critics — including targeted governments and occasionally other scholars — have alleged selective emphases and methodological or regional biases; a notable internal controversy followed its founder Robert Bernstein's 2009 public criticism of its Middle East coverage. HRW maintains that its methodology of rigorous fact-finding applies universal standards to allies and adversaries alike.
