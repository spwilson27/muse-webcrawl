The Oberammergau Passion Play (German: Oberammergauer Passionsspiele) is a passion play that has been performed every ten years since 1634 by the inhabitants of the village of Oberammergau in Bavaria, Germany. It portrays the Passion of Jesus — his trial, suffering and death — as told in the Gospels.

Origin

According to tradition, during the Thirty Years' War the bubonic plague reached Oberammergau and killed many villagers. In 1633 the village council vowed that if God spared them from further deaths, they would perform a play depicting the life and Passion of Christ every ten years. No further deaths are said to have occurred, and the first performance took place in 1634 in the village churchyard.

The production

The play involves over 2,000 performers, musicians and stage technicians, all of whom must be residents of Oberammergau; men traditionally grow their hair and beards from Ash Wednesday of the preceding year. Performances take place in the purpose-built Passion Play Theatre, an open-air stage seating about 4,500 spectators. A season typically runs from May to October and draws around half a million visitors. The play was postponed from 2020 to 2022 because of the COVID-19 pandemic.
