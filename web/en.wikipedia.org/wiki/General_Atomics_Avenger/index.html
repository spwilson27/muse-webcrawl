General Atomics Avenger

The General Atomics Avenger (formerly Predator C) is a developmental unmanned combat aerial vehicle built by General Atomics Aeronautical Systems for the United States military. Unlike the turboprop-powered MQ-1 Predator and MQ-9 Reaper, the Avenger is powered by a turbofan engine and incorporates stealth features such as an internal weapons bay.

Role: Unmanned combat aerial vehicle
Manufacturer: General Atomics Aeronautical Systems
First flight: 4 April 2009
Status: In development / experimental

Design and development

The Avenger first flew on 4 April 2009. It uses a Pratt & Whitney Canada PW545B turbofan, a "V" tail, and an S-shaped exhaust to reduce radar and infrared signatures. It can carry up to about 3,500 pounds (1,600 kg) of ordnance internally and on wing hardpoints, including the GBU-12 Paveway II laser-guided bomb.

Variants

An extended-range version, the Avenger ER, increased endurance to more than 20 hours with a longer wing and greater fuel capacity. A carrier-capable concept informed General Atomics' entry in the U.S. Navy's Unmanned Carrier-Launched Airborne Surveillance and Strike (UCLASS) program.

Specifications

The Avenger is about 44 feet (13 m) long with a wingspan of 66 feet (20 m) on the ER variant. Maximum speed is around 460 mph (740 km/h) and service ceiling exceeds 40,000 feet (12,000 m).
