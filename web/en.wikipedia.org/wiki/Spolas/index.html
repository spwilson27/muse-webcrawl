Spolas

The spolas (Greek: σπολάς) was a type of body armour used in ancient Greece. It was a cuirass made of leather rather than bronze or linen, worn by Greek soldiers, including hoplites and lighter troops, as a less expensive and more flexible alternative to the metal muscle cuirass and the linothorax.

Ancient sources distinguish the spolas from the stolidion and the linothorax; Xenophon and other writers mention it among the equipment of Greek infantry, and lexicographers describe it as a thick leather thorax. Literary evidence suggests it was constructed from heavy hides, possibly doubled or reinforced, shaped to the torso and fastened over the shoulders and sides, in a form broadly analogous to other Greek tube-and-yoke cuirasses.

Debate on construction

Because no complete example survives, the precise construction of the spolas is debated among historians and re-enactors. Some reconstructions treat it as essentially a linothorax made of leather rather than glued linen layers; others regard it as a simpler single-piece leather jerkin. Depictions in Greek art of warriors wearing apparently flexible, non-metallic torso armour are sometimes interpreted as representing the spolas, though such identifications remain uncertain.
