Bombing of Guernica
Date: 26 April 1937
Location: Guernica, Basque Country, Spain
Attackers: Condor Legion, Aviazione Legionaria
Part of: Spanish Civil War

The bombing of Guernica on 26 April 1937 was an aerial bombing of the Basque town of Guernica during the Spanish Civil War. It was carried out by Nazi Germany's Condor Legion and Fascist Italy's Aviazione Legionaria on behalf of Francisco Franco's Nationalist forces.

Background

Guernica, home to the symbolic Oak of Guernica, held cultural significance for Basques. It was market day, and the town was crowded with civilians and refugees.

The bombing

Waves of Heinkel He 111 and Junkers Ju 52 bombers, escorted by fighters, dropped high explosives and incendiaries over about three hours. Much of the town was destroyed and hundreds killed; estimates range from 170 to over 1,600.

Legacy

International outrage followed reporting by George Steer in The Times. Pablo Picasso's painting Guernica became a famous anti-war symbol.
