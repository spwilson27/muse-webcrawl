Honda Racing Corporation

The Honda Racing Corporation (HRC) is a subsidiary of the Honda Motor Company formed in 1982. The company combines participation in motorcycle racing throughout the world with the development of high-performance racing machines. Its racing activities are a central source of technology for Honda's production motorcycles, and HRC also sells racing parts and provides support to teams competing with Honda machinery.

History

Honda's racing roots go back to company founder Soichiro Honda, who entered the Isle of Man TT in 1959 and won the manufacturers' championship in the 125cc and 250cc classes by 1961. HRC was formally established on September 1, 1982, consolidating Honda's motorcycle competition activities into a single specialist company headquartered in Asaka, Saitama, Japan.

Road racing

HRC is best known for its factory effort in the MotoGP World Championship, run for many years under Repsol Honda colors. Riders such as Mick Doohan, Valentino Rossi, Nicky Hayden, Casey Stoner, and Marc Márquez won premier-class world championships on HRC machines; the Honda RC213V became one of the most successful Grand Prix motorcycles of the modern era. HRC also competes in the Superbike World Championship and in endurance racing, including repeated victories at the Suzuka 8 Hours.

Off-road and other disciplines

In motocross and supercross, HRC fields factory teams in Japan, Europe, and the United States. Its rally team won the Dakar Rally in 2020 and 2021 with the CRF450 Rally, ending KTM's long winning streak. HRC also supports trial competition through the Repsol Honda Team, which with Toni Bou has dominated both outdoor and indoor (X-Trial) world championships. In 2022, Honda consolidated its automobile racing operations—including its Formula One power unit program—under the HRC name, adding four-wheel activities in Sakura, Japan, to the corporation's remit.
