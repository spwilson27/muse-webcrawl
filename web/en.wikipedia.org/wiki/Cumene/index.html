Cumene

Chemical formulaC9H12
IUPAC nameisopropylbenzene
Appearancecolourless liquid
Boiling point~152 °C

Cumene (isopropylbenzene) is an organic compound, an aromatic hydrocarbon with an isopropyl substituent on a benzene ring. It is a colourless, flammable liquid and a constituent of crude oil and refined fuels.

Production and use

Cumene is produced industrially by the Friedel–Crafts alkylation of benzene with propylene, catalysed by phosphoric acid or, in modern plants, zeolites. Almost all cumene output is consumed in the cumene process, in which it is oxidised to cumene hydroperoxide and cleaved to phenol and acetone — the dominant industrial route to both products.

Safety

Cumene is an irritant and is listed as reasonably anticipated to be a human carcinogen by the U.S. National Toxicology Program; the cumene process itself requires careful management of the explosive hydroperoxide intermediate.
