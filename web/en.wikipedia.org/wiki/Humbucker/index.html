Humbucker

A humbucker, or double-coil pickup, is a type of guitar pickup that uses two wire coils to cancel out ("buck") the mains hum and electromagnetic interference picked up by single-coil pickups. The design was developed in the 1950s; Gibson engineer Seth Lover's PAF ("Patent Applied For") pickup, introduced in 1957, became the most famous example.

How it works

The two coils are wound in opposite directions and their magnets have opposite polarity, so interference induced equally in both coils cancels when their signals are combined, while the string signal — affected differently by the reversed magnetic field — is reinforced. The result is a quieter pickup with a thicker, warmer, higher-output sound than a single coil.

Use

Humbuckers are standard on Gibson-style guitars such as the Les Paul and are widely used in rock, metal, and jazz. Variations include mini-humbuckers, rail pickups, and coil-splitting designs that allow one coil to be switched off for a single-coil-like tone.
