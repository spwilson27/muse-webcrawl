Peeping Tom

OriginLegend of Lady Godiva, Coventry
MeaningA voyeur; one who spies on others

A Peeping Tom is a person who secretly observes others, especially for sexual gratification — a voyeur. The expression comes from the legend of Lady Godiva.

The legend

According to the later tradition attached to the Godiva story, the people of Coventry agreed to stay indoors with their shutters closed while Lady Godiva rode naked through the town in protest against her husband's oppressive taxes. A tailor named Tom, however, bored a hole in his shutters and peeped at her as she passed; in some versions he was struck blind or dead for his disobedience. The character of Tom does not appear in the earliest accounts of Godiva and was a later addition to the tale, but it gave the English language its enduring term for a voyeur.

Usage

The phrase is used both literally, for acts of voyeurism that are criminal offences in many jurisdictions, and figuratively for any prying observer. It also gives its name to Michael Powell's controversial 1960 film Peeping Tom, about a cameraman who murders women while filming them.
