The National Assembly (Hungarian: Országgyűlés) is Hungary's unicameral national legislature. It elects the President of the Republic, the Prime Minister and other high officials, adopts the constitution and laws, and approves the state budget. Since the electoral reform of 2011 it has had 199 members, reduced from 386, elected for four-year terms.

Composition and elections

Members are elected by a mixed system: 106 in single-member constituencies by first-past-the-post voting and 93 from national party lists, with votes cast for losing constituency candidates also counting toward the lists ("fragment votes"). The assembly meets in the monumental Hungarian Parliament Building on the bank of the Danube in Budapest, completed in 1904 and one of the largest parliament buildings in the world. Its presiding officer is the Speaker of the National Assembly, second in the order of precedence after the President.

History

Hungary's parliamentary tradition reaches back to the medieval Diet, which met in various cities and from the nineteenth century in Pest. The modern National Assembly emerged in the democratic transition of 1989–1990, when the first free elections since 1945 were held. Amendments to the electoral system under the governments of Viktor Orbán, and the adoption of the Fundamental Law of 2011, reshaped the institution, reducing its size and consolidating a system that has returned large Fidesz majorities in successive elections since 2010.
