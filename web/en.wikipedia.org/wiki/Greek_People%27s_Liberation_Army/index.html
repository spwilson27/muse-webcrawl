The Greek People's Liberation Army (ELAS; Greek: Ελληνικός Λαϊκός Απελευθερωτικός Στρατός) was the military arm of the National Liberation Front (EAM), the largest resistance organisation in Axis-occupied Greece during the Second World War. Founded in 1942 and strongly influenced by the Communist Party of Greece, ELAS grew to control large parts of mountainous Greece — "Free Greece" — by 1943.

Activities

ELAS guerrillas harassed German and Italian occupation forces, most famously taking part in Operation Harling in November 1942, when Greek resistance fighters and British commandos destroyed the Gorgopotamos railway viaduct. ELAS also fought rival resistance groups such as EDES in what amounted to a first round of civil war during the occupation.

Disbandment

After the liberation of Greece in 1944, tensions between ELAS and the British-backed government erupted in the Dekemvriana fighting in Athens in December 1944. Under the Treaty of Varkiza in February 1945, ELAS agreed to disarm and disband. Many of its former fighters were later persecuted, and the conflict fed into the Greek Civil War of 1946–1949.
