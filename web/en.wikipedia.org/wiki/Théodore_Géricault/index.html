Théodore Géricault

Jean-Louis André Théodore Géricault (26 September 1791 – 26 January 1824) was a French painter and lithographer, one of the pioneers of the Romantic movement. Though he died at 32, his dramatic, emotionally charged canvases profoundly influenced younger artists, notably Eugène Delacroix.

The Raft of the Medusa

His masterpiece, The Raft of the Medusa (1818–19), depicts survivors of the wreck of the French frigate Méduse clinging to a makeshift raft. Based on a contemporary scandal — incompetent leadership had caused the shipwreck and the abandonment of 147 people on a raft, of whom only 15 survived — the monumental canvas (about 5 × 7 metres) caused a sensation at the 1819 Paris Salon and became an icon of French Romanticism. Géricault interviewed survivors, studied corpses and built a scale model of the raft in preparation.

Later work

Other major works include The Charging Chasseur (1812), Wounded Cuirassier (1814), racehorse scenes painted in England, and a late series of portraits of the insane commissioned by Dr Étienne Georget. Weakened by riding accidents and illness, Géricault died in Paris in 1824.
