Székely Land

Székely Land (Hungarian: Székelyföld; Romanian: Ținutul Secuiesc) is a historic and ethnographic region in eastern Transylvania, in central Romania, inhabited mainly by the Székelys, a Hungarian-speaking ethnic group. The region comprises the modern counties of Harghita and Covasna and part of Mureș County, with main towns including Miercurea Ciuc (Csíkszereda), Sfântu Gheorghe (Sepsiszentgyörgy), Odorheiu Secuiesc (Székelyudvarhely), and Târgu Mureș (Marosvásárhely). Hungarians form a large majority in Harghita (about 85%) and Covasna (about 74%) counties.

History

In the medieval Kingdom of Hungary, the Székelys were organized as a military frontier community in self-governing territorial "seats" (székek), enjoying collective privileges in exchange for border defence — a status they retained until the administrative reforms of 1876. After the First World War, the Treaty of Trianon (1920) assigned Transylvania, including Székely Land, to Romania. Hungary briefly regained the northern part under the Second Vienna Award (1940–1944). Between 1952 and 1968 a Hungarian Autonomous Region (later Mureș-Hungarian Autonomous Region) existed in the area under communist Romania; it was abolished by the Ceaușescu regime.

Autonomy movement

Since the fall of communism, Székely political organizations — notably the Democratic Alliance of Hungarians in Romania and the Székely National Council — have campaigned for territorial autonomy for the region, with periodic large demonstrations such as the Székely Freedom Day marches. Autonomy proposals have repeatedly failed in the Romanian parliament, and the question — together with Hungarian-language education, bilingual signage, and the use of the Székely flag — remains a recurring point of tension in Romanian–Hungarian relations.

Culture

Székely culture is marked by carved wooden gates (székelykapu), distinctive folk dress, runic-influenced wood carving traditions, and a strong Roman Catholic heritage in Csík alongside Reformed and Unitarian communities. Regional symbols include the Székely flag — azure with a golden band, sun, and crescent moon — adopted by the Székely National Council in 2004.
