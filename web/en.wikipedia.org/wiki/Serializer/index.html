Serializer

In computer science and telecommunications, a serializer is a circuit or software component that converts data from a parallel form into a serial form. In electronics, the serializer takes multiple parallel input lines and multiplexes them onto a single output line, transmitting the bits one after another. Its counterpart, the deserializer, performs the reverse operation; the pair is commonly known as a SerDes (serializer/deserializer).

In digital electronics

Hardware serializers are widely used in high-speed communication links where sending data in parallel over many traces would require excessive board area, power, and connector pins, and would suffer from skew between lines. A serializer typically consists of a shift register or a multiplexer tree driven by a clock that is a multiple of the parallel data rate. Technologies such as PCI Express, Serial ATA, Ethernet physical layers, and HDMI all rely on serializers operating at multiple gigabits per second.

In software

The term is also applied metaphorically to software components that perform data serialization — the translation of data structures or object state into a format (such as JSON, XML, or Protocol Buffers) that can be stored or transmitted and reconstructed later. In web frameworks, a serializer class commonly defines how complex objects, such as database model instances, are converted to and from native data types and rendered formats.
