Ralphie the Buffalo is the live mascot of the Colorado Buffaloes at the University of Colorado Boulder. A female American bison, Ralphie leads the football team onto the field at Folsom Field at the start of each half, running a lap of the field guided by five student handlers — one of the most celebrated traditions in American college football.

History

Colorado students first adopted a buffalo mascot in 1934, initially a costumed student and later a rented bison calf. A live buffalo began appearing regularly in 1949. The name "Ralphie" dates to 1966, when the freshman class purchased a buffalo and named her Ralphie; the name (despite every Ralphie being female, since males grow too large and aggressive) has been kept by each successor.

Successive mascots are numbered: Ralphie I served 1966–1978, and subsequent Ralphies have each run for roughly a decade. Ralphie V (2008–2019) became especially well known; Ralphie VI ("Ember") debuted in 2021 and retired after the 2024 season, with Ralphie VII taking over in 2025.

The run and handlers

At each home game Ralphie is led from her trailer pen by five handlers — varsity student athletes selected through competitive tryouts — who guide her at speeds that can exceed 20 mph (32 km/h) around the field in a horseshoe pattern before she is returned to her pen. Handlers train with her on a ranch outside Boulder, where she lives under the care of the Ralphie Live Mascot Program, funded by donations.

Recognition

Ralphie's run is frequently ranked among the best traditions and entrances in college football, and the mascot has appeared in national advertising and at events including a Denver Broncos game and the university's commencement ceremonies. Retired Ralphies live out their lives on ranch land in Colorado.
