AGM-179 JAGM

The AGM-179 Joint Air-to-Ground Missile (JAGM) is an American air-to-surface missile developed by Lockheed Martin to replace the AGM-114 Hellfire, the BGM-71 TOW, and the AGM-65 Maverick in United States service. It is launched from rotary-wing aircraft, unmanned aircraft, and other platforms.

Design

The JAGM combines the Hellfire Romeo's airframe, motor, and warhead with a new dual-mode seeker uniting semi-active laser guidance with a millimeter-wave radar, enabling fire-and-forget engagements in poor weather and against countermeasures. A later increment, JAGM-MR (Medium Range), adds an imaging infrared channel for a tri-mode seeker and extends range.

Development and service

The program grew out of the cancelled Joint Common Missile effort. Development testing took place through the 2010s, and the missile entered low-rate production in 2018, reaching initial operational capability on the AH-64E Apache and later the AH-1Z Viper. It has been exported to allied operators of the Apache.
