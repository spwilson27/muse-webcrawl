The Old Town of Prague (Czech: Staré Město pražské) is a medieval settlement of Prague, Czech Republic. It was separated from the outside by a semi-circular moat and wall, connected to the Vltava river at both ends; the moat is now covered by streets (Revoluční, Na Příkopě, and Národní) that remain the boundary of the cadastral district. Notable places in the Old Town include Old Town Square, the Astronomical Clock, the Estates Theatre, and Charles University.

History

From its earliest days, around the 9th century, the Old Town was made up of settlements spreading from Prague Castle along the right bank of the Vltava. A marketplace existed by the 11th century at what is now Old Town Square, and the settlement received town privileges in the 13th century, ringed by the fortifications of Wenceslaus I. In 1348 Charles IV founded the New Town beyond the walls, and in 1784 the Old Town was merged with the other Prague towns into a single city. The adjacent Jewish quarter, Josefov — birthplace of Franz Kafka — was largely demolished in a slum clearance between 1893 and 1913, though its synagogues, cemetery, and town hall survive.

Old Town Square

The square has been the city's public centre since the Middle Ages. Around it stand the Old Town Hall (founded 1338) with its Astronomical Clock (Orloj, installed 1410), the Gothic Church of Our Lady before Týn, the Baroque St. Nicholas Church, and Kinský Palace. In its centre rises the Jan Hus Memorial (unveiled 1915). The square witnessed the execution of 27 Bohemian lords after the Battle of White Mountain in 1621, the 1948 Communist coup's proclamation from the Kinský Palace balcony, and the celebrations of the Velvet Revolution in 1989.
