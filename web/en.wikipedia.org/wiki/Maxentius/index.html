Maxentius

Marcus Aurelius Valerius Maxentius (c. 278 – 28 October 312) was a Roman emperor who ruled Italy and North Africa from 306 until his death in battle against Constantine the Great at the Battle of the Milvian Bridge. The son of the emperor Maximian, he seized power in Rome in defiance of the tetrarchic system but was never recognised as legitimate by the other tetrarchs.

Rise to power

Passed over in the succession arrangements of 305, Maxentius remained in retirement near Rome. When the emperor Galerius attempted to extend taxation to Rome and disband the Praetorian Guard, the city revolted; on 28 October 306 Maxentius was proclaimed emperor with Praetorian and senatorial support. His father Maximian emerged from retirement to support him, though the two later quarrelled. Maxentius defeated invasions by Severus (who was captured and killed) and by Galerius, who was forced to retreat from Italy.

Rule

Maxentius presented himself as the defender of Rome and its traditions, styling himself conservator urbis suae ("preserver of his city"). He undertook a major building programme in Rome, including the Basilica of Maxentius in the Forum, the Circus of Maxentius and the mausoleum of his son Romulus on the Appian Way, and extensive work on the Aurelian Walls. He was broadly tolerant of Christians after initial ambiguity. His rule was troubled by the revolt of Domitius Alexander in Africa, which cut Rome's grain supply, and by worsening relations with Constantine.

Death

In 312 Constantine invaded Italy, defeating Maxentius's forces at Turin and Verona. At the Milvian Bridge just north of Rome on 28 October 312, Maxentius's army was routed and Maxentius himself drowned in the Tiber as the bridge of boats collapsed. Constantine's victory — which Christian tradition linked to his vision of the cross — left him master of the western empire. The Senate condemned Maxentius's memory, though Constantine completed and rededicated his basilica.
