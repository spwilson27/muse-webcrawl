Operation Uranus (Russian: Опера́ция «Ура́н», Operatsiya "Uran") was the successful Soviet strategic offensive of 19–23 November 1942 during the Battle of Stalingrad in the Second World War. The operation encircled the German Sixth Army, the Fourth Panzer Army and their Romanian allies in the Stalingrad pocket, and is regarded as the turning point of the war on the Eastern Front.

Planning

The plan was developed by Generals Georgy Zhukov and Aleksandr Vasilevsky during the autumn of 1942. While the German Sixth Army and Fourth Panzer Army bled themselves in street fighting inside Stalingrad, their long flanks were held by Romanian, Italian and Hungarian armies with weak anti-tank defences. The Soviets secretly massed over a million men, 13,000 guns, nearly 900 tanks and 1,100 aircraft on both sides of the city, under strict deception measures.

The offensive

On 19 November, after a massive artillery barrage in fog and snow, the Southwestern and Don Fronts smashed through the Romanian Third Army north-west of Stalingrad; the following day the Stalingrad Front broke the Romanian Fourth Army to the south. Soviet tank and cavalry corps raced into the depth, and on 23 November the pincers met near Kalach-on-Don, completing the encirclement of some 265,000–300,000 Axis troops.

Consequences

Hitler forbade a breakout and relied on an airlift that delivered only a fraction of the supplies required. Field Marshal Erich von Manstein's relief attempt, Operation Winter Storm, failed in December, and the Soviet Operation Little Saturn threatened the entire German position in the Caucasus. The Sixth Army, starving and freezing, surrendered at the end of January 1943 — a catastrophe from which Germany never recovered the strategic initiative in the east.
