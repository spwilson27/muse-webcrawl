New Jerusalem Monastery

LocationIstra, Moscow Oblast, Russia
Founded1656
FounderPatriarch Nikon
Full nameResurrection Monastery (Voskresensky Monastery)

New Jerusalem Monastery, also known as the Resurrection (Voskresensky) Monastery, is a major monastery of the Russian Orthodox Church located in the town of Istra in Moscow Oblast, about 60 km northwest of Moscow. It was founded in 1656 by Patriarch Nikon of Moscow as a patriarchal residence and as a deliberate, measured copy of the holy places of Palestine: the surrounding landscape was renamed to evoke the Holy Land — the river Istra became the Jordan, hills took the names of Mount Zion, the Mount of Olives, and Gethsemane — so that Russian believers could make pilgrimage without travelling to Ottoman-held Jerusalem.

Architecture

The centrepiece, the Resurrection Cathedral, was modelled on the Church of the Holy Sepulchre in Jerusalem and built to drawings and measurements brought from the original. It contains a reproduction of the Aedicule over the tomb of Christ and a vast rotunda crowned by a tented roof. Nikon's reforms and the monastery's construction were entangled with the schism (Raskol) that split the Russian Church; after Nikon's fall in 1666, work was completed under his successors and the Tsar.

Later history

The monastery was secularised under Catherine the Great, revived in the 19th century as one of Russia's most visited pilgrimage sites, and closed by the Soviet authorities in 1919, becoming a museum. It suffered severe damage in 1941, when retreating German forces blew up parts of the complex, including the great bell tower. Post-war and especially post-1990 restoration has returned it to the Church and progressively reconstructed the cathedral and walls, including a full rebuilding of the bell tower in the 2010s. The monastery takes its name from the heavenly city described in Revelation — see New Jerusalem.
