Shirakami-Sanchi (白神山地, "white god mountain range") is a mountainous region in the northern part of Japan's main island of Honshu, straddling the border of Aomori and Akita prefectures. It contains the last remaining virgin stand of Siebold's beech forest in Japan, part of a primeval beech forest that once covered much of northern Japan.

Description

The core zone of about 17,000 hectares is largely roadless and undeveloped. The forest is home to Japanese black bears, Japanese serows, golden eagles, and black woodpeckers. Notable features include the Anmon Falls and the Juniko (Twelve Lakes) area.

World Heritage status

Shirakami-Sanchi was among the first sites in Japan inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List, in 1993, recognized for its undisturbed beech forest ecosystem.
