Birmingham Back to Backs

The central courtyard of Court 15, the last surviving back-to-back court in Birmingham.

The Birmingham Back to Backs (also known as Court 15) are the last surviving back-to-back houses in Birmingham, England, now preserved as a museum by the National Trust. Located at 50–54 Inge Street and 55–63 Hurst Street, at the edge of the city's Chinese Quarter, the houses were built in the nineteenth century to accommodate the rapidly growing population of industrial Birmingham.

Back-to-back housing

Back-to-back houses were built sharing rear walls, arranged around a communal courtyard, with access to half the houses only through the court. Cheap and quick to erect, they packed working-class families into tiny rooms with shared privies, wash houses and water pumps; by the mid-nineteenth century some 200,000 people in Birmingham lived in such courts. Conditions were notoriously crowded and unsanitary, and back-to-backs were banned from new construction by the Housing and Town Planning Act of 1909, though existing examples remained occupied for decades.

The museum

Court 15 was built in stages from the late 1830s to the 1870s and remained occupied until 1966; its occupants included a glass-eye maker, a jeweller, a cabinet maker and a tailor, reflecting Birmingham's trades. Restored by the National Trust with the Birmingham Conservation Trust, the houses opened as a museum in 2004. Several interiors are furnished as they would have appeared in successive periods — the 1840s, 1870s, 1930s and 1970s — and the courtyard retains a communal wash house. Visits are by guided tour only.
