The NFC North is one of the four divisions of the National Football Conference (NFC) in the National Football League (NFL). It comprises the Chicago Bears, Detroit Lions, Green Bay Packers, and Minnesota Vikings — four of the league's oldest franchises, concentrated in the American Midwest.

History

The division's core dates to the NFL's Western Conference and the NFC Central, created after the AFL–NFL merger in 1970, which for many years also included the Tampa Bay Buccaneers. When the NFL realigned into eight four-team divisions in 2002, Tampa Bay moved to the NFC South and the remaining teams formed the NFC North. The division is famous for some of the league's fiercest and longest-running rivalries, especially Bears–Packers, which dates to 1921.

Characteristics

Known for cold-weather football and historically strong defences — reflected in nicknames like the Packers' "Frozen Tundra" of Lambeau Field and Chicago's "Monsters of the Midway" — the division champion receives an automatic home playoff game, and its teams frequently contend for wild-card berths.
