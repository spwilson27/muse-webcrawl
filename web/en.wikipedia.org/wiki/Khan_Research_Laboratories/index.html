Khan Research Laboratories

Dr. A. Q. Khan Research Laboratories
Location: Kahuta, Punjab, Pakistan
Founded: 1976
Founder: Abdul Qadeer Khan
Field: Uranium enrichment, missile development

The Dr. A. Q. Khan Research Laboratories (KRL), formerly known at various times as Project-706 and the Engineering Research Laboratories, is a Pakistani federally funded research and development laboratory located in Kahuta, Punjab province. Established in 1976 by the metallurgist Abdul Qadeer Khan, it was the centre of Pakistan's uranium enrichment programme, producing the highly enriched uranium used in the country's first nuclear weapons, tested in May 1998.

History

After India's 1974 nuclear test, Prime Minister Zulfikar Ali Bhutto authorised a crash programme to develop nuclear weapons. Abdul Qadeer Khan, who had worked at the URENCO enrichment consortium in the Netherlands, returned to Pakistan and was placed in charge of the enrichment effort in 1976. At Kahuta, Khan's team built gas centrifuges based on URENCO designs and, by the early 1980s, was enriching uranium. The site was renamed in Khan's honour by President Muhammad Zia-ul-Haq in 1981.

In addition to enrichment, KRL developed the Ghauri series of liquid-fuelled ballistic missiles, derived from North Korean Nodong technology, and conducted research in metallurgy, electronics and materials science.

Proliferation controversy

In 2004, Abdul Qadeer Khan confessed on national television to having transferred nuclear technology through a clandestine international network supplying Iran, Libya and North Korea with centrifuge designs and components. He was pardoned by President Pervez Musharraf and placed under house arrest, and Pakistan denied that the laboratory as an institution was involved, attributing the network to Khan personally. Revelations about the network led to investigations in several countries and to Libya's abandonment of its nuclear programme.
