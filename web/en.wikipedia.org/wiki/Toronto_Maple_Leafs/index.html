The Toronto Maple Leafs (officially the Toronto Maple Leaf Hockey Club and often referred to simply as the Leafs) are a professional ice hockey team based in Toronto. They compete in the National Hockey League (NHL) as a member of the Atlantic Division in the Eastern Conference. The club is one of the "Original Six" NHL teams and one of the league's founding franchises, established in 1917.

History

The team began play in 1917 as the Toronto Arenas, winning the Stanley Cup in the NHL's inaugural season. Renamed the Toronto St. Patricks in 1919 (winning another Cup in 1922), the club was bought by Conn Smythe in 1927 and renamed the Maple Leafs. Under Smythe, the Leafs built Maple Leaf Gardens (1931) and won the Cup again in 1932.

Toronto's dynasty years came in the 1940s under coach Hap Day and captain Ted "Teeder" Kennedy, winning five Stanley Cups between 1942 and 1949, including the famous 1942 comeback from a 3–0 deficit against Detroit. A second dynasty under Punch Imlach won four Cups in the 1960s (1962, 1963, 1964 and 1967). The 1967 championship — won by the oldest team in NHL history — was the last of the Original Six era.

Since 1967 the Leafs have endured the longest Stanley Cup drought in the NHL, marked by the turbulent Harold Ballard ownership years, near-misses in 1993 and 2002, and repeated early playoff exits in the modern era despite star players such as Darryl Sittler, Mats Sundin, Doug Gilmour and Auston Matthews. The team remains one of the most valuable and widely supported franchises in hockey; Leafs games are a central institution of Toronto sporting life.

Home arenas

The Leafs played at Maple Leaf Gardens from 1931 to 1999, then moved to the Air Canada Centre (now Scotiabank Arena), shared with the Toronto Raptors.

Rivalries

Toronto's historic rivals include the Montreal Canadiens (the oldest rivalry in the NHL), the Ottawa Senators ("Battle of Ontario"), the Detroit Red Wings and the Boston Bruins.
