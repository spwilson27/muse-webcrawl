Ancestral domain

Related conceptIndigenous land rights
Philippine lawIndigenous Peoples' Rights Act of 1997 (RA 8371)

Ancestral domain is a concept in law and anthropology referring to the lands, territories and resources that indigenous peoples have traditionally owned, occupied or used. It encompasses not only agricultural and residential land but also forests, waters, hunting and fishing grounds, and sacred and burial sites held under native title since time immemorial.

In the Philippines

The term is especially associated with the Philippines, where the Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act of 1997 (Republic Act No. 8371) recognises the rights of Indigenous Cultural Communities and Indigenous Peoples to their ancestral domains. The National Commission on Indigenous Peoples issues Certificates of Ancestral Domain Title (CADTs), formalising communal ownership, and communities prepare Ancestral Domain Sustainable Development and Protection Plans to govern resource use.

General principles

Recognition of ancestral domain rests on the idea that indigenous tenure predates the modern state, so title arises from continuous occupation rather than from a government grant. International instruments such as the UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples affirm rights to traditional territories and require free, prior and informed consent for projects affecting them.
