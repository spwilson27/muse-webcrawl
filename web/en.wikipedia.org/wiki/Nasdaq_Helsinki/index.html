Nasdaq Helsinki, formerly the Helsinki Stock Exchange (Finnish: Helsingin Pörssi), is the principal stock exchange of Finland, located in Helsinki. It is part of Nasdaq Nordic, the Nordic and Baltic exchange group operated by Nasdaq, Inc., and lists the shares of most major Finnish companies, historically dominated by Nokia at its peak.

History

The exchange was founded in 1912 and for most of the twentieth century operated as a cooperative association of its members in a neoclassical building in central Helsinki. Trading was fully automated by the end of the 1980s, when the HETI system replaced open outcry. In 2003 the exchange merged with the Swedish OM group to form OMX; OMX was acquired by Nasdaq in 2008, and the exchange was rebranded Nasdaq OMX Helsinki and later simply Nasdaq Helsinki.

Market structure

The exchange operates a main market and the Nasdaq First North Growth Market for smaller companies. Its best-known benchmark is the OMX Helsinki 25 index, composed of the 25 most traded shares. Listed companies span sectors from banking and forestry to technology and gaming, including names such as Nordea (before its move of headquarters to Helsinki made it a Finnish listing), Kone, Neste, UPM and Nokia.
