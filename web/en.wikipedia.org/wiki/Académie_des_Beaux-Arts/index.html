The Académie des Beaux-Arts (French for "Academy of Fine Arts") is a French learned society and one of the five academies of the Institut de France. It was created in 1816 by merging the older Académie de peinture et de sculpture (founded 1648), the Académie de musique, and the Académie d'architecture.

Role

The Academy brings together elected members — artists, architects, composers, engravers, photographers and filmmakers — organised in sections. It awards prizes, manages foundations and bequests supporting the arts, owns historic properties such as the Villa Medici in Rome (home of the Prix de Rome), and advises the French state on artistic matters.

Historical influence

Through the École des Beaux-Arts and the official Salon exhibitions, the Academy long defined the standards of academic art in France and strongly influenced art education across Europe and the Americas in the 19th century.
