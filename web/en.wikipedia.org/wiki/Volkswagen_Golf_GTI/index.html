Volkswagen Golf GTI

Volkswagen Golf GTI
Manufacturer: Volkswagen
Production: 1976–present
Class: Hot hatch / sport compact
Layout: Front-engine, front-wheel-drive

The Volkswagen Golf GTI is the high-performance version of the Volkswagen Golf compact car, first introduced in June 1976. The GTI is widely credited with popularizing the "hot hatch" concept — a practical hatchback family car endowed with sports-car performance. The initials stand for Gran Turismo Iniezione (Grand Touring Injection), referring to its fuel-injected engine.

History

The original GTI was developed in secret by a small team of Volkswagen engineers led by Anton Konrad. It used a 1.6-litre fuel-injected engine producing 110 PS, giving a top speed of about 182 km/h (113 mph) in a body fitted with distinctive red grille trim, tartan ("Clark plaid") seat upholstery, and a golf-ball gear knob. A right-hand-drive version reached the United Kingdom in 1979.

Generations

  • Mk1 (1976–1983) – the original; later 1.8-litre versions followed.
  • Mk2 (1984–1992) – larger body; 16-valve and supercharged G60 variants.
  • Mk3 (1992–1998) – first with the VR6 engine option in related models.
  • Mk4 (1998–2005) – more refined; 1.8T turbocharged engine.
  • Mk5 (2005–2009) – acclaimed return to form with a 2.0-litre turbo FSI engine.
  • Mk6 (2009–2013) – evolution of Mk5 with electronic differential lock (XDS).
  • Mk7 (2013–2020) – MQB platform; up to 230 PS, Performance pack available.
  • Mk8 (2020–present) – 245 PS 2.0-litre turbo, digital cockpit.

Legacy

The Golf GTI's blend of speed, practicality and affordability influenced generations of sporty hatchbacks from rival manufacturers and is regularly cited among the most important cars of the 20th century.
