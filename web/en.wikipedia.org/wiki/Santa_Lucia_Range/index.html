The Santa Lucia Range (also the Santa Lucia Mountains) is a mountain range in the California Coast Ranges of west-central California, running about 105 miles (170 km) from southeast of Carmel and Monterey southeast to the vicinity of San Luis Obispo. The range rises precipitously from the Pacific Ocean, creating the famous Big Sur coastline, and reaches its highest elevation at Junipero Serra Peak (5,862 ft / 1,787 m).

Geography and ecology

The seaward slopes are among the steepest coastal gradients in the contiguous United States — at Cone Peak the summit rises over 5,100 feet within about three miles of the shore. The range divides the Salinas Valley to the east from the Pacific; major drainages include the Carmel River, Big Sur River, and the headwaters of the Nacimiento and San Antonio rivers. Much of the range lies within Los Padres National Forest, including the Ventana Wilderness, and the eastern portion within Fort Hunter Liggett.

Vegetation grades from coastal redwood groves in moist canyon bottoms, through mixed evergreen forest and oak woodland, to chaparral and knobcone and Coulter pine on dry ridges. The range is the historic heartland of the reintroduced California condor, released in Big Sur by the Ventana Wildlife Society, and its steep, roadless country supports mountain lions, black bears and one of California's most significant remaining wild trout streams.

History and culture

The range takes its name from Saint Lucy of Syracuse, bestowed by the Spanish explorer Sebastián Vizcaíno in 1602. It was home to the Esselen and Salinan peoples; Spanish missions at Carmel, Soledad and San Antonio de Padua ringed its margins. In the 20th century the Big Sur portion became synonymous with a literary and artistic colony — Henry Miller, Robinson Jeffers (whose Tor House stands at the range's northern foot), Edward Weston and others — and with Highway 1, the state scenic route engineered along the range's coastal flank in the 1930s.

The Tassajara Zen Mountain Center, the oldest Japanese Buddhist monastery outside Asia, sits in a remote valley within the range, and the Esalen Institute perches on its cliffs to the south. The range has burned repeatedly in major wildfires, including the Marble Cone (1977), Basin Complex (2008), Soberanes (2016) and Dolan (2020) fires.
