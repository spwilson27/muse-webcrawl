Picornaviridae

Picornaviridae is a family of viruses in the order Picornavirales. The name derives from pico-, meaning small, and RNA, referring to their small positive-sense single-stranded RNA genomes. Vertebrates, including humans, serve as natural hosts. The family includes many important human and animal pathogens, such as poliovirus, the rhinoviruses that cause the common cold, hepatitis A virus, and foot-and-mouth disease virus.

Structure

Picornaviruses are non-enveloped viruses with an icosahedral capsid about 30 nanometres in diameter. The capsid is composed of 60 protomers, each made up of four structural proteins (VP1–VP4 in most genera). Inside the capsid is a single molecule of positive-sense single-stranded RNA of roughly 7,000 to 8,800 nucleotides, with a small viral protein, VPg, covalently attached to its 5′ end and a polyadenylated 3′ end.

Genome and replication

The genome is translated as a single polyprotein, which is cleaved by virus-encoded proteases into structural and non-structural proteins. Translation is initiated at an internal ribosome entry site (IRES) rather than by the cap-dependent mechanism used by host mRNAs. Replication occurs in the cytoplasm in association with rearranged host membranes, and new virions are typically released by cell lysis, although some picornaviruses can also be released in vesicles.

Genera and diseases

The family contains several dozen genera. Notable examples include:

  • Enterovirus — poliovirus, coxsackieviruses, echoviruses, enterovirus D68
  • Rhinovirus — the principal cause of the common cold
  • Hepatovirus — hepatitis A virus
  • Aphthovirus — foot-and-mouth disease virus of livestock
  • Cardiovirus — encephalomyocarditis virus, Theiler's virus
  • Parechovirus — human parechoviruses
