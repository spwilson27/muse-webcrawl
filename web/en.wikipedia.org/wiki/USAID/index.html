The United States Agency for International Development (USAID) was an agency of the United States government primarily responsible for administering civilian foreign aid and development assistance. Established by President John F. Kennedy in 1961 under the Foreign Assistance Act, USAID was for decades the largest single bilateral aid agency in the world, operating in more than 100 countries.

Mission and programs

USAID's work spanned global health (including the President's Emergency Plan for AIDS Relief, PEPFAR), food security (Feed the Future), humanitarian and disaster relief through its Bureau for Humanitarian Assistance, education, democracy and governance, water and sanitation, and economic development. Its humanitarian programs distributed aid marked "From the American People".

History

The agency traced its lineage to the Marshall Plan and Truman's Point Four program. For most of its existence it operated as an independent federal agency under the foreign policy guidance of the Secretary of State, with missions in dozens of countries and a workforce of Foreign Service officers, civil servants and contractors.

Dismantling in 2025

In early 2025, the second Trump administration moved to dismantle USAID: most staff were placed on leave or terminated, its headquarters closed, and its functions were largely absorbed into the Department of State, with the agency's remaining programs formally transferred on July 1, 2025. The move was contested in court and debated internationally, with critics warning of effects on global health programs, including HIV/AIDS treatment and famine relief, and defenders arguing the agency was wasteful and misaligned with US interests.

Criticism

Throughout its history USAID was criticised both for tying aid to US political and commercial interests and for inefficiencies; evaluators also credited it with major achievements, including contributions to smallpox-era public health campaigns, oral rehydration therapy, and large-scale HIV treatment.
