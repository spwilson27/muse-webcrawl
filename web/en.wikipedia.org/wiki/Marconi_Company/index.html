The Marconi Company was a British telecommunications and engineering company founded by Guglielmo Marconi in 1897 as the Wireless Telegraph & Signal Company to exploit his inventions in radio telegraphy. Based for most of its life in Chelmsford, Essex, it became one of the pioneers of long-distance radio communication, broadcasting, radar, and telecommunications equipment.

History

Marconi registered his first patent for wireless telegraphy in 1896 and formed his company the following year. In 1899 it achieved the first wireless transmission across the English Channel, and in December 1901 Marconi received the first transatlantic radio signal at Signal Hill, Newfoundland. The company, renamed Marconi's Wireless Telegraph Company in 1900, built a global network of coastal stations and equipped ships with wireless sets; its operators played a famous role in the rescue of survivors of the RMS Titanic in 1912.

The company's Chelmsford works — opened in 1912 in Hall Street and later at New Street — hosted some of the earliest experimental entertainment broadcasts in Britain, including Dame Nellie Melba's famous concert of June 1920. The Marconi Company was one of the founding shareholders of the British Broadcasting Company (the future BBC) in 1922.

Later years

In 1946 the company was acquired by English Electric, and in 1968 it passed to the General Electric Company (GEC) in GEC's takeover of English Electric. As GEC-Marconi it became a major supplier of defence electronics, radar, avionics and communications systems. In 1999 GEC renamed itself Marconi plc and refocused on telecommunications equipment; the company suffered a catastrophic collapse in the dot-com crash of 2001, and most of its businesses were sold. The Marconi name was finally retired in 2006 when Ericsson acquired the bulk of the remaining company, while the defence interests had earlier passed into BAE Systems.
