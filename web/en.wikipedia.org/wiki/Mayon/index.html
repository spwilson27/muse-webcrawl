Mayon (Central Bikol: Bulkan Mayon), also known as Mount Mayon and Mayon Volcano, is an active stratovolcano in the province of Albay in the Bicol Region of Luzon, Philippines. Rising 2,463 metres above the Albay Gulf, it is renowned for its almost perfectly symmetrical cone, often cited as the world's most perfect volcanic cone, and is the most active volcano in the Philippines.

Eruptions

Mayon has erupted more than fifty times in recorded history. Its most destructive eruption, on 1 February 1814, buried the town of Cagsawa, killing around 1,200 people; the belfry of the ruined Cagsawa church still protrudes from the lava field and is a famous landmark. Explosive eruptions in 1897, 1993 and 2006 caused further deaths, and in January 2018 lava fountains and ash columns forced the evacuation of tens of thousands of residents. A permanent danger zone extends six kilometres from the crater.

Geology and ecology

The volcano is an andesitic stratovolcano built up by alternating deposits of lava and pyroclastic material, part of the Bicol volcanic chain. Its slopes are protected within Mayon Volcano Natural Park, established in 2000, which supports tropical forest and endemic wildlife.

Folklore

Mayon's name derives from the Bikol word magayon, "beautiful". Local legend associates the mountain with the tragic lovers Daragang Magayon and Pangaronon, the volcano rising over the heroine's grave.
