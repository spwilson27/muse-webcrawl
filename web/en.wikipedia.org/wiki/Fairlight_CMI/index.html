Fairlight CMI

The Fairlight CMI (Computer Musical Instrument) is a digital synthesizer, music sampler and digital audio workstation introduced in 1979 by the Australian company Fairlight, founded by Peter Vogel and Kim Ryrie. It was one of the first digital sampling synthesizers and had a major influence on 1980s popular music.

History

Vogel and Ryrie developed the CMI from earlier digital synthesis experiments. Series I (1979) offered 8-bit sampling; Series II (1982) added the famous Page R sequencer; Series III (1985) raised quality to 16-bit and added more voices and MIDI.

Features

Users could record real sounds, edit waveforms on a green-screen monitor with a light pen, and sequence patterns graphically. Its library of preset sounds — such as the orchestral hit (ORCH5) — became signatures of 1980s pop, hip hop and film scores.

Legacy

Extremely expensive when new, the CMI helped define sampling culture before cheaper samplers such as the E-mu Emulator and Akai S900 appeared. Peter Vogel later released iOS recreations.
