Extremely large telescope (ELT)
Primary mirror25 m (82 ft) or larger
ExamplesELT (39.3 m), TMT (30 m), GMT (24.5 m)
Key technologySegmented mirrors, adaptive optics

An extremely large telescope (ELT) is an astronomical observatory featuring an optical telescope with a primary mirror of diameter 20 metres or more, designed for the ultraviolet, visible or infrared wavelengths. ELTs are the successors to the 8-to-10-metre class telescopes of the 1990s and rely on segmented primary mirrors, active optics and advanced adaptive optics to achieve their collecting area and resolution.

Projects

  • The Extremely Large Telescope of the European Southern Observatory, with a 39.3-metre segmented primary of 798 hexagonal segments, is under construction on Cerro Armazones in Chile; its name was shortened from "European Extremely Large Telescope".
  • The Thirty Meter Telescope (TMT), with a 30-metre primary of 492 segments, is planned by an international partnership for Mauna Kea, Hawaii, or La Palma.
  • The Giant Magellan Telescope (GMT) will combine seven 8.4-metre mirrors for an effective aperture of about 24.5 metres, at Las Campanas Observatory in Chile.

Science goals

ELT-class instruments are expected to directly image Earth-like exoplanets and characterise their atmospheres, resolve stellar populations in distant galaxies, measure the expansion of the universe in real time (the "Sandage test"), and study the first stars and galaxies. A cancelled even larger concept, the 100-metre Overwhelmingly Large Telescope, helped define the technology path for the current generation.
