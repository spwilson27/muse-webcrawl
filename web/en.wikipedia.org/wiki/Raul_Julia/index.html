Raúl Rafael Juliá y Arcelay (March 9, 1940 – October 24, 1994) was a Puerto Rican actor. He rose to fame with his work on stage, particularly with the New York Shakespeare Festival, and later achieved international success in film. His career spanned four decades of theatre, film and television, and he is remembered for roles ranging from the political prisoner Valentin in Kiss of the Spider Woman to Gomez Addams in The Addams Family and Addams Family Values.

Early life

Juliá was born in Floral Park, San Juan, Puerto Rico, into a family that owned a pioneer local restaurant chain, La Cueva del Chicken Inn. Educated at Colegio Espíritu Santo, he initially bowed to family expectations by studying law at the University of Puerto Rico, but abandoned legal studies to pursue acting, first locally and then in New York, where he moved in 1964 and supported himself with odd jobs while training and auditioning.

Stage and film career

In New York he joined Joseph Papp's New York Shakespeare Festival, gaining acclaim in productions including Titus Andronicus and Two Gentlemen of Verona, and appeared in the original Broadway cast of the musical The Threepenny Opera as Mack the Knife, earning Tony Award nominations. He also played Dracula on Broadway and Rafael in the rock opera Two Gentlemen of Verona.

His film breakthrough came with Kiss of the Spider Woman (1985), for which he received a Golden Globe nomination and the National Board of Review Award for Best Actor. Other major films include Compromising Positions (1985), The Morning After (1986), Tequila Sunrise (1988), Moon over Parador (1988), Romero (1989) — in which he played the assassinated Archbishop Óscar Romero — Presumed Innocent (1990), The Rookie (1990), The Addams Family (1991) and Addams Family Values (1993). His final film, Street Fighter (1994), cast him as the villain M. Bison; he took the role, by his own account, to make a film his children could enjoy.

Death and legacy

Juliá, who had been diagnosed with stomach cancer, suffered a stroke in October 1994 and died on 24 October 1994, aged 54. His body was flown to Puerto Rico, where a state funeral was held. Posthumously, he won the Emmy Award and the Golden Globe for his performance as Chico Mendes in the television film The Burning Season (1994). Beyond acting, he was active in humanitarian causes, notably with The Hunger Project, and lent his name and time to Latino theatre and mentoring initiatives.
