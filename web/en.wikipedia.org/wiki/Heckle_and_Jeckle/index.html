Heckle and Jeckle are twin cartoon magpie characters created by Paul Terry for the Terrytoons studio. They are a pair of identical yellow-billed magpies who speak in incongruous accents — Heckle in a Brooklyn/New York voice and Jeckle in a refined English one — and who calmly outwit their adversaries through trickery, teamwork, and unflappable deadpan humour. Their signature sign-off is some variant of "Well, goodbye, chum!" — "Cheerio, chum!"

History

The characters debuted in The Talking Magpies (released January 1946), in which a farmer couple, tired of blizzards, decide to move to a warmer climate — selling the farm to the magpies. Some fifty theatrical cartoons followed through 1966, making Heckle and Jeckle among Terrytoons' most popular stars alongside Mighty Mouse. Their usual antagonists included the bulldog Dimwit, the fox Chesty, and various cats and farmers.

The duo transitioned to television on CBS in the 1950s (in The Heckle and Jeckle Cartoon Show, 1956–1971) and returned in Filmation's The New Adventures of Mighty Mouse and Heckle & Jeckle (1979–1981). They also headlined their own comic books in the 1950s.

Characters and style

Unlike many cartoon duos, Heckle and Jeckle were not hero and sidekick but true equals, finishing each other's plans and sharing the same dry wit; their unflappability under provocation distinguished them from more frantic contemporaries. The name is a play on Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde and the verb "to heckle".
