Health claim

A health claim is a statement on a food label or in advertising that suggests a relationship between consuming a food or ingredient and health, including claims that a food reduces the risk of a disease. Health claims are regulated in many jurisdictions to prevent misleading consumers.

Regulation

In the United States, the Food and Drug Administration authorises health claims supported by significant scientific agreement, and permits qualified health claims with disclaimers where evidence is weaker. Structure/function claims (e.g. "calcium builds strong bones") follow separate rules. In the European Union, the European Food Safety Authority evaluates claims, and only authorised claims listed in EU law may be used; a large proportion of submitted claims, notably for botanicals and probiotics, were rejected.

Types

Claims are commonly divided into disease risk-reduction claims, nutrient function claims and other function claims. Nutrient content claims ("low fat", "high fibre") are related but distinct, describing composition rather than health effects.
