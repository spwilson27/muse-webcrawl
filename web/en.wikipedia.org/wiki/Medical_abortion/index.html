Medical abortion


Mifepristone and misoprostol, the two medicines most commonly used

A medical abortion, also known as medication abortion or non-surgical abortion, occurs when drugs are used to bring about an abortion. The most widely used regimen combines mifepristone with misoprostol; misoprostol alone is an alternative where mifepristone is unavailable. Medical abortion has been shown to be effective through the first trimester, and regimens are also used in the second trimester.

Regimens

The standard combined regimen involves taking mifepristone, a progesterone receptor antagonist, followed one to two days later by misoprostol, a prostaglandin analogue that induces uterine contractions and expulsion of the pregnancy. Reported effectiveness for the combined regimen in early pregnancy is around 95–99%. Methotrexate combined with misoprostol is an older, less commonly used alternative.

Safety and use

The World Health Organization includes the regimen on its List of Essential Medicines and its guidelines endorse self-management of medical abortion in early pregnancy under appropriate conditions. Side effects commonly include cramping and bleeding heavier than a menstrual period; serious complications are uncommon. Since its approval in France and China in 1988 and in the United States in 2000, medication abortion has grown to account for the majority of abortions in many countries, and access expanded further with telemedicine prescribing and mail delivery.

History

Mifepristone (RU-486) was developed by the French pharmaceutical company Roussel Uclaf in the 1980s. Misoprostol, originally marketed for preventing gastric ulcers, was found to induce abortion and was used off-label in Brazil and elsewhere before formal regimens were established.
