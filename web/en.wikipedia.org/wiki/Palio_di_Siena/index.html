Palio di Siena

LocationPiazza del Campo, Siena, Italy
Dates2 July and 16 August each year
First runMedieval origins; modern form from 1633
Competitors10 of the 17 contrade

The Palio di Siena (known locally simply as Il Palio) is a horse race held twice each year, on 2 July and 16 August, in Siena, Italy. Ten horses and riders, bareback and dressed in the colours of the city wards (contrade), represent ten of the seventeen contrade in a race around the Piazza del Campo, on which a thick layer of earth has been laid. The July race, the Palio di Provenzano, honours the Madonna of Provenzano, and the August race, the Palio dell'Assunta, honours the Assumption of Mary.

History

The Palio's origins lie in medieval public games in Siena, including races run across the city and other contests in the Campo. Racing in the shell-shaped piazza in its modern circular form became established in the 17th century, with the first such Palio run in 1633. The contrade system — seventeen districts with their own emblems, churches, museums and fierce rivalries — grew up alongside the race and still structures much of Sienese civic life.

The race

Only ten contrade compete in each Palio: the seven that did not run in the corresponding race of the previous year, plus three drawn by lot. Horses are assigned to the contrade by lot in the tratta, blessed in the contrada church ("va' e vinci" — go and win), and ridden bareback by hired jockeys (fantini). The race itself consists of three laps of the piazza, about a kilometre in total, and usually lasts little more than a minute. A horse can win even without its rider, provided it keeps its head ornament (spennacchiera). The prize is the palio itself: a painted silk banner, newly commissioned for each race.

Festivities

Each Palio is preceded by days of trials, processions, flag-throwing displays and open-air contrada dinners, and by the Historical Parade (Corteo Storico) in Renaissance costume immediately before the race. The intensity of contrada loyalty — and of the rivalries, alliances and behind-the-scenes dealings surrounding jockeys and horses — makes the Palio far more than a sporting event for the Sienese.
