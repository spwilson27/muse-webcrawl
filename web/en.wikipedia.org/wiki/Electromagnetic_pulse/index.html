Electromagnetic pulse

An electromagnetic pulse (EMP), also referred to as a transient electromagnetic disturbance (TED), is a brief burst of electromagnetic energy. The origin of an EMP can be natural or artificial, and it can occur as an electromagnetic field, as an electric field, as a magnetic field, or as a conducted electric current. The electromagnetic interference caused by an EMP can disrupt communications and damage electronic equipment; an EMP such as a lightning strike can physically damage objects such as buildings and aircraft.

Characteristics

An EMP is characterised by a very fast rise time and short duration compared with ordinary electromagnetic signals, and by energy spread across a broad range of frequencies. Pulses can couple into electrical and electronic systems through antennas, power and data cables, and apertures, inducing damaging voltages and currents. Types of EMP include lightning electromagnetic pulses (LEMP), electrostatic discharge (ESD), meteoric EMP from the passage of a meteoroid, coronal mass ejections (solar EMP), and the nuclear electromagnetic pulse (NEMP) produced by a nuclear explosion.

Nuclear EMP and HEMP

A high-altitude nuclear explosion produces a high-altitude electromagnetic pulse (HEMP) through the Compton scattering of gamma rays in the upper atmosphere, creating electric fields that can extend over a continental area. The 1962 Starfish Prime test, detonated 400 km above the Pacific, caused electrical damage in Hawaii some 1,400 km away. HEMP is conventionally described in three phases (E1, E2, E3): a fast, intense E1 spike, an intermediate E2 component similar to lightning, and a slow E3 magnetohydrodynamic component that can induce currents in long conductors such as power grids. These effects have led to military hardening programmes and to wider concern about the vulnerability of civilian infrastructure.

Non-nuclear weapons and protection

Non-nuclear EMP weapons, sometimes called e-bombs, generate intense pulses using explosively pumped flux compression generators or high-power microwave sources, and are intended to disable electronics over a local area. Protection against EMP effects includes Faraday cages, shielding, surge suppression, filtering, and the military hardening standards applied to critical systems. Management of EMP risk is a specialised branch of electromagnetic compatibility engineering.
