Bellini (cocktail)

A Bellini is a cocktail made with Prosecco and white peach purée or juice. It originated in Venice, Italy, and is one of Italy's most famous drinks. The mixture of sparkling wine and peach gives the drink its characteristic pink hue, said to recall the colour of a saint's toga in a painting by the Venetian artist Giovanni Bellini, after whom the cocktail is named.

Bellini
TypeCocktail (wine cocktail)
Ingredients10 cl Prosecco; 5 cl fresh white peach purée
PreparationPour peach purée into a chilled flute, add Prosecco, stir gently
ServedStraight up, in a champagne flute
OriginHarry's Bar, Venice, c. 1934–1948

History

The Bellini was invented sometime between 1934 and 1948 by Giuseppe Cipriani, founder of Harry's Bar in Venice. Because white peaches are seasonal, the drink was originally a seasonal special; it became a year-round favourite after a French entrepreneur began shipping fresh white-peach purée to Harry's Bar in New York. Cipriani named it for the painter Giovanni Bellini after an exhibition of his works in Venice in 1948. The International Bartenders Association lists the Bellini among its official cocktails.

Variations

Using nectarine or other fruit purées yields related drinks; the Puccini uses mandarin juice, the Rossini strawberry purée, and the Tintoretto pomegranate juice — all named, like the original, after Italian artists. A non-alcoholic version substitutes soda or sparkling water for the Prosecco. Frozen and bottled ready-to-drink Bellinis are also widely sold.
