Dhū Nuwās (Arabic: ذُو نُوَاس), whose regnal name was Yūsuf Asʾar Yathʾar (also given as Yūsuf ibn Sharhabil), was the last king of the Himyarite Kingdom of Yemen, ruling in the early 6th century CE (c. 517/523–525). A convert to Judaism — his name means "He of the Sidelocks" — he is remembered chiefly for the massacre of the Christians of Najran, an event echoed in Syriac martyrologies and traditionally associated with the Qur'anic story of the "People of the Ditch" (Ashab al-Ukhdud).

Reign and the Najran massacre

Dhu Nuwas seized the Himyarite throne and campaigned against the Christian city of Najran, whose inhabitants refused to renounce their faith. According to Christian sources such as the Book of the Himyarites and the letters of Simeon of Beth Arsham, thousands were executed around 523 CE, many burned in trenches of fire. Himyarite inscriptions of Yusuf celebrate his campaigns against Aksumite garrisons and their allies, though they present the wars in different terms.

Fall

The persecution prompted intervention by the Christian Kingdom of Aksum (Ethiopia), encouraged by the Byzantine Empire. Around 525 CE the Aksumite king Kaleb invaded, defeated and killed Dhu Nuwas — legend holds that he rode his horse into the sea rather than be captured — and installed a client ruler, ending Himyar's independence. The episode forms part of the background to the later Persian occupation of Yemen and to pre-Islamic Arabian history.
