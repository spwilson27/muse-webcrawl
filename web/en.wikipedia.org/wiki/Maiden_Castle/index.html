Maiden Castle, Dorset

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The massive concentric chalk ramparts of Maiden Castle rolling across the Dorset Downs
The massive concentric chalk ramparts of Maiden Castle rolling across the Dorset Downs

Maiden Castle is a large Iron Age hill fort about 2.5 km south-west of Dorchester in Dorset, England — among the largest and most complex hillforts in Europe, with multiple ramparts enclosing some 19 hectares. Occupied from the Neolithic (a causewayed enclosure and long barrow underlie it) through the Roman period, it is famous for Mortimer Wheeler's 1930s excavation and its 'war cemetery' of the Roman conquest era.

History

The Iron Age defences — up to four lines of rampart and ditch with elaborately staggered entrances, a maze against attackers — were built and rebuilt between about 600 BC and the Roman invasion. After the conquest (which Wheeler dramatised as a storming by Vespasian's legion; modern reassessment is more cautious), a Romano-British temple stood within, and the site's name ('mai dun', perhaps 'great hill') outlived its people.

Excavation

Wheeler's 1934–37 dig made Maiden Castle the showpiece of modern British field archaeology — its skeletons with spine injuries became iconic images of the Roman conquest in schoolbooks — and its archive remains a touchstone in debates about invasion narratives in archaeology.
