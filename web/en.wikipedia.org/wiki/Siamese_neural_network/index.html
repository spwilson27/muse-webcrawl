Siamese neural network

A Siamese neural network (sometimes called a twin neural network) is an artificial neural network architecture that uses two or more identical subnetworks — identical configuration, parameters, and weights — to process separate input vectors and produce comparable output vectors, typically embeddings. By comparing these outputs with a distance or similarity function, the network learns a metric under which similar inputs lie close together and dissimilar inputs far apart.

History

The architecture was introduced in the early 1990s for signature verification by researchers at AT&T Bell Laboratories, including Jane Bromley and Yann LeCun, who trained two identical networks with shared weights and a contrastive energy function to decide whether two signatures came from the same person. The approach later powered face verification systems, most famously the DeepFace and FaceNet line of work, where a triplet variant — processing an anchor, a positive example, and a negative example — was trained with the triplet loss introduced by Google researchers in 2015.

Training

Siamese networks are typically trained with pairs or triplets rather than class labels. The contrastive loss pulls together embeddings of matching pairs and pushes non-matching pairs apart by at least a margin. Triplet loss requires the anchor–positive distance to be smaller than the anchor–negative distance by a margin, making mining of informative ("hard") triplets a central practical concern. Because only relative similarity must be learned, Siamese networks are well suited to one-shot learning, in which a class is represented by a single example and new inputs are classified by nearest-neighbour comparison in the embedding space.

Applications

Applications include face and signature verification, fingerprint and speaker recognition, visual object tracking (SiamFC and successors), near-duplicate detection, sentence-pair similarity and question answering in natural language processing, drug discovery (matching molecular structures), and entity resolution in databases. Modern sentence embedding models such as Sentence-BERT are direct descendants of the Siamese architecture.
