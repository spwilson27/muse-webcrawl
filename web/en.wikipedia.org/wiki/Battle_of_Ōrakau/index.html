Battle of Ōrakau

The Battle of Ōrakau, fought from 31 March to 2 April 1864 in the Waikato region of New Zealand's North Island, was the last major engagement of the Invasion of the Waikato during the New Zealand Wars. Some 300 Māori defenders, including women and children, under leaders of Ngāti Maniapoto, Ngāti Raukawa and Waikato iwi — among them Rewi Maniapoto — held a hastily built pā at Ōrakau against a British and colonial force of about 1,400 men under Brigadier-General George Carey. The battle is remembered above all for the defenders' famous reply to a call to surrender and for their desperate breakout on the final day.

The site of the Ōrakau pā, near modern Kihikihi.

The siege

The pā at Ōrakau was incomplete, lacked a water supply, and was quickly surrounded. British artillery and Armstrong guns bombarded the position while sappers dug a sap toward the defences. When called upon to surrender — with an offer that the women and children could leave — the defenders replied with words that became legendary in New Zealand: "Ka whawhai tonu mātou, Āke! Āke! Āke!" — "We will fight on forever, forever, forever!" A separate offer for the women to come out was answered, according to tradition, by Ahumai Te Paerata: "If the men die, the women and children must die also."

Breakout and aftermath

On the afternoon of 2 April, with ammunition and water exhausted, the defenders made a sudden mass breakout through the British lines to the south-east, crossing the Puniu River under fire. Around 160 Māori were killed, including many wounded who were bayoneted, while British losses were about 17 killed and 50 wounded. The survivors escaped into the King Country. Ōrakau effectively ended the Waikato war; the Māori King movement withdrew beyond the aukati (boundary line) and large areas of Waikato land were subsequently confiscated. The battle has been commemorated in memorials, in Rudall Hayward's 1925 film Rewi's Last Stand, and in continuing calls for a national day of remembrance of the New Zealand Wars.
