Rent (musical)

Rent is a rock musical with music, lyrics and book by Jonathan Larson. Loosely based on Giacomo Puccini's 1896 opera La bohème, it tells the story of a group of impoverished young artists struggling to survive in New York City's East Village under the shadow of HIV/AIDS.

History

Larson worked on the musical for years before it premiered off-Broadway at New York Theatre Workshop in 1996. Larson died suddenly of an aortic dissection the night before the off-Broadway premiere. The show transferred to Broadway's Nederlander Theatre in April 1996, won the Pulitzer Prize for Drama and the Tony Award for Best Musical, and ran for 12 years, closing in September 2008 after 5,123 performances — one of the longest runs in Broadway history.

Plot and songs

Over the course of a year, filmmaker Mark, songwriter Roger, professor Collins, drag performer Angel, dancer Mimi, performance artist Maureen and others confront eviction, addiction, illness and loss. Well-known numbers include "Seasons of Love", "La Vie Bohème", "One Song Glory" and "Take Me or Leave Me".

Adaptations

A film adaptation directed by Chris Columbus was released in 2005, featuring much of the original cast, and a live television version, Rent: Live, aired in 2019.
