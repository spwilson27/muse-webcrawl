Treaty ports

Treaty ports were ports in China, Japan and Korea opened to foreign trade and residence by unequal treaties imposed by Western powers and, later, Imperial Japan in the 19th and early 20th centuries.

China

The first five Chinese treaty ports — Canton (Guangzhou), Amoy (Xiamen), Foochow (Fuzhou), Ningpo (Ningbo) and Shanghai — were opened by the Treaty of Nanking (1842) ending the First Opium War. Later treaties, including the Treaty of Tientsin (1858) and the Convention of Peking (1860), added many more, including inland ports on the Yangtze. Foreign residents enjoyed extraterritoriality.

Japan and Korea

Japan opened Shimoda and Hakodate under the Convention of Kanagawa (1854) and further ports after 1858; it renegotiated the treaties by the 1890s. Korea opened ports after the Japan–Korea Treaty of 1876.

Abolition

Extraterritorial rights in China were largely relinquished by Britain and the United States in 1943. See Unequal treaties and Shanghai International Settlement.
