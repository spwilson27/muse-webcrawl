Dissolution of Czechoslovakia

The dissolution of Czechoslovakia, known in Czech and Slovak as the division of Czechoslovakia and nicknamed the Velvet Divorce, took effect on 1 January 1993 and was the self-determined and peaceful division of the federation of Czechoslovakia into the independent countries of the Czech Republic and Slovakia.

Dissolution of Czechoslovakia
Date: 1 January 1993
Predecessor: Czech and Slovak Federative Republic
Successors: Czech Republic, Slovakia

Background

Czechoslovakia was created in 1918. After the Velvet Revolution of 1989 ended Communist rule, disputes arose over the federation's structure, name (the "Hyphen War"), economic reform speed, and Slovak national aspirations.

Negotiations

After the June 1992 elections, Czech leader Václav Klaus and Slovak leader Vladimír Mečiar negotiated the split; Václav Havel resigned as president rather than preside over it. The Federal Assembly passed the dissolution law on 25 November 1992. No referendum was held, despite polls suggesting many citizens favoured preserving the union.

Division and aftermath

Assets were divided roughly 2:1 by population; the Czechoslovak koruna initially continued before separate currencies were introduced in February 1993. Both states joined NATO and the European Union (2004), and maintain close relations; citizens generally regard the divorce as peaceful and successful.
