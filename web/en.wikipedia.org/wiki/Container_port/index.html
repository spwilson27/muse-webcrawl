Container port

A container port or container terminal is a facility where cargo containers are transshipped between different transport vehicles, such as container ships, trains and trucks, or stored pending transfer. Container ports are the critical nodes of the global containerised freight network that has carried the large majority of world trade in manufactured goods since the late 20th century.

Operations

A terminal typically comprises berths with deep-water quays, ship-to-shore gantry cranes that load and discharge vessels, a paved container yard where boxes are stacked in rows, and equipment — rubber-tyred or rail-mounted gantry cranes, straddle carriers, reach stackers and terminal tractors — to move containers between ship, yard and gate. Incoming trucks pass through a gate complex where documentation and container condition are checked. Terminal operating systems plan stowage, yard positions and equipment movements; automation has advanced furthest at terminals in Rotterdam, Singapore and Chinese ports, where automated stacking cranes and driverless vehicles are common.

Measurement

Throughput is measured in TEUs (twenty-foot equivalent units). The busiest container ports in the world are dominated by East Asia: Shanghai has ranked first since 2010, handling over 47 million TEU in 2022 and 49 million in 2023, followed by Singapore, Ningbo-Zhoushan, Shenzhen, Guangzhou and Busan. The largest ports in Europe are Rotterdam, Antwerp-Bruges and Hamburg; in North America, Los Angeles and Long Beach form the largest complex.

History

Containerisation began commercially in 1956 with Malcolm McLean's converted tanker Ideal-X and spread internationally from 1966. Standardisation of corner fittings and dimensions under ISO rules allowed ports worldwide to invest in specialised terminals, displacing the labour-intensive break-bulk docks and transforming port cities. Dredging and crane outreach have grown steadily with ship sizes, which now exceed 24,000 TEU for the largest ultra-large container vessels.
