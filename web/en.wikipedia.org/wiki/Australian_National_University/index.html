The Australian National University (ANU) is a public research university located in Canberra, the capital city of Australia. Its main campus in Acton encompasses seven teaching and research colleges, in addition to several national academies and institutes. Founded in 1946 by an act of the Australian Parliament, ANU was originally established as a postgraduate research university, with a remit to build national capability in fields of importance to the young nation. It remains the only university in Australia created by federal legislation.

History

The university's establishment was championed by Prime Minister Ben Chifley's government, advised by figures including the physicist Mark Oliphant, the historian Keith Hancock, and the economist H. C. "Nugget" Coombs. Its four founding research schools covered physical sciences, social sciences, Pacific studies, and medicine (the John Curtin School of Medical Research). Undergraduate teaching began in 1960 when ANU amalgamated with Canberra University College. Brian Schmidt, a professor of astronomy at ANU, shared the 2011 Nobel Prize in Physics for the discovery of the accelerating expansion of the universe.

Structure and research

ANU's research institutes include the Research School of Astronomy and Astrophysics (at Mount Stromlo and Siding Spring observatories), the Research School of Earth Sciences, the Crawford School of Public Policy, the Strategic and Defence Studies Centre, and the Australian National Dictionary Centre, which compiles the Australian National Dictionary. The university is a member of the Group of Eight and the International Alliance of Research Universities.

Rankings and reputation

ANU is regularly ranked among the world's top universities and is particularly noted for politics and international studies, social sciences, humanities, and environmental science. Alumni include prime ministers Kevin Rudd and (as a graduate student) Bob Hawke, several Nobel laureates (including Howard Florey, John Eccles, John Harsanyi, Rolf Zinkernagel, Peter Doherty, and Brian Schmidt as faculty), and numerous Rhodes scholars.

Campus

The 145-hectare Acton campus lies beside Lake Burley Griffin and the Canberra city centre and houses more than 10,000 trees. Residential halls and colleges accommodate a large share of the student body relative to other Australian universities. University House, opened in 1954, is heritage-listed as an early example of post-war modernist architecture in Australia.
