Brooklyn Botanic Garden

The Japanese Hill-and-Pond Garden at Brooklyn Botanic Garden.

The Brooklyn Botanic Garden (BBG) is a botanical garden in the borough of Brooklyn in New York City. Founded in 1910 and located on 52 acres (21 ha) adjacent to Prospect Park, the garden holds over 14,000 taxa of plants and attracts nearly a million visitors each year.

History

The garden was established in 1910 under the direction of its first director, C. Stuart Gager, on land set aside within the Prospect Park reservation. Its early development included the Native Flora Garden and the beginnings of its celebrated Japanese garden, opened in 1915 and considered one of the finest Japanese-style gardens outside Japan.

Gardens and collections

Major features include the Cherry Esplanade, famous for its spring cherry blossom festival (Hanami / Sakura Matsuri), the Cranford Rose Garden, the Shakespeare Garden, the Fragrance Garden designed for the visually impaired, the Steinhardt Conservatory with its bonsai museum and aquatic house, and the Children's Garden, one of the oldest children's gardens in a botanic institution in the United States.

The garden's herbarium holds roughly 300,000 specimens, and its library is among the largest horticultural collections in the country.
