Laurence Sterne

Born: 24 November 1713, Clonmel, Ireland

Died: 18 March 1768 (aged 54), London, England

Occupation: Novelist, clergyman

Notable works: Tristram Shandy, A Sentimental Journey

Laurence Sterne (24 November 1713 – 18 March 1768) was an Anglo-Irish novelist and Anglican cleric. He is best known for his novels The Life and Opinions of Tristram Shandy, Gentleman (1759–1767) and A Sentimental Journey Through France and Italy (1768). He also published sermons, wrote memoirs, and was involved in local politics.

Life

Sterne was born in Clonmel, County Tipperary, the son of an army ensign. Educated at Jesus College, Cambridge, he was ordained and became vicar of Sutton-on-the-Forest, near York, in 1738. He spent much of his life in Yorkshire, marrying Elizabeth Lumley in 1741, and serving as a prebendary of York Minster.

Tristram Shandy

The first two volumes of Tristram Shandy appeared in 1759 and made Sterne a celebrity in London and across Europe. The novel's experimental digressions, typographical play, and self-reflexive narrator influenced writers from Diderot to Joyce and the modernists. Seven further volumes followed over the next eight years.

Later years

Suffering from tuberculosis, Sterne travelled in France and Italy seeking better health. A Sentimental Journey, unfinished at his death in London in 1768, launched the vogue for the "sentimental" novel. His works influenced European literature, and his name gave rise to the adjective "Shandean".
