Torres Strait Islands
Location: Torres Strait, Queensland, Australia
Islands: 274, ~17 inhabited
Population: ~4,500 on islands (many more on mainland)
Peoples: Torres Strait Islanders

The Torres Strait Islands are a group of at least 274 small islands in the Torres Strait, the waterway separating far northern Queensland, Australia, from the island of New Guinea. They are part of the state of Queensland.

People and culture

The Indigenous inhabitants, Torres Strait Islanders, are a Melanesian people culturally distinct from Aboriginal Australians, speaking languages including Kalaw Lagaw Ya and Meriam Mir, and Torres Strait Creole. Island groups include the Top Western, Western, Central, Eastern and Inner Islands; Thursday Island is the administrative centre.

History

The strait is named for Spanish navigator Luís Vaz de Torres, who sailed through in 1606. Queensland annexed the islands in 1879. Islanders served notably in WWII, and the 1992 Mabo decision — brought by Mer Islander Eddie Mabo — overturned terra nullius in Australian law. Today climate change and sea-level rise are major concerns for low-lying islands. See Torres Strait Islanders and Mabo.
