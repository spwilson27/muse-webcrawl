Desmond Tutu

Desmond Mpilo Tutu
Born 7 October 1931, Klerksdorp, South Africa
Died 26 December 2021 (aged 90), Cape Town
Archbishop of Cape Town (1986–1996); Nobel Peace Prize laureate (1984)

Desmond Mpilo Tutu (7 October 1931 – 26 December 2021) was a South African Anglican bishop and theologian, known for his work as an anti-apartheid and human rights activist. He was Bishop of Johannesburg from 1985 to 1986 and then Archbishop of Cape Town from 1986 to 1996, in both cases the first black African to hold the position. Theologically, he sought to fuse ideas from black theology with African theology.

Anti-apartheid activism

Tutu rose to prominence as general secretary of the South African Council of Churches (1978–1985), using the platform to campaign against apartheid and to call for economic sanctions and divestment. In 1984 he was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize for his opposition to South Africa's brutal system of racial segregation. After apartheid ended, President Nelson Mandela appointed him chair of the Truth and Reconciliation Commission (1995–1998), which investigated human rights abuses under apartheid and helped define South Africa's transition — Tutu coined the image of the nation as the "Rainbow Nation".

Later life

In retirement Tutu remained an outspoken moral voice on HIV/AIDS, poverty, and conflicts including the Israeli–Palestinian issue, and he championed LGBTQ rights, famously saying he would rather go to hell than to a homophobic heaven. He co-founded The Elders in 2007 and received honours including the US Presidential Medal of Freedom (2009) and the Templeton Prize (2013). He died in Cape Town on 26 December 2021 and was given a state funeral at St George's Cathedral.
