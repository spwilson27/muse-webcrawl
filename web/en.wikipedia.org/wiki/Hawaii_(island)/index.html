The island of Hawaiʻi (Hawaiian: Hawaiʻi) is the largest island in the Hawaiian archipelago and in the United States, and gives its name to the state of Hawaii. To distinguish it from the state, it is usually called the Big Island. Administratively it constitutes Hawaiʻi County. The island is built from five overlapping shield volcanoes and is still growing at its south-eastern shore, where Kīlauea has erupted almost continuously in recent decades.

Geography and geology

The five volcanoes are Kohala (extinct), Mauna Kea (dormant and the highest point in the state, 4,205 m), Hualālai (active), Mauna Loa (active, the largest shield volcano on Earth) and Kīlauea (active). Because of the rain-shadow of the great mountains, the island has strikingly diverse climates — from the wet windward coast around Hilo to the dry resort coast of Kona and the snow that occasionally caps Mauna Kea, home to some of the world's premier astronomical observatories.

History

Hawaiʻi island was the home of Kamehameha the Great, who united most of the Hawaiian Islands by 1795 and founded the Kingdom of Hawaii; Captain James Cook was killed at Kealakekua Bay on the island in 1779. In the nineteenth and twentieth centuries sugar plantations, cattle ranching (centred on Parker Ranch, one of the largest in the US) and coffee farming in Kona reshaped the island's economy and population.

Economy and sights

Today tourism dominates, centred on Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park (a UNESCO World Heritage Site), black- and green-sand beaches, Kona coffee farms and the resorts of the Kohala Coast. Hilo hosts the University of Hawaiʻi at Hilo and the Merrie Monarch Festival, the premier hula competition.
