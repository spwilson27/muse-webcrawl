Frontex

Frontex, officially the European Border and Coast Guard Agency, is an agency of the European Union tasked with border control of the European Schengen Area in coordination with the border and coast guards of Schengen Area member states. It is headquartered in Warsaw, Poland.


Frontex headquarters, Warsaw

History

Frontex was established by Council Regulation (EC) 2007/2004, adopted on 26 October 2004, and began operations on 3 October 2005. Its name derives from the French frontières extérieures ("external borders"). In 2016, in response to the European migrant crisis, the agency was expanded and renamed the European Border and Coast Guard Agency, though it retained the Frontex name. A 2019 regulation further expanded its mandate, providing for a standing corps of 10,000 border guards by 2027.

Tasks

The agency coordinates joint operations at the EU's external borders, conducts risk analyses, supports member states in returning third-country nationals who are staying illegally, and assists in training national border guards. It manages the European Border Surveillance System (EUROSUR) and works alongside Europol and national authorities.

Criticism

Frontex has faced criticism from human rights organisations over alleged involvement in illegal pushbacks of migrants, particularly in the Aegean Sea. Investigations by the EU anti-fraud office OLAF and the European Parliament led to the resignation of executive director Fabrice Leggeri in April 2022.
