Hakim Abul-Qasim Ferdowsi Tusi (c. 940 – c. 1020/1025), known simply as Ferdowsi, was a Persian poet and the author of the Shahnameh ("The Book of Kings"), the national epic of Iran and one of the longest poems ever written by a single author. He is widely credited with preserving the Persian language and pre-Islamic Iranian mythology and history.

Life

Ferdowsi was born into a family of landowners (dehqans) in the village of Paj near Tus in Khorasan, then part of the Samanid Empire. The dehqan class preserved much of Iran's oral and written heritage from before the Arab conquest. Around 977 he began versifying the prose Shahnameh of Abu Mansur, incorporating the thousand verses of the murdered poet Daqiqi. He laboured on the epic for some 35 years; the final version was completed around 1010 and presented to Sultan Mahmud of Ghazni.

According to famous later accounts, Mahmud rewarded the poet far less generously than promised — paying in silver dirhams rather than gold, or insulting his work — and a bitter Ferdowsi is said to have given the money away. The stories are partly legendary, but the poet's own verses complain of neglect. He died in Tus around 1020–1025, in relative poverty.

The Shahnameh

The Shahnameh comprises roughly 50,000 rhyming couplets recounting the mythical and historical past of Iran from the creation of the world and the first king, Gayumarth, through the legendary Pishdadian and Kayanian dynasties, Alexander the Great and the Parthian and Sasanian empires, to the Arab conquest of the 7th century CE. Its heroes include Jamshid, Zahhak, Fereydun, and above all the champion Rostam, whose tragedy of unknowingly killing his son Sohrab is one of world literature's best-known episodes.

Legacy

Ferdowsi deliberately wrote in Persian with a minimum of Arabic loanwords, and his epic is considered foundational to the survival of New Persian as a literary language. His mausoleum in Tus, completed in 1934 for the Ferdowsi millenary celebrations, is a major pilgrimage site; Ferdowsi University of Mashhad and Tehran's Ferdowsi Square bear his name.
