The River Nene is a river that flows through the counties of Northamptonshire, Cambridgeshire, Lincolnshire and Norfolk in England. It is the tenth-longest river in the United Kingdom, at 100 miles (161 km), and is navigable for 88 miles (142 km) from Northampton to the sea.

Course and pronunciation

The Nene has three main sources near Badby, Naseby and Yelvertoft in Northamptonshire. It flows through Northampton, Wellingborough, Irthlingborough, Thrapston, Oundle, and Peterborough, then across the Fens through Wisbech to its mouth on The Wash at Sutton Bridge.

The pronunciation of the name varies along its course: in Northamptonshire it is usually pronounced "Nen" (rhyming with "hen"), while around Peterborough and in the Fens "Neen" (rhyming with "seen") is common.

Navigation

The river was made navigable to Northampton by improvements completed in 1761, later linked to the Grand Union Canal via the Northampton Arm. The Nene connects with the Middle Level Navigations at Peterborough, giving access to the Great Ouse system. The tidal river below Peterborough is guarded by the Dog in a Doublet sluice and lock.

Flooding

The Nene valley flooded severely at Easter 1998, when heavy rain inundated parts of Northampton, Wellingborough and other towns, prompting major investment in flood defences along the valley.
