Radar

Radar
DefinitionRadio detection and ranging — locating objects by reflected radio waves
Key WWII roleAir defence (Chain Home, the Dowding system)
[Image: A rotating radar antenna dish against a stormy sky]

Radar (radio detection and ranging) is a system that uses radio waves to determine the distance, direction and speed of objects. Developed secretly in several countries in the 1930s, it proved decisive in the Battle of Britain and the war at sea (Ship and aircraft detection).

Civil uses

Radar now guides air traffic, tracks weather (precipitation maps), measures speeding cars, maps planets (Venus through its clouds) and assists spacecraft docking. Solid-state phased arrays steer beams electronically, and radar sensors enable driver-assistance and the satellites' synthetic-aperture imaging.
