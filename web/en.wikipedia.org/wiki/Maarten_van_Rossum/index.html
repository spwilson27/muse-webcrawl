Maarten van Rossum (c. 1478 – 7 June 1555) was a Dutch field commander from the Duchy of Guelders who for decades waged war against the Habsburg Netherlands on behalf of Duke Charles of Guelders. A master of raiding warfare — the kleine oorlog of plundering expeditions — he became notorious in Holland and Brabant, where "Van Rossum" was a byword for sudden devastating descents.

Career

Van Rossum rose in the service of Charles of Egmond, Duke of Guelders, during the long Guelders Wars. His raids repeatedly struck deep into Habsburg territory: in 1517 his forces ravaged Holland, in 1527–1528 he sacked The Hague, and in 1542, in alliance with France during an imperial war, he launched his most famous campaign, marching through Brabant, burning the suburbs of Antwerp and Leuven, and raiding as far as the walls of Brussels before withdrawing.

Later life

When Guelders passed to the Habsburgs after Duke William of Jülich-Cleves-Berg renounced it by the Treaty of Venlo (1543), Van Rossum entered imperial service under Mary of Hungary, an unusual end for the Empire's former scourge. He died of the plague in Antwerp in 1555. His fortified house at Zaltbommel, the Maarten van Rossumhuis, survives as a museum.
