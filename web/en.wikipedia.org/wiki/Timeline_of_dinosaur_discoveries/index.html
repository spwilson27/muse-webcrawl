Timeline of dinosaur discoveries

This timeline of dinosaur discoveries records significant finds and interpretations of dinosaur fossils from antiquity to the present.

Early finds

Fossil bones were known in China and Europe for centuries and were variously attributed to dragons or giants. In 1677 Robert Plot illustrated a Megalosaurus femur fragment from Oxfordshire. William Buckland formally described Megalosaurus in 1824, followed by Gideon Mantell's Iguanodon (1825) and Hylaeosaurus (1833). Richard Owen coined the name Dinosauria in 1842.

19th and 20th centuries

The American "Bone Wars" between Edward Drinker Cope and Othniel Charles Marsh in the 1870s–1890s produced many famous genera including Triceratops, Stegosaurus and Diplodocus. The 20th century added finds from Mongolia (Roy Chapman Andrews' expeditions, including the first recognised dinosaur eggs), Canada, and Africa, and in 1969 John Ostrom's work on Deinonychus sparked the "dinosaur renaissance" linking dinosaurs to birds.

Modern era

From the 1990s, feathered dinosaurs from Liaoning, China (beginning with Sinosauropteryx, 1996) transformed understanding, and new species are now named at a rate of roughly one per week from finds worldwide.
