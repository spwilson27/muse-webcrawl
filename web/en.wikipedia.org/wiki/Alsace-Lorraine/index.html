Alsace-Lorraine

Alsace-Lorraine (German: Elsass-Lothringen) was the name given to the Imperial Territory created by the German Empire in 1871 after annexing most of Alsace and the northern part of Lorraine from France following the Franco-Prussian War. The territory, some 14,500 km², was administered directly by the emperor through a governor (Statthalter) rather than as a federal state, and its status was a permanent grievance in French politics.

Under the German Empire

The annexation, formalised by the Treaty of Frankfurt (1871), was driven by strategic and nationalist arguments; inhabitants who opted for French nationality were required to emigrate. The region's population was largely German-speaking, though with a significant French-speaking minority in Lorraine. Germanisation policies, the Zabern (Saverne) affair of 1913 — in which military arrogance toward Alsatian civilians caused a national scandal — and conscription kept tensions alive. The territory gained its own constitution and parliament (Landtag) in 1911.

Return to France

After the First World War, the Treaty of Versailles (1919) returned Alsace-Lorraine to France without a plebiscite. Germany annexed the area again de facto during the Second World War, and many Alsatians were conscripted into the Wehrmacht (the Malgré-nous). Since 1945 the region has remained French, today forming part of the Grand Est region, though it retains distinctive local law and the Alsatian dialect.
