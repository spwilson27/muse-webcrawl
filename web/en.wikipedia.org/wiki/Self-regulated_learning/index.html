Self-regulated learning

Self-regulated learning (SRL) is an educational psychology framework describing how learners take control of their own learning: setting goals, selecting strategies, monitoring progress, managing motivation and environment, and evaluating outcomes. It is not a single ability but a set of processes that can be taught and developed.

Models

Barry Zimmerman's social-cognitive model, the most influential, describes a cyclical loop of three phases: forethought (goal setting, strategic planning, and motivational beliefs such as self-efficacy), performance (self-control strategies and self-observation while working), and self-reflection (self-judgement and reaction, which feed into the next cycle). Paul Pintrich's model similarly crosses phases of regulation with areas regulated: cognition, motivation and affect, behaviour, and context. Other models, such as Monique Boekaerts's, emphasise emotion and the balance between mastery and well-being goals.

Strategies and evidence

Self-regulated learners typically set specific proximal goals, use strategies such as elaboration, organisation, rehearsal and self-testing, seek help appropriately, keep records, structure their study environment, and attribute outcomes to controllable causes. SRL skills correlate with academic achievement across ages and domains, and interventions — strategy instruction, metacognitive prompts, diaries, and feedback — can improve them, especially for struggling learners. The construct overlaps with metacognition but is broader, encompassing motivation and behaviour. It has become central to research on online and blended learning, where the absence of a teacher's external structure places heavier demands on learners' self-regulation, and to work on lifelong learning.
