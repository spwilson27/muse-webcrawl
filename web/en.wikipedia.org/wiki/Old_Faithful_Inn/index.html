The Old Faithful Inn is a hotel located in Yellowstone National Park, Wyoming, United States, with a view of the Old Faithful geyser. The inn has a multi-story log lobby, flanked by long frame wings. It is one of the few log hotels still standing in the United States, and was the largest log hotel in the world when it opened in 1904; it remains among the largest log structures anywhere.

Design

Architect Robert Reamer, hired by the Northern Pacific Railway's Yellowstone Park Association, designed the building in the rustic style that came to define National Park Service architecture. Local lodgepole pine logs and rhyolite stone form the structure. The lobby rises some 65 feet (about 20 m) through seven storeys of balconies built from gnarled, twisted lodgepole branches gathered in the park, and is centred on an enormous stone fireplace with a hand-crafted iron-and-copper clock. A "crow's nest" platform near the roof was long used by musicians entertaining guests below.

East and west wings added in 1913 and 1927 brought the inn to 327 rooms. The inn was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1987. It survived the great Yellowstone fires of 1988, when firefighters and a sprinkler system saved the building as flames reached its grounds. The inn is open seasonally, typically from May to October, and reservations for its rooms are famously difficult to obtain.
