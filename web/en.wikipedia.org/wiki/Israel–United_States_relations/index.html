Israel–United States relations

Israel–United States relations are the bilateral relations between the State of Israel and the United States of America. The United States was the first country to recognise the provisional government of Israel in May 1948, and the relationship has since developed into a close political, military and economic partnership, often described by American officials as an unbreakable bond.

History

American support for a Jewish homeland was expressed in the 1940s, and President Harry S. Truman extended de facto recognition minutes after Israel's declaration of independence on 14 May 1948. Relations deepened after the Six-Day War of 1967, after which the United States became Israel's principal supplier of arms and diplomatic support. The Camp David Accords of 1978, brokered by President Jimmy Carter, led to the Egypt–Israel peace treaty.

Military and economic aid

Israel is the largest cumulative recipient of American foreign assistance since the Second World War. A ten-year memorandum of understanding signed in 2016 committed $38 billion in military aid for 2019–2028. The United States also supports Israeli missile-defence systems such as Iron Dome, David's Sling and Arrow. Trade and scientific cooperation are extensive, and the two countries maintain a free trade agreement dating to 1985.

Diplomatic issues

Points of friction have included Israeli settlement policy in the occupied territories, the status of Jerusalem — recognised by the United States as Israel's capital in 2017, when the American embassy was moved there — and policy toward Iran's nuclear programme.
