Loop algebra

Loops in a Lie group give rise to the loop algebra

In mathematics, a loop algebra is a certain type of Lie algebra, an infinite-dimensional algebra associated to a finite-dimensional Lie algebra g. The loop algebra consists of maps from the circle S1 into the Lie group or algebra — intuitively, polynomials or smooth functions in a loop parameter.

Construction

Given a Lie algebra g, its (polynomial) loop algebra is the tensor product of g with the ring of Laurent polynomials in one variable: elements are finite sums of terms x ⊗ tn for x ∈ g and integers n. The Lie bracket is defined pointwise in the loop parameter: [x ⊗ tm, y ⊗ tn] = [x, y] ⊗ tm+n.

Affine Lie algebras

Loop algebras are the step before affine Kac–Moody algebras: a certain central extension of a loop algebra, together with a derivation, gives an (untwisted) affine Lie algebra. They arise naturally in conformal field theory and string theory, where the modes of currents on a worldsheet form a loop algebra, and in the theory of integrable systems. Replacing Laurent polynomials by Laurent series or smooth loops gives closely related completions.
