j-invariant

In mathematics, Felix Klein's j-invariant, or j function, is a modular function of weight zero defined on the upper half-plane. It is the unique such function that is holomorphic, has a simple pole at the cusp, and takes the values 1728 at i and 0 at e2πi/3. The j-invariant completely classifies elliptic curves over the complex numbers up to isomorphism.

Real part of the j-invariant as a function of the nome q on the unit disk

Definition

The j-invariant can be defined in terms of the Eisenstein series via the modular discriminant Δ and the function g2:

j(τ) = 1728 g2(τ)³ / Δ(τ)

Its Fourier expansion (q-expansion) at the cusp begins

j(τ) = 1/q + 744 + 196884q + 21493760q² + 864299970q³ + …

Monstrous moonshine

The coefficients of this expansion famously encode dimensions of representations of the Monster group — 196884 = 196883 + 1 — an observation of John McKay that led to the "monstrous moonshine" conjectures, proven by Richard Borcherds (work recognized by a Fields Medal in 1998).

Special values and properties

For special (CM) points τ, j(τ) is an algebraic integer; the near-integer eπ√163 ≈ 640320³ + 744 relates to j((1+√−163)/2) = −640320³, an instance of the class number 1 problem. Rational values of the j-invariant characterize the nine Heegner numbers.
