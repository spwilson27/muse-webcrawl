Avian influenza

Avian influenza, commonly called bird flu, is a type of influenza caused by viruses adapted to birds. The type with the greatest risk is highly pathogenic avian influenza (HPAI). Avian influenza viruses are influenza A viruses that occur naturally among wild aquatic birds worldwide and can infect domestic poultry and other bird and animal species.

Strains and hosts

Influenza A viruses are classified by their surface proteins haemagglutinin (H) and neuraminidase (N), giving subtypes such as H5N1, H7N9 and H5N8. Most circulate in wild birds as low-pathogenic strains causing little illness, but H5 and H7 subtypes can mutate into highly pathogenic forms that devastate poultry flocks, prompting mass culls.

Transmission to humans

Human infections have occurred mostly after close contact with infected birds; sustained human-to-human transmission has not been observed, though health authorities monitor the viruses because of their pandemic potential. H5N1 first infected humans in Hong Kong in 1997 and has had a high reported case-fatality rate. Since 2020, an H5N1 clade has spread globally in wild birds, spilled into mammals including mink, sea lions and dairy cattle, and destroyed record numbers of poultry.

Control measures include biosecurity, culling infected flocks, surveillance of wild birds and, in some countries, poultry vaccination. Human cases are treated with antivirals such as oseltamivir.
