Omoto-kyo

Japanese大本教 (Ōmoto-kyō)
Founded1892, Ayabe, Kyoto, Japan
FoundersNao Deguchi; Onisaburō Deguchi
TypeJapanese new religion (Shinto-derived)

Oomoto (大本, Ōmoto, literally "great origin" or "great foundation"), also known as Oomoto-kyō (大本教), is a Japanese new religion founded in 1892 by Nao Deguchi. It is often counted among the Shinto-derived new religions and is well known internationally for its early and enthusiastic promotion of the constructed language Esperanto.

History

Nao Deguchi, an illiterate peasant woman from Ayabe, began receiving revelations in 1892 which were recorded in the Ofudesaki. Her son-in-law Onisaburō Deguchi, a charismatic organiser and co-author of the movement's massive scriptural commentary Reikai Monogatari ("Tales from the Spirit World"), built Oomoto into one of the largest new religions of prewar Japan, with perhaps millions of adherents. Its millenarian message and criticism of the state led to two suppressions by the Imperial Japanese government, in 1921 and 1935 (the Second Oomoto Incident), in which its headquarters were dynamited and its leaders imprisoned for lèse-majesté.

Beliefs and practices

Oomoto teaches the worship of the creator deity and the unity of religions, holding that all faiths spring from one source. It promotes world peace, interfaith cooperation, organic farming and the arts, maintains a major centre in Kameoka as well as Ayabe, and adopted Esperanto as a sacred universal language, hosting Esperanto activities and publishing in the language to this day. The martial art of aikido was founded by Morihei Ueshiba, who was deeply influenced by Onisaburō Deguchi and Oomoto's teachings.
