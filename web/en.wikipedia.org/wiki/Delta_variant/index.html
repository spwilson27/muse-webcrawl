The Delta variant (B.1.617.2) is a variant of SARS-CoV-2, the virus that causes COVID-19. First identified in India in late 2020, it was designated a variant of concern by the World Health Organization in May 2021 and became the globally dominant strain during mid-to-late 2021, before being displaced by the Omicron variant.

Characteristics

Delta was substantially more transmissible than earlier variants — estimated to be roughly twice as contagious as the original strain and about 40–60 percent more transmissible than the Alpha variant — with a shorter incubation period and higher viral loads. Evidence suggested it also carried an increased risk of hospitalization compared with Alpha, while COVID-19 vaccines remained effective against severe disease, particularly after two doses, though protection against mild infection was reduced.

Spread and impact

The variant drove India's devastating second wave in spring 2021 and subsequent surges in the United Kingdom, the United States, and many other countries, prompting renewed restrictions and accelerating vaccination campaigns. Its numerous AY.* descendant lineages (sometimes called "Delta plus" when carrying the K417N mutation) spread widely. By early 2022 Omicron had almost entirely replaced Delta worldwide.
