Hanafuda

Hanafuda (Japanese: 花札, "flower cards") are Japanese traditional playing cards used to play a number of games. The deck consists of 48 cards divided into twelve suits of four cards each, one suit for each month of the year, with each suit decorated with a different flower or plant.

History

Playing cards were introduced to Japan by Portuguese traders in the mid-16th century. After the Tokugawa shogunate banned cards with foreign designs during its isolationist period, Japanese makers disguised cards with abstract floral designs; through successive bans the designs became ever more stylised, eventually producing hanafuda. In 1889, Fusajiro Yamauchi founded Nintendo Koppai (later Nintendo) to manufacture and sell hand-made hanafuda cards painted on mulberry bark.

The cards

The twelve suits are: pine (January), plum blossom (February), cherry blossom (March), wisteria (April), iris (May), peony (June), bush clover (July), susuki grass with the moon (August), chrysanthemum (September), maple (October), willow with rain (November), and paulownia (December). Each suit has plain cards plus special "animal", "ribbon" and "bright" cards.

Games

The most popular games played with hanafuda include Koi-Koi, in which players form scoring combinations (yaku), and Hachi-Hachi. In Korea the cards, known as Hwatu, are used for the game Go-Stop.
