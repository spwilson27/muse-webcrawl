Irish name

A name in the Irish language consists of a given name and a surname. Surnames in Irish are generally patronymic in origin, formed with Ó ("descendant of") or Mac ("son of"), with corresponding feminine forms Ní and Nic. Irish-language names often have anglicised equivalents that developed during centuries of English administration.

Surnames

The prefix Ó (as in Ó Néill, "descendant of Niall") becomes O' in English; Mac (as in Mac Cárthaigh) gives Mac or Mc. In Irish, a woman's surname changes form depending on marital status and whether the surname begins with Ó or Mac: for example, Seán Ó Cadhain is married to Máire Ní Chadain, where the surname is lenited after Ní.

Given names

Traditional Irish given names include Seán (John), Máire (Mary), Pádraig (Patrick), Bríd (Bridget), Sinéad, Aoife, Ciarán, Niamh, Oisín and Saoirse. Many derive from early Irish saints, mythology or descriptive epithets.

Patronymics and bynames

In medieval Ireland, names also used mac literally to identify a person's father, and descriptive bynames (such as rua, "red-haired", or mór, "great") were common. The Irish naming system historically differed from the fixed hereditary surnames that became standard in much of Europe.
