Pāli Canon

The Pāli Canon is the complete scriptural collection of Theravada Buddhism, preserved in the Pāli language. It is also known as the Tipiṭaka ("three baskets"), and is the most complete surviving early Buddhist canon.

Contents

The canon is divided into three general categories: the Vinaya Piṭaka, containing rules for monks and nuns; the Sutta Piṭaka, discourses attributed to the Buddha and his chief disciples, organised into five Nikāyas including the Dīgha, Majjhima, Saṃyutta, Aṅguttara and Khuddaka collections; and the Abhidhamma Piṭaka, systematic works on doctrine and psychology.

History

The texts were transmitted orally for centuries before being committed to writing in Sri Lanka in the 1st century BCE, traditionally at Aluvihare during the reign of King Vaṭṭagāmaṇi. The standard edition used today derives from the Sixth Buddhist Council held in Yangon in 1954–56. Theravada tradition holds the canon to record the Buddha's own teaching; scholars view it as a layered record of early Buddhist doctrine, with its core among the oldest surviving records of the Buddha's discourses.
