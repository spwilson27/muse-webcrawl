Robert Banks Jenkinson, 2nd Earl of Liverpool (7 June 1770 – 4 December 1828) was a British Tory statesman who served as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom from 1812 to 1827. He held many important cabinet offices, including Foreign Secretary, Home Secretary, and Secretary of State for War and the Colonies. At nearly fifteen years, his tenure was the second-longest premiership of the 19th century.

Early career

Jenkinson was educated at Charterhouse and Christ Church, Oxford, and entered Parliament in 1790. He witnessed the storming of the Bastille era unrest while travelling in France. Under William Pitt the Younger he became Master of the Mint and, in 1801, Foreign Secretary in Addington's government, negotiating the Treaty of Amiens with France. He later served as Home Secretary and, from 1809, Secretary of State for War and the Colonies under Spencer Perceval.

Premiership

After Perceval's assassination in 1812, Liverpool became Prime Minister. His government steered Britain through the final phase of the Napoleonic Wars, the Congress of Vienna, and the War of 1812 with the United States. Post-war economic distress brought unrest — the Corn Laws (1815), the Peterloo Massacre (1819), and the repressive Six Acts — but also, in the 1820s, a turn toward liberal Toryism: tariff reform, the repeal of the Combination Acts, and criminal law reform under colleagues such as Huskisson, Peel, and Canning. The administration also saw Catholic emancipation become the dominant unresolved question.

Later life

In February 1827 Liverpool suffered a severe stroke and resigned; he died the following year at Coombe House, Kingston upon Thames. Though long dismissed as a reactionary mediocrity, modern historians credit his political management and longevity in office.
