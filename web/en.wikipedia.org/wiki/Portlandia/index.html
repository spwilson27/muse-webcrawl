Portlandia

Portlandia is an American sketch comedy television series starring Fred Armisen and Carrie Brownstein, set in and around Portland, Oregon. Created by Armisen, Brownstein, and director Jonathan Krisel, the show aired on IFC from 2011 to 2018 for eight seasons and gently satirized the city's hipster, artisanal, and politically progressive culture. The series takes its name from the Portlandia statue in downtown Portland.

Background

Armisen and Brownstein first collaborated on the web series "ThunderAnt," whose sketches set in a feminist bookstore — with characters Toni and Candace — carried into Portlandia as the recurring "Women & Women First" bookstore sketches. Brownstein, a member of the band Sleater-Kinney, had long lived in Portland. The pilot's opening musical sketch, "The Dream of the '90s Is Alive in Portland," became a viral signature of the show.

Recurring themes and sketches

Recurring bits include the hyper-local sourcing satire "Is it local?" (traced to a chicken named Colin), "Put a Bird on It," feminist bookstore owners Toni and Candace, militant bike messenger Spike, and mayor's-office sketches featuring Kyle MacLachlan as Portland's mayor. Guest stars included Steve Buscemi, Kumail Nanjiani, and former Portland mayor Sam Adams appearing as the mayor's assistant.

Reception and legacy

The series won a Peabody Award in 2012 and multiple Emmy nominations, and is credited with both boosting Portland's national profile and tourism and cementing the city's quirky image — sometimes to locals' ambivalence. Several phrases from the show entered popular usage.
