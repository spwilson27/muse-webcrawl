Special: Random

Special:Random is a special page of Wikipedia that redirects the reader to a randomly selected article from the encyclopedia's main namespace. It exists to help readers discover unfamiliar topics and to help editors find articles in need of attention.

How it works

Visiting the random-article link selects one article with (approximately) equal probability from all articles in the main namespace — more than six million on the English Wikipedia. Redirects are followed, so the reader lands on a substantive article rather than a redirect page. Variants of the feature can restrict the draw to a particular namespace, such as categories, templates or user pages.

Uses

  • Serendipitous reading — a way to browse beyond one's usual interests, perhaps landing on the hollandaise sauce, the Wars of the Roses, or the protein myosin.
  • Editing — random articles surface neglected pages for copy-editing, sourcing and categorisation.
  • Games and challenges — "random article" games, in which players navigate between two random articles using only internal links, are a popular pastime.

History

The feature has existed almost since Wikipedia's founding in 2001 and was among the earliest special pages. The random link appears in the sidebar of every page, under the navigation menu, alongside current events and the contents overview.
