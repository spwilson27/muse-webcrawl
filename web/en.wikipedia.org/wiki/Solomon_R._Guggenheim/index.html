Solomon R. Guggenheim

Solomon Robert Guggenheim (July 2, 1861 – November 3, 1949) was an American businessman and art collector. A member of the Guggenheim family, he made his fortune in mining, and is best known for establishing the Solomon R. Guggenheim Foundation and the Solomon R. Guggenheim Museum in New York City.

Early life and business career

Guggenheim was born in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, the son of Meyer Guggenheim, a Swiss immigrant who built a fortune in mining and smelting. After studying in Switzerland, Solomon joined the family business and helped develop its interests in mines in Mexico, Alaska and the American West, including the Yukon Gold Company. He retired from active business around 1919 to devote himself to art collecting.

Art collecting

Guggenheim began collecting older masters in the 1890s, but in the late 1920s, guided by the German artist and adviser Hilla von Rebay, he turned decisively to modern abstract art. He amassed one of the world's great collections of non-objective painting, including major works by Wassily Kandinsky, Rudolf Bauer, Piet Mondrian, Pablo Picasso and Marc Chagall. In 1937 he established the Solomon R. Guggenheim Foundation to foster the appreciation of modern art.

The museum

The collection was first displayed in 1939 at the Museum of Non-Objective Painting on East 54th Street in New York. In 1943 Guggenheim and Rebay commissioned Frank Lloyd Wright to design a permanent museum. Wright's famous spiral building on Fifth Avenue opened in October 1959, a decade after Guggenheim's death in 1949. The Guggenheim has since become the centre of an international network of museums, including the Guggenheim Museum Bilbao.
