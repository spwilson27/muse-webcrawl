Decree 770

Decree 770 was a legislative decree of the Socialist Republic of Romania, signed in 1966 by Nicolae Ceaușescu, which severely restricted access to abortion and contraception. It was one of the most notorious pronatalist policies of twentieth-century Europe and remained in force until the Romanian Revolution of 1989.

Background

Before 1966, Romania had one of the most permissive abortion laws in Europe, under a 1957 decree that liberalised the procedure. In the mid-1960s the Ceaușescu regime became alarmed by falling birth rates and a negative natural population increase, viewing a larger population as a requirement for economic growth and national strength.

Provisions

The decree made abortion illegal except in narrowly defined circumstances, including danger to the life of the mother, the presence of serious hereditary disease, pregnancy resulting from rape or incest, the woman being over 45 years of age, or the woman already having four children in her care. Contraceptive sales were restricted, taxes were imposed on childless adults, and divorce was made more difficult. Women were subjected to regular gynaecological examinations in the workplace in an effort to detect pregnancies and prevent terminations, earning the officials who carried them out the grim popular nickname of the "menstrual police".

Effects

The birth rate initially doubled in 1967, producing the large cohort known in Romania as the decreței ("children of the decree"). However, maternal mortality from unsafe illegal abortions rose sharply, and Romania came to have the highest maternal mortality rate in Europe. Large numbers of unwanted children were placed in state orphanages, whose appalling conditions were exposed to the world after 1989.

Repeal

Decree 770 was among the very first laws repealed after the overthrow of Ceaușescu in December 1989.
