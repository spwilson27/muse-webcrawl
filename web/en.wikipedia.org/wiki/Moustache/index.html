Moustache

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Overview

A moustache (American spelling mustache) is a strip of facial hair grown above the upper lip. Moustaches have been worn in many styles throughout history, and may be groomed with wax, trimmed with scissors or a razor, and combined with a beard or worn alone. The word derives from the French moustache, ultimately from Greek mystax, "upper lip, moustache".

History

Depictions of shaven men with moustaches survive from ancient Egypt and Iran; the Pazyryk burials of about 300 BC include a horseman with a moustache. In Europe, moustaches became widespread in the 19th century, strongly associated with the military: many armies encouraged or even required them, and the British Army only dropped its moustache regulation in 1916. The early 20th century saw neatly trimmed styles, including the toothbrush moustache later indelibly associated with Adolf Hitler. Moustaches declined after the Second World War, revived in the 1970s and 1980s, and have since cycled in and out of fashion.

Styles

Standard named styles recognised by the World Beard and Moustache Championships include the handlebar (ends curled upward), the walrus (thick and drooping over the lip), the chevron (thick and straight, famously worn by Tom Selleck), the pencil moustache (a thin line just above the lip), the horseshoe, the English, the Dali — thin points bent upward, after Salvador Dalí — and the Fu Manchu.

Culture

Since 2003 the Movember movement has encouraged men to grow moustaches each November to raise awareness and funds for men's health issues, particularly prostate and testicular cancer and mental health. In some professions and services, such as military dress codes, rules about moustaches remain formal; elsewhere the moustache serves as a playful marker of retro style.
