The Belém Palace (Portuguese: Palácio de Belém) is a palace in the Belém district of Lisbon, Portugal. Formerly a royal residence, it is today the official residence of the President of the Portuguese Republic.

History

The palace originated in the mid-16th century with houses built by Manuel de Noronha; it was later owned by the Counts of Aveiras and purchased by King John V in 1726, who remodelled it as a royal retreat. The complex comprises a central block of five connected buildings arranged around a patio, flanked by gardens overlooking the Tagus. Queen Maria I added elements in the 18th century, and the palace served variously as a royal residence, a residence for visiting heads of state, and, after 1910, the seat of the presidency.

Today

The palace houses the President's offices and residence, the Museum of the Presidency (Museu da Presidência da República), and state rooms used for official ceremonies. Its formal gardens, stables — now home to the National Coach Museum's older annex — and the changing of the guard ceremony are notable features. It stands opposite the Jerónimos Monastery, near the Tagus waterfront.
