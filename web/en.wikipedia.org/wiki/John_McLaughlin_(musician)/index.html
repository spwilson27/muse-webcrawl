John McLaughlin (musician)

John McLaughlin (born 4 January 1942), also known as Mahavishnu, is an English guitarist, bandleader, and composer. A pioneer of jazz fusion, his music combines elements of jazz with rock, Indian classical music, Western classical music, flamenco and blues. He is regarded as one of the most influential guitarists of the twentieth century.

After playing with several prominent British blues and jazz bands in the 1960s, McLaughlin moved to the United States, where he played with Tony Williams's Lifetime and with Miles Davis, appearing on Davis's albums In a Silent Way and Bitches Brew. He then formed the Mahavishnu Orchestra, whose albums The Inner Mounting Flame (1971) and Birds of Fire (1973) became landmarks of jazz-rock fusion.

Career

McLaughlin also led the acoustic group Shakti, which blended Indian music with jazz, and co-founded the Guitar Trio with Paco de Lucía and Al Di Meola, whose 1981 live album Friday Night in San Francisco is among the best-selling acoustic guitar recordings. In later decades he has toured and recorded with his band the 4th Dimension.

Style

McLaughlin is known for his extremely fast, precise alternate picking, his use of Indian rhythmic cycles, and his exploration of unusual time signatures and scales drawn from Eastern music.
