Alpine meadow

An alpine meadow is a meadow ecosystem found in mountainous regions above or around the tree line, in the alpine tundra belt. Alpine meadows are characterized by short-stature grasses, sedges, and flowering herbs adapted to cold temperatures, intense sunlight, strong winds, and a short growing season.

Ecology

Because the growing season is brief, alpine plants are mostly perennials that grow slowly, flower quickly, and store energy underground. Typical species include alpine forget-me-nots, gentians, edelweiss, and cushion plants. Meadows are maintained by snowmelt moisture and by the exclusion of trees, which cannot survive the harsh conditions above the treeline.

Distribution

Alpine meadows occur in the Alps, the Himalayas, the Rocky Mountains, the Andes, the Altai, and other high ranges worldwide. In Europe, many were traditionally mown for hay and grazed by cattle and sheep in summer transhumance; abandonment of these practices is leading to shrub encroachment in some areas.

Threats

Climate warming is allowing shrubs and trees to advance upslope, shrinking alpine meadow habitat and threatening specialized species.
