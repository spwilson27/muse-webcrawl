IMOCA 60

The IMOCA 60 is a class of 60-foot (18.29 m) racing monohull sailing yacht governed by the International Monohull Open Classes Association (IMOCA). Designed for solo and double-handed ocean racing, the boats are best known as the class of the Vendée Globe round-the-world race and The Ocean Race.

IMOCA 60
Class: Development (box rule) monohull
Length overall: 60 ft (18.29 m)
Mast height: max 29 m
Features: canting keel, daggerboards/foils, carbon construction
Signature race: Vendée Globe

Class rules

IMOCA 60s are built to a "box rule" that fixes maximum length, beam, draft, and mast height and imposes strict stability and safety requirements — including self-righting ability — while leaving designers freedom in hull shape, appendages, and rig. Boats are built largely of carbon fiber, carry water ballast and a canting keel, and since the mid-2010s many have used hydrofoils ("foilers") that lift the hull and dramatically increase downwind speeds.

Racing

The class stars in the Vendée Globe (solo, non-stop, unassisted circumnavigation), the Route du Rhum transatlantic race, the Transat Jacques Vabre (double-handed), and the crewed Ocean Race. Modern foiling IMOCAs have covered more than 600 nautical miles in 24 hours single-handed and complete the Vendée Globe in around 65–75 days.

Development

Successive rule changes have standardized the keel and mast (one-design components) to control costs and improve reliability while encouraging foil development. The class has also adopted sustainability measures, including rules encouraging bio-based materials and the use of boats as ocean-data collection platforms.
