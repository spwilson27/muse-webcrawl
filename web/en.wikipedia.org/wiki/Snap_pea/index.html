Snap pea

The snap pea, also known as the sugar snap pea, is an edible-pod pea cultivar group (Pisum sativum var. macrocarpon). Unlike snow peas, whose pods are flat, snap peas have thick, rounded pods that are crisp, sweet and eaten whole along with the peas inside.

History

The modern sugar snap pea was developed by plant breeder Calvin Lamborn, who crossed a snow pea with a mutant shelling pea, releasing the 'Sugar Snap' cultivar in 1979. It won the All-America Selections gold medal the same year and became a popular garden and commercial vegetable. Lamborn later developed further snap pea varieties, including stringless types.

Cultivation and culinary use

Snap pea plants are climbing vines that generally require support. Older varieties have a fibrous "string" along the pod seam that is removed before eating; newer varieties are bred to be stringless. Snap peas are eaten raw as a snack, in salads, or lightly cooked in stir-fries and side dishes, since prolonged cooking destroys their crisp texture.
