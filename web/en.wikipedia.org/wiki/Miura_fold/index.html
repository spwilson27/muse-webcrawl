Miura fold

The Miura fold (ミウラ折り, Miura-ori) is a method of folding a flat surface into a much smaller volume. It was invented by Japanese astrophysicist Kōryō Miura and is a well-known example of practical origami applied to engineering.

Structure

The fold consists of a tessellation of parallelograms arranged in a zigzag pattern of mountain and valley folds. Because the creases are coupled, the sheet has effectively a single degree of freedom: it can be opened or closed by pulling or pushing on opposite corners, with the whole surface unfolding or collapsing in one smooth motion.

Applications

The Miura fold was designed for deploying large solar panel arrays on spacecraft, allowing a large membrane to be stowed compactly for launch and unfolded reliably in orbit; it was used on the Japanese Space Flyer Unit in 1995. The same principle has since been applied to folding maps, packaging, deployable shelters, medical devices such as stents, and research into mechanical metamaterials.
