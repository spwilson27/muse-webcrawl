Washington Heights is a neighborhood in the northernmost part of the New York City borough of Manhattan. It is named for Fort Washington, a fortified position held by Continental Army troops during the Revolutionary War and captured by the British at the Battle of Fort Washington in 1776. The neighborhood occupies the high ridge — including the highest natural point on Manhattan island, in Bennett Park — between the Hudson and Harlem rivers.

History

Before subway lines reached upper Manhattan in the early twentieth century, the area was largely farmland and country estates. Rapid development followed, and by the 1930s the neighborhood had become home to large German Jewish, Irish and Greek communities; the German Jewish refugees of the 1930s–40s gave part of it the nickname "Frankfurt on the Hudson". After the 1960s, Washington Heights became the centre of New York's Dominican community, the largest Dominican population outside the Dominican Republic, with Spanish-language businesses dominating Broadway and St. Nicholas Avenue.

Landmarks and institutions

The neighborhood's landmarks include the George Washington Bridge (1931) and its bus station, the Little Red Lighthouse (Jeffrey's Hook Light) beneath the bridge, Fort Tryon Park and The Cloisters — the Metropolitan Museum's medieval art branch near its northern edge — and the Morris–Jumel Mansion, Manhattan's oldest surviving house, which served as Washington's headquarters in 1776. Major institutions include the Columbia University Irving Medical Center and NewYork-Presbyterian Hospital around 168th Street, and Yeshiva University just to the north in Hudson Heights. The musical and film In the Heights by Lin-Manuel Miranda is set in the neighborhood.
