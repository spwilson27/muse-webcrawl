Yasujirō Ozu

Born12 December 1903, Tokyo, Japan
Died12 December 1963, Tokyo
OccupationFilm director, screenwriter
Notable worksLate Spring, Tokyo Story, An Autumn Afternoon

Yasujirō Ozu (小津 安二郎, 12 December 1903 – 12 December 1963) was a Japanese filmmaker widely regarded as one of the greatest and most influential directors in cinema history. His films typically portray ordinary family life in Japan with a distinctive, restrained style.

Career and style

Ozu began at Shochiku studios in 1923 and directed his first film in 1927. After silent comedies and social dramas, he developed the family dramas (shomin-geki) for which he is best known, frequently collaborating with screenwriter Kōgo Noda and actor Chishū Ryū. Signature devices include the low "tatami shot" camera position, direct cuts rather than fades, still "pillow shots" of empty rooms and landscapes, and frontal, formally composed dialogue scenes.

Legacy

Tokyo Story (1953) is routinely ranked among the greatest films ever made, topping the Sight and Sound directors' poll in 2012. Ozu died on his 60th birthday in 1963; his gravestone at Engaku-ji in Kamakura bears the single character mu ("nothingness").
