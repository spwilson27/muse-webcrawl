Red yeast rice


Red yeast rice (紅麴米), a fermented rice product coloured by the mold Monascus purpureus.

Red yeast rice (simplified Chinese: 红曲米; traditional Chinese: 紅麴米; pinyin: hóngqūmǐ) is a bright reddish-purple fermented rice, cultivated with the mold Monascus purpureus. It is used extensively in Chinese cuisine as a natural food colouring and flavouring agent, and in traditional Chinese medicine. In Japan it is known as beni-kōji, and in the Philippines as angkak.

Production

Red yeast rice is produced by fermenting cooked, non-glutinous rice with strains of the mold Monascus purpureus. The mold pigments—monascorubramine, rubropunctatin, monascin and related azaphilones—give the grains their characteristic deep red to purple colour. The dried product may be sold whole or ground into a powder.

Culinary uses

Red yeast rice is used to colour a wide variety of dishes, including Peking duck, char siu pork, pickled tofu, and certain Chinese pastries and rice wines. It imparts a subtle flavour and an appetising red hue historically associated with good fortune.

Medicinal use and controversy

During fermentation, Monascus produces compounds called monacolins, the best known being monacolin K, which is chemically identical to the cholesterol-lowering drug lovastatin. Red yeast rice supplements have therefore been marketed for cholesterol control, but the variable and unregulated monacolin content has raised safety and regulatory concerns, particularly in the United States and Europe. Some batches have also been contaminated with citrinin, a nephrotoxic mycotoxin. In 2024, supplements containing beni-kōji in Japan were recalled after reports of kidney damage and hospitalisations.
