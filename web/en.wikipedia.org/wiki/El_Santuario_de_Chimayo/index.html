El Santuario de Chimayo

El Santuario de Chimayó is a Roman Catholic church in Chimayó, New Mexico, in the United States. The sanctuary is a National Historic Landmark and is famous as a pilgrimage site, sometimes called the "Lourdes of America", receiving almost 300,000 visitors per year. It is dedicated to Nuestro Señor de Esquipulas (Our Lord of Esquipulas).

History

The church was built between 1814 and 1816 by Bernardo de la Encarnación Abeyta on land associated with a miraculous crucifix. According to local tradition, around 1810 Abeyta saw a light emanating from the ground and dug up a crucifix, which was taken to the parish church at Santa Cruz three times but each time returned by itself to the spot where it was found; a chapel was therefore built on that site.

The holy dirt

A small room in the church, el pocito, contains a pit of "holy dirt" (tierra bendita) which many believe has healing powers. Pilgrims rub the dirt on their bodies or consume it. The site is especially busy during Holy Week, when thousands make the pilgrimage on foot from Santa Fe and beyond, some walking more than 90 miles from Albuquerque.

Architecture

The adobe church features two distinctive bell towers with wooden spires, a cruciform plan, and an interior decorated with folk religious art, including a large reredos painted by the santero José Rafael Aragón.
