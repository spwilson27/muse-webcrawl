PEN International

PEN International (often styled PEN International, formerly International PEN) is a worldwide association of writers, founded in London in 1921. It promotes literature and campaigns for freedom of expression, and is one of the oldest international non-governmental organisations.

History

PEN was founded by the English poet and playwright Catherine Amy Dawson Scott, with John Galsworthy as its first president. The name was originally an acronym for "Poets, Essayists and Novelists", later broadened to include writers of all forms. Early members included Joseph Conrad, George Bernard Shaw and H. G. Wells.

Work

PEN International coordinates more than 100 autonomous PEN centres in over 100 countries. Its Writers in Prison Committee, founded in 1960, campaigns on behalf of persecuted writers, and PEN publishes case lists of writers who have been imprisoned, threatened or killed for their work. The organisation holds consultative status with UNESCO and the United Nations.

PEN Charter

Members subscribe to the PEN Charter, first adopted in 1948, which pledges them to oppose the suppression of freedom of expression, to dispel hatreds of race, class and nation, and to champion the idea of one humanity living in peace in one world.
