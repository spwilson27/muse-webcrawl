Online publishing

Online publishing (also called digital publishing or web publishing) is the production and distribution of books, journals, newspapers, magazines, and other content over the internet. It emerged in the late twentieth century as an alternative and complement to print publishing, radically lowering distribution costs and enabling instant global reach.

History

Precursors included text distributed through early networks and bulletin board systems. The growth of the World Wide Web in the 1990s led newspapers and magazines to establish web editions, and projects such as Project Gutenberg made large libraries of public-domain texts available digitally. Academic publishing moved substantially online through digital libraries and repositories, and the open access movement pressed for research outputs to be freely available.

Forms

Online publishing encompasses ebooks, online newspapers and magazines, blogs, wikis, scholarly journals, digital libraries, and self-publishing platforms. Distribution models include subscription sites, paywalled services, advertising-supported sites, and freely accessible open content.

Impact

Digital distribution eliminated many printing and shipping costs and enabled search, hyperlinking, and multimedia. It also disrupted established business models: advertising revenue migrated online, print circulation declined, and publishers experimented with metered paywalls and membership models. Online publishing raised persistent questions about preservation, since digital content can be altered or removed, and about copyright enforcement in an environment where copying is trivial.
