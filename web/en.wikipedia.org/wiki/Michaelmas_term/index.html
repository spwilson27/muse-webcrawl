Michaelmas term

The Michaelmas term is the first academic term of the year at a number of British and Irish universities, including Oxford and Cambridge, as well as many schools. It is named after the Feast of St Michael and All Angels (Michaelmas), which falls on 29 September. The term runs through the autumn, roughly from October to December.

At the ancient universities

At the University of Oxford, Michaelmas term is one of three terms — Michaelmas, Hilary (spring) and Trinity (summer) — and teaching takes place in eight-week "full terms". Cambridge uses the sequence Michaelmas, Lent and Easter. The term includes freshers' week, matriculation ceremonies, and (at Oxford) the traditional calendar of collections and termly examinations in some subjects. Many other institutions, including Durham and Lancaster, use the same terminology.

Legal year

Michaelmas is also one of the four sittings of the courts of England and Wales, running from the start of October to just before Christmas, alongside the Hilary, Easter and Trinity sittings. The legal year traditionally opens with a service at Westminster Abbey and a procession of judges.

Other uses

Michaelmas was traditionally a quarter day in England, when rents fell due and servants were hired; Michaelmas daisies, which flower in late September, are named for the feast.
