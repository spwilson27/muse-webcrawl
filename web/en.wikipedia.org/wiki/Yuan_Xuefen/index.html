Yuan Xuefen

Yuan Xuefen (26 March 1922 – 19 February 2011) was a Chinese Yue opera performer and reformer, widely credited as the founder of the modern form of Yue opera (Yueju) and the creator of the Yuan school of performance. She is regarded as one of the most influential figures in twentieth-century Chinese theatre.

Born in Shengzhou, Zhejiang province — the home region of Yue opera — Yuan began performing as a child in the all-female troupes characteristic of the art form. In 1942, in Shanghai, she launched a celebrated reform of Yue opera, modernising staging, costumes, lighting and repertoire, and giving the genre contemporary, realistic themes. Her production of Sister Xianglin (Xianglin Sao), adapted from Lu Xun's story "The New Year's Sacrifice", was a landmark of the reform movement.

Career

In 1944 Yuan founded the Snow Voice Troupe (Xuesheng Jutuan). Her celebrated works include The Romance of the Western Chamber (Xi Xiang Ji), The Butterfly Lovers (Liang Shanbo yu Zhu Yingtai) and A Dream of Red Mansions (Hong Lou Meng). After 1949 she helped establish the Shanghai Yue Opera Theatre, leading the troupe that created the productions later filmed and seen throughout China. She served in cultural and political posts, including membership of the National People's Congress standing bodies, and championed the professional status of women performers.

Legacy

The Yuan school (Yuan pai) is known for its understated, lyrical singing and naturalistic acting. Yuan Xuefen trained generations of performers, and her reforms are credited with transforming Yue opera from a regional entertainment into a major national art form, second in popularity in China only to Peking opera.
