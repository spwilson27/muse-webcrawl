Technophobia

Technophobia (from Greek téchnē, "art, skill, craft" and phóbos, "fear") is the fear of, or aversion to, new technology or complex devices, especially computers. The term is used both for a clinical anxiety response and, more loosely, for a general cultural or occupational resistance to technological change. Its opposite is technophilia.

Prevalence and forms

Studies of computer anxiety have found that a substantial minority of users experience apprehension when using computers, ranging from mild discomfort to avoidance that interferes with work. Technophobia can manifest in several forms, including fear of damaging equipment, anxiety about being monitored, distrust of automation, and concern that technology will displace human jobs or skills.

Researchers have sometimes divided technophobes into categories such as the "resistant" (who avoid technology), the "hesitant" (who use it reluctantly and anxiously), and the "cognitive" technophobe (who outwardly uses technology but harbours doubts about its effects).

History

Anxieties about machines predate the digital age. The Luddites of early nineteenth-century England destroyed textile machinery they believed threatened their livelihoods, and the word "Luddite" has since become a general label for opponents of new technology. The Industrial Revolution, electrification, nuclear power, and genetic engineering each provoked waves of public unease. In the late twentieth and early twenty-first centuries, debate shifted to computers, the internet, smartphones, and artificial intelligence.

Causes and treatment

Contributing factors include lack of training, negative early experiences with devices, age-related changes, media portrayals of technology as dangerous, and genuine concerns about privacy, safety, or employment. Where the fear is severe, it may be treated like other specific phobias, with gradual exposure and cognitive behavioural techniques; in workplace settings, training, supportive design, and patient technical assistance usually reduce anxiety substantially.

In popular culture

Technophobia is a recurring theme in science fiction, from Mary Shelley's Frankenstein (1818) to films about runaway computers and robots. Such stories often channel broader social anxieties about the pace of change and the loss of human control.
