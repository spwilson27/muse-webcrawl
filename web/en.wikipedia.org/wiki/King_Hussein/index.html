King Hussein

Hussein bin Talal (14 November 1935 – 7 February 1999) was King of Jordan from 11 August 1952 until his death in 1999. As a member of the Hashemite dynasty, he was a 40th-generation direct descendant of the Islamic prophet Muhammad.

Early life and education

Hussein was born in Amman as the eldest child of Talal bin Abdullah and Zein Al-Sharaf. He attended Victoria College in Alexandria, Egypt, and Harrow School in England, before receiving military training at the Royal Military Academy Sandhurst. He became king at the age of 16 after his grandfather, King Abdullah I, was assassinated in Jerusalem in 1951 and his father Talal was forced to abdicate due to mental illness.

Reign

Hussein ruled Jordan through a period of regional turmoil. Early in his reign, Jordan controlled the West Bank and East Jerusalem following the 1948 Arab–Israeli War. Jordan lost the West Bank to Israel in the Six-Day War of 1967. In 1970–71, Hussein ordered the expulsion of the Palestine Liberation Organization from Jordan in the conflict known as Black September. He formally renounced Jordan's claim to the West Bank in 1988, and signed the Israel–Jordan peace treaty in 1994, making Jordan the second Arab state to normalize relations with Israel.

Personal life and death

Hussein married four times and had eleven children. His fourth wife, Queen Noor, survived him. He was an avid pilot, motorcyclist and amateur radio operator (call sign JY1). Hussein died of non-Hodgkin lymphoma on 7 February 1999 and was succeeded by his eldest son, Abdullah II.
