The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo

The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo (Swedish: Män som hatar kvinnor, lit. "Men Who Hate Women") is a psychological thriller novel by Swedish author Stieg Larsson. Published posthumously in 2005, it is the first book in the Millennium series.

Plot

Disgraced journalist Mikael Blomkvist is hired by retired industrialist Henrik Vanger to investigate the disappearance of his niece Harriet, who vanished in 1966 from the family's island estate. Aided by Lisbeth Salander — a brilliant, antisocial hacker with a dragon tattoo — Blomkvist uncovers a history of murder, Nazism and abuse within the powerful Vanger family, and exposes the corrupt financier Hans-Erik Wennerström whose libel suit had ruined Blomkvist's career.

Characters

  • Lisbeth Salander — a 24-year-old hacker and researcher with a photographic memory, a ward of the state, and a survivor of abuse.
  • Mikael Blomkvist — investigative journalist and co-owner of Millennium magazine.
  • Henrik Vanger — former CEO of the Vanger Corporation.

Publication and reception

Larsson died of a heart attack in 2004, shortly after delivering the manuscripts of the first three novels. The series became a global phenomenon, selling tens of millions of copies. The novel won the Glass Key Award and has been praised for its plotting and its unflinching depiction of violence against women, though some critics found that violence graphic.

Adaptations

A Swedish film adaptation starring Noomi Rapace was released in 2009, followed by David Fincher's English-language film in 2011 starring Rooney Mara and Daniel Craig. The series was continued after Larsson's death by David Lagercrantz and later Karin Smirnoff.
