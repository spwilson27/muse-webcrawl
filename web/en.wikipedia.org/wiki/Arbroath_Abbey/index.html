Arbroath Abbey

Arbroath Abbey
Founded: 1178
Founder: William the Lion
Order: Tironensian (Benedictine)
Location: Arbroath, Angus, Scotland

Arbroath Abbey, in the Scottish town of Arbroath, Angus, was founded in 1178 by King William the Lion for a community of Tironensian Benedictine monks from Kelso Abbey. It was dedicated to Saint Thomas Becket, and William himself was buried in the abbey church in 1214. The abbey is best known as the place where, in 1320, the Declaration of Arbroath was drawn up.

History

Richly endowed with lands, churches, and trading rights, Arbroath became one of the wealthiest abbeys in Scotland, and its abbots were prominent figures in national affairs. The abbey church, built of local red sandstone, was consecrated in 1233. The complex suffered during the Wars of Scottish Independence and again at the Reformation, after which the buildings decayed into a quarry for the town.

Declaration of Arbroath

On 6 April 1320 the Scottish barons sent a letter to Pope John XXII — the Declaration of Arbroath — asserting Scotland's status as an independent kingdom and Robert the Bruce's right to the throne. Composed at the abbey, it is among the most famous documents of Scottish history.

The Stone of Destiny

In 1951, after the Stone of Scone was removed from Westminster Abbey by Scottish students, it was left at Arbroath Abbey before being recovered by police — a nod to the abbey's symbolic role in Scottish national identity. Surviving ruins, including the distinctive round "O" window in the south transept, are cared for by Historic Environment Scotland.
