Manx people

Regions: Isle of Man; diaspora in England, USA, Canada, Australia
Languages: English, Manx
Religion: Predominantly Christianity (Anglican, Methodist, Catholic)

The Manx people (Manx: Ny Manninee) are a Celtic ethnic group originating on the Isle of Man in the Irish Sea, a self-governing British Crown Dependency. Their ancestry combines Gaelic and Norse elements, reflecting the island's history of Irish settlement and Viking rule.

History

The island was settled by Gaelic speakers in the early centuries AD and came under Norse control from the 9th century, forming part of the Kingdom of Mann and the Isles. Norse governance left a lasting legacy, including the Tynwald, the island's parliament, which claims to be the oldest continuous parliamentary body in the world. Mann later passed to Scotland and then to English lordship, but retained its distinct laws and institutions.

Language

The Manx language (Gaelg), a Goidelic Celtic language closely related to Irish and Scottish Gaelic, declined during the 19th and 20th centuries; its last native speaker, Ned Maddrell, died in 1974. A revival movement has since restored Manx to schools, broadcasting, and daily use, and it is now taught as an immersion medium at the Bunscoill Ghaelgagh primary school.

Culture

Manx identity is symbolised by the triskelion — three armoured legs joined at the thigh — and by the motto Quocunque jeceris stabit ("whichever way you throw it, it will stand"). Cultural traditions include Manx music and dance, the tailless Manx cat, the four-horned Loaghtan sheep, and folklore centred on figures such as the moddey dhoo and the buggane.
