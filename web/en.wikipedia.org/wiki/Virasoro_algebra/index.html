Virasoro algebra

The Virasoro algebra is a complex Lie algebra that is the unique (nontrivial) central extension of the Witt algebra of polynomial vector fields on a circle. Named after the Argentine-Italian physicist Miguel Ángel Virasoro, who introduced the corresponding structure (the Virasoro–Shapiro model) in string theory in 1970, it is of central importance in two-dimensional conformal field theory and in string theory, where it encodes the infinitesimal conformal symmetries of the worldsheet or of a two-dimensional critical system.

Definition

The Virasoro algebra is generated by elements Ln for integers n and a central element c (the central charge), with commutation relations

[Lm, Ln] = (m − n) Lm+n + (c/12) (m³ − m) δm+n,0.

The central element commutes with all generators. Without the central term, the generators satisfy the Witt algebra relations; the term proportional to c is the unique nontrivial two-cocycle, so the Virasoro algebra is the universal central extension of the Witt algebra.

Representation theory

Physically relevant representations are highest-weight representations: a primary state is annihilated by all Ln for n > 0 and is an eigenstate of L0 with eigenvalue h, the conformal weight. Descendant states are obtained by applying lowering operators L−n. The structure of these Verma modules — their null vectors and unitarity — underlies the classification of conformal field theories. For special values of c (the discrete series c = 1 − 6/(m(m+1)) for integer m ≥ 2) and corresponding weights, the unitary minimal models arise, exactly solved theories describing critical points such as the two-dimensional Ising model (c = 1/2).

Applications

In string theory, two copies of the Virasoro algebra (left- and right-moving) impose the Virasoro constraints on physical states; the requirement of anomaly cancellation fixes the critical dimension (26 for the bosonic string, 10 for superstrings). In statistical mechanics, the algebra governs correlation functions and finite-size scaling at two-dimensional critical points. Extensions include the super-Virasoro algebras and W-algebras. Related mathematical developments include the Monster vertex algebra and monstrous moonshine, where Virasoro representation theory plays a key role.

History

The Witt algebra was studied by Élie Cartan in 1909; its central extension appeared in work by Gelfand and Fuchs (1968) in the cohomology of Lie algebras of vector fields. Virasoro's 1970 paper introduced the operators in the dual resonance model context, giving the algebra its physical name.
