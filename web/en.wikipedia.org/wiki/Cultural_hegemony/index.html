Cultural hegemony
Theorist: Antonio Gramsci
Work: Prison Notebooks (1929–35)
Field: Marxist theory, cultural studies

Cultural hegemony is the dominance of a culturally diverse society by the ruling class through ideology and culture, such that the worldview of the rulers comes to be accepted as common sense. The concept was developed by Italian Marxist Antonio Gramsci in his Prison Notebooks.

Concept

Gramsci argued that capitalist rule rests not only on coercion (the state, police, army) but on consent manufactured in civil society — schools, churches, media and families. A ruling group leads by presenting its interests as universal; subordinate groups may internalise this "common sense".

War of position

Counter-hegemony requires a long "war of position": building alternative institutions and organic intellectuals who articulate the worldview of subaltern classes, before any "war of manoeuvre" against the state.

Influence

The idea shaped cultural studies, education theory, postcolonial studies and analyses of media power, and is related to cultural relativism debates and to the culture war concept.
