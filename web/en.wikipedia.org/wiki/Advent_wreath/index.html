The Advent wreath is a Christian tradition that symbolizes the passage of the four weeks of Advent in the liturgical calendar of the Western church. It is traditionally a Lutheran practice, although it has spread to many other Christian denominations. The wreath is a horizontal evergreen circle bearing four candles, one lit on each Sunday of Advent; a fifth, white candle is sometimes placed in the centre and lit on Christmas Eve or Christmas Day.

History

The modern Advent wreath was created in 1839 by Johann Hinrich Wichern, a Protestant pastor in Hamburg who ran the Rauhes Haus mission school. Impatient children asked daily whether Christmas had arrived, so Wichern built a large wooden ring from a cartwheel, with twenty small red candles and four large white candles, lighting one each day. Smaller wreaths with four candles for the Sundays caught on in German homes and churches and spread internationally in the 20th century.

Symbolism

The circular evergreen wreath signifies eternal life and God's unending love. In many Western traditions the candles are purple (or blue), with the third candle rose-coloured for Gaudete Sunday. Themes assigned to the four candles commonly include hope, peace, joy, and love; the central white candle represents Christ.

Use

Advent wreaths are used in churches, where they are often suspended or placed near the altar, and in homes on dining tables, with a candle lit each Sunday, frequently accompanied by prayers or readings. In Scandinavian countries the wreath is a near-universal domestic decoration in December.
