The 2007 Peru earthquake (also called the Pisco earthquake) was a magnitude 8.0 earthquake that struck the central coast of Peru on 15 August 2007, lasting about two minutes. Its epicentre lay in the Pacific Ocean some 150 km south-southeast of Lima, near the cities of Pisco, Ica and Chincha Alta.

Damage

The Ica Region was devastated. In Pisco, roughly 85% of buildings were damaged or destroyed, and the cathedral collapsed during an evening service, killing many worshippers. Adobe construction, widespread in the affected towns, performed disastrously. In total 595 people died, about 2,300 were injured and tens of thousands of homes were destroyed. A tsunami warning was issued; a modest tsunami struck parts of the coast, flooding the fishing village of Lagunillas.

Response

The government of President Alan García declared a state of emergency. International aid arrived from across Latin America, the United States, Spain and aid agencies, though distribution to remote areas was criticised as slow. Reconstruction in Pisco and Chincha dragged on for years and became a lasting political issue in Peru.

Geology

The quake resulted from subduction of the Nazca Plate beneath the South American Plate, rupturing a segment of the plate boundary offshore central Peru.
