Walden Pond

Walden Pond is a pond in Concord, Massachusetts, in the United States, formed by retreating glaciers 10,000–12,000 years ago. It is celebrated as the site where writer Henry David Thoreau lived in a cabin from 1845 to 1847 and wrote his classic book Walden (1854).

Thoreau's experiment

On land owned by his friend Ralph Waldo Emerson, Thoreau built a small cabin near the pond's shore and lived there for two years, two months and two days, seeking a simpler, more deliberate life. His account, Walden; or, Life in the Woods, combined memoir, natural observation and philosophical reflection, and became a foundational text of American transcendentalism and environmental writing. The cabin site is now marked by stone posts and a cairn, and a replica cabin stands near the reservation entrance.

The pond today

Walden Pond is a kettle lake about 31 hectares (76 acres) in area and up to about 31 metres (102 feet) deep, with no surface inlet or outlet. The surrounding Walden Woods and the pond are protected as the Walden Pond State Reservation, a National Historic Landmark. The pond is a popular swimming, walking and fishing destination; its clear waters and wooded shores are managed to balance recreation with conservation of the historic landscape.
