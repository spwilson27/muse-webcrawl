The Ouija (pronounced "wee-jee"), also known as a spirit board or talking board, is a flat board marked with the letters of the alphabet, the numbers 0–9, and the words "yes", "no", "hello" and "goodbye", used with a small heart-shaped pointer called a planchette. Participants place their fingers lightly on the planchette during a séance, and the pointer is believed to move to spell out messages from spirits. The board grew out of 19th-century spiritualism and was commercialised in the United States in 1890–91 by the Kennard Novelty Company.

History

Talking boards appeared amid the spiritualist craze that followed the American Civil War. Businessman Elijah Bond patented the commercial board in 1891; company lore held that the name "Ouija" was obtained by asking the board itself, which allegedly explained it meant "good luck"—though the word is popularly derived from the French oui and German ja, both meaning "yes". William Fuld took over production and popularised the board in the early 20th century. Sales passed to Parker Brothers in 1966 and to Hasbro in 1991, which markets it as a game despite continuing occult associations, strengthened in popular culture by the 1973 film The Exorcist.

Explanation

Scientists explain the planchette's movement by the ideomotor effect: unconscious muscular movements of the participants, demonstrated since Michael Faraday's experiments on table-turning in 1853. The Ouija has nevertheless been taken seriously by many users and condemned by some religious groups as a tool of divination or demonic contact.
