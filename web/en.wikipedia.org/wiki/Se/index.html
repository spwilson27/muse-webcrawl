Se

Se may refer to several topics. This page serves as a general overview of the term in its principal senses.

Language and writing

In Japanese kana, せ (hiragana) and セ (katakana) represent the mora "se", derived from the kanji 世 and 世 respectively in their cursive and abbreviated forms. In linguistics, "Se" also appears as a notation in various transcription systems.

Chemistry

Se is the chemical symbol for selenium, the element with atomic number 34, a nonmetal with properties intermediate between sulfur and tellurium, discovered by Jöns Jacob Berzelius in 1817. Selenium is an essential trace nutrient but toxic in large amounts, and is used in photocells, solar cells, and glass manufacture.

Geography and other uses

Sé and Se are names or components of names of settlements in several countries, including parishes in Portugal. In computing and other contexts, "SE" (capitalised) commonly stands for software engineering, standard edition, or south-east on compass points; the lowercase form appears in internet country-code and language codes such as .se for Sweden.
