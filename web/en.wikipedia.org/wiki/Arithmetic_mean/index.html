Arithmetic mean

Comparison of arithmetic mean, median and mode

FieldStatistics, mathematics
Also calledAverage

In mathematics and statistics, the arithmetic mean (pronounced arr-ith-MET-ik), or simply the mean or the average (when the context is clear), is the sum of a collection of numbers divided by the count of numbers in the collection. The collection is often a set of results from an experiment, an observational study, or a survey. The term "arithmetic mean" is preferred in some mathematics and statistics contexts because it helps distinguish it from other kinds of means, such as geometric and harmonic means.

Arithmetic means are used frequently in economics, anthropology, history, and almost every academic field to some extent. For example, per capita income is the arithmetic average income of a nation's population. While the arithmetic mean is often used to report central tendencies, it is not a robust statistic: it is greatly influenced by outliers (values much larger or smaller than most others).

Definition

The arithmetic mean of a data set of n values x1, x2, ..., xn is the sum of the values divided by n. It is commonly denoted by x̄ ("x bar"). If the data set is a population, the mean is often denoted by the Greek letter μ (mu); for a sample, the sample mean x̄ serves as an estimator of the population mean.

Properties

The arithmetic mean is the point that minimises the sum of squared deviations from the data values. The sum of the deviations of the values from their mean is always zero. When several quantities grow at combined rates, the arithmetic mean of the growth rates may not reflect the overall growth, in which case the geometric mean is more appropriate.
