Southport Pier

Southport Pier is a pleasure pier in Southport, Merseyside, England. Opened in August 1860, it was the first iron pier built purely for recreation in Britain, and at about 1,100 metres (3,600 feet) it is the second-longest pier in the country, after Southend Pier.

History

The pier was designed by James Brunlees and built by W. and J. Galloway. Because the sea at Southport recedes a great distance at low tide, the pier stretches far across the sands merely to reach the water. In 1868 it was lengthened and acquired a pierhead; steamers once called for excursions to Blackpool and the Isle of Man. A pier tramway has run along its length in various forms since 1863 — the first pier railway in the country — and the current tram service continues today.

Restoration

After years of decay, the Grade II listed pier was restored around 2000–2002 with lottery funding, receiving a new deck, a modern pavilion and period-style railings. It remains a focal point of the resort, alongside the Marine Lake and Lord Street.
