The Ted Lindsay Award, formerly known as the Lester B. Pearson Award, is presented annually to the "most outstanding player" in the National Hockey League (NHL) as voted by fellow members of the NHL Players' Association (NHLPA). It is the players' counterpart to the Hart Memorial Trophy, which is voted by hockey writers.

History

The award was first presented at the end of the 1970–71 season and was named for Lester B. Pearson, a former Prime Minister of Canada and Nobel Peace Prize laureate who had himself been a hockey player and coach. On 29 April 2010, the NHLPA renamed the trophy in honour of Ted Lindsay, the Detroit Red Wings star who was instrumental in founding the players' association in 1957.

Winners

Wayne Gretzky won the award a record five times during his career. Other multiple winners include Mario Lemieux, Connor McDavid, Sidney Crosby, Alexander Ovechkin, Guy Lafleur, and Steve Yzerman. Phil Esposito was the first recipient in 1971.
