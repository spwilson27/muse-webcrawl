Sun Koshi

The Sun Koshi, also spelled Sunkoshi (Nepali: सुनकोशी, "golden river"), is a river in Nepal that forms part of the Koshi or Sapta Koshi river system. Rising in Tibet, where its upper course is known as the Bhote Koshi, it flows southeast through the Himalayas and joins the Arun and Tamor rivers to form the Sapta Koshi.

Course

The river enters Nepal near Kodari on the Araniko Highway, runs roughly parallel to the highway through Sindhupalchok District, and passes Dolalghat, where it is joined by the Indravati. It then flows east and southeast through deep gorges before meeting the Arun and Tamor at Tribeni.

Recreation and hazards

The Sun Koshi is one of Nepal's best-known whitewater rafting and kayaking rivers, famous for long multi-day expeditions. Its basin is prone to glacial lake outburst floods and devastating landslides, such as the 2014 Jure landslide, which dammed the river and forced large evacuations.
