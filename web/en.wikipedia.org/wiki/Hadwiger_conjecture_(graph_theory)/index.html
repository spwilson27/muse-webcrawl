Hadwiger conjecture (graph theory)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A graph requiring six colours has K6 as a minor: contract each colour-linked region to a single vertex.

In graph theory, the Hadwiger conjecture states that if a graph G requires k colours in any proper vertex colouring of its vertices (that is, if its chromatic number is k), then the complete graph Kk can be obtained from G by deleting edges and vertices and by contracting edges. The conjecture was proposed by Hugo Hadwiger in 1943 and remains open in general; it has been described by Bollobás, Catlin, and Erdős as "one of the deepest unsolved problems in graph theory".

Statement

Equivalently: there is no k-chromatic graph that excludes Kk as a minor. Contrapositively, every graph with no Kk minor is (k − 1)-colourable. Because having a large complete-graph minor implies high connectivity and density, the conjecture links colouring, minors, and degeneracy in a strong way.

Known cases

  • k ≤ 4: straightforward; the case k = 4 was shown by Hadwiger himself (and by Dirac).
  • k = 5: Wagner proved in 1937 that it is equivalent to the four colour theorem; hence true after Appel and Haken's 1976 proof of that theorem.
  • k = 6: proved by Robertson, Seymour, and Thomas (1993), also using the four colour theorem, for which they won the Fulkerson Prize.
  • k ≥ 7: open. The best general bounds show that a graph with chromatic number k has a complete minor of order growing almost linearly in k (up to logarithmic factors; results of Kostochka, Thomason, and later improvements).

Related results

The stronger Hajós conjecture (that a k-chromatic graph contains a subdivision of Kk) is false for large k (counterexamples by Catlin), which makes Hadwiger's minor version the plausible correct strengthening. A list-colouring analogue and fractional versions have also been studied, and a famous related result — the Robertson–Seymour graph minor theorem — guarantees that minor-closed families are characterised by finite sets of forbidden minors, though it does not settle this conjecture.
