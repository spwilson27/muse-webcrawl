Action (philosophy)

In philosophy, an action is an event brought about by an agent acting intentionally — that is, for reasons and under a conception of what they are doing. Philosophy of action investigates the nature of action, how actions are distinguished from mere happenings or bodily movements, and how intentions, beliefs and desires explain and cause what agents do.

Basic and complex actions

Philosophers distinguish basic actions, performed directly (such as raising one's arm), from actions performed by doing something else (such as signalling by raising one's arm). Donald Davidson argued that a single event can fall under many action descriptions — the so-called "accordion effect" — and that reasons can be causes of action.

Key questions

Central problems include the nature of intention and intentional action (Elizabeth Anscombe's Intention, 1957), weakness of will, the individuation of actions, and the relation between action explanation and the philosophy of mind. The field bears on ethics, free will and the law's concept of voluntary conduct.
