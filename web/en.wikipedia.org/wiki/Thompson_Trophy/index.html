EventNational Air Races, United States
First awarded1930
FormatClosed-course pylon race for unlimited aircraft
NamesakeJohn L. Thompson / Thompson Products, Inc.

The Thompson Trophy race was one of the most prestigious events of the American National Air Races, a closed-course speed race around pylons for the fastest landplanes in the United States. Sponsored by Thompson Products (later TRW), it was first flown in 1930 and became the headline event of the Cleveland air races of the 1930s.

History

Early winners included Charles "Speed" Holman and Jimmy Doolittle, who won in 1932 in the Gee Bee R-1. Roscoe Turner won the trophy three times (1934, 1938, 1939), more than any other pilot. After the Second World War the race resumed with converted fighter aircraft such as the P-51 Mustang and F2G Corsair.

End of the races

The 1949 Cleveland races ended in tragedy when a competing F2G crashed into a nearby home, killing a mother and child; the Thompson Trophy races were discontinued in their original form, though the name was later revived for jet and other classes at the Reno Air Races.
