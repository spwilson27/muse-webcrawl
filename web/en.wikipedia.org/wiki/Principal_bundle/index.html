Principal bundle

Placeholder image: diagram of a principal bundle projection

In mathematics, a principal bundle is a mathematical object that formalizes some of the essential features of the Cartesian product X × G of a space X with a group G. Analogous to the product, a principal bundle lacks a preferred choice of identity element, and locally looks like such a product while possibly carrying a more complicated overall topology.

Principal bundle
FieldDifferential geometry, topology
DataSpace P, group G, base M, projection π: P → M
ActionFree and transitive right action of G on fibres

Definition

A principal G-bundle over a base space M is a fibre bundle π : P → M together with a continuous right action of a topological (or Lie) group G on P such that G preserves the fibres of π and acts freely and transitively on them. Each fibre is then homeomorphic to G itself, though without a canonical "identity" point.

Examples

  • The frame bundle of a smooth manifold, whose fibre at each point is the set of all ordered bases of the tangent space, with structure group GL(n, ℝ).
  • The Hopf fibration S1 → S3 → S2, a principal circle bundle.
  • Covering spaces are principal bundles with discrete structure group.
  • In physics, gauge theories are formulated on principal bundles, with connections corresponding to gauge potentials.

Associated bundles

Given a principal G-bundle and a representation of G on a space F, one can form an associated bundle with fibre F; vector bundles such as tangent bundles arise this way from frame bundles.
