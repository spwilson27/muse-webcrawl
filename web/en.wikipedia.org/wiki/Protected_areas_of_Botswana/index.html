Protected areas of Botswana

Botswana has one of the highest proportions of protected land of any country in the world: roughly 17% of its territory is set aside in national parks and game reserves, with a further large share designated as Wildlife Management Areas, bringing the total under some form of conservation management to around 38–45%. The system is administered by the Department of Wildlife and National Parks under the National Parks Act and the Wildlife Conservation and National Parks Act of 1992.

National parks

  • Chobe National Park — famous for its very large elephant population and the Chobe riverfront.
  • Makgadikgadi Pans National Park — seasonal wetlands and one of Africa's largest zebra migrations.
  • Nxai Pan National Park — including the Baines Baobabs.
  • Kgalagadi Transfrontier Park — shared with South Africa, known for black-maned Kalahari lions.

Game reserves

  • Central Kalahari Game Reserve — one of the largest protected areas in Africa, at over 52,000 km².
  • Moremi Game Reserve — covering much of the eastern Okavango Delta, renowned for wildlife viewing.
  • Khutse Game Reserve and the Mannyelanong, Maun, and Gaborone Game Reserves near urban areas.

The Okavango Delta, largely protected through Moremi and surrounding Wildlife Management Areas, was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2014. Botswana's parks form part of the Kavango–Zambezi Transfrontier Conservation Area (KAZA), the world's largest transfrontier conservation area.
