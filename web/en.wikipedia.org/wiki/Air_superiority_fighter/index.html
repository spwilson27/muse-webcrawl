Air superiority fighter

An air superiority fighter (also called an air dominance fighter) is a fighter aircraft designed to seize and maintain control of enemy airspace — air superiority — as its primary mission, typically by defeating opposing fighters in air-to-air combat. Such aircraft are usually fast, highly manoeuvrable, equipped with powerful radars and beyond-visual-range missiles, and are among the most expensive military aircraft per unit.

Development

The concept emerged from Second World War experience, where fighters such as the P-51 Mustang cleared skies ahead of bombers. Pure air superiority designs reached prominence in the Cold War with the McDonnell Douglas F-15 Eagle (first flight 1972), developed after Vietnam-era lessons and intelligence on Soviet aircraft, and its Soviet counterpart, the Sukhoi Su-27. Fifth-generation types — the Lockheed Martin F-22 Raptor, Chengdu J-20 and Sukhoi Su-57 — add stealth, sensor fusion and supercruise. The Eurofighter Typhoon was also designed primarily for the air superiority role.

Distinction from other fighters

Air superiority fighters differ from interceptors, which sacrifice manoeuvrability for speed and climb to engage bombers, and from multirole fighters, which balance air-to-air and air-to-ground missions. In practice many modern air superiority designs have gained secondary ground-attack capability, but their sensors, performance and cost remain optimised for counter-air operations.
