Donkey Kong is a 1981 platform arcade video game developed and published by Nintendo. It was designed by Shigeru Miyamoto, his first major game, and is one of the most important titles of the golden age of arcade video games. In the game, the player controls Jumpman — later renamed Mario — who must climb a construction site of girders and ladders to rescue his girlfriend (later named Pauline) from the giant ape Donkey Kong, who hurls barrels and other obstacles at him.

The first stage of Donkey Kong. The game introduced Mario and was among the first video games to tell a story through gameplay.

Development and release

Nintendo of America needed a hit to replace the failed arcade game Radar Scope. Miyamoto, an artist, designed a game inspired by Popeye and King Kong after Nintendo failed to secure the Popeye licence. Donkey Kong became a massive commercial success, selling tens of thousands of cabinets in America and establishing Nintendo in the North American market. It was ported to numerous home systems, most famously the ColecoVision and the NES.

Lawsuit

Universal Studios sued Nintendo in 1982, claiming Donkey Kong infringed its rights to King Kong. Nintendo, defended by lawyer John Kirby, won the case — and famously named the character Kirby after its attorney. The victory was pivotal for the young Nintendo of America.

Legacy

The game launched two of Nintendo's biggest franchises: Mario and Donkey Kong, the latter continuing with the Donkey Kong Country series in the 1990s. The original arcade game remains famous for competitive high-score play, documented in the 2007 film The King of Kong, and for its "kill screen" on level 22, caused by an integer overflow in the bonus timer.
