Treaty of Washington (1871)

Signed8 May 1871
LocationWashington, D.C.
PartiesUnited Kingdom, United States

The Treaty of Washington was a treaty concluded in 1871 between the United Kingdom and the United States for settling various disputes between the two governments. The treaty was signed on 8 May 1871 in Washington, D.C., by a Joint High Commission of British and American negotiators, with the Canadians (then a British dominion) also represented by Prime Minister John A. Macdonald.

Issues settled

The Alabama Claims

The most famous dispute concerned the Alabama Claims: damages sought by the United States from Britain for Confederate commerce raiders, above all CSS Alabama, built in British shipyards during the American Civil War. The treaty set rules of neutral duty and sent the claims to arbitration in Geneva. In 1872 the tribunal awarded the United States $15,500,000 in gold — a landmark in the development of international arbitration.

Other questions

  • The San Juan boundary dispute over the islands between Vancouver Island and the mainland was referred to the German Emperor Wilhelm I, who ruled in favour of the United States (the "Pig War" settlement);
  • Fisheries disputes were settled by granting reciprocal access to Atlantic fisheries, with a payment to Britain later fixed at $5,500,000 by the Halifax Commission;
  • Navigation of the St. Lawrence River and boundary lakes was opened reciprocally;
  • Claims of British subjects arising from the Civil War and of Americans from the Fenian raids were processed by a joint commission (the Fenian claims were ultimately rejected).

Significance

The treaty is widely regarded as a milestone in peaceful dispute resolution and a foundation of the subsequent "Great Rapprochement" between Britain and the United States.
