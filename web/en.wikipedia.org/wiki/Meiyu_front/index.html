Meiyu front

The Meiyu front, also known as the Baiu front in Japan, is a persistent, nearly stationary weak baroclinic zone that stretches from the east coast of China through Taiwan, across the East China Sea into southern Japan during late spring and early summer. It is responsible for the East Asian rainy season — called méiyǔ (梅雨, "plum rains") in China, tsuyu or baiu in Japan, and jangma in Korea — bringing prolonged, heavy rainfall from roughly June into July, with marked year-to-year variability in its duration and intensity.

Meteorology

The front forms where warm, moist tropical air from the western Pacific subtropical high meets cooler mid-latitude air. Convective systems repeatedly develop along the front and move eastward along it, producing episodes of torrential rain, thunderstorms, flooding, and landslides. The front migrates northward through the season and its withdrawal marks the onset of the hottest part of summer. A related early-season stationary front affects South China before the main Meiyu period.

Impacts

The rains are vital for rice cultivation and water resources but regularly cause destructive floods in the Yangtze basin and southern Japan; notably severe Meiyu seasons contributed to the 1998 China floods and record rain events in July 2020. Unusually short or displaced rain seasons ("empty plum rains") can instead produce drought and heat waves.
