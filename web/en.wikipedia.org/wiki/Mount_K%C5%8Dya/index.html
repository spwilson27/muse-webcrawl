Mount Kōya (Kōyasan) is a mountain basin in Wakayama Prefecture, Japan, and the center of Shingon Buddhism, an important school of Japanese Buddhism. The settlement was founded in 816 by the priest Kūkai, known posthumously as Kōbō Daishi, who made it the headquarters of his esoteric school.

Sites

The mountain town contains more than a hundred temples, many offering lodgings (shukubō) to pilgrims and visitors. Major sites include Kongōbu-ji, the head temple of Kōyasan Shingon Buddhism; the Danjō Garan sacred precinct with its great Konpon Daitō pagoda; and Okunoin, Japan's largest cemetery, where the mausoleum of Kūkai stands amid ancient cedar trees and over 200,000 memorial monuments.

Access and heritage

Mount Kōya is reached by the Nankai Electric Railway and a cable car from Gokurakubashi Station. In 2004 it was inscribed as part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Sacred Sites and Pilgrimage Routes in the Kii Mountain Range". Traditional pilgrimage routes, including the Kōyasan chōishi-michi, still lead up the mountain.
