Downtown Las Vegas is the historic centre of Las Vegas, Nevada, and the original gambling district of the city before the rise of the Las Vegas Strip. Centred on Fremont Street, it lies within the city limits of Las Vegas proper — unlike the Strip, which is in the unincorporated communities of Paradise and Winchester. Downtown was the site of the 1905 land auction that founded the city, at the junction of the San Pedro, Los Angeles and Salt Lake Railroad.

Gambling history

When Nevada legalised gambling in 1931, downtown's Fremont Street — nicknamed "Glitter Gulch" for its dense neon signage — became the city's casino centre, home to early properties such as the Golden Nugget (1946), Binion's Horseshoe and the Golden Gate, Las Vegas's oldest hotel and casino, which opened in 1906 as the Hotel Nevada. Legal gambling famously began downtown: casinos here received some of the state's first gaming licences.

Decline and revival

From the 1960s the centre of gravity shifted to the Strip, and downtown declined. Revitalisation began with the Fremont Street Experience, a barrel-vaulted LED canopy over five blocks of Fremont Street opened in 1995. In the 2000s and 2010s the Downtown Project, backed by Zappos CEO Tony Hsieh, invested hundreds of millions of dollars in the Fremont East entertainment district, the Downtown Container Park, and start-ups and small businesses, while historic casinos such as the Golden Nugget and The D Las Vegas were renovated and new resorts, notably Circa Resort & Casino (2020), opened. Downtown also contains the Arts District, the Smith Center for the Performing Arts and the Mob Museum.
