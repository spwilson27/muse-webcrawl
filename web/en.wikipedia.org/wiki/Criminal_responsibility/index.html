Criminal responsibility

Criminal responsibility (or criminal liability) is the legal obligation of a person to answer for an offence they have committed. Generally it requires proof of both a prohibited act (actus reus) and a culpable mental state (mens rea), subject to recognised defences.

Elements

A person is criminally responsible when they voluntarily commit the actus reus of an offence with the required fault element — intention, knowledge, recklessness or, for some offences, negligence. Strict-liability offences dispense with mens rea for one or more elements.

Exemptions and defences

Children below the age of criminal responsibility cannot be held liable. Mental disorder may negate responsibility through the insanity defence, and automatism, intoxication, mistake and duress can also affect liability. Corporations may bear criminal responsibility for offences committed on their behalf.
