The gaokao (高考), formally the National College Entrance Examination, is the standardized university entrance examination held annually in the People's Republic of China. Taken by final-year high school students over two to four days each June, it is the principal criterion for admission to Chinese universities and is widely regarded as one of the most competitive and consequential examinations in the world, with more than 13 million candidates in recent years.

Content and format

The examination typically covers Chinese language, mathematics and a foreign language (usually English), plus subjects chosen by the student from physics, chemistry, biology, history, geography and politics, under reforms being rolled out province by province. Total scores, commonly out of 750, determine admission tiers, and the exam is administered under strict security, with traffic quieted near test centers and heavy penalties for cheating.

History

A unified entrance exam was introduced in 1952, suspended during the Cultural Revolution, and restored under Deng Xiaoping in 1977, when 5.7 million candidates sat for fewer than 300,000 places — an event remembered as a turning point in modern Chinese education. Its cultural prestige echoes the imperial civil service examinations (keju) that selected officials for over a millennium.

Criticism

Critics cite the immense psychological pressure on students, regional disparities in admission quotas, and the disadvantage faced by migrant children, while defenders praise the gaokao as a comparatively meritocratic route to mobility.
