Mountain gorilla


A silverback mountain gorilla in Rwanda.

The mountain gorilla (Gorilla beringei beringei) is one of the two subspecies of the eastern gorilla. Two populations are found: one in the Virunga volcanic mountains of Central/East Africa, within three national parks (Mgahinga in Uganda, Volcanoes in Rwanda and Virunga in the Democratic Republic of Congo), and another in Bwindi Impenetrable National Park in Uganda.

Mountain gorillas are distinguished from lowland gorillas by longer, thicker fur adapted to cold mountain climates at elevations of 2,200–4,300 metres. Adult males (silverbacks) can weigh around 195 kg and lead cohesive family groups, communicating through a repertoire of vocalisations and displays.

Conservation

The population fell to a few hundred by the late 20th century owing to poaching, habitat loss, disease and regional conflict. Primatologist Dian Fossey studied them in the Virungas from 1967 until her murder in 1985, bringing their plight to world attention. Intensive conservation — including gorilla tourism, veterinary care and anti-poaching patrols — has since raised numbers to just over 1,000 individuals, and in 2018 the IUCN reclassified the mountain gorilla from Critically Endangered to Endangered.
