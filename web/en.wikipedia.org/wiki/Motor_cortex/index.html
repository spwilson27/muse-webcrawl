Motor cortex

The motor cortex is the region of the cerebral cortex in the posterior frontal lobe involved in the planning, control, and execution of voluntary movements. It comprises the primary motor cortex, the premotor cortex, and the supplementary motor area.

Primary motor cortex

The primary motor cortex (Brodmann area 4) lies in the precentral gyrus. Its output neurons, including the giant Betz cells, project through the corticospinal tract to spinal motor circuits. The body is represented topographically in an inverted "motor homunculus," with disproportionately large areas devoted to the hands and face. Stimulation experiments by Fritsch and Hitzig, and later Wilder Penfield, established this somatotopic map.

Premotor and supplementary areas

The premotor cortex, on the lateral surface, guides movements using sensory information and is active during movement preparation and observation. The supplementary motor area, on the medial surface, contributes to internally generated movement sequences and bimanual coordination.

Clinical significance

Damage to the motor cortex or its descending pathways — for example from stroke — produces weakness or paralysis (upper motor neuron signs) on the opposite side of the body. Motor cortex plasticity underlies recovery and is exploited by rehabilitation and brain–computer interface research.
