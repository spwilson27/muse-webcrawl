Subdivisions of Tanzania

The United Republic of Tanzania is administratively divided into 31 regions (Swahili: mkoa, plural mikoa), including five regions in the semi-autonomous archipelago of Zanzibar. The regions are further subdivided into districts (Swahili: wilaya), of which there are more than 180, and the districts are in turn divided into divisions, wards (Swahili: kata), and villages.

Structure

Each region is headed by a Regional Commissioner appointed by the President, while districts are administered by District Commissioners. Local government is provided by elected district, town, municipal, and city councils. The capital, Dodoma, lies in the region of the same name, while Dar es Salaam remains the country's largest city and principal commercial centre.

Historical development

At independence in 1961, Tanganyika had 17 provinces. Following the union with Zanzibar in 1964 and successive rounds of decentralisation, regions were repeatedly split to bring administration closer to the population; four new regions (Geita, Katavi, Njombe, and Simiyu) were created in 2012, and Songwe Region was created in 2016, bringing the total to 31.
