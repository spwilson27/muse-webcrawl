Pāli Canon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Pāli Canon is the standard collection of scriptures in the Theravada Buddhist tradition, as preserved in the Pāli language. It is the most complete surviving early Buddhist canon, and one of the first to be written down, in Sri Lanka in the first century BCE. It is also known as the Tipiṭaka ("Three Baskets").

Contents

The canon is divided into three general categories, or piṭaka: the Vinaya Piṭaka, containing rules for monks and nuns; the Sutta Piṭaka, containing discourses attributed to the Buddha and his disciples, organised into five nikāyas (Dīgha, Majjhima, Saṃyutta, Aṅguttara and Khuddaka); and the Abhidhamma Piṭaka, containing systematic philosophical and doctrinal analyses.

History

According to tradition the teachings were recited at councils after the Buddha's death and transmitted orally for centuries before being committed to writing at Aluvihare, Sri Lanka, during the reign of King Vaṭṭagāmaṇi (1st century BCE). Commentaries, notably those of Buddhaghosa (5th century CE), accompany the canon. The Pali Text Society, founded in 1881, published romanised editions and English translations.
