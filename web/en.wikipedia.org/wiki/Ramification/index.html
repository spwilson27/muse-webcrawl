Ramification

Ramification is a concept in mathematics, principally in algebraic number theory and algebraic geometry, describing how a prime ideal or a point "splits" — or fails to split in the generic way — in an extension or under a morphism.

In algebraic number theory

In an extension of number fields, a prime ideal of the smaller ring of integers may factor in the larger ring with some prime ideals appearing with exponent greater than one; such primes are said to ramify, and the exponent is the ramification index. Only finitely many primes ramify: exactly those dividing the discriminant of the extension. Ramification is measured more finely by the different ideal and studied through decomposition and inertia groups.

In geometry

For a morphism of Riemann surfaces or algebraic varieties, a point is a ramification point if the map fails to be locally invertible there, as with the map z ↦ z² at the origin. The Riemann–Hurwitz formula relates the genera of two curves through the total ramification of a map between them.
