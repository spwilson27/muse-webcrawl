Multinomial distribution

In probability theory, the multinomial distribution is a generalization of the binomial distribution. It models the probability of counts for each side of a k-sided die rolled n times. For each trial, the outcome is one of k mutually exclusive categories with fixed probabilities p1, …, pk.

Definition

Let Xi be the number of times outcome i was observed over the n trials. The probability mass function is:

P(X1 = x1, …, Xk = xk) = n! / (x1! ··· xk!) · p1x1 ··· pkxk

where the counts sum to n. The coefficient is known as the multinomial coefficient.

Properties

The expected value of Xi is n·pi, its variance is n·pi(1 − pi), and the covariance between distinct components is −n·pipj. Each individual component follows a binomial distribution. The multinomial distribution is the conjugate prior family partner of the Dirichlet distribution in Bayesian statistics.
