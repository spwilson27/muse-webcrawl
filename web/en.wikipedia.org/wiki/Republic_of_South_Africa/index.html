South Africa, officially the Republic of South Africa (RSA), is the southernmost country in Africa. It is bounded by the Atlantic and Indian Oceans and shares borders with Namibia, Botswana, Zimbabwe, Mozambique, Eswatini, and the enclaved kingdom of Lesotho. With about 60 million people speaking twelve official languages, it is one of the world's most linguistically diverse states.

History

The Union of South Africa was formed in 1910 from the Cape, Natal, Transvaal, and Orange River colonies. From 1948 the National Party government institutionalised racial segregation as apartheid, drawing international condemnation and sanctions. A republic was declared in 1961. Apartheid ended with negotiations in the early 1990s; the first universal-suffrage elections in 1994 brought Nelson Mandela and the African National Congress to power.

Geography and economy

South Africa has varied topography, from the Kalahari in the northwest to the Drakensberg escarpment in the east, and exceptional biodiversity including the Cape Floristic Region. It is Africa's most industrialised economy, rich in gold, platinum, coal, and diamonds, though it faces high unemployment and inequality.
