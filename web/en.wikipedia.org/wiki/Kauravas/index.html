Kaurava

Kaurava is a Sanskrit term for the descendants of King Kuru, the legendary ancestor of the royal house at the centre of the Hindu epic Mahabharata. In its narrower and usual sense it denotes the hundred sons of the blind king Dhritarashtra and his wife Gandhari — led by the eldest, Duryodhana — who are the principal antagonists of their cousins, the five Pandavas.

Story

Gandhari, granted a boon of a hundred sons, gave birth after an unnaturally long pregnancy to a hard mass of flesh, which the sage Vyasa divided into one hundred and one portions incubated in pots of ghee, producing the hundred Kaurava brothers and their sister Dushala. Duryodhana, born with ill omens, grew up jealous of his Pandava cousins and repeatedly plotted against them — poisoning Bhima, burning the house of lac, and, with his maternal uncle Shakuni's loaded dice, stripping Yudhishthira of kingdom, brothers, and wife Draupadi in the infamous gambling match.

Refusing all compromise after the Pandavas' exile, Duryodhana led the eleven-akshauhini Kaurava army in the Kurukshetra War, fielding legendary commanders — Bhishma, Drona, Karna, and Shalya. By the war's end all hundred brothers were dead, Duryodhana falling last in a mace duel with Bhima, and only a handful of their allies survived. Gandhari, in her grief, cursed Krishna and his Yadava clan to perish likewise.
