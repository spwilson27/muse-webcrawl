Pulkovo Observatory

The Pulkovo Astronomical Observatory (Russian: Пулковская астрономическая обсерватория), near Saint Petersburg, Russia, is the principal astronomical observatory of the Russian Academy of Sciences. Opened in 1839 on the Pulkovo Heights 19 km south of the city, it became famous in the nineteenth century as the "astronomical capital of the world" for its precision astrometry and fundamental star catalogues.

History

Tsar Nicholas I authorised the observatory, designed by architect Alexander Brullov, and its first director was the German-Russian astronomer Friedrich Georg Wilhelm von Struve. Its 15-inch refractor was then among the world's largest, and in 1885 a 30-inch refractor — the world's largest at installation — was added. Pulkovo's meridian work produced reference catalogues used worldwide, and the Pulkovo meridian served as the prime meridian of the Russian Empire.

War and rebuilding

The observatory was almost completely destroyed during the Siege of Leningrad, its buildings on the front line and much of its library and instruments lost; several staff were also victims of the Great Purge of 1936–37, when many Pulkovo astronomers were arrested. Rebuilt after the war and reopened in 1954, it expanded into astrophysics and hosted instruments including the RATAN-600 radio telescope's predecessor programmes at its branch site. The historic site is part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing of Saint Petersburg.
