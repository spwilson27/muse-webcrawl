SOLAS

SOLAS
Full name: International Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea
Signed: 1 November 1974 (current version)
Depositary: International Maritime Organization

The International Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea (SOLAS) is the most important international treaty governing the safety of merchant ships. It specifies minimum standards for the construction, equipment and operation of ships, covering subdivision and stability, fire protection, life-saving appliances, radio communications (including the GMDSS), safety of navigation, and the carriage of cargoes including dangerous goods.

History

The first SOLAS convention was adopted in 1914 in response to the sinking of RMS Titanic in 1912. Successive versions followed in 1929, 1948 and 1960. The current version, adopted in 1974, introduced the "tacit acceptance" amendment procedure, allowing technical updates to enter into force quickly; it has been amended many times, notably after disasters such as the capsizing of the Herald of Free Enterprise and the loss of the Estonia.

Scope

SOLAS generally applies to ships engaged on international voyages, with flag states responsible for certifying compliance and port states empowered to inspect visiting vessels.
