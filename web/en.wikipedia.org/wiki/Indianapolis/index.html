Indianapolis

State: Indiana, United States

County: Marion County

Founded: 1821

Population (2020): approx. 887,600

Nickname: "Circle City", "Crossroads of America"

Indianapolis, colloquially known as Indy, is the capital and most populous city of the U.S. state of Indiana and the county seat of Marion County. It had a population of about 888,000 at the 2020 census, making it among the largest cities in the Midwest. The city's name combines "Indiana" with the Greek polis (city).

History

Indianapolis was founded in 1821 as a planned state capital near the geographic centre of Indiana, on land ceded by the Lenape and Miami peoples. Alexander Ralston, who had worked on the plan of Washington, D.C., surveyed a mile-square grid centred on Monument Circle. The city grew with the arrival of the railroad in 1847 and became a major rail and manufacturing hub; during the Civil War it was an important Union logistics centre. In 1970 city and county governments were consolidated under "Unigov".

Sport

Indianapolis bills itself the "Racing Capital of the World": the Indianapolis Motor Speedway in nearby Speedway hosts the Indianapolis 500, the largest single-day sporting event in the world by attendance. The city is also home to the NFL's Indianapolis Colts and the NBA's Indiana Pacers, and it hosted Super Bowl XLVI in 2012.

Geography and economy

The city lies on the White River in central Indiana, on flat till plain terrain. Major employers include Eli Lilly and Company, Salesforce, and the health networks Indiana University Health and Community Health, alongside logistics firms drawn by the city's position at the junction of several interstate highways — the origin of its "Crossroads of America" nickname.
