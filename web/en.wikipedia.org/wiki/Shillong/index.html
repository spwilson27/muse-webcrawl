Shillong

Shillong is the capital and largest city of the Indian state of Meghalaya. Situated in the Khasi Hills at an average elevation of about 1,496 metres (4,908 ft) above sea level, it is known as the "Scotland of the East" for its rolling hills, pine forests, and cool climate. The city is the headquarters of the East Khasi Hills district.

History

Shillong grew as a British hill station in the 19th century and became the capital of composite Assam in 1874. After Meghalaya was carved out as a separate state in 1972, Shillong remained its capital. The city was severely damaged by the great Assam earthquake of 1897 and rebuilt thereafter.

Culture

The majority of the population belongs to the Khasi people, a matrilineal society, and Christianity is the predominant religion. Shillong is famous across India for its Western-music culture and rock bands, earning it the nickname "Rock Capital of India." Institutions include North-Eastern Hill University, the Don Bosco Museum of indigenous cultures, and IIM Shillong.

Attractions

Popular sites include Ward's Lake, Elephant Falls, Shillong Peak (the highest point in Meghalaya), Umiam Lake (Barapani) to the north, and the city's many churches, including the Cathedral of Mary Help of Christians. The nearby town of Cherrapunji and village of Mawsynram, among the wettest places on Earth, are common excursions.
