Garching

Garching bei München is a town in the district of Munich, Bavaria, Germany, located immediately north of Munich on the river Isar. With a population of roughly 18,000, it is best known as one of Germany's principal science locations: its northern district, Hochbrück and the research campus (Forschungscampus Garching), host a dense cluster of university institutes and research organisations that give the town the self-description "Universitäts- und Forschungsstadt" (university and research city).

The research campus

The campus began in 1957 with the FRM I research reactor — its containment building, the Atomei ("atomic egg"), became a local landmark — and grew to include the Technical University of Munich's faculties of physics, chemistry, mechanical engineering, informatics and mathematics; several Max Planck Institutes (for astrophysics, extraterrestrial physics, plasma physics and quantum optics); the headquarters of the European Southern Observatory (ESO); the Leibniz Supercomputing Centre, home of SuperMUC; and the FRM II neutron source. The ASDEX Upgrade tokamak of the Max Planck Institute for Plasma Physics operates here. The campus is served by the Garching-Forschungszentrum station of the Munich U-Bahn (line U6), whose extension opened in 2006.

History

First mentioned in documents in the 10th century (as Gouarichinga), Garching remained a farming village on the Munich gravel plain until the 20th century. It received town status in 1990. The district of Hochbrück, historically an industrial and military area, and Dirnismaning complete the town's quarters.

Geography and transport

The Isar meadows along the town's eastern edge are a protected landscape area. The A9 motorway and the U6 underground line link Garching directly to central Munich, and Munich Airport lies about 20 minutes to the northeast.
