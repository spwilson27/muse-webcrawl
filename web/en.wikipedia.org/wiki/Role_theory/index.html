Role theory

Role theory is a concept in sociology and social psychology that considers most everyday activity to be acting out socially defined categories—such as mother, manager, teacher—each of which is a set of rights, duties, expectations, norms and behaviours a person must face and fulfil.

Drawing on the theatre metaphor developed by George Herbert Mead, Ralph Linton and later Erving Goffman's dramaturgy, role theory holds that behaviour is guided by expectations held by the individual and by others. Key concepts include role conflict (incompatible demands between roles), role strain, role ambiguity, and role exit.

Bruce Biddle's 1979 synthesis organised the field around expectations, consensus and conformity. Role theory is applied in organisational behaviour, gender studies, family sociology and international relations theory.
