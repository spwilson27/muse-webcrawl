Boehm system

The Boehm system is a fingering and keywork system for woodwind instruments developed by German inventor and flutist Theobald Boehm (1794–1881). Originally designed for the flute between 1832 and 1847, it was later adapted to the oboe and, most successfully, the clarinet.

Design

Boehm repositioned tone holes according to acoustic principles rather than finger convenience, and used ring keys and needle springs so that holes too large or distant for the fingers could be covered mechanically. On the clarinet, the system developed by Hyacinthe Klosé and Louis Buffet became the standard in most of the world.

Use

Boehm-system flutes are universal; Boehm clarinets dominate outside Germany and Austria, where the Oehler system persists. See also Clarinet and Western concert flute.
