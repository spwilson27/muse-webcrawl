Truancy
Definition:Unapproved absence from compulsory schooling
Related concepts:School refusal, chronic absenteeism, dropout

Truancy is any intentional, unjustified, unauthorised, or illegal absence from compulsory education. It is a term used to describe absences caused by a student of their own free will, and usually does not refer to legitimate excused absences, such as those related to medical conditions. Truancy is often associated with terms like playing truant, skipping school, or bunking off.

Causes

Research links truancy to a range of factors: disengagement from school, academic difficulty, bullying, family instability, poverty, caring responsibilities, mental health problems, and peer influence. Persistent absence is regarded as an early warning sign for later problems including school dropout, unemployment, and involvement with the criminal justice system.

Responses

Responses to truancy vary by jurisdiction. Historically, truancy officers (attendance officers) were employed to enforce compulsory education laws, and parents could be fined or prosecuted. Modern approaches increasingly emphasise early intervention, attendance monitoring, mentoring, family support, and restorative rather than purely punitive measures. In the United States, chronic absenteeism — missing 10 per cent or more of the school year — is tracked as an accountability measure under federal education law.

By country

In the United Kingdom, persistent absence is defined by the Department for Education, and local authorities may issue penalty notices to parents. In Australia, state education departments employ attendance officers and community liaison programs, including for Indigenous communities where absence rates are higher. Several countries employ dedicated truancy courts or family courts to address habitual absence.
