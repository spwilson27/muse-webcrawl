Trepopnea

Trepopnea is dyspnea (shortness of breath) that occurs when lying on one lateral side but not the other. It is the positional counterpart of orthopnea (breathlessness when lying flat) and platypnea (breathlessness when upright).

Causes

Trepopnea most often results from asymmetric lung or pleural disease. In unilateral pleural effusion, patients typically breathe more comfortably lying on the unaffected side, which keeps the better lung uppermost; conversely, in some unilateral parenchymal diseases, lying with the good lung dependent ("good lung down") improves ventilation–perfusion matching and oxygenation. It can also occur with a large unilateral lung mass, pneumonectomy, or a mobile intracardiac mass such as a ball-valve thrombus or atrial myxoma.

Clinical significance

Asking a breathless patient which side they prefer to lie on can give a clue to the side and nature of the underlying pathology. See dyspnea and orthopnea.
