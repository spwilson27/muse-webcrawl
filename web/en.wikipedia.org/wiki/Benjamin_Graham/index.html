Benjamin Graham

Benjamin Graham (born Benjamin Grossbaum; 9 May 1894 – 21 September 1976) was a British-born American economist, professor and investor known as the "father of value investing". His books Security Analysis (1934, with David Dodd) and The Intelligent Investor (1949) founded the discipline of fundamental security analysis, and his students included Warren Buffett.

Life and career

Graham was born in London to Jewish parents and moved to New York City as an infant. After his father's death the family fell into poverty. He graduated from Columbia University in 1914 as salutatorian and, after briefly teaching, began work on Wall Street. In 1926 he formed the Graham–Newman partnership, which survived the 1929 crash (with heavy losses that shaped Graham's insistence on a margin of safety) and operated until 1956.

From 1928 to 1956 Graham taught at Columbia Business School, where his course on security analysis influenced a generation of investors. Warren Buffett, who took Graham's class and later worked at Graham–Newman, called The Intelligent Investor "by far the best book on investing ever written".

Investment philosophy

Graham distinguished investment — thorough analysis promising safety of principal and an adequate return — from speculation. His allegory of "Mr. Market", an obliging but emotionally unstable business partner who offers to buy or sell every day, teaches investors to exploit market volatility rather than be guided by it. Quantitative "net-net" investing — buying stocks below their net current asset value — was an early Graham technique, later supplemented by broader criteria for "defensive" and "enterprising" investors.
