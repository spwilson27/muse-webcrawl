Luna Park (Coney Island, 1903)

Luna Park
LocationConey Island, Brooklyn, New York
Opened16 May 1903
Closed1946
FoundersFrederic Thompson and Elmer Dundy

Luna Park was an amusement park at Coney Island, Brooklyn, New York City, that opened in 1903 and was the second of Coney Island's three great original parks, after Steeplechase Park and before Dreamland. Built by Frederic Thompson and Elmer "Skip" Dundy, it was named after Dundy's sister's eponymous airship in their "Trip to the Moon" attraction — and lent its name to "Luna Park" amusement parks around the world.

Attractions

The park's centrepiece was A Trip to the Moon, a simulated space voyage carried over from the 1901 Pan-American Exposition. Around a lagoon lit by a million electric light bulbs rose minarets, spires, and attractions including the Shoot-the-Chutes, the infant incubator exhibit of Dr. Martin Couney, exotic animal shows, and spectacles re-creating the Boer War and the eruption of Mount Vesuvius. In 1903 the elephant Topsy was publicly electrocuted at the park after killing a handler.

Decline and closure

Fires in 1944 destroyed much of the park, and Luna Park closed for good in 1946; the site was later redeveloped. A new, unrelated Luna Park opened nearby in 2010 on part of the former Astroland site, reviving the historic name.
