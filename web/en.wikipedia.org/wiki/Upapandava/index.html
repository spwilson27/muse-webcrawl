In the Hindu epic Mahabharata, the Upapandavas (Sanskrit: उपपाण्डव, Upapāṇḍava, literally "junior Pandavas") are the five sons of Draupadi, the wife of the five Pandava brothers. Each son was born to one of the brothers: Prativindhya by Yudhishthira, Sutasoma by Bhima, Srutakirti by Arjuna, Satanika by Nakula, and Srutasena by Sahadeva. They are sometimes collectively called the Draupadeyas.

In the epic

The Upapandavas were born during the Pandavas' years of exile and were raised largely at their maternal grandfather Drupada's court at Kampilya and later at Krishna's Dvaraka, where they were trained in warfare by Krishna's son Pradyumna and by Arjuna. In the Kurukshetra War they fought on the Pandava side: Prativindhya is credited with holding off Drona, and the brothers distinguished themselves against the Kaurava forces.

Death

On the night following the eighteenth and final day of the war, Ashvatthama, accompanied by Kripa and Kritavarma, crept into the Pandava camp while its warriors slept. Mistaking the five sleeping brothers for the Pandavas themselves — or, in some tellings, deliberately killing them as surrogates — Ashvatthama beheaded the Upapandavas and presented the heads to the dying Duryodhana. The massacre extinguished Draupadi's line and left the Pandavas' heir as Abhimanyu's unborn son, Parikshit. Draupadi's grief prompted Arjuna's vow to kill Ashvatthama, whom Krishna ultimately cursed to wander the earth.
