Archie Mason Griffin (born 21 August 1954) is an American former football running back who played for the Ohio State Buckeyes in college and the Cincinnati Bengals in the National Football League (NFL). He is the only player ever to win the Heisman Trophy twice, receiving college football's most prestigious award in both 1974 and 1975.

College career

Playing for coach Woody Hayes at Ohio State from 1972 to 1975, Griffin rushed for 5,589 yards, then an NCAA record, and set a national record by rushing for 100 yards or more in 31 consecutive games. The Buckeyes won four Big Ten championships and played in four Rose Bowls during his career. Griffin won the Heisman Trophy as a junior in 1974 and repeated in 1975, a feat that remains unmatched; he also won the Maxwell Award and was a two-time unanimous All-American. His number 45 was retired by Ohio State in 1999.

Professional career

Griffin was selected by the Cincinnati Bengals in the first round of the 1976 NFL draft. Undersized by professional standards at 5 ft 9 in, he played seven seasons for Cincinnati (1976–1982), rushing for 2,808 yards, and appeared in Super Bowl XVI, a loss to the San Francisco 49ers. He later played briefly in the United States Football League with the Jacksonville Bulls.

After football

Griffin returned to Ohio State, where he served for many years in athletics administration and as president and CEO of the Ohio State University Alumni Association. He was inducted into the College Football Hall of Fame in 1986 and the Rose Bowl Hall of Fame. The Heisman Trophy Trust has often invited him to present the award, and his two trophies remain one of the most celebrated achievements in college football history.
