Rishi Valley School is a co-educational boarding school in Andhra Pradesh, India, founded in 1926 by the philosopher Jiddu Krishnamurti. It is run by the Krishnamurti Foundation India and is located in a valley—the Rishi Valley—in the Rayalaseema region, about 15 kilometres from the town of Madanapalle and roughly 140 kilometres north-east of Bengaluru. The school is frequently ranked among India's leading residential schools.

The campus occupies about 375 acres of dry, ancient hills at the southern tip of the Deccan Plateau. Education at Rishi Valley emphasizes the Krishnamurti philosophy of self-inquiry, freedom from conditioning, and a close relationship with nature, alongside a conventional academic curriculum leading to the Indian Certificate of Secondary Education (ICSE) and Indian School Certificate (ISC) examinations.

History

Krishnamurti established the school in 1926 after searching for a site that would embody his educational vision. Classes began in Guindy, Madras, before the school settled permanently at Rishi Valley. The name derives from the sage (rishi) said to have meditated in the valley; the dominant geographical feature is Rishikonda hill.

Education

The school admits around 360 students in classes 4 through 12, with small class sizes and a house system in which students and teachers live in hostels together. There are no formal examinations in the junior school, and competitive grading is deliberately de-emphasized. Assemblies, nature study, and discussions form a regular part of school life.

Rishi Valley Institute and rural work

Closely associated with the school is the Rural Education Centre, which runs satellite schools for children from surrounding villages, and health and environmental programmes. The valley has been designated a bird sanctuary, and extensive afforestation and water-conservation work has transformed the once-barren campus into a green enclave. The afforestation effort, begun in the 1980s, planted hundreds of thousands of trees and revived the valley's water table.
