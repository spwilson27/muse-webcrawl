Sophie Charlotte of Hanover

Born: 30 October 1668, Iburg Castle, Osnabrück

Died: 1 February 1705 (aged 36), Hanover

Consort: Frederick I of Prussia

House: Hanover

Father: Ernest Augustus, Elector of Hanover

Mother: Sophia, Electress of Hanover

Sophie Charlotte of Hanover (30 October 1668 – 1 February 1705) was the first Queen consort in Prussia as the wife of King Frederick I. She was the only daughter of Ernest Augustus, Elector of Hanover, and his wife Sophia of the Palatinate. Her eldest brother, George Louis, later succeeded to the British throne as King George I.

Life

Sophie Charlotte was born at Iburg Castle near Osnabrück, where her father was then prince-bishop. She grew up in Hanover and accompanied her mother on a journey to Italy in 1672–73. In 1684 she married Frederick, then Electoral Prince (Kronprinz) of Brandenburg, whose first wife Elisabeth Henriette of Hesse-Kassel had died the year before.

The marriage produced two sons: Frederick Augustus (1685–1686), who died in infancy, and Frederick William (1688–1740), who later reigned as Frederick William I, the "Soldier King" of Prussia.

Queen in Prussia

When her husband crowned himself King in Prussia at Königsberg on 18 January 1701, Sophie Charlotte became the first Prussian queen. Intellectually gifted and fluent in several languages, she was a noted patron of music, philosophy and the arts. She maintained a close friendship and famous correspondence with the philosopher Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz, who was a frequent guest at her court. She founded the palace of Charlottenburg in Berlin, originally called Lietzenburg, which became a centre of courtly and intellectual life; the Berlin Academy of Sciences was also established under her patronage in 1700.

Known for her wit — Voltaire later remarked on her intelligence — she took little interest in politics and often distanced herself from the ceremonials of her husband's court.

Death and legacy

Sophie Charlotte died of pneumonia in Hanover on 1 February 1705 while visiting her mother. After her death, Frederick renamed Lietzenburg Palace and its surrounding settlement Charlottenburg in her honour. The district of Berlin-Charlottenburg still bears her name.
