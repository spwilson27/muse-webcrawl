Acrow Bridge

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Acrow Bridge is an American manufacturer of modular steel bridging and shoring systems, and the name is also applied generically to its panel bridge designs. The company, headquartered in Parsippany, New Jersey, developed from the business founded around the Acrow prop — an adjustable steel support for formwork invented in the 1930s by Swiss-born engineer William de Vigier in London. The bridging business grew out of the tradition of the Bailey bridge, the celebrated British modular bridge of the Second World War.

Panel bridges

The Acrow 700XS panel bridge system consists of prefabricated steel panels, transoms, stringers and deck units that pin and bolt together on site without specialised plant. Bridges can be assembled in single or multiple spans, in single- or multi-lane widths, and launched from one bank by cantilever methods using a launching nose. Because components are interchangeable, spans can be lengthened, strengthened or dismantled and reused.

Uses

Acrow bridges are used by military engineers for line-of-communication bridging, by highway authorities as temporary detour bridges during construction, and for emergency replacement after floods, earthquakes and other disasters. The company has supplied bridging for disaster relief and infrastructure projects in well over a hundred countries, including permanent rural access bridges in developing regions.
