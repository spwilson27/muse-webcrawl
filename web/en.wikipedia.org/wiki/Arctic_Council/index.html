The Arctic Council is a high-level intergovernmental forum that addresses issues faced by the Arctic governments and the indigenous people of the Arctic region. It was established by the Ottawa Declaration of 19 September 1996. The eight countries forming the membership are Canada, Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway, Russia, Sweden, and the United States. In addition to the member states, the Council includes six Permanent Participant organisations representing Arctic indigenous peoples, and a growing number of observer states and organisations.

History

The Council grew out of the Arctic Environmental Protection Strategy (AEPS), launched in 1991 with the Rovaniemi Declaration. The Ottawa Declaration of 1996 transformed the arrangement into a broader forum covering sustainable development as well as environmental protection. The Arctic Council Secretariat was permanently established in Tromsø, Norway, in 2013.

Following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, the other seven member states paused cooperation with Russia in the Council. Limited project work resumed later without Russian participation, and Norway's chairship (2023–2025) sought to preserve the institution's core functions.

Structure

Member states

The eight Arctic states are Canada, the Kingdom of Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway, the Russian Federation, Sweden, and the United States. Chairmanship rotates among them every two years.

Permanent Participants

Six indigenous organisations hold Permanent Participant status: the Aleut International Association, the Arctic Athabaskan Council, the Gwich'in Council International, the Inuit Circumpolar Council, the Russian Association of Indigenous Peoples of the North (RAIPON), and the Saami Council. They sit at the table with member states in all Council activities, though formal decision-making rests with the states.

Working groups

Six working groups carry out the Council's scientific and technical work, including the Arctic Monitoring and Assessment Programme (AMAP), Conservation of Arctic Flora and Fauna (CAFF), Protection of the Arctic Marine Environment (PAME), the Emergency Prevention, Preparedness and Response group (EPPR), the Arctic Contaminants Action Program (ACAP), and the Sustainable Development Working Group (SDWG).

Agreements

Three legally binding agreements have been negotiated under the Council's auspices: the 2011 Arctic Search and Rescue Agreement, the 2013 Agreement on Cooperation on Marine Oil Pollution Preparedness and Response, and the 2017 Agreement on Enhancing International Arctic Scientific Cooperation.
