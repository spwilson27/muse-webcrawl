Tyler Burge (born 1946) is an American philosopher who is a professor of philosophy at the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA). He has made contributions to many areas of philosophy, including the philosophy of mind, philosophy of language, epistemology, and the history of philosophy, in particular the philosophy of Gottlob Frege.

Work

Burge is best known for his development of anti-individualism (also called social externalism), the view that the contents of a person's thoughts and beliefs depend in part on their social and physical environment. His 1979 paper "Individualism and the Mental" presented a series of thought experiments involving a patient who complains of arthritis in his thigh: since arthritis is by definition an ailment of the joints, the patient's thought content depends on the usage of the linguistic community around him, and would differ in a counterfactual community where "arthritis" covered thigh pains as well. The argument extended Hilary Putnam's externalism about natural kind terms to mental content generally.

Burge has also written extensively on perception, self-knowledge, and the epistemology of testimony, defending the idea that a thinker has a defeasible a priori entitlement to accept what is presented as true by a rational source. His later work, including the book Origins of Objectivity (2010), develops a theory of perceptual objectivity and representation.
