Blues Incorporated

Alexis Korner (left) and Cyril Davies

Blues Incorporated was an English blues band formed in London in 1961 by Alexis Korner and Cyril Davies. It was the first amplified R&B band in Britain and acted as a finishing school for many of the musicians who would define British rock in the 1960s.

History

Korner and Davies opened the London Blues and Barrelhouse Club and, frustrated by the dominance of traditional jazz, formed Blues Incorporated to play Chicago-style electric blues. The band held a residency at the Ealing Jazz Club from March 1962, attracting crowds of young enthusiasts. Their album R&B from the Marquee, released in 1962 on Decca, was the first full-length British blues record.

Members and legacy

An extraordinary number of future stars passed through or sat in with the band, including Charlie Watts, Jack Bruce, Ginger Baker, Mick Jagger, Brian Jones, Keith Richards, Eric Burdon, and Graham Bond. Jagger's singing with Blues Incorporated led directly to the formation of the Rolling Stones. Davies left in late 1962 to form his own All-Stars, and died in 1964; Korner continued various incarnations of the band until the late 1960s.

Blues Incorporated is widely credited with igniting the British blues boom and, through its alumni, with shaping the sound of British rock.
