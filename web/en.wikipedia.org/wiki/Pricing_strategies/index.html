Pricing strategies are the methods companies use to set the prices of their products and services in order to achieve objectives such as maximising profit, gaining market share, or positioning a brand. Pricing is one of the four elements of the marketing mix (the "four Ps": product, price, place, promotion).

Common strategies

  • Cost-plus (markup) pricing — adding a standard margin to unit cost; simple but ignores demand.
  • Value-based pricing — pricing according to the customer's perceived value rather than cost.
  • Competition-based pricing — setting prices relative to rivals' prices.
  • Penetration pricing — launching at a low price to win market share quickly.
  • Price skimming — launching high and lowering over time to capture successive segments of willingness to pay.
  • Psychological pricing — using price points such as $9.99 that appear materially lower than they are.
  • Premium pricing — maintaining a high price to signal quality or exclusivity.
  • Dynamic pricing — adjusting prices in response to demand, common in airlines, hotels and ride-hailing.
  • Bundle and freemium pricing — combining products, or offering a free basic tier with paid upgrades.

Price discrimination

Some strategies charge different prices to different customers for the same product — by segment, time, location or version (yield management and versioning). Economists classify discrimination as first, second or third degree depending on how finely the seller can segment buyers.

Considerations

Effective pricing must account for production costs, demand elasticity, competitor behaviour, regulation and the product life cycle. Modern practice increasingly uses experimentation (A/B testing) and data analysis to estimate willingness to pay.
