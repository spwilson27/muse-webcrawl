Ōita Prefecture (大分県) is a prefecture of Japan located on the north-eastern coast of the island of Kyūshū. Its capital and largest city is Ōita.

Geography

The prefecture is mountainous, with volcanic peaks including Mount Kujū, and faces the Seto Inland Sea and the Pacific Ocean. It is famous for its hot springs: Beppu and Yufuin are among Japan's most renowned onsen resorts, and Beppu produces the largest volume of hot spring water in the country.

History

The prefecture was formed from the former provinces of Bungo and Buzen after the Meiji Restoration. In the 16th century the Christian daimyō Ōtomo Sōrin ruled from the region, making it an early centre of trade with Portugal.

Culture and sport

Ōita is known for its "One Village, One Product" movement, kabosu citrus, and sekitori (mackerel) cuisine. The city of Ōita hosted matches of the 2002 FIFA World Cup and the 2019 Rugby World Cup at its domed stadium.
