Coefficient of utilization

The CU expresses the fraction of lamp lumens reaching the work plane

Symbol: CU

Field: Lighting design / illuminating engineering

Used in: The lumen (zonal cavity) method of lighting calculation

The coefficient of utilization (CU) is a measure of the efficiency with which a lighting fixture (luminaire) delivers light to a working plane in a given room. It is defined as the ratio of the luminous flux (lumens) that actually reaches the work plane to the total lumens emitted by the lamps in the luminaire. A related and sometimes synonymously used term is the utilization factor. Because some light is absorbed by the fixture itself, by walls and ceiling, or is directed away from the work surface, the CU is always less than 1.

Determining the coefficient

Manufacturers publish CU tables for each luminaire based on photometric testing. The correct value depends on:

Room proportions, expressed through the room cavity ratio (RCR), calculated from the room's length, width and the height of the cavity between the luminaires and the work plane; larger, shallower rooms and lower mounting heights generally yield higher coefficients.

Surface reflectances — the proportions of light reflected by the ceiling cavity, walls and floor cavity. Light-coloured surfaces return more light to the work plane.

Luminaire distribution — direct fixtures that throw most light downward have higher CUs for the work plane than indirect fixtures that bounce light off the ceiling.

Use in design

The CU is the central input to the lumen method (zonal cavity method), in which the average illuminance on the work plane is computed as the total installed lamp lumens multiplied by the CU and a light-loss factor, divided by the room area. Lighting designers use it to determine how many fixtures are needed to meet a target illuminance (for example, 500 lux for office work). Modern practice increasingly supplements or replaces tabulated CUs with point-by-point computer simulation using luminaire photometric files.
