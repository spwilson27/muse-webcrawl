Home Counties

The Home Counties are the counties of England that surround London. The term has no single official definition, but typically includes Berkshire, Buckinghamshire, Hertfordshire, Essex, Kent, Surrey and Sussex (now East and West Sussex), with Middlesex — largely absorbed into Greater London — also historically included. Hampshire, Bedfordshire and Oxfordshire are sometimes added.

Origin and usage

The name is thought to derive from the counties "close to home" — that is, to the capital — from which London drew food, services and, in earlier centuries, troops. The term was in common use by the later nineteenth century. Because the Home Counties contain much of London's commuter belt, they are associated in British culture with affluence, suburbia and middle-class respectability; phrases such as "Home Counties accent" refer to the standard southern English speech of the region.

Definitions

Various official bodies have used their own lists: the Metropolitan Police District, wartime civil defence regions, and cricket's former Home Counties Championship (contested by county clubs around London) each drew the boundary differently. In 1965 the creation of Greater London absorbed much of Middlesex and parts of Kent, Surrey, Essex and Hertfordshire, but the surrounding ceremonial counties retained their identities and county councils.

Character

The Home Counties combine dense commuter towns and suburbs with protected countryside such as the Chilterns, the North Downs and the Surrey Hills (the Metropolitan Green Belt limits sprawl). They are among the most prosperous parts of the United Kingdom and are closely tied to London's economy by rail and motorway networks.
