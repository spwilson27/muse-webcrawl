Distracted driving

Distracted driving
TypesVisual, manual, cognitive
Major sourceMobile phone use (texting, calls, apps)
CountermeasuresLegislation, hands-free laws, public campaigns, vehicle technology

Distracted driving is the act of driving while engaged in other activities that divert the driver's attention away from the road. Distractions are commonly classed as visual (taking one's eyes off the road), manual (taking one's hands off the wheel), and cognitive (taking one's mind off driving); texting on a mobile phone combines all three and is considered especially dangerous.

Scope and effects

Road-safety authorities attribute a substantial share of crashes to distraction. In the United States, the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration has reported roughly 3,000–3,500 deaths per year in distraction-affected crashes in recent years (about 8–9% of fatal crashes), and studies estimate that using a phone increases crash risk several-fold. Other common distractions include eating, adjusting controls, interacting with passengers, and drowsiness-related inattention, though drowsiness is sometimes classified separately.

Law and prevention

Many jurisdictions ban handheld phone use or texting while driving, with primary enforcement in an increasing number of U.S. states and points-and-fine regimes elsewhere. Countermeasures also include public-awareness campaigns, graduated licensing restrictions for novice drivers, "do not disturb while driving" phone modes, and driver-monitoring systems in newer vehicles.
