Narses (c. 478/479 – c. 573/574) was, with Belisarius, one of the two great generals of the Byzantine emperor Justinian I. An Armenian-born eunuch who rose through the palace service to become grand chamberlain and one of the most trusted men in the empire, Narses at the age of about seventy-three took command in Italy and completed the Byzantine reconquest, destroying the Ostrogothic kingdom in the battles of Taginae (552) and Mons Lactarius (553) and decisively defeating a Frankish-Alamannic invasion at the Volturno (554).

Rise at court

Nothing certain is known of Narses' early life before he appears in Constantinople as an official of the imperial bedchamber, eventually becoming praepositus sacri cubiculi (chief chamberlain) and keeper of the privy purse. Eunuchs — excluded from the throne by Roman custom — were prized as loyal instruments of imperial power, and Narses proved his worth during the Nika riots of 532, when, according to Procopius, he helped split the factions of the Hippodrome by bribing the leaders of the Blues, easing the revolt's suppression. Thereafter he served as the emperor's confidential agent and treasurer on sensitive missions, including the delicate financing and diplomacy of the wars in Italy.

First Italian command, 538–539

In 538 Justinian sent Narses to Italy with 7,000 reinforcements — officially to assist Belisarius, but the divided command produced friction and contributed to the loss of Milan, the worst disaster of the war. Narses was recalled in 539. The episode taught Justinian the costs of divided authority: when he returned to Italy twelve years later, Narses held undivided command and generous funds.

Conquest of Italy, 551–554

After Belisarius' second recall and the Gothic revival under Totila, Justinian appointed the elderly Narses commander-in-chief in 551, equipping him with a large army — perhaps 20,000–30,000 men, including Lombard, Herul, and Gepid allies — and full treasury support. Marching around the head of the Adriatic, Narses descended into Italy in 552 and met Totila at Taginae (Busta Gallorum) in Umbria, where Byzantine dismounted archers in a crescent formation destroyed the Gothic charge; Totila died of his wounds. Narses then took Rome, and in October 553 destroyed the last Gothic army under Teia at Mons Lactarius on the slopes of Vesuvius. In 554 he crushed a large Frankish and Alamannic invasion under the brothers Lothar and Buccelin at the Battle of the Volturno. The Pragmatic Sanction of 554 reorganised Italy as a Byzantine province.

Governor of Italy and death

Narses remained in Italy as its effective ruler, rebuilding cities, restoring aqueducts and administration, and governing from Rome. Later tradition — likely embroidered — alleged that, recalled in disgrace by the emperor Justin II after complaints from the Romans and an insult from the empress Sophia (that he should return to spinning wool with the women), the embittered Narses invited the Lombards to invade Italy, which they did in 568. The story is doubted by most historians. Narses died in Rome around 573/574, reportedly aged ninety-five.
