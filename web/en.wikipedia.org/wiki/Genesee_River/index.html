Genesee River
Length: about 157 mi (253 km)
Source: Potter County, Pennsylvania
Mouth: Lake Ontario at Rochester, New York

The Genesee River is a river in New York and Pennsylvania in the United States, flowing northward for about 157 miles (253 km) from its headwaters in Potter County, Pennsylvania, to Lake Ontario at Rochester, New York. It is unusual among American rivers in flowing from south to north through the entire width of western New York. Its name derives from an Iroquoian word often translated as "beautiful valley" or "pleasant valley".

Course

Rising near Gold, Pennsylvania, the river enters New York and winds through Allegany County before entering Letchworth State Park, where it plunges over three major waterfalls through a deep gorge sometimes called the "Grand Canyon of the East". The Mount Morris Dam, completed in 1952 for flood control, stands at the park's north end. Downstream the river crosses the broad Genesee Valley—the historic heartland of the Seneca Nation—and flows through the city of Rochester, where it drops over three more falls, including the 96-foot High Falls, before reaching Charlotte and Lake Ontario.

History and use

The valley was long inhabited by the Seneca, and the river powered Rochester's flour mills, giving the city its early nickname "Flour City". The Genesee Valley Canal, and later the Erie Canal's aqueduct over the river at Rochester, made it a transport artery. Today the river supports recreation, whitewater releases, fishing, and hydroelectric generation at Rochester. Pollution from industry once severely degraded water quality; cleanup efforts since the 1970s have substantially improved the lower river.
