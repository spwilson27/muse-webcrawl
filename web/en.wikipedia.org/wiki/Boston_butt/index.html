Boston butt is a cut of pork that comes from the upper part of the shoulder from the front leg of the pig. Despite its name, it has nothing to do with the rump of the animal — the corresponding rear cut is the ham. Boston butt is the classic cut for American barbecue pulled pork, prized for its rich marbling and connective tissue that melt during long, slow cooking.

Name

The name dates to colonial New England. Less prized cuts of pork were packed into barrels — called "butts" — for storage and shipment, and the way Boston butchers cut and packed the shoulder became known as the "Boston butt". The term was in print by the late 19th century and spread across the United States.

Characteristics

A Boston butt typically weighs 2.5–4 kg (5–9 lb) and contains the blade bone (part of the scapula) plus several well-exercised muscles separated by fat and collagen. Because the muscles work hard, the meat is tough when cooked quickly but becomes tender and juicy after hours of low-temperature cooking, which converts the collagen to gelatin. It is often sold with a fat cap and may include part of the neck.

Uses

Boston butt is the cut of choice for Carolina-style pulled pork, smoked for 10 or more hours and shredded. It is also used for pork roasts, country-style ribs (cut from the blade end), sausage making, Mexican carnitas and pozole, and Filipino dishes. The lower shoulder, the picnic ham or picnic shoulder, is a separate, leaner cut.
