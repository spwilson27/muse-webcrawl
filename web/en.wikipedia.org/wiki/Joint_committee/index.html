Joint committee

A joint committee is a committee made up of members of the two chambers of a bicameral legislature. Joint committees are usually established with narrow jurisdictions and normally lack the authority to report legislation directly to the floor; instead they conduct studies, hold hearings, perform oversight, and coordinate matters that concern both houses.

United States

In the United States Congress, joint committees consist of members of both the Senate and the House of Representatives. The four standing joint committees are the Joint Committee on Taxation, the Joint Economic Committee, the Joint Committee on the Library, and the Joint Committee on Printing. Congress also creates temporary joint select committees, such as deficit-reduction "super committees", for particular tasks. A related body, the conference committee—a type of joint committee—is convened ad hoc to reconcile different versions of a bill passed by the two chambers. Leadership of joint committees typically rotates between the houses with each Congress.

Other legislatures

Joint committees are used in many other bicameral systems, including the Parliament of the United Kingdom, where joint committees of the House of Commons and House of Lords examine issues such as human rights, statutory instruments, and draft bills; the Parliament of Australia, which maintains joint statutory and standing committees such as the Parliamentary Joint Committee on Intelligence and Security; and in Canada and India, where joint parliamentary committees may be constituted to investigate specific matters of public importance.
