Rudolf I (1 May 1218 – 15 July 1291), also known as Rudolf of Habsburg, was the first Habsburg to become King of Germany, reigning from 1273 until his death. A relatively minor Swabian count at his election, his victory over King Ottokar II of Bohemia and his enfeoffment of his sons with Austria laid the foundation of Habsburg power in central Europe for more than six centuries.

Early life and election

Rudolf was Count of Habsburg, with lands in the Aargau and Alsace. Following the Great Interregnum after the death of Frederick II, the prince-electors, wary of a too-powerful king, elected Rudolf at Frankfurt on 1 October 1273; he was crowned at Aachen later that month. Pope Gregory X recognised his election in return for promises to protect papal lands and abandon claims in Italy.

Reign

Rudolf worked to restore royal authority, recover alienated imperial lands (Revindikationen), curb the robber barons and enforce public peace. His great rival, Ottokar II of Bohemia, refused homage; after being placed under the imperial ban, Ottokar was defeated and killed at the Battle on the Marchfeld (26 August 1278). In 1282 Rudolf invested his sons Albert and Rudolf with the duchies of Austria and Styria — the origin of the Habsburg hereditary lands. He never obtained the imperial crown from the pope. Rudolf died at Speyer in 1291 and was buried in Speyer Cathedral.

Legacy

Rudolf's reign ended the Interregnum and established the pattern of electing kings who built territorial house power; his acquisition of Austria transformed the Habsburgs from regional counts into one of Europe's great dynasties.
