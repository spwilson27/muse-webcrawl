Personal data store
TypeData management concept
Related conceptsSelf-sovereign identity, Data silo

A personal data store (PDS), also known as a personal data vault or personal information management system, is a service or platform that allows an individual to store, manage and control their own personal data in one place, and to decide who may access it and for what purposes. The concept emerged in response to growing concern that large commercial platforms accumulate vast amounts of user data under their own control, leaving individuals with little visibility or say over how that data is used.

Overview

Personal data stores are typically designed around the principle of user-centric data management: instead of each company holding its own copy of a person's data, the individual maintains a canonical copy and grants applications permissioned access to it. Technologies associated with the concept include decentralised storage, verifiable credentials and standards such as the Solid project, initiated by World Wide Web inventor Tim Berners-Lee, which defines "pods" (personal online datastores) that users can host themselves or entrust to a provider.

Features

Common features of personal data stores include granular access controls, audit logs of who has accessed data, the ability to revoke access, and interoperability with identity systems. Proponents argue that PDSs could reduce data breaches by limiting duplication, support data portability rights such as those granted by the General Data Protection Regulation, and enable new business models in which individuals licence their data directly.

Criticism

Critics note that centralising one's data in a single store creates an attractive target for attackers, and that usability challenges may limit adoption among non-technical users. Questions also remain about liability, business viability and the degree to which incumbent platforms would interoperate with such systems.
