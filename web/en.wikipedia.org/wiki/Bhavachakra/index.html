Bhavachakra

Bhavachakra
NamesWheel of life; wheel of samsara
TraditionBuddhism (esp. Tibetan Buddhism)

The bhavachakra (Sanskrit: भवचक्र; Tibetan: སྲིད་པའི་འཁོར་ལོ་, srid pa'i 'khor lo), the "wheel of life", is a symbolic representation of samsara (the cycle of birth, death, and rebirth) found on the outside walls of Tibetan Buddhist temples and monasteries in the Indo-Tibetan region. It is a popular teaching aid and meditation object.

Structure

The wheel is held by Yama, the lord of death, symbolizing that all beings in samsara are subject to impermanence and death. At the hub, three animals — a pig (or boar), a snake, and a bird (rooster) — bite each other's tails, representing the three poisons of ignorance, attachment, and aversion that keep the wheel turning. The main disc is divided into six sections depicting the six realms of rebirth: the realms of gods, asuras (demigods), humans, animals, hungry ghosts (pretas), and hells. An outer ring of twelve images illustrates the twelve links (nidanas) of dependent origination. Outside the wheel stand a Buddha figure, pointing toward liberation, and a moon symbolizing nirvana.

Origins

According to tradition, the design was devised by the Buddha himself as a gift and teaching device for a king (identified variously as Bimbisara or Rudrayana). Bhavachakra paintings remain a standard feature at the entrance of Tibetan monasteries.
