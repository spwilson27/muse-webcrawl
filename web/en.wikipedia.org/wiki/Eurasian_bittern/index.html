The Eurasian bittern or great bittern (Botaurus stellaris) is a wading bird in the heron family Ardeidae. It is a large, chunky, brown bird with a thick neck and a dagger-like bill, famed for its cryptic plumage and the remarkable booming call of the male, which can carry for several kilometres across reedbeds.

Description

Bitterns measure 69–81 cm (27–32 in) in length with a wingspan of 100–130 cm. The plumage is buffy-brown heavily streaked and barred with black, providing excellent camouflage among the reed stems of its wetland habitat. When alarmed, the bird adopts an upright "bitterning" posture, pointing its bill skyward and swaying with the reeds.

Behaviour

The species is usually solitary and feeds on fish, eels, amphibians, crustaceans and insects, hunting by walking slowly through shallow water or waiting motionless at the reed edge. The male is polygynous and produces its far-carrying boom by inflating its oesophagus and expelling air, a sound produced mainly at dawn and dusk in the breeding season.

Distribution and habitat

Eurasian bitterns breed in reedbeds across temperate Europe and Asia, from the British Isles to the Pacific. Northern populations migrate south and west in winter. Habitat loss through drainage of wetlands caused major declines in the twentieth century, but targeted reedbed restoration has aided recovery in several countries, including the United Kingdom.
