Lucy the Elephant

Lucy the Elephant, Margate City
LocationMargate City, New Jersey, U.S.
Built1881
Height65 ft (19.7 m)
DesignationNational Historic Landmark (1976)

Lucy the Elephant is a six-story elephant-shaped building constructed of wood and tin sheeting in 1881 by James V. Lafferty in what is now Margate City, New Jersey, about five miles (8 km) southwest of Atlantic City. Lucy is the oldest surviving roadside tourist attraction in America.

History

Lafferty, a real-estate speculator, built the elephant — originally called the "Elephant Bazaar" — to attract buyers to his shore lots; he held a patent for animal-shaped buildings and built similar elephants at Coney Island and Cape May, both since demolished. Lucy later served as a tavern, a hotel, and a private summer home. A spiral staircase in a hind leg leads to rooms inside the body and a howdah with views of the ocean.

By the 1960s Lucy had fallen into disrepair and was threatened with demolition. Local residents formed the Save Lucy Committee, raised funds, and in 1970 moved the 90-ton structure to a city-owned lot. Lucy was restored and designated a National Historic Landmark in 1976. The structure survived Hurricane Sandy in 2012 with minor damage and remains open for tours.
