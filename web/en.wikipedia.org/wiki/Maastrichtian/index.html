Maastrichtian

The Maastrichtian is, in the International Chronostratigraphic Chart, the uppermost age (and stage) of the Late Cretaceous epoch, spanning from 72.1 to 66 million years ago. It was preceded by the Campanian and is followed by the Danian of the Paleogene. The Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event, which killed the non-avian dinosaurs, marks its end.

Maastrichtian
Period: Late Cretaceous
Age: 72.1–66 million years ago
Preceded by: Campanian
Followed by: Danian (Paleocene)
Named after: Maastricht, Netherlands

Definition

The stage was introduced by André Dumont in 1849 and named for the Dutch city of Maastricht, near which chalk deposits with characteristic fossil faunas, including the mosasaur Mosasaurus, were quarried.

Life

Maastrichtian faunas included the last dinosaurs — Tyrannosaurus, Triceratops, Ankylosaurus and hadrosaurs — alongside mosasaurs and plesiosaurs in the seas. The stage ended abruptly with the Chicxulub impact and the associated mass extinction.
