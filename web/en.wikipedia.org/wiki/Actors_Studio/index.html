The Actors Studio is a membership organization for professional actors, theatre directors, and playwrights, located at 432 West 44th Street in the Hell's Kitchen neighbourhood of Manhattan, New York City. Founded in 1947 by Elia Kazan, Cheryl Crawford, and Robert Lewis — alumni of the Group Theatre — it became the most influential acting workshop in the United States, the home of "Method acting" derived from the system of Konstantin Stanislavski, and a training ground for many of the performers who transformed American film acting in the second half of the Golden Age of Hollywood.

History

The Studio was conceived as a place where established professionals could continue to develop their craft in private sessions, free of commercial pressure. Under Lee Strasberg, who became artistic director in 1951, its sessions emphasized emotional memory, improvisation, and psychological truth. Members and students over the decades included Marlon Brando, James Dean, Marilyn Monroe, Montgomery Clift, Paul Newman, Al Pacino, Robert De Niro, Dustin Hoffman, and many others. Monroe's attendance from 1955, and her bequest of much of her estate to Strasberg, symbolized the institution's gravitational pull on stars seeking artistic credibility, as seen in her work in The Misfits.

The Actors Studio Drama School

From 1994 to 2005 the Studio partnered with The New School to offer a Master of Fine Arts program, and since 2006 it has run the Actors Studio Drama School at Pace University. From 1994 the Studio was also the setting of the television program Inside the Actors Studio, hosted by James Lipton, which brought its name to a global audience.

Influence

The Studio's approach — and internal debates about Strasberg's version of the Method versus rival interpretations by Stella Adler and Sanford Meisner — reshaped acting across theatre and film, privileging realism, emotional authenticity, and improvisation. Its alumni and ethos remain touchstones for performers, and its roster of cultural icons is unmatched among acting schools.
