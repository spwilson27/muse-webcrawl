The Lordship of Ireland (Dominium Hiberniae), sometimes referred to retrospectively as Anglo-Norman Ireland, was the part of Ireland ruled by the King of England (styled as "Lord of Ireland") and controlled by loyal Anglo-Norman lords between 1177 and 1542. The lordship was created following the Norman invasion of Ireland in 1169–1171. It lasted until 1541, when a new Kingdom of Ireland was established by the Crown of Ireland Act; the Parliament of that kingdom declared Henry VIII King of Ireland and not merely Lord.

History

Because the authority of the Lordship's government was often limited to the area around Dublin known as the Pale, it can be seen as one of the several political entities that then coexisted on the island, alongside the Gaelic kingdoms and lordships that retained native rule. Henry II, King of England, who claimed to be "Lord of Ireland", invaded the island in 1171, partly to rein in Strongbow, the Norman leader whose conquests threatened to create a rival kingdom. In 1175 the Treaty of Windsor attempted to divide authority between Henry and the Irish high king Ruaidrí Ua Conchobair, but the arrangement soon collapsed.

The legal basis for the English claim rested partly on Laudabiliter, a papal bull attributed to Pope Adrian IV (the only English pope), which purported to authorise Henry's intervention. The Lordship was formally held as a papal fief: in 1213 King John surrendered Ireland (with England) to Pope Innocent III and received it back as a feudal holding.

Administration and decline

Governance was exercised through a Lord Lieutenant (later Lord Deputy) based at Dublin Castle, a Parliament of Ireland (first meeting in 1264), and common-law courts modelled on England's. From the late 13th century, the Gaelic Irish mounted a sustained resurgence, and by the 15th century effective royal control had shrunk to the Pale. The Statutes of Kilkenny (1366) attempted to halt the assimilation of the "Old English" settlers into Gaelic culture. Only with the Tudor reconquest in the 16th century did English authority extend across the island, culminating in the creation of the Kingdom of Ireland in 1542.
