The Seventh Seal

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The knight and Death playing chess on the shore beneath a grey sky: cinema's most famous game]

The Seventh Seal (Swedish: Det sjunde inseglet) is a 1957 Swedish historical fantasy film written and directed by Ingmar Bergman. Set in plague-ravaged fourteenth-century Sweden, it follows the knight Antonius Block (Max von Sydow), returned from the Crusades in spiritual desolation, who challenges Death (Bengt Ekerot, in the white-faced, black-cloaked image that has become universal) to a game of chess to buy time for one meaningful act.

The title comes from the Book of Revelation — the silence in heaven when the seventh seal is opened, in the biblical phrase the film quotes. It won the Special Jury Prize at Cannes and established Bergman internationally; its images (the chess game, the procession of flagellants, the final dance of death along the horizon) are among cinema's most quoted.

Themes and making

Bergman, a pastor's son, wrote the play Wood Painting for his acting students and expanded it into the film, shot mainly at Råsunda studios with exteriors at Hovs Hallar. Block's confession to Death disguised as a priest — demanding knowledge, not faith, and finding heaven silent — is modern cinema's frankest statement of religious doubt; against it the film sets the holy family of Jof, Mia and their baby, the visionaries the knight's single act saves.

Legacy

The Seventh Seal made art-house cinema a world phenomenon and Bergman its presiding intelligence. Its medievalism is deliberately theatrical (Bergman called its history a pretext), and its questions — God's silence, death's courtesy — have kept it in permanent repertory and parody alike, from Woody Allen's one-acters to Bill & Ted.
