Blockade of Nagorno-Karabakh

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Blockade of Nagorno-Karabakh
Date12 December 2022 – October 2023
LocationLachin corridor, Nagorno-Karabakh
CauseClosure of the Lachin corridor by Azerbaijani protesters and later a checkpoint
OutcomeAzerbaijani military offensive, dissolution of the Republic of Artsakh, exodus of ethnic Armenians

The blockade of Nagorno-Karabakh was a blockade of the breakaway Republic of Artsakh by Azerbaijan that began on 12 December 2022, when Azerbaijani protesters identifying themselves as environmental activists blocked the Lachin corridor, the only road connecting the ethnic Armenian population of Nagorno-Karabakh with Armenia.

Humanitarian situation

The closure halted imports of food, fuel and medicine to the region's roughly 120,000 residents. In April 2023 Azerbaijan installed a checkpoint on the corridor. The International Court of Justice ordered Azerbaijan to ensure unimpeded movement along the corridor, and the International Committee of the Red Cross reported severe shortages.

End of the blockade

On 19 September 2023 Azerbaijan launched a military offensive; Artsakh's authorities capitulated within a day and agreed to disarm and dissolve the republic. Almost the entire Armenian population fled to Armenia within the following weeks, effectively ending the blockade.
