The Ogaden is a region in eastern Ethiopia, forming the greater part of the modern Somali Region. It is a largely arid plateau inhabited predominantly by ethnic Somalis, principally of the Ogaden clan of the Darod, from which the region takes its name. The region borders Somalia to the east and south, Djibouti to the north, and the Ethiopian regions of Oromia and Afar to the west.

History

The Ogaden came under Ethiopian imperial control during the expansions of Emperor Menelik II in the late 19th century, a rule formalised by treaties with Britain, France, and Italy that divided Somali-inhabited territories among colonial powers. After the Second World War the area was administered by Britain before being handed to Ethiopia in 1948–1954, against the wishes of many inhabitants.

Ogaden War and insurgency

In 1977 Somalia, under Siad Barre, invaded the Ogaden in support of Somali self-determination, beginning the Ogaden War (1977–78). Ethiopia, backed by the Soviet Union and Cuba, repelled the invasion. An insurgency by the Ogaden National Liberation Front (ONLF), founded in 1984, continued for decades; a peace agreement between the ONLF and the Ethiopian government was signed in 2018. The region contains significant natural gas reserves, notably the Calub and Hilala fields, whose exploitation has been a source of both hope and conflict.
