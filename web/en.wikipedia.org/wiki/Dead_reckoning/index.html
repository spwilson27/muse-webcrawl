Overview

Dead reckoning is the method of advancing a known position by the course steered and the distance run, allowing as best one can for wind and current. For most of maritime history it was the only way to track a ship out of sight of land: between occasional latitude observations, the navigator's world was a dead-reckoning plot.

Its weakness is that every error — in the compass, the log that measured speed, the estimate of leeway — accumulates, so after days at sea a dead-reckoning position might be out by tens or hundreds of miles. The wrecks caused by that uncertainty drove the search for a true longitude method told in the history of longitude. Modern inertial navigation is dead reckoning rebuilt in electronics — superb, but still drifting until corrected by an outside fix.
