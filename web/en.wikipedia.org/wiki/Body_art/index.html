Body art

Body art is art made on, with, or consisting of the human body. It ranges from ancient and indigenous practices—tattoos, scarification, body painting, and piercing—to the avant-garde performance works of the 1960s and 1970s, in which artists such as Marina Abramović, Chris Burden, and the Viennese Actionists used their own bodies, sometimes enduring pain or risk, as the medium of the work.

Traditional forms

Humans have decorated their bodies for tens of thousands of years. Tattooing is attested on the mummified body of Ötzi the Iceman (c. 3300 BCE); henna (mehndi) painting is traditional in South Asia and the Middle East; scarification has been practiced in parts of Africa and Papua New Guinea as a rite of passage and mark of identity; and full-body painting accompanies ritual and festival in many cultures. In the West, tattooing and piercing moved from subcultures into mainstream fashion during the late 20th century.

Body art as fine art

In contemporary art, "body art" refers to a movement emerging in the 1960s in which the artist's body became canvas, material, and subject—related to performance art, happenings, and conceptual art. Works ranged from Yves Klein's Anthropométries (models as "living brushes") to endurance pieces by Abramović and Ulay, and Orlan's surgical performances of the 1990s. Because the works are ephemeral, they typically survive as photographs and film.
