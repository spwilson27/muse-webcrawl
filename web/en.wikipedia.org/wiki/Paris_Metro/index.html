The Paris Métro is the rapid transit system of Paris, France, operated by the RATP. Opened in 1900 for the Exposition Universelle, it is one of the densest underground networks in the world, with stations on average only about 550 metres apart in the city centre. It is famous for its Art Nouveau station entrances designed by Hector Guimard.

History

The first line opened between Porte de Vincennes and Porte Maillot in July 1900. Engineer Fulgence Bienvenüe directed construction of most of the early network, earning the nickname "father of the Métro". Rubber-tyred trains were introduced on some lines from the 1950s, and fully automated driverless operation began with Line 14 in 1998 and Line 1 in 2011.

Network

The system has 16 lines identified by number and colour. Lines 3bis and 7bis are short branches. The Grand Paris Express project is adding four new lines and around 200 km of track in the suburbs during the 2020s.
