Kingston Fossil Plant coal fly ash slurry spill

The Kingston Fossil Plant coal fly ash slurry spill was an environmental disaster that occurred on 22 December 2008 near Harriman, Tennessee, when a dike at the Tennessee Valley Authority's Kingston Fossil Plant failed, releasing about 1.1 billion gallons (4.2 million cubic metres) of coal fly ash slurry into the Emory River and surrounding land.

Impact

The spill covered some 300 acres, destroyed several homes, and contaminated the Emory and Clinch rivers with ash containing heavy metals such as arsenic, lead and mercury. It was the largest fly ash release in United States history.

Cleanup and aftermath

TVA undertook a cleanup costing over a billion dollars, completed in 2015, dredging ash from the river and consolidating the remainder in an on-site landfill. The disaster prompted the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency to adopt the first federal regulations for coal ash disposal in 2014. Litigation followed over the health of cleanup workers exposed to the ash.
