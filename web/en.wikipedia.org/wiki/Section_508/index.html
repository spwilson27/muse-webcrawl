Section 508

Section 508
Section 508 mandates accessible federal information technology
Part ofRehabilitation Act of 1973
Amended1998 (Workforce Investment Act); standards refreshed 2017

Section 508 is a provision of the United States Rehabilitation Act of 1973 (29 U.S.C. § 794d) that requires federal agencies to make their electronic and information technology accessible to people with disabilities. Enacted in its modern form by amendments in 1998, it obliges agencies to ensure that federal employees with disabilities, and members of the public with disabilities seeking information or services, have access to and use of information and data comparable to that afforded to people without disabilities, unless doing so would impose an undue burden.

Scope and standards

Section 508 applies to all information and communication technology (ICT) that federal agencies develop, procure, maintain, or use, and its requirements flow down to vendors through federal procurement rules. Technical standards are set by the U.S. Access Board. In 2017 the Board published a long-awaited "refresh" of the standards (effective for compliance from 2018), which harmonised them with the international Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.0 levels A and AA and with the European EN 301 549 standard.

Compliance and enforcement

Agencies must consider accessibility when specifying and buying ICT, and vendors commonly document conformance in a Voluntary Product Accessibility Template (VPAT), issued as an Accessibility Conformance Report. Individuals may file administrative complaints with the offending agency or bring civil actions under the enforcement provisions applicable to section 504 of the Act. The Department of Justice reports periodically to Congress on agency compliance, and the General Services Administration provides guidance and testing tools. Section 508 covers only the federal sector; private employers and public accommodations are governed instead by the Americans with Disabilities Act, and state governments often adopt similar rules of their own.
