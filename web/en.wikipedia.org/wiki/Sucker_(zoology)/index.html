Sucker (zoology)

In zoology, a sucker is an adhesive or grasping organ found in many animals, used to attach to a surface or to prey. Suckers work principally by suction: muscular action reduces the pressure inside a cup-shaped structure, so that external (usually water or air) pressure holds the organ firmly against the substrate.

Cephalopods

The best-known suckers line the arms of octopuses and the arms and tentacles of squid and cuttlefish. Octopus suckers are muscular, lined with a cuticle, and operated one-by-one under fine nervous control; they also carry chemoreceptors, giving the octopus a sense of "taste by touch". Squid suckers are often ringed with chitinous teeth or modified into hooks.

Other animals

Remoras bear a large sucking disc on the head, derived from a modified dorsal fin, with which they hitchhike on sharks and other hosts. Leeches have anterior and posterior suckers for locomotion and feeding; tapeworms attach with suckers (and often hooks) on the scolex. Some fish (climbing gouramis, hillstream loaches), bats (sucker-footed bats), and insects also possess adhesive discs or suckers.

Biomimetics

Cephalopod suckers have inspired artificial suction grippers for robotics and medical devices, which mimic their soft rims and pressure differentials.
