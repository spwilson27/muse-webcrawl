Sea cucumber

Sea cucumber
Class: Holothuroidea
Phylum: Echinodermata
Species: about 1,700 known

Sea cucumbers are marine animals of the class Holothuroidea, echinoderms related to sea stars and sea urchins. They have elongated, soft bodies with leathery skin and reduced internal skeletons of microscopic ossicles, and are found on or in the seabed of oceans worldwide, from shallow reefs to the deep-sea floor.

Biology

Most sea cucumbers are deposit feeders, sweeping sediment or filtering plankton with tentacles around the mouth; they play an important role in bioturbation and nutrient recycling. Some species defend themselves by expelling sticky Cuvierian tubules or by eviscerating internal organs, which they later regenerate. They breathe through a branching respiratory tree in the cloaca.

Human use

Dried sea cucumber (trepang or bêche-de-mer) is a prized food in East and Southeast Asian cuisines and traditional medicine, supporting major fisheries; overexploitation has led to conservation listings for some species.
