Chimayó is a census-designated place in Rio Arriba and Santa Fe counties in the U.S. state of New Mexico, located in the foothills of the Sangre de Cristo Mountains along the "High Road to Taos."

El Santuario de Chimayó

The village is best known for El Santuario de Chimayó, a Roman Catholic church built around 1816 that is a major pilgrimage site, especially during Holy Week, when thousands walk to the shrine. The chapel contains a small pit of earth believed by many pilgrims to have healing properties.

Culture

Chimayó is also known for its heirloom red chile peppers and for a weaving tradition carried on by families such as the Ortegas and Trujillos, whose blankets and textiles are prized nationally.
