Hammer Film Productions

Hammer Film Productions Ltd
Founded: 1934 (revived 2007)
Founders: William Hinds, Enrique Carreras
Industry: Film production
Headquarters: London, England
Known for: Gothic horror films
Studio: Bray Studios, Berkshire

Hammer Film Productions Ltd is a British film production company based in London. Founded in 1934, it is best known for a series of Gothic horror films made from the mid-1950s to the 1970s — featuring vivid colour, graphic (for the time) violence and a stable of stars including Peter Cushing and Christopher Lee — which revived the classic monsters of Universal's earlier horror cycle and made "Hammer Horror" a byword in popular culture.

Early years

The company grew out of comedian and businessman William Hinds's ventures and the distribution firm Exclusive Films run by Hinds and Enrique Carreras. Hammer produced a varied slate of modest British features before the Second World War and resumed after it with crime films and adaptations of BBC radio serials. The success of the science-fiction horror film The Quatermass Xperiment (1955) pointed the way forward.

The Gothic horror cycle

In 1957 Hammer released The Curse of Frankenstein, directed by Terence Fisher and starring Cushing as Baron Frankenstein and Lee as the Creature — the first Frankenstein film in colour and a major international hit. It was followed by Dracula (1958, US title Horror of Dracula), with Lee's aristocratic Count becoming the definitive screen Dracula for a generation. Operating from Bray Studios in Berkshire and distributing through major American studios, Hammer produced a stream of horror and thriller titles — sequels to Frankenstein and Dracula, The Mummy (1959), The Hound of the Baskervilles (1959), The Brides of Dracula (1960) — alongside successes in other genres such as the prehistoric adventure One Million Years B.C. (1966) and the swashbuckler and war films of its wider output. Directors Fisher, Freddie Francis and John Gilling, writer-producer Jimmy Sangster, composer James Bernard and production designer Bernard Robinson gave the films their distinctive style.

Decline and revival

By the 1970s changing tastes, competition from more explicit American horror and financing difficulties eroded Hammer's position; production dwindled and largely ceased by the mid-1980s, though the brand persisted in licensing and television. The company was revived after a change of ownership in 2007, returning to production with films including the box-office hit The Woman in Black (2012), starring Daniel Radcliffe — for a time the most successful British horror film in decades — followed by The Quiet Ones (2014) and The Lodge (2019).
