John Wood, the Younger

Born25 February 1728, Bath, England
Died18 June 1782 (aged 54), Eagle House, Batheaston, England

John Wood, the Younger (25 February 1728 – 18 June 1782) was an English architect, mainly active in Bath, where he completed the great Palladian set-pieces begun by his father, John Wood the Elder.

Works in Bath

Trained in his father's office, the younger Wood took over the family practice on his father's death in 1754, completing the Circus, which the elder Wood had designed. His masterpiece is the Royal Crescent (1767–1775), a sweeping elliptical terrace of thirty town houses behind a unified Ionic façade — one of the supreme achievements of Georgian urban design. He also designed the Bath Assembly Rooms (1769–1771), the Grand Pump Room's successors in the social architecture of the spa, and terraces including Camden Crescent and Somerset Place, as well as Buckland House in Oxfordshire.

Wood's work consolidated Bath's image as a classical stone city, a legacy recognised when the city was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site. He died in 1782 and is commemorated, with his father, in the city's architecture and street names.
