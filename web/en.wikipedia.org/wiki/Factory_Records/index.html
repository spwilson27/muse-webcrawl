Factory Records

Factory Records was a Manchester-based British independent record label founded in 1978 by Tony Wilson and Alan Erasmus. It became famous for its releases by Joy Division, New Order, Happy Mondays and other artists of the Manchester music scene, and for the distinctive design work of Peter Saville, who gave catalogue numbers (prefixed FAC) to virtually everything the label — and its associated nightclub, the Haçienda — produced.

History

Factory grew out of the Factory club nights at the Russell Club in Hulme, Manchester. Wilson, a Granada Television presenter, launched the label after the success of those nights. Its first release, A Factory Sample (1979), was a double 7-inch featuring Joy Division, the Durutti Column, John Dowie and Cabaret Voltaire. Joy Division's Unknown Pleasures (1979) and Closer (1980), and after Ian Curtis's death, New Order's records, financed the label and the Haçienda (FAC 51), which opened in 1982 and became central to the acid house and "Madchester" scenes.

Ethos and decline

Factory was notorious for its informal, artist-friendly arrangements — famously, its artists owned their own work and were free to leave — and for spending lavishly: the Haçienda lost money for years, and famously expensive sleeves (such as New Order's "Blue Monday", the best-selling 12-inch single of all time, for which the label reportedly lost money on each copy) strained its finances. Factory Records went bankrupt in November 1992. Its catalogue was later acquired by London Records. Wilson's story, and the label's, was dramatised in the 2002 film 24 Hour Party People.

Key artists

Joy Division, New Order, Happy Mondays, the Durutti Column, A Certain Ratio, Orchestral Manoeuvres in the Dark (briefly), James, and Section 25 were among its notable acts.
