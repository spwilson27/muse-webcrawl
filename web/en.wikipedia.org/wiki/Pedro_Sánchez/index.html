Pedro Sánchez

Pedro Sánchez Pérez-Castejón (born 29 February 1972) is a Spanish politician who has served as Prime Minister of Spain since 2018 and as Secretary-General of the Spanish Socialist Workers' Party (PSOE) since 2017, having previously held the post from 2014 to 2016. He came to power in June 2018 through the first successful motion of no confidence in Spain's democratic history, against Mariano Rajoy, and has since led minority and coalition governments — including, from 2020, Spain's first coalition government since the 1930s — surviving repeated elections and crises with a reputation for political resilience.

Early life and rise

Sánchez was born in Madrid's Tetuán district and educated at the Lycée Français and the Complutense University, where he studied economics; he later earned a doctorate in economics and business. He worked as an adviser in the European Parliament and for the Spanish government during the Kosovo War, served as a Madrid city councillor, and entered Congress in 2009. In July 2014 he won the PSOE's first membership ballot for leader, presenting himself as a renewal candidate.

Ouster and comeback

After two inconclusive general elections (2015 and 2016) and a disastrous party crisis over whether to allow Rajoy's investiture, Sánchez resigned as leader in October 2016 and gave up his parliamentary seat rather than abstain. Driving around Spain to meet party members — the famous tour in his Peugeot 407 — he defeated the party establishment's candidate Susana Díaz in the May 2017 leadership primary and returned as Secretary-General.

Prime Minister

On 1 June 2018, after the Audiencia Nacional's Gürtel corruption verdict against the ruling PP, Sánchez won a motion of no confidence 180–169 and became Prime Minister — as a leader with no parliamentary seat at the time. He called and won elections in April and November 2019; in January 2020 he formed a coalition with Unidas Podemos, Spain's first coalition government of the democratic era. His governments governed through the COVID-19 pandemic, the 2021 La Palma eruption, the economic fallout of Russia's invasion of Ukraine, and passed labour reform, minimum-income and euthanasia laws and large minimum-wage rises. After calling a snap election for July 2023 following regional losses, his PSOE finished second but he assembled a parliamentary majority — including a controversial amnesty agreement with Catalan separatist parties — and was re-invested in November 2023 at the head of a PSOE–Sumar coalition.
