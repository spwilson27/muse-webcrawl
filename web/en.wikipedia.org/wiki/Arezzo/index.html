Arezzo (Italian: [aˈrettso]) is a city and comune in Italy and the capital of the province of the same name, located in Tuscany. Arezzo is about 80 kilometres (50 miles) southeast of Florence at an elevation of 296 metres (971 ft) above sea level. It is also the namesake for the region.

History

Described by Livy as one of the Capita Etruriae (Etruscan capitals), Arezzo (Latin: Arretium) was one of the most powerful Etruscan cities. The city became a Roman ally after the Battle of Sentinum in 295 BC and later an important centre of metalwork and pottery, particularly the red-glazed terra sigillata known as Arretine ware.

In the Middle Ages Arezzo was a free commune, often Ghibelline in sympathy against Florence and Siena. In 1289 it was defeated at the Battle of Campaldino, in which Dante Alighieri participated as a cavalryman. The city came under Florentine control in 1384. Arezzo is the birthplace of Guido d'Arezzo, the inventor of modern musical notation; of the poet Petrarch; and of the artist and art historian Giorgio Vasari.

Main sights

  • Arezzo Cathedral — Gothic church begun in 1278, housing frescoes and stained glass by Guillaume de Marcillat.
  • Basilica of San Francesco — famed for Piero della Francesca's fresco cycle The Legend of the True Cross.
  • Pieve di Santa Maria — Romanesque parish church with a celebrated sculpted façade.
  • Piazza Grande — the sloping main square, lined with Vasari's loggia.

Culture

Arezzo hosts the Giostra del Saracino, a medieval jousting tournament held twice yearly, in which knights representing the city's four quarters charge at a wooden target in the Piazza Grande. The city's historic centre was featured in Roberto Benigni's 1997 film Life Is Beautiful.
