Htilominlo

Htilominlo (Burmese: ထီးလိုမင်းလို; 1175–1210), also known as Nandaungmya, was a king of the Pagan dynasty of Burma (Myanmar) from 1211 to 1235. His reign marked the beginning of the decline of the Pagan Empire, as rising tax-free religious landholdings eroded the crown's revenue base.

Accession

According to chronicle tradition, Htilominlo was chosen as heir by his father Narapatisithu by an omen: the princes stood around a white umbrella, which tilted toward him — hence his name, "the king the umbrella wished". The name is also interpreted as "the king desired by the three worlds".

Reign

A devout Buddhist, Htilominlo completed the Htilominlo Temple, the last of the great Burmese-style temples at Bagan, as well as the Mahabodhi Temple. He largely delegated administration to a council of ministers — often cited as an early form of government by privy council — and devoted himself to religion, while the kingdom's economic foundations weakened as more land passed to tax-exempt monastic estates.

Death and succession

Htilominlo died in 1235 and was succeeded by his son Kyaswa.
