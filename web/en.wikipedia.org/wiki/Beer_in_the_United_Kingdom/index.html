Beer in the United Kingdom

Beer in the United Kingdom has a tradition stretching back centuries and is centred on the pub. The United Kingdom is known for top-fermented ales — bitter, pale ale, porter and stout — served as cask-conditioned "real ale", though lager has accounted for the majority of sales by volume since the late 20th century. Brewing is a significant industry, and beer duty and pub culture remain subjects of public debate.

History

Beer has been brewed in Britain since Neolithic times, and ale flavoured with herbs rather than hops was the staple drink of medieval England; hops were adopted widely from the Low Countries in the 16th century. Industrial brewing developed in London in the 18th century with porter and later in Burton upon Trent, whose sulfate-rich water suited pale ales exported throughout the British Empire — most famously India pale ale (IPA). The 19th century saw the rise of great brewing dynasties and tied-house pub estates.

Measures such as the Beerhouse Act 1830 liberalised retailing, while temperance movements and two world wars brought restrictions, weaker gravities and shortened pub hours. Consolidation in the mid-20th century produced national brewing groups that promoted keg beer; the Campaign for Real Ale (CAMRA), founded in 1971, led a revival of cask ale. The Beer Orders of 1989 forced the big brewers to divest pubs, reshaping the industry into pub companies and independent brewers. Since the early 2000s, progressive beer duty relief has supported a boom in small and craft breweries, taking the total number of UK breweries above 1,800.

Styles

  • Bitter — well-hopped pale ale, usually served on draught
  • Pale ale / IPA — from Burton's originals to modern heavily hopped craft versions
  • Porter and stout — dark ales originating in London; includes milk stout and imperial stout
  • Brown ale, mild, barley wine, old ale — traditional English styles
  • Lager — the largest-selling category, mostly international brands brewed under licence
  • Scottish ales — historically designated by shilling categories (60/-, 70/-, 80/-)

Cask ale

Cask-conditioned beer undergoes a secondary fermentation in the container from which it is served and is dispensed without added carbon dioxide, traditionally by hand pump through a beer engine. CAMRA's definition of "real ale" and its annual Great British Beer Festival, held since 1977, have been central to the style's survival.

Measures and law

Draught beer in the UK is legally served in pints (568 ml) or fractions thereof. Beer is taxed by alcohol content through beer duty, reformed in 2023 into a strength-based system with a draught relief for pub sales.
