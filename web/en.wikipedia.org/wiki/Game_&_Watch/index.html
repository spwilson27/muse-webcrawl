Game & Watch

Game & Watch is a line of handheld electronic games produced by Nintendo from 1980 to 1991. Designed by Gunpei Yokoi, each unit played a single game on a segmented liquid-crystal display and also functioned as a clock and alarm — hence the name "Game & Watch". The series sold an estimated 43.4 million units worldwide across 59 main titles and is credited with popularising handheld gaming, paving the way for the Game Boy.

History

Yokoi conceived the product after watching a bored businessman playing with an LCD calculator on a bullet train. Using cheap, mature calculator technology and button cells, Nintendo released the first model, Ball, in April 1980. The line expanded into several series: the silver and gold Wide Screen models, the clamshell Multi Screen series (beginning with Oil Panic in 1982), the New Wide Screen series, Tabletop and Panorama models, and the Crystal Screen series. The Multi Screen design — two screens in a folding case — later inspired the Nintendo DS.

Gameplay and design

Games were simple score-attack challenges with two difficulty modes ("Game A" and "Game B"), using pre-formed LCD segments rather than pixels. Characters were drawn in fixed poses that lit up in sequence to simulate movement. The character used in several games, a generic everyman, was later developed into the fighter Mr. Game & Watch in the Super Smash Bros. series. The cross-shaped D-pad, introduced on the Game & Watch version of Donkey Kong (1982), became a standard of game controller design.

Legacy

Nintendo has re-released Game & Watch titles in the Game & Watch Gallery compilations and produced modern tribute hardware, including the Game & Watch: Super Mario Bros. (2020) and The Legend of Zelda (2021) units. Original units are sought-after collectibles.
