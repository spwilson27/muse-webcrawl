Minisatellite

A minisatellite is a tract of repetitive DNA in which a short sequence motif, typically 10–60 base pairs long, is tandemly repeated many times. Minisatellites are longer than microsatellites (whose repeat units are only a few base pairs) and shorter than the large satellite DNA arrays found at centromeres. They occur especially near telomeres and are among the most variable regions of the genome.

Variability and uses

Because replication slippage and recombination change the number of repeat units, minisatellite loci — called variable number tandem repeats (VNTRs) — differ greatly between individuals. In 1985 Alec Jeffreys showed that probing many minisatellites at once produces a pattern unique to each person, giving rise to DNA fingerprinting, long used in forensics and paternity testing before being largely superseded by microsatellite (STR) profiling. Instability at particular minisatellites is also linked to disease, and minisatellite length variation remains a tool in population genetics.
