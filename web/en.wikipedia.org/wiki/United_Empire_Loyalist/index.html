United Empire Loyalists (or simply Loyalists) were American colonists who remained loyal to the British Crown during and after the American Revolutionary War. Tens of thousands left the newly independent United States, resettling in British North America (present-day Canada), the Bahamas, and elsewhere in the British Empire.

In Canada, the title "United Empire Loyalist" was formally recognised, and Loyalists and their descendants were entitled to use the post-nominal letters "UE". Lord Dorchester's 1789 proclamation granted the distinction to those who had adhered to the unity of the Empire before the Treaty of Separation in 1783.

Legacy

Loyalist settlement transformed the demographics and politics of British North America, leading to the creation of New Brunswick in 1784 and Upper Canada (Ontario) in 1791. Black Loyalists, who had been promised freedom for their service, settled in Nova Scotia, and many later migrated to Sierra Leone. Descendants of Loyalists remain an honoured group in Canadian historical memory, commemorated by the United Empire Loyalists' Association of Canada.
