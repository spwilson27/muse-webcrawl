The Scottish Fantasy in E-flat major, Op. 46, is a fantasia for violin and orchestra (with prominent harp) by the German composer Max Bruch, written in 1879–1880. Subtitled "Free use of Scottish folk melodies", the work draws on traditional Scottish tunes and is one of Bruch's most popular compositions, rivalling his Violin Concerto No. 1 in the repertoire.

Composition and premiere

Bruch, who had a long-standing fascination with folk music, composed the fantasy in Berlin during the winter of 1879–80. It was premiered in Liverpool in 1881 by the Spanish virtuoso Pablo de Sarasate, for whom Bruch later wrote other works, and to whom the fantasy is associated. The composer conducted.

Music

The work is cast as an introduction and four movements played with only brief pauses. The opening Grave leads to an Adagio cantabile based on the tune "Auld Rob Morris". The second movement, Allegro, is a lively scherzo on "Hey Tuttie Tatie" — the melody Robert Burns used for "Scots Wha Hae" — complete with bagpipe-like drones. A lyrical Andante sostenuto follows, using "I'm a-doun for lack o' Johnnie", and the finale, Allegro guerriero, is a martial movement built on "Scots Wha Hae" itself, ending with a brilliant coda. The harp is given an unusually prominent role, evoking the Celtic bardic tradition.

Reception

The Scottish Fantasy has remained a concert favourite and a staple of violinists' repertoire, frequently recorded by leading soloists. Its tunefulness exemplifies Bruch's conservative Romantic style, which fell from critical fashion in the early 20th century but retained its audience appeal.
