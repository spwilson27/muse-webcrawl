Levitical cities

The Levitical cities were 48 cities in the ancient Kingdoms of Israel and Judah set aside for the tribe of Levi, who were not allocated their own territorial inheritance in the Promised Land. According to the Book of Joshua (chapter 21) and 1 Chronicles 6, each city came with surrounding pastureland (migrash) extending from the city walls, and the cities were assigned by lot to the Levitical clans: the Kohathites, Gershonites, and Merarites, with thirteen reserved for the priestly descendants of Aaron.

Biblical description

The institution is commanded in Numbers 35, where Moses is instructed to give the Levites cities to dwell in from the inheritance of the other tribes, since the Levites' inheritance was the priesthood itself. Six of the 48 cities doubled as cities of refuge, where a person accused of manslaughter could flee until trial: Kedesh, Shechem, and Hebron west of the Jordan, and Bezer, Ramoth-Gilead, and Golan to the east.

List and debate

The cities named include Hebron, Libnah, Jattir, Beth-Shemesh, and Gezer in the south and west; Shechem, Golan, Kedesh, and Kartan in the north; and others distributed through every tribal territory, so that no Israelite lived far from Levitical teaching and adjudication — a rationale emphasized by later commentators such as Maimonides. Biblical scholars debate the list's historicity: some regard it as an idealized administrative scheme of the Persian or late monarchic period, noting overlaps and variations between the Joshua and Chronicles versions; others connect it to genuine town lists from the divided monarchy.

Significance

Scattered among all the tribes, the Levites functioned as teachers of Torah, judges, and custodians of worship, and their cities formed a religious infrastructure for the nation. Rabbinic tradition held that the pasturelands of Levitical cities could not be sold permanently or built over, preserving their open land in perpetuity.
