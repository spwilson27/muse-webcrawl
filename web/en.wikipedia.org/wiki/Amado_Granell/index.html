Amado Granell Mesado (5 November 1898 – 12 May 1972) was a Spanish military officer of the Spanish Republican Army during the Spanish Civil War and later a lieutenant in the Free French Forces. He is remembered as the first Allied officer to enter the Hôtel de Ville during the Liberation of Paris on 24 August 1944, leading the Spanish soldiers of La Nueve, the 9th Company of the Régiment de marche du Tchad in General Leclerc's 2nd Armored Division.

Biography

Granell was born in Burriana, in the province of Castellón. He served in the Spanish Legion in Morocco before the Civil War, during which he fought for the Republic and rose to command positions in the Republican army. After the Republican defeat in 1939 he fled to French North Africa, where he was interned in a camp before joining the Free French forces.

La Nueve and the liberation of Paris

Granell became the second-in-command and, in combat, the effective spearhead leader of La Nueve, a company composed mostly of Spanish Republican exiles whose half-tracks bore names like "Guadalajara", "Teruel" and "Don Quijote". On the evening of 24 August 1944, Granell's column was the first to reach the Paris city hall, where he was received by Georges Bidault and the Resistance leadership; a famous photograph of him at the Hôtel de Ville appeared on the front page of Libération the next day. Because he was Spanish, his role was long downplayed in French official memory and was only widely recognised in Spain and France from the 2000s onward.

Later life

Granell declined offers to continue a military career in France and returned quietly to Spain in the 1950s, running a small restaurant in Sueca, Valencia, while staying in contact with fellow exiles. He died in 1972. In 2017 the city of Paris named a garden in his honour (the Jardin des Combattants de la Nueve), and memorial plaques commemorate him in Paris, Burriana and Valencia.
