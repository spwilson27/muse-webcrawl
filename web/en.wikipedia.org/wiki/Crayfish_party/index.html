Crayfish party

A crayfish party (Swedish: kräftskiva) is a traditional summertime celebration in Sweden and Finland in which people gather to eat crayfish boiled with dill and to drink aquavit and beer, usually outdoors in the evening. The parties mark the crayfish season in August and are among the most characteristic of Nordic summer customs.

History

Crayfish had been eaten in Sweden since the Middle Ages, at first chiefly by the aristocracy, and for centuries their capture was restricted by law to protect the stocks, producing a short, eagerly awaited season beginning in August. In the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, crayfish parties spread from the upper classes to the wider population, helped by rail transport that brought fresh crayfish to the cities. When legal restrictions on the fishing season were eventually relaxed, the tradition of the August premiere party remained firmly in place. Disease (crayfish plague) devastated the native noble crayfish in Swedish waters, and much of the supply is now imported, notably from Turkey, Spain, and Louisiana, supplemented by the introduced signal crayfish.

Customs

The crayfish are boiled in salted water flavoured with crown dill and eaten cold, sucked and picked by hand. A party is traditionally accompanied by bread, butter, a strong cheese such as Västerbotten cheese, and schnapps — typically aquavit — with a comic drinking song (snapsvisa) sung before each small glass. Guests wear conical paper hats and bibs decorated with crayfish and the "man in the moon", and paper lanterns, often showing a smiling full moon face, are hung around the table as dusk falls. The atmosphere is deliberately informal and boisterous.

In Finland the corresponding celebration (Finnish: rapujuhlat, Swedish-speaking Finns: kräftskiva) begins with the crayfish season on 21 July and follows much the same pattern.
