Hoori

Hoori (火折尊), also known as Hohodemi (火遠理命) and by his full title Amatsuhiko Hikohohodemi no Mikoto, is a figure in Japanese mythology, appearing in the Kojiki (712) and Nihon Shoki (720). He was a grandson of the sun goddess Amaterasu—son of Ninigi, who descended to earth, and the blossom princess Konohanasakuya-hime—and in the imperial genealogy he stands as the grandfather of Emperor Jimmu, Japan's legendary first emperor.

The lost fishhook

Hoori, a hunter of the mountains, exchanged roles for a day with his elder brother Hoderi, a fisherman of the sea. Fishing, Hoori lost Hoderi's prized fishhook. Despite offering hundreds of replacement hooks forged from his own sword, Hoori could not appease his brother, and in despair he wandered the seashore, where the sea god's messenger guided him to the palace of Watatsumi, the dragon god of the sea, beneath the waves.

Marriage and return

At the sea palace Hoori was welcomed and married Watatsumi's daughter, Toyotama-hime, living there three years. Homesick at last, he received back the lost hook—found in the throat of a red sea bream—together with two tide-controlling jewels, the shiofuru-tama (tide-raising jewel) and shiohiru-tama (tide-ebbing jewel). Returning, he used the jewels to subdue his unrelenting brother, who submitted and became the ancestor of the Hayato people. Toyotama-hime later came ashore to bear their son, Ugayafukiaezu, but transformed into a dragon (or crocodile/shark form) during childbirth; discovered in her true shape against her wish, she returned to the sea, leaving the child to be raised by her sister Tamayori-hime, whom he later married. Their son was Jimmu.

Significance

The Hoori myth links the heavenly imperial line to the sea and to the subjugated peoples of southern Kyushu, and its motifs—the lost hook, the underwater palace, the tabooed childbirth—recur throughout Japanese folklore, most famously in the tale of Urashima Tarō.
