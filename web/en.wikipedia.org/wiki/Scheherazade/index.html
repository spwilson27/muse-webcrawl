Scheherazade (from Persian Shahrzād) is the legendary queen and storyteller of the frame tale of the Middle Eastern collection of folk tales known as One Thousand and One Nights.

Scheherazade narrating to King Shahryar.

The frame tale

King Shahryar, betrayed by his first wife, resolves to marry a new bride each day and have her executed the following morning. Scheherazade, the learned daughter of the royal vizier, volunteers to marry him. On the wedding night she begins a tale but leaves it unfinished at dawn, promising to complete it the next night. Captivated, the king spares her life to hear the ending — and each night she finishes one story and begins another, continuing for 1,001 nights. By the end she has borne the king three sons, and Shahryar, his heart softened, abandons his cruel vow and makes her his queen.

Stories told

Within the frame, Scheherazade narrates famous tales including "Aladdin and the Magic Lamp", "Ali Baba and the Forty Thieves", and "The Seven Voyages of Sinbad the Sailor" (the latter two were added to the collection by later translators such as Antoine Galland).

Cultural legacy

Scheherazade has inspired numerous works, notably Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov's symphonic suite Scheherazade (1888), ballets by the Ballets Russes, and countless literary adaptations. She remains an emblem of the power of storytelling itself.
