Globalisation

Globalisation (or globalization) is the process of interaction and integration among people, companies, and governments worldwide. It encompasses growing international trade, investment, migration, cultural exchange, and the spread of technology, and is driven by advances in transport and communications.

History

Although long-distance exchange dates to ancient silk roads, modern globalisation accelerated in the 19th century with steamships, railways, and the telegraph, and again after 1945 through institutions such as the IMF, World Bank, and GATT/WTO. Container shipping and the internet deepened integration from the 1970s onward.

Debate

Supporters credit globalisation with lifting hundreds of millions from poverty and lowering consumer prices. Critics point to job displacement, inequality, cultural homogenisation, and systemic financial risk, and anti-globalisation movements have protested at summit meetings since the 1999 Seattle WTO protests.
