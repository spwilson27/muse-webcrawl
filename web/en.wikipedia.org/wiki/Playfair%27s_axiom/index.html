Playfair's axiom

In geometry, Playfair's axiom is an axiom that can be used instead of the fifth postulate of Euclid (the parallel postulate). It states: In a plane, given a line and a point not on it, at most one line parallel to the given line can be drawn through the point.

History

The axiom is named after the Scottish mathematician John Playfair, who popularised this formulation in his 1795 edition of Euclid's Elements, although the statement itself goes back at least to Proclus in the fifth century AD. Because it is logically equivalent to Euclid's parallel postulate (assuming the other axioms), many textbooks present Playfair's version as the standard form of the parallel postulate.

Relation to non-Euclidean geometry

Denying Playfair's axiom leads to non-Euclidean geometries: in hyperbolic geometry, infinitely many parallels pass through the point, while in elliptic geometry none do. The equivalence of the axiom with Euclid's fifth postulate can be proved within absolute (neutral) geometry.
