Art provenance

Art provenance is the documented history of ownership of a work of art, from its creation to the present. A complete, verifiable provenance helps establish authenticity, legal title and historical context, and strongly affects market value.

Research

Provenance researchers use bills of sale, auction and exhibition catalogues, inventories, correspondence, photographs, inscriptions and labels on the work's reverse, and catalogues raisonnés. Gaps — especially for European works between 1933 and 1945 — raise concern about Nazi looting; the 1998 Washington Principles on Nazi-Confiscated Art urge museums to research holdings and seek just solutions, leading to high-profile restitutions to heirs of Jewish collectors.

Importance

Provenance can expose forgeries when a claimed ownership chain is anachronistic or fabricated, as in the Wolfgang Beltracchi scandal. Conversely a prestigious collection history can add a premium. Databases such as the Art Loss Register record stolen and claimed works.
