Stewart Island (Māori: Rakiura, lit. "the glowing skies", a reference to the aurora australis sometimes visible from the island) is New Zealand's third-largest island, located 30 kilometres (19 miles) south of the South Island, across Foveaux Strait. It is a roughly triangular island with a total land area of 1,746 km² (674 sq mi). Its 164-kilometre (102 mi) coastline is deeply indented by Paterson Inlet (east), Port Pegasus (south), and Mason Bay (west). The island is generally hilly (rising to 980 metres / 3,215 feet at Mount Anglem) and densely forested. Flightless birds, including penguins, thrive there, as there are fewer introduced predators.

History

The island's Māori name, Rakiura, is commonly interpreted as "the island of glowing skies", a reference to the southern lights. The island was first charted by Europeans in 1770 by Captain James Cook, who mistakenly recorded it as a peninsula of the South Island; it was not until 1809 that Captain William Stewart, commanding the sealing vessel Pegasus, correctly charted it as an island. Stewart also gave his name to the island and to Foveaux Strait's charts. In 1841 the island's name was officially recorded as Stewart Island.

Rakiura was purchased by the Crown from Tītī Islands (Muttonbird Islands) Māori in 1864 for £6,000. From the 1860s, Norwegian whalers and sealers settled at the island, and a fishing industry developed around oysters, crayfish (rock lobster), blue cod, and paua.

Geography and settlement

The only settlement is Oban, on Halfmoon Bay in Paterson Inlet, which has a permanent population of around 400 people. Most of the island is protected as Rakiura National Park, established in 2002 and covering about 85% of the island's area. The island is accessible by ferry from Bluff and by air from Invercargill. Its human inhabitants are served by diesel-generated electricity and a small road network around Oban.

Flora and fauna

Stewart Island is an important refuge for native birds. The southern tokoeka, a subspecies of brown kiwi, is common and, unusually, is active during the day. Other birds include the Stewart Island robin, yellowhead (mohua), saddleback (tīeke), and the endangered kākāpō on nearby Codfish Island / Whenua Hou. The island's forests are dominated by rimu, kamahi, and southern rātā.
