Gallica is the digital library of the Bibliothèque nationale de France (BnF) and its partners. Online since 1997, it provides free access to millions of digitised documents: printed materials (books, press and periodicals), manuscripts, graphic and photographic materials, maps and plans, sheet music, and sound recordings, drawn from the BnF's collections and those of partner institutions across France, including the libraries of fellow members of the Gallica network.

Collections and services

Gallica's holdings are predominantly in French and largely cover the medieval to early 20th-century heritage, with extensive runs of national and regional newspapers, illustrated press, learned journals and classic editions. Texts are available as page images and, for much printed matter, as OCR full text searchable in the interface; materials are disseminated via IIIF image APIs and OAI-PMH, and curated selections are promoted in the "Gallica marque-pages" and partner blogs. A white-label platform, Gallica marque blanche, lets partner libraries operate their own branded digital libraries on the same infrastructure.

History

Launched initially as a demonstration of image-mode digitisation in 1997, Gallica expanded after 2000 into mass digitisation programmes — including, after 2007, tenders for OCR-based digitisation of hundreds of thousands of out-of-copyright volumes per year — and absorbed partner collections such as digital libraries from regional archives and university libraries.
