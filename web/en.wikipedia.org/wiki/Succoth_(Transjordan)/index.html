Succoth (Transjordan)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Succoth (Hebrew: סוכות, Sukkoth, "booths") is a place named in the Hebrew Bible, identified with a site in the Jordan Valley east of the River Jordan, in the territory later associated with the tribe of Gad. It should not be confused with the Egyptian place of the same name mentioned in the Exodus narrative.

Biblical accounts

According to the Book of Genesis, Jacob, returning from Paddan Aram, built a house at Succoth and made booths (sukkot) for his livestock, from which the place took its name. In the Book of Joshua, Succoth is listed in the valley within the allotment of Gad. The Book of Judges recounts that the men of Succoth refused bread to Gideon's army pursuing the Midianite kings, and were punished with briers and thorns. In 1 Kings, the plains near Succoth were where Solomon's foundries cast the bronze vessels for the Temple, "in the clay ground between Succoth and Zarethan". Psalm 60 and Psalm 108 mention the "Valley of Succoth".

Identification

Succoth is commonly identified with Tell Deir 'Alla, a mound at the mouth of the Jabbok valley in modern Jordan. Excavations there uncovered a Late Bronze Age sanctuary and, famously, the Deir Alla Inscription, a text in an Aramaic-related dialect concerning the seer Balaam, known from the Book of Numbers. Some scholars have proposed neighbouring tells instead, and the identification remains debated.
