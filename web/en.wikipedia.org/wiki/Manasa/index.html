Manasa is a Hindu goddess of snakes, worshipped mainly in Bengal, Jharkhand, Assam and other parts of northeastern India, chiefly for the prevention and cure of snakebite and for fertility and prosperity. She is the sister of the great serpent Vasuki and, in many traditions, a daughter of the sage Kashyapa.

Mythology

The medieval Bengali narrative poems known as the Manasa Mangal Kavyas tell how Manasa sought worship among mortals, winning over the reluctant merchant Chand Sadagar only after a series of calamities befell his family, culminating in the death of his son Lakhindar by snakebite on his wedding night and the devotion of his daughter-in-law Behula, who journeyed to restore her husband to life.

Worship

Manasa is typically depicted seated on a lotus or standing amid a canopy of serpent hoods, and is worshipped during the monsoon month of Shravan, when snakes are most active. Aniconic forms include a branch of the manasa (sisal) plant, an earthen pot, or a mud snake image.
