Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act of 1997

Full title: Republic Act No. 8371

Enacted: 28 July 1997

Signed: 29 October 1997 (President Fidel V. Ramos)

Country: Philippines

The Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act of 1997 (IPRA), officially Republic Act No. 8371, is a Philippine law that recognises and promotes the rights of indigenous cultural communities and indigenous peoples in the Philippines. It was signed into law by President Fidel V. Ramos on 29 October 1997 and is regarded as landmark legislation, among the first of its kind in Asia.

Provisions

The Act recognises four bundles of rights: rights to ancestral domains and lands; rights to self-governance and empowerment; social justice and human rights; and cultural integrity. It establishes the concept of native title over ancestral domains, requires the free and prior informed consent (FPIC) of communities before projects proceed on their land, and creates the National Commission on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP) to implement the law and issue Certificates of Ancestral Domain Title.

Legal challenges

In Cruz v. Secretary of Environment and Natural Resources (2000), the Supreme Court of the Philippines upheld the Act's constitutionality by a split vote, rejecting the argument that recognising native title conflicted with the Regalian doctrine that all public lands belong to the state.

Implementation and criticism

Implementation has been criticised as slow and underfunded: delineation and titling of ancestral domains have lagged, FPIC processes have been disputed in mining and energy cases, and overlapping claims with other agencies persist. Nonetheless, millions of hectares have been covered by ancestral domain titles, and the Act remains the cornerstone of indigenous rights policy in the Philippines.
