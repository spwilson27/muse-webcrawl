The Circassian languages are a subgroup of the Northwest Caucasian language family spoken by the Circassian people. They are generally divided into two main literary languages: Adyghe (West Circassian) and Kabardian (East Circassian), which themselves cover a continuum of dialects.

Features

Circassian languages are known for their very large consonant inventories and small vowel systems; some dialects distinguish more than sixty consonant phonemes, including ejective and labialised sounds. They are polysynthetic and ergative, with complex verb morphology that can encode the subject, objects, location and other information within a single verb form.

Status and distribution

Adyghe is an official language of the Republic of Adygea, and Kabardian of Kabardino-Balkaria and Karachay-Cherkessia in Russia. Following the expulsions of the 19th century, large speaker communities also exist in the diaspora, especially in Turkey, though language use there has declined. Both literary languages are written in Cyrillic-based alphabets.
