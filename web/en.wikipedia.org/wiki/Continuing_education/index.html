Continuing education

Continuing education is an all-encompassing term for post-secondary learning activities and programmes undertaken by adults after leaving formal initial education. It includes university extension and adult education courses, professional development, workplace training, and non-credit personal enrichment classes. Within regulated professions, continuing education is often a formal requirement for maintaining a licence to practise.

Forms

Continuing education is delivered by university extension divisions (such as Harvard Extension School and Oxford's Department for Continuing Education), community colleges, professional associations, employers and online platforms offering massive open online courses (MOOCs) and microcredentials. Programmes may award certificates, digital badges, or continuing education units (CEUs), where one CEU typically represents ten contact hours of instruction.

Professional requirements

Many professions mandate periodic continuing education: medicine (continuing medical education, CME), law (continuing legal education, CLE), accounting, nursing, teaching, engineering and pharmacy among them. Requirements usually specify a number of credit hours per licence period and are intended to keep practitioners current with evolving standards and knowledge. Critics note variable quality and the risk that requirements become box-ticking exercises, while supporters cite patient and public safety.

Adult and lifelong learning

Beyond professional mandates, continuing education is a pillar of lifelong learning policy: UNESCO, the OECD and the European Union promote adult learning for employability, digital skills and civic participation. Historically it grew from 19th-century mechanics' institutes, university extension lectures, workers' education movements and institutions such as Denmark's folk high schools and Britain's Open University (founded 1969), which opened degree study to adults at scale.
