The Cunard Line is a British-American cruise line operated by Carnival UK and owned by Carnival Corporation & plc. Founded in 1840 by Samuel Cunard as the British and North American Royal Mail Steam-Packet Company, it pioneered regular transatlantic steamship services and became one of the most famous shipping lines in history.

History

Cunard won the British government's mail contract for a transatlantic service, and its first ship, the paddle steamer Britannia, crossed from Liverpool to Halifax and Boston in 1840. The line became known for fast, safe Atlantic crossings and competed for the Blue Riband. Its celebrated liners included Lusitania (torpedoed in 1915 with the loss of 1,198 lives), Mauretania, Aquitania, Queen Mary (1936) and Queen Elizabeth (1940). In 1934 the line merged with its rival White Star Line, forming Cunard-White Star, before resuming the Cunard name in 1950. Queen Elizabeth 2 entered service in 1969 and became one of the most famous ships in the world.

Modern fleet

Cunard was acquired by Carnival Corporation in 1998. Its flagship, Queen Mary 2 (2004), is the only true ocean liner still in regular transatlantic service. She was joined by Queen Victoria (2007), Queen Elizabeth (2010) and Queen Anne (2024). The fleet is registered in Hamilton, Bermuda, and the ships carry Cunard's traditional red funnels with black tops.
