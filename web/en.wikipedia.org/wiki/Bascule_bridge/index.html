Bascule bridge

A bascule bridge (also referred to as a drawbridge or lifting bridge) is a type of moveable bridge with a counterweight that continuously balances a span, or leaf, throughout its upward swing to provide clearance for boat traffic. The term derives from the French bascule, meaning a seesaw. Bascule bridges may be single-leaf or double-leaf and are the most common type of moveable span.

Types

Designs are classified by how the leaf rotates. The trunnion bascule pivots on a fixed axle; the rolling lift bridge, patented by William Scherzer, rolls back on a track; and the heel trunnion type pivots while the counterweight swings below deck. Chicago and Strauss designs are famous variants, and London's Tower Bridge (1894) uses two large trunnion leaves.

Operation

Because the counterweight balances most of the leaf's weight, bascules can open quickly with modest power. They are widely used for road and rail crossings of navigable waterways in cities such as Chicago, where more than 50 moveable bridges span the river system.
