Notting Hill (film)

Notting Hill is a 1999 British romantic comedy film directed by Roger Michell and written by Richard Curtis. It stars Julia Roberts as Anna Scott, a famous American actress, and Hugh Grant as Will Thacker, a shy London travel-bookshop owner whose life changes when she walks into his shop in Notting Hill. The cast also includes Rhys Ifans as Will's eccentric lodger Spike, Hugh Bonneville, Emma Chambers, Tim McInnerny, Gina McKee and James Dreyfus.

Plot

After a chance meeting and a spilled glass of orange juice, Will and Anna begin an unlikely romance, complicated by her fame, the intrusions of the press, and the reappearance of her American boyfriend. When a scandal drives her to hide at Will's flat and the tabloids discover her, the couple split. In the celebrated final sequence, Will races across London to her press conference, where Anna declares that she will stay in Britain indefinitely — asking, in the film's most famous line, as "just a girl, standing in front of a boy, asking him to love her".

Reception

The film was a major commercial success, grossing over $360 million worldwide and becoming the highest-grossing British film of its time. It received positive reviews, won the Audience Award at the BAFTAs, and helped cement the Richard Curtis "rom-com" formula established by Four Weddings and a Funeral. Its soundtrack features Elvis Costello's cover of "She" and Ronan Keating's "When You Say Nothing at All".
