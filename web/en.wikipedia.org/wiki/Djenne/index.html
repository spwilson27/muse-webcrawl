Djenne

Djenné
Country: Mali
Region: Mopti
Population: approx. 33,000 (2009 census)
Known for: Great Mosque of Djenné, mud-brick architecture, historic trade centre

Djenné (also spelled Djenne) is a town and an urban commune in the Inland Niger Delta region of central Mali. The town is the administrative centre of the Djenné Cercle, one of the eight subdivisions of the Mopti Region. Historically, the town was a centre of trade and learning, linked for centuries with Timbuktu across the Niger River, and it is famous for its distinctive mud-brick (Banco) architecture.

History

The original settlement, known as Djenné-Jeno, was founded around 250 BC and is one of the oldest known cities in sub-Saharan Africa; it flourished as a terminus of trans-Saharan trade in gold, salt and slaves. The present town was founded nearby around the 13th century. Under the Mali and Songhai empires it became a major commercial and scholarly centre, and later passed through Bambara, Fulani and French colonial rule.

Great Mosque

The Great Mosque of Djenné is the largest mud-brick building in the world and the finest example of Sudano-Sahelian architecture. The current structure, built in 1907 on the site of earlier mosques, is replastered annually in a communal festival, the Crépissage. The old town, including the mosque, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1988 under the name "Old Towns of Djenné".

Economy

Djenné's weekly Monday market, held in the square in front of the Great Mosque, draws traders and visitors from across the region. Fishing in the Niger and Bani rivers, agriculture in the floodplain, and tourism are the main economic activities.
