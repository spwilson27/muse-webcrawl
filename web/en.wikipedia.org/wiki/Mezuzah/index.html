A mezuzah (Hebrew: מְזוּזָה, "doorpost"; plural mezuzot) is a piece of parchment inscribed with specific Hebrew verses from the Torah, which Jews fix to the doorposts of their homes in fulfilment of the commandments in Deuteronomy: "And you shall write them upon the doorposts of your house and upon your gates."

The scroll

The parchment (klaf) is prepared by a qualified scribe (sofer) who writes, in black ink with a quill, the two passages containing the Shema — Deuteronomy 6:4–9 and 11:13–21 — in 22 lines of Ashurit script. On the reverse the scribe writes the divine name Shaddai (שדי), an acronym for "Guardian of the doors of Israel". A single letter error invalidates the scroll, so mezuzot are traditionally checked by an expert twice every seven years.

Affixing

The scroll is rolled, placed in a protective case, and fixed to the right-hand doorpost (as one enters), in the upper third of the frame, with a blessing recited. Ashkenazi custom slants the mezuzah inward at the top; Sephardi custom places it vertically. It is attached to the doors of nearly all rooms of a Jewish home (bathrooms and small closets excepted), and many Jews touch it and kiss their fingers on passing.

Interpretation

Beyond the literal commandment, the mezuzah is understood as a reminder of God's presence and commandments when leaving and entering, and has traditionally been regarded as a symbol of Jewish identity and a source of protection for the household. Karaite Jews and Samaritans interpret the commandment differently, in the Samaritan case inscribing the Ten Commandments on stone at the doorway.
