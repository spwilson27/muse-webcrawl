Twelver Shi'a Islam

Adherentsc. 150–200 million worldwide
Main regionsIran, Iraq, Azerbaijan, Bahrain, Lebanon
Language of textsArabic, Persian

Twelver Shi'ism (Arabic: الاثنا عشرية, al-Ithnā ʿAshariyya) is the largest branch of Shia Islam, comprising roughly 85% of all Shias. Twelvers believe in twelve divinely ordained leaders, the Twelve Imams, beginning with Ali ibn Abi Talib and ending with Muhammad al-Mahdi.

Doctrine

Central to Twelver belief is the doctrine of the Imamate: the Imams are spiritual and political successors to the Prophet Muhammad, infallible and divinely guided. The twelfth Imam, al-Mahdi, is believed to have entered occultation (ghayba) in the 9th century and to be alive, hidden, and destined to return as the Mahdi before the Day of Judgment. Twelver theology also emphasises divine justice and human free will.

Practice and scholarship

Twelver religious life centres on the Quran, the hadith of the Prophet and the Imams, and the guidance of jurists (mujtahids) followed through taqlid. Major centres of scholarship are Qom in Iran and Najaf in Iraq. Mourning rituals for Husayn ibn Ali, especially during Muharram and Ashura, are prominent. In Iran, the doctrine of the Guardianship of the Jurist (velayat-e faqih) underpins the constitution of the Islamic Republic.
