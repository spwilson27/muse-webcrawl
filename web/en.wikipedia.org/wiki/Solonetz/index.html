Solonetz

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Solonetz (from Ukrainian solonets, ultimately from sil, "salt") is a Reference Soil Group of the World Reference Base for Soil Resources (WRB). Solonetz soils are characterised by a subsurface horizon rich in sodium and clay — a natric horizon — with columnar or prismatic structure, high pH, and poor physical properties for agriculture. They occur mainly in semi-arid and arid regions with level topography, including the steppes of Ukraine, southern Russia and Kazakhstan, the Hungarian puszta, the Canadian prairies, and parts of Australia, Argentina, and South Africa.

Formation and properties

Solonetz typically forms when a saline soil (solonchak) is leached: as soluble salts are washed downward, sodium ions occupy a large share of the cation exchange complex. The dispersed clay is translocated into the subsoil, building a dense, slowly permeable natric horizon with distinctive rounded column tops. Exchangeable sodium percentages above 15 and pH values commonly between 8.5 and 10 make the soil alkaline and hostile to many plants; when wet the subsoil swells and becomes nearly impermeable, and when dry it hardens and cracks.

Use and reclamation

Untreated solonetz supports only salt- and sodium-tolerant pasture. Reclamation centres on replacing exchangeable sodium with calcium — usually by applying gypsum — followed by leaching with irrigation water and improving drainage. Deep ploughing and organic amendments can also help. In dryland regions many solonetz soils are used for grazing or left as natural steppe.
