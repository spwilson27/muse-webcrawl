Mycolic acids are long-chain fatty acids found in the cell walls of the Mycolata taxon of bacteria, including Mycobacterium tuberculosis. They form a waxy, hydrophobic outer layer that makes mycobacteria resistant to many antibiotics, disinfectants, and the host immune response.

Structure

Mycolic acids are alpha-alkyl, beta-hydroxy fatty acids with total chain lengths of up to about 90 carbon atoms in M. tuberculosis. They are covalently attached to arabinogalactan in the cell wall and also occur as trehalose dimycolate ("cord factor").

Medical significance

The mycolic acid layer is responsible for the acid-fast staining property used to diagnose tuberculosis. Drugs such as isoniazid act by inhibiting mycolic acid synthesis, and the acids are targets of ongoing vaccine and drug research.
