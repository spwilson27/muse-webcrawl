Indole

Indole is an aromatic, heterocyclic organic compound with the formula C8H7N. It has a bicyclic structure, consisting of a six-membered benzene ring fused to a five-membered pyrrole ring. Indole occurs widely in nature and is produced by a variety of bacteria; it is also the core structure of many important biomolecules and pharmaceuticals.

Occurrence and properties

Indole is a solid at room temperature with a paradoxical odour: faecal at high concentrations but floral — like jasmine and orange blossom — at low concentrations, where it is used in perfumery. It is formed in the intestine by bacterial degradation of the amino acid tryptophan.

Biological significance

The indole ring is the defining feature of the amino acid tryptophan, the neurotransmitter serotonin, the hormone melatonin and the plant hormone indole-3-acetic acid (auxin). Numerous alkaloids, including strychnine and LSD, contain indole-derived structures, and the indole scaffold is common in drugs such as the triptan migraine medicines.

Synthesis

Classical syntheses include the Fischer indole synthesis (1883), which condenses aryl hydrazines with carbonyl compounds, and the Madelung and Leimgruber–Batcho methods. Industrially, indole is obtained from coal tar.
