The Lancia Stratos HF (Tipo 829), known as the Lancia Stratos, is a rear mid-engined sports car and rally car made by Italian manufacturer Lancia. Designed by Marcello Gandini at Bertone from the radical Stratos Zero concept of 1970, it was the first car designed from scratch specifically for rally competition, and it powered Lancia to three consecutive World Rally Championship manufacturers' titles in 1974, 1975 and 1976.

Design

The Stratos has an extremely short wheelbase, a steel monocoque centre section with front and rear subframes, and fibreglass body panels that hinge open for rapid servicing. Its wrap-around windscreen gives helicopter-like visibility. Power comes from the Ferrari Dino's 2.4-litre V6, producing about 190 hp in road (Stradale) trim and up to around 280–320 hp in competition form; a turbocharged Group 5 version contested circuit races such as the Giro d'Italia automobilistico.

Competition

In the hands of drivers such as Sandro Munari, Björn Waldegård and Bernard Darniche, the Stratos won the Monte Carlo Rally three times among many major victories. Only 492 road and competition cars were built, the minimum required for Group 4 homologation. A modern reinterpretation, the New Stratos, was produced in a very small series by Manifattura Automobili Torino from 2018, based on the Ferrari F430 Scuderia.
