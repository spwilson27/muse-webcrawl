The American Palladium Eagle is a palladium bullion coin issued by the United States Mint. Authorised by the American Eagle Palladium Bullion Coin Act of 2010, the first coins were released in 2017, making it the first palladium coin issued by the United States and the first new metal added to the American Eagle bullion programme since the platinum coin of 1997.

Design

The obverse reproduces Adolph A. Weinman's Winged Liberty design from the Mercury dime of 1916, showing Liberty wearing a winged cap. The reverse features a high-relief eagle adapted from Weinman's 1907 medal design for the American Institute of Architects. Each coin contains one troy ounce of 99.95% pure palladium and has a face value of 25 dollars.

Production

The bullion version debuted in September 2017 and sold out quickly; proof and uncirculated collector versions followed in later years, with finishes rotating among the Mint's facilities. Mintages have been modest compared with the gold and silver Eagles, reflecting the smaller palladium market and the high price of the metal.

Background

The authorising legislation required the palladium to be sourced from United States mines where possible; the Stillwater Mine in Montana, then the only American palladium mine, supplied metal for the programme. Feasibility and marketing studies mandated by the Act preceded the first issue by several years.
