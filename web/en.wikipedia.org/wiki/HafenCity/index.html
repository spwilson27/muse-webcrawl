HafenCity (German: HafenCity Hamburg) is a quarter (Stadtteil) of Hamburg, Germany, located in the Hamburg-Mitte borough. It is one of the largest urban redevelopment projects in Europe, built on the site of the former free port area along the northern bank of the River Elbe, immediately south of the historic Speicherstadt warehouse district.

History

The idea of converting the old harbour basins south of the Speicherstadt into a new inner-city district emerged in the 1990s. In 1997, Hamburg's mayor Henning Voscherau publicly announced plans for the project, and the master plan by the Dutch-German team Kees Christiaanse / ASTOC was adopted in 2000. Because the area lay within the customs-free port zone, the free port boundary had to be redrawn before construction could begin.

Development

Development is managed by HafenCity Hamburg GmbH, a city-owned company. The district covers roughly 157 hectares and, when complete, is expected to house around 15,000 residents and provide workplaces for up to 45,000 people, along with hotels, shops, schools, a university and cultural institutions. The land is raised to about 8–9 metres above sea level on artificial mounds (Warften) to protect against storm surges, while quays and promenades remain at the historic lower level.

Landmarks

The district's best-known landmark is the Elbphilharmonie concert hall, opened in January 2017, built atop the Kaispeicher A warehouse at the western tip of HafenCity. Other notable elements include the Marco Polo Tower, the Unilever House, the Hamburg Maritime Museum in the nearby Speicherstadt, HafenCity University, and a series of new bridges and squares designed to link the district with the old town.

Transport

HafenCity is served by the U4 underground line of the Hamburg U-Bahn, with stations at Überseequartier and HafenCity Universität, as well as by bus lines, ferries on the Elbe, and a dense network of pedestrian and cycle routes.
