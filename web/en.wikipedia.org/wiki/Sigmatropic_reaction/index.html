Sigmatropic reaction

In organic chemistry, a sigmatropic reaction is a pericyclic reaction in which a σ (sigma) bond migrates across a conjugated π system, accompanied by a reorganisation of the π bonds, with no atoms gained or lost. The migrating group (often hydrogen or a carbon group) detaches from one atom and attaches to another while the overall number of σ and π bonds is preserved. Sigmatropic rearrangements are classified with the notation [i,j], counting the atoms over which each end of the breaking bond travels, and they are governed by the Woodward–Hoffmann rules for concerted pericyclic processes.

Notation and mechanism

The order [i,j] is assigned by numbering, from the original σ bond, along each fragment to the atom where the new bond forms. A [1,5]-hydrogen shift, for example, moves a hydrogen across a five-atom chain: the H–C bond breaks at position 1 and a new H–C bond forms at position 5. Thermal reactions proceed suprafacially when the total electron count is of the form 4n+2 (as in [1,5]-H shifts and [3,3] shifts); [1,3] shifts, with 4 electrons, would require geometrically difficult antarafacial geometry and are generally not observed thermally.

Important examples

  • [3,3]-Sigmatropic rearrangements — the most useful class. The Cope rearrangement converts one 1,5-diene into another; its oxygen variant, the oxy-Cope rearrangement (especially the anionic oxy-Cope, accelerated by many orders of magnitude), and the Claisen rearrangement of allyl vinyl ethers to γ,δ-unsaturated carbonyl compounds are workhorse reactions in synthesis. Related variants include the aza-Claisen, Ireland–Claisen (of silyl ketene acetals) and Johnson–Claisen rearrangements.
  • [2,3]-Sigmatropic rearrangements — such as the Wittig [2,3] rearrangement of allylic ethers and the sulfoxide (Mislow–Evans) rearrangement, proceed through five-membered envelope-like transition states with high stereocontrol.
  • [1,5]-Hydrogen shifts — rapid and common in cyclopentadienes, which scramble their substituents, and in trienes.
  • [1,7]-Hydrogen shifts — occur antarafacially in trienes; the photochemical [1,7]-H shift in the synthesis of vitamin D in the skin (previtamin D) is a famous biological example.

Because the reactions are concerted and proceed through ordered chair-like or boat-like transition states, sigmatropic rearrangements transfer stereochemical information predictably and are heavily used in total synthesis of natural products.
