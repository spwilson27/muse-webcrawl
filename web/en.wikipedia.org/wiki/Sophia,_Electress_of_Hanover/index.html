Sophia, Electress of Hanover

Sophia of the Palatinate
Born: 14 October 1630, The Hague, Dutch Republic
Died: 8 June 1714, Herrenhausen, Hanover
Spouse: Ernest Augustus, Elector of Hanover
Issue: George I of Great Britain; Sophia Charlotte, Queen in Prussia

Sophia, Electress of Hanover (born Princess Sophia of the Palatinate; 14 October 1630 – 8 June 1714) was the Electress of Hanover from 1692 to 1698 and, through the Act of Settlement 1701, the designated heir presumptive to the thrones of England, Scotland and Ireland. Her son succeeded as George I, founding the Hanoverian line of British monarchs.

Life

Sophia was the twelfth child of Frederick V, Elector Palatine (the "Winter King" of Bohemia), and Elizabeth Stuart, daughter of James VI and I. Born in exile at The Hague, she married Ernest Augustus of Brunswick-Lüneburg in 1658; he became the first Elector of Hanover in 1692. Their court at Herrenhausen was a centre of learning — Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz was a close friend and correspondent.

Succession

As the senior surviving Protestant descendant of James I, Sophia was named heir by the English Act of Settlement 1701, which excluded Catholic claimants. She died on 8 June 1714, weeks before Queen Anne (1 August 1714), so the crown passed directly to her son George I. Under the Act, all British monarchs since have descended from her, and her legitimate Protestant descendants remain the basis of the succession.
