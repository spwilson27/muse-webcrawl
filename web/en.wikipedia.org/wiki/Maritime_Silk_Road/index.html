Maritime Silk Road

Maritime Silk Road
RegionSoutheast Asia, Indian Ocean, East Africa, Middle East
Period2nd century BCE – 15th century CE (peak under Song and Yuan China)
Major portsGuangzhou, Quanzhou, Malacca, Calicut, Hormuz, Aden, Alexandria
Key goodsSilk, porcelain, spices, tea, glassware, ivory

The Maritime Silk Road or Maritime Silk Route is the maritime section of the historic Silk Road network that connected China with Southeast Asia, the Indian subcontinent, the Arabian Peninsula, East Africa and, via the Red Sea and Mediterranean, Europe. Unlike the better-known overland routes across Central Asia, the sea routes carried the bulk of trade by volume for much of their history.

Routes

Ships sailed from Chinese ports such as Guangzhou and Quanzhou (the latter known to medieval travellers as Zaiton) through the South China Sea, past Vietnam and the Malay Peninsula, through the Strait of Malacca, and across the Indian Ocean to Sri Lanka and the Malabar Coast of India. From there, Arab, Persian and Indian traders carried goods to the Persian Gulf, the Red Sea ports of Arabia and Egypt, and onward to the Mediterranean. Seasonal monsoon winds governed the sailing calendar, dictating when fleets could move in each direction.

Trade and exchange

The route moved silk, ceramics and later tea westward, and spices, ivory, glassware, incense and precious stones eastward. It was also a channel for the movement of religions — Buddhism, Hinduism and later Islam spread along it — as well as technologies, crops and artistic styles. Chinese junks, Arab dhows and Indian vessels all plied parts of the network, exchanging cargoes at entrepôts such as Malacca and Calicut.

Later history

The voyages of the Ming admiral Zheng He (1405–1433) marked a spectacular state-sponsored high point, after which Ming China turned away from official maritime expeditions. Trade continued through private merchants and was transformed after 1498 by the arrival of European powers sailing around Africa. In the twenty-first century, China revived the name in its "21st Century Maritime Silk Road", one component of the Belt and Road Initiative announced in 2013.
