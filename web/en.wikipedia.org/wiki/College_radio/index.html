College radio is radio broadcasting by students at colleges and universities. Stations are typically licensed as non-commercial educational broadcasters and are run largely by student volunteers, with programming that ranges across freeform music, campus news, sports coverage and talk. Because they are not driven by advertising ratings, college stations historically championed alternative, indie and underground music that commercial radio ignored.

History

Experimental student broadcasting began in the 1920s; stations such as Union College's in Schenectady were among the earliest in the United States. In the 1960s the FCC created low-power Class D licences that let many colleges start FM stations cheaply. During the 1980s, college radio became a key tastemaker for alternative rock, helping launch acts such as R.E.M. and the Pixies, and trade charts like those of the CMJ New Music Report tracked college playlists nationally.

Role and challenges

College stations provide training in broadcasting and a platform for local musicians and diverse voices. Many now also stream online. Since the 2000s some universities have sold their stations' licences — often valuable FM frequencies — to public radio networks, prompting debate about the loss of student media and local programming.
