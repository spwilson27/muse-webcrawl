Arita (有田町, Arita-chō) is a town located in Nishimatsuura District, Saga Prefecture, Japan. It is famous as the birthplace of Japanese porcelain, known as Arita ware (有田焼, Arita-yaki), and its ceramics tradition dates back more than 400 years.

History

Porcelain production in Arita began in the late 16th century, when Korean potters were brought to Japan during the invasions of Korea under Toyotomi Hideyoshi. One of these potters, Yi Sam-pyeong (also known as Kanagae Sanbee), is credited with discovering kaolin deposits at Mount Izumi in Arita around 1616, enabling the first production of porcelain in Japan.

During the Edo period, Arita porcelain — often called Imari ware after the nearby port of Imari from which it was shipped — was exported to Europe by the Dutch East India Company, where it became highly prized. Styles such as Kakiemon, with its enamel overglaze decoration, influenced European porcelain makers at Meissen and elsewhere.

Geography

Arita lies in the western part of Saga Prefecture, near the border with Nagasaki Prefecture. The town is surrounded by mountains, and its kilns were historically sited along valleys where clay, water and firewood were available.

Culture and tourism

The town preserves numerous historic kiln sites, potteries and the famous Tonbai walls built from discarded kiln bricks. The Arita Ceramic Fair (Arita Tōki-ichi), held each year during Golden Week, attracts large numbers of visitors. The Kyushu Ceramic Museum in Arita houses an extensive collection of regional ceramics.
