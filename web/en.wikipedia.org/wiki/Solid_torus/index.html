Solid torus

In mathematics, a solid torus is the topological space formed by sweeping a disk around a circle, or equivalently the product space of a two-dimensional disk and a circle: D² × S¹. It is bounded by a torus surface. Intuitively, it is a torus "filled in" — like a bagel or doughnut including its interior, or an inner tube with the air included. By contrast, the ordinary torus is only the surface.

A solid torus: the product of a disk with a circle, bounded by a torus.

Topological properties

The solid torus is a compact, connected 3-manifold with boundary. Its fundamental group is infinite cyclic, π1 = ℤ, since it deformation-retracts onto its core circle. It is a standard building block in 3-manifold topology: gluing two solid tori together along their boundary surfaces produces the closed lens spaces (and, for particular gluings, S² × S¹ or the 3-sphere). This gluing construction, and the way a knot complement can be completed by attaching a solid torus (Dehn surgery), are central tools in the study of knots and 3-manifolds.

A neighbourhood of an embedded circle in a 3-manifold is a solid torus, which is why solid tori appear naturally as tubular neighbourhoods of knots and links. A standard, "unknotted" embedding of the solid torus in ℝ³ or S³ has as its complement another solid torus; the common boundary is the standard torus, and the decomposition of the 3-sphere into two solid tori is the simplest Heegaard splitting.

Geometry

Taken as a solid of revolution, a solid torus generated by rotating a disk of radius r whose centre is at distance R from the axis has volume 2π²Rr², by Pappus's centroid theorem. Cutting a solid torus open along a meridional disk yields a cylinder-like solid; loops that wind around the core (longitudinal) cannot be contracted, whereas meridional loops bound disks in the solid.
