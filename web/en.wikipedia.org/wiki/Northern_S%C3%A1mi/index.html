Northern Sámi

Northern Sámi (Northern Sami: davvisámegiella) is the most widely spoken of the Sámi languages, with roughly 20,000 speakers in the northern parts of Norway, Sweden, and Finland. It belongs to the western group of Sámi languages within the Uralic family.

Status and use

Northern Sámi has official status in a number of municipalities in Norway's administrative area for the Sámi language, and it is used in education, broadcasting (notably NRK Sápmi), and publishing. Its modern orthography, written in an extended Latin alphabet with letters such as á, č, đ, ŋ, š, ŧ, and ž, was standardised in 1979 across the three countries.

Linguistic features

Like other Sámi languages, Northern Sámi is agglutinative, marks nouns with a rich case system, distinguishes singular, dual, and plural in pronouns and verbs, and exhibits consonant gradation — systematic alternation of consonant strength in word stems. Dialects fall into three main groups: Torne, Finnmark, and Sea Sámi.
