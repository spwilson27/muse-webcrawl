Torger Christian "Toto" Wolff (born 12 January 1972) is an Austrian billionaire investor and motorsport executive. He is the team principal and chief executive officer of the Mercedes-AMG Petronas Formula One Team, and holds a 33% stake in the team.

Early life and racing

Wolff was born in Vienna to a Polish mother and a Romanian father. He began his career in motorsport as a racing driver, competing in the Austrian and German Formula Ford championships and winning his class at the 1994 24 Hours Nürburgring. A serious crash at the Nordschleife in 2009, while attempting a lap record, left him with lasting injuries, though he recovered fully enough to continue business life.

Business career

Wolff founded the investment companies Marchfifteen (1998) and Marchsixteen (2004), focusing on technology and industrial investments. He invested in the Williams Formula One team and joined its board, becoming executive director. In 2013 he moved to Mercedes, becoming executive director and acquiring a 30% stake (later 33%) in the Mercedes F1 team, alongside Niki Lauda (10%) and Daimler.

Mercedes team principal

Under Wolff's leadership, Mercedes dominated Formula One's hybrid era, winning eight consecutive Constructors' Championships (2014–2021) and seven consecutive Drivers' Championships (Lewis Hamilton 2014, 2015, 2017–2020; Nico Rosberg 2016) — an unprecedented run of success. Wolff is known for an intense, data-driven management style and his prominent media profile, heightened by the Netflix series Drive to Survive.

His wife, Susie Wolff, is a former racing driver and served as managing director of the F1 Academy and previously CEO of the Venturi Formula E team.
