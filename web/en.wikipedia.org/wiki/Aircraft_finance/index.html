Aircraft finance

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Airline fleets represent enormous capital commitments

Aircraft finance refers to the specialised financing structures used to acquire commercial aircraft. Because new airliners cost tens to hundreds of millions of dollars, airlines rarely pay cash; the industry relies on a mix of debt, leasing, export credit and capital-markets instruments.

Main methods

  • Leasing — airlines lease a large share of the world fleet from lessors such as AerCap. Operating leases keep aircraft off the airline's balance sheet; finance leases approximate ownership
  • Secured loans and mortgages — bank debt secured on the airframe
  • Export credit agency (ECA) financing — government-backed loans supporting Airbus and Boeing exports
  • EETCs — Enhanced Equipment Trust Certificates sold to capital-market investors
  • Islamic and Japanese structures — including Japanese operating leases (JOL), used for tax and investor reasons
  • Sale and leaseback — an airline sells delivered aircraft to a lessor and leases them back, releasing capital

Regulation and registration

The 2001 Cape Town Convention created an international registry of interests in aircraft, reducing lender risk and borrowing costs. Residual value risk, maintenance status and jurisdictional repossession rights are central considerations.

Market

Annual delivery financing needs are on the order of $100 billion; the balance among banks, lessors, ECAs and capital markets shifts with credit conditions, as during the 2008 financial crisis and the COVID-19 pandemic.
