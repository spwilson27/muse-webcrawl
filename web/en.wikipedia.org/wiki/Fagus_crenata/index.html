Fagus crenata

Fagus crenata, known as the Siebold's beech, Japanese beech, or buna, is a deciduous tree of the beech genus, Fagus, of the family Fagaceae. It is native to Japan, where it is widespread in primeval forests and forms one of the dominant trees of Japan's cool temperate forests, including the Shirakami-Sanchi UNESCO World Heritage site.

FamilyFagaceae
GenusFagus
SpeciesF. crenata
Native rangeJapan (Hokkaido to Kyushu)

Description

It reaches 35 metres in height. The crown is rounded and the bark is smooth and grey. The simple leaves are alternate, broadly ovate, with 7–11 pairs of veins and a crenate margin. The nuts, called buna nuts, ripen in autumn and feed many forest animals.

Ecology and uses

Japanese beech forests are noted for heavy winter snow and for masting cycles that support bears, birds, and small mammals. The wood is used for furniture, utensils, and traditional crafts, and the species is often planted as an ornamental tree.
