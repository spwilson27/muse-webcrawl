Macrotermitinae

The Macrotermitinae are a subfamily of termites in the family Termitidae, notable for their cultivation of fungi. Genera such as Macrotermes, Odontotermes and Microtermes grow the fungus Termitomyces on combs of chewed plant material inside their nests, in one of the most striking examples of symbiosis and agriculture outside human society.

Fungus growing

Worker termites collect dead plant matter, pass it through their guts, and deposit it as a spongy comb. The Termitomyces fungus grows on the comb, breaking down lignin and cellulose into food the termites can digest. The termites then eat both the fungus and the comb material, achieving an efficient external digestive system.

Mounds

Macrotermes species build some of the largest insect structures on Earth: cathedral mounds in African savannas that can exceed eight metres in height. The mounds' complex architecture regulates temperature and gas exchange for the colony and its fungus gardens below ground.

Distribution

Macrotermitinae are found across sub-Saharan Africa and southern and Southeast Asia, where they are major decomposers and ecosystem engineers, strongly influencing soil structure and nutrient cycling.
