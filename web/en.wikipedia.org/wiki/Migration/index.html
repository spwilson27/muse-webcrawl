Migration

Migration most commonly refers to human migration — the movement of people from one place to another with intentions of settling, permanently or temporarily, at a new location. The term also covers animal migration and other senses.

TypesInternal, international, forced, voluntary
TermsEmigrant, immigrant, refugee

Human migration

People migrate for work, education, family, safety and environment. Push and pull factors shape flows, from historic movements of Indigenous peoples to modern labour migration. International migrants numbered about 281 million in 2020.

Animal migration

Many species migrate seasonally — birds, wildebeest, salmon, monarch butterflies — to breed or find food. See also Pastoralism for nomadic movement with herds, and Marcus Garvey for the Back-to-Africa movement.
