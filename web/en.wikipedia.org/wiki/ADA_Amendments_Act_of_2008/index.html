The ADAAA broadened the definition of disability under the Americans with Disabilities Act.

The ADA Amendments Act of 2008 (ADAAA) is an Act of Congress, enacted September 25, 2008, and effective January 1, 2009, that amended the Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990 (ADA) and other disability nondiscrimination laws. It broadened the definition of "disability" and rejected several United States Supreme Court decisions that Congress viewed as having improperly narrowed the ADA's coverage.

Background

In decisions such as Sutton v. United Air Lines (1999) and Toyota Motor Manufacturing, Kentucky, Inc. v. Williams (2002), the Supreme Court held that impairments must be assessed in their mitigated state (for example, with medication or corrective lenses) and that "substantially limits" required a demanding standard. Disability advocates and members of Congress argued these rulings excluded people Congress intended to protect, such as individuals with epilepsy, diabetes, cancer, and multiple sclerosis.

Provisions

The ADAAA provides that the definition of disability "shall be construed in favor of broad coverage." It states that an impairment that is episodic or in remission is a disability if it would substantially limit a major life activity when active; that mitigating measures (other than ordinary eyeglasses or contact lenses) must not be considered; and it expanded the list of major life activities to include major bodily functions. It also eased the standard for the "regarded as" prong of the disability definition.

Implementation

The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission issued final regulations implementing the ADAAA in 2011, confirming that conditions such as cancer, diabetes, epilepsy, HIV infection, and major depressive disorder will ordinarily qualify as disabilities. The amendments apply to employment (Title I), public services (Title II), and public accommodations (Title III) under the ADA.
