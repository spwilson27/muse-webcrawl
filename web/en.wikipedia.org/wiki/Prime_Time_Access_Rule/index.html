Prime Time Access Rule

The Prime Time Access Rule (PTAR) was a broadcasting regulation instituted in the United States by the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) in 1970 and repealed in 1996. It restricted the amount of network programming that television stations in the top 50 markets could air during prime time.

Provisions

Effective from the autumn of 1971, the rule barred network affiliates in the largest markets from broadcasting more than three hours of network prime-time programming per night, freeing the 7:00–8:00 p.m. Eastern "access" hour for local or syndicated programming. Its goals were to increase diversity of program sources, encourage local production, and limit the dominance of the three major networks.

Effects

The access hour became home to syndicated game shows such as Wheel of Fortune and Jeopardy!, entertainment news programs like Entertainment Tonight, and off-network situation comedy reruns. The rule, together with the related financial interest and syndication rules, reshaped the economics of American television production.

Repeal

With the rise of cable television and new networks, the FCC concluded the rule no longer served its purpose and repealed it, effective in 1996, though most stations continued similar scheduling patterns by habit and economics.
