Fried chicken restaurant

A fried chicken restaurant is a fast food restaurant, often a chain, that specializes in fried chicken, usually pieces of chicken that have been floured or battered and then pan-fried, deep fried, or pressure fried. Originating in the American South, the format was popularized internationally from the 1950s onward by franchises such as KFC, founded by Harland Sanders, and copied by numerous competitors.

History and chains

KFC began franchising in 1952 and became one of the first American fast-food chains to expand internationally. Other major chains include Popeyes (founded 1972 in Louisiana, known for spicy chicken), Church's Chicken, Bojangles, Chick-fil-A (known for chicken sandwiches), and Jollibee in the Philippines. Side dishes typically include fries, coleslaw, mashed potatoes, biscuits and corn.
