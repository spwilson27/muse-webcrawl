Haredevil Hare is a 1948 Looney Tunes cartoon directed by Chuck Jones and written by Michael Maltese. The short stars Bugs Bunny and is notable as the debut of Marvin the Martian (unnamed in this film, billed simply as "Commander, Flying Saucer X-2") and his dog, K-9. It is one of the earliest cartoons to use a rocket trip to the Moon as its premise.

Plot

Scientists lure a reluctant Bugs Bunny into a rocket with carrots and blast him off to the Moon, where newspapers hail the "first" — actually unwilling — rabbit in space. On landing, Bugs encounters a diminutive Martian in a Roman-style helmet and sneakers, who is preparing to blow up the Earth with his "Illudium Q-36 Explosive Space Modulator" because it obstructs his view of Venus. Bugs steals the modulator, and in the ensuing chase K-9, the Martian's green dog, retrieves it. Bugs manipulates the two against each other and recovers the device, ending the cartoon hanging from the cratered Moon, calling for help — answered only by the audience's laughter.

Production and legacy

Mel Blanc provides all the voices; Marvin's distinctive, soft-spoken, nasal delivery was developed for this cartoon. The space-modulator gag and Marvin himself became fixtures of later Jones cartoons, beginning with The Hasty Hare (1952), and Marvin went on to become one of the most merchandised Looney Tunes characters. The cartoon's rocket, styled after contemporary science-fiction illustration, and its topical references to post-war rocketry give it a distinctly 1948 flavour.
