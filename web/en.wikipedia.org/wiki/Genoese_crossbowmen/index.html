Genoese crossbowmen

The Genoese crossbowmen (Italian: Balestrieri genovesi) were a famous corps of professional soldiers from the Republic of Genoa, renowned throughout medieval Europe for their skill with the crossbow. They served as mercenaries in many armies between the 11th and 16th centuries and were considered among the finest missile troops of their age.

Equipment and tactics

A Genoese crossbowman typically carried a powerful composite crossbow, spanned with a belt hook or windlass, and fought as part of a team that included a shield-bearer holding a large pavise — a tall shield, often painted with the red cross of Genoa or the arms of an employer — behind which the crossbowman could shelter while reloading. This combination of firepower and protection made them effective in sieges, naval warfare and pitched battles.

Service and battles

Genoese crossbowmen fought in the Crusades, in the fleets and armies of Genoa itself, and in the pay of France, Spain, Milan and other powers. They are best remembered for their role at the Battle of Crécy (1346), where, serving in the French army, they were sent forward tired and without their pavises (which were still with the baggage) and were outshot by English longbowmen before being ridden down in confusion. Despite this famous defeat, demand for their services continued for two more centuries, until firearms displaced the crossbow.
