Shrovetide is the pre-Lenten season in the Western Christian calendar, comprising the days immediately before Ash Wednesday and culminating in Shrove Tuesday. The name comes from the English word shrive — to hear confession and grant absolution — reflecting the custom of confessing one's sins before the fasting and penance of Lent began.

Customs

Shrovetide combined religious preparation with feasting to use up rich foods — eggs, butter, milk and meat — forbidden or restricted during Lent. In Britain and Ireland this produced the tradition of Pancake Day on Shrove Tuesday, including pancake races (the one at Olney in Buckinghamshire is said to date from 1445) and "mob football" games such as the Royal Shrovetide Football played at Ashbourne in Derbyshire, in which whole towns compete to carry a ball to goals miles apart. In many countries Shrovetide merges with Carnival (Mardi Gras), with processions, masquerades and street parties; in Scandinavia, Fastelavn is marked with decorated birch branches and cream-filled buns (fastelavnsboller or semlor).

Liturgical character

Traditionally the season extended back through the Sundays of Septuagesima, Sexagesima and Quinquagesima, three weeks of gradual preparation for Lent; in the modern Roman calendar these pre-Lenten Sundays were folded into Ordinary Time after the Second Vatican Council, but Shrove Tuesday customs persist widely in both Catholic and Protestant cultures.
