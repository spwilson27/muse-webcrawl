Springfield, Vermont

County: Windsor
State: Vermont
Chartered: 1761
Population: about 9,000

Springfield is a town in Windsor County, Vermont, United States, situated along the Black River near the Connecticut River. Its population was about 9,000 at recent censuses. Chartered in 1761, Springfield became one of the centres of the American precision machine-tool industry, earning it a place in industrial history disproportionate to its size.

History

The falls of the Black River provided water power for gristmills and, in the nineteenth century, for machine shops. James Hartness, inventor and later governor of Vermont, made the Jones and Lamson Machine Company a leader in turret lathes and optical comparators; the town also hosted Fellows Gear Shaper Company and Bryant Chucking Grinder. During the Second World War Springfield's plants were vital to armaments production. The decline of New England manufacturing later in the twentieth century hit the town hard, and it has since worked to diversify its economy.

Attractions and culture

Springfield is home to the Hartness House, with its underground tunnel and Hartness's turret telescope; the Eureka Schoolhouse (1790), among Vermont's oldest one-room schools; and the Stellafane convention of amateur telescope makers held nearby since the 1920s. In 2007 Springfield won a national contest to host the premiere of The Simpsons Movie, competing as the "real" Springfield of the television series.

Geography

The town covers roughly 49 square miles in the Connecticut River valley of southeastern Vermont, bordering New Hampshire across the river. Vermont Route 11 and Interstate 91 serve the town.
