Medium (website)

Medium is an American online publishing platform developed by Evan Williams and launched in August 2012. It is owned by Medium Corporation (formerly A Medium Corporation). The platform hosts amateur and professional publications and is regularly regarded as a blog host. Williams, co-founder of Blogger and Twitter, developed Medium as a way to publish writings and documents longer than Twitter's character limit.

History

Medium launched in 2012 as part of Williams's company The Obvious Corporation. It initially emphasised long-form writing, clean typography, and collaborative editing features. Over time it introduced publications, a partner program (launched 2017) that pays writers based on reader engagement, and a subscription membership costing a monthly fee that gives readers access to paywalled articles while funding writers.

The company has undergone several strategic shifts, including layoffs in 2017 when it moved away from an advertising-based model toward subscriptions. Tony Stubblebine became CEO in 2022. Medium has also integrated with the fediverse, launching a Mastodon instance, and has taken positions on AI-generated content and content moderation.

Features

Writers publish stories with minimal formatting controls, organise them into publications, and receive responses and "claps" from readers. Readers follow authors and topics, and a recommendation system surfaces stories. Member-only stories are distributed through the Medium Partner Program, in which authors earn from the time paying members spend reading their work.

Reception

Medium has been praised for its readable design and for hosting prominent essays and company announcements, and criticised for inconsistent paywall strategies, content discoverability, and the quality mix of open publishing. Several well-known publications have started on, moved to, or left the platform over its history.
