Cook County, Illinois

Cook County is the most populous county in the U.S. state of Illinois and the second-most populous county in the United States after Los Angeles County, California. As of the 2020 census, its population was about 5.28 million. Its county seat is Chicago, the most populous city in Illinois and the third-most populous city in the United States.

Geography

Cook County lies in northeastern Illinois on the southwestern shore of Lake Michigan. It is the center of the Chicago metropolitan area and contains Chicago plus 134 municipalities. More than 40 percent of Illinois residents live in the county.

Government

The county is governed by a board of commissioners and an elected president. Cook County operates one of the largest unified court systems in the United States and a major public health system, including Stroger Hospital. The Forest Preserve District maintains tens of thousands of acres of open land.

History

The county was created in 1831 and named for Daniel Pope Cook, an early Illinois statesman and the state's first attorney general. Chicago was incorporated as a town in 1833 and as a city in 1837. The Great Chicago Fire of 1871 and the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition shaped the county's rapid growth.
