Benjamin "Bennie" Moten (13 November 1894 – 2 April 1935) was an American jazz pianist and bandleader. He led the Kansas City Orchestra, the most important of the territory bands of the American Midwest, and was a central figure in the development of the riff-based, blues-influenced style that became known as Kansas City jazz and later fed into swing.

Career

Moten was born and raised in Kansas City, Missouri. He studied piano and played in local groups before forming his own band around 1918. From 1923 his band recorded for Okeh Records, and from 1926 for Victor, cutting influential sides such as "South" (1928), which became a standard, and "Moten Swing" (1932), a tune later adapted by Count Basie as part of his repertoire.

By the early 1930s Moten had absorbed many of the finest musicians in Kansas City, including pianist Count Basie, tenor saxophonist Ben Webster, trumpeter Oran "Hot Lips" Page, singer Jimmy Rushing, and bassist Walter Page. The band's 1932 Victor recordings are regarded as a direct precursor of the classic Basie style.

Death and legacy

Moten died on 2 April 1935 in Kansas City following an unsuccessful tonsillectomy, undergoing surgery while on the road to recovery from illness. After his death, Count Basie took over much of the band's personnel, forming the group that became the Count Basie Orchestra, which carried the Kansas City sound to national fame. Moten was posthumously inducted into the Big Band and Jazz Hall of Fame.
