Tarsus (skeleton)

The tarsus is a cluster of seven articulating bones in each foot situated between the lower end of the tibia and fibula of the lower leg and the metatarsus. In tetrapods generally it is the corresponding set of bones in the hind limb or lower leg. The largest tarsal bone, the calcaneus, forms the heel and bears much of the body's weight.

The seven tarsal bones

The proximal row consists of the talus, which sits in the ankle mortise formed by the tibia and fibula and transmits weight from the leg to the foot, and the calcaneus below it, into which the Achilles tendon inserts. In front of these lie the navicular and cuboid, and the three cuneiform bones (medial, intermediate and lateral), which articulate with the metatarsals and help form the arches of the foot.

In other animals

Tarsals are found in all tetrapods. In birds, the tarsals are fused with neighbouring bones into the tibiotarsus and tarsometatarsus; in horses and other ungulates the tarsus corresponds to the hock joint.

Clinical significance

Tarsal fractures, especially of the calcaneus from falls from height, and conditions such as tarsal coalition (an abnormal fusion between tarsal bones) can cause significant foot pain and stiffness.
