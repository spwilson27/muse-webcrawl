Ardem Patapoutian (born 2 October 1967) is a Lebanese-American molecular biologist and neuroscientist. He is a professor at Scripps Research in La Jolla, California, and an investigator of the Howard Hughes Medical Institute. In 2021 he shared the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with David Julius for discoveries of receptors for temperature and touch.

Early life and education

Patapoutian was born in Beirut to an Armenian family — his father is the poet and writer Sarkis Vahakn — and studied at the American University of Beirut before emigrating to the United States in 1986, escaping the Lebanese Civil War. He received his bachelor's degree in cellular and developmental biology from the University of California, Los Angeles, in 1990 and his PhD in biology from the California Institute of Technology in 1996, working with Barbara Wold. After postdoctoral research at the University of California, San Francisco, he joined Novartis before moving to Scripps Research in 2000.

Research

Patapoutian's laboratory works on somatosensation — how physical stimuli such as pressure, temperature, and pain are detected. In 2010 his team identified Piezo1 and Piezo2, large mechanically activated ion channels that convert physical force into electrical signals; the name derives from the Greek piesi, "pressure". Piezo2 proved to be the principal sensor of light touch and proprioception, while Piezo1 serves in blood vessels, red blood cells, and other tissues, with mutations causing human diseases of blood volume and lymphatic development. His group has also characterised sensors involved in pain and temperature, including the menthol and cold receptor TRPM8 in earlier work on the TRP channel family.

Honours

Besides the Nobel Prize, Patapoutian has received the Rosenstiel Award, the Kavli Prize in Neuroscience (2020, shared), and the BBVA Foundation Frontiers of Knowledge Award, and is a member of the United States National Academy of Sciences.
