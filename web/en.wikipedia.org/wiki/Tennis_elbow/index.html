Tennis elbow

Tennis elbow, or lateral epicondylitis, is a condition in which the outer part of the elbow becomes painful and tender. The pain may extend into the forearm and wrist and is caused by overuse and degeneration of the tendons that attach at the lateral epicondyle of the humerus, particularly the extensor carpi radialis brevis.

Signs and causes

Symptoms include pain when gripping, shaking hands, or extending the wrist. Despite the name, most sufferers do not play tennis; it is common among workers doing repetitive arm motions, plumbers, painters, and other racket and manual occupations. Histology usually shows tendinosis — disorganised collagen without significant inflammation.

Treatment

Most cases resolve over months with activity modification, physiotherapy (including eccentric strengthening), braces, and analgesics. Corticosteroid injection gives short-term relief but may worsen long-term outcomes; surgery is reserved for persistent cases.
