Allylbenzene

Chemical formulaC9H10
Molar mass118.18 g/mol
AppearanceColourless liquid
Density0.893 g/cm³
Boiling point156 °C (313 °F)
IUPAC nameprop-2-en-1-ylbenzene

Allylbenzene is an organic compound with the formula C6H5CH2CH=CH2. It consists of a benzene ring attached to an allyl group. It is a colourless liquid and the parent member of the allylbenzene class of compounds found widely in plant essential oils.

Related compounds

Substituted allylbenzenes are important flavour and fragrance chemicals and plant metabolites, including eugenol (from cloves), safrole (from sassafras), estragole (from tarragon and basil), myristicin (from nutmeg) and apiol (from parsley). Many are biosynthesised via the phenylpropanoid pathway from phenylalanine. Allylbenzene itself is prepared in the laboratory and used as a synthetic intermediate; it readily isomerises to the conjugated propenylbenzene (β-methylstyrene) under basic or metallic catalysis.
