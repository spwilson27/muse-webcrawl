Gansu


The Zhangye Danxia landform, a noted landscape of Gansu.

Gansu (Chinese: 甘肃; pinyin: Gānsù) is a landlocked province in Northwest China. Its capital and largest city is Lanzhou, in the southeast part of the province. The seventh-largest administrative district by area at 453,700 square kilometres (175,200 sq mi), Gansu lies between the Tibetan and Loess plateaus and borders Mongolia, Inner Mongolia, Xinjiang, Qinghai, Sichuan, Shaanxi and Ningxia. The Yellow River passes through the southern part of the province.

History

The name Gansu is a compound of Gan (甘), from the Ganzhou prefecture near Zhangye, and Su (肃), from Suzhou, modern Jiuquan. The province was a key corridor of the Silk Road through the narrow Hexi Corridor, and sites such as the Mogao Caves at Dunhuang and the western end of the Great Wall at Jiayuguan preserve its heritage. The region was devastated by the 1920 Haiyuan earthquake and suffered heavily in the famine of 1928–1930.

Geography

Gansu is mostly mountainous and arid, ranging from the Gobi Desert in the north to the Qilian Mountains, whose snowmelt feeds the Hexi Corridor oases. The population is about 25 million, including significant Hui, Tibetan, Dongxiang and other minorities.

Economy

Historically one of China's poorest provinces, Gansu's economy rests on agriculture in the oases, mining (including nickel at Jinchang), petrochemicals around Lanzhou, and growing wind and solar power generation in the desert north-west.
