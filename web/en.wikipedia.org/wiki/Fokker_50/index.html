The Fokker 50 is a turboprop-powered regional airliner designed and produced by the Dutch aircraft manufacturer Fokker. It was developed as a successor to the highly successful Fokker F27 Friendship, retaining the basic airframe while incorporating new engines, avionics and systems.

Development

Fokker announced the Fokker 50 (initially the F27 Mark 050) in the early 1980s, alongside the jet-powered Fokker 100. The design replaced the F27's Rolls-Royce Dart engines with two Pratt & Whitney Canada PW125B turboprops driving six-bladed composite propellers, added an electronic flight instrument system (glass cockpit), and introduced numerous smaller refinements, including new windows and an improved interior. The prototype first flew on 28 December 1985, and certification followed in 1987.

Design

The Fokker 50 is a high-wing cantilever monoplane with a pressurised cabin seating 50 passengers in a four-abreast layout (extendable to 56 in high-density configuration), two pilots, and retractable tricycle landing gear. Its cruise speed is about 530 km/h (286 knots) and its typical range roughly 1,700 km.

Variants

  • Fokker 50 – standard passenger version.
  • Fokker 60 – stretched military utility version developed for the Royal Netherlands Air Force, with a large cargo door; four built.
  • Maritime patrol and freighter conversions of retired airframes.

Operational history

Production ended in 1997 when Fokker went bankrupt, after 213 aircraft had been built. Major operators included KLM Cityhopper, Lufthansa CityLine, Air Baltic, Skyways and VLM Airlines. Although diminishing in number, the type remains in service with smaller regional and charter operators, and support is provided by Fokker Services.
