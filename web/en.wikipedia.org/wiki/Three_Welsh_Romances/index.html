Three Welsh Romances

The Three Welsh Romances (Welsh: Y Tair Rhamant) are three Middle Welsh prose tales associated with the Mabinogion. They are:

  • Owain, or the Lady of the Fountain (Owain, neu Iarlles y Ffynnon)
  • Peredur son of Efrawg (Peredur fab Efrawg)
  • Geraint son of Erbin (Geraint fab Erbin)

All three are Arthurian romances, and survive in the White Book of Rhydderch (c. 1350) and the Red Book of Hergest (c. 1400). They correspond broadly to the romances of Chrétien de Troyes — Yvain, the Knight of the Lion, Perceval, the Story of the Grail, and Erec and Enide — and scholars have long debated the relationship between the Welsh and French versions: whether they derive from a common source, or whether the Welsh tales adapted Chrétien's poems (or vice versa).

The tales

Owain recounts how the knight Owain defends a magical fountain, wins the hand of the Lady of the Fountain, loses her through neglect, goes mad, and is healed and reconciled with the help of a lion he rescues. Peredur follows a naive youth raised in the wilderness who comes to Arthur's court, becomes a knight, and journeys through a series of wonders — including a procession bearing a bleeding lance and a salver with a severed head, an early Welsh analogue of the Grail procession in Chrétien's unfinished Perceval. Geraint tells of the knight Geraint and his wife Enid, whose marriage is strained by his suspicion and her obedience through a punishing quest, ending in reconciliation.

Scholarship

The romances are generally dated to the late 12th or 13th centuries. Their polished narrative style suggests professional storytellers (cyfarwyddiaid) reshaping older Welsh and wider Celtic tradition for courtly audiences familiar with French romance. They are usually published as part of the Mabinogion, most famously in Lady Charlotte Guest's translation.
