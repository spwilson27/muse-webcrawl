Brahmana

The Brahmanas (Sanskrit: ब्राह्मण) are Vedic śruti works attached to the Samhitas (the main collections of mantras of the four Vedas). They are mostly prose texts that explain the meaning of the hymns and provide detailed instructions for the performance of rituals and sacrifices (yajna), together with myths and legends illustrating their rationale.

Content

Each Veda has its own Brahmanas. Among the best-known are the Aitareya Brahmana of the Rigveda, the Shatapatha Brahmana of the Shukla Yajurveda, and the Jaiminiya and Kaushitaki Brahmanas. The Brahmanas are generally dated to roughly the early first millennium BCE.

Significance

The Brahmana texts mark a shift from the hymns of the Samhitas toward elaborate ritual exegesis, and they form a bridge to the Aranyakas and Upanishads, which turn toward philosophy and interiorized sacrifice.
