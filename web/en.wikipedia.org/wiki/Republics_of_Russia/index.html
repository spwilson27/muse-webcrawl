Republics of Russia

The republics of Russia are a type of federal subject of the Russian Federation, constitutionally described as states that have their own constitution, president or head, legislature, and official languages alongside Russian. There are 24 republics, most established for particular ethnic groups native to their territory.

Overview

Republics include Tatarstan, Bashkortostan, Sakha (Yakutia), Chechnya, Dagestan, and Karelia, among others. Unlike other federal subjects, republics may establish their own state languages. In practice, federal centralization since 2000 — including the harmonisation of regional laws and the abolition of direct elections of regional heads for a period — has narrowed their formal autonomy.

Origins

The republics descend from the autonomous soviet socialist republics (ASSRs) and autonomous oblasts of the USSR, which were elevated in status during the "parade of sovereignties" of 1990–1991 as the Soviet Union dissolved.
