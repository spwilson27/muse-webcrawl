Gir National Park and Wildlife Sanctuary, also known as Sasan Gir, is a forest and national park near Talala Gir in Gujarat, India. It is the only place in the world where the Asiatic lion is found in the wild, and is considered one of the most important protected areas in Asia because of its biodiversity.

History

The Gir forest was once the hunting ground of the Nawabs of Junagadh. Asiatic lion numbers had crashed to roughly a dozen animals by the early twentieth century owing to hunting and habitat loss; the Nawab of Junagadh banned hunting and afforded the lions protection. The area was declared a wildlife sanctuary in 1965, and the core zone became a national park in 1975. Under sustained conservation, the lion population recovered steadily; the 2020 lion census counted 674 lions in the greater Gir landscape.

Geography and flora

Gir lies in the Kathiawar peninsula of western Gujarat. The terrain is rugged hills, valleys and plateaus drained by perennial and seasonal rivers including the Hiran, Shetrunji, Datardi, Shingoda, Machhundri and Raval. The vegetation is primarily dry deciduous forest dominated by teak, intermixed with acacia, ber, jamun, flame of the forest and other species; it is the largest dry deciduous forest in western India.

Fauna

Besides Asiatic lions, Gir supports one of the largest leopard populations in India, and large numbers of chital, sambar, nilgai, four-horned antelope, chinkara, wild boar and langurs. Predators and scavengers include the striped hyena, golden jackal and Indian fox. Marsh crocodiles inhabit the Kamleshwar reservoir and rivers. The park records over 300 bird species, including the crested serpent eagle, brown fish owl and Indian pitta, and around 38 species of mammals, 37 of reptiles and more than 2,000 insect species.

People

The forest is home to Maldhari pastoralist communities, who live in settlements called nesses and graze buffalo and cattle in the sanctuary, coexisting with lions despite occasional livestock losses. Siddi communities of African origin also live in villages near the park.
