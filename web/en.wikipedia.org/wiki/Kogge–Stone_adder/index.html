Kogge–Stone adder

In computing, the Kogge–Stone adder (KSA) is a parallel prefix form of carry-lookahead adder. It generates carry signals in O(log n) time and is widely considered the standard form of adder used in high-performance processors.

Design

The adder computes generate and propagate signals for bit groups of doubling size (1, 2, 4, 8, ...) across log2(n) stages, so all carries are known after a logarithmic number of steps. This speed comes at the cost of greater area and fan-out than alternatives such as the Brent–Kung adder, which trades slightly more delay for a much smaller wiring footprint.

History

It is named after Peter M. Kogge and Harold S. Stone, who published the parallel algorithm in a 1973 paper on solving recurrence equations on parallel computers.
