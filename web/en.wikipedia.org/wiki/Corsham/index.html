Corsham

Corsham is a historic market town and civil parish in west Wiltshire, England. It is at the southwestern edge of the Cotswolds, just off the A4, about 28 miles (45 km) southwest of Swindon, 20 miles (32 km) southeast of Bristol and 8 miles (13 km) northeast of Bath. The parish had a population of roughly 13,000 at the 2021 census.

History

Corsham grew around the wool trade and quarrying of Bath stone from underground quarries in the area. Its High Street is lined with stone buildings of the 16th to 18th centuries, including the Flemish weavers' cottages and the Hungerford Almshouses (1668). Corsham Court, an Elizabethan house rebuilt in the 18th century, houses a noted collection of Old Master paintings.

Underground facilities

Former stone quarries around Corsham were taken over by the military in the 20th century. The Central Government War Headquarters, a vast Cold War bunker codenamed "Burlington", was built beneath the town to house the government in the event of nuclear war. The nearby MoD Corsham site is a major Ministry of Defence communications and IT centre, and RAF Rudloe Manor was associated with UFO lore in popular culture.
