House of the Dragon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A dragon soaring over King's Landing in a promotional still style image]
A dragon soaring over King's Landing in a promotional still style image

House of the Dragon is an American fantasy drama television series created by Ryan Condal and George R. R. Martin for HBO. A prequel to Game of Thrones (2011–2019), it adapts material from Martin's Fire & Blood (2018), chronicling the House Targaryen at its zenith and the succession war — the 'Dance of the Dragons' — that destroys it, beginning some 200 years before the original series. It premiered on 21 August 2022 to HBO's largest premiere audience, and won the Golden Globe for Best Drama Series for its first season.

Story

King Viserys I names his daughter Rhaenyra heir over the realm's expectations of a son; his later marriage to Alicent Hightower produces rival sons, and on his death the court splits into 'Black' and 'Green' factions whose contest — fought with dragons on both sides — consumes the family and the realm. The series is adapted as in-universe history, with the showrunners selecting among the book's conflicting accounts.

Production and reception

Filmed in Britain, Spain, Portugal and Croatia with extensive dragon effects, the first season earned praise for returning the franchise to court intrigue; its time jumps and recastings were debated. A second season aired in 2024, shortened to eight episodes, with further seasons planned to complete the Dance. The series stands as streaming's demonstration that a prequel can revive a franchise's prestige after a divisive finale.
