Voter registration

[Image: A citizen completing a voter registration form at a government office]

Voter registration (or enrolment) is the process by which eligible citizens are recorded on an electoral roll entitling them to vote. In some countries registration is automatic, compiled by the state from population registers; in others, notably the United States, the burden falls on individual voters to register before a deadline, and registration practices are a perennial subject of political debate because they affect who votes (see Political participation).

United States

Registration is administered by states and localities, with deadlines typically up to 30 days before an election. The National Voter Registration Act of 1993 ("Motor Voter") requires states to offer registration at motor-vehicle offices and by mail, and limits removal of voters from the rolls. Many states have since adopted online registration, automatic registration at government agencies, and — in a minority — same-day registration allowing voters to register at the polls, long available in North Dakota's case without registration at all. Debates centre on the integrity of the rolls versus access: list-maintenance "purges", proof-of-citizenship and identification requirements, and the disenfranchisement of people with felony convictions are all contested, with supporters describing safeguards against fraud and opponents describing barriers that fall unevenly on minorities, students and frequent movers.

Other countries

In much of Europe, enrolment is automatic from civil or residence registers. In the United Kingdom, household registration gave way to individual electoral registration in 2014, with an annual canvass and fines (rarely imposed) for failing to register; registration is compulsory in Australia, where the electoral commission maintains the roll and enrolment drives accompany elections under compulsory voting. Canada maintains a permanent National Register of Electors. In many countries an electoral roll is also used for jury selection, redistricting and verifying petition signatures.
