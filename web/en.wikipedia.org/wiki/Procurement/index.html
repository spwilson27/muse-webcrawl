Procurement

Procurement is the process by which organisations acquire goods, services, and works from external suppliers. It encompasses the whole cycle from identifying a need and specifying requirements, through market engagement, solicitation and evaluation of bids, contract award and management, to receipt, payment, and review. Procurement is sometimes used narrowly for the transactional act of purchasing, while the broader end-to-end discipline is called procure-to-pay or, more strategically, strategic sourcing within supply chain management.

[Image: The procurement cycle]

The procurement process

  1. Need identification and specification — defining what is required, in what quantity and quality.
  2. Sourcing — identifying potential suppliers; issuing requests for information, proposals, or quotations (RFI/RFP/RFQ) or running a competitive tender.
  3. Evaluation and selection — assessing bids on price, quality, delivery, risk, and increasingly sustainability and social criteria; negotiation.
  4. Contracting — awarding and signing a contract or issuing a purchase order.
  5. Ordering, receipt, and payment — the "three-way match" of purchase order, goods receipt, and invoice before payment.
  6. Contract and supplier management — monitoring performance, managing relationships, and reviewing outcomes.

Types

Public procurement by governments is typically regulated by law (e.g. the WTO Agreement on Government Procurement and EU procurement directives) to ensure competition, transparency, equal treatment, and value for money, and accounts for a large share of GDP in most economies. Direct procurement covers inputs embodied in an organisation's products (raw materials, components); indirect procurement covers goods and services supporting operations (IT, facilities, professional services). Digital platforms and e-procurement systems automate much of the process, and sustainable procurement standards such as ISO 20400 integrate environmental and social considerations.
