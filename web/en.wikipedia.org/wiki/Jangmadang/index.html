Jangmadang

A general market (jangmadang) in North Korea

Hangeul장마당
Literal meaning"Market grounds"
LocationNorth Korea
TypeFarmer's markets / general markets, formal and informal

Jangmadang (Korean: 장마당, literally "market grounds") are North Korean local markets — farmers' markets, black markets and counter-economies — where individuals trade consumer goods, food, and imported products. The jangmadang emerged into a major economic force during and after the North Korean famine of the 1990s (the "Arduous March"), when the collapse of the state Public Distribution System forced ordinary people to rely on private trade for survival.

History

Small farmers' markets had long been tolerated for the sale of private garden-plot produce, but their scale and scope expanded dramatically after 1994 as state rations failed. Markets spread from rural counties to large cities; traders — disproportionately women, who were less bound to state workplaces — sold smuggled Chinese goods, home-manufactured items and diverted aid supplies. A 2002 economic reform measure formally recognised "general markets", and purpose-built market halls were constructed in many cities.

Regulation and crackdowns

The state's relationship with the markets has oscillated between tolerance and repression. Traders generally must hold permits and pay stall fees, and authorities periodically restrict what may be sold, who may trade, or the hours of operation. A disastrous currency revaluation in 2009, intended partly to curb market wealth, caused widespread hardship and was partially rolled back. Under Kim Jong Un, markets have been largely tolerated as an indispensable distribution network, though they remain formally subordinate to the socialist plan.

Economic significance

Analysts estimate that a large share of North Koreans' everyday consumption now passes through jangmadang, and the markets are a principal channel through which foreign media, mobile phones and market information enter the country. A class of wealthy traders known as donju ("masters of money") has emerged, investing in real estate and wholesale trade. Defector testimony and satellite imagery studies document hundreds of official markets operating nationwide.
