Water recycling

Water recycling, also called water reclamation or water reuse, is the process of treating wastewater so that it can be used again — for irrigation, industrial processes, groundwater recharge, environmental flows, or, after advanced treatment, drinking water supply.

Types of reuse

Non-potable reuse — the most common — supplies treated effluent for landscape and agricultural irrigation, toilet flushing and cooling towers, often through distinctive purple pipes. Indirect potable reuse returns highly treated water to an aquifer or reservoir before it is withdrawn again, as practised in Orange County, California's Groundwater Replenishment System. Direct potable reuse, pioneered at scale in Windhoek, Namibia, blends treated water directly into the drinking supply.

Treatment and safety

Modern schemes combine membrane filtration (microfiltration and reverse osmosis) with ultraviolet disinfection and advanced oxidation to remove pathogens, pharmaceuticals and other micropollutants. Regulators monitor recycled water to standards often stricter than those for conventional sources, though public acceptance — the "yuck factor" — remains a practical obstacle.

Benefits

Recycling reduces pressure on rivers and aquifers, cuts the energy cost of importing water, and provides a drought-proof local supply, which is why water-stressed regions including Singapore (NEWater), Israel and Australia have invested heavily in it.
