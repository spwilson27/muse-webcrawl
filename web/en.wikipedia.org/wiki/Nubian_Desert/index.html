Nubian Desert

The Nubian Desert is a desert in northeastern Africa, lying in the eastern part of the Sahara in northern Sudan and southern Egypt. It covers an area of roughly 400,000 square kilometres between the Nile River and the Red Sea, in the region historically known as Nubia.

Geography

Unlike the sand seas of the western Sahara, the Nubian Desert is predominantly a rocky plateau of sandstone, with gravel plains, outcrops of older basement rock and dry valleys (wadis). Sand dunes occur mainly near the western margins. The region is one of the driest inhabited-adjacent landscapes on Earth, receiving almost no rainfall; the climate is hyper-arid with very hot summers. There are no oases of significance within the desert itself, and settlement is concentrated along the Nile valley that crosses it in a great bend, passing towns such as Wadi Halfa, Abu Hamed and Atbara.

History and resources

The desert was crossed in antiquity by caravan and military routes linking Egypt with the kingdoms of Kush, and its wadis contain gold-bearing rocks that were mined from Pharaonic times onward. The Abu Hamed reach forced river traffic onto desert shortcuts across the bend of the Nile. In the modern era the Sudan Military Railway was built across the desert from Wadi Halfa to Abu Hamed during the Mahdist War campaign of 1896–1898, and a desert road and railway still traverse the region. Archaeological sites, rock inscriptions and ancient mines are scattered through its wadis.
