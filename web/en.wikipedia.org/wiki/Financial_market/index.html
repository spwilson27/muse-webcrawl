Financial market

A financial market is a market in which people trade financial securities and derivatives at low transaction costs. Some of the securities include stocks and bonds, raw materials and precious metals, which are traded in the commodity markets. The term "market" sometimes means what are more strictly exchanges, organizations that facilitate the trade of financial securities, e.g., a stock exchange or commodity exchange.

Functions

Financial markets channel savings from lenders to borrowers, provide liquidity so assets can be sold quickly, enable price discovery through supply and demand, and allow risk to be transferred and shared, for example through derivatives. They also impose market discipline on companies and governments whose securities are traded.

Types of financial markets

Capital markets

Capital markets trade long-term instruments: stock markets (equities) and bond markets (debt). New issues are sold in the primary market — for instance in an initial public offering — while existing securities change hands in the secondary market.

Money markets

Money markets deal in short-term borrowing and lending, typically under a year: Treasury bills, commercial paper, certificates of deposit and repurchase agreements.

Other markets

Derivatives markets trade futures, options and swaps; the foreign exchange market trades currencies and is the largest market in the world by turnover; commodity markets trade energy, metals and agricultural products.
