Comparative army officer ranks of Europe

This article is a comparison of the officer ranks of the armies of European states. Rank structures in Europe are broadly aligned through the NATO rank scale (STANAG 2116), which assigns codes from OF-1 (lieutenant grades) to OF-10 (field marshal grades) so that corresponding ranks in different countries can be matched despite different national titles and insignia.

NATO rank codes

The NATO officer grades group ranks into general/flag officers (OF-6 to OF-10), field/senior officers (OF-3 to OF-5), and company/junior officers (OF-1 and OF-2). Not every country maintains every grade, and some titles — notably the various forms of "brigadier" — may be a general officer rank in one army and a senior appointment rather than a rank in another.

Variation between countries

Rank names reflect national traditions: Poland and several post-Soviet states retain ranks derived from the Soviet pattern, including colonel-general grades and "marshal" ranks, while Scandinavian armies use compact rank systems with fewer grades. Some states, such as Ireland and Switzerland, have small forces with their own conventions. Insignia generally use stars (pips), bars, oak leaves or sleeve lace, with the broad NATO code bracket allowing equivalence to be read across systems.
