GUIDO Music Notation

GUIDO Music Notation is a computer music notation format designed to adequately represent music notation from simple melodies to complex orchestral scores in a logical (content-describing) way, using a plain-text notation inspired by the LaTeX typesetting system. It was conceived by Holger H. Hoos, Keith A. Hamel and Kai Renz, and its formal syntax was presented in the late 1990s; further development has been carried out notably by Dominique Fober and the Grame research centre in Lyon, France.

Design

The notation is named after Guido of Arezzo (c. 991 – after 1033), the Benedictine monk credited with inventing modern staff notation. GUIDO files are human-readable text: notes are written with their names and octave registers, durations follow in parentheses or with numeric suffixes, and higher-level constructs such as slurs, clefs, dynamics and articulations are expressed with backslash tags, e.g. \slur( c d e ). Sequences are enclosed in braces and parallel voices in brackets.

[ \clef<"g2"> \key<"D"> \meter<"4/4">
  { c/4 d e f# g } ]

Implementations

The GUIDO engine, developed at Grame, renders GUIDO descriptions to graphical scores on multiple platforms, including a WebAssembly/JavaScript library used for online score display. GUIDO is one of several text-based score formats, alongside ABC notation, LilyPond, Humdrum and the XML-based MusicXML and MEI formats.
