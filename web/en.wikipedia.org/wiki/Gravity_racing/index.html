Gravity racing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Gravity racing is a form of motorsport in which vehicles race downhill powered only by gravity, without engines or other propulsion. The category includes soapbox derby cars, billy carts, street luge, gravity bikes, and downhill skateboarding, as well as organised events for purpose-built "gravity cars" on closed public roads.

History

Children's coaster-car racing dates to the early twentieth century, when youngsters built cars from wooden crates and pram wheels. The Soap Box Derby, founded in Dayton, Ohio, in 1933 after newspaper photographer Myron Scott observed boys racing homemade cars, became an international youth institution, with its All-American final held in Akron, Ohio. Adult gravity racing grew from the 1970s onward, with street luge and downhill skateboarding developing in California, and "bogie" and cartie races long established in Britain and Australia.

Vehicle design

Because the only energy source is the descent, design concentrates on minimising losses:

  • Rolling resistance — narrow, hard, high-pressure tyres or precision wheels on quality bearings.
  • Aerodynamics — faired, low bodies and a reclined driver position.
  • Mass — within class limits, heavier vehicles conserve momentum and are less affected by drag, though they accelerate more slowly at the start.
  • Braking and steering — mechanical brakes are mandatory in sanctioned events; steering geometry must remain stable at speed.

Purpose-built adult gravity cars can exceed 100 km/h on steep courses. Events such as the Red Bull Soapbox Race emphasise creativity, while downhill time-trial championships reward pure speed.

Safety

Sanctioned races require helmets and protective clothing, hay-bale or barrier protection on corners, and course inspection. Street luge and downhill skateboarding at World Cup level use full leathers and full-face helmets.
