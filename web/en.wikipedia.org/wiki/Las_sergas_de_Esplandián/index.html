Las sergas de Esplandián

Las sergas de Esplandián (The Adventures of Esplandián) is a Spanish romance of chivalry written by Garci Rodríguez de Montalvo and first published in Seville in 1510. It is the fifth book in the Amadís de Gaula cycle and follows the adventures of Esplandián, son of Amadís.

Queen Calafia and California

The novel is famous for its description of a mythical island called California, "very close to the side of the Terrestrial Paradise", ruled by the Black queen Calafia and populated entirely by women who rode griffins and used weapons of gold. The book's popularity is the source of the name of the present-day region of California, later applied by Spanish explorers to the peninsula and coast of western North America.
