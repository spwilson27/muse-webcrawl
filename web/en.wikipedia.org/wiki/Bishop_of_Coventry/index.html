The Bishop of Coventry is the ordinary of the Church of England Diocese of Coventry in the Province of Canterbury. The diocese covers most of the county of Warwickshire, and the bishop's seat (cathedra) is at Coventry Cathedral. The title has a long medieval history but the modern diocese dates from 1918.

History

In the Middle Ages, the bishops of the Mercian see at Lichfield transferred their seat at various times to Chester and to Coventry; from the 12th century the diocese was commonly styled Coventry and Lichfield, and the bishop was elected by both cathedral chapters, with the Bishop of Coventry and Lichfield ranking among the leading prelates of England. At the Reformation the Coventry connection lapsed and the see continued at Lichfield. Coventry meanwhile lay in the Diocese of Worcester. The modern Diocese of Coventry was created in 1918 out of the Diocese of Worcester, with the medieval church of St Michael — raised to cathedral status — largely destroyed in the Coventry Blitz of 14 November 1940. The new cathedral, designed by Basil Spence and consecrated in 1962, stands beside the ruins of the old.

Role

The Bishop of Coventry is assisted by the suffragan Bishop of Warwick. The diocese is known for its ministry of reconciliation, centred on the Community of the Cross of Nails founded after the destruction of the cathedral in 1940, and its partnerships with dioceses and centres in areas of conflict worldwide. Among notable 20th-century holders was John Gibbs and, in the medieval period, figures such as William de Cornhill and Roger de Meyland.
