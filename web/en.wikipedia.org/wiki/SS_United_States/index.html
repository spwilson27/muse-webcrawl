SS United States is a retired American ocean liner, built in 1950–1951 for the United States Lines. She is the largest ocean liner constructed entirely in the United States and the fastest ever built, and she remains the holder of the Blue Riband for the fastest transatlantic crossing by a passenger liner.

Design and service

Designed by William Francis Gibbs, the ship was built at Newport News, Virginia, partly to US Navy specifications so that she could be converted quickly into a troopship carrying some 14,000 troops. Her steam turbines — among the most powerful fitted to a merchant ship — propelled her at speeds exceeding 38 knots during trials. On her maiden voyage in July 1952 she crossed the Atlantic in 3 days, 10 hours and 40 minutes, taking the Blue Riband from RMS Queen Mary.

The liner carried passengers including four US presidents and was known for her fireproof construction, using extensive aluminium in her superstructure. Declining passenger numbers in the age of the jet airliner led to her withdrawal from service in 1969.

Later years

After decades of lay-up, ownership changes and preservation campaigns by the SS United States Conservancy, plans to redevelop her failed to reach fruition. In 2024, Okaloosa County, Florida, acquired the ship with the intention of sinking her as the world's largest artificial reef off the Florida Panhandle, with an associated land-based museum.
