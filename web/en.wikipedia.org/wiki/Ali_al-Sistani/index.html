Ali al-Sistani

Grand Ayatollah Sayyid Ali al-Husayni al-Sistani (born 4 August 1930) is an Iranian-born Iraqi Shia marja', widely regarded as the leading religious authority for Twelver Shia Muslims in Iraq and among the most influential Shia clerics worldwide.

Life

Born in Mashhad to a clerical family claiming descent from the Prophet Muhammad, Sistani studied in Qom under Grand Ayatollah Borujerdi and moved to Najaf in 1951, studying under Abu al-Qasim al-Khoei. After Khoei's death in 1992 he emerged as the leading marja' in Iraq, surviving persecution under Saddam Hussein while keeping a quietist distance from direct rule.

Role after 2003

After the US-led invasion, Sistani's fatwas shaped Iraq's transition: he demanded directly elected constitutional assemblies, mobilised voters in the 2005 elections, and repeatedly called for restraint during sectarian violence. In June 2014, after the fall of Mosul to ISIS, his fatwa calling able men to defend Iraq mobilised tens of thousands into what became the Popular Mobilization Forces. He has advocated a civil state and criticised corruption and foreign interference, meeting Pope Francis in Najaf in March 2021.

Teachings

His rulings are published in works such as Islamic Laws and A Code of Practice for Muslims in the West; his office runs extensive charitable and educational networks funded by khums donations.
