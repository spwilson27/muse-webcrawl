Harrowing of Hell

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Christ harrowing Hell, a staple image of medieval and Orthodox iconography (the Anastasis).

The Harrowing of Hell (Latin: Descensus Christi ad Inferos, "the descent of Christ into Hell") is the Christian doctrine that, between his crucifixion and resurrection, Jesus Christ descended into the realm of the dead (Hades or the Limbo of the Fathers), proclaimed victory over death and Satan, and liberated the righteous souls held there since the beginning of the world. The phrase survives in the Apostles' Creed: "he descended into hell."

Sources

The doctrine rests on New Testament hints — Matthew 12:40, Ephesians 4:9, and especially 1 Peter 3:18–20, in which Christ "proclaimed to the spirits in prison" — developed dramatically in the apocryphal Gospel of Nicodemus (4th–5th century), which narrates Satan and Hades arguing as Christ shatters the bronze gates, binds Satan, and raises Adam, Eve, the patriarchs and prophets. In Eastern Orthodoxy the event is depicted in the Anastasis icon, the standard image of the Resurrection itself.

Theology

Western scholastics, including Thomas Aquinas, taught that Christ descended to the limbo of the fathers to free the just of the Old Testament, who could not enter heaven before redemption, but not to the hell of the damned. Reformers disputed the article: Calvin read it as Christ's suffering of hell's torments on the cross, while Luther retained a literal triumphant descent. The teaching answers the question of how the righteous who lived before Christ could be saved.

In art and literature

The Harrowing was one of the most popular subjects of medieval drama — a set piece of the English mystery cycles — and of visual art from Byzantine icons to Fra Angelico and the Limbourg brothers. Dante's Inferno (canto IV) describes Limbo still populated by virtuous pagans, noting that Christ had emptied it of the Hebrew patriarchs. The word "harrow" here derives from Old English hergian, to harry or despoil: Christ raids Hell and carries off its captives.
