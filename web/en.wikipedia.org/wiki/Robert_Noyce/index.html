Robert Noyce

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: An early integrated circuit held between fingers, 1960s style photograph]

Robert Noyce (1927–1990) was an American physicist and entrepreneur who co-invented the practical integrated circuit and co-founded both Fairchild Semiconductor and Intel — earning the nickname 'the Mayor of Silicon Valley'.

In 1959, at Fairchild, Noyce developed the silicon planar integrated circuit — components and their connections fabricated in one piece of silicon — independently of Jack Kilby's germanium device at Texas Instruments the year before. Noyce's version, manufacturable at scale, is the ancestor of every modern chip.

The traitorous eight

Noyce was one of the 'traitorous eight' who left Shockley Semiconductor in 1957 to found Fairchild, seeding the culture of spin-off companies that became Silicon Valley's engine. At Intel (founded 1968 with Gordon Moore), he backed the culture and the trajectory that produced the microprocessor industry.

He championed federal support for semiconductor research through the SEMATECH consortium in the 1980s.
