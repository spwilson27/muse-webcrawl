Chain (order theory)

In mathematics, in the area of order theory, a chain is a subset of a partially ordered set that is totally ordered — that is, a subset in which every two elements are comparable. By contrast, an antichain is a subset in which no two distinct elements are comparable.

Definitions

Formally, if (P, ≤) is a partially ordered set, a chain is a subset C ⊆ P such that for all x, y ∈ C, either x ≤ y or y ≤ x. A chain is maximal if it is not a proper subset of any other chain. The Hausdorff maximal principle, equivalent to the axiom of choice, asserts that every partially ordered set has a maximal chain. Zorn's lemma is a related equivalent statement.

The length of a chain is often measured by the number of elements in it minus one. Dilworth's theorem and Mirsky's theorem relate chains and antichains in finite posets, and a poset is said to satisfy the chain condition (such as the ascending or descending chain condition) if chains of a certain form must stabilise.
