Port Sudan

Port Sudan is a port city in eastern Sudan and the capital of the Red Sea State. Situated on the Red Sea coast, it is Sudan's main seaport and handles the great majority of the country's international trade.

History

Port Sudan was founded by the British in 1909 as the terminus of a new railway line linking the Red Sea to the River Nile at Atbara, replacing the older port of Suakin to the south, whose harbor could not accommodate modern steamships. The city grew around its deep-water harbor, which includes container terminals and an oil terminal exporting Sudanese and South Sudanese crude oil by pipeline.

Geography and tourism

The city lies on a flat coastal plain backed by the Red Sea Hills. Its surroundings include coral reefs noted for scuba diving, and the Sanganeb Marine National Park offshore. Since the outbreak of civil war in Sudan in 2023, Port Sudan has served as the de facto seat of the Sudanese government and the main entry point for humanitarian aid.
