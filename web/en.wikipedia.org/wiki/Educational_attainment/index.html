Educational attainment

Educational attainment is a term used by statisticians to refer to the highest level of education that an individual has completed. It is measured in censuses and surveys and used to compare populations, labour outcomes, and inequality.

Measurement

Categories typically include less than high school, high school diploma, some college, associate, bachelor's, master's, and doctoral or professional degrees. International comparisons use the ISCED classification maintained by UNESCO.

Trends

Attainment has risen sharply worldwide since the mid-20th century: secondary completion became near-universal in high-income countries, and tertiary attainment among 25–34 year-olds in OECD countries averages over 45%, with women now exceeding men in most OECD states. Large gaps persist by income, race/ethnicity, and region.

Effects

Higher attainment is associated with higher earnings, lower unemployment, better health, and higher civic participation, though causation and credential inflation are debated.
