Nafion

Nafion is a brand name of a sulfonated tetrafluoroethylene-based fluoropolymer-copolymer discovered in the late 1960s by Walther Grot of DuPont (now produced by Chemours). It is the first of a class of synthetic polymers with ionic properties, called ionomers. Nafion's unique ionic properties result from incorporating perfluorovinyl ether groups terminated with sulfonate groups onto a tetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) backbone. Nafion has received a considerable amount of attention as a proton conductor for proton exchange membrane (PEM) fuel cells because of its excellent thermal and mechanical stability and high proton conductivity.

Structure and properties

The PTFE backbone makes Nafion chemically inert and hydrophobic, while the sulfonic-acid-terminated side chains are strongly hydrophilic. In the presence of water the material phase-separates on the nanometre scale into hydrophobic domains and interconnected water-filled channels lined with sulfonate groups; protons move through these channels by vehicular and Grotthuss mechanisms. Typical membranes are designated by thickness and equivalent weight, e.g. Nafion 117 (7 mil thick, 1100 EW). Conductivity of fully hydrated Nafion at room temperature is around 0.1 S/cm, but falls sharply at low humidity and the material softens above roughly 100 °C, limiting high-temperature fuel cell operation.

Production

Nafion is produced by copolymerising tetrafluoroethylene with a perfluorinated vinyl ether sulfonyl fluoride comonomer, extruding the melt-processable precursor into film, and hydrolysing the sulfonyl fluoride groups to sulfonic acid or sulfonate salt forms.

Applications

The first major use was as the membrane in chlor-alkali cells for producing chlorine and caustic soda, replacing mercury and asbestos diaphragm processes. Nafion membranes are the benchmark electrolyte in PEM fuel cells and PEM water electrolysers for green hydrogen, and are used in vanadium redox flow batteries, sensors, humidity actuators ("ionic polymer-metal composites"), superacid catalysis (Nafion-H), and laboratory reference electrodes. It is also applied as permselective coatings on electrodes and in electrochemical synthesis.

Environmental considerations

As a per- and polyfluoroalkyl substance (PFAS), Nafion-type ionomers have attracted attention in debates over "forever chemicals", though the polymer itself is of low solubility and bioavailability; byproducts of fluoropolymer manufacture are the main concern.
