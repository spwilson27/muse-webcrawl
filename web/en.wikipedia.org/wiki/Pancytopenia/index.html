Pancytopenia

SpecialtyHaematology
DefinitionReduction of red blood cells, white blood cells and platelets
CausesAplastic anaemia, marrow infiltration, hypersplenism, drugs, infections

Pancytopenia is a medical condition in which there is a reduction in the number of red and white blood cells and platelets in the circulating blood. It is not a disease itself but a finding with many possible causes, broadly grouped into decreased bone marrow production, increased peripheral destruction or sequestration (as in hypersplenism), or a combination.

Pancytopenia affects all three major blood cell lines

Causes

Central causes include aplastic anaemia, myelodysplastic syndromes, acute leukaemias and other marrow infiltration, myelofibrosis, megaloblastic anaemia from vitamin B12 or folate deficiency, paroxysmal nocturnal haemoglobinuria, certain infections (such as HIV and visceral leishmaniasis), and toxicity from chemotherapy, radiation or drugs. Splenic sequestration in portal hypertension can reduce all cell lines despite a functioning marrow.

Presentation and diagnosis

Symptoms reflect the individual cytopenias: fatigue and pallor from anaemia, infections from neutropenia, and bleeding or bruising from thrombocytopenia. Investigation includes a full blood count and blood film, nutritional and viral studies, and usually a bone marrow aspiration and biopsy to distinguish marrow failure from peripheral causes. Treatment is directed at the underlying cause, with supportive transfusion and infection management.
