Tourism in Spain

Tourism in Spain is a major contributor to national life and one of the largest tourism industries in the world. Spain consistently ranks among the most visited countries internationally, receiving more than 80 million international visitors in peak years, and tourism accounts for a substantial share of the country's gross domestic product and employment. The headquarters of the World Tourism Organization is located in Madrid.

History

Modern mass tourism in Spain developed during the 1960s, when the government promoted the country as an affordable sun-and-beach destination under slogans such as "Spain is different." Package tourism transformed the Mediterranean coast and the Balearic and Canary Islands, bringing millions of visitors from northern Europe. After Spain's transition to democracy and entry into the European Community in 1986, tourism diversified into cultural, urban, and rural segments.

Destinations

The most visited regions include Catalonia, the Balearic Islands, the Canary Islands, Andalusia, and the Valencian Community. Major cities such as Barcelona and Madrid attract visitors with museums, architecture, and festivals, while historic cities including Granada, Seville, Córdoba, and Toledo are noted for their heritage. Spain has one of the largest numbers of UNESCO World Heritage Sites of any country. Beach resorts on the Costa del Sol, Costa Blanca, and Costa Brava remain major draws, and the Camino de Santiago pilgrimage route attracts walkers from around the world.

Significance and challenges

Tourism is a leading source of foreign revenue and employment, but its concentration in coastal areas and major cities has raised concerns about seasonality, environmental pressure, water use, and the effects of short-term rentals on housing in cities such as Barcelona and Palma, where protests against overtourism have occurred.
