Rolling mill

A rolling mill is a machine — and by extension the industrial installation containing it — for rolling metal: passing stock between pairs of rotating rolls to reduce its thickness, change its cross-section or impart a desired shape. Rolling is the most widely used metal-forming process, producing plate, sheet, rail, structural sections, bar and rod.

Configurations

Roll stands are classified by the number and arrangement of rolls: two-high (including reversing mills), three-high, four-high, and cluster mills such as the twenty-high Sendzimir mill used for thin, hard strip. Small-diameter work rolls are backed by larger rolls to resist bending under rolling loads. Mills may be arranged in tandem trains for continuous production.

Hot and cold rolling

In hot rolling, slabs or billets are heated above the metal's recrystallisation temperature (for steel, around 1,200 °C) and reduced in roughing and finishing stands. In cold rolling, hot-rolled strip is further reduced at room temperature for tighter tolerances and better surface finish, then annealed. Modern integrated plants cast steel continuously and roll it in a linked sequence, with computerised gauge control throughout.
