The Mongolian language is the official language of Mongolia and the most widely spoken member of the Mongolic language family. In the narrow sense it refers to the Khalkha dialect group on which the standard language of Mongolia is based; in a broader sense "Mongolian" covers the central dialect continuum spoken in Mongolia and in Inner Mongolia, China, where standard Mongolian is based on the Chakhar dialect. Related varieties include Buryat in Russia and Siberia and Oirat (including Kalmyk) in the west.

History

The earliest substantial Mongolian text is the Secret History of the Mongols (c. 1228–1240), written in Middle Mongolian after the death of Genghis Khan. Earlier Mongolic-speaking peoples, such as the Khitan, left inscriptions in related languages. Middle Mongolian was written with the Uyghur-derived script adopted in the empire, in Chinese transcription, and in the square 'Phags-pa script devised in 1269 for Kublai Khan. Classical Mongolian, in the traditional vertical script, served as the literary standard from the 17th century until the 20th.

Scripts

Mongolia adopted the Latin alphabet briefly in the 1930s and then Cyrillic in 1941–46, which remains dominant in everyday use, though the traditional script is taught in schools and its official use has been promoted by law since 2020, with a plan to use both scripts in official documents. In Inner Mongolia the traditional script is still written in the classical vertical form and printed widely. The traditional script is written in vertical columns running left to right — unique among major vertical writing systems.

Grammar and phonology

Mongolian is an agglutinative language with subject–object–verb word order, vowel harmony, and a case system of roughly seven to eight cases (nominative, genitive, accusative, dative-locative, ablative, instrumental, comitative). It has no grammatical gender and marks voice, aspect and causation through rich verbal suffixation. Khalkha phonology features a contrast between aspirated and unaspirated stops, palatalised consonants, and vowel harmony based on tongue-root position — the traditional pharyngeal/non-pharyngeal distinction.

Dialects and status

The main dialect groups are Khalkha (standard in Mongolia), Chakhar and other Inner Mongolian dialects, Ordos, and the more divergent Kharchin and Khorchin. Buryat and Kalmyk-Oirat are usually treated as separate languages within the family. Mongolian is not considered endangered in Mongolia, where it is spoken by the overwhelming majority of the population, but Mongolic varieties in China face pressure from Mandarin Chinese.
