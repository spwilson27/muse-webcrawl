Geppetto is a fictional character in Carlo Collodi's novel The Adventures of Pinocchio (1883). An elderly, impoverished woodcarver, he carves the puppet Pinocchio from a talking log and becomes the boy's father figure, whose love and sacrifices anchor the story's moral arc.

Character in the novel

Geppetto — a Tuscan pet form of Giuseppe (Joseph) — lives alone in a humble one-room dwelling, wearing a yellow wig that earns him the nickname "Polendina" (cornmeal mush) from local children. Given a piece of pine wood by his friend Maestro Ciliegia ("Master Cherry"), he carves a marionette he names Pinocchio. The puppet is ungrateful and runs away; Geppetto is briefly jailed for abusing him. Later, Geppetto sells his only coat to buy Pinocchio a spelling book, searches for him across land and sea in a small boat, and is swallowed by the Terrible Dogfish, in whose belly Pinocchio eventually finds him and carries him to safety. In the end Pinocchio's transformation into a real boy rewards Geppetto's devotion with restored health and comfort.

Adaptations

In Disney's animated film Pinocchio (1940), Geppetto — voiced by Christian Rub — is softened into a kindly toymaker living with his cat Figaro and goldfish Cleo, and the Monstro the Whale episode adapts Collodi's dogfish. The character has been played in live action by many actors, including Martin Landau (1996), Roberto Benigni (2019) and Tom Hanks (2022), and he appears as a central figure in Guillermo del Toro's stop-motion Pinocchio (2022), voiced by David Bradley.
