John Devoy

John Devoy (Irish: Seán Ó Dubhuí; 3 September 1842 – 29 September 1928) was an Irish republican leader who, from exile in the United States, became the chief organiser and fundraiser of the Fenian movement and of Clan na Gael for over five decades.

Early life and imprisonment

Born near Kill, County Kildare, Devoy joined the French Foreign Legion as a young man before returning to Ireland to organise within the Irish Republican Brotherhood, recruiting among British soldiers. Arrested in 1866, he was sentenced to fifteen years' penal servitude for treason-felony. Released in 1871 on condition of exile, he sailed for New York.

American career

In America Devoy led Clan na Gael and edited the Gaelic American newspaper. He organised the daring 1876 Catalpa rescue of six Fenian prisoners from Western Australia, championed the "New Departure" alliance between physical-force nationalists and constitutional politicians, and channelled American money and support to the Land League and later to the organisers of the Easter Rising of 1916, maintaining close contact with Roger Casement and the Irish Volunteers.

Later life

Devoy supported the Anglo-Irish Treaty of 1921 and visited the Irish Free State in 1924 to a hero's welcome. He died in Atlantic City in 1928; his remains were returned to Ireland and buried in Glasnevin Cemetery.
