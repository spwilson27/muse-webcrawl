Bounding overwatch

TypeInfantry small-unit tactic
PurposeMovement under possible enemy fire
Also known asLeapfrogging, fire and movement

In bounding overwatch, elements alternate between moving and covering.

Bounding overwatch (also known as leapfrogging, moving overwatch, or the buddy system) is a military tactic of alternating movement and cover, in which units take turns advancing while other units provide cover fire from positions of overwatch. It is a core technique of fire and movement used by infantry from the fire-team level up to battalions with armoured support.

Procedure

The unit is divided into two or more elements. One element takes up a position from which it can observe and fire on likely enemy positions — the overwatch element. The other element then moves ("bounds") to a new position, ideally covered from the enemy's view by terrain or by the overwatch element's suppressive fire. Once in position, the moving element becomes the overwatch element and the previous overwatch element bounds past it in turn. The process repeats, so the unit as a whole advances while some part of it is always stationary, in cover, and ready to fire.

Variants

In successive bounds, the moving element halts on a line level with the overwatch element; in alternating bounds it passes beyond it, which is faster but riskier. Bounds are kept short — a few seconds of movement — where enemy fire is expected, since a moving soldier is most vulnerable in the open. The same principle is used by tanks and armoured fighting vehicles advancing by bounds between hull-down positions, and by aircraft in mutual support.

The technique trades speed for security: bounding overwatch is much slower than travelling overwatch, in which a trailing element merely observes while the lead element moves continuously, but it greatly reduces the chance that the whole unit is caught moving in the open by surprise fire.
