Powiat

Country: Poland

Level: Second-level administrative subdivision

Number: 380 (314 land counties and 66 city counties)

A powiat (pronounced [ˈpɔvjat]; plural: powiaty) is the second-level unit of local government and administration in Poland, equivalent to a county, district or prefecture in other countries. A powiat is part of a larger unit, the voivodeship (province), and is itself subdivided into gminas (municipalities/communes).

Types

There are two types of powiat. Land counties (powiaty ziemskie) are territorial units governed by an elected county council (rada powiatu) and an executive board headed by a starosta. City counties (miasta na prawach powiatu, "cities with powiat rights") are cities, usually with more than 100,000 inhabitants or former voivodeship capitals, that perform county functions themselves; they are run by a city council and a directly elected mayor.

Responsibilities

Powiats are responsible for matters exceeding the scope of a single gmina, including secondary education, public health services and hospitals, county roads, public transport, building supervision, environmental protection, and employment services.

History

The powiat dates to the second half of the 14th century in the Kingdom of Poland and existed throughout the history of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth. Abolished in 1975 under the communist government, powiats were restored by the local government reforms that took effect on 1 January 1999, which created a three-tier structure of voivodeships, powiats and gminas.
