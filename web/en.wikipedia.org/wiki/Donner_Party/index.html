Donner Party

Date1846–1847
LocationSierra Nevada, California (near Truckee Lake, now Donner Lake)
Participants87 emigrants (Donner and Reed families and others)
Outcome48 survivors; some resorted to cannibalism

The Donner Party, sometimes called the Donner–Reed Party, was a group of American pioneers who migrated to California in a wagon train from the Midwest. Delayed by a series of mishaps and mistakes, they spent the winter of 1846–1847 snowbound in the Sierra Nevada mountain range. Some of the emigrants resorted to cannibalism to survive, eating the bodies of those who had succumbed to starvation, sickness, and exposure. Of the 87 members of the party, 48 survived.

The Pioneer Monument at Donner Memorial State Park stands 22 feet (6.7 m) tall — the reported depth of the snow that trapped the party.

Journey

The party set out from Springfield, Illinois, in April 1846, following the California Trail. At Fort Bridger they made the fateful decision to follow the untested "Hastings Cutoff" promoted by Lansford W. Hastings, a shortcut across Utah's Wasatch Mountains and the Great Salt Lake Desert that cost them weeks and many animals. Further delays in the Sierra foothills meant they attempted the final pass — near present-day Donner Pass — just as an early, severe winter began in late October 1846.

Winter and rescue

Snowed in near Truckee Lake (now Donner Lake) and Alder Creek, the emigrants built rough cabins and endured months of starvation. A small snowshoe party, later called the "Forlorn Hope," set out in December to seek help; most of its members died, and the survivors survived in part by cannibalism. Four relief parties reached the camps between February and April 1847. The last survivor, Lewis Keseberg, was found in mid-April 1847.

Legacy

The Donner Party became one of the most notorious episodes of the American westward migration. Reports of cannibalism shocked the nation and temporarily slowed emigration to California. Donner Memorial State Park and Donner Lake in California are named for the party.
