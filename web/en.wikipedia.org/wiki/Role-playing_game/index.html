Role-playing game

A role-playing game (RPG) is a game in which players assume the roles of characters in a fictional setting and create narratives collaboratively. Actions succeed or fail according to rules, often involving dice rolls, and characters develop over time.

The tabletop form began with Dungeons & Dragons (1974) by Gary Gygax and Dave Arneson, evolving from miniature wargames. A gamemaster (GM) describes the world and adjudicates outcomes while players describe their characters' actions in sessions or campaigns.

Forms

RPGs include pen-and-paper tabletop games, live action role-playing (LARP), and electronic forms: computer RPGs (CRPGs), Japanese RPGs, action RPGs, tactical RPGs, and massively multiplayer online role-playing games (MMORPGs) such as World of Warcraft. Common elements include character attributes, experience points, levels, quests and fictional races and classes.
