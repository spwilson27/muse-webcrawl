's-Hertogenbosch (Dutch: [ˌsɛrtoːɣə(m)ˈbɔs] ⓘ, literally "The Duke's Forest"), colloquially known as Den Bosch, is a city and municipality in the Netherlands, and the capital of the province of North Brabant. The city was founded in 1185 by Duke Henry I of Brabant as a fortified town at the confluence of the Aa and Dommel rivers, near the Meuse.

History

Den Bosch grew into one of the largest cities of the northern Netherlands in the late Middle Ages. The painter Hieronymus Bosch (c. 1450–1516) was born and worked here; the Jheronimus Bosch Art Center and a statue in the market square commemorate him. The Gothic St. John's Cathedral (Sint-Janskathedraal) is a masterpiece of Brabantine Gothic architecture. During the Eighty Years' War the "impregnable" city was finally captured for the Dutch Republic by Frederick Henry after the celebrated Siege of 's-Hertogenbosch in 1629.

Culture and sights

The Binnendieze, a network of canals running beneath the old town, is a popular tourist attraction navigated by boat. The city is known for its carnival (when it is renamed Oeteldonk), the Bossche bol pastry, and the Noordbrabants Museum and Design Museum Den Bosch. FC Den Bosch plays at De Vliert stadium.
