The Thien Mu Pagoda (Vietnamese: Chùa Thiên Mụ, "Pagoda of the Celestial Lady") is a historic Buddhist temple on the bank of the Perfume River in Huế, Vietnam. Founded in 1601, it is one of the oldest and most revered religious sites in the country and an unofficial symbol of the former imperial capital.

History

According to legend, an old woman — the "celestial lady" — appeared on the hill where the pagoda stands, prophesying that a lord would build a pagoda for the country's prosperity. Hearing the story, the Nguyễn lord Nguyễn Hoàng ordered the temple built in 1601. Successive Nguyễn rulers enlarged it; in 1710 Lord Nguyễn Phúc Chu cast the great bell, weighing over two tonnes, whose sound is said to carry ten kilometres. The seven-storey octagonal Phước Duyên Tower, 21 metres high, was added in 1844 under Emperor Thiệu Trị, each storey honouring a Buddha of a past age.

Modern history

The pagoda is closely associated with the monk Thích Quảng Đức, who lived there and in 1963 drove from the temple to Saigon, where he immolated himself in protest against the persecution of Buddhists under President Ngô Đình Diệm; the Austin car he drove is preserved at the pagoda. The temple was also a centre of Buddhist protest movements in the 1980s and remains an active monastery and major visitor attraction in Huế.
