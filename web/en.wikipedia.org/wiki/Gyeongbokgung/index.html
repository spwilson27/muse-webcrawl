Gyeongbokgung

Gyeongbokgung (Korean: 경복궁), also known as Gyeongbok Palace, is the largest and principal royal palace of the Joseon dynasty, located in Jongno District, Seoul, South Korea. Built in 1395, three years after the founding of Joseon by King Taejo, it served as the main palace and seat of government for much of the dynasty's five centuries.

History

The palace was established in 1395 at the foot of Mount Bugak in the new capital, Hanseong (Seoul). It was destroyed by fire during the Japanese invasions of 1592–1598 (the Imjin War) and lay in ruins for nearly 270 years until it was rebuilt in 1867 under the regency of the Heungseon Daewongun. During the Japanese colonial period (1910–1945), most of its buildings were demolished and the Government-General Building was erected on the site; that building was itself removed in 1995–1996.

Architecture and restoration

The palace complex is aligned on a north–south axis entered through the Gwanghwamun gate. Major buildings include Geunjeongjeon, the throne hall where coronations and state ceremonies were held; Sajeongjeon, the king's executive office; Gangnyeongjeon and Gyotaejeon, the royal living quarters; Gyeonghoeru, a great pavilion on an artificial lake used for state banquets; and Hyangwonjeong, a small pavilion on an island in Hyangwonji pond. Since 1990 the South Korean government has carried out a long-term restoration programme, and the palace grounds also house the National Palace Museum of Korea and the National Folk Museum of Korea. The changing of the Royal Guard ceremony is a popular daily attraction.
