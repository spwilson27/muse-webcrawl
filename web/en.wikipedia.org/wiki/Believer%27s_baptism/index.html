Believer's baptism

Believer's baptism
Also called: credobaptism
Traditions: Baptists, Pentecostals, Anabaptists, Churches of Christ
Method: usually immersion
Contrast: infant baptism (paedobaptism)

Believer's baptism (also known as credobaptism, from the Latin credo, "I believe") is the practice of baptizing only those who are able to make a conscious profession of faith in Jesus Christ. It is distinguished from infant baptism, in which young children or infants are baptized. Churches that practise believer's baptism include Baptists, most Pentecostals, Anabaptists such as the Mennonites and Amish, the Churches of Christ, and many evangelical and non-denominational churches.

Theology

Adherents argue that the New Testament links baptism with repentance and personal faith, citing passages such as Acts 2:38 and Mark 16:16, and note that every baptism described in the New Testament involves converts who had heard and believed the gospel. Because an infant cannot consciously repent or believe, believer's-baptism churches hold that infant baptism does not fulfil the biblical pattern. Many such churches therefore re-baptize converts who were baptized as infants, a practice that historically led to their opponents calling them "Anabaptists" ("re-baptizers").

Mode and subjects

Believer's baptism is most commonly administered by full immersion in water, though some groups practise affusion (pouring). The age at which baptism occurs varies; children as young as five or six may be baptized in some churches, while others wait until adolescence. The baptism is often preceded by a public profession of faith and, in some traditions, by a period of instruction or catechesis.

History

The practice became prominent during the Radical Reformation of the 16th century, when Anabaptist groups in Switzerland, Germany, and the Netherlands rejected infant baptism and were persecuted by both Catholic and magisterial Protestant authorities. English Baptists in the 17th century, and later the Stone-Campbell Restoration Movement and the Pentecostal movement, carried the practice worldwide.
