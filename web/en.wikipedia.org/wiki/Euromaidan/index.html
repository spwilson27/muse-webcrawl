Euromaidan

Euromaidan was a wave of demonstrations and civil unrest in Ukraine, which began on 21 November 2013 with large protests in Maidan Nezalezhnosti (Independence Square) in Kyiv. The protests were sparked by President Viktor Yanukovych's sudden decision not to sign the European Union–Ukraine Association Agreement, instead choosing closer ties to Russia, and grew into a broader movement against corruption and authoritarianism that culminated in the February 2014 Revolution of Dignity.

Course of the protests

After riot police violently dispersed student protesters on 30 November 2013, crowds swelled to hundreds of thousands and protesters occupied the square and nearby buildings, building barricades and self-defence units. The government passed sweeping anti-protest laws in January 2014; clashes on Hrushevsky Street produced the first deaths. Between 18 and 20 February the violence peaked: snipers and security forces killed more than a hundred protesters and several police in central Kyiv. On 21 February an EU-mediated agreement was signed, but protesters demanded Yanukovych's resignation; he fled the capital that night, and on 22 February parliament voted to remove him and call new elections.

Aftermath

Russia denounced the change of government as a coup and responded by occupying and annexing Crimea and backing armed separatists in the Donbas, beginning the Russo-Ukrainian War. In Ukraine the Maidan is commemorated as the Revolution of Dignity, and the slain protesters are honoured as the "Heavenly Hundred." Ukraine signed the EU Association Agreement in 2014 and has since moved steadily toward European integration.
