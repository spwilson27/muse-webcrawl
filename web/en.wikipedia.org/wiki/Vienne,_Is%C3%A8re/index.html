Vienne, Isère

Vienne is a commune in southeastern France, in the department of Isère in the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region, on the banks of the river Rhône about 35 km south of Lyon. It is renowned for its exceptionally well-preserved Roman monuments.

History

Known in antiquity as Vienna, the town was a major city of the Allobroges and became an important Roman colony. Its Roman remains include the Temple of Augustus and Livia, the theatre, the pyramid (a remnant of the Roman circus) and extensive archaeological collections at Saint-Pierre. In the Middle Ages Vienne was an archbishopric and hosted the Council of Vienne (1311–1312), at which Pope Clement V suppressed the Knights Templar.

Culture

Jazz à Vienne

Since 1981 the town has hosted Jazz à Vienne, one of Europe's leading jazz festivals, held each summer in the ancient Roman theatre.
