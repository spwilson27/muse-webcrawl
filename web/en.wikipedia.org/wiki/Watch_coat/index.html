Watch coat

A watch coat is a heavy woollen overcoat worn by sailors and naval personnel on watch in cold weather. Typically made of thick, dark navy-blue wool, it is knee-length or longer, double-breasted, and cut generously to be worn over uniforms and to shed wind and spray while standing exposed deck watches.

History and use

Watch coats were standard issue in many navies during the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, valued for warmth on exposed bridges and decks before the era of heated wheelhouses and modern waterproof foul-weather gear. Related garments include the bridge coat worn by officers — a shorter, tailored version of the watch coat — and the pea coat worn by enlisted sailors. The term is also applied in civilian contexts to a heavy overcoat styled after the naval garment.

Characteristics

Traditional watch coats are made of dense melton or beaver wool, feature large buttons that can be fastened with gloved hands, deep pockets, and a high collar that can be turned up against the weather. They were designed to retain warmth even when damp and to withstand the salt environment of shipboard life.
