Drum and bass

Drum and bass (often abbreviated as DnB, D&B, or drum n bass) is a genre of electronic dance music characterised by fast breakbeats (typically 165–185 beats per minute) with heavy bass and sub-bass lines, samples, and synthesizers. Emerging in the early 1990s from the UK jungle scene, it grew via club nights, pirate radio and raves into one of the most enduring genres of UK electronic music.

Characteristics

Drum and bass is built around chopped and re-sequenced breakbeats — most famously the "Amen break" — layered over deep, often Reese-style basslines. Tracks frequently feature an MC, samples from reggae and dub, and long atmospheric intros before a "drop." Key early artists include Goldie, LTJ Bukem, Roni Size, Andy C, and producers on labels such as Metalheadz, RAM Records and Hospital Records.

History

The genre evolved from jungle around 1993–94 as producers emphasised bass weight and polished production over ragga samples. Goldie's Timeless (1995) and Roni Size & Reprazent's Mercury Prize-winning New Forms (1997) brought mainstream attention. Substyles later ranged from the soulful "liquid funk" to the aggressive, futuristic neurofunk and jump-up sounds. In the 2020s drum and bass enjoyed a major chart resurgence in the UK and Australasia.
