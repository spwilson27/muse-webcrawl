Chandrayaan-3

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Chandrayaan-3
OperatorISRO
Launch14 July 2023
Landing23 August 2023, near lunar south pole region
ElementsVikram lander, Pragyan rover, propulsion module
Vikram and Pragyan on the Moon.

Chandrayaan-3 was India's third lunar exploration mission and its triumphant answer to the loss of the Chandrayaan-2 lander in 2019. On 23 August 2023 its Vikram lander touched down at about 69°S latitude — farther south than any previous landing — making India the fourth nation to achieve a soft lunar landing and the first in the Moon's coveted south-polar region.

The Pragyan rover rolled down its ramp and explored for one lunar day, measuring sulfur and other elements in the soil and taking the temperature profile of the polar regolith before both craft slept through the lunar night, as designed. The landing date is now celebrated in India as National Space Day.

Significance

Chandrayaan-3's success — on a budget reported around $75 million — cemented India's standing among the leading space powers and sharpened international interest in the lunar south pole, where water ice in shadowed craters could support the Artemis program and other future bases. The propulsion module was later manoeuvred back to Earth orbit as a bonus experiment.
