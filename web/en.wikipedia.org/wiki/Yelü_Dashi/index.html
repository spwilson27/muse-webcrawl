Yelü Dashi (耶律大石; 1087–1143), also known by his temple name Emperor Dezong of Western Liao, was a Khitan prince of the Liao dynasty and the founder of the Qara Khitai (Western Liao) empire in Central Asia. After the fall of the Liao to the Jurchen Jin dynasty, he led a band of followers westward and carved out a new empire in the lands of modern Xinjiang, Kyrgyzstan, Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan — one of the most remarkable state-building feats of the medieval world.

Early life

Yelü Dashi was a member of the imperial Yelü clan and unusually well educated for a Khitan prince: he passed the imperial examinations and held the jinshi degree, serving as a scholar-official and military commander under Emperor Tianzuo. When the Jurchen under Aguda destroyed Liao armies in Manchuria, Dashi was captured in 1122 but escaped and briefly joined the resistance around the last Liao emperor.

Migration west

Disagreeing with Tianzuo's leadership, Dashi departed in 1124 with some 200 horsemen, riding northwest to the Liao garrison town of Kedun on the Orkhon River. There he gathered the Khitan and allied tribal forces of the northern frontier — sources speak of some 10,000 cavalry — styling himself king (wang). In 1130 he advanced west into the territory of the Qarluqs and the Eastern Qarakhanids, taking Balasagun in the Chu valley (present-day Kyrgyzstan) and making it his capital. In 1134 he accepted the submission of Kashgar and Khotan and took the imperial title, with the Central Asian title Gurkhan, "universal khan".

Victory over the Seljuks

In 1137 Dashi's forces defeated the Western Qarakhanids at Khujand. When the Seljuk sultan Ahmad Sanjar marched east to restore his authority, Dashi annihilated his army at the Battle of Qatwan, near Samarkand, on 9 September 1141. Samarkand and Bukhara submitted, and the Qara Khitai became the dominant power in Central Asia. News of the defeat of a Muslim sultan by an eastern king travelling west helped feed the European legend of Prester John.

Legacy

Dashi ruled his multi-ethnic, multi-religious empire with notable pragmatism, retaining Chinese-style administration and Liao titles alongside local customs, and levying modest taxes. He died in 1143, succeeded by his widow Xiao Tabuyan as regent. The empire he founded endured until its usurpation by Kuchlug in 1211 and absorption into the Mongol Empire in 1218.
