Special nuclear material

Special nuclear material (SNM) is a term used by the United States Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) to describe fissile materials that require strict controls because of their usefulness in nuclear weapons or nuclear fuel. The definition is set out in the Atomic Energy Act of 1954.

Definition

Special nuclear material consists of:

  • Plutonium
  • Uranium-233
  • Uranium enriched in the isotopes uranium-233 or uranium-235

The term does not include "source material" (natural uranium and thorium ores and their concentrates) or byproduct material, which are regulated separately.

Categories

For safeguards and physical-protection purposes, the NRC and the Department of Energy grade special nuclear material into categories (Category I, II, and III) according to quantity, enrichment, and form. Category I, sometimes called "strategic special nuclear material," covers quantities of greatest concern, such as 5 kilograms or more of plutonium or highly enriched uranium, and is subject to the most stringent material control and accounting (MC&A), security, and transportation requirements.

Regulation

Possession, use, transfer, import, and export of special nuclear material in the United States generally require a license from the NRC (or, for defense programs, control by the Department of Energy and the National Nuclear Security Administration). International safeguards on SNM are monitored by the International Atomic Energy Agency under the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty.
