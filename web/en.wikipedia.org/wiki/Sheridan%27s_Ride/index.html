Sheridan's Ride

Sheridan's Ride refers to the famous ride of Union Major General Philip Sheridan on 19 October 1864, during the American Civil War, from Winchester, Virginia, to the battlefield of Cedar Creek, where he rallied his retreating army and turned a morning defeat into a decisive victory.

Background

Sheridan, commanding the Army of the Shenandoah, had been in Winchester when Confederate forces under Jubal Early launched a surprise dawn attack on the Union camps along Cedar Creek, driving the Federals from their positions and capturing artillery and supplies.

The ride

Hearing the guns from Winchester, some 12–20 miles (about 20–30 km) from the field, Sheridan mounted his black horse Rienzi and galloped south along the Valley Pike, meeting streams of stragglers as he went. Waving his hat and shouting for the men to turn back, he inspired the retreating soldiers to rally. Reaching the field in the early afternoon, Sheridan reorganised his lines and launched a counterattack late in the day that routed Early's army and recovered the lost guns, along with many Confederate pieces.

Legacy

The episode was immortalised in Thomas Buchanan Read's popular poem "Sheridan's Ride" (1864), which was widely recited and credited with helping Abraham Lincoln's re-election campaign. Sheridan's horse Rienzi was later preserved and displayed at the Smithsonian Institution.
