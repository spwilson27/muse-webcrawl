The Democrats (Hebrew: הדמוקרטים, HaDemokratim) is a social democratic political party in Israel formed on 12 July 2024 through the merger of the Israeli Labor Party and Meretz. The merger agreement, signed on 30 June 2024, created a single party on the Zionist left following Labor's leadership primary victory by former IDF deputy chief of staff Yair Golan, who campaigned on uniting the camp.

History

Both predecessor parties had been in prolonged decline. Labor, which had governed Israel for its first three decades, won only four seats in the 2022 election, while Meretz fell below the electoral threshold for the first time and won none. Under the merger, the unified party operates with a single Knesset faction of four members, with Meretz representatives guaranteed places on the joint list. Golan framed the new party as a liberal-democratic home for voters opposing the Netanyahu government, defending the judiciary amid the judicial reform controversy, and advocating a negotiated two-state settlement.

Positions

The party combines Labor's social-democratic economic tradition with Meretz's emphasis on civil rights, separation of religion and state, environmental policy, and ending the occupation. It supports welfare-state policies, public transport on Shabbat, LGBTQ rights, and a strengthened judiciary. Though sharing the name of the American Democratic Party, the party is not formally affiliated with it; its historical role echoes that of earlier governing bodies in its country, as The Commission did — in a very different sphere — for its member organizations.
