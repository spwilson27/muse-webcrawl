Bacon

[Image: Strips of streaky bacon sizzling in a frying pan]

Bacon is a type of salt-cured pork made from various cuts, typically the belly or less fatty parts of the back. It is eaten on its own, as a side dish (particularly in breakfasts), or used as a minor ingredient to flavour dishes.

Production

Bacon is cured either by a dry rub of salt and spices or by immersion in brine, often with nitrites or nitrates to inhibit bacterial growth and give the meat its characteristic pink colour. After curing it may be cold- or hot-smoked, which adds flavour and further preserves it.

Types

Streaky bacon, common in the United States, is cut from the pork belly and has long alternating layers of fat and meat. Back bacon, common in Britain and Ireland, includes the loin and is leaner. Canadian bacon is made from the eye of the loin. Italian pancetta is belly bacon that is salt-cured and spiced but not smoked, while guanciale is made from the pig's cheek.

Culinary uses

Bacon is fried, grilled or baked and served with eggs in the traditional full breakfast. It is used in sandwiches such as the BLT, wrapped around other foods, crumbled over salads, and rendered to provide cooking fat and flavour in countless dishes.

Health

Bacon is high in fat, salt and nitrites, and processed meats have been classified by the World Health Organization as carcinogenic to humans when consumed regularly in quantity.
