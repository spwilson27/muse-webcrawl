Sports medicine

Sports medicine is a branch of medicine that deals with physical fitness and the treatment and prevention of injuries related to sports and exercise. Although most sports teams have employed team physicians for many years, sports medicine emerged as a distinct specialty during the 20th century, and it is now a recognised medical subspecialty in many countries. Practitioners include physicians from primary care, orthopaedic surgery, physical medicine and rehabilitation and other backgrounds, working alongside physiotherapists, athletic trainers, sports scientists, nutritionists and psychologists.

Scope

Sports medicine covers the diagnosis and management of musculoskeletal injuries such as ligament sprains, muscle strains, fractures, dislocations, tendinopathies and concussion; exercise prescription for health and chronic disease; pre-participation screening; nutrition and supplementation; performance enhancement and anti-doping; and the medical care of teams and mass-participation events. Common interventions range from load management, rehabilitation exercise programmes and bracing to arthroscopic surgery, such as anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction.

Public health role

Beyond elite sport, sports and exercise medicine promotes physical activity in the general population—sometimes summarised as "exercise is medicine"—addressing sedentary behaviour and conditions such as obesity, diabetes and cardiovascular disease. Professional organisations include the American College of Sports Medicine (founded 1954) and the International Federation of Sports Medicine (FIMS, founded 1928).
