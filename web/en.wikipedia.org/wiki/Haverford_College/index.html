Haverford College is a private liberal arts college in Haverford, Pennsylvania, United States. Founded in 1833 by members of the Religious Society of Friends (Quakers), it is one of the oldest institutions of higher learning in the United States and is consistently ranked among the top liberal arts colleges in the country. Although founded as a men's college, Haverford became fully coeducational in 1980.

Academics

Haverford offers Bachelor of Arts and Bachelor of Science degrees across some 31 majors, with small classes and a strong emphasis on undergraduate research; all students complete a senior thesis. The college is a member of the Tri-College Consortium with nearby Bryn Mawr College and Swarthmore College, and of the Quaker Consortium with the University of Pennsylvania, allowing cross-registration.

Honor Code

The college is known for its student-run Honor Code, ratified annually by the student body since 1897. The code governs academic integrity — exams are unproctored and often take-home — and social responsibility, and is administered by an elected Honor Council. Quaker values of consensus and community continue to shape campus life.

Campus

The 200-acre (81 ha) campus, the oldest planned college landscape in the United States, functions as an arboretum and includes Founders Hall (1833), a nature trail, and a duck pond. Athletic teams, the Black Squirrels, compete in the NCAA Division III Centennial Conference; Haverford's cricket team is the only varsity cricket team in the United States.

Notable alumni

Alumni include Nobel laureates, the actor Chevy Chase, journalist Dave Barry, and numerous scholars, judges, and public servants. Haverford also runs the Center for Peace and Global Citizenship.
