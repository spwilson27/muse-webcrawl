Jonathan Richman

Jonathan Michael Richman (born May 16, 1951) is an American singer, songwriter, and guitarist. In 1970, he founded the Modern Lovers, an influential proto-punk band whose songs included "Roadrunner". Since the mid-1970s, Richman has performed as a solo artist, known for his earnest, childlike delivery, spare arrangements, and songs sung in English, Spanish, French, and Italian.

Early life

Richman grew up in Natick, Massachusetts. Deeply influenced by the Velvet Underground, he moved to New York City as a teenager and briefly lived in the orbit of the band before returning to Boston, where in 1970 he formed the Modern Lovers with John Felice, David Robinson, and Rolfe Anderson; Jerry Harrison and Ernie Brooks later joined.

The Modern Lovers

The original Modern Lovers recorded demos in 1972–73, including "Roadrunner" and "Pablo Picasso", produced with input from John Cale. The band split before releasing an album; the recordings were issued in 1976 as The Modern Lovers, now considered a landmark of proto-punk. Richman then re-formed the group in a quieter, more innocent style, scoring a UK hit with "Egyptian Reggae" in 1977.

Solo career

From the 1980s onward, Richman toured relentlessly, usually accompanied only by a drummer (longtime collaborator Tommy Larkins), releasing albums such as Jonathan Sings! (1983), I, Jonathan (1992), and ¡Longaniza!-era Spanish-language recordings. He appeared as the wandering troubadour narrator in the Farrelly brothers' film There's Something About Mary (1998). His songs, including "That Summer Feeling", are celebrated for their sincerity and simplicity.
