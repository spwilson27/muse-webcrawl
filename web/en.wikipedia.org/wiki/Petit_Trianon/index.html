The Petit Trianon (French for "small Trianon") is a Neoclassical château located on the grounds of the Palace of Versailles in Versailles, France. It was built between 1762 and 1768 during the reign of King Louis XV. It is celebrated as a masterpiece of early French Neoclassicism, designed by the architect Ange-Jacques Gabriel.

Louis XV originally commissioned the building in the orbit of his mistress, Madame de Pompadour, who died before its completion; it was subsequently used by Madame du Barry. In 1774, on his accession, Louis XVI gave the Petit Trianon to his wife, Marie Antoinette, who made it her private retreat and remodelled its gardens in the English landscape style.

Architecture

The château is a cube of dressed stone with facades that vary on each side: the garden front is articulated by four Corinthian columns, while the courtyard front employs pilasters. Its restrained decoration — fluted pilasters, simple cornices, and square windows — marks the turn away from Rococo ornament toward classical severity.

The grounds include the French Pavilion, the Temple of Love, the Queen's Theatre, and Marie Antoinette's Hamlet, a rustic model village built from 1783.
