Kayōkyoku

Kayōkyoku (歌謡曲)
Literal meaning: "song tunes"
Era: c. 1920s–1980s
Country: Japan
Related: enka, J-pop

Kayōkyoku (Japanese: 歌謡曲, "song tunes") was the dominant form of Japanese popular music from the 1920s until the late 1980s, when it was gradually superseded by the term J-pop. The style blended Western popular song forms and instrumentation with Japanese melodic and lyrical sensibilities, and formed the commercial mainstream of the Japanese recording industry for most of the 20th century.

Characteristics

Kayōkyoku songs were typically written by professional songwriting teams for star singers, used Western scales and orchestral or band accompaniment, and were marketed through records, radio and later television programmes such as NHK's Kōhaku Uta Gassen. Sub-genres ranged from sentimental enka to mood kayō and folk-influenced styles.

History

The genre emerged in the late 1920s with hits such as "Tokyo March" and flourished after the Second World War with singers like Hibari Misora. In the 1970s idol singers came to dominate the charts. From the late 1980s, as Japanese artists took more creative control and Western-style pop production spread, the industry term "J-pop" replaced kayōkyoku, which is now used retrospectively for the earlier repertoire.
