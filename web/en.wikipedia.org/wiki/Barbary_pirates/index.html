Overview

The Barbary pirates, or Barbary corsairs, were Muslim pirates and privateers who operated from North Africa — chiefly the ports of Salé, Algiers, Tunis and Tripoli on the "Barbary Coast" — from the 16th century into the 19th. In Ottoman service under the Barbarossa brothers, Algiers became the corsair capital; the regencies of Algiers, Tunis and Tripoli licensed raiding of Christian shipping and coastal towns across the Mediterranean and as far as Iceland (the Turkish Abductions of 1627) and Ireland (the sack of Baltimore, 1631).

Decline

Corsairing rested on tribute and ransom economics: European states and the young United States paid the regencies for immunity — until the US fought the Barbary Wars (1801–05, 1815), Britain bombarded Algiers (1816, under Lord Exmouth) to free Christian slaves, and France invaded Algiers in 1830, ending the system. Estimates that over a million Europeans were enslaved between 1530 and 1780 (after Robert C. Davis) are widely cited and debated in scale.
