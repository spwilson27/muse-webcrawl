The Texas National Guard is part of the Texas Military Forces and the National Guard of the United States. It comprises the Texas Army National Guard and the Texas Air National Guard. With roughly 18,000 soldiers and 2,000 airmen, it is one of the largest state National Guard organisations in the country.

Dual mission

Like other state National Guards, the Texas National Guard has a dual state-federal status. Under state authority (Title 32 or state active duty), the governor employs it for disaster response, search and rescue, and security missions. Under federal authority (Title 10), its units can be mobilised for overseas combat and support operations, as occurred extensively in Iraq and Afghanistan after 2001, when Texas provided some of the largest Guard deployments of any state.

Major formations and missions

The Army Guard's senior headquarters is the 36th Infantry Division, famed from the Second World War, with brigades including armoured, infantry, sustainment, and combat aviation units spread across armouries statewide. The Air Guard includes the 147th Attack Wing at Ellington Field (flying MQ-9 Reapers), the 136th Airlift Wing at Fort Worth (C-130s), and the 149th Fighter Wing at Lackland (F-16 training). State missions have included the response to Hurricane Harvey in 2017, COVID-19 support, wildfire fighting, and the extended border-security activation Operation Lone Star beginning in 2021.
