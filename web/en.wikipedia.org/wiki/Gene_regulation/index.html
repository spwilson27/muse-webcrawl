Regulation of gene expression

Regulation of gene expression, or gene regulation, includes a wide range of mechanisms used by cells to increase or decrease the production of specific gene products (proteins or RNA). Sophisticated programs of gene expression are widely observed in biology — for example, to trigger developmental pathways, respond to environmental stimuli, or adapt to new food sources. Virtually any step of gene expression can be modulated, from transcriptional initiation to RNA processing and protein modification.

The operon model

Gene regulation was first understood in bacteria through the lac operon of Escherichia coli, described by François Jacob and Jacques Monod in 1961. The operon groups genes for lactose metabolism under a single promoter controlled by a repressor protein; when lactose is present, it inactivates the repressor and the genes are transcribed. Operons may be inducible (like lac) or repressible (like the trp operon for tryptophan synthesis), providing economical, coordinated control.

Eukaryotic regulation

In eukaryotes, regulation is layered. Transcription factors bind promoters, enhancers and silencers that may lie far from the gene, looping DNA to the transcription machinery. Epigenetic mechanisms — DNA methylation, histone modification and chromatin remodelling — stably switch genes on or off and underlie cell differentiation, genomic imprinting and X-chromosome inactivation. After transcription, alternative splicing, mRNA export and stability, microRNAs and small interfering RNAs, and translational control further tune output; RNA interference earned the 2006 Nobel Prize for Andrew Fire and Craig Mello.

Development and disease

Master regulatory genes such as the Hox genes pattern the body axis in animals, and cascades of regulated expression drive embryogenesis from a single fertilised egg. Misregulation is central to disease: oncogenes and tumour suppressor genes are regulators thrown out of balance in cancer, and many developmental disorders arise from mutations in transcription factors or enhancers.
