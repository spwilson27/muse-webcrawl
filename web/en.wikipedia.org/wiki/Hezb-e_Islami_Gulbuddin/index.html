Hezb-e Islami Gulbuddin


Party flag

Hezb-e Islami Gulbuddin (HIG) is an Afghan political party and former militant group led by Gulbuddin Hekmatyar. It originated in 1975–1976 when Hekmatyar split from the original Hezb-e Islami movement; the faction led by Maulvi Yunus Khalis became known as Hezb-e Islami Khalis, while Hekmatyar's faction took the name of its leader.

History

During the Soviet–Afghan War (1979–1989), HIG was among the largest of the Afghan mujahideen factions and received substantial support channelled through Pakistan's Inter-Services Intelligence, as well as funding from the United States and Saudi Arabia. It was known for its strict Islamist ideology and its frequent clashes with rival mujahideen groups.

After the fall of the communist government in 1992, Hekmatyar twice served briefly as Prime Minister of Afghanistan (1993–1994 and 1996), and his forces were responsible for heavy rocket attacks on Kabul during the civil war. Following the rise of the Taliban, HIG lost ground and Hekmatyar went into exile in Iran.

After 2001

After the 2001 United States invasion, HIG regrouped and fought against the Afghan government and coalition forces, and was designated a terrorist organization by the United States. In September 2016 the Afghan government of Ashraf Ghani signed a peace deal with Hekmatyar, granting him immunity; he returned to Kabul in 2017. After the Taliban takeover of August 2021, the party's position changed again, and Hekmatyar remained in Kabul for a time before restrictions were placed on his activities.
