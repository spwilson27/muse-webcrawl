Luna 16 was an uncrewed space mission of the Soviet Luna program. Launched on 12 September 1970, it was the first robotic probe to land on the Moon and return a sample of lunar soil to Earth, and the first sample return accomplished by the Soviet Union.

Luna 16, the first successful robotic lunar sample return mission

Mission profile

Luna 16 entered lunar orbit on 17 September 1970 and soft-landed on 20 September in Mare Fecunditatis (the Sea of Fertility), in an area roughly 100 kilometres from where Luna 13 had landed. After landing, an automatic drill bored into the surface to a depth of about 35 centimetres and collected a sample of regolith, which was placed in a sealed spherical capsule in the ascent stage.

Less than an hour after drilling, the ascent stage blasted off from the Moon on a direct trajectory to Earth — no mid-course correction was required. On 24 September 1970 the capsule re-entered Earth's atmosphere and parachuted to a landing in Kazakhstan, delivering approximately 101 grams of dark grey lunar soil. The material was later exchanged in part with Apollo samples for comparative study. Follow-on missions Luna 20 (1972) and Luna 24 (1976) repeated the feat.
