Extrusion

Extrusion is a process used to create objects of a fixed cross-sectional profile by pushing material through a die of the desired cross-section. Its two main advantages over other manufacturing processes are its ability to create very complex cross-sections and to work materials that are brittle, because the material only encounters compressive and shear stresses.

Process

The process begins with stock material, often a heated metal billet, placed in a container in the machine. The material is forced through the die by a hydraulic or mechanical ram. Extrusion may be hot or cold: hot extrusion is done at elevated temperature to keep the material workable, while cold extrusion is done at or near room temperature, producing stronger parts with tighter tolerances. Plastics extrusion uses a rotating screw to melt and convey polymer through a die, producing pipes, films, and profiles in a continuous process. Food extrusion is used for pasta, snacks, and pet food.

Materials and products

Aluminium is the most commonly extruded metal, used for window frames, structural sections, and heat sinks. Copper, lead, steel, and magnesium are also extruded. Extruded products include tubes, rods, rails, and hollow sections of great length.
