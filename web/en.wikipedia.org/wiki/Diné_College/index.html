Diné College

Diné College is a public tribal land-grant college in Tsaile, Arizona, serving the Navajo Nation. Founded in 1968 as Navajo Community College, it was the first tribally controlled and accredited collegiate institution in the United States, and it remains the flagship of the tribal college movement.

The college was established by the Navajo Tribal Council to provide higher education grounded in Navajo language, culture, and philosophy. Its main campus at Tsaile, near the Chuska Mountains, is noted for its distinctive architecture, including the six-story glass Hogan-style Ned A. Hatathli Center. Diné College operates additional centers across the Navajo Nation in Arizona, New Mexico, and Utah.

Academically, the college offers associate degrees and a small number of bachelor's programs, with coursework organized around the Diné educational philosophy of Sa'ah Naagháí Bik'eh Hózhóón — learning that proceeds through thinking, planning, living, and reflecting. It is a member of the American Indian Higher Education Consortium and is accredited by the Higher Learning Commission.
