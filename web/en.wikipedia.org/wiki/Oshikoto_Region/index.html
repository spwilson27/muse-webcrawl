Oshikoto Region

CountryNamibia
CapitalOmuthiya
Area~38,685 km²
Population~240,000 (2023 census, approx.)

The Oshikoto Region is one of the fourteen regions of Namibia. Its capital is Omuthiya, which replaced Tsumeb as the regional capital in 2008. The region takes its name from Lake Otjikoto, a sinkhole lake near Tsumeb — "oshikoto" referring to the lake in the Ndonga dialect of Oshiwambo.

Geography

Oshikoto lies in north-central Namibia. Its northern part falls within the densely settled Owambo basin, while the south is dominated by the mining town of Tsumeb and commercial farmland. The eastern edge of Etosha National Park, around the Namutoni rest camp and the King Nehale gate, lies within the region, as does Lake Otjikoto, where retreating German forces dumped weapons and equipment in 1915.

Economy and towns

Subsistence agriculture — mahangu (pearl millet), cattle and goats — supports much of the population in the north. Tsumeb, long a major copper-mining and smelting centre, anchors the south; other towns include Omuthiya, Oniipa, Onayena and Okankolo. The B1 highway crosses the region, linking Windhoek with the Angolan border.

Constituencies

The region is divided into eleven electoral constituencies, including Eengodi, Guinas, Okankolo, Olukonda, Omuthiya, Onayena, Oniipa, Onyaanya, Tsumeb, Nehale lyaMpingana and King Nehale.
