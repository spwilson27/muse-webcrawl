Today (BBC Radio 4)

Today, colloquially known as the Today programme, is BBC Radio 4's long-running early-morning news and current affairs programme. First broadcast on 28 October 1957, it is the station's most popular show and one of the most influential news programmes in Britain, with an audience of several million listeners.

Format

The programme airs from 6 to 9 am on weekdays (shorter on Saturdays) and combines news bulletins, interviews with politicians and public figures, sports coverage, weather, and the Thought for the Day religious reflection. Its interviews — including the flagship 8.10 am slot — frequently set the day's political agenda; presenters have included John Humphrys, James Naughtie, Evan Davis, Mishal Husain, Justin Webb, Amol Rajan and Emma Barnett.

History

Originally launched on the BBC Home Service as a light alternative to news, the programme grew in seriousness under editors such as Julian Holland and Rod Liddle, whose tenure brought controversies including the 2003 Gilligan broadcast that led to the Hutton Inquiry.
