Bowers Museum
Art museum in Santa Ana, California
Opened: 1936
Focus: world cultures, Native American and Pacific art

The Bowers Museum is an art museum in Santa Ana, California, founded in 1936 through a bequest from Charles W. Bowers, an Orange County land developer, and his wife Ada. Housed in a Mission Revival-style building, the museum is one of the largest in Orange County and is known for its exhibitions of world art and culture.

Collections and exhibitions

Permanent galleries cover the art of Native California, the ancient Americas, Oceania, Africa, and Asia, including the Gemstone Carvings gallery and exhibitions of Chinese art and culture. The museum has hosted popular travelling exhibitions, notably displays of the Terracotta Army of Xi'an and of treasures from the Forbidden City, which drew record crowds. An associated site, the Kidseum, provides hands-on children's programming.

History

After expansion and a major renovation completed in the 1990s, the museum grew to roughly ten times its original size. It operates as a city-owned facility managed by a nonprofit corporation and is a prominent cultural institution in Orange County.
