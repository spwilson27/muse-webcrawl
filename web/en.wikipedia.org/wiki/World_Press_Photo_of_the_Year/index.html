The World Press Photo of the Year is the top prize of the World Press Photo contest, organised by the independent World Press Photo Foundation in Amsterdam, Netherlands. The award honours the photographer whose single image, in the jury's judgement, best encapsulates an issue, event or trend of major journalistic significance from the preceding year.

History

The contest began in 1955, when Dutch photojournalists invited colleagues from other countries to enter an international competition. It has since grown into the world's most prestigious photojournalism contest, attracting tens of thousands of entries from more than a hundred countries each year. Winning photographs form a travelling exhibition that opens at the Nieuwe Kerk in Amsterdam and tours dozens of cities, accompanied by a yearbook.

Famous winning images

Iconic winners include the 1972 photograph of Phan Thị Kim Phúc fleeing a napalm attack in Vietnam (Nick Ut), the 1985 portrait of Afghan refugee Sharbat Gula by Steve McCurry (a National Geographic cover that won in its category era), the 2011 image of a veiled Yemeni woman holding an injured relative (Samuel Aranda), and Amber Bracken's 2021 photograph of crosses marking a former residential school site in Kamloops, Canada.

Categories

Alongside the Photo of the Year, the contest awards prizes in categories such as Contemporary Issues, Environment, General News, Long-Term Projects, Nature, Portraits, Sports and Spot News, restructured in 2022 into a regional model with global winners selected from regional category winners.
