Bible society

A Bible society is a non-profit organization, usually ecumenical in nature, devoted to translating, publishing, and distributing the Bible at affordable prices. Bible societies are typically non-denominational and aim to make the scriptures available to as many people as possible, in as many languages as possible, without doctrinal comment.

History

The modern Bible society movement began in the early 19th century. The British and Foreign Bible Society was founded in 1804, inspired in part by the story of Mary Jones, a Welsh girl who walked a long distance to buy a Bible in her own language. The American Bible Society followed in 1816. Similar societies spread across Europe and the world, often working alongside missionary movements.

United Bible Societies

In 1946, national Bible societies formed the United Bible Societies (UBS), a global fellowship that coordinates translation work and distribution. Bible societies have been instrumental in translating the Bible, in whole or in part, into thousands of languages, and in developing modern translation principles, braille editions, and audio scriptures.

Activities

Bible societies translate, print, and distribute Bibles, New Testaments, and scripture portions; support literacy programmes; and provide scriptures to churches, prisons, hospitals, and the armed forces, often at subsidised prices or free of charge.
