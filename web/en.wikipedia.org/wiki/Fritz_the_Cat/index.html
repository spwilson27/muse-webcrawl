Fritz the Cat is a comic strip created by Robert Crumb. Set in a "supercity" of anthropomorphic animals, the strip focuses on Fritz, a hedonistic, unemployed art-school dropout who drifts through a series of picaresque adventures in 1960s America, satirising college life, race relations, the counterculture and the political left of the period. The character first appeared in Help! magazine in January 1965 and became Crumb's most popular creation during the underground comix movement.

Film adaptation

In 1972, Fritz was adapted into an animated feature film directed by Ralph Bakshi, the first animated film to receive an X rating in the United States. Made independently of Crumb's wishes, it was a major box-office success, grossing over $90 million worldwide on a budget under $1 million, and spawned a sequel, The Nine Lives of Fritz the Cat (1974), made without Bakshi's involvement.

End of the strip

Crumb grew to resent the character's fame and the film adaptation, and in a story published in 1972 he had Fritz murdered by a jilted ex-girlfriend with an ice pick, effectively ending the strip. The collected stories have been reprinted in The Complete Fritz the Cat and remain among the best-known works of the underground comix era.
