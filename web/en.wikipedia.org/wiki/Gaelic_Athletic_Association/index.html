The Gaelic Athletic Association (GAA; Irish: Cumann Lúthchleas Gael [ˈkʊmˠən̪ˠ ˈl̪ˠuːθçlʲəsˠ ɡeːl̪ˠ]) is an Irish international amateur sporting and cultural organisation, focused primarily on promoting indigenous Gaelic games and pastimes, which include the traditional Irish sports of hurling, camogie, Gaelic football, Gaelic handball, and rounders. The association also promotes Irish music and dance, as well as the Irish language and other aspects of Irish culture.

It is the largest sporting organisation in Ireland, with over 500,000 members and more than 2,200 clubs. Games are administered on an all-Ireland basis, with structures at parish, county, provincial and national levels.

History

The GAA was founded on 1 November 1884 at a meeting in Hayes' Hotel, Thurles, County Tipperary, called by Michael Cusack, a teacher and nationalist from County Clare, and Maurice Davin, an athlete. The founders sought to preserve and promote traditional Irish games such as hurling and Gaelic football, which were declining under the influence of English sports like soccer, rugby and cricket. Archbishop Thomas Croke of Cashel became the association's first patron, and Croke Park in Dublin — purchased in 1913 — bears his name.

Structure

The GAA is a democratic organisation structured on the traditional parishes and counties of Ireland. Each member club elects officers; clubs elect delegates to county boards, which send delegates to provincial councils and to the annual Congress, the association's supreme legislative body. The President (Uachtarán) serves a three-year term. Players at all levels traditionally retain amateur status — no player, manager or official is paid to play.

Competitions

The flagship competitions are the All-Ireland Senior Hurling Championship and the All-Ireland Senior Football Championship, whose finals at Croke Park each September are among the largest sporting events in Ireland. County teams contest provincial championships leading to the All-Ireland series; clubs compete in county, provincial and All-Ireland club championships.

Rule 42 and Croke Park

For many years the GAA's rules barred "foreign games" — notably soccer and rugby — from being played at GAA grounds (the ban on members playing or watching such games was lifted in 1971, but the ground-use rule, Rule 42, remained). In 2005 the rule was relaxed, allowing Ireland's rugby and soccer internationals to be played at Croke Park during the redevelopment of Lansdowne Road.
