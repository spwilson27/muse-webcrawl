Maurice of Nassau

Maurice of Nassau, Prince of Orange (Dutch: Maurits van Nassau; 14 November 1567 – 23 April 1625) was stadtholder of the United Provinces of the Netherlands from 1585 until his death. The second son of William the Silent, he is remembered as one of the great military reformers of early modern Europe and the commander who consolidated the Dutch Republic during the Eighty Years' War against Spain.

Early life and appointment

Maurice was born at Dillenburg Castle in Germany. After the assassination of his father in 1584, he was appointed stadtholder of Holland and Zeeland in 1585, aged only seventeen, and later of Utrecht, Guelders and Overijssel. Working in political partnership with the land's advocate Johan van Oldenbarnevelt, he took command of the Dutch States Army.

Military reforms

With his cousin William Louis of Nassau-Dillenburg, Maurice systematically reformed the army, drawing on classical Roman models studied at Leiden University. His reforms included smaller, more manoeuvrable tactical units; volley fire by rotating ranks of musketeers; rigorous drill and discipline; standardised weapons; regular pay; and the professionalisation of siege warfare and military engineering. These reforms turned the Dutch army into a model studied across Europe.

Campaigns

In the "Ten Glory Years" of the 1590s Maurice captured Breda (by the famous ruse of the peat boat, 1590), Zutphen, Deventer, Nijmegen, Steenwijk, Coevorden, Groningen (1594), and the eastern fortresses in 1597. In 1600 he won the Battle of Nieuwpoort in Flanders, a rare pitched battle of the war, though the campaign's strategic aims failed. He defended Ostend's long siege (1601–1604) indirectly by taking 's-Hertogenbosch's outposts and Sluis. The Twelve Years' Truce (1609–1621) effectively recognised Dutch independence.

Later years

During the Truce, religious and political conflict between Maurice and Oldenbarnevelt — aligned with the Remonstrant and Counter-Remonstrant disputes — ended with Oldenbarnevelt's arrest and execution in 1619 and Maurice's ascendancy. War resumed in 1621; Maurice died in 1625 during the siege of Breda, which fell to Spinola shortly afterwards. He was succeeded by his half-brother Frederick Henry.
