World's Strongest Man competition

The World's Strongest Man is an international strongman competition held every year. Organised by American event management company IMG, a subsidiary of Endeavor, it is broadcast in the US during summers and in the UK around Christmas time. Competitors qualify based on placing in the top three at the four to eight Giants Live events each year.

The concept was developed in 1977 by Scotsman David Webster and Dr Douglas Pope for CBS. The first contest was held at Universal Studios in California and featured competitors primarily from American football and bodybuilding backgrounds. Bruce Wilhelm won the first two editions.

Format

The competition traditionally consists of qualifying heats, in which groups of athletes compete across several events, with the top finishers progressing to a final. Classic events include the Atlas Stones, the deadlift, the Hercules Hold, the truck pull, the log lift and the Fingal's Fingers. Points are awarded per event, and the athlete with the highest total after the final events is crowned World's Strongest Man.

Notable champions

Poland's Mariusz Pudzianowski holds the record with five titles (2002, 2003, 2005, 2007, 2008). Iceland has produced multiple champions including Jón Páll Sigmarsson, Magnús Ver Magnússon and Hafþór Júlíus Björnsson, who won in 2018. Other multiple champions include Žydrūnas Savickas of Lithuania (four titles) and Brian Shaw of the United States (four titles). Tom Stoltman of Scotland won in 2021 and 2022.
