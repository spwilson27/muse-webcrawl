Koninklijke Philips N.V., commonly shortened to Philips, is a Dutch multinational conglomerate founded in Eindhoven in 1891. Once one of the largest electronics companies in the world, Philips has since refocused on health technology, divesting its historic lighting and consumer electronics businesses.

History

Gerard Philips and his father Frederik founded the company to manufacture incandescent lamps and other electrical products. The firm grew rapidly, employing Anton Philips, who expanded its international sales, and it became closely associated with the city of Eindhoven, where it sponsored the PSV Eindhoven football club (founded as a works team in 1913).

Philips was a major innovator throughout the twentieth century. Its research laboratory, the NatLab, produced fundamental work in physics and electronics. The company introduced the rotary electric shaver (the Philishave), co-developed the Compact Cassette with its debut in 1963, the Compact Disc with Sony (launched 1982), and played a central role in the development of DVD and Blu-ray formats. During the Second World War the company operated under German occupation; Frits Philips, the last family member in management, is remembered for helping to save Jewish employees.

Refocus on healthcare

Beginning in the 2000s, Philips reshaped itself around healthcare. Its lighting division was spun off (eventually becoming Signify), and its consumer electronics operations were licensed or sold. The company acquired medical technology firms such as Respironics and Volcano Corporation, building leading positions in diagnostic imaging, image-guided therapy, patient monitoring, and sleep and respiratory care. In 2021 Philips recalled millions of Respironics sleep apnoea and ventilator devices over concerns about degrading sound-abatement foam, a crisis that led to major litigation and a large settlement in the United States.

Operations

Headquartered in Amsterdam, Philips organises its activities into segments including Diagnosis & Treatment, Connected Care, and Personal Health, the latter retaining well-known products such as Sonicare toothbrushes and Avent mother-and-child care products.
