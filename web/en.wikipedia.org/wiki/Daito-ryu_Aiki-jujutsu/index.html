Daito-ryu Aiki-jujutsu

Daitō-ryū Aiki-jūjutsu (大東流合気柔術) is a Japanese martial art first widely taught in the early 20th century by Takeda Sōkaku. It claims descent from arts transmitted within the Takeda family of the Aizu domain, tracing its legendary origins to Shinra Saburō Minamoto no Yoshimitsu in the 11th century.

The art's curriculum includes jujutsu joint locks and throws, aiki-no-jutsu techniques that use timing and body alignment rather than force, and weapons work. Its techniques are organised in scrolls (such as the Hiden Mōroku) conferred with licences of transmission.

Influence

Several of Takeda's students founded major arts, most notably Morihei Ueshiba, founder of aikido; other derivatives include hakko-ryu and Korean hapkido, whose founder Choi Yong-sool studied with Takeda.
