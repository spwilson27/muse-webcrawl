Easter egg (media)

An Easter egg is a message, image, or feature hidden in a video game, film, book, or other media. The term was coined around 1979 by Steve Wright of Atari to describe a hidden message in the video game Adventure (1980), in reference to an Easter egg hunt. In Adventure, programmer Warren Robinett secretly embedded his name in the game because Atari did not credit developers at the time; a player who found a specific invisible pixel could reveal the text "Created by Warren Robinett."

Examples and uses

Easter eggs now appear across media: hidden levels and developer rooms in games, concealed jokes in software (such as flight simulators hidden in spreadsheet programs), post-credits scenes and background details in films, and secret messages in albums and DVDs. Though usually harmless jokes or tributes, Easter eggs have also drawn scrutiny when they concealed unappreciated content or security risks, and some software organisations prohibit them because undocumented code complicates testing and trust.
