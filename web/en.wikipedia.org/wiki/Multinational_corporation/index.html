Multinational corporation

[Image: a world map graphic with glowing lines linking corporate offices across continents]

A multinational corporation (MNC) or transnational corporation is a company that owns or controls production or services in more than one country, coordinating subsidiaries from a home-country headquarters. MNCs — Apple, Toyota, Nestlé, Unilever, Samsung and thousands of others — account for a large share of world trade, much of it intra-firm, and are the chief agents and symbols of Globalization.

Operation and controversy

Firms expand abroad through foreign direct investment for markets, resources, efficiency or strategic assets, organising global supply chains that became politically salient after COVID-19 disruptions. Their scale raises persistent questions: transfer pricing and tax avoidance (addressed by the OECD's global minimum corporate tax, agreed 2021), bargaining power over host governments, labour and environmental standards in supplier factories, and cultural influence. Home governments promote their champions; host countries court their investment while regulating it. The largest MNCs' revenues rival the GDP of mid-sized countries — a comparison economists qualify, since GDP measures value added, not sales.
