Sudbury, Suffolk

Sudbury (/ˈsʌdbəri/) is a market town in the south of Suffolk, England, on the River Stour near the Essex border, about 60 miles (97 km) north-east of London. At the 2021 census its population was around 13,600. Sudbury was the birthplace of the artist Thomas Gainsborough and is surrounded by the countryside famously painted by him and by John Constable, an area often marketed as "Constable Country."

History

Sudbury is recorded in the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle for 797 as Suðberie ("south borough"), a market settlement on the Stour, and appears in the Domesday Book of 1086. In the later Middle Ages it prospered on the wool and cloth trade, enriched by Flemish weavers who settled in the town; its wealth built its large parish churches, St Gregory's, St Peter's, and All Saints'. Simon of Sudbury, Archbishop of Canterbury, was born here and was beheaded during the Peasants' Revolt of 1381. Silk weaving later became a lasting local industry — Sudbury remains a centre of British silk production — and the Stour's navigation supported maltings, mills, and brickworks.

Landmarks and culture

Gainsborough's House, the Georgian townhouse where Thomas Gainsborough was born in 1727, is now a national museum and art gallery dedicated to the artist, reopened after major redevelopment in 2022. The town's water meadows along the Stour, common lands grazed for centuries, survive as a distinctive landscape at its edge. St Peter's Church on Market Hill now serves as an arts and community venue. Sudbury is the terminus of a branch railway line from Marks Tey, marketed as the Gainsborough Line.
