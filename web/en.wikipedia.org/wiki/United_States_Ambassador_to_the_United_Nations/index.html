The United States ambassador to the United Nations is the leader of the U.S. delegation to the United Nations and head of the United States Mission to the United Nations. The position is formally known as the "Permanent Representative of the United States of America to the United Nations, with the rank and status of Ambassador Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary, and Representative of the United States of America in the Security Council of the United Nations."

Role

The ambassador represents the United States in the UN Security Council, where the U.S. holds a permanent seat and veto power, and in plenary sessions of the General Assembly. The post has often been held by prominent political figures rather than career diplomats, and under several administrations it has carried cabinet rank. The ambassador is assisted by a deputy ambassador and by specialized representatives for management and reform, political affairs, and other portfolios. The mission is headquartered at 799 United Nations Plaza in New York City.

History

The first permanent representative was Edward Stettinius Jr., the former Secretary of State who had chaired the American delegation at the 1945 San Francisco Conference. Subsequent notable holders have included Henry Cabot Lodge Jr., Adlai Stevenson II — who famously confronted the Soviet ambassador during the Cuban Missile Crisis in 1962 — Daniel Patrick Moynihan, Jeane Kirkpatrick, Madeleine Albright, John Negroponte, John Bolton, Zalmay Khalilzad, Susan Rice, Samantha Power, and Nikki Haley.

Cabinet status

Whether the ambassador sits in the President's cabinet has varied by administration. Presidents Clinton, Obama, and Biden afforded the role cabinet rank; other administrations have not. Regardless of cabinet status, the ambassador serves at the pleasure of the President and is a principal voice of U.S. foreign policy in international forums.
