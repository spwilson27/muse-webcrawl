Norm (mathematics)

In mathematics, a norm is a function from a real or complex vector space to the non-negative real numbers that behaves like a notion of length or size. A vector space equipped with a norm is a normed vector space.

Definition

A norm on a vector space V over a field of real or complex numbers is a function p : V → ℝ satisfying, for all scalars a and vectors u, v:

  • Positive definiteness: p(v) ≥ 0, and p(v) = 0 only if v = 0.
  • Absolute homogeneity: p(av) = |a| p(v).
  • Triangle inequality (subadditivity): p(u + v) ≤ p(u) + p(v).

A function satisfying all but positive definiteness is a seminorm.

Examples

The familiar Euclidean length in ℝn is the 2-norm. More generally the p-norms, (Σ|xi|p)1/p, include the taxicab (1-norm) and, in the limit, the maximum norm. Norms on matrices, function spaces (such as Lp spaces), and the absolute value on ℝ or ℂ are further examples. Every norm induces a metric, d(u, v) = p(u − v), making a normed space a topological vector space.
