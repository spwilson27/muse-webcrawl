Electronic funds transfer at point of sale


A card payment terminal used for EFTPOS transactions

Electronic funds transfer at point of sale (EFTPOS) is a payment system which allows the use of payment cards at payment terminals located at points of sale. The customer inserts or taps a debit card or credit card, the terminal communicates with the cardholder's bank to verify funds and authorize the transaction, and the amount is transferred from the customer's account to the merchant's account.

How it works

When a purchase is made, the terminal reads the card's chip, token, or magnetic stripe, and the customer authenticates with a personal identification number (PIN) or signature. An authorization request travels through the acquiring bank and card network to the issuing bank, which checks the balance and fraud rules and returns an approval or decline, usually within a few seconds. Settlement of funds between banks typically occurs in a batch later the same day or the next business day.

Regional systems

The term EFTPOS is especially common in Australia and New Zealand, where the domestic debit scheme is branded eftpos (lowercase). In Australia the system was launched in the mid-1980s and became a near-universal feature of retail. New Zealand claims one of the world's highest rates of EFTPOS usage per capita, with the system introduced there around 1985. Other countries operate equivalent domestic debit systems, such as Interac in Canada and girocard in Germany.

Developments

Contactless payments using near-field communication, mobile wallets, and QR-code payments have extended the basic card-present model, while security has been strengthened by the EMV chip standard and tokenization.
