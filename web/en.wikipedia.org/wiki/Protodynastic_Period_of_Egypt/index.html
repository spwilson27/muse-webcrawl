The Protodynastic Period of Egypt, also called Dynasty 0, refers to the final phase of the Predynastic period, roughly 3200–3000 BCE, during which the earliest Egyptian kings ruled immediately before the unification of Upper and Lower Egypt and the beginning of the First Dynasty. It corresponds broadly to the later part of the Naqada III archaeological culture.

Kings of Dynasty 0

Rulers known from inscriptions, seal impressions and royal tombs at Abydos include Iry-Hor, Ka, and "Scorpion" (Scorpion II), as well as possibly Crocodile and other shadowy kings. Their names are written in early serekhs — the palace-façade device that framed royal names — showing that the ideology of kingship was already formed before unification. Tomb U-j at Abydos (attributed to Scorpion I) contained some of the earliest known examples of Egyptian writing, bone and ivory tags dated to about 3320 BCE.

State formation

During this period rival Upper Egyptian polities centred on Abydos (Thinis), Naqada and Hierakonpolis competed and merged, extending control northward into the Nile Delta. Trade networks reached into the southern Levant (Canaan), where Egyptian pottery and administrative items are found, and into Nubia. Increasing social stratification, craft specialization, large-scale brewery and winery installations, and monumental mud-brick tombs mark the emergence of the pharaonic state.

Unification

The period culminates with Narmer, often identified with Menes, the traditional unifier and first king of the First Dynasty. The Narmer Palette from Hierakonpolis shows the king wearing both the White Crown of Upper Egypt and the Red Crown of Lower Egypt, symbolizing the "Union of the Two Lands". Debate continues over whether unification was a single military conquest or a gradual political process completed over several reigns.
