Luca Signorelli

Luca Signorelli (c. 1450 – 16 October 1523) was an Italian Renaissance painter from Cortona, particularly known for his ability as a draughtsman and his use of foreshortening. His masterpiece is considered to be the series of frescoes on the Last Judgment in Orvieto Cathedral, which influenced Michelangelo.

Life

Signorelli was born in Cortona, in Tuscany, and was probably a pupil of Piero della Francesca. He worked in many central Italian cities, including Perugia, Siena, Rome — where he contributed to the walls of the Sistine Chapel around 1481–1482 — and Florence, where he had connections with the Medici court. He remained active in his native Cortona, holding civic offices there, until late in life.

Works

His most celebrated achievement is the fresco cycle in the San Brizio Chapel of Orvieto Cathedral (1499–1504), depicting the Antichrist, the Resurrection of the Flesh, the Damned cast into Hell and the Elect in Paradise. The vigorous, anatomically exact nudes of these scenes were studied by Michelangelo before he painted his own Last Judgment.

Other notable works include the frescoes in the cloister of Monte Oliveto Maggiore illustrating the life of St Benedict, the Sant'Onofrio altarpiece in Perugia, and numerous Madonnas and portraits characterised by sculptural modelling and strong chiaroscuro.
