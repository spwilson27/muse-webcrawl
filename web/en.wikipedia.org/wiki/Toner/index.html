Toner

TypePowder mixture
CompositionPolymer resin, carbon black or pigments, charge control additives, wax
Used inLaser printers, photocopiers
Particle sizeTypically 5–15 micrometres

Toner is a powder mixture used in laser printers and photocopiers to form the printed text and images on paper via the electrophotographic (xerographic) process. In its early form it consisted of a mix of carbon powder and iron oxide; modern toner particles are engineered composites of plastic polymer, pigments and additives, ground or chemically grown to a closely controlled size so that print quality, colour accuracy and fusing behaviour are consistent.

How it works

A photoconductor drum is electrostatically charged and exposed to light shaped by the page image; toner, given an opposite charge, adheres only to the areas retaining the latent image. The powder is transferred to paper and then fused by heated rollers, whose pressure and heat melt the polymer so it bonds to the paper fibres. Colour machines use cyan, magenta, yellow and black (CMYK) toners. Unlike inkjet ink, toner does not dry out, and prints resist water, but toner cartridges represent a large share of a printer's running costs.

Manufacture and handling

Conventional toner is made by compounding and pulverising; "chemical" or polymerised toner is grown in emulsion, giving rounder, smaller particles and sharper images. Toner is fine enough to be an irritant; spills should not be vacuumed with ordinary domestic cleaners because the particles can pass through filters and the static charge creates a hazard. Waste toner is generally treated as controlled waste, and many manufacturers run cartridge recycling programmes.
