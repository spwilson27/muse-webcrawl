MacGillycuddy's Reeks

MacGillycuddy's Reeks (Irish: Na Cruacha Dubha, "the black stacks") is a sandstone and siltstone mountain range in the Iveragh Peninsula in County Kerry, Ireland. Stretching slightly over 19 kilometres (12 miles), it includes the highest peaks in Ireland: Carrauntoohil (1,038.6 m), Beenkeragh (1,008.2 m) and Caher (1,000 m), along with more than 100 other named summits over 600 metres.

Name and geology

The range takes its name from the MacGillycuddy clan, historically one of the principal families of the area, whose head was known as the MacGillycuddy of the Reeks. The mountains were formed of Old Red Sandstone during the Devonian period and were later sculpted by glaciation, producing sharp arêtes, corries, and U-shaped valleys such as the Hag's Glen, a popular approach route for climbing Carrauntoohil.

Recreation and conservation

The Reeks are among Ireland's most popular hillwalking areas. Much of the range lies within the MacGillycuddy's Reeks European Site and related conservation designations, and parts adjoin Killarney National Park. Access has historically been over private farmland, a matter of ongoing discussion between landowners and recreational users.
