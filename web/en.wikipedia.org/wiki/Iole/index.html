Iole

In Greek mythology, Iole was a princess of Oechalia, the daughter of King Eurytus and Antioche. Renowned for her beauty, she became the unwitting cause of the death of the hero Heracles.

Myth

Eurytus, who had taught Heracles archery, promised Iole's hand to whoever could defeat him and his sons in an archery contest. Heracles won but was refused the prize, Eurytus fearing the hero's madness. Later, after Iphitus, one of Eurytus's sons, was killed, Heracles returned, sacked Oechalia, slew Eurytus and his remaining sons, and took Iole captive.

Death of Heracles

Heracles intended to marry Iole, but his wife Deianeira, fearing displacement, sent him a tunic smeared with the blood of the centaur Nessus, believing it a love charm. The blood proved a deadly poison; in agony Heracles built his own funeral pyre on Mount Oeta and died, bequeathing Iole to his son Hyllus, who later married her. The story is told in Sophocles' tragedy "Women of Trachis" and in Ovid's "Metamorphoses".
