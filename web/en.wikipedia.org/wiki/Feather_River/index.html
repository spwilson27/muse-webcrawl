Feather River
SourceSierra Nevada, California
MouthSacramento River, at Verona
Length~114 km (71 mi) (main stem)
Basin size~16,600 km²

The Feather River is the principal tributary of the Sacramento River, in the Sacramento Valley of Northern California. Its main stem is about 71 miles (114 km) long, formed below Oroville Dam by forks draining the northern Sierra Nevada; its length to its most distant headwater tributary exceeds 210 miles (340 km). The river drains the Feather River Canyon, famous for gold rush history and the Western Pacific Railroad's scenic route.

Course and dams

The North, Middle, South, and West Branch forks converge in and around Lake Oroville, impounded by Oroville Dam — the tallest earthfill dam in the United States and the centrepiece of the California State Water Project. Below the dam the river flows south past Oroville, Marysville, and Yuba City to join the Sacramento River at Verona.

History and ecology

The basin was a major centre of the California gold rush, including hydraulic mining whose debris later raised the riverbed and worsened flooding. Today the river supports important Chinook salmon and steelhead runs and supplies water to farms and cities statewide.
