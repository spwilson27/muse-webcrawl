Hydroxocobalamin

Hydroxocobalamin is a form of vitamin B12 produced by bacteria and used medically to treat vitamin B12 deficiency and cyanide poisoning. It is one of the cobalamins, in which a cobalt atom coordinated within a corrin ring bears a hydroxyl group as its upper ligand. In the body it is converted to the active coenzyme forms methylcobalamin and adenosylcobalamin.

Medical uses

For B12 deficiency — including pernicious anemia and deficiency following gastric surgery — hydroxocobalamin is given by intramuscular injection and is preferred over cyanocobalamin in some countries because it is retained in the body longer, allowing less frequent dosing. As an antidote (sold as Cyanokit), high-dose intravenous hydroxocobalamin binds cyanide ions to form cyanocobalamin, which is excreted in urine; it is a first-line treatment for cyanide poisoning from smoke inhalation and other causes. A characteristic side effect is temporary red discoloration of the skin and urine, and it can interfere with some laboratory tests and dialysis machines.
