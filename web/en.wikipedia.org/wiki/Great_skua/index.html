The great skua (Stercorarius skua), known in Scotland as the bonxie, is a large seabird in the skua family Stercorariidae. It breeds in Iceland, Norway, the Faroe Islands, and the Scottish islands, with a few pairs on mainland Scotland, and winters out at sea in the Atlantic Ocean and occasionally reaches the Mediterranean.

Description

The great skua is 50–58 cm long with a 125–140 cm wingspan. Adults are streaked greyish brown with a black cap, stout hooked bill, and conspicuous white wing flashes. It is an aggressive pirate of the seas, deliberately harassing gulls, terns, and even gannets to steal their catches (kleptoparasitism), and it also kills and eats other seabirds, including puffins and, occasionally, birds as large as herring gulls.

Behaviour and conservation

Great skuas breed colonially on coastal moorland and islands, laying two eggs and vigorously defending nests against intruders, including humans. Population numbers crashed in 2022–2023 after the species was heavily affected by the highly pathogenic avian influenza (H5N1) outbreak, with some colonies losing more than half of breeding adults.
