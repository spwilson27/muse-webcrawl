Management

[Image: a manager leading a team meeting around a whiteboard of plans]

Management is the administration of an organisation — business, non-profit or public body — including the activities of setting strategy and coordinating the efforts of staff to accomplish objectives through available resources. 'Managers do things right; leaders do the right things' runs a common distinction (attributed to Bennis, after Drucker).

Theories and functions

Classical theory began with Frederick Taylor's scientific management (1911) — time study and standardisation on the shop floor — and Henri Fayol's administrative functions (planning, organising, commanding, coordinating, controlling), later codified as planning, organising, leading and controlling. The Hawthorne studies (1920s–30s) launched the human-relations school, emphasising groups and attention; Peter Drucker made management a general social discipline (The Practice of Management, 1954; management by objectives). Modern practice spans strategy, operations, Marketing, people management and finance, taught in MBA programmes and endlessly debated — lean production, total quality, agile methods — while empirical research links management quality to national productivity differences.
