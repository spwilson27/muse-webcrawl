Valley of Mexico

LocationCentral Mexico
Elevationc. 2,240 m (7,350 ft) above sea level
Principal cityMexico City
Historic lakesTexcoco, Xochimilco, Chalco, Zumpango, Xaltocan

The Valley of Mexico (Spanish: Valle de México) is a highland plateau in central Mexico, roughly 9,600 square kilometres in area, surrounded by mountains and volcanoes of the Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt. Lying at over 2,200 metres above sea level, it has been the demographic and political heart of Mexico since pre-Columbian times and today contains Mexico City and its vast metropolitan area.

Geography and hydrology

The valley is an endorheic basin with no natural outlet. In pre-Hispanic times it held a connected system of shallow lakes — Texcoco (saline), Xochimilco, Chalco, Zumpango, and Xaltocan — on which the Mexica built their capital, Tenochtitlan, with causeways, dikes, and chinampa gardens. After the Spanish conquest, colonial and later governments drained the lakes through canals and tunnels to control flooding; the modern city now faces subsidence as the lakebed clays compact, plus chronic water shortages.

History

The valley hosted some of Mesoamerica's greatest cities, including Cuicuilco, Teotihuacan, and later the Aztec capital Tenochtitlan, before Mexico City was built on its ruins. The surrounding peaks include the volcanoes Popocatépetl and Iztaccíhuatl to the southeast.

Environment

The basin's geography traps air pollution over the metropolis, a famous environmental problem that has improved since the 1990s through emissions controls. Remnant wetlands at Xochimilco preserve part of the pre-Hispanic chinampa landscape and are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
