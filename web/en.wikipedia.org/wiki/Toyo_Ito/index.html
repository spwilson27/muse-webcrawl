Toyo Ito

Toyo Ito (伊東 豊雄, Itō Toyoo; born 1 June 1941) is a Japanese architect known for fusing the physical and virtual worlds in buildings of lightness, fluidity, and structural daring. He received the Pritzker Architecture Prize in 2013, recognised as a creator of timeless buildings and a mentor to generations of younger architects, including Kazuyo Sejima and Ryue Nishizawa of SANAA.

Career

Ito graduated from the University of Tokyo in 1965 and worked for Kiyonori Kikutake before founding his own office in 1971, first called Urban Robot and then Toyo Ito & Associates. His early houses — the Aluminium House and his own White U (1976), a U-shaped concrete shell enclosing a courtyard for his widowed sister — explored sealed, inward-looking forms against the chaos of Tokyo.

Sendai and after

The Sendai Mediatheque (2001) made Ito world-famous: seven glass plates of floors threaded by thirteen lattice "tubes" of bundled steel columns, swaying like seaweed — a radically transparent public building that survived the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake with little damage. Later works include the serpentine Serpentine Gallery Pavilion in London (2002, with Cecil Balmond), the Tama Art University Library in Tokyo (2007), whose concrete arches grow from the ground like a forest, the Tod's Omotesando building wrapped in a concrete pattern of zelkova branches, and the National Taichung Theater in Taiwan (2016), a continuous cave-like surface of curved walls.

Later work and mentorship

After the 2011 disaster Ito led the "Home-for-All" project, building communal houses for displaced communities in Tōhoku. The Pritzker jury noted that throughout his career Ito had "developed and refined an architecture that is both personal and forward-looking" while generously nurturing younger talent through his office and his architecture museum on Ōmishima island.
