Chinese literature
Language: Chinese (Classical and vernacular)
Period: c. 13th century BCE – present
Major forms: Poetry, historiography, novel, drama, essay

Chinese literature extends over thousands of years, from oracle-bone inscriptions and the earliest poetry anthologies to the great Ming and Qing novels and the modern literature of the twentieth and twenty-first centuries. It is one of the world's oldest continuous literary traditions.

Classical literature

The Classic of Poetry (Shijing), the Book of Documents, and the works of Confucius and Mencius formed the Confucian canon. Historiography was exemplified by Sima Qian's Records of the Grand Historian. Tang dynasty poetry — Li Bai, Du Fu, Wang Wei — is often regarded as a golden age, followed by Song ci lyrics and Yuan drama.

The novel

The Four Great Classical Novels are Romance of the Three Kingdoms, Water Margin, Journey to the West, and Dream of the Red Chamber by Cao Xueqin, the last often considered the pinnacle of Chinese fiction.

Modern literature

The May Fourth Movement promoted vernacular baihua writing. Lu Xun, author of The True Story of Ah Q, is regarded as the founder of modern Chinese literature. Later figures include Ba Jin, Lao She, Mo Yan (Nobel laureate, 2012) and contemporary science-fiction writer Liu Cixin.
