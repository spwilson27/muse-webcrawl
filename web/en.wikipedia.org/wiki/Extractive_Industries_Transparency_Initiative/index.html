Extractive Industries Transparency Initiative

The Extractive Industries Transparency Initiative (EITI) is a global standard for the good governance of oil, gas and mineral resources. It seeks to address the key governance issues in the extractive sectors and is implemented by more than 50 countries, whose governments commit to disclosing information along the extractive industry value chain — from how extraction rights are awarded to how revenues travel through the government.

The EITI standard covers oil, gas and mining revenues in implementing countries.

History

The initiative grew out of the "Publish What You Pay" campaign by NGOs and concern about the "resource curse" — the paradox that resource-rich countries often grow more slowly and govern worse than resource-poor ones. British prime minister Tony Blair announced the EITI at the World Summit on Sustainable Development in Johannesburg in 2002, and the first EITI conference was held in London in 2003. The International Secretariat opened in Oslo in 2007.

How it works

Implementing countries form multi-stakeholder groups of government, industry and civil society that oversee reporting. Companies disclose what they pay and governments disclose what they receive; the figures are reconciled by an independent administrator and published in EITI reports. The 2019 EITI Standard (updated in 2023) expanded requirements to systematic disclosure, beneficial ownership of companies, contract transparency, and environmental and gender-related reporting.

Assessment

Supporters credit the EITI with normalising revenue disclosure and informing public debate in resource-rich states. Critics note that transparency alone does not guarantee accountability, that some implementing countries restrict civil-society participation, and that validation and suspension decisions can be contentious.
