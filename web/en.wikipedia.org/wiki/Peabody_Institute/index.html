The Peabody Institute of the Johns Hopkins University is a music conservatory and research library in the Mount Vernon neighbourhood of Baltimore, Maryland. Founded in 1857 by the philanthropist George Peabody, it is the oldest continuously active conservatory of music in the United States, and its George Peabody Library is celebrated as one of the most beautiful library interiors in America.

History

George Peabody, a Massachusetts-born merchant banker who made his fortune in Baltimore and London, announced his gift of funds for an institute devoted to the "music, art, and literature" of Baltimore in 1857. Construction of the east wing, designed by Edmund G. Lind, was interrupted by the Civil War; the institute was dedicated in 1866, with Peabody himself attending. The con­servatory gave its first classes in 1868, and the library building — whose spectacular reading room rises five storeys of ornamented cast-iron balconies around a skylit central hall — opened in 1878. The collections hold some 300,000 volumes, strong in British and American literature, archaeology, and the history of science.

The conservatory quickly became a centre of American musical life; its orchestra and faculty series introduced Baltimore audiences to works performed elsewhere in the United States for the first time, and composers and virtuosos from Tchaikovsky to Rachmaninoff visited. In 1977 the financially independent Peabody affiliated with Johns Hopkins University, becoming its music and dance division; a 1985 agreement made the relationship permanent.

Today

Peabody enrols roughly 700 degree students in the Conservatory — in performance, composition, conducting, jazz, music education, recording arts, and dance (a BFA added in 2018) — and several thousand children and adults in its community Preparatory division, founded in 1894. Its graduates include pianist André Watts, composer Dominick Argento, and singer Jessye Norman studied in its orbit through master classes; alumni have won Grammy Awards and positions in major orchestras worldwide. The campus centres on its historic buildings around Mount Vernon Place, near the Walters Art Museum and the Washington Monument. In 2018 the institute announced the Breakthrough Curriculum, reorienting conservatory training toward community engagement and career flexibility, and launched a programme offering full-tuition scholarships for undergraduate and most graduate students beginning in the 2020s, funded by a major philanthropic gift.
