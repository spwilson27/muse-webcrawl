Thirteen Colonies

The Thirteen Colonies were a group of British colonies on the Atlantic coast of North America during the 17th and 18th centuries. Grievances against the imperial government led the 13 colonies to begin uniting in 1774, and they formed the Continental Congress, which effectively became their joint government. In 1776 the colonies declared their independence and formed the United States of America. The 13 colonies later became the 13 original US states.

Population and society

By 1775 the colonies had a population of roughly 2.4 million, including some 500,000 enslaved people of African descent. Colonists considered themselves British and enjoyed considerably more self-government than was common in the British Empire: each colony had an elected assembly, and local affairs were largely managed locally. Economies varied by region — New England traded and fished, the Middle Colonies farmed grain, and the Southern Colonies relied on plantation agriculture worked by enslaved labour.

Road to revolution

After the French and Indian War (1754–1763), Britain sought to tax the colonies to help pay for their defence, imposing the Stamp Act (1765) and the Townshend Acts (1767). Colonists resisted on the principle of "no taxation without representation". Escalating confrontations — the Boston Massacre (1770), the Boston Tea Party (1773) and the punitive Intolerable Acts (1774) — led the colonies to convene the First Continental Congress. Fighting began at Lexington and Concord in April 1775, and on 4 July 1776 the Second Continental Congress adopted the Declaration of Independence.
