Charles A. S. Hall

Charles A. S. Hall (born 1943) is an American systems ecologist and biophysical economist, best known for developing the concept of energy return on investment (EROI, also written EROEI) — the ratio of energy delivered by an energy source to the energy used to obtain it. He is professor emeritus at the State University of New York College of Environmental Science and Forestry (SUNY-ESF) in Syracuse.

Career

Hall studied marine biology and earned his PhD under Howard T. Odum at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, working within Odum's programme of systems ecology and energy analysis. He spent much of his career at SUNY-ESF, where he taught systems ecology and wrote textbooks including Quantifying Sustainable Development (with others) and Energy and the Wealth of Nations (2018, with Kent Klitgaard).

EROI

In the early 1980s Hall and colleagues applied ecological energetics to fuels, asking how much energy society gains from oil, coal, nuclear and renewable sources after subtracting the energy costs of extraction and processing. Their studies documented the decline in EROI of U.S. oil and gas production from roughly 100:1 in the 1930s to much lower values as the easiest resources were depleted. Hall argued that a complex society requires fuels with a minimum EROI of roughly 3:1 at the wellhead — and considerably more "at the point of use" — to maintain its infrastructure.

Biophysical economics

Hall is a leading figure in biophysical economics, which grounds economic analysis in the laws of thermodynamics and ecology rather than in monetary abstractions alone. With collaborators he developed the "cheetah" model of predator–prey energetics as a metaphor for fossil-fuel depletion, and has written on peak oil, sustainability and the relationship between energy surplus and economic growth.
