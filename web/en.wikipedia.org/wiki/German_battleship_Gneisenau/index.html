Gneisenau
Class: Scharnhorst-class battleship
Launched: 8 December 1936
Commissioned: 21 May 1938
Armament: 9 × 28 cm guns
Fate: Scuttled as a blockship, March 1945

Gneisenau was a German capital ship, alternatively described as a battleship and battlecruiser, of Nazi Germany's Kriegsmarine. She was the second vessel of the Scharnhorst class, named after the Prussian field marshal August von Gneisenau. Laid down in 1935 and launched in 1936, she displaced over 32,000 tons and was armed with nine 28 cm (11-inch) guns—smaller than those of contemporary foreign battleships but compensated by heavy armour protection and high speed of about 31 knots.

Career

With her sister ship Scharnhorst, Gneisenau operated in the Atlantic from late 1939, sinking the British armed merchant cruiser HMS Rawalpindi. In April 1940 the sisters covered the invasion of Norway (Operation Weserübung), duelling with the battlecruiser HMS Renown and later sinking the aircraft carrier HMS Glorious and her two escorting destroyers in June 1940. In early 1941, Operation Berlin took the pair into the Atlantic, where they sank or captured 22 merchant ships totalling about 116,000 tons, before putting into Brest, France.

At Brest the ships endured repeated RAF bombing. In February 1942 they made the famous "Channel Dash" (Operation Cerberus) back to Germany, during which Gneisenau struck a mine. Days after reaching Kiel, a bombing raid detonated her forward magazine, wrecking her bow and killing over a hundred crew. Plans to rebuild her with six 38 cm guns at Gdynia (Gotenhafen) were abandoned in 1943 after Hitler ordered the decommissioning of the big ships, and her armament was removed for coastal batteries.

Fate

In March 1945 the hulk was scuttled as a blockship in the harbour entrance at Gotenhafen. The wreck was raised and broken up by Poland after the war, around 1947–1951.
