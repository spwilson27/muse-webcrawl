Overview

Greenwich Mean Time (GMT) is the mean solar time at the Royal Observatory in Greenwich, London, long used as the world's time standard and the basis of the prime meridian of longitude (0°). Railways in Britain adopted it in the 1840s — 'railway time' — and an international conference in 1884 recommended Greenwich as the common zero of longitude and time reckoning.

Since 1972 civil time worldwide has been based on Coordinated Universal Time (UTC), which is kept within 0.9 seconds of the astronomical time scale derived from GMT. In everyday British usage GMT is still the name of the winter time zone in the United Kingdom, with British Summer Time (BST, UTC+1) in summer.
