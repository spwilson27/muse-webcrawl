The 2012 French presidential election was held on 22 April and 6 May 2012. Socialist candidate François Hollande defeated the incumbent president, Nicolas Sarkozy of the centre-right UMP, becoming the first Socialist president of France since François Mitterrand and only the second incumbent of the Fifth Republic to be voted out of office.

First round

Ten candidates stood. Hollande led the first round with 28.6% to Sarkozy's 27.2%. Marine Le Pen of the National Front achieved the far right's best-ever result at that time with 17.9%, ahead of Jean-Luc Mélenchon (Left Front, 11.1%) and centrist François Bayrou (9.1%).

Runoff

The campaign centred on the eurozone debt crisis, unemployment, taxation and immigration. Hollande campaigned as "Mr Normal", promised to renegotiate the EU fiscal compact toward growth, and proposed a 75% top income-tax rate on earnings over €1 million. Sarkozy sought support from Le Pen's voters with a hard line on immigration and security. In the televised debate and the runoff on 6 May, Hollande won with 51.64% of the vote.

Aftermath

Hollande was inaugurated on 15 May 2012, and the Socialist Party went on to win the June legislative elections, giving the new president a parliamentary majority.
