East Nusa Tenggara

East Nusa Tenggara (Indonesian: Nusa Tenggara Timur; abbreviated NTT) is a province of Indonesia, comprising the eastern part of the Lesser Sunda Islands. It consists of more than 500 islands, the largest being the western half of Timor, Flores, Sumba, and the Alor Archipelago. The provincial capital is Kupang, on West Timor. The province has a population of about 5.5 million and an area of roughly 47,900 km². It borders East Timor, which occupies the eastern half of the island of Timor.

Geography

East Nusa Tenggara is the driest province in Indonesia, with a long dry season dominated by hot winds from Australia. Its volcanic arc islands include Flores, home to the three coloured crater lakes of Kelimutu, and Rinca and Komodo islands, part of Komodo National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site famous for the Komodo dragon. The province's waters, including the Savu Sea, are rich in marine biodiversity.

People and economy

The population is ethnically and linguistically diverse, including Atoni, Manggarai, Sumbanese, and Lamaholot peoples, and is unusual in Indonesia for being majority Christian (Catholic and Protestant), a legacy of Portuguese and Dutch contact. The economy is largely agricultural — maize, coffee, and livestock — supplemented by seaweed farming, fishing, sandalwood, and growing tourism centred on Labuan Bajo and Komodo. The province remains among Indonesia's least developed, with infrastructure and drought major challenges.
