Adam in Islam

Adam (Arabic: آدم, romanized: ʾĀdam) is believed by Muslims to be the first human being and the first prophet of Islam. In the Quran, God (Allah) creates Adam from clay, breathes life into him, teaches him "the names of all things," and appoints him as His vicegerent (khalifa) on Earth. Adam's wife, Hawwa (Eve), is his companion in the Garden.

In the Quran

The Quran recounts Adam's story in several chapters (notably Al-Baqara, Al-A'raf, Ta-Ha, and Sad). God announces to the angels His intention to place a vicegerent on Earth; when He creates Adam and teaches him the names, the angels are commanded to prostrate before him. All obey except Iblis (Satan), who refuses out of pride, arguing that he, made of fire, is superior to a being made of clay, and is expelled.

Adam and his wife dwell in the Garden but are forbidden a particular tree. Deceived by Satan's whispers, they eat from it, their nakedness becomes apparent to them, and they are sent down to Earth. Unlike in the Christian doctrine of original sin, Adam's lapse is met with repentance and divine forgiveness: God teaches Adam words of prayer and accepts his repentance, promising guidance for humanity. His sons include Cain and Abel (Qabil and Habil), whose story is told in Surah Al-Ma'idah.

In tradition

Hadith and later Islamic literature elaborate on Adam's creation on a Friday, his stature, his descent at Adam's Peak in Sri Lanka (in some traditions), and his role as the father of mankind (Abu al-Bashar). Adam is counted the first of the prophets, followed by Noah, Abraham, Moses, Jesus, and Muhammad. Sufi writers developed rich interpretations of Adam as the archetype of the perfected human being.
