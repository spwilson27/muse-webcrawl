Llŷn Peninsula

LocationGwynedd, north-west Wales
AreaApprox. 400 km² (150 sq mi)
DesignationArea of Outstanding Natural Beauty (part)

The Llŷn Peninsula (Welsh: Penrhyn Llŷn) extends about 48 km (30 miles) into the Irish Sea from north-west Wales, south-west of the island of Anglesey. It lies within the county of Gwynedd and forms part of the historic county of Caernarfonshire. Much of the peninsula's coastline is designated an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty.

Geography

The peninsula is bounded by Caernarfon Bay to the north, Cardigan Bay to the south, and the Menai Strait to the east. Its landscape is a patchwork of small fields, scattered farms, and volcanic hills such as Yr Eifl. Principal settlements include Pwllheli, Nefyn, Abersoch, Criccieth, and Aberdaron at the tip, beyond which lies Bardsey Island (Ynys Enlli).

Culture and language

Llŷn is one of the strongholds of the Welsh language, with a high proportion of Welsh speakers. The peninsula has a strong tradition of Welsh literature and Nonconformist chapels, and pilgrims historically travelled to Bardsey Island, known as the island of 20,000 saints.

Economy

Farming, fishing, and tourism are the main industries. Abersoch is a popular sailing and watersports resort, while much of the coast is protected for its scenery and wildlife.
