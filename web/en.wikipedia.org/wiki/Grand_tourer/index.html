Grand tourer

A grand tourer (from Italian gran turismo), often abbreviated GT, is a type of high-performance car designed for high-speed, long-distance driving. Grand tourers combine the performance of a sports car with the refinement, comfort, and luggage capacity needed for continental touring, typically in a front-engined, rear-wheel-drive two-door coupé body with a 2+2 seating arrangement.

Characteristics

Unlike a pure sports car, which prioritises lightness and handling, a grand tourer is generally larger and heavier, with a powerful engine — often a V8 or V12 — a compliant ride, a luxurious interior, and amenities for relaxed cruising at sustained high speeds. The emphasis is on covering great distances quickly and comfortably rather than on lap times.

History

The concept descends from the fast touring cars of 1930s Europe, built for events such as the Mille Miglia. The term gained currency after the Second World War with Italian cars such as the 1947 Alfa Romeo 6C 2500 and the early Ferraris. Iconic grand tourers include the Aston Martin DB5 and DB11, the Jaguar E-Type and XK, the Bentley Continental GT, the Mercedes-Benz S-Class Coupé and SL, the Maserati GranTurismo, and the Ferrari Roma and 812 Superfast.

GT in racing

"GT" also denotes production-based racing categories — including GT3 and GT4 — in which modified road-going sports cars compete in endurance and sprint series worldwide, and the Gran Turismo racing class of the Le Mans 24 Hours.
