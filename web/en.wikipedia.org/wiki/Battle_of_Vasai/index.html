The Battle of Vasai (also known as the Battle of Bassein) was a campaign fought between the Maratha Empire and the Portuguese rulers of Vasai (Bassein), a fortified town north of Mumbai on India's west coast. It culminated in the Maratha capture of Vasai Fort in May 1739.

Background

The Portuguese had held Vasai since 1534 and made it the capital of their northern province in India, with a wealthy fortified settlement and churches inside its walls. Under Peshwa Bajirao I, the Marathas sought to expel the Portuguese from the northern Konkan. After preliminary operations took Thane, Salsette Island and other positions in 1737, the Marathas under Bajirao's brother Chimaji Appa laid siege to Vasai itself.

The siege

The siege was long and costly. Maratha forces reduced surrounding forts, cut supplies, and mined the walls; one mine explosion that breached the defences also killed many Maratha troops when the Portuguese fired into the confusion. Disease and monsoon conditions afflicted both sides. After months of pressure and with no relief possible, the Portuguese garrison capitulated in May 1739; the surviving defenders were permitted to withdraw, and the Maratha flag was raised over Vasai Fort on 16 May 1739 (dates vary slightly by source).

Aftermath

The victory ended two centuries of Portuguese power in the northern Konkan, leaving them with only Goa, Daman and Diu in India. For the Maratha Empire it was a defining triumph, celebrated in Maharashtra to this day; the bells of Vasai's churches were carried off as trophies, one of which is famous at the Bhimashankar temple.
