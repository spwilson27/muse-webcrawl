The Battle off Samar was one of four major naval engagements of the Battle of Leyte Gulf in October 1944, and is frequently described as one of the greatest last stands in naval history. A small American task unit of escort carriers and their screen fought off a vastly superior Japanese surface force including the battleship Yamato.

Background

Under the Shō-Gō 1 plan, Vice Admiral Takeo Kurita's Center Force was to cross the Philippine archipelago and fall upon the American invasion shipping in Leyte Gulf. Kurita's force survived air attack in the Sibuyan Sea and passed the San Bernardino Strait unopposed at night, because Admiral William Halsey's fast carriers had been drawn north by a decoy force. At dawn on 25 October Kurita encountered Task Unit 77.4.3 — "Taffy 3" — six slow escort carriers with three destroyers and four destroyer escorts under Rear Admiral Clifton Sprague, supporting the Leyte landings.

The battle

Sprague ordered his carriers to launch every available aircraft and fled eastward into a rain squall while his destroyers made smoke and launched desperate torpedo counterattacks. The destroyers Johnston, Hoel and the destroyer escort Samuel B. Roberts charged the Japanese battle line, inflicting torpedo damage and drawing fire. Aircraft from Taffy 3 and the neighbouring Taffy 2 attacked continuously, sinking three heavy cruisers. Two escort carriers, Gambier Bay and (to a kamikaze attack later that day) St. Lo, were lost. After more than two hours, and with the Leyte anchorages nearly in reach, Kurita — exhausted, uncertain of American strength, and believing he faced fast fleet carriers — broke off the action and retired through San Bernardino Strait.

Legacy

The stand of Taffy 3 saved the Leyte beachhead from bombardment. The surviving crews received a Presidential Unit Citation, and the battle has been celebrated in numerous histories as an extraordinary mismatch overcome by determination and improvisation.
