Crossfader

A crossfader on a DJ mixer essentially serves as two faders connected side-by-side, but in opposite directions. It allows the DJ to fade one source out while fading another source in at the same time with a single control. This is extremely useful for DJing records (turntablism, beat juggling and cutting) and for seamless transitions between songs.

Design

Crossfaders may be implemented with analog potentiometers or, in modern digital mixers, as software controls. Battle mixers designed for scratch DJs feature crossfaders with very short cut-in distances and adjustable curve settings, ranging from a smooth gradual fade to a sharp "cut" in which the signal switches almost instantly at the ends of the fader's travel.

Techniques

The crossfader is central to turntablism: scratches such as the chirp, flare, crab and transformer are produced by rapidly opening and closing the crossfader while moving the record with the other hand. Club and radio DJs instead typically use longer curves for smooth blending between tracks.
