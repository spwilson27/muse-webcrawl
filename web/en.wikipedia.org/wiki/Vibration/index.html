Vibration

Vibration is a mechanical phenomenon whereby oscillations occur about an equilibrium point. The word comes from Latin vibrationem ("shaking, brandishing"). The oscillations may be periodic, such as the motion of a pendulum, or random, such as the movement of a tire on a gravel road.

Types

Free vibration occurs when a system with inertia and restoring force is displaced and released, oscillating at its natural frequency; damping gradually dissipates the energy. Forced vibration occurs when a time-varying disturbance — an unbalanced rotating machine, an engine, wind — acts continuously on a system. If the forcing frequency coincides with a natural frequency, resonance occurs and the amplitude can grow dangerously large; the collapse of the Tacoma Narrows Bridge in 1940 is the classic example. Damped vibration describes oscillations that lose energy over time through friction or other resistance.

Analysis and control

Vibration is modelled with mass, stiffness and damping elements and analysed using equations of motion and modal analysis. Engineers measure vibration with accelerometers and use the data to diagnose faults in rotating machinery, such as imbalance, misalignment and worn bearings. Control techniques include stiffening, damping treatments, vibration isolation mounts and tuned mass dampers used in tall buildings. Excessive vibration also affects health: whole-body vibration and hand-arm vibration are regulated occupational hazards.
