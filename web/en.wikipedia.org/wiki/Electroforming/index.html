Electroforming

Electroforming is a metal forming process in which a part is produced by electrodeposition of metal onto a conductive model, known as a mandrel or matrix. The deposited metal reproduces the surface of the mandrel with extreme fidelity; after deposition to the required thickness, the part is separated from the mandrel, which may be reusable or sacrificial.

Process

The mandrel is given the inverse (negative) form of the desired part and, if non-conductive, is made conductive with a coating. It is immersed in an electroplating bath — nickel and copper are the most common electroforming metals — and metal ions are deposited onto it under a controlled current density, often over hours or days. The finished shell is then removed. Because the deposit copies the mandrel surface at microscopic scale, electroforming achieves tolerances and surface detail beyond most other forming methods.

Applications

Electroforming is used to make moulds for lenses and compact discs, printing plates, fine meshes and foils, waveguides and bellows, rocket engine components, and precision parts for aerospace and medical devices. The LIGA process extends the technique to microstructures. It was historically used to produce printing plates from type (electrotypes) and matrices for vinyl records.
