History of Oman
Key periods: Magan civilisation; Ibadi imamate; Portuguese occupation of the coast (1507–1650); Al Said dynasty (1744– ); Sultanate

The history of Oman is among the oldest in Arabia. The region, known to Mesopotamians as Magan, was a major source of copper and a maritime trading partner of the Indus Valley in the Bronze Age. Islam arrived in the 7th century CE, and Oman adopted Ibadi Islam, electing its own imams.

Maritime empire

The Portuguese seized Muscat and the coast from 1507 until expelled in 1650. Under the Ya'rubi and then the Al Said dynasty (from 1744), Oman built a maritime empire reaching Zanzibar and the East African coast; Said bin Sultan moved his capital to Zanzibar in 1840, and after 1861 the empire split into separate Omani and Zanzibari sultanates.

Modern era

The interior imamate and the coastal sultanate were reunified after the Jebel Akhdar War of the 1950s. Sultan Qaboos bin Said ruled from 1970 to 2020, using oil revenues to modernise the country while keeping it neutral in regional conflicts. He was succeeded by his cousin Haitham bin Tariq.
