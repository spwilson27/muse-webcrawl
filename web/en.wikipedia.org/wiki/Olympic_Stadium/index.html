Olympic Stadium

Olympic Stadium is the name usually given to the main stadium of an Olympic Games. An Olympic stadium is the site of the opening and closing ceremonies. Many, though not all, hold the athletics (track and field) events as well. The term is also used for stadiums built specifically for, or primarily associated with, an edition of the Olympic Games.

Requirements and legacy

Since Amsterdam 1928, the Olympic stadium has traditionally hosted the opening and closing ceremonies, with the Olympic cauldron lit for the duration of the Games. Modern Olympic stadiums have typically seated between 60,000 and 100,000 spectators for the Games, although many are reconfigured or reduced in capacity afterwards. The legacy use of such large stadiums has been a recurring concern in Olympic planning, with several venues falling into disuse or being repurposed for football, athletics, concerts, or other sports.

Notable stadiums

Famous Olympic stadiums include the Olympiastadion in Berlin (1936), the Olympic Stadium in Helsinki (1952), the Stadio Olimpico in Rome (1960), the National Stadium in Tokyo (1964, rebuilt for 2020), the Olympic Stadium in Montreal (1976), the "Bird's Nest" Beijing National Stadium (2008), and the London Stadium (2012). Some cities, such as Athens and Los Angeles, have used stadiums for two editions of the Games.
