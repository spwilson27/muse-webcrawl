Viterbo

Viterbo
Country: Italy
Region: Lazio
Province: Viterbo (VT)
Population: approx. 67,000

Viterbo is a city and comune in the Lazio region of central Italy, the capital of the province of Viterbo. It is situated about 80 kilometres (50 miles) north of Rome on the Via Cassia, in the foothills of the Cimini Mountains. The historic centre of the city is surrounded by medieval walls, still mainly intact, built during the 11th and 12th centuries.

History

The area was settled by the Etruscans, and the city was later conquered by Rome. In the Middle Ages Viterbo grew in importance as a residence of the popes; during the 13th century several papal elections were held there, including the longest conclave in history (1268–1271), after which the cardinals were famously locked in — giving rise to the word "conclave". The Papal Palace (Palazzo dei Papi) remains one of the city's emblematic landmarks.

Main sights

  • Palazzo dei Papi – the 13th-century papal palace with its elegant loggia.
  • Viterbo Cathedral – dedicated to Saint Lawrence, rebuilt after wartime damage.
  • San Pellegrino quarter – one of the best-preserved medieval districts in Italy.
  • Thermal springs – the Terme dei Papi and other baths outside the city, used since Etruscan and Roman times.

Macchina di Santa Rosa

Each year on 3 September, the city celebrates the transport of the Macchina di Santa Rosa, a huge tower about 30 metres high carried through the streets by a hundred porters (facchini). The festival is inscribed on UNESCO's list of Intangible Cultural Heritage.
