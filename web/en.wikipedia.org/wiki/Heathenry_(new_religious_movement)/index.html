Heathenry (new religious movement)

Heathenry, also termed Heathenism, Ásatrú, Odinism, or Germanic Neopaganism, is a contemporary Pagan new religious movement inspired by the pre-Christian religions of the Germanic peoples of early medieval Europe. Practitioners — called Heathens — seek to revive or reconstruct the beliefs and practices attested in Old Norse and Old English sources, venerating gods such as Odin, Thor, and Freyja, along with ancestors and land spirits (landvættir).

Practices and beliefs

Core rituals include the blót, a sacrificial offering (today usually of drink or food rather than animals), and the sumbl, a ceremonial round of toasts. Many Heathens follow an ethic summarized as the Nine Noble Virtues, drawn from the Eddic poem Hávamál, and some use the runes for divination. Heathenry is polytheistic and generally decentralized: local groups called kindreds or hearths worship autonomously, and national organizations exist in Iceland (Ásatrúarfélagið, founded 1972 and officially recognized in 1973), the United States, Britain, and Scandinavia.

History and controversies

The movement grew from 19th- and 20th-century romantic interest in Germanic heritage and from the 1960s–70s countercultural pagan revival that also nourished modern Druidry. A persistent internal division separates universalist Heathens, who welcome all comers, from folkish Heathens, who tie the religion to ethnic ancestry; the latter position has attracted criticism and, in some quarters, association with far-right politics, which most Heathen organizations publicly reject.
