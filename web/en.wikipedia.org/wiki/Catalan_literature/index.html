Catalan literature

Catalan literature (or Valencian literature) is the body of literature written in the Catalan language, produced in Catalonia, the Valencian Community, the Balearic Islands, Andorra, and other Catalan-speaking territories. It begins in the Middle Ages with the sermons known as the Homilies d'Organyà (late 12th or early 13th century), among the oldest literary texts in the language.

Medieval golden age

The Majorcan philosopher Ramon Llull (c. 1232–1315/16) was the first major writer to use a Romance vernacular for philosophical and scientific works, producing novels such as Blanquerna and an enormous corpus in Catalan, Latin, and Arabic. The 15th century saw a brilliant flowering in Valencia: the poetry of Ausiàs March, Joanot Martorell's chivalric novel Tirant lo Blanch (1490) — praised by Cervantes — and the work of writers such as Joan Roís de Corella and Isabel de Villena. Catalan also produced great chronicles, including those of James I, Bernat Desclot, Ramon Muntaner, and Peter IV.

Decline and Renaixença

From the 16th century, as political power shifted to Castile, Catalan letters declined in prestige — the period traditionally called the Decadència. The 19th-century Renaixença (Renaissance) revived literary Catalan, symbolised by Jacint Verdaguer's epics L'Atlàntida and Canigó, and by the restored Jocs Florals poetry contests. In the early 20th century, Modernisme and Noucentisme brought figures such as Joan Maragall, Eugeni d'Ors, Josep Carner, and Carles Riba.

Modern period

The Spanish Civil War and Franco's dictatorship (1939–1975), which suppressed Catalan in public life, drove many writers into exile. Notable 20th-century authors include Mercè Rodoreda, whose novel La plaça del Diamant (1962; The Time of the Doves) is the most widely translated Catalan novel, Salvador Espriu, Joan Sales, and Josep Pla, whose prose works are monuments of the language. Since the restoration of democracy and Catalan autonomy, literature in Catalan has flourished across fiction, poetry, drama, and children's writing.
