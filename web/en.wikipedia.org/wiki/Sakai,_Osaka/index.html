Sakai, Osaka

Sakai (堺市)
Country: Japan
Region: Kansai
Prefecture: Osaka
Area: 149.8 km²
Population: ~820,000

Sakai (Japanese: 堺市, Sakai-shi) is a city in Osaka Prefecture, Japan, on the shore of Osaka Bay south of the city of Osaka. It is a designated city with a population of around 820,000, making it one of the largest cities in the Kansai region. Sakai is famous for the Mozu Tombs, among the largest burial mounds in the world, and for its centuries-old production of knives and other cutlery.

The Daisenryō Kofun in Sakai, one of the world's largest tombs.

History

Sakai grew around its natural harbour and flourished in the medieval period as an autonomous merchant city — sometimes called the "Venice of the Orient" — governed by a council of wealthy merchants (egōshū). In the 16th century it was a major centre of trade with China and Europe and of tea culture, home to the tea master Sen no Rikyū, and an important producer of firearms. Oda Nobunaga brought the city under central control in 1569.

Landmarks and industry

The Mozu Tomb Cluster, including the Daisenryō Kofun attributed to Emperor Nintoku — 486 metres long — was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2019 as part of the Mozu-Furuichi Kofun Group. Sakai remains renowned for handcrafted kitchen knives and blades, a craft tradition said to descend from its gunsmiths, and is also known for bicycles (Shimano was founded here in 1921).
