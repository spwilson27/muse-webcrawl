Stop order

A stop order, also referred to as a stop-loss order, is an order placed with a broker to buy or sell a specific stock once the price of the stock reaches a specified price, known as the stop price. When the stop price is reached, a stop order becomes a market order. A buy stop order is placed above the current market price; a sell stop order is placed below it.

Uses

Sell stop orders are most commonly used to limit losses on a long position: if the price falls to the stop level, the position is sold automatically. Buy stop orders may be used to limit losses on short positions or to enter a position once a price level has been breached, for example in breakout trading strategies. A trailing stop adjusts the stop price as the market moves favourably, locking in gains while leaving room to run.

Stop-limit orders and risks

A related order, the stop-limit order, becomes a limit order rather than a market order when triggered, specifying the worst price at which the trade may execute; this protects against poor fills but risks the order not executing at all. Market stop orders offer no price protection: in fast-moving or gapping markets the fill may occur far from the stop price. Cascades of triggered stop orders can also accelerate price moves, a phenomenon implicated in events such as flash crashes.
