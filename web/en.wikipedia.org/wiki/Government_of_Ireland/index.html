Government of Ireland

Formation: 1937 (current constitution)
Head of government: Taoiseach
Seat: Government Buildings, Dublin
Legislature: Oireachtas

The Government of Ireland (Irish: Rialtas na hÉireann) is the executive authority of Ireland and collectively exercises the executive power of the State. The government is headed by a prime minister called the Taoiseach and a deputy prime minister, the Tánaiste, and has its seat in Government Buildings on Merrion Street in Dublin. The current written constitution of Ireland was adopted in 1937, with the state's legal name being "Ireland" and its description as a republic added by statute in 1949.

Constitutional basis

Under Articles 28 of the Constitution of Ireland, the Government consists of between seven and fifteen members appointed by the President on the nomination of the Taoiseach and approved by Dáil Éireann, the lower house of the Oireachtas. The Government must enjoy the confidence of Dáil Éireann. It is collectively responsible for the Departments of State administered by its members, and acts by meeting together as a cabinet.

Composition

The head of government is the Taoiseach, who nominates the Tánaiste and other ministers, each of whom leads a government department such as Finance, Foreign Affairs, Health, Education, Justice, and Enterprise. The Attorney General advises the Government on matters of law and is not a member of the Government but attends cabinet meetings. Ministers of State (junior ministers) may also be appointed to assist at departments.

Functions

The Government conducts the State's domestic and foreign policy, controls the public finances (the Minister for Finance presents the annual budget), directs the Defence Forces (subject to the President as supreme commander), and prepares most legislation. The wider public service, including the civil service, An Garda Síochána (the police), and state agencies, operates under the authority of the respective ministers.
