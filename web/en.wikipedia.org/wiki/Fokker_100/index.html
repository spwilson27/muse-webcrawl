The Fokker 100 is a regional jet airliner manufactured by the Dutch company Fokker. It is a stretched, re-engined development of the Fokker F28 Fellowship, carrying up to about 109 passengers in a single-class layout. At its introduction it was among the best-selling regional jets, and Fokker considered it one of its most successful products.

Development and design

The Fokker 100 was announced in 1983 alongside the turboprop Fokker 50. Compared with the F28, it has a longer fuselage (about 5.5 m more), an increased wingspan, and two Rolls-Royce Tay turbofan engines mounted at the rear fuselage, replacing the F28's Rolls-Royce Speys. The flight deck was modernized with an early glass cockpit. The prototype first flew on 30 November 1986, and the type entered service with Swissair in 1988.

Operational history

Major customers included American Airlines, US Airways, KLM Cityhopper, and TAM Airlines of Brazil. Production ended in 1997 after Fokker's bankruptcy in 1996; 283 aircraft were built. Many Fokker 100s were retired from mainline service in the 2000s and 2010s, but a substantial fleet found a second career in Australia, where operators such as Alliance Airlines, QantasLink (Network Aviation), and Virgin Australia Regional Airlines fly them on mining charter and regional services, as well as in Iran.

Specifications

Typical capacity is 97–109 passengers; length is 35.53 m and wingspan 28.08 m. With two Rolls-Royce Tay Mk 650-15 engines of about 67 kN thrust each, the aircraft cruises at around Mach 0.77 (845 km/h) and has a range of roughly 3,170 km.
