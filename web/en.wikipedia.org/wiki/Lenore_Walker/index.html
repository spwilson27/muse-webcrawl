Lenore Walker

Lenore Edna Walker (born October 3, 1942) is an American psychologist and academic who founded the Domestic Violence Institute and authored The Battered Woman (1979), the foundational study of intimate partner violence. She is best known for formulating the cycle of abuse theory and for introducing the concept of battered woman syndrome into psychological and legal discourse.

Work

Based on interviews with several hundred battered women in Colorado, Walker's research identified a recurring three-phase pattern in abusive relationships: (1) a tension-building phase, (2) an acute battering incident, and (3) a phase of loving contrition or "honeymoon" behavior, after which tension builds again. Drawing on Martin Seligman's learned helplessness theory, she argued that repeated, unpredictable abuse can leave victims perceiving escape as impossible, a framework she termed battered woman syndrome.

Influence

Walker's work helped transform public and professional understanding of domestic violence from a private family matter into a subject of clinical research, public policy, and criminal law. She has served as an expert witness in numerous trials involving battered women who killed their abusers, and the admissibility and scientific status of battered woman syndrome testimony have been debated in courts and journals ever since. Walker taught for many years at Nova Southeastern University in Florida and continues to write and lecture on trauma and violence against women.
