Senpai and kōhai

Senpai (先輩, "senior") and kōhai (後輩, "junior") are complementary terms describing an informal hierarchical interpersonal relationship in Japanese society, found in schools, universities, sports clubs, businesses and martial arts dōjō. A senpai is the more experienced member of a pair or group; the kōhai is the less experienced counterpart.

The relationship

The relationship is reciprocal and based on seniority—usually determined by when a person joined the organisation rather than by age or ability alone. The senpai is expected to mentor, guide and protect the kōhai, transmitting skills and social norms; the kōhai is expected to show respect, humility and deference, expressed through honorific language (keigo) such as addressing the senpai by the suffix -senpai. In school clubs, senpai often train new members and organise activities, while kōhai handle routine chores.

Origins and influence

The pairing reflects Confucian influences on Japanese social organisation, emphasising respect for elders and hierarchical harmony. Analogous senior–junior relationships exist elsewhere in East and Southeast Asia. The terms are widely known abroad through anime and manga, where the senpai–kōhai dynamic is a common character device, and "senpai" has entered English-language internet slang.
