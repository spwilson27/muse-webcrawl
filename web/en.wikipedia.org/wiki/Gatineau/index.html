Gatineau (/ˈɡætɪnoʊ/; French: [ɡatino]) is a city in western Quebec, Canada. It is the fourth-largest city in the province after Montreal, Quebec City, and Laval. Gatineau is located on the northern bank of the Ottawa River, immediately across from Ottawa, Ontario, Canada's capital, with which it forms the National Capital Region. As part of that region, Gatineau hosts many federal government departments and the Canadian Museum of History.

History

The area was first settled by Algonquin peoples, and later became a centre of the fur trade and timber industry. In 1800, Philemon Wright, a settler from Massachusetts, founded a farming and lumber community at the Chaudière Falls that became known as Wright's Town, later Hull. The present city of Gatineau was created on 1 January 2002, when the former cities of Aylmer, Buckingham, Gatineau, Hull, and Masson-Angers were amalgamated into a single city, which took the name Gatineau.

Geography

Gatineau sits at the confluence of the Gatineau River and the Ottawa River. Much of the northern part of the city is covered by Gatineau Park, a large conservation area administered by the National Capital Commission. The city has a humid continental climate with hot summers and cold, snowy winters.

Demographics

According to the 2021 Canadian census, Gatineau had a population of 291,041. A large majority of residents are francophone, though the city also has a significant anglophone minority, and bilingualism is widespread, in part because of the large number of federal public servants who live there.

Economy

The federal government is the dominant employer in Gatineau, with large complexes such as Place du Portage and Terrasses de la Chaudière housing thousands of public servants. The city is also home to the Casino du Lac-Leamy and several museums, including the Canadian Museum of History, one of the most visited museums in Canada.
