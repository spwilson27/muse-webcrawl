Legio VI Victrix

Legio VI Victrix ("Victorious Sixth Legion") was a legion of the Imperial Roman army. Founded in the civil wars of the late Roman Republic, the legion served for centuries, spending most of its history on the northern frontier of Roman Britain, where it was based at Eboracum (modern York) and helped to build and garrison Hadrian's Wall.

History

Republic and early Empire

The legion's origins are disputed: Julius Caesar raised a Sixth Legion for his campaigns, and a Legio VI was reconstituted by Octavian (the future Augustus) around 41 BC. The Octavianic legion fought at Perusia and in the wars following Caesar's assassination, receiving the cognomen Victrix for its victories. Under Augustus it was stationed in Hispania Tarraconensis, taking part in the Cantabrian Wars, and it remained in Spain for roughly a century.

Britain

In AD 119 the legion was transferred to Britain, where it replaced Legio IX Hispana at Eboracum. Legio VI supplied much of the labour for the construction of Hadrian's Wall from the 120s, and its soldiers left building inscriptions along the frontier. Detachments served in the Antonine Wall campaign in Scotland, and in the third century elements of the legion supported the Severan campaigns in Caledonia. The legion acquired additional honorific titles, including Pia Fidelis. It remained in northern Britain into the late Roman period and disappears from record in the early fifth century, when Roman authority in Britain ended.

Legacy

The legion's long presence in York left numerous inscriptions, altars, and tombstones, many now in the Yorkshire Museum. Its emblem is generally taken to be the bull, as with other legions of Caesarian origin.
