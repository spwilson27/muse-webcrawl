The Steam Deck is a handheld gaming computer produced by Valve Corporation, released on 25 February 2022. The device runs games from the Steam library on a custom AMD APU using SteamOS, a Linux-based operating system, with Valve's Proton compatibility layer allowing Windows games to run without modification by developers. It can be used as a handheld or connected to a monitor through a dock in the manner of a desktop PC.

Hardware

The original model has a 7-inch LCD touchscreen at 1280×800 resolution, flanked by twin analogue sticks, two trackpads, a D-pad, ABXY buttons and shoulder triggers. Storage options at launch were 64 GB eMMC, 256 GB NVMe SSD and 512 GB NVMe SSD, all expandable via microSD. A revision with a 7.4-inch HDR OLED display, larger battery, Wi-Fi 6E and a lighter body, the Steam Deck OLED, was released in November 2023, replacing the upper LCD models.

Software

SteamOS 3.0 is based on Arch Linux with the KDE Plasma desktop available in desktop mode. Valve's Deck Verified programme rates games in the Steam catalogue for compatibility: Verified, Playable, Unsupported or Unknown. Proton, a fork of Wine developed with CodeWeavers, translates Windows API calls so most Windows titles run transparently.

Reception and impact

The Steam Deck was widely praised for bringing PC gaming to a console-like handheld at aggressive prices, and it is credited with popularising a new category of handheld gaming PCs, followed by competitors such as the ASUS ROG Ally and Lenovo Legion Go. Valve reported the device was a multi-million-unit seller, and the top configurations were frequently supply-constrained during its first year.
