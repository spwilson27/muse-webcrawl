Eddington luminosity

Eddington luminosity
Named for: Arthur Eddington
Value: ≈ 3.2×104 (M/M☉) L☉
Also called: Eddington limit

The Eddington luminosity, also referred to as the Eddington limit, is the maximum luminosity a body (such as a star) can achieve when there is balance between the force of radiation acting outward and the gravitational force acting inward.

Derivation

Equating radiation pressure on free electrons (Thomson scattering) with gravity gives LEdd ≈ 4πGMmpc/σT, roughly 1.26×1031 watts per solar mass. When luminosity exceeds this, radiation drives outflows.

Applications

The limit constrains accretion onto black holes and neutron stars, the brightness of quasars, and mass loss in very massive stars. Some sources transiently exceed it, and super-Eddington accretion is invoked for rapid early black-hole growth. Named after Arthur Stanley Eddington. See also Accretion.
