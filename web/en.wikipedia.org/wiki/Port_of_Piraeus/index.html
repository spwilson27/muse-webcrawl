Port of Piraeus

The Port of Piraeus is the largest seaport in Greece and one of the largest passenger ports in Europe, serving as the maritime gateway of Athens since the fifth century BCE. Located on the Saronic Gulf about 12 kilometres southwest of central Athens, it handles ferries to the Aegean islands and Crete, cruise ships, and a major container terminal.

History

Themistocles fortified Piraeus around 493 BCE, building the Long Walls that linked Athens to its harbours at Kantharos, Zea, and Munichia; the port underpinned Athenian naval power and grain imports for centuries. In the modern era it grew with independent Greece, absorbing a large refugee population after 1922 and becoming the country's industrial and shipping heart.

Modern development

In 2016 the Chinese shipping group COSCO acquired a majority stake in the Piraeus Port Authority as part of China's Belt and Road Initiative, making Piraeus a flagship project. Under COSCO's investment the container port has climbed into the ranks of Europe's busiest, with throughput exceeding five million TEUs a year, while the passenger port continues to serve millions of ferry travellers annually.
