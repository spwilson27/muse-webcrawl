The National Council (German: Nationalrat) is one of the two houses of the Austrian Parliament and the dominant chamber in the legislative process. Its 183 members are elected by direct popular vote under a system of proportional representation, with seats allocated in three tiers — local, regional and federal — so that voters can also cast preference votes for individual candidates.

Functions

The National Council initiates and passes federal legislation, usually in co-operation with the Federal Council (Bundesrat), which represents the nine states but can generally only delay, not block, laws. The federal government, headed by the Chancellor, is accountable to the National Council, which can force the government or an individual minister to resign through a vote of no confidence. The National Council also elects, together with the Federal Council in the Federal Assembly, no part of the largely ceremonial federal presidency — the President is elected directly by the people — but it can vote to impeach the President or to call a referendum on removal.

Elections

Legislative elections are normally held every five years; the minimum voting age is 16, among the lowest in the world. Parties must clear a 4% national threshold or win a constituency seat to enter the Council. After the 2024 election, the Freedom Party (FPÖ) emerged as the largest party for the first time, ahead of the People's Party (ÖVP) and the Social Democrats (SPÖ), with NEOS and the Greens also represented.
