Bürgerliches Gesetzbuch

Enacted: 1896
Effective: 1 January 1900
Jurisdiction: Germany
Subject: Civil law

The Bürgerliches Gesetzbuch (BGB), the German Civil Code, is the principal codification of private law in Germany. It entered into force on 1 January 1900 and was a major project of the German Empire, unifying the fragmented private law of the German states.

Structure

The BGB is divided into five books: General Part, Law of Obligations, Property Law, Family Law, and Law of Succession. Its abstract, systematic style, influenced by Pandectist scholarship, made it a model for civil codes in countries including Japan, Brazil, Greece and China.

Development

Drafting began after German unification; a first commission was appointed in 1874 and the code was promulgated in 1896. It has been substantially amended since, notably in family law and the 2002 modernisation of the law of obligations.
