Response to intervention

Response to intervention (commonly abbreviated RTI) is a multi-tier approach to the early identification and support of students with learning and behaviour needs. The RTI process begins with high-quality instruction and universal screening of all children in the general education classroom. Struggling learners are provided with interventions at increasing levels of intensity to accelerate their rate of learning.

Three-tier model

Tier 1 is high-quality, scientifically based classroom instruction, in which all students receive screening to identify those who may need additional support. Tier 2 provides targeted, small-group interventions to students who are not progressing adequately, with progress monitored frequently. Tier 3 involves intensive, individualized interventions; students who still do not respond may be referred for a comprehensive evaluation for special education services.

Background

RTI was promoted in the United States following the reauthorization of the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA) in 2004, which permitted schools to use a student's response to scientific, research-based intervention as part of the process for identifying specific learning disabilities, as an alternative to the older IQ–achievement discrepancy model.

Essential components

The essential components of RTI include universal screening, increasingly intensive levels of instructional intervention, frequent progress monitoring, data-based decision making, and implementation fidelity. RTI is often implemented within a broader multi-tiered system of supports (MTSS) that also addresses behaviour.
