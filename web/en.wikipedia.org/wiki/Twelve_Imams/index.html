Twelve Imams

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Twelve Imams are the spiritual and political successors to the Islamic prophet Muhammad in the Twelver branch of Shia Islam, including the Alawite and Alevi sub-branches. According to Twelver theology, the Twelve Imams are exemplary human individuals who not only rule over the Muslim community (Ummah) with justice, but are also able to keep and interpret the divine law (sharia) and the esoteric meaning of the Quran.

The words and deeds of Muhammad and the imams are a guide and model for the Muslim community to follow; as a result, Muhammad and the imams are believed to be free from error and sin, a doctrine known as Ismah or infallibility, and must be chosen by divine decree, or nass, through Muhammad.

The Imams

The twelve imams are:

  1. Ali ibn Abi Talib (Amir al-Mu'minin)
  2. Hasan ibn Ali
  3. Husayn ibn Ali
  4. Ali Zayn al-Abidin
  5. Muhammad al-Baqir
  6. Ja'far al-Sadiq
  7. Musa al-Kazim
  8. Ali al-Rida
  9. Muhammad al-Jawad
  10. Ali al-Hadi
  11. Hasan al-Askari
  12. Muhammad al-Mahdi

With the exception of Ali, the first imam, all were descendants of Muhammad through his daughter Fatima. The imams are regarded as infallible (ma'sum) and as the authoritative interpreters of religion.

The Mahdi and the Occultation

The twelfth imam, Muhammad al-Mahdi, is believed by Twelvers to be alive but hidden from the world in a state called the Occultation (ghayba), since 874 CE. The minor occultation was followed by the major occultation, during which the imam has no direct representatives. Twelvers believe the Mahdi will return at the end of time as the saviour of humanity, accompanied by Jesus (Isa), to fill the world with justice.

Significance

Belief in the Twelve Imams is a defining doctrine of Twelver Shi'ism, the largest branch of Shia Islam, followed by the majority of Muslims in Iran, Iraq, Azerbaijan and Bahrain, and by significant minorities elsewhere. Their shrines, notably at Karbala, Najaf, Mashhad, Qom and Samarra, are major centres of pilgrimage.
