Single-payer healthcare

Single-payer healthcare is a type of universal healthcare in which the costs of essential healthcare for all residents are covered by a single public system (hence "single-payer"). In a single-payer system, the government (or a government-run fund) pays for healthcare services, while the delivery of care may remain largely in private hands — as in Canada, where physicians are mostly independent practitioners billing the provincial insurance plan — or may be combined with public provision, which is technically a different model (as in the United Kingdom's National Health Service, sometimes classified separately as a "national health service" model).

Examples

Canada's system under the Canada Health Act is the archetypal single-payer model: each province or territory administers a single public insurer for medically necessary hospital and physician services, private duplicate insurance for those services is restricted, and providers bill the plan directly. Taiwan's National Health Insurance (established 1995) is another frequently cited single-payer system, covering the whole population through one public insurer with predominantly private providers. Medicare in Australia combines a public insurer with mixed public and private provision.

Debate in the United States

In the United States, "single-payer" proposals — often under the slogan "Medicare for All" — would replace private insurance for most services with an expanded federal programme. Supporters point to lower administrative overhead, universal coverage and stronger price negotiation; opponents argue the transition costs, tax increases, loss of private plan choice and risks of longer waits under fixed budgets. Polls and economic studies differ widely on net national cost, and the term is sometimes used loosely for any strongly public universal system.

Terminology

"Single-payer" describes the financing mechanism, not the ownership of hospitals or employment of clinicians. Systems with multiple competing public insurers (for example, Germany's sickness funds) are "multi-payer" even when universal.
