Backstroke

Backstroke or back crawl is a swimming stroke performed on the back, with the arms moving in an alternating circular motion and the legs performing a flutter kick. It is one of the four strokes regulated by FINA, and the only one in which swimmers start in the water rather than diving from a block.

Technique

The swimmer lies supine, with the body kept as horizontal as possible. Each arm in turn enters the water little-finger first above the shoulder, sweeps through the water in a bent-arm pull, and recovers above the surface. The legs kick alternately from the hips with pointed toes. Breathing is easier than in other strokes because the face remains out of the water, but the swimmer cannot see the direction of travel and relies on lane ropes, flags, and counting strokes to judge the wall.

Competition

Backstroke events are held over 50 m, 100 m, and 200 m at the Olympic Games, and the stroke forms part of the individual medley and medley relay. Turns are governed by special rules permitting the swimmer to roll onto the front for a flip turn in the final stroke. Backstroke start wedges have been introduced at major competitions to aid the start.

History

Swimming on the back was practised in ancient times, and backstroke entered the Olympic programme at the Paris Games of 1900. The stroke evolved from a simultaneous arm action, similar to an inverted breaststroke, to the modern alternating windmill action during the early twentieth century.
