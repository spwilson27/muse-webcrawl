The New Economic Mechanism (Hungarian: Új gazdasági mechanizmus) was a major economic reform launched in Hungary on 1 January 1968 under the leadership of János Kádár. It was the most comprehensive attempt within the Soviet bloc to combine elements of a market economy with socialist central planning, and it underpinned the relatively liberal system known as "Goulash Communism."

Provisions

The reform abolished compulsory enterprise plan directives: state firms were no longer given binding output targets and input allocations, but were instead guided by economic regulators such as taxes, credit conditions, price rules, and subsidies. Enterprises gained freedom to choose their product mix, suppliers, and (within limits) customers, and to retain part of their profits for investment and worker bonuses.

Prices were divided into three categories — fixed, limited (within a band), and free — with roughly half of producer prices and a growing share of consumer prices eventually liberalized. Agriculture was left largely to cooperatives with substantial autonomy, and a tolerated "second economy" of private small-scale activity flourished.

History

Prepared by economists including Rezső Nyers, the reform was introduced after the consolidation that followed the crushed Hungarian Revolution of 1956. Conservative opposition within the party, pressure from Moscow, and the shocks of the 1970s oil crises led to partial recentralization after 1972–73, when the largest enterprises were placed back under tighter supervision. Reform efforts resumed in modified form in the late 1970s and 1980s, including the legalization of small private businesses and Hungary's accession to the International Monetary Fund and World Bank in 1982.

Legacy

The New Economic Mechanism gave Hungarians the highest standard of living in the Eastern Bloc and a degree of consumer abundance unusual under state socialism. It is generally credited with softening the system's rigidities, though critics note it never introduced genuine market competition, hard budget constraints, or private ownership of major assets, and Hungary accumulated heavy foreign debt during the 1970s and 1980s.
