Quark–gluon plasma

Phase of QCD matter
Temperature: > ~155 MeV
Studied at: RHIC, LHC

Quark–gluon plasma (QGP) is an interacting localised assembly of quarks and gluons at thermal equilibrium, a phase of quantum chromodynamics (QCD) matter that exists only at extremely high temperature and/or density. In ordinary matter, quarks are confined inside hadrons; in QGP they are deconfined and move freely over nuclear volumes.

Production

QGP is produced in ultra-relativistic heavy-ion collisions, first studied at CERN's SPS and Brookhaven's Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider (RHIC), and later at much higher energies in the ALICE experiment at the Large Hadron Collider. Colliding gold or lead nuclei at near light speed create fireballs reaching trillions of kelvin, briefly recreating conditions microseconds after the Big Bang.

Properties

Experiments show the plasma behaves as a nearly perfect liquid with very low shear viscosity to entropy ratio, strongly interacting and opaque to jets of high-energy quarks ("jet quenching"). The transition from hadronic matter to QGP at low baryon density is a smooth crossover around a temperature of roughly 155 MeV, as computed by lattice QCD.

Significance

Studying QGP informs understanding of the early universe, neutron star interiors, and the strong interaction in the non-perturbative regime.
