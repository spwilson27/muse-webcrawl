Star Wars sequel trilogy

The sequel trilogy continued the Skywalker saga three decades after Return of the Jedi.

The Star Wars sequel trilogy is the third trilogy of the main Star Wars franchise, an American space opera created by George Lucas. It is produced by Lucasfilm under the Walt Disney Company, which acquired Lucasfilm in 2012. The trilogy consists of Episode VII: The Force Awakens (2015), Episode VIII: The Last Jedi (2017), and Episode IX: The Rise of Skywalker (2019).

The trilogy follows Rey, a young scavenger from the desert planet Jakku who discovers she is strong with the Force, as she becomes entangled in the conflict between the Resistance and the First Order, a military regime risen from the remnants of the Galactic Empire. Returning characters from the original trilogy include Luke Skywalker, Han Solo, and Leia Organa.

Development

Disney's acquisition of Lucasfilm for $4.05 billion in October 2012 included Lucas's treatments for a sequel trilogy. Kathleen Kennedy served as executive producer. Michael Arndt was initially hired to write Episode VII before J. J. Abrams and Lawrence Kasdan took over scripting duties. Production emphasized practical effects and location shooting, a deliberate return to the aesthetic of the original trilogy.

Reception

The trilogy was a major commercial success: The Force Awakens grossed over $2 billion worldwide, becoming the highest-grossing film of 2015. Critical and audience responses were more divided for the later installments, with The Last Jedi praised by critics but polarizing among fans, and The Rise of Skywalker receiving the trilogy's most mixed reviews. Together the three films grossed over $4.4 billion at the global box office.
