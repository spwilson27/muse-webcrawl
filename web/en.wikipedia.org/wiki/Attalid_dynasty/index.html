The Attalid dynasty was a Hellenistic dynasty that ruled the city of Pergamon and, at its height, much of western Anatolia, from 282 BC until 133 BC. Founded by Philetaerus, a lieutenant of Lysimachus who seized the fortress and treasury of Pergamon, the dynasty took its name from his nephew and successor Attalus I, the first to claim the title of king.

Kings

  • Philetaerus (282–263 BC), dynast
  • Eumenes I (263–241 BC)
  • Attalus I Soter (241–197 BC)
  • Eumenes II Soter (197–159 BC)
  • Attalus II Philadelphus (160–138 BC)
  • Attalus III Philometor (138–133 BC)

Rise and rule

Attalus I won prestige by defeating the Galatians and expanded Pergamene territory at the expense of the Seleucid Empire, fighting as an ally of Rome in the Macedonian Wars — an alliance that brought the kingdom its greatest extent after the Treaty of Apamea (188 BC). Under Eumenes II, Pergamon became a great centre of Hellenistic culture: the Library of Pergamum, rivalling Alexandria's, the Great Altar of Pergamon, and a rebuilt acropolis date to this era. The city also gave its name to parchment (pergamena), whose production Pergamene craftsmen were credited with perfecting.

End of the kingdom

The childless Attalus III, in an extraordinary act, bequeathed his kingdom to the Roman Republic in his will. After the revolt of the pretender Aristonicus (Eumenes III) was suppressed, Rome organised the territory as the province of Asia in 129 BC.
