Saigō Takamori

Saigō Takamori (23 January 1828 – 24 September 1877) was a Japanese samurai and statesman from Satsuma Domain, one of the Three Great Nobles of the Meiji Restoration. Regarded as the "last true samurai", he helped overthrow the Tokugawa shogunate, then died leading the Satsuma Rebellion against the Meiji government he had helped create.

Born23 January 1828, Kagoshima
Died24 September 1877, Shiroyama
ConflictBoshin War, Satsuma Rebellion

Restoration

Saigō forged the Satchō Alliance, commanded imperial forces in the Boshin War (1868–69) and negotiated Edo's bloodless surrender with Katsu Kaishū. In the Meiji government he advocated care for ex-samurai; he resigned in 1873 after his proposal to go to Korea (Seikanron) was rejected.

Rebellion and legend

In 1877 disaffected Satsuma samurai rallied to him; defeated at Shiroyama, he died — by tradition via assisted suicide. Despite rebellion he was posthumously pardoned in 1889 and enshrined in popular culture, including a famous statue in Ueno Park.
