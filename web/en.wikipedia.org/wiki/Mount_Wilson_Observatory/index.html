The Mount Wilson Observatory is an astronomical observatory in Los Angeles County, California, on the summit of Mount Wilson in the San Gabriel Mountains. Founded in 1904 by George Ellery Hale with Carnegie Institution funding, it was for decades the world's leading observatory and the site of discoveries that transformed cosmology.

Discoveries

Hale built first the 60-inch reflector (1908), then the 100-inch Hooker telescope (1917), the largest in the world until 1949. With the Hooker, Edwin Hubble identified Cepheid variables in the Andromeda "nebula", proving it a separate galaxy (announced 1925), and, with Milton Humason, established the expansion of the universe (1929). Harlow Shapley used Mount Wilson observations to place the Sun away from the galactic centre, and Walter Baade later refined the cosmic distance scale there. Michelson's stellar interferometer on the Hooker tube made the first measurement of a star's diameter (Betelgeuse, 1920).

The observatory today

Growing Los Angeles light pollution shifted front-line research elsewhere, but the mountain still hosts the 150-foot solar tower, the Snow solar telescope, Georgia State University's CHARA interferometric array on the historic telescopes, and public outreach run by the Mount Wilson Institute. The site survived close approaches by the Station Fire (2009) and Bobcat Fire (2020).
