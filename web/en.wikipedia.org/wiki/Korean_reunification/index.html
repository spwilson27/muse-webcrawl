Korean reunification

Korean reunification is the hypothetical future unification of North Korea (the Democratic People's Republic of Korea) and South Korea (the Republic of Korea) into a single Korean state. The two states have been divided since 1945, and separated by the Korean Demilitarized Zone since the 1953 armistice that ended the Korean War. Both constitutions historically claimed the whole peninsula, and reunification long remained an official goal on both sides.

Background

Korea was annexed by Japan in 1910 and divided at the 38th parallel in 1945 into Soviet and American occupation zones, which crystallised into two rival states in 1948. The Korean War (1950–1953) began as an attempt at forcible unification by the North and ended in stalemate. Decades of hostility alternated with thaws: the July 4th North–South Joint Statement of 1972, the 1991 Basic Agreement, and the June 15th Joint Declaration of 2000 following Kim Dae-jung's Sunshine Policy, which led to summits, family reunions and the joint Kaesong Industrial Region.

Scenarios and obstacles

Proposed paths include negotiated federation or confederation (the North's long-standing "one country, two systems" formula), gradual integration on the European model, or sudden unification through collapse of the North, analogous to German reunification in 1990 — a comparison often drawn but complicated by far wider economic disparities between the Koreas and by the North's nuclear arsenal. Estimated costs of absorption run into the trillions of dollars. Public support in the South, especially among younger generations, has declined as the division has lengthened, and in early 2024 Kim Jong Un formally repudiated reunification, declaring the South a separate hostile state and demolishing the Arch of Reunification in Pyongyang.

Symbols

The Korean Unification Flag — a blue silhouette of the peninsula on white — has been carried by joint Korean teams at the Olympics and other sporting events, most prominently at Pyeongchang in 2018, when the two Koreas fielded a unified women's ice hockey team.
