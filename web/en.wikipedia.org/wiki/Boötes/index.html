Boötes is a large northern constellation dominated by Arcturus, the brightest star in the northern celestial hemisphere.

Boötes (/boʊˈoʊtiːz/ boh-OH-teez) is a constellation in the northern sky, located between 0° and 60° northern latitude. Its name comes from the Greek Βοώτης, traditionally rendered "the Herdsman" or "the Ploughman," though other interpretations link the figure to a bear-driver chasing Ursa Major and Ursa Minor around the pole — hence his alternative name Arctophylax, "Bear-keeper." One of the 48 constellations described by the 2nd-century astronomer Ptolemy, Boötes remains one of the 88 modern constellations. It contains Arcturus, an orange giant that is the brightest star in the northern celestial hemisphere and the fourth-brightest star in the night sky overall.

History and mythology

Boötes is one of the oldest named constellations; a figure corresponding to the Herdsman may already have been recognised in Babylonian star-lore as SHU.PA. In Greek myth the figure was variously identified: as Arcas, son of Callisto (the bear he follows); as a ploughman whose invention of the plough earned him a place in the sky, sometimes identified with Triptolemus or with Philomelos, son of Demeter; or as Icarius, the Athenian who learned winemaking from Dionysus. The constellation's kite-shaped asterism of six bright stars makes it easy to locate, and Arcturus can be found by "following the arc" of the handle of the Big Dipper — the mnemonic "arc to Arcturus."

Characteristics

Boötes borders Draco and Ursa Major to the north, Canes Venatici and Coma Berenices to the west, Virgo to the south, and Corona Borealis and Hercules to the east. It is best visible in late spring in the Northern Hemisphere. The now-obsolete constellation Quadrans Muralis, source of the Quadrantid meteor shower's name, lay within what is now northern Boötes. The Boötes Void — an enormous, nearly empty region of the universe about 330 million light-years across, discovered in 1981 — lies in the direction of, though far beyond, this constellation.

Notable stars

  • Arcturus (α Boötis) — orange giant (K0 III), about 37 light-years away, magnitude −0.04; the star used by astronomers in 1635-era photometry experiments and the focus of the 1933 Chicago World's Fair opening stunt, in which its light switched on the fair's lights.
  • Muphrid (η Boötis) — a nearby subgiant, about 37 light-years away.
  • Nekkar (β Boötis) — a yellow giant of magnitude 3.5 marking the herdsman's head.
  • Seginus (γ Boötis) and Rho Boötis — fainter members of the kite asterism.
  • Izar (ε Boötis) — a celebrated double star, sometimes called Pulcherrima ("most beautiful") for its orange-and-blue contrast in telescopes.
  • Merga (38 Boötis) and others complete the traditional figure.

Deep-sky objects

Boötes is relatively sparse in bright deep-sky objects. Its best-known galaxy is NGC 5248, a spiral galaxy about 59 million light-years away. The globular cluster NGC 5466, roughly 51,000 light-years distant, is notable for the tidal stellar streams trailing from it. The constellation also contains several galaxy groups studied in surveys of large-scale structure, an area of the sky scrutinised by wide-field surveys because of the Boötes Void.
