Ithaca (Greek: Ιθάκη, Itháki) is a Greek island in the Ionian Sea, off the northeast coast of Kefalonia, famous in literature as the home of Odysseus, hero of Homer's Odyssey. It is a separate regional unit of the Ionian Islands region, and its capital and largest settlement is the port of Vathy.

Geography

The island is about 29 km long and up to 6.5 km wide, formed of two mountainous lobes joined by a narrow isthmus at Aetos. Its highest peak, Mount Neriton, rises to about 806 m. Deep, sheltered bays cut into its coasts, and the population lives in a small number of villages including Vathy, Stavros, Kioni and Frikes.

Homer's Ithaca

In the Odyssey, Ithaca is the kingdom of Odysseus, who returns there after ten years of wandering following the Trojan War. The correspondence between Homer's description and the modern island has been debated since antiquity; Heinrich Schliemann excavated on the island, and sites such as the "School of Homer" at Agios Athanasios and the Cave of the Nymphs are traditionally linked to the poem. Alternative theories have argued for neighbouring Paliki (Kefalonia) as the Homeric island.

Modern history

Ithaca was ruled successively by Byzantines, Normans, the Venetian Republic (1504–1797), France, a Russo-Ottoman condominium and the British-protected United States of the Ionian Islands, before union with Greece in 1864. The devastating 1953 Ionian earthquake destroyed much of its building stock, which was rebuilt with French and other assistance. Today the island's economy rests on tourism, fishing and olive oil.
