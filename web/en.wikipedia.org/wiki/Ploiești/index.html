Ploiești (Romanian pronunciation approximately "plo-YESHT") is a city and county seat of Prahova County in Wallachia, Romania, about 56 km north of Bucharest. It is one of Romania's principal industrial cities and historically one of the world's earliest centres of petroleum extraction and refining.

History

Legend attributes the town's founding to a shepherd named Moș Ploae, and it was first documented in the reign of Michael the Brave at the end of the sixteenth century. Ploiești grew as a trading and crafts centre, and in 1870 it was the scene of the short-lived "Republic of Ploiești," an anti-monarchist uprising against Prince Carol I.

The discovery of oil in the surrounding Prahova valley transformed the city: one of the world's first oil refineries opened near Ploiești in 1856–57, and Romania ranked among the earliest commercial oil producers. During the Second World War, the Ploiești oilfields supplied a large share of Nazi Germany's petroleum, making the city a major Allied bombing target. The low-level American attack of 1 August 1943, Operation Tidal Wave, was one of the costliest US air missions of the war; further raids in 1944 destroyed much of the refining capacity before Soviet forces arrived in August 1944.

Economy

Refining and petrochemicals remain central to the local economy, including the Brazi and Teleajen refineries and the historic Astra Română works, though production has declined since the communist era. Ploiești is also a road and rail hub and a gateway to the Prahova Valley resorts. Notable residents have included the playwright Ion Luca Caragiale, associated with the nearby village of Haimanale, and the city is home to the Oil and Gas University of Ploiești (founded 1948) and the Clock Museum (Muzeul Ceasului), unique in Romania.
