Water supply and sanitation in South Africa

Water supply and sanitation in South Africa is characterised by high formal access but ageing infrastructure and inequality.

Water supply and sanitation in South Africa is characterised by relatively high levels of access compared with other African countries, alongside persistent challenges of inequality, ageing infrastructure, water scarcity and municipal finances. South Africa is a water-scarce country, with average rainfall well below the world average and most major river systems already heavily allocated.

Institutions

National policy is set by the Department of Water and Sanitation. Bulk water is supplied by regional water boards and entities such as Rand Water and Umgeni Water, while municipalities are constitutionally responsible for retail water and sanitation services. The Water Services Act (1997) and National Water Act (1998) govern the sector, the latter establishing public trusteeship of water resources and catchment management agencies.

Access and policy

Since 1994, extending services to previously underserved communities has been a national priority; millions gained access to piped water and sanitation. South Africa's Free Basic Water policy, introduced in 2001, provides a basic lifeline amount of water (commonly 6 kilolitres per household per month) free of charge. Nevertheless, informal settlements and rural areas continue to face unreliable services, and events such as the Cape Town "Day Zero" drought crisis of 2017–18 and cholera outbreaks linked to failing wastewater plants have highlighted infrastructure decay and maintenance backlogs.
