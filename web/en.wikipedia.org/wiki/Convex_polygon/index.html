Convex polygon

A convex polygon is a polygon that is the boundary of a convex set: the line segment between any two points of the polygon — including its interior — lies entirely within the polygon. Equivalently, every interior angle of a convex polygon is at most 180 degrees, and every diagonal lies entirely inside the polygon.

Properties

Every triangle is convex. A polygon is convex if and only if it is the intersection of finitely many half-planes, one for each edge. For a convex polygon with vertices in order, all cross products of successive edge vectors have the same sign, which gives a simple O(n) convexity test. A convex polygon is the convex hull of its vertices, and its interior angles sum to (n−2)·180° for n sides.

Strict convexity

A convex polygon is strictly convex if no three vertices are collinear, so that every interior angle is strictly less than 180°. Regular polygons are strictly convex (for n ≥ 3).

Applications

Convex polygons appear in collision detection, clipping in computer graphics (the Sutherland–Hodgman algorithm clips against a convex window), and as feasible regions of two-variable linear programs.
