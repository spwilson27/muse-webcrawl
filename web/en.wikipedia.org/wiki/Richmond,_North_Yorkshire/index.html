Richmond, North Yorkshire

Richmond is a market town and civil parish in North Yorkshire, England, on the River Swale at the eastern gateway to Swaledale. It gave its name to Richmondshire and, through its dukes and earls, to many other places named Richmond around the world.

History

The town was founded in 1071 by Alan Rufus, a Breton companion of William the Conqueror, who built Richmond Castle — one of the earliest Norman stone castles in England, with the great keep added by Conan IV, Duke of Brittany. The town grew around the castle and its large cobbled market place.

Landmarks

Landmarks include Richmond Castle, the Georgian Theatre Royal (1788, one of Britain's oldest working theatres), the ruins of Easby Abbey nearby, and the Green Howards Regimental Museum. Nearby Catterick Garrison is the largest British Army garrison in the world.
