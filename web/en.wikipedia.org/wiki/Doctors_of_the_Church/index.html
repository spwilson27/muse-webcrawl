Doctors of the Church

Doctor of the Church
Latin: doctor ecclesiae
Total recognised: 38 (Catholic Church)
First recognised: Athanasius, Basil, Gregory of Nazianzus, John Chrysostom (East); Ambrose, Jerome, Augustine, Gregory the Great (West)

Doctor of the Church (Latin: doctor, 'teacher') is a title given by the Catholic Church, and in some traditions by other churches, to saints recognised as having made a significant contribution to theology or doctrine through their research, study, or writing.

Criteria

Traditionally three requirements are listed: eminent learning (eminens doctrina), a high degree of sanctity (insignis vitae sanctitas), and proclamation by the Church. Only canonised saints are named.

History

The original eight—four Greek Fathers and four Latin Fathers including Augustine of Hippo and Jerome—were later joined by figures such as Thomas Aquinas, Bonaventure, Catherine of Siena, Teresa of Ávila, and Thérèse of Lisieux. Four women are among the Catholic Doctors.

List

Recent additions include John of Ávila and Hildegard of Bingen (2012), Gregory of Narek (2015), Irenaeus of Lyon (2022), and John Henry Newman, proclaimed in 2025. Eastern Orthodox and Anglican traditions use the title more informally for great teachers of the faith.
