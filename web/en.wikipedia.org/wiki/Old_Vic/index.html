Old Vic

Location: The Cut, Lambeth, London
Opened: 1818
Capacity: about 1,067

The Old Vic is a 1,000-seat, not-for-profit producing theatre in the London Borough of Lambeth, on the corner of The Cut and Waterloo Road, near Waterloo station. Established in 1818 as the Royal Coburg Theatre, it was later renamed the Royal Victoria Theatre; "Old Vic" derives from "Victoria".

History

Under Emma Cons from 1880 and then her niece Lilian Baylis from 1912, the theatre became famous for producing the complete works of Shakespeare and for making theatre and opera accessible to working-class audiences. Baylis also founded the companies that became the Royal Ballet and English National Opera at Sadler's Wells. From 1963 to 1976 the Old Vic was the home of the National Theatre company under Laurence Olivier. In the 2000s, under artistic director Kevin Spacey (2004–2015), the theatre regained an international profile; Matthew Warchus became artistic director in 2015.

The building

The theatre, a Grade II* listed building, has been rebuilt and altered several times. It was damaged by bombing in the Second World War and reopened in 1950. A major refurbishment was completed in 2019, improving accessibility and facilities.
