The San Onofre Nuclear Generating Station (SONGS) is a permanently closed nuclear power plant located on the Pacific coast in San Onofre, near San Clemente in Orange County, California, within the Camp Pendleton Marine Corps base area. Its twin spherical containment domes became a famous landmark visible from Interstate 5. The plant's two operating reactors were shut down in 2012 after problems with replacement steam generators, and in June 2013 the operator, Southern California Edison, announced the plant would close permanently.

History

Unit 1, a first-generation Westinghouse pressurised water reactor, entered service in 1968 and was retired in 1992. Units 2 and 3, larger Combustion Engineering reactors of about 1,100 megawatts each, entered service in 1983 and 1984. At full power the station supplied electricity to roughly 1.4 million homes in Southern California.

Steam generator failure and closure

Units 2 and 3 received replacement steam generators in 2009–2011. In January 2012 a small radioactive leak from a worn tube led to the shutdown of Unit 3, and inspections found unexpectedly rapid tube wear in both units' new generators, caused by vibration from fluid-elastic instability. Both reactors remained offline while investigations continued. In June 2013 Edison concluded that a restart was uneconomic amid regulatory uncertainty and retired both units, making San Onofre one of the most prominent U.S. plant closures of the era. A settlement apportioned the multi-billion-dollar closure and replacement-power costs between the utility and ratepayers.

Decommissioning and spent fuel

Decommissioning and demolition of the plant began in earnest in 2020 and is expected to take about a decade. Spent nuclear fuel remains stored on site in dry casks at an Independent Spent Fuel Storage Installation, a subject of local controversy because of the site's coastal location and the absence of a federal repository.
