Ezra

Ezra (fl. 5th century BCE), also called Ezra the Scribe and Ezra the Priest in the Book of Ezra, was a Jewish scribe (sofer) and priest (kohen). In Greco-Latin Ezra is called Esdras. According to the Hebrew Bible, he was a descendant of Sraya, the last High Priest to serve in the First Temple, and a close relative of Joshua, the first High Priest of the Second Temple. He returned from Babylonian exile and reintroduced the Torah in Jerusalem.

Biblical narrative

The Book of Ezra describes how Artaxerxes, king of Persia, commissioned Ezra to lead a group of exiles back to Jerusalem and to teach the laws of God. Ezra is celebrated for reading the Torah aloud to the assembled people (as recounted in the Book of Nehemiah, chapter 8) and for measures enforcing the law, including the dissolution of intermarriages. Jewish tradition credits Ezra with a central role in the formation of the canon and the institutions of post-exilic Judaism; he is sometimes called a second Moses.

Historicity and dating

Scholars debate whether Ezra preceded or followed Nehemiah, and whether his mission is dated to the reign of Artaxerxes I (458 BCE) or Artaxerxes II (398 BCE). The figure of Ezra also appears in later literature, including the apocryphal 2 Esdras, where he receives apocalyptic visions and dictates restored scriptures. In Islam, Ezra is identified by many commentators with Uzair, mentioned in the Quran.
