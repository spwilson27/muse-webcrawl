Existential crisis

An existential crisis is a moment or period in which an individual questions whether their life has meaning, purpose, or value, often accompanied by anxiety, confusion about identity, and confrontation with mortality and freedom. The concept is central to existentialism.

Existential crisis
Related: Existentialism, angst, absurdism
Thinkers: Kierkegaard, Sartre, Camus, Frankl

Description

Triggers can include major life transitions, loss, trauma, or the so-called midlife and quarter-life crises. Common features are a sense of meaninglessness, isolation, awareness of death, and questioning of choices and authenticity.

Philosophical background

Søren Kierkegaard wrote of despair and the leap of faith; Jean-Paul Sartre argued existence precedes essence; Albert Camus explored the absurd; Viktor Frankl's logotherapy proposed meaning can be found even in suffering.

Resolution

Approaches include existential psychotherapy, creating new commitments and values, and what some writers call passing through stages from crisis to engagement and renewed purpose.
