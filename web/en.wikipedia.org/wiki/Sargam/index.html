Sargam

The seven sargam syllables
OriginIndian classical music
SyllablesSa Re Ga Ma Pa Dha Ni (Ṡa)

Sargam refers to singing the notes of a musical composition in Indian classical music instead of the words, using the solfa syllables sa, re, ga, ma, pa, dha, ni. The name is an acronym of the first four syllables: Sa-Re-Ga-Ma. Sargam is the Indian equivalent of Western solfège (do, re, mi, fa, sol, la, ti).

The syllables

The seven notes (svaras) of the octave are Shadja (Sa), Rishabha (Re), Gandhara (Ga), Madhyama (Ma), Panchama (Pa), Dhaivata (Dha), and Nishada (Ni), returning to the upper Sa. Sa and Pa are fixed, while the other notes have variant forms (komal, or flattened, and tivra, or sharpened).

Use

Sargam is fundamental to musical training in both Hindustani and Carnatic traditions: students learn scales and ragas by singing sargam before attempting compositions. Virtuoso sargam singing — rapid, precisely pitched passages sung to the syllables — is also a feature of performance in khyal, Carnatic concerts, and much Indian film and popular music.
