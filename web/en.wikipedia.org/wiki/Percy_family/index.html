Percy family

The Percy family is an English noble family, one of the most powerful in northern England for much of the Middle Ages and beyond. Descended from William de Percy, a Norman companion of William the Conqueror, the family held vast estates in Yorkshire and later Northumberland, and its head has held the earldom — and since 1766 the dukedom — of Northumberland.

History

William de Percy was rewarded after the Conquest with lands centred on Topcliffe in Yorkshire. Through marriage in the 12th century the family acquired the estates of the de Lucy heiress, and in 1309 Henry de Percy bought Alnwick Castle, which remains the family seat. The Percys were repeatedly at the centre of English politics: Henry "Hotspur" Percy was killed rebelling against Henry IV at the Battle of Shrewsbury in 1403 (an episode dramatised by Shakespeare), and successive earls were involved in the Wars of the Roses, the Pilgrimage of Grace, and the Rising of the North in 1569.

Dukes of Northumberland

The male line failed in 1670, but the name and estates passed through Elizabeth Percy to the Seymour and then the Smithson families; Hugh Smithson, who took the name Percy, was created 1st Duke of Northumberland in 1766. The present duke resides at Alnwick Castle, which is also well known as a filming location, including for the Harry Potter films.

Legacy

The Percy lion and crescent badge, the Percy Cross in various northern towns, and numerous Percy tenantry columns and monuments testify to the family's enduring presence in Northumberland. Branches of the family also held the earldom of Worcester and titles including Baron Percy.
