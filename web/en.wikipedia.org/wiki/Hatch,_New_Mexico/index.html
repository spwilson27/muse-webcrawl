Hatch is a village in Doña Ana County, New Mexico, United States. The population was 1,539 at the 2020 census. Hatch styles itself the "Chile Capital of the World" and is famed for the green and red chile peppers grown in the surrounding Hatch Valley along the Rio Grande; every Labor Day weekend the village hosts the Hatch Chile Festival, which draws tens of thousands of visitors.

History

Hatch owes its existence to the Atchison, Topeka and Santa Fe Railway, which reached the area in the 1880s; the settlement was named for Edward Hatch, a Civil War general and commander of the District of New Mexico. The community was originally called Santa Barbara, was flooded by the Rio Grande in 1874, and was re-established at the present site around the railroad. Irrigation from Elephant Butte Dam (completed 1916) and Caballo Reservoir transformed the valley into intensively farmed cropland.

Geography

Hatch lies in the Hatch Valley of the Rio Grande rift in south-central New Mexico, about 41 miles northwest of Las Cruces on Interstate 25. The village covers roughly 3.1 square miles of land. The surrounding agriculture — chile peppers, onions, pecans, lettuce and cotton — depends on irrigation water stored upstream.

Hatch chile

"Hatch chile" refers to peppers of the New Mexico chile cultivars grown in the Hatch Valley; the name is protected by New Mexico state law, which since 2011 has required certification for peppers marketed as New Mexico chile to prevent out-of-state produce being sold under the name. The annual festival, begun in the 1970s, features roasting, a parade and a chile queen.
