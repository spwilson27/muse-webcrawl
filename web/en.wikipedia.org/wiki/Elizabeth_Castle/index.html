Elizabeth Castle is a castle and military fortification on a tidal islet within St Aubin's Bay, in the parish of Saint Helier on the island of Jersey. Built over a 300-year period beginning in 1594, it defended the island and its harbour for more than 400 years. The castle is accessible on foot at low tide along a causeway, or by amphibious ferry at high tide.

History

Construction began under Sir Walter Raleigh, Governor of Jersey, who named the castle after Queen Elizabeth I. During the English Civil War it was a Royalist stronghold; the future Charles II took refuge there in 1646 and again in 1649, before the castle surrendered to Parliamentarian forces in December 1651 after a seven-week siege, when a shell struck the medieval chapel used as a powder store.

The fortifications were repeatedly modernised, including work under German occupation during the Second World War, when the island's defences were incorporated into the Atlantic Wall. After the war the castle was bought by the States of Jersey and is now managed by Jersey Heritage as a museum and visitor attraction.

Description

The castle occupies two linked rocky islets and comprises upper and lower wards, a parade ground, barracks, powder magazines and the governor's house. Exhibitions inside cover the military history of Jersey, including the militia and the castle's role through both world wars.
