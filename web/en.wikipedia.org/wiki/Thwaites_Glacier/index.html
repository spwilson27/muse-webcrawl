Thwaites Glacier is an unusually broad and fast-moving Antarctic glacier flowing into Pine Island Bay, part of the Amundsen Sea, east of Mount Murphy in Marie Byrd Land. Together with Pine Island Glacier, it is one of the major ice streams draining the West Antarctic Ice Sheet, and it has been described by scientists as one of the most important glaciers in the world in terms of future sea-level rise, earning it the nickname the "Doomsday Glacier".

Retreat and collapse risk

Surface speeds of Thwaites exceed 2 kilometres per year near its grounding line. Studies using satellite data have shown that the glacier has been retreating and losing mass at an accelerating rate since the 1990s, partly because warm ocean water melts its floating ice shelf from below. Thwaites already contributes about 4% of global sea-level rise. Because the bedrock beneath it slopes downward inland, the glacier is considered vulnerable to marine ice sheet instability; its collapse could destabilise neighbouring parts of the West Antarctic Ice Sheet, which together hold enough ice to raise global sea levels by several metres over centuries.

Research

The International Thwaites Glacier Collaboration, a joint UK–US research programme launched in 2018, studies the glacier's past and likely future behaviour using ice-penetrating radar, ocean robots, and drilling through the ice shelf.
