Professional engineer

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A professional engineer (PE in the United States; P.Eng. in Canada; comparable titles include Chartered Engineer, CEng, in the United Kingdom) is an engineer who is licensed and legally authorised to practise engineering to the public, to sign and seal engineering documents, and to take responsibility for engineering work affecting public safety, health and welfare.

Licensing

Licensure typically requires an accredited engineering degree, passing a fundamentals examination, several years of supervised professional experience (often four), and passing a professional practice examination. Licensed engineers are bound by a code of ethics enforced by the licensing board, and may be disciplined or lose their licence for negligence or misconduct.

Scope

In many jurisdictions a licence is legally required to offer engineering services to the public, to serve as an expert witness, or to approve designs for buildings, bridges, water systems and other public works. Engineers working in industry under the so-called "industrial exemption" (e.g., designing products inside a manufacturing firm in much of the US) may practise without a licence, though many obtain one voluntarily.

International equivalents

Canada regulates the title through provincial and territorial associations (e.g., Professional Engineers Ontario). The UK Engineering Council registers Chartered Engineers and Incorporated Engineers. EUR ING is a European title coordinated by Engineers Europe (formerly FEANI). Mutual-recognition accords such as the Washington Accord align educational standards across countries.
