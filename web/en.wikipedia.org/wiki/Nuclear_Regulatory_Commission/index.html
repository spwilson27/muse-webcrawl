Nuclear Regulatory Commission

The United States Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) is an independent agency of the United States government responsible for protecting public health and safety in relation to nuclear energy. It regulates the civilian use of nuclear materials, including commercial nuclear power plants, research reactors, fuel-cycle facilities and the medical and industrial uses of radioactive material.

History

The NRC was created by the Energy Reorganization Act of 1974 and began operating in January 1975, taking over the regulatory functions of the former Atomic Energy Commission, whose combined promotional and regulatory roles had been criticised as a conflict of interest. The agency's safety regime was reshaped after the Three Mile Island accident of 1979, and again following the 2011 Fukushima Daiichi disaster in Japan, when it imposed new requirements for beyond-design-basis events on US plants.

Organisation and functions

The NRC is headed by five commissioners appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate, one of whom serves as chair. Headquartered in Rockville, Maryland, with four regional offices, it employs around 3,000 staff and has an annual budget of roughly $1 billion, most of it recovered from licensee fees. Its responsibilities include licensing new reactors and licence renewals, inspection of operating plants (including resident inspectors at each site), regulation of nuclear waste storage and transport, and oversight of plant decommissioning. It does not regulate military nuclear facilities, which fall to the Department of Energy and the Navy.
