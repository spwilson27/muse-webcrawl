Old Bailey

The Central Criminal Court of England and Wales, commonly known as the Old Bailey after the street on which it stands, is a criminal court building in central London. It is the most famous criminal court in the country and hears major cases from Greater London and, occasionally, other parts of England and Wales.

History

The court stands on the site of the notorious Newgate Prison, which was demolished in 1902. The present building, designed by E. W. Mountford, opened in 1907. It stands on or near the line of the medieval city wall — the "bailey" from which it takes its name. The dome is topped by a bronze statue of Lady Justice holding a sword and scales; contrary to popular belief, the figure is not blindfolded.

Famous trials

Historic trials at the Old Bailey include those of Oscar Wilde, Dr Crippen, the Kray twins, the Yorkshire Ripper Peter Sutcliffe, and former MP Jeffrey Archer. The Proceedings of the Old Bailey, published from 1674 to 1913, have been digitised and are a major resource for historians.

The building today

The court has 18 courtrooms. Major extensions were added in the twentieth century, and the building remains a working courthouse of the Crown Court.
