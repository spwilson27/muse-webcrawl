THX 1138

THX 1138 is a 1971 American social science fiction film co-written and directed by George Lucas in his feature film directorial debut. Produced by Francis Ford Coppola's American Zoetrope, it stars Robert Duvall and Donald Pleasence. The film is set in a dystopian future in which the citizens of an underground city are controlled by mandatory drug use and android police, and emotions and sexual desire are suppressed.

Plot

THX 1138 (Duvall) works in a factory building the android police who enforce the society's laws. His roommate LUH 3417 secretly replaces his medication with inert pills, and as the drugs wear off THX experiences genuine emotion and falls in love with her. Reported by his eventual roommate SEN 5241, THX is arrested, tried and imprisoned in a featureless white void, from which he escapes in a climactic chase, finally climbing to the surface as the sun sets.

Background

The film was developed from Lucas's student short Electronic Labyrinth: THX 1138 4EB, made at the USC School of Cinematic Arts. It was a commercial failure on release but has since become a cult classic, praised for its stark visual style and minimalist sound design by Walter Murch. In 2004 Lucas released a director's cut with digitally altered and extended scenes. The title later lent its name to Lucas's THX sound certification company.
