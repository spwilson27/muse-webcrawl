Académie Française

Formation1635
FounderCardinal Richelieu
Membership40 members ("les immortels")
HeadquartersInstitut de France, Paris

The Académie Française is France's principal authority on matters pertaining to the French language. Officially established in 1635 by Cardinal Richelieu, the chief minister to King Louis XIII, the Académie consists of forty members known informally as les immortels (the immortals), elected for life.

Role

The Académie's best-known task is the publication of the official dictionary of the French language, the Dictionnaire de l'Académie française, first published in 1694. It also advises on terminology and grammar and awards literary prizes. New members are received in a formal ceremony under the dome of the Institut de France, where they deliver a speech in praise of their predecessor.

History

Begun as an informal circle of men of letters meeting in Paris, the body was given royal letters patent in 1635 and charged with giving rules to the language. It was suppressed during the Revolution and restored under Napoleon as part of the Institut de France.
