Alizarin

IUPAC name1,2-Dihydroxyanthracene-9,10-dione
FormulaC₁₄H₈O₄
AppearanceRed-orange crystals
SourceMadder root; synthetic from anthraquinone
UsesDye (Turkey red); pigment (madder lake, rose madder)

Alizarin is an organic compound, 1,2-dihydroxyanthraquinone, that has been used throughout history as a prominent red dye, principally for dyeing textile fabrics. It is the main colouring matter of the madder plant root, used since antiquity in the Middle East, Egypt, and Europe.

History

Madder was cultivated for millennia, and cloth dyed with it has been found in ancient Egyptian tombs. In 1868, German chemists Carl Graebe and Carl Liebermann determined alizarin's structure and synthesised it from anthracene — the first synthesis of a major natural dye. Commercial production of synthetic alizarin from 1869 onward rapidly destroyed the madder-farming industry.

Uses

With metal mordants, alizarin forms insoluble coloured complexes ("lakes") in shades from red to brown and purple; the famous Turkey red process produced a brilliant, fast red on cotton. Its aluminium and calcium lakes are the pigments known as madder lake and rose madder, long used by artists. Alizarin derivatives remain in use as dyes, pigments, and biological stains, such as alizarin red S for detecting calcium.
