Smouldering myeloma

Smouldering myeloma (also spelled smoldering myeloma in American English), or smouldering multiple myeloma (SMM), is a plasma cell disorder intermediate between monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance (MGUS) and active multiple myeloma. It is characterised by an increased level of monoclonal protein (M protein) and/or an increased proportion of clonal plasma cells in the bone marrow, but without the end-organ damage (the "CRAB" features — hypercalcaemia, renal failure, anaemia, bone lesions) or other myeloma-defining events that require treatment.

Diagnostic criteria

Under International Myeloma Working Group criteria, smouldering myeloma is defined by both:

  • Serum monoclonal protein (IgG or IgA) of 3 g/dL or more, or urinary monoclonal protein of 500 mg or more per 24 hours, and/or 10–60% clonal plasma cells in the bone marrow; and
  • Absence of myeloma-defining events or amyloidosis.

Progression and risk stratification

Smouldering myeloma progresses to active multiple myeloma at roughly 10% per year for the first five years after diagnosis, about 3% per year for the next five years, and about 1% per year thereafter — contrasting with MGUS, which progresses at only about 1% per year. Risk models, such as the Mayo Clinic "20/2/20" model, use bone marrow plasma cell percentage above 20%, M protein above 2 g/dL and a serum free light chain ratio above 20 to identify high-risk patients, in whom early treatment may delay progression. Trials such as QuiRedex and E3A06 showed that lenalidomide-based therapy can prolong progression-free survival in high-risk SMM, though watchful waiting with regular monitoring remains standard for most patients.

Management

Because SMM is asymptomatic, patients are generally monitored with periodic blood tests, serum free light chain assays, imaging and bone marrow assessment rather than treated immediately. Patients are counselled about symptoms of progression — bone pain, fatigue, recurrent infection and unintentional weight loss — that should prompt reassessment.
