Hari Singh

Sir Hari Singh GCSI GCIE KCVO (23 September 1895 – 26 April 1961) was the last ruling Maharaja of the princely state of Jammu and Kashmir in British India. He reigned from 1925 until the state's accession to India in 1947 and remained titular head until 1952, when the monarchy was abolished.

Reign: 23 September 1925 – 17 November 1952

Predecessor: Pratap Singh

Successor: Monarchy abolished (Karan Singh as head of state)

Born: 23 September 1895, Jammu

Died: 26 April 1961 (aged 65), Bombay

Dynasty: Dogra (Jamwal)

Early life

Hari Singh was born at Amar Mahal Palace, Jammu, the son of Raja Amar Singh, brother of Maharaja Pratap Singh. He was educated at Mayo College, Ajmer, and received military training at the Imperial Cadet Corps, Dehradun. In 1915 he was appointed commander-in-chief of the state forces. He succeeded his uncle Pratap Singh as Maharaja in 1925.

Reign

As ruler, Hari Singh introduced a number of reforms: he made primary education compulsory, prohibited child marriage and infanticide, opened state temples to low-caste Hindus, and enacted laws against begar (forced labour). In 1931, political agitation among his largely Muslim subjects led to riots and the appointment of the Glancy Commission, which recommended reforms. The Praja Sabha, a partially elected legislative assembly, was established in 1934.

At the Round Table Conference in London in 1930–31, Hari Singh spoke in favour of an all-India federation while insisting on the internal autonomy of the princely states.

Accession and later life

On the partition of India in 1947, Hari Singh initially sought to keep Jammu and Kashmir independent, signing a standstill agreement with Pakistan. In October 1947, an invasion by tribal militias from Pakistan's North-West Frontier Province prompted him to seek Indian military assistance; on 26 October 1947 he signed the Instrument of Accession to India, a decision that triggered the Indo-Pakistani War of 1947–48 and lies at the root of the Kashmir conflict.

In 1949 he stepped aside in favour of his son, Yuvraj Karan Singh, who became regent and later Sadr-i-Riyasat. Hari Singh spent his remaining years in Bombay, where he died in 1961.
