Ogdoad (Egyptian)

In ancient Egyptian religion, the Ogdoad (from Greek ὀγδοάς, "the eightfold") was a group of eight primordial deities worshipped principally at Hermopolis (Egyptian Khmunu, "City of the Eight"). The eight gods formed four male–female pairs representing the fundamental qualities of the primeval chaos that existed before creation.

The eight deities

The four pairs, with the male gods typically depicted as frogs or frog-headed men and the females as snakes or snake-headed women, were:

  • Nu and Naunet — the primeval waters;
  • Heh and Hauhet — infinity or eternity;
  • Kek and Kauket — darkness;
  • Amun and Amunet — hiddenness or air.

Names and membership vary somewhat between sources; some texts substitute pairs representing concepts such as formlessness or obscurity.

Cosmology

In the Hermopolitan creation myth, the interaction of the Ogdoad's forces within the primeval waters produced an explosive emergence — variously described as a mound, an island of flame, or a cosmic egg — from which the sun god (Ra or Atum) arose, bringing light and beginning the creation of the ordered world. Later Hermopolitan theology also venerated Thoth, the ibis-headed god of wisdom, as the city's chief deity. The Ogdoad also appears in Theban theology, where Amun's primeval aspect connects the two systems.
