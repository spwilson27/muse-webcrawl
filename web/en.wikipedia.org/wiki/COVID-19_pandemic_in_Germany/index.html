COVID-19 pandemic in Germany

The COVID-19 pandemic in Germany was part of the worldwide pandemic of coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) caused by severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2). The virus was confirmed to have reached Germany on 27 January 2020, when the first case was identified near Munich, Bavaria, linked to an employee of the automotive supplier Webasto who had contact with a visiting colleague from Wuhan, China.

Course of the pandemic

Early clusters appeared in Bavaria and, after carnival celebrations, in the district of Heinsberg in North Rhine-Westphalia. In March 2020, the federal government under Chancellor Angela Merkel and the sixteen state governments agreed on nationwide restrictions including school closures, bans on large gatherings, and a "lockdown" beginning 22 March. Germany's comparatively low first-wave death rate was attributed to extensive testing capacity, a well-resourced hospital system, and the younger age profile of early cases.

Later waves in autumn and winter 2020–21 and 2021–22 prompted renewed restrictions, including a "lockdown light" in November 2020 and a harder lockdown from December 2020 into spring 2021. The vaccination campaign began on 27 December 2020. The Robert Koch Institute served as the national public health institute responsible for disease monitoring and reporting. Overall, Germany recorded more than 38 million confirmed cases and over 170,000 deaths over the course of the pandemic period.

Response

Government measures included the Corona-Warn-App contact-tracing application, mask mandates on public transport, extensive economic support packages (Kurzarbeit short-time work and aid to businesses), and later the 2G/3G access rules based on vaccination, recovery, or testing status.
