Federally funded research and development centers

Federally funded research and development centers (FFRDCs) are public-private partnerships that conduct research and development for the United States Government. They are administered by universities, non-profit corporations, or industrial firms under long-term contracts.

FFRDC

Number: ~42 (NSF master list)
Major sponsors: Dept. of Energy, Dept. of Defense, NSF, NASA, DHS

History

The first FFRDCs arose in World War II, notably the Radiation Laboratory at MIT and Los Alamos. RAND Corporation (1948) became the archetypal think-tank FFRDC for the Air Force. The model expanded during the Cold War to national laboratories and systems engineering centers.

Characteristics

FFRDCs have a special long-term relationship with their sponsor, access beyond normal contracting, and must be free of conflicts of interest and not compete with industry. Examples include Los Alamos, Lawrence Livermore, Sandia, Brookhaven and Argonne national laboratories, MIT Lincoln Laboratory, Jet Propulsion Laboratory, MITRE, Aerospace Corporation, and CNA.

Governance

Designation and oversight follow Federal Acquisition Regulation 35.017 and an NSF-maintained master list.
