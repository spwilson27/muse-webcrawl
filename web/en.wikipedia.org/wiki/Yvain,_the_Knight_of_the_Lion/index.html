Yvain, the Knight of the Lion

Yvain, the Knight of the Lion (French: Yvain ou le Chevalier au lion) is a romance by the French poet Chrétien de Troyes, written in Old French verse in the 1170s, contemporary with his Lancelot, the Knight of the Cart. It tells of Sir Yvain, a Knight of the Round Table, who wins the love of the lady Laudine, loses it through his obsession with chivalric adventure, and regains it after madness, penance and the faithful help of a lion he rescues. It is considered one of the masterpieces of medieval literature and the finest of Chrétien's romances.

Plot summary

Yvain journeys to a magic fountain in the forest of Brocéliande that raises a storm when water is poured on its stone, and defeats its guardian, Esclados, whom he pursues and kills. With the help of the clever maid Lunete, he marries Esclados's widow Laudine. On Gawain's urging he leaves for tournaments, promising to return within a year; he overstays, and Laudine's messenger renounces him. Stricken mad, Yvain lives wild in the forest until cured by a magic ointment. He rescues a lion from a serpent, and the grateful beast becomes his companion. As the anonymous "Knight of the Lion" he defends the disinherited, frees captives from the giant Harpin and the demons of the Pesme Avanture castle, and is finally reconciled with Laudine through Lunete's stratagem.

Sources and adaptations

Chrétien's tale draws on Welsh tradition: the Welsh prose tale Owain, or the Lady of the Fountain, one of the Three Welsh Romances associated with the Mabinogion, tells a closely related story from the figure of Owain mab Urien. Hartmann von Aue adapted Chrétien's poem into Middle High German as Iwein (c. 1200), and there are Old Norse and Middle English versions (Ywain and Gawain).
