The Regent's Canal is a canal across the northern part of central London, England. It runs 8.6 miles (13.8 km) from the Paddington Arm of the Grand Union Canal in the west to the Limehouse Basin and the River Thames in the east, passing through Camden Town, Islington, and Hackney.

History

Proposed by Thomas Homer in 1802 and authorised by Act of Parliament in 1812, the canal was built under the direction of architect John Nash, for whom it formed part of the Regent's Park development, and engineer James Morgan. The first section opened in 1816 and the canal was completed to Limehouse in 1820. It carried coal and building materials for over a century until road transport displaced commercial traffic in the mid-20th century.

The canal today

The towpath is now a popular route for walkers and cyclists. The canal passes London Zoo, the houseboats of Little Venice, Camden Lock Market, and the Islington tunnel. A branch, the Hertford Union Canal, links it to the Lee Navigation.
