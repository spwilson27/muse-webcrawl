Politics of Georgia (country)

Parliament of Georgia, Tbilisi

The politics of Georgia take place in a framework of a parliamentary representative democratic republic, with a ceremonial president as head of state and a prime minister as head of government. Since independence from the Soviet Union in 1991, Georgia's political life has been marked by periods of instability, reformist governments, and a persistent orientation—contested in recent years—toward integration with the European Union and NATO.

History

Georgia's first post-Soviet president, Zviad Gamsakhurdia, was overthrown in a coup in 1992 and succeeded by Eduard Shevardnadze. The peaceful Rose Revolution of 2003 forced Shevardnadze's resignation and brought Mikheil Saakashvili to power; his government pursued sweeping economic liberalisation, anti-corruption reforms, and close ties with the United States, but also fought the 2008 Russo-Georgian War, after which Russia recognised the breakaway regions of Abkhazia and South Ossetia as independent.

Recent politics

In 2012 the Georgian Dream coalition, founded by billionaire Bidzina Ivanishvili, defeated Saakashvili's United National Movement in parliamentary elections—Georgia's first peaceful, democratic transfer of power. Constitutional reforms completed in 2018 converted Georgia into a parliamentary republic, with the president elected indirectly from 2024. Georgian Dream has remained in power since 2012; its governments have faced opposition protests and criticism from Western partners over democratic backsliding, particularly after the adoption of a controversial "foreign influence" law in 2024, which led the EU to effectively freeze Georgia's accession process.

Elections

The Parliament of Georgia has 150 members, elected since 2024 by fully proportional representation with a 5 per cent threshold. Municipal elections choose local councils (sakrebulo) and mayors. Elections have generally been competitive, though international observers have noted problems including pressure on voters and misuse of administrative resources.
