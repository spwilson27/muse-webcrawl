The Thing is a 1982 American science fiction horror film directed by John Carpenter, written by Bill Lancaster, and starring Kurt Russell. Based on John W. Campbell Jr.'s 1938 novella Who Goes There?, it tells of the crew of an Antarctic research station who encounter a shape-shifting alien organism that perfectly imitates the life forms it assimilates.

Plot

After a Norwegian helicopter pursues a sled dog into their station, the Americans investigate the ruined Norwegian camp and recover a deformed, burned corpse. The dog — the Thing in disguise — mutates and attacks. Biologist Blair concludes that if the organism reaches civilisation it could assimilate all life on Earth within a few years. Paranoia consumes the group as they devise a blood test to expose the impostor; in the famous final scene, MacReady and Childs, the last survivors, sit amid the burning wreckage, each unsure whether the other is human.

Effects and reception

The film's practical creature effects, designed chiefly by Rob Bottin, remain benchmarks of the genre. On release the film was a box-office disappointment and drew hostile reviews for its bleakness and gore; it was rediscovered on home video and is now considered one of the greatest horror films ever made. A prequel of the same name followed in 2011.
