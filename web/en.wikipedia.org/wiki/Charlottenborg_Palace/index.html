Charlottenborg Palace (Danish: Charlottenborg Slot) is a large Baroque mansion on Kongens Nytorv in central Copenhagen, Denmark. Built between 1672 and 1683 in the Dutch Baroque style, it is one of the earliest great townhouses of Copenhagen and has been home to the Royal Danish Academy of Fine Arts since 1754.

History

The palace was built for Ulrik Frederik Gyldenløve, the illegitimate son of King Frederick III and viceroy of Norway, on land at the new King's Square. Originally called Gyldenløve's Palace, it was designed in imitation of Dutch classicist Baroque palaces, with a three-winged main block around a courtyard facing the square. In 1699 King Christian V bought the palace for his widow, Queen Charlotte Amalie, after whom it was renamed Charlottenborg. It served as her dower residence until her death in 1714.

The Academy

In 1754 the palace was ceded to the newly founded Royal Danish Academy of Fine Arts, which still has its seat there. Generations of Denmark's leading artists and architects have taught and studied at Charlottenborg, and the Academy's annual spring exhibition, held in the palace's exhibition building, has been a fixture of Danish art life since the 19th century. That building now houses Kunsthal Charlottenborg, one of Denmark's leading venues for contemporary art, as well as the Danish National School of Performing Arts' former facilities and the Royal Danish Academy's library.

Architecture

The palace is built of brick with sandstone details, its main façade toward Kongens Nytorv articulated by pilasters and a central projection crowned by a pediment. Behind lies a lower courtyard wing complex. Additions in the 19th century included the exhibition building along Nyhavn (1883), notable for its rich brick ornament.
