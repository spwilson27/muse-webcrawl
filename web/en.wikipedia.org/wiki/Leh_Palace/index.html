Leh Palace is a former royal palace overlooking the town of Leh in Ladakh, in the Indian Himalayas. It was constructed circa 1600–1610 by King Sengge Namgyal of the Namgyal dynasty, the ruler remembered as the "Lion King" of Ladakh. Modelled in part on the Potala Palace in Lhasa, which it predates in its present form, the palace is built of stone, wood, mud, and sand in the medieval Tibetan style, rising nine storeys above the old town.

History

The palace served as the residence of the Ladakhi royal family until the mid-19th century, when Dogra forces from Jammu invaded Ladakh and the family moved to the Stok Palace. During the siege the upper floors suffered damage, and the building was largely abandoned thereafter. The royal apartments occupied the upper storeys, while the lower floors served as stables, storerooms, and quarters for retainers.

Architecture and present day

Thick tapering walls, projecting wooden balconies, and small windows characterise the structure, designed to withstand earthquakes and the severe Ladakhi winters. Inside, the palace contains temple rooms with surviving murals and a small museum displaying thangkas, jewellery, ceremonial dress, and paintings. The Archaeological Survey of India has undertaken extensive restoration, and the palace is now a major tourist attraction in Leh, offering panoramic views over the Indus valley and the Stok range. A notable feature is the Victory Tower (Namgyal Tsemo) on the ridge above, built to commemorate Ladakhi victories over Balti invaders.
