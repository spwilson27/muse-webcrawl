High-speed rail in Italy

Italy operates one of Europe's pioneering high-speed rail networks. The first European high-speed line, the Florence–Rome Direttissima, opened in stages between 1977 and 1992, and Italy's "Alta Velocità" network now links Turin, Milan, Bologna, Florence, Rome, Naples and Salerno, with trains reaching 300 km/h in commercial service. Infrastructure is managed by Rete Ferroviaria Italiana (RFI).

Services

State operator Trenitalia runs the Le Frecce services — Frecciarossa, Frecciargento and Frecciabianca — using trains such as the ETR 500 and the Frecciarossa 1000 (ETR 400), the latter designed for 360 km/h. Since 2012 Italy has also had the world's first private high-speed operator, NTV (Nuovo Trasporto Viaggiatori), whose Italo trains (AGV and Pendolino EVO) compete directly with Trenitalia on the main corridors — the first case of on-track competition in high-speed rail.

Network

Principal high-speed lines include Turin–Milan, Milan–Bologna, Bologna–Florence (largely in tunnel through the Apennines), Rome–Naples and Naples–Salerno. Lines use 25 kV AC electrification with ETCS Level 2 signalling. Major projects under construction or planned include the Turin–Lyon base tunnel, the Brenner Base Tunnel approaches, the Naples–Bari line and extensions toward the south.
