AmeriCorps is a United States federal program of national service administered by the federal agency of the same name (formerly the Corporation for National and Community Service). Members commit to full-time or part-time service — often a year — addressing community needs in education, public safety, health, disaster response, and the environment, in exchange for a modest living allowance and an education award.

History

AmeriCorps was created by the National and Community Service Trust Act of 1993, signed by President Bill Clinton, and launched in 1994. It consolidated earlier service programs, including VISTA (Volunteers in Service to America, founded 1965) and the National Civilian Community Corps (NCCC), modelled partly on the Civilian Conservation Corps of the 1930s. The Edward M. Kennedy Serve America Act of 2009 authorised a major expansion.

Programs

AmeriCorps State and National funds grants to nonprofits and public agencies. AmeriCorps VISTA places members with projects aimed at alleviating poverty. AmeriCorps NCCC is a team-based residential program for 18- to 26-year-olds that responds to disasters and infrastructure projects. AmeriCorps Seniors includes the Foster Grandparent and Senior Companion programs.

Benefits

On completing a term of service, members receive the Segal AmeriCorps Education Award, usable toward student loans or further education, with an amount tied to the maximum Pell Grant.
