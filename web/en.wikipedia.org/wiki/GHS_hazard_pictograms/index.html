GHS hazard pictograms

Hazard pictograms form part of the international Globally Harmonized System of Classification and Labelling of Chemicals (GHS). Two sets of pictograms are included within the GHS: one for the labelling of containers and for workplace hazard warnings, and a second set for use during the transport of dangerous goods. Either the GHS set or the transport set is chosen, depending on the target audience, but the two are not used together for the same hazard.

Design

The GHS pictograms for physical, health and environmental hazards consist of a black symbol on a white background with a red diamond-shaped border (a square set on a point). They are intended to be immediately recognisable and to convey hazard information across language barriers.

The pictograms

  • Exploding bomb — explosives, self-reactives, organic peroxides
  • Flame — flammables, pyrophorics, self-heating substances, substances emitting flammable gases
  • Flame over circle — oxidisers
  • Gas cylinder — gases under pressure
  • Corrosion — corrosive to metals, skin corrosion, serious eye damage
  • Skull and crossbones — acute toxicity (fatal or toxic)
  • Exclamation mark — irritant, skin sensitiser, acute toxicity (harmful), narcotic effects
  • Health hazard (silhouette with starburst) — carcinogen, mutagen, reproductive toxicant, respiratory sensitiser, aspiration hazard
  • Environment (dead tree and fish) — hazardous to the aquatic environment

Transport pictograms

Transport pictograms follow the UN Recommendations on the Transport of Dangerous Goods (Model Regulations) and use a variety of colours and class numbers, such as orange for explosives, green for non-flammable gases, and half-white/half-black for corrosives. They differ in shape and colour conventions from the supply-and-use GHS set.
