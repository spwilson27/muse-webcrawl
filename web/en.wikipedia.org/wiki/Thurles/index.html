Thurles

Thurles (Irish: Durlas Éile) is a town in County Tipperary, Ireland, situated on the River Suir. It is best known as the birthplace of the Gaelic Athletic Association (GAA), which was founded at Hayes' Hotel in Liberty Square on 1 November 1884, and is home to Semple Stadium, one of Ireland's principal hurling venues.

History

The town's name derives from Durlas Éile, "strong fort of (the territory of) Éile". Thurles grew around a medieval borough and was associated with the Butler family. In the nineteenth century it developed as a market and railway town on the Dublin–Cork line.

Sport

On 1 November 1884 Michael Cusack, Maurice Davin and others founded the Gaelic Athletic Association in the billiard room of Hayes' Hotel to promote Irish games. Semple Stadium, named after Tom Semple, has a capacity of around 45,000 and traditionally hosts Munster hurling finals. The local club Thurles Sarsfields is among the most successful in Tipperary hurling.

Landmarks and education

Landmarks include the Cathedral of the Assumption (the seat of the Archdiocese of Cashel and Emly), St. Mary's Famine Church, and the ruins of Black Castle near the square. The town hosts a campus of the Technological University of the Shannon (formerly LIT Tipperary) and St. Patrick's College, a former seminary.
