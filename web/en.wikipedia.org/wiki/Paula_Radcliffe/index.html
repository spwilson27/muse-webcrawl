Paula Jane Radcliffe MBE (born 17 December 1973) is a British former long-distance runner. She is a three-time winner of the London Marathon and the New York City Marathon, a former world champion in the marathon, half marathon and cross country, and held the women's world marathon record of 2:15:25 for 16 years.

Early career

Radcliffe grew up in Bedfordshire and joined Bedford & County Athletic Club as a junior. She won the world junior cross country title in 1992 and competed at the 1996 and 2000 Olympic Games on the track, finishing fourth in the 10,000 metres at Sydney 2000.

Marathon career

Radcliffe made her marathon debut at London in 2002, winning in 2:18:56. In October 2002 she ran 2:17:18 at the Chicago Marathon, a world record, and in April 2003 she improved it to 2:15:25 at the London Marathon — a record that stood until Brigid Kosgei broke it in 2019. She won the world marathon title in Helsinki in 2005 and the New York City Marathon in 2004, 2007 and 2008.

Olympic disappointments

Despite her dominance elsewhere, Olympic success eluded her: illness and injury blighted her Athens 2004 marathon, where she dropped out late in the race, and she finished 23rd at Beijing 2008 while running with a stress fracture. Injury kept her out of the London 2012 Games.

Later life

Radcliffe ran her final competitive marathon at London in 2015. She has been a prominent campaigner against doping in athletics, released her blood-test data publicly in 2015 to answer questions about it, and works as a broadcaster and commentator, including for the BBC. She was appointed MBE in 2002 and won the BBC Sports Personality of the Year award the same year.
