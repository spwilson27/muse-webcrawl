Lionel Barrymore
Born: Lionel Herbert Blythe, 28 April 1878, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, U.S.
Died: 15 November 1954 (aged 76), Van Nuys, California, U.S.
Occupation: Actor, director, composer
Family: Barrymore family

Lionel Barrymore (born Lionel Herbert Blythe; April 28, 1878 – November 15, 1954) was an American actor of stage, screen and radio and a film director. He won an Academy Award for Best Actor for his performance in A Free Soul (1931) and is best known to modern audiences for his role as villainous Mr. Potter in Frank Capra's 1946 film It's a Wonderful Life.

Early life and career

Barrymore was the eldest child of actors Maurice Barrymore and Georgiana Drew, and the elder brother of Ethel and John Barrymore — the celebrated "Royal Family of Broadway". He began acting on stage as a child and joined D. W. Griffith's Biograph company in 1909, appearing in dozens of silent films before returning to the stage, then returning to Hollywood with the coming of sound.

Film career

Under contract to MGM, Barrymore became one of the most versatile character actors of the studio era, playing Dr. Gillespie in the long-running Dr. Kildare film series, and giving acclaimed performances in Grand Hotel (1932), Dinner at Eight (1933), You Can't Take It with You (1938), Duel in the Sun (1946) and Key Largo (1948). He also directed several films, including Madame X (1929).

Later life

Arthritis and a hip injury confined Barrymore to a wheelchair from the late 1930s, a disability written into his roles. He also composed orchestral music, worked extensively in radio, and played Ebenezer Scrooge in an annual radio broadcast of A Christmas Carol for two decades. He died in 1954 and has two stars on the Hollywood Walk of Fame.
