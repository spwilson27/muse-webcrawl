Cold wave

A cold wave (known in some regions as a cold snap, cold spell or deep freeze) is a weather phenomenon that involves a cooling of the air or the invasion of very cold air over a large area. It is distinguished from a mere cold snap by duration and severity: temperatures fall well below the climatological average for the region and season, often for several consecutive days, driven by a mass of cold, dense air moving in from high latitudes.

Causes

Cold waves typically result from the southward (in the Northern Hemisphere) displacement of polar or Arctic air, often associated with a weakened or distorted polar jet stream and blocking high-pressure patterns. Radiative cooling under clear skies and snow cover can intensify surface cold. Phenomena such as sudden stratospheric warming can displace the polar vortex and trigger severe cold outbreaks weeks later over mid-latitudes.

Effects

  • Health: hypothermia, frostbite, and excess winter mortality, particularly among the elderly; cold weather is statistically associated with more deaths than hot weather worldwide.
  • Agriculture: freeze damage to citrus, stone fruit, and other sensitive crops; a single severe freeze can destroy entire harvests.
  • Infrastructure: bursting water pipes, transport disruption, and surging energy demand that can overload power grids.
  • Water bodies: rapid ice formation on lakes and rivers, occasionally producing phenomena such as ice jams and flooding.

Notable cold waves

  • The Great Freeze of 1894–95, which devastated Florida's citrus industry.
  • The Year Without a Summer (1816), following the eruption of Mount Tambora — a climate anomaly rather than a synoptic cold wave, but often classed with extreme cold events.
  • The January 2019 North American polar vortex, bringing wind chills below −50 °C to parts of the US Midwest.
  • The February 2021 Texas cold wave, which caused catastrophic power-grid failure across the state.

Countermeasures

Forecasters issue cold weather advisories and warnings; mitigation includes insulating pipes, crop protection (smudge pots, irrigation), emergency shelters, and winterization of energy infrastructure.
