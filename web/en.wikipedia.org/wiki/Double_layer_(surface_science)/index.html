Double layer (surface science)

A double layer (DL, also called an electrical double layer, EDL) is a structure that appears on the surface of an object when it is exposed to a fluid. The object might be a solid particle, a gas bubble, a liquid droplet, or a porous body. The DL refers to two parallel layers of charge surrounding the object: the first layer, the surface charge (either positive or negative), consists of ions adsorbed onto the object, while the second layer is composed of ions attracted to the surface charge via the Coulomb force, electrically screening the first layer.

Models

The structure of the double layer has been described by successive models: the Helmholtz model of a rigid layer of counter-ions, the Gouy–Chapman model of a diffuse layer, and the Stern model, which combines a compact inner layer with a diffuse outer layer. The characteristic thickness of the diffuse layer is given by the Debye length. Double layers play a fundamental role in colloid stability, electrokinetics, and the operation of supercapacitors.
