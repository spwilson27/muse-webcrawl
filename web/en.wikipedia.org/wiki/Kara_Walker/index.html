Kara Elizabeth Walker (born 26 November 1969) is an American contemporary artist who explores race, gender, sexuality, violence and identity in her work. She is best known for her room-size tableaux of black cut-paper silhouettes, usually mounted directly on white gallery walls, which restage the imagery of slavery and the antebellum American South in a deliberately unsettling, nightmarish register.

Career

Walker was born in Stockton, California, and moved with her family to Georgia as a teenager. She earned her BFA from the Atlanta College of Art in 1991 and her MFA from the Rhode Island School of Design in 1994. That same year her panoramic mural Gone: An Historical Romance of a Civil War as It Occurred b'tween the Dusky Thighs of One Young Negress and Her Heart caused a sensation in New York. In 1997, at age 27, she became one of the youngest recipients of a MacArthur Foundation "genius" fellowship. She later served as Tepper Chair in Visual Arts at Rutgers University and has exhibited widely, including a major retrospective organised by the Walker Art Center.

Major works

Her monumental 2014 installation A Subtlety, or the Marvelous Sugar Baby, commissioned by Creative Time at the former Domino Sugar Refinery in Brooklyn, centred on a 35-foot-high sphinx-like figure of a woman, coated in white sugar, attended by molasses and resin child figures — a meditation on sugar, slavery and industrial labour that drew some 130,000 visitors. Her 2019 Tate Modern Hyundai Commission, Fons Americanus, installed a 13-metre working fountain in the Turbine Hall reimagining the Victoria Memorial as a bitter monument to the British Empire and the transatlantic slave trade.

Reception

Walker's use of stereotype and graphic imagery has been celebrated for exposing the fantasies underpinning American racism, and also criticised — notably by some older African American artists in the late 1990s — for reproducing degrading imagery for a predominantly white art market. She has consistently defended the work as an exploration of how history and desire are imagined rather than a literal depiction of the past.
