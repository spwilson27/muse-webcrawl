A psychologist is a professional who practices psychology — the scientific study of mind and behaviour — and/or applies psychological knowledge in clinical, educational, organisational or research settings. In most jurisdictions the title is legally protected: those using it must hold specified qualifications and a licence or registration. Psychologists who provide healthcare typically assess, diagnose and treat mental disorders and psychological distress using psychological methods, principally psychotherapy and psychological testing, rather than medication — the distinction from psychiatrists, who are physicians.

Areas of practice

Major specialties include clinical psychology (assessment and treatment of mental illness), counselling psychology (adjustment and life problems), neuropsychology (brain–behaviour relationships), health psychology, forensic psychology (legal settings), educational/school psychology, occupational or industrial–organisational psychology, and sport psychology. Many psychologists work primarily as researchers or university teachers in experimental fields such as cognitive, developmental and social psychology.

Training and licensing

Routes vary by country. In the United States and Canada, independent clinical practice generally requires a doctorate (PhD or PsyD) in psychology, supervised clinical hours and a licensing examination (the EPPP). In the United Kingdom, practitioner titles such as "clinical psychologist" require a professional doctorate and registration with the Health and Care Professions Council. In much of continental Europe a master's degree plus supervised practice qualifies psychologists for most roles. The scientist–practitioner (Boulder) model, endorsed at a 1949 conference, holds that clinicians should be trained as both researchers and practitioners.

Methods

Clinical psychologists administer and interpret standardised tests of intelligence, personality and neuropsychological functioning, conduct diagnostic interviews, and deliver evidence-based therapies including cognitive behavioural therapy and psychodynamic therapy. Research psychologists employ experiments, longitudinal studies, surveys and computational modelling. All are bound by professional codes of ethics covering confidentiality, informed consent and competence, administered by bodies such as the American Psychological Association (founded 1892) and the British Psychological Society (founded 1901).
