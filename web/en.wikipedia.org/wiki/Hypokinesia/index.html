Hypokinesia

Specialty: Neurology
Associated with: Parkinson's disease and other basal ganglia disorders

Hypokinesia is a decrease in the amplitude and speed of voluntary movement. It is one of the cardinal motor features of Parkinson's disease, in which it is often grouped within the broader term bradykinesia (slowness of movement); strictly, bradykinesia refers to slowness, akinesia to delayed or absent movement initiation, and hypokinesia to movements that become progressively smaller — for example, handwriting that shrinks across a sentence (micrographia) or finger taps that decrement in size and speed.

Causes

Hypokinesia arises chiefly from dysfunction of the basal ganglia, particularly loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta in Parkinson's disease. It also occurs in other parkinsonian syndromes (multiple system atrophy, progressive supranuclear palsy), after certain strokes, and as an adverse effect of dopamine-blocking drugs such as antipsychotics. In cardiology, "hypokinesia" has a separate meaning: reduced contraction of a region of the heart wall, assessed by echocardiography after myocardial infarction or in cardiomyopathy.

Assessment and treatment

Neurological examination tests repetitive movements — finger taps, hand opening and closing, foot taps — looking for decrement in amplitude and speed as well as hesitations and arrests. Treatment targets the underlying cause: dopaminergic therapy (levodopa) in Parkinson's disease, withdrawal of offending drugs, and physical and speech therapy to maintain mobility and function. Deep brain stimulation can improve motor features in selected patients with advanced Parkinson's disease.
