The Free State of Lippe (German: Freistaat Lippe) was a state of the German Reich from 1918 to 1947, created after the abdication of Prince Leopold IV of Lippe during the German Revolution of 1918–19. Its capital was Detmold.

History

Lippe had been a principality of the German Empire. Following the revolution, a workers' and soldiers' council took power, and the prince abdicated on November 12, 1918. The Free State adopted a democratic constitution in 1920 and was governed for most of the Weimar period by coalitions in which the Social Democrats were prominent.

In January 1933 the Nazi Party, seeking a success in the run-up to the Reichstag elections, concentrated its resources on the Lippe state election — a propaganda campaign remembered as the "Lippe breakthrough" — though the Nazis still won only a plurality of the vote. Under Nazi rule Lippe lost real self-government in the Gleichschaltung, but formally remained a state.

Dissolution

After the Second World War Lippe lay in the British occupation zone. On January 21, 1947, by British military ordinance, the Free State was merged into the new state of North Rhine-Westphalia, following negotiations in which Lippe obtained certain concessions for its territory, recorded in the "Lippe Points" (Lippische Punktationen). Its last head of government, Heinrich Drake, became president of the Detmold administrative region. The former state corresponds roughly to the modern district of Lippe.
