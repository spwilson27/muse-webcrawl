Sons of God

Sons of God
Hebrew: בְּנֵי־הָאֱלֹהִים (bene ha-elohim)
Key texts: Genesis 6:1–4; Job 1:6, 2:1, 38:7; Psalms 29, 82, 89

The phrase "sons of God" (Hebrew bene ha-elohim, "sons of the gods/God") appears in the Hebrew Bible and related literature, most famously in Genesis 6:1–4, where the sons of God take wives from the "daughters of men" and father the Nephilim.

Interpretations

Interpretations have varied across the centuries. In the Second Temple period, texts such as the Book of Enoch understood the sons of God as fallen angels (the Watchers) whose union with human women produced giants. In the Book of Job, the sons of God present themselves before the LORD as members of a heavenly court, among them the Adversary (ha-satan). Later Jewish and Christian interpreters often read the Genesis passage differently: the sons of God as descendants of Seth, as rulers or nobles, or as men of high status, avoiding a literal angel–human union.

Broader usage

In the New Testament, "sons of God" (Greek huioi theou) is applied to believers — those led by the Spirit (Romans 8) and the peacemakers (Matthew 5:9). In ancient Near Eastern context, similar language described members of a divine council surrounding a high god, a framework echoed in several Psalms.
