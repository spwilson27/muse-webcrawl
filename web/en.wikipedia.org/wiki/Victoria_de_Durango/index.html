Victoria de Durango, commonly referred to as Durango, is the capital and largest city of the Mexican state of Durango. The city, which is also the municipal seat of the Municipality of Durango, is located in the Valley of Guadiana in northeastern Durango, in the Sierra Madre Occidental region. It was founded on 8 July 1563 by the Basque explorer Francisco de Ibarra.

The historic centre of Durango

History

The city was officially founded as Villa de Durango, named after the town of Durango in the Spanish province of Biscay. Francisco de Ibarra, a Basque conquistador, led expeditions through the region in search of silver. The colonial city grew as a centre of mining and administration for the province of Nueva Vizcaya. During the Mexican War of Independence and the Mexican Revolution, the region saw significant fighting. Durango was the birthplace of Pancho Villa and a stronghold of revolutionary forces in the north.

Geography and climate

Durango sits at an elevation of about 1,890 metres (6,200 ft) above sea level. It has a semi-arid climate moderated by its altitude, with warm summers, mild winters and most rainfall falling between June and September.

Culture

The city's historic centre, with its cathedral and colonial buildings, reflects its mining-era prosperity. Durango is also known for its long association with film: the surrounding desert and mountain landscapes hosted many Westerns, earning it the nickname "La Tierra del Cine" (Land of Cinema), with productions including films starring John Wayne. The city is famous for its scorpions, celebrated in local crafts and folklore.
