Mu'awwidhatayn

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Overview

The Mu'awwidhatayn (Arabic: المعوذتين), sometimes written Muawwidhatayn, are the last two chapters (surahs) of the Qur'an — Al-Falaq (chapter 113, "The Daybreak") and An-Nās (chapter 114, "Mankind") — taken together. Their name derives from the Arabic root meaning "to seek refuge", as both begin with the formula "Say: I seek refuge with the Lord…". Together with Surah Al-Ikhlāṣ (112) they are frequently recited for protection.

Content

Al-Falaq (five verses) is a prayer for refuge with the Lord of the daybreak from the evils of creation, of darkness, of witches who "blow on knots", and of the envier. An-Nās (six verses) seeks refuge with the Lord, King and God of mankind from the "retreating whisperer" — Satan or evil impulses — who whispers into human breasts, "from among jinn and mankind".

Use in devotion

According to hadith, the Prophet Muhammad recited these chapters for protection and recommended them morning and evening, blowing over himself and wiping his hands over his body when ill; they are a standard part of the daily adhkār (remembrances), commonly recited three times after the dawn and sunset prayers along with Al-Ikhlāṣ. They are also recited over children and the sick, and in ruqyah (healing recitation). A well-known tradition holds that no comparable protective words had been revealed before these two surahs.
