Politics of Eritrea

The politics of Eritrea take place in the framework of a unitary one-party presidential republic. The People's Front for Democracy and Justice (PFDJ), successor to the Eritrean People's Liberation Front (EPLF) that won independence from Ethiopia in 1991, is the only legally permitted political party. President Isaias Afwerki has headed the government since independence was formally recognised in 1993. No national elections have ever been held, and the constitution ratified in 1997 has never been implemented.

History

Eritrea won its independence after a thirty-year war (1961–1991) against Ethiopia. In the 1993 UN-monitored referendum, over 99 percent voted for independence. A constituent assembly ratified a constitution in 1997 providing for multi-party elections, but its implementation was suspended after the Eritrean–Ethiopian War of 1998–2000. In September 2001 the government closed the independent press and imprisoned senior officials (the "G-15") who had called for democratic reform; many remain detained without trial.

Government

The President appoints ministers, judges and regional administrators; the National Assembly, largely composed of PFDJ central committee members and appointees, has rarely met. Local and regional elections have been held under tight party control. Since 2018, when Eritrea made peace with Ethiopia, the government has announced plans for political reform and a new constitution, but no timetable has been published.

Human rights and national service

International organisations describe Eritrea as one of the world's most closed states: there is no free press, religious practice is restricted to four recognised faiths, and national military service — officially 18 months — is frequently extended indefinitely, a practice a UN commission of inquiry said may amount to enslavement. Hundreds of thousands of Eritreans have fled the country. The government rejects these characterisations as politically motivated.

Foreign relations

Eritrea's foreign policy has been marked by border disputes with Ethiopia, Djibouti and Yemen, UN sanctions (imposed 2009, lifted 2018), and a strategic posture of self-reliance. Eritrean forces intervened in Ethiopia's Tigray War beginning in 2020.
