Peter Carl Fabergé

BornMay 30, 1846, Saint Petersburg, Russian Empire
DiedSeptember 24, 1920, Lausanne, Switzerland
OccupationJeweller, goldsmith

Peter Carl Fabergé (May 30, 1846 – September 24, 1920) was a Russian jeweller and goldsmith best known for the famous Fabergé eggs, jewelled Easter eggs made for the Russian imperial family. As head of the House of Fabergé in Saint Petersburg, he transformed his father's jewellery business into one of the largest and most prestigious firms in the world.

Career

Born Karl Gustavovich Fabergé in Saint Petersburg to a family of Huguenot descent, he trained as a goldsmith in Saint Petersburg, Frankfurt and Florence before taking over the family firm in 1870, and its sole leadership in 1882. In 1885 Tsar Alexander III commissioned the first Imperial Easter egg, the Hen Egg, as a gift for his wife Maria Feodorovna. The eggs became an annual tradition continued by Nicholas II, who commissioned two each year, one for his mother and one for his wife Alexandra. Of the 50 Imperial eggs made, most survive in museum and private collections.

Later life

The House of Fabergé employed hundreds of craftsmen and produced hardstone figures, jewellery, clocks and objets de fantaisie admired across Europe. The firm was nationalised after the Russian Revolution of 1917; Fabergé fled to Switzerland, where he died in Lausanne in 1920. The Fabergé name was later revived for luxury goods, and the eggs remain among the most celebrated works of the jeweller's art.
