The "From Hell" letter is a letter that was posted on 15 October 1888 and received the following day by George Lusk, chairman of the Whitechapel Vigilance Committee, during the Whitechapel murders attributed to Jack the Ripper. It is considered by many Ripperologists to be the most likely of the hundreds of contemporary letters to have genuinely come from the murderer, because it was accompanied by half of a human kidney.

Contents

The letter, written in a crude hand with irregular spelling, read in part: "From hell. Mr Lusk, Sor, I send you half the Kidne I took from one woman prasarved it for you tother piece I fried and ate it was very nise…" It was signed "Catch me when you can Mishter Lusk". The enclosed kidney fragment was examined by Dr Thomas Openshaw, who reported that it came from the left side of a human body and had been preserved in spirits of wine.

Significance

The kidney was significant because the victim Catherine Eddowes had had her left kidney removed by her killer during the "double event" of 30 September 1888, and Eddowes was known to have suffered from Bright's disease, which some accounts claimed the fragment also showed. The original letter and kidney have since been lost; only photographs and a facsimile survive, and the authenticity of the letter remains debated.
