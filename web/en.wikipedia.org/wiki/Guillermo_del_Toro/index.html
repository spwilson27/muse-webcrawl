Guillermo del Toro

Guillermo del Toro Gómez (born 9 October 1964) is a Mexican filmmaker, author and actor, widely regarded as one of the most imaginative directors in contemporary cinema. His work is characterised by dark fairy-tale imagery, sympathy for monsters, elaborate practical effects, and Catholic and insect motifs.

Born in Guadalajara, del Toro made his feature debut with Cronos (1993). His acclaimed Spanish-language films include The Devil's Backbone (2001) and Pan's Labyrinth (2006), the latter winning three Academy Awards. In Hollywood he directed Mimic, Blade II, the Hellboy films, Pacific Rim, Crimson Peak and Nightmare Alley.

The Shape of Water (2017) won the Academy Awards for Best Picture and Best Director, and his stop-motion Pinocchio (2022) won the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature. With Alfonso Cuarón and Alejandro G. Iñárritu he forms the "Three Amigos" of Mexican cinema.
