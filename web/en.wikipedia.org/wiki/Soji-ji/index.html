Sōji-ji

Sōji-ji (總持寺) is one of the two head temples (daihonzan) of the Sōtō school of Zen Buddhism in Japan, the other being Eihei-ji in Fukui Prefecture. Located in Tsurumi-ku, Yokohama, Kanagawa Prefecture, it serves as a major training monastery (senmon sōdō) where monks practise zazen and the monastic regimen established by the school's founders, Dōgen and Keizan.

History

The temple traces its origin to Morooka-dera, a temple founded in the Nara period, traditionally dated to 740 and attributed to the monk Gyōki, on the Noto Peninsula in present-day Ishikawa Prefecture. In 1321 the Zen master Keizan Jōkin, regarded as the second patriarch of Japanese Sōtō Zen, converted the temple to Zen and renamed it Shogaku-zan Sōji-ji. Under Keizan and his successor Gasan Jōseki, Sōji-ji became the centre from which Sōtō Zen spread through much of Japan.

The temple was destroyed by fire in 1898. Rather than rebuild in Noto, the school relocated the head temple to Tsurumi in Yokohama, where the new complex was completed in 1911; a branch temple, Sōji-ji Soin, remains at the original Noto site.

Layout and practice

The Yokohama precinct contains the standard halls of a Zen monastery, including the Dharma hall (hattō), Buddha hall (butsuden), monks' hall (sōdō), and the Denshōkaku and other training buildings. Training monks (unsui) follow a strict daily schedule of zazen meditation, work practice (samu), sutra chanting, and communal meals taken in the traditional manner with ōryōki bowls. The temple is also associated with Tsurumi University, founded by the Sōtō school near the temple grounds.
