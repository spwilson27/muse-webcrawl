Polynomial long division

In algebra, polynomial long division is an algorithm for dividing a polynomial by another polynomial of the same or lower degree, a generalised version of the familiar arithmetic technique called long division. It can be done easily by hand, because it separates an otherwise complex division problem into smaller ones.

Method

The dividend is rewritten with all terms, including zero coefficients for any missing powers. The leading term of the dividend is divided by the leading term of the divisor to give the first term of the quotient; this term is multiplied by the divisor and subtracted from the dividend. The process is repeated with the resulting polynomial until the degree of the remainder is less than the degree of the divisor.

Example

Dividing x³ − 12x² − 42 by x − 3 using long division yields the quotient x² − 9x − 27 with remainder −123, so x³ − 12x² − 42 = (x − 3)(x² − 9x − 27) − 123.

Applications

Polynomial long division is used to factor polynomials, find roots via the factor theorem, simplify rational functions, and perform partial fraction decomposition. Synthetic division is a shorthand variant for dividing by a linear factor of the form x − a.
