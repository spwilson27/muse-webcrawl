Heian Palace

Heian Palace (Daidairi)
Location: Heian-kyō (Kyoto), Japan
Built: 794
Function: Imperial residence and government centre

The Heian Palace (大内裏, Daidairi) was the original imperial palace of Heian-kyō (present-day Kyoto), the capital of Japan from 794 to 1227. It served as the imperial residence and administrative centre during most of the Heian period (794–1185).

Layout

Modelled on Chinese Tang-dynasty palace planning, the rectangular walled enclosure lay at the northern centre of the grid-planned capital. It contained the emperor's residence (Dairi), the Great Hall of State (Daigokuden) used for ceremonies, ministries, and court offices arrayed in courtyards.

History

Repeated fires destroyed many palace buildings, and over time emperors increasingly resided in temporary palaces (sato-dairi) outside the enclosure. The palace was effectively abandoned after a great fire in 1227, and its grounds were later absorbed into the growing city. A section of the modern Kyoto Imperial Palace area and markers in the city preserve its memory; the Daigokuden was partially reconstructed in reduced form at Heian Shrine in 1895.
