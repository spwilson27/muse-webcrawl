Crowd control is the management of large gatherings of people to maintain order and safety, practised by event organisers, venue operators, security stewards, and police. It encompasses both crowd management — the planned, preventive organisation of venues, flows, and communication — and reactive control measures used when disorder occurs.

Crowd dynamics

Danger in crowds is usually a matter of density rather than panic. At densities above about 4–5 people per square metre, shockwaves can propagate through a crowd and individuals may be unable to breathe — the cause of most fatalities in so-called "stampedes", which are more accurately crowd crushes. Disasters at Hillsborough (1989, 97 deaths), the Love Parade in Duisburg (2010, 21 deaths), Itaewon, Seoul (2022, 159 deaths), and the Astroworld festival (2021, 10 deaths) drove reforms in event planning and venue design.

Techniques

  • Design and planning: separate entry/exit flows, capacity limits, sightlines, real-time density monitoring.
  • Barriers and queuing systems: pens, lanes, and front-of-stage barriers with escape hatches.
  • Stewarding and communication: trained staff, PA announcements, and information screens.
  • Policing: ranging from dialogue and liaison policing to containment ("kettling"), mounted units, and, in riot situations, less-lethal weapons such as tear gas and water cannon — tactics that remain controversial.
