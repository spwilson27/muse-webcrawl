Hypercholesterolemia

Hypercholesterolemia, also called high cholesterol, is the presence of high levels of cholesterol in the blood. It is a form of hyperlipidemia and hyperlipoproteinemia (elevated levels of lipoproteins in the blood).

Causes

Elevated cholesterol is influenced by diet, obesity, and inherited genetic diseases such as familial hypercholesterolemia, caused by mutations in the LDL receptor gene, and by other conditions including diabetes mellitus, hypothyroidism, kidney disease and certain medications. Most cases are polygenic and lifestyle-related.

Signs and complications

Hypercholesterolemia itself usually causes no symptoms, but long-standing elevation of LDL cholesterol promotes atherosclerosis, increasing the risk of coronary heart disease, heart attack and stroke. In familial forms, cholesterol deposits may appear as xanthelasma around the eyes and tendon xanthomas.

Treatment

Management begins with lifestyle measures: a diet lower in saturated fat, weight loss, physical exercise and smoking cessation. Medications, chiefly statins, are used when risk remains high; other agents include ezetimibe, bile acid sequestrants and PCSK9 inhibitors. Screening of blood lipids is recommended in adults, and familial hypercholesterolemia often requires early and intensive treatment.
