Salamis (Greek: Σαλαμίνα, Salamina) is the largest island in the Saronic Gulf, about 1.6 km off the coast of Piraeus in Attica, Greece. It is famous in history as the site of the Battle of Salamis in 480 BC, in which the Greek fleet defeated the invading Persians, and as the legendary home of the heroes Ajax and Teucer.

History

According to myth, Salamis was ruled by Telamon, father of Ajax the Great and Teucer, heroes of the Trojan War. In the 7th–6th centuries BC the island was fought over by Athens and Megara; a law attributed to Solon forbade even proposing to abandon the Athenian claim. Salamis provided ships and sailors to the Athenian navy and served as the refuge of the Athenian population during the Persian invasion of 480 BC.

Battle of Salamis

In September 480 BC, after the fall of Thermopylae, the allied Greek fleet under the strategic direction of Themistocles lured the much larger Persian fleet into the narrow straits between Salamis and the mainland. The crowded Persian ships were unable to manoeuvre, and the Greeks won a decisive victory that forced Xerxes to withdraw much of his force from Greece.

Modern island

Today Salamis is a working-class suburb and holiday island of greater Athens, connected by frequent ferries to Perama and Piraeus and by a car ferry at Paloukia. Its main town is Salamina, and it hosts a Greek naval station. The island preserves the cave associated with Euripides, who is said to have written there, and Byzantine monasteries such as Faneromeni.
