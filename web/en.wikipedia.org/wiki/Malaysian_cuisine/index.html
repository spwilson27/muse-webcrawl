Malaysian cuisine is the cooking tradition of Malaysia, reflecting the country's multicultural population of Malays, Chinese, Indians, Peranakans, and numerous indigenous groups. Heavy use is made of spices, chillies, coconut milk, belacan (shrimp paste), lemongrass, and pandan.

Regional traditions

Malay cooking centres on rice served with curries, rendang, and sambals; nasi lemak — rice cooked in coconut milk with anchovies, peanuts, egg, and sambal — is widely regarded as the national dish. Malaysian Chinese cuisine adapted dishes such as Hokkien mee, char kway teow, and bak kut teh, while Malaysian Indian cuisine contributed banana-leaf meals, roti canai, and nasi kandar. Peranakan (Nyonya) cooking blends Chinese ingredients with Malay spices, producing laksa and ayam pongteh.

Hawker culture

Street food sold at hawker stalls and kopitiams (coffee shops) is central to Malaysian food culture; cities such as Penang's George Town are internationally celebrated for dishes including asam laksa and nasi kandar.

Ingredients and staples

Rice is the staple, supplemented by noodles, breads such as roti, and glutinous rice preparations. Islam's influence means pork is absent from Malay cooking, while Hindu practice excludes beef from many Indian dishes; most ingredients are prepared to halal standards in mainstream outlets.
