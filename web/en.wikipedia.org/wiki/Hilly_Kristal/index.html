Hillel "Hilly" Kristal (September 23, 1931 – August 28, 2007) was an American club owner, promoter, and musician, best known as the founder of the legendary New York City music club CBGB, which opened in 1973 in the Bowery neighbourhood of Manhattan and became the birthplace of American punk rock and new wave, launching the careers of the Ramones, Talking Heads, Patti Smith, Television, Blondie, and many others.

Early life and career

Kristal was born in New York City and raised on a farm in New Jersey. He studied violin as a child and later studied music and voice, performing as a singer; he appeared with the Radio City Music Hall chorus and briefly pursued a singing career. Before opening CBGB he managed the famous Village Vanguard jazz club and promoted concerts. In 1966 he opened a bar called Hilly's in the West Village, and later another Hilly's on the Bowery at 315 Bowery, in a building beneath a flophouse.

CBGB

In December 1973 Kristal renamed his Bowery bar CBGB & OMFUG — short for "Country, BlueGrass, and Blues and Other Music For Uplifting Gormandizers" — intending to feature roots music. Instead, the club became home to a new generation of underground rock bands. Kristal's rule that bands had to play original material helped turn CBGB into the crucible of punk and new wave: Television built the club's first stage for regular Sunday shows in 1974, followed by Patti Smith, the Ramones, Blondie, Talking Heads, the Dead Boys, and the Cramps.

Kristal was known for his gruff, shrewd management style and for taking a share of bands' merchandise and publishing interests through his company, a practice that sometimes caused disputes. Nevertheless, CBGB became a cultural landmark and Kristal its public face. He also ran the spin-off CBGB Record Canteen and a CBGB gallery and lounge nearby.

Later years and death

CBGB closed on October 31, 2006, after a protracted rent dispute with the Bowery Residents' Committee, with a final concert by Patti Smith. Kristal campaigned to save the club and planned to relocate it to Las Vegas. He died of complications from lung cancer on August 28, 2007, aged 75, before those plans could be realized.

Legacy

Kristal is remembered as one of the pivotal figures of punk rock, not as a musician but as the impresario who gave the scene a home. He was portrayed by Alan Rickman in the 2013 film CBGB. In 2017, the corner of Bowery and Second Avenue near the club was co-named in honour of the scene he fostered.
