North Bengal

North Bengal (Bengali: উত্তরবঙ্গ, Uttôrbônggô) is a term used for the north-western part of Bangladesh and northern part of the Indian state of West Bengal. It broadly comprises the Rajshahi and Rangpur divisions of Bangladesh and the Jalpaiguri, Malda and Cooch Behar divisions of West Bengal, lying north of the Ganges–Padma river system and south of the Himalayan foothills.

Geography

The region is drained by the Brahmaputra (Jamuna), Teesta, Mahananda and Atrai rivers. The Barind Tract, an elevated Pleistocene terrace, occupies much of the western part, while the eastern districts are low-lying floodplains prone to seasonal flooding. The Siliguri Corridor, sometimes called the "Chicken's Neck", connects the region to the rest of India.

History

Parts of North Bengal formed the ancient kingdom of Pundravardhana, with its capital at Mahasthangarh, one of the earliest urban centres in Bengal. The region later passed through the Gupta, Pala and Sena empires before coming under Muslim rule in the thirteenth century. After the Partition of India in 1947, the region was divided between India and East Pakistan (later Bangladesh); the princely state of Cooch Behar acceded to India in 1949.

Economy

Agriculture dominates, with rice, jute, tea, tobacco and mangoes as principal crops. The tea gardens of the Dooars and Terai at the foothills are a major industry, and Rajshahi is famed for its mangoes and silk.

Major cities

Important urban centres include Siliguri, Jalpaiguri, Cooch Behar, Malda and Darjeeling in India, and Rajshahi, Rangpur, Dinajpur and Bogra in Bangladesh.
