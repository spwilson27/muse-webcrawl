Conrad IV (25 April 1228 – 21 May 1254) was the only son of Emperor Frederick II by his second wife, Yolanda of Jerusalem. He inherited the crowns of Germany (as king of the Romans), Jerusalem and Sicily on his father's death in 1250, and was the last Hohenstaufen to attempt to rule the whole of the family inheritance.

Reign

Conrad had been elected King of Germany in 1237 in his father's lifetime and acted as regent there during Frederick's absences in Italy. On becoming king in 1250 he faced a papacy determined to destroy the Hohenstaufen: Innocent IV, having deposed Frederick at the Council of Lyon, refused to recognise Conrad and supported rival anti-kings, William of Holland among them. Conrad defeated his opponents at the Battle of Frankfurt in 1251 and marched into Italy in 1252 to assert his rights in Sicily, but he died of malaria (or possibly fever) at Lavello in 1254, leaving an infant son, Conradin. With his death the imperial power in Germany dissolved into the Great Interregnum.
