Real property

TypeLegal concept (property law)
IncludesLand and fixtures permanently attached to it
ContrastPersonal property

In English common law, real property, real estate, or immovable property is land (including the air above and ground below) and any buildings or structures permanently affixed to it, together with rights associated with the land.

Historical background

The term derives from feudal land tenure: land was "real" because disputes over it could be resolved by "real actions" that recovered the land itself, rather than merely damages. All land in common-law systems is ultimately held of the Crown or state, with individuals holding estates such as the fee simple or leasehold.

Estates and interests

Interests in real property include freehold estates (fee simple, life estate), leaseholds, easements, liens, and mineral rights. Ownership is recorded in land registration or deed-recording systems that provide public notice of title.

Transfer

Transfers occur by deed, inheritance, or court order, and typically require writing under the Statute of Frauds. Real property law varies widely by jurisdiction and underlies conveyancing, landlord–tenant law, and zoning.
