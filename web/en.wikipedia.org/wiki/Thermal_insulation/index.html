Thermal insulation

Thermal insulation
PurposeReduction of heat transfer between objects or regions
Measured byThermal conductivity (k-value); R-value (resistance)
Common materialsFibreglass, mineral wool, cellulose, polystyrene foam, polyurethane foam, aerogel
ApplicationsBuildings, piping, refrigeration, spacecraft, clothing

Thermal insulation is the reduction of heat transfer (thermal energy) between objects in thermal contact or within range of radiative influence. Materials used as thermal insulators — with low thermal conductivity — impede the flow of heat by conduction, convection and radiation, maintaining a temperature difference between separated regions. Thermal insulation is essential in building construction, industrial processes, refrigeration, aerospace engineering and protective clothing.

Principles

Heat flows by three mechanisms. Insulating materials oppose conduction by consisting largely of trapped gas pockets in a solid matrix — still air is a poor conductor. They suppress convection by keeping the gas pockets small, and reflective surfaces (radiant barriers) reduce radiative transfer. The performance of an insulator is expressed by its thermal conductivity k and, in building practice, by its R-value: the greater the R-value, the better the insulation.

Materials

Common insulators include fibreglass and mineral (rock) wool batts; loose-fill cellulose made from recycled paper; rigid foams of expanded or extruded polystyrene and polyisocyanurate; spray polyurethane foam; cork; and high-performance materials such as silica aerogel and vacuum insulated panels. Historically, asbestos was widely used until its health hazards led to bans. Choice of material balances insulating value, cost, fire behaviour, moisture resistance and environmental impact.

Applications

In buildings, insulation of walls, roofs and floors slashes heating and cooling energy demand and is mandated by building codes in most climates; whole-building standards such as Passivhaus depend on heavy insulation combined with airtightness. In industry, insulation conserves process heat and protects workers from hot surfaces; in cryogenics and spacecraft, multilayer insulation of reflective films protects against extreme temperatures.
