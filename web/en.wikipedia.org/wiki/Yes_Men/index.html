The Yes Men

Years active: 1999–present
Known for: Culture jamming, impersonation hoaxes
Key members: Jacques Servin (Andy Bichlbaum), Igor Vamos (Mike Bonanno)

The Yes Men are a culture jamming activist duo known for staging elaborate impersonations of corporate and government representatives in order to satirise and expose what they regard as unethical practices. Their method, which they call "identity correction", involves posing as powerful people and organisations in order to say publicly what those entities, in the group's view, ought to say.

Early actions

The group first attracted attention in 1999 with a parody website mimicking that of the World Trade Organization, which led to genuine invitations to speak at conferences on the WTO's behalf. Accepting these invitations, members delivered absurd presentations — including a proposal for a "management leisure suit" with a built-in "Employee Visualization Appendage" — that audiences often received without objection, a reaction the group used to highlight the conformity of business culture.

Notable hoaxes

In 2004, a Yes Men member appeared on BBC World television impersonating a Dow Chemical spokesman and announced that Dow accepted full responsibility for the Bhopal disaster and would liquidate a subsidiary to compensate victims; Dow's stock briefly lost billions in value before the hoax was exposed. In 2009, the group distributed a fake edition of the New York Times dated in the future, filled with desired headlines, and staged a false announcement that the United States Chamber of Commerce had reversed its position on climate change legislation.

Films

The group's activities are documented in three feature films: The Yes Men (2003), The Yes Men Fix the World (2009), which premiered at the Sundance Film Festival, and The Yes Men Are Revolting (2014), which focuses on climate change. The films blend documentary footage of the hoaxes with analysis of corporate power and media complicity.
