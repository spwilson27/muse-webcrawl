Deprivatization

Deprivatization, also called re-nationalization or remunicipalization (at the local level), is the process by which ownership or operation of an asset, service, or company is transferred from private hands back into public ownership or control. It is the reverse of privatization.

Motives

Governments undertake deprivatization for a variety of reasons: failure or bankruptcy of a private operator, poor service quality or underinvestment by private owners, excessive prices, the desire to treat a service as a public good, strategic or security concerns, or ideological commitments to public ownership. It may be carried out by negotiated purchase, by legislation, or by allowing a concession or franchise to expire and taking the service in-house.

Examples

Notable instances include the return of water services to municipal control in cities such as Paris, which remunicipalised its water supply in 2010, and Berlin; the temporary nationalisation of banks in several countries during the 2008 financial crisis; and the bringing of rail operations back into public hands in the United Kingdom, a process accelerated under the Passenger Railway Services (Public Ownership) Act 2024. In Latin America, governments in Bolivia, Venezuela, and Argentina renationalised utilities, hydrocarbons, and pension funds during the 2000s.

Debate

Supporters argue that public ownership aligns essential services with public interests and can capture profits for reinvestment; critics contend that political control can reintroduce inefficiency, underpricing, and fiscal risk, and that renationalisation may deter investment if investors fear expropriation without adequate compensation — a constraint imposed by many constitutions and investment treaties.
