Piquette

Piquette, the traditional farm workers' drink of French vineyards

Piquette is a low-alcohol, lightly sparkling French drink made by adding water to grape pomace — the skins, seeds and stems left over after pressing grapes for wine — and fermenting the mixture. Traditionally the drink of vineyard labourers, it typically reaches only 4–8% alcohol by volume.

History

Piquette-like drinks have ancient roots, resembling the Roman lora, the second wine given to slaves and soldiers. In France winegrowers traditionally reserved piquette for their workers and for domestic consumption, since the pomace retained sugars and flavour after pressing. Sale of piquette was for long periods prohibited in France, partly because it evaded wine taxes and appellation rules, and production declined through the twentieth century.

Modern revival

In the 2010s piquette experienced a revival among natural wine producers in France, the United States, Australia and elsewhere. Made with minimal intervention and often bottled as a pét-nat style refresher, it appeals to demand for low-alcohol, sustainable drinks and lets wineries extract value from a by-product. In the European Union, piquette may now be produced under defined conditions but cannot generally be marketed simply as "wine".
