Steel mill

A steel mill or steelworks is an industrial plant for the manufacture of steel. It may be an integrated steel works carrying out all steps of steelmaking from smelting iron ore to rolled product, but may also be a plant where steel semi-finished casting products are made from molten pig iron or from scrap.

Integrated mill

An integrated steel mill has all the functions for primary steel production:

  • iron making (conversion of ore to liquid iron),
  • primary steel making (conversion of pig iron to liquid steel),
  • casting (solidification of the liquid steel),
  • rough rolling (reducing the size of blooms), and
  • product rolling (finished shapes, bars, sheets, and so forth).

The major raw materials are iron ore, limestone and coke. Byproducts of the process include slag and blast furnace gas. Large integrated mills are traditionally located near sources of ore or coal, or near ports able to receive them in bulk.

Minimills

Since the mid-20th century, minimills have melted steel scrap, rather than iron ore, in electric arc furnaces and cast it directly into products via continuous casting. Minimills tend to produce long products such as bars and structural shapes, although many now produce flat products as well. They require much less capital and labour than integrated works and transformed the steel industry in the United States from the 1960s onward, led by companies such as Nucor.

Processes

Primary steelmaking uses either the basic oxygen furnace, in which oxygen is blown through molten pig iron to burn off excess carbon, or the electric arc furnace. Secondary steelmaking in ladles refines composition and temperature before continuous casting. Most steel today is continuously cast rather than poured into ingots.
