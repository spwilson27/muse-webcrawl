Articulation Index

The Articulation Index (AI) is a measure, ranging from 0 to 1, used to predict the intelligibility of speech transmitted through a communication system or heard in a given acoustic environment. An AI of 0 indicates no speech is intelligible, while 1 indicates perfect intelligibility.

Calculation

The method divides the speech spectrum into frequency bands (typically one-third-octave or octave bands) and computes, for each band, the proportion of the speech dynamic range that is audible above background noise and reverberation. Band values are weighted by their importance to intelligibility and summed. The concept grew out of work by Harvey Fletcher and colleagues at Bell Telephone Laboratories in the 1920s, later formalised by French and Steinberg (1947).

Related measures

The AI underlies later metrics including the Speech Intelligibility Index (SII, ANSI S3.5) and the Speech Transmission Index (STI), which is simpler to measure directly and widely used in room acoustics and public-address system design, including privacy assessment of offices and healthcare spaces.
