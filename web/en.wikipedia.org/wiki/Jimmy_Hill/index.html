Jimmy Hill

James William Thomas Hill (22 July 1928 – 19 December 2015) was an English footballer, manager, chairman, and broadcaster who became one of the most influential figures in modern English football.

Playing and union career

Hill played as an inside forward for Brentford and Fulham. As chairman of the Professional Footballers' Association he led the successful 1961 campaign to abolish the £20-per-week maximum wage for footballers.

Management and broadcasting

As manager of Coventry City (1961–1967), working with chairman Derrick Robins, he modernized the club — introducing the "Sky Blue Revolution," a new nickname, kit, and matchday experience — and led it from the Third Division to the First Division. He then became a pioneering television executive and presenter, helping to shape ITV's football coverage and presenting the BBC's Match of the Day from 1973 to 1988. He later returned to Coventry as managing director and chairman, and also worked in the United States and Saudi Arabia and directed football at Fulham.

Legacy

Hill is credited with innovations including all-seater stadiums advocacy and the three-points-for-a-win system, adopted in England in 1981. A statue of him stands outside Coventry's stadium, and the Jimmy Hill Way is named in his honour.
