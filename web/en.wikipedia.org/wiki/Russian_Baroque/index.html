Russian Baroque

Russian Baroque is the stage of Russian architecture and decorative art spanning roughly the late seventeenth to the mid-eighteenth century, in which Western European Baroque forms were adopted and adapted to Russian traditions. Art historians commonly distinguish several phases, including Moscow (or Naryshkin) Baroque, Petrine Baroque, and the later Elizabethan Baroque.

Phases

Naryshkin Baroque (c. 1680s–1700s) appeared in Moscow, typified by churches built by the Naryshkin family, such as the Church of the Intercession at Fili: red-brick, tiered towers lavishly trimmed with white stone carving that fused traditional Russian forms with Western ornament.

After Peter the Great founded Saint Petersburg in 1703, Petrine Baroque — practised by architects such as Domenico Trezzini — turned toward more restrained Dutch and northern European models for the new capital. Under Empress Elizabeth (1741–1762), the style reached its most exuberant phase in the work of Bartolomeo Rastrelli, whose palaces — the Winter Palace, the Catherine Palace at Tsarskoye Selo, and the Smolny Convent — combined Italianate grandeur with vivid colour and gilded decoration, before Catherine the Great's taste shifted Russia toward Neoclassicism.
