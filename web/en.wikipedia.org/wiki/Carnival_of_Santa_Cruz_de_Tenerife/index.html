Carnival of Santa Cruz de Tenerife

Carnival of Santa Cruz de Tenerife
LocationSanta Cruz de Tenerife, Canary Islands, Spain
DatesWeeks around Shrove Tuesday (February)
StatusFestival of International Tourist Interest (1980)
Key eventsQueen's Gala, parades, Burial of the Sardine

The Carnival of Santa Cruz de Tenerife (Spanish: Carnaval de Santa Cruz de Tenerife) is one of the largest carnivals in the world and Spain's most famous, held each February in the capital of Tenerife in the Canary Islands. It is often compared to the Rio Carnival, with which Santa Cruz is twinned.

Features

The carnival is renowned for the Gala for the Election of the Carnival Queen, in which candidates parade in spectacular costumes that can weigh hundreds of kilograms and are months in the making. Street parties draw hundreds of thousands of revellers, accompanied by musical groups unique to the festival: murgas (satirical singing troupes), comparsas (samba-style dance groups) and rondallas. The celebrations close with the Burial of the Sardine (Entierro de la Sardina), a mock funeral procession.

History

Carnival festivities in Tenerife date to the 18th century. Under the Franco dictatorship, carnival was officially banned, but in Santa Cruz it continued semi-clandestinely under the name "Winter Festivities" (Fiestas de Invierno) — the only Spanish carnival to survive the ban. It was declared a Festival of International Tourist Interest in 1980. In 1987 the carnival set a Guinness World Record when more than 200,000 people danced outdoors to a performance by Celia Cruz.
