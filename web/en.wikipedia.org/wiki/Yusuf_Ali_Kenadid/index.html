Yusuf Ali Kenadid (Somali: Yuusuf Cali Keenadiid; died 1911) was a Somali sultan and the founder of the Sultanate of Hobyo in the late 19th century. He is also credited with inventing the Osmanya script, a writing system for the Somali language.

Founding of Hobyo

Yusuf Ali was a member of the Majeerteen clan family that ruled the Sultanate of Majeerteen from Aluula. After a falling-out with his cousin, Sultan Osman Mahamuud, he carved out his own state further south along the Indian Ocean coast, establishing the Sultanate of Hobyo in the 1870s–1880s. From the port of Hobyo he controlled trade routes into the interior and levied customs on shipping. In 1888–1889 he signed a treaty accepting an Italian protectorate, using the alliance to strengthen his position against rivals including the sultanates of the north and, later, the Dervish movement.

The Osmanya script

Around 1920 — in the tradition attributed to Yusuf Ali and his son Osman Yusuf Kenadid, who developed and promoted it — the Kenadid family produced the Osmanya alphabet, a phonetic script designed specifically for Somali. It was adopted for official correspondence in the sultanates and saw use in print before being superseded by the Latin-based Somali orthography officially adopted in 1972. Osmanya is encoded in Unicode and remains a symbol of Somali cultural nationalism.

Later life

Relations with Italy deteriorated, and in 1911 the Italian colonial authorities exiled Yusuf Ali to Mogadishu, where he died that year. His son, Ali Yusuf Kenadid, succeeded him as sultan. The Hobyo sultanate was eventually absorbed into Italian Somaliland in the 1920s.
