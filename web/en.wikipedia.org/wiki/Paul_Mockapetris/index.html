Paul Mockapetris

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Paul Mockapetris
BornPaul V. Mockapetris, 1948
Known forInventing the Domain Name System (1983)
[Image: Portrait photograph of Paul Mockapetris in the 2000s]

Paul V. Mockapetris (born 1948) is an American computer scientist and Internet pioneer, known as the inventor of the Domain Name System (DNS), the distributed naming system that translates human-readable domain names into IP addresses.

In 1983, at USC's Information Sciences Institute, Mockapetris was asked by Jon Postel to reconcile competing proposals for replacing the ARPANET's single HOSTS.TXT file — a centrally maintained table that every site copied nightly and that could not scale with the growing network. His design, specified in RFCs 882 and 883 (1983) and refined in RFCs 1034 and 1035 (1987), was hierarchical and distributed: delegable zones, authoritative name servers, caching resolvers. Mockapetris also wrote the first implementation, JEEVES.

DNS became one of the Internet's load-bearing designs — arguably the largest distributed database in the world — and one of its most durable: the protocol of 1987, extended but never replaced, now answers trillions of queries a day. Mockapetris later worked in industry (including as chief scientist at Nominum) and received the IEEE Internet Award and induction into the Internet Hall of Fame.
