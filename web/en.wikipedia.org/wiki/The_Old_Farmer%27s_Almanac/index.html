The Old Farmer's Almanac

The Old Farmer's Almanac is an annual reference book containing weather forecasts, planting charts, astronomical data, recipes, and articles on farming, gardening, and folklore. Founded in 1792 by Robert Bailey Thomas, it is the oldest continuously published periodical in North America, traditionally published by Yankee Publishing of Dublin, New Hampshire.

Weather forecasts

The Almanac is famous for its long-range weather predictions, prepared a year or more in advance. Thomas devised a forecasting method combining sunspot cycles, climatology, and meteorology; the exact formula remains a closely guarded secret, kept, per legend, in a black box in the Almanac's offices. The book claims a high accuracy rate, though independent analyses have found its forecasts only modestly better than chance.

History and features

Robert B. Thomas edited the almanac until his death in 1846. The distinctive format includes a hole drilled in the upper-left corner so the book can be hung on a nail or string, a feature retained to this day. A famous legend recounts a proofreading error in an early edition — "rain, hail and snow" in July — which Thomas refused to correct and which a July snowstorm supposedly vindicated. Content includes tide tables, sunrise and sunset times, full-moon names, gardening calendars, and trivia.
