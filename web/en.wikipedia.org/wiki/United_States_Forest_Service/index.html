The United States Forest Service (USFS) is an agency within the U.S. Department of Agriculture that administers the nation's 154 national forests and 20 national grasslands in the United States. The agency manages about 193 million acres (780,000 km²) of land, an area about the size of Texas. Its work covers timber and watershed management, recreation, grazing, wildlife habitat, research, and the suppression and prevention of wildland fire.

History

The origins of the agency lie in the conservation movement of the late nineteenth century. The Forest Reserve Act of 1891 authorized the President to set aside public land as forest reserves; the Forest Management Act of 1897 gave the reserves a management framework oriented to watershed protection and a sustainable timber supply. In 1905, under President Theodore Roosevelt, administration of the reserves was transferred from the Interior Department to the Department of Agriculture's new Forest Service, headed by Gifford Pinchot, the first professionally trained American forester. Pinchot defined the agency's ethos of utilitarian conservation: the "greatest good of the greatest number in the long run".

Catastrophic fires in 1910 — the "Big Burn" in Idaho and Montana — entrenched aggressive fire suppression as agency policy for much of the twentieth century, a stance later reconsidered as ecologists recognized fire's ecological role. The Multiple-Use Sustained-Yield Act of 1960 formally required management for recreation, range, timber, watershed and wildlife on an equal footing, and the National Forest Management Act of 1976 imposed detailed land-management planning. In recent decades, escalating wildfire seasons in the western United States have made fire management the agency's largest program area.

Organization and activities

The Forest Service is led by a Chief who reports to the Undersecretary for Natural Resources and Environment. It is organized into nine geographic regions, each headed by a regional forester, with individual forests supervised by forest supervisors and districts run by district rangers. Beyond land management, the agency operates:

  • Research and development — a network of research stations and the Forest Products Laboratory in Madison, Wisconsin.
  • State and private forestry — technical and financial assistance to state agencies, tribal governments and private forest landowners.
  • International programs — forestry cooperation and disaster assistance abroad.
  • Law enforcement and fire — the agency fields thousands of firefighters, engines, smokejumpers and airtankers, and created the enduring fire-prevention symbol Smokey Bear (from 1944, with the Ad Council and the wartime Forest Service campaigns).
