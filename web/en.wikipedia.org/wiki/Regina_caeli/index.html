Regina caeli

Regina caeli (Latin for "Queen of Heaven") is one of four Marian antiphons sung or recited within the Liturgy of the Hours and the Roman Catholic liturgy. It is traditionally said or sung during Eastertide, from Compline of Holy Saturday until Pentecost, replacing the Angelus at other times of year.

Text

The antiphon reads: "Regina caeli, laetare, alleluia. Quia quem meruisti portare, alleluia. Resurrexit, sicut dixit, alleluia. Ora pro nobis Deum, alleluia" — "Queen of Heaven, rejoice, alleluia. For He whom you did merit to bear, alleluia. Has risen, as He said, alleluia. Pray for us to God, alleluia."

The chant's origins are uncertain, though legend associates it with Pope Gregory the Great in the sixth century. It has been set to music by many composers, including Mozart, Brahms and numerous polyphonists of the Renaissance.
