Maurice-Joseph-Louis Gigost d'Elbée (21 March 1752 – 6 January 1794) was a French royalist general, at the outset second in command and then commander-in-chief of the Catholic and Royal Army formed during the War in the Vendée against the French Revolution.

War in the Vendée

A retired cavalry officer living at Beaupréau, d'Elbée was called upon by local peasants in March 1793 to lead their revolt against revolutionary conscription. Under Cathelineau's overall command he fought at Thouars, Fontenay, and Saumur. When Cathelineau died of wounds in July 1793, d'Elbée was elected commander-in-chief.

Defeat and death

Badly wounded at the second battle of Luçon and again at Cholet in October 1793, he was carried with the army during the Virée de Galerne and evacuated to the island of Noirmoutier. When republican forces took the island in January 1794, d'Elbée, unable to stand from his wounds, was executed by firing squad seated in an armchair, alongside his wife, who was shot the following day.

Legacy

He is remembered for an act of clemency after the battle of Chemillé in April 1793, when he persuaded his men to spare republican prisoners by reciting the Lord's Prayer and pointing to its words "forgive us our trespasses" — an episode known as the Pater de d'Elbée.
