Dangerous Drugs Act 1952

The Dangerous Drugs Act 1952 (Malay: Akta Dadah Berbahaya 1952), is a Malaysian law which was enacted to make further and better provision for the regulation of the importation, exportation, manufacture, sale, and use of opium and of certain other dangerous drugs and substances, to make special provision relating to the jurisdiction of courts in respect of offences thereunder and their trial, and for purposes connected therewith. It is among the most prominent pieces of criminal legislation in Malaysia, noted for its very severe penalties.

History

The Act originated as the Dangerous Drugs Ordinance 1952 of the Federation of Malaya (Ordinance No. 30 of 1952), in force from 1 November 1952, and was revised in 1980 as Act 234. It replaced colonial opium-control legislation and consolidated control over opium, cannabis, cocaine, morphine and later synthetic drugs.

Trafficking and the death penalty

Section 39B provides for trafficking in dangerous drugs above prescribed threshold quantities (for example, 50 grams of heroin or morphine, 200 grams of cannabis, or 50 grams of methamphetamine). Trafficking originally carried a mandatory death penalty. Following reforms, including the Abolition of Mandatory Death Penalty Act 2023, courts may instead impose long prison terms of 30–40 years with whipping for section 39B offences. Section 39A(2) also imposes heavy mandatory minimum sentences for possession above certain weights, and section 39C penalizes repeat drug use.

Presumptions

The Act is controversial for its statutory presumptions: persons found in possession of drugs above certain quantities are presumed to be trafficking, and persons found with drugs in their custody are presumed to know their nature, reversing the usual burden of proof. Malaysia's Federal Court has debated the constitutionality and construction of these presumptions in cases such as Alma Nudo Atenza v PP (2019).

Enforcement

The Act is enforced by the Royal Malaysia Police's Narcotics Criminal Investigation Department and the Royal Malaysian Customs Department, and is supplemented by the Drug Dependants (Treatment and Rehabilitation) Act 1983 for addicts sent to rehabilitation centres.
