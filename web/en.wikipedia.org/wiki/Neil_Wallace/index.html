Neil Wallace (born 1942) is an American economist and monetary theorist, notable for his work on overlapping generations models, the legal restrictions theory of the demand for money, and policy irrelevance results in macroeconomics. He received his PhD from the University of Chicago in 1970.

Contributions

Wallace helped develop the overlapping generations framework as a setting for studying fiat money, building on Paul Samuelson's model. With Thomas Sargent he wrote influential work on rational expectations, including "Rational expectations and the theory of economic policy" and the "unpleasant monetarist arithmetic" (Sargent–Wallace, 1981). His 1980 "The overlapping generations model of fiat money" and the 1983 paper "A legal restrictions theory of the demand for 'money' and the role of monetary policy" are canonical references. The "Wallace irrelevance" result concerns conditions under which government open-market operations (a Modigliani–Miller-type theorem for government finance) do not affect allocations.

Career

Wallace taught for many years at the University of Minnesota and was associated with the Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis; he later held chairs at Pennsylvania State University and the University of Miami. He is a fellow of the Econometric Society and a member of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences.
