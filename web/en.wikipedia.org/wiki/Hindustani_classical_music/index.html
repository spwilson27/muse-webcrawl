Hindustani classical music

Hindustani classical music is the classical music tradition of the northern regions of the Indian subcontinent, also called North Indian classical music. It diverged from Carnatic music, the southern tradition, around the 12th–13th centuries, absorbing Persian and Central Asian influences through the Delhi Sultanate and Mughal courts.

Hindustani classical music
Region: North India, Pakistan, Nepal, Bangladesh
Core concepts: Raga, tala, khyal, dhrupad
Instruments: Sitar, tabla, sarod, bansuri, harmonium, tanpura

Principles

Music is built on raga — melodic frameworks for improvisation associated with moods and times of day — and tala, rhythmic cycles such as teental (16 beats) played on tabla or pakhawaj. A drone from tanpura or harmonium anchors the tonic. Performances move from slow unmetered alap through composed sections to fast improvisation.

Forms

Dhrupad, the oldest vocal form, is austere and spiritual, associated with the Dagar family. Khyal, developed in the 18th century and linked to Amir Khusrau's legacy and Sadarang, is now dominant, valuing improvisation. Lighter forms include thumri, dadra, ghazal, bhajan and tappa. Instrumental gat compositions mirror vocal structures.

Gharanas and transmission

Knowledge passed through guru–shishya discipleship within gharanas (stylistic schools) such as Gwalior, Agra, Jaipur-Atrauli, Kirana, Patiala and Rampur-Sahaswan. Noted artists include Allauddin Khan, Bade Ghulam Ali Khan, Amir Khan, Bhimsen Joshi, Kishori Amonkar, Ravi Shankar, Ali Akbar Khan, Vilayat Khan, Zakir Hussain and Hariprasad Chaurasia.

History

Roots lie in Vedic chant and the Natya Shastra and Brihaddeshi. Court patronage under Akbar — whose musician Tansen is legendary — and later princely states and All India Radio shaped the modern concert tradition.
