Walmer Castle is an artillery fort originally constructed by Henry VIII in Walmer, Kent, between 1539 and 1540. It formed part of the King's Device programme to protect the south coast of England against invasion from France and the Holy Roman Empire, and defended the strategically important Downs anchorage off the English Channel. Together with Deal Castle and Sandown Castle it formed a chain of coastal defences.

The castle has a symmetrical design with a low central keep surrounded by four rounded bastions. From the 18th century it became the official residence of the Lord Warden of the Cinque Ports; famous residents included the Duke of Wellington, who died at the castle in 1852, and Queen Elizabeth the Queen Mother, a regular visitor during her tenure as Lord Warden (1978–2002).

History

Built at a cost of nearly £27,000 as part of the 1539 Device programme, Walmer was garrisoned during the English Civil War and saw action during the Second Anglo-Dutch War. After 1708 it increasingly served as a residence rather than a fortification. William Pitt the Younger, as Lord Warden, entertained at Walmer and developed its grounds. The Duke of Wellington's rooms are preserved, including the armchair in which he died and a pair of his boots.

Grounds

The castle's gardens were developed extensively in the 1990s, when a new garden was created to mark the 95th birthday of the Queen Mother, Lord Warden at the time. The grounds include a woodland walk, a kitchen garden and the Glen, a converted chalk quarry.
