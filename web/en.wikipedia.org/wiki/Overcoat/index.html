Overcoat

An overcoat is a type of long coat intended to be worn as the outermost garment, usually extending below the knee. Overcoats are typically made from heavy materials such as wool or wool blends, and are worn in cold weather over indoor clothing or a suit.

History

The overcoat developed in the 18th and 19th centuries from earlier greatcoats worn by soldiers and travellers. By the Victorian era, distinct styles had emerged, including the Chesterfield, the ulster, the paletot and the raglan coat. Military greatcoats influenced civilian fashion through both World Wars.

Styles

Common styles include the single-breasted Chesterfield with its velvet collar, the double-breasted polo coat in camel hair, the trench coat with its belt and storm flaps (technically a raincoat), and the duffle coat with toggle fastenings. Overcoats are generally more formal than shorter jackets and are cut to fit over layers.
