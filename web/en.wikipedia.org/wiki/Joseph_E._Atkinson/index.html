Joseph E. Atkinson

Joseph E. Atkinson (December 23, 1865 - May 8, 1948) was a Canadian newspaper editor and publisher who transformed the Toronto Star into one of Canada's largest and most influential newspapers. His commitment to social justice and the "Atkinson Principles" continue to guide the paper.

Early life

Atkinson was born in Newcastle, Canada West, into a poor family; his father died when he was young and his mother ran a small shop. He left school early, worked in a general store, and taught himself bookkeeping and shorthand before finding work at a newspaper in Port Hope, then moving to the Toronto World and later the Globe, where he became a parliamentary reporter in Ottawa.

The Toronto Star

In 1899 Atkinson was recruited by a group of Liberal backers to take over the struggling Toronto Evening Star. As managing editor and later publisher and controlling shareholder, he built the Star into Canada's largest-circulation daily. He campaigned for old-age pensions, unemployment insurance, public health care and other social programs, crusaded against monopolies, and supported Canada's war effort in both world wars while remaining a fierce critic of government waste and corruption.

The Atkinson Principles and Foundation

Atkinson's will directed that his controlling interest in the Star be held by a charitable foundation, the Atkinson Foundation, rather than sold to commercial interests. The principles he articulated - a strong, united and independent Canada; social justice; individual and civil liberties; community and civic engagement; the rights of working people; and public ownership of natural monopolies - remain the stated editorial foundations of the Toronto Star.

Death and legacy

Atkinson died in Toronto in 1948. The Atkinson Foundation, funded by his estate, remains a major Canadian philanthropic organization supporting social and economic justice.
