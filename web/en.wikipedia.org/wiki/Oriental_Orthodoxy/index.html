Oriental Orthodoxy

Oriental Orthodox Churches
Classification: Non-Chalcedonian Christianity
Members: ~60 million
Churches: Coptic, Syriac, Armenian, Ethiopian, Eritrean, Malankara

Oriental Orthodoxy is the communion of Eastern Christian churches adhering to miaphysite Christology and rejecting the Council of Chalcedon (451). They are distinct from the Eastern Orthodox Church, which accepts Chalcedon.

Churches

The communion comprises the Coptic Orthodox Church, Syriac Orthodox Church, Armenian Apostolic Church, Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, Eritrean Orthodox Tewahedo Church, and Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church, each autocephalous and led by a patriarch or catholicos.

Theology and history

Following Cyril of Alexandria's formula of "one incarnate nature of God the Word", Oriental Orthodox theologians rejected the Chalcedonian Definition as implying two natures in a divisive sense. Schism hardened through imperial politics and differing terminology. Modern agreed statements with Eastern Orthodox and Roman Catholic churches recognise substantial Christological agreement, though full communion has not been restored.

Worship

Liturgies include the Alexandrine, West Syriac, Armenian, and Ethiopian rites, noted for ancient chant, fasting disciplines, and, in Ethiopia, observance of Saturday as well as Sunday.
