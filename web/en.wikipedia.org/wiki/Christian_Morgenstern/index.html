Christian Otto Josef Wolfgang Morgenstern (6 May 1871 – 31 March 1914) was a German author and poet, best known for his nonsense verse — whimsical, punning poems full of invented words and playful logic — collected in works such as the Galgenlieder ("Gallows Songs"). He is one of the best-loved comic poets in the German language.

Life

Morgenstern was born in Munich into a family of painters. Tuberculosis, inherited in his family, shadowed his life; from 1906 he spent much of his time in sanatoriums in the Alps. He worked as a journalist and editor in Berlin, translated Henrik Ibsen and other Scandinavian authors, and married Margareta Gosebruch von Liechtenstern in 1910. He died of tuberculosis in Merano in 1914.

Work

His famous creations include Herr Palmström and Herr von Korf, whose adventures satirise pedantry, bureaucracy and bourgeois logic. Poems such as "The Midnight Mouse" and "The Aesthetic Weasel" (in Max Knight's celebrated translations) show his mixture of grotesque humour and formal virtuosity. His later work turned increasingly serious and philosophical, influenced by theosophy and the anthroposophy of Rudolf Steiner, though published mostly posthumously.
