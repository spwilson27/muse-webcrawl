Pio of Pietrelcina (born Francesco Forgione; 25 May 1887 – 23 September 1968), widely known as Padre Pio, was an Italian Capuchin friar, priest, and mystic. He became famous for bearing the stigmata — wounds corresponding to those of the crucified Jesus — for most of his life, and for reported supernatural gifts including bilocation, prophecy, and the reading of souls in confession. He is venerated as a saint by the Roman Catholic Church.

Early life

Forgione was born to poor farming parents in Pietrelcina in the province of Benevento. Deeply pious from childhood, he entered the Capuchin novitiate at Morcone in 1903, taking the name Pio, and was ordained a priest in 1910. Plagued throughout his life by ill health, he was drafted into the Italian army during the First World War but discharged on medical grounds. In 1916 he was assigned to the friary of Our Lady of Grace at San Giovanni Rotondo in the Gargano mountains, where he remained for the rest of his life.

Stigmata and ministry

On 20 September 1918, while praying before a crucifix, Padre Pio received visible wounds in his hands, feet, and side, which he bore for the remaining fifty years of his life, wearing fingerless gloves to cover the wounds on his hands. The phenomenon drew enormous crowds and intense scrutiny. Church authorities, uncertain and at times suspicious, subjected him to medical examinations and for a period in the 1920s and early 1930s restricted his public ministry. Millions of pilgrims nevertheless came to San Giovanni Rotondo to attend his Masses and make their confessions to him; he spent many hours a day in the confessional.

Works and death

Padre Pio founded prayer groups around the world and, using donations from the faithful, built the Casa Sollievo della Sofferenza ("Home for the Relief of Suffering"), a large modern hospital inaugurated in 1956. He died on 23 September 1968, and some 100,000 people attended his funeral. He was beatified in 1999 and canonised by Pope John Paul II on 16 June 2002; his feast day is 23 September. His body, exhumed in 2008, is displayed in a glass casket at the Sanctuary of Saint Pio of Pietrelcina, which draws millions of pilgrims a year.

Assessment

Historians and physicians have debated the origin of his wounds; the Church's canonisation process weighed the medical evidence and testimony of his spiritual character. Whatever the historical debates, Padre Pio remains one of the most popular Catholic saints of the modern era, and devotion to him is global.
