Castle Sween is one of the oldest stone castles in Scotland, standing on the eastern shore of Loch Sween in Knapdale, Argyll. It dates to the late 12th century and is attributed to Suibhne (Sven) mac Eadulf, ancestor of the MacSweens, from whom both castle and loch take their names.

History

The original castle was a roughly square enclosure with thick curtain walls and round corner towers, later augmented by a rectangular keep. The MacSweens lost Knapdale in the 13th century; the castle passed through the hands of the Menteiths, the Stewart and MacDonald lords, and the Campbells, who held it from the later Middle Ages. It was burned in 1647 during the Wars of the Three Kingdoms by Alasdair Mac Colla and thereafter abandoned.

Today

The ruin, in the care of Historic Environment Scotland, stands beside a caravan park near Achnamara. Its early date and simple plan make it an important example of the first stone castles built by Gaelic-Norse lords in the west of Scotland.
