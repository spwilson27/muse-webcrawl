Dapper Dan (designer)

Daniel Day (born 8 August 1944), known professionally as Dapper Dan, is an American fashion designer and haberdasher from Harlem, New York. In the 1980s his boutique pioneered luxury streetwear by remaking high-fashion monogram prints into custom garments for hip-hop artists, athletes and hustlers, long before the fashion houses themselves embraced the style.

Career

Day opened Dapper Dan's Boutique on East 125th Street in Harlem in 1982. Unable to buy the luxury goods his customers wanted, he taught himself textile printing and manufactured garments — jackets, tracksuits, car interiors — covered in the logos of Gucci, Louis Vuitton and Fendi. His clientele included Eric B. & Rakim, LL Cool J, Salt-N-Pepa, Mike Tyson and the boxer-jacket look immortalised on album covers. The boutique operated around the clock as a cultural salon of the hip-hop golden age.

Litigation and closure

Luxury brands pursued Day for trademark infringement; a 1992 raid connected to a suit involving Fendi (whose legal team included a young Sonia Sotomayor) closed the boutique. Day continued designing underground for private clients.

Gucci collaboration and recognition

In 2017 Gucci's cruise collection included a jacket closely modelled on a Dapper Dan design for Olympian Diane Dixon; the ensuing outcry over appropriation led Gucci to apologise and partner with Day. In 2018 he opened a Harlem atelier with Gucci's backing — the first luxury-brand partnership of its kind — and launched Dapper Dan x Gucci collections, followed by collaborations with Gap (2022). His memoir, Dapper Dan: Made in Harlem (2019), recounts his life from numbers-running to fashion legitimacy.
