Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company Limited (TSMC) is a Taiwanese multinational semiconductor contract manufacturing and design company headquartered in the Hsinchu Science Park. Founded in 1987 by Morris Chang with support from the Taiwanese government, it pioneered the "pure-play foundry" model: manufacturing chips designed by other companies rather than selling its own products. TSMC is the world's largest contract chipmaker and manufactures the overwhelming majority of the world's most advanced logic chips, making it one of the most strategically important companies on earth — sometimes called Taiwan's "silicon shield".

Role in the industry

TSMC's customers include Apple (its largest), Nvidia, AMD, Qualcomm and Broadcom; its leading-edge nodes (5 nm, 3 nm and 2 nm in the mid-2020s) are used for flagship smartphone processors and AI accelerators. Rivals Samsung Foundry and Intel Foundry trail in advanced-node share. Concern about concentration in Taiwan — exposed to both earthquakes and cross-strait tensions with China — has driven TSMC to build new fabs in Arizona (USA), Kumamoto (Japan) and Dresden (Germany), with the Arizona investment announced as reaching $165 billion.

History

Morris Chang, a veteran of Texas Instruments, was recruited by the Taiwanese government to build a semiconductor industry and founded TSMC in 1987 as a joint venture with Philips and Taiwanese investors. The foundry model enabled the rise of "fabless" chip companies such as Nvidia and Qualcomm. Chang retired in 2018; under successors Mark Liu and C. C. Wei, TSMC's market value passed $1 trillion in the mid-2020s amid the AI boom.
