Life in the United Kingdom test

The Life in the United Kingdom test, often called the Life in the UK test, is a computer-based examination required of most people seeking indefinite leave to remain in the United Kingdom or naturalisation as a British citizen. It was introduced in November 2005 for naturalisation applicants and extended to settlement applicants in 2007.

Content

The test consists of 24 multiple-choice questions drawn from the official handbook Life in the United Kingdom: A Guide for New Residents, covering British history, traditions, customs, laws, and the political system. Candidates have 45 minutes to complete the test and must answer at least 18 questions correctly (75%) to pass.

Administration

Tests are taken at approved test centres across the UK, and candidates must bring specified identification and proof of address. The test fee has been set at £50 for many years. Exemptions apply to applicants under 18 or 65 and over, and to some people with long-term medical conditions.

Criticism

The test has been criticised for containing obscure historical trivia, for errors and outdated material in the handbook, and for including questions that many British-born citizens would struggle to answer. Reform proposals have periodically been debated in Parliament.
