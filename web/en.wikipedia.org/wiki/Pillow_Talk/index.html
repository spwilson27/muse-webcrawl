Pillow Talk

Pillow Talk is a 1959 American romantic comedy film in CinemaScope directed by Michael Gordon and starring Doris Day and Rock Hudson. The film, which also features Tony Randall and Thelma Ritter, was a major box-office success and established the template for the "sex comedy" genre of the early 1960s.

Plot

Interior decorator Jan Morrow and songwriter Brad Allen, who have never met, are forced to share a telephone party line. Jan resents Brad's monopolising the line to romance a succession of women, while Brad, upon meeting her, is smitten and courts her under an assumed identity as a naive Texan named Rex Stetson. When Jan discovers the deception, comic complications ensue before the couple reconcile.

Reception and legacy

The screenplay by Russell Rouse and Clarence Greene won the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay, and Doris Day received her only Oscar nomination for the role. Pillow Talk was the first of three hit pairings of Day and Hudson, followed by Lover Come Back (1961) and Send Me No Flowers (1964). In 2009 it was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry as culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant.
