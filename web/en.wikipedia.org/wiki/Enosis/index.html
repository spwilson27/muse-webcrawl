Enosis

Enosis (Greek: Ένωσις, "union") is the movement of various Greek communities living outside Greece for incorporation of the regions that they inhabit into the Greek state. The term is most closely associated with the union of Cyprus with Greece, a goal pursued by the island's Greek Cypriot majority for over a century and at the heart of the Cyprus problem. More broadly, enosis formed part of the Megali Idea, the nineteenth- and early twentieth-century Greek national project of uniting all Greek-populated lands — including Crete, the Dodecanese, Northern Epirus and parts of Anatolia — within a single Greek state.

Cyprus

Greek Cypriot demands for enosis grew after Britain took over administration of Cyprus from the Ottoman Empire in 1878 and annexed it in 1914. A church-organised plebiscite in 1950 recorded overwhelming Greek Cypriot support for union with Greece. When Britain refused, the underground organisation EOKA, led by Georgios Grivas, launched an armed campaign (1955–1959) aimed at enosis. The campaign ended instead with the Zürich and London Agreements of 1959, which created an independent Republic of Cyprus in 1960 under a power-sharing constitution that expressly banned both enosis and partition (taksim).

Intercommunal violence in 1963–64 drove the Turkish Cypriots from government, and in 1974 the Greek military junta sponsored a coup in Cyprus explicitly intended to achieve enosis. Turkey responded with a military intervention, seizing the northern third of the island — a division that persists despite the failure of the pro-enosis coup. With Greece's accession to the European Community and later Cyprus's own accession to the European Union in 2004, enosis faded as a practical political demand; most Greek Cypriots came to favour an independent, federal Cypriot state within the EU.

Other cases

Enosis was achieved in several historical cases: the Ionian Islands were ceded by Britain to Greece in 1864, Crete united with Greece in 1913 after decades of revolt and international administration, and the Dodecanese were transferred from Italy in 1947. The Greek Cypriot and Greek national aspiration in Northern Epirus (southern Albania) was never realised.
