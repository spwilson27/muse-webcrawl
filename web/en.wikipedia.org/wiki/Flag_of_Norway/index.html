The flag of Norway is red with an indigo blue Nordic cross fimbriated in white that extends to the edges of the flag; the vertical part of the cross is shifted to the hoist side. It was designed by Fredrik Meltzer and adopted by parliament in 1821, during Norway's union with Sweden.

History

Norway historically used the Danish flag, and after 1814 a succession of union flags. Meltzer's design combined the red and white of Denmark with blue, said to symbolise liberty by association with the flags of France, the Netherlands, the United Kingdom and the United States. During the union with Sweden, Norwegian flags bore a union mark in the canton; the "pure" flag was restored for merchant use in 1898, following years of political campaigning in the so-called flag struggle.

State and war flag

The state flag and war flag are swallow-tailed versions of the national flag. The Norwegian flag law and flag regulations specify official flag days and flying times, which vary with latitude and season.
