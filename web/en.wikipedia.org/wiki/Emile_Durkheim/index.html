Émile Durkheim

Born: 15 April 1858, Épinal, France

Died: 15 November 1917, Paris

David Émile Durkheim was a French sociologist who established sociology as an academic discipline. His work on social facts, solidarity, religion and suicide remains foundational to social theory.

Major works

The Division of Labour in Society (1893) contrasted mechanical solidarity of traditional societies with organic solidarity of modern interdependence. The Rules of Sociological Method (1895) defined social facts as external and constraining. Suicide (1897) showed even private acts vary with social integration and regulation, distinguishing egoistic, altruistic, anomic and fatalistic suicide. The Elementary Forms of Religious Life (1912) argued religion is society worshipping itself, grounded in the sacred/profane distinction and collective effervescence.

Legacy

Durkheim founded the journal L'Année sociologique, shaped French education and socialisation theory, and mentored his nephew Marcel Mauss. Functionalists such as Talcott Parsons built directly on him.
