Mongols in China

Mongols in China are one of the 56 ethnic groups officially recognised by the People's Republic of China. Numbering about six million, most live in the Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region, with significant populations in Liaoning, Jilin, Heilongjiang, Xinjiang, Qinghai, and other provinces. In fact, more ethnic Mongols live in China than in the independent state of Mongolia.

History

Mongol tribes of the steppe were united by Genghis Khan in 1206, and his successors conquered all of China, founding the Yuan dynasty (1271–1368) under Kublai Khan. After the Yuan fell to the Ming, the Northern Yuan continued on the steppe, and Mongol groups were gradually brought under Qing rule in the 17th century. The Qing administered Mongolia through banners and leagues, a system that persisted into the 20th century. Following the fall of the Qing, Outer Mongolia became independent (eventually the Mongolian People's Republic), while Inner Mongolia remained within China and became an autonomous region in 1947.

Culture and language

Mongols in China speak various Mongolic languages and dialects, and in Inner Mongolia the Mongolian language is written in the classical vertical script rather than the Cyrillic used in Mongolia. Traditional culture centres on pastoral nomadism, though most Chinese Mongols are now settled farmers or urban residents. Tibetan Buddhism (the Gelug school) is the predominant traditional religion, alongside shamanic survivals.
