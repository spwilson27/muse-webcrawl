Nyctaginaceae, the four o'clock family, is a family of flowering plants in the order Caryophyllales. It contains some 290 species in about 33 genera, distributed mainly in tropical and subtropical regions of the Americas, with representatives in other warm regions of the world.

Characteristics

Members of the family are trees, shrubs, vines and herbs. Their flowers typically lack true petals; what appears to be a colourful perianth is a petaloid calyx, and in genera such as Bougainvillea the showy parts are brightly coloured bracts surrounding small flowers. The fruit is distinctive: a single seed surrounded by the persistent, hardened base of the calyx tube, forming a structure called an anthocarp.

Notable genera

Well-known members include Bougainvillea, widely grown as an ornamental climber; Mirabilis, including the four-o'clock flower Mirabilis jalapa; Boerhavia; and Pisonia, a genus of tropical trees. Several species are used in traditional medicine, and some are weeds of agriculture.
