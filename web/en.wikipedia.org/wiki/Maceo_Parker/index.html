Maceo Parker (born February 14, 1943) is an American funk and soul jazz saxophonist, best known for his work with James Brown in the 1960s and 1970s, and with George Clinton's Parliament-Funkadelic and Prince in later decades. Brown's famous stage command — "Maceo, I want you to blow!" — made his name synonymous with the funk saxophone solo.

Career

Parker grew up in a musical family in Kinston, North Carolina, playing in a family band with his brothers. In 1964, Brown hired drummer Melvin Parker and took Maceo along as well; Maceo's alto became a defining voice of the J.B.'s sound on records such as "Papa's Got a Brand New Bag", "I Got You (I Feel Good)" and "Cold Sweat".

After disputes with Brown, Parker joined Parliament-Funkadelic in the mid-1970s, alongside fellow Brown alumni Fred Wesley and Bootsy Collins. From the 1990s he built a successful solo career — his slogan "98% funk, 2% jazz" — releasing albums such as Roots Revisited and Mo' Roots, and he toured extensively with Prince in the 2000s.
