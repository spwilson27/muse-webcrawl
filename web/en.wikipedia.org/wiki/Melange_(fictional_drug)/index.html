Melange (often called simply "the spice") is a fictional drug in Frank Herbert's Dune universe, central to the 1965 novel Dune and its sequels. Produced only on the desert planet Arrakis, melange extends human lifespan, heightens mental abilities and, in large doses, grants prescience. Because the Spacing Guild's Navigators depend on it to fold space safely, melange is the most valuable substance in the Imperium — "he who controls the spice controls the universe".

Origin

Melange is created in the life cycle of the giant sandworms of Arrakis. Sandtrout, the larval phase of the worm, encyst water deep beneath the desert; fungal excretions from these cysts are forced to the surface in a "pre-spice mass", a violent eruption that kills the sandtrout. Exposed to sun and air, the material becomes melange, which is harvested mechanically by spice harvesters — at constant risk from sandworms, which are drawn to the vibrations of harvesting machinery.

Effects and uses

Regular consumption prolongs life and health, and turns the consumer's eyes entirely blue — the "Eyes of Ibad" — a mark of the desert Fremen. Melange is strongly addictive: withdrawal is universally fatal. The Bene Gesserit use spice-based poisons in the Water of Life ritual to awaken ancestral memory in Reverend Mothers. In extreme doses, as with Paul Atreides, it can unlock full prescient vision, allowing the user to perceive past and possible futures.

Role in the plot

Control of Arrakis and its spice is the prize over which Houses Atreides and Harkonnen contend in Dune. Paul Atreides' monopoly of the spice gives him leverage over the Spacing Guild and the Emperor himself. In later novels, the God Emperor Leto II restricts the supply to enforce his Golden Path; millennia later, the Tleilaxu learn to synthesise melange in axlotl tanks, breaking the Arrakis monopoly.
