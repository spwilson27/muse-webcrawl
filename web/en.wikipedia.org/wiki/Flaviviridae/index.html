Flaviviridae

Flaviviridae is a family of enveloped, positive-sense single-stranded RNA viruses. The family includes four genera — Flavivirus, Pestivirus, Hepacivirus, and Pegivirus — and contains many pathogens of major medical and veterinary importance. The name derives from yellow fever virus (Latin flavus, "yellow"), the type species of the genus Flavivirus.

RealmRiboviria
OrderAmarillovirales
GeneraFlavivirus, Hepacivirus, Pegivirus, Pestivirus

Characteristics

Flaviviridae virions are spherical, enveloped particles roughly 40–60 nm in diameter. Their genomes are linear, positive-sense RNA molecules of about 9–13 kilobases encoding a single polyprotein that is cleaved into structural and non-structural proteins. Members of the genus Flavivirus are mostly transmitted by arthropods — mosquitoes or ticks — while hepaciviruses and pegiviruses are blood-borne and pestiviruses infect livestock.

Important members

  • Yellow fever virus, dengue virus, Zika virus, West Nile virus, Japanese encephalitis virus, and tick-borne encephalitis virus (genus Flavivirus)
  • Hepatitis C virus (genus Hepacivirus), a major cause of chronic liver disease
  • Bovine viral diarrhea virus and classical swine fever virus (genus Pestivirus)
  • Human pegivirus (formerly GB virus C; genus Pegivirus)
