Prometheus Unbound is a four-act lyrical drama by the English Romantic poet Percy Bysshe Shelley, first published in 1820. It reimagines the Greek myth of Prometheus, the Titan who stole fire for humanity and was chained to a rock by Zeus (Jupiter) in punishment. Shelley rewrote the lost conclusion of Aeschylus's trilogy: instead of reconciling with Jupiter, his Prometheus triumphs through the power of love and the withdrawal of hatred.

Summary

The drama opens with Prometheus, after three thousand years of torment, recalling and retracting the curse he once laid on Jupiter. This spiritual change sets in motion the tyrant's fall: Demogorgon, a shapeless power in the underworld, ascends to dethrone Jupiter. Prometheus is freed by Hercules and reunited with his beloved Asia, whose journey with her sister Panthea forms the visionary second act. The fourth act, added after the rest was complete, is a cosmic celebration by the Earth, Moon and spirits of humanity's liberation into a world ruled by love and equality.

Themes

Written in the aftermath of the Napoleonic wars and the Peterloo Massacre, the poem embodies Shelley's revolutionary hope that tyranny can be overcome by non-violent moral transformation rather than vengeance. It is considered his masterpiece and one of the greatest long poems of the Romantic period, though it was long deemed unstageable and is read as a closet drama. Shelley's wife Mary Shelley had published Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus in 1818, and critics often read the two works as companion meditations on the Promethean myth.
