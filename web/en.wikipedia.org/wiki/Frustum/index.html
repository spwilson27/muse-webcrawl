Frustum

In geometry, a frustum (plural: frusta or frustums) is the portion of a solid — normally a cone or pyramid — that lies between two parallel planes cutting it. Cutting a pyramid with a plane parallel to its base yields a pyramidal frustum; cutting a cone likewise yields a conical frustum, also called a truncated cone.

Volume

The volume of a pyramidal frustum with height h and lower and upper base areas A₁ and A₂ is V = (h/3)(A₁ + A₂ + √(A₁A₂)). For a conical frustum with radii R and r, the volume is V = (πh/3)(R² + Rr + r²). A formula for the volume of a truncated pyramid appears in the ancient Egyptian Moscow Mathematical Papyrus (c. 1850 BC), considered a masterpiece of ancient geometry.

Applications

The term is widely used in computer graphics, where the viewing frustum is the region of space visible to a camera or eye; geometry outside it can be culled from rendering (frustum culling). Frustum-shaped forms also occur in architecture, optics, and engineering.
