Mohawk people

The Mohawk people (Mohawk: Kanienʼkehá꞉ka, "People of the Place of Flint") are an Indigenous people of North America, originally from the Mohawk Valley in present-day upstate New York. They are the easternmost nation of the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois Confederacy), and as such are styled the "Keepers of the Eastern Door." The Mohawk language is an Iroquoian language; today Mohawk communities are located in New York State, Ontario and Quebec, including Akwesasne (St. Regis), Kahnawake, Kanesatake, Tyendinaga and the Six Nations of the Grand River.

History

The Mohawk were among the five founding nations of the Iroquois Confederacy, united under the Great Law of Peace. Their location made them the first of the Haudenosaunee to trade regularly with Europeans — first the Dutch at Fort Orange (Albany), then the English — obtaining firearms that reshaped the balance of power in the northeast during the Beaver Wars of the 17th century. The Mohawk also figured prominently in the Covenant Chain alliance with Britain.

During the American Revolutionary War the Confederacy split; Mohawk leader Joseph Brant (Thayendanegea) led forces allied with the Crown. After the war, many Mohawk resettled in British Canada, receiving the Haldimand Proclamation grant along the Grand River (1784) and the Bay of Quinte lands that became Tyendinaga. Others remained in New York or at missions in Quebec, Kahnawake and Kanesatake having grown from 17th-century mission settlements near Montreal.

Culture and society

Traditional Mohawk society is matrilineal, organised into three clans — Wolf, Bear and Turtle — with clan mothers holding authority in the selection of chiefs (roia:ne). Settlement life centred on longhouses, and agriculture on the "Three Sisters" of corn, beans and squash. In the 20th century Mohawk ironworkers from Kahnawake and Akwesasne became famous for high-steel construction on skyscrapers and bridges in New York City and across North America, a tradition dating to the 1880s and commemorated after the 1907 Quebec Bridge collapse, which killed 33 Kahnawake men.

The Mohawk have also been prominent in assertions of Indigenous sovereignty, including the 1990 Oka Crisis at Kanesatake and the defence of Akwesasne's border-crossing rights under the Jay Treaty. Language revitalisation programmes, including immersion schools at Kahnawake and Akwesasne, have produced a new generation of Mohawk speakers.
