Moraine

A moraine is any accumulation of unconsolidated debris — soil and rock, sometimes called glacial till — that occurs in regions covered by glaciers, either currently glaciated or formerly glaciated. The debris may have been plucked from the valley floor as the glacier advanced, or fallen onto the glacier from the slopes above. Moraines may be composed of silt like rock flour, sand, gravel, and boulders, and they form some of the most characteristic landforms of glaciated terrain.

Lateral moraines form ridges along the sides of a glacier; when two glaciers merge, their inner lateral moraines combine into a medial moraine.

Formation

As a glacier moves, it erodes the land beneath and beside it and transports the debris. Material is deposited when the ice melts or when the glacier's forward movement is balanced by melting at its edge, allowing debris to pile up at a stationary margin. Because glaciers act like conveyor belts, enormous quantities of sediment can accumulate even without the ice margin itself moving.

Types

Lateral moraine

A ridge of debris deposited along the side of a glacier, derived mainly from rockfall and avalanches from the valley walls.

Medial moraine

A ridge running down the middle of a glacier, formed when two glaciers merge and the lateral moraines at their junction combine.

Ground moraine

A gently rolling blanket of till deposited beneath a glacier or left behind as it melts, often forming fertile but stony plains.

Terminal (end) moraine

A ridge built at the furthest point reached by a glacier, marking its maximum advance. Recessional moraines are similar ridges formed during temporary halts in a glacier's retreat. The terminal moraines of the Laurentide Ice Sheet form prominent hills across Long Island, Cape Cod, and the northern United States.

Significance

Moraines record the former extent and behaviour of glaciers, making them vital evidence for reconstructing past climates. Dams of moraine debris can impound glacial lakes, whose sudden drainage may cause outburst floods such as the Missoula floods.
