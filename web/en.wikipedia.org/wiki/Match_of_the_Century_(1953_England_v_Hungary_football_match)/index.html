The Match of the Century is the name given to the international friendly football match played between England and Hungary at Wembley Stadium on 25 November 1953, which Hungary won 6–3. It was the first time England had lost at home to a team from outside the British Isles, and the result is regarded as a watershed in English football, exposing the tactical and technical conservatism of the English game.

Background

Hungary, coached by Gusztáv Sebes, were the reigning Olympic champions and were in the middle of an unbeaten run that would stretch to 32 matches; their side, the "Mighty Magyars", included Ferenc Puskás, Sándor Kocsis, Nándor Hidegkuti, Zoltán Czibor and József Bozsik. England, though in decline, still considered themselves the game's masters and had never been beaten at Wembley by foreign opposition.

The match

Hungary scored within the first minute through Hidegkuti, playing in a revolutionary deep-lying centre-forward role that dragged England's centre-half Harry Johnston out of position and disorganised the English defence. Hidegkuti completed a hat-trick, Puskás scored twice — including a famous goal in which he dragged the ball back with the sole of his boot, sending captain Billy Wright sliding past "like a fire engine going to the wrong fire", in the celebrated contemporary phrase — and Kocsis added another. England replied through Jackie Sewell, Stan Mortensen and an Alf Ramsey penalty, but were comprehensively outplayed; the 6–3 scoreline flattered them.

England's lineup included goalkeeper Gil Merrick, Alf Ramsey, Billy Wright, Harry Johnston, Stan Mortensen, Stanley Matthews and Tom Finney's contemporaries, among them George Robb and Ernie Taylor.

Aftermath

Six months later, in May 1954, Hungary beat England 7–1 in Budapest — still England's heaviest defeat. The Wembley defeat prompted deep soul-searching in English football about coaching, tactics and player development; its lessons are often credited with beginning the modernisation that eventually produced England's 1966 World Cup win. Hungary, favourites for the 1954 World Cup, lost the final to West Germany in the "Miracle of Bern".
