Advanced driver-assistance system

Image placeholder: sensors of a typical ADAS suite

An advanced driver-assistance system (ADAS) is an electronic system in a vehicle that uses sensors — cameras, radar, lidar and ultrasonic transducers — together with software to assist the driver in driving and parking functions. ADAS features are intended to reduce human error, a factor in the great majority of road accidents, and they form the technological bridge between conventional cars and fully self-driving vehicles.

Common features

Widespread ADAS functions include adaptive cruise control, which maintains a set gap to the vehicle ahead; lane departure warning and lane-keeping assist; automatic emergency braking and forward collision warning; blind spot monitoring; parking assistance and surround-view cameras; traffic sign recognition; driver attention monitoring; and adaptive headlights. Safety bodies such as Euro NCAP increasingly reward or require several of these systems, and the European Union has mandated a core set of them on new cars since 2022–2024.

Automation levels

Under the SAE J3016 taxonomy, most ADAS functions correspond to Level 1 (a single automated function such as steering or acceleration) or Level 2 (combined steering and speed control, with the driver remaining fully responsible and required to supervise). Marketing terms such as "self-driving" for Level 2 systems have been criticised as contributing to driver over-reliance.

Limitations

ADAS performance degrades in poor weather, with obscured or non-standard road markings, and in unusual traffic scenarios. Studies have also raised concerns about driver complacency, high repair costs for sensor-equipped bumpers and windscreens, and inconsistent naming of features across manufacturers.
