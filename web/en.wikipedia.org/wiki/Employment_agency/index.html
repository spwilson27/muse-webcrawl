Employment agency

Employment agency

Type: Intermediary / staffing firm

Services: Recruitment, temporary staffing, executive search

Clients: Employers and jobseekers

Major firms: Adecco, Randstad, ManpowerGroup

An employment agency is an organisation that matches employers to employees. Agencies range from small local businesses to some of the world's largest employers: global staffing groups such as Adecco, Randstad, and ManpowerGroup supply millions of temporary workers. In most countries employment agencies are regulated — in the United Kingdom by the Employment Agencies Act 1973, for example — and charging fees to jobseekers is often restricted or prohibited.

Types

Private agencies

Private agencies earn fees, usually paid by the employer, for filling permanent positions (often a percentage of the first year's salary), for executive search ("headhunting") at senior levels, and for supplying temporary staff. In the typical temporary-staffing ("temping") model, the worker is employed and paid by the agency but works under the direction of the client firm, which pays the agency an hourly rate.

Public employment services

Governments run public employment services — such as jobcentres in the UK and the American Job Centers in the United States — that help unemployed people find work free of charge, administer unemployment benefits, and provide training and advice.

History

Fee-charging registries for domestic servants existed from the 17th century, and modern agencies date from the late 19th and early 20th centuries; one of the first in the United States was opened by Fred Winslow in 1893. The temporary-help industry expanded rapidly after the Second World War, with firms such as Kelly Services (1946) and Manpower (1948).

Criticism

Agencies have been criticised for precarious working conditions, low pay, and — in some countries — exploitation of migrant workers through illegal fees; international labour standards under ILO Convention No. 181 (1997) seek to regulate private employment agencies while recognising their role.
