Fort Mose Historic State Park

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fort Mose Historic State Park is a Florida State Park in St. Johns County, near St. Augustine, commemorating Fort Mose (pronounced "Moh-say"), formally Gracia Real de Santa Teresa de Mose. Established in 1738 by the Spanish governor of Florida, it was the first legally sanctioned free African settlement in what is now the United States.

History

Spanish Florida offered freedom to enslaved people who escaped from British colonies and converted to Catholicism. In 1738, Governor Manuel de Montiano established Fort Mose for freedmen and their families, about two miles north of St. Augustine. Its militia, led by Francisco Menéndez, an African-born former slave, helped defend St. Augustine, notably during the British attack of 1740. The community was abandoned after Florida passed to Britain in 1763, its residents relocating to Cuba.

The park

Today the site is a National Historic Landmark with a visitor center and museum; no original structures survive above ground, but archaeological remains document the settlement. Annual reenactments commemorate the Flight to Freedom of the freedom seekers who travelled south to Spanish Florida.
