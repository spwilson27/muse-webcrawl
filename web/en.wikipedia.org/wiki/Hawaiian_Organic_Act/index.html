Hawaiian Organic Act

The Hawaiian Organic Act, formally An Act To provide a government for the Territory of Hawaii (31 Stat. 141), was enacted by the United States Congress and signed by President William McKinley on April 30, 1900. It established the government of the Territory of Hawaii following annexation in 1898.

Provisions

The act created a territorial government with a governor appointed by the President of the United States, a bicameral legislature consisting of a Senate and House of Representatives elected by the people, and a delegate to the U.S. House of Representatives. It extended the U.S. Constitution and federal laws to Hawaii, established a judiciary, and defined citizenship, voting qualifications, and land laws.

Subsequent history

The Organic Act governed Hawaii until statehood in 1959. It was amended several times, notably to adjust suffrage and labor provisions, and its land provisions—including the Hawaiian Homes Commission Act of 1920—shape Hawaiian land law to this day.
