Advanced Micro Devices, Inc. (AMD) is an American multinational semiconductor company headquartered in Santa Clara, California. It is the principal rival to Intel in x86 processors and to Nvidia in graphics and AI accelerators.

History

AMD was founded in 1969 by Jerry Sanders and other Fairchild Semiconductor alumni. It became a second-source maker of Intel's x86 chips, then a direct competitor; its Athlon (1999) first reached 1 GHz, and the Opteron (2003) introduced the 64-bit x86-64 extension that Intel later adopted. Buying ATI in 2006 brought Radeon graphics in-house. After near-collapse in the mid-2010s, AMD spun off its factories as GlobalFoundries and, under CEO Lisa Su (from 2014), staged a celebrated comeback with the Zen architecture: Ryzen for desktops, EPYC for servers, and chips for both major game consoles.

Recent years

AMD acquired FPGA maker Xilinx for about $49 billion in 2022 and Pensando for systems software. Its Instinct MI300 accelerators target the AI data-centre market dominated by Nvidia, while EPYC has taken substantial server CPU share from Intel. Like Nvidia, AMD is fabless, with most advanced chips manufactured by TSMC using tools from ASML.
