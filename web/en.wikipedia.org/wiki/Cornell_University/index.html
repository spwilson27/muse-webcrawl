Cornell University is a private Ivy League land-grant research university based in Ithaca, New York. The university was founded in 1865 by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White. Since its founding, Cornell has been a co-educational and non-sectarian institution where admission has not been restricted by religion or race.

Cornell's main campus in Ithaca spans 745 acres on East Hill overlooking Cayuga Lake. The university also operates Weill Cornell Medicine in New York City and a campus in Education City, Qatar. Four of its colleges are state-supported statutory colleges operated in partnership with the State University of New York.

History

Ezra Cornell, a telegraph magnate, offered land and an endowment, and secured New York's land-grant allocation under the Morrill Act. Andrew Dickson White, the university's first president, shaped its broad curriculum combining liberal arts with practical and scientific studies, and its ethos of openness — captured in Ezra Cornell's founding statement: "I would found an institution where any person can find instruction in any study."

Academics and research

Cornell comprises seven undergraduate colleges and seven graduate divisions, with strong programmes in agriculture, engineering, hotel administration, architecture, veterinary medicine and the sciences. Its faculty and alumni include dozens of Nobel laureates; notable figures associated with the university include Carl Sagan, Richard Feynman, Hans Bethe and Toni Morrison. Cornell Lab of Ornithology, the Arecibo Observatory (managed by Cornell until 2011) and a leading high-energy synchrotron are among its major research assets.
