Lansdowne Road

Lansdowne Road (Irish: Bóthar Lansdún) was a sports stadium in Dublin, Ireland, owned by the Irish Rugby Football Union. Located in Ballsbridge beside the River Dodder and the DART station of the same name, it was the home of Irish rugby union and, for many years, of the Republic of Ireland football team. Founded in 1872, it was the oldest rugby union ground in the world still in use for internationals when it closed.

History

The ground was established by Henry Dunlop, who organised Ireland's first athletic championships there. The first rugby international was played in 1878, when Ireland hosted England. The stadium was rebuilt several times, acquiring stands on all sides; its final capacity was about 48,000, including standing terraces. It hosted soccer internationals from 1971 and famous concerts, including U2.

Redevelopment

Lansdowne Road closed at the end of 2006 and was demolished in 2007. The new Aviva Stadium, with a capacity of about 51,700, opened on the site in 2010 and hosts rugby and soccer internationals.
