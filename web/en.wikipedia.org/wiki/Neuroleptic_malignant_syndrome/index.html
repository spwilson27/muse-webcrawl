Neuroleptic malignant syndrome

SpecialtyEmergency medicine, psychiatry
SymptomsHigh fever, muscle rigidity, altered mental status, autonomic instability
CausesDopamine antagonist medications
TreatmentStop the drug, cooling, dantrolene, bromocriptine

Neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS) is a life-threatening reaction that can occur in response to antipsychotic (neuroleptic) or antiemetic medications. Symptoms include high fever, confusion, rigid muscles, variable blood pressure, sweating, and a fast heart rate. Complications may include rhabdomyolysis, high blood potassium, kidney failure, and seizures.

Causes

Any medication that blocks dopamine receptors can potentially trigger NMS, most often typical antipsychotics such as haloperidol and fluphenazine, but also atypical agents and antiemetics such as metoclopramide and promethazine. Abrupt withdrawal of dopaminergic drugs such as levodopa in Parkinson's disease can produce a similar picture ("parkinsonism–hyperpyrexia syndrome"). The underlying mechanism is thought to involve blockade of dopamine D2 receptors in the hypothalamus and nigrostriatal pathways, producing hyperthermia and lead-pipe rigidity.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is clinical and one of exclusion. Laboratory findings commonly include a markedly raised creatine kinase (CK), leukocytosis, and low serum iron. Important differential diagnoses include serotonin syndrome (which typically features hyperreflexia and clonus and a faster onset after serotonergic drugs), malignant hyperthermia (triggered by volatile anaesthetics or succinylcholine), severe catatonia, and central nervous system infection.

Treatment

The offending medication must be stopped immediately, with aggressive supportive care: intravenous fluids, active cooling, and correction of electrolytes. Specific therapies include dantrolene, which reduces muscle contraction, and dopamine agonists such as bromocriptine or amantadine; electroconvulsive therapy may be used in refractory cases. With early recognition, mortality has fallen from historical figures above 30% to under 10% in modern series.
