Schmitt trigger

A Schmitt trigger is a comparator circuit with hysteresis implemented by applying positive feedback to the input of a comparator or amplifier. The output switches between two saturated states, but the input voltage needed to make it switch from low to high is higher than the voltage at which it switches back from high to low. This dead band between the two thresholds makes the circuit immune to noise on a slowly changing or noisy input.

History and principle

The circuit was invented by the American scientist Otto H. Schmitt in 1934, while studying nerve impulse propagation — he called it a "thermionic trigger". In a classic implementation with an operational amplifier, positive feedback through a resistor divider sets an upper threshold VT+ and a lower threshold VT−; the width of the hysteresis band equals the difference between them. As long as input noise is smaller than the band, the output remains clean and digital.

Applications

Schmitt triggers are used to condition signals: debouncing mechanical switches, cleaning up sensor outputs, converting sine waves to square waves, and serving as the input stages of many digital logic gates (for example the 74HC14 hex Schmitt inverter). A Schmitt trigger with an RC network forms a simple relaxation oscillator, and the same hysteresis principle appears in thermostats and other bang-bang controllers.
