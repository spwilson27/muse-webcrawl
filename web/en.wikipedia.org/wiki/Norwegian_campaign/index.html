Norwegian campaign

The Norwegian campaign (8 April – 10 June 1940) was the battle for Norway during the Second World War, fought between Nazi Germany and the Allies (principally Britain, France and Poland) together with Norwegian forces. It began with the German invasion, Operation Weserübung, on 9 April 1940, and ended with the withdrawal of Allied forces and the escape of King Haakon VII and the Norwegian government into exile.

Background

Both sides had plans for Norway: Germany depended on Swedish iron ore shipped through the ice-free port of Narvik, and the Allies contemplated mining Norwegian waters (Operation Wilfred) and landing forces to cut off the ore traffic and aid Finland. Germany struck first.

The campaign

German forces seized Oslo, Bergen, Trondheim, Stavanger, Kristiansand and Narvik in a bold combined operation, though the Norwegian coastal battery at Oscarsborg sank the cruiser Blücher in the Oslofjord, delaying the occupation of the capital and allowing the king and government to escape. Allied troops landed at Namsos, Åndalsnes and Narvik; the central Norway landings failed under Luftwaffe attack, but at Narvik a combined Allied-Norwegian force defeated General Dietl's mountain troops and took the town on 28 May — the first German land defeat of the war. The German invasion of France in May forced the Allies to evacuate in early June (Operation Alphabet); the aircraft carrier HMS Glorious was sunk during the withdrawal with heavy loss of life.

Outcome

Germany occupied Norway for the rest of the war, installing Vidkun Quisling's collaborationist regime, while Norway's king, government and large merchant fleet continued the war from exile. The campaign inflicted serious losses on the German navy and contributed to the fall of Neville Chamberlain's government in Britain, bringing Winston Churchill to power.
