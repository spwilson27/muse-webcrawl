A British Overseas Territories citizen (BOTC) is a class of British national held by people connected with a British Overseas Territory. The status was created by the British Nationality Act 1981 as "British Dependent Territories citizenship" and renamed when the territories themselves were renamed "overseas territories" by the British Overseas Territories Act 2002.

Acquisition

BOTC is generally acquired by birth, descent or registration in connection with a particular territory — such as Anguilla, Bermuda, the British Virgin Islands, the Cayman Islands, Gibraltar, the Falkland Islands, Montserrat, the Turks and Caicos Islands or St Helena. Most territories also have their own local immigration status, often called "belonger status", which is separate from BOTC and governs the right to live and work in that territory.

Rights

Before 2002, BOTC alone generally carried no right of abode in the United Kingdom, which was a source of grievance — most famously for the inhabitants of St Helena. The British Overseas Territories Act 2002 restored full British citizenship to most BOTCs (except those connected only with the Sovereign Base Areas of Cyprus), so most BOTCs now also hold British citizenship and can live and work in the UK. BOTC passports are issued in the name of the relevant territory.
