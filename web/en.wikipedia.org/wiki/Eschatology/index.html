Eschatology

Eschatology (from Ancient Greek ἔσχατος éskhatos "last" and -logy) concerns expectations of the end of the present age, human history, or of the world itself. The end time (also called end times, end of time, end of days, last days, final days, doomsday, eschaton) is a future described in the eschatologies of several world religions and mythologies as a time of tribulation leading to a final divine purpose.

Religious eschatologies

Abrahamic religions maintain a linear cosmology, with end-time scenarios containing themes of transformation and redemption. In Judaism, the end times are associated with the coming of the Messiah and the resurrection of the dead. In Christianity, eschatology centres on the Second Coming of Christ, the resurrection, the Last Judgment and the new heaven and new earth, with the Book of Revelation as its key text; interpretive traditions include premillennialism, amillennialism and postmillennialism. Islamic eschatology features the signs of the Hour, the Mahdi, the return of Isa (Jesus), and the Day of Judgment (Yawm al-Qiyamah). Zoroastrianism — whose Frashokereti, the renovation of the world, is among the earliest developed eschatologies — influenced later traditions, while Hindu and Buddhist cosmologies envision cyclical ages: the Kali Yuga of Hinduism and, in Buddhism, the future coming of Maitreya.

Secular and scholarly study

The term also covers secular "end of the world" scenarios — nuclear war, pandemics, artificial intelligence, and climate change feature in contemporary risk studies — and academic study of apocalypticism, millenarian movements and their social effects.
