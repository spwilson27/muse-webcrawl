Acronym

An acronym is an abbreviation formed from the initial letters of a phrase and pronounced as a word — for example, NASA (National Aeronautics and Space Administration), laser (light amplification by stimulated emission of radiation) and scuba. Abbreviations pronounced as a sequence of letters, such as FBI or URL, are often distinguished as initialisms, though usage varies and many style guides treat acronym as the umbrella term.

Usage and style

Acronyms proliferated in the twentieth century with large government, military and technical institutions. Some have become ordinary words and lost their capitalisation (laser, radar, scuba), while others retain capitals. Guides differ on punctuation — most modern styles omit full stops. Recursive acronyms, in which one letter stands for the acronym itself (as in GNU, "GNU's Not Unix"), are a tradition in computing. Backronyms reinterpret an existing word as if it were an acronym.

History

Although initial-letter abbreviations are ancient — the Roman SPQR, and the Christian fish symbol from the Greek acronym ΙΧΘΥΣ — the English word "acronym" itself was coined in 1943, from Greek akro- (tip, beginning) and -onym (name). Their mass adoption followed the alphabet agencies of the New Deal, Second World War military terminology and post-war international organisations such as NATO and UNESCO.
