Rules of golf

The Rules of Golf are a set of standard regulations and procedures by which the sport of golf should be played. They are jointly written and administered by The R&A (formerly part of the Royal and Ancient Golf Club of St Andrews) and the United States Golf Association (USGA). The rules cover how the game is played, equipment standards, and the conduct expected of players.

Overview

The central principle of the rules is that the ball should be played as it lies and the course played as it is found. Players are expected to apply the rules to themselves, call penalties on themselves, and act with integrity — golf is largely self-officiated. The modern rules are organised into 25 principal rules (reduced from 34 in the major modernisation that took effect on 1 January 2019), covering topics such as teeing areas, playing the ball, bunkers, penalty areas, putting greens, free relief, and penalty relief.

History

The earliest surviving written rules of golf were drawn up in 1744 by the Gentlemen Golfers of Leith (later the Honourable Company of Edinburgh Golfers) for the first Challenge for the Silver Club. Those 13 articles covered matters such as teeing the ball within a club-length of the hole and the treatment of loose impediments. The Royal and Ancient published its own code in 1754, and over the following centuries the rules grew steadily more elaborate as equipment and courses changed.

Key concepts

  • Stroke play and match play: the two main forms of competition. In stroke play the total number of strokes counts; in match play holes are won, lost or halved against an opponent.
  • Penalty areas: bodies of water and other areas defined by the committee, marked with yellow or red stakes or lines, from which penalty relief may be taken.
  • Out of bounds: areas beyond the boundaries of the course, usually marked with white stakes; a ball out of bounds requires a stroke-and-distance penalty.
  • Handicap: a numerical measure of a golfer's potential, administered under the World Handicap System, allowing players of different abilities to compete equitably.

Equipment rules

The rules also prescribe specifications for clubs and balls, including limits on clubhead size, groove dimensions, and ball velocity and distance performance, tested and published in lists of conforming equipment by The R&A and USGA.
