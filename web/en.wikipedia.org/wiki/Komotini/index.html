Komotini

Komotini (Greek: Κομοτηνή; Turkish: Gümülcine) is a city in northeastern Greece, the capital of the regional unit of Rhodope and of the region of Eastern Macedonia and Thrace. It has a population of roughly 50,000–55,000 and lies in the historical region of Thrace, near the foothills of the Rhodope Mountains and the Maritsa (Evros) plain.

History

The area was settled in antiquity, and a fortified town stood nearby in the Byzantine era; Komotini itself is first securely attested in the 14th century. It came under Ottoman rule in the 1360s and remained an Ottoman town for more than five centuries, acquiring mosques, baths, and markets. During the Balkan Wars it changed hands between Bulgaria and Greece, and by the Treaty of Neuilly (1919) and the Treaty of Lausanne (1923) it was confirmed as part of Greece.

Under the Lausanne settlement, the Muslim minority of Western Thrace was exempted from the Greco-Turkish population exchange, giving Komotini a lasting mixed character of Greek Orthodox, Muslim Turkish-speaking, Pomak, and Roma communities — reflected in its bilingual streetscapes, mosques alongside churches, and minority schools.

Modern city

Komotini is the seat of the Democritus University of Thrace, founded in 1973, whose student population animates the city's centre, and hosts several university departments, the Archaeological Museum of Komotini, and collections of Thracian folk art. Local sights include the Byzantine fortress walls, the Ottoman-era clock tower, and the Imaret of the old town. The surrounding region is known for agriculture — tobacco, cotton, and wheat — and for the Nestos and Rhodope mountain landscapes to the north.
