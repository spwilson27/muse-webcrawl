Fort Fisher was a Confederate fort during the American Civil War. It protected the port approaches of Wilmington, North Carolina, from 1861 until its fall in January 1865. Located on the peninsula known as Federal Point (now part of Kure Beach), the fort guarded New Inlet, one of two entrances to the Cape Fear River, and kept Wilmington open to blockade runners supplying the Confederate armies — earning it the nickname the "Gibraltar of the South".

Construction

Construction began in 1861 as a series of sand and earth batteries. Under Colonel William Lamb, who took command in July 1862, the fort was expanded into the largest earthwork fortification in the Confederacy. Its works consisted of a land face stretching roughly 480 yards across the peninsula, with a series of mounds and traverses mounting heavy guns, and a sea face extending about a mile along the Atlantic shore. Earthen walls up to 30 feet thick absorbed artillery fire far better than masonry forts. A palisade fence and electrically detonated torpedoes (mines) defended the land approaches.

First Battle, December 1864

In December 1864 a Union expedition under Major General Benjamin Butler and Rear Admiral David Dixon Porter attempted to take the fort. The attack opened with the explosion of the powder boat USS Louisiana, intended to breach the walls but ineffective. A massive naval bombardment on 24–25 December was followed by a partial landing, but Butler withdrew his troops against orders, and the expedition failed.

Second Battle, January 1865

A second, larger expedition under Major General Alfred Terry returned in January 1865. After the heaviest naval bombardment of the war — some 600 guns firing over several days — Union infantry assaulted the land face on 15 January 1865 while sailors and Marines attacked the sea face as a diversion. After hours of fierce hand-to-hand fighting along the traverses, the fort fell. Its capture closed Wilmington, the Confederacy's last major seaport open to blockade running, and hastened the end of the war. The fort's commander, General William H. C. Whiting, was mortally wounded in the battle.

Preservation

About ten percent of the fort's land face survives, preserved as the Fort Fisher State Historic Site, with a visitor centre and museum. Much of the remainder was lost to ocean erosion and to the construction of a World War II training base and airfield. The site was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1961.
