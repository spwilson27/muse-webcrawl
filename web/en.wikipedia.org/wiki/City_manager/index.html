City manager

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A city manager is a professional administrator appointed by a city council to serve as the municipality's chief executive in the council–manager form of government used by a large share of American municipalities (and adapted internationally). The council sets policy, adopts the budget and hires (and can dismiss) the manager; the manager hires department heads, prepares the budget, administers services and advises the council — a deliberate separation of politics from administration modelled on the corporate board–CEO relationship and on Progressive Era reform ideals (the plan was first adopted by smaller cities in the 1900s–1910s; Staunton, Virginia (1908) and Dayton, Ohio (1914) are the usual pioneer examples).

City managers are typically career professionals (organised in the ICMA, founded 1914) serving at the council's pleasure with an average tenure of several years per city; the form coexists with the strong-mayor model, and larger cities increasingly hybridise (elected executives with professional chief administrative officers). The model exports the premise of the Permanent secretary and the merit system to local government: competence hired on the market, accountable to elected politicians but not elected themselves.
