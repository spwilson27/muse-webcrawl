Pathogen

A pathogen is any organism or agent that causes disease in a host. Pathogens include bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoa, helminths (parasitic worms), and prions. The study of pathogens is central to microbiology, immunology, and epidemiology.

Types

Bacterial pathogens such as Mycobacterium tuberculosis and viral pathogens such as influenza viruses cause many major infectious diseases. Fungal pathogens are especially dangerous to immunocompromised people and to crops. Prions — misfolded proteins — cause transmissible spongiform encephalopathies. Some pathogens are obligate (they must infect a host to reproduce) while opportunistic pathogens cause disease only when defences are weakened.

Transmission and defence

Pathogens spread by routes including droplets and aerosols, food and water, vectors such as mosquitoes, and direct contact. Hosts resist through barriers, innate and adaptive immunity, and medical interventions including vaccines, antibiotics, and antivirals.
