Madaba

Madaba (Arabic: مادبا) is the capital city of the Madaba Governorate in central Jordan, located about 30 kilometres southwest of the capital, Amman. The city is best known for its Byzantine and Umayyad mosaics, especially the Madaba Map, a large mosaic map of the Holy Land dating to the sixth century AD.

History

Madaba was known in antiquity as Medeba, a Moabite town mentioned in the Bible and in the Mesha Stele. It changed hands among the Nabataeans, Romans, and Byzantines, and flourished as a Christian town with numerous mosaic-floored churches. After an earthquake in 749 and subsequent decline, the site was largely abandoned until the late nineteenth century, when Christian settlers from Karak resettled it — a resettlement that led to the discovery of the famous mosaics beneath the modern town.

The Madaba Map

The Madaba Map, discovered in 1884 during the construction of the Greek Orthodox Church of St George, is the oldest known cartographic depiction of the Holy Land, dating to between 542 and 570 AD. The surviving portion depicts the region from Lebanon to the Nile Delta, with Jerusalem prominently shown at its centre.

Today

Modern Madaba has a population of roughly 100,000 and remains a significant centre of Jordan's Christian community as well as a popular tourist destination. Mount Nebo, traditionally regarded as the place from which Moses viewed the Promised Land, lies a short distance to the northwest.
