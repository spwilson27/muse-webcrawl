The Monumentum Ancyranum is the Temple of Augustus and Rome in Ankara, Turkey, famous for preserving the most complete copy of the Res Gestae Divi Augusti, the account of his deeds composed by the first Roman emperor Augustus.

The inscription

Augustus directed that his Res Gestae be inscribed on bronze pillars in front of his mausoleum in Rome; provincial copies were carved on temples. The Ancyra copy survives on the walls of the temple's cella: the Latin text inside and a Greek translation outside. Together the two versions allow the reconstruction of nearly the whole document, making the inscription one of the most important sources for the reign of Augustus.

History

The temple, built in the late 1st century BC in the Phrygian sanctuary city of Ancyra, was later converted into a church in the Byzantine period and adjoining a mosque (the Hacı Bayram Mosque) in Ottoman times. The inscription was copied by the Flemish diplomat Ogier Ghiselin de Busbecq in 1555 and published widely thereafter.
