Spanish conquest of the Inca Empire

The Spanish conquest of the Inca Empire, also known as the conquest of Peru, was one of the most important campaigns in the Spanish colonization of the Americas, ending the largest empire in pre-Columbian America.

Conquest of the Inca Empire
Date1532–1572
LocationAndes, western South America
Spanish leaderFrancisco Pizarro
Inca rulersAtahualpa; Manco Inca; Túpac Amaru
ResultSpanish victory; Viceroyalty of Peru

Background

The Inca Empire (Tawantinsuyu) was weakened by smallpox arriving ahead of the Spanish and by a civil war between Atahualpa and Huáscar after the death of Huayna Capac (c. 1527).

Cajamarca and after

Francisco Pizarro, with fewer than 200 men, ambushed and captured Atahualpa at Cajamarca on 16 November 1532. Atahualpa filled a room with gold and silver as ransom but was executed in 1533. Spanish forces took Cusco, installed puppet rulers, and faced a great rebellion by Manco Inca (1536–37), who established a Neo-Inca State at Vilcabamba.

End of resistance

Conquistador civil wars followed. The Vilcabamba state survived until 1572, when Viceroy Toledo captured and executed the last Sapa Inca, Túpac Amaru. Disease, war and exploitation caused catastrophic population decline.
