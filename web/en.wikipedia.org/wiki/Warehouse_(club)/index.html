Warehouse (club)

The Warehouse
Location206 South Jefferson Street, Chicago, Illinois
Opened1977
Known forBirthplace of house music

The Warehouse was a nightclub in Chicago, Illinois, United States, widely regarded as the birthplace of house music. Opened in 1977 in a three-storey industrial building in the West Loop, it was a members-only club catering largely to gay Black and Latino men.

Frankie Knuckles and house music

The club's resident DJ, Frankie Knuckles — later dubbed the "Godfather of House" — developed a distinctive style at the Warehouse, mixing disco, soul, European electronic records and drum-machine rhythms into continuous, high-energy sets. The style, and the music sold as "warehouse music" at the Imports Etc. record store, was soon shortened to "house music".

Legacy

The Warehouse closed in 1987, but its sound, carried on by clubs such as the Music Box and DJs like Ron Hardy, shaped electronic dance music worldwide. In 2004 Chicago renamed a stretch of Jefferson Street near the site "Frankie Knuckles Way", and the building has been recognised as a landmark of the city's cultural history.
