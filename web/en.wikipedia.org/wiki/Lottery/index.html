Lottery

A lottery is a form of gambling that involves the drawing of numbers at random for a prize. Some governments outlaw lotteries, while others endorse them to the extent of organizing a national or state lottery. Lotteries come in many formats: the prize may be fixed cash, goods, or a share of the receipts (a pari-mutuel pool), and the draw may select numbers, symbols, or winning tickets.

History

The first recorded lotteries offered money prizes in the Low Countries in the 15th century, where towns held public lotteries to raise funds for fortifications and the poor; the word derives from the Dutch loterij. England's first state lottery was chartered by Elizabeth I in 1567. Lotteries financed everything from the Jamestown colony to bridges, canals, churches, and universities — early Harvard, Yale, and Princeton all benefited. Corruption and fraud led many states and countries to ban lotteries in the 19th century, before their revival in the 20th century as state revenue sources.

Modern lotteries

Modern games include numbers draws (players pick a combination, such as 6 from 49), scratchcards/instant lotteries, and multi-jurisdiction games with rollover jackpots such as Powerball and Mega Millions in the United States and EuroMillions in Europe, which have produced prizes exceeding one billion dollars. A substantial share of revenue — commonly 20–40 per cent — goes to the state or designated good causes, with the remainder divided between prizes and retailer/operator costs.

Probability and criticism

The odds of a jackpot in a 6/49 game are about 1 in 13.98 million, and in modern multi-state games as low as 1 in 300 million; lotteries almost always have a negative expected value for players. Critics describe state lotteries as a regressive "tax on the poor", note their advertising, and point to problem gambling; supporters emphasise voluntary participation and funding for education and good causes.

Payment and taxes

Winners typically choose between an annuity paid over 20–30 years and a smaller lump sum; in the United States winnings are taxable income, whereas in many other countries (e.g. the UK and Canada) prizes are tax-free at the point of payment.
