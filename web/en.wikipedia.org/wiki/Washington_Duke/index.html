Washington Duke

Born18 December 1820, Orange County, North Carolina
Died8 May 1905, Durham, North Carolina
OccupationTobacco industrialist, philanthropist
ChildrenBenjamin Newton Duke, James Buchanan Duke

Washington Duke (1820–1905) was an American tobacco industrialist and philanthropist who founded W. Duke, Sons & Co., the tobacco company that grew into the American Tobacco Company. His sons Benjamin and James Buchanan Duke built the family business into a dominant trust and endowed Duke University, which bears the family name.

Life and business

Duke farmed in Orange County, North Carolina, and served in the Confederate navy during the American Civil War. Returning home in 1865 to a ruined farm, he began processing tobacco by hand in a small log structure, selling it as "Pro Bono Publico" from a wagon. The business grew with his sons' involvement; in the 1880s the firm adopted the Bonsack cigarette-rolling machine, moved into Durham, and in 1890 merged into the American Tobacco Company.

Philanthropy

A devout Methodist, Duke gave generously to Methodist causes and to Trinity College, persuading it to move from Randolph County to Durham in 1892; the college later became Duke University after a major endowment by his son James. Duke's statue stands on the university's East Campus.
