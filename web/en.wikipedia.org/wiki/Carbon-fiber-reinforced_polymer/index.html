Carbon-fiber-reinforced polymer, carbon-fibre-reinforced plastic, or carbon-fiber reinforced-thermoplastic (CFRP, CRP, CFRTP), is an extremely strong and light fibre-reinforced plastic that contains carbon fibres. The fibres are embedded in a binding polymer, usually a thermoset resin such as epoxy, and carry the load while the matrix holds them in shape and transfers stress.

Properties

CFRP has one of the highest specific strengths and stiffnesses of any engineering material — comparable in stiffness to steel at a fraction of the weight, with density around 1.6 g/cm³. Its properties are strongly anisotropic, depending on fibre layup, and it can be tailored by stacking woven or unidirectional plies in chosen orientations. Disadvantages include brittleness, high cost, electrical conductivity, and difficult recycling.

Applications

CFRP is used extensively in aerospace (the Boeing 787 and Airbus A350 airframes are about half composite by weight), Formula One monocoques, high-end automobiles, bicycle frames, fishing rods, wind-turbine blades, and strengthening of concrete structures. Manufacturing methods include prepreg lay-up with autoclave curing, resin transfer moulding, filament winding, and pultrusion.
