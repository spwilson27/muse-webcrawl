Bromocriptine, originally marketed as Parlodel, is an ergoline derivative and a dopamine agonist that is used in the treatment of pituitary tumours, Parkinson's disease, hyperprolactinaemia, neuroleptic malignant syndrome and, as an adjunct, type 2 diabetes.

Bromocriptine was patented in 1968 and approved for medical use in 1975. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Medical uses

Bromocriptine is used to treat acromegaly and conditions associated with hyperprolactinaemia, including prolactin-secreting pituitary adenomas (prolactinomas), where it suppresses prolactin secretion and can shrink tumour size. It restores ovulation in women whose fertility is impaired by high prolactin. In Parkinson's disease it is used as an adjunct to levodopa. A quick-release formulation (Cycloset) is approved as an adjunct to diet and exercise in type 2 diabetes.

Pharmacology

Bromocriptine is a potent agonist at dopamine D2 receptors and a partial agonist or antagonist at various serotonin and adrenergic receptors. Its prolactin-lowering effect results from stimulation of D2 receptors on pituitary lactotroph cells.

Side effects

Common side effects include nausea, vomiting, headache, dizziness and orthostatic hypotension, especially at the start of treatment. Rare but serious effects include valvular heart disease, pulmonary and retroperitoneal fibrosis, and impulse control disorders. Bromocriptine is an ergot derivative derived from ergot alkaloids produced by the fungus Claviceps purpurea.
