Tauentzienstraße (colloquially often shortened to "der Tauentzien") is a major shopping street in the western inner city of Berlin, in the districts of Charlottenburg and Schöneberg. It runs for about 700 metres between Wittenbergplatz in the east and Breitscheidplatz in the west, where it continues into the Kurfürstendamm. Together with the Ku'damm, it forms the principal retail axis of Berlin's City West.

Name

The street was named in 1864 after the Prussian general Bogislav Friedrich Emanuel Graf Tauentzien von Wittenberg (1760–1824). It belongs to the so-called "Generalszug" (Generals' Train), a sequence of streets and squares in the area named after commanders of the Wars of Liberation against Napoleon.

History and sights

Laid out in the 19th century as part of the Hobrecht Plan for Berlin's expansion, the street developed into a fashionable boulevard of the "New West". Its best-known building is the Kaufhaus des Westens (KaDeWe) at Wittenbergplatz, opened in 1907 and still one of the largest department stores in continental Europe. The eastern end is marked by the monumental Wittenbergplatz U-Bahn station.

In the central reservation near Breitscheidplatz stands the sculpture Berlin (1987) by Brigitte and Martin Matschinsky-Denninghoff, an abstract pair of interlocking steel loops that became a symbol of the divided and later reunified city. The street suffered heavy damage in the Second World War and was rebuilt in the postwar decades; during the Cold War it was one of West Berlin's main commercial streets.

Transport

The street is served by the Wittenbergplatz and Augsburger Straße stations of the Berlin U-Bahn (lines U1, U2, and U3), and by numerous bus routes. Its western end at Breitscheidplatz adjoins Zoologischer Garten station and the Kaiser Wilhelm Memorial Church.
