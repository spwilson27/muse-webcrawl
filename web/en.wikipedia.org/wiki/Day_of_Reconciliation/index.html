Day of Reconciliation

The Day of Reconciliation is a public holiday in South Africa held annually on 16 December. The holiday came into effect in 1995 after the end of apartheid, with the intention of fostering reconciliation and national unity among South Africans.

History

The date 16 December was already significant to both Afrikaners and Black South Africans. Afrikaners commemorated it as the Day of the Vow, marking the Voortrekker victory over the Zulu kingdom at the Battle of Blood River in 1838. For the liberation movement, 16 December 1961 marked the founding of Umkhonto we Sizwe, the armed wing of the African National Congress.

Observance

The post-apartheid government retained the date but gave it a new meaning centered on reconciliation. Observances include speeches, cultural events, and annual themes promoting social cohesion, and it forms part of South Africa's broader national reconciliation project that included the Truth and Reconciliation Commission.
