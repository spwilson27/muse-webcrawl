Partisan game

In combinatorial game theory, a partisan game (often spelled partizan) is a game in which the moves available to the two players, conventionally called Left and Right, are not the same. This contrasts with an impartial game, where both players always have the same options from any position.

Examples

Chess is partisan because each player moves only their own pieces. In the abstract games studied by John Horton Conway, such as Domineering and Hackenbush, positions are written {L | R}, listing Left's possible moves to the left of the bar and Right's to the right. Partisan games have a richer structure than impartial ones: their values are not all numbers, and include infinitesimals and "hot" games such as switches.

Theory

The theory of partisan games is developed in Conway's On Numbers and Games and in Winning Ways for Your Mathematical Plays by Berlekamp, Conway and Guy. The surreal numbers arise as a special class of partisan game values.
