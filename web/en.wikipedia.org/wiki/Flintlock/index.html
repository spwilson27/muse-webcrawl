Flintlock is a general term for any firearm that uses a flint-striking ignition mechanism, or the term may also apply to the mechanism itself. The true flintlock, developed in France in the early 17th century, superseded the earlier snaplock, snaphaunce, and wheellock mechanisms and remained in military and sporting use for more than two centuries, until it was replaced by the percussion cap in the 19th century.

Mechanism

In a flintlock, a cock (hammer) holds a sharpened piece of flint in its jaws. When the trigger is pulled, the cock swings forward and the flint strikes a hinged steel plate called the frizzen (or "battery"), shaving off sparks of molten metal while simultaneously knocking the frizzen open. The sparks fall into the priming pan, igniting a small charge of fine gunpowder, and the flame passes through a touch hole in the barrel to ignite the main propellant charge. The lock can be set at half-cock, a safety position from which the weapon cannot be fired, giving rise to the expression "going off half-cocked."

History

Earlier flint-striking systems included the snaplock of Scandinavia and the snaphaunce of the Low Countries. The French court gunsmith Marin le Bourgeoys is generally credited with combining the features of these predecessors into the true flintlock around 1610, including the vertically acting sear and the combined frizzen and pan cover. The design spread rapidly: flintlock muskets became standard infantry weapons across Europe and the Americas, famously including the French Charleville and the British Brown Bess. Flintlock pistols and rifles were carried through the American Revolutionary War and the Napoleonic Wars.

Replacement

Following the invention of the percussion cap in the 1820s, flintlocks were widely converted to the more weather-resistant percussion system ("caplock"), and by the 1860s breech-loading cartridge arms had rendered both systems obsolete for military use. Flintlocks remain in use today by black-powder enthusiasts, historical reenactors, and traditional hunters.
