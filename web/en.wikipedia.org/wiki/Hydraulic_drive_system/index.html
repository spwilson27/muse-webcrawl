Hydraulic drive system

A hydraulic drive system is a drive or transmission system that uses pressurised hydraulic fluid to power machinery. A pump, driven by an electric motor or engine, pressurises the fluid; control valves direct the flow; and actuators — hydraulic cylinders for linear motion or hydraulic motors for rotary motion — convert the fluid power back into mechanical work.

Principle

The system operates on Pascal's law: pressure applied to a confined fluid is transmitted undiminished throughout the fluid. Because small forces on a pump can generate large forces at an actuator of larger area, hydraulic drives achieve very high power density and force multiplication, which is why they are used in excavators, presses, aircraft flight controls, ship steering gear and industrial machinery.

Components

  • Reservoir storing the hydraulic fluid
  • Pump (gear, vane or piston type) generating flow
  • Valves controlling pressure, direction and flow rate
  • Actuators: hydraulic cylinders and hydraulic motors
  • Filters, accumulators and coolers maintaining fluid condition

Hydrostatic transmissions

A common variant is the hydrostatic transmission, in which a variable-displacement pump drives a hydraulic motor in a closed loop, providing stepless speed control in vehicles such as lawn tractors, combine harvesters and some locomotives.
