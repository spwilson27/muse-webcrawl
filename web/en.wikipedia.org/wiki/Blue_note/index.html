In jazz and blues, blue notes (also "worried notes") are notes that are sung or played at a slightly flattened pitch compared with the standard major scale — most characteristically the flattened third, fifth and seventh degrees. These inflections, often involving pitch bending between the minor and major third, are a defining feature of blues tonality and of the many genres descended from it.

Origins and theory

Blue notes are generally traced to the encounter between African musical practices — with their flexible, neutral intervals — and European harmony in the music of enslaved African Americans. On instruments with fixed pitches such as the piano, players suggest blue notes by sounding the minor and major third together or in quick succession, or by using grace notes. The blues scale is commonly described as the minor pentatonic scale with an added flattened fifth, but actual blues practice is more fluid: the "blue third" sits between minor and major, and singers slide through it expressively.

Influence

Blue-note inflections permeate jazz improvisation, rhythm and blues, rock and roll, country and soul. Composers such as George Gershwin carried them into concert music, most famously in Rhapsody in Blue. The term also gave its name to Blue Note Records, the celebrated jazz label founded in 1939.
