Katipunan

Katipunan
Full name: Kataastaasan, Kagalanggalangang Katipunan ng mga Anak ng Bayan (KKK)
Founded: 7 July 1892, Manila
Founder: Andrés Bonifacio and others
Purpose: Philippine independence from Spain

The Katipunan, officially the Kataastaasan, Kagalanggalangang Katipunan ng mga Anak ng Bayan ("Supreme and Venerable Association of the Children of the Nation"), abbreviated KKK, was a Philippine revolutionary secret society founded in Manila in 1892 to overthrow Spanish colonial rule through armed revolt. Its best-known founder and leader was Andrés Bonifacio.

History

The society was formed on the night of 7 July 1892, after the arrest and exile of José Rizal, by Bonifacio, Deodato Arellano, Ladislao Diwa, Teodoro Plata and others linked to the reformist Liga Filipina. It spread through Manila and the Tagalog provinces using a triangle system of recruitment, Masonic-inspired rituals and passwords, and published the newspaper Kalayaan to win converts.

Revolution

Discovered by the Spanish authorities in August 1896, the Katipunan launched the Philippine Revolution with the Cry of Pugad Lawin. Factional conflict between Bonifacio's Magdiwang and Emilio Aguinaldo's Magdalo councils led to Bonifacio's arrest and execution in 1897, after which Aguinaldo assumed leadership. The society was superseded by the revolutionary government it had created.
