Triumphal entry into Jerusalem

The triumphal entry into Jerusalem is an event in the life of Jesus recounted in all four canonical Gospels, marking the beginning of the Passion narrative. According to the accounts, Jesus rode into Jerusalem on a donkey (in some tellings, on a colt) while crowds spread cloaks and branches on the road before him and shouted acclamations including "Hosanna". Christians commemorate the event on Palm Sunday, the first day of Holy Week.

CommemoratedPalm Sunday
SourcesMatthew 21; Mark 11; Luke 19; John 12
LocationJerusalem, via Bethphage and the Mount of Olives

Gospel accounts

The Synoptic Gospels describe Jesus sending disciples ahead to Bethphage to fetch a donkey and its colt, fulfilling, in Matthew's telling, the prophecy of Zechariah 9:9 about a humble king riding on a donkey. John's Gospel places the entry after the raising of Lazarus and emphasises the crowd that had gathered in Jerusalem for the Passover festival. As Jesus descended the Mount of Olives, the crowds greeted him as a royal and messianic figure.

Interpretation

Scholars note that the entry deliberately evokes royal imagery — the donkey recalls Solomon's coronation mount in 1 Kings — while its humility contrasts with the military processions of Roman authority entering the city at festival time. Within days, according to the narratives, Jesus was arrested and crucified.
