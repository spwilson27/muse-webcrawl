Federal Work-Study Program

The Federal Work-Study Program (FWS), also called the College Work-Study Program, is a United States federal student aid programme that provides funds for part-time employment to help undergraduate and graduate students finance the costs of postsecondary education. It was established as part of the Economic Opportunity Act of 1964 and is now authorised under Title IV of the Higher Education Act of 1965.

Operation

Funds are allocated to participating colleges and universities, which administer the programme and match a share of wages — generally the federal government pays up to 75% of a student's earnings. Jobs may be on campus, with government agencies, or with non-profit organisations, and community-service and reading-tutoring positions are encouraged. Students are paid at least the federal minimum wage and may earn up to the amount of their award, determined by financial need as shown on the FAFSA.

Eligibility

Eligibility depends on financial need, enrolment at a participating institution, and satisfactory academic progress. Roughly 600,000 students receive work-study earnings in a typical year.
