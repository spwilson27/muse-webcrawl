Sociology of art

Field: Sociology, art history

The sociology of art is a subfield of sociology that studies the social dimensions of art: how societies produce, distribute, receive and value artworks, and how art reflects and shapes social life. It bridges sociology, art history and cultural studies.

Approaches

Marxist approaches, from Lukács to the Frankfurt School, read art through ideology and class. Arnold Hauser's Social History of Art narrated styles through social change. Howard Becker's Art Worlds treated art as collective activity of artists, suppliers, critics and audiences. Pierre Bourdieu analysed taste, cultural capital and the field of cultural production. Erwin Panofsky's iconology, while art-historical, modelled how artworks encode a culture's worldview.

Topics

Research covers patronage and markets, museums and canon formation, audiences and reception, artistic careers and socialisation, and art's role in identity and resistance — including state doctrines such as socialist realism treated in the main crawl.
