Inner Niger Delta

The Inner Niger Delta is the inland delta of the Niger River in central Mali. It forms where the river, flowing northeast into the edge of the Sahara, slows, splits into numerous branches and loses much of its water to evaporation and infiltration, before its channels recombine and continue north toward Timbuktu.

Description

The delta covers a vast seasonal floodplain — commonly cited at around 20,000 to 30,000 square kilometres at peak flood — dotted with lakes such as Lake Debo. Its flood cycle, driven by rains far upstream in Guinea, arrives months later in the delta and sustains fishing, rice farming and transhumant cattle herding. The historic town of Djenné, with its great mud mosque, lies on an island within the delta region.

The ecosystem is under pressure from upstream dams, irrigation abstraction and variable rainfall, which affect the height and timing of the flood on which local livelihoods depend.
