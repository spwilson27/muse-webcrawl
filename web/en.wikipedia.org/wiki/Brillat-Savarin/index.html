Jean Anthelme Brillat-Savarin

Born1 April 1755, Belley, France
Died2 February 1826, Paris, France
OccupationLawyer, politician, gastronomist
Known forThe Physiology of Taste (1825)

Jean Anthelme Brillat-Savarin (French pronunciation: [ʒɑ̃ ɑ̃tɛlm bʁija savaʁɛ̃]; 1 April 1755 – 2 February 1826) was a French lawyer, politician and gastronome. He is best known as the author of Physiologie du goût (The Physiology of Taste), published in December 1825, shortly before his death, which has remained in print ever since and is considered a foundational work of gastronomic literature.

Life

Brillat-Savarin was born in Belley, in the Bugey region, into a family of lawyers. He studied law, chemistry and medicine at Dijon and practised law in his home town. In 1789 he was elected a deputy to the Estates-General and later to the National Constituent Assembly. During the Reign of Terror he fled France, living in Switzerland, the Netherlands, and the United States, where he supported himself by teaching French and playing violin in a theatre orchestra in New York. He returned to France in 1796 and spent the rest of his career as a judge of the Court of Cassation in Paris.

The Physiology of Taste

His famous work, written in a witty, anecdotal style of "meditations" on food and eating, opens with a set of aphorisms, among them: "Tell me what you eat, and I will tell you what you are"; "The discovery of a new dish does more for human happiness than the discovery of a star"; and "Dessert without cheese is like a beautiful woman with one eye missing." The book discusses the senses, appetite, digestion, restaurants and the pleasures of the table, and reflects Brillat-Savarin's view of cooking as both science and art.

Legacy

Brillat-Savarin's name is commemorated in the Brillat-Savarin cheese, a rich triple-cream cow's milk cheese from Burgundy created in the 1930s, and in the baba-like cake known as the savarin. His hometown of Belley celebrates him as its most famous son.
