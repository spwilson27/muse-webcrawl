Coadjoint representation

In mathematics, the coadjoint representation of a Lie group G is the dual of the adjoint representation. If 𝔤 denotes the Lie algebra of G, then the corresponding action of G on 𝔤*, the dual space of 𝔤, is called the coadjoint action. More precisely, for g ∈ G and μ ∈ 𝔤*, the coadjoint action is defined by pairing against the adjoint action:

⟨Ad*(g) μ, X⟩ = ⟨μ, Ad(g⁻¹) X⟩

for every X ∈ 𝔤. The orbits of the coadjoint action, called coadjoint orbits, are fundamental objects in representation theory and symplectic geometry.

Coadjoint orbits and symplectic structure

A coadjoint orbit 𝒪μ = {Ad*(g) μ : g ∈ G} carries a canonical symplectic form discovered by Kirillov, Kostant, and Souriau. If ξ𝒪 denotes the vector field generated by ξ ∈ 𝔤 on the orbit, the Kirillov–Kostant–Souriau form is defined by

ωμ(ξ𝒪, η𝒪) = ⟨μ, [ξ, η]⟩.

This makes every coadjoint orbit a homogeneous symplectic manifold, and the converse is nearly true: every homogeneous symplectic manifold of a connected Lie group is, up to covering, a coadjoint orbit.

Applications

The coadjoint representation is central to Kirillov's orbit method, which establishes a correspondence between coadjoint orbits and the irreducible unitary representations of a Lie group — exact for nilpotent groups and a guiding principle in general. In classical mechanics, coadjoint orbits appear as phase spaces of mechanical systems with symmetry: for example, the phase space of a rigid body is a coadjoint orbit of the rotation group SO(3), and the Euler equations of rigid body motion, as well as the equations of ideal fluid dynamics in the formulation of Arnold, can be written as geodesic or Hamiltonian flows on coadjoint orbits of (infinite-dimensional) Lie groups.
