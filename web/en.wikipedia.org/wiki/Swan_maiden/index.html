Swan maiden

The swan maiden (German: Schwanenjungfrau) is a mythical creature who shape-shifts from human form to swan form by means of a magical feather garment (or, in some variants, a key or ring). The motif is found in folktales worldwide: a man spies on swan maidens bathing, steals the feather robe of one of them so she cannot fly away, and marries her; years later she recovers the garment — often with the unintended help of her children — and returns to the sky, sometimes leaving her husband to undertake a quest to win her back.

Distribution and variants

The tale is classified in the Aarne–Thompson–Uther index around ATU 400, "The Man on a Quest for His Lost Wife". Variants occur across Europe, Siberia, East and Southeast Asia, and the Americas. The Japanese Hagoromo legend features a tennin (celestial maiden) whose feather robe is taken by a fisherman; the Chinese story of the Cowherd and the Weaver Girl is a related tale of a celestial bride. In Norse tradition, valkyries such as those in the Völundarkviða appear as swan maidens.

Interpretations

Folklorists have read the story as concerning marriages across worlds — sky and earth, wild and domestic — and the incompatibility that eventually reasserts itself; the theft of the garment is often treated as a form of coercion whose consequences the tale explores. The motif influenced Tchaikovsky's Swan Lake, in which Odette is cursed to swan form, and continues to appear in modern fantasy.
