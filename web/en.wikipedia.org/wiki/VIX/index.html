VIX

The VIX is a real-time stock market index representing the market's expectation of future volatility. Created by the Chicago Board Options Exchange (Cboe), it is calculated from the prices of S&P 500 index (SPX) options with near-term expiration dates and expresses expected 30-day volatility as an annualized percentage.

VIX
Full name: Cboe Volatility Index
Launched: 1993 (current method 2003)
Nickname: The fear gauge
Typical range: 12–25; spikes above 40 in crises

Interpretation

A VIX of 20 implies expected annualized volatility of 20%, or roughly 5.8% over the next 30 days. The index tends to spike during market stress — exceeding 80 in 2008 and in March 2020 — and is mean-reverting. The media dubbed it the 'fear index'.

Products

VIX futures trade since 2004 and options since 2006; exchange-traded products tracking them allow speculation on volatility but suffer roll costs from contango. In February 2018 ('Volmageddon') the collapse of the inverse product XIV contributed to a sharp market fall.
