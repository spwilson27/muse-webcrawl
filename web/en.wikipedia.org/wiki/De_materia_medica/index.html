De materia medica (Latin for "On medical material") is a pharmacopoeia of medicinal plants and the medicines that can be obtained from them. The five-volume work was written between 50 and 70 AD by Pedanius Dioscorides, a Greek physician in the Roman army. It was widely read for more than 1,500 years and remained the principal reference work on pharmacology until the early 19th century.

Content

The work describes roughly 600 plants, 35 animal products and 90 minerals, noting their appearance, habitat, preparation and therapeutic uses. Unlike many ancient writers, Dioscorides based his accounts on first-hand observation and military travel across the Roman world, and he organised material by drug action rather than alphabetically.

Influence

Copied and translated into Syriac, Arabic, Persian and Latin, De materia medica underpinned Islamic and European pharmacy through the Middle Ages and Renaissance. The finest surviving copy, the illustrated Vienna Dioscurides (c. 512 AD), was made for the Byzantine princess Juliana Anicia. Its name gave the phrase materia medica to the whole discipline of medicinal substances.
