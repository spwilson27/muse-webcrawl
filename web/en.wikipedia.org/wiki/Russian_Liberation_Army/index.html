The Russian Liberation Army (Russian: Русская освободительная армия, Russkaya osvoboditel'naya armiya, abbreviated ROA), also known as the Vlasov army, was a collaborationist formation composed of Soviet citizens — many of them prisoners of war — that fought under German sponsorship against the Soviet Union during the Second World War. It was led by the captured Red Army general Andrey Vlasov, who presented it as an army fighting Stalinism rather than Russia.

Origins

From 1941, hundreds of thousands of Soviet auxiliaries (Hiwis), Cossack units and eastern battalions served with the Wehrmacht, while Vlasov, captured in July 1942, issued anti-Stalinist appeals such as the Smolensk Declaration (December 1942). Nazi racial policy long blocked a unified Russian formation; only in November 1944, as Germany was losing, was the Committee for the Liberation of the Peoples of Russia (KONR) proclaimed in Prague and the ROA formally established, eventually fielding a few divisions, notably the 1st Division under Sergei Bunyachenko.

Final months

The ROA's combat record was brief. Elements fought on the Oder front in April 1945. In May 1945 Bunyachenko's division controversially turned against the Germans to aid the Prague uprising, then tried to surrender to the Americans. Under the Yalta repatriation agreements, most ROA personnel handed over or captured by the Western Allies were forcibly repatriated to the USSR, where leaders were tried and executed — Vlasov was hanged in Moscow on 1 August 1946 — and many rank-and-file were imprisoned in the Gulag.

Assessment

The ROA remains deeply contentious: condemned as treason in Soviet and much Russian historiography, it has also been portrayed by some as a "third force" of anti-Stalinist patriots, a view complicated by its dependence on Nazi Germany and the crimes of the regime it served. Its insignia used the blue St Andrew's cross on a white field.
