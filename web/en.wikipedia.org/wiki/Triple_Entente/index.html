The Triple Entente (from French entente, "understanding") describes the informal understanding between the Russian Empire, the French Third Republic and the British Empire, built upon the Franco-Russian Alliance of 1892, the Entente Cordiale of 1904 between Paris and London, and the Anglo-Russian Convention of 1907.

Formation

The agreements were not a formal military alliance of all three powers, but they settled colonial disputes—France and Britain over Egypt and Morocco, Britain and Russia over Persia, Afghanistan and Tibet—and signalled a common front against the growing power of Germany and its Triple Alliance partners.

First World War

On the outbreak of the First World War in 1914, the three Entente powers formed the core of the Allied side. By the Treaty of London in 1915, Italy left the Triple Alliance and joined the Entente. The alliance dissolved as an effective partnership after Russia's withdrawal following the October Revolution of 1917.
