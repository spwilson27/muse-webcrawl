The economy of Chile is a market-oriented, high-income economy ranked among the most prosperous and competitive in Latin America. Chile is the world's largest producer of copper, which together with other mining products dominates its exports; it is also a major exporter of lithium, fruit, wine, salmon and forestry products. The country is a member of the OECD and is noted for open trade policies, with free-trade agreements covering most of the world economy.

History

Chile's economy historically rested on mineral exports — nitrates in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, then copper, nationalised in 1971 under President Salvador Allende. After the 1973 military coup, the government of Augusto Pinochet implemented sweeping free-market reforms designed by the "Chicago Boys": privatisation, trade liberalisation, deregulation and a private pension system (AFP). A severe crisis in 1982 forced pragmatic adjustments. Democratic governments after 1990 retained the open model while expanding social spending, reducing poverty dramatically over three decades. Social discontent over inequality and services erupted in the 2019 protests, leading to (ultimately unsuccessful) constitutional replacement processes.

Sectors

Mining, centred on the state company Codelco and private giants such as Escondida, accounts for roughly half of export earnings; northern Chile's lithium brines have become strategically important for electric-vehicle batteries. Agriculture benefits from counter-seasonal production for Northern Hemisphere markets — grapes, cherries, wine and farmed salmon are leading exports. Services, finance, retail and tourism dominate employment, especially in Santiago.

Macroeconomic framework

Chile is known for an independent central bank with inflation targeting, a structural-balance fiscal rule, and sovereign wealth funds that saved copper windfalls during the 2000s commodity boom. Growth has slowed since the mid-2010s, and debates continue over pensions, taxation, inequality and the balance between mining income and diversification.
