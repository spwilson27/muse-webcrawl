Tomáš Garrigue Masaryk (7 March 1850 – 14 September 1937) was a Czechoslovak statesman, philosopher, and sociologist. He founded Czechoslovakia and served as its first president, earning the title "President Liberator."

Early life and scholarship

Born in Moravia to a Slovak father and Moravian mother, Masaryk studied at the University of Vienna, where his doctoral thesis treated suicide as a social phenomenon. He became a professor of philosophy at Charles University in Prague, defended the accused in the antisemitic Hilsner trial, and sat in the Austrian Reichsrat.

Founding of Czechoslovakia

During the First World War Masaryk went into exile, organizing Czechoslovak Legions and lobbying Allied leaders — especially Woodrow Wilson — for an independent Czecho-Slovak state. The republic was proclaimed on October 28, 1918, and Masaryk was elected president, being re-elected three times.

Legacy

Masaryk embodied the democratic, humanist ideals of the First Republic; his name graces Masaryk University in Brno and avenues in cities around the world.
