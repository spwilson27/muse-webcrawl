Learning disability

Learning disability, learning disorder, or learning difficulty (British English) is a condition in the brain that causes difficulties comprehending or processing information and can be caused by several different factors. Given the difficulty learning in a typical manner, this does not exclude the ability to learn in a different manner. In North American usage, "specific learning disorder" refers to difficulties in particular academic skills in people of otherwise typical intelligence; in the United Kingdom, "learning disability" more often denotes intellectual disability, while "learning difficulty" covers specific conditions such as dyslexia.

Types

Specific learning disorders are classified by the skill affected:

  • Dyslexia — difficulties with accurate or fluent word reading and spelling.
  • Dysgraphia — difficulties with written expression and handwriting.
  • Dyscalculia — difficulties with number sense, arithmetic facts, and calculation.

Related conditions frequently discussed alongside learning disabilities include dyspraxia (developmental coordination disorder), auditory and visual processing disorders, and nonverbal learning disorder. Learning disabilities often co-occur with ADHD.

Causes and identification

Learning disorders are neurodevelopmental in origin, with substantial heritability, and reflect differences in how the brain processes information rather than low intelligence, poor teaching, or sensory impairment. Identification typically combines educational history, standardised achievement testing, and assessment of cognitive processing; older models required a discrepancy between IQ and achievement, while newer approaches use response to intervention.

Education and support

In the United States, the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA) guarantees eligible students an Individualized Education Program (IEP) with specialised instruction and accommodations; Section 504 plans provide accommodations in other settings. Effective interventions emphasise explicit, structured teaching of the affected skill — for example, systematic phonics for dyslexia — together with assistive technology and extra time on assessments.
