Mode of transport

The principal transport modes: road, rail, air and water

A mode of transport (or transport mode, or means of transport) is a general term used to distinguish the different kinds of facility and vehicle used to move people and goods. Modes are defined by the infrastructure they use and the medium through which they travel.

Principal modes

The main modes are road (cars, buses, lorries, bicycles and walking along road networks), rail (trains and trams), air (aeroplanes and helicopters), and water or maritime (ships, ferries and boats on seas, rivers and canals). Pipelines form a further mode used exclusively for freight such as oil, gas and water. Journeys combining modes are called intermodal or multimodal transport.

Characteristics

Modes differ in speed, capacity, cost, energy use, emissions, safety and the geography they can serve. Shipping carries the great majority of world freight by volume at low cost but slowly; air transport is fastest and most expensive; rail and road occupy intermediate positions for passengers and goods. Transport policy analyses how journeys are distributed among modes — the modal share — and how that distribution might be changed (modal shift).
