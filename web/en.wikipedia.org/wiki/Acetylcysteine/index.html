Acetylcysteine

Other namesN-acetylcysteine, NAC
FormulaC5H9NO3S
RoutesOral, intravenous, inhaled
UsesParacetamol overdose; mucolytic

Acetylcysteine, also known as N-acetylcysteine (NAC), is a medication used to treat paracetamol (acetaminophen) overdose and to loosen thick mucus in conditions such as cystic fibrosis and chronic bronchitis. It is the acetylated form of the amino acid cysteine and is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Medical uses

In paracetamol poisoning, acetylcysteine acts as an antidote by replenishing hepatic glutathione, which detoxifies the toxic metabolite NAPQI; given within about eight hours of ingestion it greatly reduces the risk of liver failure. It is administered orally or by intravenous infusion according to protocols such as the Prescott regimen, guided by blood paracetamol concentrations plotted on a nomogram.

As a mucolytic, inhaled or oral acetylcysteine breaks disulfide bonds in mucus glycoproteins, reducing viscosity. NAC is also sold as a dietary supplement and has been investigated in psychiatry and for contrast-induced kidney injury, with mixed evidence.

Side effects

Common side effects include nausea and vomiting; intravenous use can cause anaphylactoid reactions with flushing and rash, usually managed by pausing the infusion and giving antihistamines.
