A film poster is a poster used to promote and advertise a film primarily to persuade paying customers into a theater to see it. Studios often print several posters varying in size and content for various domestic and international markets. They normally contain an image with text; the text usually includes the film title in large lettering and often the names of the main actors, arranged according to contractual billing.

History

Early film posters of the 1890s–1910s were generic stock images announcing a type of film rather than a specific title. As the star system developed, posters came to feature painted portraits of the stars. The mid-20th century is considered a golden age of poster illustration, with artists such as Saul Bass creating minimalist modernist designs, while later decades saw highly detailed painted art by Drew Struzan (for Star Wars and Indiana Jones) and others. Since the 1990s, photography and digital compositing have largely replaced painted posters.

Types

  • Teaser (advance) posters — released months ahead, often with minimal imagery.
  • Character posters — one for each main character.
  • One-sheets, quads (UK), lobby cards, standees and billboards — formats for theatres and outdoor sites.

Collecting

Vintage posters are widely collected and can command high prices; an original poster for Metropolis (1927) designed by Heinz Schulz-Neudamm is among the most valuable, with one copy selling for over a million dollars.
