High Court of Justiciary

Established1672
JurisdictionScotland
LocationEdinburgh; sits throughout Scotland
HeadLord Justice General

The High Court of Justiciary is the supreme criminal court in Scotland. The High Court is both a trial court — hearing the most serious criminal cases, such as murder and rape, which must be tried on indictment — and a court of appeal for criminal cases. It sits permanently in Edinburgh and on circuit in cities including Glasgow and Aberdeen.

History and composition

The court was established in 1672, succeeding earlier justiciars' courts, and its jurisdiction was preserved by the Acts of Union 1707. Its judges are the same senators of the College of Justice who sit in the civil Court of Session, holding criminal office as Lords Commissioners of Justiciary. The head of the court is the Lord Justice General, assisted by the Lord Justice Clerk.

Procedure and appeals

Trials are by a jury of fifteen, and verdicts may be guilty, not guilty, or not proven — the distinctive third verdict of Scots law. Convictions require a simple majority of the jury. Appeals against conviction or sentence are heard by a bench of at least three judges; uniquely for criminal matters in the United Kingdom, there is no appeal from the High Court to the UK Supreme Court except on devolution and certain compatibility issues, making it the final criminal court for Scotland in most cases.
