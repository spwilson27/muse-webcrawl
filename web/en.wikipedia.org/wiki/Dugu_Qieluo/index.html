Dugu Qieluo (Chinese: 獨孤伽羅; 544 – 10 September 602), formally Empress Wenxian (文獻皇后), was an empress of the Chinese Sui dynasty and the wife of its founder, Emperor Wen (Yang Jian). She was one of the most powerful and influential empresses in Chinese history, participating actively in her husband's governance to the point that the couple were informally known as the "Two Saints" (二聖).

Background

Dugu Qieluo was the seventh daughter of Dugu Xin, a senior general of Western Wei and Northern Zhou of Xianbei origin. In 557, when she was 13, she married Yang Jian, the eldest son of the general Yang Zhong. After her father was forced to commit suicide in a political struggle later that year, Yang Jian swore to her that he would never take concubines, a vow that shaped the famously monogamous character of their marriage.

As empress

When Yang Jian seized the throne from Northern Zhou in 581 and founded the Sui dynasty, he created her empress. The couple worked closely together: Emperor Wen held court discussions with her present, and officials sometimes delivered reports to them jointly. Empress Dugu was known for frugality and diligence, but also for jealousy and severity; her intervention in the removal of Crown Prince Yang Yong in favour of Yang Guang (the future Emperor Yang) had far-reaching consequences for the dynasty. She died in 602 at Renshou Palace and was buried with full honours; Emperor Wen followed her in death two years later.
