Bengal famine of 1770
Date: 1769-1773
Location: Bengal, Bihar

The Bengal famine of 1770 was a catastrophic famine that struck Bengal and Bihar under East India Company rule between 1769 and 1773, killing an estimated 10 million people, roughly one third of the population.

Causes

Failure of the monsoon in 1768-69 caused severe drought and crop failure. Company revenue demands continued to be collected despite the catastrophe, worsening mortality.

Consequences

Large areas were depopulated, agriculture collapsed in districts, and the famine contributed to later scrutiny of Company rule in Britain and reforms in Bengal's administration.
