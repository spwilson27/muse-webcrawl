Omega Point

The Omega Point is a concept of a future state of the universe in which all matter and consciousness converge into a supreme point of complexity and unity. The term was coined by the French Jesuit priest and palaeontologist Pierre Teilhard de Chardin (1881–1955), who described it in works such as The Phenomenon of Man as the endpoint of evolution: as humanity and consciousness (the "noosphere") become ever more complex and unified, they approach a divine state of ultimate convergence with God.

Teilhard de Chardin's concept

Teilhard saw evolution as a directional process moving from the geosphere through the biosphere to the noosphere, the sphere of human thought. The Omega Point lies ahead of this process as its goal and attractor: a maximum state of complexity and consciousness toward which the universe is converging, which Teilhard identified with Christ. His ideas were influential but controversial within the Catholic Church during his lifetime.

Later uses

The physicist Frank J. Tipler later used the term in his "Omega Point theory", a speculative cosmological proposal in which intelligent life in a collapsing universe could achieve effectively infinite information processing as the universe approaches its final singularity. Tipler's version has been widely criticised by physicists as pseudoscientific.
