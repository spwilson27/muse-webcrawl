Shah

Shah (Persian: شاه, Šāh) is a title given to the kings and emperors of Iran (historically Persia) and neighbouring lands influenced by Persian culture. Derived from Old Persian xšāyaθiya, "king," it forms part of grander titles such as Shahanshah ("King of Kings") and appears in derivative forms across South and Central Asia, including padshah and the chess term "check" (from shāh).

History

The word descends from Old Persian xšāyaθiya, the title of Achaemenid rulers. Sasanian emperors styled themselves šāhān šāh ērān ud anērān, "King of Kings of Iran and non-Iran." After the Islamic conquest of Persia, the title continued under dynasties such as the Saffarids, Samanids, and Buyids, and reached its greatest later prominence under the Safavids (1501–1736), Qajars (1789–1925), and Pahlavis (1925–1979). The last shah, Mohammad Reza Pahlavi, was overthrown in the Iranian Revolution of 1979.

Related titles

Padshah ("master king") was used by Ottoman sultans and Mughal emperors; shahzada denotes a prince. In chess, terminology inherited via Persian includes "check" (shāh, a warning that the king is threatened) and "checkmate" (shāh māt, "the king is helpless/dead").

Use beyond Iran

Persianate courts from Anatolia to India adopted shah-based titles, and the word survives as a common surname (Shah) among Persians, South Asians, and others, including in the Ismaili and Gujarati communities.
