The Langhe (Italian pronunciation: [ˈlaŋɡe]; Piedmontese: Langhe) is a hilly area south and east of the river Tanaro in the provinces of Cuneo, Asti and Alessandria in Piedmont, northern Italy. It is celebrated for its wines, cheeses, hazelnuts and truffles — particularly the white truffle of Alba, the main market town of the region. The area is conventionally divided into the Bassa Langa ("low Langhe"), famous for its wines, and the Alta Langa ("high Langhe"), more rugged and devoted historically to hazelnuts and sheep farming.

Wine

The Langhe is one of the world's great wine landscapes. The Nebbiolo grape here produces Barolo and Barbaresco, two of Italy's most prestigious red wines, alongside Dolcetto d'Alba, Barbera d'Alba and the white Arneis. Vineyards blanket the steep marl and sandstone hills around the villages of Barolo, La Morra, Monforte d'Alba, Serralunga d'Alba, Neive and Barbaresco. In 2014 the vineyard landscapes of the Langhe, together with Roero and Monferrato, were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as an outstanding example of the interaction between people and the vine.

Culture and history

The region's hilltop villages, castles and towers reflect its medieval fragmentation among communes and noble families. The Langhe is the literary homeland of writers Cesare Pavese and Beppe Fenoglio, whose works depict its peasant world and the partisan resistance of 1943–45. The city of Alba is also home to the Ferrero confectionery group, maker of Nutella, founded there in 1946 and a major consumer of the local Tonda Gentile hazelnuts.
