Sea Cadets

The Sea Cadets is a national youth charity in the United Kingdom and the country's largest maritime youth organisation. Sponsored by the Royal Navy and organised through the Marine Society & Sea Cadets, it gives young people aged 10 to 18 an environment where they can learn teamwork, leadership, and nautical skills on a Royal Navy ethos.

The origins of the Sea Cadets go back to 1856, when sailors returning from the Crimean War founded orphanages and training establishments for boys; the Navy League formally organised Sea Cadet units from 1899, and the corps was recognised by the Admiralty during the Second World War.

Activities

Cadets (and Junior Sea Cadets aged 10–12) parade at local units, usually called "Training Ships", learning seamanship, sailing, rowing, powerboating, drill, first aid, and engineering. Offshore training takes place aboard the charity's vessels, including the square-rigged brig TS Royalist. Cadets may progress through rates from New Entry to Cadet Petty Officer and gain nationally recognised qualifications.

International Sea Cadets

Similar organisations exist in many countries, including the United States Naval Sea Cadet Corps, the Royal Canadian Sea Cadets, and Sea Cadet corps in Australia, New Zealand, and elsewhere, united through the International Sea Cadet Association.
