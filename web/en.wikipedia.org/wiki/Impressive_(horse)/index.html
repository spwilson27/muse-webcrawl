Impressive, American Quarter Horse stallion

Impressive (April 15, 1969 – March 20, 1995) was an American Quarter Horse stallion who became one of the breed's most influential halter sires. Despite never being shown widely himself after earning his AQHA Champion title and a World Champion halter stallion title in 1974, he sired numerous world champions and reshaped the conformation ideals of the halter horse industry toward heavy muscling.

HYPP controversy

Impressive is best known as the source of hyperkalemic periodic paralysis (HYPP), a genetic muscle disorder traced to a mutation in the sodium channel gene that he passed to his descendants. Because he sired over 2,250 foals and his bloodline spread widely through Quarter Horses, Appaloosas, and Paints, the disease became a major concern. Genetic testing can identify carriers, and the AQHA eventually adopted rules restricting registration of horses homozygous for the mutation.

Legacy

Impressive was inducted into the American Quarter Horse Hall of Fame in 2011. His influence persists both in the muscled phenotype prized in halter competition and in the breed's genetic testing policies.
