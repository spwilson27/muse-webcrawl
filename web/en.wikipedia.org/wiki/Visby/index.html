Visby is an urban area in Sweden and the seat of Gotland Municipality in Gotland County, on the island of Gotland, with about 24,000 inhabitants. Visby is a popular vacation destination for Scandinavians during the summer months, receiving thousands of tourists every year, and is known for its remarkably well-preserved medieval town wall and numerous church ruins.

The old town of Visby was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1995, cited as an outstanding example of a north European medieval walled trading town which preserves with exceptional completeness a townscape and assemblage of high-quality ancient buildings.

History

The earliest history of Visby is uncertain, but it is believed to have been a trading centre from around 900 AD. In the early Middle Ages it was a centre of trade, and in the 12th century the Cathedral of Saint Mary was constructed. In the 13th century, Visby joined the Hanseatic League and prospered as a major Baltic trading port.

In 1361, Valdemar IV of Denmark conquered Gotland, defeating the Gotlandic peasant army at the Battle of Visby outside the town walls. The town subsequently declined as trade shifted, and many of its grand churches were abandoned after the Reformation, leaving the picturesque ruins visible today.

The town wall

The Visby City Wall (Ringmuren) is a medieval defensive wall surrounding the old town. Built mainly in the 13th and 14th centuries, it stretches about 3.4 kilometres and retains 27 of its original ground towers, making it one of the best-preserved medieval city walls in northern Europe.

Events

Visby hosts the annual Medieval Week (Medeltidsveckan) in August, one of Sweden's largest medieval festivals, and Almedalen Week (Almedalsveckan), the country's foremost political forum, held each summer.
