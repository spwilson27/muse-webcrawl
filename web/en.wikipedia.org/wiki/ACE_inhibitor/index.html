ACE inhibitor

An ACE inhibitor blocks the conversion of angiotensin I to angiotensin II.
Drug classAngiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors
UseHypertension, heart failure, diabetic nephropathy
ExamplesLisinopril, enalapril, ramipril, captopril
MechanismInhibition of ACE; reduced angiotensin II and aldosterone

Angiotensin-converting-enzyme inhibitors (ACE inhibitors) are a class of medication used primarily for the treatment of high blood pressure and heart failure. They work by causing relaxation of blood vessels as well as a decrease in blood volume, which leads to lower blood pressure and decreased oxygen demand from the heart.

ACE inhibitors inhibit the angiotensin-converting enzyme, a component of the renin–angiotensin system (RAS), which converts angiotensin I to angiotensin II — a potent vasoconstrictor — and also degrades bradykinin, a vasodilator. The first ACE inhibitor, captopril, was derived from peptides found in the venom of the Brazilian pit viper Bothrops jararaca and was approved for medical use in 1980.

Medical uses

ACE inhibitors are widely prescribed for hypertension, symptomatic heart failure, asymptomatic left ventricular dysfunction, and prevention of diabetic kidney disease. Commonly prescribed members of the class include lisinopril, enalapril, ramipril, perindopril and benazepril. They are often combined with diuretics or calcium channel blockers.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include a persistent dry cough (attributed to increased bradykinin levels), dizziness, headache, and elevated blood potassium (hyperkalemia). A rare but serious adverse effect is angioedema, a rapid swelling of the deeper layers of the skin and mucous membranes. ACE inhibitors are contraindicated in pregnancy because of fetotoxicity, and they must be used cautiously in patients with renal artery stenosis.

History

In the 1950s and 1960s, researchers studying the venom of the Brazilian arrowhead viper found peptides that potentiated bradykinin and inhibited ACE. Work by Sérgio Ferreira, John Vane, David Cushman and Miguel Ondetti led to captopril, the first orally active ACE inhibitor, developed at Squibb and launched in 1980.
