Queen Noor of Jordan (born Lisa Najeeb Halaby; 23 August 1951) is the American-born fourth wife and widow of King Hussein of Jordan, and was queen consort of Jordan from their marriage in 1978 until the King's death in 1999. She is known in Jordan as Noor Al-Hussein ("Light of Hussein"). Since 1999 she has held the style of Queen Noor as queen dowager.

Early life and education

Halaby was born in Washington, D.C., into a family of Syrian-Lebanese and Swedish descent. Her father, Najeeb Halaby, was a naval aviator, airline executive, and head of the Federal Aviation Administration. She was educated at schools in New York and Washington and graduated from Princeton University in 1974 with a degree in architecture and urban planning, having belonged to Princeton's first coeducational freshman class.

Career before marriage

After Princeton she worked in Australia and Iran on urban planning projects and joined the design team for the new international airport in Tehran. She later worked for Royal Jordanian Airlines in Amman, where she first became acquainted with King Hussein.

Marriage and role as queen

She married King Hussein on 15 June 1978, converting to Islam and taking the name Noor. The couple had four children: Hamzah (born 1980), Hashim (1981), Iman (1983) and Raiyah (1986). As queen she founded institutions including the Noor Al Hussein Foundation (1985), the Jubilee School for gifted students, and the Jerash Festival of Culture and Arts, and she promoted education, women's roles in development, and cultural heritage preservation.

Later life and advocacy

After King Hussein's death in 1999, Noor remained active internationally. She is a founding member of Global Zero, a movement for the elimination of nuclear weapons, and has served on the boards of conservation and humanitarian organisations, including as president of United World Colleges (1995–2000). Her memoir, Leap of Faith: Memoirs of an Unexpected Life (2003), became a bestseller.
