2G

2G (short for second generation) is a family of digital cellular network technologies that succeeded the analog 1G systems. The first commercial 2G network, based on the GSM standard, was launched by Radiolinja in Finland in 1991. Unlike analog 1G, 2G encrypted conversations digitally, used spectrum far more efficiently, and introduced data services — most famously SMS text messaging.

Standards

The dominant 2G standards were GSM (used by most of the world), cdmaOne (IS-95, common in North America and parts of Asia), the Japanese PDC, and iDEN. Packet-data upgrades GPRS ("2.5G") and EDGE ("2.75G") raised data rates from the original 9.6 kbit/s circuit-switched service to a few hundred kbit/s in practice.

Sunset

As carriers refarmed spectrum for 4G LTE and 5G, 2G networks were shut down in many countries beginning in the late 2010s — AT&T closed its US 2G GSM network in 2017, and many European and Asian operators followed in the early 2020s. Legacy 2G still supports machine-to-machine devices such as smart meters and alarms in some markets.
