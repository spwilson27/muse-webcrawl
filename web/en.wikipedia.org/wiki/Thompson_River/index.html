The Thompson River is the largest tributary of the Fraser River in British Columbia, Canada, flowing through the dry interior plateau country of the province's south-central region. It is formed at Kamloops by the confluence of the North Thompson and South Thompson rivers, and flows westward to join the Fraser at Lytton.

Geography

The Thompson drains a large basin of lakes, grasslands and forested mountains. The main stem below Kamloops passes through a deep, arid canyon famous for whitewater rafting, and its valley carries both national railways and the Trans-Canada Highway through the Interior.

Ecology and history

The river supports important runs of Pacific salmon, including sockeye, chinook and steelhead trout. It is named for the fur trader and explorer David Thompson, who travelled the region in the early nineteenth century; the valley is the traditional territory of the Secwepemc and Nlaka'pamux peoples.
