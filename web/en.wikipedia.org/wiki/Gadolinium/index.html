Gadolinium is a chemical element with symbol Gd and atomic number 64. It is a silvery-white, malleable, ductile rare-earth metal belonging to the lanthanide series, named after the mineral gadolinite and ultimately after the Finnish chemist Johan Gadolin.

Discovery

Gadolinium was detected spectroscopically by Jean Charles Galissard de Marignac in 1880 in the mineral samarskite, and its oxide was isolated by Paul-Émile Lecoq de Boisbaudran in 1886.

Properties and uses

Gadolinium is ferromagnetic at temperatures slightly below room temperature, with a Curie point near 20 °C, and has an exceptionally high neutron capture cross-section. Its compounds are used in magnetic resonance imaging contrast agents, phosphors, magnetocaloric refrigeration research, and neutron shielding and control applications in nuclear reactors.
