Stunning

Stunning is a process in animal slaughter in which an animal is rendered unconscious and insensible to pain before it is killed, usually by bleeding. Rendering an animal unconscious prior to slaughter was mandated in much of Europe in the twentieth century on animal welfare grounds: a properly stunned animal feels no pain during exsanguination and does not experience the distress of restraint during killing. Stunning is required by law before slaughter in the European Union, the United Kingdom, and many other countries, subject to exemptions for religious slaughter such as halal and shechita.

Methods

Mechanical

The captive bolt pistol fires a retractable steel bolt into the animal's forehead, causing concussion and brain damage; penetrative devices are used for cattle, while non-penetrative (mushroom-head) devices are used for calves and small animals. Free-bullet firearms and percussive blows are also used in some settings. Mechanical stunning of cattle destroys brain tissue, and the carcass is subsequently checked for specified risk materials.

Electrical

Electrical stunning passes a current through the brain sufficient to induce a generalised epileptic seizure (measured as a minimum current, e.g. for pigs and poultry). Poultry are commonly stunned in an electrified water bath into which their shackled heads are immersed; head-only and head-to-body systems are used for pigs and sheep, the latter also causing cardiac arrest. Correct current and duration are critical — too little paralyses without unconsciousness.

Gas

Controlled-atmosphere stunning exposes animals, mainly pigs and poultry, to high concentrations of carbon dioxide or inert gases such as argon and nitrogen, producing loss of consciousness through hypercapnia or anoxia. CO2 is aversive at high concentrations, and gas mixtures are an active area of welfare research.

Assessment

Effective stunning is monitored by checking the absence of corneal and palpebral reflexes, rhythmic breathing, vocalisation and righting reflexes. Animals showing signs of consciousness must be re-stunned immediately. Meat quality is also affected: poor stunning contributes to defects such as blood splash and, in pigs, pale soft exudative (PSE) meat.

Religious slaughter

Jewish shechita and some interpretations of Islamic halal require the animal to be uninjured at the moment of the throat cut, leading to exemptions from pre-stunning requirements in various jurisdictions — a recurring subject of legal and ethical debate. Some halal authorities accept reversible (recoverable) electrical stunning, and the majority of halal meat in countries such as the UK is in fact pre-stunned.
