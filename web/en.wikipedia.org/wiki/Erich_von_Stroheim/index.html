Erich Oswald Hans Carl Maria von Stroheim (born Erich Oswald Stroheim; 22 September 1885 – 12 May 1957) was an Austrian-American film director and actor, known for his uncompromisingly detailed and naturalistic silent films and for his screen persona as "the man you love to hate". His masterpiece Greed (1924) is frequently cited among the greatest films ever made, despite having been drastically cut by the studio against his wishes.

Early life

Born in Vienna to a middle-class Jewish family — his father a hat maker — Stroheim emigrated to the United States in 1909. In Hollywood he reinvented himself as an aristocratic Austrian officer, an identity he cultivated for the rest of his life; much of his claimed military and noble biography was fabricated.

Directing career

After working as an actor and assistant, including on D. W. Griffith's Intolerance, Stroheim directed Blind Husbands (1919) and Foolish Wives (1922), pioneering a cynical, meticulously realistic style and enormous production detail. Greed, adapted from Frank Norris's novel McTeague, was cut from roughly nine hours to under two and a half by MGM; the excised footage is lost. Studio conflicts over length and budget, culminating in the troubled Queen Kelly (1929), effectively ended his American directing career.

Acting career

Stroheim continued as a character actor, notably in Jean Renoir's La Grande Illusion (1937) as Captain von Rauffenstein, and in Billy Wilder's Sunset Boulevard (1950) as Max von Mayerling, the former director turned butler — a performance that earned him an Academy Award nomination.
