2014 Scottish independence referendum

A referendum on Scottish independence from the United Kingdom was held in Scotland on 18 September 2014. The referendum question was, "Should Scotland be an independent country?", which voters answered with "Yes" or "No". The "No" side won with 55.3% of the vote.

Background

The Scottish National Party (SNP), which had formed a minority government in 2007, won an outright majority in the 2011 Scottish Parliament election, giving it a mandate to hold a referendum. The Edinburgh Agreement of October 2012 between the UK and Scottish governments settled the legal basis, the timing and the single-question format. The franchise was extended to 16- and 17-year-olds, a first for a major UK vote, and to resident EU and Commonwealth citizens.

Campaign

The Yes Scotland campaign, backed by the SNP, Scottish Greens and others, argued Scotland could be a fairer, wealthier independent state using North Sea oil revenues and keeping the pound in a currency union. The Better Together campaign, backed by Labour, the Conservatives and the Liberal Democrats, warned of risks over currency, EU membership, pensions and public spending. In the final week, after polls narrowed sharply, the three main UK party leaders issued "The Vow" promising extensive new devolved powers.

Result and aftermath

Turnout of 84.6% was the highest for any UK-wide or Scottish election or referendum since the introduction of universal suffrage; only four of 32 council areas voted Yes. First Minister Alex Salmond announced his resignation the day after the vote, succeeded by Nicola Sturgeon. The Smith Commission and the Scotland Act 2016 delivered additional devolved powers, while the 2016 Brexit referendum — in which Scotland voted 62% to Remain — renewed the independence debate.
