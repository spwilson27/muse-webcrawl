Raiffeisenbank is a common name for cooperative banks in German-speaking and several other European countries. The name derives from Friedrich Wilhelm Raiffeisen (1818–1888), a German mayor and pioneer of rural credit cooperatives, who founded the first such lending associations in the 1860s to free farmers from dependence on moneylenders.

Raiffeisen banks are owned by their members, who are typically also their customers, and operate on the cooperative principles of self-help, self-governance, and self-responsibility. Local banks are usually linked in regional associations and central institutions that provide liquidity, payments, and other services.

By country

Germany

In Germany, local cooperative banks often carry the name Volksbank or Raiffeisenbank (the latter historically serving rural areas). They belong to the National Association of German Cooperative Banks (BVR) and together form one of the pillars of the German banking system alongside public savings banks and private commercial banks.

Austria

The Austrian Raiffeisen Banking Group is one of the country's largest banking groups, organized in three tiers: local Raiffeisen banks, regional Raiffeisenlandesbanks, and Raiffeisen Bank International, which is also active across Central and Eastern Europe.

Switzerland

Raiffeisen Switzerland is a cooperative group of hundreds of local banks and one of the country's largest retail banking groups. In the Netherlands, the former Raiffeisen-Boerenleenbank merged in 1972 to form Rabobank.
