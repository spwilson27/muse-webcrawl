How the Grinch Stole Christmas (also known as Dr. Seuss' How the Grinch Stole Christmas or simply The Grinch) is a 2000 American Christmas fantasy comedy film directed by Ron Howard, who also produced it with Brian Grazer, from a screenplay by Jeffrey Price and Peter S. Seaman. Based on Dr. Seuss's 1957 children's book of the same name, it was the first Dr. Seuss book to be adapted into a full-length feature film. The film stars Jim Carrey in the title role, with Taylor Momsen, Jeffrey Tambor, Christine Baranski, Bill Irwin, and Molly Shannon in supporting roles, and Anthony Hopkins as narrator.

Plot

The film expands the book's story: Whoville lies inside a snowflake, and the outcast Grinch, who lives on Mount Crumpit with his dog Max, despises Christmas because of childhood humiliation at school over his appearance and a shaving mishap, inflicted through the cruelty of classmate Augustus May Who, now the mayor. After the young Cindy Lou Who invites him to be Holiday Cheermeister, the Grinch is again publicly humiliated by the mayor and resolves on revenge: disguised as Santa Claus, he steals the Whos' presents and decorations. When the town nevertheless celebrates, the Grinch realizes Christmas is about more than possessions, saves the runaway sleigh, returns the gifts, and is welcomed to the feast.

Reception

The film was released on November 17, 2000, and received mixed reviews from critics, who praised Carrey's performance and the visual design but criticized the darker, expanded storyline. It was nevertheless a major commercial success, spending four weeks as the number-one film in the United States and grossing $345 million worldwide, becoming one of the highest-grossing Christmas films of all time. The film won the Academy Award for Best Makeup, with Rick Baker's prosthetic make-up for Carrey requiring hours of daily application.
