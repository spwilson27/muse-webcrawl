Grande Armée

Active1804–1815
CountryFrench Empire
SizeUp to c. 685,000 (1812)

The Grande Armée ("Great Army") was the main military force commanded by Napoleon Bonaparte during the Napoleonic Wars. Originally named the Army of the Coasts of the Ocean and assembled at Boulogne for a planned invasion of England, it received the name Grande Armée in 1805 when Napoleon turned it east against Austria and Russia.

Organisation

Napoleon organised the army into largely self-contained corps d'armée, each commanded by a Marshal of the Empire and combining infantry, cavalry, artillery, and support services, able to fight alone for a day until reinforced. This corps system, together with mass conscription, living off the land, and rapid marching, allowed the Grande Armée to outmanoeuvre the more rigid armies of its opponents. Its elite formations included the Imperial Guard, divided into Old, Middle, and Young Guard, and heavy cavalry such as the cuirassiers, supported by horse artillery.

Campaigns

The Grande Armée won its most celebrated victories in the War of the Third Coalition — culminating at Austerlitz on 2 December 1805 — and destroyed the Prussian army at Jena–Auerstedt in 1806. For the invasion of Russia in 1812 it numbered roughly 685,000 men, the largest army yet assembled in Europe, drawn from France and its allies and client states across the continent. The Russian campaign destroyed it: disease, starvation, battle, and the winter retreat from Moscow reduced the main body to a remnant. Rebuilt forces fought on in 1813–1814 and again during the Hundred Days of 1815, before final defeat at Waterloo.

Legacy

The Grande Armée is remembered for its meritocratic promotion, its regimental eagles modelled on Roman standards, and its profound influence on military organisation; the corps system it pioneered remains the basis of modern army structure.
