The Westland WS-61 Sea King is a British licence-built version of the American Sikorsky SH-3 Sea King helicopter, built by Westland Helicopters at Yeovil. The aircraft differs considerably from the American version, with Rolls-Royce Gnome engines, British avionics and anti-submarine warfare equipment, and a fully computerised flight control system. The Sea King served the Royal Navy in anti-submarine and airborne early warning roles and the Royal Air Force for decades as its principal search and rescue helicopter.

Development

Westland had a long history of licence production of Sikorsky designs, and in the 1960s the Royal Navy selected the Sea King to replace the Westland Wessex in the anti-submarine role. The first Westland-built Sea King flew on 7 May 1969, and the HAS.1 entered service with the Fleet Air Arm in 1970. Successive marks added improved radar, ESM and weapons; the type carried torpedoes, depth charges and could deliver nuclear depth bombs in the Cold War years.

Roles

Anti-submarine warfare was the primary Fleet Air Arm role, flown from carriers and large warships. Search and rescue variants (RAF HAR.3/3A and RN HAR.5) in distinctive yellow or grey/red schemes saved thousands of lives around the UK coast before the role was privatised in 2015. After the loss of HMS Sheffield in the Falklands War exposed the lack of airborne early warning, the Sea King was hurriedly converted to the AEW role with a Thorn-EMI Searchwater radar in an inflatable radome — the "bagger" — leading to the ASaC.7, which served until 2018. A troop-carrying variant, the Commando (HC.4, the "Junglie"), supported the Royal Marines.

Exports and retirement

Westland Sea Kings and Commandos were exported to Australia, India, Egypt, Germany, Norway, Pakistan, Qatar and others; several remain in service abroad. The type saw combat in the Falklands, the Gulf, the Balkans, Sierra Leone and Afghanistan. The last UK military Sea Kings were retired in 2018, ending nearly five decades of British service.
