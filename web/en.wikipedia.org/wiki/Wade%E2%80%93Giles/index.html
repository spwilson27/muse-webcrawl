Wade–Giles

Wade–Giles (simplified Chinese: 威妥玛拼音; traditional Chinese: 威妥瑪拼音) is a romanization system for Mandarin Chinese. It developed from a system produced by Thomas Francis Wade during the mid-19th century, and was given completed form with Herbert A. Giles's Chinese–English Dictionary of 1892.

History

Wade–Giles was the mainstream system in the English-speaking world for most of the 20th century. It was used in standard reference books and in English-language books published before 1979 in much of the world. It has since been largely supplanted by Hanyu Pinyin, developed in mainland China and adopted internationally. Its legacy survives in spellings such as "Peking", "Taoism", "kung fu", and "tai chi".

Features

The system distinguishes aspirated and unaspirated consonants using apostrophes (e.g. p vs p'), a feature often dropped in popular usage, which causes ambiguity. It romanizes the syllable written jī in pinyin as chi.
