The Federally Administered Tribal Areas (FATA; Urdu: وفاق کے زیرِ انتظام قبائلی علاقہ جات) were a semi-autonomous tribal region in north-western Pakistan, bordering Afghanistan's provinces along the Durand Line. Administered directly by Pakistan's federal government under arrangements inherited from British India, FATA consisted of seven tribal agencies — Bajaur, Mohmand, Khyber, Orakzai, Kurram, North Waziristan and South Waziristan — and six frontier regions adjoining the settled districts. On 31 May 2018, following the 25th Amendment to the Constitution of Pakistan, FATA was merged into the province of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, ending its separate status.

Governance

For over a century FATA was governed not by Pakistan's ordinary laws but by the Frontier Crimes Regulation (FCR) of 1901, under which a federal Political Agent exercised executive and judicial powers, working through councils of tribal elders (jirgas) and the principle of collective territorial responsibility. Residents were largely outside the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court and high courts, and political parties were long barred from operating in the region — reforms in 2011 first extended the Political Parties Act to FATA. The area's Pashtun population governed daily life substantially by the customary code of Pashtunwali.

History

The tribal belt was a buffer between British India and Afghanistan, never fully subdued by the British, and it retained a special status at Pakistan's independence in 1947. After 2001 the region became a central theatre of the war in Afghanistan's spillover: Al-Qaeda and Taliban fighters, including the Pakistani Taliban (TTP), established strongholds there, and the Pakistani military mounted a series of major operations, notably in Swat (2009), South Waziristan (2009) and North Waziristan (Operation Zarb-e-Azb, 2014), displacing millions of residents at their peak. The area was also the focus of the US drone campaign in Pakistan.

Merger with Khyber Pakhtunkhwa

Long-standing demands to mainstream the region culminated in the FATA Reforms and the 25th Constitutional Amendment of May 2018, signed by President Mamnoon Hussain on 31 May 2018. The merger extended Pakistan's courts, police and provincial assembly representation to the tribal districts and abolished the FCR, though implementation of policing, land records and development funding has remained a work in progress.
