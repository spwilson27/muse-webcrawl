Ordesa y Monte Perdido National Park

Ordesa y Monte Perdido National Park
Location: Huesca province, Aragon, Spain
Area: 156 km²
Established: 16 August 1918
Status: UNESCO World Heritage Site (as part of Pyrénées – Mont Perdu, 1997)

Ordesa y Monte Perdido National Park (Parque Nacional de Ordesa y Monte Perdido) is a national park in the Spanish Pyrenees, in the province of Huesca, Aragon. Created in 1918 — the second national park in Spain — it protects some of the most dramatic mountain scenery in Europe, centred on Monte Perdido (3,355 m), the highest limestone massif on the continent.

Landscape

The park is structured around four deep valleys carved by glacial and fluvial erosion: the Ordesa Valley, an immense glacial canyon with walls up to 800 m high; the Añisclo Canyon; the Escuaín Gorges; and the Pineta Valley. Waterfalls such as the Cola de Caballo ("Horse's Tail") and the Gradas de Soaso are among its best-known features.

Flora and fauna

Altitude ranges from about 700 m to over 3,300 m, giving a succession of habitats from riverside forest to alpine meadows and rock desert. Wildlife includes the Pyrenean chamois (sarrio), marmots, lammergeiers (bearded vultures), golden eagles, and wild boar; the last Pyrenean ibex (bucardo) died in the park in 2000.

Protection

The park forms the Spanish part of the transboundary UNESCO World Heritage Site Pyrénées – Mont Perdu, shared with France's Pyrenees National Park, recognised for both its natural beauty and its pastoral cultural landscape.
