The Tower of the Juche Idea, commonly known as the Juche Tower, is a monument in Pyongyang, North Korea. Completed in 1982 to commemorate the 70th birthday of Kim Il-sung, it stands on the east bank of the Taedong River, directly opposite Kim Il-sung Square. At 170 metres (560 ft) tall, it is among the tallest monumental columns in the world, deliberately slightly taller than the Washington Monument.

Design

The tower is built of 25,550 blocks of white stone — one for each day of Kim Il-sung's life up to his 70th birthday — and is topped by a 20-metre illuminated metal flame representing the flame of the Juche ideology. It is clad in stone and has 70 floors in its internal structure, with a lift to a viewing platform beneath the flame.

At its base stands a group of three statues, each about 10 metres high, depicting a worker, a peasant and an intellectual holding a hammer, sickle and writing brush — the emblem of the Workers' Party of Korea. The base also contains a wall of hundreds of commemorative plaques sent by foreign Juche study groups and admirers, and six smaller sculptural groups stand nearby on the plaza.

Symbolism

The monument embodies Juche, the official state ideology of self-reliance developed by Kim Il-sung. Along with the Arch of Triumph and the Monument to Party Founding, it is one of the signature landmarks of central Pyongyang and a standard stop on official tours.
