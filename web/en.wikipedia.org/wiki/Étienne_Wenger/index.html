Étienne Charles Wenger (born 1952) is a Swiss educational theorist and practitioner, best known for his work with anthropologist Jean Lave on situated learning and for developing the concept of communities of practice.

Career

Wenger studied computer science at the University of Geneva and earned his PhD in artificial intelligence at the University of California, Irvine, in 1990. At the Institute for Research on Learning in Palo Alto, he collaborated with Jean Lave, and their 1991 book Situated Learning: Legitimate Peripheral Participation reframed learning as increasing participation in social practice rather than the internalisation of abstract knowledge.

Communities of practice

In his solo book Communities of Practice: Learning, Meaning, and Identity (1998), Wenger developed a full social theory of learning, defining a community of practice by mutual engagement, a joint enterprise, and a shared repertoire. Later works, including Cultivating Communities of Practice (2002, with Richard McDermott and William Snyder) and Digital Habitats (2009, with Nancy White and John D. Smith), applied the framework to organisations, knowledge management, and online technology. His more recent work, including Learning in Landscapes of Practice (2015), addresses how learning spans multiple communities and regimes of competence. Wenger works as an independent researcher and consultant based in California.
