Torricelli

Evangelista Torricelli (15 October 1608 – 25 October 1647) was an Italian physicist and mathematician, best known for inventing the barometer and for his work on the geometry of indivisibles and the motion of projectiles and fluids.

Life

Born in Rome (then in the Papal States), Torricelli studied under Benedetto Castelli and became Galileo Galilei's secretary and companion during the last months of Galileo's life. On Galileo's death in 1642 he succeeded him as court mathematician and philosopher to Ferdinando II, Grand Duke of Tuscany, in Florence. He died there of typhoid fever in 1647, aged 39.

Work

In 1643–44 Torricelli and Vincenzo Viviani performed the famous experiment of filling a glass tube with mercury and inverting it in a dish; the column stood at about 76 cm, leaving an empty space above — the "Torricellian vacuum" — and Torricelli correctly explained the height as balanced by the pressure of the air, thereby inventing the mercury barometer and founding the science of atmospheric pressure. His Opera geometrica (1644) developed Cavalieri's method of indivisibles and described "Gabriel's horn" (Torricelli's trumpet), a solid of finite volume but infinite surface area. Torricelli's law states that the speed of fluid efflux from an orifice equals the speed a body would gain falling from the surface of the fluid to the orifice. The torr, a unit of pressure, is named after him.
