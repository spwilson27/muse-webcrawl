Hyperplane

In geometry, a hyperplane is a subspace whose dimension is one less than that of its ambient space. If the ambient space is three-dimensional, a hyperplane is an ordinary plane; in two dimensions it is a line, and in one dimension it is a point. In an n-dimensional space, a hyperplane divides the space into two half-spaces and is itself an (n − 1)-dimensional affine subspace.

Definition

In a vector space, a hyperplane is a translation of a linear subspace of codimension one. In coordinates it is the set of points satisfying a single nonzero linear equation. The coefficients define a normal vector perpendicular to the hyperplane.

Properties

A hyperplane through the origin is a linear subspace; other hyperplanes are affine. Given two points on opposite sides, the segment joining them crosses the hyperplane. Hyperplanes play a central role in linear algebra, optimization and machine learning: in a support vector machine, the decision boundary is a hyperplane chosen to maximize the margin between classes.
