Loch Leven is a fresh water loch in Perth and Kinross, Scotland, lying immediately to the east of the town of Kinross. Roughly triangular in shape, it covers about 13.3 square kilometres and is the largest lowland loch in Scotland. It should not be confused with the sea loch of the same name on the west coast near Glencoe.

Islands and history

The loch contains several islands, the most famous of which is Castle Island, home to Lochleven Castle. Mary, Queen of Scots was imprisoned in the castle from June 1567 after her defeat at Carberry Hill; she escaped in May 1568 with the help of the Douglas family, who held the castle, and fled to England shortly afterwards. Other islands include St Serf's Island, the largest, which holds the remains of a Culdee monastery and priory, and Reed Bower, Alice's Bower and Scart Island.

Nature and recreation

Loch Leven is internationally important for its wintering wildfowl, including large flocks of pink-footed geese, and is designated a Ramsar site, a Site of Special Scientific Interest and a national nature reserve managed with an RSPB reserve at Vane Farm on the southern shore. The loch was historically renowned for its brown trout fishery, and Loch Leven trout were transplanted to waters around the world. A heritage trail of about 21 km now circles the shoreline.
