List of crossings of the River Thames

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The River Thames flows 215 miles (346 km) from Thames Head in Gloucestershire to the North Sea. More than 200 bridges, tunnels and other crossings span it, from the small footbridges near its source, through the weirs and locks of its navigable reaches and the famous bridges of London, to the Queen Elizabeth II Bridge at the Dartford Crossing near its estuary. This list proceeds downstream from source to sea, naming selected major crossings.

Upper Thames (source to Oxford)

  • Thames Head Bridge — near the source of the river.
  • Cricklade bridges — including the Town Bridge on the old Ermin Way.
  • Lechlade — Halfpenny Bridge (1792) and St John's Bridge.
  • Radcot Bridge — site of a battle in 1387; among the oldest surviving bridges on the river.
  • Newbridge — a 13th-century bridge, among the oldest on the Thames.
  • Swinford Toll Bridge — a Georgian toll bridge (1769) still charging 5p.

Oxford to Reading

  • Osney Bridge, Oxford
  • Folly Bridge, Oxford
  • Gasworks Bridge, Oxford
  • Donnington Bridge, Oxford
  • Abingdon Bridge
  • Clifton Hampden Bridge
  • Wallingford Bridge
  • Goring and Streatley Bridge
  • Whitchurch Bridge — a privately owned toll bridge.
  • Reading Bridge and Caversham Bridge
  • Sonning Bridge

Reading to Windsor

  • Henley Bridge (1786) — at Henley-on-Thames, home of the Royal Regatta.
  • Marlow Bridge — suspension bridge by William Tierney Clark (1832).
  • Maidenhead Railway Bridge — Brunel's brick arch bridge (1839), once the widest brick span in the world.
  • Maidenhead Bridge (1777)
  • Windsor Bridge and Windsor Railway Bridge (Brunel, 1849)
  • Albert Bridge and Datchet Bridge
  • Staines Bridge and Staines Railway Bridge

Tunnels and crossings east of Tower Bridge

  • Thames Tunnel (1843) — Marc Brunel's tunnel between Wapping and Rotherhithe, the first tunnel under a navigable river; now part of the London Overground.
  • Tower Subway (1870) — now carries water mains.
  • Rotherhithe Tunnel (1908) — road tunnel.
  • Greenwich foot tunnel (1902) and Woolwich foot tunnel (1912).
  • Blackwall Tunnel (1897 / 1967) — road tunnels under the river at Poplar.
  • Jubilee line and Docklands Light Railway tunnels.
  • Woolwich Ferry (in operation since the 14th century; current service from 1889) — the last vehicle ferry in London.
  • Dartford Crossing — the Queen Elizabeth II Bridge (1991) southbound and the Dartford Tunnel (1963/1980) northbound; the easternmost permanent crossing.
