Denis Theodore Goldberg (11 April 1933 – 29 April 2020) was a South African social campaigner, who was active in the struggle against apartheid. He was accused No. 3 in the Rivonia Trial, alongside Nelson Mandela and Walter Sisulu, and was imprisoned for 22 years. He was a member of the South African Communist Party and of the ANC, and served as the technical officer of Umkhonto we Sizwe, the ANC's armed wing.

Early life

Goldberg was born in Cape Town to a working-class Jewish family, the son of Lithuanian Jewish immigrants. He studied civil engineering at the University of Cape Town and became politically active through the Modern Youth Society and the Congress of Democrats, the white ally of the ANC in the Congress Alliance.

Rivonia and imprisonment

As a technical officer of Umkhonto we Sizwe, Goldberg helped establish its first training camp in South Africa and worked on explosives and weapons manufacture. He was arrested in the police raid on Liliesleaf Farm in Rivonia in July 1963 and charged with sabotage and conspiracy to overthrow the government. At the Rivonia Trial of 1963–64 he was sentenced to life imprisonment — four concurrent life sentences. As the only white defendant, he was sent not to Robben Island but to Pretoria Central Prison, where he served 22 years. He escaped briefly in 1979 along with Tim Jenkin and Stephen Lee, though he was not part of that escape; the escape by Jenkin, Lee and Alex Moumbaris was a separate famous incident. Goldberg was released in 1985 on condition that he leave South Africa.

Later life

In exile in London, Goldberg worked for the ANC and the Anti-Apartheid Movement and founded the charity Community HEART to support health and education projects in South Africa. He returned to South Africa in 2002, served as an adviser to the Minister of Water Affairs and Forestry, and spent his later years in Hout Bay, Cape Town, writing, speaking and campaigning, including through the Denis Goldberg House of Hope arts centre for young people. His memoir The Mission: A Life for Freedom in South Africa appeared in 2010. He died of lung cancer in April 2020.
