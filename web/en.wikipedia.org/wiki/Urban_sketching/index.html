Urban sketching is the practice of drawing on location — capturing, from direct observation, the places where the artist lives or travels. Although artists have sketched cities for centuries, urban sketching became a named global movement in the late 2000s with the founding of the Urban Sketchers community by Seattle-based journalist and illustrator Gabriel Campanario, who began a blog in 2007 and a Flickr group in 2008 for correspondents around the world to share location drawings.

Manifesto

The Urban Sketchers manifesto commits members to draw on location, tell the story of their surroundings, record time and place, be truthful to the scenes witnessed, use any medium, support one another, and share drawings online. The ethos is documentary and inclusive: drawings are made in the moment rather than from photographs, and skill level is secondary to observation and narrative.

Organisation and activities

Urban Sketchers is a nonprofit organisation that accredits regional chapters in hundreds of cities; local groups hold regular "sketch crawls" in which members draw together in a neighbourhood, market, station or museum and compare sketchbooks at the end. An annual International Symposium, first held in Portland, Oregon in 2010, has since travelled to cities including Lisbon, Barcelona, Singapore, Manchester, Chicago, Amsterdam and Auckland, with workshops and lectures. Educational programmes and grants support reportage and the teaching of sketching.

Media and style

Typical media are portable: ink pen, graphite, watercolour and sketchbooks. Approaches range from rapid gesture sketches to sustained architectural studies, often annotated with handwritten notes. Practitioners distinguish urban sketching from studio landscape painting by its reportorial immediacy and its roots in travel sketching and visual journalism.
