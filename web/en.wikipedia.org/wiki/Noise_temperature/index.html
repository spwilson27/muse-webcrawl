Noise temperature

Noise temperature is a measure used in radio engineering to express the noise power produced by a component or system in terms of an equivalent temperature. A device with noise power spectral density N is assigned a noise temperature T = N/k, where k is Boltzmann's constant, as if the noise came from a resistor at that temperature.

Applications

Antenna noise temperature summarises the noise an antenna receives from the sky, ground and surroundings: a satellite ground-station antenna pointed at cold sky may have a noise temperature of a few tens of kelvin. Amplifier noise temperature characterises low-noise amplifiers (LNAs) used in radio astronomy and deep-space communications, where cryogenic cooling lowers physical — and thus noise — temperature.

Relation to noise figure

Noise temperature and noise figure are convertible: F = 1 + T/T₀, with T₀ = 290 K the standard reference temperature. Total system noise temperature for cascaded stages follows the Friis formulas, so the first stage dominates system noise.
