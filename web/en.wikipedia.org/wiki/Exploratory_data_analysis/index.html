Exploratory data analysis

In statistics, exploratory data analysis (EDA) is an approach to analyzing data sets to summarize their main characteristics, often using statistical graphics and other data visualization methods. EDA was promoted by the American mathematician John Tukey, notably in his 1977 book Exploratory Data Analysis, to encourage statisticians to explore data and formulate hypotheses that could be tested on new data.

Philosophy

Tukey contrasted EDA with confirmatory data analysis (CDA), likening EDA to detective work — searching for patterns, anomalies, and structure — and CDA to judicial work, evaluating evidence for pre-specified hypotheses. A statistical model can be used or not, but EDA primarily aims to see what the data can tell us beyond formal modelling.

Techniques

Characteristic tools include the five-number summary, box plots, histograms, stem-and-leaf displays, scatter plots, and robust statistics such as medians and trimmed means, which resist the influence of outliers. Tukey also invented or popularized re-expression (power transformations), residuals analysis, and trimeans. Modern EDA is often performed interactively in environments such as R and Python, and forms the first phase of data science and machine learning projects.
