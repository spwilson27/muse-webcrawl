Rail transport in South Africa is the most developed on the African continent. The network, mostly built to the 1,067 mm "Cape gauge", connects the country's ports with the industrial heartland of Gauteng and links neighbouring countries including Botswana, Lesotho, Mozambique, Namibia, Eswatini, and Zimbabwe.

History

The first railways were built in the 1860s in Natal and the Cape Colony. The discovery of diamonds at Kimberley and gold on the Witwatersrand drove rapid expansion, and Cecil Rhodes's Cape-to-Cairo ambitions pushed lines far into the interior. After Union in 1910 the system was consolidated as South African Railways and Harbours. Steam gave way to extensive electrification (3 kV DC and 25 kV and 50 kV AC sections) and diesel operation in the later twentieth century.

Freight

Transnet Freight Rail operates heavy-haul export corridors of world significance: the Sishen–Saldanha iron ore line (Orex), whose trains are among the longest and heaviest in the world, and the Mpumalanga–Richards Bay coal line. Bulk exports of coal, iron ore, manganese, and containers dominate traffic. In recent years, cable theft, vandalism, and locomotive shortages caused severe capacity declines, pushing freight onto roads and prompting reforms to allow third-party access to the network.

Passenger services

PRASA operates Metrorail commuter services in the major cities and long-distance Shosholoza Meyl trains, both badly affected by infrastructure theft and underinvestment. The Gautrain, opened in 2010 for the FIFA World Cup, is a modern standard-gauge rapid transit line linking Johannesburg, Pretoria, and OR Tambo International Airport. Luxury tourist trains include the Blue Train and Rovos Rail.
