Mechanical license

A mechanical license is a licence that grants certain limited permissions to work with non-sampled, pre-existing, copyrighted musical compositions, permitting the licensee to reproduce and distribute the composition in audio recordings (traditionally called "mechanical" reproductions, after the player-piano rolls for which the right was first created). It concerns the underlying song — melody and lyrics — as distinct from a particular sound recording of it.

Compulsory licence in the United States

Under Section 115 of the US Copyright Act, once a composition has been recorded and distributed to the public with the copyright owner's authority, anyone may obtain a compulsory mechanical licence to record and distribute their own version (a "cover") by serving notice and paying the statutory royalty rate, without negotiating permission. The licence does not allow changing the basic melody or fundamental character of the work, and it covers audio-only recordings; synchronising music to video requires a separate sync licence.

The Harry Fox Agency, founded in 1927, long served as the principal clearinghouse issuing mechanical licences on behalf of music publishers. The statutory per-track rate for physical and permanent-download sales stood for many years at 9.1 cents before being raised in the Phonorecords IV settlement, with rates phasing upward from 2023.

Streaming and the Music Modernization Act

Interactive streaming services historically obtained mechanical licences composition by composition; the Music Modernization Act of 2018 replaced this with a blanket licence for digital services, administered by the Mechanical Licensing Collective (the MLC), which began operating in 2021 and matches royalties to songwriters and publishers. Outside the United States, mechanical rights are commonly administered by collecting societies such as MCPS in the United Kingdom.
