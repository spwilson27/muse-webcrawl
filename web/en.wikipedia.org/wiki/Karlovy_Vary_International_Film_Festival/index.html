Karlovy Vary International Film Festival

The Karlovy Vary International Film Festival (Czech: Mezinárodní filmový festival Karlovy Vary) is a film festival held annually in July in Karlovy Vary, Czech Republic. It is one of the oldest film festivals in the world and is Central and Eastern Europe's leading film event, recognized by FIAPF in its top "A" competitive category.

History

The festival was founded in 1946 in the spa towns of Karlovy Vary and Mariánské Lázně, moving permanently to Karlovy Vary in 1948. During the communist era the festival alternated years with the Moscow Film Festival and its programming was shaped by the politics of the Eastern Bloc. The Prague Spring of 1968 brought a freer festival, but the ensuing "normalization" period returned the event to state control.

After the Velvet Revolution, the festival was refounded in the 1990s under the leadership of actor Jiří Bartoška, its longtime president, and program director Eva Zaoralová. Since 1994 it has been held every year. The main venue is the Brutalist Hotel Thermal, completed in 1977, whose large cinema hosts the opening and closing ceremonies.

Programme and awards

The festival's Crystal Globe competition presents world and international premieres of feature films. The top prize, the Crystal Globe for Best Feature Film, carries a monetary award. Other sections include Horizons, presenting notable recent titles; the East of the West competition (later Proxima), devoted to Central and Eastern European cinema; and retrospectives. The festival also awards a Crystal Globe for Outstanding Artistic Contribution to World Cinema, whose recipients have included major international directors and actors.

The festival is known for its informal, audience-friendly atmosphere: thousands of young visitors camp in the spa town, and bag-carrying students are a characteristic sight. Attendance regularly exceeds 100,000 admissions.
