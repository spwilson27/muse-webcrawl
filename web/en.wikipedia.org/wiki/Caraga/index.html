Caraga, officially the Caraga Administrative Region (designated as Region XIII), is an administrative region in the Philippines occupying the northeastern section of Mindanao. The region comprises five provinces: Agusan del Norte, Agusan del Sur, Dinagat Islands, Surigao del Norte, and Surigao del Sur, along with six cities including the regional center and highly urbanized city of Butuan.

History

The region's name is popularly said to derive from the word kalag, meaning "spirit" or "soul", in reference to the animistic beliefs of its early inhabitants. Before Spanish colonization, the area was home to the Manobo, Mamanwa, Surigaonon, and Butuanon peoples, and the ancient polities centered on Butuan conducted trade with China and Southeast Asia as early as the 10th century; the Butuan boat (balangay) remains a symbol of early Filipino seafaring.

Caraga was created as a region on February 23, 1995, through Republic Act No. 7901, initially composed of the provinces of Agusan del Norte, Agusan del Sur, and Surigao del Norte; Surigao del Sur was transferred to the region in 2000, and the Dinagat Islands became a separate province in 2006.

Geography

Caraga is bounded by the Bohol Sea to the north, the Philippine Sea and the Pacific Ocean to the east, and the regions of Davao and Northern Mindanao to the south and west. The Diwata Mountains run along the eastern coast, while the Agusan River basin — drained by one of the country's longest rivers — dominates the interior. The region includes Siargao Island, internationally known for surfing at Cloud 9, and the vast Agusan Marsh wildlife sanctuary.

Economy

The economy is based on agriculture (rice, corn, coconut, banana, and oil palm), fishing, forestry, and mining. Caraga is a major source of nickel, iron, gold, and chromite; the Dinagat Islands and Surigao provinces host extensive laterite nickel mining. Timber and wood processing have historically been centered on Butuan, once dubbed the "Timber City of the South". Tourism has grown rapidly around Siargao and the Enchanted River in Hinatuan.

Demographics

The population was about 2.8 million in the 2020 census. Surigaonon, Butuanon, Cebuano, and Manobo languages are spoken across the region, alongside Filipino and English.
