Snohomish County is a county in the U.S. state of Washington, in the northern part of the Seattle metropolitan area. Its county seat and largest city is Everett. With a population of about 828,000 at the 2020 census, it is the third most populous county in Washington. The county takes its name from the Snohomish people, a Lushootseed-speaking Indigenous group of the region.

Geography

The county stretches from the shore of Puget Sound in the west to the crest of the Cascade Range in the east, encompassing lowland valleys along the Snohomish, Stillaguamish, and Skykomish rivers, national forest land, and mountain peaks including Glacier Peak, an active volcano in its northeast. Its communities range from suburbs of Seattle such as Lynnwood, Edmonds, and Bothell, to mill and farming towns such as Snohomish, Monroe, Arlington, and Marysville.

History and economy

Snohomish County was created out of Island County in 1861. Timber and milling drove its early economy, with Everett founded as a planned mill town in the 1890s. In the 20th century, aerospace became dominant: Boeing's Everett factory, opened in 1967 to build the 747, is among the largest buildings in the world by volume and remains a major employer, alongside naval Station Everett, healthcare, and technology employers. Paine Field airport began commercial passenger service in 2019.

Events

On March 22, 2014, a catastrophic landslide near Oso destroyed a neighbourhood and killed 43 people, the deadliest landslide in U.S. history. In 2020, the first confirmed case of COVID-19 in the United States was diagnosed in Snohomish County. The county government operates under a home-rule charter with an elected county executive and county council.
