Magnetic permeability

In electromagnetism, permeability is the measure of magnetization produced in a material in response to an applied magnetic field. It is typically represented by the Greek letter μ (mu). In SI units, permeability is measured in henries per metre (H/m). For a material, permeability relates the magnetic flux density B to the magnetic field strength H through the relation B = μH.

Vacuum permeability and relative permeability

The permeability of free space, μ0, is a physical constant with a value of approximately 4π × 10−7 H/m; since the 2019 revision of the SI it is determined experimentally rather than defined exactly. The ratio of a material's permeability to μ0 is its relative permeability μr, a dimensionless quantity. Diamagnetic materials have μr slightly less than 1, paramagnetic materials slightly more than 1, and ferromagnetic materials such as iron have values in the hundreds or thousands.

Behaviour of materials

In ferromagnetic materials permeability is not constant: it depends on the applied field, rising to a maximum and falling as the material approaches magnetic saturation, and it exhibits hysteresis. Engineers characterize such materials by their B–H curves. High-permeability alloys such as mu-metal are used for magnetic shielding, and permeability is a key design parameter in inductors, transformers, and electric motors.
