George Reid

Sir George Houstoun Reid GCB, GCMG, KC (25 February 1845 – 12 September 1918) was an Australian politician, the fourth Prime Minister of Australia.

Born: Johnstone, Scotland
Died: London, England
Office: Prime Minister of Australia (1904–1905); Premier of New South Wales (1894–1899)

George Reid was an Australian politician who served as the fourth Prime Minister of Australia from 1904 to 1905, having previously been Premier of New South Wales (1894–1899). He led the Free Trade Party and was the last prime minister from that tradition before its merger into the anti-socialist fusion of 1909.

Early life

Reid was born in Johnstone, Renfrewshire, Scotland, and emigrated with his family to Melbourne in 1852, later settling in Sydney. He worked as a clerk in the New South Wales public service, studied law at night, and was called to the bar in 1879. He won fame as a witty, popular orator and as the author of the pamphlet Five Free Trade Essays.

New South Wales premier

Elected to the New South Wales Legislative Assembly in 1880, Reid became premier in 1894. His government reformed land and income taxation and the civil service, and — despite his own doubts about aspects of the scheme — he led New South Wales into the Federation of Australia in 1901 after campaigning against the first draft constitution and then accepting the amended bill. His conditional stance earned him the nickname "Yes-No Reid".

Prime Minister

In the federal parliament Reid succeeded Edmund Barton as leader of the Free Traders. After the fall of the Watson Labor government in August 1904, Reid formed a ministry that passed the landmark Conciliation and Arbitration Act and legislation providing for the federal capital site. He resigned in July 1905 when Labor and the Protectionists combined against him.

Later years

Reid led the opposition until retiring in 1908 to become Australia's first High Commissioner to the United Kingdom in London. In 1916 he won a seat in the British House of Commons and died in London in 1918. The Canberra suburb of Reid and the federal Division of Reid are named after him.
