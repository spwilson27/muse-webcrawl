Penetrance

A pedigree in which some genotype carriers do not express the trait.

In genetics, penetrance is the proportion of individuals carrying a particular variant (allele) of a gene who actually express the associated trait or phenotype. When every carrier expresses the trait, penetrance is complete; when some carriers show no sign of it, penetrance is said to be incomplete or reduced. Penetrance is a population-level measure, expressed as a percentage, and is distinct from expressivity, which describes the degree or severity of expression among those who do show the trait.

Measurement and causes

Penetrance is estimated from pedigrees and population studies. Incomplete penetrance can arise from modifier genes, environmental influences, epigenetic effects, age (many disorders are age-dependent, so penetrance rises over a lifetime), and random developmental variation. For example, pathogenic variants in the BRCA1 gene substantially raise breast and ovarian cancer risk but are not fully penetrant: not every carrier develops cancer.

Examples

Conditions often cited include Huntington's disease, in which full-penetrance alleles with 40 or more CAG repeats almost invariably cause disease in a normal lifespan, while alleles of 36–39 repeats show reduced penetrance; autosomal dominant retinoblastoma (about 90% penetrant); and polydactyly, a classic example of incomplete penetrance in introductory genetics. In plant and animal breeding, penetrance estimates guide predictions of how often a genotype will produce the desired phenotype.
