Nemanjić dynasty

The Nemanjić dynasty (Serbian: Немањићи) was the most important dynasty of medieval Serbia, ruling from 1166 to 1371. Founded by Stefan Nemanja, the dynasty transformed Serbia from a Byzantine dependency into a kingdom (from 1217) and then an empire (from 1346), reaching its height under Stefan Dušan. Eleven of its rulers were canonised as saints by the Serbian Orthodox Church, a church whose autocephaly the dynasty secured under Saint Sava.

Origins and rise

Stefan Nemanja, Grand Župan of Raška, united the Serbian lands and founded the dynasty in 1166; he later abdicated, became a monk (Saint Simeon), and died at Hilandar Monastery on Mount Athos, which he had founded with his son Sava. His son Stefan the First-Crowned received a royal crown in 1217, while Sava obtained autocephaly for the Serbian Church in 1219.

Expansion and empire

Successive kings, notably Stefan Milutin, expanded Serbian territory, developed mining and trade, and endowed great monasteries such as Gračanica and Dečani. Stefan Dušan (reigned 1331–1355) conquered much of the Balkans from the Byzantine Empire, had himself crowned Emperor of the Serbs and Greeks in 1346, and promulgated a famous law code (Dušan's Code, 1349/1354). The empire stretched from the Danube to the Gulf of Corinth.

Decline

Under Dušan's son Stefan Uroš V, "the Weak," imperial authority fragmented among regional lords. Uroš died childless in 1371, ending the dynasty; the same year the Battle of Maritsa opened the way for Ottoman expansion into Serbia.

Legacy

The Nemanjić era is regarded as the golden age of medieval Serbia. Its rulers' endowments — Studenica, Žiča, Sopoćani, Dečani, Gračanica — are among the finest monuments of Byzantine-influenced art, several now UNESCO World Heritage Sites, and the dynasty's saints remain central to Serbian national and religious identity.
