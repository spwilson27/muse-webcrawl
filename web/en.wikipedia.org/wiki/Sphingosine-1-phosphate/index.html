Sphingosine-1-phosphate

FormulaC18H38NO5P
TypeSphingolipid signalling molecule
ReceptorsS1PR1–S1PR5 (G protein-coupled receptors)
SynthesisPhosphorylation of sphingosine by sphingosine kinases (SK1, SK2)

Sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P) is a signalling sphingolipid, also known as a bioactive lipid mediator. It is produced by the phosphorylation of sphingosine, catalysed by the enzymes sphingosine kinase 1 and 2, and degraded by S1P lyase and phosphatases. S1P circulates in blood and lymph at relatively high concentrations, largely bound to albumin and to the lipoprotein HDL, while its concentration in most tissues is kept low — a gradient with major consequences for immune cell trafficking.

Receptors and biology

S1P acts extracellularly on five G protein-coupled receptors, S1PR1 through S1PR5 (formerly called Edg receptors). Through these receptors S1P regulates cell survival, proliferation, migration, and differentiation; vascular development and integrity; angiogenesis; and heart rate. Its best-understood role is in the immune system: lymphocytes use S1PR1 to sense the S1P gradient between lymphoid organs (low) and lymph and blood (high), which guides their egress from the thymus and secondary lymphoid organs into the circulation. Disruption of S1P signalling causes lymphopenia because lymphocytes are sequestered in lymph nodes.

Clinical significance

The immunosuppressant drug fingolimod (FTY720) is a structural analogue of sphingosine; after phosphorylation in vivo it acts as a functional antagonist of S1PR1, trapping lymphocytes in lymph nodes. Fingolimod was approved in 2010 as the first oral therapy for relapsing multiple sclerosis. Later, more selective S1P receptor modulators include siponimod, ozanimod, and ponesimod, used in multiple sclerosis and, for ozanimod, ulcerative colitis. Because S1PR1 is also expressed on atrial myocytes, first doses of these drugs can cause transient bradycardia. S1P and its producing enzymes are also implicated in cancer, where sphingosine kinase 1 is frequently upregulated and promotes tumour growth and resistance to therapy.
