Signet ring

A signet ring is a ring bearing on its flat top surface (the bezel) a design, usually a family crest, coat of arms, monogram, or other emblem, traditionally used as a personal seal. The design is engraved in reverse (intaglio) so that when pressed into hot sealing wax or soft clay it leaves the image in relief. Signet rings have been worn since ancient times and historically served as a mark of authority and authenticity on documents.

History

The use of signet rings dates back to ancient Egypt, where pharaohs and officials used scarab-shaped signets, and to ancient Mesopotamia, where cylinder seals served a similar purpose. In ancient Greece and Rome, signet rings were common personal possessions; Roman citizens used them to seal letters and legal documents, and the ring of a deceased person was sometimes destroyed to prevent forgery.

During the Middle Ages, signet rings became essential instruments of governance. Nobles, bishops, and merchants sealed charters and correspondence with their signets, and a lost or captured signet could compromise its owner's authority. By the early modern period, the engraved metal matrice and the signet ring were standard tools of administration.

Religious use

In the Roman Catholic Church, bishops wear an episcopal ring, traditionally a signet used to seal official documents. The pope wears the Ring of the Fisherman, which depicts Saint Peter fishing and was historically used to seal papal briefs; it is ceremonially defaced after a pope's death or resignation. Ecclesiastical signets were also used by cardinals and abbots.

Modern use

Today signet rings are worn largely as jewellery and as marks of family heritage, school or club affiliation, or personal style. They are traditionally worn on the little finger (pinky) of the non-dominant hand, though customs vary by country and family. Heraldic signets are often engraved with a crest rather than a full coat of arms, and in British tradition the crest is engraved for sealing — that is, in reverse — while in some other traditions it is engraved to be read directly.
