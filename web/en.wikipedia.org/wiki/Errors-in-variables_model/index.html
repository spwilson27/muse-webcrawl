Errors-in-variables model

In statistics, an errors-in-variables model or measurement error model is a regression model that accounts for measurement errors in the independent variables. It contrasts with standard regression models, such as ordinary least squares, which assume the explanatory variables are measured without error.

Attenuation bias

When a regressor is measured with classical (random, uncorrelated) error, ordinary least squares estimates of its coefficient are biased toward zero—a phenomenon known as attenuation bias or regression dilution. In the simple linear case, the OLS estimate converges to the true coefficient multiplied by a reliability ratio λ = σ²x* / (σ²x* + σ²u), which is less than one. Measurement error in the dependent variable alone does not bias coefficients, though it inflates standard errors.

Estimation methods

Because the true regressor is unobserved, errors-in-variables models are generally not identifiable without extra information. Common approaches include instrumental variables estimation, repeated measurements or validation samples, known measurement-error variance adjustments, total least squares (Deming regression), and latent-variable or Bayesian methods.
