Harold Ramis

Harold Allen Ramis
Born: 21 November 1944, Chicago, Illinois
Died: 24 February 2014, Glencoe, Illinois
Occupations: Actor, comedian, director, writer
Known for: Ghostbusters, Groundhog Day, Caddyshack

Harold Allen Ramis (November 21, 1944 – February 24, 2014) was an American actor, comedian, and filmmaker. He was a performer with Chicago's Second City troupe and head writer of SCTV, and became one of the most successful comedy filmmakers of his generation.

Career

Ramis co-wrote National Lampoon's Animal House (1978) and directed Caddyshack (1980) and National Lampoon's Vacation (1983). He played Dr. Egon Spengler in Ghostbusters (1984) and its sequel, which he co-wrote with Dan Aykroyd, and directed the critically acclaimed Groundhog Day (1993) and Analyze This (1999).

Legacy

His films influenced a generation of comedians and directors including Judd Apatow and Adam Sandler. He died in 2014 from complications of autoimmune inflammatory vasculitis.
