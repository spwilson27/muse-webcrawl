Fernando Botero Angulo (19 April 1932 – 15 September 2023) was a Colombian figurative artist and sculptor, regarded as the most recognized and quoted living artist from Latin America during his lifetime. His signature style, dubbed "Boterismo," depicts people and figures in large, exaggerated volume, representing political satire and a keen sense of humour. His works — monumental bronze sculptures and sensuous paintings of inflated still lifes, bullfighters, dancers, and Latin American life — are displayed in public places across the world, from Park Avenue in New York to the Champs-Élysées in Paris.

Life and career

Botero was born in Medellín, the second of three sons of a travelling salesman who died when Botero was four. As a teenager he briefly trained as a matador, but a frightening encounter in the ring ended that ambition; meanwhile he earned money illustrating for the newspaper El Colombiano. He won second prize at the Salón de Artistas Colombianos in Bogotá in 1952, using the money to travel to Europe, where he studied the Old Masters in Madrid and Florence — above all Masaccio, Piero della Francesca, and Rubens — whose monumental, volumetric figures shaped his own art.

The decisive breakthrough came in 1956–57, when Botero painted a mandolin with an absurdly small sound hole and discovered that exaggerating volume created a sense of space, sensuality, and monumentality. After periods in Mexico and New York — where in 1961 the Museum of Modern Art acquired his Mona Lisa, Age Twelve — he settled for much of each year in Paris from 1973, with studios also in Pietrasanta, Italy, where his bronzes were cast, and later in Monaco.

Style and themes

Botero insisted his figures were not "fat" but expanded to give them volume and sensuality: "An artist is attracted to a certain kind of form without knowing why. You adopt a position intuitively; only afterwards do you attempt to rationalize it or even justify it." His subjects include reinterpretations of Old Master paintings, Latin American street scenes, bullfighting — a lifelong passion — and pointed political works: a series on the violence of Colombia's drug wars, and in 2005 a widely exhibited series of paintings and drawings on the Abu Ghraib prison abuse scandal, which he donated to university museums.

Philanthropy and legacy

Botero donated hundreds of his own works and pieces from his personal collection (including paintings by Picasso, Monet, and other European masters) to museums in Bogotá and Medellín, forming the Museo Botero (opened 2000) and enriching the Museum of Antioquia; admission to view them is free, by his stipulation. He died of pneumonia in Monaco on 15 September 2023, aged 91, and was mourned in Colombia with days of national commemoration. His sculptures remain landmarks in Medellín's Botero Plaza and in cities worldwide.
