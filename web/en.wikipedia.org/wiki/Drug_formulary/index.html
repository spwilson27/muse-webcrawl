Drug formulary

A drug formulary, or simply formulary, is a list of prescription drugs — both generic and brand name — that are preferred by a health plan, hospital, or health system and are covered or stocked for use. Historically, formularies were books of recipes and instructions for preparing medicines, such as pharmacopoeias.

Modern use

Modern formularies are maintained by pharmacy and therapeutics committees that evaluate safety, efficacy, and cost-effectiveness. Insurers organise formularies into tiers with different cost-sharing: lower tiers of generic drugs typically carry lower copayments, while specialty drugs occupy higher tiers. Drugs not on the formulary may require prior authorisation or exceptions. National examples include the WHO Model List of Essential Medicines and the British National Formulary, which guides prescribing in the United Kingdom.
