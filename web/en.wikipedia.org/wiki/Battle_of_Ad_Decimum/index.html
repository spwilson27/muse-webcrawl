The Battle of Ad Decimum took place on 13 September 533 AD between the armies of the Byzantine Empire under Belisarius and the Vandal Kingdom under King Gelimer. It was fought near the tenth milestone (Latin: ad decimum) outside Carthage and was the opening land battle of Justinian I's Vandalic War to reconquer North Africa.

Belisarius's army on the march to Carthage, 533 AD.

The battle

Gelimer planned a three-pronged ambush: his brother Ammatas would attack the Byzantine vanguard from Carthage, his nephew Gibamund would strike the Byzantine left flank with 2,000 men, and Gelimer himself would fall on the rear. The plan failed through poor coordination. Ammatas arrived early and was killed attacking piecemeal; Gibamund's force was destroyed by Byzantine Hunnic cavalry; and when Gelimer reached the field and saw his brother's corpse, he lost heart at the decisive moment, allowing Belisarius to rally his scattered cavalry and drive the Vandals from the field.

Aftermath

Belisarius entered Carthage two days later to find the city undefended, its gates open and lamps lit. The victory began the collapse of the Vandal Kingdom, completed after the Battle of Tricamarum in December 533 — an astonishingly rapid reconquest described by the historian Procopius, who accompanied the expedition.
