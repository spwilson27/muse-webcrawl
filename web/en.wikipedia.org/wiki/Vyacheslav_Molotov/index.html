Vyacheslav Molotov

Vyacheslav Mikhailovich Molotov
Born: 9 March 1890, Kukarka
Died: 8 November 1986, Moscow
Premier of the Soviet Union: 1930–1941
Minister of Foreign Affairs: 1939–1949, 1953–1956

Vyacheslav Mikhailovich Molotov (9 March 1890 – 8 November 1986) was a Soviet politician and diplomat, an Old Bolshevik and a leading figure in the government of the Soviet Union from the 1920s to the 1950s. His birth surname was Skryabin; his revolutionary pseudonym, Molotov, derives from the Russian word molot, "hammer".

Career under Stalin

Molotov served as Chairman of the Council of People's Commissars (head of government) from 1930 to 1941, overseeing collectivisation and the first Five-Year Plans. In 1939 he replaced Maxim Litvinov as foreign minister and negotiated the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact with Nazi Germany, which divided Eastern Europe into spheres of influence. During the Second World War he was deputy head of government and a member of the State Defence Committee, negotiating with the Western Allies at the Tehran, Yalta and Potsdam conferences.

Postwar and fall

Molotov remained foreign minister until 1949 and returned to the post after Stalin's death in 1953. He opposed Nikita Khrushchev's de-Stalinisation and in 1957 joined the failed "Anti-Party Group" attempt to remove Khrushchev. In disgrace, he was exiled as ambassador to Mongolia and then to the International Atomic Energy Agency in Vienna, and expelled from the Communist Party in 1962.

Later life and legacy

Molotov lived in retirement for nearly three decades, unrepentant about his role in Stalin's purges, and was readmitted to the party in 1984, two years before his death at 96. The improvised petrol bomb was nicknamed the "Molotov cocktail" by Finns during the Winter War of 1939–40 — a sarcastic reference to Molotov's claim that Soviet bombers were dropping food supplies on Finland.
