McIlhenny Company

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Bottles of Tabasco sauce with their diamond labels, and barrels of pepper mash ageing at Avery Island]
Bottles of Tabasco sauce with their diamond labels, and barrels of pepper mash ageing at Avery Island

The McIlhenny Company is a family-owned American manufacturer of Tabasco brand pepper sauce, founded in 1868 by Edmund McIlhenny at Avery Island, Louisiana. Still owned and operated by McIlhenny's descendants — one of America's notable multi-generational family businesses — it produces the world's most widely distributed hot sauce, sold in more than 190 countries and labelled in 22 languages, from peppers aged in oak barrels on its salt-dome island.

History

Edmund McIlhenny, a New Orleans banker displaced by the Civil War, began growing Mexican tabasco peppers on his in-laws' Avery Island estate and bottled his first sauce in 1868 (famously in cologne bottles). The company — incorporated in 1870s as the heat spread — weathered hurricanes (raising its levees after Rita, 2005), built a company town and wildlife sanctuary on the island (the Jungle Gardens and Bird City egret refuge, founded by his son Edward Avery McIlhenny), and expanded the Tabasco line (green jalapeño, chipotle, habanero, sriracha) from the 1990s.

Production

Peppers are hand-picked at matched redness (judged against a painted stick, le petit bâton rouge), mashed with Avery Island salt, and aged up to three years in white-oak barrels; the mash is then blended with vinegar, strained and bottled. Seed stock is held in reserve in multiple locations — an early lesson from the island's vulnerability. Visitors tour the factory and gardens on Avery Island, a working salt dome in the Louisiana bayous.
