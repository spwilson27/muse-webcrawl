Tiergarten Schönbrunn (literally "Schönbrunn Animal Garden"), also called Schönbrunn Zoo, is a zoo in the grounds of Schönbrunn Palace in Vienna, Austria. Founded in 1752 as an imperial menagerie, it is the oldest continuously operating zoo in the world. It forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site of Schönbrunn Palace and its gardens and is one of Vienna's most popular attractions, drawing over two million visitors in good years.

History

The menagerie was established by Francis I, husband of Maria Theresa, who had a keen interest in natural history. Its Baroque layout — thirteen enclosures radiating around a central octagonal pavilion where the imperial couple took breakfast while watching the animals — survives today. The zoo was opened to the public in 1778, initially only on Sundays and only to "respectably dressed" visitors.

In the early 20th century, under director Alois Kraus, it became one of Europe's leading zoos; one of its elephant houses hosted what is often cited as the first elephant born in captivity in a zoo (1906). Both World Wars severely reduced the collection — after 1945 only a few hundred animals remained — but the zoo was rebuilt and modernised from the 1980s onward under a privatised management company.

Animals and conservation

The zoo keeps around 700 species. It is noted for giant pandas (housed since 2003 under an agreement with China, with several cubs born), Siberian tigers, polar bears, an aquarium and rainforest house, and a historic monkey house that is among the oldest surviving zoo buildings. Schönbrunn participates in European breeding programmes for endangered species.
