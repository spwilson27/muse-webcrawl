Tongdosa (Korean: 통도사, "Salvation of the World through Mastery of Truth") is a major Buddhist temple in Yangsan, South Gyeongsang Province, South Korea. Founded in 646 by the monk Jajang during the reign of Queen Seondeok of Silla, it is one of the Three Jewels Temples of Korean Buddhism, representing the Buddha jewel.

Relics and layout

Unlike most Korean temples, Tongdosa's main hall, the Daeungjeon, contains no statue of the Buddha. Instead, worship is directed toward a diamond altar platform (Geumgang Gyedan) behind the hall, said to enshrine relics of the Buddha — including a skull bone, a kasaya robe and begging bowl fragments — brought from China by Jajang. The temple complex contains numerous halls and hermitages and preserves many designated cultural treasures.

Recognition

In 2018 Tongdosa was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as part of the "Sansa, Buddhist Mountain Monasteries in Korea" listing.
