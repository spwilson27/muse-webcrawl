August Wilhelm von Hofmann (8 April 1818 – 5 May 1892) was a German chemist who made considerable contributions to organic chemistry. His research on aniline helped lay the basis of the aniline-dye industry, and his research on coal tar laid the groundwork for practical methods of extracting benzene and toluene and converting them into nitro compounds and amines. Hofmann's discoveries include formaldehyde, hydrazobenzene, the isonitriles, and allyl alcohol.

Early life and education

Hofmann was born at Giessen, the son of the architect Johann Philipp Hofmann. He entered the University of Giessen in 1836 to study law and philology, but soon turned to chemistry under Justus von Liebig, whose assistant he became. He took his doctorate in 1841 with a dissertation on the chemical investigation of coal tar.

Career in London

In 1845 Hofmann was invited to London to become the first director of the newly founded Royal College of Chemistry. He remained in England for twenty years, building a distinguished school of research. His students included William Henry Perkin, who in 1856 discovered mauveine, the first synthetic aniline dye, while working in Hofmann's laboratory. Hofmann himself developed a series of magenta dyes, one of which was named Hofmann's violets.

Return to Germany

In 1864 Hofmann returned to Germany, holding chairs first at the University of Bonn and then at the University of Berlin. In 1867 he was a co-founder of the German Chemical Society (Deutsche Chemische Gesellschaft) and served as its president repeatedly. He died in Berlin in 1892.

Contributions

Hofmann developed methods for the exhaustive methylation of amines followed by elimination — the Hofmann elimination — and the conversion of amides to amines with loss of the carbonyl carbon, the Hofmann rearrangement. He also made major contributions to the understanding of the structure of amines, coining the classification of amines as primary, secondary, and tertiary. His textbook Einleitung in die moderne Chemie (1865) was widely used and translated.

Honours

Hofmann was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1851, received its Royal Medal in 1854 and the Copley Medal in 1875. He was ennobled in 1888 on the occasion of his seventieth birthday. The German Chemical Society's August Wilhelm von Hofmann Memorial Lecture and medal commemorate his name.
