Carinthian Slovenes

The Carinthian Slovenes (German: Kärntner Slowenen; Slovene: Koroški Slovenci) are the indigenous Slovene-speaking minority in the Austrian state of Carinthia. They are recognised as an autochthonous ethnic group with rights protected by the Austrian State Treaty of 1955.

Carinthian Slovenes
Region: Southern Carinthia, Austria
Languages: Slovene (dialects), German
Status: Recognised minority

History

Slavic settlement of the region dates to the early Middle Ages. After the First World War, the 1920 Carinthian plebiscite kept southern Carinthia in Austria. During the Nazi era the minority faced persecution and deportation, and many joined the partisan resistance. Postwar Austria guaranteed bilingual schooling and topographical signs, though the placement of bilingual village signs (the Ortstafelstreit) remained contentious for decades.

Culture

Carinthian Slovene culture is expressed through bilingual schools, media such as the weekly Novice, publishing, choirs, and political organisations. Numbers have declined through assimilation; censuses record tens of thousands of Slovene speakers.
