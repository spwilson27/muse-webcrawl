The George Washington Bridge is a double-decked suspension bridge spanning the Hudson River between Washington Heights in Manhattan, New York City, and Fort Lee, New Jersey. Carrying Interstate 95 and US Routes 1/9, it is one of the busiest motor-vehicle bridges in the world.

Construction

The bridge was designed by Swiss-American engineer Othmar Ammann for the Port Authority of New York and New Jersey. Construction began in 1927; when it opened on 24 October 1931 its 3,500-foot main span was by far the longest in the world, nearly double the previous record, and it remained so until the Golden Gate Bridge opened in 1937. The bridge was named for George Washington, whose troops had held the area during the Revolutionary War.

The steel towers were originally intended to be clad in granite, a plan dropped for economy during the Great Depression; the exposed steel lattice, initially seen as unfinished, came to be celebrated by architects such as Le Corbusier as a masterpiece of engineering expression.

Lower level and later work

Ammann's design allowed a second deck to be hung beneath the first. The lower level — nicknamed the "Martha Washington" — opened on 29 August 1962, raising capacity to 14 lanes, the most of any suspension bridge. A bus station designed by Pier Luigi Nervi opened above the New York approach in 1963. The bridge also carries pedestrians and cyclists on sidewalks.

Significance

The George Washington Bridge anchors the northern crossing of the Hudson at New York and is a critical link in the Northeast Corridor highway network. In September 2013, unannounced closures of its Fort Lee access lanes caused the "Bridgegate" political scandal in New Jersey.
