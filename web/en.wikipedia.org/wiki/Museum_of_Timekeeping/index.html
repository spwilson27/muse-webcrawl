Museum of Timekeeping

The Museum of Timekeeping is a museum in Upton, Nottinghamshire, England, dedicated to the history of clocks, watches and other timekeeping devices. It is located at Upton Hall, the former headquarters of the British Horological Institute, and was known for many years as the British Horological Institute Museum before adopting its present name in 2017.

History

The British Horological Institute was founded in 1858 to promote education and standards in clock and watch making. Its collection grew over the following century and a half through donations of significant horological artefacts, tools and documents. The institute moved to Upton Hall in 1972, and the museum developed alongside its library and teaching activities.

Collection

The museum holds an extensive collection of clocks, watches, tools, and horological literature. Among its most notable exhibits is one of the speaking clocks used by the British telephone network, along with early marine chronometers, longcase clocks, tower clock movements, and examples relating to the development of the electric clock. The collection also includes items connected with John Harrison, the celebrated maker of marine timekeepers who solved the problem of determining longitude at sea.

Exhibitions and activities

The museum opens to the public on selected days, often coinciding with special events and open days when volunteers demonstrate working clocks and explain the craft of horology. Temporary exhibitions explore themes such as timekeeping at sea, the history of the wristwatch, and the restoration of historic timepieces.
