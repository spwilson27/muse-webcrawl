Gottlieb Wilhelm Leitner

Gottlieb Wilhelm Leitner (14 October 1840 – 22 March 1899) was a Hungarian-born British orientalist, linguist, and educator. Born in Pest, Hungary, he showed an extraordinary gift for languages and was employed as an interpreter by the British during the Crimean War while still a teenager.

Leitner studied at King's College London and became a professor of Arabic and Mohammedan law there while still in his early twenties. In 1864 he went to India as principal of Government College, Lahore, where he founded numerous schools and literary associations and was instrumental in founding the University of the Punjab. He championed education in the vernacular and in Oriental languages against exclusively English-medium instruction.

He travelled widely in Central Asia, including expeditions to Dardistan, and published extensively on the languages and peoples of the region. Returning to England, he founded the Oriental Institute at Woking and the Woking Mosque (the Shah Jahan Mosque), the first purpose-built mosque in Britain. He was buried at Brookwood Cemetery.
