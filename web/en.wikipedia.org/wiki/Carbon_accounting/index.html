Carbon accounting

Carbon accounting, or greenhouse gas accounting, is the process of measuring and reporting the amount of greenhouse gases — usually expressed in carbon dioxide equivalent (CO2e) — emitted directly and indirectly by an organisation, product, project, or territory.

Carbon accounting
Unit: t CO2e
Framework: GHG Protocol
Scopes: 1 (direct), 2 (energy), 3 (value chain)

Scopes

The widely used Greenhouse Gas Protocol divides emissions into Scope 1 (direct emissions from owned sources), Scope 2 (indirect emissions from purchased energy), and Scope 3 (all other value-chain emissions, upstream and downstream). Scope 3 often accounts for the majority of a company's footprint and is the hardest to measure.

Uses

Carbon accounts underpin corporate climate targets, emissions trading schemes, carbon taxes, product footprints, and disclosures under regimes such as the EU Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive. Standards include ISO 14064 and the GHG Protocol Corporate Standard.
