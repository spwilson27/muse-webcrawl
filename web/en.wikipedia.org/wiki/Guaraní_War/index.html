Guaraní War

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Guaraní War (Spanish: Guerra Guaranítica, Portuguese: Guerra Guaranítica) of 1756, sometimes called the War of the Seven Reductions, was a conflict fought between the Guaraní people of the Jesuit reductions in Paraguay and joint Spanish–Portuguese forces. The war was the outcome of the 1750 Treaty of Madrid, which drew a new border between the Spanish and Portuguese colonial possessions in South America.

Background

By the terms of the Treaty of Madrid, Spain ceded to Portugal the territory of the seven Jesuit mission towns (Siete Pueblos de las Misiones) east of the Uruguay River — San Miguel, Santo Ángel, San Lorenzo Mártir, San Nicolás, San Juan Bautista, San Luis Gonzaga and San Francisco de Borja — in exchange for the Portuguese settlement of Colônia do Sacramento on the Río de la Plata. The roughly 30,000 Guaraní inhabitants of the reductions were ordered to abandon their homes and move west of the Uruguay, an order they refused to obey.

Course of the war

Initial joint Spanish–Portuguese attempts to enforce the demarcation in 1754 were resisted and delayed. In February 1756 a combined force of about 3,000 Spanish and Portuguese soldiers advanced against the missions. The Guaraní leader Sepé Tiaraju was killed in a skirmish on 7 February 1756, three days before the decisive engagement. On 10 February 1756 the Guaraní army, commanded by Nicolás Ñeenguirú, was defeated at the Battle of Caiboaté, where around 1,500 Guaraní were killed for the loss of only a handful of allied soldiers.

Aftermath

After the defeat the seven mission towns passed under Portuguese control. The Treaty of Madrid was annulled by the Treaty of El Pardo in 1761 and the territory returned to Spain, but the episode gravely damaged the Jesuit reductions and contributed to the climate that led to the expulsion of the Jesuits from Spanish America in 1767. The story of Sepé Tiaraju, canonised in popular tradition in Rio Grande do Sul, was later dramatised in the film The Mission (1986).
