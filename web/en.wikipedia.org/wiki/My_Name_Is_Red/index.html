My Name Is Red (Turkish: Benim Adım Kırmızı) is a 1998 Turkish novel by Orhan Pamuk, translated into English by Erdağ Göknar in 2001. The novel, set in Istanbul in the winter of 1591 during the reign of Sultan Murat III, blends a murder mystery with a meditation on art, love, and the clash between Ottoman miniature painting and the European Renaissance style of representation. It became Pamuk's most commercially successful book and contributed to his receiving the Nobel Prize in Literature in 2006.

Plot summary

The Sultan has secretly commissioned an illustrated book celebrating his reign, to be painted in the European style — including perspective and individual portraits — which many miniaturists regard as blasphemous. One of the illuminators working on the book, Elegant Effendi, is murdered and thrown down a well; the novel opens with his corpse narrating. Black, a former apprentice, returns to Istanbul after twelve years and is enlisted by his uncle Enishte, who directs the secret project, to find the murderer — while also pursuing his love for Enishte's widowed daughter Shekure. When Enishte himself is murdered, suspicion falls on the three master miniaturists known as Butterfly, Stork, and Olive.

Narration

The novel is famous for its polyphonic narration: chapters are told in the first person by a shifting cast of narrators, including the corpse, Black, Shekure, the murderer (whose identity is withheld), a picture of a horse, a coin, a tree, Satan, and the colour red itself, which gives the novel its title.

Themes

The central theme is the conflict between East and West expressed through art: whether the artist should paint as Allah sees the world, in the timeless collective style of the Persian and Ottoman masters, or embrace individual style, signature, and portraiture as the Venetians do. The novel also treats love, storytelling, and death within the coffeehouse culture of Ottoman Istanbul.

Awards

The English translation won the International Dublin Literary Award (IMPAC) in 2003, shared between Pamuk and Göknar.
