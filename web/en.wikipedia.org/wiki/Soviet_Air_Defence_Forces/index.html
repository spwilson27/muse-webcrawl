The Soviet Air Defence Forces (Russian: Войска противовоздушной обороны, Voyska Protivovozdushnoy Oborony, abbreviated PVO) were the air defence branch of the Soviet Armed Forces, responsible for defending Soviet airspace against enemy aircraft and missiles. Uniquely among major powers, the USSR maintained air defence as a fully independent armed service, separate from the Soviet Air Forces, from 1948 until the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991.

Organisation and equipment

The PVO combined three components: interceptor aviation (IA-PVO) flying aircraft such as the MiG-25, MiG-31 and Su-27; surface-to-air missile troops operating the S-75, S-125, S-200 and S-300 systems; and radio-technical (radar) troops providing early warning, integrated into an automated command network. A separate service, the Strategic Rocket Forces, handled ballistic missiles, while the PVO also maintained the Moscow ABM system.

Notable incidents

The PVO was at the centre of several famous Cold War episodes: its S-75 missile shot down Francis Gary Powers' U-2 over Sverdlovsk on 1 May 1960; its interceptors forced down Korean Air Lines Flight 007 in 1983 with the loss of all 269 aboard; and in 1987 the West German teenager Mathias Rust penetrated its defences to land a light aircraft in Red Square, prompting a purge of the service's leadership. After 1991 the PVO's Russian components were merged into the Russian Air Force in 1998.
