St. Petersburg 1914 chess tournament

The St. Petersburg 1914 chess tournament was one of the most famous chess tournaments ever held. Organised under the patronage of Tsar Nicholas II, it took place in St. Petersburg, Russia, in April and May 1914, only weeks before the outbreak of the First World War. The tournament is remembered as the occasion on which the title of Grandmaster was first formally used: the Tsar conferred it on the five finalists.

The participants of St. Petersburg 1914

Format

Eleven players took part in a preliminary round-robin. The top five finishers advanced to a double round-robin final, carrying forward their preliminary scores. The field included World Champion Emanuel Lasker, the rising Cuban star José Raúl Capablanca, Akiba Rubinstein, Siegbert Tarrasch, Frank Marshall, and the Russian masters Alexander Alekhine and Aron Nimzowitsch.

Results

Capablanca dominated the preliminary, finishing 1½ points clear of the field, and seemed certain to win the tournament. In the final, however, Lasker produced one of the great performances of his career, scoring 7 out of 8 — including a famous victory over Capablanca with an Exchange Ruy Lopez — to overtake him and win the tournament. Legacy

The five finalists — Lasker, Capablanca, Alekhine, Tarrasch and Marshall — are often called the "original grandmasters". The tournament confirmed Lasker's supremacy, announced Capablanca as his future challenger, and marked Alekhine's arrival among the world's elite.
