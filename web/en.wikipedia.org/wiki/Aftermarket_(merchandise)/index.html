Aftermarket (merchandise)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia. For the jet engine component, see Afterburner; for the sky phenomenon, see Afterglow — both unrelated.

The aftermarket is the market for replacement parts, accessories, consumables and services sold after the sale of the original equipment. It is most prominent in the automotive industry, where it comprises all parts and products sold for vehicles after their original sale by the manufacturer — from brake pads, batteries and tyres to performance upgrades, body kits and audio systems.

Structure

Aftermarket suppliers range from the original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) selling branded spares through dealer networks, to independent manufacturers of equivalent "pattern" parts, remanufacturers, and retailers and installers. Products not made by the original manufacturer are often called aftermarket parts even when they match OEM specifications. Prices are typically lower than dealer-channel parts, though quality varies, and warranty law in many jurisdictions (such as the US Magnuson–Moss Warranty Act) restricts manufacturers from voiding warranties merely because aftermarket parts are used.

Other industries

Analogous aftermarkets exist for aircraft components, consumer electronics accessories, computer peripherals, agricultural and industrial machinery, and software add-ons. The concept overlaps with the "secondary market" and with debates over the right to repair.
