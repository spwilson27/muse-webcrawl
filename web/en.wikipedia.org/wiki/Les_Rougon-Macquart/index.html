Les Rougon-Macquart

Les Rougon-Macquart is a cycle of twenty novels by French writer Émile Zola, published between 1871 and 1893. Subtitled "the natural and social history of a family under the Second Empire", it traces the descendants of Adélaïde Fouque across five generations, examining how heredity and environment shape lives in the France of Napoleon III (1852–1870).

The cycle

Zola planned the series as a fictional application of the scientific method to literature — the programme he set out in his essay The Experimental Novel (1880). The two branches of the family, the legitimate Rougons and the illegitimate Macquarts, spread through every level of society: politics, finance, the army, the church, commerce, the railways, the working class, and the peasantry. Individual volumes include La Fortune des Rougon (1871), L'Assommoir (1877), his breakthrough study of alcoholism in working-class Paris; Nana (1880); Germinal (1885), about a coal miners' strike and often considered his masterpiece; La Bête humaine (1890); and the concluding Le Docteur Pascal (1893), in which the family tree is reviewed.

Themes and legacy

Recurring themes are the corrupting force of money and appetite, the weight of inherited alcoholism and mental instability, and the dynamism of modern Paris — its department stores (Au Bonheur des Dames), markets (Le Ventre de Paris), and stock exchange (L'Argent). Together with Balzac's Comédie humaine, to which Zola consciously responded, the Rougon-Macquart cycle is a defining monument of French naturalism and of the 19th-century novel.
