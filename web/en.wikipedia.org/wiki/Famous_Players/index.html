Famous Players Film Company

The Famous Players Film Company was an American film production company founded in 1912 by Adolph Zukor with the slogan "Famous Players in Famous Plays". It became one of the foundations of the modern Hollywood studio system and, through a series of mergers, evolved into Paramount Pictures.

History

Zukor founded the company after acquiring the American distribution rights to the French film Queen Elizabeth (1912), starring Sarah Bernhardt. Its success persuaded him that feature-length films starring celebrated stage actors could attract middle-class audiences. Famous Players produced adaptations of stage successes such as The Prisoner of Zenda and Tess of the d'Urbervilles, and signed stars including Mary Pickford, Marguerite Clark and John Barrymore.

Merger

In 1916, Famous Players merged with the Jesse L. Lasky Feature Play Company to form Famous Players–Lasky Corporation, which distributed its films through Paramount Pictures. In 1927 the corporation adopted the Paramount name outright. Zukor remained a dominant figure in the industry for decades.
