Persecution of Falun Gong

The persecution of Falun Gong is the campaign initiated in July 1999 by the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) to eliminate the spiritual practice of Falun Gong in China. The campaign has involved propaganda, censorship, mass arrests, forced labour, imprisonment, and reported torture of practitioners. It is carried out in part through the extra-legal 610 Office, created specifically to oversee the suppression.

Background

Falun Gong, a qigong-based spiritual discipline introduced by Li Hongzhi in 1992, grew rapidly in China during the 1990s, with government estimates of 70–100 million practitioners. Relations with the authorities deteriorated over criticism in state media and the withdrawal of official recognition. On 25 April 1999, some 10,000 practitioners gathered peacefully near Zhongnanhai in Beijing to appeal, an event that alarmed the leadership.

The crackdown

On 20 July 1999 the CCP under Jiang Zemin banned Falun Gong, labelling it an illegal organisation and later a "cult". Hundreds of thousands of practitioners are estimated to have been detained in re-education through labour camps, prisons, and detention centres. Human rights organisations have documented extensive reports of torture and deaths in custody. Allegations of forced organ harvesting from prisoners of conscience, investigated by the Kilgour–Matas report and others, remain a subject of international concern and dispute.

International response

Governments, the United Nations, and rights groups including Amnesty International and Human Rights Watch have condemned the campaign. Falun Gong practitioners abroad have mounted sustained protests, media campaigns, and lawsuits against Chinese officials. Beijing maintains that the measures are lawful actions against a harmful organisation.
