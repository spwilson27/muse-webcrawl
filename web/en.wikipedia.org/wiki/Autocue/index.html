Autocue

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Autocue is a British company and brand name for a teleprompter — a display device that prompts the person speaking with an electronic visual text of a speech or script. The word has become a genericised trademark in the United Kingdom and Commonwealth countries, where any teleprompter is commonly called an Autocue; in the United States the equivalent generic term is "teleprompter," originally the trademark of the TelePrompTer Company.

History

Autocue was founded in London in 1955 by Jess Oppenheimer's British counterparts — the system was developed and marketed in Britain from the mid-1950s as television newsreading moved from memorised scripts and cue cards to scrolling prompts. The company's devices used a one-way mirror (beamsplitter glass) mounted in front of the camera lens, reflecting text from a monitor below so that a presenter could read while looking directly into the camera.

Technology

Modern Autocue systems are software-driven: scripts are edited in a newsroom computer system and scrolled on monitors at a speed controlled by an operator or by the presenter with a hand or foot control. Conference prompters use paired glass panels on either side of a lectern, familiar from political speeches. The company has expanded into video production software and services while remaining headquartered in London with offices worldwide.
