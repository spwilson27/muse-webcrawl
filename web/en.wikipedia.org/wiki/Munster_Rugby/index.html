Munster Rugby is one of the four professional provincial rugby union teams of Ireland, representing the province of Munster. The Munster branch of the Irish Rugby Football Union was founded in 1879, and the team turned professional in 1995. Munster compete in the United Rugby Championship and the European Rugby Champions Cup, and play most home games at Thomond Park in Limerick, a ground famous for its atmosphere and for the silence observed during kicks at goal.

Munster's red jersey and the fervour of Thomond Park are central to the province's identity.

History

Munster's most celebrated result came in 1978, when they defeated the touring New Zealand All Blacks 12–0 at Thomond Park — the only Irish side ever to beat New Zealand, a feat commemorated in the play and book Alone It Stands. In the professional era Munster became one of Europe's strongest teams, reaching the first two Heineken Cup finals in 2000 and 2002 and losing both before finally winning the trophy in 2006, beating Biarritz 23–19 at the Millennium Stadium, and again in 2008, defeating Toulouse 16–13.

Recent years

Munster won the Celtic League/Magners League in 2003, 2009, and 2011. After a long trophyless spell and the death of head coach and former captain Anthony Foley in 2016, which profoundly affected the province, Munster ended a twelve-year wait for silverware by winning the United Rugby Championship in 2023, beating the Stormers 19–14 in the final in Cape Town under head coach Graham Rowntree.

Identity and supporters

Munster are renowned for one of the largest and most passionate supporter bases in club rugby, built on the team's history of defying wealthier opponents. The anthem "Stand Up and Fight" and the song "The Fields of Athenry" are staples of match days, and the province's motto is "To the brave and faithful, nothing is impossible", taken from the MacCarthy crest.
