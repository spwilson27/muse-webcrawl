The Banqueting House, on Whitehall in the City of Westminster, London, is the grandest surviving building of the Palace of Whitehall and the only component of the palace to remain intact after a fire in 1698. It was designed by Inigo Jones and built between 1619 and 1622 for King James I. It is celebrated as the first building in England built in a fully classical, Palladian style.

Architecture and the Rubens ceiling

The building is a great double-cube hall, lit by large windows, above a vaulted undercroft. In 1636 a painted ceiling by Peter Paul Rubens was installed, commissioned by Charles I; its panels celebrate James I and the divine right of kings, and it is the only Rubens ceiling painting still in its original location.

Historical events

On 30 January 1649, King Charles I was executed on a scaffold erected outside the Banqueting House, passing to it through one of its windows. After the 1698 fire it became a chapel royal, and in the 19th century it housed the Royal United Services Institute museum. Today it is managed by Historic Royal Palaces and used for events and visits.
