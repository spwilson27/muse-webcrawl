Sandpaper

TypeAbrasive sheet material
ComponentsAbrasive particles, paper/cloth/film backing, binder
Common abrasivesAluminium oxide, silicon carbide, garnet, emery, zirconia alumina, ceramic alumina
UsesWoodworking, metalworking, finishing, surface preparation

Sandpaper, in modern usage also called coated abrasive or simply abrasive paper, is a material that consists of sheets of paper, cloth, or other backing with abrasive material glued to one face. It is used to remove small amounts of material from surfaces, either to make them smoother (for example in painting and wood finishing), to remove a layer of material (such as old paint), or sometimes to make the surface rougher in preparation for gluing or bonding.

Despite the name, modern sandpaper is made neither with sand nor, in many grades, with paper. The abrasive grains are most commonly aluminium oxide or silicon carbide, graded by particle size and held to the backing with resin or glue binders. Sandpaper is classified by its grit size: coarser grits with larger particles remove material quickly, while finer grits are used for finishing.

History

The earliest recorded example of a sandpaper-like material dates to 13th-century China, when crushed shells, seeds, and sand were bonded to parchment with natural gum. In the biblical period, Native Americans also used sand glued to shark skin as a form of sandpaper. Glass paper, made with crushed glass particles, was produced in London by John Oakey from 1833, and by 1900 companies such as Franz Buehler's and the Minnesota Mining and Manufacturing Company (3M) were mass-producing coated abrasives. Waterproof silicon carbide paper became widespread in the early 20th century.

Types

Sandpaper varies by abrasive mineral, backing, and bonding. Garnet is a traditional woodworking abrasive; emery is used on metals; silicon carbide is hard and sharp and available in waterproof grades; aluminium oxide is the most common general-purpose abrasive; zirconia alumina and ceramic alumina are used for heavy stock removal in metalworking. Backings range from lightweight paper for hand sanding to heavy cloth and fiber used for belts and discs.

Grit sizes

Grit size refers to the size of the particles of abrasive material embedded in the paper. Several standards are used, the most common in North America being the CAMI (Coated Abrasive Manufacturers Institute) scale and in Europe the FEPA "P" scale. In both, smaller numbers indicate coarser grits. Typical ranges run from extra-coarse P12–P36 through medium P60–P120 to very fine P1000 and beyond for polishing. See also abrasive and grit size.
