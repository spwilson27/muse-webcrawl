Horseshoe crab

Horseshoe crabs are marine and brackish water arthropods of the family Limulidae and the only living members of the order Xiphosura. Despite their name, they are not true crabs or crustaceans: they are chelicerates, most closely related to arachnids such as spiders, ticks, and scorpions.

Description

Horseshoe crabs have three main parts to the body: the head region, known as the "prosoma", the abdominal region or "opisthosoma", and the spine-like tail or "telson". The smooth shell or carapace is shaped like a horseshoe and is greenish grey to dark brown in colour.

Species and significance

There are four living species: the Atlantic horseshoe crab (Limulus polyphemus), and three Indo-Pacific species. Horseshoe crab blood contains Limulus amebocyte lysate (LAL), a clotting agent used to test bacterial endotoxin contamination in medical applications. Often called "living fossils", horseshoe crabs have changed little in appearance over hundreds of millions of years.
