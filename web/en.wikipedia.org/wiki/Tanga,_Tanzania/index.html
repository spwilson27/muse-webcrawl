Tanga is both the name of the port city on the northern coast of Tanzania, close to the Kenyan border, and the surrounding Tanga Region. It is the second-largest port in Tanzania after Dar es Salaam and historically one of the oldest settlements on the Swahili Coast. The city lies on a sheltered bay opposite Pemba Island, at the mouth of no major river, and serves the sisal- and tea-growing hinterland of the Usambara Mountains.

History

Tanga was a trading post on the Swahili coast long before European contact, dealing with Persian, Arab and Indian merchants. In the late 19th century it became a principal administrative and commercial centre of German East Africa; the Germans built the northern railway (Usambara Railway) from Tanga toward Moshi and the Kilimanjaro region, developed the port, and planted the sisal estates for which the region became known. In November 1914, early in the First World War, a British amphibious landing at Tanga was repulsed by German colonial forces under Paul von Lettow-Vorbeck in the Battle of Tanga, remembered as the "Battle of the Bees" after swarms disturbed during the fighting attacked both sides. After the war Tanga passed to British administration under the Tanganyika mandate.

Economy and landmarks

Tanga's economy rests on its port — handling sisal, coffee, tea, cotton and general cargo — together with cement production, steel rolling and other industry. Landmarks include the German-era clock tower and buildings, the Urithi Tanga Museum, the nearby Amboni Caves (the largest limestone cave system in East Africa), the Tongoni ruins, and the Toten Island ruins in the bay. The city is the terminus of the revived northern railway line and has an airport.
