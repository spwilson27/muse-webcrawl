Chambéry

Chambéry (French pronunciation: [ʃɑ̃beʁi]; Arpitan: Chambèri) is a city in the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region of eastern France and the prefecture of the Savoie department. With a population of about 60,000, it was the historic capital of the Duchy of Savoy and the first capital of the House of Savoy before the dynasty moved its seat to Turin. The city lies in a valley between the Bauges and Chartreuse massifs, south of Lake Bourget.

History

The site, known in Roman times as Lemencum, grew at a crossroads of routes over the western Alps. In 1232 Count Thomas I of Savoy acquired the town, and Chambéry became the capital of the Savoyard state; the Château des Ducs de Savoie, begun under the counts, housed the Shroud of Turin in its Sainte-Chapelle before the relic was transferred to Turin in 1578, after the court had moved there in 1563. The city thereafter remained the administrative capital of Savoy proper.

Jean-Jacques Rousseau lived in and around Chambéry in the 1730s with Madame de Warens at the house known as Les Charmettes, a period described in his Confessions as the happiest of his life. Chambéry was annexed by revolutionary France in 1792, restored to the Kingdom of Sardinia in 1815, and definitively joined France with the rest of Savoy by the Treaty of Turin in 1860. The city suffered heavy bombing in May 1944, when Allied raids targeted its railway marshalling yards.

Sights

  • Château des Ducs de Savoie — seat of the prefecture; its carillon of 70 bells is among the largest in Europe.
  • Fontaine des Éléphants (1838) — the city's emblem, honouring the Comte de Boigne, who made his fortune in India; locals call it "les quatre sans culs" ("the four without backsides").
  • Chambéry Cathedral — housing the largest ensemble of trompe-l'œil painting in Europe.
  • Les Charmettes — Rousseau's house, now a museum.
  • Musée des Beaux-Arts and the old town's arcaded streets and courtyards.

Economy and transport

Chambéry is a regional administrative, commercial, and university centre (Université Savoie Mont Blanc) and an access point for Alpine ski resorts. It is served by the Chambéry–Challes-les-Eaux railway station on the line to Turin via the Mont Cenis tunnel, and by Chambéry Airport near Lake Bourget.
