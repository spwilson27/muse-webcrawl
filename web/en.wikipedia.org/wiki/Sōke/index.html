Sōke

Sōke (宗家) is a Japanese term meaning "head of the house" or "grandmaster". In the traditional Japanese arts — most notably the classical martial arts (koryū) — the sōke is the hereditary or appointed head of a school (ryūha), holding ultimate authority over its curriculum, licensing and traditions.

Role

The sōke is the living embodiment of the school's lineage, which may trace back centuries. Only the sōke can issue the highest licences, such as menkyo kaiden (licence of total transmission), and can authorise changes to the kata or teaching of the school. Succession may pass within a family, by adoption, or to a chosen senior student, depending on the tradition of the school.

Iemoto system

The sōke concept is part of the broader iemoto system that governs many traditional Japanese arts, including tea ceremony, flower arrangement, calligraphy, and classical dance. The iemoto (headmaster) embodies the tradition and grants teaching licences through a hierarchy of students. Outside traditional arts, the title is sometimes used loosely in modern martial arts, which can give rise to contested claims of sōke status.
