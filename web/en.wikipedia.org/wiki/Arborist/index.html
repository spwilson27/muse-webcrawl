Arborist

An arborist at work pruning a tree.

An arborist, or tree surgeon, is a professional in the practice of arboriculture, which is the cultivation, management, and study of individual trees, shrubs, vines, and other perennial woody plants in dendrology and horticulture. Arborists generally focus on the health and safety of individual plants and trees, unlike foresters, who manage forests and harvest timber, or loggers, who fell trees for wood products.

Work

Arborists prune trees to maintain clearance for roads and utilities, remove dead or hazardous limbs, shape specimens, and fell trees where necessary, often by climbing with ropes and a harness or by using aerial work platforms. They also diagnose and treat pests and diseases, cable and brace weak limbs, plant and transplant trees, and assess tree risk for property owners, municipalities, and insurers. Because the work involves heights, chainsaws, and falling timber, it is statistically among the more dangerous occupations.

Certification

Professional standards are maintained by bodies such as the International Society of Arboriculture (ISA), whose Certified Arborist credential requires experience and an examination, and, in the United Kingdom, the Arboricultural Association. Famous historical arborists include John Davey, often called the father of tree surgery, whose company, founded in 1880, pioneered the profession in North America.
