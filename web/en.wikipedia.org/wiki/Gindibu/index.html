Gindibu

Gindibu (Akkadian: Gi-in-di-bu-ʾu) was an Arab king, or sheikh, active in the 9th century BC. He is known from the Kurkh Monolith, an inscription of the Assyrian king Shalmaneser III, which records that at the Battle of Qarqar in 853 BC Gindibu contributed 1,000 camels to the coalition of Levantine states that faced the Assyrian army.

Historical significance

Gindibu's appearance at Qarqar is one of the earliest mentions of Arabs — and specifically of an Arab ruler — in the historical record, and the first attestation of camels used as a military force in the Near East on a large scale. The coalition he joined included Hadadezer of Damascus, Ahab of Israel, and Irhuleni of Hamath, among others. Shalmaneser claimed victory in his inscription, though he campaigned in the region repeatedly in later years, suggesting the coalition was not destroyed.

The name Gindibu has been analysed as an early form of an Arabic name (possibly related to jundub, "locust" or a personal name element); his camel force underscores the role of North Arabian tribes in the politics of the Levant at the dawn of the Iron Age kingdoms' resistance to Assyria.
