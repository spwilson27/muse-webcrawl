World of Warcraft (WoW) is a massively multiplayer online role-playing game developed and published by Blizzard Entertainment. Released in 2004, it is set in the Warcraft fantasy universe and became the most popular MMORPG of all time, reaching a peak of 12 million subscribers in 2010 and remaining a major title decades after launch.

Gameplay

Players create a character from one of two factions — the Alliance or the Horde — choosing a race and a class such as warrior, mage, or priest. Characters explore the world of Azeroth, complete quests, fight monsters and other players, and form groups for instanced dungeons and large-scale raids. Progress is tracked through character levels, equipment ("gear"), professions, and achievements. End-game activities include mythic dungeons, rated player-versus-player arenas and battlegrounds, and collecting mounts, pets, and transmogrification appearances.

Expansions

Major expansions have successively enlarged the game: The Burning Crusade (2007), Wrath of the Lich King (2008), Cataclysm (2010), Mists of Pandaria (2012), Warlords of Draenor (2014), Legion (2016), Battle for Azeroth (2018), Shadowlands (2020), Dragonflight (2022), and The War Within (2024), the latter opening a multi-expansion "Worldsoul Saga". In 2019 Blizzard launched World of Warcraft Classic, recreating the original game, which itself progressed through re-releases of early expansions.

Cultural impact

WoW has been studied by academics (including the famous "Corrupted Blood" incident of 2005, used as a model of epidemic spread), parodied in television such as South Park's Emmy-winning "Make Love, Not Warcraft", and credited with shaping guilds, e-sports races to world-first raid clears, and the business model of subscription and live-service games.
