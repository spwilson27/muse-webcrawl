Water gas

Water gas is a synthesis gas produced by passing steam over red-hot coke or other carbonaceous fuel. It consists mainly of carbon monoxide and hydrogen, together with smaller amounts of carbon dioxide and nitrogen. Because its flame is intensely hot and it contains flammable CO, water gas was widely used in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries as a fuel gas and as a chemical feedstock.

Water gas
Composition~40–50% CO, ~45–50% H2, balance CO2, N2, CH4
FormationC + H2O → CO + H2 (endothermic)
RelatedProducer gas, coal gas, synthesis gas

Production

In the classic process, air is first blown through a bed of incandescent coke to raise its temperature (the "blow"), then steam is passed over the hot carbon (the "run"), reacting endothermically to form carbon monoxide and hydrogen: C + H2O → CO + H2. The cycle alternates to keep the fuel bed hot. Passing the gas over iron oxide catalysts drives the water-gas shift reaction, CO + H2O → CO2 + H2, increasing hydrogen yield.

Uses

"Blue water gas", named for its blue flame, had a higher calorific value than coal gas but little illuminating power; for town lighting it was enriched with oil vapours to make carburetted water gas. Water gas was also a source of hydrogen for ammonia synthesis and other chemical processes, and an industrial fuel for furnaces and welding. Its high carbon monoxide content made it extremely poisonous, and leaks caused many deaths before natural gas displaced manufactured gas.
