Shingon Buddhism

Shingon Buddhism
Founder: Kūkai (774–835)
Headquarters: Mount Kōya, Japan
Tradition: Vajrayana (esoteric Buddhism)

Shingon Buddhism (Japanese: "True Word") is one of the major schools of Japanese Buddhism and one of the few surviving Vajrayana lineages in East Asia. It was founded by the monk Kūkai (posthumously Kōbō Daishi), who brought esoteric teachings from Tang China in 806.

Teachings

Shingon holds that enlightenment is attainable in this very body through the "three mysteries" — mudra (body), mantra (speech) and mandala visualisation (mind) — aligned with the cosmic Buddha Mahāvairocana. Its key scriptures are the Mahāvairocana and Vajraśekhara sutras, represented in the paired Womb Realm and Diamond Realm mandalas. Its headquarters is the monastic complex on Mount Kōya.
