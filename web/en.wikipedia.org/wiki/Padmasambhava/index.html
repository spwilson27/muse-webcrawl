Padmasambhava (Sanskrit: "He who is born from a lotus"), also known as Guru Rinpoche, was an 8th-century Buddhist master from the Indian subcontinent who is credited with introducing Vajrayana (Tantric) Buddhism to Tibet and Bhutan. Tibetans revere him as a "second Buddha"; his influence is greatest in the Nyingma school, the oldest tradition of Tibetan Buddhism.

Life and legend

According to traditional accounts, Padmasambhava was miraculously born as a fully formed child on a lotus in Lake Dhanakosha in the kingdom of Oddiyana, in the north-west of the Indian subcontinent, and was adopted by King Indrabodhi. Renouncing royal life, he studied and practised tantra in charnel grounds and at great seats of learning such as Nalanda, acquiring fame as a powerful yogin and subduer of demons.

Padmasambhava was invited to Tibet by King Trisong Detsen, on the advice of the scholar-monk Śāntarakṣita, whose efforts to establish Buddhism at court were said to be obstructed by local spirits. Padmasambhava subdued the obstructing deities, binding them as protectors of the Dharma, and together with Śāntarakṣita founded Samye, Tibet's first Buddhist monastery, consecrated around 779. He initiated the first Tibetan monks and oversaw, with a team of translators, an immense project of rendering Indian Buddhist scriptures into Tibetan.

Teachings and terma

Padmasambhava is associated with teachings presented as terma — "treasures" of text and objects concealed in the earth, in caves, and in the minds of disciples, to be discovered at the right time by prophesied revealers (tertöns). His consort Yeshe Tsogyal is traditionally credited with recording and concealing many of these teachings. The most famous text attributed to him in the West is the Bardo Thodol, known as the Tibetan Book of the Dead, a terma revealed by Karma Lingpa in the 14th century. His mantra, Oṃ āḥ hūṃ vajra guru padma siddhi hūṃ, is recited throughout the Tibetan Buddhist world, and the Seven-Line Prayer to him is among the most beloved Tibetan liturgies.

Iconography and legacy

Padmasambhava is depicted in eight principal manifestations, most commonly as a richly dressed figure wearing a lotus hat, holding a vajra and a skull-cup, with a khatvanga staff resting in the crook of his arm. Giant statues of Guru Rinpoche stand across the Himalayas, including the great statue at Rewalsar (Tso Pema) in India and the Guru Nangsi Zilnon statue overlooking Samdruptse in Sikkim. The Paro Taktsang ("Tiger's Nest") monastery in Bhutan, where he is said to have meditated in a cave, is one of the most sacred sites of the Himalayan Buddhist world.
