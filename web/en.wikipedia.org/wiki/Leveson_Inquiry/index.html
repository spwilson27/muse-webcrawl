Leveson Inquiry

The Leveson Inquiry was a judicial public inquiry into the culture, practices and ethics of the British press, chaired by Lord Justice Leveson. It was announced on 13 July 2011 by Prime Minister David Cameron in the wake of the News International phone hacking scandal, which had revealed that journalists at the News of the World had hacked the voicemail of murdered schoolgirl Milly Dowler, relatives of deceased British soldiers, and victims of the 7 July 2005 London bombings.

Proceedings

The inquiry sat in the Royal Courts of Justice from November 2011, hearing evidence from 337 witnesses and receiving statements from hundreds more. Witnesses included victims of press intrusion, serving and former newspaper editors and proprietors — among them Rupert Murdoch — politicians including three prime ministers, police officers, and celebrities such as Hugh Grant, J. K. Rowling, and Sienna Miller. Core participants received legal representation funded from public money, and the hearings were televised.

Report

The Leveson Report was published on 29 November 2012. Running to nearly 2,000 pages across four volumes, it concluded that the existing Press Complaints Commission was inadequate and that a new independent regulator was needed, recognized by a statutory body but free of government and industry control. Leveson stated there had been a "recklessness" in portions of the press and found no evidence of widespread police corruption, though he criticized overly close relationships between politicians and media proprietors.

Aftermath

The main political parties negotiated a Royal Charter establishing a recognition panel, but most major publishers refused to join the recognized regulator (IMPRESS) and instead formed IPSO, which is not recognized under the charter system. A planned second part of the inquiry, into unlawful conduct by specific newspapers and police corruption, was cancelled by the government in 2018, a decision criticized by victims' campaigners such as Hacked Off.
