Star Wars

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The original 1977 poster-style tableau: Luke with his lightsaber raised, Leia beside him, Darth Vader's helmet looming over the starfield]

Star Wars is a 1977 American epic space opera film written and directed by George Lucas, the first instalment of the Star Wars franchise and the fourth episode of the 'Skywalker Saga' in internal chronology. It follows Luke Skywalker, a farm boy on the desert planet Tatooine, drawn by two droids and the Jedi Knight Obi-Wan Kenobi into the Rebel Alliance's fight against the Galactic Empire — rescuing Princess Leia, learning the Force, and destroying the Death Star in a trench run that channels every serial and war film Lucas loved.

Made for $11 million and expected to fail, it became the highest-grossing film of its era, saved 20th Century Fox, industrialised the modern blockbuster (with Jaws), founded Industrial Light & Magic, and spawned the largest entertainment franchise in history — sequels, prequels, spin-offs, the Disney acquisition of Lucasfilm for $4.05 billion in 2012, and an ocean of merchandise.

Making

Lucas, frustrated that he could not film Flash Gordon, wrote his own myth from Joseph Campbell's monomyth, Kurosawa's The Hidden Fortress, Westerns and war films; the production in Tunisia and Elstree was miserable — models unfinished, the crew sceptical, the studio ready to close it down. John Williams's symphonic score, Ben Burtt's sound design (the lightsaber's hum) and the editors' rescue job in post-production are credited with carrying the film over its rough shoot. It won six Academy Awards.

The phenomenon

Star Wars fixed the grammar of the modern franchise: the sequel trilogy (The Empire Strikes Back, 1980, generally ranked the series' best; Return of the Jedi, 1983), prequels (1999–2005), Disney sequels (2015–19) and streaming series (The Mandalorian onward). Its imagery — Vader's breath, the twin suns, 'May the Force be with you' (the greeting universally known) — is global common property, and arguments about its special editions' alterations are a culture war of their own.
