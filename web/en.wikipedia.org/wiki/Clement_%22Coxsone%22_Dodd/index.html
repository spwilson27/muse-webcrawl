Clement "Coxsone" Dodd

Clement Seymour "Coxsone" Dodd (26 January 1932 – 5 May 2004) was a Jamaican record producer who was hugely influential in the development of ska and reggae in the 1960s and 1970s. His nickname "Coxsone" derived from Alec Coxon, a Yorkshire cricketer whose bowling style his own was compared to in his youth.

Career

Dodd began by running a sound system, Sir Coxsone's Downbeat, in Kingston in the 1950s, travelling to the United States to buy rhythm and blues records. As American music styles changed, he began recording local musicians, and in 1963 he opened Studio One at 13 Brentford Road in Kingston — one of Jamaica's first recording studios owned by a Black Jamaican. His label, also called Studio One and often dubbed "the Motown of Jamaica", launched or developed the careers of Bob Marley and the Wailers, the Skatalites, Alton Ellis, Ken Boothe, Dennis Brown, Burning Spear, and many others. House musicians at the studio included the Skatalites and later the Soul Brothers and Sound Dimension, whose rhythms became foundational reggae "riddims".

Later life and legacy

Dodd relocated to New York in the late 1970s as Jamaica's music scene shifted, though Studio One releases continued and its catalogue was extensively reissued. He was awarded the Order of Distinction by the Jamaican government. He died of a heart attack in Kingston in 2004, shortly after attending a ceremony at which Brentford Road was renamed Studio One Boulevard.
