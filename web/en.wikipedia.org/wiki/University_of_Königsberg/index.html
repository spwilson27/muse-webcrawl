University of Königsberg

University
Founded: 1544
Dissolved: 1945
Location: Königsberg, East Prussia (now Kaliningrad, Russia)
Name: Albertina

The University of Königsberg (German: Albertus-Universität Königsberg), known as the Albertina, was the university of Königsberg in East Prussia. It was founded in 1544 by Duke Albert of Prussia as the second Protestant academy after the University of Marburg, and it operated for four centuries.

History

Modeled on the University of Kraków, the Albertina had faculties of theology, law, medicine, and philosophy. Its most famous professor was Immanuel Kant, who studied and taught there his entire life, becoming rector in 1786. The mathematician David Hilbert was born in Königsberg and educated at the university, which also employed Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi and hosted the Königsberg Observatory directed by Friedrich Wilhelm Bessel, who made the first successful measurement of stellar parallax there in 1838.

New building and final years

A grand new main building on Paradeplatz opened in 1862. The university celebrated its 400th anniversary in 1944, shortly before RAF bombing raids devastated Königsberg in August 1944 and the Soviet siege of 1945 ended German Königsberg. Staff and students were evacuated westward, and the university ceased to exist in 1945.

Successor

After the war, the Soviet Union established an institution on the site, today's Immanuel Kant Baltic Federal University in Kaliningrad, which claims the Albertina's heritage; Kant's tomb by the cathedral remains a city landmark.
