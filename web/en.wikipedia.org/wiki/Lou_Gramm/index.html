Lou Gramm

Lou Gramm (born Louis Andrew Grammatico, May 2, 1950) is an American singer and songwriter, best known as the original lead vocalist of the rock band Foreigner. His powerful tenor powered hits including "Cold as Ice", "Hot Blooded", "Double Vision", "Urgent", "Juke Box Hero", "Waiting for a Girl Like You" and the number-one ballad "I Want to Know What Love Is".

Career

Born in Rochester, New York, Gramm sang with the band Black Sheep, whose albums were released on Capitol in the mid-1970s. In 1976 he joined Mick Jones in the new Anglo-American group Foreigner. Their debut album (1977) sold millions, and through the late 1970s and 1980s Foreigner became one of the world's biggest rock acts. Gramm also pursued a solo career, releasing Ready or Not (1987), which included the hit "Midnight Blue", and Long Hard Look (1989).

Illness and later years

In 1997 Gramm was diagnosed with a craniopharyngioma, a benign brain tumour; surgery affected his voice and stamina, though he returned to performing after recovery. He left Foreigner in 2003 amid friction with Jones, but reunited with the band for selected appearances from 2017 onward for its 40th anniversary. Gramm and Jones were inducted into the Songwriters Hall of Fame in 2013, and Foreigner entered the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2024. Gramm's autobiography, Juke Box Hero: My Five Decades in Rock 'n' Roll, appeared in 2013.
