Leica

Leica is a brand of cameras, lenses, binoculars and other optical equipment made by the German company Ernst Leitz GmbH of Wetzlar, now Leica Camera AG. The name combines the founder's surname, Leitz, with the first syllable of camera. Leica created the first practical 35 mm film camera, the Leica I of 1925, and thereby founded modern photojournalism and street photography.

History

The optical works founded by Ernst Leitz in Wetzlar in 1869 (successor to Carl Kellner's institute of 1849) manufactured microscopes before employee Oskar Barnack built the Ur-Leica prototype in 1913–14 — a compact camera using 35 mm cine film, intended as an exposure meter for movie cameras. Delayed by the First World War, the design was marketed in 1925 as the Leica I (Model A). In 1930–32 the interchangeable-lens and rangefinder models, culminating in the Leica II, established the classic form. The Leica M3 of 1954 became one of the most influential cameras ever made.

Cultural significance

Small, quiet and superbly built, Leicas were the tools of Henri Cartier-Bresson, Robert Capa, Alfred Eisenstaedt and many other defining photographers of the twentieth century. During the Second World War, Leitz employees helped Jewish staff and their families escape Nazi Germany, an episode later called the "Leica Freedom Train".

Products

The current range includes the M rangefinder line (film and digital), the SL mirrorless system, the Q fixed-lens compact cameras, and lenses made to famously exacting tolerances in Wetzlar. Leica's red dot logo, introduced in 1964, is one of the most recognisable marks in photography. The wider Leica brand also extends to Leica Microsystems, Leica Geosystems and Leica Biosystems, now separate companies.
