United States Marine Band

The United States Marine Band is the premier band of the United States Marine Corps and the oldest continuously active professional musical organisation in the United States. Established by an Act of Congress on 11 July 1798, it is known as "The President's Own" — a title reflecting its unique mission of providing music for the President of the United States and the Commandant of the Marine Corps. The band performs at the White House, state ceremonies, presidential inaugurations and funerals, and in public concerts and tours.

History

The original 1798 Marine Corps legislation authorised a "drum major, fife major and 32 drums and fifes". The band moved with the government to Washington in 1800 and played for President John Adams at the White House on New Year's Day 1801; under President Thomas Jefferson, who took a personal interest in its repertoire, it became central to official Washington music. In 1861 an act of Congress fixed its modern instrumentation and organisation.

Sousa era

The band's most famous leader was John Philip Sousa, director from 1880 to 1892, who raised it to world standard, composed many of his celebrated marches for it — including "Semper Fidelis" (1888), later the Marine Corps' official march, and "The Washington Post" — and began its tradition of annual concert tours in 1891. Sousa's successors have maintained the band at the highest level; notable later directors include William F. Santelmann and Colonel John R. Bourgeois. Colonel Jason K. Fettig became the band's 28th director in 2014.

Today

The full Marine Band of about 160 members includes the Marine Orchestra and Marine Chamber Ensembles. It performs several hundred commitments a year, gives free public concerts in Washington, records extensively, and undertakes a national concert tour each autumn. Unusually for a service band, its members enlist specifically for duty with "The President's Own" and do not undergo standard Marine recruit training, holding the rank of Staff Sergeant on entry.
