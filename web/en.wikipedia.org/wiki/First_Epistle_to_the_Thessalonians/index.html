The First Epistle to the Thessalonians, commonly abbreviated 1 Thessalonians, is a book of the New Testament of the Christian Bible. It is a letter written by the apostle Paul (together with Silvanus and Timothy) to the church in Thessalonica, in modern-day Greece, and is widely regarded by scholars as the earliest surviving Christian text and the earliest of Paul's letters, dating to about AD 49–51.

Background

According to the Acts of the Apostles, Paul founded the Thessalonian church during his second missionary journey but was driven out by opposition after only a short stay. Concerned for the young community, he sent Timothy from Athens (or Corinth) to strengthen it, and wrote this letter in response to Timothy's encouraging report — probably from Corinth around AD 50.

Contents

  • Chapters 1–3: Thanksgiving for the Thessalonians' faith, love, and endurance under persecution; Paul defends his ministry and expresses longing to revisit them.
  • Chapter 4: Exhortations to holiness and mutual love; then, responding to grief over believers who had died, Paul teaches that the dead in Christ will rise first at the parousia (Second Coming), followed by the living — the passage from which the doctrine of the "rapture" is partly derived.
  • Chapter 5: The Day of the Lord will come "like a thief in the night"; closing instructions on community life, prayer, and prophecy.

Themes

The letter's overriding concern is eschatological: the young church expected Christ's return imminently, and Paul comforts them about deceased members while urging watchful, sober living. It also stresses manual labour and quiet conduct ("work with your hands"), love of the brethren, and joy under affliction. Famous phrases include "pray without ceasing" (5:17) and "Prove all things; hold fast that which is good" (5:21, KJV).

Authenticity

Unlike its companion letter, 2 Thessalonians — whose authorship is debated — 1 Thessalonians is accepted as genuinely Pauline by the overwhelming majority of scholars, making it a primary source for the beliefs of the earliest Christian communities within twenty years of the crucifixion.
