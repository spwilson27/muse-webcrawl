Ben Nicholson

Born10 April 1894, Denham, Buckinghamshire, England
Died6 February 1982, London, England
Known forPainting, relief sculpture
MovementAbstract art, St Ives School

Benjamin Lauder Nicholson (10 April 1894 – 6 February 1982) was an English painter of abstract compositions (sometimes in low relief) and still-life subjects. The son of the painters William Nicholson and Mabel Pryde, he briefly attended the Slade School of Fine Art before travelling in Europe and the United States. He was a central figure in the development of abstract art in Britain.

Early work

Nicholson's early paintings were still lifes influenced by Cubism. In 1924 he painted his first abstract works, and in 1927, after a visit to the Cornish fishing village of St Ives, he encountered the naive paintings of Alfred Wallis, whose directness influenced him deeply.

Abstraction and the white reliefs

During the 1930s Nicholson was associated with the Paris-based Abstraction-Création group and with artists such as Piet Mondrian and Naum Gabo. His celebrated carved white reliefs of 1934–35, composed of circles and rectangles cut into board and painted white, are among the purest expressions of British abstract art. He co-edited the influential volume Circle: International Survey of Constructive Art (1937).

Later career

Nicholson married the sculptor Barbara Hepworth in 1938 (they divorced in 1951); together they made St Ives a centre of modern art. He won the first Guggenheim International Painting Prize in 1956 and the São Paulo Biennale's international painting prize in 1957. A major retrospective was held at Tate Britain in 1955. He continued working in London and Switzerland until his death in 1982.
