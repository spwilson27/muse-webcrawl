Miguel López de Legazpi

Miguel López de Legazpi (c. 1502 – August 20, 1572), also known as El Adelantado, was a Spanish explorer and conquistador who established the first Spanish settlement in the Philippines, founding Cebu in 1565 and Manila in 1571. He served as the first governor-general of the Spanish East Indies from 1565 until his death.

Early life

Legazpi was born into a minor noble Basque family and spent some three decades as a colonial administrator in New Spain (Mexico), where he rose to senior posts in Mexico City and amassed considerable wealth.

The 1564 expedition

Chosen by Viceroy Luis de Velasco to lead an expedition across the Pacific, Legazpi sailed from Navidad, Mexico, in November 1564 with five ships and about 380 men. His chief pilot was the Augustinian friar Andrés de Urdaneta. The expedition reached Cebu in April 1565, where Legazpi formed a blood compact (sandugo) with Datu Sikatuna of Bohol and founded the settlement of San Miguel (later Cebu), the first Spanish colony in the archipelago. A major achievement of the voyage was Urdaneta's discovery of the return route across the northern Pacific — the tornaviaje — which made the Manila galleon trade possible for the next 250 years.

Manila and death

In 1571 Legazpi conquered and refounded Manila, making it the capital of the Spanish East Indies. He died of a stroke in Manila in August 1572 and is buried in San Agustin Church in Intramuros. The city of Legazpi in Albay province is named after him, as is the Order of the Spanish heritage he established in the Pacific.
