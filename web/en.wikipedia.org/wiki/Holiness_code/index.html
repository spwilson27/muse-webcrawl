Holiness code

The Holiness code is a term used in biblical studies for the collection of laws in chapters 17–26 of the Book of Leviticus in the Hebrew Bible. It is called the Holiness code because of its frequent refrain "You shall be holy, for I the LORD your God am holy" (Leviticus 19:2). In documentary-hypothesis scholarship it is usually treated as a distinct stratum (H) within, or editorially related to, the Priestly source (P).

Contents

The code addresses sacrifice and the handling of blood (ch. 17), prohibited sexual relations (ch. 18), ethical and ritual commandments including love of neighbour and care for the poor (ch. 19), penalties (ch. 20), rules for priests (chs. 21–22), festivals (ch. 23), the sabbatical and jubilee years (ch. 25), and blessings and curses concluding the book's legal material (ch. 26). Chapter 19, sometimes called the "little Torah", mixes ritual and moral precepts and contains the command "you shall love your neighbour as yourself" (19:18).

Scholarly discussion

Scholars debate the code's date and its relationship to the rest of Leviticus: some see it as a later Holiness school redaction of earlier priestly material, others as an originally independent composition. Its emphasis on holiness extending beyond the priesthood to all Israel and the land distinguishes it from surrounding Priestly texts.
