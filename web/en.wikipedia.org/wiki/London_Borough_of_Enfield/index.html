The London Borough of Enfield is a London borough in north London. It was created in 1965 from the merger of the former municipal boroughs of Enfield, Edmonton and Southgate. It is the northernmost London borough and borders Hertfordshire to the north and the boroughs of Barnet, Haringey, Waltham Forest, Epping Forest's district and Broxbourne.

Enfield Town, the borough's administrative centre.

History and character

Enfield includes Edmonton, where the Royal Small Arms Factory at Enfield Lock produced the famous Lee–Enfield rifle used by the British Army for much of the 20th century. The world's first cash machine (ATM) was installed at a Barclays bank branch in Enfield Town in 1967. The western part of the borough is semi-rural, including Enfield Chase, Crews Hill's garden centres and the open farmland of the green belt; the east, along the Lea Valley, is more industrial and densely populated.

Districts and transport

Principal districts include Enfield Town, Edmonton, Southgate, Palmers Green, Winchmore Hill, Bush Hill Park, Cockfosters and Ponders End. The borough is served by the Piccadilly line at its western edge, the Victoria line at nearby Seven Sisters, and several Lea Valley and Great Northern rail lines, as well as the North Circular Road (A406) and the M25 at its northern boundary.

Governance

Enfield London Borough Council is elected every four years. Large regeneration schemes, notably the Meridian Water development on former industrial land beside the Lea Valley, are planned to add thousands of homes.
