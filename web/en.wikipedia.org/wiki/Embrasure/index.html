Embrasure

An embrasure (or crenel) is the opening in a battlement between the raised portions known as merlons. In modern usage it more generally denotes an opening in a wall or parapet, often splayed on the inside, through which weapons may be fired.

Fortification

In medieval fortification, embrasures allowed archers and crossbowmen to shoot at attackers while remaining protected. The interior splay widened the field of fire while the narrow exterior opening minimized the target presented to the enemy. With the advent of gunpowder artillery, embrasures were adapted for cannon in fortress walls and later in the casemates of warships and coastal batteries.

Other uses

The term is also used in architecture for a splayed enlargement of a door or window opening toward the inside of a wall, improving the admission of light. In dentistry, an embrasure refers to the V-shaped space between the proximal surfaces of two adjacent teeth.
