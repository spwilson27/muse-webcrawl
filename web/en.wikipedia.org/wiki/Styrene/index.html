Styrene is an organic compound with the chemical formula C6H5CH=CH2, also known as vinylbenzene or phenylethylene. It is a colourless oily liquid with a sweet smell that evaporates easily and polymerises readily. Styrene is one of the world's most important industrial monomers: tens of millions of tonnes are produced each year as the precursor to polystyrene and numerous copolymers.

Production

Nearly all styrene is made by the catalytic dehydrogenation of ethylbenzene, itself produced by alkylating benzene with ethylene. Ethylbenzene vapour is passed with steam over an iron-oxide catalyst at around 600 °C. Styrene occurs naturally in small amounts in plants such as cinnamon and in foods, and was first isolated in the 19th century from the resin storax (hence the name).

Uses and safety

Polymerisation gives polystyrene, ABS plastic, styrene-butadiene rubber (SBR) and latexes, unsaturated polyester resins and ion-exchange resins. Styrene is classified by the IARC as "probably carcinogenic to humans" (Group 2A), and occupational exposure in reinforced-plastics manufacture is regulated because of effects on the nervous system and irritation of eyes and airways. The 2020 styrene gas leak at Visakhapatnam, India, which killed at least 11 people, illustrated the hazards of storing the reactive monomer.
