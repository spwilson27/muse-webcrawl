Mass casualty incident

A mass casualty incident (MCI; also called a multiple-casualty incident) is an emergency situation in which the number of victims overwhelms the response capacity of local emergency services and hospitals, requiring special organisation, mutual aid, and triage to do the greatest good for the greatest number of patients. Such incidents may result from transportation disasters, fires, explosions, natural disasters, hazardous materials releases, or acts of violence.

Response

MCI response is typically organised under an incident command system, with designated incident commanders, staging areas, and casualty collection points. The first arriving unit establishes command, sizes up the scene, and requests additional resources. Patients are sorted by triage systems such as START (Simple Triage and Rapid Treatment), which classifies victims by coloured tags: red (immediate), yellow (delayed), green (minor, "walking wounded"), and black or grey (deceased or expectant). Treatment and transport are prioritised accordingly, and hospitals activate disaster plans to clear beds and recall staff.

Triage principles

Unlike ordinary emergency medicine, where the most gravely injured patient is treated first, mass casualty triage aims to maximise overall survival: patients who cannot be saved with available resources may be categorised as expectant so that responders can concentrate on salvageable victims.
