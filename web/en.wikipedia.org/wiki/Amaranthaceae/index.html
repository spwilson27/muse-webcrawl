Amaranthaceae

Amaranthaceae
Order: Caryophyllales
Genera: about 165
Species: about 2,000

Amaranthaceae is a family of flowering plants commonly known as the amaranth family, in reference to its type genus Amaranthus. In the APG system, the family includes the former goosefoot family Chenopodiaceae and contains about 165 genera and 2,040 species, making it the most species-rich lineage within the order Caryophyllales.

Description

Most species are annual or perennial herbs, though some are shrubs or small trees. Many are adapted to saline or arid soils, and the family includes numerous C4 photosynthetic species. Many members produce betalain pigments rather than anthocyanins, a hallmark of the Caryophyllales.

Economic importance

The family includes important crops such as spinach, beetroot, sugar beet, chard, quinoa, and grain amaranths, as well as popular ornamentals such as cockscomb (Celosia) and globe amaranth. Some species, such as Palmer amaranth, are serious agricultural weeds.
