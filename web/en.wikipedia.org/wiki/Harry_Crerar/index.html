General Henry Duncan Graham "Harry" Crerar (28 April 1888 – 1 April 1965) was a senior officer of the Canadian Army and the country's leading field commander in the Second World War. He commanded the First Canadian Army during the campaigns in North-West Europe in 1944–45, including the Battle of the Scheldt and the liberation of the Netherlands.

Early life and First World War

Crerar was born in Hamilton, Ontario, and educated at Upper Canada College and the Royal Military College of Canada at Kingston. Trained as an artillery officer, he served with distinction in the First World War, rising to command a brigade of Canadian Corps artillery and earning the Distinguished Service Order.

Interwar years

Between the wars Crerar held a series of staff appointments, attended the Staff College at Camberley in England, and became one of the small professional cadre around which the Canadian Army expanded after 1939. He served as Chief of the General Staff in 1940–41 before proceeding overseas.

Second World War

Crerar commanded the 2nd Canadian Infantry Division and then I Canadian Corps in England and Italy. In March 1944 he took command of the First Canadian Army, the largest field formation ever commanded by a Canadian. Under his leadership the army fought through Normandy in the operations around Falaise, cleared the Channel ports, opened the Scheldt estuary to shipping for Antwerp, and in early 1945 advanced through the Reichswald in Operation Veritable before crossing the Rhine and liberating much of the Netherlands.

Later life

Crerar retired in 1946 and served as aide-de-camp to Kings George VI and Queen Elizabeth II. He died in Ottawa in 1965 and was buried with full military honours.
