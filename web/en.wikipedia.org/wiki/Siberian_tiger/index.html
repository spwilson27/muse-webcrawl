Siberian tiger

The Siberian tiger, also called the Amur tiger (Panthera tigris altaica), is a population of the tiger subspecies Panthera tigris tigris native to the Russian Far East, northeastern China, and possibly North Korea. It once ranged across the Korean Peninsula and into Manchuria, but was reduced by hunting to near extinction in the 1940s.

Description

The Siberian tiger is among the largest cats ever to exist. Males typically weigh 180–306 kg (400–675 lb) with a head-and-body length of 190–230 cm, though historical reports are larger. Its pale orange coat with widely spaced dark stripes and thick fur suit it to cold, snowy forests of birch, Korean pine, and Mongolian oak. Prey include wild boar, red deer, sika deer, roe deer, and Manchurian wapiti.

Conservation

Protected by the Soviet Union in 1947—the first country to grant the tiger full protection—the population recovered from a low of a few dozen to an estimated 480–540 individuals in Russia in the 2015 census, with a small population in China bolstered by the Northeast China Tiger and Leopard National Park (est. 2021). Major threats are poaching and habitat fragmentation. It is classified as Endangered on the IUCN Red List.
