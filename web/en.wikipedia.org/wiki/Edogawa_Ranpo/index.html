Edogawa Ranpo

Edogawa Ranpo
Birth name: Tarō Hirai
Born: 21 October 1894
Died: 28 July 1965
Genre: Detective fiction, ero guro

Tarō Hirai (1894–1965), better known by the pen name Edogawa Ranpo (also Rampo), was a Japanese author and critic who played a major role in the development of Japanese mystery and thriller fiction. His pen name is a rendering of Edgar Allan Poe, whom he admired.

Career

His debut 'The Two-Sen Copper Coin' (1923) is considered the first modern Japanese detective story. He created detective Kogoro Akechi, who debuted in 'The Case of the Murder on D. Hill' and later faced the Fiend with Twenty Faces in popular boys' novels. Works such as 'The Human Chair', 'Strange Tale of Panorama Island' and 'The Caterpillar' blend detection with the erotic-grotesque (ero guro).

Legacy

Ranpo founded the Mystery Writers of Japan; its Edogawa Rampo Award honours new writers. His work has been widely adapted to film and anime. See also Japanese literature.
