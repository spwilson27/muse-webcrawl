King mackerel

Kingdom: Animalia

Family: Scombridae

Genus: Scomberomorus

Species: S. cavalla

The king mackerel (Scomberomorus cavalla), also called the cavalla or kingfish, is a migratory species of mackerel found in the western Atlantic Ocean and the Gulf of Mexico. It is an important fish for both commercial fleets and recreational anglers.

Description

The king mackerel is a medium-sized fish, commonly encountered at about 80 cm in length and 5–14 kg in weight, though specimens over 40 kg have been recorded. It has a streamlined, silvery body without the bars or spots seen in some relatives; juveniles bear yellowish spots similar to those of the Spanish mackerel. The lateral line drops sharply below the second dorsal fin, a key identification feature.

Distribution and behaviour

The species ranges from the Gulf of Maine to Brazil, and is common in the Gulf of Mexico and off the southeastern United States. King mackerel are pelagic predators that feed on sardines, anchovies, squid and other small fishes, often in loose schools. They undertake seasonal migrations between warm summer feeding grounds and warmer winter waters.

Fisheries and consumption

King mackerel are taken by hook-and-line, gillnets and trolling, and are prized as a sport fish. Because they are large, long-lived predators, they accumulate mercury, and health authorities advise limiting consumption — especially for children and pregnant women. The flesh is oily and is eaten grilled, smoked or as steaks.
