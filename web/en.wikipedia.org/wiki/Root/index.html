Root

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In vascular plants, the root is the organ — typically underground — that anchors the plant, absorbs water and mineral nutrients from the soil, and stores food. Roots grow downward (positive gravitropism), branch extensively, and together the root systems of a single plant can total many kilometres of fine roots and root hairs.

Types and structure

Plants have taproots (a main root with branches — carrot, oak) or fibrous root systems (many similar roots — grasses). The growing tip is protected by a root cap and produces cells in zones of division, elongation and maturation; root hairs just behind the tip create most of the absorptive surface. Many roots partner with mycorrhizal fungi that greatly extend their reach for phosphorus and water.

Uses and ecology

Roots are major foods (carrots, cassava, sweet potatoes, sugar beet), medicines (ginseng, liquorice) and soil engineers: they prevent erosion, pump carbon underground and host much of soil biodiversity. Adventitious roots arise from stems — in mangrove stilts, maize braces and climbing ivies. Root damage, waterlogging and soil compaction are common limits on plant growth.
