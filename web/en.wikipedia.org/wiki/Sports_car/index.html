Sports car

A sports car is a car designed with an emphasis on dynamic performance, such as handling, acceleration, top speed, the thrill of driving and racing capability. Sports cars originated in Europe in the early 1900s and are currently produced by numerous manufacturers in standard production runs, in contrast to more exclusive supercars and hypercars.

Traditional sports cars were typically two-seat roadsters—open-top cars with minimal weather protection—exemplified by early MG, Triumph and Morgan models in Britain and the Alfa Romeos and Fiats of Italy. The classic layout is front-engine, rear-wheel drive, though mid-engine and rear-engine designs (such as the Porsche 911) and all-wheel drive have become common. The Mazda MX-5, launched in 1989, revived the lightweight roadster formula and became the best-selling two-seat sports car in history.

Definitions and categories

There is no universally agreed definition of a sports car; the term is used variously for pure two-seat performance cars, grand tourers designed for high-speed long-distance comfort, and high-performance variants of ordinary cars ("sports sedans" and "hot hatches"). Regulatory and insurance definitions likewise differ between countries. Related categories include muscle cars and pony cars in North America, which emphasise straight-line acceleration, and supercars, which prioritise extreme performance and exclusivity.

Racing

Sports car racing is one of the oldest forms of motorsport, encompassing endurance events such as the 24 Hours of Le Mans, the Daytona 24 Hours and the Sebring 12 Hours, contested by purpose-built prototypes and production-based GT cars under series including the FIA World Endurance Championship and the IMSA SportsCar Championship.
