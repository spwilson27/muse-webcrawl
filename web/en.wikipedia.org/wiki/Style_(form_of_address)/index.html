Style (form of address)

A style of office, or form of address, is an official or legally recognised form of address used with, and as a title for, a person when second-person pronouns must be avoided or a dignified form is desired. Styles are commonly attached to royalty, nobility, clergy, judges, and holders of high political office. Examples include "His Majesty" for a king, "Her Royal Highness" for certain princesses, "His Holiness" for the Pope, "The Right Honourable" for privy counsellors in the United Kingdom, and "His Excellency" for ambassadors and heads of state.

Usage

Styles may be used in direct address, in reference (third person), and in formal correspondence, with conventions varying by country and institution. In many republics, styles are simpler or absent altogether; for example, the President of the United States is formally addressed as "Mr. President" or "Madam President" rather than by an honorific style, although "The Honorable" is used in written reference.

Historical development

The elaborate system of styles developed in European courts from late antiquity and the Byzantine Empire, spreading through feudal and diplomatic practice. Treaties and protocol manuals, such as those produced after the Congress of Vienna, codified many usages that persist in modern diplomacy.
