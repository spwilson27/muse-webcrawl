Chamber of Deputies (Mexico)

The Chamber of Deputies (Spanish: Cámara de Diputados) is the lower house of the Congress of the Union, the bicameral legislature of Mexico. Together with the Senate of the Republic, it exercises the legislative power of the federation under the Constitution of 1917. The chamber meets in the Legislative Palace of San Lázaro in Mexico City.

Composition and election

The chamber has 500 deputies. Three hundred are elected by plurality vote in single-member federal electoral districts, and 200 are allocated by proportional representation from closed party lists in five multi-state electoral regions (circunscripciones), with rules designed to limit over-representation: no party may hold more than 300 seats overall or exceed its national vote share by more than eight percentage points. Deputies serve three-year terms; consecutive re-election, prohibited for most of the post-revolutionary era, was restored by a 2014 constitutional reform, allowing deputies to serve up to four consecutive terms (12 years) beginning with those elected in 2018 and later.

Powers

The Chamber of Deputies shares most legislative functions with the Senate but holds exclusive powers over the federal budget of expenditures (Presupuesto de Egresos), which it approves alone; it also oversees the Superior Audit of the Federation, can bring impeachment proceedings (juicio político) against public officials, and serves as the electoral college in certain exceptional contingencies for appointing an interim president. Revenue laws must originate in the Chamber of Deputies.

History

Bicameralism in Mexico dates to the Constitution of 1824. The Senate was suppressed under the Constitution of 1857 and restored in 1874, since when the Chamber of Deputies has sat as the popular chamber. The mixed electoral system, with a substantial proportional-representation tier, was developed through electoral reforms beginning in 1963 ("party deputies") and consolidated in 1977 and later reforms, which progressively opened the chamber to opposition parties after decades of dominance by the Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI).
