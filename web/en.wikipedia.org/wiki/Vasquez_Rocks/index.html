Vasquez Rocks

Vasquez Rocks Natural Area Park is a 932-acre park in the Sierra Pelona Mountains of northern Los Angeles County, California, near Agua Dulce. It is famous for its dramatic tilted rock formations, which rise at steep angles from the desert floor and have served as a filming location for hundreds of movies, television episodes and commercials.

The park's signature formation tilts nearly 50 degrees from horizontal.

Geology

The formations are sedimentary sandstone and conglomerate layers deposited about 25 million years ago in an ancient basin. Tectonic activity along the San Andreas and associated faults later uplifted and tilted the strata — some beds now stand at angles approaching 50 degrees — and erosion sculpted the exposed edges into jagged fins and slabs.

History

The area was long inhabited by the Tataviam people. The rocks take their name from Tiburcio Vásquez, a notorious Californio bandit of the 1870s who repeatedly used the formations as a hideout to evade capture. Los Angeles County acquired the land in stages beginning in the 1970s, and the park was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 1972.

Filming location

Vasquez Rocks is one of the most recognisable locations in Hollywood history. It appears in classic Westerns, in Blazing Saddles, The Flintstones (1994), and in numerous science-fiction productions. It is especially beloved by Star Trek fans: the rocks form the backdrop for Captain Kirk's fight with the Gorn in the original-series episode "Arena", and the location has reappeared in several Trek films and series. Other productions include Buffy the Vampire Slayer, Power Rangers, Short Circuit and Michael Jackson's "Black or White" video.

Recreation

The park offers hiking and equestrian trails, including a segment of the Pacific Crest Trail, rock climbing, picnicking and an interpretive centre covering local geology and Native American history.
