Medieval Times

Medieval Times Dinner and Tournament is a family dinner theater featuring staged medieval-style games, sword-fighting, and jousting, performed by costumed actors and trained horses in castle-like venues. Guests eat a four-course meal without utensils while watching knights compete.

History

The concept originated in Spain, where the first shows were staged in the 1970s by the company that later expanded to the United States. The first American location opened in Kissimmee, Florida, in 1983. The chain grew across North America, with castles in cities including Buena Park (California), Lyndhurst (New Jersey), Schaumburg (Illinois), Dallas (Texas), Myrtle Beach (South Carolina), Atlanta (Georgia), Baltimore/Washington (Maryland), Toronto (Canada), and Scottsdale (Arizona). The company is headquartered in Irving, Texas.

The show

Guests are seated in sections color-coded to one of several knights (historically six), whom they cheer during the tournament. The performance typically includes Andalusian horses performing dressage, falconry, jousting passes with breakaway lances, sword fights, and a story line involving a king, queen, and a villainous challenger. The fixed menu traditionally includes tomato bisque, garlic bread, roasted chicken, sweet corn, herb-basted potatoes, and a pastry dessert, with drinks served by "serfs" and "wenches."

In popular culture

The chain gained wide exposure through an extended scene in the 1996 film The Cable Guy, and it is frequently referenced in American popular culture as a distinctive example of themed entertainment dining.
