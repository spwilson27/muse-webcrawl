Cognitive symptom

Cognitive symptoms are disturbances in mental functions such as perception, attention, memory, language, reasoning and executive function that accompany neurological or psychiatric disorders. They are non-motor or non-affective manifestations that can substantially affect a person's ability to carry out everyday activities, work and social functioning.

Overview

Cognitive symptoms are reported in a wide range of conditions, including dementia, schizophrenia, depression, multiple sclerosis, traumatic brain injury and long COVID. In schizophrenia, cognitive impairment is considered a core feature of the illness and a major determinant of functional outcome, sometimes grouped with positive and negative symptoms.

Types

Commonly assessed domains include:

  • Attention and processing speed
  • Working memory and episodic memory
  • Executive function, such as planning and cognitive flexibility
  • Language and verbal fluency
  • Visuospatial abilities
  • Social cognition

Assessment

Cognitive symptoms are measured with screening instruments such as the Mini-Mental State Examination and the Montreal Cognitive Assessment, as well as batteries such as the MATRICS Consensus Cognitive Battery developed for clinical trials in schizophrenia.

Treatment

Management addresses the underlying condition and may include cognitive remediation therapy, psychosocial rehabilitation and pharmacological treatment. Cognitive symptoms often respond less well to conventional medication than other symptom domains, making them a focus of ongoing drug development.
