Maybach

Maybach (German: [ˈmaɪbax]) is a German luxury car brand that first existed independently from 1909 to 1939 and again, under Daimler, from 1997. Since 2015 the name has been used for the ultra-luxury sub-brand Mercedes-Maybach, applied to enhanced versions of Mercedes-Benz models.

Origins

Wilhelm Maybach, technical director of Daimler-Motoren-Gesellschaft and designer of the pioneering 1901 Mercedes, left Daimler in 1907. In 1909 he founded the Maybach Motorenbau company in Friedrichshafen with his son Karl, initially building engines for Zeppelin airships. After the First World War the firm turned to complete luxury automobiles: from 1921 to 1939 Maybachs — most famously the huge twelve-cylinder Zeppelin DS 7 and DS 8 — were among the most prestigious and expensive cars in Germany. During the Second World War, Maybach produced engines for German tanks, including the Tiger and Panther.

Revival

Daimler-Benz acquired the company in 1960. The marque was revived in 1997 with the Mercedes-Benz Maybach concept and in 2002 as a standalone ultra-luxury brand with the Maybach 57 and 62 saloons, built to order in Sindelfingen. Sales disappointed — only a few thousand cars against projections — and Daimler discontinued the standalone marque in 2013 after losses on each car. In 2015 the name returned as Mercedes-Maybach, beginning with a long-wheelbase, V12 version of the S-Class, later joined by GLS and EQS SUV variants. These have sold far more successfully, particularly in China, the United States and South Korea.
