Swing dance is a group of social dances that developed with the swing style of jazz in the 1920s to 1940s, although the earliest of these dances predate the swing era. The best known of these dances is the Lindy Hop, which originated in Harlem, New York City, in the early 1930s and remains popular worldwide. Most swing dances are characterised by an emphasis on improvisation, a syncopated rhythm, and a joyful, energetic style.

History

Swing dances grew out of African American dance traditions, blending European partner-dance forms with African rhythms, improvisation, and vernacular steps. The Savoy Ballroom in Harlem, one of the few racially integrated ballrooms of its era, was the epicentre of the style, where dancers such as Frankie Manning and the troupe Whitey's Lindy Hoppers developed spectacular aerial moves ("air steps"). Big band leaders such as Duke Ellington, Count Basie, Benny Goodman, and Chick Webb provided the music.

During the Second World War, American servicemen carried swing dancing around the world. After the war the dances declined in popularity as musical tastes changed, but a revival beginning in the 1980s — driven by dancers in the United States, Sweden, and Britain seeking out surviving original dancers — restored Lindy Hop and related dances to a global social dance scene, with festivals such as Herräng Dance Camp in Sweden drawing thousands of participants.

Forms

  • Lindy Hop – the original swing dance, danced in 8-count and 6-count patterns with an open embrace and swing-out.
  • Charleston – a 1920s dance incorporated into Lindy Hop, danced solo, partnered, or in groups.
  • Balboa – a close-embrace dance from Southern California suited to fast tempos.
  • Collegiate Shag – a fast, energetic 6-count dance popular with college students in the 1930s.
  • East Coast Swing – a simplified form derived from Lindy Hop, taught widely by ballroom studios.
  • West Coast Swing – a smoother, slotted dance developed in California, danced to a wide range of popular music.
  • Boogie-woogie – the European competition form of swing dancing.

Music

Swing dance is traditionally danced to swing jazz in 4/4 time, characterised by a strong rhythm section, riffs, and a "swung" subdivision of the beat. Classic tempos range from slow blues to more than 300 beats per minute for Balboa and Shag. Modern scenes also dance to jump blues, rhythm and blues, and contemporary music in the swing idiom.
