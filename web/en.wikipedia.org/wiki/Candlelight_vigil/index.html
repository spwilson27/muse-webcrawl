Candlelight vigil

A candlelight vigil or candlelit vigil is an outdoor assembly of people carrying candles, held after sunset to show support for a cause, to mourn, or to commemorate an event or person. Vigils are typically peaceful, solemn gatherings, often involving prayer, song, speeches or silence, and they may be religious or secular in character.

Purposes

Vigils are commonly held after disasters, acts of violence or the death of a public figure, as an expression of communal grief and solidarity with victims. They are also used as a form of protest or witness: in South Korea, mass candlelight rallies in 2016–17, involving millions of participants, preceded the impeachment of President Park Geun-hye. In Hong Kong, an annual candlelight vigil in Victoria Park commemorated the victims of the 1989 Tiananmen Square crackdown from 1990 until it was banned in 2020.

Form

Because candles are fragile and easily extinguished, participants often shield the flame with paper cups or use battery-powered candles. The word vigil derives from the Latin vigilia, a night watch; in Christian practice a vigil is a service of prayer held on the eve of a feast or through the night before a funeral.

Notable examples

  • Vigils following the September 11 attacks in 2001.
  • Annual June 4 candlelight vigils in Hong Kong (1990–2019).
  • The 2016–17 South Korean candlelight protests.
  • Community vigils held after mass shootings and natural disasters worldwide.
