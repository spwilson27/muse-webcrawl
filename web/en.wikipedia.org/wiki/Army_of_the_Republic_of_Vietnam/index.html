The Army of the Republic of Vietnam (ARVN; Vietnamese: Lục quân Việt Nam Cộng hòa) was the ground forces branch of the Republic of Vietnam Military Forces, the armed forces of South Vietnam. Created out of the Vietnamese National Army in 1955, the ARVN fought throughout the Vietnam War against the communist People's Army of Vietnam (PAVN) and the Viet Cong, principally alongside the United States and other allies, until the fall of Saigon on 30 April 1975.

History

The ARVN grew from the French-backed Vietnamese National Army of the State of Vietnam. After the 1954 Geneva Accords and the establishment of the Republic of Vietnam under Ngô Đình Diệm, the United States took over training and equipping it through the Military Assistance Advisory Group. Built initially as a conventional army to repel a Korean-style invasion, it struggled against guerrilla insurgency in the early 1960s, a weakness exposed at the Battle of Ap Bac (1963).

During the American build-up after 1965, US and other allied forces took much of the main-force fighting role, but ARVN units bore heavy combat throughout, including during the 1968 Tet Offensive, when ARVN and territorial forces defended Saigon and the cities. Under the "Vietnamization" programme of President Nixon, the ARVN expanded to over one million men in all services and took over the ground war; it repelled the 1972 Easter Offensive with massive US air support, and fought the cross-border Operation Lam Son 719 into Laos in 1971.

Final campaigns and dissolution

After the 1973 Paris Peace Accords and the withdrawal of US forces, reductions in American aid, fuel and ammunition shortages, and PAVN's growing strength undermined the ARVN. In the spring offensive of 1975, PAVN forces overran the Central Highlands and the northern provinces; despite stands at places such as Xuân Lộc, the South Vietnamese state collapsed and the ARVN was dissolved when President Dương Văn Minh surrendered on 30 April 1975.

Organisation

The ARVN was organised into four corps (I–IV Corps) covering the four military regions, with infantry divisions, airborne and Marine divisions held as general reserve, Ranger groups, armoured brigades, artillery and special forces. Alongside it served the Regional and Popular Forces ("Ruff-Puffs"), territorial militia responsible for local security.
