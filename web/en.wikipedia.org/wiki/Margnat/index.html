Margnat was a French professional cycling team that existed in the early 1960s (1960–1965), sponsored by the Margnat bicycle company, and associated with the wines of the same name in some seasons. The name is best remembered in connection with the French rider André Darrigade and with the brief but successful squads that raced the Tour de France and the classics in the first half of the decade.

History

The Margnat firm produced racing bicycles in post-war France, and its eponymous trade team was formed for the 1960 season. In its various forms — including spells under co-sponsorship names such as Margnat–Rochet and Margnat–Paloma — the team competed in the Tour de France, the Vuelta a España and the one-day classics.

Riders and results

The sprint star André Darrigade, one of the most prolific stage winners in Tour history, rode for the team in the mid-1960s and won Tour de France stages in Margnat colours. Other notable riders associated with the squad included French and Italian professionals of the period who achieved stage wins and high placings in the spring classics. The team folded after the 1965 season as the economics of French trade-team sponsorship changed, and the Margnat name disappeared from the professional peloton.
