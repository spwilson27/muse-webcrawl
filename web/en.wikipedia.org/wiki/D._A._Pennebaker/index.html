Donn Alan Pennebaker (July 15, 1925 – August 1, 2019) was an American documentary filmmaker and one of the pioneers of Direct Cinema. In 2012 he received an Academy Honorary Award (Oscar) for lifetime achievement.

Career

Pennebaker studied electrical engineering at Yale and helped engineer early portable synchronous-sound film equipment, a technical breakthrough that made Direct Cinema possible. As part of Robert Drew's Drew Associates, he worked on Primary (1960) and Crisis (1963). With Richard Leacock he founded Leacock-Pennebaker, and in 1963 he established his own company.

His Dont Look Back (1967), chronicling Bob Dylan's 1965 England tour, is routinely ranked among the greatest documentaries ever made; its opening "cue card" sequence for "Subterranean Homesick Blues" anticipated the music video. Monterey Pop (1968) documented the 1967 Monterey International Pop Festival with landmark performances by Jimi Hendrix, Janis Joplin, and Otis Redding. With his wife and collaborator Chris Hegedus he made The War Room (1993), an Academy Award-nominated account of Bill Clinton's 1992 presidential campaign, and many later films including Kings of Pastry (2009). Pennebaker died at his home in Sagaponack, New York, in 2019.
