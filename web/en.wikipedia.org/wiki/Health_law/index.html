Health law

Health law is the body of law governing the healthcare industry, patients' rights, and public health.

Health law is a field of law that encompasses federal, state, and local statutes, regulations, case law, and treaties governing the health care industry and the provision of medical care. It touches the relationships between providers, patients, insurers, and regulators, and overlaps with public health law, bioethics, and administrative law.

Areas

Major areas include: licensure and regulation of hospitals and professionals; medical malpractice and negligence; patient rights, informed consent, and confidentiality — in the United States, the privacy and security rules of HIPAA (1996); fraud and abuse law, including the False Claims Act, the Anti-Kickback Statute and the Stark Law on physician self-referral; antitrust in hospital and insurer markets; regulation of drugs and devices by agencies such as the FDA; reproductive rights and end-of-life law; and emergency powers in public health crises, such as quarantine and vaccination mandates.

International dimension

International health law includes the World Health Organization's International Health Regulations (2005), which govern responses to cross-border health emergencies, and the Framework Convention on Tobacco Control. The right to health is recognised in international human rights law, notably Article 12 of the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights.
