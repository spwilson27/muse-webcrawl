Haberdashery

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Image placeholder: Haberdashery

Haberdashery refers to the goods sold by a haberdasher: small articles used in sewing and dressmaking, such as buttons, ribbons, zips, thread, needles and pins, and — in American English usage — men's clothing accessories and furnishings. A haberdasher's shop is a haberdashery. The word's origin is uncertain; one theory connects it to the Anglo-Norman hapertas, a kind of cloth, while others suggest a derivation from an old phrase for assorted small wares.

History

Haberdashers were established traders in medieval London, originally dealing in luxury goods imported from the continent. The Worshipful Company of Haberdashers, incorporated in 1448, is one of the Great Twelve Livery Companies of the City of London, ranked eighth in the order of precedence. It later became associated with the silk and velvet trades and, through charitable foundations, with schools — including Haberdashers' schools in Monmouth, London and Elstree. Saint Nicholas is regarded as a patron saint of haberdashers.

Usage by region

In British English, haberdashery denotes sewing notions and the department of a store selling them; a shop devoted to men's hats was historically a separate trade (the milliner or hatter). In American English, "haberdashery" more commonly means a men's outfitter selling suits, shirts, ties and accessories. The divergence reflects the different evolution of the retail trades in the two countries.
