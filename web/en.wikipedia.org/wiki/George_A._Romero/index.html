George Andrew Romero (4 February 1940 – 16 July 2017) was an American-Canadian film director best known for his series of zombie apocalypse films, beginning with Night of the Living Dead (1968), which is widely credited with creating the modern zombie genre.

Career

Romero studied at Carnegie Mellon University in Pittsburgh and made commercials before co-writing and directing Night of the Living Dead on a small budget. Its success led to sequels including Dawn of the Dead (1978), set in a shopping mall and noted for its satire of consumerism, and Day of the Dead (1985). His other films include Martin (1977), Creepshow (1982), written by Stephen King, and Land of the Dead (2005).

Legacy

Romero's ghouls — slow-moving, flesh-eating undead overcome only by destroying the brain — set the template imitated by decades of films, television, and games. He died in Toronto in 2017.
