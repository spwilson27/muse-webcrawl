Dundalk

Dundalk (Irish: Dún Dealgan, meaning "Dealgan's fort") is the county town of County Louth, Ireland. It lies on the Castletown River near where it flows into Dundalk Bay on the east coast, roughly halfway between Dublin and Belfast. With a population of around 43,000 in the 2022 census, it is the largest town in Ireland by population outside the cities. The town is situated close to the border with Northern Ireland and sits on the M1 motorway corridor.

History

The area has been inhabited since the Neolithic period, and the town takes its name from a fort associated with the legendary Dealgan. In Irish mythology the region is linked with the hero Cú Chulainn, who is said to have been born and raised on the Muirthemne plain nearby. The Normans established a fortified town here in the twelfth century, and Dundalk stood for centuries at the northern edge of the Pale. It suffered severely in the Irish Confederate Wars and the Williamite War, and in the modern era it was affected by its proximity to the border during the Troubles.

Dundalk was historically a centre of brewing (the Great Northern Brewery), tobacco manufacture, and engineering associated with the Great Northern Railway works. Today its economy includes pharmaceuticals, technology, and education; Dundalk Institute of Technology serves the region. The town is home to Dundalk F.C., one of Ireland's most successful football clubs, based at Oriel Park, which reached the group stage of the UEFA Europa League in 2016.
