Authorization is the function of specifying access rights and privileges to resources, and, in computer security, the process of determining whether an authenticated subject is permitted to perform a requested operation. Authorization necessarily follows authentication, which establishes who the subject is; authorization then decides what that subject may do.

Access control models

Systems implement authorization through several models. Access control lists (ACLs) attach lists of permitted subjects to each resource. Role-based access control (RBAC) assigns permissions to roles and users to roles, simplifying administration in large organisations. Attribute-based access control (ABAC) evaluates policies over attributes of the user, resource and environment. In capability-based security, possession of an unforgeable token itself constitutes authorization to access the referenced resource.

Principles

Good authorization design follows the principle of least privilege — subjects receive only the permissions necessary for their tasks — and separation of duties, which divides sensitive operations among multiple parties to deter fraud. Deny-by-default posture, in which anything not explicitly permitted is refused, is the standard secure configuration.

Standards and frameworks

OAuth 2.0 is an authorization framework that lets third-party applications obtain limited access to user accounts via tokens, without exposing credentials. XACML is an XML-based policy language for ABAC. In operating systems and databases, authorization appears as file permissions, privilege levels and grant/revoke statements.
