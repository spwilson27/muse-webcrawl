Joseph Kasa-Vubu

Joseph Kasa-Vubu, alternatively Kasavubu (c. 1915 – 24 March 1969), was a Congolese politician and statesman who served as the first President of the Republic of the Congo (today the Democratic Republic of the Congo) from 1960 until 1965, when he was deposed by Joseph-Désiré Mobutu.

Early life and political career

Kasa-Vubu was born near Tshela in the Mayombe region of the Belgian Congo, of Kongo heritage. Educated in Catholic seminaries and trained as a teacher, he worked as a clerk in the colonial administration in Léopoldville (Kinshasa). In 1954 he became president of ABAKO (Alliance des Bakongo), the cultural-political association of the Kongo people, and transformed it into one of the Congo's first nationalist movements. In 1957 he was elected mayor of Dendale commune in Léopoldville, one of the first Congolese elected to public office.

Presidency and the Congo Crisis

After ABAKO's strong showing in the May 1960 elections, Kasa-Vubu was elected head of state by parliament and became President at independence on 30 June 1960, with Patrice Lumumba as Prime Minister. Within days the Congo Crisis erupted: the army mutinied, Katanga seceded under Moise Tshombe, and Belgium and the superpowers intervened. On 5 September 1960 Kasa-Vubu dismissed Lumumba, precipitating a constitutional crisis that ended with Colonel Mobutu's first coup. Kasa-Vubu remained president through the crisis years, appointing successive governments including that of Cyrille Adoula, and presiding over the reintegration of Katanga in 1963 and the adoption of the Luluabourg Constitution in 1964.

Overthrow and death

On 25 November 1965, amid renewed deadlock between Kasa-Vubu and Tshombe, army commander Mobutu seized power in a second coup, deposing the President. Kasa-Vubu retired under house surveillance to Boma, where he died in 1969.
