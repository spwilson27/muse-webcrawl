The 2030 FIFA World Cup will be the 24th FIFA World Cup, scheduled for 2030. It will be hosted mainly by Morocco, Portugal and Spain, with three centenary celebration matches played in Argentina, Paraguay and Uruguay to mark 100 years since the first World Cup in Uruguay in 1930. The tournament will be the first World Cup held across three continents — Africa, Europe and South America.

Bidding and award

The joint Iberian–Moroccan bid was the sole candidate after a rival South American bid and other interested parties withdrew or merged. FIFA's Council agreed the arrangement in October 2023, and the hosts were formally confirmed by an extraordinary FIFA Congress on 11 December 2024, alongside Saudi Arabia's award of the 2034 tournament.

Format

Like the 2026 tournament, the 2030 edition will feature 48 teams. All six host nations involved — Morocco, Portugal, Spain, Argentina, Paraguay and Uruguay — will qualify automatically.

Venues

Spain is expected to provide the largest share of stadiums, including the Camp Nou and Santiago Bernabéu; Portugal's venues include the Estádio da Luz; Morocco plans several stadiums including a very large new arena near Casablanca. The venue for the final has not been definitively settled.
