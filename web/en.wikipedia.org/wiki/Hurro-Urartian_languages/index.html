Hurro-Urartian languages

The Hurro-Urartian languages are an extinct language family of the ancient Near East, comprising only two known languages: Hurrian and Urartian. Both were spoken in parts of Anatolia, northern Mesopotamia, and the Armenian Highlands during the Bronze Age and early Iron Age.

The languages

Hurrian was spoken by the Hurrians, attested from the late 3rd millennium BCE and flourishing in the Mitanni kingdom of the 15th–14th centuries BCE; sources include the famous Hurrian "Letter of Mittani" preserved at Amarna. Urartian was the language of the kingdom of Urartu around Lake Van, attested mainly in royal inscriptions from the 9th to 6th centuries BCE. Both languages were written in cuneiform, are agglutinative, and show ergative alignment.

Classification

Hurro-Urartian is generally treated as an isolated family with no demonstrated relatives. Proposed links — for example to the Northeast Caucasian languages (the "Alarodian" hypothesis) or to Kassite — remain unproven and are not widely accepted.
