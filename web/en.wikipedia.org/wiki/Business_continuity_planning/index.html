Business continuity planning

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Related standard: ISO 22301
Related field: Disaster recovery

Business continuity planning (BCP) is the process of creating systems of prevention and recovery to deal with potential threats to a company, ensuring that personnel and assets are protected and able to function quickly in the event of a disaster. It involves defining the procedures an organization must follow before, during and after disruptive events—from natural disasters and fires to cyberattacks and pandemics—so that critical business functions continue at an acceptable level.

Process

A typical BCP programme includes a business impact analysis identifying critical functions and their maximum tolerable downtime; a risk assessment of threats and vulnerabilities; and the specification of recovery objectives such as the recovery time objective (RTO) and recovery point objective (RPO). Strategies may include alternate work sites, redundant IT systems, manual workarounds and supply-chain diversification.

Testing and maintenance

Plans are validated through tabletop exercises, simulations and full failover tests, and updated as the organization changes. The international standard ISO 22301 sets out requirements for a business continuity management system.

Relation to disaster recovery

Disaster recovery focuses on restoring IT systems and data after a disruption and is commonly treated as one component of the broader discipline of business continuity, which also addresses people, premises, suppliers and communications.
