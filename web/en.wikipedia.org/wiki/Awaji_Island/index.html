Awaji Island

Awaji Island (Japanese: 淡路島, Awaji-shima) is an island in Hyōgo Prefecture, Japan, in the eastern part of the Seto Inland Sea between the islands of Honshū and Shikoku. With an area of about 593 km², it is the largest island of the Inland Sea. In Japanese mythology Awaji was the first island created by the gods Izanagi and Izanami.

Geography and economy

The island is administratively divided among the cities of Awaji, Sumoto and Minamiawaji, with a population of roughly 130,000. It is connected to Honshū by the Akashi Kaikyō Bridge (opened 1998, then the world's longest suspension bridge) and to Shikoku by the Ōnaruto Bridge across the Naruto Strait, famous for its tidal whirlpools. Agriculture—onions, lettuce, flowers, and dairy and beef cattle—is important, as is tourism; the architect Tadao Ando designed several notable buildings on the island, including the Awaji Yumebutai complex and the Water Temple (Hompuku-ji).

History and culture

Historically the province of Awaji, the island supplied food to the imperial court. It is traditionally regarded as the birthplace of Japanese puppet theatre (ningyō jōruri), precursor of bunraku. The island's northern tip lies close to Kobe and was affected by the Great Hanshin earthquake of 1995, whose epicentre was just north of Awaji; the Nojima fault that ruptured is preserved as an earthquake memorial.
