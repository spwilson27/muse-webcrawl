F. W. Murnau

Friedrich Wilhelm Murnau (born Friedrich Wilhelm Plumpe; December 28, 1888 – March 11, 1931) was a German film director, one of the most influential of the silent era, a leading figure of German Expressionism and the Kammerspielfilm movement.

F. W. Murnau
Born: 1888, Bielefeld, Germany
Died: 1931, Santa Barbara, California
Films: Nosferatu, The Last Laugh, Sunrise, Faust

Career

After service as a pilot in WWI, Murnau directed Nosferatu (1922), an unauthorised Dracula adaptation; The Last Laugh (1924), famed for its "unchained camera"; Faust (1926); and in Hollywood Sunrise: A Song of Two Humans (1927), winner at the first Academy Awards. He died in a car crash shortly before the release of Tabu (1931), co-directed with Robert Flaherty.
