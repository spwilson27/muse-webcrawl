The Global Environment Facility (GEF) is a multilateral partnership and financial mechanism that provides grants and blended finance for projects addressing global environmental problems. Established on the eve of the 1992 Rio Earth Summit, it serves as the financial mechanism for several multilateral environmental agreements, including the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change, the Convention on Biological Diversity, the UN Convention to Combat Desertification, the Stockholm Convention on Persistent Organic Pollutants and the Minamata Convention on Mercury. Since its founding it has provided more than US$26 billion in grants and mobilised far larger amounts in co-financing for thousands of projects in over 160 countries.

History

The GEF was created in 1991 as a three-year pilot programme within the World Bank, working with the UN Development Programme (UNDP) and UN Environment Programme (UNEP), initially with about US$1 billion. At the Rio Summit its role as the funding arm of the new conventions was recognised, and in 1994 it was restructured into an independent, permanent institution with its own Assembly (all member countries), a 32-member Council, a Secretariat in Washington, D.C., and the World Bank serving as trustee. Funding is replenished by donor governments every four years; the eighth replenishment (GEF-8, 2022–2026) totalled about US$5.33 billion.

Areas of work

GEF grants target six focal areas: biodiversity, climate change mitigation, land degradation, international waters, chemicals and waste, and sustainable forest management, with additional integrated programmes on food systems, cities and ecosystems. It also administers dedicated climate funds — the Least Developed Countries Fund (LDCF) and the Special Climate Change Fund (SCCF) — and hosts the secretariat of the Adaptation Fund (shared arrangements vary over time). Projects are implemented through 18 accredited "GEF Agencies", including UN bodies, multilateral development banks and large conservation NGOs such as WWF and Conservation International.

Governance and evaluation

The GEF Council, meeting twice a year, approves work programmes under a double-majority rule requiring support from both a majority of member countries and a majority of contributions. An Independent Evaluation Office and a Scientific and Technical Advisory Panel (STAP) review results and scientific quality. The Facility also runs the Small Grants Programme, delivered by UNDP, which funds community-based organisations directly. Civil society organisations participate through the GEF-CSO Network, and the partnership recognises the rights of Indigenous peoples through a dedicated advisory group.
