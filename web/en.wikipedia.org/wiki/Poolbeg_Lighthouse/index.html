Poolbeg Lighthouse

Poolbeg Lighthouse is an active lighthouse at the entrance to Dublin Port in Ireland, standing at the end of the Great South Wall in Dublin Bay. Painted bright red, it is a well-known landmark and the destination of a popular pier walk of about four kilometres from the shore at Ringsend.

History

A light was first established at Poolbeg in 1767, when it was among the first lighthouses in the world to be lit by candlepower. The present tower, designed under the direction of the engineer John Rennie and built of granite, dates from 1820, when the light was converted to oil. It originally stood stone-coloured and was painted red in the early twentieth century to distinguish it from the green North Bull Lighthouse across the channel.

Characteristics

The lighthouse was automated in 1976 and is operated by Dublin Port. It works in tandem with the North Bull Lighthouse to mark the dredged shipping channel into the port. The walk along the Great South Wall to the lighthouse offers wide views over Dublin Bay toward Howth and Dún Laoghaire and is a favourite excursion for Dubliners.
