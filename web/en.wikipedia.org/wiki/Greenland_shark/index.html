Greenland shark

[Image: Greenland shark]

The Greenland shark (Somniosus microcephalus), also known as the gurry shark or grey shark, is a large shark of the family Somniosidae native to the waters of the North Atlantic and Arctic Oceans. It is one of the largest living sharks and among the longest-lived vertebrates known.

Longevity

Radiocarbon dating of eye-lens proteins published in 2016 suggested a lifespan of at least 272 years, with the largest females estimated at around 400 years old, making the species the longest-lived vertebrate. Greenland sharks grow extremely slowly, about 1 cm per year, and females may not mature until roughly 150 years of age.

Biology

The shark's tissues contain high concentrations of urea and trimethylamine N-oxide, making its flesh toxic if eaten fresh; it is nonetheless prepared in Iceland as the fermented delicacy hákarl. Many individuals are nearly blind owing to the parasitic copepod Ommatokoita elongata attached to the cornea.
