Henry (unit)

The henry (symbol: H) is the SI unit of electrical inductance. It is named after the American scientist Joseph Henry, who discovered electromagnetic induction independently of and around the same time as Michael Faraday. If a current changing at a rate of one ampere per second induces an electromotive force of one volt, the inductance of the circuit is one henry:

1 H = 1 V·s/A = 1 Wb/A

Definition

In SI base units, the henry is kg·m²·s⁻²·A⁻². Inductors in practical circuits are commonly rated in millihenries (mH) and microhenries (µH), since a one-henry inductor is large. The henry was adopted by the International Electrical Congress in 1893 and is a derived unit of the International System of Units.

Applications

Inductance measured in henries governs the behaviour of coils, transformers, motors, and filters, and together with capacitance determines the resonant frequency of LC circuits used in radio and signal processing.
