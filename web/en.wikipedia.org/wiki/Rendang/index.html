Rendang is an Indonesian spicy meat dish originating from the Minangkabau region in West Sumatra, Indonesia. It has spread across Indonesia to the cuisines of neighbouring Southeast Asian countries such as Malaysia, Singapore, Brunei and the Philippines. Rendang is a piece of meat — most commonly beef (rendang daging) — slow-cooked in coconut milk and a paste of mixed ground spices, which includes ginger, galangal, turmeric leaves, lemongrass and chillies.

Preparation

The cooking process changes the meat from boiled to fried as the liquid evaporates; the slow cooking (often four hours or more) allows the meat to absorb the spices and become tender, and the sauce caramelises into a dark, rich coating. Two recognised varieties exist: dry rendang, cooked until almost all liquid is gone, and wet rendang (kalio), which retains more sauce.

Cultural significance

Rendang is traditionally served at ceremonial occasions by the Minangkabau, such as wedding feasts and Eid al-Fitr. In 2011 and 2017, rendang was voted first in CNN's "World's 50 best foods" readers' polls. It was officially recognised as one of Indonesia's national dishes in 2018.
