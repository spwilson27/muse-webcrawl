China–Chile Free Trade Agreement

The China–Chile Free Trade Agreement is a free trade agreement between the People's Republic of China and the Republic of Chile, signed in November 2005 and in force from October 2006. It was China's first free trade agreement with a Latin American country and the first it negotiated with a non-Asian economy.

Background

Chile was the first South American country to establish diplomatic relations with the People's Republic of China (in 1970) and the first Latin American country to sign a bilateral agreement with China supporting its accession to the World Trade Organization. Negotiations for a free trade deal were announced at the APEC summit in Santiago in 2004 and concluded in five rounds during 2005.

Provisions

The agreement eliminated tariffs immediately on most traded goods, with further reductions phased in over periods of up to ten years; by the end of the implementation period, roughly 97% of products traded between the two countries were covered by zero tariffs. It was later supplemented by agreements on trade in services (signed 2008, in force 2010) and on investment (2012), and an upgrade negotiated in 2017 further expanded market access, including for Chilean agricultural exports such as cherries, wine, salmon and fruit, and eased customs procedures.

Trade effects

Bilateral trade grew rapidly after the agreement entered into force, and China became Chile's largest trading partner. Chilean exports to China are dominated by copper and copper products, along with lithium, fruit, wine and seafood, while Chile imports chiefly manufactured goods, machinery and electronics from China.
