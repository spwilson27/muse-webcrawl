Sébastien Le Prestre de Vauban (1 May 1633 – 30 March 1707), commonly called Vauban, was a French military engineer and Marshal of France who worked under Louis XIV. He is considered the foremost military engineer of his age: he designed or rebuilt some 300 fortifications and directed more than 50 sieges, revolutionising both siege warfare and fortress design. Twelve of his works were inscribed by UNESCO as the "Fortifications of Vauban" World Heritage Site in 2008.

Early life and career

Born into minor Burgundian nobility at Saint-Léger-de-Fourcheret (now Saint-Léger-Vauban), Vauban joined the army of the Prince de Condé during the Fronde at 17, was captured by royal troops, and was won over to the service of Cardinal Mazarin. As a junior engineer he distinguished himself in sieges in the Franco-Spanish War and came to the attention of the war minister Louvois and of Louis XIV.

Fortifications

As Commissioner General of Fortifications (1678), Vauban developed the pré carré — a double line of fortresses guarding France's northern frontier — and built or rebuilt strongholds including Lille, Strasbourg, Besançon, Briançon, Mont-Dauphin and Mont-Louis. His fortresses refined the bastioned trace italienne into systematic "first", "second" and "third" systems adapted to terrain.

Siegecraft

Vauban made siege warfare methodical and dramatically reduced its cost in lives: his system of parallel trenches (used first at Maastricht in 1673), ricochet fire, and the socket bayonet and other innovations let him take fortresses on predictable timetables. He is credited with directing 53 sieges and never losing one he personally conducted.

Later life and writings

Made Marshal of France in 1703, Vauban also wrote on taxation and the economy; his Projet d'une dîme royale (1707), proposing a proportional income tax, was suppressed and likely hastened his fall from favour. He died in Paris on 30 March 1707. Napoleon later moved his heart to Les Invalides, calling Vauban's treatise on siegecraft a work of genius.
