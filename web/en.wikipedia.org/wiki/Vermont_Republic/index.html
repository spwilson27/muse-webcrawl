Vermont Republic

The Vermont Republic (1777–1791) was an independent state in New England that existed for fourteen years before joining the United States as the fourteenth state, Vermont. It arose from the disputed New Hampshire Grants, land claimed by both New Hampshire and New York, whose settlers — led by Ethan Allen and the Green Mountain Boys — resisted New York's authority.

Independence

In January 1777 delegates declared the territory independent, first as New Connecticut and then as Vermont, and in July 1777 adopted a constitution at Windsor that was among the most democratic of its era: it abolished adult slavery, provided for universal male suffrage without property qualifications, and mandated public schools. The republic coined its own copper money, operated a postal service, and conducted foreign relations, including negotiations with Britain (the Haldimand Affair) that helped keep pressure on New York.

Admission to the Union

After settling New York's land claims with a payment of $30,000, Vermont was admitted to the Union on 4 March 1791, balancing the admission of Kentucky the following year. It remains one of a handful of present U.S. states — with Texas, California and Hawaii — that were once sovereign republics.
