Special educational needs

Special educational needs (SEN), also called special educational needs and disabilities (SEND) in England, is a term used in the United Kingdom and some other countries to describe the needs of children and young people who have a learning difficulty or disability that makes it harder for them to learn than most others of the same age. Learners with SEN require special educational provision — educational help that is additional to, or different from, that generally provided in mainstream schools.

Categories of need

In England, the SEND Code of Practice groups needs into four broad areas:

  • Communication and interaction — including speech, language and communication needs (SLCN) and autism spectrum condition
  • Cognition and learning — including moderate and severe learning difficulties, dyslexia, dyscalculia and dyspraxia
  • Social, emotional and mental health difficulties — including attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) and anxiety
  • Sensory and/or physical needs — including visual impairment, hearing impairment and physical disability

Provision

Most pupils with SEN are educated in mainstream schools with graduated support, often described as "SEN support", in which teachers assess, plan, deliver and review interventions in cycles. Pupils with more complex needs may receive an Education, Health and Care (EHC) plan following a statutory assessment by the local authority; the plan sets out the provision the child is legally entitled to receive. Some pupils attend special schools or specialist units that cater for particular categories of need. In the United States, the analogous framework is provided by the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA), under which eligible children receive an Individualized Education Program (IEP) and are entitled to a free appropriate public education in the least restrictive environment.

Historical development

The modern framework in England and Wales owes much to the Warnock Report of 1978, which popularised the concept of special educational needs in place of earlier categories of handicap, and to the Education Act 1981 and later legislation. The Children and Families Act 2014 replaced statements of SEN with EHC plans and extended the system to cover young people up to the age of 25.

Debates

Debated issues include the identification and possible over- or under-identification of needs, funding and local authority capacity, waiting times for assessment, outcomes for pupils with SEN, and the balance between inclusive mainstream education and specialist provision.
