TNM staging system

The TNM Classification of Malignant Tumours (TNM) is a globally recognised standard for classifying the anatomical extent of the spread of malignant tumours (cancer staging). It has gained wide international acceptance for many solid tumour cancers, though it is not directly applicable to leukaemias or tumours of the central nervous system. The system is developed and maintained by the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) and published in parallel by the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC); the current edition is the eighth (2017).

Parameters

LetterMeaningValues
TSize or direct extent of the primary tumourTX (cannot be assessed), T0 (no evidence), Tis (in situ), T1–T4 (increasing size/invasion)
NSpread to regional lymph nodesNX, N0 (none), N1–N3 (increasing involvement)
MDistant metastasisM0 (none), M1 (present)

Subdivisions (e.g. T1a, T1b, T1c, often by tumour diameter) refine many categories, and separate definitions are given for each cancer type. Prefixes modify the classification: c denotes clinical staging before treatment, p pathological staging after surgical resection, y staging after neoadjuvant therapy, and r staging of a recurrence. Once T, N and M values are assigned, they are combined into an overall stage group — typically Stage I, II, III or IV — which summarises prognosis and guides treatment.

Purpose

Staging allows clinicians to plan treatment, estimate prognosis, evaluate treatment results and compare outcomes between institutions, and provides a common language for clinical trials and cancer registries. The system was devised by Pierre Denoix in France between 1943 and 1952; the UICC published the first pocketbook edition in 1968, and the AJCC and UICC unified their classifications in 1987.
