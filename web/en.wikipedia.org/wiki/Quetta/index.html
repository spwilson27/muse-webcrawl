Quetta (Pashto: کوټه; Balochi: کویٹہ; Urdu: کوئٹہ) is the capital and largest city of the Pakistani province of Balochistan. It is situated in a river valley near the western border with Afghanistan, surrounded by mountains, at an average elevation of about 1,680 metres (5,510 feet) above sea level, making it one of Pakistan's high-altitude cities. It is nicknamed the "Fruit Garden of Pakistan" for the orchards and dried fruits produced in the surrounding region.

Quetta lies in a valley ringed by mountains near the Bolan Pass.

History

The name Quetta derives from the Pashto word Kwatta, meaning a fort. The area came under British control in 1876 during the First Anglo-Afghan War era and became an important military garrison and administrative centre; the British founded the Command and Staff College there in 1905. The city was largely destroyed by a devastating earthquake on 31 May 1935, in which tens of thousands died, and was subsequently rebuilt. After 1947 Quetta became the capital of Balochistan province in independent Pakistan.

Geography and economy

Quetta lies near the Bolan Pass, one of the principal routes between Central Asia and the Indian subcontinent. Its economy is based on trade, fruit production (apples, grapes, pomegranates, melons), and its role as a market for goods moving to and from Afghanistan and Iran. The city's population includes Pashtun, Baloch, Hazara, and other communities.
