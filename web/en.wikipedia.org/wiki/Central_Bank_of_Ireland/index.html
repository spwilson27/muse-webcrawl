Central Bank of Ireland

The Central Bank's headquarters in Dublin's Docklands.

The Central Bank of Ireland (Irish: Banc Ceannais na hÉireann) is the central bank of Ireland and the country's financial services regulator. Established in its original form in 1943, it is part of the European System of Central Banks and, since Ireland adopted the euro, implements monetary policy set by the European Central Bank.

History

The bank succeeded the Currency Commission of the Irish Free State. It issued Irish pound banknotes and coins until the euro changeover in 2002. The Central Bank and Financial Services Authority of Ireland Act reorganised it in 2003, and after the Irish banking crisis of 2008–2010 — during which the bank was criticised for regulatory failings — a further reform in 2010 created a single integrated regulator under a governor with enhanced powers.

Functions

The bank is responsible for price and financial stability, prudential supervision of banks, insurers, and investment firms, consumer protection in financial services, operation of payment systems, and publication of economic statistics and forecasts. Since 2017 its headquarters have been on North Wall Quay in the Dublin Docklands, on the site of the unfinished headquarters of Anglo Irish Bank. Its governor is appointed by the President of Ireland on the advice of the government.
