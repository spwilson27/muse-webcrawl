The long jump is a track and field event in which athletes combine speed, strength and agility in an attempt to leap as far as possible from a takeoff point. Along with the triple jump, it is one of the two standard jumping events held at the Olympic Games, and it was also part of the ancient Greek pentathlon.

Technique

The athlete sprints down a runway (typically 40–45 metres) and jumps from a wooden takeoff board, 20 cm wide, into a sand-filled pit. The distance is measured from the edge of the board to the nearest break in the sand made by any part of the body. If the athlete steps beyond the board, the jump is ruled a foul.

Three principal airborne techniques are used to counteract forward rotation: the hang, the sail, and the hitch-kick, in which the athlete cycles the legs in the air. Sprint speed at takeoff is the single greatest determinant of distance.

History

The long jump was one of the events of the ancient Olympic pentathlon, performed with hand weights called halteres. It has been contested at every modern Olympic Games since 1896 for men and since 1948 for women.

The most famous competition in the event's history came at the 1991 World Championships in Tokyo, where Mike Powell jumped 8.95 m to break Bob Beamon's legendary 8.90 m world record from the 1968 Mexico City Olympics, defeating Carl Lewis in a celebrated duel. Powell's record still stands. Beamon's jump had exceeded the previous record by 55 cm and remained unbeaten for nearly 23 years.

Records

The women's world record of 7.52 m was set by Galina Chistyakova of the Soviet Union in 1988. The standing long jump, once an Olympic event, is no longer contested at elite level.
