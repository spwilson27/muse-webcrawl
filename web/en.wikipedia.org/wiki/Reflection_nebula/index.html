Reflection nebula

Reflection nebula
Type: Interstellar cloud
Mechanism: Starlight scattered by dust
Characteristic colour: Blue
Examples: The Pleiades nebulosity (Merope Nebula), Witch Head Nebula, Iris Nebula

In astronomy, a reflection nebula is a cloud of interstellar dust that reflects the light of a nearby star or stars. The illuminating stars are not hot enough to ionise the gas, as in an emission nebula, so the nebula shines purely by scattered starlight and shares the stars' spectrum.

Why blue?

The scattering efficiency of the dust grains depends on wavelength, favouring blue light in much the same way that Rayleigh scattering makes the daytime sky blue. Reflection nebulae therefore appear characteristically blue, for example in the nebulosity surrounding the Pleiades cluster and in the Iris Nebula (NGC 7023).

History and properties

In the 1910s Vesto Slipher's spectroscopy of the Pleiades nebulosity showed that its spectrum matched that of the illuminating stars, proving that the nebula shines by reflection; Edwin Hubble later established the relationship between nebula brightness and the star's distance and magnitude. Reflection nebulae are often found around young stars still embedded in their natal material, and together with dark and emission nebulae they map the structure of star-forming regions.
