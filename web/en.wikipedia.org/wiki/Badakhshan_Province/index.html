Badakhshan Province (Pashto/Dari: بدخشان) is one of the 34 provinces of Afghanistan, located in the far northeast of the country. It borders Tajikistan to the north, Pakistan to the south, and shares Afghanistan's only short border with China in the Wakhan Corridor to the east. Its capital is Fayzabad.

Geography

The province is dominated by the Hindu Kush and Pamir mountain ranges, with peaks exceeding 7,000 metres, including Noshaq (7,492 m), Afghanistan's highest point, on the Pakistan border. The Panj River forms much of the northern boundary with Tajikistan. The Wakhan Corridor, a narrow panhandle created during the 19th-century "Great Game" between Britain and Russia, extends to the Chinese frontier.

History

Badakhshan is famed for the lapis lazuli mines of Sar-e Sang, worked for more than 6,000 years and the historic source of the prized blue stone used in ancient Egyptian and Mesopotamian art. The region passed through Persian, Greek, Arab, Mongol, and Afghan hands; Marco Polo praised its gems and horses. In the recent era, Badakhshan was one of the few Afghan provinces never fully controlled by the Taliban before 2021, serving as a base for the Northern Alliance.

People and economy

The population is predominantly Tajik, with Pamiri peoples (Shughni, Wakhi, Ishkashimi and others) in the eastern districts, many of them Ismaili Muslims, alongside Uzbek and Kyrgyz minorities. Dari and Pamiri languages predominate. The economy rests on subsistence agriculture, livestock, and mining of lapis lazuli and other gems; the province is also noted for opium poppy cultivation in some districts.
