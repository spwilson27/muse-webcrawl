Hack squat

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The hack squat is a squat exercise variation that primarily targets the quadriceps. The name is generally credited to the wrestler and strongman George Hackenschmidt, who popularised the movement in the early twentieth century: in its original free-weight form, the barbell is held behind the legs at arm's length and the lifter squats down and stands up, keeping the torso upright.

Machine hack squat

The modern hack squat is usually performed on a dedicated machine. The lifter stands on an angled platform with the back against a sliding back pad and the shoulders under padded supports; the sled travels along fixed rails as the knees bend and extend. Because the machine stabilises the torso and removes much of the balance and spinal-loading demand of a free barbell squat, lifters can emphasise the quadriceps — particularly with a lower foot placement, which increases knee flexion — but the trade-off is greater stress on the knee joint and reduced involvement of the posterior chain.

Variations and technique

In the free-weight (barbell) hack squat, the bar is gripped behind the body with an overhand grip, heels sometimes elevated on a block, and the lifter rises by extending the knees and hips while keeping the arms straight. Foot position alters the emphasis: feet lower and closer together on the machine platform stress the outer quadriceps, while higher placement brings in more glute and hamstring. The reverse hack squat, performed facing into the machine, is another popular variant for the glutes and hamstrings.
