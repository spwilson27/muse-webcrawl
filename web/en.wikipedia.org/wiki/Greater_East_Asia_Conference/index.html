The Greater East Asia Conference (大東亞會議) was an international summit held in Tokyo from 5 to 6 November 1943, in which Japan hosted the heads of state of various component members of the Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere. The gathering was intended to showcase Asian unity against Western colonialism under Japanese leadership, as the tide of the Pacific War was turning against Japan.

Proceedings and declaration

Chaired by Prime Minister Hideki Tojo, the conference adopted the Joint Declaration of Greater East Asia, which proclaimed principles of coexistence and co-prosperity, mutual respect for sovereignty and independence, cultural exchange, and the abolition of racial discrimination — language deliberately echoing the Atlantic Charter. In practice the participating regimes (Manchukuo, Wang Jingwei's Reorganised National Government of China, Ba Maw's Burma, Laurel's Second Philippine Republic, and Thailand) were Japanese allies or client states; Subhas Chandra Bose attended for India as an observer.

Significance

Historians generally regard the conference as a propaganda exercise: real power in the Sphere remained entirely Japanese. Nonetheless it was the first international summit in which non-white heads of government gathered to proclaim an anti-colonial programme, and some participants, notably Bose and Ba Maw, used it to advance genuine nationalist goals. Analogies are sometimes drawn with the Afro-Asian Bandung Conference of 1955.
