Disaster film

A disaster film or disaster movie is a film genre that has an impending or ongoing disaster as its subject and primary plot device. Such disasters may include natural disasters (floods, earthquakes, asteroid collisions), accidents (shipwrecks, plane crashes), or large-scale attacks. A subgenre of action films, disaster films usually feature large ensemble casts and multiple parallel subplots.

History

Precursors appeared early in cinema history, but the genre flourished in the 1970s with producer Irwin Allen's successes such as Airport (1970), The Poseidon Adventure (1972), and The Towering Inferno (1974). After a decline in the 1980s, advances in computer-generated imagery revived the genre in the 1990s with films such as Twister (1996), Independence Day (1996), Titanic (1997), Armageddon (1998), and later The Day After Tomorrow (2004) and 2012 (2009).

Characteristics

Disaster films typically follow a group of characters attempting to survive the catastrophe, interweaving their personal stories. They are known for spectacle-driven set pieces, special effects, and star-studded casts. Critics have often been divided on the genre, though many entries were major box-office successes.
