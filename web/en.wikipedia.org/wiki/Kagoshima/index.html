Kagoshima

Kagoshima (鹿児島市, Kagoshima-shi) is the capital city of Kagoshima Prefecture at the south-western tip of Kyushu in Japan. It has been nicknamed the "Naples of the Eastern world" for its bay location, hot climate and the striking stratovolcano Sakurajima across Kagoshima Bay. The city's population is roughly 600,000.

History

Kagoshima was the castle town of the Shimazu clan, lords of the Satsuma Domain, and one of the most powerful domains of the Edo period. It was a centre of the movement that overthrew the Tokugawa shogunate: figures of the Meiji Restoration such as Saigō Takamori and Ōkubo Toshimichi were born and raised here. In 1863 British warships bombarded the city in retaliation for the Namamugi Incident, and in 1877 it was the last battleground of the Satsuma Rebellion. The city was heavily damaged by air raids in 1945.

Geography

The city faces Sakurajima, an active volcano that was an island until the 1914 eruption joined it to the Ōsumi Peninsula. Minor eruptions drop ash on the city frequently, and residents are accustomed to carrying umbrellas against ashfall.

Attractions

Sites include Sengan-en, the Shimazu clan garden; the Shiroyama observatory; the Museum of the Meiji Restoration; and Kagoshima Aquarium. Ferries cross the bay to Sakurajima, and the city is the gateway to Yakushima and the Amami Islands.
