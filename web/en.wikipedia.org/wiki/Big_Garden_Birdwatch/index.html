Big Garden Birdwatch

The Big Garden Birdwatch is an annual citizen science survey run by the Royal Society for the Protection of Birds (RSPB) in the United Kingdom. Participants spend one hour during a designated weekend in late January counting the birds that land in their garden, local park, or other outdoor space, and report the highest number of each species seen at one time.

History

The event began in 1979 as an activity for junior RSPB members and grew into one of the world's largest wildlife surveys, with hundreds of thousands of participants submitting counts of millions of birds each year. A companion event, the Big Schools' Birdwatch, involves schoolchildren.

Results and purpose

The long-running data set tracks trends in common garden birds. Results have documented steep declines in species such as the house sparrow and starling, alongside increases in species such as the goldfinch and woodpigeon. The survey raises public awareness of bird conservation and helps direct research.
