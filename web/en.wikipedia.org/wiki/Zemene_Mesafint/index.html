The Zemene Mesafint (Amharic: ዘመነ መሳፍንት, "Era of the Princes" or "Era of the Judges") was a period in Ethiopian history between 1769 and 1855 during which the emperors of the Solomonic dynasty were reduced to figureheads and real power lay with regional warlords — the rases and dejazmatches — who fought one another for control of the capital, Gondar, and the puppet emperor on its throne.

Origins

The era is conventionally dated from 1769, when Ras Mikael Sehul of Tigray deposed and murdered Emperor Iyoas I, shattering the authority built up at Gondar since Fasilides. There followed a succession of short-lived emperors installed and removed by kingmakers. The Yejju dynasty of Wollo — Muslim Oromo converts who became the effective rulers of the empire for much of the period — supplied the dominant rases, beginning with Ras Aligaz and his brother Ali I.

Political fragmentation

Ethiopia splintered into competing provinces: Tigray under Ras Wolde Selassie, Gojjam under its own lords, Shewa under a line of kings who effectively governed independently, and Gondar itself passed from hand to hand in dozens of battles. Imperial chronicles record some emperors being deposed and restored multiple times. Yet the era was not one of pure anarchy: provincial courts flourished, trade continued, church scholarship and Ge'ez literature persisted, and the ideology of a single divinely sanctioned emperor survived intact — which is why reunification, when it came, could be framed as restoration.

End of the era

The Zemene Mesafint ended with the rise of Kassa Hailu, a former soldier and frontier lord who defeated the Yejju at the Battle of Ayshal in 1853, captured Gondar, and in 1855 was crowned Emperor Tewodros II, inaugurating modern Ethiopian reunification.

Name

The name alludes to the biblical Book of Judges, a time when "there was no king in Israel: every man did that which was right in his own eyes."
