Beta function

The beta function B(x, y) for real arguments

In mathematics, the beta function, also called the Euler integral of the first kind, is a special function defined for complex numbers x and y with positive real parts by

B(x, y) = ∫₀¹ tx−1 (1 − t)y−1 dt.

It was studied by Leonhard Euler and Adrien-Marie Legendre, and was given its name by Jacques Binet. The beta function is symmetric: B(x, y) = B(y, x).

Relation to the gamma function

The beta function is related to the gamma function by the identity

B(x, y) = Γ(x) Γ(y) / Γ(x + y),

analogous to the way binomial coefficients relate factorials; indeed, for positive integers the beta function yields the reciprocal of a binomial coefficient times a simple factor. Other useful identities include the reflection formula B(x, 1 − x) = π / sin(πx), and integral representations over trigonometric functions, such as B(x, y) = 2 ∫₀π/2 sin2x−1θ cos2y−1θ dθ.

Applications

The beta function appears throughout mathematics and physics. In probability it is the normalising constant of the beta distribution and appears in the beta-binomial distribution. In string theory, the Veneziano amplitude for open-string scattering is given by the beta function — historically one of the motivations for the theory's development. It also arises in the evaluation of Feynman diagrams, in combinatorics, and in the study of the incomplete beta function, which gives the cumulative distribution function of the binomial distribution.
