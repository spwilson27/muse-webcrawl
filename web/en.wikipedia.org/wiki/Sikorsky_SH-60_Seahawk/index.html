The Sikorsky SH-60/MH-60 Seahawk (or Sea Hawk) is a twin turboshaft engine, multi-mission United States Navy helicopter in service since 1984. Based on the United States Army's UH-60 Black Hawk and the Sikorsky S-70 family, the Seahawk can be deployed aboard any air-capable frigate, destroyer, cruiser, fast combat support ship, littoral combat ship, amphibious assault ship, or aircraft carrier. It has operated in anti-submarine warfare (ASW), anti-surface warfare (ASuW), naval special warfare support, search and rescue, combat search and rescue, medical evacuation, and vertical replenishment roles.

Development and design

The Navy selected Sikorsky's design in 1977 for the Light Airborne Multi-Purpose System (LAMPS III) requirement, seeking a shipboard helicopter to extend the sensors and weapons of surface combatants. Navalization changes from the Black Hawk include folding rotor blades and a hinged tail for shipboard storage, a tail wheel moved forward (to suit small flight decks), corrosion protection, more powerful engines in later models, a RAST (Recovery Assist, Secure and Traverse) landing system hook, sonobuoy launchers, and an aft-mounted MAD (magnetic anomaly detector) bird on ASW variants.

Variants

The original SH-60B Seahawk was the LAMPS III ASW platform; the SH-60F "Ocean Hawk" provided carrier inner-zone ASW with a dipping sonar; the HH-60H "Rescue Hawk" supported special warfare and combat rescue. These were progressively replaced by two remanufactured types: the MH-60R "Romeo", combining the B and F missions with a glass cockpit, APS-147 radar, dipping sonar, and Hellfire missiles, and the MH-60S "Sierra" (Knighthawk lineage), which performs mine countermeasures, logistics, and armed helicopter missions. Export versions (S-70B and MH-60R) serve with many navies including Australia, Spain, Greece, Turkey, Japan (as the Mitsubishi-built SH-60J/K/L), South Korea, India, and Saudi Arabia.
