Isaaq

The Isaaq (Somali: Reer Sheekh Isxaaq) is a major Somali clan family. Its members traditionally inhabit the northern Horn of Africa, principally Somaliland, where they form the majority population, as well as parts of Ethiopia's Somali Region and Djibouti.

Origins and structure

Clan tradition traces descent from Sheikh Ishaaq ibn Ahmed, a scholar said to have arrived from Arabia in the 12th or 13th century and settled at the coastal town of Maydh. The clan family divides into major sub-clans including the Habr Awal, Habr Garhajis (Eidagale and Habr Yunis), Habr Je'lo, Arap, Ayub and Tol Je'lo.

History

Isaaq merchants dominated the trade of the ports of Berbera and Zeila, exporting frankincense, livestock and hides. Under British Somaliland, the region remained a protectorate until independence in 1960 and union with the former Italian Somaliland. Opposition to Siad Barre's regime produced the Somali National Movement (SNM), drawn largely from the Isaaq, which fought a guerrilla war in the 1980s; the regime's response included the destruction of Hargeisa and Burao and mass killings of Isaaq civilians in 1988, events often described as the Isaaq genocide. In 1991 the SNM declared the restoration of Somaliland's independence.

Notable people

Prominent Isaaq figures include poet Hadrawi, former Somaliland president Ahmed Mohamed Mohamoud "Silanyo", and Edna Adan Ismail, founder of a maternity hospital in Hargeisa.
