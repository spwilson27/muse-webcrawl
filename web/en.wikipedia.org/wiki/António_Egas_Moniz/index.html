António Egas Moniz

António Egas Moniz
Born: 29 November 1874, Avanca, Portugal
Died: 13 December 1955, Lisbon, Portugal
Known for: Cerebral angiography; prefrontal leucotomy
Awards: Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine (1949)

António Caetano de Abreu Freire Egas Moniz (29 November 1874 – 13 December 1955), known as Egas Moniz, was a Portuguese neurologist and statesman. He is credited with developing cerebral angiography, a foundational technique in medical imaging, and with introducing the prefrontal leucotomy (lobotomy) for the treatment of severe mental illness, for which he shared the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1949.

Career

Moniz studied medicine at the University of Coimbra and trained in neurology in France. He became the first professor of neurology at the University of Lisbon. Alongside his medical career he was active in politics, serving as a deputy, as Portugal's minister of foreign affairs, and as head of the Portuguese delegation at the Paris Peace Conference after the First World War.

Cerebral angiography

In 1927 Moniz successfully performed the first cerebral angiogram, injecting a radiopaque sodium iodide solution into the carotid artery to make the blood vessels of the brain visible on X-ray. The technique revolutionised the diagnosis of brain tumours, aneurysms and vascular malformations.

Leucotomy

In 1935, working with the surgeon Almeida Lima, Moniz began performing prefrontal leucotomies, severing connections between the frontal lobes and the rest of the brain in patients with severe psychiatric illness. The procedure spread internationally, but its often devastating side effects, and the arrival of antipsychotic medication in the 1950s, led to its abandonment; Moniz's Nobel Prize has remained controversial ever since.
