Olam Ha-Ba

Olam Ha-Ba (Hebrew: עוֹלָם הַבָּא, "The World to Come") is an important concept in Jewish eschatology. It refers to the afterlife and the world that follows the Messianic Age (Olam Ha-Zeh, "This World", being the present world).

Rabbinic views

In rabbinic literature, Olam Ha-Ba is associated with the resurrection of the dead (techiyat ha-metim) and a perfected world in which the righteous receive their reward. The Mishnah states that "all Israel has a portion in the World to Come", with certain exceptions. Maimonides described Olam Ha-Ba as a spiritual existence of the soul after death, free of physical needs, while other authorities envisioned a renewed earthly life following the resurrection.

Relationship to other concepts

Olam Ha-Ba is often discussed together with Gan Eden (the Garden of Eden as the abode of righteous souls), Gehinnom (a realm of purification, generally limited to twelve months), and the coming of the Messiah. Interpretations vary widely among Jewish movements, from literal belief in resurrection to metaphorical understandings.
