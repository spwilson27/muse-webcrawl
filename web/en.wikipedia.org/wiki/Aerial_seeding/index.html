Aerial seeding

Aerial seeding
MethodBroadcasting seed from aircraft or drones
PlatformsFixed-wing agricultural aircraft, helicopters, UAV drones
UsesCover crops, pasture, reforestation, post-fire revegetation
PayloadSeed, seed balls (seed, clay, compost)

Aerial seeding is a technique of sowing seeds by distributing them through the air, using an aircraft, helicopter or drone equipped with a spreader or hopper. It allows large or inaccessible areas — wetlands, steep slopes, burned forest — to be seeded quickly and without driving machinery over the ground.

History

Experiments began in the 1920s and 1930s; in 1929, aircraft were used to reseed denuded mountain slopes in Hawaii. After the Second World War, agricultural aviation grew rapidly, and aerial application became routine for sowing rice into flooded paddies in California and Australia, oversowing pasture in New Zealand hill country (together with aerial topdressing of fertiliser), and broadcasting cover crops into standing corn and soybean fields in the United States.

Technique

Aircraft fly low, overlapping passes guided by GPS, while a spinning spreader or pneumatic system meters out seed. Success depends on seed size and weight, wind, soil moisture and seed-to-soil contact; germination is typically less uniform than drilled seed, so higher seeding rates are used. For reforestation, seeds are often delivered in seed balls — pellets of clay, compost and seed that protect the seed from predators and drying — a method popularised by Masanobu Fukuoka's natural farming.

Modern developments

Drone-based companies now plant tree seeds with pneumatic pods that fire seeds into the soil, targeting post-wildfire restoration and carbon-offset reforestation, though survival rates remain debated and generally trail conventional planting.
