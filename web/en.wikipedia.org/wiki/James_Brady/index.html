James Scott Brady (August 29, 1940 – August 4, 2014) was an American public official and gun control advocate who served as the 17th White House press secretary during the presidency of Ronald Reagan. He was gravely wounded during the attempted assassination of Reagan on March 30, 1981, and remained partially paralyzed for the rest of his life.

Early life and career

Brady was born in Centralia, Illinois, and graduated from the University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign in 1962. He worked in public relations and in a series of government posts, including in the Department of Housing and Urban Development and the Department of Transportation, before joining Reagan's presidential campaign in 1980.

Assassination attempt and injury

On March 30, 1981, gunman John Hinckley Jr. opened fire outside the Washington Hilton Hotel. Brady was struck in the head by a bullet that passed through his brain. He survived after emergency surgery but was left with impaired speech, partial paralysis, and memory problems. He never returned to full duties as press secretary, though he retained the title for the remainder of Reagan's presidency; Larry Speakes and later Marlin Fitzwater served as acting press secretaries.

Gun control advocacy

With his wife Sarah, Brady became a leading campaigner for gun control. Their efforts culminated in the Brady Handgun Violence Prevention Act of 1993, commonly called the Brady Act, which mandated federal background checks on firearm purchasers and imposed a waiting period for handgun sales. The organization they worked with was renamed the Brady Campaign to Prevent Gun Violence in his honor.

Death and legacy

Brady died on August 4, 2014. The medical examiner ruled his death a homicide, caused by the gunshot wound he had received 33 years earlier. In 2000 the White House press briefing room was renamed the James S. Brady Press Briefing Room. He received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1996.
