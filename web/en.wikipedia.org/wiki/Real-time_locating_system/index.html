Real-time locating system

A real-time locating system (RTLS) is a system used to automatically identify and track the location of objects or people in real time, usually within a building or other bounded area. Wireless tags attached to assets or worn as badges communicate with fixed sensors, and a software platform computes and displays the tags' positions as they move.

Technologies

RTLS implementations use a variety of radio and non-radio technologies, including ultra-wideband (UWB) radio, which offers decimetre-level accuracy through time-of-flight measurement; Wi-Fi and Bluetooth Low Energy, which trade accuracy for reuse of existing infrastructure; active and passive RFID; infrared; and ultrasound. Position is computed by trilateration, multilateration from time-difference-of-arrival, or signal-strength fingerprinting.

Applications

Hospitals use RTLS to locate infusion pumps and wheelchairs, monitor hand-hygiene compliance and protect infants and wandering patients. In manufacturing and logistics the systems track tools, work in progress and forklifts; in warehousing they support inventory accuracy; and in offices and museums they enable wayfinding and workflow analytics. Accuracy requirements range from zone-level presence detection to centimetre-level precision.

Standards and privacy

The ISO/IEC 24730 series standardises RTLS terminology and interfaces. Because the same technology can track people, deployments raise workplace privacy questions, and many organisations restrict personnel tracking to safety-related uses.
