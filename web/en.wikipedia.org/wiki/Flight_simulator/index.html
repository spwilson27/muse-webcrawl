Flight simulator

A flight simulator is a device that artificially re-creates aircraft flight and the environment in which it flies, for pilot training, design, or other purposes. It includes replicating the equations that govern how aircraft fly, how they react to applications of flight controls, the effects of other aircraft systems, and how the aircraft reacts to external factors such as air density, turbulence, wind shear, cloud, and precipitation.

Training simulators

Full flight simulators (FFS) qualified to the highest levels (such as Level D) combine a replica cockpit, motion platform, and high-fidelity visual system, and can be used for zero-flight-time type rating training. Lower-level flight training devices lack motion or full visual systems.

Home and entertainment simulation

Flight simulation is also a popular software genre, with titles such as Microsoft Flight Simulator and X-Plane simulating general aviation and airliners on home computers, often with support for realistic cockpits built by hobbyists.

History

Early training devices included the Sanders Teacher and the Link Trainer of 1929, widely used during the Second World War. Computer-based simulation expanded rapidly from the 1960s onward.
