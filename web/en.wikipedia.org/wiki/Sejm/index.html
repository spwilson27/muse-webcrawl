Sejm

The Sejm (Polish pronunciation roughly "same") is the lower house of the bicameral parliament of Poland, the upper house being the Senate. The Sejm consists of 460 deputies (poseł) elected by proportional representation for four-year terms. It is the dominant chamber in the legislative process and the body to which the government is primarily accountable.

History

The name derives from an old Polish word for an assembly or gathering. The Sejm of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, developing from the fifteenth century, became one of Europe's earliest parliamentary institutions, bringing together the king, the Senate, and the Chamber of Deputies elected by local sejmiks (regional assemblies) of the nobility. It is notorious for the liberum veto, by which a single deputy's objection could dissolve the session, a practice blamed for paralysing the Commonwealth in the eighteenth century.

After Poland regained independence in 1918, the Legislative Sejm drafted the March Constitution of 1921. In the communist Polish People's Republic the Sejm was formally the highest organ of state power but remained largely a rubber stamp, apart from brief periods of greater assertiveness. The partially free elections of June 1989, in which the Sejm was re-established alongside a freely elected Senate, marked a turning point in the collapse of communism in Central Europe.

Composition and powers

Deputies are elected in multi-member constituencies by open-list proportional representation with the d'Hondt method; a 5% national threshold applies to parties and 8% to coalitions. The Sejm is presided over by its Marshal. Its powers include passing statutes (which the Senate may amend or reject, subject to override by the Sejm), adopting the state budget, granting and withdrawing confidence in the Council of Ministers, ratifying major treaties, appointing key officials such as the president of the National Bank of Poland, and impeaching officials before the Tribunal of State.

The Sejm and Senate together, sitting as the National Assembly, act in special cases such as receiving the presidential oath and considering the president's capacity to govern.
