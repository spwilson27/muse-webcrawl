Kalinjar Fort

LocationBanda district, Uttar Pradesh, India
RegionBundelkhand, Vindhya range
BuildersChandela dynasty (9th–11th centuries)
ElevationAbout 700 m above sea level

Kalinjar is a fortress-city in the Bundelkhand region of Uttar Pradesh, India. The fort crowns a rocky hill of the Vindhya range south of the town of Banda and was strategically coveted for centuries as the key to the plains of northern India.

History

Kalinjar rose to prominence under the Chandela dynasty, whose kings refortified it in the ninth to eleventh centuries and also built the celebrated temples of nearby Khajuraho. It withstood sieges by Mahmud of Ghazni and was contested by the Pratiharas, the Delhi Sultanate and the Mughals. Sher Shah Suri died at Kalinjar in 1545 when a gunpowder explosion occurred during his siege of the fort. The Bundela rajas later held it, and in 1812 it passed to the British. During the revolt of 1857 the fort was held against the British for a time by local forces.

Description

The hilltop enclosure contains palaces, rock-cut reservoirs, Jain shrines and Hindu temples, most famously the Neelkanth Mahadev temple with its carved Shivalinga and the nearby natural rock-cut figure of Vishnu reclining (Swarga Kund and Chaube Mahal are among its noted structures). Inscriptions at the site record the deeds of Chandela rulers.
