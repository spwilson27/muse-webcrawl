Capel Celyn was a Welsh-speaking village in the Tryweryn valley in Merionethshire (now Gwynedd), north Wales. It was drowned in 1965 when the valley was flooded to create the Llyn Celyn reservoir, built to supply water to the city of Liverpool in England. The flooding of Capel Celyn became one of the most emotive episodes in modern Welsh history and a powerful catalyst for Welsh nationalism.

The flooding

In 1957 Liverpool City Council promoted a private Act of Parliament to build the reservoir, bypassing Welsh local planning authorities. Despite the opposition of 35 of the 36 Welsh Members of Parliament, the Liverpool Corporation Act was passed. Around 70 people were evicted from the village, which included a chapel (from which the village took its name), a school, a post office, a cemetery and a dozen farms.

The campaign slogan Cofiwch Dryweryn — "Remember Tryweryn" — painted on a ruined wall near Llanrhystud, became an enduring symbol of Welsh national feeling. In 2005 Liverpool City Council formally apologised for the insensitivity of the original decision.

Legacy

The episode greatly boosted support for the Welsh nationalist party Plaid Cymru, which won its first seat at Westminster in the 1966 Carmarthen by-election. During droughts, falling water levels in Llyn Celyn occasionally reveal the remains of the village.
