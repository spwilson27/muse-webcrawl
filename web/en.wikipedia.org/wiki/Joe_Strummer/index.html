Joe Strummer (born John Graham Mellor, 21 August 1952 – 22 December 2002) was an English musician, singer and songwriter, best known as the co-founder, lyricist, rhythm guitarist and lead vocalist of the punk rock band the Clash. With Mick Jones, Strummer wrote most of the band's material, giving punk an explicitly political voice that ranged across reggae, dub, rockabilly and world music; the Clash's London Calling (1979) is routinely ranked among the greatest albums ever made. Strummer later fronted the Mescaleros and became an elder statesman of British rock.

Early life

Strummer was born in Ankara, the son of a British diplomat, and spent his childhood in Turkey, Egypt, Mexico and Germany before being sent to boarding school in England. Drawn to rock and roll, he played in pub-rock bands in Newport and London, notably the 101ers, and adopted the name "Strummer" in self-deprecating reference to his rudimentary guitar technique. In 1976, impressed by the Sex Pistols, he accepted manager Bernie Rhodes's invitation to join a new band with Mick Jones and Paul Simonon — the Clash.

The Clash

The Clash's self-titled debut (1977) channelled the anger of unemployed Britain in songs like "White Riot" and "Career Opportunities". London Calling (1979), a double album embracing ska, jazz, soul and Americana, made them international stars, and Sandinista! (1980) — a triple album whose title honoured the Nicaraguan revolutionaries — extended their reach. Combat Rock (1982) produced the hits "Rock the Casbah" and "Should I Stay or Should I Go". Strummer fired Jones in 1983, a decision he later lamented, and the band dissolved in 1986 after the poorly received Cut the Crap.

Later work and death

Strummer acted (notably in Alex Cox's Straight to Hell and Jim Jarmusch's Mystery Train), scored films, temporarily fronted the Pogues, and recorded the solo album Earthquake Weather (1989). In 1999 he formed Joe Strummer and the Mescaleros, releasing Rock Art and the X-Ray Style (1999) and Global a Go-Go (2001) to warm acclaim. In November 2002 he performed with Mick Jones on stage for the first time in nearly twenty years, fuelling reunion hopes. Weeks later, on 22 December 2002, he died suddenly at home in Somerset of an undiagnosed congenital heart defect, aged 50. The Mescaleros album Streetcore was completed and released posthumously in 2003. The Clash were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame that year, and Strummer's Glastonbury campfire gatherings, "Strummerville", inspired a charity supporting aspiring musicians.
