Marion Anne Perrine "Marine" Le Pen (born 5 August 1968) is a French lawyer and politician who served as president of the National Rally (RN), formerly the National Front, from 2011 to 2021. She was the party's candidate in the presidential elections of 2012, 2017, and 2022, reaching the second round in the latter two and losing to Emmanuel Macron both times. She has served as a Member of the National Assembly for Pas-de-Calais's 11th constituency since 2017, and led the RN group in the Assembly from 2022.

Early life and legal career

Le Pen was born in Neuilly-sur-Seine, the youngest of three daughters of Jean-Marie Le Pen, founder of the National Front. In 1976 her family survived a bombing of their Paris apartment block. She studied law at Panthéon-Assas University, earning a master's degree in law and a postgraduate diploma in criminal law, and practised as a lawyer from 1992 to 1998, frequently representing illegal immigrants in court. She directed the National Front's legal department before entering electoral politics.

Leadership of the party

After holding regional and European mandates, Le Pen won the National Front leadership in 2011, succeeding her father. She pursued a strategy of "de-demonisation" (dédiabolisation), seeking to soften the party's image, expel extremist members — including, in 2015, her own father after he repeated controversial remarks about the Holocaust — and move the party toward economic protectionism and welfare policies for French citizens. In 2018 the party renamed itself National Rally. Under her leadership its vote share rose steadily; she stepped down as president in 2021 to focus on the presidential campaign and was succeeded by Jordan Bardella in 2022.

Presidential campaigns

Le Pen finished third in 2012 with 17.9% of the vote. In 2017 she reached the runoff with 21.3% in the first round but lost to Macron with 33.9%. In 2022, campaigning on purchasing power and opposition to immigration, she won 23.2% in the first round and 41.5% in the runoff — the far right's best result in French presidential history. In the snap legislative elections of 2024, the RN and its allies finished first in the first-round vote, though the party placed third in seats after tactical voting.

Legal case

In March 2025, a Paris court convicted Le Pen and other RN figures of embezzling European Parliament funds through fictitious assistant contracts. She was sentenced to four years' imprisonment (two suspended, two under electronic monitoring), fined, and barred from holding public office for five years with immediate effect — a ruling she appealed, which put her eligibility for the 2027 presidential election in doubt.

Positions

Le Pen advocates restrictions on immigration, "national priority" for French citizens in jobs, housing, and welfare, withdrawal from the integrated command of NATO, and referendums on major policies. Her economic programme mixes protectionism with support for small businesses and, in later campaigns, cost-of-living measures such as cuts to energy taxes.
