Shing-Tung Yau (born April 4, 1949) is a Chinese-American mathematician. He is a professor of mathematics at Tsinghua University and professor emeritus at Harvard University. Yau is known for his work in differential geometry and geometric analysis, and is widely considered one of the most influential mathematicians of his generation.

Early life and education

Yau was born in Shantou, Guangdong, and grew up in Hong Kong. He studied at the Chinese University of Hong Kong and received his PhD from the University of California, Berkeley, in 1971 under the supervision of Shiing-Shen Chern.

Contributions

In 1976 Yau proved the Calabi conjecture, establishing the existence of Ricci-flat metrics on Kähler manifolds; the resulting spaces are now called Calabi–Yau manifolds and play a central role in string theory. With Richard Schoen he proved the positive mass theorem in general relativity. He has also made fundamental contributions to the study of minimal surfaces, harmonic maps, and eigenvalue problems.

Awards

Yau received the Fields Medal in 1982, the MacArthur Fellowship in 1985, the Crafoord Prize in 1994, the US National Medal of Science in 1997, and the Wolf Prize in Mathematics in 2010.
