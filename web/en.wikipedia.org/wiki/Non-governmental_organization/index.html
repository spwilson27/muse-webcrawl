Non-governmental organization

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A non-governmental organization (NGO) is an organization that generally is formed independent from government, typically a nonprofit entity funded by donations and run by volunteers and paid staff. The category spans humanitarian relief and development organisations (Oxfam, MSF), human-rights monitors (Amnesty International, Human Rights Watch), environmental campaigns (Greenpeace), professional and industry bodies, foundations and think tanks — a residual class held together by being neither state nor market.

Role and scrutiny

The term entered formal usage with the United Nations (Charter Article 71 providing for ECOSOC consultation with NGOs), and the sector's explosion from the 1970s tracks decolonization's aftermath, Cold War funding streams, and the governance turn in development (donors routing aid through NGOs as states were rolled back). NGOs deliver a large share of humanitarian relief and development services, set agendas (the landmine ban and the ICC were NGO-campaigned into existence), monitor elections and abuses, and litigate strategically. Criticism runs on two flanks: accountability (unelected, donor-answerable, often Northern-headquartered organisations speaking for Southern publics) and authoritarian pushback (foreign-agent laws in Russia, India, Hungary, Egypt and elsewhere restricting NGO funding and operation as alleged foreign interference) — the "closing civic space" that groups like CIVICUS document year by year.
