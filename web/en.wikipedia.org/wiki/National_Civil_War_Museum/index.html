National Civil War Museum

Location: Harrisburg, Pennsylvania
Opened: 2001
Type: History museum
Collections: American Civil War artefacts

The National Civil War Museum is a museum in Harrisburg, Pennsylvania, dedicated to the American Civil War (1861–1865). It presents the conflict from both Union and Confederate perspectives without a partisan slant.

History

The museum was championed by Harrisburg mayor Stephen R. Reed and opened in 2001 in Reservoir Park. Harrisburg was a major Union logistical hub and training centre (Camp Curtin) and was threatened during the Gettysburg campaign, giving the city strong Civil War connections despite hosting no major battle.

Exhibits

Galleries trace the war from its causes in slavery and sectional conflict through secession, the major campaigns, the home front, emancipation and Reconstruction. Artefacts include uniforms, weapons, medical equipment and personal items, including objects associated with Robert E. Lee and Abraham Lincoln. The museum became affiliated with the Smithsonian Institution in 2009.
