Pilton village, host to Worthy Farm and the Glastonbury Festival.

Pilton is a village and civil parish in Somerset, England, situated about 3 miles east of Shepton Mallet and south-west of Glastonbury. It is best known internationally as the home of Worthy Farm, the site of the Glastonbury Festival, the world's largest greenfield music and performing arts festival.

History

The village has ancient roots and is recorded in Domesday Book. Its parish church of St John the Baptist is a Grade I listed building of largely thirteenth-to-fifteenth-century date. Pilton was once noted for cider making; in recent years a cider farm in the parish has revived the tradition. The village lies on the route of the old Fosse Way Roman road and near the line of the former Somerset and Dorset Joint Railway.

Glastonbury Festival

In September 1970 the dairy farmer Michael Eavis held the small Pilton Pop, Blues & Folk Festival at Worthy Farm. The event grew into the Glastonbury Festival, which now draws around 200,000 people in most years, transforming the quiet parish for a week each summer. A village hall, playing field improvements and other amenities have been funded from festival proceeds.
