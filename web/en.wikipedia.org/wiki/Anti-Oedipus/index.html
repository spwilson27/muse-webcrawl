Anti-Oedipus: Capitalism and Schizophrenia (French: L'Anti-Œdipe: Capitalisme et schizophrénie) is a 1972 book by the French philosopher Gilles Deleuze and the psychoanalyst Félix Guattari. It is the first volume of their collaborative work Capitalism and Schizophrenia, the second being A Thousand Plateaus (1980).

Written in the wake of the May 1968 events in France, the book mounts a critique of psychoanalysis, structuralism and capitalism, arguing that desire is a productive, machinic force that orthodox Freudian theory wrongly shackles to the Oedipus complex and the family.

Summary

Deleuze and Guattari propose schizoanalysis as an alternative to psychoanalysis. Against Freud's view of the unconscious as a theatre of representation governed by lack and law, they describe "desiring-machines" and flows of desire that are coded and decoded by social formations. Capitalism, they argue, decodes and deterritorialises flows — of money, labour and desire — while simultaneously reterritorialising them, and it tends to produce schizophrenia as its exterior limit. The authors famously rework Marx and Freud together, treating libidinal economy and political economy as one and the same economy of flows.

Key concepts

  • Desiring-production — the process by which desire produces reality and bodies.
  • Body without organs — the unproductive surface on which desiring-machines register their effects.
  • Schizoanalysis — the proposed method of analysing desire's investments in the social field.
  • Deterritorialisation and reterritorialisation — the decoding of flows and their recapture by new codes.

Reception and influence

Anti-Oedipus was a major intellectual event in 1970s France and became a foundational text of post-structuralist thought, though it was attacked by orthodox psychoanalysts and some Marxists, and later critics have debated its celebration of schizophrenia. Michel Foucault contributed a preface to the English translation (1977), describing the book as "an Introduction to the Non-Fascist Life". Its ideas influenced later movements in political theory, art and accelerationist philosophy.
