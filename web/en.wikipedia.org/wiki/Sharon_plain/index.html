Sharon plain

The Sharon plain, Israel's central coastal plain.

The Sharon plain (Hebrew: HaSharon) is the central section of the Israeli coastal plain. The plain lies between the Mediterranean Sea to the west and the Samarian Hills, 15 kilometers (9.3 mi) to the east. It stretches from Nahal Taninim (Crocdile Stream) in the north to the Yarkon River in the south, at the northern limit of Tel Aviv, a total of about 90 kilometers (56 mi). The Sharon region is often further subdivided, with the central Sharon around the cities of Netanya and Herzliya, and the southern Sharon around Ra'anana, Kfar Saba, and Hod HaSharon.

Geography

The plain is part of Israel's Mediterranean coastal strip and is relatively narrow, widening from about 8 kilometers in the south to around 15 kilometers further north. Its soils are predominantly sandy red loam (hamra) and kurkar (calcareous sandstone) ridges running parallel to the coast. The region is crossed by several short streams, including Nahal Alexander and Nahal Poleg, which flow from the hills to the sea.

Historically, much of the Sharon was marshy and covered by oak forest, the "forest of Sharon" mentioned in ancient sources. Drainage and clearance in the late Ottoman and British Mandate periods transformed it into one of the most fertile agricultural districts of the country.

History

The Sharon is mentioned in the Bible and in Egyptian texts of the New Kingdom period. In antiquity the Via Maris, the great coastal highway linking Egypt with Syria and Mesopotamia, passed through the plain, and the port city of Caesarea Maritima, built by Herod the Great, became its principal settlement in the Roman period. In the 19th and early 20th centuries the plain was reclaimed for agriculture, becoming famous for its citrus groves, which gave rise to the Jaffa orange industry.

Agriculture and settlement

Citrus cultivation long dominated the Sharon, but urbanization and water costs have shifted production toward avocados, flowers, and field crops. The plain is now one of the most densely settled parts of Israel, containing the cities of Netanya, Herzliya, Ra'anana, Kfar Saba, Hod HaSharon, Ramat HaSharon, and parts of the Tel Aviv metropolitan area (Gush Dan).

Transport

The Coastal Highway (Highway 2) and the older Highway 4 run north–south through the plain, as does the coastal railway line with stations serving the Sharon cities. Highway 5 crosses the plain east–west from the Mediterranean coast toward Ariel in the West Bank hills.
