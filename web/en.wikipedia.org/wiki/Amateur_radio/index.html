Amateur radio, also known as ham radio, is the use of the radio frequency spectrum for purposes of non-commercial exchange of messages, wireless experimentation, self-training, private recreation, radiosport, contesting, and emergency communications. The term "amateur" is used to specify "a duly authorised person interested in radioelectric practice with a purely personal aim and without pecuniary interest" (either direct monetary or other similar reward) and to differentiate it from commercial broadcasting, two-way radio, or professional two-way radio services.

History

Amateur radio developed in the early 20th century as experimenters built their own spark-gap transmitters. The Radio Act of 1912 in the United States confined amateurs to wavelengths of 200 metres and shorter, then considered useless; amateurs soon proved these "short waves" could propagate worldwide. During both world wars, amateur activities were suspended in many countries and operators' skills were used by the military. After 1945 the hobby expanded with surplus equipment, single-sideband voice, and later digital modes and amateur satellites (beginning with OSCAR 1 in 1961).

Licensing

Operators must pass examinations on radio theory, regulations, and operating practice and receive a call sign from their national regulator. Licence classes grant different frequency and power privileges. Historically, proficiency in Morse code was required for access to shortwave bands; most countries removed that requirement in the 2000s.

Activities

Amateurs communicate by voice, Morse code (CW), and digital modes such as FT8, packet radio, and amateur television. Popular pursuits include DXing (contacting distant stations), contesting, satellite operation, moonbounce (EME), portable operation from summits (SOTA) and parks (POTA), and providing emergency communications through groups such as ARES and RACES after disasters.

Frequency allocations

The International Telecommunication Union allocates bands across the spectrum, from the 2200-metre band (135.7–137.8 kHz) through HF favourites such as 40 metres (7 MHz) and 20 metres (14 MHz), VHF/UHF bands such as 2 metres (144 MHz) and 70 centimetres (430 MHz), up to microwave allocations.
