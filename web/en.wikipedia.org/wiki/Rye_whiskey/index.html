Rye whiskey

TypeWhiskey
RegionUnited States (esp. Pennsylvania, Maryland, Kentucky); Canada
Main ingredientRye grain (≥51% for U.S. straight rye)

Rye whiskey is a whiskey distilled primarily from rye grain. In the United States, rye whiskey must be made from a mash of at least 51% rye, distilled to no more than 80% alcohol by volume and aged in charred new oak barrels; "straight" rye is aged at least two years. Canadian whisky is also traditionally called rye whisky or simply "rye", even when rye is a minor component of the mash.

History

Rye whiskey was the predominant American whiskey of the colonial and early national periods, produced by Scots-Irish and German settlers in Pennsylvania and Maryland. Disputes over the federal excise tax on it sparked the Whiskey Rebellion of 1791–1794. Prohibition (1920–1933) devastated the industry, and bourbon and blended whiskies afterwards eclipsed straight rye; a craft-distilling revival since the early 2000s has restored rye to prominence.

Character

Rye grain gives the spirit a spicier, drier, more peppery and fruity character than corn-based bourbon. It is the classic base of the Manhattan, Old Fashioned (originally), Sazerac and Whiskey Sour families of cocktails.
