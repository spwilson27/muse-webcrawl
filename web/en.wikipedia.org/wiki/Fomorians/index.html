The Fomorians or Fomori (Old Irish: Fomóire, "under-demons" or "ones from beneath the sea") are a supernatural people in Irish mythology who represent the hostile powers of chaos, darkness and nature — blight, famine, drought and the stormy sea. In the pseudo-historical scheme of the Lebor Gabála Érenn they are present in Ireland from the beginning, opposing each successive wave of settlers in the Mythological Cycle.

Description

Medieval texts describe many Fomorians as misshapen: one-eyed, one-armed or one-legged. Their first named leader in Ireland, Cichol Gricenchos, is depicted with six toes on each foot. Later tradition made them giants and sea-raiders based on Tory Island and the Hebrides, exacting tribute of children, cattle and corn from the peoples of Ireland. Scholars have suggested that the Fomorians preserve a memory of the gods of a pre-Celtic or chthonic pantheon, and that their name may mean "those beneath the sea" — fitting their association with the sea's destructive powers, storms and piracy.

Mythology

The Fomorians fought Partholón's settlers at Mag Itha, oppressed the Nemedians with heavy tribute until the Nemedians rose and were nearly annihilated, and intermarried uneasily with the Tuatha Dé Danann: the beautiful Fomorian prince Elatha fathered Bres upon Ériu of the Tuatha Dé, and Bres — made king of the Tuatha Dé — ruled so tyrannically that he became a byword for oppression, imposing Fomorian tribute and humiliating the gods. The conflict culminated in the second Battle of Mag Tuired, in which the champion Lugh — himself half-Fomorian, being Balor's grandson — cast a sling-stone through the destructive eye of his grandfather Balor, driving it out the back of his head so that its gaze destroyed the Fomorian host. After the battle the Fomorians' power in Ireland was broken; in the tale, Lugh spares Bres in exchange for the secrets of agriculture.
