Gesture drawing

A gesture drawing is a laying in of the action, form, and pose of a model or figure. Typically, an artist draws a series of poses taken by a model in a short period of time, often as little as 30 seconds or as long as two minutes. Gesture drawing is a fundamental exercise in life drawing and animation training, intended to capture the overall movement, weight and rhythm of a pose rather than its details.

Purpose

The primary purpose of gesture drawing is to facilitate the study of the human figure in motion. Such studies aid the artist in depicting the figure's action, balance and expression, and they help train the eye to see the line of action running through the pose before secondary forms are considered. Because the poses are brief, the artist cannot rely on careful measurement and must instead summarise the figure into a few confident, economical lines.

Practice

In a typical life-drawing class, gesture exercises open the session as a warm-up: the model takes a sequence of short poses, and students draw with loose, flowing strokes, often in charcoal, ink or a soft pencil on large, cheap paper. Teachers such as Kimon Nicolaïdes, in The Natural Way to Draw (1941), made gesture study the foundation of drawing instruction. Animators at studios such as Disney adopted the practice to give drawn characters a convincing sense of life and movement.

Relation to other exercises

Gesture drawing is often contrasted with sustained drawing and with contour drawing: it prioritises the whole over the parts and movement over description. It is frequently combined with quick "croquis" studies, a term borrowed from French fashion and art schools.
