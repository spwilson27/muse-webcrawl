The petroleum industry in Venezuela is the country's dominant economic sector. Venezuela holds the world's largest proved oil reserves, estimated at over 300 billion barrels, concentrated in the extra-heavy crude of the Orinoco Belt and the conventional fields of the Maracaibo Basin. Oil has historically provided the overwhelming majority of Venezuela's export earnings.

History

Commercial production began in the early twentieth century, and by 1928 Venezuela was the world's leading oil exporter. The industry was nationalised in 1976 with the creation of the state company Petróleos de Venezuela, S.A. (PDVSA), which for decades was regarded as one of the best-run national oil companies. Venezuela was a founding member of OPEC in 1960. Production peaked at over 3 million barrels per day in the late 1990s.

Decline

Following the 2002–03 general strike, the government of Hugo Chávez dismissed much of PDVSA's workforce and increased state control and fiscal demands on the company. Underinvestment, loss of technical expertise, expropriations of foreign partners' assets, falling prices, corruption and, from 2017–2019, United States sanctions caused production to collapse to a fraction of its former level by 2020 — one of the steepest declines in oil-industry history — with severe consequences for the Venezuelan economy.
