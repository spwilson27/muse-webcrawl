A security clearance is a status granted to individuals allowing them access to classified information or restricted areas after a thorough background investigation. Clearances are required for many positions in government, the military, intelligence agencies, and defense contracting. Holding a clearance does not by itself grant access to any particular information; access additionally requires a legitimate "need to know."

Investigation and adjudication

Applicants typically complete a detailed questionnaire covering residence, employment, foreign contacts, finances, drug use, and criminal history. Investigators interview references and check records, and some positions require a polygraph examination. Adjudicators evaluate the "whole person" against guidelines concerning allegiance, foreign influence, financial considerations, criminal conduct, and other factors. Clearances are subject to periodic reinvestigation or continuous evaluation, and can be suspended or revoked.

Levels

In the United States, the principal levels correspond to the classification of information: Confidential, Secret, and Top Secret. Programs involving especially sensitive information may require additional access approvals, such as Sensitive Compartmented Information (SCI) or Special Access Programs (SAPs). Other countries operate analogous systems, such as the United Kingdom's Counter Terrorist Check, Security Check, and Developed Vetting.
