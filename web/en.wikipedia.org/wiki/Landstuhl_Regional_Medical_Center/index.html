The Landstuhl Regional Medical Center (LRMC) is a United States Army military hospital near Landstuhl, Germany, and the largest American military hospital outside the United States. It serves US service members, their families and other beneficiaries across Europe, Africa and the Middle East, and is above all known as the first major treatment point for American and coalition casualties evacuated from combat zones downrange.

Role

Founded in 1953 as the 320th General Hospital (later the 2nd General Hospital) during the Cold War buildup, the hospital sits on the Kirchberg hill overlooking Landstuhl, close to Ramstein Air Base, through which aeromedical evacuation flights arrive. During the wars in Iraq and Afghanistan, nearly all seriously wounded American troops passed through Landstuhl: casualties stabilised at forward surgical facilities were flown to Ramstein, treated at LRMC — typically within 24–72 hours of wounding — and then flown on to Walter Reed and other hospitals in the United States. Over the course of those wars the centre treated tens of thousands of wounded, ill and injured patients from dozens of coalition nations, as well as some local and enemy casualties. It has been designated an American College of Surgeons Level II trauma centre and is the only US trauma centre of its level overseas.

Future

The aging hilltop facility is to be replaced by the new Rhine Ordnance Barracks Medical Center near Ramstein, a joint Army–Air Force hospital under construction since the 2010s and among the largest US military construction projects in Germany; when it opens, Landstuhl's inpatient services will move there and the LRMC name and mission will transfer.
