The Maltese Falcon is a 1930 detective novel by American writer Dashiell Hammett, originally serialised in the magazine Black Mask beginning in September 1929. It introduces the private detective Sam Spade and is regarded as a defining work of hardboiled crime fiction, notable for its spare, objective prose — Hammett describes only what characters say and do — and its unsparing dialogue.

Plot

San Francisco private eyes Sam Spade and Miles Archer are hired by a woman calling herself Miss Wonderly to shadow a man named Thursby. Archer and Thursby are both murdered that night. The client, really Brigid O'Shaughnessy, draws Spade into a hunt for a jewel-encrusted statuette of a falcon, once tribute from the Knights of Malta to Charles V of Spain, sought also by the urbane Casper Gutman ("the Fat Man"), the nervous Joel Cairo and the gunsel Wilmer Cook. When the falcon finally arrives it proves to be a fake of lead; and Spade, despite his involvement with Brigid, turns her over to the police for Archer's murder: "I won't play the sap for you."

Adaptations and legacy

The novel was filmed three times, most famously in 1941 by John Huston, starring Humphrey Bogart as Spade — a landmark of film noir. The book appears on numerous lists of the greatest English-language novels of the twentieth century, and Sam Spade became the archetype of the hardboiled private eye.
