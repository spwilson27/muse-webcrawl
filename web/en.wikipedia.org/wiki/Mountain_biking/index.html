Mountain biking

Downhill mountain biking on a forest trail

Mountain biking is a sport of riding bicycles off-road, often over rough terrain, usually on specially designed mountain bikes. Mountain bikes share similarities with other bikes but incorporate features designed to enhance durability and performance in rough terrain, such as air or coil-sprung shocks, wide knobbly tyres, disc brakes and wide handlebars.

History

The sport's modern form emerged in the mid-1970s on Mount Tamalpais in Marin County, California, where riders including Gary Fisher, Charlie Kelly and Joe Breeze raced modified cruiser bikes down fire roads and built the first purpose-made frames. It became an Olympic sport at the 1996 Atlanta Games in the cross-country discipline.

Disciplines

Mountain biking encompasses cross-country (XC), trail riding, all-mountain/enduro, downhill (DH), freeride, dirt jumping, and trials. Cross-country racing rewards endurance on mixed circuits, while downhill is a time trial against the clock on steep, technical descents, usually reached by lift. The UCI Mountain Bike World Cup runs series in both disciplines.

Equipment and advocacy

Riders commonly wear helmets (full-face for downhill), gloves and protective pads. Trail access and environmental impact are major issues for the sport; organisations such as the International Mountain Bicycling Association (IMBA) promote sustainable trail building and rider etiquette.
