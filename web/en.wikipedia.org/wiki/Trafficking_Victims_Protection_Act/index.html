Trafficking Victims Protection Act

Trafficking Victims Protection Act of 2000
EnactedOctober 28, 2000
Signed byPresident Bill Clinton
Codified22 U.S.C. § 7101 et seq.

The Trafficking Victims Protection Act (TVPA) is a United States federal law enacted in 2000 as part of the Victims of Trafficking and Violence Protection Act (Public Law 106-386). It was the first comprehensive federal legislation to address human trafficking, and it established the framework — often summarized as the "3 Ps": prevention, protection of victims, and prosecution of traffickers — that continues to structure American anti-trafficking policy.

Provisions

The TVPA criminalized trafficking in persons with severe penalties, including forced labour and sex trafficking offences, and provided for restitution to victims. It created protections for foreign victims in the United States, including the T visa, which allows victims of severe trafficking who cooperate with law enforcement (with exceptions for minors) to remain in the country and eventually seek permanent residence. The Act also authorized assistance programmes for victims and established the State Department's Office to Monitor and Combat Trafficking in Persons.

Trafficking in Persons Report

The Act requires the State Department to issue an annual Trafficking in Persons (TIP) Report ranking governments by their efforts against trafficking into tiers. Countries placed on Tier 3 — those neither meeting minimum standards nor making significant efforts — may face restrictions on non-humanitarian U.S. assistance, making the report a significant diplomatic instrument.

Reauthorizations

The TVPA has been reauthorized and amended several times, notably in 2003, 2005, 2008 (the William Wilberforce Trafficking Victims Protection Reauthorization Act), 2013 (as an amendment to the Violence Against Women Act), and through later legislation in 2018–2019. Successive versions expanded protections for child victims, addressed trafficking in supply chains and online marketplaces, and strengthened data collection.
