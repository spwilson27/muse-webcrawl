Brazilian Space Agency

Agência Espacial Brasileira

Founded: 10 February 1994

Headquarters: Brasília, Brazil

Parent: Ministry of Science, Technology and Innovation

The Brazilian Space Agency (Portuguese: Agência Espacial Brasileira, AEB) is the civilian authority in Brazil responsible for the country's space programme. It operates the Alcântara Launch Center in Maranhão, near the equator, which offers significant fuel advantages for launches.

History

The AEB was created in 1994, replacing the earlier military-led Brazilian Commission for Space Activities. Brazil's programme suffered a major setback in 2003, when an explosion during preparations for a VLS-1 launch at Alcântara killed 21 technicians.

Programmes

Brazil has developed Earth-observation satellites in partnership with China under the CBERS (China–Brazil Earth Resources Satellite) programme, and launched the Amazônia-1 satellite, the first Earth-observation satellite designed and built entirely in Brazil, in 2021.
