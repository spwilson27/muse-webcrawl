The Silver Ring Thing (SRT) is an American evangelical Christian sexual abstinence program founded in 1995 by Denny Pattyn in Yuma, Arizona, and later based near Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. The program encourages teenagers to pledge abstinence from sex until marriage, symbolized by wearing a silver ring — typically inscribed with a reference to 1 Thessalonians 4:3–4 — on the ring finger of the left hand until it is replaced by a wedding ring.

A silver ring of the kind given to participants who take the abstinence pledge.

Program

Silver Ring Thing events are staged as high-energy, concert-style presentations held in churches, schools, and arenas, combining music, drama, comedy, and testimonies with a presentation of the Christian gospel. At the conclusion, attendees are invited to make a public commitment to abstinence and receive a silver ring, priced into the event materials, as a reminder of the pledge. The organization also ran follow-up materials and online accountability resources, and operated an international arm in the United Kingdom, South Africa, and other countries under names such as the "Silver Ring Thing" tour.

Funding and controversy

The organization received United States federal funding for abstinence education during the 2000s. In 2005 the American Civil Liberties Union sued the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, arguing that federal funds were being used to promote religion through the Silver Ring Thing's overtly evangelistic content. The department suspended the program's grant pending corrective changes, and the case was settled in 2006 with an agreement that federally funded activities would not include religious content.

Like other abstinence-only programs, Silver Ring Thing has been criticized by researchers and public-health advocates who question the effectiveness of pledge-based approaches, while supporters argue the program gives teenagers a positive, peer-supported alternative. The organization later rebranded its events under the name "Unaltered."
