Henry W. Coe State Park is a California state park in the Diablo Range east of Morgan Hill. At about 87,000 acres (35,000 ha), it is the largest state park in northern California and one of the largest in the state park system. Its rugged terrain of steep ridges, oak woodland, grassland, and chaparral is a popular destination for backpacking, mountain biking, and horseback riding.

History

The park is named for Henry Willard Coe, a rancher who owned the land and built a home at Pine Ridge. After his death, his daughter Sada Coe Robinson donated some 12,500 acres to Santa Clara County in 1953 in his memory, and the land became a state park in 1959. Over the following decades the park expanded greatly through purchases and donations of surrounding ranches, including the former Bell Station area to the east. The park headquarters and visitor center occupy the original Coe Ranch site.

Landscape and recreation

The park protects two major watersheds, Coyote Creek and the Pacheco Creek drainage. Popular routes include the trail to China Hole, a swimming hole on Coyote Creek, and the climb to Mount Sizer, the park's high point at about 3,216 feet (980 m). More than 250 miles of trails and ranch roads cross the park. Spring wildflower displays are a major attraction, and the park is also used by mountain bikers, including for the annual Coe mountain bike events. Much of the park burned in the 2020 SCU Lightning Complex fires, after which it largely recovered.

Wildlife

Wildlife includes black-tailed deer, bobcats, mountain lions, wild pigs, coyotes, golden eagles, and the endangered California red-legged frog and California tiger salamander in its stock ponds.
