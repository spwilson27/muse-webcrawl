ʻIolani Palace

ʻIolani Palace in Honolulu, Hawaii, is the only royal palace on soil of the United States. It was the official residence of the rulers of the Kingdom of Hawaii, from King Kalākaua, who built it in 1882, to Queen Liliʻuokalani, the last monarch, who was deposed in 1893.

History

The palace stands on a site long associated with Hawaiian royalty. Kalākaua's ornate new palace, built in the American Florentine style at a cost of about $360,000, was remarkably modern, with electric lighting installed before even the White House had it. After the overthrow of the monarchy in 1893, the palace served as the capitol of the Republic, Territory, and State of Hawaii.

Museum

Government offices moved out in 1969, and after extensive restoration the palace opened as a museum in 1978, administered by the Friends of ʻIolani Palace. The throne room, state dining room, and the Queen's imprisonment room — where Liliʻuokalani was held under house arrest in 1895 — are among its notable interiors.
