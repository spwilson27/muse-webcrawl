Moroccan franc

The Moroccan franc (Arabic: فرنك) was the currency of French Morocco from 1921 until 1957, when it was replaced by the Moroccan dirham as preparations for monetary reunification following independence. It continued to circulate alongside the dirham until 1960, at a rate of 100 francs = 1 dirham. In the Spanish protectorate in Morocco, the franc was equivalent to the Spanish peseta.

History

Before the First World War the Moroccan rijal was worth 10 French francs. The franc was introduced in 1921 under the French protectorate, replacing the rijal at a rate of 10 francs = 1 rijal, and was issued by the Banque d'État du Maroc. Like the French franc, it was divided into 100 centimes.

Coins and banknotes

Coins were issued in denominations of 25 and 50 centimes and 1, 2, 5, 10 and 20 francs, initially bearing the name of the Empire Chérifien and later the Kingdom of Morocco, with inscriptions in French and Arabic. Aluminium and aluminium-bronze issues predominated after the Second World War. Banknotes were issued by the State Bank of Morocco in denominations from 5 to 5,000 francs, featuring Moroccan landscapes, architecture and portraits of the Sultan/King.
