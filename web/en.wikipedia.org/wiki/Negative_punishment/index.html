Negative punishment

Time-out from positive reinforcement is a widely used form of negative punishment.

Negative punishment, also known as punishment by contingent removal or a penalty, is a concept in operant conditioning. It occurs when a behavior is followed by the removal of a favorable stimulus — such as taking away a liked toy, privilege, or reward — and the behavior consequently decreases in frequency in the future.

Terminology

In the framework developed by B. F. Skinner and later behavior analysts, "negative" does not mean "bad" but "subtractive": a stimulus is removed. "Punishment" refers to the effect — a reduction in the future likelihood of the behavior. Negative punishment contrasts with positive punishment, in which an aversive stimulus is added (for example, a reprimand), and with negative reinforcement, in which removal of an aversive stimulus increases a behavior.

Examples

  • Time-out: a child is removed from a reinforcing environment for a brief period after misbehavior.
  • Response cost: a fine or the loss of tokens or points in a token economy system contingent on unwanted behavior.
  • Revoking privileges, such as losing driving privileges or screen time after breaking rules.

Research in applied behavior analysis indicates that, to be effective, punishment contingencies should be immediate, consistent, and paired with reinforcement for alternative appropriate behaviors. Negative punishment is generally considered less aversive than positive punishment and is widely used in parenting programs and classroom management, though ethicists and clinicians emphasize that reinforcement-based strategies should be preferred wherever possible.
