Concentrated solar power

Concentrated solar power (CSP, also known as concentrating solar power or concentrated solar thermal) systems generate solar power by using mirrors or lenses to concentrate a large area of sunlight onto a small receiver. Electricity is generated when the concentrated light is converted to heat (solar thermal energy), which drives a heat engine (usually a steam turbine) connected to an electrical power generator, or powers a thermochemical reaction.

A solar power tower plant, one of the main CSP configurations.

Technology

Four main CSP technologies are in use: the parabolic trough, the solar power tower, the concentrating linear Fresnel reflector, and the dish Stirling system. Parabolic troughs are the most commercially mature, using curved mirrors to focus sunlight onto a receiver tube carrying a heat transfer fluid. Power towers use an array of flat, movable mirrors called heliostats to focus sunlight on a central tower receiver. CSP plants can incorporate thermal energy storage, typically molten salt, allowing electricity generation after sunset — a key advantage over photovoltaics.

History

The first modern CSP plants, the SEGS facilities in California's Mojave Desert, were built in the 1980s. A second wave of deployment followed in Spain and the United States in the late 2000s and 2010s, with notable plants including Ivanpah in California and Noor in Morocco, among the largest solar complexes in the world.
