Semiclassical gravity

Semiclassical gravity is the approximation to the theory of quantum gravity in which matter fields are treated quantum mechanically while the gravitational field remains classical. In semiclassical gravity, the Einstein field equations are modified so that the source of spacetime curvature is the expectation value of the stress–energy tensor of the quantum matter fields:

Gμν = 8πG ⟨ψ| T̂μν |ψ⟩

where the right-hand side is the expectation value of the stress–energy tensor operator in the quantum state |ψ⟩ of the matter fields, and the left-hand side is the classical Einstein tensor.

Applications

The most famous application of semiclassical gravity is the prediction of Hawking radiation: applying quantum field theory in the curved spacetime of a black hole, Stephen Hawking showed in 1974 that black holes emit radiation with a thermal spectrum, implying that they have a temperature and slowly evaporate. Semiclassical methods are also used in cosmology, for example to model quantum fluctuations during cosmic inflation, which are believed to have seeded the large-scale structure of the universe, and in studying the Unruh effect for accelerated observers.

Consistency issues

Semiclassical gravity can be viewed as an approximation that is valid when the fluctuations of the stress–energy tensor are small compared with its expectation value, and when quantum-gravitational effects (important at the Planck scale) can be neglected. Whether the semiclassical equation is fundamentally consistent is debated: coupling a classical field to a quantum expectation value raises questions about measurement and the collapse of the wavefunction, and experiments of the Page–Geilker type have been discussed as tests. Proposed alternatives include stochastic gravity, which adds noise terms related to stress–energy fluctuations.

Relation to quantum gravity

Semiclassical gravity is not a complete theory of quantum gravity. It is expected to emerge as a limiting case of any full theory — such as string theory or loop quantum gravity — in regimes where spacetime is smooth and fields are far from the Planck scale.
