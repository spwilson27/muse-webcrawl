Dual carriageway

A dual carriageway is a class of highway with carriageways for traffic travelling in opposite directions separated by a central reservation or median. It is the British term for what North American usage calls a divided highway. Roads in the UK conforming to the standard may have two or more lanes in each direction and speed limits up to the national maximum of 70 mph for cars.

Characteristics

Unlike a motorway, a dual carriageway may have at-grade junctions, roundabouts, gaps in the central reservation and direct access from adjoining properties, and it may legally be used by cyclists, pedestrians and slow vehicles unless specifically prohibited. High-quality examples with full grade separation are sometimes informally called "expressways".

History

The first purpose-built dual carriageways in Britain date from the 1920s and 1930s, including sections of the Great West Road and the Kingston Bypass. After the Second World War, dual carriageways became the principal standard for trunk roads not built as motorways, and the designation "A-road dual carriageway" remains common across the country.
