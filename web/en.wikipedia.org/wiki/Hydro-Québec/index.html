Hydro-Québec

Hydro-Québec is a Canadian Crown corporation, owned by the Government of Quebec, that manages the generation, transmission and distribution of electricity in Quebec. Headquartered in Montreal, it is one of the world's largest producers of hydroelectricity, operating more than 60 generating stations with an installed capacity of roughly 37,000 MW, and supplies some of the lowest residential electricity rates in North America.

History

Hydro-Québec was created in 1944 when the provincial government of Adélard Godbout nationalised Montreal Light, Heat and Power. Under Premier Jean Lesage and minister René Lévesque, the remaining private utilities were nationalised in 1962–63 during the Quiet Revolution, under the slogan "Maîtres chez nous" ("masters in our own house").

Major projects

The corporation developed vast hydroelectric complexes in northern Quebec, notably the Manic-Outardes project on the North Shore and the James Bay Project, launched in 1971, whose La Grande complex includes the Robert-Bourassa generating station (5,616 MW). Agreements with the Cree and Inuit, including the James Bay and Northern Quebec Agreement (1975), reshaped later development. Hydro-Québec also pioneered 735 kV transmission lines and exports power to Ontario, New Brunswick and the northeastern United States.

Research and operations

Its research institute, IREQ, in Varennes conducts work on power systems, robotics and energy storage. The utility serves about 4.5 million customers and has been a major contributor of dividends to the Quebec government.
