Heinkel He 178

Role: Experimental jet aircraft
Manufacturer: Heinkel
First flight: 27 August 1939
Designer: Siegfried and Walter Günter
Powerplant: 1 × Heinkel HeS 3 turbojet, 4.9 kN (1,100 lbf)

The Heinkel He 178 was the world's first aircraft to fly under the power of a turbojet engine, and the first practical jet aircraft. Developed privately by the German company Heinkel as a proof-of-concept vehicle for the turbojet engine designed by Hans von Ohain, it first flew on 27 August 1939, piloted by Erich Warsitz, only days before the outbreak of the Second World War.

Development and flight

Ernst Heinkel, a keen advocate of high-speed flight, funded von Ohain's turbine development after 1936. The engine, the HeS 3, burned liquid fuel and used a centrifugal compressor. The airframe was a small, shoulder-winged monoplane of duralumin with wooden wings, tailwheel landing gear and the jet intake in the nose. After taxi trials and a brief hop on 24 August, Warsitz made the first true flight on 27 August 1939 at Marienehe airfield near Rostock. Top speed was about 598 km/h (372 mph) and endurance only about ten minutes.

Outcome

Heinkel demonstrated the aircraft to officials of the Reich Air Ministry in November 1939, but they showed little interest, preferring the axial-flow engine programmes pursued at Junkers and BMW that led to the Messerschmitt Me 262. The He 178 was later displayed in Berlin and was destroyed in an air raid in 1943. Despite its official neglect, the flight proved the practicality of turbojet propulsion and began the jet age in aviation.

Specifications

  • Crew: 1
  • Length: 7.48 m (24 ft 6 in)
  • Wingspan: 7.2 m (23 ft 7 in)
  • Maximum speed: 598 km/h (372 mph)
