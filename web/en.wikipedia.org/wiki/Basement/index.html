Basement


A finished basement

A basement is one or more floors of a building that are completely or partly below ground level. Basements provide space for utilities such as furnaces, water heaters and electrical distribution, and are commonly used for storage, laundry, workshops, or, when "finished" with flooring, ceilings, insulation and heating, as additional living space such as recreation rooms, bedrooms or rental units.

Construction and regional variation

A basement floor is typically a concrete slab with foundation walls of masonry or poured concrete extending below the frost line, which is why basements are common in cold climates of North America, where footings must be deep in any case. They are rare where the water table is high (as in Florida and Louisiana), where permafrost or shallow bedrock makes excavation costly, or in regions with mild climates where slab-on-grade construction suffices. Variants include the walk-out basement, exposed on one side by sloping terrain, and the daylight basement with full-size windows on one wall.

Issues

Below-grade spaces are prone to water intrusion, flooding and radon accumulation, addressed by perimeter drains, sump pumps, waterproofing and radon mitigation systems. Building codes generally require habitable basement rooms to have an emergency egress window or door large enough for escape and firefighter access. Radon and mould are the principal health concerns associated with finished basements.
