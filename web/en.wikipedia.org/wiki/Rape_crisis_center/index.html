A rape crisis center provides confidential support to survivors of sexual violence.

A rape crisis center (RCC) is a community-based organization that provides free, confidential services to survivors of sexual assault, their families, and their friends. Services typically include 24-hour crisis hotlines, short-term counseling, support groups, hospital and court accompaniment, and advocacy within the medical, legal, and social-service systems. Many centers also run prevention-education programs in schools and communities.

History

Rape crisis centers emerged from the second-wave feminist movement in the early 1970s, when activists argued that police, hospitals, and courts routinely blamed victims and failed to respond to sexual violence. The first center is generally credited to Washington, D.C., where the D.C. Rape Crisis Center opened in 1972; a center in Ann Arbor, Michigan, opened the same year. Early centers were staffed largely by volunteers and operated from borrowed office space, relying on consciousness-raising groups both to recruit workers and to develop their model of survivor-centered advocacy.

During the later 1970s and 1980s many centers professionalized, hired paid staff, and accepted government funding, a shift that generated debate within the movement about whether institutionalization diluted their original feminist politics. By the 2000s there were well over a thousand rape crisis programs in the United States, most affiliated with state coalitions and the national organization RAINN (Rape, Abuse & Incest National Network), founded in 1994, which operates the National Sexual Assault Hotline.

Services

  • Crisis hotlines staffed around the clock by trained advocates.
  • Hospital accompaniment during forensic medical examinations (rape kits).
  • Legal advocacy, including help obtaining protective orders and support through criminal proceedings.
  • Counseling — individual short-term counseling and peer support groups.
  • Prevention education in schools, workplaces, and communities.
  • Shelter referral and assistance with housing, in coordination with domestic-violence programs.
