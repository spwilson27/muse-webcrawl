First Nagorno-Karabakh War

The First Nagorno-Karabakh War was an ethnic and territorial conflict that took place from February 1988 to May 1994 in and around the Nagorno-Karabakh region of southwestern Azerbaijan, between the majority-ethnic Armenian population of Nagorno-Karabakh, backed by Armenia, and the Republic of Azerbaijan. As the Soviet Union dissolved, the dispute escalated from demonstrations and pogroms into full-scale war. The war ended with an Armenian victory and a Russian-brokered ceasefire that left Nagorno-Karabakh and seven surrounding Azerbaijani districts under the control of the unrecognised Republic of Artsakh, a situation that lasted until Azerbaijan's offensives of 2020 and 2023.

Origins

Nagorno-Karabakh, an Armenian-majority region, had been made an autonomous oblast within Soviet Azerbaijan in 1923. In February 1988 its regional soviet voted to join Soviet Armenia, setting off mass demonstrations in Yerevan and Stepanakert and counter-demonstrations in Baku. Anti-Armenian pogroms in Sumgait (February 1988) and Baku (January 1990), and anti-Azerbaijani violence in Armenia, drove hundreds of thousands of refugees in both directions and destroyed the possibility of co-existence. Moscow imposed direct rule on Karabakh in 1989 but returned it to Azerbaijan in late 1991, and as the USSR collapsed the region declared independence.

Course of the war

Full-scale fighting began in early 1992. The massacre of Azerbaijani civilians at Khojaly in February 1992 was the war's worst single atrocity. Armenian forces captured Shusha in May 1992 and opened the Lachin corridor linking Karabakh to Armenia. In 1993 they advanced beyond Karabakh itself, seizing the Azerbaijani districts of Kalbajar, Agdam, Fuzuli, Jabrayil, Qubadli and Zangilan, displacing their Azerbaijani populations and prompting UN Security Council resolutions demanding withdrawal. Azerbaijani counter-offensives in the winter of 1993–94 regained some ground around Horadiz but failed to change the overall outcome.

Ceasefire and aftermath

In May 1994 Russia brokered the Bishkek Protocol, a ceasefire that froze the conflict with Armenian forces in control of almost all of Nagorno-Karabakh and about a fifth of Azerbaijan's internationally recognised territory in total under Armenian control. The unrecognised Nagorno-Karabakh Republic (later Artsakh) governed the region for nearly three decades, while negotiations under the OSCE Minsk Group failed to produce a settlement. The frozen conflict thawed into major fighting in 2016 and the full-scale Second Nagorno-Karabakh War of 2020, in which Azerbaijan recovered most of the lost territories.
