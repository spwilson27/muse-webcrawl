Battle of Flamborough Head

The Battle of Flamborough Head was a naval battle fought on 23 September 1779 off the Yorkshire coast of England during the American Revolutionary War. A small American–French squadron under Captain John Paul Jones, in the frigate Bonhomme Richard, engaged the British frigate HMS Serapis and the sloop HMS Countess of Scarborough, which were escorting a Baltic convoy.

The battle

Jones's squadron had been raiding British commerce around the British Isles. On 23 September the British convoy of more than forty merchantmen, homeward bound from the Baltic, was sighted off Flamborough Head. The escorts, Captain Richard Pearson in the 44-gun Serapis and the 20-gun Countess of Scarborough, interposed themselves while the convoy escaped to safety under Scarborough Castle.

The ensuing duel between Bonhomme Richard and Serapis became one of the most famous single-ship actions of the age. The two ships became locked together and fought at point-blank range for over three hours. According to legend, when called on to surrender, Jones replied, "I have not yet begun to fight!" In fact, it was Serapis that eventually struck her colours, while the shattered Bonhomme Richard sank two days later; Jones transferred to the captured frigate.

Aftermath

Although the convoy was saved and Pearson was knighted for his defence, the capture of a Royal Navy frigate in home waters was a propaganda triumph for the American cause and made Jones famous throughout Europe. The battle remains the signature action of John Paul Jones's career.
