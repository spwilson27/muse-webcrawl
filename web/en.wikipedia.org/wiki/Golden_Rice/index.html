Golden Rice

DevelopersIngo Potrykus, Peter Beyer

TraitBeta-carotene (provitamin A) biosynthesis in grain

Golden Rice is a variety of rice (Oryza sativa) produced through genetic engineering to biosynthesise beta-carotene, a precursor of vitamin A, in the edible parts of the plant. It was developed to address vitamin A deficiency, which causes blindness and increased mortality in hundreds of thousands of children annually in parts of Africa and Southeast Asia where rice is a staple food.

Development

Golden Rice was created by Ingo Potrykus of the Swiss Federal Institute of Technology and Peter Beyer of the University of Freiburg, with results published in Science in 2000. The first version produced modest amounts of beta-carotene using genes from daffodil and a soil bacterium. In 2005, researchers at Syngenta produced Golden Rice 2, substituting a maize gene that raised carotenoid levels up to 23 times higher. Ownership and licensing arrangements made the technology available free for humanitarian use in developing countries.

Regulation and deployment

Food safety regulators in several countries, including the United States FDA, Health Canada and Food Standards Australia New Zealand, concluded that Golden Rice is as safe as conventional rice. In 2019 the Philippines became the first country to approve it for direct use as food, feed and processing, and in 2021 for commercial propagation, with limited deployment to farming communities following. Its roll-out has been slowed by legal challenges from environmental groups — in 2024 a Philippine court revoked the biosafety permit pending further review.

Controversy

Golden Rice has been a focal point in the wider debate over genetically modified crops. Supporters, including over 100 Nobel laureates who signed a 2016 letter criticising Greenpeace, argue it can prevent blindness and death; opponents question its efficacy relative to dietary diversification and supplementation programmes, and object to corporate control of seed systems. Trials were also marred by a 2012 feeding-study ethics scandal in China, which led to retraction of a paper and sanctions against researchers.
