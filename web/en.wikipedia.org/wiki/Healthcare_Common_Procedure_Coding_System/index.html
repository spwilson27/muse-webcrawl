Healthcare Common Procedure Coding System

HCPCS (pronounced "hick-picks") is a set of health care procedure codes based on the American Medical Association's Current Procedural Terminology (CPT).

The Healthcare Common Procedure Coding System (HCPCS) is a collection of standardised codes that represent medical procedures, supplies, products, and services, used on claims submitted to Medicare, Medicaid, and other health insurers in the United States. It was established in 1978 by the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS) to standardise billing.

Levels

HCPCS has two levels. Level I comprises the five-digit numeric CPT codes maintained by the American Medical Association, describing physician and outpatient procedures. Level II codes — a letter followed by four digits — cover services and items not in CPT, such as ambulance services, durable medical equipment (wheelchairs, hospital beds), prosthetics and orthotics, and injectable drugs (the "J codes"). Level III local codes were eliminated in 2003 under HIPAA standardisation.

Use

Providers append modifiers to HCPCS codes to give additional information. Codes are updated annually by CMS, with quarterly updates for some categories. Accurate coding determines reimbursement and is a focus of programme-integrity audits.
