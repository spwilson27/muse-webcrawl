Clovis is a city in and the county seat of Curry County, New Mexico, United States. Located in the eastern plains of the state near the Texas border, it had a population of about 38,567 at the 2020 census. Clovis is the principal city of the Clovis micropolitan area and, together with nearby Portales, forms a regional centre for agriculture, rail transport, and the military presence at Cannon Air Force Base.

History

The area is famous in archaeology for the nearby Blackwater Draw site, where in the 1930s distinctive fluted spear points—Clovis points—were found with mammoth bones. The Clovis culture was long regarded as the earliest widespread culture of North America, dating to roughly 13,000 years ago, and the town gave the culture its name.

The modern city was founded in 1906 when the Atchison, Topeka and Santa Fe Railway built through the area. It was named, by tradition, at the suggestion of a railway official's daughter after Clovis, first king of the Franks. Cannon Air Force Base, west of the city, was established in 1942 and is now home to the 27th Special Operations Wing.

Music heritage

Clovis is notable in rock-and-roll history as the location of the Norman Petty Recording Studio, where Buddy Holly and the Crickets recorded "That'll Be the Day" and other hits in 1957, and where Roy Orbison and Waylon Jennings also recorded. The studio and the Clovis music festival remain local landmarks.

Geography and climate

Clovis sits on the high plains of the Llano Estacado at about 1,301 metres (4,268 ft) above sea level. It has a semi-arid climate with hot summers, cool winters, and limited rainfall. The economy rests on agriculture (peanuts, wheat, cattle, and dairy), the railway, and Cannon AFB. A large cheese plant operated by Southwest Cheese is among the biggest employers.
