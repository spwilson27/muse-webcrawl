Sveti Grgur

Sveti Grgur (Italian: San Gregorio) is an uninhabited island in the Adriatic Sea, in the Kvarner Gulf of Croatia, located between the islands of Rab and Krk, south of the island of Prvić. Covering about 6.4 square kilometres, it is best known as the site of a Yugoslav women's political prison camp that operated from 1949 to 1956, associated with the Goli Otok camp for men on the neighbouring island. The island's name means "Saint Gregory" in Croatian.

Prison camp

After the Tito–Stalin split of 1948, Yugoslavs suspected of loyalty to the Cominform were interned without trial. Women prisoners — some 850 are estimated to have passed through Sveti Grgur — were held in harsh conditions, performing forced labour such as quarrying and carrying stones, and subjected to a regime of mutual punishment and "re-education" similar to that on Goli Otok. The camp closed in 1956. Notable prisoners included the writer and journalist Ženi Lebl, whose memoir documented the camp.

Geography and today

The island is largely barren karst with sparse vegetation. Fallow deer were introduced for hunting in the twentieth century and now roam freely. Today Sveti Grgur is uninhabited and visited by excursion boats; the ruins of the prison complex remain, overgrown and unrestored, drawing interest as a site of difficult heritage alongside Goli Otok.
