Ashley Down is an area of Bristol, England, in the suburb of Bishopston, north of the city centre. It is best known as the site of the orphanages founded by the Prussian-born evangelist George Müller in the 19th century, whose large stone buildings still dominate the area, and as the home of the County Ground, the home of Gloucestershire County Cricket Club.

The Müller orphanages

George Müller began caring for orphans in Bristol in 1836, initially in his own home in Wilson Street. As numbers grew he leased land at Ashley Down and built five large orphan houses between 1849 and 1870, eventually accommodating more than 2,000 children at a time. Müller famously relied on unsolicited donations in answer to prayer and never made direct appeals for money. By the time of his death in 1898, some 10,000 orphans had passed through the homes. The work continued into the 20th century before the children were moved to smaller family-style homes; the Ashley Down buildings were later used by Bristol City Council, as a technical college, and have partly been converted to housing.

Cricket

The County Ground on Nevil Road has been the home of Gloucestershire County Cricket Club since 1889. W. G. Grace played for the county, and the venue has hosted One Day International matches, including games at the 1983, 1999 and 2019 Cricket World Cups.

Ashley Down station

A new railway station, Ashley Down, opened on 28 September 2024 on the Filton line between Bristol Temple Meads and Filton Abbey Wood, serving the local community on the site of the former Ashley Hill station, which had closed in 1964.
