Full flight simulator

A full flight simulator (FFS) is a term used by national aviation authorities (NAA) for a high-technical standard of flight simulator. It is a full-size replica of a specific aircraft type's cockpit, mounted on a motion platform, and qualified for use in pilot training and checking, including zero flight time training for type ratings.


A Level D full flight simulator

Features

An FFS reproduces the flight deck layout, instruments and controls of the aircraft, and simulates its aerodynamic and systems behaviour using data packages supplied by the aircraft manufacturer. It provides a collimated visual system with a wide field of view showing a computer-generated outside world, a six-degree-of-freedom motion system (usually a hexapod Stewart platform), control loading that mimics the feel of the real controls, and realistic sound.

Qualification levels

Regulators such as the FAA and EASA qualify FFS devices at levels A through D, with Level D the highest: it requires the most complete modelling, including buffet, ground effect and realistic motion cueing, and allows pilots to complete a type rating entirely in the simulator ("zero flight time" training for experienced pilots). Simulators must be re-evaluated periodically against qualification test guides to retain certification.
