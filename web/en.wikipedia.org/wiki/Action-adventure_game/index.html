Action-adventure game

An action-adventure game is a video game genre that combines core elements from both the action game and adventure game genres. Action-adventure games typically feature real-time combat, traversal and reflex-based challenges alongside exploration, puzzle-solving, item collection and narrative progression characteristic of adventure games.

Definition and characteristics

Players usually control a single character in a persistent world, fighting enemies in real time while solving puzzles that gate progress — for example finding a key item to open a new area. The genre is broad and often overlaps with platformers, role-playing games, stealth games and survival horror. Common features include upgradeable abilities, inventories, checkpoints, and worlds that open up as new tools are acquired.

History

The genre traces back to Atari's Adventure (1980), which translated adventure-game exploration into real-time action. It was popularised by series such as The Legend of Zelda, Metroid, Tomb Raider and Resident Evil, and later by franchises including Grand Theft Auto, Uncharted, Assassin's Creed and God of War. Action-adventure is among the most commercially successful video game genres.

Subgenres

Notable forms include survival horror, stealth games, and "Metroidvania" games, in which exploration of an interconnected world is gated by abilities gained along the way. Open-world action-adventure games give the player a large environment to explore freely between story missions.
