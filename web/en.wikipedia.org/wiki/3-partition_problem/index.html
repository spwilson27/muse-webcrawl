The 3-partition problem is a problem in computer science and combinatorics. Given a multiset of 3m positive integers, the question is whether the numbers can be partitioned into m subsets, each containing exactly three elements, such that the sum of the numbers in each subset is the same. That common sum must be one m-th of the total sum of all the numbers.

Definition

Formally, the input consists of a positive integer m and a bound B, together with 3m integers a1, …, a3m such that B/4 < ai < B/2 and the total sum is mB. The task is to decide whether the numbers can be divided into m triples each summing to exactly B. The size constraints ensure that any subset summing to B must have exactly three elements, which gives the problem its clean structure.

Complexity

The 3-partition problem is one of the best-known examples of a strongly NP-complete problem: it remains NP-complete even when the numbers in the input are bounded by a polynomial in the input size (encoded in unary). It is listed as problem [SP15] in Garey and Johnson's Computers and Intractability (1979). This distinguishes it from the related two-way partition problem, which is only weakly NP-complete and admits a pseudo-polynomial-time dynamic programming algorithm.

Because of its strong NP-completeness and rigid structure, 3-partition is a standard starting point for proving the hardness of scheduling, packing and multiprocessor allocation problems, including results showing that certain bin-packing and machine-scheduling problems have no efficient approximation schemes unless P = NP.
