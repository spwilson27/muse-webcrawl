Footloose is a 1998 musical based on the 1984 film of the same name. The music is by Tom Snow (among others), the lyrics by Dean Pitchford (with additional lyrics by Kenny Loggins), and the book by Pitchford and Walter Bobbie.

Synopsis

Teenager Ren McCormack moves from Chicago to the small town of Bomont after his parents' divorce. He is shocked to learn that dancing and rock music have been banned by the town council, led by the Reverend Shaw Moore, who imposed the ban after a car accident killed four local young people — including his own son. Ren falls for the reverend's rebellious daughter Ariel and, with his friend Willard, campaigns to overturn the ban, culminating in a confrontation in which Ren argues that dancing is a celebration of life. The council relents, and the town's young people finally hold their dance.

Productions

The musical premiered on Broadway at the Richard Rodgers Theatre on 22 October 1998 and ran for 709 performances, closing on 2 July 2000. Despite mixed reviews, it received four Tony Award nominations. Productions followed in London's West End (2006 and 2017) and numerous tours and amateur stagings. Well-known songs include "Footloose", "Let's Hear It for the Boy", "Almost Paradise", and "Holding Out for a Hero".
