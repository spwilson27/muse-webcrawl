Democratic Republic of Armenia

The Democratic Republic of Armenia (DRA), also known as the First Republic of Armenia, was the first modern Armenian state, proclaimed on 28 May 1918 as the Russian Empire collapsed in the aftermath of the October Revolution and the Transcaucasian Federation dissolved. Its capital was Yerevan, and it endured until 2 December 1920.

History

The republic was governed principally by the Armenian Revolutionary Federation (Dashnaktsutyun); its first prime minister was Hovhannes Kajaznuni. From birth the DRA was in crisis: it was flooded with hundreds of thousands of Armenian refugees fleeing the genocide in the Ottoman Empire, beset by famine and typhus, and at war at various times with the Ottoman Empire, Georgia, and Azerbaijan. Armenian forces halted the Ottoman advance at the Battle of Sardarabad (May 1918), securing the state's survival, but the punitive Treaty of Batum stripped it of territory.

In August 1920 the Treaty of Sèvres promised Armenia a large territory ("Wilson's Armenia"), but the Turkish National Movement under Mustafa Kemal rejected it. In the Turkish–Armenian War of autumn 1920, Turkish forces captured Kars and Alexandropol; simultaneously the Red Army entered from the north. On 2 December 1920 the government signed power over to the communists, and Armenia became a Soviet republic.

Legacy

28 May is celebrated in modern Armenia as Republic Day, and the First Republic is honoured as the foundation of Armenian statehood restored in 1991.
