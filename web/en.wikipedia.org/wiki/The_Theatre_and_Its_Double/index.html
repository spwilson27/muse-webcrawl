The Theatre and Its Double

AuthorAntonin Artaud
Published1938
Original titleLe Théâtre et son double
SubjectTheatre theory; Theatre of Cruelty

The Theatre and Its Double (Le Théâtre et son double) is a collection of essays and manifestos by the French dramatist Antonin Artaud, published in 1938. It is the foundational text of the Theatre of Cruelty and one of the most influential works of twentieth-century theatre theory.

Contents

The volume includes the two manifestos for the Theatre of Cruelty (1932–33), the essay "The Theatre and the Plague", "No More Masterpieces", and Artaud's letters on cruelty and on language. Artaud argues that Western theatre has become enslaved to written dialogue and psychology; against this he proposes a theatre of gesture, sound, light and spatial poetry that assaults the senses of the audience — cruelty meaning not gore but rigour, necessity and metaphysical shock, intended to work on spectators as the plague works on a city: purging and revealing.

Influence

Though Artaud's own productions largely failed in his lifetime, the book profoundly influenced post-war experimental theatre — including Peter Brook, Jerzy Grotowski and the Living Theatre — and later performance art and directors such as Peter Brook's "The Empty Space" generation. Its call to treat the stage as a space of physical, metaphysical force remains a touchstone for avant-garde practice.
