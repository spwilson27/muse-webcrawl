Latent heat

During a phase change, added heat changes state, not temperature.
SI unitjoule per kilogram (J/kg)
Defined byJoseph Black, c. 1760s

Latent heat is the energy absorbed or released by a substance during a change in its physical state (phase) that occurs without changing its temperature. The term was introduced around 1762 by the Scottish chemist Joseph Black. The latent heat of fusion refers to melting or freezing, and the latent heat of vaporization to boiling or condensation.

Role in weather

Latent heat is central to meteorology: evaporation of water from oceans stores energy in water vapour, which is released when the vapour condenses in clouds, fuelling storms and driving atmospheric circulation. The release of latent heat during condensation warms rising air parcels and underlies the distinction between dry and moist adiabatic lapse rates.
