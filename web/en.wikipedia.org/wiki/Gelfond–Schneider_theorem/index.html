Gelfond–Schneider theorem

FieldNumber theory
Proved byAlexander Gelfond, Theodor Schneider
Year1934
ConcernsTranscendental numbers

In mathematics, the Gelfond–Schneider theorem establishes a major class of transcendental numbers and was proved in 1934 by Alexander Gelfond and, independently, Theodor Schneider. It answered the seventh of Hilbert's problems.

Statement

If a and b are algebraic numbers with a ≠ 0, 1, and b is not a real rational number, then any value of ab is a transcendental number.

Examples

  • 2√2, known as the Gelfond–Schneider constant, is transcendental.
  • eπ, Gelfond's constant, is transcendental, since eπ = (−1)−i.
  • √2√2 is transcendental; this also gives a classic example of an irrational number raised to an irrational power yielding a rational-or-irrational puzzle resolved by the theorem.

History

David Hilbert had conjectured in 1900 that numbers such as 2√2 are transcendental. Special cases were proved by Gelfond in 1929 and Kuzmin in 1930 before the full result appeared in 1934. The theorem was later extended by Alan Baker's work on linear forms in logarithms.
