E85 is an abbreviation typically referring to an ethanol fuel blend of 85% denatured fuel ethanol and 15% gasoline or other hydrocarbon. In the United States, the exact ratio of fuel ethanol to hydrocarbon may vary according to ASTM International specification D5798, which specifies the allowable ethanol content in E85 as ranging from 51% to 83%.

Properties

E85 has a higher octane rating than regular gasoline, which permits higher compression ratios and can improve performance in engines tuned for it, but it contains roughly 25–30% less energy per unit volume, so fuel economy is proportionally lower. Ethanol's high heat of vaporisation also aids charge cooling in turbocharged engines, making E85 popular in performance tuning. The fuel is sold for use in flexible-fuel vehicles (FFVs), which can run on any blend up to E85; using E85 in a conventional vehicle can damage fuel-system components.

Availability and debate

Use is greatest in Brazil (where high-ethanol fuel is widespread), the US Midwest near corn-producing regions, and Sweden, which promoted E85 in the 2000s. Debate continues over the net greenhouse-gas benefit of corn-based ethanol, land use, and food-versus-fuel effects, versus the renewable and domestic-supply advantages claimed by supporters.
