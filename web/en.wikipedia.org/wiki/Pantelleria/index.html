Pantelleria

LocationStrait of Sicily, Italy
Known forCapers, Passito wine, dammusi
[Image: A dammuso house among Pantelleria's lava fields]
A dammuso house among Pantelleria's lava fields

Pantelleria is an Italian volcanic island in the Strait of Sicily, closer to Tunisia (about 60 km) than to Sicily (100 km). It is famed for its caper bushes, sweet Passito wine and black-lava landscape of dammusi houses.

Character

The island's domed stone dammusi, of Arab-era design, shelter its zibibbo vines, which are trained low in hollows (alberello pantesco, UNESCO-listed) against the sirocco. The Lago Specchio di Venere fills a caldera with alkaline hot springs. Pantelleria was a prized and fought-over outpost from Carthaginian times (as Cossyra) to the Second World War, when its capture in 1943 (Operation Corkscrew) preceded the invasion of Sicily. Giorgio Armani's retreat here made it fashionable; ferry and air links connect Trapani and Palermo.
