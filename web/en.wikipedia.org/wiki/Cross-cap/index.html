Cross-cap

In mathematics, a cross-cap is a two-dimensional surface in 3-space consisting of a Möbius strip-like structure whose single boundary circle is pinched to a point — formally, it can be constructed by taking a disc and identifying antipodal points of its boundary, producing a model of the real projective plane with a self-intersection.

Properties

The cross-cap is a non-orientable surface. Because the real projective plane cannot be embedded in three-dimensional Euclidean space without self-intersection, the cross-cap is an immersion with a line of double points ending in Whitney umbrella singularities.

Related surfaces

Other models of the projective plane include the Roman surface and Boy's surface. In topology, attaching a cross-cap (equivalently, a Möbius band) to a surface is a basic operation in the classification of non-orientable surfaces.
