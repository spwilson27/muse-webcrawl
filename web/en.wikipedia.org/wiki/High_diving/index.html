High diving

High diving is the sport of diving into water from relatively great heights — in competition, 20 metres for women and 27 metres for men — much higher than the 10-metre platform of Olympic diving. Divers enter the water at speeds exceeding 85 km/h, almost always feet-first because of the forces involved, and safety scuba divers wait in the water below.

History

The sport developed from cliff diving traditions (such as at Acapulco, Mexico) and "high diving" exhibitions at fairs. It was formalised as a competitive sport with the Red Bull Cliff Diving World Series (founded 2009), held at natural and urban venues worldwide. World Aquatics (formerly FINA) staged high diving at the World Aquatics Championships from 2013 (Barcelona), with events in temporary towers erected over harbours and lakes.

Technique and safety

Competitors perform somersaults and twists during a descent lasting about three seconds, using the "barani" and other take-off conventions adapted from platform diving, and finish with a controlled feet-first "rip" entry. Judging criteria mirror those of diving. Because of impact risks — including possible loss of consciousness — rescue divers and medical staff are mandatory at events.
