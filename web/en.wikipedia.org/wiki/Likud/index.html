Likud (Hebrew: הַלִּיכּוּד, romanised: HaLikud, lit. "The Consolidation"), officially the Likud – National Liberal Movement, is a major right-wing political party in Israel. It was founded in 1973 by Menachem Begin and Ariel Sharon as an alliance of several right-wing parties; it became a unified party in 1988. Its ideology historically combines Revisionist Zionism, security hawkishness, support for Israeli settlements and free-market economics.

History

Likud achieved its breakthrough in the 1977 election — the Mahapakh ("upheaval") — ending nearly three decades of Labor dominance and making Begin prime minister. The Begin government signed the Camp David Accords and the 1979 peace treaty with Egypt, Begin sharing the Nobel Peace Prize, while also expanding West Bank settlements and launching the 1982 Lebanon War. Yitzhak Shamir led the party through the late 1980s national-unity governments and the Madrid Conference.

Benjamin Netanyahu first led Likud to victory in 1996, becoming Israel's youngest prime minister. After defeat in 1999 he temporarily retired; Ariel Sharon took over, won elections in 2001 and 2003, implemented the 2005 disengagement from Gaza and then split to form Kadima, taking Likud's centrist wing with him. Netanyahu returned as leader in 2005, became prime minister again in 2009 and dominated Israeli politics for most of the following decade and a half — the country's longest-serving prime minister. After brief opposition in 2021–22, Likud returned to power in December 2022 at the head of a right-wing and religious coalition, which governed through the Gaza war that followed the 7 October 2023 Hamas attacks. Its controversial judicial-overhaul program triggered mass protests in 2023 before being largely suspended by the war.

Platform and support base

Likud's support draws heavily on Mizrahi and working-class voters, residents of development towns and settlements, and religious-traditional voters, complementing its formal alliances with ultra-Orthodox and far-right parties. The party historically supported privatisation and market liberalisation (especially under Netanyahu as finance minister in 2003–05) while retaining populist welfare rhetoric. It opposes a Palestinian state under current leaderships, supports annexationist claims in the Jordan Valley and settlement blocs in varying formulations, and emphasises a strong military and a close alliance with the United States.
