Little Belt

Little Belt (Lillebælt)
Location: between Jutland and Funen, Denmark
Length: ~50 km
Width: 800 m – 28 km

The Little Belt (Danish: Lillebælt) is a strait between the Danish island of Funen and the Jutland Peninsula. It is one of the three Danish Straits connecting the Baltic Sea to the Kattegat and the North Sea, together with the Great Belt and Øresund. The strait is about 50 kilometres long and ranges from roughly 800 metres at its narrowest to 28 kilometres wide.

Geography

The Little Belt has an irregular, winding course with deep channels (up to about 80 metres) and numerous small islands, including Fænø and Brandsø. The towns of Middelfart and Fredericia lie on opposite shores at its northern end. The area is noted for its population of harbour porpoises, among the densest in the world.

Bridges

Two bridges cross the strait at Middelfart: the Old Little Belt Bridge (1935), carrying rail and road traffic, and the New Little Belt Bridge (1970), a suspension bridge carrying the E20 motorway. The strait was also the site of the 1658 crossing of the frozen Belts by the Swedish army of Charles X Gustav during the Dano-Swedish War.
