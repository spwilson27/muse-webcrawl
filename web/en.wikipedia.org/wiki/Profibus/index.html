Profibus

Profibus

Type: Fieldbus for industrial automation

Developed: 1989, Germany

Variants: PROFIBUS DP, PROFIBUS PA

Standard: IEC 61158 / IEC 61784

Profibus (usually styled PROFIBUS, from Process Field Bus) is a standard for fieldbus communication in automation technology, first promoted in 1989 by BMBF, the German department of education and research, and subsequently maintained by PROFIBUS & PROFINET International (PI). It is one of the most widely installed fieldbus networks worldwide.

Variants

PROFIBUS DP (Decentralised Peripherals) is designed for fast communication between controllers and distributed I/O devices, operating at speeds up to 12 Mbit/s over RS-485 cabling with a master–slave token-passing protocol. PROFIBUS PA (Process Automation) runs at 31.25 kbit/s over Manchester-coded, bus-powered segments suitable for intrinsically safe use in hazardous areas of process plants, carrying power and data on the same pair of wires.

Protocol

Profibus uses a hybrid medium-access method: token passing between masters coordinates the bus, while masters poll their slaves cyclically. PROFIBUS DP evolved through versions DP-V0 (cyclic data exchange), DP-V1 (acyclic access and extended diagnostics, required by PA devices via couplers) and DP-V2 (isochronous operation and slave-to-slave communication).

Relationship to Profinet

Profibus's Ethernet-based successor, PROFINET, builds on similar application profiles and has become the leading Industrial Ethernet system in the same ecosystem, though Profibus remains extensively deployed in factories and process plants.
