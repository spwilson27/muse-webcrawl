Barolo

Barolo is a red Denominazione di Origine Controllata e Garantita (DOCG) wine produced in the northern Italian region of Piedmont. It is made from the Nebbiolo grape and is often described as Italy's greatest wine, nicknamed "the wine of kings, the king of wines." Barolo must be aged for at least 38 months, of which at least 18 months must be in wood; the Riserva designation requires five years of aging.

Characteristics

Barolo is typically garnet in color, lightening to brick-orange with age. It is known for aromas of roses, tar, dried herbs, cherries, and truffles, with firm tannins and high acidity that give it great longevity. Traditionalists age the wine for long periods in large Slavonian oak casks (botti), while "modernists" use shorter macerations and small French oak barriques; the rivalry between the two schools became famous as the "Barolo Wars" of the 1980s and 1990s.

Production zone

The production area centers on the Langhe hills around the town of Barolo and includes eleven communes, among them La Morra, Monforte d'Alba, Serralunga d'Alba, and Castiglione Falletto, each giving somewhat different expressions of Nebbiolo depending on soil and exposure. The vineyard landscapes of Barolo form part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site inscribed in 2014.
