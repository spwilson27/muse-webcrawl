Geraldton

Geraldton (Wajarri: Jambinu) is a coastal city in the Mid West region of Western Australia, located 424 kilometres (263 mi) north of Perth on the Indian Ocean. With an urban population of roughly 38,000, it is a major regional centre and port, exporting iron ore, grain, and other minerals and agricultural produce from the surrounding region, and supporting a significant western rock lobster fishing industry.

History

The area is the traditional land of Wajarri and other Aboriginal peoples. The townsite was surveyed in 1850 and named after Charles Fitzgerald, Governor of Western Australia from 1848 to 1855. The nearby lead mines at Northampton made Geraldton an important colonial port, and in 1888 it was linked by railway to Northampton; a line to Perth followed via Mullewa and Midland.

On 19 November 1941 the Australian light cruiser HMAS Sydney was sunk off the coast with all 645 hands after a battle with the German raider Kormoran. The hilltop HMAS Sydney II Memorial, with its dome of 645 silver seagulls, is now the city's most prominent landmark; the wrecks were located in 2008.

Geography and economy

Geraldton has a Mediterranean climate with hot, dry summers and mild winters, and is known for strong afternoon sea breezes that make it a popular windsurfing and kitesurfing destination. Its port handles iron ore from the Mid West, and the city serves mining, agriculture, and tourism, including trips to the Abrolhos Islands offshore. St Francis Xavier Cathedral, designed by Monsignor John Hawes, is a noted heritage building.
