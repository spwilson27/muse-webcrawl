The wyvern (/ˈwaɪvərn/; sometimes spelled wivern) is a type of mythical dragon with two legs, two wings and a barbed or knotted tail, common in European heraldry, folklore and modern fantasy. The word derives from the Old French guivre (originally from Latin vipera, "viper"). Unlike the heraldic dragon, which has four legs, the wyvern stands on two, with its winged forelimbs used only for flight.

Heraldry

Wyverns are frequent charges and supporters in British armory, associated with families and institutions in the West of England and Wales. The wyvern was the badge of the Midland Railway and its successor London, Midland and Scottish Railway — visible on Derby's crest — and appears on the arms of numerous schools, regiments and companies. Its frequent use has made it a popular emblem of the English Midlands.

Folklore and fiction

In legend, wyverns were said to haunt particular hills and pools in England, as in the tales of the Mordiford wyvern near Hereford and the dragon of Wantley (usually depicted with more limbs). In modern fantasy literature, films and games such as Dungeons & Dragons, the wyvern is typically distinguished from the "true" four-legged dragon and often given a venomous sting in its tail.
