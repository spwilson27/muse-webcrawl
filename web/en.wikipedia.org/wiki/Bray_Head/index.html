Bray Head (Irish: Ceann Bhré) is a hill and headland at the southern end of the seafront of the town of Bray in County Wicklow, Ireland, on the shore of the Irish Sea. Rising 241 metres (791 ft) above the town, it is a prominent local landmark, topped by a stone cross erected for the Holy Year of 1950, and a popular walking destination with views over the Wicklow coast and, on clear days, across to Wales.

Geology and nature

Bray Head is composed of Cambrian quartzite and shale, part of the same ancient rock series as the nearby Sugar Loaf mountains. The headland is designated a Special Area of Conservation for its dry heath and vegetated sea cliffs, and it hosts colonies of seabirds including fulmars, and peregrine falcons have nested on its crags. A colony of feral goats roams the hill.

Walks and transport

A waymarked path climbs from the seafront to the summit cross, and the Bray to Greystones Cliff Walk — one of Ireland's most popular coastal trails — runs around the seaward slopes of the headland between the two towns, a distance of about 7 km. The Dublin–Rosslare railway line, engineered by Isambard Kingdom Brunel's assistants in the 1850s, passes through tunnels under Bray Head along the cliff edge, a stretch famous for its scenic views and its history of cliff falls and diversions.
