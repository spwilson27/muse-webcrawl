Rewards for Justice Program

Rewards for Justice
Founded: 1984
Administered by: U.S. Department of State, Diplomatic Security Service
Purpose: Rewards for information on terrorism and other threats

The Rewards for Justice Program (RFJ) is a United States government rewards program administered by the Diplomatic Security Service of the U.S. Department of State. Established by the 1984 Act to Combat International Terrorism, it offers monetary rewards for information that leads to the arrest or conviction of individuals involved in international terrorism against U.S. persons or property, prevents terrorist acts, or identifies the location of wanted terrorist leaders.

Scope

Rewards have ranged from tens of thousands of dollars to some of the largest bounties ever offered by the U.S. government. A reward of up to $25 million was offered for information leading to Osama bin Laden, and similar sums were associated with other al-Qaeda and Islamic State leaders. The Secretary of State may authorise rewards exceeding $25 million in certain circumstances. Since its inception the program has paid out well over $200 million to more than a hundred informants.

Expansion

The program's remit has broadened beyond terrorism. It now also offers rewards concerning foreign interference in U.S. elections, North Korean sanctions evasion and illicit activity, and other designated threats. Tips may be submitted confidentially, including via encrypted messaging applications and a Tor-based website.

Notable cases

Information obtained through the program contributed to the 2003 capture of Saddam Hussein's sons Uday and Qusay in Mosul, Iraq, for which a combined reward of $30 million was paid. The program also publicised rewards connected with the 1993 World Trade Center bombing, which helped lead to the capture of Ramzi Yousef in 1995.
