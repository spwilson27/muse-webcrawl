Anchoïade

TypeDip / condiment
Place of originFrance (Provence)
Main ingredientsAnchovies, olive oil, garlic, capers, vinegar or lemon juice

Anchoïade is a Provençal dip and condiment made from pounded anchovies, olive oil and garlic, originating in the south of France. The anchovies — traditionally salt-cured fillets, desalted and boned — are pounded in a mortar (or blended) with garlic and capers while olive oil is worked in to form a thick, savoury paste; a few drops of vinegar or lemon juice and sometimes black pepper or herbs are added for balance.

Serving

Anchoïade is classically served as an appetiser with an array of raw vegetables for dipping — celery, radishes, carrots, fennel, sweet peppers and cauliflower — as well as hard-boiled eggs and crusty bread. In Nice and the surrounding region it accompanies the assortment of crudités also paired with bagna càuda, its warm Piedmontese cousin. It may also be spread on toast, used to season salads and grilled fish, or stirred into sauces.

Variations

Recipes vary by household: some include black olives (approaching a lighter tapenade), others add figs in the Niçois manner for a sweet-salt contrast, or a little mustard. The paste keeps for several days refrigerated under a film of olive oil.
