Dado (architecture)

In architecture, the dado is the lower part of a wall, below the dado rail (chair rail) and above the skirting board. The word is borrowed from Italian, where dado means a die or plinth block.

Traditionally the dado and skirting were treated differently from the upper wall, in materials such as wood panelling, lincrusta or tiles, to protect the plaster from furniture and damp. In classical architecture the term also denotes the cubic middle part of a pedestal, between its base and cornice.
