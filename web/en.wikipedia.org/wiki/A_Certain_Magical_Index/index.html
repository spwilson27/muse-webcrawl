A Certain Magical Index (Japanese: とある魔術の禁書目録, Toaru Majutsu no Indekusu) is a Japanese light novel series written by Kazuma Kamachi and illustrated by Kiyotaka Haimura, published by ASCII Media Works under its Dengeki Bunko imprint. The original series ran from April 2004 to October 2010 and was followed by sequel series including New Testament: A Certain Magical Index (2011–2019) and Genesis Testament: A Certain Magical Index (from 2020).

Plot

The story is set in Academy City, a technologically advanced city in western Tokyo where students are developed into espers with scientifically induced psychic powers. Toma Kamijo, a high-school student classified as Level 0, possesses a mysterious right hand, the "Imagine Breaker", which negates any supernatural phenomenon it touches. One morning he finds a young English nun named Index hanging on his balcony railing; she is on the run from the Church of England's secret Necessarius division because her mind contains the Index Librorum Prohibitorum — 103,000 forbidden magical grimoires. Toma's defence of Index draws him into the intersecting conflicts of the science side and the magic side, whose magicians, churches, and conspiracies operate behind the scenes of modern society.

Characters

  • Toma Kamijo — the protagonist, an unlucky Level 0 esper whose right hand cancels supernatural powers.
  • Index — a nun of the Anglican Church whose perfect memory holds 103,000 grimoires.
  • Mikoto Misaka — a Level 5 electromaster, the third-ranked esper of Academy City, and protagonist of the spin-off A Certain Scientific Railgun.
  • Accelerator — the strongest Level 5 esper, able to control vectors; protagonist of the spin-off A Certain Scientific Accelerator.
  • Kaori Kanzaki — a saint-class magician of Necessarius.

Media

The series has been adapted into a manga (from 2007), three seasons of television anime by J.C.Staff (2008–2009, 2010–2011, and 2018–2019), and the feature film A Certain Magical Index: The Movie – The Miracle of Endymion (2013). Its spin-off manga A Certain Scientific Railgun has received its own multi-season anime adaptation. Video games, including a 2011 fighting game and the mobile game Imaginary Fest, have also been released. By the late 2010s the franchise had tens of millions of copies in circulation, making it one of the best-selling light novel series of all time.

Reception

The series has repeatedly placed in the Kono Light Novel ga Sugoi! rankings, and its world-building — contrasting a rigid science side with a conspiratorial magic side — is frequently cited as a major influence on subsequent light novel works.
