Pigeon racing

Release of racing pigeons at the start of a race

Pigeon racing is the sport of releasing specially trained homing pigeons, known as racing homers, at a measured distance from their home lofts and timing their return. The winning bird is the one recording the highest average speed over the course, calculated from its release-to-arrival time and the distance flown.

History

The sport developed in Belgium in the early nineteenth century, where breeders crossed several varieties to produce birds capable of flying long distances at high speed and returning reliably. It spread to the Netherlands, France, Britain and the United States, becoming strongly associated with working-class communities, mining towns and industrial regions. Belgium remains a centre of the sport, and top Belgian racing pigeons command very high prices at auction, particularly from buyers in China and Taiwan.

The race

Before a race, each bird is registered and fitted with a numbered leg ring; modern races use electronic timing systems in which a chip ring is scanned automatically when the bird enters its loft. Birds are transported to the release point in specially designed trucks or trains and liberated simultaneously. Races range from short sprints of around 100 kilometres to marathon events of 1,000 kilometres or more, such as the famous international race from Barcelona.

Training and breeding

Young birds are trained with progressively longer "tosses" from increasing distances. Fanciers select breeding stock for endurance, navigation and speed. Motivation techniques, such as the widowhood system — in which cocks race home to a hen shown to them before basketing — are widely used to sharpen a bird's desire to return.

Controversies

The sport has faced criticism over losses of birds to predators, bad weather and exhaustion, the use of performance-enhancing drugs, and gambling associated with big-money "one loft" races, notably in South Africa's Million Dollar Pigeon Race.
