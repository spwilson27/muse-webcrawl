Eye of Providence

The Eye of Providence (or the all-seeing eye) is a symbol that depicts an eye, often enclosed in a triangle and surrounded by a ray of light or a glory, meant to represent divine providence and the observation of humankind by God. The eye within a triangle also represents the Christian Trinity. In the modern era, a notable depiction of the eye can be found on the reverse of the Great Seal of the United States, which appears on the United States one-dollar bill.

History

Religious use of an all-seeing eye dates back to ancient mythology, including the Egyptian Eye of Horus. In medieval and Renaissance European iconography, the eye surrounded by a triangle became a common Christian symbol of the omniscient God and the Trinity, appearing in churches and religious paintings such as Pontormo's Supper at Emmaus (1525). In 1782 the symbol was adopted for the reverse of the Great Seal of the United States, placed above an unfinished pyramid of thirteen steps, signifying that the nation was still growing under the watchful care of Providence.

Freemasonry and popular culture

The all-seeing eye also appears in Masonic iconography as a reminder that one's deeds are observed by God, though its Masonic use postdates the Great Seal. In popular culture the symbol is frequently associated with conspiracy theories concerning Freemasonry and the Illuminati, associations not supported by the historical record of its adoption.
