Grey mould

Grey mould is a common and serious plant disease caused by the fungus Botrytis cinerea. The disease is named for the characteristic fuzzy grey-brown growth — masses of fungal spores borne on dark stalks — that covers infected plant tissue. It affects more than two hundred plant species and is a major cause of losses in soft fruit, grapes, vegetables and ornamental plants, both in the field and after harvest.

Symptoms and spread

Infection typically begins in wounds or in fading flowers, producing soft, water-soaked spots that become covered with the grey mould. The fungus thrives in cool, damp, poorly ventilated conditions and spreads by wind-borne spores and by contact between diseased and healthy produce. In grape growing it has a double character: destructive grey rot on ripe fruit, but in particular conditions it produces "noble rot", which concentrates sugars and flavours and is essential to sweet wines such as Sauternes and Tokaji. Control relies on sanitation, removing infected material, improving air circulation, careful storage, and fungicides, although B. cinerea is notorious for rapidly developing fungicide resistance.
