Eventing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Eventing (also known as three-day eventing or horse trials) is an equestrian event where a single horse and rider combination compete against other competitors across the three disciplines of dressage, cross-country, and show jumping. This event has its roots in a comprehensive cavalry test that required mastery of several types of riding. The competition may be run as a one-day event (ODE), where all three events are completed in one day, or as a three-day event, in which the tests are spread over three days.

History

Eventing was developed from military tests for cavalry officers' horses, which had to be obedient and elegant on parade (dressage), fit and bold enough to carry dispatches across country (cross-country), and sound and supple enough to continue working the following day (show jumping). It was introduced at the Olympic Games in Stockholm in 1912 and has remained an Olympic discipline ever since; women first competed in 1964.

Phases

Dressage tests the horse's obedience, suppleness and harmony with the rider through a prescribed sequence of movements in an arena. In cross-country, horse and rider gallop over a course of up to several kilometres jumping solid, natural-looking obstacles such as logs, ditches, water complexes and banks; refusals and falls incur penalties, and exceeding the optimum time adds time faults. Show jumping (stadium jumping) is held over coloured, knock-down fences in an arena and tests that the horse remains rideable and careful after the exertions of cross-country.

Scoring and levels

Scores from all three phases are combined into a single penalty score, with the lowest total winning. International competitions governed by the FEI are graded from CCI1* to CCI5*; the handful of five-star events — including Badminton, Burghley, Kentucky, Luhmühlen, Pau and Adelaide — are the sport's most prestigious. Safety has been a major focus since the 1990s, leading to frangible fence technology and extensive research into horse and rider falls.
