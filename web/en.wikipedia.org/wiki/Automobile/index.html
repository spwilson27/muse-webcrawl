Automobile

[Image: a red 1960s-style saloon car and a modern silver electric car side by side in front of a city skyline]

An automobile, or car, is a wheeled motor vehicle used for transportation, typically seating one to eight people, driven by an internal combustion engine or electric motor, and used mainly on roads. Cars are among the defining technologies of modern life: over a billion are in use worldwide, and the automotive industry is one of the largest manufacturing sectors.

Most definitions specify that automobiles run primarily on roads, have four wheels, carry people rather than goods (goods vehicles being trucks), and are controlled by a driver — increasingly assisted, or in experimental cases replaced, by automated systems.

History

Karl Benz patented the Benz Patent-Motorwagen in 1886, generally regarded as the first practical automobile. Early cars were luxury goods made one by one; Henry Ford's moving assembly line and the Model T (1908) made them mass-market products, and by the 1920s the car had reshaped American cities and suburbs.

Post-war decades brought motorways, global mass motorisation, and growing concerns about safety, congestion and pollution, met by regulation (seat belts, emissions standards) and by technical change from fuel injection to catalysers.

Electrification and the present

Battery-electric vehicles, pioneered at scale by Tesla and now produced by nearly all major manufacturers, have grown rapidly in the 2010s–2020s, driven by falling battery costs and climate policy; China became the world's largest car market and a leader in electric vehicles. Hybrid and hydrogen fuel-cell vehicles offer alternatives, while driver-assistance and self-driving systems continue to develop.

The car's future is debated in terms of electrification, automation, shared mobility and urban planning, but it remains the dominant mode of personal transport in most wealthy countries.
