Iowa Writers' Workshop

Type: Graduate writing programme
Established: 1936
Location: University of Iowa, Iowa City

The Iowa Writers' Workshop is a graduate-level creative writing programme at the University of Iowa in Iowa City. Founded in 1936, it is widely regarded as the first and most influential programme of its kind, and it pioneered the "workshop" model in which students' manuscripts are critiqued by peers and faculty. Graduates receive the Master of Fine Arts degree.

History

The Workshop grew out of the university's early acceptance of creative work for graduate degrees. Under director Paul Engle (1941–1965) it gained national prominence and attracted international students. Iowa City was later designated a UNESCO City of Literature in 2008, the first in the United States.

Alumni and influence

Alumni include Flannery O'Connor, John Irving, Raymond Carver (briefly), T. C. Boyle, Sandra Cisneros, and Michael Cunningham; numerous graduates have won Pulitzer Prizes and National Book Awards. The Workshop's success inspired hundreds of similar MFA programmes, though its pedagogy and cultural dominance have also attracted critical debate.
