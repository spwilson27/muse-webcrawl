Premenstrual syndrome

Premenstrual syndrome (PMS) is a pattern of physical and emotional symptoms that occur in the luteal phase of the menstrual cycle, in the one to two weeks before menstruation, and improve within a few days after the period begins. Symptoms commonly include mood swings, irritability, anxiety, fatigue, bloating, breast tenderness, headaches, and food cravings.

Signs and symptoms

More than 200 possible symptoms have been associated with PMS, but the most typical are irritability, tension, dysphoria, bloating, and breast pain. Around 20–30% of women meet clinical criteria for PMS, while a smaller proportion — roughly 3–8% — experience the severe form, premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD), in which mood symptoms predominate and significantly impair functioning.

Cause

The exact cause is unclear, but symptoms are linked to cyclical changes in ovarian hormones and to altered sensitivity of neurotransmitter systems, particularly serotonin. PMS occurs only in ovulatory cycles and is absent during pregnancy and after menopause. Genetic factors appear to contribute, as concordance is higher in identical twins.

Diagnosis and management

Diagnosis is based on a documented pattern of symptoms, often confirmed by prospective daily symptom charting over two cycles. Management includes lifestyle measures (exercise, stress reduction, dietary changes), cognitive behavioral therapy, and medications such as selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs), which may be taken continuously or only in the luteal phase, and hormonal contraceptives that suppress ovulation.
