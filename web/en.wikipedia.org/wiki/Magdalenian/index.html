Magdalenian

The Magdalenian cultures were late Upper Paleolithic cultures of western Europe, dating from about 17,000 to 12,000 years ago. They are named after the type site of La Madeleine, a rock shelter in the Vézère valley in the Dordogne, France. The Magdalenian represents the final phase of the European Upper Paleolithic sequence, following the Solutrean.

Material culture

Magdalenian toolkits are notable for finely worked bone and antler implements, including harpoons with single or double rows of barbs, spear-throwers (atlatls), needles and ornaments, alongside microlithic stone tools. Seasonal hunting focused on reindeer and horse, reflected in the settlement patterns of sites in France, Spain and Germany.

Art

The period produced some of the most famous Paleolithic art. Cave paintings at Altamira and the later phases of Lascaux, detailed engravings on bone and antler, and carved spear-throwers date to Magdalenian times. Portable art includes figurines and decorated tools showing a high degree of naturalism.

Decline

As the climate warmed at the end of the last Ice Age, Magdalenian cultures gave way to Mesolithic adaptations such as the Azilian in southern France and Spain.
