Malhar Rao Holkar (16 March 1693 – 20 May 1766) was a prominent noble and general of the Maratha Empire and the founder of the Holkar dynasty that ruled the Indore State. Rising from humble origins as a shepherd's son, he became one of the most powerful sardars of the Peshwa's court and, as Subedar of Malwa, built Indore into a major centre of Maratha power in central India.

Early life and rise

Malhar Rao was born in Jejuri near Pune into a Dhangar (shepherd) family. He entered the service of the sardar Kadam Bande and later joined the forces of Peshwa Bajirao I, who recognised his talent and granted him command of a cavalry contingent and, in 1732, effective control of Malwa. He campaigned widely across northern India, participating in the Maratha expansion toward Delhi and the Punjab, and played an important role in the politics of the Mughal court.

Later campaigns and death

Holkar was one of the senior commanders of Maratha expansion in the north in the 1750s. His forces took part in the Maratha campaigns in the north during the period preceding the Third Battle of Panipat (1761); his son Khanderao had earlier been killed at the siege of Kumbher in 1754. Malhar Rao remained active in Malwa until his death at Alampur in 1766. He was succeeded briefly by his grandson Male Rao and then by his daughter-in-law Ahilyabai Holkar, who became one of India's most celebrated rulers.
