Liberal Republican Party

The Liberal Republican Party was an American political party organised in 1870 by reform-minded Republicans dissatisfied with the corruption of President Ulysses S. Grant's administration and with Radical Reconstruction policies in the South. Led by figures such as Senator Carl Schurz of Missouri, it advocated civil service reform, free trade, amnesty for former Confederates and an end to military rule in the South.

Election of 1872

At its Cincinnati convention in May 1872 the party nominated newspaper editor Horace Greeley for president and Missouri governor Benjamin Gratz Brown for vice president. The Democratic Party, lacking a strong candidate of its own, also nominated the Greeley ticket. Greeley ran a chaotic campaign and was heavily defeated by Grant, winning only 43.8 percent of the popular vote and carrying six states. He died shortly after the election, before the Electoral College met, and the party disintegrated.

Legacy

Although short-lived, the Liberal Republican revolt foreshadowed later reform movements within the Republican Party, including the Mugwumps of 1884, and helped shift national policy towards reconciliation with the white South.
