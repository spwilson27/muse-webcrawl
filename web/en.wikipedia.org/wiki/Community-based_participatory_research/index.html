Community-based participatory research

CBPR treats community members as equal partners in every phase of research.

Community-based participatory research (CBPR) is a partnership approach to research that equitably involves community members, organisational representatives, and academic researchers in all aspects of the research process — from defining the questions, through collecting and interpreting data, to disseminating and acting on the results. It is widely used in public health, social work, education, and environmental justice research.

Principles

Key principles articulated by scholars such as Barbara Israel and colleagues include: recognising the community as a unit of identity; building on community strengths and resources; facilitating collaborative, equitable partnerships; promoting co-learning and capacity building among all partners; balancing knowledge generation with action for mutual benefit; focusing on local relevance and ecological perspectives on health; involving partners in disseminating findings; and committing to long-term partnership and sustainability. CBPR draws on the traditions of action research associated with Kurt Lewin and the participatory action research movements of Latin America, Africa, and Asia, including the work of Paulo Freire and Orlando Fals Borda.

Applications

CBPR has been used to study environmental health hazards such as air pollution in fence-line communities, occupational health, HIV prevention, obesity and diabetes prevention, and health disparities affecting marginalised groups. Funders such as the US National Institutes of Health and the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention have supported CBPR through dedicated programmes, reflecting evidence that community partnership improves the relevance, recruitment, and uptake of research.

Challenges

Practitioners note that genuine partnership takes time, can strain academic timelines and tenure expectations, and raises questions about power, data ownership, and authorship. Ethical review procedures designed for investigator-led studies may not fully accommodate shared decision-making, prompting calls for community review mechanisms alongside institutional review boards.
