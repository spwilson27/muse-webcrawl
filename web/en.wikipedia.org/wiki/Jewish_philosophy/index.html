Jewish philosophy is the engagement of Jewish thinkers with general philosophical questions and the philosophical interpretation of Judaism, running from Philo of Alexandria's allegorical fusion of Torah and Plato through the medieval rationalism of Saadia Gaon and Maimonides to modern figures such as Martin Buber (I and Thou), Franz Rosenzweig and Emmanuel Levinas.

Themes

Recurring questions include creation versus eternity, divine attributes and negative theology, the reasons for the commandments, election and universalism, theodicy after catastrophe — searingly after the Holocaust — and the relation of halakha (law) to ethics. Kabbalah offered a theosophical counterpoint to rationalism, and the two currents repeatedly fertilised each other.
