Wear OS (also known simply as Wear, and formerly Android Wear) is a version of Google's Android operating system designed for smartwatches and other wearables. It pairs with mobile phones running Android or iOS and provides notifications, voice interaction through Google Assistant, health and fitness tracking, and downloadable watch faces and apps from the Google Play Store.

History

The platform was announced in March 2014 as Android Wear, with the LG G Watch and Samsung Gear Live among the first devices. Android Wear 2.0 (2017) added standalone apps and a Play Store on the watch itself. In March 2018 the platform was rebranded as Wear OS by Google.

Wear OS 3, announced in 2021, was a major reworking developed jointly with Samsung, merging elements of Samsung's Tizen-based watch software into the platform; it debuted on the Samsung Galaxy Watch 4 series, with Google launching its own Pixel Watch in 2022. Wear OS 4 (2023) and Wear OS 5 (2024), based on newer Android versions, emphasised battery efficiency, accessibility, and new watch-face formats.

Features and hardware

Wear OS integrates Google Fit and third-party health platforms for activity, heart rate, and sleep tracking, supports Google Wallet payments over NFC, LTE connectivity on some models, and Tiles — swipeable glanceable screens. Devices have been made by Samsung, Google, Fossil, Mobvoi (TicWatch), Xiaomi, OnePlus, and luxury brands such as TAG Heuer and Montblanc, most powered by Qualcomm Snapdragon Wear or Samsung Exynos chips.
