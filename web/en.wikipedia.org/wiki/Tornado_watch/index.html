A tornado watch is a statement issued by national weather forecasting agencies — in the United States, by the Storm Prediction Center (SPC) of NOAA and the National Weather Service — indicating that atmospheric conditions in a defined area are favourable for the development of tornadoes and severe thunderstorms. A watch means "be prepared": tornadoes are possible in and near the watch area, and people should review their safety plans and be ready to act should a tornado warning be issued or a tornado be sighted.

Definition and criteria

Tornado watches are issued in consultation between the SPC and local National Weather Service offices, usually for areas of about 25,000 square miles covering parts of one or several states, and typically last four to eight hours. A watch is issued when forecasters judge that the environment — instability, moisture, wind shear, and a lifting mechanism — can support supercell thunderstorms capable of producing tornadoes; watches often also cover the threat of large hail and damaging winds. A specially worded watch, marked a Particularly Dangerous Situation (PDS) watch, is issued rarely, when there is a high confidence of multiple strong or violent (EF2+) tornadoes.

Watch versus warning

A watch is distinct from a tornado warning, which is issued by local NWS offices when a tornado has been sighted by spotters or law enforcement, or is indicated by weather radar. The warning covers a much smaller area and means "take action now." Watches are identified by a sequential number for the year and are disseminated through NOAA Weather Radio, the Emergency Alert System, and media outlets; they were historically described as "red box" watches because of the colour used to outline them on weather maps.
