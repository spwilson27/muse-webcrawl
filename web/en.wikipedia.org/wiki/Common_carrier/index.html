Common carrier

Railways, airlines, pipelines and telecommunications networks may all be common carriers.

A common carrier in common law countries is a person or company that transports goods or people for the general public and is legally responsible for any possible loss of the goods during transport. Common carriers offer services to the public under a licence or authority provided by a regulatory body, and in return accept obligations to serve all customers on non-discriminatory terms.

Legal status

Common carriers are distinguished from private or contract carriers, who may choose their customers. Under common law a common carrier is strictly liable for loss or damage to goods entrusted to it, subject to exceptions such as acts of God, acts of public enemies, the inherent vice of the goods, and the fault of the shipper. Carriers may limit liability by contract within limits set by statute, such as the Carmack Amendment for interstate rail and road freight in the United States.

Sectors

The concept applies to railways, airlines, shipping lines, freight forwarders, bus companies, oil and gas pipelines, and telecommunications providers. In telecommunications law, "common carrier" status under Title II of the US Communications Act of 1934 has been central to debates over the regulation of telephone companies and broadband internet providers, including the net neutrality controversy.
