Reidemeister torsion

In mathematics, Reidemeister torsion (also called R-torsion, Franz torsion or Reidemeister–Franz torsion) is a topological invariant of manifolds introduced by Kurt Reidemeister in 1935 for 3-manifolds, and extended by Werner Franz in work on lens spaces.

Definition

The invariant is constructed from a chain complex associated to a space together with a representation of its fundamental group. It combines the determinants of basis-change maps on the homology of the complex into a single element, defined up to a small ambiguity, of a field or of the multiplicative group of units of a ring.

Significance

Reidemeister torsion was historically important as one of the first invariants capable of distinguishing spaces that are homotopy equivalent but not homeomorphic — notably, it classifies lens spaces up to homeomorphism. It is a precursor of Whitehead torsion and is related to analytic invariants through the Cheeger–Müller theorem, which equates it with Ray–Singer analytic torsion.
