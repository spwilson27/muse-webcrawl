Dharmachakra

The dharmachakra (Sanskrit: धर्मचक्र, "Wheel of Dharma"; Pali: dhammacakka) is one of the oldest and most important symbols in Indian religions, above all Buddhism, where it represents the teaching of the Buddha and the path to enlightenment. The wheel's rolling forward symbolises the Buddha "turning the wheel of Dharma" — setting his teaching in motion with his first sermon at Sarnath.

Symbolism

The wheel typically has eight spokes, representing the Noble Eightfold Path. Its three parts — hub, spokes and rim — are sometimes read as the Three Jewels of Buddhism (Buddha, Dharma and Sangha) or as the three trainings of ethics, concentration and wisdom. In Jainism the wheel appears at the heart of the ahimsa symbol, and in Hinduism a chariot wheel or discus is associated with Vishnu and with righteous kingship (the chakravartin).

History

The dharmachakra crowns the pillars erected by the Mauryan emperor Ashoka in the 3rd century BCE; the Lion Capital of Ashoka, whose wheel now appears on the flag of India, is the most famous example. The symbol spread with Buddhism across Asia and appears in temple architecture, flags and emblems — including the Dhvaja and the emblem of Mongolia, and on the Thai royal insignia. The related bhavachakra, the Tibetan "wheel of life", depicts samsara rather than the Dharma itself.
