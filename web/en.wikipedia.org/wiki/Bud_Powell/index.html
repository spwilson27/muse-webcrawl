Bud Powell

Bud Powell, a leading figure of bebop piano.

Earl Rudolph "Bud" Powell (September 27, 1924 – July 31, 1966) was an American jazz pianist and composer. A leading figure of bebop, he adapted the fast, linear improvisational language of Charlie Parker and Dizzy Gillespie to the piano, revolutionizing jazz piano playing and influencing virtually every jazz pianist who followed, including Thelonious Monk's younger contemporaries, Bill Evans and Herbie Hancock.

Life and career

Born in Harlem, New York, Powell was a child prodigy encouraged by Monk, who became his mentor. He recorded with Cootie Williams in 1944, participated in the landmark 1953 Massey Hall concert with Parker, Gillespie, Charles Mingus and Max Roach, and made classic trio recordings for Blue Note and Verve, including "Un Poco Loco", "Bouncing with Bud", "Dance of the Infidels" and "Parisian Thoroughfare". His compositions such as "Hallucinations" and "The Glass Enclosure" are standards of the bebop repertoire.

Powell's career and health were blighted by mental illness, alcoholism and a 1945 police beating that caused chronic headaches; he spent periods in psychiatric hospitals and underwent electroconvulsive therapy. He lived in Paris from 1959 to 1964, where his friendship with expatriate fan Francis Paudras later inspired the film 'Round Midnight (1986). Powell died of tuberculosis and malnutrition in New York in 1966, aged 41.
