Ap and Bp stars

Ap and Bp stars are chemically peculiar stars of spectral types A and B which show certain anomalies in their spectra: overabundances of elements such as strontium, chromium, and europium, and strong magnetic fields.

Ap stars are a type of chemically peculiar star (hence the "p") of spectral type A, whose spectra show strong absorption lines of elements such as strontium, chromium, europium, and other rare earths. The hotter counterparts of spectral type B are called Bp stars. These stars also have strong magnetic fields, typically of kilogauss strength, and rotate more slowly than normal A and B stars.

Characteristics

The chemical peculiarities are thought to arise from atomic diffusion in the stars' stable, slowly rotating atmospheres: radiative levitation pushes some heavy elements upward while gravity settles others, producing atmospheres with highly non-solar compositions. Because the magnetic field is usually tilted relative to the rotation axis (the oblique rotator model), the visible hemisphere changes as the star rotates, causing periodic variations in brightness, spectrum, and measured magnetic field.

Subtypes

Important subgroups include the rapidly oscillating Ap (roAp) stars, which exhibit high-overtone, low-degree non-radial pulsations with periods of about 5 to 24 minutes, and the silicon stars, in which silicon lines are particularly strong. Well-known Ap stars include α² Canum Venaticorum, the prototype of a class of variable stars, and Przybylski's star, famous for its extreme and still-debated abundance anomalies.
