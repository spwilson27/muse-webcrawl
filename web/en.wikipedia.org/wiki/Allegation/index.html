Allegation

Allegation
Type: Claim or assertion
Status: Asserted but not yet proved
Related: Accusation, evidence, burden of proof

In law, an allegation is a claim of an unproven fact by a party in a pleading, which the party then undertakes to prove. An allegation generally forms one part of a legal case and states a fact that is relevant to the disposition of the case. It differs from evidence, which is what is presented to prove or disprove the allegation.

Types

Civil allegations appear in a complaint, in which a plaintiff asserts facts said to entitle them to relief. Criminal allegations appear in an indictment, information or charge, asserting that the accused committed an offence; these remain allegations — consistent with the presumption of innocence — until proved beyond a reasonable doubt or admitted by a guilty plea.

Use in media

Journalists and editors commonly use "allegation" and "alleged" for unproven claims, especially about crimes or misconduct, both for accuracy and to reduce the risk of defamation. An allegation is not itself a finding, and reputable reporting seeks comment from the person alleged against.

False allegations

A false allegation is a claim that is untrue and made without supporting evidence, whether mistakenly, recklessly or deliberately. False allegations may give rise to remedies such as defamation claims, and knowingly false reports to police may themselves be offences, though researchers and courts distinguish unproven, unfounded and demonstrably false cases.
