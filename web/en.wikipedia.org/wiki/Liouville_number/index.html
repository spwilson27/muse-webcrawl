Liouville number

In number theory, a Liouville number is a real number x with the property that, for every positive integer n, there exist integers p, q with q > 1 such that |x − p/q| < 1/qn. In other words, a Liouville number can be approximated "extremely well" by rational numbers.

Liouville's theorem and transcendence

Joseph Liouville proved in 1844 that algebraic numbers of degree n cannot be approximated better than a certain bound; hence every Liouville number must be transcendental. These were the first numbers explicitly proved to be transcendental.

Liouville's constant

The classic example is the Liouville constant, Σk≥1 10−k! = 0.110001000000000000000001000..., whose decimal expansion has 1s in the factorial positions and 0s elsewhere. Although Liouville numbers are uncountable, they form a set of Lebesgue measure zero and are dense in the reals.
