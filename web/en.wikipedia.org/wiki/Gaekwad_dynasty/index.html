The Gaekwad dynasty (also spelled Gaikwad, Gaekwar or Gaikwar) was a prominent Maratha dynasty that ruled the princely state of Baroda in present-day Gujarat, India, from the early 18th century until 1947. The family rose as commanders in the Maratha army; the surname is traditionally said to derive from gae (cow) and kavad (gate), commemorating an ancestor who sheltered a cow.

History

The dynasty was founded by Pilaji Rao Gaekwad, a Maratha general who conquered Songadh in 1719 and established his power in Gujarat under the authority of the Peshwa; his son Damaji Rao II secured Baroda itself. After the Third Anglo-Maratha War, Baroda entered into treaty relations with the British and became one of the largest and richest princely states, entitled to a 21-gun salute.

Its most celebrated ruler, Maharaja Sayajirao Gaekwad III (reigned 1875–1939), turned Baroda into a model state: he introduced free and compulsory primary education, founded the Bank of Baroda and a university, built the Lakshmi Vilas Palace, patronised artists such as Raja Ravi Varma, and employed the young B. R. Ambedkar in state service. The last ruling Maharaja, Pratap Singh Rao Gaekwad, signed the instrument of accession in 1949, merging Baroda into the Indian Union.
