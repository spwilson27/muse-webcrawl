The Dahlak Archipelago is an island group located in the Red Sea near Massawa, Eritrea. It consists of two large and 124 small islands. The archipelago is known for its pearl fisheries, its marine biodiversity, and its long history as a trading and cultural crossroads between Africa and Arabia.

History

The islands were known to Roman and Greek geographers, and pearls from Dahlak were traded across the ancient world. Dahlak was one of the first places in East Africa to adopt Islam, around the 7th–8th centuries; the Dahlak Sultanate later ruled the islands, leaving Arabic-inscribed tombstones and cisterns. The islands subsequently came under Ottoman, Egyptian, Italian colonial, and Ethiopian control before becoming part of independent Eritrea in the 1990s. During the Cold War, the Soviet Union maintained a naval facility on the islands.

Geography and environment

The islands are low, arid, and ringed by coral reefs in one of the warmest seas on Earth. The surrounding waters, part of the Dahlak Marine National Park, support coral reefs, mangroves, dugongs, turtles, and rich fish life largely undisturbed because of the small human population. The inhabitants, concentrated on Dahlak Kebir, speak Dahalik (a Semitic language related to Tigre) and Afar, and traditionally live by fishing and pearl diving.
