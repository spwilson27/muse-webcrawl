Edinburgh derby

The Edinburgh derby is the football rivalry between Heart of Midlothian (Hearts) and Hibernian (Hibs), the two senior clubs of Edinburgh, Scotland. It is one of the oldest rivalries in world football: the clubs first met on Christmas Day 1875, in a match Hearts won 1–0. Hearts are based at Tynecastle Park in the west of the city, while Hibernian play at Easter Road in the north-east.

History

Hibernian were founded by Irish Catholic immigrants in 1875 and Hearts in 1874; the early rivalry carried sectarian undertones, though these have largely faded and support is now drawn mainly along geographic lines. The teams meet in league and cup competition whenever they are in the same division, traditionally including a New Year fixture. The record victory in the fixture is Hearts' 10–2 win in 1893. In the 2012 Scottish Cup Final — the first all-Edinburgh final since 1896 — Hearts defeated Hibernian 5–1 at Hampden Park, a result Hibs supporters associate with the subsequent ending of their 114-year wait for the trophy in 2016.

Records

More than 300 competitive derbies have been played. Hearts hold the overall advantage in wins. Notable matches include the 1973 New Year game, won 7–0 by Hibernian at Tynecastle, and the 2006 Scottish Cup semi-final, won 4–0 by Hearts. Both clubs have had periods in the second tier, so the frequency of the derby varies by era; in some seasons there have been as many as five meetings.
