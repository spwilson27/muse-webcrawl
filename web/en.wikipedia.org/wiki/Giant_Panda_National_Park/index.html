Giant Panda National Park

Location: Sichuan, Shaanxi, and Gansu, China

Area: 27,134 km²

Established: October 29, 2021

Governing body: National Forestry and Grassland Administration

The Giant Panda National Park is a large protected area in western China established to protect the habitat of the giant panda (Ailuropoda melanoleuca). Officially established on October 29, 2021, the park spans about 27,134 square kilometres across the provinces of Sichuan, Shaanxi, and Gansu, uniting dozens of previously separate nature reserves, including the Wolong National Nature Reserve, into a single management system.

Establishment

China's national park system was piloted from 2016, and the Giant Panda National Park was one of the first five national parks formally established in October 2021, alongside parks for the Siberian tiger and Amur leopard, the Sanjiangyuan (headwaters of the Yangtze, Yellow, and Mekong rivers), Hainan's tropical rainforest, and Wuyishan. The park consolidated 73 existing reserves and protected areas that had previously been administered separately.

Geography and habitat

The park covers mountainous terrain in the Min, Qionglai, Liangshan, Daxiangling, Xiaoxiangling, and Qinling mountain ranges. Elevations range widely, supporting bamboo forests in the understory of mixed coniferous and broadleaf forests that provide food and shelter for pandas. The park connects previously fragmented panda habitats, aiming to allow isolated populations to interbreed and improve the species' genetic diversity.

Wildlife

Around 1,300 wild giant pandas — roughly 70 percent of China's wild panda population — live within the park. Other protected species include the red panda, golden snub-nosed monkey, takin, snow leopard, Chinese giant salamander, and numerous endemic plants.

People

Tens of thousands of people live within the park's boundaries. Management plans include relocation from core protected zones, conversion of farmland, and employment of local residents as rangers and eco-tourism workers, with grazing and logging restrictions balanced against compensation programs.
