Tamil-Brahmi

Tamil-Brahmi, also known as Tamili or Damili, is a variant of the Brahmi script used to write inscriptions in Old Tamil in the southern part of India, found mainly in Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Andhra Pradesh and Sri Lanka. It was in use from roughly the 3rd century BCE to the 4th century CE and is the earliest attested writing system for the Tamil language.

Characteristics

Like other Brahmi derivatives, Tamil-Brahmi is an abugida written left to right. It adapted the parent Brahmi script to the phonology of Tamil, which lacks the aspirated and voiced consonants of Indo-Aryan languages. Scholars have identified several special features and letter forms, and the script shows a distinctive system for representing pure consonants (the pulli), a problem later Tamil scripts solved differently.

Inscriptions

Tamil-Brahmi inscriptions are most famously found in rock-cut Jain and Buddhist cave shelters, recording donations of stone beds and shelters to monks, as well as on pottery, coins, seals and hero stones. Sites such as Mangulam, Jambai and Pugalur have yielded important early inscriptions, including records of Sangam-era chieftains. Sherds bearing Tamil-Brahmi graffiti have also been excavated at ports such as Arikamedu and at Red Sea and Sri Lankan sites, attesting to early maritime trade.

Dating and decipherment

The script was deciphered in the early 20th century by scholars including K. V. Subrahmanya Aiyer. Radiocarbon dates from sites such as Porunthal and Keezhadi have been used to argue for origins possibly as early as the 5th–6th centuries BCE, a chronology that remains debated among epigraphists.
