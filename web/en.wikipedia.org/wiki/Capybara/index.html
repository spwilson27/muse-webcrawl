Capybara

The capybara (Hydrochoerus hydrochaeris) is the largest living rodent, native to South America. A member of the genus Hydrochoerus, it is a highly social, semi-aquatic mammal found near lakes, rivers, and wetlands across most of the continent east of the Andes.

Capybara
Scientific name: Hydrochoerus hydrochaeris
Family: Caviidae
Mass: 35–65 kg
Status: Least Concern

Description

Capybaras have barrel-shaped, partially web-footed bodies covered in coarse reddish-brown fur, blunt snouts, and eyes, ears, and nostrils high on the head so they can stay mostly submerged. They can remain underwater for up to five minutes to hide from predators such as jaguars, caimans, and anacondas.

Behaviour

Capybaras live in groups of typically 10–20 individuals, graze mainly on grasses, and are strong swimmers. They are hunted for meat and hide in parts of their range and are also farmed. Their calm temperament has made them popular in zoos worldwide.
