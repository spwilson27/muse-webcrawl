Azimuth

[ Image placeholder: Diagram of azimuth and altitude ]

An azimuth (from Arabic al-sumūt, "the directions") is the angle, measured in a horizontal plane, between a reference direction — usually true north — and the direction to an observed object, measured clockwise. Azimuths are expressed in degrees from 0° to 360°: north is 0°, east 90°, south 180° and west 270°. Together with altitude (elevation angle) it specifies a direction in the horizontal coordinate system used in astronomy, surveying, navigation and artillery.

Definitions and conventions

In geodesy and navigation the azimuth of a line is normally reckoned clockwise from north; older astronomical practice sometimes measured from the south point. A back azimuth differs from the forward azimuth by 180°. Because compasses point to magnetic north, converting between magnetic and true azimuths requires the local magnetic declination. In spherical coordinates, the azimuthal angle φ is the analogue measured in the reference plane from a chosen axis.

Uses

  • Astronomy — locating stars in the alt-azimuth system used by most telescopes mounts.
  • Surveying and mapping — describing traverse legs and property boundaries (bearings).
  • Navigation — compass courses and celestial fixes at sea and in the air.
  • Military — fire direction, often measured in mils (6400 per circle in NATO practice).
  • Communications — orienting directional antennas and satellite dishes.
