Optometry is a healthcare profession that involves examining the eyes and related structures for defects or abnormalities. Medical optics is the science of the measurement of light and design of instruments and lenses. Optometrists are healthcare professionals who usually provide primary eye care through comprehensive eye examinations. They diagnose and manage diseases or disorders of the visual system, the eye and associated structures, and prescribe corrective lenses (glasses or contact lenses), vision therapy, and in many jurisdictions certain medications.

History

The term "optometry" comes from the Greek words ὄψις (opsis, "view") and μέτρον (metron, "measure"). Spectacles appeared in Italy in the late 13th century, and for centuries lens-grinding remained a craft of spectacle makers. In the early 20th century, optometry emerged as a licensed, university-trained profession; in the United States, the first optometry laws date to 1901 (Minnesota), and by 1921 all states licensed the practice. The profession expanded its scope through the late 20th century to include diagnostic and, in most US states, therapeutic pharmaceutical agents.

Practice

A comprehensive eye examination typically includes measurement of visual acuity, refraction with a phoropter or retinoscope, binocular vision testing, intraocular pressure measurement (tonometry) for glaucoma screening, slit-lamp examination of the anterior eye, and ophthalmoscopy of the retina. Optometrists correct refractive errors — myopia, hyperopia, astigmatism, and presbyopia — and detect conditions such as cataracts, glaucoma, macular degeneration, and diabetic retinopathy, referring patients to ophthalmologists for surgery or specialist care when required.

Education and regulation

In North America, optometrists complete a four-year Doctor of Optometry (OD) degree after undergraduate study, followed by optional residency training. In the United Kingdom and many Commonwealth countries, optometrists qualify with a bachelor's or master's degree in optometry and register with a regulatory body such as the General Optical Council. The scope of practice varies by country and, in the US, by state.
