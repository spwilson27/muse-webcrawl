Celastrus

Celastrus, commonly known as staff vine, staff tree or bittersweet, is a genus of about 40 species of shrubs and woody vines in the family Celastraceae, native to East Asia, Australasia, Africa, and North and South America.

FamilyCelastraceae
GenusCelastrus L.
Speciesabout 40

Description

The plants are deciduous or evergreen climbers with alternate, simple, toothed leaves and inconspicuous greenish-white flowers. The ornamental fruit is a three-lobed capsule that splits open to reveal seeds clothed in a bright red or orange aril; the sexes are usually on separate plants, so both male and female plants are needed for fruit.

Species

Well-known species include Celastrus scandens, the American bittersweet of North America, and Celastrus orbiculatus, the Oriental bittersweet of East Asia. The latter has become a serious invasive plant in eastern North America, where its vigorous vines smother and uproot trees and it hybridises with the native species, threatening it genetically. Celastrus paniculatus, the black oil plant of India, is used in Ayurvedic medicine, its seed oil traditionally taken as a nootropic and remedy for memory loss.

Toxicity

The fruits and other parts are considered poisonous to humans and livestock if eaten in quantity, though birds consume the seeds and disperse the plants.
