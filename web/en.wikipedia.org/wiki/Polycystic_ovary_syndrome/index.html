Polycystic ovary syndrome

Polycystic ovary syndrome
SpecialtyGynaecology, endocrinology
SymptomsIrregular periods, excess androgen effects, acne, infertility
PrevalenceRoughly 6–12% of women of reproductive age

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal disorder affecting women of reproductive age. It is characterised by a combination of excess androgen (male hormone) activity, irregular or absent ovulation, and ovaries containing many small follicles. PCOS is a leading cause of infertility and is strongly associated with insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes.

Signs and symptoms

Features include irregular or infrequent menstrual periods, hirsutism (excess hair growth), acne, scalp hair thinning, weight gain, and difficulty conceiving. Long-term risks include type 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome, cardiovascular disease, endometrial cancer, depression, and obstructive sleep apnoea.

Diagnosis

The widely used Rotterdam criteria (2003) require two of three findings after excluding other causes: irregular ovulation, clinical or biochemical signs of excess androgen, and polycystic ovarian morphology on ultrasound. The condition was first described in modern form by Stein and Leventhal in 1935.

Causes

The causes are incompletely understood and involve genetic predisposition interacting with environmental factors. Insulin resistance and compensatory hyperinsulinaemia drive ovarian androgen production in many patients; obesity commonly worsens the condition but many lean women are also affected.

Management

There is no cure; treatment targets symptoms and risks. Lifestyle modification and weight loss are first-line. Combined oral contraceptives regulate periods and reduce androgen effects; metformin may improve insulin sensitivity and ovulation; ovulation-inducing agents such as letrozole are used for infertility.
