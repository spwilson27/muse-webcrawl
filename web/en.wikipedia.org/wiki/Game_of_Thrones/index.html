Overview

Game of Thrones is an American fantasy drama television series created by David Benioff and D. B. Weiss for HBO, adapted from George R. R. Martin's novel series A Song of Ice and Fire. Set on the fictional continents of Westeros and Essos, it follows interwoven storylines of noble houses fighting for the Iron Throne as an ancient enemy (the White Walkers) returns after being dormant for millennia.

Reception and legacy

The series became HBO's biggest hit and a global cultural phenomenon, praised for its production scale, moral ambiguity and ensemble acting, and it won a record 59 Primetime Emmy Awards. Its later seasons, which outpaced Martin's published books, drew criticism for compressed plotting, and the final season (2019) divided audiences sharply (a fan petition for a remake gathered millions of signatures) — yet the franchise expanded with the acclaimed prequel House of the Dragon (2022–). Its theme by Ramin Djawadi and locations such as Dubrovnik (King's Landing) became tourist landmarks.
