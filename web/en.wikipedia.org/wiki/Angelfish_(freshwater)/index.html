Angelfish (freshwater)

KingdomAnimalia
FamilyCichlidae
GenusPterophyllum
SpeciesP. scalare, P. altum, P. leopoldi
OriginAmazon River basin, South America

Freshwater angelfish are fish of the genus Pterophyllum in the cichlid family, native to the Amazon basin, the Orinoco basin, and rivers of the Guiana Shield in South America. The most commonly kept species is Pterophyllum scalare.

Description

Angelfish have a distinctive laterally compressed, disc-shaped body with elongated dorsal, anal, and pelvic fins, giving them a triangular silhouette. The tall, narrow body is an adaptation for manoeuvring among submerged roots and plants. Wild fish are silvery with dark vertical bars; decades of selective breeding have produced varieties including marble, gold, koi, and veil-tailed forms.

Behaviour

Angelfish form monogamous pairs and are dedicated parents: both parents fan and guard the eggs, which are laid on flat surfaces, and tend the free-swimming fry. They are generally peaceful community fish but may prey on very small tankmates.

In the aquarium

Among the most popular tropical aquarium fish, angelfish require tall tanks, warm water (about 24–30 °C), and soft, slightly acidic conditions similar to their native blackwater habitats.
