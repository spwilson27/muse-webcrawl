The Derby Stakes, popularly known as the Epsom Derby or simply the Derby, is a Group 1 flat horse race in England open to three-year-old colts and fillies. It is run at Epsom Downs over about one and a half miles each June, and is Britain's richest and most prestigious thoroughbred race.

History

The race was founded in 1780 by the 12th Earl of Derby; legend says its name was decided by a coin toss with Sir Charles Bunbury, whose horse Diomed won the first running. Together with the Oaks (also at Epsom), the 2,000 Guineas, the 1,000 Guineas and the St Leger, it forms the British Classics; winning the Derby, 2,000 Guineas and St Leger constitutes the colts' Triple Crown.

The course

Epsom's horseshoe-shaped course, with its descent and climb, the camber of the home straight and the famous Tattenham Corner, is regarded as a unique test of a young horse's balance and stamina.

Notable winners

Famous winners include Nijinsky, Shergar, Galileo and Sea the Stars. Lester Piggott rode a record nine Derby winners; Aidan O'Brien is the most successful trainer.
