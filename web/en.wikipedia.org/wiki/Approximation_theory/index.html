Approximation theory

Approximation theory is a branch of mathematics concerned with how functions can best be approximated by simpler functions, and with quantitatively characterizing the errors introduced thereby. Typical approximants include polynomials, rational functions, splines, and trigonometric polynomials, and the theory supplies the foundations for much of numerical analysis and computer computation of mathematical functions.

Best approximation

A central problem asks for the approximant minimizing the maximum error over an interval (the Chebyshev, or minimax, criterion). Chebyshev's equioscillation theorem characterizes best polynomial approximations: the error of the optimal degree-n polynomial attains its maximum magnitude, with alternating signs, at least n+2 times. Chebyshev polynomials themselves are near-optimal building blocks for interpolation and are used in the Remez algorithm to compute minimax approximations numerically.

Rates and methods

Approximation theory also studies how the error decays as the approximant's complexity grows: smooth functions admit rapidly converging polynomial approximations, while functions with singularities converge slowly unless rational functions or adaptively refined splines are used. The Weierstrass approximation theorem guarantees that any continuous function on a closed interval can be uniformly approximated by polynomials.
