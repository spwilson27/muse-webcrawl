Ponta Delgada is the largest municipality and the executive capital of the Autonomous Region of the Azores in Portugal. It is located on São Miguel Island, the largest and most populous in the archipelago. Ponta Delgada is the Azores' principal city and its main port and commercial centre, and it hosts the seat of the regional government presidency and the University of the Azores' main campus.

History

Settlement of the area began shortly after the discovery of São Miguel in the 15th century. The name Ponta Delgada ("thin point") refers to a narrow headland of volcanic rock where early colonists landed. The settlement grew around its sheltered anchorage and was raised to the status of a city by King John III of Portugal in 1546, after the earlier capital, Vila Franca do Campo, was devastated by an earthquake in 1522. The city was attacked during periods of conflict between Portugal and its rivals, and its fort of São Brás, built in the 16th century, still guards the harbour.

Geography and sights

The municipality stretches along the southern and western coasts of São Miguel and includes the parishes of the urban core — São José, São Pedro and São Sebastião — where the historic centre stands. Noted landmarks include the mother church of São Sebastião, the city gates (Portas da Cidade, erected in 1783), the Carlos Machado Museum, and the harbour promenade. Ponta Delgada is the usual starting point for visitors to the island's volcanic sights, including the twin lakes of Sete Cidades and the hot springs of Furnas. João Paulo II Airport, just west of the city, is the busiest airport in the Azores.

Economy

The city's economy centres on port services, commerce, dairy and agricultural processing from the island's farms, tourism, and public administration. The Portas do Mar cruise terminal receives large numbers of transatlantic cruise ships.
