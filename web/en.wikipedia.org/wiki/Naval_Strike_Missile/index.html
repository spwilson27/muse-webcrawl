The Naval Strike Missile (NSM) is a Norwegian anti-ship and land-attack cruise missile developed by Kongsberg Defence & Aerospace. Designed for sea-skimming flight and high manoeuvrability, it entered service with the Royal Norwegian Navy in the early 2010s and has since been adopted by several other navies, including those of Poland, the United States, Germany, and Australia.

Design

The NSM is a subsonic, turbojet-powered missile weighing about 400 kg. It navigates by GPS-aided inertial guidance with terrain contour matching, and homes in the terminal phase using an imaging infrared seeker capable of autonomous target recognition. Its titanium blast-fragmentation warhead weighs about 125 kg. A vertically launched and air-launched variant, the Joint Strike Missile (JSM), was developed for carriage inside the weapons bay of the F-35 Lightning II.

Service

Norway employs the NSM on its Fridtjof Nansen-class frigates and Skjold-class corvettes, and Poland operates it in coastal defence batteries. The United States Navy selected the missile for its Littoral Combat Ships and Constellation-class frigates, and the US Marine Corps adopted a ground-launched version, known as NMESIS, for expeditionary anti-ship missions.
