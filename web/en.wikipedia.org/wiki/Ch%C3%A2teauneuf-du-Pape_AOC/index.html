Châteauneuf-du-Pape AOC

Châteauneuf-du-Pape is a French wine appellation d'origine contrôlée (AOC) located around the village of Châteauneuf-du-Pape in the southern Rhône wine region of France. It is one of the most famous wines of France, produced predominantly as red wine from Grenache, Syrah, and Mourvèdre grapes.

History

The name means "the Pope's new castle," referring to the summer residence built for the Avignon papacy in the 14th century under Pope John XXII. In 1936 Châteauneuf-du-Pape became one of the first wines granted AOC status; its rules, drafted under Baron Pierre Le Roy of Château Fortia, became a model for the French appellation system.

Wines

Thirteen grape varieties are permitted in the appellation. The red wines are typically full-bodied and high in alcohol, while a small amount of white Châteauneuf-du-Pape is also produced. The vineyards are famous for their galets roulés, large rounded stones that retain heat and aid ripening.
