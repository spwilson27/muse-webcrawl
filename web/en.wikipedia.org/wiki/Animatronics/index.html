Animatronics

[Image: An Audio-Animatronics figure performing at a theme park attraction]

Animatronics is the use of mechatronic puppets and figures — combining mechanical, electronic, hydraulic or pneumatic actuation — to imitate life. Animatronic figures are used in film special effects, theme parks, museums and entertainment restaurants.

History

The field has roots in clockwork automata of earlier centuries. Walt Disney's Imagineering coined "Audio-Animatronics" for the singing birds of the Enchanted Tiki Room (1963) and figures such as Abraham Lincoln at the 1964 New York World's Fair. In cinema, Stan Winston Studio's full-size Tyrannosaurus for Jurassic Park (1993) showed animatronics working alongside computer graphics. Chuck E. Cheese and ShowBiz Pizza chains popularised animatronic stage bands in the 1970s–80s.

Technology

Figures are built around articulated armatures driven by servo motors or cylinders, synchronised to audio and show control systems, and covered in moulded skins of silicone or latex.
