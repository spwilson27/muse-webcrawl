Roman Agora

The Roman Agora is located to the north of the Acropolis and to the east of the Ancient Agora of Athens, in the modern Plaka district. It functioned as a market and civic centre during the Roman period, when the site of the older Greek agora had become occupied by monuments and public buildings.

History

The Roman Agora was constructed between 19 and 11 BC with donations from Julius Caesar and Augustus. Built on a rectangular plan measuring about 98 by 106 metres, it was surrounded by stoas housing shops, with an Ionic propylon known as the Gate of Athena Archegetis, dedicated in 11 BC, forming its western entrance. A large fountain house, an odeon, and public latrines were among its facilities.

Monuments

The best-known monument of the area is the Tower of the Winds (Horologion of Andronicus of Cyrrhus), an octagonal marble tower that served as a sundial, water clock, and weather vane. Other remains include the Fethiye Mosque, built in the early Ottoman period, and the Agoranomion, a building associated with the market officials (agoranomoi).
