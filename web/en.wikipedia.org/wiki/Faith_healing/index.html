Faith healing

The laying on of hands, a common faith-healing practice

Faith healing is the practice of prayer and gestures (such as the laying on of hands) believed by some to elicit divine intervention in spiritual and physical healing, especially as practised in certain Christian traditions, including Pentecostalism, charismatic movements and Christian Science. Believers attribute cures to the power of God acting through prayer, anointing with oil, pilgrimages to shrines such as Lourdes, or the ministry of a faith healer.

History

Healing through prayer is described in the New Testament, where Jesus and the apostles are depicted curing illness, and the Epistle of James commends prayer and anointing for the sick. Faith healing became a prominent movement in the late 19th and 20th centuries with evangelists such as John Alexander Dowie, Aimee Semple McPherson, Oral Roberts and Kathryn Kuhlman, and large "healing crusades" and televangelism.

Scientific assessment and criticism

Scientific reviews have found no reliable evidence that faith healing cures disease beyond placebo effects, spontaneous remission and misdiagnosis; celebrated stage healers have been investigated for fraud, including the use of concealed information about audience members. Medical and legal concern is greatest where faith healing replaces effective treatment — especially for children — and a number of deaths have led to prosecutions and to changes in laws that had exempted faith-based treatment from child-neglect statutes in some US states.

Supporters respond that prayer complements rather than replaces medicine and point to personal testimonies of recovery; studies of intercessory prayer, including a large 2006 trial on cardiac bypass patients, found no effect on outcomes.
