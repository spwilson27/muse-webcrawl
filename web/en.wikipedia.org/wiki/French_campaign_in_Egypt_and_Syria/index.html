French campaign in Egypt and Syria

The French campaign in Egypt and Syria (1798–1801) was a Napoleonic campaign in the Ottoman territories of Egypt and Palestine, executed by the French First Republic against Great Britain and the Ottoman Empire during the War of the Second Coalition. Commanded initially by Napoleon Bonaparte, the expedition aimed to establish a French presence in the Middle East, threaten British India, and outflank Britain.

Course

The French seized Malta and Alexandria and defeated the Mamluks at the Battle of the Pyramids (July 1798), but the destruction of the French fleet by Nelson at the Battle of the Nile left the army stranded. Napoleon advanced into Syria in 1799 but failed to take Acre and returned to Egypt; he departed for France in August 1799. His successor Kléber was assassinated in 1800, and the remaining French forces capitulated to the British at Alexandria in 1801.

Scientific legacy

The expedition's large corps of scholars (the savants) produced the Description de l'Égypte and discovered the Rosetta Stone, founding modern Egyptology — a legacy that long outlived the military failure.
