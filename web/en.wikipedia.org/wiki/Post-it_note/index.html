Post-it note

Manufacturer: 3M
Inventors: Spencer Silver and Art Fry
Introduced: 1980 (as Post-it Note)
Standard colour: Canary yellow

A Post-it note (or sticky note) is a small piece of paper with a strip of re-adherable adhesive on its back, designed for temporarily attaching notes to documents and surfaces. The product is made by 3M and sold in many sizes, shapes and colours, most famously the 3-by-3-inch canary yellow square.

Invention

In 1968, 3M scientist Spencer Silver developed a weak, pressure-sensitive adhesive while trying to create a super-strong one. In 1974 his colleague Art Fry, frustrated that bookmarks kept falling out of his choir hymnal, realised the weak adhesive was ideal for a reusable bookmark that could stick without damaging pages. After an initially failed launch as "Press 'N Peel" in 1977, 3M gave away free samples in Boise, Idaho — the "Boise Blitz" — and relaunched the product successfully in 1980.

Use and culture

Post-it notes became a ubiquitous office supply and a tool for brainstorming, project planning and design methods such as affinity diagramming. They have also been used for public art installations and large-scale pixel-style murals on office windows. The adhesive's microsphere structure was patented; after patent expiry, many competitors sell generic sticky notes.
