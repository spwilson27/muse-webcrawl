The Old Devils is a novel by British author Kingsley Amis, published in 1986. It won the Booker Prize that year and is considered one of Amis's finest works.

Plot

The novel is set in South Wales and centres on a group of elderly friends — retired professionals and their wives — whose comfortable routines of drinking, gossip, and reminiscence are disturbed by the return of Alun and Rhiannon Weaver. Alun, a former member of the circle, has become a minor celebrity as a professional Welshman and media personality in London. His return stirs old affections, rivalries, and regrets, particularly between Rhiannon and Malcolm Cellan-Jones, who loved her in their youth. The "old devils" of the title are the compulsions of age: drink, lust, resentment, and the fear of death, which the characters confront with a mixture of comedy and melancholy.

Reception

The novel was praised for its compassionate portrait of old age, its comic observation, and its treatment of Welsh identity and the effects of devolution-era cultural politics. It was adapted for television by the BBC in 1992.
