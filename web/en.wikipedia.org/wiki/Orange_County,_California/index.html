Orange County (officially the County of Orange) is a county located in the Los Angeles metropolitan area in Southern California. As of the 2020 census, the population was 3,186,989, making it the third-most-populous county in California, the sixth-most-populous in the United States, and more populous than 19 American states. Its county seat is Santa Ana, and its largest city by population is Anaheim.

History

The county was formed in 1889 from the southern portion of Los Angeles County, after a campaign by local boosters; it was named for the orange groves that then covered much of the region. Agriculture dominated until the postwar era, when freeway construction, defense and aerospace industry, and master-planned communities such as Irvine transformed it into a largely suburban county. In 1994 the county filed what was then the largest municipal bankruptcy in U.S. history, following losses in a leveraged investment pool.

Geography

Orange County is bordered by Los Angeles County to the north, San Bernardino and Riverside counties to the east and northeast, San Diego County to the south, and the Pacific Ocean to the west. The Santa Ana River is its principal waterway. The county includes beach cities such as Huntington Beach ("Surf City USA"), Newport Beach, and Laguna Beach, and the Santa Ana Mountains in its eastern reaches.

Economy and attractions

The county is home to Disneyland Resort in Anaheim, Knott's Berry Farm in Buena Park, and major shopping destinations such as South Coast Plaza. Irvine is a hub for technology and education, hosting the University of California, Irvine. Tourism, technology, healthcare, and finance are major sectors.

Politics

Long known as a Republican stronghold, Orange County has become politically competitive in recent decades, voting for Democratic presidential candidates in 2016 and thereafter and electing members of both parties to Congress.
