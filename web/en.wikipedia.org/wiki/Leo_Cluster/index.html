Leo Cluster

Leo Cluster (Abell 1367)
Constellation: Leo
Distance: approx. 330 million light-years
Brightest member: NGC 3842

The Leo Cluster (Abell 1367) is a galaxy cluster in the constellation of Leo, at a distance of approximately 330 million light-years (about 100 megaparsecs). Together with the Coma Cluster (Abell 1656) it is one of the two major clusters making up the Coma Supercluster.

Properties

Unlike the dense, regular Coma Cluster, the Leo Cluster is irregular and relatively loose, containing a high proportion of spiral galaxies — an indication of an overall younger dynamical age and ongoing assembly. Its brightest galaxy is the elliptical NGC 3842, and other prominent members include NGC 3862, NGC 3837 and the starburst galaxy NGC 3862's companions. At least two subgroups within the cluster appear to be merging.

Observation

The cluster spans several degrees of sky near the border of Leo and Coma Berenices and is a popular target for deep-sky imaging. X-ray observations show hot intracluster gas, though less luminous than in richer clusters.
