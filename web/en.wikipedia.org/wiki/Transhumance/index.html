Transhumance

Transhumance is a type of pastoralism or nomadism, a seasonal movement of livestock between fixed summer and winter pastures. In montane regions (vertical transhumance), flocks ascend to high mountain pastures in summer and return to sheltered lowland valleys in winter; in the Mediterranean and other lowland regions (horizontal transhumance), herds move over long distances between complementary grazing grounds. Unlike full nomadism, transhumant communities maintain a permanent home base where part of the population remains.

Practice and history

The practice is ancient and worldwide: Alpine dairy farming in Austria, Switzerland and France; the drove roads (cañadas) of Spain, regulated by the medieval Mesta guild; the tratturi of Italy; sheep runs in the British uplands; and movements of herders in the Balkans, Anatolia, Iran, the Sahel and the Andes. Journeys follow customary routes with rights of way, overnight resting places and markets, and were historically major conduits of trade, news and culture.

Modern status

Motor transport, fencing, land enclosure and changing rural economies have reduced transhumance, though it persists in many regions and is increasingly valued for maintaining semi-natural grassland habitats, preventing wildfire fuel build-up and producing prized cheeses such as those of the Alpine Alpage. In 2019 and 2023, UNESCO inscribed transhumance on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity for a growing group of European countries.
