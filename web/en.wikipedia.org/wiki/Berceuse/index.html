Berceuse

A berceuse is a musical cradle song, usually in a rocking 6/8 metre.

A berceuse (from French bercer, "to rock" or "to cradle") is a musical composition, usually for solo piano, in the character of a lullaby or cradle song. It is typically a gentle, rocking piece in 6/8 time with a repetitive, soothing accompaniment pattern suggesting the motion of a cradle, over which a tender melody unfolds. Although derived from a song form, most famous berceuses are instrumental character pieces.

Characteristics

The berceuse is defined by mood rather than strict form: a soft dynamic, a regular oscillating bass, and a melody of simple, songlike character. Like the nocturne and the barcarolle, it became a vehicle in the 19th century for refined piano writing that could range from innocent simplicity to considerable sophistication beneath a calm surface.

Notable examples

  • Frédéric Chopin, Berceuse in D-flat major, Op. 57 (1843–44), perhaps the most famous example: a serene melody over an unchanging tonic–dominant ostinato, elaborated in ever more ornate variations.
  • Gabriel Fauré wrote berceuses throughout his career, including the first piece of the Dolly suite for piano duet, Op. 56.
  • Johannes Brahms is best remembered for his vocal lullaby "Guten Abend, gute Nacht" (Wiegenlied, Op. 49 No. 4), one of the world's most famous cradle songs.
  • Ferruccio Busoni, Berceuse élégiaque (1909), a somber orchestral reworking of a piano piece from his Elegien, written in memory of his mother.
  • Igor Stravinsky included a berceuse for the Firebird in his 1910 ballet The Firebird, where the hero lulls the monster Kashchei's guard to sleep.
  • George Gershwin composed a berceuse as the lullaby movement of his String Quartet (1919).
