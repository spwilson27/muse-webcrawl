Eshu

Eshu (Yoruba: Èṣù), also known as Elegba or Elegua, is an orisha of the Yoruba religion of West Africa. He is the messenger of the gods, the guardian of crossroads, and a trickster figure who mediates between the human and divine realms. Eshu carries prayers and sacrifices to the other orishas and enforces the laws of the cosmos; no ritual can succeed without first acknowledging him.

Role and character

Eshu is the divine intermediary: sacrifices offered to any orisha must pass through him, and he is customarily given the first offering in ceremonies. He governs crossroads, doorways, and markets — places of transition and choice — and is associated with chance, uncertainty, and the disruption of order. His trickster aspect teaches through mischief: stories describe him walking between two friends wearing a hat coloured differently on each side, provoking a quarrel over its colour to demonstrate the relativity of perception.

Eshu is linked to the Ifá divination system, in which he is both the witness of destiny and the agent who can disturb it. His colours are red and black, his number is three, and his emblems include the hooked staff (ogò) and laterite stones or carved wooden figures with elongated, double-faced heads representing his ability to see in all directions.

In the African diaspora

Through the transatlantic slave trade, devotion to Eshu travelled to the Americas. In Afro-Cuban Santería he is syncretised with the Holy Child of Atocha and Saint Anthony of Padua and venerated as Eleguá, guardian of roads who must be honoured first in ritual. In Brazilian Candomblé he is Exu, and in Haitian Vodou related crossroads functions are associated with Papa Legba. Early Christian missionaries sometimes mistranslated Eshu as the Devil; Yoruba practitioners reject this identification, noting that Eshu serves the supreme being Olodumare and enforces — rather than opposes — cosmic order.
