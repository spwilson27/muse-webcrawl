RER B

The RER B is one of five lines in the Réseau Express Régional (RER), the hybrid commuter and rapid transit system serving Paris and the Île-de-France region. It runs from north to south, connecting Charles de Gaulle Airport and Mitry-Claye in the northeast with Robinson and Saint-Rémy-lès-Chevreuse in the south. It is operated jointly by RATP and SNCF, with a change of driver traditionally at Gare du Nord.

History

The southern part of the line originated as the Ligne de Sceaux, opened in the 19th century and modernized and extended underground by the Paris Métro operator to Luxembourg and later Châtelet–Les Halles. The full RER B was inaugurated in 1977; the northern extension to Charles de Gaulle Airport and the interconnection with SNCF lines at Gare du Nord were completed in the early 1980s, creating through services between the two operators despite differences in electrification (1.5 kV DC in the south, 25 kV AC in the north), which dual-voltage MI 79 and MI 84 trains handle.

Usage

RER B is the second-busiest RER line, serving roughly 900,000–1,000,000 passengers on busy days. It links both Paris airports (Orly via the Orlyval connection at Antony, and Charles de Gaulle directly), the Stade de France at Saint-Denis, the Latin Quarter, and university and research centres in the southern suburbs. Reliability and capacity on the shared tunnel section with RER D between Châtelet–Les Halles and Gare du Nord have been longstanding concerns.
