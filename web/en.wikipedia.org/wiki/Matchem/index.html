Matchem

Matchem (1748 – 21 February 1781), sometimes styled Match 'em, was a British Thoroughbred racehorse and a hugely influential sire. He was one of only three 18th-century stallions — with Herod and Eclipse — from whose male line all modern Thoroughbred racehorses descend.

Background and racing career

Matchem was a bay stallion bred by John Holmes of Snaith, Yorkshire, and sold to William Fenwick of Bywell, Northumberland. He was sired by Cade, a son of the Godolphin Arabian, one of the three foundation sires of the Thoroughbred breed.

Matchem did not race until the age of five — unhurried by modern standards — but then proved a formidable staying racehorse, winning races at Newmarket, York, Lincoln and elsewhere, often over long distances in heat racing. Among his victories were the King's Plate races and high-value sweepstakes. His career winnings amounted to a substantial sum for the era, and he was regarded as one of the finest racehorses of his generation.

Stud career

Matchem retired to stud in 1758 and stood at Bywell and later at Oxcroft, near Newmarket. He was the leading sire in Great Britain and Ireland in 1772, 1773, and 1774. His most important offspring included the stallions Conductor and Alfred, and his descendants continued the Godolphin Arabian sire line when the direct line of his grandsire faded.

Legacy

Genetic studies of Thoroughbreds show that nearly all modern racehorses trace their Y-chromosome to one of three foundation stallions' lines: Matchem (representing the Godolphin Arabian), Herod (the Byerley Turk line), and Eclipse (the Darley Arabian line) — of which the Darley Arabian/Eclipse line accounts for the overwhelming majority today. Notably, Matchem is also the only one of the three whose sire line passes through racing descendants in an unbroken line to a horse of his own era with documented pedigree through Cade. Matchem died on 21 February 1781, aged 33, an exceptional age for the time. The Matchem Stakes, a race for two-year-olds at Newmarket, is named in his honour.
