Early-warning radar
Purpose: Long-range detection of aircraft and missiles

An early-warning radar is a radar system used primarily for the long-range detection of approaching aircraft, and later ballistic missiles, so that defences and command authorities can be alerted as early as possible.

History

The first operational network was Britain's Chain Home, built before and during the Second World War to detect German bombers. During the Cold War the United States and Soviet Union built vast early-warning chains, including the Distant Early Warning (DEW) Line across the Canadian Arctic and the Ballistic Missile Early Warning System (BMEWS), designed to give warning of nuclear attack.

Technology

Early-warning radars operate at relatively low frequencies for maximum range, using large fixed antennas or phased arrays. Over-the-horizon radars bounce signals off the ionosphere to detect targets thousands of kilometres away. Missile warning is now supplemented by space-based infrared satellites.
