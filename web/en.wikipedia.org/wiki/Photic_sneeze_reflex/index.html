Photic sneeze reflex

Synonym: Autosomal dominant compelling helio-ophthalmic outburst (ACHOO) syndrome
Trigger: Sudden exposure to bright light
Inheritance: Autosomal dominant

The photic sneeze reflex causes sneezing on sudden exposure to bright light.

The photic sneeze reflex, also jocularly called ACHOO syndrome (autosomal dominant compelling helio-ophthalmic outburst), is a genetic trait that causes people to sneeze when suddenly exposed to bright light, such as stepping out of a building into sunlight. Estimates suggest it affects somewhere between roughly one in ten and one in three people worldwide.

Mechanism

The reflex is thought to arise from cross-activation between the optic and trigeminal pathways: the intense signal from the pupillary light reflex, carried near the trigeminal nerve that controls the sneeze response to nasal irritation, is misread by the brain as nasal stimulation, triggering a sneeze. Sneezes typically occur only on the initial transition to bright light, not during continued exposure.

Inheritance and significance

The trait is inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern, sometimes with several affected generations in a family. It is generally harmless, though it has been studied in connection with pilots and drivers momentarily blinded by sneezing in bright conditions, and it is sometimes exploited deliberately by people who feel a sneeze "stuck" and look toward a light to trigger it. Aristotle already wondered about sneezing at the sun in his Problems.
