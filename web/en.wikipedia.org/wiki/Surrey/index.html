Surrey is a ceremonial county in South East England, bordering Greater London to the north-east. It is one of the home counties and among the most affluent areas of the United Kingdom. The administrative centre is Reigate, while Guildford is the historic county town.

Geography

The North Downs, a chalk ridge, run east–west across the county, and the Surrey Hills are designated an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty. Leith Hill (294 m) is the highest point in South East England. The River Thames forms the northern boundary, and the River Wey flows through Guildford and Weybridge. Despite its proximity to London, Surrey is the most wooded county in England, with woodland covering about a quarter of its area.

History

Surrey's name derives from the Old English Sūþrīge, meaning "southern district" of the Middle Saxons. The county saw the sealing of Magna Carta at Runnymede in 1215. In the 19th and 20th centuries, the expansion of the railways turned Surrey into commuter country for London, and much of it is protected by the Metropolitan Green Belt.

Settlements and economy

Major towns include Guildford, Woking, Epsom, Redhill, and Staines-upon-Thames. The county hosts the University of Surrey in Guildford, Royal Holloway (in Egham, just across the Berkshire border historically), Epsom Downs Racecourse — home of the Derby — and the Brooklands motor racing circuit at Weybridge, the world's first purpose-built racing track.
