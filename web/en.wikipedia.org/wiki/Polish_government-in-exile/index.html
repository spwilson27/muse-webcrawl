Polish government-in-exile

General Władysław Sikorski, prime minister of the government-in-exile, 1939–1943.

The Polish government-in-exile, formally the Government of the Republic of Poland in Exile, was the government of Poland formed after the invasion of Poland in September 1939 and the occupation of Poland by Nazi Germany, the Soviet Union and the Slovak Republic, which brought to an end the Second Polish Republic.

Formation and wartime role

After the fall of Poland, the government re-formed first in Paris and then, after the fall of France, in London. Led initially by President Władysław Raczkiewicz and Prime Minister (and commander-in-chief) General Władysław Sikorski, it directed the Polish Armed Forces in the West and maintained diplomatic relations with the Allied governments. It also maintained links with the Polish Underground State in occupied Poland, including the Home Army (Armia Krajowa).

Relations with the Soviet Union, restored by the Sikorski–Mayski agreement of 1941, broke down in 1943 after the government demanded an investigation of the Katyn massacre. Sikorski died in an air crash at Gibraltar in July 1943 and was succeeded by Stanisław Mikołajczyk.

Loss of recognition

In 1945, following the Yalta Conference, the Western Allies withdrew recognition from the London government in favour of the Soviet-backed Provisional Government of National Unity in Warsaw. The government-in-exile nevertheless continued to operate from London for decades, refusing to recognise the communist government in Poland and maintaining the constitutional continuity of the Republic.

Dissolution

After the fall of communism, the last president-in-exile, Ryszard Kaczorowski, handed the insignia of the presidency to the democratically elected President Lech Wałęsa in December 1990, and the government-in-exile dissolved itself, recognising the Third Polish Republic as the continuation of the Polish state.
