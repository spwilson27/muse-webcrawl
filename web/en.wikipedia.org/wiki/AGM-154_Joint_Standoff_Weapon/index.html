AGM-154 Joint Standoff Weapon

AGM-154 JSOW
Type: Glide bomb / standoff air-to-surface missile
Manufacturer: Raytheon
In service: 1999–present
Guidance: GPS/INS; imaging infrared (JSOW-C)

The AGM-154 Joint Standoff Weapon (JSOW) is a glide bomb family developed by the United States Navy and Air Force and produced by Raytheon. It allows strike aircraft to attack defended targets from outside the range of standard anti-aircraft defenses.

Design

After release, the JSOW deploys folding wings and glides to its target, guided by GPS and inertial navigation. Depending on release altitude, its range exceeds 100 kilometres in some profiles. The AGM-154A carries combined-effects bomblets, the AGM-154B carried anti-armour submunitions, and the AGM-154C uses a unitary warhead with an imaging infrared seeker for precision attack against specific targets.

Use

The weapon has been used in combat since Operation Southern Watch in 1999, and in operations over Iraq and Afghanistan, and has been exported to several allied air forces.
