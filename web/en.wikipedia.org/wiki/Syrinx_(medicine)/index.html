Syrinx (medicine)

In medicine, a syrinx is a rare, fluid-filled neuroglial cavity within the spinal cord (syringomyelia), the brain stem (syringobulbia), or the brain. Syringes are typically elongated, tubular cavities that expand slowly over years, damaging the central cord from within.

Causes

A syrinx most often develops in association with a Chiari malformation, in which cerebellar tissue protrudes into the spinal canal and disturbs cerebrospinal fluid flow. Other causes include spinal cord tumours, trauma, haemorrhage, and arachnoiditis. When the cavity is simply a dilated central canal lined by ependymal cells, it is sometimes called hydromyelia.

Symptoms and treatment

Expanding cavities interrupt the decussating pain and temperature fibres of the spinothalamic tract, producing a characteristic "cape-like" loss of pain and temperature sensation over the shoulders and arms with preserved touch. Weakness and muscle wasting may follow. Treatment is directed at the underlying cause — for example, posterior fossa decompression for Chiari malformation — and may include shunting of the cavity.
