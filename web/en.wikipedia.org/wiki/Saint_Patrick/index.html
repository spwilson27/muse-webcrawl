Saint Patrick

Saint Patrick (Latin: Patricius; Irish: Pádraig; c. 385–461) was a 5th-century Romano-British Christian missionary and bishop, the primary patron saint of Ireland, credited with bringing Christianity to the island. His feast day, 17 March, is celebrated worldwide as Saint Patrick's Day.

Bornc. 385, Roman Britain
Died17 March 461 (trad.), Saul, Ireland
Feast17 March
PatronageIreland

Life

According to his Confessio, Patrick was captured by Irish raiders at 16 and spent six years as a shepherd slave before escaping, becoming a priest, and returning as a missionary. Legends credit him with using the shamrock to explain the Trinity and banishing snakes from Ireland (post-glacial Ireland had none). See also Patrick Pearse, named for him.

Veneration

Associated places include Croagh Patrick, Armagh and Downpatrick, his traditional burial place.
