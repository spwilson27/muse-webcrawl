Horse mackerel


Atlantic horse mackerel, Trachurus trachurus

Horse mackerel is a common name for several species of fish in the jack family, Carangidae, especially those of the genus Trachurus. The name is also applied in Japan to the Japanese jack mackerel (Trachurus japonicus), known as aji, and in some regions to members of the scad genera. The best-known species is the Atlantic horse mackerel, Trachurus trachurus, found throughout the eastern Atlantic and the Mediterranean.

Description

Horse mackerels are silvery, laterally compressed, schooling fish with a distinctive row of bony scutes along the lateral line. Atlantic horse mackerel commonly reach 30–50 cm in length. They are pelagic predators feeding on crustaceans, squid, and smaller fish, and they form large shoals that migrate seasonally, moving inshore and northward in warmer months.

Fisheries

Horse mackerels are commercially important across their range. They are caught mainly by trawlers and purse seiners and sold fresh, frozen, smoked, canned, or processed into fish meal and surimi-type products. Stocks in the Northeast Atlantic and off southern Africa have been subject to quota management, and the Chilean jack mackerel (Trachurus murphyi) fishery in the South Pacific is one of the classic cases of stock collapse and slow recovery under international management.
