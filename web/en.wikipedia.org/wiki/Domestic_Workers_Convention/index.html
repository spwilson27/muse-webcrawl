The Domestic Workers Convention, formally the Convention concerning Decent Work for Domestic Workers (ILO Convention No. 189), is an International Labour Organization treaty adopted on 16 June 2011 at the 100th session of the International Labour Conference in Geneva. It is accompanied by Recommendation No. 201. The convention establishes the first global labour standards for domestic workers — cleaners, cooks, nannies, carers, gardeners and others employed in or for private households.

Content

The convention requires ratifying states to ensure domestic workers enjoy effective protection against abuse, harassment and violence; fair terms of employment; a minimum wage where one exists; reasonable working hours with at least 24 consecutive hours of weekly rest; and social security protection, including maternity benefits, not less favourable than those of workers generally. It also sets a minimum age for domestic work, addresses the particular situation of migrant domestic workers and live-in workers, and regulates private employment agencies that recruit them.

Ratification

The convention entered into force on 5 September 2013, twelve months after the second ratification (the Philippines followed Uruguay as early ratifiers). Since then it has been ratified by more than thirty countries in Europe, Latin America, Africa and Asia. Campaigns by the International Domestic Workers Federation and national unions continue to press for wider ratification and implementation, given that the ILO estimates tens of millions of domestic workers worldwide, the large majority of them women.
