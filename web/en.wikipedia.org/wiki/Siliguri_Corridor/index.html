Siliguri Corridor

The narrow Siliguri Corridor — the "Chicken's Neck" — links Northeast India to the rest of the country.

LocationWest Bengal, India

BordersNepal, Bangladesh, Bhutan

The Siliguri Corridor, popularly called the "Chicken's Neck", is a narrow strip of land in the Indian state of West Bengal that connects the northeastern states of India to the rest of the country. At its narrowest it is only about 20–22 kilometres wide, squeezed between Nepal to the northwest, Bangladesh to the south, and Bhutan and the Chinese border (at the Chumbi Valley) to the north. All land transport between mainland India and the eight northeastern states — by road, rail and pipeline — passes through this corridor, making it one of India's most strategically sensitive areas.

Origins

The corridor is a product of the Partition of 1947: when Bengal was divided and East Pakistan (now Bangladesh) created, the northeast was left attached to India only by this slender neck, named after the city of Siliguri at its centre. The corridor was further shaped by the accession of Sikkim to India in 1975.

Strategic significance

Because a severing of the corridor would cut off some 45 million people in the northeast, India maintains a heavy military and infrastructure presence in the area, and the corridor features prominently in strategic analyses of a potential India–China conflict: the Chinese-controlled Chumbi Valley points toward its northern edge, and the 2017 Doklam standoff occurred nearby. India has sought to reduce its dependence on the corridor by developing alternative routes, including transit through Bangladesh and the Kaladan Multi-Modal Transit Transport Project via Myanmar's Sittwe port.

The corridor today

Siliguri is a major commercial and logistics hub for the northeast, the Darjeeling hills and neighbouring countries. National highways and the Northeast Frontier Railway funnel through the corridor, alongside oil, gas and data links.
