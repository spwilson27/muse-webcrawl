Scalloway

Country: Scotland
Islands: Shetland
Population: approx. 1,200

Scalloway harbour and castle.

Scalloway (Old Norse: Skálavágr) is a village and small port on the west coast of the Shetland Mainland, Scotland. It was the ancient capital of Shetland before the seat of administration moved to Lerwick in 1708, and remains an important harbour for fishing and the North Sea oil industry support services, as well as a campus of the University of the Highlands and Islands.

History

Scalloway's sheltered bay made it a natural early settlement; its name, from Old Norse, means "bay of the large houses". Scalloway Castle was built in 1600 by Patrick Stewart, 2nd Earl of Orkney, whose oppressive rule ended in his execution in 1615. The village grew as a fishing port, trading fish for goods with Hanseatic merchants.

During the Second World War, Scalloway was the home base of the "Shetland Bus", the clandestine boat operation that carried agents and refugees between Shetland and German-occupied Norway. A memorial and the Scalloway Museum commemorate the operation and the village's wider history.
