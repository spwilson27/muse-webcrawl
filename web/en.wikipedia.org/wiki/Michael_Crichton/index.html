John Michael Crichton (October 23, 1942 – November 4, 2008) was an American author, screenwriter and filmmaker. Trained as a physician at Harvard Medical School, he became one of the best-selling novelists of the late 20th century, known for techno-thrillers that grounded speculative science in meticulous research. His books have sold more than 200 million copies worldwide.

Career

Crichton first attracted wide attention with The Andromeda Strain (1969), about scientists racing to contain an extraterrestrial microorganism. Novels such as The Terminal Man (1972), The Great Train Robbery (1975), Eaters of the Dead (1976), Congo (1980) and Sphere (1987) established his reputation. Jurassic Park (1990), in which dinosaurs are recreated by genetic engineering and chaos theory undermines the park built to contain them, became a phenomenon when Steven Spielberg's film adaptation appeared in 1993. Later bestsellers included Rising Sun (1992), Disclosure (1994), The Lost World (1995), Timeline (1999), Prey (2002) and State of Fear (2004).

Film and television

Crichton wrote and directed films including Westworld (1973) and Coma (1978), and created the long-running television medical drama ER (1994–2009), based on his experiences as a medical student. Many of his novels were adapted into successful films.

Views and legacy

A popular lecturer on science and public policy, Crichton testified before the US Senate on climate change and criticised what he called politicised science; his sceptical novel State of Fear drew both praise and criticism. He died of lymphoma in 2008. Several manuscripts, including Pirate Latitudes (2009) and Micro (2011), were published posthumously.
