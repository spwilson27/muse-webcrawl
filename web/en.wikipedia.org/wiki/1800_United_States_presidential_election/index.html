1800 United States presidential election

The 1800 United States presidential election was the fourth quadrennial presidential election. It was held from Friday, October 31 to Wednesday, December 3, 1800. In what is sometimes called the "Revolution of 1800", the Democratic-Republican Party candidate, Vice President Thomas Jefferson, defeated the incumbent President, John Adams of the Federalist Party. The election was a political realignment that ushered in a generation of Democratic-Republican leadership, but a tie in the Electoral College between Jefferson and his running mate Aaron Burr produced a constitutional crisis resolved only by the House of Representatives.

Background

The campaign was among the most bitter in American history. Federalists attacked Jefferson as an atheist and radical, while Democratic-Republicans attacked Adams as a monarchist and condemned the Alien and Sedition Acts. Because electors in 1800 did not cast separate ballots for president and vice president, every Democratic-Republican elector voted for both Jefferson and Burr, producing a 73–73 tie between them, ahead of Adams's 65 votes.

Contingent election

Under the Constitution, the tie threw the choice to the outgoing, Federalist-controlled House of Representatives, voting by state delegation. Many Federalists preferred Burr to Jefferson and deadlocked the House through 35 ballots in February 1801. On the 36th ballot, enough Federalist representatives abstained — influenced in part by Alexander Hamilton's lobbying against Burr — to give Jefferson the majority of state delegations. Jefferson was inaugurated on March 4, 1801, marking the first peaceful transfer of power between rival parties in the young republic.

Aftermath

The crisis exposed the flaw in the original electoral procedure and led directly to the Twelfth Amendment, ratified in 1804, which required separate electoral ballots for president and vice president.
