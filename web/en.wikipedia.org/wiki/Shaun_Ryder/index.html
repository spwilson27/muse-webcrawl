Shaun Ryder

Shaun William George Ryder (born 23 August 1962) is an English singer, songwriter and poet. He is the lead singer of the Happy Mondays and was a central figure of the Madchester scene of the late 1980s and early 1990s, which fused indie rock with rave culture in Manchester.

Career

Ryder formed Happy Mondays in Salford in 1980 with his brother Paul Ryder on bass, Mark "Bez" Berry as dancer and percussionist, Mark Day, Paul Davis and Gary Whelan. Produced by Martin Hannett and later Paul Oakenfold and Steve Osborne, the band's albums Bummed (1988) and Pills 'n' Thrills and Bellyaches (1990) — the latter including the hit singles "Step On" and "Kinky Afro" — made them emblematic of the era centred on the Haçienda nightclub and Factory Records.

The band's excesses, including Ryder's well-documented drug addiction, contributed to Factory's collapse and the group's split in 1993. Ryder then formed Black Grape with Bez and others, scoring a number one album with It's Great When You're Straight... Yeah (1995). He has since reunited Happy Mondays several times, appeared as a contestant on I'm a Celebrity... Get Me Out of Here! in 2010, finishing as runner-up, and become a familiar television personality. His memoir Twisting My Melon was published in 2011.
