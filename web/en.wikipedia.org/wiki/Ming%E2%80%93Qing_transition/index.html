Ming–Qing transition

The Ming–Qing transition, also known as the Manchu conquest of China, was the period of conflict between the declining Ming dynasty and the rising Qing dynasty of the Manchus, spanning roughly from 1618 to 1683, during which Qing replaced Ming as the ruling dynasty of China.

Course

Nurhaci unified the Jurchen tribes and founded the Later Jin state, renamed Qing by his son Hong Taiji. Ming, weakened by fiscal crisis, famine, epidemics and the rebellions of Li Zicheng and Zhang Xianzhong, lost Beijing in 1644 — first to Li's Shun forces, then to the Qing, admitted through Shanhaiguan by the Ming general Wu Sangui. Southern Ming loyalist regimes resisted for decades; the last major resistance, Koxinga's kingdom of Tungning on Taiwan, fell in 1683.

Consequences

The wars and accompanying disasters caused catastrophic population loss — estimates of deaths run into the tens of millions. The Qing consolidated rule over China proper and later expanded into Mongolia, Tibet, Xinjiang and Taiwan.
