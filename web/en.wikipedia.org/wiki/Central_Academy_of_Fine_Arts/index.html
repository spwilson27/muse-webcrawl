The Central Academy of Fine Arts (CAFA; Chinese: 中央美术学院) is the leading art academy in China and the only institution of higher education in fine arts directly under the administration of the Chinese Ministry of Education. Based in Beijing, it is widely regarded as the most prestigious and selective art school in the country, with an admission rate famously lower than that of China's top comprehensive universities.

History

The academy traces its origins to the National School of Fine Arts founded in Beijing in 1918, the first national school of fine arts in modern China. Xu Beihong, a pioneer of modern Chinese painting who blended Chinese ink techniques with Western realism, became its president and, in 1950, the first president of the newly named Central Academy of Fine Arts. The academy moved to its current campus in Wangjing, Chaoyang District, in 2001; its art museum, designed by Japanese architect Arata Isozaki, opened in 2008.

Academics

CAFA comprises schools devoted to Chinese painting, fine arts (oil painting, printmaking, sculpture, mural painting), design, architecture, humanities, and experimental art, among others. Its faculty and alumni include many of the defining figures of 20th- and 21st-century Chinese art, and the academy operates a major museum and the CAFA Art Museum in Beijing.
