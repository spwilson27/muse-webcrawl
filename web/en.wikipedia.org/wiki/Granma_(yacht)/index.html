Granma (yacht)

TypeYacht
Length13.25 m (43 ft 6 in)
Launched1943
FateMuseum ship, Havana

Granma is a 60-foot (18 m) yacht that was used to transport 82 fighters of the Cuban Revolution from Mexico to Cuba in November 1956. The expedition, led by Fidel Castro, aimed to overthrow the regime of Cuban dictator Fulgencio Batista. The yacht's name is said to come from the nickname of its former American owner ("Granma" being short for "grandma").

History

The diesel-powered yacht was reportedly built in 1943. Castro's movement purchased it in Tuxpan, Veracruz, Mexico, for the expedition to Cuba. On 25 November 1956, the Granma departed Tuxpan carrying 82 members of the 26th of July Movement, including Fidel Castro, his brother Raúl Castro, Che Guevara, and Camilo Cienfuegos. The vessel was designed for about 12 passengers and was severely overloaded.

After a rough seven-day crossing, the yacht ran aground in a mangrove swamp at Playa Las Coloradas, near Los Cayuelos in what is now Granma Province, on 2 December 1956 — two days later than planned, so a coordinated uprising in Santiago de Cuba was not synchronized with the landing. Most of the expeditionaries were killed or captured shortly afterwards, but a small core of survivors regrouped in the Sierra Maestra mountains and waged the guerrilla war that eventually triumphed in January 1959.

Legacy

The province where the yacht landed was renamed Granma Province. The official newspaper of the Communist Party of Cuba, founded in 1965, is named Granma. The yacht itself is preserved under glass at the Museum of the Revolution in Havana.
