Greek euro coins

Greek euro coins are a series of euro coins minted by Greece. Greece joined the eurozone in 2001, a year after meeting the convergence criteria, and the euro replaced the drachma in circulation on 1 January 2002. Each of the eight denominations has a distinct national design, chosen from Greek history and mythology, depicting famous figures and scenes of Greek culture. The coins were designed by Georgios Stamatopoulos, and the national side also includes the denomination written in Greek and the word ΕΛΛΗΝΙΚΗ ΔΗΜΟΚΡΑΤΙΑ ("Hellenic Republic").

Designs

The €2 coin depicts Europa being carried off by Zeus in the form of a bull, a scene taken from a mosaic in Sparta. The €1 coin reproduces the Athenian "owl" tetradrachm of the 5th century BC, with the owl of Athena. The 50 cent coin shows Eleftherios Venizelos; the 20 cent, Ioannis Kapodistrias; and the 10 cent, Rigas Feraios (Velestinlis). The small coins depict ships: the 5 cent an ancient Greek trireme, the 2 cent a corvette of the War of Independence era, and the 1 cent a modern tanker.

Circulation

Because Greece's minting capacity was initially limited, some 2002 Greek euro coins were struck in Finland, France and Spain; these bear small mint marks (S, F and E respectively) in one of the stars of the design. Greek coins are legal tender throughout the euro area. Greece has also issued numerous €2 commemorative coins, beginning with the coin for the 2004 Athens Olympic Games — the first commemorative €2 coin issued by any member state.
