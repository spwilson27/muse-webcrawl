Manis is a genus of pangolins, the scaly anteaters, within the family Manidae. It comprises the Asian pangolins, distributed across South, East and Southeast Asia. The name derives from the Latin manes, referring to spirits or ghosts, in allusion to the animals' secretive nocturnal habits.

Species

The genus traditionally includes four living species: the Chinese pangolin (Manis pentadactyla), the Indian pangolin (Manis crassicaudata), the Philippine pangolin (Manis culionensis) and the Sunda pangolin (Manis javanica). Some taxonomists have proposed splitting the genus, placing some species in other genera, but Manis remains widely used for the Asian forms; the African pangolins are placed in the genera Smutsia and Phataginus.

Characteristics

Like all pangolins, species of Manis are covered in large overlapping scales made of keratin, have long extensible tongues for feeding on ants and termites, and lack teeth. They are mostly nocturnal and solitary; several species are skilled climbers with prehensile tails, while others are primarily ground-dwelling diggers.

Conservation

All Manis species are threatened by poaching for their meat and for scales used in traditional medicine, as well as by habitat loss. The Chinese and Philippine pangolins are listed as Critically Endangered, and the Indian and Sunda pangolins as Endangered, on the IUCN Red List. All pangolins are listed in Appendix I of CITES, prohibiting international commercial trade.
