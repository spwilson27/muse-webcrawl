Procter & Gamble

The Procter & Gamble Company (P&G)
Founded: 1837, Cincinnati, Ohio
Founders: William Procter, James Gamble
Industry: Consumer goods
Brands: Tide, Pampers, Gillette, Crest, Ariel, Oral-B

The Procter & Gamble Company (P&G) is an American multinational consumer goods corporation headquartered in Cincinnati, Ohio, and one of the world's largest makers of household and personal care products.

History

Candlemaker William Procter and soapmaker James Gamble, immigrants from England and Ireland who had married sisters, formed their partnership in 1837. The company supplied soap and candles to the Union Army during the Civil War. In 1879 it launched Ivory soap, marketed as "99 and 44/100% pure." P&G pioneered brand management (Neil McElroy's 1931 memo), sponsored radio and television "soap operas" to advertise detergents, and introduced Tide (1946), Crest fluoride toothpaste (1955) and Pampers disposable diapers (1961).

Operations

P&G organises its portfolio in segments including fabric and home care, baby and family care, beauty, grooming (after acquiring Gillette in 2005 for about $57 billion), and health care. In the 2010s it divested many food and smaller brands to focus on its largest products. It sells in roughly 180 countries and employs over 100,000 people.
