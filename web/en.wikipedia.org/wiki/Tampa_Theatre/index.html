Tampa Theatre

Location711 N. Franklin Street, Tampa, Florida
OpenedOctober 15, 1926
ArchitectJohn Eberson
StyleMediterranean Revival movie palace

The Tampa Theatre is a historic movie palace in downtown Tampa, Florida, opened in 1926 and designed by noted theatre architect John Eberson. It is one of the best-preserved examples of his "atmospheric" theatres, whose auditorium recreates a Mediterranean courtyard under a ceiling of twinkling stars.

History

Built at a cost of about $800,000, the theatre opened with the silent film Ace of Cads accompanied by its Mighty Wurlitzer organ. By the 1970s the grand single-screen palace was threatened with demolition, but a community campaign and the City of Tampa saved it; it reopened in 1977 under the management of the Tampa Theatre Foundation and was listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

Present day

Today the Tampa Theatre operates as a non-profit film and cultural centre showing independent, foreign, and classic films as well as concerts and special events. A second screen, named for founding president John Bell, opened in 2024 in an adjacent restored storefront.
