Tropical wave

A tropical wave moving westward across the Atlantic

A tropical wave (also called an easterly wave, tropical easterly wave, or African easterly wave in the Atlantic basin) is a type of atmospheric trough, an elongated area of relatively low air pressure oriented north to south, which moves from east to west across the tropics.

Characteristics

Tropical waves typically form in the easterly trade-wind flow on the equatorward side of the subtropical ridge. Areas of disturbed weather, showers and thunderstorms often develop along and behind the wave axis, and the wave modulates rainfall as it passes. In the Atlantic, many waves form over West Africa — partly in association with the African easterly jet and complexes of thunderstorms over the continent — and travel across the ocean toward the Caribbean and the Americas.

Role in tropical cyclones

A large proportion of Atlantic tropical cyclones, including most major hurricanes, develop from tropical waves. Given sufficient moisture, warm sea-surface temperatures and low wind shear, the convection associated with a wave can organise into a tropical depression and then a storm. Many waves, however, produce only unsettled weather and never develop further.
