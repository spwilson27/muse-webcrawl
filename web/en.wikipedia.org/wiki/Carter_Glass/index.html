Carter Glass

Carter Glass (January 4, 1858 – May 28, 1946) was an American newspaper publisher and Democratic politician from Lynchburg, Virginia. He represented Virginia in both houses of the United States Congress and served as Secretary of the Treasury under President Woodrow Wilson from 1918 to 1920.

Early life and career

Glass was born in Lynchburg and entered the newspaper business as a young man, eventually owning the Lynchburg News and the Daily Advance. He entered politics as a state senator and was a delegate to the Virginia constitutional convention of 1901–1902, where he helped design provisions that effectively disenfranchised African American voters.

Congress and Treasury

Elected to the U.S. House of Representatives in 1902, Glass chaired the House Banking and Currency Committee and was the House sponsor of the Federal Reserve Act of 1913. As Secretary of the Treasury he oversaw the final Liberty Loan campaigns and the transition of the Federal Reserve System to peacetime. In 1920 he was appointed to the U.S. Senate, where he served until his death in 1946. He was the primary Senate author of the Glass–Steagall Act of 1933, which separated commercial and investment banking and created federal deposit insurance.

Later years

A fiscal conservative and states'-rights Democrat, Glass broke with President Franklin D. Roosevelt over New Deal measures and the 1937 court-packing plan. He died in Washington, D.C., in 1946.
