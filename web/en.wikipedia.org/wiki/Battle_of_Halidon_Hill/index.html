The Battle of Halidon Hill was fought on 19 July 1333 between English forces under King Edward III and a Scots army led by Sir Archibald Douglas. It was a crushing English victory and a landmark in medieval warfare for its demonstration of the power of the English longbow.

Background

Edward III had renewed English claims over Scotland and was besieging Berwick. Douglas marched to relieve the town and was forced to attack the English in a strong position on Halidon Hill.

The battle

The Scots advanced uphill across marshy ground into massed archery fire. Whole ranks were shot down before reaching the English men-at-arms; among the Scottish dead were the Earl of Ross, the Earl of Sutherland and thousands of common soldiers. Douglas himself was killed.

Aftermath

Berwick surrendered and was permanently annexed to England. The battle's tactics — dismounted men-at-arms flanked by longbowmen — foreshadowed English successes in the Hundred Years' War.
