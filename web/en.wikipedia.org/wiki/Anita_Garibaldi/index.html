Anita Garibaldi

Ana Maria de Jesus Ribeiro di Garibaldi, best known as Anita Garibaldi (30 August 1821 – 4 August 1849), was a Brazilian revolutionary and the wife and comrade-in-arms of the Italian revolutionary Giuseppe Garibaldi. She fought alongside him in the Ragamuffin War in southern Brazil, in Uruguay, and in the Italian wars of independence, and is celebrated in both Brazil and Italy as the "Heroine of the Two Worlds".

Life in South America

Born in Laguna, Santa Catarina, to a poor family of Azorean Portuguese descent, she was married at fourteen to Manuel Duarte de Aguiar. She met Garibaldi in 1839, when he was fighting for the separatist Riograndense Republic, and joined him aboard his ship; she fought in the battles of Imbituba and Laguna, learned to ride and shoot, and was captured and escaped during the battle of Curitibanos after searching the battlefield for Garibaldi's body. The couple married in Montevideo in 1842 and had four children. In Uruguay she took part in the defence of Montevideo and in Garibaldi's Italian Legion campaigns.

Italy and death

In 1848 Anita followed Garibaldi to Italy for the First Italian War of Independence. In 1849 she joined the defence of the Roman Republic against French forces, and during the retreat from Rome, pregnant and ill with malaria, she died near Ravenna on 4 August 1849. Her remains were eventually reburied in Rome in 1932, where an equestrian monument on the Janiculum Hill commemorates her, overlooking the city near Garibaldi's own statue.

Legacy

Anita Garibaldi is a national heroine in Brazil—her name is borne by municipalities in Santa Catarina—and an enduring symbol of the Risorgimento in Italy. Her life has inspired novels, films, and monuments in both countries.
