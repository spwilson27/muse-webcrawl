Willie Hugh Nelson (born April 29, 1933) is an American singer, guitarist and songwriter. He was one of the main figures of the outlaw country subgenre that developed in the late 1960s as a reaction to the conservative restrictions of the Nashville sound. The critical success of his album Shotgun Willie (1973), combined with the commercial success of Red Headed Stranger (1975) and Stardust (1978), made Nelson one of the most recognised artists in country music.

Nelson wrote standards such as "Crazy" (a hit for Patsy Cline), "Funny How Time Slips Away" and "On the Road Again". He co-founded Farm Aid in 1985, acted in more than thirty films, and is known for his activism, his braided hair, and his battered classical guitar, Trigger, which he has played since 1969.

Early life

Nelson was born in Abbott, Texas, during the Great Depression and raised by his grandparents after his parents separated. He wrote his first song at age seven and joined his first band at ten, playing gospel and polka dance halls around Hill County.

Songwriter in Nashville

After stints as a radio DJ and a move to Nashville in 1960, Nelson found success as a songwriter: Faron Young took "Hello Walls" to number one, and Patsy Cline's recording of "Crazy" (1961) became a standard. His own singing career, constrained by Nashville production conventions, lagged behind.

Outlaw country and stardom

Retiring briefly to Texas in the early 1970s, Nelson grew his hair, settled in Austin's countercultural music scene, and recorded for Atlantic Records. Red Headed Stranger, a sparse concept album Columbia doubted, topped the country charts and made him a star. With Waylon Jennings, Johnny Cash and Kris Kristofferson he later formed the supergroup The Highwaymen. His annual Fourth of July Picnic became a Texas institution.

Later career

Nelson has released more than 100 studio albums, won twelve Grammy Awards, and duetted across generations and genres. In 1990 the IRS seized his assets over a $32 million tax bill; he settled with the novelty album The IRS Tapes: Who'll Buy My Memories?. He was inducted into the Country Music Hall of Fame in 1993 and the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2023.

Activism and personal life

Nelson co-founded Farm Aid with Neil Young and John Mellencamp to support family farmers, advocates for cannabis legalisation and biofuels, and has campaigned for animal welfare. He lives at his Luck Ranch near Spicewood, Texas.
