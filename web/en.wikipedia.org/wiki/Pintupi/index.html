Pintupi

Overview

The Pintupi are an Aboriginal Australian people of the Western Desert, whose country lies around Lake Mackay and the Gibson Desert of Western Australia and the Northern Territory. They are part of the Western Desert cultural bloc and speak Pintupi-Luritja, a Western Desert language. Communities include Kintore (Walungurru), Kiwirrkurra and Papunya, where the Western Desert art movement began in 1971–72 with the Papunya Tula artists — the spark of the contemporary Aboriginal acrylic painting movement.

The Pintupi Nine

In 1984 a family group of Pintupi who had been living traditionally in the Gibson Desert walked into Kiwirrkurra — among the last Aboriginal people in Australia to make first contact with settler society. The "Pintupi Nine" story is a landmark of 20th-century Australian history; several of the women later became notable painters. Most Pintupi had been removed to missions and settlements (Haasts Bluff, Papunya) in the mid-20th century, and many returned to homelands in the outstation movement of the 1970s–80s.
