Canada–United States Free Trade Agreement

The Canada–United States Free Trade Agreement (CUSFTA), commonly called the Free Trade Agreement (FTA) in Canada, was a trade agreement between Canada and the United States signed on 2 January 1988 and brought into force on 1 January 1989. It eliminated a wide range of tariffs and other trade barriers between the two countries over a ten-year period and established mechanisms for resolving trade disputes.

Background

Free trade with the United States had been a recurring question in Canadian politics since the nineteenth century; a reciprocity agreement had been negotiated in 1911 but was rejected by Canadian voters. In 1985 the Royal Commission on the Economic Union (the Macdonald Commission) recommended that Canada seek comprehensive free trade with the United States. Prime Minister Brian Mulroney's government opened negotiations in 1986, led on the Canadian side by Simon Reisman, and an agreement in principle was reached in October 1987.

Provisions

The agreement phased out tariffs on goods in stages ending in 1998, liberalised trade in services and investment, opened government procurement, and included special arrangements for energy and agriculture. Its most innovative feature was a binational panel system for reviewing antidumping and countervailing-duty decisions (Chapter 19), together with a general dispute-settlement mechanism (Chapter 18).

Political controversy

The FTA was bitterly contested in Canada. Opponents, including the Liberal and New Democratic parties and nationalist and labour groups, warned of job losses, pressure on social programmes and erosion of Canadian sovereignty and culture. The 1988 federal election was fought largely over the agreement; the Progressive Conservatives won a majority and implemented it. In the United States the agreement passed Congress with broad support.

Legacy

The FTA was superseded in 1994 by the North American Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA), which extended the framework to Mexico; NAFTA was in turn replaced by the Canada–United States–Mexico Agreement (CUSMA/USMCA) in 2020. Bilateral trade expanded substantially under the agreement, though economists continue to debate its effects on Canadian manufacturing and productivity.
