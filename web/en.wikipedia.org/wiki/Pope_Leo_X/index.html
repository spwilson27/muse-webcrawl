Pope Leo X (Italian: Leone X; born Giovanni di Lorenzo de' Medici, 11 December 1475 – 1 December 1521) was head of the Catholic Church from 9 March 1513 until his death in December 1521. The second son of Lorenzo the Magnificent, he is known for granting indulgences to fund the rebuilding of St. Peter's Basilica — a practice that helped provoke Martin Luther's Ninety-five Theses and the Protestant Reformation — and for his lavish patronage of Renaissance art and learning.

Early life

Giovanni de' Medici was groomed for the Church from childhood, receiving benefices as a boy and being made a cardinal at thirteen, though he only took up the post publicly three years later. After the Medici were expelled from Florence in 1494, he travelled in Europe; the family returned to power in Florence in 1512, and Giovanni was elected pope the following year, at 37, not yet ordained a priest.

Pontificate

Leo presided over the close of the Fifth Lateran Council and pursued Italian politics, securing Medici rule in Florence and placing his nephew in Urbino after a costly war. His promotion of indulgence preaching in Germany, organised in part through Albert of Mainz and the Fugger banking house, prompted Luther's protest in 1517. Leo condemned Luther's teachings in the bull Exsurge Domine (1520) and excommunicated him in Decet Romanum Pontificem (1521).

A great patron, Leo continued the rebuilding of St. Peter's, supported Raphael and the Vatican workshops, fostered Greek studies, and made Rome a centre of Renaissance culture — expenditures that severely strained the papal treasury.
