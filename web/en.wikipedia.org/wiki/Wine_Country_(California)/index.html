Wine Country (California)

Vineyards in Napa Valley, part of California's Wine Country

Wine Country is the region of Northern California, in the United States, known worldwide as a premier wine-growing region. The region is famed for its wineries, its cuisine, Michelin star restaurants, boutique luxury hotels, luxury resorts, historic architecture, and culture. Viticulture and wine-making have been practiced in the region since the Spanish missionaries from Mission San Francisco Solano established the first vineyards in 1825.

There are over 1,700 wineries in the North Coast American Viticultural Area alone, which covers more than six counties, and more than 400 in Napa Valley itself. The best known parts of Wine Country are the Napa Valley and Sonoma Valley in Napa and Sonoma counties respectively, though the term is also applied to parts of Mendocino and Lake counties, and the wine regions have expanded into other areas of Northern California as well.

History

The earliest vineyards in the area were planted by Spanish missionaries in the early 19th century. Commercial winemaking began in earnest in the 1850s, with pioneers such as Agoston Haraszthy, who founded Buena Vista Winery in Sonoma in 1857 and is often called the father of California wine. The industry suffered setbacks from phylloxera in the late 19th century and from Prohibition (1920–1933), and by the mid-20th century the region's wines were little known internationally.

The turning point came at the Paris Wine Tasting of 1976, often called the "Judgment of Paris," when California wines from Stag's Leap Wine Cellars and Château Montelena defeated French wines in blind tastings, establishing the region's reputation for world-class wine. Robert Mondavi, who founded his winery in Oakville in 1966, was a central figure in promoting Napa Valley wines and improving quality standards.

Geography and appellations

Wine Country lies north of San Francisco Bay, in a landscape of valleys, rolling hills, and coastal mountains. Marine air and fog from the Pacific cool the region at night, producing a long growing season favorable to wine grapes. The North Coast AVA encompasses grape-growing regions in six counties, and within Napa Valley there are sub-appellations including Stags Leap District, Oakville, Rutherford, Howell Mountain, and Los Carneros.

Principal grape varieties include Cabernet Sauvignon, Chardonnay, Merlot, Pinot noir, Sauvignon blanc, and Zinfandel. Sonoma County, with its greater climatic diversity, produces a wider range of styles, while Napa Valley is most famous for its Cabernet Sauvignon.

Tourism

Wine tourism is a major part of the regional economy, drawing millions of visitors annually to tasting rooms, wine trains, spas, and restaurants. The town of Yountville is known for restaurants such as The French Laundry, and the Napa Valley Wine Train carries visitors through the valley. Harvest season in the autumn, known as "crush," is a peak period of activity and tourism.
