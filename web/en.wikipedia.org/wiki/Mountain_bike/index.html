Mountain bike

A mountain bike (MTB) is a bicycle designed for off-road cycling. Mountain bikes share similarities with other bicycles but incorporate features to enhance durability and performance on rough terrain: wide, knobbly tyres, suspension forks and often rear suspension, powerful brakes, lower gear ratios for steep climbs, and sturdy frames.

History

The sport emerged in the 1970s in Marin County, California, where riders such as Gary Fisher, Charlie Kelly and Joe Breeze raced modified cruisers down Mount Tamalpais. The first mass-produced mountain bike, the Specialized Stumpjumper, appeared in 1981. Mountain biking grew rapidly, entering the Olympic Games in 1996 as a cross-country event.

Types and disciplines

Modern categories include cross-country (XC), trail, enduro, downhill and dirt jumping bikes, distinguished by suspension travel and geometry. Wheel sizes of 27.5-inch ("650b") and 29-inch ("29er") dominate, and electrically assisted e-MTBs have become widespread. Drivetrains commonly use a single chainring with a wide-range cassette, and dropper seatposts allow on-the-fly saddle adjustment.
