Sushen

The forested river valleys of the Amur and Songhua

The Sushen were an ancient people mentioned in Chinese historical records as living in the far north-east, in the region of modern Manchuria and the Russian Far East. They are among the earliest peoples recorded in the region and are often regarded by historians as ancestors, or among the ancestors, of the Tungusic peoples, including the Jurchen and Manchu.

In Chinese records

The Sushen appear in early texts such as the Book of Documents tradition and later dynastic histories. A famous account describes Sushen envoys presenting tribute of arrows made from husun wood with stone arrowheads to the court of King Cheng of Zhou (11th century BC); a near-identical tribute story is retold for later dynasties, and Han-dynasty records place the Sushen beyond the frontier of the commanderies in the north-east. The Records of the Three Kingdoms describes them as hunters and archers living in the cold forests east of Fuyu (Buyeo).

Identity and successors

Scholars commonly link the Sushen with the later Yilou of the Han–Wei period, the Wuji and Mohe of the Sui–Tang era, and ultimately the Jurchen who founded the Jin dynasty (1115–1234) — a chain of names that Chinese historiography applied to successive Tungusic groups in the same region. Archaeological cultures of the Amur and Ussuri valleys, known for pig husbandry, hunting, and distinctive pottery, are candidates for the material remains of these peoples, though direct identification remains debated.
