Prabhakaran, leader of the LTTE

Velupillai Prabhakaran (Tamil: வேலுப்பிள்ளை பிரபாகரன்; 26 November 1954 – 18 May 2009) was a Sri Lankan Tamil guerrilla leader and the founder and supreme leader of the Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam (LTTE), a militant organisation that sought to create an independent Tamil state, Tamil Eelam, in the north and east of Sri Lanka. Under his leadership the LTTE waged a civil war against the Sri Lankan state for more than three decades.

Early life

Prabhakaran was born in the coastal town of Valvettithurai on the Jaffna Peninsula, the youngest of four children. Influenced by Tamil nationalist politics and angered by discrimination against Tamils, he became involved in militant activism as a teenager. In 1975 he assassinated Alfred Duraiappah, the mayor of Jaffna — often described as the first major political assassination by a Tamil militant.

Founding of the LTTE

In 1976 Prabhakaran founded the Tamil New Tigers, soon renamed the Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam. He built the LTTE into a highly disciplined, centralised organisation with land forces, the Sea Tigers naval wing, the Air Tigers light aircraft unit, the elite Black Tigers suicide cadre, and an international network for fundraising and arms procurement. He eliminated rival Tamil militant groups and consolidated sole control over the Tamil insurgency.

Civil war

After the anti-Tamil pogroms of July 1983, the conflict escalated into full-scale civil war. The LTTE fought the Sri Lankan armed forces, and from 1987 to 1990 also fought the Indian Peace Keeping Force sent under the Indo-Lanka Accord. The LTTE was blamed for the assassinations of Indian prime minister Rajiv Gandhi (1991) and Sri Lankan president Ranasinghe Premadasa (1993), and was designated a terrorist organisation by more than 30 countries. A Norwegian-brokered ceasefire in 2002 gave the LTTE control of a de facto state in the north and east, but the truce collapsed and war resumed in 2006.

Death

In the final offensive of 2008–2009, the Sri Lankan military recaptured all LTTE-held territory. Prabhakaran was killed on 18 May 2009 near the Nanthikadal lagoon at Mullivaikkal, and the government declared the war over the next day, ending 26 years of conflict.

Legacy

Prabhakaran remains a deeply polarising figure: revered by many Tamils as a national hero and freedom fighter, and regarded by others — including governments that proscribed the LTTE — as a terrorist responsible for suicide bombings, child soldier recruitment, and the murder of political opponents.
