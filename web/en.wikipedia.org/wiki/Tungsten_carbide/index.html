Tungsten carbide

Tungsten carbide (chemical formula WC) is a chemical compound of tungsten and carbon, an extremely hard, dense ceramic material roughly twice as stiff as steel, with a Young's modulus around 530–700 GPa and a hardness rivalling diamond on some scales. It melts at about 2,870 °C.

Production and use

Tungsten carbide is produced by reacting tungsten metal powder with carbon at high temperature. In industry it is usually combined with a cobalt binder in a cemented carbide (hardmetal), made by powder metallurgy. Cemented carbides are used for cutting tools, drill bits, mining and tunnelling equipment, dies, wear parts, and ammunition cores. Tungsten carbide rings are popular in jewellery for their scratch resistance.

Properties

WC has a hexagonal crystal structure and very low electrical resistivity for a ceramic. Its extreme hardness comes with brittleness, which the metallic binder in cemented carbides helps to offset.
