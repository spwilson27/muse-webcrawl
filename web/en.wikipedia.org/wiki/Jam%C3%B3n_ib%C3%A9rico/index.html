Jamón ibérico

TypeCured ham
Place of originSpain and Portugal
Main ingredientIberian pig hind leg
Curing time12–48 months

Jamón ibérico ("Iberian ham") is a variety of cured leg of pork produced in Spain and Portugal from the Iberian pig. It is considered one of the finest cured hams in the world, particularly the grade known as jamón ibérico de bellota, made from free-range pigs fattened on acorns (bellotas) in oak parklands called dehesas.

Production

After slaughter, the hind legs are salted, washed, and hung to dry and mature in cellars (bodegas) for between one and four years. During the final fattening period known as the montanera, the best pigs roam oak forests and consume large quantities of fallen acorns, which gives the fat a distinctive oleic-acid-rich, nutty flavour and a texture that melts at room temperature.

Classification

Spanish labelling law classifies jamón ibérico by the pig's breed purity and diet, indicated by coloured seals: black for 100% Iberian acorn-fed ham, red for crossbred acorn-fed, green for free-range cereal-fed, and white for intensively farmed cereal-fed. Protected designations of origin include Jabugo, Guijuelo, Dehesa de Extremadura and Los Pedroches.
