Epaminondas

Epaminondas (c. 419/411 – 362 BC) was a Greek general and statesman of the 4th century BC who transformed the Ancient Greek state of Thebes, leading it out of Spartan subjugation into a pre-eminent position in Greek politics, called the Theban hegemony. His oblique phalanx tactics shattered the myth of Spartan military supremacy at the Battle of Leuctra (371 BC).

Life

Educated in philosophy by Lysis of Tarentum, Epaminondas lived modestly and incorruptibly. With his friend Pelopidas, commander of the Sacred Band, he helped liberate Thebes from a Spartan garrison in 379 BC and rebuilt the Boeotian League.

Leuctra and the invasions of the Peloponnese

At Leuctra he massed his left wing 50 shields deep against the Spartan right, winning a decisive victory that killed King Cleombrotus and hundreds of Spartiates. He then invaded Laconia — the first foreign army to do so in living memory — and liberated Messenia, founding Messene and crippling Sparta's helot economy permanently.

Death

Epaminondas was killed at the Battle of Mantinea (362 BC) even as his army won. Ancient writers including Xenophon and Diodorus praised him; Cicero called him the first man of Greece. With his death, Theban hegemony collapsed, leaving the vacuum Philip of Macedon would fill.
