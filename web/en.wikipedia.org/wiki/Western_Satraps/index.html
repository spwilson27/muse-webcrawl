Western Satraps

The Western Satraps, or Western Kshatrapas, were Saka rulers of western and central India, governing territories corresponding roughly to modern Gujarat, Maharashtra, Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh, from about AD 35 to AD 405. Some 27 independent kings are known, belonging mainly to two families, the Bhadramukha (or Kardamaka) and the Kshaharata dynasties.

History

The Kshaharatas, including Bhumaka and Nahapana, rose first; Nahapana was defeated by the Satavahana king Gautamiputra Satakarni around AD 124. The Kardamaka line founded by Chastana then became dominant. The dynasty's most famous ruler, Rudradaman I (c. AD 130–150), issued the celebrated Sanskrit Junagadh rock inscription recording repairs to the Sudarshana lake. The Western Satraps fought repeated wars with the Satavahanas and later the Guptas, until Chandragupta II conquered their kingdom around AD 395–405.

Coinage

The Western Satraps issued abundant silver coins modelled on Indo-Greek and Roman prototypes, bearing the king's portrait with legends in Greek, Brahmi and Kharoshthi scripts; they are important dated evidence for the political history of ancient India.
