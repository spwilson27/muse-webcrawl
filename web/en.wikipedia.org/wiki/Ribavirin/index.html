Ribavirin

Ribavirin, also known as tribavirin, is an antiviral medication used to treat respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) infection, hepatitis C, and some viral hemorrhagic fevers. For hepatitis C it was historically used in combination with interferon-based regimens; for RSV, an aerosol form is used in severe cases in infants. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Mechanism

Ribavirin is a guanosine analogue that interferes with viral RNA synthesis. Proposed mechanisms include inhibition of inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase (depleting guanine nucleotides), direct inhibition of viral RNA polymerase, and lethal mutagenesis of viral genomes through error catastrophe.

Side effects

Major adverse effects include hemolytic anemia and teratogenicity; ribavirin is strictly contraindicated in pregnancy. Other effects include fatigue, nausea, rash, and cough when inhaled.
