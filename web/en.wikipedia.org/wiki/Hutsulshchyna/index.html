Hutsulshchyna

Hutsulshchyna (Ukrainian: Гуцульщина) is the ethnographic and historical region of the Ukrainian Carpathians inhabited by the Hutsuls, an ethnic group of Ukrainian highlanders. The region lies mainly in the south of Ivano-Frankivsk Oblast and adjoining parts of Chernivtsi and Zakarpattia oblasts, with a portion extending across the border into northern Romania.

Geography

Hutsulshchyna is a mountainous area of the Eastern Carpathians, drained by the upper Prut, Cheremosh and Tysa rivers. Its best-known town is Yaremche, and the historic centre of Hutsul life includes villages such as Verkhovyna, Kosiv and Kryvorivnia. The region contains some of Ukraine's highest peaks, including Hoverla.

Culture

Hutsul culture is distinguished by its wooden architecture, including distinctive wooden churches and houses; richly decorated woven textiles and embroidery; woodcarving and metalwork; the long alpine horn called the trembita; and a pastoral economy based on sheep and cattle grazing on high mountain pastures (polonynas). The region's dialect forms part of the south-western group of Ukrainian dialects. The Hutsul dialect and customs were famously depicted in Mykhailo Kotsiubynsky's novella Shadows of Forgotten Ancestors (1911) and Sergei Parajanov's 1965 film of the same name.
