ODAP

ODAP (oxalyldiaminopropionic acid), systematically β-N-oxalyl-L-α,β-diaminopropionic acid and commonly abbreviated β-ODAP, is a neurotoxic amino acid found in the seeds of the grass pea, Lathyrus sativus. It is the causative agent of neurolathyrism, a condition of irreversible spastic paralysis of the legs that occurs in people who rely heavily on grass pea as a staple food during droughts and famines.


Chemical name: β-N-oxalyl-L-α,β-diaminopropionic acid
Source: Grass pea (Lathyrus sativus) seeds

Mechanism and effects

β-ODAP is structurally similar to the neurotransmitter glutamate and acts as an agonist at AMPA-type glutamate receptors. Sustained overactivation causes excitotoxic damage to upper motor neurons in the spinal cord, producing stiffness and weakness of the legs that may progress to permanent paralysis. Symptoms typically follow months of a diet in which grass pea supplies a large fraction of calories, as has occurred historically in India, Bangladesh, Ethiopia, and parts of Europe during famines.

Context

Grass pea is an exceptionally hardy, drought-resistant crop, which makes it a famine food of last resort; the tension between its agronomic resilience and its toxicity has motivated breeding programmes aimed at low-ODAP varieties, as well as food preparation methods — soaking, boiling, and fermenting — that reduce the toxin's concentration.
