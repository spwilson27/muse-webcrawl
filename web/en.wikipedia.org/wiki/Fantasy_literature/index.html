Fantasy literature

Fantasy literature is literature set in an imaginary universe, often but not always without any locations, events, or people from the real world. Magic, the supernatural, and magical creatures are common in many of these imaginary worlds.

History

Fantastic elements appear in epics such as Gilgamesh and The Odyssey, medieval romance, and fairy tales. Modern fantasy formed in the 19th and early 20th centuries with George MacDonald, William Morris, and Lord Dunsany, and was transformed by J. R. R. Tolkien's The Hobbit (1937) and The Lord of the Rings (1954–55) and C. S. Lewis's Narnia books.

Subgenres

High fantasy, low fantasy, sword and sorcery, urban fantasy, and dark fantasy are prominent subgenres. Notable modern authors include Ursula K. Le Guin, Robert Jordan, Brandon Sanderson, George R. R. Martin, and N. K. Jemisin. See Fantasy and Speculative fiction.
