House of Khalifa

The House of Khalifa (Arabic: آل خليفة, romanized: ʾĀl Khalīfa) is the ruling family of the Kingdom of Bahrain. The Al Khalifa are Sunni Muslims belonging to the Utub tribal confederation, which migrated from the Arabian mainland to Kuwait and then to the peninsula of Qatar before conquering Bahrain in 1783. Members of the family have ruled Bahrain continuously ever since.

Conquest of Bahrain

In 1783 Ahmed bin Muhammad Al Khalifa, known as Ahmed Al Fateh ("Ahmed the Conqueror"), led the Utub in taking Bahrain from Persian control. The Al Khalifa ruled from Zubarah on the Qatari coast and later from Riffa and Muharraq in Bahrain itself. During the 19th century the family entered into treaty relations with Britain, and Bahrain became a British protected state under agreements of 1861, 1880 and 1892, a status that lasted until independence in 1971.

Modern Bahrain

Isa bin Ali Al Khalifa ruled for over six decades (1869–1932). His successors oversaw the discovery of oil in 1932 — the first on the Arab side of the Persian Gulf — and the development of the modern Bahraini state. Sheikh Isa bin Salman Al Khalifa led Bahrain to independence in 1971 as an emirate. His son, Hamad bin Isa Al Khalifa, succeeded in 1999, proclaimed Bahrain a kingdom in 2002, and introduced a new constitution. The king's uncle, Khalifa bin Salman Al Khalifa, served as prime minister from 1970 until his death in 2020, making him one of the longest-serving prime ministers in the world; he was succeeded by Crown Prince Salman bin Hamad Al Khalifa.

Family and government

Members of the Al Khalifa occupy many senior positions in the Bahraini government, armed forces and economy, a concentration of power that has been a central issue in Bahraini politics, including during the protests of 2011. The family's ancestral homes include Riffa Fort and the palaces at Sakhir and Riffa.
