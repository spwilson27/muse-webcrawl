Sukumara Kurup (born Gopalakrishnan Sukumara Kurup) is an Indian fugitive, notorious for one of the longest-running manhunts in the history of Kerala police. In 1984, Kurup, who worked in Abu Dhabi, allegedly staged his own death in order to claim a life-insurance payout of about ₹8 lakh (800,000 rupees). He is accused of murdering Chacko, a film representative who resembled him, and burning the body in Kurup's car near Karuvatta, Alappuzha, on the night of 21–22 January 1984 to pass the corpse off as his own.

The case

The investigation quickly unravelled when the body was identified as Chacko's and police found inconsistencies in the story; Kurup fled before he could be arrested. Several accomplices, including Kurup's brother-in-law Bhaskara Pillai, his driver Ponnappan, and his friend Shahu, were tried and convicted, while Kurup himself was declared a proclaimed offender and has never been caught. He has remained on the Kerala Police's most-wanted list for four decades, with reported "sightings" periodically surfacing in Indian media, though none has led to an arrest.

In popular culture

The case has inspired several Malayalam films, including Pinneyum (2016) and Kurup (2021), a fictionalised biographical crime thriller starring Dulquer Salmaan, which renewed public interest in the case.
