The All India Kisan Sabha (AIKS; All India Peasants' Union) is a peasants' movement and farmers' organisation in India. It was founded on 11 April 1936 at Lucknow, with Swami Sahajanand Saraswati as its first president and N. G. Ranga as general secretary, to mobilise India's peasantry against landlordism and colonial rule.

History

The Kisan Sabha movement grew out of provincial peasant organisations of the 1920s and 1930s, including the Bihar Provincial Kisan Sabha founded by Sahajanand in 1929. The founding session at Lucknow, held alongside the Indian National Congress session, adopted a Kisan Manifesto demanding abolition of the zamindari system, reduction of land revenue and rent, and relief from rural debt, which influenced the Congress agrarian programme at Faizpur.

After independence, the AIKS was closely associated with the Communist Party of India and its successors; following the communist split of 1964, there came to be two organisations bearing the name — one affiliated with the CPI and one with the CPI(M) — both claiming descent from the original Sabha. The AIKS played a major role in peasant struggles including the Tebhaga movement in Bengal and the Telangana armed struggle. In recent decades the Kisan Sabhas have been prominent in farmers' agitations, including the 2020–21 Indian farmers' protest against the farm laws, in which they participated as part of the Samyukt Kisan Morcha coalition.

Organisation

The organisations claim memberships in the millions, concentrated in states such as Kerala, West Bengal, Bihar, Maharashtra and Punjab, and publish journals and hold regular national conferences.
