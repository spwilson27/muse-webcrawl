Astrological age

Astrological age
DurationAbout 2,160 years per age (one twelfth of the ~25,800-year precession cycle)

An astrological age is a period in astrological theory, roughly 2,160 years long, determined by the slow precession of the equinoxes. Because the vernal equinox point drifts westward along the ecliptic through the zodiacal constellations over a cycle of about 25,800 years, it spends roughly one twelfth of that time — about 2,160 years — in each sign. Astrologers hold that each age is characterised by the qualities of its sign and that major civilisational trends reflect the age in progress.

The ages in history

Commonly proposed correspondences include the Age of Taurus (the bull cults and agricultural civilisations of the ancient Near East and Egypt), the Age of Aries (the era of the ram — sacrificial cults, the Exodus narrative and iron-age empires), and the Age of Pisces, roughly coinciding with the Christian era, whose fish symbolism figures prominently in early Christianity. The anticipated Age of Aquarius became a popular cultural motif in the twentieth century — through the musical Hair ("the dawning of the Age of Aquarius") and New Age thought — associated with ideals of humanitarianism, technology and spiritual renewal.

When do ages begin?

There is no consensus on the exact dates of transition, because the boundaries of the constellations are irregular and astrologers differ on whether to use sign boundaries or constellation boundaries. Proposed start dates for the Age of Aquarius range across several centuries, from the fifteenth century to the thirty-sixth; the astronomical (constellation-based) transition is often placed around the 26th century. The concept itself rests on observations known since Hipparchus measured precession in the second century BC, though its astrological elaboration is largely modern.
