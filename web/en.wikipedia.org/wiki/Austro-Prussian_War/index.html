Austro-Prussian War (Seven Weeks' War)
Date14 June – 23 August 1866
LocationBohemia, Germany, Italy, Adriatic Sea
BelligerentsPrussia, Kingdom of Italy and allies vs. Austrian Empire and allies
ResultPrussian–Italian victory; Peace of Prague; dissolution of the German Confederation

The Austro-Prussian War, also known as the Seven Weeks' War, was a war fought in 1866 between the Austrian Empire and the Kingdom of Prussia, with each also being aided by various allies within the German Confederation. Prussia also allied with the Kingdom of Italy, linking this conflict to the Third Independence War of Italian unification.

The decisive Battle of Königgrätz, 1866.

Background

The war grew out of the rivalry between Austria and Prussia for leadership of the German states, sharpened by the dispute over the administration of Schleswig and Holstein after the Second Schleswig War (1864). Prussian Chancellor Otto von Bismarck manoeuvred Austria into appearing the aggressor and secured the neutrality of France and the alliance of Italy.

Course

Prussian armies under Helmuth von Moltke used railways, the telegraph and breech-loading needle guns to defeat Austria's German allies and invade Bohemia. The war was decided at the Battle of Königgrätz (Sadowa) on 3 July 1866, one of the largest battles of the century. Italy, though beaten at Custoza and at sea near Lissa, still gained Venetia through the settlement.

Aftermath

The Peace of Prague (23 August 1866) dissolved the German Confederation, excluded Austria from German affairs, and let Prussia annex Hanover, Nassau, Hesse-Kassel, Frankfurt and the Elbe duchies and form the North German Confederation — a decisive step toward the unification of Germany in 1871.
