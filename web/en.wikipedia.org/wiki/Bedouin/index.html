Bedouin

The Bedouin (from Arabic badawī, 'desert dwellers') are pastorally nomadic Arab tribes who have historically inhabited the desert regions in the Arabian Peninsula, North Africa, the Levant and Mesopotamia.

RegionsArabian Peninsula, Sinai, Negev, Sahara
LanguageBedouin Arabic dialects
LivelihoodCamel and goat herding

Life and society

Bedouin society is tribal and kin-based, organised in clans, valuing hospitality, honour and oral poetry. Seasonal migration followed water and pasture for camels, sheep and goats — a classic form of pastoralism.

Modern era

During the 20th century many Bedouin settled, voluntarily or under government pressure, in villages and cities in Saudi Arabia, Jordan, Israel, Egypt and Syria, while retaining distinct identity and customs.
