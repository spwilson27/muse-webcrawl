Dahalik language

Dahalik (also spelled Dahaalik, Dahalik, or Dahlik) is an Afroasiatic language of the Semitic branch spoken on the Dahlak Archipelago of Eritrea in the Red Sea. It is spoken by roughly 2,500–3,000 people, principally on the islands of Dahlak Kebir, Nora, and Dehil. Dahalik was only brought to the attention of the wider linguistic community in the 1990s and was formally described in the 2000s, notably by the Eritrean linguist Saleh Mahmud Idris and the French linguist Marie-Claude Simeone-Senelle.

Classification and features

Dahalik belongs to the North Ethiosemitic group and is most closely related to Tigre and Tigrinya, though it is not mutually intelligible with either. It retains some archaic Semitic features lost in its relatives, alongside influences in vocabulary from Arabic and Afar arising from the islanders' maritime trading history. Unlike other Ethiopian Semitic languages, Dahalik is traditionally written in the Arabic script when written at all, though it remains primarily an oral language.

Status

The language is considered endangered: younger speakers increasingly shift to Arabic or Tigrinya, and Dahalik is not used as a medium of instruction in schools. Documentation efforts since the 2000s have produced the first dictionaries and grammatical descriptions.
