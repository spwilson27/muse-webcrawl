Focke-Wulf Fw 189

The Focke-Wulf Fw 189 Uhu (Eagle Owl) was a German twin-engine, twin-boom, three-seat tactical reconnaissance and army cooperation aircraft used by the Luftwaffe during the Second World War, noted for its extensively glazed central nacelle.

Design and service

Designed by Kurt Tank and first flown in 1938, the Fw 189 entered service in 1941 and proved rugged, manoeuvrable, and popular with crews, especially on the Eastern Front.

Its twin Argus engines and twin-boom layout gave excellent visibility for observation, artillery spotting, and light bombing.

Production

About 864 were built, including production in occupied France; the type survived in small numbers postwar in other air forces.
