The rugged jebel of Dhofar was the heartland of the rebellion.

The Dhofar Rebellion, also known as the Dhofar War, was an insurgency in the province of Dhofar in Oman from 1963 to 1976. It began as a revolt by the Dhofar Liberation Front (DLF) against the repressive rule of Sultan Said bin Taimur and his neglect of Dhofar, and developed into a Marxist-led insurgency under the Popular Front for the Liberation of the Occupied Arabian Gulf (PFLOAG), supported by South Yemen, the Soviet Union, and China.

Course of the war

The rebellion began in 1963 and escalated after 1967, when the British withdrawal from Aden gave the rebels a sanctuary and supply base in South Yemen. In July 1970, the Sultan was deposed by his son Qaboos bin Said in a British-backed palace coup. Qaboos modernised Oman, abolished his father's restrictions, and pursued a counter-insurgency combining military operations with development — wells, schools, clinics, and amnesty — under the slogan of winning hearts and minds.

Foreign involvement

Britain, Oman's treaty ally, provided officers (including SAS personnel) seconded to the Sultan's Armed Forces; the Battle of Mirbat (1972), in which a small SAS team and local forces repelled a large rebel attack, became famous. Imperial Iran sent an expeditionary force from 1973, and Jordan also contributed troops and engineers. Major operations, including the construction of fortified lines across the jebel, progressively cut rebel supply routes.

Outcome

By late 1975 the rebels were militarily defeated, and Sultan Qaboos declared the war over on 20 March 1976. The conflict is widely studied as a successful counter-insurgency campaign.
