Fiberboard

Fiberboard (American English) or fibreboard (British English) is a type of engineered wood product made from wood fibres. Types include particle board, medium-density fiberboard (MDF) and hardboard. Fibreboard is sometimes used as a synonym for particle board, but strictly it refers to products made by felting and pressing wood fibres together, with or without added adhesives.

Types and density classes

Fibreboard is classified by density: low-density fibreboard (insulation board), medium-density fibreboard (MDF) and high-density fibreboard (hardboard, such as Masonite). In the wet process, fibres are formed into a mat in water and pressed, relying on the natural lignin in the wood for bonding; in the dry process, resins are added. Products are used in construction (sheathing, insulation, underlayment), furniture manufacture and packaging.

History

Fibreboard was first made in England in the late 19th century, and the first commercial plant in the United States was established in the 1920s. Mass production of hardboard followed William Mason's invention of Masonite in 1924.
