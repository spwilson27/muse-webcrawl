Human body weight

Human body weight is a person's mass or weight. Body weight is measured in kilograms throughout most of the world, and in stones and pounds in the United States and, informally, the United Kingdom. Average adult weights vary widely by population, sex, nutrition and socioeconomic conditions; global estimates put the average adult human mass at about 62 kg, with regional averages ranging from under 50 kg in parts of Asia to around 80 kg in North America.

Measurement and indices

Weight is measured with scales, ideally at a consistent time of day. The body mass index (BMI), defined as mass in kilograms divided by the square of height in metres, is the standard screening measure relating weight to height: the World Health Organization classifies adult BMI below 18.5 as underweight, 18.5–24.9 as normal, 25–29.9 as overweight and 30 and above as obesity. BMI is a population-level tool and does not directly measure body composition; athletes and others with high muscle mass may be misclassified.

Variation and health

Children's weight is assessed against growth charts of weight-for-age and weight-for-height percentiles. Changes in weight reflect the balance of energy intake and expenditure; both excess weight and underweight are associated with increased risks of disease, including type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease and certain cancers at the high end, and immune suppression and osteoporosis at the low end. Ideal body weight formulas — such as the Devine formula used in medicine dosing — estimate a target weight from height and sex. Record extremes include Jon Brower Minnoch, the heaviest recorded human, who weighed approximately 635 kg (1,400 lb) at his peak.
