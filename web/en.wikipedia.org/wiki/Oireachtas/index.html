Leinster House, Dublin, home of the Oireachtas since 1922.

The Oireachtas is the legislature of Ireland. Under the Constitution of Ireland it consists of the President of Ireland and two houses: Dáil Éireann, the directly elected lower house, and Seanad Éireann, the upper house or senate. Its sole power of making laws for the state is vested in the Oireachtas, subject to the Constitution.

Composition

Dáil Éireann is elected by proportional representation (single transferable vote) from multi-seat constituencies and is by far the more powerful house: the government (Taoiseach and ministers) is nominated by and accountable to the Dáil, and money bills originate there. Seanad Éireann has 60 members — 43 elected by vocational panels, six elected by university graduates, and eleven nominated by the Taoiseach — and can delay, but not ultimately block, most legislation.

Functions

Bills must generally pass both houses and be signed by the President to become law as Acts of the Oireachtas. The Oireachtas also scrutinises the executive through question time and an extensive committee system, approves budgets, and may refer certain bills to the people in a referendum. It sits in Leinster House in Dublin, a former ducal palace acquired by the state in the 1920s.

History

The Oireachtas succeeded the revolutionary Dáil of 1919–1922 and was first constituted under the Irish Free State in 1922, before being placed on its present constitutional footing by Bunreacht na hÉireann in 1937.
