Mars Cube One (MarCO) was a NASA mission consisting of two small satellites, MarCO-A and MarCO-B, that flew to Mars alongside the InSight lander in 2018. Built in the 6U CubeSat form factor — each about the size of a briefcase — they were the first CubeSats to operate in deep space, and they successfully relayed InSight's landing telemetry to Earth in near real time.

Mission

Launched on 5 May 2018 aboard the same Atlas V rocket as InSight, the two CubeSats separated and flew independently to Mars, making their own trajectory corrections. During InSight's entry, descent and landing on 26 November 2018, they received the lander's UHF signals and retransmitted them to Earth via experimental high-gain antennas, providing coverage that would otherwise have required waiting for an orbiter relay. MarCO-B also returned a parting image of Mars as it flew past.

Design and outcome

Each spacecraft carried a flat-panel reflectarray high-gain antenna, a miniature X-band radio, cold-gas propulsion and deployable solar panels. The nicknames "Wall-E" and "Eva", after the Pixar film characters, referenced the thruster valve sound likened to Wall-E's. Contact was lost in early January 2019 as the craft receded from the Sun; the mission, costing about $18.5 million, was declared complete and is considered a highly successful technology demonstration that opened the way for small spacecraft on interplanetary missions.
