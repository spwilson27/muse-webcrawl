Neodymium magnet

Neodymium magnet
Composition: Nd2Fe14B
Maximum energy product: up to ~52 MGOe
Curie temperature: 310–400 °C
Invented: 1984 (Sumitomo / General Motors)

A neodymium magnet (also known as an NdFeB, NIB or Neo magnet) is the most widely used type of rare-earth magnet. It is a permanent magnet made from an alloy of neodymium, iron and boron forming the tetragonal crystalline structure Nd2Fe14B. Developed independently in 1984 by General Motors and Sumitomo Special Metals, neodymium magnets are the strongest type of permanent magnet commercially available.

Properties

Neodymium magnets have replaced other magnet types in many applications requiring strong permanent magnets in compact volumes, including cordless tool motors, hard disk drives, magnetic fasteners, wind turbine generators and headphones. Their strength allows smaller, lighter magnets to do the work of larger ferrite magnets.

Manufacture and grades

Magnets are produced by powder metallurgy (sintered) or by rapid solidification (bonded). Grades are designated by their maximum energy product, from N35 to N52, with letters indicating operating temperature ratings. The material is brittle and vulnerable to corrosion, so magnets are typically plated with nickel, zinc or epoxy.

Hazards

The strong forces between larger magnets can pinch skin, fracture brittle magnets into sharp shards, and pose a serious intestinal hazard if small magnets are swallowed.
