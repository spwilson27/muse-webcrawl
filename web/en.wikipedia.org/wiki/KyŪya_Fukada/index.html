Kyūya Fukada (深田 久弥, Fukada Kyūya, 11 March 1903 – 21 March 1978) was a Japanese writer and mountaineer active during the Shōwa period. He is best known for his 1964 book Nihon Hyaku-meizan ("100 Famous Japanese Mountains"), which popularised the idea of climbing a selected set of one hundred notable Japanese peaks.

Life

Fukada was born in what is now Kaga, Ishikawa Prefecture. He developed a love of mountains while a student, climbing extensively in the Japanese Alps and elsewhere in Japan. He studied at Tokyo Imperial University but left without graduating to pursue writing. Alongside his mountaineering literature he was an accomplished fiction writer in his early career, before concentrating on essays about mountains, travel and the people who climb them.

100 Famous Japanese Mountains

In Nihon Hyaku-meizan, based on his own climbs, Fukada selected one hundred mountains according to their elevation, natural beauty, distinctive character and place in Japanese history and culture, devoting an essay to each. The book received the Yomiuri Prize and became a classic of Japanese mountain literature; his list helped inspire the modern Japanese hiking boom and the pursuit of "completing" the hundred summits. He died of a stroke in 1978 while climbing Mount Mao in Yamanashi Prefecture.
