5 (five) is a number, numeral and digit. It is the natural number following 4 and preceding 6, and is a prime number. It is the third prime number, after 2 and 3, and the only prime that is the sum of two consecutive primes (2 + 3). Humans, and many other animals, have five digits on each hand and foot, which is probably why base-10 counting systems became dominant.

In mathematics

Five is the third prime number and the first good prime. It is a Fermat prime (22 + 1), a safe prime, a Wilson prime, and the only prime that belongs to two pairs of twin primes (in (3, 5) and (5, 7)). The five Platonic solids are the only convex regular polyhedra; the simplest is the tetrahedron with four faces, and there are exactly five such solids in three dimensions. The Abel–Ruffini theorem states that polynomial equations of degree five or higher cannot in general be solved by radicals. In graph theory, the complete graph K5 is the smallest non-planar complete graph, central to Kuratowski's theorem.

In the Fibonacci sequence, 5 is both a Fibonacci number and, with 8, part of the only non-trivial pair in which one is twice the other plus one. A pentagon has five sides, and the pentagram has been a significant symbol in mathematics and culture since antiquity.

In science

Five is the atomic number of boron. Most starfish exhibit pentaradial symmetry with five arms. In human anatomy, there are five senses in the classical enumeration (sight, hearing, smell, taste and touch) and five digits on each limb. Weather services rate hurricanes on the five-category Saffir–Simpson scale and tornadoes on the Enhanced Fujita scale, both topping out at category/EF5.

In culture

The number five holds significance in many religions: Islam has Five Pillars, the Torah consists of five books (the Pentateuch), and the Sikh symbol and baptismal articles include five items. The Olympic rings are five interlaced rings representing inhabited continents. In music, a quintet has five performers, and the pentatonic scale has five notes per octave.
