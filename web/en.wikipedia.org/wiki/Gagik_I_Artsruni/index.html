Gagik I Artsruni (Armenian: Գագիկ Ա Արծրունի; died 936/937) was an Armenian prince of the Artsruni dynasty who ruled the kingdom of Vaspurakan, in south-eastern historic Armenia south of Lake Van, first as prince (904–908) and then as king (908–936/937). Renowned for his diplomatic skill, he manoeuvred between the Bagratid kings of Armenia, the Arab emirs of Azerbaijan, and the Abbasid Caliphate, raising Vaspurakan to its greatest extent and prosperity.

Reign

Gagik inherited the principality of Vaspurakan after a period of division among the Artsruni princes. Through alliances — sealing marriages with the Bagratids and submitting nominally to the Sajid emir Yusuf ibn Abi'l-Saj when expedient — he unified the Artsruni lands and extended his authority over neighbouring districts. In 908 the caliph al-Muqtadir recognised him as king, a counterweight to the Bagratid king Smbat I. Gagik subsequently reconciled with Smbat's successor Ashot II and maintained Vaspurakan's autonomy for the rest of his reign.

Building works

Gagik's most famous foundation is the palatine Church of the Holy Cross on the island of Aghtamar in Lake Van, built between 915 and 921 by the architect-monk Manuel as part of a royal complex including a palace and harbour. The church is celebrated for its extensive exterior relief sculpture depicting biblical and royal scenes, including a celebrated image of Gagik presenting a model of the church to Christ.

Legacy

The chief source for Gagik's reign is the History of the House of the Artsrunik by Thomas Artsruni, continued by anonymous authors. Under Gagik, Vaspurakan enjoyed a cultural flowering; his reign is regarded as the kingdom's golden age before its eventual cession to the Byzantine Empire in 1021 under his descendant Senekerim-Hovhannes.
