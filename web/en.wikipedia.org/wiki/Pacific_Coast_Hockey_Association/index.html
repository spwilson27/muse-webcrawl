Pacific Coast Hockey Association

SportIce hockey
Founded1911
Ceased1926
CountriesCanada, United States

The Pacific Coast Hockey Association (PCHA) was a professional ice hockey league in western Canada and the northwestern United States that operated from 1912 to 1924. Founded by the Patrick brothers, Frank and Lester, it rivalled the eastern National Hockey Association and later the National Hockey League for players and for the Stanley Cup.

History

The Patricks built artificial-ice arenas in Vancouver and Victoria and stocked their league with star players lured from the east. PCHA innovations included numbered jerseys, the blue line, penalty shots, and allowing forward passing in the neutral zone — many later adopted by the NHL. PCHA teams won the Stanley Cup several times, including the Vancouver Millionaires in 1915 and the Seattle Metropolitans in 1917, the first American team to win the Cup.

Decline

Financial pressures led the league to merge operations with the Western Canada Hockey League in 1924, and the last PCHA franchises were sold to the NHL in 1926, ending major-league professional hockey on the west coast for decades.
