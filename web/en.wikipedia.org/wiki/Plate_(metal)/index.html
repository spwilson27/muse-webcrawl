Plate (metal)

Metal plate is a thick, flat piece of metal, distinguished from thinner sheet metal by its thickness. In steel industry conventions, plate is commonly defined as material over about 4.76 mm (3/16 in) thick, while sheet is thinner material. Plate is typically produced by hot rolling and is supplied in standard widths and lengths.

Production

Steel plate is rolled from slabs in plate mills or on reversing mills, then levelled, cut to size, and sometimes heat-treated (normalised, quenched and tempered) to achieve the required mechanical properties. Common grades include structural steels used in construction and pressure-vessel steels for boilers and storage tanks.

Uses

Plate is used for ship hulls and decks, bridges, pressure vessels, heavy machinery, armour plate, storage tanks, and the webs and flanges of large structural members. Thicker, hardened plates serve in mining and earth-moving equipment where abrasion resistance is required.

Related terms

Related flat-rolled products include strip, foil, and checker (tread) plate, which has a raised pattern for grip. "Plating" in other contexts refers to coating one metal with another.
