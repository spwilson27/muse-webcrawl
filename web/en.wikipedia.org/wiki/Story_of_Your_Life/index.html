"Story of Your Life" is a science fiction novella by American writer Ted Chiang, first published in the anthology Starlight 2 in 1998, and later collected in Chiang's Stories of Your Life and Others (2002). It won the Nebula Award for Best Novella in 1998 and the 1999 Theodore Sturgeon Award, and was nominated for the Hugo Award. The novella was adapted into the 2016 film Arrival, directed by Denis Villeneuve and starring Amy Adams.

Plot

The story is narrated by linguist Dr. Louise Banks as a letter addressed to her daughter. After alien spacecraft appear around the world, Banks is recruited by the United States military to communicate with the aliens, called heptapods because of their seven limbs. Working with physicist Gary Donnelly, she studies the heptapods' spoken language and their circular written language, Heptapod B, whose semagrams are written as complete wholes rather than in sequential order.

As Banks learns Heptapod B, she begins to perceive time the way the heptapods do: simultaneously, rather than sequentially. Her narration interleaves the contact story with scenes from the life of her daughter — who, the story gradually reveals, has not yet been born and will die at the age of 25. Knowing the whole of her future, Banks nonetheless chooses to live it, marrying Donnelly and having the child. The story explores themes of determinism, free will, and the Sapir–Whorf hypothesis that language shapes thought.

Themes

The novella contrasts sequential, causal human cognition with the heptapods' simultaneous mode of awareness, framed through variational principles in physics such as Fermat's principle of least time, which can be described either causally or teleologically. Chiang has said the story arose from his interest in these principles and in the experience of knowing how something will end while still experiencing it.

Adaptation

The film Arrival (2016), with a screenplay by Eric Heisserer, adapts the novella and received critical acclaim, winning the Academy Award for Best Sound Editing and earning a nomination for Best Picture.
